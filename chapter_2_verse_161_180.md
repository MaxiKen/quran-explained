## Verse 161

Verse 161 says: *"Surely those who disbelieve and die as disbelievers are condemned by Allah, the angels, and all of humanity."*

In simple terms, the verse states the fate of the ones who persist in disbelief to the end: surely those who disbelieve and die as disbelievers are condemned by Allah, the angels, and all of humanity.

The verse's condition is the point: "those who disbelieve and die as disbelievers." The verse does not condemn the one who disbelieves and then believes; the verse condemns the one who remains in the disbelief until death. The two acts — the disbelief and the dying upon it — are the state that is condemned. Ibn Kathir: Allah states that those who disbelieve in Him and remain in this state until they die — it is upon them that the curse falls. The verse's severity is the severity of the end: the door of the repentance is open in life (2:160); the verse speaks of the one who leaves the door closed.

The curse is named in three: the curse of Allah, of the angels, and of all humanity — combined (*ajma'in*). The commentators explain the layers: the curse of Allah is His removal of the person from His mercy; the curse of the angels is their supplication against him; the curse of mankind is the general condemnation. Ibn Abbas explains the sense: the believers' prayers against each other end up as the curses upon these people. Al-Jalalayn: they deserve the curse in this life and the next; "people" here is said to be either people in general or a specific reference. The verse's three-fold curse is the declaration of the universal rejection: the one who rejects the Lord is rejected by the Lord, by the creation, and by the human conscience.

The verse also completes the preceding passage: 2:159–160 had spoken of those who concealed the Book and their exception — the repentance; the verse now speaks of the ones who not only concealed but persisted in the disbelief to the end: for them the curse. The commentators note (Ibn Kathir) that the verse establishes that it is lawful to curse the disbelievers — and that the scholars differ over cursing a specific disbeliever whose end is not known, because we do not know how Allah will make his end; as for the disbelievers as a whole, the curse is lawful. The careful distinction: the verse itself curses the state — the disbelief and the dying upon it; it is the state that is the object.

In modern life, the verse is the statement of the seriousness of the final state. The religion of the Qur'an is not a sentiment that may be exchanged at any time; the condition of the heart at the end is the condition of the account. The verse's three-fold curse is not the language of an enemy; it is the verdict of the reality: the one who lived in the rejection of the truth and died in it is rejected by the truth — by the Lord, by the creation, and by the conscience that knows. And the verse's warning is paired with the verse before it: the door of the repentance is open — the verse is the reason to enter the door.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 162

Verse 162 says: *"They will be in Hell forever. Their punishment will not be lightened, nor will they be delayed ˹from it˺."*

In simple terms, the verse states the permanence of the punishment: they will be in Hell forever. Their punishment will not be lightened, nor will they be delayed from it.

The verse's first clause is the permanence: *khalidina fiha* — "they will abide therein" — the endless abiding in the Fire. The commentators read the word with the full weight: the eternal curse until the Day of Resurrection, and after that, in the Fire of Jahannam, where their punishment will neither be lightened — *la yukhaffaf 'anhum* — nor will they be reprieved — *wa la hum yunzarun*. Ibn Kathir: the torment will not be changed or tempered for even an hour; rather, it is continuous and eternal. The verse's permanence is the permanence the verses of the Qur'an state for the end of the ones who die in the rejection: the door of the world is closed, the door of the regret is closed, and the punishment of the state is not a passing punishment.

"Their punishment will not be lightened" — the negative is the prophecy of the human hope: the hope that the punishment, however severe, will be softened, that the severity will be reduced, that the torment will be eased with time. The verse says: no. And "nor will they be delayed from it" — *la hum yunzarun* — the hope of the reprieve: the delay, the respite, the postponement, the chance. The verse says: no. The two denials are the denial of the two hopes of the human heart in the punishment: the hope of the mercy that lessens and the hope of the delay that postpones.

The verse's position is exact: it follows the verse of the curse (2:161) and completes the sentence: the curse is the removal; the Fire is the place; the permanence is the duration; the punishment neither lightened nor delayed. The passage is the full description of the end of the rejection: from the state of the disbeliever to the curse, from the curse to the Fire, from the Fire to the permanence.

In modern life, the verse is the statement of the finality that the modern world tries to soften and relativize. The modern mind is reluctant to speak of the finality of the punishment — the reluctance to think of the eternal severity. The verse is the Qur'an's honest statement: the end of the persistent rejection is the Fire, and the Fire is the permanent. The verse's purpose, however, is not the despair — it is the urgency: the verse of the permanence stands beside the verse of the repentance (2:160), and the two together form the message: the door is open now, and the end is final.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 163

Verse 163 says: *"Your God is ˹only˺ One God. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Most Compassionate, Most Merciful."*

In simple terms, the verse states the foundation of the whole religion: your God is only One God. There is no god worthy of worship except Him — the Most Compassionate, Most Merciful.

The verse is the second statement of the tawhid in the surah (cf. 2:255 — the verse of the Throne, which begins with the same la ilaha illa Huwa), and it states the doctrine in its most concise form. Ibn Kathir: Allah mentions that He is the only deity, and that He has no partners or equals. He is Allah, the One and Only, the Sustainer, and there is no deity worthy of worship except Him. The verse's grammar is the emphasis: the negation of the entire class — there is no god — followed by the exception — except Him. The verse of the one God: the God of the believers, of the People of the Book, of all humanity — the One.

"The Most Compassionate, Most Merciful" — the verse closes with the two names: *al-Rahman al-Rahim*. The naming is deliberate: the God of the One is the God of the mercy. The Qur'an has been establishing, through the whole passage — the creation, the provision, the punishment, the curse, the Fire — the reality of the One: and the One is the Rahman, the Rahim: the One whose mercy covers the creation, whose compassion is the source of the provision, whose mercy is offered in the repentance (2:160) and whose severity is the consequence of the rejection. Ibn Kathir notes that the greatest name of Allah is contained in these two ayat: the verse here and 2:255, and he quotes the statement concerning the two verses: "And your God is One God... Alif-Lam-Mim. Allah! La ilaha illa Huwa, the Ever Living, the Sustainer."

The verse also answers the context: the People of the Book had disputed over the direction, over the prophets, over the religion; the verse brings the dispute down to its base: the God is one — the God of them all, the One. The plurality of the directions, the plurality of the communities' claims, the plurality of the prophets — the One behind them all. The verse is the ground of the whole argument: the dispute is not over who the God is; the dispute is over the worship of the One.

In modern life, the verse is the creed in its simplest form: there is no god except Allah. The verse's meaning in the modern world: the "gods" of the modern world — the money, the power, the nation, the self, the desire, the systems — the verse says the God is the One, and the worship that is not the worship of the One is the worship of the false. The verse is not a metaphysical claim for the philosophers; it is the orientation of the whole life: the one God, the one orientation, the one worship.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 164

Verse 164 says: *"Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth; the alternation of the day and the night; the ships that sail the sea for the benefit of humanity; the rain sent down by Allah from the skies, reviving the earth after its death; the scattering of all kinds of creatures throughout; the shifting of the winds; and the clouds drifting between the heavens and the earth—˹in all of this˺ are surely signs for people of understanding."*

In simple terms, the verse turns to the signs of the One God in the creation: indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth; the alternation of the day and the night; the ships that sail the sea; the rain that revives the dead earth; the creatures scattered; the winds; the clouds — in all of this are surely signs for people of understanding.

The verse is the evidence for the verse of the One God: the One is known by the signs of His creation. The verse lists the signs in an ordered sequence: the heavens and the earth — the frame; the alternation of day and night — the rhythm; the ships — the use of the sea; the rain — the life of the earth; the creatures — the dispersal of the life; the winds and clouds — the movement of the atmosphere. The sequence is the world: from the largest to the most intimate — the heavens, the earth, the day, the night, the sea, the rain, the life, the winds, the clouds. And every sign is the sign of the One: no sign points to many; the signs all point to the One who made them all.

The commentators read each sign with its wisdom. Ibn Kathir and the classical tafsir note the ships — which sail through the sea with that which benefits people — as the proof of the One who gives the water the property of bearing the ships, who harnesses the sea for the trade and the provision of humanity. The rain: the One who sends the water from the sky and makes the earth alive after its death — the resurrection in the sign: the dead earth, the rain, the life — the sign of the resurrection and the One who raises. The winds: the One who turns them — sometimes from the east, sometimes from the west, sometimes carrying the mercy, sometimes the torment. The clouds: the ones *musakhkhar* — made subservient — between the heaven and the earth: the obedient clouds, the controlled weather, the subservient sky.

The verse closes with the qualification: "signs for people of understanding" — *li-qawmin ya'qilun* — for the people who use the intellect, who reflect, who understand. The sign is not the sign for everyone: the sign is given, and the understanding is the condition. The farmer sees the rain as the luck; the believer sees the rain as the sign. The crew sees the wind as the condition; the believer sees the wind as the controlled mercy. The verse's "people of understanding" is the definition: the signs are for the ones who use the reason the Creator gave them — the answer to the demand for the sign (the People of the Book had asked for a sign, the commentators note the context): the signs are the creation, and the understanding is the response.

In modern life, the verse is the Qur'an's invitation to the scientific wonder. The modern person, with the telescope and the microscope, the satellite and the laboratory, sees the same signs — the heavens, the earth, the winds, the clouds, the life — and the verse says: the signs are the signs of the One; the sign is the creation; the understanding is the response. The modern person who has the knowledge without the recognition, who sees the order without the Ordainer, has the sign without the understanding — and the verse's purpose is exactly the reverse: the knowledge of the creation is the road to the knowledge of the Creator. The verse is the Qur'an's harmonization of the knowledge and the faith: the two are not in competition; the signs are for the understanding.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 165

Verse 165 says: *"Still there are some who take others as Allah's equal—they love them as they should love Allah—but the ˹true˺ believers love Allah even more. If only the wrongdoers could see the ˹horrible˺ punishment ˹awaiting them˺, they would certainly realize that all power belongs to Allah and that Allah is indeed severe in punishment."*

In simple terms, the verse reveals the nature of the false worship: still there are some who take others as Allah's equal — they love them as they should love Allah — but the true believers love Allah even more. If only the wrongdoers could see the punishment, they would realize that all power belongs to Allah and that He is severe in punishment.

The verse defines the *shirk* — the association — by the love. The ones who take others as the equals of Allah do not necessarily deny Allah; they associate the love: they love the other as they should love Allah. The verse's point is the definition of the worship: the worship is the love, and the false worship is the divided love. The one who sets the equal of Allah sets the equal in the affection, in the devotion, in the fear, in the hope, in the orientation — the love that belongs to the One is given to the many.

"But the true believers love Allah even more" — the verse's contrast: the believers love Allah with the love that exceeds. The verse's phrase *ashaddu hubban lillah* — "more intense in love for Allah" — is not a comparison merely in the degree; it is the doctrine of the true love: the believers' love is the love that has no rival. The false worshippers love their equals with the love that should be Allah's — and the believers love Allah with the love that is the love without the equal.

"If only the wrongdoers could see the punishment awaiting them" — the verse's second point: the wrongdoers would see at the punishment what they do not see now: that all power belongs to Allah and He is severe in punishment. The verse's "if only" is the expression of the missed understanding: the wrongdoers' love of the rivals is the misunderstanding of the power; they loved the false because they mistook the power; at the punishment, they will see: the power is Allah's — the only power, the absolute power, the severe punishment of the One.

The commentators note the context: the verse speaks of the polytheists and their idols, and the Qur'an uses the same language for every devotion that is given to what should be given to Allah. The verse's contrast between the love of the false and the love of the true is the essence of the religion: the religion of the believers is not the doctrine of the proof alone; it is the love of the One that exceeds the love of the all. Maududi's note: whoever ascribes to any being other than God any of the attributes of God, or recognizes the claim of anyone save God to be entitled to the rights over His creatures, is in fact setting up that being as a rival to God and placing him on the same plane as God.

In modern life, the verse is the examination of the false worship of the modern world. The modern person may not bow to an idol; but the verse's criterion is the love: the person who loves the money, the status, the nation, the power, the ideology, the self with the love that should be Allah's — has set the rival. The verse's test: what is loved with the all-consuming love? What is the devotion of the heart? And the verse's hope: the believers' love of Allah is the love beyond all loves — the love that centers the life, and the One who deserves the love is the One whose power is the only power, and whose punishment is the severe.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 166

Verse 166 says: *"˹Consider the Day˺ when those who misled others will disown their followers—when they face the torment—and the bonds that united them will be cut off."*

In simple terms, the verse states the scene of the Day: consider the Day when those who misled others will disown their followers — when they face the torment — and the bonds that united them will be cut off.

The verse's scene is the reversal of the false worship in the Hereafter. In the world, the followers trusted their leaders, idols, and false gods — they devoted themselves, they loved them, they relied on them (the love, the association of 2:165). The verse says: at the Day, the misleaders disown the followers — *tabarra'a* — the declaration of the innocence, the dissociation, the denial: "we are not responsible for you; we did not tell you to follow." The followers who followed are now disowned by the ones who led.

The commentators read the verse with the Qur'an's parallel statements: Ibn Kathir notes that the angels, whom the people claimed to worship, will declare their innocence in the Hereafter: "We declare our innocence (from them) before You. It was not us they worshipped" (28:63). The misleaders may be the leaders, the idols, the angels, the Shaitans — the entire class of the false objects of the devotion — and the verse states their common response at the torment: the disowning. When they face the torment — the verse says — they disown: the terror of the punishment is the moment of the dissociation; the ones who were followed in the ease now disown in the punishment.

"And the bonds that united them will be cut off" — the verse's closing: the *asbab* — the bonds, the connections, the ties — that united them will be cut. The verse is the end of the false union: the union of the followers and the leaders, the union of the sect and the sect, the union of the devotion and the object — the union that the false worship created is cut at the Day. Ibn Kathir and the classical tafsir note this as the total severance: the relations, the unions, the connections of the world that were built on the false worship are severed at the truth of the Day.

In modern life, the verse is the warning to the ones who follow and the ones who lead. The follower who gives the devotion of the heart to the leader, the movement, the ideology, the guru, the authority — the verse says the devotion's end is the disowning: the leader will not carry the follower; the ideology will not answer; the ties that were the comfort of the world are cut at the truth. And the verse is the warning to the leader who exploits the devotion — the one who takes the trust of the followers for the power and the gain — the disowning and the severance are the end. The verse's truth: the only union that survives the Day is the union with the One who is not a misleader — the union of the love that the believers reserve for Allah alone.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 167

Verse 167 says: *"The ˹misled˺ followers will cry, "If only we could have a second chance, we would disown them as they disowned us." And so Allah will make them remorseful of their misdeeds. And they will never ˹be able to˺ leave the Fire."*

In simple terms, the verse records the followers' cry: if only we could have a second chance, we would disown them as they disowned us. And so Allah will make them remorseful of their misdeeds. And they will never be able to leave the Fire.

The verse's function is the demonstration of the deception. The followers now see the truth — the misleaders disowned them, and the followers say: if only we could have a second chance, we would disown them — the same disowning. The verse's irony: the followers would do exactly what the misleaders did — the same denial, the same dissociation. The false relationship, the false devotion, the false union (2:166) is now the model of the followers' own supposed repentance: they would disown.

Ibn Kathir explains: this means "if we only had a chance to go back to the life so that we could disown them (their idols, leaders, etc.), shun their worship, ignore them and worship Allah Alone instead." But — Ibn Kathir adds — they utter a lie in this regard, because if they were given the chance to go back, they would only return to what they were prohibited from doing. The verse's point: the followers' cry is the cry of the regret, but it is not the proof of the truth of their intention: the regret is the exposure of the misdeeds, and the intention of the second chance is the projection of the same failure. The Qur'an elsewhere states the same: had they been returned, they would go back to what they were forbidden (6:28).

"And so Allah will make them remorseful of their misdeeds" — the verse: the remorse is the divine exposure. The word *hasarat* — the regrets, the sorrows, the "oh-ifs" — Allah shows them the misdeeds in the sorrow. The verse's point: their deeds are shown to them — the devotion to the false, the trust in the misleaders, the rejection of the truth — and the showing is the sorrow.

"And they will never be able to leave the Fire" — the verse's conclusion: the remorse is not the escape. The Fire is the permanence; the door of the world is closed; the regret is the truth of the exposure, and the exposure is the truth of the eternal state. The verse's progression: the misleaders disown (166), the followers cry for the second chance (167), the remorse is shown, the Fire is eternal.

In modern life, the verse is the examination of the second chance. The human heart lives on the dream of the second chance: if I could go back, I would do it differently. The verse's point: the second chance is not the guarantee of the change. The person who claims the second chance and then would return to the same misdeeds — the claim would be the lie. The verse's warning: the regret of the world is not the repentance; the regret that would return to the same failure is the proof of the failure; and the true repentance is the change in the life that is lived — the change that is not the projection of the would-be second chance but the actual turn in the only life that is given.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 168

Verse 168 says: *"O humanity! Eat from what is lawful and good on the earth and do not follow Satan's footsteps. He is truly your sworn enemy."*

In simple terms, the verse addresses all humanity: mankind is commanded to eat from what is lawful and good on the earth and not to follow the footsteps of Satan, who is truly the sworn enemy.

The verse's address — "O humanity!" (*ya ayyuha al-nas*) — is the universal address. The verse is not addressed to the believers or the People of the Book; it is addressed to the whole of mankind. The provision is the common provision; the world is the common world; the lawful and the good are for all. The verse's universalism: the religion of the Qur'an is not the religion of a community's provision; the provision of the earth is the provision of the Lord for all the children of Adam.

"Eat from what is lawful and good on the earth" — the verse's command has two conditions: *halal* (lawful) and *tayyib* (good, wholesome). The two are the Qur'an's standard of the consumption: the lawful — the thing not forbidden by the revelation; and the good — the thing pure, wholesome, not harmful to the body or the mind, not the product of the injustice or the exploitation. Ibn Kathir notes: He has allowed them to eat any of the pure lawful things on the earth that do not cause harm to the body or the mind. The verse is the answer to the prohibitions of the Jahiliyyah — the practice of the *bahirah*, the *sa'ibah*, the *wasilah*, the *ham* — the animals set free and consecrated to the idols, the arbitrary food taboos. Al-Jalalayn records the occasion: when some said that it was unlawful to take a camel that has been let loose.

"And do not follow Satan's footsteps" — the verse's second command: the *khutuwat* — the footsteps, the steps, the paths of Shaitan. Ibn Kathir, from Qatadah and As-Suddi: every act of disobedience to Allah is among the footsteps of Shaitan. The verse's metaphor: the footsteps are the incremental paths — the tempting steps, the small stages, the gradual leading — the path of the Shaitan is the path of the steps: not one leap, but the series of the steps that lead, step by step, into the disobedience. Ibn Abbas, as reported: any vow or oath made while angry is among the footsteps of Shaitan. The verse's command: do not follow the steps — the steps of the unlawful, the steps of the arbitrary prohibitions, the steps of the whisper.

"He is truly your sworn enemy" — the verse's closing: the enemy is the *'aduw* — the open enemy, the sworn enemy (the verse's final word is the emphasis). The Qur'an elsewhere: "Surely Shaitan is an enemy to you, so take (treat) him as an enemy" (35:6); "Will you then take him and his offspring as protectors and helpers rather than Me while they are enemies to you?" (18:50). The verse's warning: the enemy who does not appear as the enemy — the one who whispers the "small" steps, the one who dresses the disobedience in the respectability of the custom and the tradition and the "do not" of the false piety — is the enemy. And the warning's structure: the lawful and the good (the command) and the enemy (the warning) — the provision is the lawful and good; the enemy's trick is the forbidden dressed as the good.

In modern life, the verse is the foundation of the ethics of the consumption. The modern world consumes — and the verse's standard: lawfulness and goodness. The halal — the thing permitted by the religion; and the tayyib — the thing good, wholesome, clean, not the product of the exploitation, the cruelty, the corruption, the harm. And the verse's warning: the enemy's footsteps are the modern steps — the deliberate small compromises, the incremental acceptances, the series of the steps that lead from the lawful to the unlawful. Ibn Kathir records the hadith (Muslim and at-Tirmidhi) of the man on the long journey — hair untidy, covered in dust, raising his hands to the sky, "O Lord! O Lord!" — yet his food from the unlawful, his drink from the unlawful, his clothes from the unlawful, nourished by the unlawful — how can the supplication be accepted? The verse: the lawful is the foundation of the whole relation with the Lord.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 169

Verse 169 says: *"He only incites you to commit evil and indecency, and to claim against Allah what you do not know."*

In simple terms, the verse states the content of the Shaitan's incitement: he only incites you to commit evil and indecency, and to claim against Allah what you do not know.

The verse's structure is the three levels of the incitement. The first: the *su'* — the evil, the harm, the wrong — every act of disobedience, as Qatadah and As-Suddi read the footsteps of the previous verse. The second: the *fahsha'* — the indecency, the shamefulness, the immorality — the greater evil, the acts of the open shamelessness. Ibn Kathir: "Your enemy, Satan, commands you to commit evil acts and what is worse than that, such as adultery and so forth." The third: the claim against Allah without knowledge — "and to say about Allah what you know not."

The verse's progression is the escalation: from the wrong (the act of disobedience) to the indecency (the shameless act) to the false claim against Allah (the lie about the religion, the fabricated command, the invented prohibition, the doctrine without knowledge). The third is the worst: the Shaitan's ultimate work is not only the sin; it is the religion of the sin — the claim about Allah that makes the sin appear as the command of Allah. The verse's "what you know not" is the boundary: the religion cannot be claimed without the knowledge; the claim about Allah — the halal and the haram, the doctrine, the law — must be the knowledge, not the invention. Ibn Kathir: this includes every innovator and disbeliever — the one who says about Allah without knowledge.

The verse's position in the passage: the previous verse commanded the lawful and the good and the non-following of the footsteps; the verse now reveals what the footsteps lead to: the evil, the indecency, and the false claims against Allah. The verse is the exposure of the enemy's program: the enemy's pattern is not the direct invitation to the denial — it is the incremental steps (168), the evil, the indecency, the false religion (169). And the verse answers the context of the whole passage: the Jewish scholars who concealed the Book and the Jews who practiced the arbitrary taboos and the idolaters and their prohibitions — the false claims against Allah were the product of the enemy's footsteps.

In modern life, the verse is the diagnostic of the false religion. The verse gives the three tests: the evil, the indecency, and the claim against Allah without the knowledge. The modern world has its false claims: the religions of the invention, the doctrines of the fabricated authorities, the halal and haram of the self-interest, the traditions that forbid what the revelation allows and allow what the revelation forbids, the preaching of the corruption. The verse's test: does the teaching produce the evil and indecency? Does the claim against Allah come from the knowledge? The verse's standard is the standard of the whole Qur'an: the religion of Allah is the knowledge of the revelation — not the invention of the enemy's steps.

## Verse 170

Verse 170 says: *"When it is said to them, "Follow what Allah has revealed," they reply, "No! We ˹only˺ follow what we found our forefathers practicing." ˹Would they still do so,˺ even if their forefathers had ˹absolutely˺ no understanding or guidance?"*

In simple terms, the verse exposes the argument of the blind imitation: when it is said to them, "Follow what Allah has revealed," they reply, "No! We only follow what we found our forefathers practicing." Would they still do so, even if their forefathers had absolutely no understanding or guidance?

The verse's dialogue is the record of the objection. The revelation comes: follow what Allah has revealed — the command, the qibla, the halal and the haram, the whole religion. The response: "No! We only follow what we found our forefathers practicing." The verse's *bal* — "No!" — is the emphatic negation: the rejection of the revelation in the name of the father. The commentators record the occasion: Ibn Ishaq from Ibn Abbas: this was revealed about a group of Jews whom the Messenger called to Islam, but they refused, saying, "Rather, we shall follow what we found our forefathers following." And the same pattern is recorded for the polytheists.

The verse's objection is the pattern of the tradition: the authority of the forefathers. The objection does not say the ancestors were right by the evidence; the objection says the ancestors were the ancestors. The verse's exposure: the "we found our forefathers practicing" is the claim of the mere ancestry — the tradition without the truth, the custom without the proof, the practice as the authority of the practice. Maududi: the only authority they have for their superstitious practices is that these have been the customs of their forefathers, and the foolish followers consider this a good authority.

"Would they still do so, even if their forefathers had absolutely no understanding or guidance?" — the verse's final question is the collapse of the argument. The ancestors who had no understanding — *la ya'qilun shay'an* — did not understand anything; and no guidance — *wa la yahtadun* — nor were they guided. The question: the tradition's authority is the authority of the tradition itself; and the ancestors — the founders of the tradition — themselves lacked both the understanding and the guidance. The verse's argument: the tradition is the chain of the ignorance — the ancestors had no understanding, and the descendants follow the ancestors. The question is the answer: the "follow our forefathers" is the argument that proves nothing; the ancestors' lack of understanding and guidance is the proof that the following of the ancestors is not the following of the truth. Ibn Kathir: they had no sound understanding or guidance.

In modern life, the verse is the challenge to every inherited authority. The modern person inherits: the family's religion, the culture's customs, the community's traditions, the authority of the elders, the practice of the ancestors. The verse's challenge: the authority of the ancestry is not the authority of the truth; the ancestors' understanding and guidance are the question. The believer is not commanded to abandon the tradition wholesale — the verse's command is to evaluate: the truth is the revelation, and the tradition is the tradition. The ancestors who had the understanding and the guidance (the prophets) are followed; the ancestors who had neither are the "we found" of the blind imitation. The verse's test: what did the ancestors follow — the command of Allah, or the custom of the age?

## Verse 171

Verse 171 says: *"The example of the disbelievers ˹not responding to the Messenger's warning˺ is like a flock not comprehending the calls and cries of the shepherd. ˹They are wilfully˺ deaf, dumb and blind so they have no understanding."*

In simple terms, the verse gives the parable of the disbelievers: the example of the disbelievers not responding to the Messenger's warning is like a flock not comprehending the calls and cries of the shepherd. They are wilfully deaf, dumb and blind so they have no understanding.

The verse's parable is the shepherd and the flock. The Messenger calls — the call of the guidance, the warning, the recitation, the command. The flock — the disbelievers — hear the sound but not the meaning: they have no understanding. The shepherd's call is the richest message — the guidance, the salvation, the whole of the truth — and the flock hears only the sound. The verse's point: the disbelievers hear the Messenger, but the hearing does not become the understanding; the call is lost in the sound.

The commentators note the two aspects of the parable. Maududi: these people are like those herds of cattle which merely hear the call and cry of the drovers and move about — they do not understand the call's meaning. And Ibn Kathir: in their injustice, misguidance and ignorance, they are just like wandering animals, not understanding what they are told; if the shepherd heralds them or calls them to what benefits them, they would not understand what is actually being said to them, for they only hear unintelligible sounds. The parable's force: the Messenger's call is the most articulate, the most intelligible, the most beneficial — the call to the guidance — and the disbelievers respond with the same comprehension as the cattle.

"They are wilfully deaf, dumb and blind" — the verse's description is the three-fold sensory block: *summun* — deaf; *bukum* — dumb; *'umyun* — blind. Ibn Kathir: they are deaf, as they do not hear the truth; mute, as they do not utter it; and blind, as they do not see or recognize its path and way. The "wilfully" of the supplied translation captures the sense of the self-imposed block: the deafness is not the physical deafness; it is the refusal of the hearing; the dumbness is not the physical muteness; it is the refusal of the confession; the blindness is not the physical blindness; it is the refusal of the sight. The verse's three-fold block is the description of the heart that has shut the senses of the truth.

"And so they have no understanding" — the verse's conclusion: *fa hum la ya'qilun* — they do not comprehend. The verse's parable is one of the Qur'an's most powerful descriptions of the failure of the warning: the call is made, the call is heard, the call is lost. The verse also carries the responsibility: the shepherd's duty is the call; the flock's comprehension is not the shepherd's responsibility. The Messenger warned; the disbelievers did not understand.

In modern life, the verse is the description of the resistance to the guidance. The modern person hears the message — the call of the truth, the call of the religion, the call of the conscience — and the verse says: the hearing is not the understanding; the resistance to the call can shut the senses of the truth: the person can be deaf to the truth that is heard, dumb to the truth that should be said, blind to the truth that is seen. And the verse's hope: the block is not the destiny — the "wilfully" is the key; the senses of the truth can be reopened. The verse's warning: the flock's comprehension of the call is the measurement of the heart, not the measurement of the call.

## Verse 172

Verse 172 says: *"O believers! Eat from the good things We have provided for you. And give thanks to Allah if you ˹truly˺ worship Him ˹alone˺."*

In simple terms, the verse addresses the believers: O believers! Eat from the good things We have provided for you. And give thanks to Allah if you truly worship Him alone.

The verse's address — "O believers!" — repeats and specifies the command of 2:168 ("O humanity! Eat from what is lawful and good"). The universal command is now the command of the believers: the whole of humanity is commanded the lawful and good (168); the believers are addressed with the same command and the additional gift of the gratitude. The verse's structure: the provision and the gratitude.

"Eat from the good things We have provided for you" — the verse's command: the *tayyibat* — the good things, the pure, the wholesome, the lawful. Ibn Kathir: "O you who believe (in the Oneness of Allah — Islamic Monotheism)! Eat of the lawful things that We have provided you with, and be grateful to Allah, if it is indeed He Whom you worship." The command's context: the believers are commanded the good things; the way of the Shaitan (which the previous verses described — the evil, the indecency, the false claims) includes the arbitrary taboos: the forbidding of the good, the prohibition of what Allah allowed. The verse: the good things are provided; eat them.

"And give thanks to Allah if you truly worship Him alone" — the verse's second command: the gratitude. The verse's conditional — "if you truly worship Him alone" — is the link between the worship and the gratitude: the one who truly worships Him alone gives thanks to the One alone. The gratitude is the evidence of the true worship: the worshipers of the many gods thank the many; the worshipers of the One thank the One. The verse's command: the provision is the gift of the One; the gratitude is the recognition; the worship is the response.

The verse's deeper point (noted by the commentators and the Ma'arif tradition): the gratitude is not only the thanks with the tongue; the gratitude is the use of the gift in the way the Giver commanded. Eat the good things, use the provisions, and give the thanks — the thanks is the use of the provision for the purpose of the Giver: the strength for the obedience, the provision for the family, the wealth for the charity, the enjoyment within the lawful. The verse's command: the believer's whole consumption is the worship — the eating of the good is the obedience, and the gratitude is the worship.

In modern life, the verse is the religion of the ordinary. The verse is not about the extraordinary; it is about the daily: the food, the provision, the consumption, the gratitude. The modern person, in the abundance, forgets the Giver: the meals, the goods, the wealth, the comfort — the verse says: the good things are the provision of the One; the gratitude is the worship; the believer's daily life — eating, thanking, using the gifts — is the realm of the religion. And the verse's condition: the worship of the One alone — the person whose life is the worship of the many (the self, the wealth, the pleasure) cannot thank the One; the gratitude of the One is the worship of the One.

## Verse 173

Verse 173 says: *"He has only forbidden you ˹to eat˺ carrion, blood, swine, and what is slaughtered in the name of any other than Allah. But if someone is compelled by necessity —neither driven by desire nor exceeding immediate need—they will not be sinful. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful."*

In simple terms, the verse states the things forbidden: He has only forbidden you to eat carrion, blood, swine, and what is slaughtered in the name of any other than Allah. But if someone is compelled by necessity — neither driven by desire nor exceeding immediate need — they will not be sinful. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

The verse's list is the four prohibitions. The first: the *maitah* — the carrion, the dead animal, the animal that died without the proper slaughter — whether it died by strangling, a violent blow, a headlong fall, the goring of horns, or being partly eaten by a wild animal (Ibn Kathir). The second: the blood — the blood that is shed (the verse's prohibition of the consumption of the blood; and the Ma'arif tradition notes the absolute sense of the prohibition: the use, the buying and selling, the benefit). The third: the flesh of swine — the prohibition of the meat whether slaughtered or not, including its fat (Ibn Kathir: because the prohibition's term includes the fat, or by the inclusion, or by analogy). The fourth: what is slaughtered in the name of any other than Allah — the offering to other than Him, whether for monuments, idols, divination, or the practices of the Jahiliyyah.

The verse's opening — "He has only forbidden you" (*innama harrama*) — is the restriction: the forbidden is the limited and the named. The verse is the answer to the arbitrary prohibitions of the Jahliliyyah (the *bahirah*, the *sa'ibah*, the *wasilah*) and the elaborate taboos of the communities: the religion's prohibition is the named list, not the endless invention. Ibn Kathir: He has not prohibited anything except the dead animals — the list — the blood, the swine, and the offerings to other than Allah.

"But if someone is compelled by necessity — neither driven by desire nor exceeding immediate need — they will not be sinful" — the verse's exception: the necessity. The word *mudtar* — the compelled one — the one whose life is in danger: the hunger that reaches the point where he must either eat or die. The exception's two conditions: *ghayra baghin* — neither driven by desire — not the rebel who seeks the food for the pleasure, not the one who declares the unlawful lawful; and *wa la 'adin* — nor exceeding immediate need — not the one who eats beyond the need, not the transgression of the limit. Ibn Kathir: "without willful disobedience" — eating the dead animal and not continuing to do so; Qatadah: without transgressing by eating from the dead animals when the lawful is available. Ibn Kathir also notes the point of the jurists: when the one in dire straits finds both the dead animal and the food belonging to other people which he could get without risking loss of hands or harm, it is not allowed for him to eat the dead animal (the hadith of 'Abbad ibn Shurahbil, Ibn Majah). The verse's exception is the mercy in the extreme, and the exception is the narrow door: the necessity, the limit, and the intention.

"Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful" — the verse's close: the names of the mercy. The verse's "they will not be sinful" and the names: the *Ghafur* — the Forgiving — pardoning for what is eaten out of necessity (Sa'id ibn Jubayr); the *Rahim* — the Merciful — who allowed the prohibited during the times of necessity. The Ma'arif tradition makes the precise point: the prohibition remains; what is forgiven is the sin — the exception is not the making lawful; the *haram* remains *haram*; the compelled one is forgiven the sin of the use. The distinction matters: the verse does not say "the forbidden is now lawful" — it says "they will not be sinful" — the mercy covers the necessity; the law is not changed.

In modern life, the verse is the balance of the law and the mercy. The religion's prohibitions are the named, the limited, the clear; the religion's mercy is the exception of the necessity: the life that is saved is the priority. And the verse's lesson for the modern discussions of the rules: the prohibition is named, the exception is the narrow, and the intention (neither driven by desire nor exceeding the need) is the test. The believer's ethics: the lawful is the norm; the forbidden is the named; the necessity is the emergency; and the emergency is not the appetite.

## Verse 174

Verse 174 says: *"Indeed, those who hide Allah's revelations, trading them for a fleeting gain consume nothing but fire into their bellies. Allah will neither speak to them on the Day of Judgment, nor will He purify them. And they will suffer a painful punishment."*

In simple terms, the verse returns to the concealment of the Book: indeed, those who hide Allah's revelations, trading them for a fleeting gain consume nothing but fire into their bellies. Allah will neither speak to them on the Day of Judgment, nor will He purify them. And they will suffer a painful punishment.

The verse's subject is the same as 2:159: the concealment. Here the verse specifies the motive: the *thaman qalil* — the small price, the fleeting gain, the paltry profit: the Jewish scholars who concealed the descriptions of the Messenger in their Books for the worldly gain — the fear of losing the status, the bribes, the position, the small benefit of the world. Ibn Kathir: the joys and delights of this earthly life. The commentators record the occasion: this was revealed about Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, Huyyay ibn Akhtab and their like (Ibn Abbas, in the Tanwir tradition) — the ones who concealed the descriptions of the Messenger for the gain.

"Consume nothing but fire into their bellies" — the verse's image: the traders consume fire. The gain was the food, the provision, the benefit — the verse says: the gain is the fire; the profit that was eaten is the fire that is eaten. The verse's metaphor is the punishment in the form of the sin: the small price consumed; the fire consumed; the belly filled with the fire. Ibn Kathir: "they eat into their bellies nothing but fire."

"Allah will neither speak to them on the Day of Judgment, nor will He purify them" — the verse's second point: the silence and the rejection. The day of the account is the day of the speech: "the Day when the speaker speaks and the accounts are made" — and the verse says: Allah will not speak to them: the silence of the Lord is the severest of the rejections — the one addressed is not addressed; the one who hid the speech of Allah in the world is not spoken to by Allah at the Day. And the purification: He will not purify them — the *tazkiyah* — the cleansing from the sin, the declaration of the purity — denied. Ibn Kathir: "nor purify them" — they will not be pronounced pure. The verse's third: "they will suffer a painful punishment" — the *'adhab alim* — the painful torment.

In modern life, the verse is the warning to the traders of the truth. The truth, the knowledge, the revelation is not the commodity: the person who hides the truth for the gain — the scholar who suppresses the knowledge for the position; the authority who conceals the evidence for the profit; the media that withholds the fact for the interest — the verse's diagnosis: the trade is the trade of the fire; the gain consumed is the fire consumed; the silence of the Lord is the end. And the verse's pairing with 2:159–160: the concealment is the condemned act; the repentance and the disclosure (2:160) are the door: the trader of the truth can return — declare the truth, and the One who is All-Forgiving and Merciful turns.

## Verse 175

Verse 175 says: *"They are the ones who trade guidance for misguidance and forgiveness for punishment. How persistent are they in pursuit of the Fire!"*

In simple terms, the verse identifies the traders: they are the ones who trade guidance for misguidance and forgiveness for punishment. How persistent are they in pursuit of the Fire!

The verse's trade: the exchange. The two sides of the exchange: the guidance (*huda*) — the truth, the revelation, the knowledge of the Messenger — in exchange for the misguidance (*dalal*); and the forgiveness (*maghfirah*) — the pardon of the Lord — in exchange for the punishment (*'adhab*). The verse's point: the ones who concealed the Book (2:174) traded the higher for the lower: the guidance for the misguidance, the forgiveness for the punishment. Ibn Kathir: they opposed the guidance — not announcing the Prophet's description they found in their Books, the news about his prophecy and the good news of his coming which the previous Prophets proclaimed, as well as following and believing in him; instead, they preferred the misguidance by denying him, rejecting him and concealing his descriptions. They sold their souls for this little profit — the profit of the world, the small price of 174 — and preferred the little gain over the guidance and the truth, believing in the Messenger and having faith in what Allah sent him with. Therefore, they have profited failure and loss in this life and the Hereafter.

"How persistent are they in pursuit of the Fire!" — the verse's closing: *fa ma asbarahum 'ala al-nar* — "how patient/enduring are they upon the Fire!" The verse's phrase is the ironic amazement: the endurance — the *sabr* — that should be the patience of the believer (2:153) is here the endurance of the traders; the verse's amazement: they endure, they persist, they press on — toward the Fire. The verse's irony: the same word used for the beautiful patience of the believers (the *sabirin* of 2:155) is used for the terrible endurance of the traders: how enduring they are — in the pursuit of the Fire! Ibn Kathir: "How bold they are (for evil deeds which will push them) to the Fire."

The verse also completes the section: 2:159–175 — the concealment of the Book, the repentance (160), the curse (161–162), the One God and the signs (163–164), the love and the rivals (165), the Day of the disowning (166–167), the lawful and the forbidden (168–173), the traders (174–175). The section's verdict on the concealers: they have traded everything.

In modern life, the verse is the analysis of the world's exchanges. Every person trades: the time, the attention, the life — in exchange for the values. The verse's trade: the guidance for the misguidance, the forgiveness for the punishment. The modern world's trades are the same: the truth for the profit, the justice for the favor, the purity for the pleasure, the faith for the ease. The verse's question: what is being exchanged? And the verse's irony: the ones who pursue the Fire with the patience that should be the pursuit of the mercy: the endurance is given to the wrong object. The verse's wisdom: the exchange is the measurement; the one who trades the guidance for the misguidance has made the worst trade imaginable.

## Verse 176

Verse 176 says: *"That is because Allah has revealed the Book in truth. And surely those who differ regarding it are totally engrossed in opposition."*

In simple terms, the verse states the reason for the traders' fate: that is because Allah has revealed the Book in truth. And surely those who differ regarding it are totally engrossed in opposition.

The verse's "that is because" (*dhalika bi-anna*) connects the punishment (174–175) to the cause: the traders were punished because the Book was revealed in truth — the truth is the standard; the rejection of the truth is the punishment; the concealment of the revealed truth is the sin. Ibn Kathir: they deserve this painful torment because Allah has revealed Books to His Messenger Muhammad and the Prophets before him, and these revelations bring about truth and expose falsehood.

The verse's first clause is the nature of the revelation: the Book — the Qur'an and the Books — revealed *bi al-haqq* — in truth. The truth is not merely the content; the truth is the manner: the revelation is the truth from the Lord — the truth that does not change, the truth that exposes the false, the truth that is the standard. The verse's point: the revelation is the truth; the one who rejects the truth has rejected the standard; the punishment of the rejection is the punishment of the truth.

"And surely those who differ regarding it are totally engrossed in opposition" — the verse's second clause: the *mukhtalifun* — those who differ — regarding the Book — are in *shiqaq ba'id* — in far/utter opposition, the deep split, the total schism. The verse's point: the difference regarding the Book is not the innocent difference; it is the opposition: the ones who differ over the Book believe in parts of it while disbelieving in others, conceal it, twist it. Al-Jalalayn: they are at variance regarding it, believing in parts of it while disbelieving in others and concealing them. The difference of the scholars over the details is one thing; the difference of the rejection — the partial belief, the concealment, the opposition — is the *shiqaq* — the schism.

The verse's "totally engrossed" — *la-fi shiqaq ba'id* — is the depth: the opposition is not the superficial disagreement; it is the deep, the far, the settled opposition. The verse's standard: the Book is the truth; the difference over the Book — when the difference is the rejection — is the schism; and the schism's origin is the same origin as the traders' sin: the preference of the part over the whole, the concealment over the truth, the gain over the guidance.

In modern life, the verse is the distinction between the honest inquiry and the opposition. The struggle over the meaning of the Book — the honest difference, the scholarly debate, the careful interpretation — is not the verse's subject; the verse's subject is the rejection: the difference that believes in part and rejects the rest, that conceals the truth it knows, that is in the deep opposition. The verse's test: is the difference the pursuit of the truth — or the settled opposition? The believer is commanded the truth of the Book and warned the schism: the opposition to the truth is not a difference; it is the position of the opposition.

## Verse 177

Verse 177 says: *"Righteousness is not in turning your faces towards the east or the west. Rather, the righteous are those who believe in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Books, and the prophets; who give charity out of their cherished wealth to relatives, orphans, the poor, ˹needy˺ travellers, beggars, and for freeing captives; who establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and keep the pledges they make; and who are patient in times of suffering, adversity, and in ˹the heat of˺ battle. It is they who are true ˹in faith˺, and it is they who are mindful ˹of Allah˺."*

In simple terms, the verse presents the definition of righteousness: righteousness is not in turning your faces towards the east or the west. Rather, the righteous are those who believe in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Books, and the prophets; who give charity out of their cherished wealth to relatives, orphans, the poor, needy travellers, beggars, and for freeing captives; who establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and keep the pledges; and who are patient in suffering, adversity, and battle. It is they who are true in faith, and they who are mindful of Allah.

The verse is one of the Qur'an's most complete definitions of the religion. The commentators note its place: after the qibla commands (142–152), the verse now says: the direction is not the religion; the turning of the faces is not the righteousness. The direction of the prayer is a command — the verse does not abolish the qibla; the verse teaches: the qibla is one part; the righteousness is the whole. Ibn Kathir: facing the east or west does not necessitate righteousness or obedience unless it is legislated by Allah. The verse was also noted as the answer to the claims of the Jews and the Christians (Al-Jalalayn: revealed in response to the claim made by the Jews and the Christians to this effect) — the claim that the direction of their worship was the righteousness.

The verse's definition has three parts: the belief, the giving, and the practice. The belief: Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Books, the prophets — the six articles of the faith in their core. Ibn Kathir: believing in Allah, that He is the only God worthy of worship; the angels as the emissaries between Allah and His Messengers; the Books as the Divinely revealed Books from Allah to the Prophets, finalized by the most honorable Book — the Qur'an; the prophets; and the Last Day. The verse's belief is the completeness: the one who believes in Allah and the Last Day and the angels and the Books and the prophets — the believer of the whole creed.

The giving: "charity out of their cherished wealth" — the Arabic *'ala hubbihi* — "in spite of love for it": the wealth is loved, and the giving is the giving of the loved for the sake of the One who gave it. The recipients: relatives, orphans, the poor, needy travellers, beggars, and the freeing of captives. The list is the social order: the kin, the vulnerable, the destitute, the displaced, the askers, and the enslaved. The verse's "out of cherished wealth" is the heart of the charity: the giving is not the giving of the surplus; it is the giving of the loved.

The practice: the prayer, the alms-tax, the pledges. "Establish prayer" — the prayer established, not merely performed; "pay alms-tax" — the zakah; "keep the pledges they make" — the promises, the contracts. And the patience: "patient in times of suffering, adversity, and in the heat of battle" — the *ba'sa'* (the suffering), the *darra'* (the adversity), the *ba's* (the battle, the heat of the fighting). The verse's patience is the completion: the one who believes and gives and prays and pledges and is patient.

"It is they who are true in faith, and they who are mindful of Allah" — the verse's verdict: the *sadiqun* — the true ones, the ones who have been truthful in the faith; the *muttaqun* — the mindful, the God-fearing. The verse's definition is the verse of the religion in its complete form: the belief, the charity, the prayer, the giving, the patience — not the face toward the east or the west, but the whole of the faith.

In modern life, the verse is the correction of every reduction of the religion. The modern world has its reductions: the religion of the ritual only — the direction, the form, the ceremony; the religion of the belief only — the creed without the practice; the religion of the charity only — the giving without the belief. The verse says: the righteousness is the whole: the belief of the heart, the giving of the wealth, the establishment of the prayer, the payment of the alms, the keeping of the pledges, the patience in the trial. And the verse's test for the modern believer: which part of the definition is missing? The one who has the form without the faith, the faith without the charity, the charity without the patience — has not the righteousness. The verse's standard is the whole.

## Verse 178

Verse 178 says: *"O believers! ˹The law of˺ retaliation is set for you in cases of murder— a free man for a free man, a slave for a slave, and a female for a female. But if the offender is pardoned by the victim's guardian, then blood-money should be decided fairly and payment should be made courteously. This is a concession and a mercy from your Lord. But whoever transgresses after that will suffer a painful punishment."*

In simple terms, the verse establishes the law of retaliation: O believers! The law of retaliation is set for you in cases of murder — a free man for a free man, a slave for a slave, and a female for a female. But if the offender is pardoned by the victim's guardian, then blood-money should be decided fairly and payment made courteously. This is a concession and a mercy from your Lord. But whoever transgresses after that will suffer a painful punishment.

The verse's background: the law of the Jahiliyyah was the collective revenge — the killing of the innocent for the killer: if a slave killed a free man, the free were killed; if a woman killed, the men were killed; if a man of one tribe killed a man of another, the whole tribe was targeted. The verse corrects: the *qisas* — the "like for like" — the retaliation is the equal: the free for the free, the slave for the slave, the female for the female — the one who killed is the one who is killed. Ma'arif: by saying "free man for a free man, slave for a slave and female for a female" it is intended to negate their absurd demand that a free man for a slave and a man for a woman should be killed in retaliation, even though he may not be the killer. The just law that Islam enforced was that the killer is the one who has to be killed. Ibn Kathir: the verse's equality is the law of equality; the Children of Israel were required to apply the law of equality in murder cases and were not allowed to offer pardons in return for blood money; the Muslim Ummah is allowed to apply the penalty or to pardon and accept the blood money.

"But if the offender is pardoned by the victim's guardian, then blood-money should be decided fairly and payment should be made courteously" — the verse's mercy: the pardon. The victim's guardians are given the right: the choice between the *qisas* and the *diyah* — the blood money, in exchange for the pardon. The verse's three conditions: "blood-money should be decided fairly" — the *ma'ruf* — the fair, the just, the customary — the claimant pursues the matter in the recognized manner without the harassment (Ma'arif); "payment should be made courteously" — the *ihsan* — the killer pays with the kindness, the completion, the prompt, without the resistance or the harm (Ibn Kathir: the killer should accept the terms of settlement without causing further harm or resisting the payment).

"This is a concession and a mercy from your Lord" — the verse's declaration: the *takhfif* — the lightening, the concession, the relief — from the Lord. The verse's point: the law of the qisas is the justice; the pardon and the blood money is the mercy — the leniency that the Lord granted to the believers; "otherwise, there would have been no choice but to face the punishment of death" (Ma'arif). The verse's "concession and mercy" is the religion's balance: the justice of the retaliation and the mercy of the pardon — the law that protects the life and the mercy that restores the life.

"But whoever transgresses after that will suffer a painful punishment" — the verse's final warning: the transgression after the settlement — the re-opening of the case, the killing of the killer after the pardon, the excess beyond the law. Ma'arif: if, after all that, anyone crosses the limit set by Allah, such as the filing of a false or doubtful case of murder, or a post-pardon re-opening of a murder case, he will be severely punished. The verse's structure: the law (the qisas), the mercy (the pardon and the diyah), the boundary (the transgression).

In modern life, the verse is the law of the sanctity of the life. The verse's lessons: the life is the sanctity — the killing of the innocent is the crime that the law must answer; the justice is the equality — the one who kills is the one who answers, without the collective and disproportionate revenge; the mercy is the pardon — the victim's family has the right of the justice and the choice of the mercy; and the boundary is the transgression — the revenge beyond the law, the re-opening, the excess. The verse's wisdom for the modern world: the justice without the mercy is the cruelty; the mercy without the justice is the lawlessness; the religion's law of the qisas is the balance: the life is protected, the equal is the justice, the pardon is the mercy, and the boundary is the transgression.

## Verse 179

Verse 179 says: *"There is ˹security of˺ life for you in ˹the law of˺ retaliation, O people of reason, so that you may become mindful ˹of Allah˺."*

In simple terms, the verse gives the wisdom of the law: there is security of life for you in the law of retaliation, O people of reason, so that you may become mindful of Allah.

The verse's paradox: the retaliation — the law that kills — is the *hayat* — the life: "there is life for you in the qisas." The paradox is the wisdom: the death of the killer is the life of the community. The law of the retaliation knows the price of the life: the one who kills knows he will be killed; the knowledge is the deterrence; the deterrence is the protection of the lives. Ibn Kathir and the classical tafsir note the point: the law's severity is the life's protection — the one who contemplates the murder contemplates the consequence; the threat of the qisas restrains the murder; the restraint is the life. The verse's "security of life" — the supplied translation's emphasis — is the life that is secured: the life of the victim's potential, the life of the community's safety, the life of the killer (who is restrained from the murder and the subsequent execution).

"O people of reason" — the verse's address: *ya uli al-albab* — the people of the minds, the people of the discernment. The verse's wisdom is for the reason: the one who reasons sees the paradox — the law of the killing is the law of the saving; the one who does not reason sees only the severity. The verse's address is the Qur'an's address to the intelligence: the religion's laws are the laws of the wisdom; the reason is the guide to the wisdom.

"So that you may become mindful of Allah" — the verse's purpose: the *taqwa* — the mindfulness. The verse's progression: the reason perceives the wisdom; the wisdom produces the mindfulness; the mindfulness is the fruit. The verse's point for the believer: the life is the gift; the law protects the life; the law is the law of the Lord; the mindfulness of the Lord is the response to the gift and the law.

In modern life, the verse is the defense of the justice that is the mercy. The modern world debates: is the capital punishment the cruelty or the protection? The verse's answer: "there is life for you in the retaliation" — the law that knows the consequences is the law that protects the life; the society that takes the murder lightly has devalued the life; the society that answers the murder with the justice has protected the life. And the verse's address — "people of reason" — the debate on the justice of the punishment is the debate of the reason: the reason sees the deterrent and the protection; the reason sees the balance: the mercy of the pardon (178), the life of the justice (179), the mindfulness of the Lord (the purpose).

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 180

Verse 180 says: *"It is prescribed that when death approaches any of you—if they leave something of value—a will should be made in favour of parents and immediate family with fairness. ˹This is˺ an obligation on those who are mindful ˹of Allah˺."*

In simple terms, the verse prescribes the will: it is prescribed that when death approaches any of you — if they leave something of value — a will should be made in favour of parents and immediate family with fairness. This is an obligation on those who are mindful of Allah.

The verse's prescription: the *wasiyyah* — the will, the bequest — is *kutiba* — prescribed: the written command. The verse's three conditions: the approach of the death — the moment of the final affairs; the leaving of *khayr* — the good, the wealth, the something of value — the one who leaves a substantial estate; and the fairness — the *ma'ruf* — the will made in the fair, the customary, the just manner. The verse's will: the parents and the immediate family — the near relatives — the ones who had been the providers, the ones who would be left.

The commentators note the history: the verse commanded the will for the parents and the relatives. Ibn Kathir: this Ayah contains the command to include parents and relatives in the will, which was obligatory, according to the most correct view, before the Ayah about inheritance was revealed. When the Ayah of inheritance was revealed (4:7 — "There is a share for men and a share for women from what is left by parents and those nearest related"), this Ayah was abrogated — the fixed shares of the inheritance were legislated by Allah; there is no will for a deserving heir. The verse's command remains in its essence: the will for the relatives who do not qualify as inheritors is recommended — up to a third — due to the general meaning of the Ayah about the will (Ibn Kathir).

"The will should be fair" — Ibn Kathir: one designates a part of the inheritance to his relatives without committing injustice against his qualified inheritors and without extravagance or stinginess — the will's fairness: the not exceeding the third, the not depriving the heirs, the not preferring the richer. The verse's "with fairness" is the standard of the bequest: the will is the justice administered at the end.

"This is an obligation on those who are mindful of Allah" — the verse's close: the *haqq* — the obligation, the due — upon the *muttaqin* — the mindful. The verse's vocabulary: the will is the obligation; the obligation is on the God-fearing. The verse's point: the fear of Allah is the accounting at the end: the one who fears Allah prepares the final affairs — the family, the parents, the relatives, the fairness — the believer's death is the believer's responsibility.

In modern life, the verse is the religion's teaching about the final affairs. The modern world avoids the death: the will, the estate, the final arrangements are postponed, avoided, feared. The verse says: the death approaches; the things of value are left; the will is the fairness; the mindfulness of Allah is the obligation. The believer's final affairs — the provision for the family, the fairness among the heirs, the will in the just manner, the distribution of the estate not by the impulse but by the justice — the verse's command: the one who is mindful prepares the end.
