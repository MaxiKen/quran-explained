# Surah Ali 'Imran (The Family of 'Imran, 3) — Verse-by-Verse Tafsir, Verses 161–170

The last batch gave Uḥud its autopsy: the archers' chain, the Messenger calling from behind, slumber versus jāhiliyyah-thought, two pardons, speech-rules about the dead, and leadership rebuilt through gentleness, *shūrā*, decision, and trust. These ten verses press deeper into integrity around spoils, the question "how could this be?", the exposure of hypocrisy, and the surah's most famous consolation — the martyrs are not dead.

What follows moves from the ethics of *ghulūl* (withholding from the common spoils) to the theology of ranks, to the favour of a messenger sent from among the believers, to the hinge-answer of the whole Uḥud movement: when disaster struck after you had struck twice as hard, and you said "how could this be?", say — it is from yourselves (165). What struck you the day the hosts met was by God's leave, to distinguish the believers (166) and expose the hypocrites (167) — those who, when told to fight or at least defend, said if we had known there would be fighting we would have come, closer that day to disbelief than to belief, saying with mouths what was not in hearts. Those who sat at home said of their brothers, had they listened to us they would not have been killed — say: then avert death from yourselves if you are truthful (168). Never think those killed in God's path are dead; they are alive with their Lord, provided for (169), rejoicing in His bounty and glad for those yet to join them — no fear on them, nor shall they grieve (170).

Five cruxes govern the reading. The first is 161 — *mā kāna li-nabiyyin an yaghulla* — prophetic integrity with spoils, and the eschatological return of whatever was withheld. The second is 165's hinge: *annā hādhā* answered by *huwa min ʿindi anfusikum* — the supplied "because of your disobedience" is a strong interpretive rendering of "from yourselves," and classical readings map it to the archers' breach and, in some reports, to the Badr ransom decision. The third is 166–167 as twin purposes of the day: make believers known, expose hypocrites whose mouth and heart split. The fourth is 168's challenge to the sitters' counterfactual — continuous with 156, now sharpened into "avert death from yourselves." The fifth is 169–170 — the living martyrs: alive *ʿinda rabbihim*, provided for, rejoicing, receiving good news about those behind them; creed and consolation held without turning martyrdom into a death-cult.

The batch answers the wound's deepest social poisons: suspicion about spoils, the cry of unfair disaster, the mouth of hypocrisy, the sitters' blame of the dead — and then replaces the image of the dead as loss with the image of the dead as alive with God.

## Verse 161

**"It is not appropriate for a prophet to illegally withhold spoils of war. And whoever does so, it will be held against them on the Day of Judgment. Then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged."**

In simple terms: it is not fitting for a prophet to take spoils dishonestly. Whoever does that will bring what he took on the Day of Judgment. Then every soul is paid in full for what it did, and no one is wronged.

**The integrity-verse that clears the Prophet and warns the community around booty.**

**"It is not appropriate for a prophet to illegally withhold spoils of war."** *Wa-mā kāna li-nabiyyin an yaghulla.*

*Mā kāna li-nabiyyin* — it is not for a prophet / it does not befit a prophet. *An yaghulla* — that he commit *ghulūl*: to withhold, pilfer, take secretly from the common spoils before fair division. The supplied "illegally withhold spoils of war" is accurate dynamic equivalence. Classical occasion-notes vary: some link a missing red cloak at Badr and the clearing of the Prophet ﷺ from any suspicion; others treat the verse as general prophetic ethics with a warning that lands on anyone handling communal wealth. Both serve. The verse first protects the office: a prophet does not steal from the common pot. Then it widens.

**"And whoever does so, it will be held against them on the Day of Judgment."** More literally: *wa-man yaghlul ya'ti bi-mā ghalla yawma al-qiyāmah* — whoever commits *ghulūl* will bring what he withheld on the Day of Rising. The image is concrete in the hadith literature: the withheld item comes with the person — animal, cloth, goods — as exposure and burden. Even without locking one narration as the only gloss, the verse's own claim is plain: *ghulūl* is not erased by time. It is carried into the open of the Last Day.

**"Then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged."** *Thumma tuwaffā kullu nafsin mā kasabat wa-hum lā yuẓlamūn.*

*Tuwaffā* — paid in full, the *w-f-y* root again: God completes the account. *Mā kasabat* — what it earned. *Lā yuẓlamūn* — they are not wronged. Continuous with the surah's earlier full-payment language (3:57, 3:76). Spoils-ethics sit inside the same justice that pays prophets and communities in full.

**Why here, after Uḥud's spoils-psychology.** Verse 152 already named world-want around what was loved when victory appeared. Verse 161 goes to the root ethics of handling the common gain. A community that left a post for booty must hear that the Prophet ﷺ is clear of *ghulūl*, that *ghulūl* returns on the Last Day, and that every soul's account is completed without wrong. The love of the world that emptied a hill is of a piece with the love of the world that pockets what belongs to the common.

**Hadith colour without invention of chains.** The tradition carries stern prophetic warnings against *ghulūl* — including appointments to collect and the rebuke of those who say "this is for you and this was gifted to me." The verse stands without needing every report. The reports illustrate how seriously the first community took the category.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** *Ghulūl* in a modern key is not only a battlefield cloak. It is the grant skimmed, the waqf redirected, the company account used as a private purse, the "gift" that only arrived because of a public trust. Verse 161 says: public trust is not private feed. What you withhold travels with you into a day when every soul is paid in full and no one is wronged — including those you shorted.

**Public trust as a prophetic ethic that becomes communal law.** Because it does not befit a prophet to commit *ghulūl*, it does not befit a community that follows him to normalise skimming. The office sets the ceiling of integrity; the Last Day sets the certainty of exposure. Uḥud's world-want and this verse's *ghulūl* are one moral family: treating the common as a private opportunity.

**Connection to 8:67–69 and the Badr ransom file.** Some classical voices read Uḥud's sting partly through the earlier decision about captives and ransom at Badr. Verse 161's spoils-ethics sit in that wider Madinan education about how victory-wealth is handled under revelation. Whether or not one privileges that link for 165, 161 itself is plain: do not withhold from the common pot.

**None will be wronged — including the ummah's weak.** *Ghulūl* often steals from the collective in ways that hit the poorest first. The verse's closing justice is good news for anyone whose share was eaten by a trusted hand. God completes accounts. The Last Day is not only threat to the thief. It is restitution-logic under a just Lord.

## Verse 162

**"Are those who seek Allah’s pleasure like those who deserve Allah’s wrath? Hell is their home. What an evil destination!"**

In simple terms: can someone who pursues God's pleasure be like someone who comes back under God's anger, whose home is Hell? What a terrible place to end.

**The binary that sorts wants into destinies.**

**"Are those who seek Allah's pleasure like those who deserve Allah's wrath? Hell is their home. What an evil destination!"** *A-fa-man ittabaʿa riḍwāna Allāhi ka-man bā'a bi-sakhaṭin min Allāhi wa-ma'wāhu jahannam; wa-bi'sa al-maṣīr.*

*Ittabaʿa riḍwāna Allāh* — followed / pursued God's good pleasure. *Bā'a bi-sakhaṭin min Allāh* — returned / came away with anger from God. The commercial verb *bā'a* (to sell/return with) keeps the sense of a transaction's outcome: one life-path ends in *riḍwān*, another in *sakhaṭ*. *Ma'wāhu jahannam* — his lodging is Hell. *Bi'sa al-maṣīr* — what an evil journey's end.

**Connection to 152's fork and 161's account.** Those who wanted the hereafter and those who wanted the world are not morally interchangeable. Verse 162 refuses the soft claim that all participants in a battle are the same before God. Following *riḍwān* and returning with *sakhaṭ* are not one posture. Hell is named for the second without the verse needing to point a finger at a named Companion of the line — the category is the warning.

**A necessary caution.** This verse must not be used to declare a specific individual an inhabitant of the Fire. The Qur'an's standing boundary holds. Categories of path are named; final assignment of particular souls is God's.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** After any shared project that mixed motives — a campaign, a company, a mosque committee — the temptation is to say everyone who showed up is equal. Verse 162 asks a harder question: who was following God's pleasure, and who was trading in a way that returns with His anger? The answer may be hidden in this life. The unlikeness of the two paths is not.

**Bā'a bi-sakhaṭ — returning with anger.** The commercial metaphor is not accidental in a stretch about spoils. One may "profit" in the world's eye and still return with *sakhaṭ*. One may "lose" on the hill and still have followed *riḍwān*. Verse 162 recalibrates the ledger after Uḥud's scoreboard.

**Hell named without theatre.** *Ma'wāhu jahannam* is plain. The verse does not decorate it. After a batch that pardoned runners and disobedient archers, this binary still stands for the path that ends in God's anger. Pardon of particular slips is not the abolition of Hell for those who choose the *sakhaṭ*-path as their residence.

**The question form — a-fa-man.** The Qur'an asks because the answer is meant to be obvious and still needs saying after a mixed day. Obvious: no, the two are not alike. Needed: because a wounded community may flatten everyone into "we all suffered" and lose moral sight. Suffering together does not make *riḍwān* and *sakhaṭ* the same.

**Sakhaṭ and the pardons.** God pardoned archers and runners (152, 155). That pardon does not empty 162. Pardon addresses slips inside a path of faith. *Sakhaṭ* names a path that returns with anger — the opposite of following *riḍwān*. Keep both the open door and the real binary.

## Verse 163

**"They ˹each˺ have varying degrees in the sight of Allah. And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do."**

In simple terms: they are at different ranks with God. God sees everything they do.

**The short verse that blocks flat equality of outcomes.**

**"They each have varying degrees in the sight of Allah. And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do."** *Hum darajātun ʿinda Allāh; wa-Allāhu baṣīrun bi-mā yaʿmalūn.*

*Hum darajātun* — they are ranks / degrees. The half-bracketed "each" correctly marks the distributive sense. *ʿInda Allāh* — with God, in His sight. Not all who fought are one rank. Not all who stayed home are one rank. Not the one who held the post and the one who ran for cloth. God sees *mā yaʿmalūn* — what they do — and ranks follow deeds under His seeing.

**Connection to 3:163 and the wider darajāt vocabulary.** The Qur'an elsewhere ranks by faith, knowledge, and sacrifice. Here the ranking lands immediately after the *riḍwān* / *sakhaṭ* binary and immediately before the favour of the Messenger is restated. Sorting is not cruelty. It is truthfulness about a day that mixed *iḥsān* and *ghulūl*-temptation, holding and fleeing, sincerity and hypocrisy.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Resist both false hierarchies (status, tribe, volume of speech) and false flatness (no one may be better because feelings might hurt). Verse 163 authorises *darajāt* under God's eye alone. Your job is the deed. His job is the rank.

**Darajātun ʿinda Allāh — ranks as comfort and as warning.** Comfort to the one who held the post and was killed: God has ranks, and holding is not lost in a flat pile of "everyone who went out." Warning to the one who wants religious reputation without the deed God sees: ranks are *ʿinda Allāh*, not *ʿinda al-nās*.

**Baṣīrun bi-mā yaʿmalūn — seeing deeds, not costumes.** Ranks track what is done. The one who looked like a fighter and ran for cloth, the one who looked ordinary and held, the one whose hypocrisy only showed when defence was invited — God sees the doing. Human ranking systems are permanently under-informed. His is not.

**Connection to the wider darajāt theme.** Spending ranks, hijrah ranks, knowledge ranks — the Qur'an's degree-language is large. Here it lands as the immediate sequel to Uḥud's mixed ledger: not all participation is one rank.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — ranks without arrogance.** Knowing God has *darajāt* should make you more careful, not more proud. You do not know your rank. You know your next deed. Work. Leave the degree with Him. Refuse both the envy that cannot stand another's visible sacrifice and the vanity that turns your own sacrifice into a ladder for stomping others.

## Verse 164

**"Indeed, Allah has done the believers a ˹great˺ favour by raising a messenger from among them—reciting to them His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom. For indeed they had previously been clearly astray."**

In simple terms: God truly favoured the believers when He sent them a messenger from among themselves, who recites His revelations to them, purifies them, and teaches them the Book and wisdom — for before that they were clearly lost.

**The favour-verse restated after the wound — so that trauma does not erase gift.**

**"Indeed, Allah has done the believers a great favour by raising a messenger from among them."** *La-qad manna Allāhu ʿalā al-mu'minīna idh baʿatha fīhim rasūlan min anfusihim.*

*Manna* — bestowed favour, conferred grace. The half-bracketed "great" marks the weight of *manna*. *Min anfusihim* — from themselves, from among them: not an angel who cannot be followed, not a stranger to their tongue and kinship field. Continuous with 3:101–104's community and with 9:128's messenger from yourselves. After Uḥud, when the Messenger's mortality has been faced (144) and his call from behind was not always heard (153), the surah insists: his sending was still favour. The wound does not cancel the gift.

**"Reciting to them His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom."** *Yatlū ʿalayhim āyātihi wa-yuzakkīhim wa-yuʿallimuhumu al-kitāba wa-al-ḥikmah.*

Four functions — the same cluster as 2:129 and 2:151 and 62:2: recite the signs, purify (*tazkiyah*), teach the Book, teach *ḥikmah*. Purification here is moral and spiritual cleansing — continuous with *tamḥīṣ* language but as the Messenger's ongoing work, not only as the battlefield's surgery. *Al-ḥikmah* — wisdom: commonly linked to the Sunnah and to the right placement of the Book in life; the verse pairs it with the Book so that revelation is not bare text without lived wisdom.

**"For indeed they had previously been clearly astray."** *Wa-in kānū min qablu la-fī ḍalālin mubīn.*

Before the Messenger, clear error. The reminder blocks nostalgia for a pre-Islamic self and blocks despair that treats Uḥud as proof the sending failed. You were astray; He favoured you; the favour stands.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** After a communal failure under a leader you love, two errors arrive: "the leader is the problem" and "the gift was never real." Verse 164, placed after Uḥud's frankness, refuses both. The Messenger from among you is still God's favour. Recitation, purification, Book, and wisdom are still the curriculum. Your prior astray-ness is still the truth that makes the favour visible.

**Four functions as the ongoing cure.** Recitation without purification can become performance. Purification without teaching can become vague mood. Book without wisdom can become weaponised letter. Wisdom without Book can become untethered taste. The Messenger's favour is the four together. After Uḥud, the community still needs all four — especially *tazkiyah* and *ḥikmah* applied to fear, spoils, and speech about the dead.

**Min anfusihim and 144.** He is from themselves and only a messenger. Both are true. Favour is not divinity. Mortality is not the cancellation of favour.

**Astray before — clearly.** *Ḍalāl mubīn* is not mild confusion. It is clear error. The favour of the Messenger is measured against that clarity. Post-Uḥud depression that says "we are no better than before" is theologically false. You have Book, wisdom, purification, recitation among you. Use them on the wound.

**Teaching as love after failure.** The Messenger who must pardon and consult (159) is the same who teaches Book and wisdom (164). Leadership after Uḥud is not only soft process. It is continued curriculum.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — gift after failure.** When a community has failed its messenger in a moment, the temptation is to treat the relationship as ruined. Verse 164, spoken into post-Uḥud air, says the sending is still *manna*. Repair the obedience. Do not throw away the favour. Recitation, purification, Book, and wisdom are still on offer for people who ran and were pardoned.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 165

**"Why is it when you suffered casualties ˹at Uḥud˺—although you had made your enemy suffer twice as much ˹at Badr˺—you protested, “How could this be?”? Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “It is because of your disobedience.” Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything."**

In simple terms: how is it that when you were hit with a loss at Uḥud — though you had hit the enemy twice as hard at Badr — you said, "How could this happen?" Say: it came from yourselves. God has power over everything.

**The hinge-answer of the Uḥud movement.**

**"Why is it when you suffered casualties at Uḥud — although you had made your enemy suffer twice as much at Badr — you protested, 'How could this be?'"** *A-wa-lammā aṣābatkum muṣībatun qad aṣabtum mithlayhā qultum annā hādhā.*

*Muṣībah* — a disaster, an affliction. The half-brackets name Uḥud and Badr as the classical pairing: Muslim losses at Uḥud (the reports say about seventy dead) versus the heavier blow they had dealt at Badr (dead and captives counted as "twice"). *Annā hādhā* — whence is this? how could this be? The cry of unfairness from a community that had tasted Badr's gift and then Uḥud's cut.

**"Say, O Prophet, 'It is because of your disobedience.'"** *Qul huwa min ʿindi anfusikum.*

Here the supplied translation chooses strongly. The Arabic is *huwa min ʿindi anfusikum* — it is from yourselves / from your own side. "Because of your disobedience" is interpretive specification, not a second Arabic clause. It is not wrong as a classical reading of *what* from yourselves meant — the archers' disobedience is the dominant mapping in Ibn Isḥāq, al-Suddī, and much of the tafsir; some routes through ʿUmar also link a dimension of the Badr ransom decision as prior earning — but the reader must hear the more open Arabic: **from yourselves**. Secondary causes in your own conduct. Not a betrayal by God. Not a proof that the promise was false. From you.

**"Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything."** *Inna Allāha ʿalā kulli shay'in qadīr.*

Power that could have given a second Badr and that could allow a wound. Power that is not the slave of the community's sense of entitlement after a prior victory. Continuous with the *ʿAzīz Ḥakīm* theology of 126.

**Why this verse must not become self-hatred alone.** "From yourselves" is moral responsibility, not the erasure of enemy aggression, of the written term of death (145, 154), or of God's leave (166). The full theology holds several truths at once: they struck you; God left it; you contributed through disobedience and world-want; He pardoned (152, 155); He still sorts and teaches. Flattening 165 into only one of those truths is how people become either fatalists or self-destroyers.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When disaster follows success, the cry *annā hādhā* is natural. Verse 165 orders a turn inward before a turn into accusation of God. Ask: what in us — our dispute, our disobedience, our want of the world, our leaving of a clear order — is this *from*? That question is how a community becomes reformable. The power of God over everything is what keeps the question from becoming despair: He can still amend what our hands have sent ahead.

**Twice as much — mithlayhā.** The comparison is meant to break the sense of unique victimhood: you had already inflicted a greater combined blow. The cry of unfairness forgets the ledger of Badr. God does not forget it. The point is not to trivialise Uḥud's seventy. The point is to stop the narrative that Muslims only ever receive and never dealt a blow.

**Qadīr as the seal.** Power over everything includes the power to let a secondary cause in your own ranks become the hinge of a day. That is not cruelty for sport. It is pedagogy under power — the same power that fulfilled the promise in the early sweep (152).

**How could this be — as a spiritual symptom.** The cry assumes that belief plus prior victory should have produced a permanent exemption from disaster. Verse 123 had already ordered gratitude after Badr so that entitlement would not grow. Verse 165 shows what entitlement sounds like when the next day cuts. The medicine is still *taqwā* and ownership of the self's contribution.

**Disobedience as the translation's chosen specification.** When you teach this verse, consider saying both: the Arabic "from yourselves," and the classical filling "your disobedience / your leaving the post / what you earned." The supplied translation jumped to the filling. The commentary restores the base and then the filling.

## Verse 166

**"So what you suffered on the day the two armies met was by Allah’s Will, so that He might distinguish the ˹true˺ believers"**

In simple terms: so what hit you on the day the two armies met was by God's permission, so that He might distinguish the true believers.

**The leave and the purpose — completing 165 without cancelling it.**

**"So what you suffered on the day the two armies met was by Allah's Will, so that He might distinguish the true believers."** *Wa-mā aṣābakum yawma l-taqā al-jamʿāni fa-bi-idhni Allāh wa-li-yaʿlama al-mu'minīn.*

*Yawma l-taqā al-jamʿān* — the day the two hosts met: Uḥud again. *Fa-bi-idhni Allāh* — then / so it was by God's leave. The disaster that is "from yourselves" in secondary cause is "by God's leave" in decree. Both sentences are required. Remove leave and the day becomes chaos or pure enemy authorship. Remove "from yourselves" and the day becomes a blank test with no moral autopsy. The Qur'an keeps both.

**"So that He might distinguish the true believers."** *Wa-li-yaʿlama al-mu'minīn.*

Same *li-yaʿlama* file as 140–142: manifestation, making known, sorting in the open. The half-bracketed "true" and "distinguish" lean into that sense and are fair. Uḥud made believers visible — those who held, those who returned to the call, those who wanted the hereafter at the post.

**Connection forward to 167.** Distinguishing believers is incomplete without exposing hypocrites. The next verse continues the purpose-chain with *wa-li-yaʿlama* implied in *wa-li-yaʿlama alladhīna nāfaqū* structure — the supplied text begins 167 with "and expose the hypocrites," continuing the grammar.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When you have owned your share of a failure ("from yourselves"), still leave room for *bi-idhni Allāh*. The leave is not an excuse. It is the frame that keeps God as Lord of the day you partly spoiled. Responsibility under sovereignty is adult religion. Sovereignty without responsibility is fatalism. Responsibility without sovereignty is practical atheism.

**Fa-bi-idhni Allāh as the hinge between 165 and 167.** From yourselves — by His leave — so that He might make believers known — and expose hypocrites. The chain is one pedagogy. Pull one link out and the theology warps. Leave all the links and Uḥud becomes readable: your contribution, His leave, His sorting purposes.

**Li-yaʿlama al-mu'minīn after min anfusikum.** First: own the secondary cause. Second: recognise the leave. Third: accept the purpose of distinction. A community that only does the first may drown in blame. A community that only does the third may become conspiracy-minded about "tests" without repentance. The verse order in 165–167 trains the full sequence.

**The day the two hosts met — named again.** Repetition of the meeting-formula keeps the reader from abstracting Uḥud into a vague "hardship." It was a day, two hosts, a meeting, a leave, a purpose.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When a failure is "from yourselves" and also "by God's leave," the practical fruit is twofold repentance and twofold trust: repent of the part that was yours; trust the Lord who still owns the day. That combination is how people stay in the religion after they have seen their own contribution to a disaster.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 167

**"and expose the hypocrites. When it was said to them, “Come fight in the cause of Allah or ˹at least˺ defend yourselves,” they replied, “If we had known there was fighting, we would have definitely gone with you.” They were closer to disbelief than to belief on that day—for saying with their mouths what was not in their hearts. Allah is All-Knowing of what they hide."**

In simple terms: and so that He might expose the hypocrites. When they were told, "Come fight in God's path, or at least defend," they said, "If we had known there would be fighting, we would have gone with you." That day they were nearer to disbelief than to belief — saying with their mouths what was not in their hearts. God knows what they hide.

**The exposure-verse — hypocrisy named by speech and distance.**

**"And expose the hypocrites."** *Wa-li-yaʿlama alladhīna nāfaqū.*

*Nāfaqū* — those who practiced *nifāq*, hypocrisy: the split between appearance and interior. Making them known is a purpose of the day, continuous with *tamḥīṣ* and sorting. Uḥud was not only a military event. It was a revealer of chests.

**"When it was said to them, 'Come fight in the cause of Allah or at least defend yourselves,' they replied, 'If we had known there was fighting, we would have definitely gone with you.'"** 

Two invitations: full fight in God's path, or at least defence (*dafʿ* / standing to protect). The refusal dresses itself as lack of knowledge: if we had known there would be fighting… The excuse is absurd on a day when two armies met — and the verse treats it as mouth-work. Classical reports place this on the hypocrites who turned back with Ibn Ubayy or sat out the logic of the day; the verse's claim is the speech-pattern and the heart-split.

**"They were closer to disbelief than to belief on that day — for saying with their mouths what was not in their hearts."** *Yawma'idhin aqrabu minhum li-l-kufri minhum li-l-īmān; yaqūlūna bi-afwāhihim mā laysa fī qulūbihim.*

*Aqrabu… li-l-kufr* — nearer to disbelief than to belief *that day*. The temporal marker matters. The verse grades proximity on the day of stress, without the commentary needing to issue a final eternal verdict on each named person. Mouths said what hearts did not hold. That split is the definition of *nifāq* in action.

**"Allah is All-Knowing of what they hide."** *Wa-Allāhu aʿlamu bi-mā yaktumūn.*

Hidden things are not hidden to God. Continuous with 154's concealment and God's knowledge of *dhāt al-ṣudūr*.

**Connection to 3:167 and 9: the munāfiqūn file.** Āl 'Imrān here does in the Uḥud register what other Madinan passages do at length: hypocrisy is exposed under pressure, not only under sermon.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Stress is a revealer. The meeting that only looked like consensus in ease splits when cost arrives. Watch for the "if we had known" excuse when the knowing was available. Watch for mouth-work that outruns the heart. The verse does not authorise a witch-hunt of labels. It authorises sobriety about proximity to disbelief when speech and heart diverge under fire.

**Fight or defend — the narrowed invitation.** Even the lesser invitation — at least defend — was refused with theatre. Hypocrisy here is not only refusing *jihād* as expansion; it is refusing the defence of the community while claiming conditional willingness. The mouth manages reputation; the heart has already chosen distance.

**Yawma'idhin — that day.** Proximity to disbelief is measured under pressure. Ease had allowed a mask. The meeting of hosts pulled the mask. That is why sorting requires days that hurt.

**Mā laysa fī qulūbihim — the definition in motion.** Hypocrisy is often taught as a noun. Here it is a present-tense verb of speaking: they say with mouths what is not in hearts. The diagnostic is available without access to the unseen: watch the gap between claimed willingness and actual response when the cost is named. God knows the hide; humans may at least stop being naive about the show.

**Defend yourselves — the mercy inside the invitation.** The verse records that they were not only called to the highest form of struggle. They were offered a lesser, defensive standing. Refusal of even that, with a false conditional, is what pushes proximity toward disbelief on that day.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 168

**"Those who sat at home, saying about their brothers, “Had they listened to us, they would not have been killed.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Try not to die if what you say is true!”"**

In simple terms: those who stayed sitting at home said about their brothers, "If they had obeyed us, they would not have been killed." Tell them: then stop death from reaching you, if you are telling the truth.

**The sitters' counterfactual — sharpened into a challenge.**

**"Those who sat at home, saying about their brothers, 'Had they listened to us, they would not have been killed.'"** *Alladhīna qālū li-ikhwānihim wa-qaʿadū law aṭāʿūnā mā qutilū.*

*Wa-qaʿadū* — while they sat / having sat back. The posture of non-going becomes the platform for blaming the dead. *Law aṭāʿūnā* — if they had obeyed us. Continuous with 156's ban on "if they had stayed with us," now with the added gall of obedience-claim: our command would have saved them. The dead are made wrong for having gone; the sitters are made sages for having sat.

**"Say, O Prophet, 'Try not to die if what you say is true!'"** *Qul fa-dra'ū ʿan anfusikumu al-mawta in kuntum ṣādiqīn.*

More closely: avert / repel death from yourselves if you are truthful. The half-bracketed "Try not to die" is a sharp dynamic rendering of *idra'ū ʿan anfusikumu al-mawt*. If staying and "obedience" to the sitters' counsel truly controlled death, then let the sitters demonstrate control over their own death. They cannot. Therefore their counterfactual about the brothers is empty. Continuous with 154's death-beds and 145's written term.

**Why this verse is merciless toward a certain kind of grief-speech.** Not toward tears. Toward the political use of the dead to justify sitting and to claim superior wisdom. God takes that speech apart with one imperative: avert death from yourselves.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** After a death in duty, the sitting voice says: if they had listened to me, they would be alive. Sometimes that voice is a parent's anguish. Sometimes it is a faction's weapon. Verse 168 allows anguish. It destroys the weapon. You who sit do not own the lever of death. Do not use the dead to crown your caution as prophecy.

**Obedience claimed by those who sat.** The gall is specific: not only "if they had stayed" but "if they had obeyed us." The sitters appoint themselves the true command. Verse 168 strips the appointment. Death does not obey them. Therefore their counterfactual is not wisdom; it is self-exaltation over graves.

**Ikhwānihim — their brothers.** Even the sitters' speech still says brothers. Kinship is not the issue. The issue is using kinship to launder a theology of sitting. Real brotherhood, in the light of 169–170, would rather hear that the dead live with God than that the dead were fools for going out.

**In kuntum ṣādiqīn — if you are truthful.** Truthfulness would mean the sitters' model actually controls death. It does not. Therefore the speech is not truthful, however much it performs concern.

**Connection to 3:156.** Same disease, sharper surgery. 156 forbade the likeness of the rejecters' speech. 168 faces the sitters with the test their claim implies.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 169

**"Never think of those martyred in the cause of Allah as dead. In fact, they are alive with their Lord, well provided for—"**

In simple terms: do not ever think that those who are killed in God's path are dead. No — they are alive with their Lord, and they are provided for.

**One of the Qur'an's great creed-consolations — the living martyrs.**

**"Never think of those martyred in the cause of Allah as dead. In fact, they are alive with their Lord, well provided for."** *Wa-lā taḥsabanna alladhīna qutilū fī sabīli Allāhi amwātan; bal aḥyā'un ʿinda rabbihim yurzaqūn.*

*Lā taḥsabanna* — do not think / reckon / suppose — with the emphatic *nūn*. Addressed first to the Prophet ﷺ and through him to every reader who looks at Uḥud's dead as pure loss. *Alladhīna qutilū fī sabīli Allāh* — those killed in God's path. *Amwātan* — dead (as the whole truth of them). *Bal aḥyā'un* — rather, they are alive. *ʿInda rabbihim* — with their Lord. *Yurzaqūn* — they are provided for, given *rizq*.

**What kind of life.** The verse asserts real life with God and real provision. It does not ask the reader to pretend the bodies were not buried. The life is *ʿinda rabbihim* — in a mode with their Lord — not a claim that the graves are empty of corpses. Classical creed holds the martyrs' lives in the *barzakh* under God's care; authentic reports in Muslim and Aḥmad describe provision and joy in forms the Prophet ﷺ taught (including the well-known green birds imagery in some routes). The commentary may note those reports as widely received colour without making any single image the only door of the verse. The verse's own floor is enough: not dead as you think; alive with their Lord; provided for.

**Uḥud's dead as primary occasion.** Qatādah, al-Rabīʿ, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and others tie these *āyāt* to the martyrs of Uḥud. The wording is general for those killed in God's path. Uḥud is the wound that made the consolation urgent; the consolation outruns one hill.

**Connection to 2:154 and 3:169.** Al-Baqarah already forbade saying of those killed in God's path that they are dead — rather alive, but you do not perceive. Āl 'Imrān returns with *ʿinda rabbihim yurzaqūn* and, in 170, joy and good news about those yet to come.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When you bury someone killed in a path of truth — or when you visit a grave of someone who died under obedience — verse 169 re-educates sight. Grief remains lawful and human. Despair that treats them as extinguished does not. They are alive with their Lord. Provision reaches them. Your job is not to manage their life with God. Your job is to walk in a way that could join the good news of the next verse.

**And the spiritual centre.** A religion that can say this over seventy graves at Uḥud without denying the blood is a religion that has somewhere to put its dead besides oblivion or propaganda.

**Lā taḥsabanna as a command on perception.** The first wound after a martyr's death is often perceptual: treating the person as ended. The Qur'an commands a different *ḥusbān* — a different reckoning. You may weep. You may not reckon them *amwāt* as the final truth.

**ʿInda rabbihim — with their Lord.** The phrase blocks both vulgar materialism (nothing after the blade) and vulgar geography (imagining the life as simply "in the grave" as a room like ours). They are with their Lord. Provision is His act. The how is His. The fact is enough for consolation and creed.

**Amwātan as totalising thought.** The ban is on thinking them dead in the way that ends the story. Bodies are buried; funerals are real; inheritance proceeds. None of that is denied. What is denied is the metaphysics of extinguishing. *Bal aḥyā'un* is the correction.

**Yurzaqūn — passive provision.** They do not provision themselves. They are provided for. The grammar keeps God as the giver and the martyrs as recipients of ongoing care. That is why the image comforts the living who fear that the dead are hungry for help they cannot give. God feeds them.

**Connection to grief that is still commanded in other forms.** The Prophet ﷺ wept. The Qur'an does not cancel tears with 169. It cancels a metaphysics. Cry. Bury. Pray. Do not say of them *amwāt* as if God were done with them. He is not. They are with their Lord.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 170

**"rejoicing in Allah’s bounties and being delighted for those yet to join them. There will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve."**

In simple terms: they rejoice in what God has given them of His bounty, and they are glad for those who have not yet joined them. There will be no fear upon them, nor will they grieve.

**The joy of the living dead — and their good news for those still here.**

**"Rejoicing in Allah's bounties and being delighted for those yet to join them."** *Fariḥīna bi-mā ātāhumu Allāhu min faḍlihi wa-yastabshirūna bi-alladhīna lam yalḥaqū bihim min khalfihim.*

*Fariḥīna* — rejoicing, glad. *Bi-mā ātāhumu Allāhu min faḍlihi* — in what God has given them of His bounty. The provision of 169 is not bare subsistence; it is *faḍl* — bounty that produces joy. *Wa-yastabshirūna* — and they receive good news / they rejoice in advance good tidings. *Bi-alladhīna lam yalḥaqū bihim min khalfihim* — concerning those who have not yet joined them from behind them: brothers still in the world who will meet them in God's path. The martyrs' joy is not closed on the self. It includes gladness for those still to come.

**"There will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve."** *Allā allā khawfun ʿalayhim wa-lā hum yaḥzanūn.*

The supplied "There will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve" reads the clause as the content of the good news about those yet to join — the standard classical reading: the martyrs are given glad tidings that those coming after them will also have no fear and no grief. A secondary grammatical possibility ties the no-fear clause more tightly to the martyrs themselves; both end in the same moral place: fear and grief are lifted in the order of God's bounty for this company. The formula *lā khawfun ʿalayhim wa-lā hum yaḥzanūn* is the Qur'an's standing seal of security on the friends of God.

**What this does to the sitters and the counterfactual.** Verses 156 and 168 tried to save the dead by rewriting the past. Verses 169–170 show the dead in a present tense the sitters cannot imagine: alive, provided, rejoicing, glad for those still behind. The real economy of the dead in God's path is not "if only they had stayed." It is "they want their brothers to know what God gave them."

**Connection to 3:171 (next batch).** The joy continues into grace and bounty and the assurance that God does not waste the believers' wage. This batch ends on the threshold of that continuation — alive, provided, rejoicing, unafraid.

**Hadith colour.** Reports that the martyrs wish their brothers knew what they received, and that God would convey the news, sit as the narrative climate of these *āyāt* in the tafsir. The verse itself already is that conveyance.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Let the dead in God's path restructure your fear. If they rejoice and are provided, your grief can be love without being annihilation-faith. If they are glad for those yet to join them, your life can be lived as someone who might yet be worth their gladness — not by chasing death, but by chasing the *riḍwān* of 162 and the trust of 160 and the *muttaqīn* portrait of 134–136. And when a community counts its graves after a wound, read 169–170 aloud before you read the factional post-mortem. The dead are not your argument. They are alive with their Lord.

**And the spiritual centre of the ten verses.** Prophets do not commit *ghulūl*; whoever does brings it on the Last Day; every soul paid in full. *Riḍwān* is not like *sakhaṭ*; Hell is an evil end. Ranks differ with God. The Messenger from among you is still God's favour — reciting, purifying, teaching Book and wisdom — after clear error. The cry "how could this be?" meets "from yourselves," under God's power. What struck you was by leave, to make believers known and hypocrites exposed — mouth not matching heart. Sitters who blame the dead are told to avert death from themselves. And those killed in the path are not dead: alive with their Lord, provided, rejoicing, glad for those behind, with no fear and no grief on the company God secures.

Uḥud's graves are not the end of the story. They are a doorway into a life the sitters cannot see. The next batch will keep the good news moving.

**Joy that includes those behind.** The martyrs' *istibshār* about those not yet joined is one of the most communal images in the Qur'an. Death in God's path does not seal a private club. It opens gladness toward the living who may yet arrive. That gladness is the opposite of the sitters' speech. The sitters use the dead to stop the living. The martyrs receive good news that encourages the living.

**Lā khawfun ʿalayhim wa-lā hum yaḥzanūn.** The seal of the friends of God. Fear of the future and grief over the past — the two axes of the tortured mind — are lifted. Whether read primarily of those yet to join or of the company as God secures it, the seal is the surah's answer to 139's *lā tahinū wa-lā taḥzanū* raised to the order of the dead who live.

**Faḍlihi — His bounty.** Joy is in bounty received, not in the enemy's loss. The martyrs' celebration is God-centred. That blocks a reading of martyrdom as primarily spite against the other side. What they rejoice in is what God gave.

**Those yet to join them — a missiology of the grave.** The dead in the path become, in God's telling, people who care that the living continue. The community at Madinah burying its seventy is told that the seventy are glad for those still behind. Carry that when you fear that going on dishonours the dead. Going on in faith is what they are glad for.


===VERSE-END===

## Synthesis of the Ten Verses

These ten verses carry the Uḥud aftermath from spoils-integrity and the cry of unfair disaster into the exposure of hypocrisy and the life of the martyrs with God.

**161–163 set integrity and ranks.** It does not befit a prophet to commit *ghulūl*; whoever withholds brings it on the Day of Rising; every soul paid in full, none wronged (161). Following God's pleasure is not like returning with God's anger — Hell the home of the latter (162). They are ranks with God; He sees what they do (163). Booty-ethics and destiny-binaries land inside the same justice.

**164 restates the favour after the wound.** God favoured the believers by sending a messenger from themselves — reciting, purifying, teaching Book and wisdom — though they were before in clear error. Trauma does not cancel the gift of the Messenger.

**165–168 answer the cry and expose the split.** When disaster struck after you had struck twice as hard, and you said "how could this be?", say: it is from yourselves; God is powerful over everything (165). What struck you the day the hosts met was by God's leave, to distinguish the believers (166) and expose the hypocrites — who, invited to fight or defend, claimed they would have come if they had known there was fighting; nearer that day to disbelief than belief; mouths not matching hearts (167). Sitters said of their brothers, if they had obeyed us they would not have been killed — say: avert death from yourselves if you are truthful (168).

**169–170 open the graves upward.** Do not think those killed in God's path dead; they are alive with their Lord, provided for (169); rejoicing in His bounty, glad for those yet to join them; no fear on them, nor shall they grieve (170). The surah's answer to despair over the seventy is not a slogan. It is ontology: a life with God, provision, joy, and good news.

**Threads carried and extended.**

- ***W-f-y / full payment*** — 161 with 3:57, 3:76: accounts completed, no wrong.
- ***World-want and spoils*** — 152's fork → 161's *ghulūl*; integrity around the common pot.
- ***From yourselves / by God's leave*** — 165 and 166 as paired truths of Uḥud's causality.
- ***Tamḥīṣ / making known*** — 140–142, 154, 166–167: believers distinguished, hypocrites exposed.
- ***Speech about the dead*** — 156 → 168's sharper challenge → 169–170's living reality.
- ***Mouth vs heart*** — 167 with the surah's long interest in what is hidden and what is said.
- ***Life of martyrs*** — 2:154 → 3:169–170: alive *ʿinda rabbihim*, provided, rejoicing.
- ***Messenger as favour*** — 164 after 144's mortality and 153's unheard call: gift restated.

**Where the supplied translation has chosen, it has been flagged.** Half-brackets ("great," "at Uḥud," "at Badr," "O Prophet," "true," "at least," "Try not to die") are interpretive aids. **"It is because of your disobedience"** for *huwa min ʿindi anfusikum* is a strong specification of "from yourselves" — classical and fair as reading, not identical to the Arabic's openness; flagged in commentary. "Illegally withhold spoils" for *yaghulla* is accurate. "Expose the hypocrites" continues 166's purpose-grammar. "Well provided for" for *yurzaqūn* is fair. "Being delighted for those yet to join them" captures *yastabshirūna*. None is distortion; each is a choice — and 165's choice needed the most care.

**The verses to leave with are 165 and 169–170 together.** The disaster is from yourselves — under God's power and leave — and the dead are not dead. A community that can hear both can repent without despair and grieve without annihilation. That is how Uḥud becomes a school instead of a grave only.

**A note on honesty in citation.** No page numbers, volumes or publication details are given, because this is a synthesized commentary rather than a citation apparatus, and inventing precise references would be worse than omitting them.

Five places in this batch required particular care.

**The first is 161 and ghulūl.** Prophetic integrity cleared; warning generalised; Last Day image of bringing what was withheld; hadith colour without fabricated chains. Occasion-notes on a missing cloak held as reported, not forced as sole asbāb.

**The second is 165.** Arabic *min ʿindi anfusikum* ("from yourselves") vs supplied "because of your disobedience" — flagged as interpretive specification. Dominant classical mapping to archers; secondary routes to Badr ransom noted. Must be held with 166's *bi-idhni Allāh*, with enemy agency, with prior pardons, without self-hatred as the only note.

**The third is 167.** Hypocrites exposed by mouth/heart split and absurd "if we had known" under meeting armies. "Nearer to disbelief that day" is graded proximity under stress, not a licence for the reader to finalise individuals' eternities.

**The fourth is 168.** Challenge to sitters' counterfactual; continuous with 156; does not ban grief; bans weaponising the dead to crown sitting as wisdom.

**The fifth is 169–170.** Alive with their Lord, provided, rejoicing, glad for those behind; Uḥud as primary occasion, wording general; hadith imagery as colour; bodies buried and souls living both true; no death-cult, no oblivion.

**On the translation.** The 165 rendering needed the clearest flag. Other choices noted above. The translation is not wrong; it has chosen, and the reader is entitled to know what was chosen.

Two boundaries observed throughout. No named individual has been assigned to Hell via 162. And no reading of the martyrs' life has been allowed to deny the burial or to turn the graves into a recruiting poster emptied of grief. The batch stops on joy and good news.
