## Sūrah-level elements

### S5 · Central theme & summary of contents (maqāṣid)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “This Surah comprises seven verses. Of these, the first three are in praise of
Allah, while the last three contain a request or a prayer on the part of man, which Allah himself has, in
His infinite mercy, taught him. The verse in between the two sets has both the features — there is an
aspect of praise, and another of prayer.” And, at the close: “the prayer for the straight path is the
essence of the Surah al-Fatihah. Since knowing and following the straight path is the real knowledge and
the real achievement in this mortal world, a mistake in picking it up right takes peoples and nations to
ruins.”

### S6 · Thematic coherence & connection (munāsabah · naẓm)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** The sūrah is read as one argument across the three tenses of a man's life: “A man's
life is subject to three states of time — past, present and future. The first two verses… remind man
that, as far as his past and present are concerned, he owes everything to Allah alone… The third verse:
*Master of the Day of Judgment* tells him that in the future too he will have to depend on Allah alone…
The three verses having made it clear that man is totally and absolutely dependent on Allah in all the
three states of his life, it logically and naturally leads to the conclusion that Allah alone is worthy of
being worshipped.”

## 1:1 — بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* In the name of Allah, the All-Merciful, the Very-Merciful.

### V1 · Qurʾānic text & translation

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “There is consensus of all the Muslims on the fact that *Bismillah al-Raḥmān
al-Raḥīm* is a verse of the Holy Qur'an, being a part of the Surah al-Naml (The Ant); and there is also an
agreement that this verse is written at the head of every Surah except the Surah al-Taubah. But there is a
difference of opinion among the Mujtahids as to whether this verse is an integral part of the Surah
al-Fatihah or of all the Surahs or not. According to the great Imam Abū Ḥanīfah, it is not an integral part
of any Surah except al-Naml; rather it is in itself an independent verse of the Holy Qur'an which has been
revealed for being placed at the beginning of every Surah in order to separate and distinguish one Surah
from another.”

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** The merits of the basmalah: “It was a custom in the Age of Ignorance (*Jāhiliyyah*)
before the advent of Islam that people began everything they did with the names of their idols or gods. It
was to eradicate this practice that the first verse of the Holy Qur'an which the Archangel Jibrāʾīl brought
down to the Holy Prophet ﷺ commanded him to begin the Qur'an with the name of Allah: *Read with the name
of your Lord* (96:1).” Al-Suyūṭī is cited that all the divine books begin with the basmalah, and the two
views are reconciled: “all the divine books share the common trait of beginning with the name of Allah,
but the words *Bismillah al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm* are peculiar to the Holy Qur'an”, since the Prophet ﷺ used
*Bismika Allāhumma* until this verse was revealed and then adopted it. And: “The Holy Prophet ﷺ has said
that no important work receives the blessings of Allah unless it is begun with His name.” The work draws
the sense of the practice: “By instructing man to begin everything with the name of Allah, Islam has given
to the whole of his life an orientation towards Allah… Thus, all the economic and worldly activities of
man, each movement and gesture, becomes transformed into an act of worship… a regular alchemy, transmuting
the profane (*dunyā*) into the sacred (*dīn*).”

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** 96:1 — *Read with the name of your Lord* — as the command from which the practice of
beginning with Allah's name comes.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** the phrase “is composed of three words — the letter *Bāʾ*, *Ism* and *Allāh*. The
preposition *Bāʾ* has several connotations in Arabic, three of which are appropriate to the occasion…
(1) Contiguity… (2) Seeking the aid of someone… (3) To seek the blessings of someone.” The three senses
give: “*With the name of Allah*; *With the help of the name of Allah*; *With the barakah of the name of
Allah*.” Since the phrase is incomplete without the act intended, “according to the rules of grammar, some
verb is taken to be understood here which should be suitable for the occasion — e.g., ‘I begin or recite
with the name of Allah,’” and “propriety demands that even this verb should be understood to occur after
the phrase, so that one does actually begin with the name of Allah and the verb does not precede His
name”. The ʿUthmānic script: “The regular Arabic script requires the letter *Bāʾ* here to be joined with
the letter *Alif*… But the ʿUthmāni manuscript has dropped the *Alif*, and joined the letter *Bāʾ* with the
letter *Sīn*, making the *Bāʾ* look like a part of the word *Ism*, so that the beginning is made, in
effect, with the name of Allah.” On the divine names: “The word *Allāh* is the greatest and the most
comprehensive of the divine names… the word ‘Allāh’ refers to the Essence, and hence this name cannot be
given to anyone except Allah. That is why this word has neither a plural nor a dual, for Allah is One and
has no associate.” On the pair: “*Raḥmān* signifies one whose mercy is common to all, and extends to the
whole universe… *Raḥīm* signifies one whose mercy is perfect in all possible ways. That is why *Raḥmān* is
the exclusive attribute of Allah… It is not permissible to qualify any created being as *Raḥmān*… Just
like the word *Allāh*, there is no dual or plural for the word *Raḥmān* too.” And: “The signification of
the word *Raḥīm*, on the contrary, does not contain anything which it should be impossible to find in a
created being… so the word *Raḥīm* may justifiably be employed in the case of a human being”, as the
Qurʾān uses it of the Prophet ﷺ (9:128). Both names “have been derived from the root *Raḥmah* (mercy),
indicating the all-pervasiveness and perfection of divine mercy”.

### V10 · Juristic rulings (aḥkām)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** On recitation: “According to the consensus of Ummah, it is a Sunnah to say
*taʿawwudh* — *Aʿūdhu billāhi mina-sh-Shayṭāni-r-rajīm* — before the recitation of the Holy Qur'an whether
in *ṣalāh* or out of *ṣalāh*”, with 16:98: *When you recite the Qur'an, seek the protection of Allah
against Satan, the rejected one*. “Saying *taʿawwudh* is peculiar to the recitation of the Holy Qur'an.
Therefore, with the exception of *tilāwah*, all other chores should be taken up by first saying
‘Bismillah’ only.” During recitation, one repeats the basmalah at each new sūrah but not the taʿawwudh,
“with the sole exception of the Surah al-Barāʾah”. On handling: “*Bismillah al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm* is a verse
of the Holy Qur'an and a part of the verse in Surah al-Naml; it is also a regular verse when it occurs
between two Surahs. It must, therefore, be treated with as much respect as the Holy Qur'an itself, and it
is not permissible to touch it without having performed *wuḍūʾ*. In the state of major ritual impurity… it
is not allowed to even read this verse as recitation of the Holy Qur'an before having taken a ritual bath.
One may, however, recite it as a form of prayer before beginning a work.”

On *ṣalāh*: “It is a Sunnah to recite ‘Bismillah’ after ‘Aʿūdhu-billāh’ at the very beginning of the first
*rakʿah* in the *ṣalāh*. But views differ as to whether it should be recited in a loud or a low voice.
Imam Abū Ḥanīfah and certain other Imams prefer it to be done in a low voice. There is a consensus on the
point that ‘Bismillah’ should be recited at the beginning of all the succeeding *rakʿahs* too… however, in
some narrations, the reciting of ‘Bismillah’ at the beginning of every *rakʿah* has been identified as
*wājib*.” And on recitation after al-Fātiḥah: “In the course of *ṣalāh*, whether one is reciting the Holy
Qur'an loudly or silently, one should not recite ‘Bismillah’ before beginning a Surah just after the Surah
Fatihah. Such a practice has not been reported either from the Holy Prophet ﷺ or from any of the first four
Khulafāʾ” — the view of Abū Ḥanīfah and Abū Yūsuf preferred in *Sharḥ al-Munyah*, *al-Durr al-Mukhtār*
and *al-Burhān*; “But Imam Muḥammad considers it to be preferable that one should recite ‘Bismillah’ if
one is reciting the Holy Qur'an in a *ṣalāh* offered silently.”

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “The word ‘Allāh’ is… the *al-Ism al-Aʿẓam* (the Great Name)… Allah is the name of
that Ultimate Reality which comprehends in Itself all the attributes of perfection, which is the creator
and sustainer, unique and peerless.” And the mercy of the two names is the motive of creation: “It points
to the fact that the creation of the heavens and the earth and the sustenance of the whole universe have
no other motivation than making manifest Allah's quality of mercy. He Himself had no need of these things,
nor could anyone compel Him to create them.” The Persian verse of Rūmī is quoted: “There was nothing —
neither our being nor our claim to be; it was Thy mercy that heard our unsaid.”

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** As above: the sacralisation of the whole of daily life by the basmalah — “his
economic and worldly needs and activities acquire the nature of the remembrance of Allah, and are counted
as acts of worship”. And: “*Bismillah* is the legendary philosopher's stone which transmutes, not copper,
but mere dust into the purest of gold.”

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V8, V10, V11, V14._

## 1:2 — الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

*[context]* All praise belongs to Allah, the Lord of all the worlds.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** On *al-ḥamd*: “The Surah begins with the words *Al-ḥamdu lillāh*, signifying that
all praise essentially belongs to Allah. Whosoever praises anything anywhere in the world is ultimately
praising Allah… Admiring anything that exists in the created world is no more than showing one's
admiration for a work of art or craft, which in fact is a praise of the artist or the craftsman.” On
*Rabb*: “Lexically, the word *Rabb* signifies ‘one who nurtures’. And ‘nurture’ implies developing a
thing by gradual stages in a manner which is conducive to its own good till it attains perfection. The
word *Rabb* is exclusive to the sacred Being of Allah, and cannot be employed in the case of any created
being without adding some qualification.” On *al-ʿālamīn*: “*Al-ʿālamīn* is the plural of *ʿālam* (world,
universe, kingdom). ‘The worlds’ include all possible forms of existence: the sky, the earth, the sun, the
moon, stars, wind and rain, the angels, the jinns, animals, plants, minerals, and, of course, men. So ‘the
Lord of all the worlds’ means that Allah alone gives nurture to all the forms of existents that are to be
found in this universe, or in the millions of universes that may lie beyond our own.” Imām Rāzī's argument
is given at length: “the existence of an indefinite space beyond our universe can be proved on the basis of
rational argument, and it is also certain that Allah is All-Powerful, so it should not be at all difficult
for Him to have created millions of other universes in this endless space.” The reports: Abū Saʿīd
al-Khudrī — “there are forty thousand worlds; our world, stretching from the East to the West, is only one
of them”; Muqātil — eighty thousand. And Rāzī's answer to the objection from the conditions of life in
space: “the inhabitants of the worlds in the outer space need not necessarily have the same physical
make-up as that of the inhabitants of our world”. The argument closes: “When Allah alone is the ultimate
cause for the nurture of the whole universe, He alone can, in reality, be worthy of praise. Thus, the
first verse of the Surah… combines in itself the praise of Allah and a subtle indication of the first and
basic principle of the Islamic creed — the oneness of God.”

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** 38:27: *We have not created in vain the heavens and the earth and what lies
between them. That is the fancy of the disbelievers*; and 51:56: *I have not created the jinn and mankind
except to worship Me* — the universe is not absurd, and man in it is not purposeless.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** The ḥadīth of the division of the prayer, from the *Ṣaḥīḥ* of Muslim on the
authority of Abū Hurayrah: “Allah has said, ‘The *ṣalāh* (i.e., the Surah al-Fatihah) is equally divided
between Me and My servant. And My servant shall be given what he prays for.’” The responses are then given
verse by verse: on *Praise belongs to Allah, the Lord of all the worlds* — “My servant has paid his homage
to Me”; on *The All-Merciful, the Very-Merciful* — “My servant has praised Me”; on *The Master of the Day
of Judgment* — “My servant has proclaimed my greatness”; on *You alone we worship, and to You alone we
pray for help* — “This verse is common to Me and My servant”; on *Guide us in the straight path* — “All
this is there for My servant. He shall be given what he prays for.” *(Mazharī.)*

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** The lexical matter of this verse is the sense of *Rabb* and the scope of
*al-ʿālamīn*, given above: nurture as gradual development to perfection; and *ʿālam* as a term taking in
all possible forms of existence, so that “the plural ‘worlds’ covers the millions of universes that may
lie beyond our own”.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “If there is only one Being in the whole universe who inherently deserves all
praise, it necessarily follows from it that this Being alone should be Worthy of adoration and worship.
Thus… although the phrase *Al-ḥamdu lillāh* has been used to signify praise, yet, by implication, it cuts
the very root of polytheism or the worship of created beings, and at the same time brings out in a
self-evident manner the first and the basic principle of the Islamic creed — Oneness of God.”

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V8, V11._

## 1:3 — الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* The All-Merciful, the Very-Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “The second verse speaks of the Divine quality of mercy, employing two adjectives,
*Raḥmān* and *Raḥīm*, which are hyperbolic terms in Arabic, and respectively connote the superabundance
and perfection of Divine mercy. The reference to this particular attribute in this situation is perhaps
intended to be a reminder of the fact that it is not through any external compulsion or inner need or any
kind of necessity whatsoever that Allah has assumed the responsibility of nurturing the whole of His
creation, but in response to the demand of His own quality of mercy. If this whole universe did not exist,
He would suffer no loss; if it does exist, it is no burden to Him.”

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** The two names are “hyperbolic terms in Arabic” (*ṣīghat al-mubālaghah*) — “the
superabundance and perfection of Divine mercy” — with the lexical distinction of *Raḥmān* and *Raḥīm*
already set out at 1:1, V8.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V8._

## 1:4 — مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

*[context]* The Master of the Day of Judgment.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “The third verse pays homage to Allah as ‘the Master of the Day of Judgment or
Requital’. The word *Mālik* has been derived from the root *milk*, which signifies possessing a thing in
such a manner that one has the right and power to dispose of it as one likes. The word *Dīn* signifies
‘Requital’. So the phrase ‘Master of the Day of Requital’ implies total mastery on the Day of Requital…
According to the commentary *al-Kashshāf*, the phrase makes a general reference to cover everything. That
is to say, on the Day of Requital the mastery over everything that exists will belong to Allah alone.”
The two questions are put and answered: “Firstly, what is this Day of Requital? Secondly, Allah being the
Master of everything even today as much as on the Day of Requital, why does this verse specifically
mention the Day of Requital? The Day of Requital or the Day of Judgment is the Day appointed by Allah to
recompense good or evil deeds.” And the reason for the specification: “The real and complete mastery over
everything, no doubt, belongs to Allah alone even in this world. Yet Allah Himself, in His beneficence and
wisdom, has granted a kind of imperfect, temporary and apparent mastery to man as well… But today, in
possessing lands or money or power, which has been given to him by way of trial, man has always been prone
to get drunk with pride and vanity.”

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “The verses of the Surah al-Muʾmin/Ghāfir (Chapter 40) serve as a commentary on the
phrase under discussion, and provide a clear account of the Day of Requital”: 40:17 — *The day they will
present themselves before Allah, and nothing of theirs will remain hidden from Allah… “Whose is the
kingdom today?” Of Allah alone, the One, the Mighty. Today everyone will be recompensed for what he has
done. Today no one will be wronged. Allah's reckoning is surely swift.*

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** *Mālik* from *milk*: “possessing a thing in such a manner that one has the right and
power to dispose of it as one likes (see *Qāmūs*)”; *Dīn*: “signifies ‘Requital’”.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** The ownership of the Day: “there shall come a Day when masters will no more be
masters and slaves no more slaves, when no one will own anything even in appearance, and the ownership and
mastery, apparent as well as real, of the whole universe will be seen to belong to none but Allah, the
Exalted.”

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “The phrase ‘Master of the Day of Judgment’ is a warning to man reeling in his
forgetfulness and self-conceit, and an intimation that all his possessions, all his relationships with
things and men are only short-lived.”

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V8, V11, V15._

## 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

*[context]* You alone we worship, and from You alone we seek help.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “The fourth verse… has a double aspect, one of praise and another of prayer”, and
the conclusion is drawn from the three preceding verses: “The three verses having made it clear that man
is totally and absolutely dependent on Allah in all the three states of his life, it logically and
naturally leads to the conclusion that Allah alone is worthy of being worshipped, for in Arabic the word
*ʿibādah* (worship) connotes showing the utmost humility and submissiveness out of an intense respect and
love for someone, and such an attitude of willing self-abasement cannot justly be adopted towards anyone
except Allah.” On seeking help: “It may also be noticed that the phrase *iyyāka nastaʿīn* does not mention
the purpose for which help is being sought. According to most of the commentators, it generalizes the idea
of the request to cover everything from acts of worship to all possible worldly or other-worldly
concerns.” And it “teaches man not to worship anyone except Allah, not to consider anyone else as being
really capable of satisfying his needs, and not to beg anyone else to satisfy these needs. It does not,
however, go against this principle if, in praying to Allah, one mentions the name of a prophet or a man of
Allah by way of a medium (*wasīlah*) for drawing the mercy of Allah upon oneself.”

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** Under “Seeking Allah's Help Directly and Indirectly” the two forms of association in
seeking help are set out: “one becomes guilty of association, if one seeks the help of an angel or prophet
or saint or any creature believing him to be omnipotent like Allah”; and the second, “the form adopted by
idolaters and associators. They admit that God alone is Omnipotent, but also believe that He has delegated
a part of His power to an angel or a prophet or a saint… and to whom one may pray for help in matters
within his jurisdiction. This is the supplication which the Holy Qur'an forbids, and against which it warns
us in the phrase ‘to You alone we pray for help’.” On miracles: “Miracles and wonders are the direct acts of
Allah, but they are manifested through prophets and saints so that people may recognize their spiritual
station — prophets and saints themselves have no powers to make such things happen”, with 8:17 — *When you
threw, it was not you that threw, but Allah threw*; 11:33; and 14:11 — *We cannot give you proof, except by
Allah's will.* The analogy: “In your room, you receive light from a lamp and air from a fan, but the lamp
and the fan do not possess in themselves an absolute power… Similarly, saints, prophets and angels, all
depend on Allah in everything they do.” And the conclusion on *wasīlah*: “Taking prophets and saints as a
medium for drawing divine mercy upon oneself is neither absolutely permissible nor absolutely forbidden…
If one does so, believing a prophet or a saint to be all-powerful, it becomes an act of *shirk* and is
hence forbidden. But if one takes a prophet or a saint to be no more than a medium or a means, it is
permissible.”

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “Acts of worship (*ʿibādah*) are not limited merely to prescribed prayers or
fasting. Imam al-Ghazzālī in his book *Arbaʿīn* has enumerated ten forms which worship can take: (1)
Prayers; (2) Prescribed alms-giving; (3) Fasting; (4) Hajj; (5) Reciting the Holy Qur'an; (6) Remembrance
of Allah in all possible situations; (7) Earning one's livelihood in accordance with the regulations of the
Sharīʿah; (8) Fulfilling one's obligations towards one's companions and neighbours; (9) Persuading people
to act righteously and dissuading them from what is reprehensible and forbidden; (10) To follow the Sunnah
of the Holy Prophet ﷺ.” And the content of tawḥīd in worship: “one should not love or fear or depend on
anyone else as one loves, or fears, or depends on Allah, nor repose one's hope in anyone else, nor consider
obedience or submission or service to another as obligatory as the worship of Allah, nor make a votive
offering or consecrate anything to anyone… nor show complete self-abasement and total humility before
anyone as one is required to do before Allah, nor engage in the particular God-oriented acts of worship
for anyone other than Allah… such as *rukūʿ* and *sujūd*.”

_Cross-heads: V16 — the treatment of *wasīlah* and of the two extremes (outright rejection or exaggerated
veneration) answers a live contemporary dispute and is recorded under contemporary application at 1:7._

_Coverage: V1, V2, V11, V14._

## 1:6 — اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ

*[context]* Guide us in the straight path.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “A problem of highest significance arises here. The teaching with regard to the
prayer for being guided in the straight path is addressed equally to all men or all Muslims and to saints
and prophets who have already received guidance and are even a source of guidance for other men. Why
should these repeatedly pray for something they already possess? The answer… depends on knowing all that is
implied by guidance.” The answer is given in degrees, after al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī: “*Hidāyah* signifies
leading someone towards his destination, gently and kindly; while guidance, in the real sense, issues forth
from Allah alone, and it has several degrees.” The **first degree** “is general, and covers everything that
exists in the universe — minerals, plants, animals etc.”: “Allah has given every creature a particular
nature and function, and guided it in a way which should correspond to its station in the scheme of
things.” The **second degree** “is not general but particular. It is limited to those creatures which are
considered to be rational, that is, men and jinns. This kind of guidance comes to every man through
prophets and revealed books.” The **third degree** “is still more particular, being special to true
believers (*Muʾminīn*) and the God-fearing (*Muttaqīn*)… and it is called *Tawfīq*. That is to say, Allah's
grace provides a man with internal and external means and circumstances which should make it easy, and
even pleasant for him to accept and act upon the guidance of the Holy Qur'an, and difficult to ignore or
oppose it. The scope of the third degree of guidance is limitless, and its levels indefinite… The agency of
this progress is the performance of virtuous deeds. All increase in virtuous deeds brings with it an
increase in divine guidance.” Hence “of all the prayers man can address to Allah, the most important is the
prayer for guidance, which has been taught to us in the very first Surah of the Holy Qur'an”. Three notes
of caution are drawn: the general and the special senses of guidance remove the apparent contradiction in
the Qurʾān's statements that Allah guides all and that He does not guide the unjust; and “The first and the
third degrees of guidance pertain to a direct act of divine grace, and no prophet can have anything to do
with it, for the function of the prophets is related only to the second degree”, so that 28:56 — *You
cannot guide whom you please* — concerns the third degree.

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** 20:50: *He gave to everything its distinctive form, and then guided it*; 87:1–2:
*Who has created all things, well proportioned them, and Who has determined and guided them*; 19:93;
17:44: *Nothing exists that does not celebrate His praise*; 24:41; 47:17: *As for those who follow the
straight path, Allah will increase their guidance*; 64:11: *And whoever believes in Allah, He guides his
heart*; 29:69: *Those who strive for Us, We will surely guide them in Our paths*; 48:20; 28:56.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** The lexical discussion of *hidāyah* — “leading someone towards his destination,
gently and kindly” — and its three degrees, from al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī's *Mufradāt al-Qurʾān*.

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “To sum up, the Qurʾānic prayer ‘guide us in the straight path’ is most
comprehensive, and certainly, one of the most important prayers taught to man. No member of the human
family can claim not to need it… Particularly so, for man lost in the anxieties of mortal life, the prayer
for the straight path is an elixir, though people do not realize it.”

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** The verse teaches the manner of supplication: “The proper method is that one should
begin by fulfilling one's obligation to praise Allah. Then, one should offer the pledge of complete
allegiance to Allah… Finally, one should pray for what one wishes to have. And there is every hope that a
prayer made in this manner will be granted.” And the rule of comprehensiveness: “in praying to Allah, one
should pray for something so comprehensive that it includes in essence all possible human goals — for
example, pray for being guided in the straight path.”

## 1:7 — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

*[context]* The path of those on whom You have bestowed Your grace, not of those who have incurred Your wrath, nor of those who have gone astray.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “The term signifies the particular way of Faith which equally avoids the two
extremes of excess and deficiency. One who follows the straight path would, in matters of doctrine and
practice both, neither go beyond the limits nor fall short of them.” The four categories of those blessed,
from 4:69: “Among them all, the prophets are the greatest. The *Ṣiddīqīn* (the constantly true) are those
who acquire spiritual perfection, and thus attain the highest rank among the followers of a prophet. In
common parlance, they are called Men of Allah, or saints. The *Shuhadāʾ* (martyrs) are those who sacrifice
even their lives for the sake of their faith (or, who bear witness to the truth, as the word admits of both
meanings). The righteous (*Ṣāliḥīn*) are those who follow the Sharīʿah completely, not only in the matter
of obligations (*wājibāt*) but also with regard to commendable (*mustaḥabb*) actions.” And the two wrong
paths: “Those who have incurred Allah's wrath are the people who, in spite of being quite familiar with the
commandments of Allah, wilfully go against them out of a calculated perversity or in the service of their
desires, or, in other words, who are deficient in obeying divine injunctions. This, for example, was the
general condition of the Jews… As for *those who go astray*, they are the people who, out of ignorance or
lack of thought, go beyond the limits appointed by Allah, and indulge in excess and exaggeration in
religious matters. This, for example, has generally been the error of the Christians who exceeded the
limits in their reverence for a prophet and turned him into a god.” The conclusion: “we do not ask for the
path of those who are the slaves of their desires… nor the path of those who are ignorant or unmindful or
misled, and indulge in excess and exaggeration in religious matters, but wish for a path between these two
extremes.”

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** 4:69: *Those whom Allah has blessed, namely, the prophets, the Ṣiddīqīn, the
Shuhadāʾ, and the righteous* — the positive identification of the straight path.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** On the key to the path being men, not a book only: “According to a ḥadīth, when the
Holy Prophet informed his Companions that, like earlier communities, his Ummah too would be divided into
seventy or seventy-two sects, and that only one among them would be on the right path, they wanted to know
as to which group it would be. The answer he gave also leads on to certain men of Allah, for he said:
‘*That which follows my way and the way of my Companions*’.”

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** The Companion ʿAdī Ibn Ḥātim relates: “when he embraced Islam and presented himself
before the Holy Prophet ﷺ with a cross hanging round his neck, the Holy Prophet ﷺ asked him to remove this
idol. Although at this time the cross did not have the kind of signification for ʿAdī which it has for
Christians, yet he was asked to shun a symbol of ‘association’ externally as well.” The conclusion: “Among
the symbols of ‘association’ are included practices like bowing (*rukūʿ*) or prostrating (*sajdah*) oneself
before anyone except Allah, or going round a person or thing in the prescribed manner of the *ṭawāf* of the
Kaʿbah.”

### V13 · History, sīrah & parables

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** The historical characterisation of the two communities: “the rebelliousness of the
Jews who not only refused to listen to the prophets of Allah but went on to kill them; on the other hand,
there is the excessive zeal of the Christians who deified a prophet.”

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** Under “The Key to the Straight Path”: the Qurʾān “has thus indicated that if one
wishes to follow the straight path, one should seek such and such men… and adopt their way. Here, the Holy
Qurʾān does not ask us to follow the ‘path of the Qurʾān’, for a book alone is not sufficient for the
grooming of man; nor does it ask us to follow ‘the path of the prophet’, for the Holy Prophet was not to be
in this world for ever… In defining the straight path too, He has also enumerated those of His servants who
find special favour with Him — all of which argues that, in trying to understand the Book of Allah and to
act upon it, one cannot solely rely on one's own study and judgment, but must turn to someone who knows.”
And the balance: “Two things are necessary for the physical and spiritual well-being and success of man —
the Book of Allah which contains guidance for every sphere of human life, and the Men of Allah who help in
making this guidance effective.” The couplet of Akbar is quoted: “Courses teach words. But men train men.”

### V16 · Contemporary issues & practical application

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** Two modern errors are addressed. On self-study: “It is common observation that many
people today, though otherwise educated, cherish the erroneous notion that one can acquire a masterly
knowledge of the Holy Qur'an and Ḥadīth merely by reading a translation or at best a commentary. But the
error of such an enterprise is self-evident. Had a book in itself been sufficient for the guidance of men,
there was no need for the prophets to be sent.” On *wasīlah*: “one finds that in this matter people
generally adopt either of the two extreme positions, outright rejection of *wasīlah* or exaggerated
veneration. The truth, however, lies between the two.” And on sectarian division: “there are two kinds of
deviations… Some people elected to follow the Book of Allah alone, ignored the Men of Allah totally and
gave no value to their teachings and explanations. Conversely, others adopted the Men of Allah as the only
criterion of truth and became indifferent to the Book of Allah. Both these ways lead to fatal error.”

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Maʿārif al-Qurʾān.** “As we have said before, the prayer which the Holy Qur'an has chosen to recommend to
everyone, in every situation and for everything one does, is the prayer for being guided in the straight
path. Just as success in the Hereafter depends on taking the straight path which leads one to Paradise, in
the same way… success in all worldly concerns too depends on keeping to the straight path… In view of the
need for the straight path in worldly and other-worldly concerns both, this is the prayer which should
constantly be on the lips and in the heart of a true Muslim — never as an empty verbal exercise, but with a
sincere intention and with the meaning of the words fully present in the mind.” The reflection on man's
natural praise of Allah is added here: “If man only considers his own being, he would find that it is a
microcosm which contains in analogical form everything contained in the macrocosm”, with 76:28 — *It is We
Who created them, and it is We Who endowed their joints with strength* — and 14:34 and 16:18 on the
countless blessings.
