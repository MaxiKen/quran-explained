## Sūrah-level record (S1–S6)

### S1 · Names & titles of the sūrah

**Al-Alūsī.** "This noble sūrah has **names, which some have carried to more than twenty** — I shall give the
ones on which the chain of transmission settles." He enumerates **twenty-two**:

> "**1.** *Fātiḥat al-Kitāb* — **because it is its beginning in the familiar order** — *not* because teaching or
> recitation in prayer is opened with it, as **al-Suyūṭī** claimed, and *not* because it was the first sūrah
> revealed: for beginning in the order of teaching or revelation would require the same order in all the
> Book's parts, and the teaching and revelation orders are not the familiar order."
> "**2.** *Fātiḥat al-Qurʾān* — by the measure just given."
> "**3–4.** *Umm al-Kitāb* and *Umm al-Qurʾān*: the ḥadīth *'Let none of you say Umm al-Kitāb — let him say
> Fātiḥat al-Kitāb'* **has no basis**; rather its being so named is established in the *Ṣaḥīḥ*s. It is called
> *umm* — mother — because the beginning, in writing, recitation, revelation or prayer, is with it, and what
> follows is its follower, **so it is like the mother after whom the child forms**; and a **banner** is also
> called *umm* because it goes first and the army follows it — hence *Umm al-Qurā* (Makkah) — **or because it
> comprises** the aims of the meanings in the Qurʾān: praise of Allah as He deserves, worship by command and
> prohibition, and warning (*waʿīd*)."
> "**5–7.** *al-Kanz* (the Treasure), *al-Wāfiyah* (the Completing), *al-Kāfiyah* (the Sufficing) — **because
> it comprises the treasured essences, so it completes and suffices**; or because **it is not halved in prayer
> and nothing else suffices in its place**."
> "**8.** *al-Asās* (the Foundation) — **because it is the root of the Qurʾān and its first sūrah**."
> "**9–13.** *Sūrat al-Ḥamd*, *Sūrat al-Shukr*, *Sūrat al-Duʿāʾ*, *Sūrat Taʿlīm al-Masʾalah* (the sūrah of
> teaching the asking), *Sūrat al-Suʾāl* (the asking) — **because it comprises these**."
> "**14–15.** *Sūrat al-Munājāh* (the intimate converse) and *Sūrat al-Tafwīḍ* (the committing) — **because the
> servant converses with his Lord by** *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*, **and by what follows, the
> committing of the affair is realised.**"
> "**16–18.** *al-Ruqyah*, *al-Shifāʾ*, *al-Shāfiyah* — **and the sound ḥadīths indicate this.**"
> "**19.** *Sūrat al-Ṣalāh* — **because it is obligatory, or a required part, in it** — the recommended-ness
> being a madhhab of some mujtahids, and a report from some on its place in supererogatory prayer; and from
> its names is *al-Ṣalāh*, by the ḥadīth *'I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant into halves'* —
> **and He meant the sūrah**."
> "**20.** *al-Nūr* — **for its great prominence through frequent use**; or for its lighting the hearts; or
> because its gathered meanings express the Qurʾān as light. **21.** *al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* — evident from what
> preceded. **22.** *al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī* — **because it is seven verses by agreement**."

**And the *sūrah*'s own etymology** (from the same introduction): the word *sūrah* may be *mahmūzah* or not;
if from *al-suʾr* — **"the remainder, because the remainder of a thing is part of it"**; if from *sūr
al-bināʾ* — **"the enclosure, or a city wall, because it encloses its verses"**; or from *al-tasawwur*,
**"rising high, because it rises by being Allah's speech"** — quoting **al-Nābighah**: *"Do you not see that
Allah has given you a **sūrah** around which every king is seen to sway?"* And: "It is also used for a lofty
station, as in the poet's word… And one statement holds it is a self-contained group of verses, so that the
verse of the Kursī [which the Prophet's report marked off] is separately titled by *tawqīf*."

### S2 · Makkī or Madanī

**Al-Alūsī.** "**It is differed over: the majority hold it Makkan — indeed, some hold it among the first of
what was revealed of the Qurʾān, and this is reported from ʿAlī, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah and most of the
Companions. And from Mujāhid: it is Madinan — and he alone said it, so much so that it is counted a slip of
his.** And it is said: **it came down at Makkah when the prayer was prescribed, and at Madinah when the
*qiblah* was turned, so that it be known that it stands in prayer as it stood**; and it is said: some of it
Makkan and some Madinan — **and its weakness is not hidden.**"

And on the proof: "**People have made much of inferring its being Makkan from the verse of al-Ḥijr** —
*wa-laqad ātaynāka sabʿan mina l-mathānī wa-l-qurʾāna l-ʿaẓīm* — and al-Ḥijr is Makkan by the scholars'
explicit statement and the report from Ibn ʿAbbās…" — **but al-Alūsī will not rest on it**: "**the strongest is
the inference from the transmission from the Companions who witnessed the revelation and the sending down —
for that depends first on interpreting *al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī* as the Fātiḥah, which, though sound and
established in the ḥadīths, is also soundly reported from Ibn ʿAbbās and others as the Seven Long Sūrahs; and
secondly on the impossibility of favouring with a thing before giving it, while Allah favoured him — ﷺ — by
the word** *innā fataḥnā laka fatḥan mubīnā* **years before the conquest** (the past tense being the
realisation of the occurrence)."

### S3 · Verse count, and the *basmalah* question

**Al-Alūsī** (the Fātiḥah's longest discussion). Statement of the issue: "**People have differed over the
*basmalah* outside al-Naml — where by agreement it is part of a verse — on ten sayings**: (1) **it is not a
verse of the sūrahs at all**; (2) **it is a verse of all of them except al-Barāʾah**; (3) **a verse of
al-Fātiḥah only**; (4) **part of a verse of al-Fātiḥah only**; (5) **a separate verse (*āyah fadhdhah*)
revealed to mark the heads of the sūrahs, for blessing and for separation**; (6) **it may be made a verse of
them and not a verse, because it was revealed twice with the two descriptions**; (7) **part of a verse of all
the sūrahs**; (8) **a verse of al-Fātiḥah and part of a verse of the other sūrahs**; (9) the reverse;
(10) **separate verses, even if revealed repeatedly.**"

His attribution of the schools: "**Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn al-Mubārak, the people of Makkah such as Ibn Kathīr, and
the people of Kūfah such as ʿĀṣim, al-Kisāʾī and others apart from Ḥamzah, and most of the Shāfiʿīs and the
Imāmiyyah — on the second** [a verse of every sūrah except al-Barāʾah]; **some Shāfiʿīs and Ḥamzah, and it is
attributed to Imām Aḥmad — on the third** [al-Fātiḥah only]; **the people of Madinah — among them Mālik — and
of Syria — among them al-Awzāʿī — and of Baṣrah — among them Abū ʿAmr and Yaʿqūb — on the fifth** [a separate
marking verse]; **and this is the well-known of our madhhab** [Ḥanafī]."

**And his own position, stated after the long refutation of al-Rāzī's sixteen proofs:** "*The sound position
of our madhhab is that* ***bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm is a separate verse, and is of the Qurʾān, though it
is not of al-Fātiḥah itself***; and **many of us have made its recitation in prayer obligatory** — and
al-Zaylaʿī in *Sharḥ al-Kanz* said the soundest is that it is *wājib*, and al-Zāhidī from *al-Mujtabā* that
**the sound position is that it is obligatory in every *rakʿah* in which recitation is obligatory, and this
is the sound report from Abū Ḥanīfah**." And: "**Whoever omits it out of forgetfulness in a *rakʿah* makes
the prostration of forgetfulness — 'the majority said its being obligatory entails it'"** (Ibn Wahbān's
verses). And from *Ghunyat al-Mutamallī*: "**it is the more cautious, and I hold it** — against Qāḍī Khān,
the author of *al-Khulāṣah* and others, and the truth is worthier of being followed." And his sharp verdict
on the opposite camp's zeal: "the saying of some on this is **the utmost tyranny of the pen**, a limit of
partisanship without mastery."

The counting consequence: "**They agreed that al-Fātiḥah is seven verses.** For him who counts the basmalah
of al-Fātiḥah [as its verse], the first verse is *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* and the beginning of the last
verse is *ṣirāṭa lladhīna*; and for him who does not count it, its beginning is *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi
l-ʿālamīn* and the beginning of the last verse is *ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim*." And in the names material:
"***al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī*** — **because it is seven verses by agreement; and we have not seen any comparable
case except *'a-raʾayta'*** [in Sūrat al-Māʿūn, counted variously]; **and the saying that it is eight or nine
is anomalous, not regarded** — or a supposition of the transmitter; **though some of them counted the
*basmalah* a verse without *anʿamta ʿalayhim*, and some the reverse** — the pivot being transmission."

**And the letter-scheme al-Alūsī reports under this head** (his own excursion): "*It is not said*: if the
Fātiḥah gathers the Qurʾān's meanings, why have **seven letters fallen out of it** — *thāʾ, jīm, khāʾ, zāy,
shīn, ẓāʾ, fāʾ*? — *for we say*: **perhaps that is to point to spiritual perfection not entailing formal
completeness**… **and it was from the dark letters (*al-ḥurūf al-ẓalmāniyyah*) not found among the openings of
the sūrahs**; **gathered, after dropping the repeated, they give:** ***'a path upon truth we hold fast'*** —
**and the luminous (*nūrāniyyah*) fourteen letters are all in it**, pointing to the **prevalence of beauty
over majesty** — the recurrence of mercy-words in the Fātiḥah. **And why the remaining seven were not dropped
from the luminous side is so that it be known the affair is mixed** — 'none feels secure from Allah's plan but
the people of loss' (7:99)… **and in the words** *nabbiʾ ʿibādī annī anā l-ghafūru l-raḥīm • wa-anna
ʿadhābī huwa l-ʿadhābu l-alīm* (15:49–50) **is a sign and what a sign of that**… **pointing to the two classes
of the believers** — *fa-minhum ẓālimun li-nafsihi wa-minhum muqtaṣidun wa-minhum sābiqun bi-l-khayrāti*
(35:32) — 'the darkness of his sins does not extinguish his light', and *'the adulterer does not commit
adultery while a believer'* is borne on *perfection*."

**And al-Rāzī's rival explanation of the seven dropped letters, and its rebuttal:** "**Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī**
made the cause of dropping these letters their **indicating punishment** — *thāʾ* from *thubūr* (ruin), *jīm*
the first letter of *Jahannam*, *khāʾ* from *khizhī* (disgrace), *zāy* and *shīn* from *zafīr* and *shahīq*
(the fire's sighs), *zāy* again from *Zaqqūm*, *shīn* from *shaqāʾ*, *ẓāʾ* the first of *ẓill* — *inṭaliqū ilā
ẓillin dhī thalāthi shuʿab* (77:30), and also from *laẓā*, *fāʾ* from *firāq* — then that Allah set against the
seven gates of Jahannam — *lahā sabʿatu abwābin li-kulli bābin minhum juzʾun maqsūm* (15:44) — the dropping of
seven letters, so that **whoever recites this sūrah, believes in it and knows its realities is safe from the
seven depths of Jahannam.**" — **Al-Alūsī**: "'The flaw in it is not hidden and its answer does not profit
him nor suffice him, for one may say: then let *dhāl*, *wāw*, *nūn*, *ḥāʾ*, *ʿayn*, *mīm* and *ghayn* fall
out too — *wāw* from *al-wayl*, *dhāl* from *al-dhillah*, *nūn* from *al-nār*, *ḥāʾ* from *al-ḥamīm*, *ʿayn*
from *al-ʿadhāb*, *mīm* from *al-mihād*, *ghayn* from *al-ghawāshī* — and the verses are plain, all of them
concerning the people of the Fire, **and the use would be the same with no difference at all**… and I do not
accept this for al-Fakhr, he being the master…" And the attribution of that scheme to **Amīr al-Muʾminīn ʿAlī**
— "when Caesar of Rome asked Muʿāwiyah about it and he did not answer, then asked ʿAlī and he answered" — "**it
has no basis**; and granting it, what the Amīr aimed at was but to alert the questioner to what is hidden from
you of the secrets."

### S4 · Faḍāʾil of the sūrah

**Al-Alūsī.** On the widely-cited *"the Fātiḥah equals two-thirds of the Qurʾān"*: he reports **al-Shihāb's**
attempt (the three *dalālāt* — *muṭābaqah*, *taḍammun*, *iltizām*), calls it "the most wondrous", then gives
his own harmonisations:

> "**The two reports may be reconciled** — that the first [tradition] was first, and the reward was multiplied
> second; **and there is no restriction on the vast mercy**; or **the difference of report is due to the
> difference of circumstance**; or **what equals a thing entire equals two-thirds of it**; or **in one or both
> reports 'the Qurʾān' means the prayer** — as in *wa-qurʾāna l-fajri inna qurʾāna l-fajri kāna mashhūdā*
> (17:78) — **and that differs with people's ranks in their recitation and prayer: so let him reflect.**"

And he cites with report-criticism: "**al-Daylamī in *al-Firdaws*, from Abū al-Dardāʾ**: *'The Fātiḥah of the
Book suffices where nothing of the Qurʾān suffices; and were the Fātiḥah of the Book placed in one pan of the
balance and the Qurʾān in the other, the Fātiḥah of the Book would outweigh the Qurʾān seven times'*" —
"**for what first comes to mind from it is superiority in reward**…" And the reasons given for the name *Umm*:
"**because it is the best of the sūrahs; or because its sanctity is as the sanctity of the whole Qurʾān; or
because it is the refuge of the people of faith; or because it is *muḥkamah*, and the *muḥkamāt* are the
Mother of the Book.**"

### S5 · Maqāṣid — what the Fātiḥah gathers

**Al-Alūsī.** "**The Fātiḥah comprises the aims of the meanings in the Qurʾān** — praise of Allah as He is
worthy of it, worship by command and prohibition, and the warning (*waʿīd*)" — and in full:

> "**Praise** is evident; **worship** either from *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*, because it is for teaching and an
> imperative is posited that it conveys — and a positive command entails, in general, the prohibition of its
> opposite — **or from** *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm* **if what is meant is the religion of Islam, or from
> the positing of 'say:* bismi llāh*', or from the [imperative force of] the postponed verb**; **and either
> from** *iyyāka naʿbudu* — **for it is an informing of their specialising Him with worship** — **and from**
> *anʿamta ʿalayhim ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim* **or from 'the day of the religion', that is, the requital**
> — **the promise and the threat**."

And the famous ascent: "**the meanings of the Books are in the Qurʾān, the meanings of the Qurʾān in the
Fātiḥah, the meanings of the Fātiḥah in the *basmalah*, and the meanings of the *basmalah* in the *bāʾ***"
(quoted from what al-Suyūṭī carried from al-Sirminī). And the four kinds of knowledge:

> "**(1) The science of *uṣūl*** — its knots the knowledge of Allah and His attributes, pointed to by *rabbi
> l-ʿālamīn, al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*; the knowledge of prophethood, intended by *anʿamta ʿalayhim*; and the return,
> indicated by *māliki yawmi l-dīn*. **(2) The science of the branches (*furūʿ*)** — its base acts of worship,
> intended by *iyyāka naʿbudu*, bodily and financial; and these need the affairs of livelihood, transactions
> and marriages, and these need governments — **so the branches are established on the roots. (3) The science
> by which perfection is obtained — ethics — the noblest of it the arrival at the Presence and travelling the
> path of uprightness**, pointed to by *iyyāka nastaʿīn* and *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm*. **(4) The science of
> narratives and reports of the past nations, the happy and the wretched, and the promise and threat joined to
> them** — intended by *anʿamta ʿalayhim ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*."

And the ordering of the sūrah: "**the first is learned from the beginning of the sūrah to *yawmi l-dīn*, the
second from *iyyāka naʿbudu* and after it, the travelling of the straight path from *ihdinā*… and the
inspection from *anʿamta ʿalayhim* and what follows.**" And the note on al-Bayḍāwī's division: "**he made
*praise* a partner to *worship*, though reason gives it no ruling — perhaps as a hint that the gratitude of a
benefactor is obligatory by reason, observing the Muʿtazilī doctrine, and al-Bayḍāwī did not mind that**" —
and al-Alūsī notes: "**and the three [classes] of people: the many whom desire and fear drive; the middle who
hope and fear; and the elect, few, who have intimacy and awe — by the three, guidance to the interests of this
life and the next is complete.**"

### S6 · Coherence & connection (*munāsabah / naẓm*)

**Al-Alūsī.** On the wisdom of the Qurʾān's being divided into sūrahs — **contrary to al-Zarkashī** — "**that
it be more lively for the reciter and more rousing to acquisition**; like the traveller: when he cuts a mile
or a league, that gives him relief and he is cheered for the road. **And when the memoriser takes up a sūrah,
he believes he has taken from Allah's Book a self-contained lot, so what he has memorised grows great in his
eyes. And also: a genus under which species and kinds are gathered is finer than to have under it a single
chapter** — **and in that is the realisation of each sūrah by itself being a miracle and a sign of Allah's
signs**; and the wisdom in their being long and short is more apparent than to be hidden."

## 1:1 — بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* In the name of Allah, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Alūsī.** The basmalah's own *abḥāth* run in three researches (he labels them *البحث*):

* **First research — is the basmalah one of the peculiarities of this community?** "**The learned Abū Bakr
  al-Tūnisī** transmitted **the consensus of the scholars of every religious community that Allah, Exalted,
  opened every scripture with it**; and **al-Suyūṭī** related from **al-Sirminī** — '*bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni
  l-Raḥīm* is the opening of every scripture' — and this transmitter went to the position that the basmalah is
  **one of the particularities**, because it is reported *'that he — ﷺ — used to write* ***bi-smika Allāhummā
  ('in Your name, O Allah')*** *until there came down* *bismi llāhi majrāhā* (11:41) *and he ordered the writing
  of* *bismi llāh*, *until there came down* *quli dʿū llāha awi dʿū l-Raḥmān* (17:110) *and he ordered the
  writing of* *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*, *until the verse of al-Naml came down and he ordered the writing
  of* *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm.'*" **Al-Alūsī's answer**: the argument does not entail the negation of the
  other scriptures' having it (their knowledge then may not have been given); what is peculiar to the Qurʾān is
  **the Arabic wording in this arrangement** — the ancient scriptures being non-Arabic (contrary to
  al-Ghayṭī), and what of them is in the Qurʾān is translated; "so perhaps these very words have a part in the
  comprehension of all the meanings, so they would not be in other than the Qurʾān as al-Sirminī supposed,
  even if there be a basmalah there."
* **Third research — the meaning of *bismi llāh*** (deferred to V8 below, and V2's own point): "**the *bāʾ* is
  either for *istiʿānah*, or *muṣāḥabah*, or adhesion, or elevation, or redundant, or for an oath** — the last
  four being nothing."

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Al-Alūsī.** The verses that carry the discussion: **11:41** (*bismi llāhi majrāhā* — Nūḥ's words, basis of
the historic-phase argument and of the *muṣāḥabah* reading of the *bāʾ*); **27:30** (*innahu min Sulaymāna
wa-innahu bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* — the scriptural model, and the "writing" argument); **17:110**
(*quli dʿū llāha awi dʿū l-Raḥmān* — the phase in which the Name was added); **96:1** (*iqraʾ bi-smi
rabbika* — the opening revelation, on which al-Rāzī built a proof; see V7); **15:87** (*sabʿan mina
l-mathānī* — the seven; the S2 and S3 arguments turn on it); **7:128** (*istaʿīnū bi-llāhi wa-ṣbirū* — cited
for *istiʿānah* with the Name); **35:32** (*fa-minhum ẓālimun li-nafsihi* — the three classes, in the
letter-scheme); **15:44** (the seven gates of Jahannam) and **77:30** (*inṭaliqū ilā ẓillin dhī thalāthi
shuʿab*) — in the letter discussion; **15:49–50** (*nabbiʾ ʿibādī…*) — for the mixed brightness of the
Fātiḥah.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Al-Alūsī.** He gathers and **weighs** the basmalah's ḥadīths — reporting them with their chains as he finds
them, then giving his verdict:

> **1. Umm Salamah** — via **al-Shāfiʿī ← Ibn Jurayj ← Abū Mulaykah ← Umm Salamah**: "**The Messenger of Allah
> ﷺ recited Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and counted** *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* **a verse**, *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi
> rabbi l-ʿālamīn* **a verse**, *al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* **a verse**, *māliki yawmi l-dīn* **a verse**, *iyyāka
> naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn* **a verse**, *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm* **a verse**, *ṣirāṭa lladhīna anʿamta
> ʿalayhim ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* **a verse**." — **Al-Alūsī**: "**Abū Mulaykah's hearing
> from Umm Salamah is not established**; and granting the contemporaneity, **this wording is not found in the
> well-known [books]**, and he perhaps related by meaning, as appears to him, from some of the following
> reports" — he then cites the four-witness *taqṭīʿ* reports: **Abū ʿUbayd, Aḥmad and Abū Dāwūd**: "**the
> Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to cut his recitation verse by verse** — *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* •
> *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* • *al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* • *māliki yawmi l-dīn*"; **Ibn al-Anbārī and
> al-Bayhaqī** with "then he would stop"; **Ibn Khuzaymah and al-Ḥākim**: "the Prophet ﷺ recited in prayer
> *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* and counted it a verse, *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* two, *al-Raḥmāni
> l-Raḥīm* three verses, *māliki yawmi l-dīn* four, and said **'thus *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*' and
> joined five fingers**"; **al-Dāraquṭnī**: "he would recite *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*, *al-ḥamdu
> li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* to its end, cutting it verse by verse and counting as the bedouins count, **and he
> counted *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* and did not count *ʿalayhim***." **Al-Alūsī's verdict on all
> four**: "**they prove only that it is a verse — and that we concede — but of the Qurʾān, not [that it is] of
> al-Fātiḥah**: and likewise the third and fourth reports prove only that he recited it in prayer and counted
> it because of stopping on it — which is also conceded by us — **it being the first verse of the Qurʾān, and
> the second being *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn***, and so on to the fifth — and he joined the fingers
> and the speech was cut off. **And the fourth report is not an explicit text either**… **and all that is
> established from this report is that the basmalah is a verse of the Qurʾān — which the two sides concede;
> as for its being of al-Fātiḥah, that is a thorn-bush to be traversed.**"
> **2. Abū Hurayrah** — **Saʿīd al-Maqburī ← his father ← Abū Hurayrah**: "**Fātiḥat al-Kitāb is seven
> verses, the first of them *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm***" — **al-Alūsī**: "**al-Ṭabarānī, Ibn Mardawayh
> and al-Bayhaqī** reported it in the wording: *'al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* — seven verses, *bismi
> llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is one of them; and it is *al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī* and *al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm*, and it is
> Umm al-Qurʾān and it is Fātiḥat al-Kitāb'"; and **al-Dāraquṭnī**: "**When you recite *al-Ḥamd*, then recite
> *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* — it is Umm al-Qurʾān, Umm al-Kitāb, and the Seven Mathānī, and *bismi
> llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is one of its verses**." — **Al-Alūsī's reading (his taʾwīl)**: "**the sense of the
> first report is: *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* to the end [are] seven verses, and the Ḥanafīs said
> this**; and the Prophet, noting the listeners' supposing, from the omission of the basmalah with that former
> doubt, that it is not a verse of the Qurʾān, **removed that supposition in a telling way, saying: *bismi
> llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is one of them — i.e., like one of them in being a verse of the Qurʾān.** And the
> sense of the second: if you mean to recite *al-Ḥamd* to its end, recite before it the basmalah…."
> **3. Abū Burdah ← his father** (al-Thaʿlabī, with his chain): "The Prophet ﷺ: *'Shall I not tell you of a
> verse that has not come down on anyone after Solomon son of David other than me?'* I said, 'Yes.' He said:
> *'With what do you open the Qurʾān when you open the prayer?'* I said, '*bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*.' He
> said: ***'It is it.'***" — and the rest of al-Thaʿlabī's reports: **Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad ← his father ← Jābir**
> ("*How do you say when you stand to prayer?*" — "*al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*" — "*Say: bismi llāhi
> l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*"); **Umm Salamah** (he used to recite basmalah then al-Ḥamd); **ʿAlī** (he would open the
> sūrah with it and said, **"whoever omits its recitation has made a deficiency"**); **Saʿīd b. Jubayr ← Ibn
> ʿAbbās** on 15:87 (*ātaināka sabʿan mina l-mathānī*): "**Fātiḥat al-Kitāb**" — and it was said to Ibn ʿAbbās,
> **"Where is the seventh?"** and he said, "***bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm***"; **Abū Hurayrah** (the
> Prophet: "**When you recite Umm al-Qurʾān, do not leave *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*, for it is one of its
> verses**"); the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh* ḥadīth in the wording: "**I have divided the prayer between Me and My
> servant into two halves. When he says *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*, Allah says: My servant has magnified
> Me. When he says *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*: My servant has praised Me. *al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*: My
> servant has extolled Me. *māliki yawmi l-dīn*: My servant has committed his affair to Me. *iyyāka naʿbudu
> wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*: This is between Me and My servant. *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm*: This is for My
> servant, and My servant shall have what he asks**"); the report of **Abū Hurayrah** in the mosque (a man
> began with *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* and the Prophet said: "**O man, you have cut off your prayer — do you not
> know that *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is of *al-Ḥamd*? Whoever leaves it has left one of its verses,
> and whoever leaves a verse of it has cut off his prayer; and there is no prayer except with it — and
> whoever leaves a verse of it, his prayer is invalid**"); **Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbayd Allāh** (the Prophet: "**Whoever
> leaves *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* has left a verse of Allah's Book**").
> **Al-Alūsī's verdicts on these**: "as for the first, second, third and sixth of [these] — with their
> weakness — and the eighth, **they do not indicate the intended point, and we say what they indicate**; **the
> fourth is *mawqūf* on Ibn ʿAbbās, and we do not concede that its ruling is *rafʿ*** — since *ijtihād* is
> possible, though we say the sound position is that a verse is known only by *tawqīf* from the Lawgiver, as
> is a sūrah — which is why they counted *Alif Lām Mīm* a verse where it occurs and did not count *Alif Lām
> Mīm Rā*; **the fifth I doubt for its wording**, or it is in the wording al-Dāraquṭnī produced; **I have no
> reliance on al-Fakhr in ḥadīths — he is not one of their memorisers, and I see that when he relates by
> meaning he alters; and I do not have al-Thaʿlabī's tafsīr to see it, for the transmission is from him; the
> seventh has no freshness of the Messenger's speech on it, nor his eloquence — he being the most eloquent of
> those who spoke the *ḍād*; one who has handled ḥadīths would judge this one fabricated**; and by my life,
> had it been sound, the Shāfiʿīs would have contented themselves with it or put it before their other proofs
> — would that he had mentioned its *isnād* so we could see it."
> **And from the other side, on the audible/secret question** (see V10): **Muslim ← Anas**: "*I prayed behind
> the Prophet ﷺ, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān and did not hear any of them recite *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni
> l-Raḥīm*"; **Ibn ʿAbbās**: "*The Prophet ﷺ did not say it aloud until he died*"; **Aḥmad and al-Nasāʾī**
> with an *isnād* on the criterion of the two Sheikhs: "**they used not to say *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni
> l-Raḥīm* aloud**"; **al-Ṭabarānī**: "the Messenger of Allah ﷺ **used to conceal** *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni
> l-Raḥīm*, and Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān and ʿAlī"; **Ibn al-Mughaffal**: his son said it aloud and he said,
> "**O my son, beware of novelty in Islam — I prayed behind the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and
> ʿUthmān, and they began the recitation with *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*… so when you pray, say
> *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn***" — i.e., al-Alūsī reads it: **say it aloud and conceal the basmalah**
> — "and it is the madhhab of al-Thawrī, Ibn al-Mubārak, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn al-Zubayr, ʿAmmār b. Yāsir, al-Ḥasan
> b. Abī al-Ḥusayn, al-Shaʿbī, al-Nakhaʿī, Qatādah, ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, al-Aʿmash, al-Zuhrī, Mujāhid,
> Aḥmad and a great many others." **And on the audibility reports**: "**of the reports of saying it aloud none
> is sound except Ibn ʿAbbās's, which al-Shāfiʿī reported**: *'The Messenger of Allah used to say it aloud'* —
> and it is **opposed by what preceded from him** [his own "he did not say it aloud until he died"], or is
> borne on **his saying it aloud at times** to show that it is recited in prayer, as ʿUmar said the *thanāʾ*
> aloud for teaching… **and al-Dāraquṭnī, who wrote a book on saying it aloud, was adjured by some Mālikīs to
> name the sound one, and he said: 'No report of saying it aloud is sound.'**" And on Anas's six contradictory
> reports: "**it is not harmful to us — the stronger *isnād* is preferred at contradiction, and here it is
> what agrees with us** [the criterion of the two Sheikhs]; and the charge of lying against a transmitter who
> opposes, on Anas, **is lighter in my eyes than the charge of lying on Anas himself against the Messenger of
> Allah ﷺ and the foremost of his Companions.**"

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Alūsī.** The chapter's great debate — al-Rāzī's sixteen proofs that the basmalah is a verse of al-Fātiḥah,
and al-Alūsī's refutations (the whole runs some thirteen printed pages; his own note: **"al-Fakhr made the
saying long in this place and brought sixteen proofs… and I stand, by Allah's enabling, his refuter — and there
is no boasting — and the champion of my madhhab with Allah's support."** He adds his own history: "**I used
before to count the noble Shāfiʿīs my goal and did not count myself but one of them — the freshness of their
sayings had taken my heart as Laylā of ʿĀmir took Qays's — until I came to be occupied with the sayings of the
noble Ḥanafīs and lodged in their meadows of anemones…**")). His verdict on the sixteen as a set: **"most of
it is answered, and some of it does not stand as a proof against us, because the sound position of our madhhab
is that *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is a separate verse, of the Qurʾān, though not of al-Fātiḥah itself;
and many of us have made its recitation in prayer obligatory…"** — and then **the two reports of *qasamtu
al-ṣalāh*** (the Muslim wording without the basmalah being counted in the division, against al-Thaʿlabī's):

> "**And this shows that the basmalah is not of al-Fātiḥah, and that it [the Fātiḥah] is seven without it**,
> since He made the middle *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*, the three before it for Allah and the three
> after it for the servant; **and in it is no negation of its being of the Qurʾān.** And no doubt this report
> is sounder than al-Thaʿlabī's report — **and I do not put Thaʿlabī before Muslim** — and likewise than
> al-Sijistānī's… and **where a trustworthy transmitter contradicts one more trustworthy than himself, by
> addition or omission, his ḥadīth is anomalous**; and this is not of the category of affirmation and negation
> as those ungrounded in the art supposed… **and one who loads 'half' onto half of meaning or kind is void of
> fairness.**"

And his own methodological terse notes against al-Rāzī's individual proofs (compressed): the *baʿḍiyyah*
comparison "the fifth [proof] is like the fifth" — i.e., **"we do not concede that its being obligatory at
the head of the Fātiḥah entails its being a verse of it; and his inference here from *iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika* is
very weak in ways more visible than the sun"**; "**the sixth proof is the strongest of what he infers for the
basmalah's being of the Qurʾān; as for its being of the Fātiḥah, no**" — and he chides those who on that
ground attacked the Qurʾān question: "**fairness is half of religion, and submission to the truth is of the
believers' character**"; "**the seventh is for us, not against us**"; "**the eighth is far from establishing
what it turns on — the weakening of our master Abū Ḥanīfah's word — there are firm mountains in the way**";
"**the ninth is like the fifth, by the measure of the arrow**; and his inference from *'every matter of
moment…'* is nothing: for the Fātiḥah is part of the prayer opened by the *takbīr* joined to the intention,
which is a pillar of it — and if it is not opened with the basmalah, it is a truncated [act]... and likewise
the bowing and prostration, the nearest a servant comes to his Lord — each of them a matter of moment — so
if not opened with the basmalah it is truncated" — and so on through the rest: "**the remaining proofs are
like many of the past ones — they do not profit in the debate with us except by blackening the page and
wasting precious breaths**… **and some of what he mentioned is contradicted by what Muslim and others
reported from Abū Hurayrah** [the *qasamtu* ḥadīth]."

And the **evaluation of the *bāʾ*** among the mufassirūn: "*Istiʿānah* or *muṣāḥabah* are the candidates among
the six senses — **al-Bayḍāwī's words signal the first as weightier, al-Zamakhsharī's the second** — and the
case made for *muṣāḥabah* (more frequent; the Name as *tabarruk* rather than a base instrument; the
associators' use of their gods' names for blessing; the ḥadīth *'the naming of Allah in the heart of every
Muslim, whether he names or not'*; that blessing-with-the-Name is a meaning everyone grasps, while the
instrument-meaning is only reached by fine reflection; that making His Name an instrument for reciting the
Fātiḥah does not work for him who holds the basmalah part of the sūrah; and that making the existent as the
non-existent is a rhetorical nicety), **and al-Alūsī's own choice**: "**with me, *istiʿānah* is worthier —
indeed it is almost certainly the one** — for in it is an ado of humility and a showing of servitude that the
claim of accompaniment lacks, and in it a hint from the first glance of the dropping of one's own might and
power and the denying of the creature's independence and influence; **it is the opening of the gate of mercy
and a winning of the treasure — 'there is no might nor power save with Allah'** — and this meaning is closer to
*iyyāka nastaʿīn*, and it is the more apt with *iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika*, so that it be a complete answer to his
word ﷺ *'I am not a reciter'*; **and all they mention in support of *muṣāḥabah* is rejected**" — with his
point-by-point rebuttals (the frequency-claim, the "base instrument" presumption — *istaʿīnū bi-llāhi* is
sound and the Name is not debased — the associators' practice is closer to *istiʿānah*, instrumentality
requires presence through the whole act, the ḥadīth is more apt for *istiʿānah* since it declares the
servant's freeing himself of his own strength, etc.).

And **the omitted verb** — the mufassirūn's evaluation: "**Imām Ibn Jarīr chose the estimate *atlū* (I
recite), because what is recited is [thereby] recited** — and likewise the particular verb is suppressed for
every doer of an act, making the naming the beginning of his act — a Qurʾānic meaning like its likes, their
being necessary in the language's usage; **and by it the word of al-Ṣādiqī is repelled**… **and when this was
unclear to some, he made the estimated [verb] an imperative addressed to the servants, so that the sayer of
the spoken and the estimated be one — al-Farrāʾ chose this** — and it is reported **from Ibn ʿAbbās** —
**because Allah put the naming first as an urging of the servants to do that, which suits teaching.** **And
the grammarians went to [the estimate] as general: *abtadiʾ* (I begin)**, supported by faces: the act of
beginning can be estimated in every naming, unlike the act of recitation; and the general estimate is
worthier — *see how they estimate the governor of the *jār* standing as predicate, description, state or
relative clause by *kawn* and *istiqrār* wherever it falls, and prefer it for the generality of its
soundness*… " **And al-Alūsī's tarjīḥ**: "**what Imām [Ibn Jarīr] went to is more apt, more particular to the
aim and more complete in comprehension, for it entails the recitation occurring in its entirety, joined to the
naming, seeking help by Allah's Name over all of it** — unlike the estimate *abtadiʾ*, which does not touch
that" — with the discussions of al-Naḥwiyyūn, of *ofthe beginning being not more general than recitation*,
of the estimate *ibtidāʾī*, and of **al-Shaykh al-Akbar** (Ibn ʿArabī): "**he made this *jār* the predicate of
an omitted subject: *the beginning of the world and its appearance* — because the cause of its existence is
the divine Names, which have authority over it** — as though making it attached to what follows, since Allah
is not praised except by His Names… **and I do not see objection to him as fairness**."
And on **the name and the named**: "there has long been contention whether **the name is the very named or
other than it** — **the Ashʿarīs on the first, the Muʿtazilah on the second** — and **Fakhr al-Dīn in
*al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr* said this discussion runs the course of futility** and offered a face he claimed
fine and subtle, **and al-Shihāb spared us the labour of refuting him**; **al-Suhaylī** had a saying he
claimed to be the truth, and **Ibn al-Sayyid wrote a separate epistle in refutation**; **al-Shihāb claimed it
had until now not been freed [of difficulty], and that with his wide reading he had not seen on this question
what cools the breast or cures the ailment**… **and I, by Allah's favour, shall mention a thing which, if
accepted, is the utmost of my hope…**" — *and there the page ends and the corpus's abridgement note cuts the
argument off.*

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · ishtiqāq · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Alūsī.** The full third research, *"on its meaning"*, with the lexical and grammatical apparatus:

* **The *bāʾ***: "**either for *istiʿānah*, or *muṣāḥabah*, or adhesion (*ilṣāq*), or elevation
  (*istiʿlā*), or redundant, or for an oath — and the last four are nothing**" (their refutation and the
  preference for *istiʿānah* at V7). "**And they differed over the *jār*'s governor**: **Ibn Jarīr → *atlū*…
  the grammarians → *abtadiʾ* / *ashraʿ*… **al-Farrāʾ** chose the imperative estimate, reported from **Ibn
  ʿAbbās**… **and some went to *ibtidāʾī*** — in it a surplus suppression… **and al-Shaykh al-Akbar made this
  *jār* the predicate of an omitted subject**…"
* **Whether the *ẓarf* is *mustaqirr* or *laghw*** — with the three definitions of the grammarians
  (*laghw* = whose governor is mentioned; *mustaqirr* = whose governor is omitted or general like
  *ḥuṣūl/istiqrār*) and **his choice of the first, "so the *ẓarf* here is *mustaqirr* however the governor is
  estimated."**
* **The *kasrah* of the *bāʾ***: "**the solitary particles' right is *fatḥah*, because they are built [on
  *sukūn*], and the base in building is *sukūn* for its lightness… except that they, being words on their own,
  are the expected place of beginning — which by the *sukūn* is impossible or hard — **so its right was
  *fatḥah*, since it is the sibling of *sukūn* in the lightness sought**… **and it is distinguished among the
  particles by its being confined to particle-hood and *jarr*, and each of the two suits *kasrah***…" — with
  the counter-cases (*wāw* of conjunction, *kāf* of comparison, and the answer that in them the *jarr* is not
  their own effect) and **his verdict: "these are grammatical causes extracted after the fact, to display a
  suitability — so they do not bear discussion, for their weakness"**, with the versified sneer he quotes:
  *"My beloved's covenant and compact is weaker than a grammarian's proof"* — "**so we shall not keep the eye of
  thought awake over what is for it and against it.**"
* ***Ism* — its derivation:** "**with the Baṣrans it is one of the ten nouns whose beginnings were built on
  *sukūn*: *ibn, ibnah, ibnūm/ibnum, ism, ist, ithnān, ithnatān, imruʾ, imraʾah, aymanu llāh* and *aymu llāh*
  from it — and by the count of *ibnim*, eleven.** When they utter them they add a *hamzah*, for the ugliness
  of beginning with a *sukūn*… **and its root is *sumuww*, the *wāw* dropped for lightness and frequency of
  use** — "**and its conjugation to *asmāʾ*, *sumayy*, *summiyat*, against *awsām*, *wasīm*, *wasamtu* bears
  witness for it; and vitiation by the heart is not accepted.** And its derivation from *al-sumuww* is like
  *al-ʿuluww*, **because by its indicating what it names it raises it from the lowland of hiddenness to the
  summit of appearance and clarity**." **And the Kūfans**: "**it is from *al-simah*, because it is a mark on
  what it names; its root *wasm*, the *wāw* dropped and the *hamzah* of connection replacing it**… — **and
  they are refuted**: the *hamzah* is not known to enter what has had its head dropped; and the increase of
  *iʿlāl* is more analogically sound than the absence of a parallel; **and its being a compensation entails
  its being intended for itself, while connection entails its being intended secondarily** — compensation being
  like part of the root, comparison being the stronger."
* **The eighteen languages of *ism*** — with the versified mnemonic: "*For *al-ism* ten languages with eight —
  by the transmission of my grandfather, the sheikh of the people, the most complete of them: **smun, simāt,
  samā, ism**, and add **simah**, likewise **samāʾ** with [permitted] three [vowellings] of its first.*"
* **(His *tadabbur* on the *bāʾ*'s *kasrah*, filed at V14 below.)**

### V10 · Juristic rulings & injunctions (aḥkām)

**Al-Alūsī.** From the corpus's basmalah section — the Ḥanafī position argued:

> "**The sound position of our madhhab is that *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is a separate verse, of the
> Qurʾān, though not of al-Fātiḥah itself; and many of us have made its recitation in prayer obligatory.**
> **Al-Zaylaʿī** in *Sharḥ al-Kanz*: **the soundest is that it is *wājib***; **al-Zāhidī** from *al-Mujtabā*:
> **the sound position is that it is obligatory in every *rakʿah* in which recitation is obligatory, and this
> is the sound report from Abū Ḥanīfah**" — and **Ibn Wahbān**'s verse: *"If one omits the basmalah in a
> *rakʿah* out of forgetfulness, he prostrates [in forgetfulness] since its obligatoriness the majority
> said"*; and *Ghunyat al-Mutamallī*: "**it is the more cautious and I hold it — contrary to Qāḍī Khān, the
> author of *al-Khulāṣah* and others**"; and: "**many of our scholars made the prostration of forgetfulness
> incumbent on one who omits it**" — hence the counting of *takrār* in the reports.
> **On the Fātiḥah itself in prayer**: "**it is obligatory, or a required part, in it** — the recommended-ness
> being a madhhab of some *mujtahids*, and a report from some on supererogatory prayer"; the name *Sūrat
> al-Ṣalāh* "because it is obligatory or a required part in it"; and the ḥadīth *'I have divided the prayer
> between Me and My servant'* "**and He meant the sūrah**" (with the juristic note on *al-Ṣalāh*'s being a
> name of the Fātiḥah by *majāz* of whole-and-part or of concomitance).
> **On the *basmalah*'s being a verse**: the ten views (S3) and the *tawqīf* principle: "**the sound position
> is that a verse is known only by *tawqīf* from the Lawgiver, as is a sūrah's [delimitation] — which is why
> they counted *Alif Lām Mīm* a verse where it occurs and did not count *Alif Lām Mīm Rā***." And the ten
> views' legal consequences — whether the *muḥdith* may touch it, [whether] its recitation is obligatory — are
> what al-Rāzī's sixteenth proof tried to turn on "the safer course", and **al-Alūsī's answer: the question
> between us is not theirs to settle** (see V5/V7).
> **And on saying it aloud** (V5): the reports' weighting; the ruling he adopts **is concealment**, with the
> reports of al-Thawrī, Ibn al-Mubārak, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn al-Zubayr, ʿAmmār, al-Ḥasan b. Abī al-Ḥusayn,
> al-Shaʿbī, al-Nakhaʿī, Qatādah, ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, al-Aʿmash, al-Zuhrī, Mujāhid and Aḥmad; **and
> al-Dāraquṭnī's verdict that no *jahr* report is sound**; and the Shīʿī claim of *tawātur* from ʿAlī —
> **"rejected by Ahl al-Sunnah; the Shīʿah claimed it and went to *jahr* in both loud and quiet prayers; and
> if one acted on all they allege as *mutawātir* from the Commander he would disbelieve — so there is only
> faith in part and disbelief in part"** — with his note that ʿAlī's madhhab is a matter of what is *sabt*
> [established], and that with Ahl al-Sunnah the prevalent is what the Two Sheikhs are upon, then Abū Bakr
> when they differ — quoting the four ḥadīths on following the Companions and Abū Bakr and ʿUmar.
> **And the *takbīr*'s history in the same debate**: al-Rāzī's report that Muʿāwiya failed both the basmalah
> and the *takbīr* at the inclinations, "and this does not harm us" — the point being **jahr** — "*yes,* ***jahr*
> remains, and the discussion of it is hidden now**."

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Alūsī.** The doctrinal nodes of the section: (1) **the name and the named** — "**the Ashʿarīs on the
first** [the name is the very named], **the Muʿtazilah on the second** [other than it]", with al-Rāzī's
"course of futility" verdict, al-Shihāb's and al-Suhaylī's and Ibn al-Sayyid's contributions, and **al-Alūsī's
own promised resolution** — the corpus's page ends before the argument is given, and the editor notes the
abridgement; (2) **the two Names** as the frame of the *bāʾ*'s secrets: "**the attributes are either of beauty
or of majesty, and the first has precedence**, as the ḥadīth *'My mercy precedes My wrath'* indicates; **and
the *bāʾ* of *jarr* is a sign of that**, since it is the medium of annexation and effusion — **so its *kasrah*
and the lowering of the wing suit it**" — with the ḥadīth *'None of you will enter the Garden by his deed —
not even you, Messenger of Allah? — Not even I, unless Allah should cover me with His mercy'*; (3) **the
refutation of the Muʿtazilī "worship is known by reason"** in the maqāṣid section (V/S5 above): "**perhaps he
made praise a partner to worship as a hint that the gratitude of a benefactor is obligatory by reason,
observing the Muʿtazilī doctrine**"; (4) **the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh*'s doctrinal frame** (V5).

### V13 · Historical narratives, sīrah & parables (qiṣaṣ · sīrah)

**Al-Alūsī.** The historical material of the basmalah section: **the writing-phases of the Prophet ﷺ** (from
*bi-smika Allāhummā* to *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*, at 11:41, 17:110 and 27:30 — the report he quotes in
the first research, V2); **Sulaymān's letter** (*innahu min Sulaymāna…*, 27:30) as the model of the completed
formula; **Muʿāwiya's arrival at Madinah and his loud prayer**: "**Anas** — Muʿāwiya came to Madinah and led
the people in a loud prayer, reciting Umm al-Qurʾān **and not reciting the basmalah**; and when he finished,
**the Emigrants and the Helpers called out from every side: 'Did you forget it? Where is *bismi llāhi
l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* when you opened the recitation?' — and Muʿāwiya repeated the prayer and said it aloud.**"
— **al-Alūsī's reading**: "**the utmost its outward indicates — granting it — is that Muʿāwiya, when he did
not recite the basmalah and left the obligatory [act] and did not make the prostration of forgetfulness,
repeated the prayer so that it fall sound from defect; and for this they gave him time until he finished, to
see whether he would repair the defect with the prostration of forgetfulness or not**" — **and his retort**:
"their objecting to him for leaving an obligation repaired by prostration is not stranger than their
objecting to him in that same prayer **for leaving a mode** — as **al-Shāfiʿī himself transmitted, and al-Fakhr
himself carried**, that Muʿāwiya came to Madinah and prayed with them, **not reciting *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni
l-Raḥīm* and not saying the *takbīr* at the inclinations to bowing and prostration**; and when he gave the
salutation, the Emigrants and Helpers called: 'O Muʿāwiya, you have stolen from the prayer — where is *bismi
llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* and where is the *takbīr* at bowing and prostration?' Then he repeated the prayer
with the naming and the *takbīr* — **and this does not harm us**." And: **the era of the *jahr* controversy**
— "the claim that the reports from Anas are six and contradictory, sometimes *jahr* and sometimes concealment,
**because of fear of the Umayyads who opposed ʿAlī**" — his answer (the stronger *isnād* is preferred; the
charge against a transmitter lighter than against Anas); **al-Dāraquṭnī's book on *jahr* and the Mālikīs'
adjuration** (V5); and **the report about Caesar asking Muʿāwiya and then ʿAlī** about the seven dropped
letters — "**it has no basis**" (S3).

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Al-Alūsī.** The mystical reading of the basmalah — for which he explicitly disclaims completeness: "**and
behind it a great secret, and Allah guides to the right path.**"

> **On the *kasrah* of the *bāʾ***: "**and one of them said, from the way of allusion: the *bāʾ* was given
> *kasrah* in the basmalah to teach [the servant] the means of reaching Allah and attaching to His Names by
> the breaking of one's side and the submission and abasement of servitude — for one reaches no kind of
> knowledge except by a kind of abasement and breaking.**" — and **Ibn al-Fāriḍ**'s lines as al-Alūsī quotes
> them: *"Had you for me been a lowering dot of the *bāʾ*, you would be raised to what no stratagem attains;
> so that you see that you do not see what you counted, and that what you prepared is not a preparation"* —
> "**for lowering corresponds to raising: whoever the regard lowers in the abasement of servitude, destiny
> raises to the witnessing of the might of lordship; and this raising is not attained by stratagem but by the
> sheer divine grant. And whoever lowers himself in order to rise — his lowering is caused and his effort is
> unaccepted.**"
> **On the dot and the *bāʾ***: "**this is a matter peculiar to the *bāʾ* of the basmalah; it cannot run in the
> *bāʾ* of *jarr* absolutely**… **and with me the secret of that is that the *bāʾ* is the second rank in
> relation to the simple *alif*, stripped, preceding all ranks — so it is the sign of the Real Existence; and
> the *bāʾ* is a sign of His attributes, which the dot of the [letter's] being displays.**" — and: "when
> **al-Shiblī** was asked, 'Are you al-Shiblī?' he said: '**I am the dot beneath the *bāʾ*.'**" — and
> **al-Shaykh al-Akbar**'s verses as al-Alūsī quotes them: *"The *bāʾ* for the gnostic al-Shiblī is a
> consideration, and in its small dot for the heart a reminder; the secret of the lofty servitude blended into
> it — therefore it stood in the station of the Real, so consider; is not its reality dropped from *bismi*?
> because it is a substitute for it, and that is a burden"* — and his account that "**the attributes are of
> beauty or majesty, and the first has precedence**, as the ḥadīth *'My mercy precedes My wrath'* indicates,
> **and the *bāʾ* of *jarr* is a sign of it**… so the *kasrah* suited it and the lowering of the wing, **that
> the matter be complete and the secret appear**; and in beginning with it here is a hastening of the good
> news and a symbol that **the pivot is mercy**… **and He graduated in showing it: He symbolised with the
> *bāʾ*, pointed with *Allāh*, and made the fullest declaration with *al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm***." And: "**and
> without exception every sūrah — the Lord opened it with a symbol to His state ﷺ, out of magnifying him and
> as good news for him who gives ear while he is a witness**; and when majesty was outward in the Sūrat
> al-Barāʾah, He left the basmalah's sign and brought **an opened *bāʾ*** — for the changed state — and drew
> the curtain over the brides of beauty; **and He did not leave the symbol altogether to the Muḥammadan
> Reality**… **and we cannot be more explicit than this in this chapter for fear of the people of the veil;
> and behind it is a great secret.**"
> **And the ascension of the meanings** (S5 above): "**the meanings of the Books in the Qurʾān, of the Qurʾān
> in the Fātiḥah, of the Fātiḥah in the basmalah, of the basmalah in the *bāʾ***" — with: "**and its being
> dropped in *bismi*: because it is a substitute for it**."
> **And the etiquette of beginning**: "**and in the beginning with it here is a hastening of the good news**
>… **and the first of the rain is a drizzle, then it pours**."

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & moral admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Al-Alūsī.** His counsels within the section: on **the ordering of the *bāʾ*'s meanings**: "**in it is a
hint from the first glance of the dropping of one's might and power, and the negation of the creature's
independence and influence; it is the opening of the gate of mercy**"; on **the estimate of the governor**:
"**the estimate postponed is distanced from the field of verification**"; and, in the great refutation,
**the ethical frame he repeats**: "**the fair-mindedness is half of religion, and submission to the truth is
of the believers' character**"; "**and do not let the saying of a great one (*al-Fakhr*) be taken as
authority in the ḥadīths — he is not one of their memorisers**"; and against the **fanaticism of the pen**:
"**the saying of some on this is the utmost tyranny of the pen and a limit of partisanship without
mastery**"; and the traveller-of-thought image: "**so we shall not keep awake the eyelid of thought over what
is for it and against it**"; and his closing image from the poems of the debate: *"The son of the she-camel,
when bound in a neck-rope, cannot [match] the attack of the full-grown stallions"*.

### V16 · Contemporary issues & practical application

**Al-Alūsī.** In this 19th-century *Rūḥ al-Maʿānī* section, the contemporary application is the polemical
scene itself: (1) **the community's practice**: the obligation to **name Allah over the prayer and to
prostrate for its omission** (V10), and the reminder "**so let the servant consider where he stands**"; (2)
**the intra-school contestation** — al-Alūsī's self-description (a Shāfiʿī-formed scholar who became Ḥanafī)
and his ruling on the debate: "**the more cautious is that it is obligatory, and I hold it, contrary to Qāḍī
Khān, the author of *al-Khulāṣah* and others — and the truth is worthier of being followed**"; (3) **the
cross-confessional challenge**: on the Shīʿī claim of *tawātur* from ʿAlī — "**rejected with Ahl al-Sunnah**,
the Shīʿah claimed it and applied it to *jahr* in both prayers; **and were one to act on all they allege as
*mutawātir* from the Commander he would disbelieve — so there is only faith in part and disbelief in part**";
(4) **the charge of lying and the standing of the Companions**: "**the charge of lying against a transmitter
who opposes [the majority] on Anas is lighter with me than the charge of lying on Anas — the companion of the
Messenger of Allah — against the Messenger of Allah and the foremost of his Companions**."

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Al-Alūsī.** The basmalah's specific merits in this section: **the report of Ubayy b. Kaʿb** — "*What is the
greatest verse in the Qurʾān?* — *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* — and the Prophet confirmed him" — with
al-Alūsī's use of it (V7's tenth proof) and his note; **the four-witness standing of the *taqṭīʿ* reports**;
the **Umm Salamah**, **Abū Hurayrah**, **ʿAlī's** and **Ibn ʿAbbās's** testimonies to its being *one of its
verses* (V5); **the Fātiḥah's own faḍāʾil** (*"equal to two-thirds of the Qurʾān"*, the Abū al-Dardāʾ
report, *al-Ruqyah / al-Shifāʾ / al-Shāfiyah*) — **filed at S4 above**, since the sūrah-level faḍāʾil of the
corpus belong to the sūrah introduction, not to a verse; and **the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh* ḥadīth**, a *ḥadīth
qudsī* of the Fātiḥah as dialogue in prayer (V5).

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V6, V7, V8, V10, V11, V13, V14, V15, V16, V17._
