## 1:1 — بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* In the name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Jalālayn.** The commentary opens on the basmalah — “*In the Name of God*” — with the sense of
beginning: “*I commence and I recite*”. The three names are then unfolded:

* **In the Name of God** — “the name of a thing is that by which it can be known.”
* **Allāh** — “a name for the divine essence *qua* essence absolutely, without taking into consideration
  that it may be qualified by the attributes, or indeed taking into consideration that it may not be so
  qualified.” And: “It — Allāh — is the Greatest Name of God.”
* **the Compassionate** — “the one who causes existence and perfection to flow upon all things in the
  measure that divine wisdom requires and to the capacity of the receiving entities (*qawābil*) from the
  outset of their creation.”
* **the Merciful** (*al-Raḥīm*) — “the one who bestows the spiritual perfection (*kamāl maʿnawī*) that has
  been earmarked for the human species in the end — in the Hereafter.”

Hence, the commentary notes, “they say *O You Who are the Compassionate One of this world and of the
Hereafter* (*yā raḥmān al-dunyā wa-l-ākhira*), but say *O Merciful One of the Hereafter* (*raḥīm
al-ākhira*); the meaning then is in the perfect human all-encompassing form, general and specific mercy,
which is the locus of manifestation of the divine essence and the greatest truth with the totality of
attributes.”

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Al-Jalālayn.** “It is to this meaning that the Prophet's [ﷺ] alluded when he said ‘I have been given the
sum of all excellent speech — [the Qurʾān] — and have been sent to complete the noble character traits
(*makārim al-akhlāq*)’.”

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Jalālayn.** The lexicon of *ism*: a name is that by which its bearer is known; the divine names are
“the archetypal forms (*ṣuwar nawʿiyya*) whose specificities and ipseities (*hūwiyya*) indicate the
attributes of God and His essence, and which indicate by their very existence His countenance and by
their individuation His oneness — for these are the outward manifestations through which He can be
known.” The pair *al-Raḥmān / al-Raḥīm* is distinguished by scope: the first is the name by which the
flow of existence and perfection begins, the second the name of the perfection granted at the end; hence
the first is used of this world and the Hereafter together, the second of the Hereafter alone.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Jalālayn.** The commentary states the identity of the divine names with the essence and its
attributes: *Allāh* names the essence without consideration of the attributes; the names are the
manifestations by which He is known; and *Allāh* is “the Greatest Name of God”.

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Al-Jalālayn.** The basmalah is read as the entry into the human perfection: the Prophet ﷺ was sent “to
complete the noble character traits”. “For words constitute the realities (*ḥaqāʾiq*) of existents
(*mawjūdāt*) and their concrete substances (*aʿyān*), just as Jesus — peace be upon him — was called a
Word from God [cf. 3:39; 4:171].” The noble character traits “are the perfections of these existents and
represent their specificities, constituting the sources of their actions in their entirety, contained as
they are in the comprehensive human microcosm (*kawn insānī*).” A subtlety is noted: “the prophets —
peace be upon them — have made the letters of the alphabet analogous to the ranks of existents (*marātib
al-mawjūdāt*)”.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V5, V8, V11, V14._

## 1:3 — الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* The Compassionate, the Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Jalālayn.** “The Compassionate, the Merciful — that is to say, the One who possesses ‘mercy’ which
means *to want what is good for those who deserve it*.”

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Jalālayn.** The lexical definition of the root: *mercy* is defined as willing the good for those who
deserve it (*irādat al-khayr li-man yastaḥiqq*), which is the sense the two names carry.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V8._

## 1:4 — مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

*[context]* Master of the Day of Judgement.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Jalālayn.** “Master of the Day of Judgement — that is, the day of requite, the Day of Resurrection.
The reason for the specific mention of the Day of Judgement is that the mastery of none shall appear on
that Day except that of God, may He be exalted, as is indicated by God's words *Whose is the Kingdom
today?* — *God's*,” and the commentary notes the reason for singling out the day: on it His mastery —
and no other — appears.

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Al-Jalālayn.** The verse is read by the cross-reference Q 40:16: “as is indicated by God's words ‘Whose
is the Kingdom today?’ ‘God's’”.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Jalālayn.** On the nouns: “if one reads it *mālik* as opposed to *malik*, then this signifies that He
has possession of the entire affair on the Day of Resurrection; or else that He is ever described by this
expression, in the same way as He is described as ‘Forgiver of sin’ (*ghāfir al-dhanb*). Thus one can
validly take it as an adjective of a definite noun.”

### V9 · Variant readings (qirāʾāt)

**Al-Jalālayn.** The two canonical readings of the word are both weighed: *mālik* (possessor) and *malik*
(king), each with the meaning it adds.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V8, V9._

## 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

*[context]* You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Jalālayn.** “You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help — that is to say, we reserve worship
for You alone by way of acknowledging Your Oneness (*tawḥīd*), and so on; and we ask for Your assistance
in worship and in other things.”

_Cross-heads: V11 — the exclusive reservation of worship is stated to be the acknowledging of God's
Oneness (*tawḥīd*)._

_Coverage: V1, V2, V11 (cross-head)._

## 1:7 — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

*[context]* The path of those whom You have favoured, not of those who incurred wrath, nor of those who are astray.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Jalālayn.** The three groups are identified: *those against whom there is wrath* are “the Jews”, and
*those who are astray* are “the Christians”. “The subtle meaning implied by this substitution is that the
guided ones are neither the Jews nor the Christians.”

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Jalālayn.** The grammar of the transition from 1:6: “*the path of those whom You have favoured* — with
guidance; *alladhīna* together with its relative clause is substituted by *ghayri l-maghdūbi ʿalayhim*
(‘not the path of those against whom there is wrath’)” — the substitution (*badal*) is what carries the
negation of the two wrong paths, and the commentary draws the meaning: “the guided ones are neither the
Jews nor the Christians.”

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Al-Jalālayn.** The file closes with the author's customary sign-off, quoted as it stands: “But God knows
best what is right and to Him is the Return and the final Resort. May God bless our master Muhammad (s),
his Family and Companions, and grant them everlasting peace. Sufficient is God for us; an excellent
Guardian is He. There is no power and no strength save in God, the High, the Tremendous.” *(A closing
formula rather than an element of the verse; recorded here for completeness.)*

_Coverage: V1, V2, V8, V14 (closing formula)._
