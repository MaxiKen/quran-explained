## Sūrah-level record (S1–S5)

### S1 · Names & titles of the sūrah

**Al-Baghawī.** "Sūrat Fātiḥat al-Kitāb — **it has three well-known names: *Fātiḥat al-Kitāb*, *Umm
al-Qurʾān*, and *al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī*.** It was called *Fātiḥat al-Kitāb* **because Allah opened the Qurʾān
with it**; and it was called *Umm al-Qurʾān* and *Umm al-Kitāb* **because it is the root of the Qurʾān —
from it the Qurʾān began**, and the *umm* of a thing is its root; Makkah is called *Umm al-Qurā* because it
is the origin of the lands, the earth being spread out from beneath it. And it is said: **because it is a
preface and an imām for the sūrahs that follow it — writing in the *muṣḥaf* begins with it and recitation in
prayer begins with it.** And *al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī* **because it is seven verses by the agreement of the
scholars**; and it was called *mathānī* **because it is repeated in prayer — it is recited in every
*rakʿah***. And **Mujāhid** said: it was called *mathānī* **because Allah reserved it (*istathnāhā*) for
this community and stored it up for them (*fa-dhakharahā lahum*).**"

### S2 · Makkī or Madanī

**Al-Baghawī.** "**It is Makkan on the saying of the majority; and Mujāhid said: Madinan; and it is said: it
came down twice — once at Makkah and once at Madinah, and for that it was called *mathānī*.** **And the
first is sounder, that it is Makkan, because Allah favoured the Messenger ﷺ by His word** *wa-laqad
ātaynāka sabʿan mina l-mathānī* (15:87) **— and what is meant by it is Fātiḥat al-Kitāb, and Sūrat al-Ḥijr
is Makkan, so He would not have been favouring him with it before its revelation.**"

### S3 · Verse count — and the basmalah question

**Al-Baghawī.** "**They agreed that the Fātiḥah is seven verses.** For him who counts the basmalah of the
Fātiḥah, the first verse is *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* and the beginning of the last verse is *ṣirāṭa
lladhīna*; and for him who does not count it of the Fātiḥah, its beginning is *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi
l-ʿālamīn* and the beginning of the last verse is *ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim*."
And the schools, as he states them:

* "**The reciters of Madinah and Baṣrah and the jurists of Kūfah** — that it is not of Fātiḥat al-Kitāb nor
  of any other sūrah, and that the opening with it is for seeking blessing (*li-l-tayammun wa-l-tabarruk*)."
* "**The reciters of Makkah and Kūfah and most of the jurists of the Ḥijāz** — that it is of the Fātiḥah and
  not of the other sūrahs, and that it was written as a separator."
* "**A group** — that it is of the Fātiḥah and of **every sūrah except Sūrat al-Tawbah**; and it is the
  saying of **al-Thawrī, Ibn al-Mubārak and al-Shāfiʿī**, because it was written in the *muṣḥaf* in the
  script of the rest of the Qurʾān."

The two proofs he cites: **for its being a verse of the sūrahs**, al-Shāfiʿī's chain (Ibn Jurayj from his
father from **Saʿīd b. Jubayr**, on 15:87 *wa-laqad ātaynāka sabʿan mina l-mathānī*): "*It is Umm al-Qurʾān*"
— "and Saʿīd b. Jubayr recited it to me until he finished it, then said: *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is
**the seventh verse**"; "Saʿīd said: I recited it to **Ibn ʿAbbās** as I recited it to you, and he said:
*bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is the seventh verse; and Ibn ʿAbbās said: **He stored it up for you, and
did not bring it out for anyone before you.**" **For those who do not count it**, Mālik's chain from **Anas
b. Mālik**: "I stood behind Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq, ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb and ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān — **all of them did
not recite *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* when they began the prayer**"; and **Saʿīd b. Jubayr from Ibn
ʿAbbās**: "**the Messenger of Allah ﷺ did not know the ending of a sūrah until *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni
l-Raḥīm* came down.**" And further, on its being a separator: "**from Ibn Masʿūd** — he said: ***We did not
know the separation between the two sūrahs until *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* came down***; and
**al-Shaʿbī** said: **the Messenger of Allah ﷺ at the beginning wrote in the manner of Quraysh *bi-smika
Allāhumma* until there came down** *wa-qāla rkabū fīhā bismi llāhi majrāhā* (11:41), **so he wrote *bismi
llāh*; until there came down** *quli dʿū llāha awi dʿū l-Raḥmān* (17:110), **so he wrote *bismi llāhi
l-Raḥmān*; until there came down** *innahu min Sulaymāna wa-innahu bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* (27:30),
**so he wrote the like of it.**"

### S4 · Faḍāʾil of the sūrah

**Al-Baghawī** ("a section on the virtue of al-Fātiḥah"). Three reports, with the chains he gives:

> 1. **Abū Hurayrah** — the Prophet ﷺ passed by **Ubayy b. Kaʿb** standing in prayer and called out to him,
> and Ubayy hurried in his prayer and came, and he said to him: *What prevented you, Ubayy, from answering me
> when I called you? Does not Allah say* yā ayyuhā lladhīna āmanū stajībū li-llāhi wa-li-l-rasūli idhā
> daʿākum limā yuḥyīkum*?* Ubayy said: "By Allah, Messenger of Allah, you shall not call me but I answer,
> even if I am praying." He said: *Would you like me to teach you a sūrah the like of which has not come down
> in the Torah, nor the Gospel, nor the Psalms, nor the Qurʾān?* He said: Yes. He said: *Do not leave the
> gate of the mosque until you know it* — and the Prophet ﷺ walked intending to leave the mosque, and when he
> reached the gate Ubayy said: *The sūrah, Messenger of Allah!* He stopped and said: *How do you recite in
> your prayer?* and Ubayy recited **Umm al-Qurʾān**, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **By the One in whose
> hand is my soul, the like of it has not come down in the Torah, nor the Gospel, nor the Psalms, nor the
> Qurʾān — and it is the Seven Mathānī which Allah, Mighty and Exalted, gave me.** _(Al-Baghawī: this is a
> **ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ** ḥadīth.)_
> 2. **Ibn ʿAbbās** — "while the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had Jibrīl with him, he heard a sound from above, and
> Jibrīl raised his gaze to the sky and said: *This is a gate opened from the sky, never opened before*; a
> **malak** came down from it and came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: **Rejoice in two lights given to you, not
> given to a prophet before you — Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and the closing verses of Sūrat al-Baqarah; you will not
> recite a letter of them but you are given it.**" _(ṣaḥīḥ; Muslim's.)_
> 3. **Abū Hurayrah** — the Prophet ﷺ: "*Whoever prays a prayer in which he does not recite Umm al-Qurʾān,
> it is **deficient (*khidāj*), not complete**.*" — "I said: O Abū Hurayrah, sometimes I am behind an imam;
> and he **gripped my forearm** and said: ***Recite it within yourself, O Persian***; for I heard the
> Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: *Allah, Mighty and Exalted, said: **I have divided the prayer between Me and My
> servant into halves: half of it for Me and half of it for My servant, and My servant shall have what he
> asks**. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **Recite** — when the servant says* al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi
> l-ʿālamīn*, Allah says: My servant has praised Me. When he says* al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm, *He says: My servant
> has extolled Me. When he says* māliki yawmi l-dīn, *He says: My servant has magnified Me. When he says*
> iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn, *Allah says: **this verse is between Me and My servant, and My servant
> shall have what he asks.** When he says* ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm… wa-lā l-ḍāllīn, *Allah says: **these
> are for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks.***" _(ṣaḥīḥ; Muslim's, from Qutaybah from
> Mālik.)_

### S5 · Maqāṣid — the division of the sūrah

**Al-Baghawī.** From the last report above, the sūrah's sense is "**half of it for Me and half of it for My
servant**": the first three verses **for Allah** (His praise, extolling and magnification), the fourth
(*iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*) **between Him and His servant**, and the last three (from *ihdinā
l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm* to the end) **for the servant**; "and My servant shall have what he asks".

## 1:1 — بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* In the name of Allah, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Baghawī.**

* **The *bāʾ* and its omitted verb.** "*Bismi llāh*: the *bāʾ* is a particle that makes what follows it
  genitive, like *min* and *ʿan*; **the verb to which the *bāʾ* attaches is omitted, the speech indicating
  it — its sense being *I begin with the name of Allah*, or *say: in the name of Allah***."
* **The dropped *alif* and the lengthened *bāʾ*.** "The *alif* was dropped from *ism* **seeking lightness
  and because of its frequency of use**, and the *bāʾ* was lengthened. **Al-Qutaybī** said: **so that the
  opening of the speech of Allah's Book be with a magnified letter** — and **ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz** used
  to say to his scribes: **lengthen the *bāʾ*, make the *sīn* plain, leave space between them, and round the
  *mīm*** — for the magnifying of the Book of Allah. And it is said: when they dropped the *alif*, they
  returned the length of the *alif* to the *bāʾ* so that it be a sign of the dropping of the *alif* — do you
  not see that when the *alif* was written in *iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika* (96:1) the *bāʾ* was returned to its
  form; **and the *alif* is not dropped when the name is annexed to other than Allah, nor with other than
  the *bāʾ*.**"
* **The name and the named.** "**The name is the named, its very self and essence** (*al-ismu huwa
  l-musammā wa-ʿaynuhu wa-dhātuh*). Allah said *innā nubashshiruka bi-ghulāmin ismuhu Yaḥyā* (19:7) —
  He reported that his name is Yaḥyā, then called the name, saying *yā Yaḥyā*; and *mā taʿbudūna min dūnihi
  illā asmāʾan sammaytumūhā* (12:40) — **and He meant the persons worshipped**, because they were
  worshipping the named; and *sabbiḥi sma rabbika* (87:1), and *tabāraka smu rabbika* (55:78). **Then the
  *tasmiyah* itself is also called a *name***; and its use for the *tasmiyah* is more frequent than for the
  named." And: "if it is said, **what is the meaning of Allah's naming Himself**? — it is said: **it is
  teaching the servants how they should open the recitation**."
* **The derivation of *ism*.** "They differed over its derivation. **Al-Mubarrid of the Baṣrans**: it is
  derived from *al-sumuww*, which is height — as though it rose above its meaning and appeared over it, and
  its meaning became beneath it. **Thaʿlab of the Kūfans**: it is from *al-wasm* and *al-simah*, which is
  **the mark** — as though it is a mark for its meaning. **And the first is sounder, because its diminutive
  is *sumayy*; were it from *al-simah* its diminutive would be *wusaym***, as *al-waʿd* gives *wuʿayd*; and
  in its conjugation one says *sammaytu* — were it from *al-wasm* one would say *wasamtu*."
* **The name *Allāh*.** "**Al-Khalīl and a group**: it is a **proper name peculiar to Allah, Mighty and
  Exalted, with no derivation**, like the proper names of servants such as Zayd and ʿAmr. **And a group**:
  it is derived, then differed over its derivation: it is said from *alaha ilāhatan*, meaning *he worshipped
  worship* — and **Ibn ʿAbbās read** *wa-yadharaka wa-ilāhata-ka* (7:127), meaning *your worship* — "the
  sense being that He is worthy of worship to the exclusion of others"; and it is said its root is *ilāh*,
  by His word *wa-mā kāna maʿahu min ilāhin idhan la-dhahaba kullu ilāhin bimā khalaqa* (23:91). **Al-Mubarrid**:
  it is from the Arabs' *alahtu ilā fulān* — *I settled to him*, as the poet: *alahtu ilayhā wa-l-ḥawādithu
  jammah* — **as though creation settles to Him and is at rest in His mention**; and one says *alahtu ilayhi*
  — *I took refuge in him*, as the poet: *alahtu ilayhā wa-l-rakāʾibu wuqqaf*. And it is said the root of
  *ilāh* is *wilāh*, and the *wāw* was changed to a *hamzah* like *wishāḥ* and *ishāḥ* — **its derivation
  from *al-walah*, because the servants yearn (*yawlahūna*) to Him**, that is, they take refuge with Him in
  hardships and betake themselves to Him in needs, as every child yearns to its mother; and it is said it is
  from *al-walah*, **which is the going of the mind at the loss of one who is dear.**"
* **The two names.** "*Al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*: **Ibn ʿAbbās** said: **they are two tender names, one tenderer
  than the other.**"

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Baghawī.** Gathering the positions he reports on the two names (his condensed *ʿaqīdah* of them):

* **One meaning:** "some said: they are one meaning, like *nadmān* and *nadīm*, and their meaning is
  *Possessor of mercy*; and the mention of one after the other is **to give relish to the hearts of the
  desirous**." **Al-Mubarrid:** "it is **beneficence after beneficence and favour after favour**."
* **Two:** "some distinguished between them: ***al-Raḥmān* is in the sense of generality and *al-Raḥīm* in
  the sense of particularity: *al-Raḥmān* in the sense of the Provider in this world, universal to all
  creation; and *al-Raḥīm* in the sense of the Healer in the next world and the pardon in the next world
  for the believers in particular** — and for that it is said in supplication: ***O Raḥmān of this world and
  Raḥīm of the next***. *Al-Raḥmān* is the one whose mercy reaches creation universally, *al-Raḥīm* the one
  whose mercy reaches them particularly; and for that **other than Allah may be called *raḥīm* but other
  than Allah may not be called *raḥmān***: so **al-Raḥmān is general in meaning, specific in word, and
  al-Raḥīm general in word, specific in meaning**."
* **The attribute of mercy:** "*Al-raḥmah* is **Allah's willing of good for its people**; and it is said:
  **the leaving of punishment for one who deserves it, and the doing of good to one who does not deserve
  it** — so on the first it is **an attribute of the Essence**, and on the second **an attribute of act**."
* And on *the name being the named*, above (V8): the doctrine al-Baghawī states flatly as the sūrah's text
  requires.

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Al-Baghawī.** From the sūrah's own sense of *basmalah*: everything is opened **"for seeking blessing"**;
and the report he cites of **ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz** ordering his scribes to **lengthen the *bāʾ*, make
plain the *sīn*, space them, and round the *mīm*, out of magnifying the Book of Allah** — the reverent
script-*ādāb* of the name.

### V13 · History, sīrah & parables

**Al-Baghawī.** The historical traces used here: **ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz**'s instruction to his scribes
(below); and the naming of Makkah "*Umm al-Qurā* … because it is the origin of the lands, the earth being
spread out from beneath it" (cited as the analogue to the sūrah's name *Umm al-Qurʾān*).

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Al-Baghawī.** For the *fāʾil* of the basmalah, the author's own summary belongs to the sūrah's opening: as
the sūrah's three names, its Makkan descent and the seven-verse count are given under S1–S3, so the
*basmalah*'s reward-claims come from the faḍāʾil section (S4) — the "two lights" ḥadīth and the promise
"**you will not recite a letter of them but you are given it**".

_Coverage: V1, V8, V11, V13, V15, V17._

## 1:2 — الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

*[context]* All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Baghawī.** "*Al-ḥamdu li-llāh* — **its wording is a report (*khabar*), as though He reports that the one
deserving of praise is Allah, Mighty and Exalted; and in it is the teaching of creation, its sense being
"say: *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*".** And *al-ḥamd* is **in the sense of thanks for a blessing, and in the sense of
praise of Him for the praiseworthy traits in Him**: one says *ḥamidtu fulānan ʿalā mā asdā ilayya mina
l-niʿmah* (I praised so-and-so for the blessing he conferred on me) **and** *ḥamidtuhu ʿalā ʿilmihi wa
shajāʿatihi* (I praised him for his knowledge and his courage) — **while *al-shukr* is only for a blessing;
so *al-ḥamd* is more general than *al-shukr*, since one does not say *shakartu fulānan ʿalā ʿilmihi*; every
praiser is a thank-er and not every thank-er is a praiser.** And it is said: **al-ḥamd is with the tongue in
speech, and al-shukr is with the limbs in deed** — Allah said *wa-quli l-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī lam yattakhidh
waladā* (17:111), and said *iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukrā* (34:13)."
"*Lillāh* — **the *lām* in it is for entitlement, as one says *al-dāru li-Zayd* (the house is Zayd's).**"
And the exposition of *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* to *al-Raḥīm*: **the *Rabb* is in the sense of the owner**, as one
says to the owner of the house, *rabb al-dār*; and one says *rabb al-shayʾ* when he owns it. **And it is in
the sense of nurture and setting right**, as one says *rabba fulānun al-ḍayʿah yarubbuhā* when he completes
it and sets it right — so he is *rabb*, like *ṭabb* and *barr*." "**So Allah, Mighty and Exalted, is the
owner of the worlds and their nurturer**; and the creature is not called *al-rabb* with the definite
article — one says only *rabb kadhā*, annexed, **because the *alif* and *lām* are for comprehension and he
does not own all.**"

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Baghawī.** "***Wa-l-ʿālamīn* is the plural of *ʿālam*, having no singular from its own form.** They
differed over the *ʿālamīn*: **Ibn ʿAbbās** said: **they are the jinn and mankind, because they are the
ones addressed** — Allah said *li-yakūna li-l-ʿālamīna nadhīrā* (25:1); and **Qatādah, Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan**:
**they are all created things** — Allah said *wa-qāla firʿawnu wa-mā rabbu l-ʿālamīn? qāla rabbu l-samāwāti
wa-l-arḍi wa-mā baynahumā* (26:23–24). **And its derivation is from *al-ʿilm* and *al-ʿalāmah*; they were
named by it because the trace of the making is manifest in them.** **Abū ʿUbaydah**: **they are four
communities — the angels, mankind, the jinn and the devils** — derived from *al-ʿilm*; **and the beasts are
not called an *ʿālam* because they do not understand.**" And their number: "**Saʿīd b. al-Musayyib**: Allah
has **a thousand worlds — six hundred in the sea and four hundred on land**; **Muqātil b. Ḥayyān**: Allah
has **eighty thousand worlds — forty thousand in the sea and forty thousand on land**; **Wahb**: Allah has
**eighteen thousand worlds, this world being one of them — and what are the inhabited places among the
desolate but like a tent in a desert**; **Kaʿb al-Aḥbār**: **no one can count the number of the worlds but
Allah** — Allah said *wa-mā yaʿlamu junūda rabbika illā huwa* (74:31)."

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Al-Baghawī.** The verse-senses of *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* are fixed by: 25:1 (*li-yakūna li-l-ʿālamīna nadhīrā*
— so the *ʿālamīn* are those addressed); 26:23–24 (*rabbu l-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi wa-mā baynahumā* — so they
are all created things); and 74:31 (*wa-mā yaʿlamu junūda rabbika illā huwa* — so their number is unknown).

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Al-Baghawī.** The authorities he names for the two senses of the verse: **Ibn ʿAbbās**, **Qatādah,
Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan**, **Abū ʿUbaydah**, **Saʿīd b. al-Musayyib**, **Muqātil b. Ḥayyān**, **Wahb**, **Kaʿb
al-Aḥbār** — as above.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Baghawī.** *Rabb*'s two senses (owner; nurturer-and-setter-right) with the morphological parallel
*rabb* → *ṭabb*, *barr*; *ʿālamīn*'s having no singular from its own form; the rule that the creature is not
called *al-rabb* with the article "because the *alif* and *lām* are for comprehension and he does not own
all"; **the *lām* of *li-llāh* for entitlement** (*al-dāru li-Zayd*); and **the *ḥamd*/*shukr* distinction —
*al-ḥamd* more general, since praise may be for a trait (*ʿilm*, *shajāʿah*) while *shukr* is only for a
blessing, so "every praiser is a thank-er and not every thank-er is a praiser"**; and, it is said, **praise
with the tongue, thanks with the limbs** (17:111; 34:13). His grammatical point on the opening: **"its
wording is a report (*khabar*) … and in it is the teaching of creation, its sense being 'say: *al-ḥamdu
li-llāh*'"** — the *khabar* carrying an imperative sense.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V6, V7, V8._

## 1:3 — الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* The Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Baghawī.** The verse is treated together with 1:2 in the source (the comment runs *Rabb al-ʿālamīn,
al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm* as one passage of names): "the owner of the worlds and their nurturer"; and for the two
names his exposition of generality/particularity and of mercy as **"Allah's willing of good for its people"
/ "the leaving of punishment for one who deserves it and the doing of good to one who does not"** — the
first making mercy **an attribute of the Essence**, the second **an attribute of act** — which is filed at
1:1, V11 above, since the source itself comments on the two names once, in the basmalah.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Baghawī.** See 1:1, V11: the two names' distinction, the ruling that **other than Allah may be called
*raḥīm* but may not be called *raḥmān***, and the two descriptions of mercy (will of good; and pardon plus
beneficence) with their consequence for the attribute being **of the Essence** or **of act**.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V11._

## 1:4 — مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

*[context]* Master of the Day of Judgement.

### V9 · Variant readings (qirāʾāt)

**Al-Baghawī.** "**ʿĀṣim, al-Kisāʾī and Yaʿqūb read *mālik*; and the others read *malik*.**" And the
*idghām*: "**Abū ʿAmr read *al-Raḥīmi malik*, merging the *mīm* into the *mīm*; and thus he merges every two
letters of one kind, or one articulation point, or two close points, whether the letter is vowelless or
vowelled** — except where the first letter is doubled, or nunated, or defective, or vowelled *fatḥah*, or
the *tāʾ* of address with a vowelless letter before it of other than the two like ones — these he does not
merge; and the merging of the vowelled is in **the great *idghām***. **Ḥamzah agreed with him in merging the
vowelled** in *bayyata ṭāʾifatun* (4:81), *wa-l-ṣāffāti ṣaffā fa-l-zājirāti zajrā fa-l-tāliyāti dhikrā*
(37:1–3), *wa-l-dhāriyāti dharwā* (51:1) — he merged the *tāʾ* into what follows it of letters; **and
al-Kisāʾī and Ḥamzah agreed with him in the small *idghām***, which is the merging of the vowelless into the
vowelled — except in the *rāʾ* with the *lām*, and the *dāl* with the *jīm*; and likewise Ḥamzah — in the
report of Khallād and Khalaf — does not merge the *dāl* with the *sīn*, the *ṣād* and the *zāy*; and there is
no merging for the rest of the reciters except in counted letters."

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Baghawī.** "Some said: **the two readings have one meaning**, like *farihīn* and *fārihīn*, *ḥadhirīn*
and *ḥādhirīn* — and their meaning is **the Lord**: one says *rabb al-dār* and *mālikuhā*. And it is said:
**the *mālik* and the *malik* is the One able to bring the entities forth from non-existence into
existence, and none other than Allah is able to do that**."
"**Abū ʿUbaydah**: *mālik* is more comprehensive and wider, because one says *mālik al-ʿabd wa-l-ṭayr
wa-l-dawābb* and one does not say *malik* of these things; and because he is not an owner of a thing unless
he owns it, while one may be *malik* of a thing and not own it. **And a group said: *malik* is worthier,
because every owner of a kingdom is a *malik* and not every owner is a *malik*, and because it agrees with
the rest of the Qurʾān** — His word *fa-taʿālā llāhu l-maliku l-ḥaqq* (20:114), *al-maliku l-quddūs* (59:23)."
And on *yawm al-dīn*: "**Mujāhid**: *al-dīn* is the reckoning — Allah said *dhālika l-dīnu l-qayyim*
(9:36), that is, the upright reckoning; and *maliki l-nās* (114:2). **Ibn ʿAbbās, Muqātil and al-Suddī**:
*maliki yawmi l-dīn* — **the judge of the Day of Reckoning**; **Qatādah**: *al-dīn* is the requital, and it
falls on requital in good and evil together; one says: *as you requite, so are you requited*
(*kamā tadīnu tudānu*). **Muḥammad b. Kaʿb al-Quraẓī**: **King of a day in which nothing benefits but
religion**; **Yamān b. Rabāb**: *al-dīn* is **overpowering** — one says *dintuhu fa-dāna*, that is, I
overpowered him and he submitted; and it is said: *al-dīn* is **obedience**, that is, the day of obedience."
And the *munāsabah* of the specification: "**He specified the Day of Judgement for mention though He is
owner of all the days, because on that day the ownerships vanish and there is no sovereignty and no command
but His** — Allah said *al-mulku yawmaʾidhin al-ḥaqqu li-l-Raḥmān* (25:26), *li-mani l-mulku l-yawma?
li-llāhi l-wāḥidi l-qahhār* (40:16), *wa-l-amru yawmaʾidhin li-llāh* (82:19)."

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Baghawī.** The grammatical-and-phonetic material: *mālik*/*malik* as *fārihīn*/*farihīn* variants;
*mālik* vs *malik* scope; *al-dīn*'s senses (reckoning, requital, overpowering, obedience); and Abū ʿAmr's
*idghām* rule-set (above, V9).

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Baghawī.** The exclusivity stated in the definition: "*al-mālik* and *al-malik* is **the One able to
bring the entities forth from non-existence into existence, and none other than Allah is able to do that**"
(V7); and, from the *munāsabah*, that on the Day of Judgement **"the ownerships vanish and there is no
sovereignty and no command but His"** (V7).

_Coverage: V1, V7, V8, V9, V11._

## 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

*[context]* You alone we worship, and You alone we seek help.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Baghawī.** "*Iyyāka naʿbudu*: **«iyyā» is a pronoun word, made specific to annexation to the pronoun,
and it is used placed before the verb** — one says *iyyāka aʿnī*, *iyyāka asʾalu* — **and it is not used
placed after the verb except detached**: one says *mā ʿanaytu illā iyyāka*." "*Naʿbudu* — that is, **we make
You one and obey You humbly**; *al-ʿibādah* is **obedience with abasement and humility**; and the slave
(*al-ʿabd*) is so named **for his abasement and his submission** — one says *ṭarīq muʿabbad*, that is,
trodden down."

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Baghawī.** "*Wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*: **we ask Your help for Your worship and for all our affairs**." And
the order of the two clauses: "if it is said, **why was the mention of worship put before the seeking of
help, when seeking help comes before worship?** — **this is incumbent on one who puts *istiṭāʿah* before the
act; and we, by Allah's praise, put *tawfīq* and *istiʿānah* with the act, so there is no difference between
putting first and last.** And it is said: **seeking help is a kind of servitude**, so it is as though He
mentioned the whole of worship first and then mentioned what is of its details."

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Baghawī.** The doctrinal hinge is explicit in his answer above: "**we put *tawfīq* and *istiʿānah* with
the act**" — against those who put capacity (*istiṭāʿah*) before the act — and the definition of
*al-ʿibādah* as obedience joined with abasement, with *al-ʿabd* named for his submission.

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Al-Baghawī.** The two clauses together as the servant's disposition: **making one (*tawḥīd*) and obeying
humbly**, then asking help for it and for all affairs — the sequence the author harmonises.

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Al-Baghawī.** The ordering-aporia above and its resolution ("seeking help is a kind of servitude… the
whole of worship first, then what is of its details") is his contribution of wisdom on the verse's
arrangement.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V8, V11, V14, V15._

## 1:6 — اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ

*[context]* Guide us to the straight path.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Baghawī.** "*Ihdinā* — **guide us** (*arshidnā*); and **ʿAlī and Ubayy b. Kaʿb said: keep us firm
(*thabbitnā*)** — as one says to a man standing, *stand until I return to you*, that is, remain on what you
are on. **And this supplication from the believers, though they are already on guidance, means firmness and
means asking for more guidance, because the kindnesses and guidances of Allah do not come to an end — on the
doctrine of *Ahl al-Sunnah*.**"

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Baghawī.** "***Al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*: Ibn ʿAbbās and Jābir said: it is Islam** — and it is the saying of
**Muqātil**; **Ibn Masʿūd said: it is the Qurʾān**; **and it is related from ʿAlī *marfūʿan*: *the straight
path is the Book of Allah***; **Saʿīd b. Jubayr: the road of the Garden**; **Sahl b. ʿAbd Allāh: the way of
the Sunnah and the community**; **Bakr b. ʿAbd Allāh al-Muzanī: the way of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ**; **and
Abū al-ʿĀliyah and al-Ḥasan: the Messenger and his family and his two companions**; **and its root in the
language is the clear road (*al-ṭarīq al-wāḍiḥ*).**"

### V9 · Variant readings (qirāʾāt)

**Al-Baghawī.** "*Al-ṣirāṭ* and *sirāṭ* with the *sīn* — related by Uways from Yaʿqūb — **and it is the
root, named *sirāṭ* because it swallows (*yasraṭu*) the traveller**; and it is read with the *zāʾ*; and
**Ḥamzah read with *ishmām* of the *zāʾ***; **all of them are sound dialects; and the choice is the *ṣād*
with most of the reciters, for agreement with the *muṣḥaf*.**"

_Coverage: V1, V2, V7, V9._

## 1:7 — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

*[context]* The path of those whom You have blessed — not of those who incurred wrath, nor of those who are astray.

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Baghawī.** "*Ṣirāṭa lladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim* — that is, **whom You blessed with guidance and
success**. **ʿIkrimah**: whom You blessed with **firmness in faith and uprightness — and they are the
prophets**; and it is said: **they are everyone whom Allah kept firm in faith, of the prophets and the
believers whom Allah mentioned in His word** *fa-ulāʾika maʿa lladhīna anʿama llāhu ʿalayhim mina
l-nabiyyīna* (4:69) **to the end of the verse**. **Ibn ʿAbbās: they are the people of Moses and Jesus before
they changed their religion.** **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Zayd: they are the Prophet ﷺ and those with him.** **Abū
al-ʿĀliyah: they are the family of the Messenger ﷺ, Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, and his household.** **Shahr b.
Ḥawshab: they are the companions of the Messenger of Allah and his household.**"

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Baghawī.** "*Ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim* — meaning **the path of those with whom You are angry**
(*ghaḍibta ʿalayhim*); **and *al-ghaḍab* is the will of vengeance on the disobedient** — **and Allah's
wrath, Mighty and Exalted, does not reach the disobedient among the believers; it reaches only the
unbelievers.** *Wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* — that is, **and not of those astray from guidance**; **and the root of
*al-ḍalāl* is perishing and disappearing** — one says *ḍalla l-māʾu fī l-laban*, when it perished and
disappeared."

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Baghawī.** "**And *ghayr* here is in the sense of *lā*, and *lā* is in the sense of *ghayr*, and
therefore the coordination is permissible** — as one says *fulānun ghayru muḥsinin wa-lā mujmil* (so-and-so
is not a doer of good nor a fair dealer); **but if *ghayr* were in the sense of *siwā*, the coordination
with *lā* would not be permissible**: it is not permissible in speech, *ʿindī siwā ʿAbdillāh wa-lā Zayd*
(I have other than ʿAbdullāh and not Zayd)."

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Al-Baghawī.** The identifications he transmits: **ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb's** reading *ṣirāṭa man anʿamta
ʿalayhim ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-ghayri l-ḍāllīn*; and "*al-maghḍūb ʿalayhim* are **the Jews** and the
astray **the Christians**, **because Allah judged the Jews with wrath — *man laʿanahu llāhu wa-ghaḍiba
ʿalayhi* (5:60) — and judged the Christians with straying — *wa-lā tattabiʿū ahwāʾa qawmin qad ḍallū min
qablu* (5:77)**"; and **Sahl b. ʿAbd Allāh: *ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim* by innovation, and not the astray
from the Sunnah.**

### V3 · Letters, words & numerical features (ḥurūf · ʿadad)

**Al-Baghawī.** (from the *idghām* rules of 1:4 and the discussions of the *hāʾ*/*mīm* here noted) — the
recitation-variants of *ʿalayhim*: "**Ḥamzah read *ʿalayhim*, *ladayhim* and *ilayhim* with *ḍammah* of
their *hāʾ*s**; **Yaʿqūb gives *ḍammah* to every *hāʾ* preceded by a vowelless *yāʾ*, dual and plural,
except *bayna aydīhinna wa-arjulihinna* (60:12)**; **and the others read with *kasrah* of both: whoever gives
the *hāʾ* *ḍammah* returns it to the root, since it is *ḍammah* when isolated; and whoever gives it *kasrah*,
it is because of the vowelless *yāʾ* — and *kasrah* is the sister of the *yāʾ*. And Ibn Kathīr and Abū
Jaʿfar gave full measure (*ishbāʿ*) to every *mīm* of plural in continuous recitation when no vowelless
letter meets it; if a vowelless letter meets it, they do not; Nāfiʿ leaves the choice; and Warsh gives
*ḍammah* at the *alif* of severance; and when it is met by an *alif* of conjunction, and before the *hāʾ*
there is *kasrah* or a vowelless *yāʾ*, **Ḥamzah and al-Kisāʾī give *ḍammah* to the *hāʾ* and the *mīm*,
and Abū ʿAmr gives *kasrah* to them, and Yaʿqūb likewise when what precedes is vowelled *kasrah*; and the
others read with *ḍammah* of the *mīm* and *kasrah* of the *hāʾ* in all of it, for the *yāʾ* or the *kasrah*
before it, and the *mīm*'s *ḍammah* on the root.**"

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ) — the *Āmīn*

**Al-Baghawī.** "**It is *sunnah* for the reciter to say when he finishes the recitation of the Fātiḥah
*Āmīn*, with a pause separating it from the Fātiḥah; it is lightened, and the grammarians allow it
lengthened and shortened; and its meaning is *O Allah, hear and answer* (*allāhumma smaʿ wa-stajib*).
**Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatādah**: its meaning is **so let it be** (*kadhālika yakūn*). **Mujāhid**: **it is one of
Allah's names**. And it is said: **it is the stamp of supplication**; and it is said: **it is Allah's seal
upon His servants, by which He repels the afflictions from them, like the seal of the book that protects it
from corruption and the revealing of what is in it.**"
And the ḥadīth with its chain: "Abū ʿAlī al-Ḥusayn b. Muḥammad al-Qāḍī and Abū Ḥāmid Aḥmad b. ʿAbd Allāh
al-Ṣāliḥī → Abū Bakr Aḥmad b. Ḥasan al-Ḥīrī → Abū ʿAlī Muḥammad b. Aḥmad al-Maydānī → Muḥammad b. Yaḥyā →
ʿAbd al-Razzāq → Maʿmar → al-Zuhrī → **Ibn al-Musayyab** → **Abū Hurayrah**: the Prophet ﷺ said: **When the
imam says *ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*, say *Āmīn* — for the angels say *Āmīn* and the imam
says *Āmīn*, and whoever's *Āmīn* agrees with the *Āmīn* of the angels, his previous sins are forgiven.**
_(ṣaḥīḥ.)_"

_Coverage: V1, V2, V3, V6, V7, V8, V17._
