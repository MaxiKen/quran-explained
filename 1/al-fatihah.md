# Sūrah 1 — al-Fātiḥah (The Opening)

*7 verses · Meccan per the majority (Mujāhid said Medinan).*

**Sources cited inline:** **[Ṭabarī]** *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* · **[Qurṭubī]** *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān* ·
**[Ibn Kathīr]** *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* · **[Jalālayn]** *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* · **[Saʿdī]** *Taysīr
al-Karīm al-Raḥmān* · **[Study Quran]** *The Study Quran* (2015).

**The sūrah.** al-Fātiḥah — "the Opening" — is recited in every cycle of the prayer and opens the Book.
Its titles: *Umm al-Kitāb* / *Umm al-Qurʾān* ("Mother of the Book/Qurʾān", because it gathers the
Qurʾān's meanings), *al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī* ("the seven oft-repeated", 15:87), *al-Shifāʾ* (the Cure),
*al-Ruqyah* (remedy), *Sūrat al-Ḥamd*, *Sūrat al-Ṣalāh* [Ibn Kathīr], [Study Quran]. It has 7 verses,
25 words, 113 letters [Ibn Kathīr]. In a *ḥadīth qudsī* God says: "I have divided the prayer between
Myself and My servant into two halves; My servant shall have what he asks" — the first half praise,
the second half petition [Ibn Kathīr], [Study Quran]. Its structure: vv. 1–3 God, v. 4 the meeting with
God, vv. 5–7 the servant's response [Study Quran].

## 1:1 — بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*In the Name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful.*

**Meaning.** **[Ṭabarī]** The *bāʾ* requires an omitted verb: "I begin / I recite in the name of God."
God taught His Prophet to place His name before all his acts, and made that a practice for all creation —
so that "in the name of God" suffices, the hearer knowing the intent without the verb being spoken. Ibn
ʿAbbās, from Jibrīl: "Say, *bismi llāh* — recite with the remembrance of your Lord; stand and sit with the
remembrance of God." **[Saʿdī]** *Ism* is a singular in construct, so "in the name of God" invokes all the
beautiful names; *Allāh* is the One worshipped, entitled to worship alone for the attributes of divinity;
*al-Raḥmān*, *al-Raḥīm* are two names of His vast mercy that embraces every living thing — absolute for
those who follow His messengers, a share for the rest. **[Jalālayn]** *Allāh* names the divine essence
simply, without consideration of the attributes; *al-Raḥmān* causes existence and perfection to flow on
all things as divine wisdom requires; *al-Raḥīm* bestows the final spiritual perfection reserved for the
human species in the Hereafter. **[Study Quran]** The three names mark a descent: *Allāh* the Essence,
*al-Raḥmān* the unity of Attributes, *al-Raḥīm* the unity of acts.

**Context.** Every sūrah begins with the basmala except Sūrah 9; it also occurs within 27:30 (Solomon's
letter). Pious Muslims use it to consecrate every licit act. **[Study Quran]**, **[Ibn Kathīr]**

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** "Any important matter not begun with the Name of God is cut off" [Study Quran citing
Ṭabarī]. Al-Nasāʾī from Abū l-Malīḥ: "When your mount stumbles do not say 'Satan stumbled' — he swells
until he is like a house — but say *bismi llāh al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*, and he shrinks to the size of a fly"
[Qurṭubī]. ʿAlī to a scribe: "Make it beautiful, for a man who made it beautiful was forgiven"
[Qurṭubī]. Bishr al-Ḥāfī's conversion: he found a scrap of paper bearing God's name trodden in the road,
bought perfume with his last coins and scented it, and was told in a dream, "You have perfumed My name,
so I shall perfume yours" [Qurṭubī].

**Rulings.** Is the basmala a verse? **Mālik:** a verse of neither al-Fātiḥah nor any other sūrah.
**ʿAbdullāh b. al-Mubārak:** a verse of every sūrah. **al-Shāfiʿī:** a verse of al-Fātiḥah, hesitating
over the rest. No disagreement that it is a verse within 27:30 [Qurṭubī], [Study Quran]. In prayer, most
schools (with the Shāfiʿī and Jaʿfarī excepted) do not recite it audibly with the sūrah; Anas ibn Mālik
reported that the Prophet, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān began recitation with "Praise be to God, Lord of
the worlds" without mentioning the basmala, while Abū Hurayrah recited it aloud and said, "Among you I
perform the prayer closest to the Messenger of God's" [Study Quran], [Ibn Kathīr]. The basmala has 19
letters, matching the 19 guardians of the Fire — a protective correspondence Ibn Masʿūd drew [Qurṭubī].

**Belief.** **[Saʿdī]** The names and attributes are affirmed as the *salaf* affirmed them: He is
*Raḥmān*, *Raḥīm*, possessing the mercy by which He is described, related to the one shown mercy — all
blessings being traces of that mercy; affirmed without denial (*taʿṭīl*) or likening (*tashbīh*).

**Language.** *Allāh*: a unique name, or derived from *al-Ilāh*, "the Divine"; already known to the
pre-Islamic Arabs (43:87) [Ṭabarī], [Study Quran]. *Al-Raḥmān* is more emphatic and encompassing than
*al-Raḥīm*, which may be applied figuratively to creatures (9:128) [Study Quran], [Jalālayn].

**Cross-references.** 27:30; 15:87; 17:110 ("Call upon God, or call upon the Compassionate"); 43:87;
9:128.

**Stories & occasions.** Bishr al-Ḥāfī (above); ʿAlī and the scribe; ʿAlī's saying that the whole Qurʾān
is in the Fātiḥah, the whole Fātiḥah in the basmala, and the whole basmala in the *bāʾ* [Study Quran].

**Reflection.** The basmala consecrates the act at its beginning, as "Praise be to God" marks its
completion [Study Quran]. Every legitimate act, from recitation to rising and sitting, is placed under
God's name.

## 1:2 — الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

*Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds.*

**Meaning.** **[Ṭabarī]** *Al-ḥamd li-llāh*: thanks sincere and unmixed, to God alone, not to anything
worshipped besides Him nor to any creature — for the countless favours by which He fitted the limbs for
worship, spread provision, and called creation to the means of lasting abode in the Hereafter. So all
praise belongs to Him, first and last. **[Ibn Kathīr]** Praise of God's perfect attributes and of His
acts, which move between grace and justice; the definite article means that all praise belongs to Him.
**[Saʿdī]** *Al-ḥamd* is praise of God for the attributes of perfection and for acts that turn between
grace and justice. *Rabb*: the One who nurtures all the worlds — creating them, preparing their
instruments, and granting them favours without which they could not subsist; His nurturing is twofold:
general (creation, provision, guidance to what preserves them) and special (His nurturing of His friends
by faith, enabling them, perfecting them, driving off obstacles) — which is why the prophets' petitions
mostly address Him as *Rabb*. **[Study Quran]** *Rabb*: master obeyed, one who puts matters in order,
possessor; related to *tarbiya*, cultivation. *Al-ʿālamīn*: all existents other than God (so [Ibn Kathīr],
[Study Quran]) — all that can be seen or imagined and all that cannot [al-Rāzī, per Study Quran].

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** The *ḥadīth qudsī*: "I have divided the prayer between Myself and My servant… when
the servant says 'Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds', God says: My servant has praised Me"
[Ibn Kathīr], [Study Quran]. "The best remembrance is *lā ilāha illā llāh* and the best supplication is
*al-ḥamdu li-llāh*" (Tirmidhī, Nasāʾī, Ibn Mājah; *ḥasan gharīb*) [Ibn Kathīr]. "No servant is blessed
and says *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* but that what he was given is better than what he took" (Ibn Mājah)
[Qurṭubī], [Ibn Kathīr]. The servant whose praise the two angels could not write: "*Laka l-ḥamdu kamā
yanbaghī li-jalāli wajhika wa-ʿaẓīmi sulṭānik*" — God said: "Write it as My servant said it, until he
meets Me and I reward him for it" (Ibn Mājah) [Qurṭubī], [Ibn Kathīr]. "Verily your Lord loves praise"
(Aḥmad; al-Aswad b. Sariʿ) [Ibn Kathīr]. ʿUmar asked ʿAlī what *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* was; he said: "A word
God liked for Himself, was pleased with for Himself, and likes to be repeated" [Ibn Kathīr]. Al-Ḥasan:
"There is no blessing but that *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* is better than it" [Qurṭubī].

**Belief.** **[Saʿdī]** *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* establishes *tawḥīd al-rubūbiyya*: He alone creates, orders,
provides; He is utterly free of need and the worlds utterly in need of Him.

**Language.** *Ḥamd* is more general than *shukr*: ḥamd is praise for what one is and does, while
thanks are given only for what was done [Ibn Kathīr], [Study Quran]. The definite article marks
exclusivity: all praise, all gratitude, belong to God [Ṭabarī], [Study Quran]. **[Jalālayn]** *Rabb al-ʿālamīn*
refers to every created kind — the "worlds" being the levels of cosmic existence and the communities
within each.

**Cross-references.** 15:49–50 ("I am the Forgiving, the Merciful; and My chastisement is the painful
chastisement") and 6:165 — the pairing of warning with mercy [Ibn Kathīr]; 17:44 ("there is no thing
save that it hymns His praise").

**Reflection.** Praise answers the completed favour, as the basmala consecrates the act about to begin;
the believer's day is bracketed by both. The praise of God by God at the opening of His Book teaches the
servant to praise Him in God's own words, since he cannot praise Him as He deserves [Study Quran].

## 1:3 — الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*The Compassionate, the Merciful.*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** The One who possesses mercy — willing good for those who deserve it:
*al-Raḥmān* flowing existence and perfection upon all things; *al-Raḥīm* bestowing the final perfection
reserved for the human species in the Hereafter. **[Saʿdī]** Two names showing that He possesses vast
mercy that embraces everything and every living being, written for the God-fearing who follow His
prophets — they having absolute mercy, others a portion of it. **[Qurṭubī]** (*via* Ibn Kathīr) After
"Lord of the worlds", which carries awe, He joined the names of mercy, which carry hope, so that fear
and desire meet in His attributes and the servant is the more ready to obey.

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** "Were the believer to know what chastisement God has, none would hope for His
Garden; and were the disbeliever to know what mercy God has, none would despair of it" (Muslim, Abū
Hurayrah) [Qurṭubī], [Ibn Kathīr].

**Belief.** Mercy is an attribute of the Essence, affirmed as the *salaf* affirmed the names and
attributes, with neither denial nor likening [Saʿdī].

**Language.** *Al-Raḥmān* is stronger and more encompassing than *al-Raḥīm*; it cannot be applied to any
but God, while *raḥīm* may describe a creature (cf. 9:128, the Prophet) [Study Quran], [Ibn Kathīr].
Between the Sovereignty "over space" of v. 2 and the "Mastery of time" of v. 4, this verse shows God's
mercy encompassing all time and space (cf. 7:156) [Study Quran].

**Cross-references.** 15:49–50; 7:156 ("My mercy encompasses all things"); 33:43 ("He is Merciful to the
believers"); 17:110.

**Reflection.** The pairing teaches balance: no hope without awe, no fear without hope [Qurṭubī],
[Ibn Kathīr].

## 1:4 — مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

*Master of the Day of Judgment.*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** The day of requital, the Day of Resurrection — God's mastery is singled out
for it because on that Day no mastery appears save His (cf. 40:16). **[Ibn Kathīr]** He is Master of all
days; the Day of Recompense is named because on it ownership is cut off from all others: no one speaks
without His leave (78:38; 20:108; 11:105). Ibn ʿAbbās: "On that Day no one owns what they used to own."
**[Saʿdī]** *Mālik* is the One described by kingship — who commands and forbids, rewards and punishes,
disposes of His subjects in every way; on that Day kings and subjects, slaves and free are equal before
His greatness, awaiting His recompense.

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** "The most despicable name before God is a man who calls himself king of kings, and
there is no king but God" (Bukhārī, Muslim) [Ibn Kathīr]. "God will grasp the earth and fold the heaven
in His right hand, then say: I am the King — where are the kings of the earth? Where are the tyrants?"
(Bukhārī, Muslim) [Ibn Kathīr], [Study Quran]. Asked who the most intelligent of believers is, the
Prophet said: "The most frequent in recalling death and best prepared for what follows it"
[Study Quran]. ʿUmar: "Bring yourself to account before you are brought to account; weigh your deeds
before your deeds are weighed" [Study Quran].

**Belief.** God's sole sovereignty on the Day of Reckoning; the certainty of the meeting with God;
resurrection and requital.

**Language.** *Dīn* relates to obedience, custom, and to *dayn*, "debt" — the Day of *dīn* is the day
one's debt to God falls due; God is *al-Dayyān*, the One Who Repays (cf. 24:25) [Study Quran],
[Ibn Kathīr], [Jalālayn]. *Mālik* ("master/owner") and *malik* ("king") — both traced to the Prophet;
*malik* is said to be more emphatic, while *mālik* stresses disposal [Study Quran], [Qurṭubī].

**Cross-references.** 40:16; 24:25; 40:17; 78:38; 20:108; 11:105; 37:53 ("Shall we be recompensed?").

**Readings.** *Mālik* / *malik* — both transmitted from the Prophet; also *malk* (lightened) and *malīk*
[Qurṭubī], [Study Quran].

**Reflection.** The verse plants accountability: the Names descend from Essence (Allāh) to universal
mercy (*al-Raḥmān*), particular mercy (*al-Raḥīm*), and then the King who manages all affairs — the
second half of the sūrah then begins the servant's ascent [Study Quran].

## 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

*You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help.*

**Meaning.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** "You alone we worship and none other, and in You alone we seek help":
the first half is a declaration of innocence from *shirk*, the second a negation of any power or strength
of one's own — "the entire religion is implied by these two ideas"; some of the *salaf* said the Fātiḥah
is the secret of the Qurʾān and this word the secret of the Fātiḥah. **[Qurṭubī]** The object is placed
before the verb for emphasis and exclusivity (*ḥaṣr*): we worship You, not other than You; we seek help
from You, not other than You; the order follows "the most important first" and puts the Right of the
Worshipped before the servant's. *ʿIbāda* = obedience and humble submission; a road *muʿabbada* is one
made easy for travellers. Al-Sulamī's *Ḥaqāʾiq* from Abū Ḥafṣ al-Farghānī: "Whoever affirms *iyyāka
naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn* has cleared himself of both compulsion and self-sufficiency." **[Saʿdī]** The
fronting yields restriction; the order of worship before seeking help is the general (worship) before
the particular (help), and prioritises God's right over the servant's. Worship is a comprehensive term
for all God loves and is pleased with, inward and outward; seeking help is reliance on God for benefit
and the warding off of harm, with trust in Him. Worship is only worship when taken from the Messenger
and aimed at God's face; help is mentioned after worship because the servant needs God's help in every
act of worship — without His aid he cannot do what is commanded or shun what is forbidden. **[Study
Quran]** The shift from third person (vv. 1–4) to direct address marks the servant standing before God;
the first-person plural is humility: one speaks for all creation, not oneself alone.

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** The *ḥadīth qudsī*: "This is between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have
what he asks" [Ibn Kathīr], [Study Quran]. "There is no valid prayer for one who does not recite the
Fātiḥah of the Book" (Bukhārī, Muslim; ʿUbāda b. al-Ṣāmit) [Ibn Kathīr]. Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq: "Worship is of
three kinds — that of slaves (fear), that of hirelings (reward), and that of the free (love); the
worship of the free is the most excellent" [Study Quran].

**Belief.** The verse is a standing refutation of those who hold that the servant's own will suffices
for his acts: if the matter were in his hands he would not have been taught to ask for help and
guidance in every prayer [Qurṭubī]; **[Ṭabarī]** In the command to say "You alone we worship, You alone
we ask for help" there is the clearest proof of the falsity of the *tafwīḍ* of the Qadarīs, who claim the
servant has been given the help he needs so that his need of his Lord is lifted — for were that so, the
verse's meaning would be void.

**Language.** *ʿIbādah*: subjugation; a paved road (*muʿabbada*) is one trodden down and made smooth
[Ibn Kathīr], [Qurṭubī]. *Iyyāka* with the object fronted gives restriction (*ḥaṣr*) [Saʿdī], [Qurṭubī].
The Name is repeated in both halves lest "You we worship *and rely on another*" be imagined [Qurṭubī].

**Cross-references.** 11:123 ("worship Him and put your trust in Him"); 67:29; 73:9; 47:38 ("God is the
Rich, and you are the poor"); 6:165.

**Readings.** ʿAmr b. Qāʾid read *iyāka* (lightened *yāʾ*, *kasra* on the *hamza*) — a reading
disapproved, since it would turn the sense into "your sun we worship" (*iyā* = sunlight) [Qurṭubī].

**Reflection.** Worship and reliance are the two pillars of the servant's relation to his Lord; asking
for help is itself an act of worship, and the servant never outgrows the need for it.

## 1:6 — اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ

*Guide us upon the straight path.*

**Meaning.** **[Ṭabarī]** *Ihdinā*: grant us success, make us firm on the path — for the Prophet and
the believers, already guided, ask for steadfastness and increase, not first guidance. He rejects the
reading "give us more detail", since God does not impose a duty before making it clear, and adds: in
asking God's help there is proof against those who claim the servant's need of his Lord has ended.
**[Ibn Kathīr]** Guidance here means being directed, led, and granted success; the believer asks
constantly because he needs God every hour to remain firm — hence the command "O you who believe,
believe" (4:136): a call to continuity. **[Qurṭubī]** *Ihdinā* is the servant's petition: "show us the
path", "direct us to it", "make us incline with our hearts to the truth" (*hudā* from *al-mayl*,
inclining — cf. 7:156, and the gift *hadīya*, and the sacrificial *hadī* led to the sanctuary). Imam
Mālik's teacher Rabīʿa: the prayer's greatest petition is placed in this sūrah: half of it praise, half
of it needs — and this petition is better than any other because its words are God's own. **[Saʿdī]**
Guidance *to* the path (embracing Islam, leaving other religions) and guidance *within* the path (all
the details of the religion, in knowledge and action) — hence it is among the most comprehensive and
beneficial of supplications, and was made obligatory in every cycle of prayer, since one never ceases to
need it.

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** Ḥadīth qudsī: "This is for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks"
[Ibn Kathīr], [Study Quran]. The Prophet's parable (Aḥmad; al-Nawwās b. Samʿān): "God struck a parable:
a straight path with two walls, in which are open doors covered with curtains; a caller at the head of
the path says, 'O people, enter the path, all of you, and do not swerve'; and another caller cries out
from above the path when a man would open one of those doors: 'Woe to you, do not open it, for if you
open it you will enter it.' The path is Islam, the two walls are God's limits, the open doors are what
God has forbidden, the caller at the head of the path is the Book of God, and the caller above the path
is God's admonisher in the heart of every Muslim" [Ibn Kathīr], [Study Quran]. Abū l-ʿĀliya: "The
straight path is the Messenger of God and his two companions after him"; al-Ḥasan said, "He spoke truly
and gave good counsel" (via ʿĀṣim al-Aḥwal) [Qurṭubī].

**Language.** *Ṣirāṭ*: the clear road (cf. Jarīr: "The commander of the faithful is on a path that
stays straight when other roads bend"); *mustaqīm* = without crookedness or deviation; originally
*mustaqwim*, the vowel transferred and the *wāw* turned to *yāʾ* [Qurṭubī], [Ibn Kathīr], [Study Quran].
*Hudā*'s root is inclining [Qurṭubī].

**Cross-references.** 4:69 (the four company of the blessed); 37:23 ("lead them to the path of the
Fire"); 11:56 ("my Lord is upon a straight path"); 42:52; 90:10; 7:43; 10:9.

**Readings.** *Al-ṣirāṭ* / *al-sirāṭ* (reading with *sīn*) / *al-zirāṭ* (pure *zāʾ* — a dialect of
ʿUdhra, Kalb and Banū l-Qayn) [Qurṭubī].

**Reflection.** The servant asks in every prayer for the one thing he cannot do without; the path
described is the middle way — neither worldliness nor excess, the outward Law joined to inward
spirituality [Study Quran].

## 1:7 — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

*The path of those whom You have blessed — not of those who incur wrath, nor of those who are astray.*

**Meaning.** **[Ṭabarī]** The verse explains which path is the straight one — for every road of truth is
straight; "the path of those You have blessed" = the path of Your angels, prophets, the truthful, the
martyrs and the righteous, whom God blessed with obedience and worship (cf. 4:66–69). **[Qurṭubī]** "The
path of those You have blessed" is an apposition to the straight path, meaning: keep us firm in
guidance — for a man may be guided to a road and then cut off from it. **The majority:** the
"*maghḍūb ʿalayhim*" are the Jews; "the *ḍāllīn*" are the Christians — a report traced to the Prophet in
the narration of ʿAdī b. Ḥātim [Ibn Kathīr], [Jalālayn], [Saʿdī]. **[Ibn Kathīr]** The distinction: the
Jews knew the truth and abandoned it (hence wrath), the Christians sought the truth without finding it
through its proper sources (hence going astray); each community has both traits, but wrath belongs most
characteristically to the Jews (5:60) and misguidance to the Christians (5:77). The believers' path is
knowledge of the truth and acting upon it. **[Study Quran]** The blessed are elaborated at 4:69,
company of "the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous".

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** ʿAdī b. Ḥātim (Aḥmad; Tirmidhī — *ḥasan gharīb*): "Those who have incurred anger
are the Jews, and those who are astray are the Christians" [Ibn Kathīr]. Ḥadīth qudsī ending: "This is
for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks" [Ibn Kathīr], [Study Quran]. ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb
and Ibn al-Zubayr read "the path of *man* You have blessed" [Qurṭubī].

**Belief.** **[Qurṭubī]** In this verse is a refutation of the Qadarīs, Muʿtazilīs and Imāmīs who hold
that the servant's will alone suffices for his acts and that he creates his own deeds — for the servants
ask guidance and refuge from misguidance, and repeat it in every prayer, and plead against harm: all of
which contradicts self-sufficiency. **[Saʿdī]** The sūrah, brief as it is, contains what no other sūrah
does: the three kinds of *tawḥīd* (Lordship from "Lord of the worlds"; divinity from "You alone we
worship"; names and attributes from "praise"); prophethood ("guide us" is impossible without revelation);
recompense ("Master of the Day of Judgment"); the decree and the servant's real agency against both
Qadarīs and Jabrīs; and a refutation of every misguidance — for every innovator and strayer departs
from the straight path of knowledge and action; and sincerity (worship and seeking help for God alone).
Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds.

**Language.** "Those whom You have blessed" leaves the blessing unstated, the visible part standing for
the hidden (as the Arab poets' usage) [Ṭabarī]. *ʿAlayhim* has ten pronunciations, six transmitted from
the reciters and four from the Arab tribes [Qurṭubī]. *Ghayr* is read in the genitive as a description
of "those whom You have blessed" — i.e. not the path of the objects of wrath [Jalālayn].

**Cross-references.** 4:69; 5:60 (curse and wrath upon some of the People of the Book); 5:77 (Christians
who went astray); 42:52; 7:43; 90:10; 15:87.

**Readings.** ʿUmar and Ibn al-Zubayr: *ṣirāṭ man anʿamta ʿalayhim* [Qurṭubī].

**Reflection.** The sūrah begins with praise and ends with a petition for the company of the blessed
and deliverance from both forms of misguidance — knowledge without action, and effort without
knowledge. To it the worshipper answers *āmīn*: the sūrah is a prayer, and its "answer" is the rest of
the Book.
