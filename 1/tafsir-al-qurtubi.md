# Al-Qurṭubī — chapter 1, al-Fātiḥah

**Source work:** *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* — Abū ʿAbd Allāh Muḥammad b. Aḥmad al-Qurṭubī (d. 671/1273); in the corpus file the work is divided as the author arranges it — twenty-eight *masāʾil* on the basmalah, then "the explanation of Sūrat al-Fātiḥah" in four *abwāb* (virtues and names; revelation and rulings; the *Āmīn*; the meanings, readings and *iʿrāb*).
**Corpus file:** `tafsir-al-qurtubi/001.txt` (Arabic; footnotes stripped for reading).
**Extraction:** English; Qurʾānic clause, ḥadīth wording, transmitted report and verse of poetry verbatim in quotation; the author's long juristic and grammatical arguments condensed to their claims, with his own *tarjīḥ* ("the sound saying is…") kept as his.
**Elements found:** S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6 · V1, V2, V4, V5, V6, V7, V8, V9, V10, V11, V13, V14, V15, V16, V17.

> **Structural note (applies to this file only).** (1) Al-Qurṭubī's *first* two chapters — the twenty-eight
> *masāʾil* on the basmalah — are a treatise on the whole sūrah's constitution (its being a verse or not, its
> place in prayer, its derivation), so the sūrah-level elements S1–S6 begin only with "the explanation of
> Sūrat al-Fātiḥah"; the basmalah questions are filed under 1:1 as their matter requires. (2) His *bāb* on
> *Āmīn* is filed at V17 under 1:7, since it follows the end of the sūrah. (3) His *bāb* three (the *Āmīn*
> chapter) also carries the sūrah's division-of-the-prayer material used under S5.

## Sūrah-level record (S1–S6)

### S1 · Names & titles of the sūrah

**Al-Qurṭubī** counts **twelve names**, in "the fourth *masʾalah* on its names":

1. **al-Ṣalāh** — from "Allah's words: *I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant into two
   halves*"; the sūrah is called *prayer* because "prayer is not valid except by it".
2. **Sūrat al-Ḥamd** — "because it contains the mention of praise, as one says *Sūrat al-Aʿrāf*, *al-Anfāl*,
   *al-Tawbah* and the like."
3. **Fātiḥat al-Kitāb** — "with no disagreement among the scholars; it is so called because the recitation of
   the Qurʾān is begun with it in speech, the writing in the *muṣḥaf* is begun with it in script, and the
   prayers are begun with it."
4. **Umm al-Kitāb** — "in this name there is disagreement: the majority permitted it; **Anas, al-Ḥasan and
   Ibn Sīrīn disliked it**. Al-Ḥasan said: *Umm al-Kitāb is the lawful and the unlawful*, by 3:7 (*āyātun
   muḥkamātun hunna ummu l-kitāb*); Anas and Ibn Sīrīn said: *Umm al-Kitāb is the name of the Preserved
   Tablet*, by 43:4."
5. **Umm al-Qurʾān** — "disagreed over likewise: the majority permitted it, Anas and Ibn Sīrīn disliked it,
   and the established *aḥādīth* refute both these sayings." (Tirmidhī from Abū Hurayrah — "a *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*
   ḥadīth": "al-Ḥamdu li-llāh is *Umm al-Qurʾān* and *Umm al-Kitāb* and *al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī*.") Al-Bukhārī's
   comment: "it was named *Umm al-Kitāb* because its writing begins the *maṣāḥif* and its recitation begins
   the prayer." And Yaḥyā b. Yaʿmur: "*Umm al-Qurā* is Makkah, *Umm Khurāsān* is Marw, and *Umm al-Qurʾān* is
   Sūrat al-Ḥamd" — because "it is the first of it and contains all its sciences; and by that Makkah is
   called *Umm al-Qurā* because it is the first of the earth and from it it was spread out".
6. **al-Mathānī** — "so called because it is repeated (*tuthannā*) in every *rakʿah*; and it is said because
   it was **reserved** (*ustuthniyat*) for this community, so it did not come down on anyone before them as a
   treasure laid up for them."
7. **al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm** — "so called because it contains all the sciences of the Qurʾān: it comprises
   praise of Allah by the attributes of His perfection and majesty, the command of worship and sincerity in
   it, the confession of inability to fulfil any of it except by His help, the supplication to Him for
   guidance to the straight path, sufficing in the circumstances of the deniers, and the declaration of the
   end of the rejecters."
8. **al-Shifāʾ** — Dārimī from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī: "Fātiḥat al-Kitāb is a cure for every poison."
9. **al-Ruqyah** — "established from the ḥadīth of Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, in which the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said
   to the man who had recited over the chief of the tribe: *What made you know that it is a ruqyah?*"
10. **al-Asās** — "A man complained to al-Shaʿbī of a pain in his side and he said: *You must take the
    foundation of the Qurʾān, Fātiḥat al-Kitāb*", and he heard Ibn ʿAbbās say: "Everything has a foundation:
    the foundation of the world is Makkah, the foundation of the heavens is *ʿArībā* — the seventh heaven,
    the foundation of the earth is *ʿAjībā* — the lowest seventh earth, the foundation of the gardens is
    Jannat ʿAdn, the foundation of the Fire is Jahannam, the foundation of creation is Ādam, the foundation
    of the prophets is Nūḥ, the foundation of the Children of Israel is Yaʿqūb, the foundation of the Books
    is the Qurʾān, **the foundation of the Qurʾān is al-Fātiḥah, and the foundation of al-Fātiḥah is *bismi
    llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm***; so when you fall ill or complain, take al-Fātiḥah and you will be cured."
11. **al-Wāfiyah** ("the Complete") — "the saying of Sufyān b. ʿUyaynah, because it is not halved and does
    not admit of being shortened; if one recited half of any other sūrah in one *rakʿah* and half in another
    it would do, but were al-Fātiḥah split over two *rakʿahs* it would not do."
12. **al-Kāfiyah** ("the Sufficient") — Yaḥyā b. Abī Kathīr: "because it suffices for other than it, and
    nothing else suffices in its place"; supported by Muḥammad b. Khallād al-Iskandarānī from the Prophet ﷺ:
    "*Umm al-Qurʾān is a substitute for other than it, and nothing else is a substitute for it.*"

And separately (the third *masʾalah* of the first *bāb*), **ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib** from the Prophet ﷺ: "Fātiḥat
al-Kitāb, *Āyat al-Kursī*, *shahida llāhu annahu lā ilāha illā huwa*, and *qul allāhumma mālika l-mulk* —
**these verses are suspended by the Throne, with no veil between them and Allah**."

### S2 · Makkī or Madanī

**Al-Qurṭubī** (the second *masʾalah* of the second *bāb*). "The scholars differed whether it is Makkan or
Madinan. **Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah and Abū al-ʿĀliyah al-Riyāḥī** and others said: **it is Makkan**. **Abū
Hurayrah, Mujāhid, ʿAṭāʾ b. Yasār, al-Zuhrī** and others said: **it is Madinan**. And it is said: **half of
it came down at Makkah and half at Madinah** (related by Abū al-Layth Naṣr b. Muḥammad al-Samarqandī). **The
first is sounder**, by His word *wa-laqad ātaynāka sabʿan mina l-mathānī wa-l-Qurʾāna l-ʿaẓīm* (15:87) — and
al-Ḥijr is Makkan by consensus — and because the obligation of prayer was at Makkah, and "it is not preserved
that there was ever in Islam a prayer without *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*". And on the reports about
the first thing revealed: Ṭayyibī al-Qāḍī mentioned the disagreement (*al-Muddaththir*, *Iqraʾ*,
al-Fātiḥah); al-Bayhaqī in *Dalāʾil al-Nubuwwah* gives Abū Maysarah ʿAmr b. Shuraḥbīl's report that the
Prophet ﷺ told Khadījah of the voice he heard, and that Warqah b. Nawfal said: "When he comes to you, be
firm until you hear what he says, then come to me and tell me" — and when he was alone he was called: "*O
Muḥammad, say* bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm, al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn *until he reached* wa-lā
l-ḍāllīn*; say:* lā ilāha illā llāh." _(Al-Bayhaqī said: this report is interrupted.)_ Al-Qurṭubī's own
resolution, on the Muslim ḥadīth of Ibn ʿAbbās that a "gate of heaven was opened" and an angel came down
saying "Rejoice in two lights given to you, not given to a prophet before you: Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and the
closing verses of Sūrat al-Baqarah": "**Jibrīl came down with its recitation at Makkah and the angel came
down with its reward at Madinah** … and it has been said it is Makkan-Madinan, coming down twice — and what
we have said is preferable, for it joins the Qurʾān and the Sunnah."

### S3 · Verse, word and letter counts — and the basmalah question

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "The community is agreed that Fātiḥat al-Kitāb is **seven verses**, except what is related
from Ḥusayn al-Juʿfī that it is six — and that is *shādhdh* — and except what is related from **ʿAmr b.
ʿUbayd**, who made *iyyāka naʿbudu* a verse, making it **eight** — and that is *shādh*; and His word
*wa-laqad ātaynāka sabʿan mina l-mathānī* and the ḥadīth *I have divided the prayer* refute both sayings."
He then sets out, in the fifth *masʾalah*, on the basmalah's status:

* **Mālik** — "not a verse of al-Fātiḥah nor of any other" (*sūrah*); **[Ibn al-Mubārak]** — "a verse of
  every sūrah"; **al-Shāfiʿī** — "a verse in al-Fātiḥah, and his saying wavered over the rest of the sūrahs";
  and "there is no disagreement among them that it is a verse of the Qurʾān in Sūrat al-Naml."
* Al-Qurṭubī's verdict: "**the sound of these sayings is Mālik's**, because the Qurʾān is not established by
  isolated reports; its way is only the decisive, unbroken transmission in which there is no disagreement.
  Ibn al-ʿArabī said: *it suffices you that it is not of the Qurʾān that people disagree over it, and the
  Qurʾān is not disagreed over.*"
* The *āyāt*-count of the rest he reports: "**the people of Madinah, Syria and Baṣrah and most of the
  reciters counted *anʿamta ʿalayhim* a verse**… Qatādah from Abū Naḍrah from Abū Hurayrah: *the sixth verse
  is* anʿamta ʿalayhim. **The people of Kūfah among reciters and jurists counted the basmalah and did not
  count *anʿamta ʿalayhim***."
* On ʿAbd Allāh b. Masʿūd's *muṣḥaf* (he did not write the Fātiḥah): the report Abū Bakr al-Anbārī gives from
  al-Aʿmash (from Ibrāhīm) — ʿAbd Allāh was asked "why did you not write Fātiḥat al-Kitāb in your *muṣḥaf*?"
  and said: "**If I wrote it, I would write it with every sūrah**" — al-Anbārī's explanation: "he meant that
  every *rakʿah*'s way is to be opened with Umm al-Qurʾān before the sūrah recited after it; so he said: *I
  dispensed with writing it, I trusted the Muslims' preservation of it, and I would not fix it in one place
  and then have to write it with every sūrah*."
* **Twenty-five words** (Ibn al-ʿArabī, in the first *bāb*): "it is **twenty-five words** that contain all
  the sciences of the Qurʾān."

### S4 · Faḍāʾil of the sūrah

**Al-Qurṭubī** (first *bāb*, seven *masāʾil*). 1) Tirmidhī from **Ubayy b. Kaʿb** — the Prophet ﷺ: "*Allah
did not send down in the Torah nor in the Gospel the like of Umm al-Qurʾān — and it is the Seven Mathānī, and
it is divided between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks.*" And the Bukhārī report of
**Abū Saʿīd b. al-Muʿallā** (whose name, al-Qurṭubī notes, is reported variously; he died 74 AH "and was the
first to pray toward the Kaʿbah when it was turned"): "I was praying in the mosque and the Messenger of Allah
ﷺ called me and I did not answer him… I said: *Messenger of Allah, I was praying*; he said: *Did Allah not
say* is'tajībū li-llāhi wa-li-l-rasūli idhā daʿākum*?* Then he said: *I will teach you a sūrah which is the
**greatest sūrah in the Qurʾān** before you leave the mosque*… he took my hand, and when he was about to
leave I said: *Did you not say you would teach me a sūrah which is the greatest sūrah in the Qurʾān?* He
said: **al-Ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn — it is the Seven Mathānī and the Mighty Qurʾān which I was
given.**" And **Mujāhid's** report that "**Iblīs wailed four times** (*ranna*): when he was cursed, when he
was driven out of the Garden, when Muḥammad ﷺ was sent, and **when the Fātiḥah of the Book was sent down, and
it was sent down at Madinah**."

2) On preferment of parts of the Qurʾān over parts: "Those who said there is no preferment — Abū al-Ḥasan
al-Ashʿarī, al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr b. al-Ṭayyib, Abū Ḥātim Muḥammad b. Ḥibbān al-Bustī and a group of jurists — and
it is related from Mālik (Yaḥyā b. Yaḥyā: *the preferment of part of the Qurʾān over part is an error*; and
Mālik disliked that a sūrah be repeated and others not). Their argument: preferment suggests the deficiency
of the less preferred, the essence in all is one, the speech of Allah, in which there is no defect." **Al-Bustī:
"the Ḥadīth means that Allah does not give to the reciter of the Torah and the Gospel the like of the reward
He gives the reciter of Umm al-Qurʾān — a favour from Him to this community."** And those who affirm
preferment — **Isḥāq b. Rāhwayh** and others, and the choice of **al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr b. al-ʿArabī and Ibn
al-Ḥaṣṣār** — "because of the ḥadīth of Abū Saʿīd b. al-Muʿallā and the ḥadīth of Ubayy b. Kaʿb: the Prophet
ﷺ asked him *which verse of the Book of Allah with you is the greatest?* and he said: *Allāhu lā ilāha illā
huwa l-ḥayyu l-qayyūm*, and he struck his chest and said: **Congratulations on the knowledge, O Abū
al-Mundhir** (Bukhārī and Muslim)" — "the preferment is only by the meanings, how wondrous and how many, not
in the attribute [of speaking]; and **this is the truth**." Ibn al-ʿArabī: "the ḥadīth's silence about the
other books, like the scrolls and the Psalms, is because those named are the most excellent of them, and when
a thing is the best of the best it becomes the best of all" — and: "**in al-Fātiḥah there are attributes
that are not in others**, to the point that it is said **all the Qurʾān is in it**; it is twenty-five words
containing all the sciences of the Qurʾān; and of its honour is that Allah divided it between Himself and His
servant, that no act of nearness is valid without it, and that no other act attains its reward." And: "the
meaning of the ḥadīth of Abū Saʿīd (*the greatest sūrah*) is that it is greatest in **reward**, not that part
of the Qurʾān is better than part."

3) ʿAlī's report (Abū ʿAmr al-Dānī's *Kitāb al-Bayān*): the four "suspended by the Throne" verses (above,
S1). 8) Dārimī: **al-Shifāʾ**. 9) **al-Ruqyah** (the ḥadīth of the man who recited over the chief of the
tribe: "*What made you know that it is a ruqyah?*" — "the Imams narrated it; the man said: *Messenger of
Allah, something was cast into my heart*"). 12) **al-Kāfiyah**. And the fifth *masʾalah* of the first *bāb*:
"**al-Muhallab said: the place of the ruqyah in it is only *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*.** And it was
said: **the whole sūrah is a ruqyah**, because he ﷺ said *What made you know that it is a ruqyah* and did not
say *that there is a ruqyah in it*."

### S5 · Maqāṣid — the structure and division of the sūrah

**Al-Qurṭubī.** From the ḥadīth of the division (Bukhārī/Muslim; and al-Qurṭubī's reading of it in the fifth
*masʾalah* of the basmalah chapter):

> "*Allah, Mighty and Exalted, said: I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant into halves, and My
> servant shall have what he asks. When the servant says* al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn, *Allah says: My
> servant has praised Me. When he says* al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm, *He says: My servant has extolled Me. When he
> says* māliki yawmi l-dīn, *He says: My servant has magnified Me — and once: this is Mine, and to My servant
> [what remains]. When he says* iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn, *He says: this is between Me and My
> servant, and My servant shall have what he asks. When he says* ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm, ṣirāṭa lladhīna
> anʿamta ʿalayhim ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn, *He says: this is for My servant, and My
> servant shall have what he asks.*"

His exposition: "His word *I have divided the prayer* means **al-Fātiḥah**; He called it *prayer* because
prayer is not valid except by it. **He made the first three verses for Himself and chose them for Himself**,
and the Muslims did not differ over them; then **the fourth verse He made between Him and His servant**,
because it comprises the servant's humbling himself and seeking help from Him, and that comprises the
magnifying of Allah; then **three verses completing seven**." And the deduction from the wording "these are
for My servant" (not "these two"), from Mālik's *Muwaṭṭaʾ* — "this proves that *anʿamta ʿalayhim* is a
verse"; Ibn Bukayr said: Mālik said "*anʿamta ʿalayhim* is a verse, then the seventh verse to its end". And
the further statement he gives (in 1:6, the twenty-seventh *masʾalah*): "one of the scholars said: **Allah
placed the magnitude of supplication, and its sum, in this sūrah: half of it is the gathering of praise and
half of it is the gathering of needs**; and He made this supplication better than the supplication a
supplicant makes himself, **because this speech was spoken by the Lord of the worlds — so you supplicate with
a supplication that is His speech.**" And (in 1:2, the seventh *masʾalah*): "the Fātiḥah comprises **tawḥīd,
worship, admonition and reminder**"; and al-Qurṭubī's own view of the two halves: "**al-ḥamd is more general
than al-shukr** … praise of the praised for His attributes without prior beneficence, while *shukr* is praise
for what beneficence has brought; and on this limit our scholars said: *al-ḥamd* is more general because it
includes praise, extolling and thanks, while requital is specific to one who has done you a kindness."

### S6 · Munāsabah — the sequence of the verses

**Al-Qurṭubī.** On 1:3 (the thirteenth *masʾalah*): "He described Himself after *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* as *al-Raḥmān
al-Raḥīm*, because when His being described as *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* contains **intimidation**, He joined it with
*al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*, which contains **encouragement**, so as to join in His attributes fear of Him and hope
in Him, and so be a greater aid to His obedience and a stronger restraint — as He said: *nabbiʾ ʿibādī annī
anā l-ghafūru l-raḥīm, wa-anna ʿadhābī huwa l-ʿadhābu l-alīm* (15:49–50), and *ghāfiri l-dhanbi wa-qābili
l-tawbi shadīdi l-ʿiqāb* (40:3)" — with the Muslim ḥadīth of Abū Hurayrah: "*Were the believer to know what
is with Allah of punishment, no one would hope for His Garden; and were the unbeliever to know what is with
Allah of mercy, no one would despair of His Garden.*" And on 1:5 (the twenty-third *masʾalah*): "He **returned
from the absent to the address** (*iltifāt*)" — "for from the beginning of the sūrah to here it is report
about Allah and praise of Him"; with the parallel of 76:21–22 (*wa-saqāhum rabbuhum sharāban ṭahūrā… inna
hādhā kāna lakum jazāʾan*) and its reverse in 10:22 (*ḥattā idhā kuntum fī l-fulki wa-jarayna bihim*).

## 1:1 — بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* In the name of Allah, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

### V1 · Verse text, translation & context

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "The First — the scholars said: *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is **an oath from our Lord**
which He sent down at the head of every sūrah: He swears to His servants that this which I have set out for
you, My servants, in this sūrah is true, and that I will fulfil for you all that I have guaranteed in this
sūrah of My promise, My kindness and My goodness"; and it is "of what Allah sent down in our Book and upon
this community in particular **after Sulaymān**" — and "some scholars said: *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*
comprises the whole of the Sharīʿah, because it points to the Essence and to the Attributes; and this is
correct."

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The *basmalah*'s sense is gathered from the reports and the sense of the *bāʾ*: "**the
eleventh** — the disagreement over the meaning of the addition of *ism*: Quṭrub said it was added to
magnify His mention; al-Akhfash said it was added that the mention might pass from the ruling of an oath to
the purpose of seeking blessing, for the basis of the speech is *bi-llāh*." "**The twelfth** — whether the
*bāʾ* came on the sense of a command (*begin with the name of Allah*) or the sense of a report (*I have begun
with the name of Allah*): the first for the reciters, the second for al-Zajjāj; and on both, *bismi llāh* is
in the accusative position. And it is said: the sense is *my beginning is with the name of Allah*, so *bismi
llāh* is in the nominative as the predicate of the subject." "The ninth — our scholars said: **in it there is
a refutation of the *Qadariyyah* and whoever says that their acts are decreed *for* them** … the argument
against them is that **Allah has commanded us at the beginning of every act to open with it** … so the sense
of *bismi llāh* is *bi-llāh*; and the sense of *bi-llāh* is: **by His creating and His decreeing is reach
what is reached**."; and "some said: *bismi llāh* means *I have begun with Allah's help, His granting of
success and His blessing* — and this is a teaching from Allah to His servants, that they may mention His name
at the opening of recitation and other things, so that the opening be with the blessing of Allah."

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The second** — Saʿīd b. Abī Sukaynah said: it reached me that **ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib** saw a
man writing *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* and said to him: ***Make it beautiful, for a man made it
beautiful and was forgiven.*** Saʿīd said: it reached me that a man looked at a parchment on which was
*bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*, kissed it and placed it on his eyes, **and was forgiven** — and of this
meaning is the story of Bishr al-Ḥāfī, who when he lifted the note in which was the name of Allah and perfumed
it, his name was perfumed (mentioned by al-Qushayrī)." And the report of al-Nasāʾī from Abū al-Malīḥ from the
man who rode behind the Prophet ﷺ — the Prophet ﷺ said: "*When an animal stumbles under you, do not say 'may
Satan perish' — for he grows until he becomes like a house and says, by his strength I did it — but say*
bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm, *for he shrinks until he is like a fly*." And **ʿAlī b. al-Ḥusayn** on His
word *wa-idhā dhakarta rabbaka fī l-Qurʾāni waḥdahu wallaw ʿalā adbārihim nufūrā* (17:46): "**its meaning is:
when you say *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm***." And "Wakīʿ from al-Aʿmash from Abū Wāʾil from **ʿAbd Allāh
ibn Masʿūd** said: *whoever wishes Allah to save him from the nineteen *zabāniyah*, let him recite* bismi
llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm, *so that Allah makes for him, with every letter of it, a shield from each one*" —
hence "the *basmalah* is **nineteen letters**, by the number of the angels of the people of the Fire of whom
Allah said *ʿalayhā tisʿata ʿashar*, and they say in all their acts *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* — from
there is their strength". **Ibn ʿAṭiyyah's caution: "this is among the curiosities of tafsīr, not of the
firm science"** — and his parallels: 27 for *Laylat al-Qadr*, and the "thirty-odd letters" of *rabbanā
wa-laka l-ḥamdu ḥamdan kathīran ṭayyiban mubārakan fīhi* over which the Prophet ﷺ "saw thirty-odd angels
racing to write it".

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The philological *masāʾil*:

* **The verb-noun *basmala*.** "al-Māwardī said: one who says *bismi llāh* is called *mubasmil*; and it is a
  coined word (*lughah muwalladah*) — it has come in poetry, as ʿUmar b. Abī Rabīʿah: *laqad basmalat Laylā
  ghudhayyata laqītuhā / fa-yā ḥabba dhāka l-ḥabību l-mubasmili*." "I say: what is well known with the people
  of language is *basmala*: Yaʿqūb b. al-Sikkīt, al-Muṭarriz, al-Thaʿālibī and others say: *basmala l-rajul*
  = he said *bismi llāh*, and one says *you have done much *basmalah*** — "and like it *ḥawqala* for *lā ḥawla
  wa-lā quwwata illā bi-llāh*, *hallala* for *lā ilāha illā llāh*, *sabḥala* for *subḥāna llāh*, *ḥamdala*
  for *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*, *ḥayṣala* for *ḥayya ʿalā l-ṣalāh*, *jaʿfala* for *juʿiltu fidāka*, *ṭabqala* for
  *aṭāla llāhu baqāʾak*, *damʿaza* for *adāma llāhu ʿizzak*."
* **The *alif* of *bismi*.** "**The thirteenth** — *bismi llāh* is written without an *alif*, being dispensed
  of by the *bāʾ* of conjunction in pronunciation and writing, for the frequency of use — unlike *iqraʾ
  bi-smi rabbika* (96:1), where it is dropped for the rarity of use. They differed over dropping it with
  *al-Raḥmān* and *al-Qāhir*: al-Kisāʾī and Saʿīd al-Akhfash said the *alif* is dropped; Yaḥyā b. Waththāb
  said it is not dropped except with *bismi llāh* alone, because with it use was frequent."
* **The *bāʾ*'s *kasrah*.** "**The fourteenth** — the *bāʾ* was singled out for *kasrah* for three meanings: it
  was said, that its form match its function; and, that since the *bāʾ* only enters on nouns, it was singled
  out with the *khafḍ* that is only in nouns; and third, to distinguish it from what may be a noun among the
  particles, like the *kāf* in the poet's *wa-ruḥnā bi-kābni l-māʾi yujnabu wasṭanā* — that is, *with the like
  of the son of the water*."
* **The derivation of *ism*.** "**The fifteenth** — *ism*, its weight is *ifʿ*, and what is missing from it is
  the *wāw*, for it is from *samawtu*; its plural is *asmāʾ* and its diminutive *sumayy*." Its four dialects:
  *ism*, *usm*, *sim*, *sum* — with the verse *wa-llāhu asmāka suman mubārakan*. "**The sixteenth** — the
  *nisbah* to *ism* is *sumawiyy* (and one may say *ismiyy*), plural *asmāʾ*, and the plural of *asmāʾ* is
  *asāmī*." "**The seventeenth** — disagreement over *ism*'s derivation: **the Baṣrans: from *al-sumuww*, which
  is height and elevation** … and **the Kūfans: from *al-simah*, which is the mark, because the name is a mark
  for the one it is set for** — and the first is sounder, because the diminutive is *sumayy* and the plural
  *asmāʾ*, and diminutive and plural return things to their roots." And his doctrinal use of that: "whoever
  says the name is from height says: **Allah has not ceased to be described before the existence of creation
  and after it and at their passing, and they have no effect on His names or His attributes — and this is the
  saying of *Ahl al-Sunnah***; and whoever says it is from *simah* says: *Allah was in eternity with no name
  and no attribute, and when He created creation they made names and attributes for Him* — **and this is the
  saying of the Muʿtazilah, and it is contrary to what the community has agreed on, and greater in error than
  their saying that His speech is created**." "**The eighteenth** — on this disagreement falls the question
  of the name and the named: **the people of truth — Abū Bakr b. al-Ṭayyib al-Qāḍī, approved by Ibn Fūrak, and
  it is the saying of Abū ʿUbaydah and Sībawayh — held that the name is the named**", while Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār:
  "whoever denies the attributes among the innovators claims that there is no meaning for the namings except
  the Essence, and therefore says the name is other than the named; and whoever affirms the attributes affirms
  for the namings meanings which are the descriptions of the Essence, other than the expressions."
* **The name *Allāh*.** "**The twentieth** — *Allāh* is the greatest of His names and the most comprehensive
  of them, to the point that some scholars said it is **the greatest name of Allah**, and no other is named by
  it (and therefore it is not dualized nor pluralized) — and this is one of the two readings of His word *hal
  taʿlamu lahu samiyyā* (19:65), that is, who is named by His name *Allāh*." And "**the twenty-first** —
  disagreement over whether it is derived or coined for the Essence as a proper name: Sībawayh from al-Khalīl
  that its root is *ilāh*, like *fiʿāl*, and the *alif* and *lām* were put in place of the *hamzah*, as
  al-Sībawayh said *al-nās* is originaly *unās*; and it is said the root is *lāh*, on which the *alif* and
  *lām* entered for magnification (Sībawayh's choice, with the verse *lāhi bnu ʿammika lā afḍalta fī ḥasabin*);
  and al-Kisāʾī and al-Farrāʾ: the sense is *bismi l-Ilāh*, so they took away the *hamzah* and merged the
  first *lām* into the second, as in *lākinna huwa llāhu rabbī* — al-Ḥasan read it so". And: "it is said it is
  derived from *waliha*, when he is bewildered — *al-walah* is the going of the mind: *so Allah, the minds are
  bewildered and go astray in the realities of His attributes and in thought about knowing Him*"; and from
  al-Ḍaḥḥāk: "it was named *ilāh* because **creation betakes itself to Him in their needs and humbles
  themselves to Him in their hardships**" (al-Khalīl: *yaʾlahūna ilayhi* with the *lām* in *naṣb*, and with
  *kasrah* — two dialects); and from *al-Rabb*/height ("the Arabs said of everything raised, *lāhā*; when the
  sun rose they said *lāhat*"); and from *alaha l-rajul* when he became a devotee — "and from that His word
  *wa-yadharaka wa-ilāhata-ka* (7:127), for Ibn ʿAbbās and others said: *and your worship* — so the meaning of
  *Allāh* is **the One aimed at in worship**; and from it the monotheists' word *lā ilāha illā llāh*, meaning
  *there is no worshipped but Allah*, and the *illā* in it is in the sense of *ghayr*, not of exception."
  And "some claimed the root is the *hāʾ* which is the pronoun of the absent … then the *lām* of ownership was
  added … then the *alif* and *lām* for magnification and veneration."

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The basis of the above: "**the nineteenth** — as a consequence of this disagreement falls the
speech about the name and the named"; and "**the eighteenth** … this is the saying of *Ahl al-Sunnah* … and
the saying of the Muʿtazilah is contrary to what the community agreed on". And on the two mercy-names (which
are treated with the basmalah here since the corpus file treats the basmalah by itself): "**the twenty-second**
— disagreement over the derivation of the name *al-Raḥmān*: some said it has no derivation because it is one
of the names peculiar to Him … and because if it is derived from mercy it would not be joined to the mention
of the one shown mercy … and also, **had it been derived from mercy the Arabs would not have denied it when
they heard it**, since they did not deny their Lord's mercy — and Allah said: *wa-idhā qīla lahumu sjudū
li-l-Raḥmāni qālū wa-mā l-Raḥmān* (25:60). And when **ʿAlī** wrote at Ḥudaybiyah by the Prophet's command
*bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*, **Suhayl b. ʿAmr** said: *As for bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm, we do not
know what bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm is; but write what we know: bi-smika llāhumma.*" — "**Ibn al-ʿArabī
said: they were ignorant of the attribute, not of the One described**, proving it by their saying *and what
is al-Raḥmān?* and not *and who is al-Raḥmān?*; **Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār said: it is as though he had not read the
other verse, *wa-hum yakfurūna bi-l-Raḥmān*** (13:30)." And: "**the majority hold that al-Raḥmān is derived
from mercy, built on intensification, meaning the Possessor of mercy who has no like in it; and therefore it
is neither dualized nor pluralized, as al-Raḥīm is dualized and pluralized**"; Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār: "of what shows
the derivation is what Tirmidhī narrated, and graded sound, from **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf** — he heard the
Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: **Allah, Mighty and Exalted, said: I am al-Raḥmān; I created the womb and split
for it a name from My name: whoever joins it I join him, and whoever cuts it off I cut him off** — and this
is a text on the derivation; **so there is no meaning to disagreement**; and the Arabs' denial of it was
their ignorance of Allah and of what is due to Him." "**The twenty-third** — **al-Mubarrid claimed that
al-Raḥmān is a Hebrew (*ʿibrānī*) name**, and therefore *al-Raḥīm* came with it (with the verse of the poem:
*aw tatrukūna ilā l-Qasṣayni hijratakum / wa-masḥakum ṣulbahum raḥmāna qurbānā*) — and Abū Isḥāq al-Zajjāj
said: **this saying is to be turned away from**; and Abū al-ʿAbbās: the attribute may occur for praise; and
Quṭrub: it may be that He joined the two for **emphasis**, and in emphasis is the greatest benefit: 'it is
favour after favour, and beneficence after beneficence, strengthening the ambitions of the desirous, and a
promise whose hoper is not disappointed'" (Muḥammad b. Yazīd). And "**the twenty-fifth** — most scholars hold
that *al-Raḥmān* is **peculiar to Allah, not permitted for any other to be named by it**, by *quli dʿū llāha
awi dʿū l-Raḥmān* (17:110), matching it with the name no other shares… and *wa-sʾal man arsalnā min qablika
min rusulinā a-jaʿalnā min dūni l-Raḥmāni ālihatan yuʿbadūn* (43:45)" — "and **Musaylimah the liar**, may
Allah curse him, dared to be called **Raḥmān al-Yamāmah** … **and Allah made that description (*the liar*) a
mark by which he is known**." And: "**it has been said of His name al-Raḥmān that it is the greatest name of
Allah** — mentioned by Ibn al-ʿArabī." And "**the twenty-fourth** — are they one meaning or two? Abū ʿUbaydah:
one, like *nadmān* and *nadīm*; and it was said: **the pattern *faʿlān* is not like *faʿīl*, for *faʿlān*
falls only on the intensification of the act** — *a man ghaḍbān*, filled with anger — while *faʿīl* may be in
the sense of the doer and the done-to (ʿAmallas: *fa-ammā idhā ʿaḍḍat bika l-ḥarbu ʿaḍḍatan / fa-innaka
maʿṭūfun ʿalayka raḥīm*): **so *al-Raḥmān* is specific in name, general in act, and *al-Raḥīm* is general in
name, specific in act — this is the saying of the majority.**" Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī: "*al-Raḥmān* is a name
general in all the kinds of mercy, peculiar to Allah; *al-Raḥīm* is only in the direction of the believers,
as He said *wa-kāna bi-l-muʾminīna raḥīmā*." **Al-ʿArzamī**: "*al-Raḥmān* to all His creation in the
provinces and the gifts of the senses and the general blessings; *al-Raḥīm* to the believers in guiding them
and being gentle with them." **Ibn al-Mubārak**: "*al-Raḥmān*, if asked He gives; *al-Raḥīm*, if not asked He
is angry" — and Ibn Mājah and Tirmidhī from Abū Ṣāliḥ from Abū Hurayrah: "*Whoever does not ask Allah, He is
angry with him*" (and the poet: *Allāhu yaghḍabu in tarakta suʾalahū / wa-bunayyu Ādama ḥīna yusʾalu
yaghḍab*). And **Ibn ʿAbbās**: "they are two names, **tender** (*raqīqān*), one tenderer than the other,
i.e. more merciful" — al-Khaṭṭābī: "this is problematic, for tenderness has no entry into any of Allah's
attributes"; al-Ḥusayn b. al-Faḍl al-Bajalī: "this is a confusion of the transmitter: they are two names
**gentle** (*rafīqān*), one gentler than the other, and gentleness is of Allah's attributes" — the Prophet ﷺ:
"*Allah is Gentle and loves gentleness, and gives for gentleness what He does not give for harshness.*" And
"**the twenty-sixth** — *al-Raḥīm* is a description for the created; and because of the generality in
*al-Raḥmān* it was placed before *al-Raḥīm* in our speech, in agreement with the revelation (al-Mahdawī)"
— and: "it was said: the meaning is **by the Raḥīm you reached Allah — so *al-Raḥīm* is the description of
Muḥammad ﷺ**, His description *la-raʾūfun raḥīm* (9:128); as though the meaning were: *by the name of Allah,
al-Raḥmān, and by al-Raḥīm — that is, by Muḥammad ﷺ — you have reached Me*, through following him and what
he brought you reach My reward, My honour and the sight of My face."

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The twenty-seventh** — related from ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib, may Allah honour his face, that he
said of His word *bismi llāh*: **it is a cure from every disease and a help to every remedy**; and as for
*al-Raḥmān*, **it is a help for everyone who believes in Him, and it is a name by which no other is named**;
and as for *al-Raḥīm*, **it is for the one who repents, believes and does righteous deeds**." And the
letter-by-letter report: "some explained it by the letters — related from **ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān** that he asked
the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about the explanation of *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* and he said: *As for the
bāʾ, it is the affliction of Allah and His spirit and His radiance and His splendour; as for the sīn, the
eminence of Allah; as for the mīm, the dominion of Allah; as for Allāh, there is no god but He; as for
al-Raḥmān, **the One who inclines to the righteous and the sinful of His creation**; as for al-Raḥīm, **the
Gentle with the believers in particular***." And **Kaʿb al-Aḥbār**: "*the bāʾ is His splendour (bahāʾuhu),
the sīn His eminence than which nothing is higher, and the mīm His dominion, and He is over all things
powerful so that nothing rivals Him*." And: "it has been said that every letter is the opening of one of His
names: the *bāʾ* the key of His name *Baṣīr*, the *sīn* of *Samīʿ*, the *mīm* of *Malīk*, the *alif* of
*Allāh*, the *lām* of *Laṭīf*, the *hāʾ* of *Hādī*, the *rāʾ* of *Razzāq*, the *ḥāʾ* of *Ḥalīm*, the *nūn*
of *Nūr* — **and the meaning of all this is calling upon Allah, Mighty and Exalted, at the opening of
everything**."

And the etiquette *masāʾilah* of the basmalah — "**the eighth** — the Sharīʿah's urging to mention the
basmalah at the beginning of every act, such as eating, drinking, slaughtering, marital relations, purity and
riding the sea, and other acts than that": *fa-kulū mimmā dhukira smu llāhi ʿalayh* (6:118) and *wa-qāla
irkabū fīhā bismi llāhi majrāhā wa-mursāhā* (11:41); and the Prophet's ﷺ: "*Lock your door and mention the
name of Allah, put out your lamp and mention the name of Allah, cover your vessel and mention the name of
Allah, tie your waterskin and mention the name of Allah*"; and "*If one of you, when he intends to come to his
wife, says:* bismi llāhi, allāhumma jannibnā l-shayṭān wa-jannibi l-shayṭāna mā razaqtanā, *then if a child
is decreed between them, no Satan will ever harm him*"; and to ʿUmar b. Abī Salamah: "*O boy, name Allah, eat
with your right hand, and eat from what is next to you*"; and "*Satan makes lawful the food over which the
name of Allah is not mentioned*"; and "*Whoever does not slaughter, let him slaughter with the name of
Allah*"; and to ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ, who complained of a pain in his body since he became Muslim: "*Place
your hand on the part of your body that hurts and say* bismi llāh *three times, and say seven times:* aʿūdhu
bi-ʿizzati llāhi wa-qudratihi min sharri mā ajidu wa-uḥādhir*; then he said: "all of this is established in
the *Ṣaḥīḥ*, and al-Qurṭubī says: *I hold the well-known view of the people of language is* basmala." And
the rulings (commands/rulings on touching)? He adds: "**the sixth** — the community agreed on the
permissibility of writing *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* at the head of every book of knowledge and letters;
if the book is a *dīwān* of poetry, Mujālid from al-Shaʿbī related: **they agreed not to write *bismi llāhi
l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* before poetry**; al-Zuhrī: **the Sunnah has passed that they not write it in poetry**;
Saʿīd b. Jubayr held to writing the basmalah at the head of books of poetry and most later scholars followed
him — Abū Bakr al-Khaṭīb: *that is what we choose and prefer*." And the ḥadīths of the Sunan: "*the screen
between the jinn and the private parts of the sons of Adam, when one enters the privy, is his saying* bismi
llāh" (Ibn Mājah, Tirmidhī); and Dāraqutnī from ʿĀʾishah: "*when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ touched his
purification water he named Allah, Mighty and Exalted, then poured the water over his hands*."

### V10 · Juristic rulings (aḥkām)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The basmalah's ruling, in his own *tarjīḥ*:

* "**The whole of Mālik's and his companions' doctrine is that it is not, with them, a verse of Fātiḥat
  al-Kitāb nor of others: the one praying does not recite it in the obligatory prayer nor in other than it,
  silently nor aloud; and it is permitted to recite it in supererogatory prayers.** This is the well known of
  his doctrine. Another report from him: it is recited at the head of sūrahs in the supererogatory and not at
  the head of Umm al-Qurʾān; and Ibn Nāfiʿ related from him the beginning of recitation with it in the
  obligatory and the supererogatory, not to be left in any case. And some of the people of Madinah say that
  the basmalah is indispensable in it — among them **Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn Shihāb**; and al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad,
  Isḥāq, Abū Thawr and Abū ʿUbayd said it. **And this shows that the question is one of *ijtihād*, not
  decisive** — as some of the ignorant jurists supposed, whose saying would entail declaring Muslims
  unbelievers. And a group of scholars held that it is recited silently with the Fātiḥah: among them **Abū
  Ḥanīfah and al-Thawrī**, and it is related from ʿUmar, ʿAlī, Ibn Masʿūd, ʿAmmār and Ibn al-Zubayr, and it is
  the saying of al-Ḥakam, Ḥammād and Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal and Abū ʿUbayd, and the like is related from al-Awzāʿī
  (cited by Abū ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-Barr in *al-Istidhkār*)." Their proof: Anas's "the Messenger of Allah ﷺ led
  us in prayer and did not make us hear the recitation of *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*"; ʿAmmār b. Ruzayq
  from al-Aʿmash from Shuʿbah from Thābit from Anas: "I prayed behind the Prophet ﷺ, Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, and I
  did not hear any of them recite *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* aloud."
* His own preference: "**I say: this is a good saying, and on it the reports from Anas agree and do not
  contradict, and by it one leaves the disagreement over reciting the basmalah.**" And the reason given by
  Saʿīd b. Jubayr for the silence: "the idolaters used to attend the mosque, and when the Messenger of Allah
  ﷺ recited *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* they said: *This is Muḥammad mentioning the Raḥmān of
  al-Yamāmah* — meaning Musaylimah — **so he was commanded to lower his voice with the basmalah, and** *wa-lā
  tajhar bi-ṣalātika wa-lā tukhāfit bihā* (17:110) **came down**"; and Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm: "that remained to
  our day on that script even if the cause has gone, as the *ramal* in *ṭawāf* remained though the cause had
  gone."
* **The obligation of the Fātiḥah in prayer** (second *bāb*, fifth to ninth *masāʾil*): **Mālik**: it is
  required of the imam and the one praying alone in every *rakʿah*; Ibn Khuwayz Mindād: "Mālik's saying did
  not differ that one who forgets it in a *rakʿah* of a two-*rakʿah* prayer, his prayer is void"; when
  forgotten in one *rakʿah* of a four- or three-*rakʿah* prayer, Mālik once said repeat the prayer and once
  said prostrate the two prostrations of forgetfulness; Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr: "the sound saying is to cancel that
  *rakʿah* and bring another in its place, like one who has omitted a prostration by forgetfulness." **Al-Ḥasan
  al-Baṣrī and most of the people of Baṣrah** and al-Mughīrah b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān: "if one recited Umm al-Qurʾān
  once in the prayer it suffices him, since it is a prayer in which one has recited Umm al-Qurʾān, and it is
  complete — by his saying: *No prayer for one who does not recite Umm al-Qurʾān* — and this one has recited
  it." **Abū Ḥanīfah, al-Thawrī and al-Awzāʿī**: if one leaves it deliberately in the whole prayer and
  recites other than it, it suffices him (with a difference related from al-Awzāʿī); **Abū Yūsuf and
  Muḥammad b. al-Ḥasan**: the least is three verses, or one long verse like the verse of debt — and from
  Muḥammad b. al-Ḥasan: "I allow *ijtihād* in the measure of a verse and a comprehensible word, like *al-ḥamdu
  li-llāh*, and I do not allow it in a letter that is not speech"; **al-Ṭabarī**: the one praying recites Umm
  al-Qurʾān in every *rakʿah*, and if he does not, nothing suffices him but its like from the Qurʾān, in the
  number of its verses and letters — Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr: "this has no meaning; the designation of it and the
  text for it has singled it out for this ruling".
* **The *maʾmūm*.** The imam carries the recitation from him if he joins him bowing (consensus); if he joins
  him standing, he recites. In the silent prayer "one should not leave off recitation behind his imam, and if
  he does he has done ill and owes nothing (Mālik and his companions)"; when the imam prays aloud, "there is
  no recitation of Fātiḥat al-Kitāb nor of other than it in the well-known of Mālik's doctrine", by 7:204 and
  the Prophet's ﷺ "*Why am I disputed over the Qurʾān?*", "*When he recites, be silent*", and "*Whoever has
  an imam, the imam's recitation is his recitation*." And the dispute over the last: "it is a **weak ḥadīth**,
  from al-Ḥasan b. ʿUmārah (abandoned) and Abū Ḥanīfah (weak), both from Mūsā b. Abī ʿĀʾishah from ʿAbd Allāh
  b. Shaddād from Jābir — Dāraqutnī said: Sufyān, Shuʿbah, Isrāʾīl and others related it from Mūsā from Ibn
  Shaddād **as an interrupted report from the Prophet ﷺ, and that is the correct form**." Al-Shāfiʿī (as
  related by al-Buwayṭī and Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal): "no one's prayer is valid until he recites Fātiḥat al-Kitāb in
  every *rakʿah*, imam or follower, whether his imam prays aloud or silently" — in Iraq he said the follower
  recites when the imam is silent and does not when aloud; in Egypt he had two sayings. And Ibn Wahb, Ashhab,
  Ibn ʿAbd al-Ḥakam, Ibn Ḥabīb and the Kūfans: "the follower recites nothing, whether the imam is aloud or
  silent."
* **Al-Qurṭubī's conclusion** (ninth *masʾalah*): "**the sound of these sayings is the saying of al-Shāfiʿī,
  Aḥmad and Mālik in the other saying: that the Fātiḥah is required in every *rakʿah* for every one,
  generally**, by his saying ﷺ *No prayer for one who does not recite in it Fātiḥat al-Kitāb*, and *Whoever
  prays a prayer in which he does not recite Umm al-Qurʾān, it is deficient* — three times; and Abū Hurayrah:
the Messenger of Allah ﷺ commanded me to announce that *there is no prayer except with the recitation of
Fātiḥat al-Kitāb, and then what is added* (Abū Dāwūd). As a prostration of one *rakʿah* does not substitute
for another's, nor its bowing, so the recitation of one *rakʿah* does not substitute for another" — and he
lists the Companions who said "no prayer except with Fātiḥat al-Kitāb": ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū Hurayrah,
Ubayy b. Kaʿb, Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī, ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr, ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit, Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, ʿUthmān b.
Abī al-ʿĀṣ, Khawwāt b. Jubayr; and Ibn Mājah's ḥadīth of Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī: "*No prayer for one who does
not recite in every* rakʿah *al-Ḥamdu li-llāh and a sūrah, in an obligatory prayer or other.*" And **ʿUbādah
b. al-Ṣāmit's** ḥadīth (Abū Dāwūd), on which he relies: "the Messenger of Allah ﷺ led us in one of the
prayers in which he recited aloud, and it became confused for him; when he finished he turned his face to us
and said: *Do you recite when I recite aloud?* We said: Yes. He said: *Do not — for I say, why am I disputed
over the Qurʾān? Do not recite anything of the Qurʾān when I recite aloud, except Umm al-Qurʾān.*" And on
7:204, "it came down at Makkah, and the prohibition of speech in prayer came down at Madinah, as Zayd b.
Arqam said, so it is no proof — its object was the idolaters, as Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab said." And ʿAṭāʾ's
practice: "**ʿAṭāʾ said: *Āmīn* is a supplication; Ibn al-Zubayr would say *Āmīn* and those behind him until
the mosque surged with it.**"

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** In the fourth *bāb*'s frame — "on what al-Fātiḥah contains of meanings, readings, *iʿrāb*,
and the virtue of those who praise" — he opens with the ḥadīth report of Abū Muhammad ʿAbd al-Ghanī b. Saʿīd
al-Ḥāfiẓ from Abū Hurayrah and Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī: the Prophet ﷺ said: "*When the servant says* al-ḥamdu
li-llāh, *Allah says: My servant has spoken the truth; praise is Mine*"; and Muslim from Anas: "*Allah is
pleased with the servant who eats a meal and praises Him for it, or drinks a drink and praises Him for it*";
and **al-Ḥasan**: "there is no blessing but that *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* is better than it"; and Ibn Mājah from
Anas: "*Allah has not bestowed a blessing on a servant and he says* al-ḥamdu li-llāh, *but that what He gave
is better than what he took*"; and in *Nawādir al-Uṣūl*: "*Were the whole world, with all its furnishings, in
the hand of a man of my community, and then he said* al-ḥamdu li-llāh, *al-ḥamdu li-llāh would be better than
that*" — "the meaning: he has been given the world and then given this word after it, so the word is better,
because the world perishes and the word remains — it is of the *bāqiyāt al-ṣāliḥāt*, *wa-l-bāqiyātu
l-ṣāliḥātu khayrun ʿinda rabbika thawāban wa-khayrun amalā* (19:76)"; and the ḥadīth of the servant who said
"*O Lord, to You belongs praise as befits the majesty of Your face and the greatness of Your sovereignty*" —
"and it was too much for the two angels, so they did not know how to write it, and they went up to heaven and
said: *O our Lord, Your servant has said a saying we do not know how to write*; and Allah, who knows best
what His servant said, said: *What did My servant say?* They said:… He said: *Write it as My servant said it,
until he meets Me and I repay him for it*"; and Muslim from Abū Mālik al-Ashʿarī: "*Purity is half of faith,
and* al-ḥamdu li-llāh *fills the scale, and* subḥāna llāh wa-l-ḥamdu li-llāh *fill, or fill what is between,
the heaven and the earth*."

### V16 · Comparative religion & the earlier scriptures (ahl al-kitāb)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The reports that set the basmalah in the history of the earlier revelations: "**the third** —
al-Shaʿbī and al-Aʿmash related that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to write *bi-smika llāhumma* until he was
commanded to write *bismi llāh* and wrote it; then when *quli dʿū llāha awi dʿū l-Raḥmān* came down he wrote
*bismi llāhi l-Raḥmān*; and when *innahu min Sulaymāna wa-innahu bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* (27:30) came
down he wrote it." And in Abū Dāwūd's *Muṣannaf*: "al-Shaʿbī, Abū Mālik, Qatādah and Thābit b. ʿUmārah said:
**the Prophet ﷺ did not write *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* until Sūrat al-Naml came down**." And al-Ḥasan:
"*bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* did not come down in anything of the Qurʾān except in *Ṭā Sīn*, *innahu min
Sulaymāna wa-innahu bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*" — and al-Jurayrī said al-Ḥasan was asked about the
basmalah and said: "**in the openings of letters**". And **Ibn ʿAbbās's** words on the *Āmīn*: "the Jews
envied you for nothing as they envied you for *al-salām* and *al-taʾmīn*" (the ḥadīth of ʿĀʾishah and of Ibn
ʿAbbās in Ibn Mājah: "*The Jews envied you for nothing so much as for* Āmīn, *so say* Āmīn *much*") — "and the
people of the Book envied us because its beginning is praise of Allah and extolling of Him, then submission
to Him and humbling before Him, then a supplication for us for guidance to the straight path, then a
supplication against them, with our saying *Āmīn*." And of Moses and Aaron (in the *Āmīn* chapter): "*the
word* Āmīn *was not before us except for Mūsā and Hārūn*" (Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm's report from Anas: "*Allah gave
my community three things not given to anyone before them: the salām — which is the greeting of the people of
the Garden, the rows of the angels, and* Āmīn — *except what was from Mūsā and Hārūn*"), explained by 10:89,
*qad ujībat daʿwatukumā*, "where Mūsā supplicated against Pharaoh and Hārūn said *Āmīn*, and Allah named
Hārūn a suppliant although it was from him only the *Āmīn*, since He made that from him a supplication."

### V13 · History, sīrah & parables

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The historical episodes recorded with the basmalah: **the treaty of Ḥudaybiyah** — "when ʿAlī
wrote at Ḥudaybiyah by the Prophet's ﷺ command *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*, Suhayl b. ʿAmr said: *As for
bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm, we do not know what it is; but write what we know: bi-smika llāhumma*"; the
**Musaylimah** episode — "he named himself **Raḥmān al-Yamāmah**, and Allah put on him the description of the
liar by which he is known"; and the story of **Warqah b. Nawfal** and the first call (above, S2); and the
report that **Iblīs wailed four times**; and the incident behind the silent basmalah — "the idolaters used to
attend the mosque, and when the Prophet ﷺ recited the basmalah they said: *this is Muḥammad mentioning the
Raḥmān of al-Yamāmah*" (above, V10).

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The verses by which the basmalah's questions are resolved: 27:30 (*innahu min Sulaymāna
wa-innahu bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*) for its revelation-form; 17:110 (*wa-lā tajhar bi-ṣalātika wa-lā
tukhāfit bihā*) for the silent recitation; 96:1 (*iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika*) for the writing of the *alif*; 11:41
(*bismi llāhi majrāhā wa-mursāhā*) and 6:118 for naming at every act; 25:60 and 13:30 (*wa-hum yakfurūna
bi-l-Raḥmān*) for *al-Raḥmān*'s being known to the Arabs; and 17:110 (*quli dʿū llāha awi dʿū l-Raḥmān*) for
its being peculiar to Allah.

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The *basmalah*'s protection-reports: **Ibn Masʿūd's** "whoever wishes Allah to save him from
the nineteen *zabāniyah*, let him recite the basmalah, so that Allah makes for him a shield with every letter
of it" (nineteen letters by 74:30, *ʿalayhā tisʿata ʿashar*); and Ibn ʿAṭiyyah's caveat. And ʿAlī's: *bismi
llāh* "a cure from every disease and a help to every remedy; *al-Raḥmān* a help for everyone who believes in
Him; *al-Raḥīm* for the one who repents, believes and acts righteously"; and the report of "the four verses
suspended by the Throne" (S1).

## 1:2 — الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

*[context]* All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The third** — the Muslims are agreed that **Allah is praised for all His blessings, and
that of what Allah has blessed with is *īmān*; and this proves that *īmān* is His act and His creation**, and
the proof is His word *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* — and *al-ʿālamūn* is the totality of created things, and of that
totality is *īmān* — **not as the *Qadariyyah* say, that it is a creation belonging to them**." And "**the
sixth** — Allah praised Himself with *al-ḥamd* and opened His Book with His praise, and He did not permit
others to do that, but forbade them in His Book and on the tongue of His Prophet ﷺ: *fa-lā tuzakkū anfusakum,
huwa aʿlamu bi-mani ttqā* (53:32); and he ﷺ said: *Throw dust in the faces of the flatterers* (related by
al-Miqdād)." And its sense: "**the meaning of** *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* **is: praise has preceded
from Me to Myself before any of the worlds might praise Me — My praise of Myself to Myself in eternity was not
for a cause, while My praise of creation is mixed with causes**… and when He knew His servants' inability to
praise Him, **He praised Himself by Himself for Himself in eternity**; and the exhaustion of the servants'
capacity is itself the place of their inability before His praise — do you not see the master of the
messengers how he showed inability, saying: *I cannot count Your praise* — and the poet: *if we praise You
with good, You are as we praise, and above what we praise*." And "it was said: He praised Himself in eternity
because He knew the abundance of His blessings on His servants and their inability to fulfil the duty of His
praise, so He praised Himself on their behalf, that the blessing may be more delightful to them, since He
dropped from them by that the burden of obligation."

### V4 · Cross-references inside the sūrah

**Al-Qurṭubī.** On His word *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* as it bears on what follows: "**God described Himself after
*Rabb al-ʿālamīn* as *al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*** (recorded under S6), and the *Rabb* is what makes the later
*māliki yawmi l-dīn* a **specific after a general** — see under 1:4, where Abū ʿAlī's answer to the claim of
repetition is given."

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The ḥadīths of the *ḥamd* (first *masʾalah* of the fourth *bāb*, the *ḥamd* chapter): the
report of Abū Muhammad ʿAbd al-Ghanī al-Ḥāfiẓ from Abū Hurayrah **and** Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī — the Prophet ﷺ:
"*When the servant says* al-ḥamdu li-llāh, *Allah says: My servant has spoken the truth — praise is Mine*";
Muslim from Anas: "*Allah is pleased with the servant who eats a meal and praises Him for it, or drinks a
drink and praises Him for it*"; Ibn Mājah from Anas: "*Allah has not bestowed a blessing on a servant and he
says* al-ḥamdu li-llāh *but that what He gave is better than what he took*"; *Nawādir al-Uṣūl*: "*Were the
whole world, with all its furnishings, in the hand of a man of my community and then he said* al-ḥamdu
li-llāh, *al-ḥamdu li-llāh would be better than that*"; Ibn Mājah from Ibn ʿUmar: the two angels and the
servant's "*O Lord, to You belongs praise as befits the majesty of Your face and the greatness of Your
sovereignty*" — "write it as My servant said, until he meets Me and I repay him for it"; Muslim from Abū Mālik
al-Ashʿarī: "*Purity is half of faith, and* al-ḥamdu li-llāh *fills the scale, and* subḥāna llāh wa-l-ḥamdu
li-llāh *fill what is between the heaven and the earth*."

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** **Ibn ʿAbbās**: "*al-ḥamdu li-llāh* is **the word of every grateful one**; Ādam said it when
he sneezed; Allah said to Nūḥ *fa-quli l-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī najjānā mina l-qawmi l-ẓālimīn* (23:28);
Ibrāhīm said *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī wahaba lī ʿalā l-kibari Ismāʿīl wa-Isḥāq* (14:39); of Dāwūd and
Sulaymān *wa-qālā l-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī faḍḍalanā ʿalā kathīrin min ʿibādihi l-muʾminīn* (27:15); to His
Prophet *wa-quli l-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī lam yattakhidh waladā* (17:111); and the people of the Garden *al-ḥamdu
li-llāhi lladhī adhhaba ʿannā l-ḥazan* (35:34), *wa-ākhiru daʿwāhum ani l-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*
(10:10)" — "so it is the word of every grateful one." And **al-Ḥasan**: "there is no blessing but that
*al-ḥamdu li-llāh* is better than it." And **Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq** on *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*: "**whoever praises Him
by His attributes as He described Himself has praised Him** — for ḥamd is *ḥāʾ*, *mīm*, *dāl*: the *ḥāʾ* from
oneness (*waḥdāniyyah*), the *mīm* from dominion (*mulk*), the *dāl* from everlastingness (*daymūmiyyah*);
whoever knows Him by oneness, everlastingness and dominion has known Him, and this is the reality of *al-ḥamdu
li-llāh*." And **Shaqīq b. Ibrāhīm**: "*al-ḥamdu li-llāh* is on three faces: that when Allah gives you a thing
you know who gave it; that you be pleased with what He gave; and that so long as His strength is in your body
you not disobey Him — these are the conditions of praise." And **al-Junayd** to a man who said *al-ḥamdu
li-llāh* before him: "Complete it as Allah said: say *Rabb al-ʿālamīn*" — the man said: "and who are the
worlds, that they should be mentioned with the Truth?" He said: "Say it, my brother — for **the created, when
it is joined with the Eternal, no trace of it remains**."

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** On *al-ʿālamīn* (the eleventh *masʾalah*): "the people of explanation differed greatly over
*al-ʿālamīn*": **Qatādah**: "the worlds are the plural of *ʿālam*, and it is **every existent other than
Allah**, having no singular from its own form, like *rahṭ* and *qawm*"; and it was said: "the people of every
age are an *ʿālam* (al-Ḥusayn b. al-Faḍl, by 26:165, *ataʾtūna l-dhukrāna mina l-ʿālamīn* — that is, of
mankind)" — with al-ʿAjjāj's *fa-Khindifun hāmatu hādhā l-ʿālami* and Jarīr's *tanṣufuhu l-bariyyatu wa-huwa
sāmin / wa-yuḍḥī l-ʿālamūna lahu ʿiyālā*; **Ibn ʿAbbās**: "the *ʿālamūn* are **the jinn and mankind**, its
proof His word *li-yakūna li-l-ʿālamīna nadhīrā* (25:1) — and he was not a warner to the beasts"; **al-Farrāʾ
and Abū ʿUbaydah**: "*al-ʿālam* is an expression for those who understand, and they are **four communities:
mankind, the jinn, the angels and the devils** — the beasts are not called an *ʿālam*, because this plural is
only the plural of those who understand" (al-Aʿshā: *mā in samiʿtu bi-mithlihim fī l-ʿālamīnā*); **Zayd b.
Aslam**: "those provided for (*al-murtaziqūn*)", and the like is **Abū ʿAmr b. al-ʿAlāʾ**: "the spiritual
beings"; and it is the sense of **Ibn ʿAbbās** too: "**every living being that crawls on the face of the
earth**"; **Wahb b. Munabbih**: "Allah has **eighteen thousand worlds**, and this world is one of them";
**Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī**: "Allah has **forty thousand worlds**, and this world from its east to its west is one
world"; **Muqātil**: "the worlds are **eighty thousand worlds** — forty thousand on land and forty thousand
in the sea"; and **al-Rabīʿ b. Anas from Abū al-ʿĀliyah**: "the jinn are a world and mankind are a world, and
besides that the earth has **four corners, in each corner a thousand five hundred worlds**, created for His
worship."
Al-Qurṭubī's own *tarjīḥ*: "**the first of these sayings is the soundest, because it comprises every created
and existing thing** — its proof His word *qāla firʿawnu wa-mā rabbu l-ʿālamīn? qāla rabbu l-samāwāti
wa-l-arḍi wa-mā baynahumā* (26:23–24); then it is taken **from *al-ʿilm* and *al-ʿalāmah*, because it points
to its Maker** (as al-Zajjāj said: *al-ʿālam* is all that Allah created in this world and the next; and
al-Khalīl: *al-ʿilm*, *al-ʿalāmah* and *al-maʿlam* are what points to a thing — so the world points to its
having a Creator and a Director) — and this is clear."

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The fourth** — *al-ḥamd* in the speech of the Arabs means **complete praise** (*al-thanāʾ
al-kāmil*), and the *alif* and *lām* are **for the comprehension of the genus** of praises, so He deserves
praise in its entirety, since His are the most beautiful names and the highest attributes" — and he brings
the poets (*wa-ablahu maḥmūdi l-thanāʾi khaṣaṣtuhu / bi-afḍali aqwālī wa-afḍali aḥmadī*) and: "al-ḥamd is the
contrary of blame; *ḥamidtu l-rajul* — *aḥmaduhu ḥamdan* — and he is *ḥamīd* and *maḥmūd*; *al-taḥmīd* is
more emphatic than *al-ḥamd*; *al-ḥamd* is more general than *al-shukr*; *al-maḥmadah* is the contrary of
*al-madhmamah*; *aḥmada l-rajul*: his affair came to praise; *aḥmadtuhu*: I found him praiseworthy; a man
*ḥumadah*, like *humazah*: one who praises things more than they deserve; and *ḥamadah* of the fire is the
sound of its blazing."
"The fifth — **Abū Jaʿfar al-Ṭabarī and Abū al-ʿAbbās al-Mubarrid held that *al-ḥamd* and *al-shukr* are one
meaning, and it is not acceptable** — and Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Sulamī related it in *al-Ḥaqāʾiq* from
Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq and Ibn ʿAṭāʾ (Ibn ʿAṭāʾ: the meaning is thanks to Allah, since from Him is the favour of
teaching us it until we praised Him). **Al-Ṭabarī argued for their being one meaning by the correctness of
your saying** *al-ḥamdu li-llāh shukran* … **Ibn ʿAṭiyyah said: in truth it is a proof against what he held**,
because your saying *shukran* you have attached to *al-ḥamd* only because it is for a blessing among
blessings. And some scholars said: **thanking is more general than praising**, since it is with tongue, limbs
and heart, while praise is with the tongue only; and it was said: **praise is more general**, because in it
is the meaning of thanks and the meaning of eulogy, and it may be put in place of thanks but thanks not in
place of praise." His own conclusion: "**the sound view is that *al-ḥamd* is praise of the praised for His
attributes without any prior beneficence, and *al-shukr* is praise of the thanked for what beneficence he has
brought** — and on this limit our scholars said *al-ḥamd* is more general than *al-shukr*, because praise
falls on eulogy, on extolling and on thanks, while recompense is specific, being only a requital for one who
has done you a kindness; so *al-ḥamd* in the verse is more general since it exceeds *al-shukr*."
And: "*al-ḥamd* is also mentioned with the sense of pleasure: *balaṭuhu fa-ḥamadtuhu* — I was pleased with
him; from it *maqāman maḥmūdan* (17:79); and he ﷺ said *aḥmadu ilaykum ghasla l-iḥlīl* — I approve it for
you."
"**The seventh** — the seven reciters and the majority are agreed on **raising** the *dāl* of *al-ḥamd*; and
from **Sufyān b. ʿUyaynah and Ruʾbah b. al-ʿAjjāj** it is related with the *naṣb* of the *dāl*, and that is
on the implied *faʿala*." And Sībawayh's answer to the question what the benefit is in the nominative
sentence: "when a man says *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* with the nominative, in it is the same meaning as your saying
*ḥamidtu llāha ḥamdan*, except that **the one who raises *al-ḥamd* reports that the praise is from him and
from all creation to Allah, while the one who puts it in the accusative reports that the praise is from him
alone**"; and "another said: this is spoken as an exposure to Allah's pardon and forgiveness, magnifying and
glorifying Him, so it is the opposite of the sense of a report and in it is the sense of a request — and in
the ḥadīth: *whoever is occupied with My mention from asking Me, I give him better than I give the askers*."
And the *iltifāt*-reading of the raised form: "it was said: His praising Himself and extolling Himself is to
teach that to His servants; the meaning therefore is: **say *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*** — and this is the sense of
his saying of *iyyāka*: *say*." And the transmitted *iʿrāb*-variants: "it is related from **Ibn Abī ʿAblah**
— *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* with *ḍammah* of both *dāl* and *lām*, making the second follow the first and matching
the sounds"; and from **al-Ḥasan b. Abī al-Ḥasan and Zayd b. ʿAlī** — with *kasrah* of the *dāl*, making the
first follow the second." And on *al-Rabb*: "**the eighth** — *Rabb al-ʿālamīn*, that is, **their Owner**: everyone
who owns a thing is its *rabb*, so *al-Rabb* is the owner. And *al-Rabb* is one of Allah's names; it is not
said of other than Him except by annexation — and they said it in the Jāhiliyyah of a king: al-Ḥārith b.
Ḥilizah: *wa-huwa l-rabbu wa-l-shahīdu ʿalā yaw / mi l-ḥiyārīna wa-l-balāʾu balāʾ*" — "*al-Rabb* is the
master (*al-sayyid*), as in *udhkurnī ʿinda rabbika* (12:42) and the ḥadīth *that the slave girl give birth
to her mistress*; and *al-Rabb* is **the one who sets right, the director, the mender, the maintainer** — as
al-Harawī: "of one who undertakes the setting right of a thing and its completion one says *rabba rabbahu*,
and from it the *rabbāniyyūn* are named for their undertaking of the Books"; and *al-Rabb* is **the
worshipped**, as the poet: *a-rabbun yabūlu l-thaʿlabāni bi-raʾsihi / laqad dhalla man bālat ʿalayhi
l-thaʿālibu*" — "and for frequency one says *rabbāhu*, *rabbabahu*, *rabbatahu* (al-Naḥḥās)."
And: "**the ninth** — some scholars said **this name is Allah's greatest name**, because of the frequency with
which suppliants call by it … and because of what this description gives rise to in the relation between
*rabb* and *marbūb*, with what it comprises of care, mercy and need at every state; and they differed over
its derivation: it is said from *al-tarbiyah* — so Allah is **director of His creation and their nurturer**,
as in *wa-rabāʾibukumu llātī fī ḥujūrikum* (4:23), where the wife's daughter is called *rabībah* for the
husband's nurturing of her. **On its being director of His creation and their nurturer it is an attribute of
act; and on *al-Rabb* being the owner and master it is an attribute of the Essence.**" And "**the tenth** —
when the *alif* and *lām* are put on *rabb*, Allah is singled out by it because it is for the covenant, while
without them it is shared between Allah and His servants — one says *Allah is the Rabb of the servants* and
*Zayd is the rabb of the house*; so **Allah is the Rabb of rabbs, owning the owner and the owned, and He is
the Creator of that and its Provider; and every rabb other than Him is neither creator nor provider, and every owned thing was caused to be owned after it was not, and that is taken from His hand**; and **His
attribute differs from these meanings — this is the difference between the attribute of the Creator and of
the created**." And "**the twelfth** — the nominative and accusative are both permissible in *Rabb*: the
accusative as praise, and the nominative as a fresh start — that is, *He is the Lord of the worlds*."

### V9 · Variant readings (qirāʾāt)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The *ḥamd*-readings of the seventh *masʾalah* (above, V8): the consensus *rafʿ* of the seven
reciters; the *naṣb* attributed to Sufyān b. ʿUyaynah and Ruʾbah; Ibn Abī ʿAblah's double *ḍammah*; al-Ḥasan
b. Abī al-Ḥasan's and Zayd b. ʿAlī's *kasrah* of the *dāl*; with the parallels *ajūʾuka*, *al-sāqānu*,
*muraddifīn*/*muqattilīn*, and al-Nuʿmān b. Bashīr's *wa-yli immihā*.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** (i) "The Muslims are agreed that Allah is praised for all His blessings, **and that of His
blessings is *īmān* — and this proves *īmān* is His act and His creation, not as the *Qadariyyah* say that it
is a creation belonging to them**" (the third *masʾalah*). (ii) Against the *Qadariyyah*'s claim that
"Allah chose for them *al-ḥamd* and made it their own doing"? — see under 1:5. (iii) His praise of Himself
"was not for a cause, while His praise of creation is mixed with causes" — the created's self-praise is
"foul, for it seeks by it to attract benefits and repel harms" (the sixth *masʾalah*).

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V6, V7, V8, V9, V11, V15._

## 1:3 — الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* The Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The thirteenth** — He described Himself, after *Rabb al-ʿālamīn*, as *al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*,
because when His being described as *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* contains intimidation, He joined it to *al-Raḥmān
al-Raḥīm* for what it contains of encouragement, so as to join in His attributes fear of Him and hope in Him,
and so be a greater aid to His obedience and a stronger restraint" — with 15:49–50, *nabbiʾ ʿibādī annī anā
l-ghafūru l-raḥīm, wa-anna ʿadhābī huwa l-ʿadhābu l-alīm*, and 40:3, *ghāfiri l-dhanbi wa-qābili l-tawbi
shadīdi l-ʿiqāb*, and the Muslim ḥadīth of Abū Hurayrah: "*Were the believer to know what is with Allah of
punishment, no one would hope for His Garden; and were the unbeliever to know what is with Allah of mercy, no
one would despair of His Garden.*" And: "what is in these two names of meanings has already passed" — with
the cross-reference to the basmalah's twenty-second to twenty-sixth *masāʾil* (given under 1:1, V11).

## 1:4 — مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

*[context]* Master of the Day of Judgement.

### V9 · Variant readings (qirāʾāt)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The fourteenth** — **Muḥammad b. al-Samayqaʿ** recited *māliki* with the *naṣb*." And:
"**there are four dialects: *mālik*, *malik*, *malk* (lightened from *malik*), and *malīk***" — with the
poets: *wa-ayyāmin lanā ghurrin ṭiwālin / ʿaṣaynā l-malka fīhā an nadīnā*, and *fa-qnaʿ bimā qasama
l-malīku fa-innamā / qasama l-khalāʾiqa baynanā ʿallāmuhā*. And: "from **Nāfiʿ** is related the full
vowelling of the *kasrah* in *malik*, reading *malikī*, on the dialect of those who make the vowels full — a
dialect of the Arabs mentioned by al-Mahdawī and others."

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The fifteenth** — the scholars differed which is more emphatic, *malik* or *mālik*; **the
two readings are related from the Prophet ﷺ, Abū Bakr and ʿUmar** (Tirmidhī mentioned them). It was said:
***malik* is more general and more emphatic than *mālik*, since every king is an owner and not every owner is
a king**, and because the king's command runs over the owner in his property, so that he does not dispose of
it except by the king's direction (Abū ʿUbaydah, al-Mubarrid). And it was said: ***mālik* is more emphatic,
because he is owner of people and other than them, and the owner is more emphatic in disposal and greater**,
since to him belongs the execution of the ordinances of the law, and then with him is the increase of
ownership" (al-Fārisī's report of Ibn al-Sarrāj's citation). **Al-Fārisī's answer to the claim that *mālik*
is mere repetition of *Rabb al-ʿālamīn***: "there is no proof in this, for in the revelation there are things
of this form — the general first, then the specific: *huwa llāhu l-khāliqu l-bāriʾu l-muṣawwiru* (59:24),
where *al-Khāliq* is general and *al-Muṣawwir* is mentioned for what it contains of pointing to the making
and the existence of wisdom; and *wa-bi-l-ākhirati hum yūqinūn* after *alladhīna yuʾminūna bi-l-ghayb*
(2:3–4), where *al-ghayb* comprises the Hereafter and other than it, but it was mentioned for its greatness
… and *al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*, where *al-Raḥmān* is general and *al-Raḥīm* is mentioned after it to single out
the believers, by *wa-kāna bi-l-muʾminīna raḥīmā*." **Abū Ḥātim**: "**mālik is more emphatic in praise of the
Creator than *malik*, and *malik* is more emphatic in praise of the created than *mālik***; the difference
between them is that the owner among creatures may be other than a king, while **when Allah is an owner He is
a king**" — and **al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr b. al-ʿArabī** chose this, giving three faces: (i) you annex it to the
specific and the general — *mālik al-dār wa-l-arḍ wa-l-thawb*, as you say *mālik al-mulūk*; (ii)
it applies to the owner of the little and the much; (iii) you say *mālik al-mulk* and not *malik al-mulk*.
**Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār**: "'*mālik*' indicates ownership (*milk*) with the *kasrah*, which does not comprise
sovereignty (*mulk*) with the *ḍammah*, while *milk* comprises both — so it is worthier of emphasis" — and he
gives the further inclusions: **perfection, and therefore the sovereignty over what is below him**; 2:247,
*inna llāha iṣṭafāhu ʿalaykum wa-zādahu basṭatan fī l-ʿilmi wa-l-jism*; the ḥadīth *al-imāmah fī Quraysh*;
**capability and choice** — "necessary in a king: if he is not capable, choosing, executing his judgement and
command, his enemy overcomes him, another defeats him and his subjects despise him"; and **the seizing,
command, prohibition, promise and threat** — as Solomon's *mā liya lā arā l-hudhuda am kāna mina
l-ghāʾibīn… la-uʿadhdhibannahu ʿadhāban shadīdā* (27:20–21) — "other than that of wonderful matters and noble
meanings that are not found in *mālik*." And al-Qurṭubī adds: "some argued that *mālik* is more emphatic
**because in it is an extra letter, so its reciter has ten good deeds more than the one who recites *malik***
— **I say: this is a view of the form and not of the meaning; and the reading with *malik* is established,
and in it is meaning not in *mālik***, as we have shown."

And "**the eighteenth** — if one says: how did He say *māliki yawmi l-dīn* when the Day of Judgement does not
yet exist? — it is said: know that *mālik* is the active participle of *malaka yamliku*, and the active
participle in the speech of the Arabs may be annexed to what follows it while it has the sense of the future
act, and that is sound, rational speech with them — as you say *this is the striker of Zayd tomorrow*, i.e.
*he will strike Zayd*, and *this is the pilgrim of the House of Allah next year*, its sense *he will
perform the pilgrimage next year*. So *māliki yawmi l-dīn* is on the sense of the future: **He will own the
Day of Judgement, or in the Day of Judgement, when it comes.** And a second face: the sense of *mālik*
returns to power — **He is powerful on the Day of Judgement, or over the Day of Judgement and its bringing
into being.**" And on *yawm al-dīn*: "**the nineteenth** — Allah's word *yawm al-dīn*: *al-dīn* here is the
reckoning and the requital of deeds" — the poets *idhā mā ramawnā ramaynāhum / wa-dinnāhumū mithla mā
yuqriḍūnā* and *wa-ʿlam yaqīnan anna mulkaka zāʾilun / wa-ʿlam bi-annaka mā tadīnu tudānu*; and "**the
twenty-first** — the people of language related: *dintuhu bi-fiʿlihi daynan* (with *fatḥ*) and *dīnan* (with
*kasrah*): I requited him — and from it *al-Dayyān* in the description of the Lord, that is, the Requiter;
and in the ḥadīth: *the shrewd one is he who takes account of himself (dāna nafsahu)*, that is, he
reckons" — with the further senses: **the decree (al-qaḍāʾ)** (also related from Ibn ʿAbbās), with Ṭarafah's
*la-ʿamruka mā kānat ḥamūlatu Maʿbadin / ʿalā juddihā ḥarban li-dīnika min Muḍar*; and **obedience**, with
ʿAmr b. Kulthūm's *wa-ayyāmin lanā ghurrin ṭiwālin / ʿaṣaynā l-malka fīhā an nadīnā* — "so on this it is a
homonym". And "**the twenty-second** — **Thaʿlab said: *dāna* the man when he obeyed, and *dāna* when he
disobeyed, and *dāna* when he was mighty, and *dāna* when he was humbled, and *dāna* when he overpowered —
so it is among the** ***aḍdād*** (*the words with opposite senses*)" — and *al-dīn* is said of custom
(*al-ʿādah*) and of one's affair, and of a king's way of life (*sīrat al-malik*: Zuhayr's *lāʾin ḥalalta bi-jawwin
fī Banī Asadin / fī dīni ʿAmrin wa-ḥālat baynanā Fadak* — "he meant, in the place of ʿAmr's obedience"), and
*al-dīn* is "the disease", from al-Liḥyānī.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The sixteenth** — it is **not permitted for anyone to be named by this name nor called by
it except Allah, Mighty and Exalted**": Bukhārī and Muslim from Abū Hurayrah — the Prophet ﷺ: "*Allah will
take up the earth on the Day of Resurrection, and fold the heaven in His right hand, and then say: I am the
King — where are the kings of the earth?*"; and from him: "*The most lowly name with Allah is a man who
called himself **Malik al-Amlāk***" — Muslim adds "*no king but Allah, Mighty and Exalted*"; Sufyān said:
"like *Shāhān Shāh*"; and Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal asked Abū ʿAmr al-Shaybānī about *akhnaʿ* and he said: "the
lowest"; and from him: "*The most angering man to Allah on the Day of Resurrection and the most vicious is a
man who was called Malik al-Amlāk — no king but Allah, Glorified.*" **Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār**: "likewise *māliki
yawmi l-dīn* and *mālika l-mulk* — it should not be disagreed that this is forbidden to all creatures as
Malik al-Amlāk is forbidden; as for being described by *mālik* and *malik* — see the next."

### V10 · Juristic rulings (aḥkām)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The seventeenth** — it is permitted for one who has the concept to be described by
*mālik* and *malik*: Allah, Mighty, said *inna llāha qad baʿatha lakum Ṭālūta malikā* (2:247); and the
Prophet ﷺ said: *people of my community were shown to me as raiders in the way of Allah, riding the back of
this sea, kings on thrones or like kings on thrones*." And the prohibition of the sixteenth *masʾalah* is
thus confined to the absolute and to *Malik al-Amlāk*.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The doctrine drawn from the *malik*/*mālik* preferment: "*malik* and *mālik* both witness to
Allah's sole ownership of the Day of Judgement; **the prohibition of the name to creatures** (above) shows
that it belongs to Him alone" — and note the ordering argument: "the *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* and *al-Raḥmān
al-Raḥīm* already established His ownership of the worlds, so *māliki yawmi l-dīn* adds what they do not
have" (al-Fārisī's answer, V2 above).

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** On the *dīn* of the verse: the transmitted senses and their authorities (above, V2): the
reckoning and requital; **al-qaḍāʾ** (related from Ibn ʿAbbās); **obedience**; and the lexical material of
Thaʿlab and al-Liḥyānī.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V5, V7, V8, V9, V10, V11._

## 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

*[context]* You alone we worship, and You alone we seek help.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The twenty-third** — *iyyāka naʿbudu*: **He returned from the absent to the address
(*iltifāt*), because from the beginning of the sūrah to here it is a report about Allah and praise of Him** —
like *wa-saqāhum rabbuhum sharāban ṭahūrā* (76:21), then *inna hādhā kāna lakum jazāʾan*; and its reverse
*ḥattā idhā kuntum fī l-fulki wa-jarayna bihim* (10:22)." And: "***naʿbudu* means we obey; *al-ʿibādah* is
obedience and humbling; a road is *muʿabbad* when it is trodden down for those who use it (al-Harawī)** —
and the obligation-bound one's saying it **is an acknowledgement of lordship and a verification of Allah's
worship**, since the rest of people worship other than Him of idols and other things. ***Wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn***:
**we seek help, support and success.**" And al-Sulamī in *Ḥaqāʾiq*: "I heard Muḥammad b. ʿAbd Allāh b.
Shādhān say: I heard Abū Ḥafṣ al-Farghānī say: **whoever acknowledges *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn* is
quit of *jabr* and *qadar*.**"

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The twenty-fourth** — if it is said: why was the object placed before the verb? — it is
said: **it was placed first out of concern, and it is the way of the Arabs to place the most important
first**; it is mentioned that a bedouin reviled another and the reviled turned away from him, and the reviler
said *iyyāka aʿnī* (you I mean!), and the other said *wa-ʿanka uʿriḍu* (and from you I turn away) — both put
the most important first. And also **lest the mention of the servant and the worship precede the mention of
the Worshipped**: so *naʿbuduka wa-nastaʿīnuka* is not permitted, nor *naʿbudu iyyāka wa-nastaʿīnu iyyāka*,
which would place the verb before the pronoun of the object — rather the wording of the Qurʾān is followed"
— with al-ʿAjjāj: *iyyāka adʿū fa-taqabbal malqiya / waghfir khaṭāyāya wa-kaththir wariqī*; and "the poet's
*ilayka ḥattā balaghat iyyākā* is anomalous and not to be measured by; and **he repeated the name lest it be
imagined that *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-nastaʿīn* means other than You**."
"The twenty-fifth — **the majority of the reciters and scholars read *iyyāka* with *shaddah* of the *yāʾ* in
both places; ʿAmr b. Qāʾid read *iyāka* with *kasrah* of the *hamzah* and lightening of the *yāʾ*** — and
"that is a reading to be turned away from, **for the meaning becomes *your sun we worship*, or *your light***
— *iyāt al-shams* (with *kasrah* of the *hamzah*) is its light" (with the verse *saqathu iyātu l-shamsi illā
lithātihi*). "And **al-Faḍl al-Raqāshī read *iyyāka* with *fatḥah* of the *hamzah*** — a well-known dialect;
and **Abū al-Sawwār al-Ghanawī read *hiyyāka* in the two places** — it is a dialect: *fa-hiyyāka wa-l-amru
lladhī in tawassaʿat / mawāriduhu ḍāqat ʿalayka maṣādiruh*."
"The twenty-sixth — *wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*: **the coordination of a sentence upon a sentence**; and **Yaḥyā b.
Waththāb and al-Aʿmash read *nastaʿīn* with *kasrah* of the *nūn***, which is the dialect of Tamīm, Asad,
Qays and Rabīʿah, to show that it is from *istaʿāna*, the *nūn* being vowelled with *kasrah* as the *alif*
of conjunction is. And the root of *nastaʿīn* is *nastaʿwin*: the vowel of the *wāw* was moved to the *ʿayn*
and it became *yāʾ*; the *maṣdar* is *istiʿānah*, whose root is *istiʿwān*, the vowel of the *wāw* moved to
the *ʿayn* and it turned to *alif*, and two vowelless letters not meeting, the second *alif* was dropped
because it was extra (and it is said the first, because the second is for the meaning), and the *hāʾ*
remained as a compensation."

### V9 · Variant readings (qirāʾāt)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** As above: the *shaddah* of the majority; ʿAmr b. Qāʾid's *iyāka* (rejected); al-Faḍl
al-Raqāshī's *iyyāka* with *fatḥah*; Abū al-Sawwār al-Ghanawī's *hiyyāka*; Yaḥyā b. Waththāb's and
al-Aʿmash's *nastaʿīn* with the *nūn* vowelled *kasrah*.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The affirmation is in the *āyah* and is the author's point: "the utterance of the
obligation-bound one by it is **an acknowledgement of lordship and a verification of His worship**, since the
rest of people worship other than Him" (V2); and al-Farghānī's word that "**whoever acknowledges *iyyāka
naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn* is quit of *jabr* and *qadar***" — in the sense that the servant both worships and
asks help, neither compelled nor self-sufficient.

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "The twenty-fourth" above is quoted among the *ādāb* of speech before God: **the servant
does not put his own mention or his worship before the mention of the Worshipped, and the repetition of the
name guards the heart from imagining another addressed.**

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** Following from the above: the *iyyāka* form is "from the concern and the placing of the most
important first" — the pattern of attention the sūrah teaches; and "(they) repeated the name lest it be
imagined" — the concern for clarity of intention in worship.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V8, V9, V11, V14, V15._

## 1:6 — اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ

*[context]* Guide us to the straight path.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The twenty-seventh** — *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm*: ***ihdinā* is a supplication and a
desire from the subject to the Lord**, and the meaning is ***show us the straight path and guide us to
it, and show us the way of Your guidance that leads to Your intimacy and nearness***." And the sūrah's
placing: "one of the scholars said: **Allah placed the greatness of supplication, and its sum, in this
sūrah: half of it is the gathering of praise and half of it is the gathering of needs**; and He made this
supplication better than the one a suppliant makes himself, because this speech was spoken by the Lord of the
worlds, so you supplicate with a supplication that is His speech — and in the ḥadīth: *nothing is more
honourable to Allah than supplication*." And: "it was said: the meaning is ***guide us by the use of the
Sunnah in the performance of Your ordinances***; and it was said: **the root in it is inclining (*al-imālah*)**
— from His word *innā hudnā ilayka* (7:156), that is, we inclined; and the Prophet ﷺ went out in his illness
*yatahādā* between two men, that is, swaying; and from it *al-hadiyyah*, because it is inclined from a king
to a king; and from it *al-hady* for the animal driven to the sanctuary — **so the meaning is: incline our
hearts to the truth.**"

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**al-Fuḍayl b. ʿIyāḍ**: *al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm* is **the road of the Ḥajj** — and this is
specific, and the general is worthier. **Muḥammad b. al-Ḥanafiyyah**: "it is **the religion of Allah which
accepts no other** from the worshippers"; and **ʿĀṣim al-Aḥwal from Abū al-ʿĀliyah**: "*al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*
is **the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his two companions after him**" — and ʿĀṣim said: I said to al-Ḥasan: Abū
al-ʿĀliyah says *al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm* is the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his two companions — he said:
"**Abū al-ʿĀliyah spoke the truth and gave good counsel.**""

### V9 · Variant readings (qirāʾāt)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The twenty-eighth** — the root of *al-ṣirāṭ* in the speech of the Arabs is the road" —
with ʿĀmir b. al-Ṭufayl's *shaḥannā arḍahum bi-l-khayli ḥattā / taraknāhum adhalla mina l-ṣirāṭ* and Jarīr's
*amīru l-muʾminīna ʿalā ṣirāṭin / idhā ʿiwajja l-mawāridu mustaqīm*, and the rājiz's *fa-ṣudda ʿan nahji
l-ṣirāṭi l-qāṣid*. "And **al-Naqqāsh related: *al-ṣirāṭ* is the road in the Byzantine tongue (*bi-lughati
l-Rūm*)** — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah said: **this is very weak**." And: "**it was read *al-sirāṭ* (with *sīn*) from
*al-istirāṭ*, meaning the swallowing, as though the road swallows whoever travels it; and it was read between
the *zāʾ* and the *ṣād*; and it was read with pure *zāʾ*, and *sīn* is the root**; **Salamah related from
al-Farrāʾ**: *al-zirāṭ* with pure *zāʾ* is a dialect of ʿUdhrah, Kalb and Banū al-Qayn — and these (Kalb and
the like) say *azdaq* for *aṣdaq*; and they said *al-Azd* and *al-Asd*, and *lasiqa bihi* and *laṣiqa bihi*."
And the *iʿrāb*: "***al-ṣirāṭ* is in the accusative as the second object, because the verb of guidance
transgresses to the second object with a preposition — *fa-hdūhum ilā ṣirāṭi l-jaḥīm* (37:23) — and without
it, as in this verse**; *al-mustaqīm* is a description of *al-ṣirāṭ*, and it is the one in which there is no
crookedness and no deviation, as in *wa-anna hādhā ṣirāṭī mustaqīman fa-ttabiʿūh* (6:153); and its root is
*mustaqwim*, the vowel moved to the *qāf* and the *wāw* turning to *yāʾ* for the *kasrah* before it."

## 1:7 — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

*[context]* The path of those whom You have blessed — not of those who incurred wrath, nor of those who are astray.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The twenty-ninth** — *ṣirāṭa lladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim*: ***ṣirāṭ* is an apposition to
the first, the apposition of a thing to a thing** — as you say *Zayd came to me, your father*; **and its
meaning is: *keep our guidance enduring* — for a man may be guided to the road and then be cut off on it.**
And it was said: it is **another path**, and its meaning is the knowledge of Allah, Mighty and Exalted, and
the understanding of Him (Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad)." And on the word *alladhīna*: "the language of the Qurʾān is
*alladhīna* in the nominative, the accusative and the genitive; Hudhayl says *al-ladhūna* in the nominative,
and some Arabs say *al-ladhū*, and some *alladhī*." And on *ʿalayhim*: "in *ʿalayhim* there are ten dialects,
of which six readings are transmitted from the Imams of reciters: (1) *ʿalayhim* with *ḍammah* of the *hāʾ*
and silence of the *mīm* — the reading of the generality; (2) with *kasrah* of the *hāʾ* and silence of the
*mīm*; (3) *ʿalayhimī* with *kasrah* of the *hāʾ* and the *mīm* and a *yāʾ* after the *kasrah*; (4)
*ʿalayhimū* with *kasrah* of the *hāʾ* and *ḍammah* of the *mīm* and a *wāw* after the *ḍammah*; (5)
*ʿalayhumū* with *ḍammah* of both and a *wāw* after the *mīm*; (6) *ʿalayhum* with *ḍammah* of both without
the added *wāw*. And four faces are related from the Arabs, not cited from the reciters: *ʿalayhumī* with
*ḍammah* of the *hāʾ* and *kasrah* of the *mīm* and a *yāʾ* (related by al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī from the Arabs);
*ʿalayhumi* with the *mīm* vowelled *kasrah*, no *yāʾ*; *ʿalayhimu* with *kasrah* of the *hāʾ* and *ḍammah*
of the *mīm* without a *wāw*; and *ʿalayhimi* with both vowelled *kasrah*, no *yāʾ* after the *mīm*; **and
all of them are correct** (Ibn al-Anbārī)."

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The thirtieth** — **ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb and Ibn al-Zubayr** recited *ṣirāṭa man anʿamta
ʿalayhim*." And: "**the people differed over those blessed**: the majority of the exegetes said **He meant the
path of the prophets, the truthful, the witnesses and the righteous**, deducing it from His word *wa-man
yuṭiʿi llāha wa-l-rasūla fa-ulāʾika maʿa lladhīna anʿama llāhu ʿalayhim mina l-nabiyyīna wa-l-ṣiddīqīna
wa-l-shuhadāʾi wa-l-ṣāliḥīn* (4:69); **the verse shows that these are on a straight path, and that is what is
sought in the verse of praise — and all that has been said comes back to this, so there is no meaning in
listing the sayings.**"

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** The identification of the two groups rests on the Book: for "those who incurred wrath" —
*wa-bāʾū bi-ghaḍabin mina llāh* (2:61, 3:112) and *wa-ghaḍiba llāhu ʿalayhim* (48:6); for "those who went
astray" — *qad ḍallū min qablu wa-aḍallū kathīran wa-ḍallū ʿan sawāʾi l-sabīl* (5:77); and for the blessed —
4:69 (above).

### V9 · Variant readings (qirāʾāt)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The thirty-fourth** — **ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb and Ubayy b. Kaʿb read *ghayri l-maghḍūbi
ʿalayhim wa-ghayri l-ḍāllīn***, and from them the *naṣb* and the *khafḍ* are related **in both words**:
**the *khafḍ* as an apposition to *alladhīna*, or to the *hāʾ* and *mīm* in *ʿalayhim*, or a description of
*alladhīna*** — "and *alladhīna* is definite, and the definite is not described by the indefinite nor the
indefinite by the definite, except that *alladhīna* is not meant as a particular — it is general, so the
speech is as though you said *I pass by the like of you and honour him*; or because *ghayr* has become
definite by its being between two things with no middle between them, as you say *the living is other than
the dead*, *the still is other than the moving*, *the standing is other than the sitting* — two sayings, the
first for al-Fārisī and the second for al-Zamakhsharī." And "**the *naṣb* in the *rāʾ* is on two faces: the
*ḥāl* from *alladhīna*, or from the *hāʾ* and *mīm* in *ʿalayhim* — as though you said *You blessed them, not
wrath upon them*; or on the *istithnāʾ*, as though you said *except those who incurred wrath*; and the *naṣb*
by *aʿnī* is permissible, related from al-Khalīl.**" And "**the thirty-sixth** — the root of *al-ḍāllīn* is
*al-ḍāllilīn*: the vowel of the first *lām* was dropped, then the *lām* was merged into the *lām*, and two
vowelless letters met — the *alif*'s prolongation and the merged *lām*; and **Ayyūb al-Sakhtiyānī read *wa-lā
l-ḍaʾlīn* with an unprolonged *hamzah***, as though he fled from the meeting of the two vowelless ones — and
it is a dialect. Abū Zayd related: I heard **ʿAmr b. ʿUbayd** recite *fa-yawmaʾidhin lā yusʾalu ʿan dhanbihi
insun wa-lā jāʾinn* (55:39) and I thought he had erred, until I heard from the Arabs *daʾabbah* and
*shaʾabbah*."

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The thirty-fifth** — *lā* in His word *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*: it was differed over. **It was
said: it is redundant (*zāʾidah*) — said by al-Ṭabarī** (*wa-minhu* His word *mā manaʿaka allā tasjuda*,
7:12). **And it was said: it is an emphasis, entering lest it be imagined that *al-ḍāllīn* is coordinated on
*alladhīna*** (related by Makkī and al-Mahdawī). **And the Kūfans said: *lā* is in the sense of *ghayr*, and
it is the reading of ʿUmar and Ubayy** — as has passed." And: "on *al-ghaḍab*: *ghaḍiba ʿalayhim* is the
sense, and in the language *al-ghaḍab* is **severity** (*al-shiddah*): a man *ghaḍūb*, of severe temper;
*al-ghaḍūb* is the malignant serpent for its severity; *al-ghaḍbah* is a shield of camel hide folded one part
on another, named for its hardness. **And the meaning of *al-ghaḍab* in Allah's attribute is the will of
punishment (*irādat al-ʿuqūbah*), so it is an attribute of the Essence** — and Allah's will is of His
attributes of Essence — **or the punishment itself, and from it the ḥadīth: *charity extinguishes the wrath
of the Lord*, so it is an attribute of act.**" And: "**the thirty-third** — *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*: *al-ḍalāl* in
the speech of the Arabs is **the going away from the line of the aim and the road of truth** — from it *ḍalla
l-labanu fī l-māʾ*, the milk disappeared in the water; and *a-idhā ḍalalnā fī l-arḍ* (32:10), that is, we
disappeared in death and became dust (the poet: *a-lam tasʾal fa-tukhbiraka l-diyāru / ʿani l-ḥayyi
l-muḍallali ayna sārū*); and *al-ḍuḍalah* is a smooth stone turned over by the water in the valley; and
*al-ghaḍbah* likewise is a rock in the mountain of a colour different from it (*aw ghaḍbatun fī haḍbatin mā
amnaʿā*)."
And "**the thirty-second** — *ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*: they differed over who the
*maghḍūb ʿalayhim* and the *ḍāllīn* are; **the majority: the *maghḍūb ʿalayhim* are the Jews and the
*ḍāllīn* the Christians, and this came explained from the Prophet ﷺ in the ḥadīth of ʿAdī b. Ḥātim and the
story of his Islam** (Abū Dāwūd al-Ṭayālisī, Tirmidhī)" — and the Qurʾānic witness for it (V4 above). "And
it was said: *al-maghḍūb ʿalayhim* the idolaters and *al-ḍāllīn* the hypocrites; and it was said: *al-maghḍūb
ʿalayhim* is the one who drops the obligation of this sūrah in prayer, and *al-ḍāllīn* from its blessing —
related by al-Sulamī in *Ḥaqāʾiq* and al-Māwardī in his *Tafsīr*, **and it is nothing**; **al-Māwardī said:
this face is rejected, because what the reports contradict and the *āthār* oppose and disagreement spreads
over, this ruling may not be applied to it.** And it was said: *al-maghḍūb ʿalayhim* by following innovations
and *al-ḍāllīn* from the ways of guidance. **I say: this is good, but the Prophet's ﷺ explanation is higher
and better.**"

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The thirty-first** — **in this verse is a refutation of the *Qadariyyah*, the
*Muʿtazilah* and the *Imāmiyyah*, because they hold that the will of man is sufficient for the issuing of his
acts from him — be it obedience or disobedience — since with them man is the creator of his acts, so he has
no need, in their issuing from him, of his Lord. And Allah has declared them liars in this verse, since they
asked Him for guidance to the straight path: were the affair theirs and the choice in their hands without
their Lord, they would not have asked Him for guidance, nor repeated the request in every prayer** — and
likewise their humbling themselves to Him in the warding off of the disliked, which is what contradicts
guidance, when they said *ṣirāṭa lladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*: as they
asked Him to guide them, they asked Him not to lead them astray; and likewise they supplicate, saying *rabbanā
lā tuzigh qulūbanā baʿda idh hadaytanā* (3:8)."

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "The thirty-second" above carries the author's *ādāb* ruling on the reported view that
identifies these two groups with those who neglect the sūrah: **"it is nothing"**, and al-Māwardī: "this
face is rejected, because what reports contradict, the ruling may not be applied to it" — and al-Qurṭubī:
"this is good, but the Prophet's explanation is better."

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ) — the *Āmīn*, the third *bāb*

**Al-Qurṭubī.** "**The first** — it is *sunnah* for the reciter of the Qurʾān, after finishing the Fātiḥah,
after **a pause on the *nūn* of *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*, to say *Āmīn* — so that what is Qurʾān be distinguished
from what is not.** **The second** — it is established in the *Ummahāt* from the ḥadīth of Abū Hurayrah that
the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **When the imam says *Āmīn*, say *Āmīn* — for whoever's *Āmīn* agrees with the
*Āmīn* of the angels, his previous sins are forgiven**" — "our scholars said: forgiveness is arranged upon
four premisses contained in this ḥadīth: the imam's *Āmīn*; the *Āmīn* of those behind him; the *Āmīn* of the
angels; and the agreement of the *Āmīn* — it is said in the answer, and it is said in the time, and it is
said in the quality, of sincere supplication, by his saying ﷺ: *Call upon Allah while certain of the answer,
and know that Allah does not answer a supplication from a heedless, inattentive heart*."
"**The third** — Abū Dāwūd from **Abū Muṣabbiḥ al-Muqrāʾī**: 'we used to sit with **Abū Zuhayr al-Numayrī**,
a Companion, and he would tell the best of ḥadīth; and when one of us supplicated he said: *Seal it with
*Āmīn*, for *Āmīn* is like the seal on the page.*' Abū Zuhayr said: 'Shall I not tell you of that? We went
out with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ one night and came upon a man who was importunate in asking, and the
Prophet ﷺ stood listening to him and said: ***He has made it binding, if he seals***. A man of the people
said: *With what should he seal?* He said: ***With*** *Āmīn* — for if he seals with* Āmīn, *he has made it
binding***.'… And **Wahb b. Munabbih**: '*Āmīn* is four letters; Allah creates from every letter an angel
saying: *O Allah, forgive the one who says* Āmīn*.* And in the report: '*Jibrīl dictated to me* Āmīn *at the
end of Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and said: it is like the seal on the book*'; and in another ḥadīth: ***Āmīn* is the
seal of the Lord of the worlds**'; and another: ***Āmīn is a degree in the Garden***; the meaning: a letter by
which its sayer earns a degree in the Garden (Abū Bakr al-Harawī's explanation: 'it is Allah's stamp on His
servants, since by it He repels from them the afflictions and calamities, so it is like the seal of the book
that preserves it')."
"**The fourth** — **the meaning of *Āmīn* with most of the people of knowledge is: *O Allah, answer us*
(*allāhumma stajib lanā*)**, put in the place of supplication; and some said it is one of Allah's names —
related from Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad, Mujāhid and Hilāl b. Yasāf, and Ibn ʿAbbās related it from the Prophet ﷺ
**and it is not established** (Ibn al-ʿArabī); and it was said its meaning is *thus let it be* (al-Jawharī);
and al-Kalbī from Abū Ṣāliḥ from Ibn ʿAbbās: "I asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ what *Āmīn* means, and he
said: ***O Lord, do***"; and Muqātil: "it is a strength for supplication and a drawing down of blessing";
and Tirmidhī: "its meaning is **do not disappoint our hope**."
"**The fifth** — *Āmīn* has two dialects: the lengthened (*madd*), on the pattern *fāʿīl*, like *yāsīn*; and
the shortened, on the pattern *yamīn*" — with the poets: *yā rabbi lā taslubnī ḥubbahā abadan / wa-yarḥamu
llāhu ʿabdan qāla āmīnā* and *āmīna āmīna lā arḍā bi-wāḥidatin / ḥattā uballighahā alfayni āmīnā*; and the
short: *tabāʿada minnī Fuṭḥulu idh saʾaltuhu / amīna fa-zāda llāhu mā baynanā buʿdā*; "**and *shaddah* of
the *mīm* is an error** (al-Jawharī) — though it is related from al-Ḥasan and Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq, and it is the
saying of al-Ḥusayn b. al-Faḍl, from *amma* when he aimed — *we are aimers toward You* — as in *wa-lā
āmmīna l-bayta l-ḥarām* (5:2); and al-Jawharī: it is built on the *fatḥah*, like *ayna* and *kayfa*."
"**The sixth** — the scholars differed whether the imam says it and whether he says it aloud. **Al-Shāfiʿī,
and Mālik in the report of the Madinans, held he says it aloud**; **the Kūfans and some of the Madinans: he
does not say it aloud — the saying of al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Ḥabīb of our scholars; Ibn Bukayr: it is his
choice**; and Ibn al-Qāsim related from **Mālik that the imam does not say *Āmīn*, but those behind him say
it** — the saying of Ibn al-Qāsim and the Egyptians of Mālik's companions, whose proof is the ḥadīth of **Abū
Mūsā al-Ashʿarī**: "the Messenger of Allah ﷺ addressed us and explained our Sunnah and taught us our prayer,
saying: *When you pray, straighten your rows, then let one of you lead you; when he says* ghayri
l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn, *say* Āmīn, *and Allah will answer you*" (Muslim), and the like from
Sumayy from Abū Hurayrah (Mālik). **And the sound is the first, by the ḥadīth of Wāʾil b. Ḥujr**: "the
Messenger of Allah ﷺ, when he recited *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*, said *Āmīn*, **raising his voice with it** (Abū
Dāwūd, Dāraqutnī — and Abū Bakr said: 'this is a *sunnah* with which the people of Kūfah were alone; this is
sound'). And al-Bukhārī headed a chapter: *the imam's saying* Āmīn *aloud*. And ʿAṭāʾ said: '*Āmīn* is a
supplication; Ibn al-Zubayr would say *Āmīn* and those behind him until the mosque surged with it.' And
Tirmidhī: more than one of the people of knowledge among the Companions and those after them held that a man
raises his voice with *Āmīn*, not concealing it — and al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad and Isḥāq say it. And in the *Muwaṭṭaʾ*
and the two *Ṣaḥīḥs*, Ibn Shihāb: 'the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to say *Āmīn*.' And in the *Sunan* of Ibn
Mājah from Abū Hurayrah: '*The people have abandoned* Āmīn — and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, when he said
*ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*, said *Āmīn* until the people of the first row heard it and the
mosque echoed with it*.' And the ḥadīth of Abū Mūsā and Sumayy: their meaning is to inform of the place where
*Āmīn* is said, namely when the imam says *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*, so that the two sayings be together and they not
precede it with *Āmīn*, because of his saying ﷺ: *When the imam says* Āmīn, *say* Āmīn." And Ibn Nāfiʿ in
Ibn al-Ḥārith's book: "the follower does not say it unless he hears the imam say *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*; and if he
is far off and does not hear him, he does not say it"; Ibn ʿAbdūs: "he estimates the measure of the recitation
and says *Āmīn*."
"**The seventh** — the companions of **Abū Ḥanīfah** said: **concealing *Āmīn* is better than saying it
aloud**, because it is a supplication and Allah said *udʿū rabbakum taḍarruʿan wa-khufyatan* (7:55); and
their proof: what is related in the interpretation of His word *qad ujībat daʿwatukumā* (10:89) — "Mūsā used
to supplicate and Hārūn would say *Āmīn*, and Allah named them both suppliants." **The answer**: "concealing
supplication was better only because of what enters it of ostentation; but what pertains to the congregational
prayer is the witnessing of an outward rite, and the manifesting of a truth to which the servants are called
— and the imam was called to make manifest the recitation of the Fātiḥah, which comprises supplication and
*Āmīn* at its end; so when the supplication is of what *sunnah* has aloud, the *Āmīn* upon the supplication
follows it and runs its course — and this is clear."
"**The eighth** — **the word *Āmīn* was not before us except for Mūsā and Hārūn** (Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm in
*Nawādir al-Uṣūl* from Anas: the Prophet ﷺ said: *Allah has given my community three things not given to
anyone before them: the* salām — *which is the greeting of the people of the Garden; the rows of the angels;
and* Āmīn — *except what was from Mūsā and Hārūn*), its meaning: **Mūsā supplicated against Pharaoh and
Hārūn said *Āmīn*, and Allah said *qad ujībat daʿwatukumā*, and did not mention Hārūn's speech; and Mūsā
said *rabbanā*, and the *Āmīn* was from Hārūn, so He named him a suppliant in His revelation, since He made
that from him a supplication**." And: "it has been said that *Āmīn* is peculiar to this community, by what is
related from the Prophet ﷺ: *I have not envied you the Jews for anything as I envied you for* Āmīn" (Ibn
Mājah from Ḥammād b. Salamah from Suhayl b. Abī Ṣāliḥ from his father from ʿĀʾishah; and from Ibn ʿAbbās:
"*The Jews envied you for nothing as they envied you for* Āmīn, *so say* Āmīn *much*") — "**our scholars
said: the people of the Book envied us only because its beginning is praise of Allah and extolling Him, then
submission to Him and humbling before Him, then a supplication for us for guidance to the straight path,
then a supplication against them, with our saying *Āmīn*.**"
