# Tafsīr as-Saʿdī — chapter 1, al-Fātiḥah

**Source work:** *Taysīr al-Karīm al-Raḥmān fī tafsīr kalām al-Mannān* — ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Nāṣir al-Saʿdī (d. 1376/1956).

## Sūrah-level elements

### S2 · Period & place of revelation

**As-Saʿdī.** The sūrah is Makkan. *(Text: «وهي مكية».)*

### S5 · Central theme & summary of contents (maqāṣid)

**As-Saʿdī.** For all its brevity, this sūrah comprises what no other sūrah of the Qurʾān comprises. It contains:

* **The three kinds of divine oneness.** *Tawḥīd al-rubūbiyyah* — taken from "Lord of the worlds". *Tawḥīd al-ilāhiyyah*, the singling out of Allah in worship — taken from the name *Allāh* and from "You alone we worship and You alone we seek help". *Tawḥīd al-asmāʾ wa-l-ṣifāt*, affirming the attributes of perfection for Allah, which He affirmed for Himself and His Messenger affirmed for Him — without denial (*taʿṭīl*), without likening (*tamthīl*) and without comparison (*tashbīh*) — indicated by the word "all praise".
* **Prophethood**, in "Guide us to the straight path", since that is impossible without the message.
* **Recompense for deeds**, in "Master of the Day of Judgement", and that the recompense is just, since *al-dīn* means recompense in justice.
* **Divine decree (*qadar*)**, and that the servant is an agent in reality — against the Qadariyyah and the Jabriyyah.
* **The refutation of all people of innovation and error**, in "Guide us to the straight path", since it is knowledge of the truth and action upon it, and every innovator and every astray one is contrary to that.
* **Sincerity of the religion for Allah**, in worship and in seeking help, in "You alone we worship and You alone we seek help".

## 1:1 — بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* In the name of Allah, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**As-Saʿdī.** The sense is: *I begin with every name belonging to Allah*, since the word *ism* is a
singular in construct (*mufrad muḍāf*) and therefore covers all the most beautiful names. **Allāh** is
the one worshipped (*al-maʾlūh*), the one deserving to be singled out with worship, because He is
characterised by the attributes of divinity, which are the attributes of perfection. **al-Raḥmān,
al-Raḥīm** are two names indicating that He possesses the vast and great mercy that encompasses
everything and reaches every living being, and that He has written for the God-fearing who follow His
prophets and messengers: theirs is mercy unqualified, and all besides them have a share of it.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**As-Saʿdī.** The generality of the opening is carried by the grammar: the word *ism* is a **singular
in construct** (*mufrad muḍāf*), which is a form that is general in meaning — hence "with every name
belonging to Allah", not one name only. *(Text: «لفظ «اسم» مفرد مضاف، فيعم جميع الأسماء الحسنى».)* The
two names *al-Raḥmān* and *al-Raḥīm* are both built on the pattern of intensity and both indicate the
attribute of mercy, one being more emphatic than the other.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**As-Saʿdī.** Faith in Allah's names, His attributes and the rulings of the attributes is one of the
principles on which the salaf of the community and its imams agreed. They believe, for example, that He
is *Raḥmān Raḥīm*, possessing mercy that is an attribute of His and that relates to the one shown
mercy. All blessings are a trace of His mercy. And so it is with all the names: He is *al-ʿAlīm*,
knowledgeable, possessing knowledge by which He knows everything; *al-Qadīr*, capable, possessing power
by which He is able over everything.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V8, V11. Elements not listed are not addressed by this source for this verse._

## 1:2 — الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

*[context]* All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**As-Saʿdī.** *Al-ḥamd* is praise of Allah with the attributes of perfection and with His acts, which
move between grace and justice; all praise is His, complete, in every respect. *Rabb al-ʿālamīn*: the
*Rabb* is the one who nurtures all the worlds — and they are all that is besides Allah — by creating
them, preparing for them their means, and favouring them with great blessings which, if they lost them,
they could not continue. Whatever blessing they have is from Him.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**As-Saʿdī.** His nurture of creation is of two kinds:

* **General** — His creating them, providing for them, and guiding them to what serves their interests
  and preserves them in this world.
* **Special** — His nurture of His friends: He nurtures them with faith, grants them success in it,
  perfects them, and repels from them the hindrances that stand between them and Him. Its reality is
  the nurture of success in every good and protection from every evil.

This is likely the secret of why most of the prophets' supplications use the name *al-Rabb*: all their
requests fall under His special lordship. Thus "Lord of the worlds" proves His singleness in creation,
in governance and in blessing, the perfection of His self-sufficiency, and the total dependence of the
worlds on Him in every respect and consideration.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V11._

## 1:4 — مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

*[context]* Master of the Day of Judgement.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**As-Saʿdī.** *Al-Mālik* is the one characterised by the attribute of kingship, whose effects are that
He commands and forbids, rewards and punishes, and disposes of His possessions in every manner of
disposal. He attached kingship to the Day of Judgement — the day on which people are recompensed for
their deeds, the good and the evil.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**As-Saʿdī.** On that day the perfection of His kingship, His justice and His wisdom appears to creation
in full, and the ownership of creatures is cut off, so that kings and subjects, slaves and free are
equal in it: all submissive to His greatness, humble before His might, awaiting His recompense, hoping
His reward, fearing His punishment. For that reason He singled it out; otherwise He is Master of the Day
of Judgement and of every other day as well.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V11._

## 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

*[context]* You alone we worship, and You alone we seek help.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**As-Saʿdī.** The sense is: we single You out alone for worship and for seeking help. Since fronting the
object (*taqdīm al-maʿmūl*) yields exclusivity (*ḥaṣr*), it is as though he says: *We worship You and we
do not worship other than You; we seek help from You and we do not seek help from other than You.*
Placing worship before seeking help is of the order of the general before the specific, and out of
concern to put Allah's right before the servant's right.

*Definitions given by the author:* **worship** (*al-ʿibādah*) is a comprehensive term for everything
Allah loves and is pleased with, of sayings and deeds, outward and inward. **Seeking help**
(*al-istiʿānah*) is reliance upon Allah in bringing benefit and repelling harm, together with trust in
Him to accomplish it.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**As-Saʿdī.** The grammar carries the theology. Fronting the object before the verb — *iyyāka* before
*naʿbud* — yields **exclusivity** (*al-ḥaṣr*), which is the affirming of the ruling for the one
mentioned and its negation from all else. Placing worship before seeking help is a case of the general
before the specific (*taqdīm al-ʿāmm ʿalā al-khāṣṣ*).

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**As-Saʿdī.** Establishing the worship of Allah and seeking help from Him is the means to eternal
felicity and deliverance from all evils; there is no way to deliverance except by establishing both.
And worship is only worship when it is taken from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and intended for Allah's
face — by these two matters it becomes worship. Seeking help is mentioned after worship although it is
included in it because the servant needs Allah's help in all his acts of worship: if Allah does not help
him, he will not obtain what he wants of doing what is commanded and avoiding what is forbidden.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V8, V14._

## 1:6 — اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ

*[context]* Guide us to the straight path.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**As-Saʿdī.** The sense is: direct us, guide us and grant us success to the straight path — the clear
road that leads to Allah and to His Paradise, which is the knowledge of the truth and action upon it. So
we ask for guidance *to* the path and guidance *in* the path. **Guidance to the path** is adherence to
the religion of Islam and the abandonment of every other religion; **guidance in the path** includes
guidance in all the details of the religion, in knowledge and in deed.

### V10 · Juristic rulings (aḥkām)

**As-Saʿdī.** This supplication is among the most comprehensive and most beneficial of supplications for
the servant; and for that reason it is obligatory for a person to ask Allah by it in every cycle (*rakʿah*)
of his prayer, because of his need for it.

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**As-Saʿdī.** "Guide us to the straight path" is one of the most comprehensive of supplications and the
most beneficial of them for the servant — it gathers the request of guidance in knowledge and in deed,
in the root and in the branches.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V10, V17._

## 1:7 — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

*[context]* The path of those whom You have blessed — not of those who incurred wrath, nor of those who are astray.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**As-Saʿdī.** *The path of those whom You have blessed* — among the prophets, the truthful
(*al-ṣiddīqīn*), the martyrs and the righteous. *Not* the path of *those who incurred wrath* — those who
knew the truth and abandoned it, like the Jews and their like. Nor the path of *those who are astray* —
those who abandoned the truth out of ignorance and misguidance, like the Christians and their like.

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**As-Saʿdī.** The two closing clauses name the two diseases of the religious life: the one who knows
and abandons is the object of wrath, and the one who abandons out of ignorance and misguidance is the
one astray. Deliverance lies in knowledge joined to action, which is the straight path of 1:6 itself.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V15._
