# Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī — chapter 1, al-Fātiḥah

**Source work:** *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān* — Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī (d. 310/923); in the corpus file the text is given with the editor's footnotes, from which the chain-appraisals below are drawn.
**Corpus file:** `tafsir-al-tabari/001.txt` (Arabic, with numbered reports).
**Extraction:** English; the wording of the Qurʾānic clause, the ḥadīth matn, the transmitted report and the verse of poetry is kept verbatim, in quotation, with its number and chain; al-Ṭabarī's own argument is condensed to its essential claim.
**Elements found:** S5 · V1, V2, V4, V5, V6, V7, V8, V9, V10, V11, V13, V14, V17.

> **Structural note (applies to this file only).** (1) This work is an isnād-tafsīr: almost every exposition
> is carried by a chain, so the chains are given with the reports and the corpus editor's appraisal of a
> weak chain is recorded as such — the record distinguishes what al-Ṭabarī transmits from what he argues.
> (2) The corpus file has **no sūrah-level introduction** (no names, no Makkī/Madanī, no verse count); the
> one sūrah-level matter it contains — the argument over why al-Fātiḥah says in seven verses what two
> verses could carry — is recorded at the end, under S5. (3) Al-Ṭabarī's ruling that the *basmalah* is not
> a verse of al-Fātiḥah is a premise of his commentary on 1:3; it is recorded under 1:3, V2, where he uses it.

## 1:1 — بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* In the name of Allah, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The exposition of the basmalah is built on what the preposition *bāʾ* requires: “the *bāʾ* in
‘*bismi llāh*’ requires a verb which it brings in, and there is no verb present with it” — but the hearer
needs no verb, because the speaker's purpose is known from the act he is beginning. Hence “the sense of a
man's words, when he says ‘*bismi llāh*’ and then begins to recite a sūrah, is *I recite with the naming of
Allah*”; and so at rising or sitting, “*I rise with the naming of Allah, I sit with the naming of Allah*”.
He grounds this in the report of Ibn ʿAbbās (no. 138, and again no. 139):

> **Ibn ʿAbbās** — Abū Kurayb → ʿUthmān b. Saʿīd → Bishr b. ʿUmāra → Abū Rawq → al-Ḍaḥḥāk → Ibn ʿAbbās:
> “The first thing Jibrīl brought down to Muḥammad was: *O Muḥammad, say: I seek refuge in the Hearing,
> the Knowing, from Satan the accursed*; then he said: *Say: In the name of Allah, the Most Compassionate,
> the Most Merciful*. Jibrīl said to him: Say *bismi llāh*, O Muḥammad — that is, *recite with the mention
> of Allah your Lord, and rise and sit with the mention of Allah*.”
> _(Corpus editor's appraisal: this chain — Bishr b. ʿUmāra → Abū Rawq → al-Ḍaḥḥāk → Ibn ʿAbbās — is weak,
> as he states at no. 137 and repeats throughout.)_

He then sharpens the point against the objection that one should rather say “*bi-llāhi l-Raḥmāni
l-Raḥīm*”: the sense is not “I rise *by* Allah” but “I begin *with the naming of* Allah and His mention
before everything”, since “the servants were commanded to begin their affairs with the *naming* of Allah,
not with reporting of His greatness and His attributes, as they were commanded regarding slaughter,
hunting, eating, drinking and the rest of their acts”. He clinches it with the consensus: “there is no
disagreement among all the scholars of the community that if a man said at his slaughter ‘*bi-llāh*’ and
did not say ‘*bismi llāh*’, he has left what is prescribed for him at the slaughter” — which proves that
“*bismi llāh*” does not mean “*bi-llāh*”, nor is the name identical with the Named.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Three grammatical matters are argued at length.

* **The ellipsis (*ḥadhf*) of the governing verb.** The *bāʾ* requires a verb; the verb is dropped because
  the situation supplies it — as in the poets' ellipses: *“w-aʿlamu annanī sa-akūnu ramsan — idhā sāra
  l-nawāʿiju lā yasīru / fa-qāla l-sāʾilūna li-man ḥafartum? — fa-qāla l-mukhbirūna lahum: wazīru”*
  (“…they said: a *wazīr*” — the word “dead man” being dropped), and *“wa-raʾayti zawjaki fī l-waghā
  mutaqallidan sayfan wa-rumḥā”* (“girded with a sword and a spear” — for “carrying a spear”, since a spear
  is not girded).
* **“Name” standing for “naming”.** The Arabs put a noun in place of a *maṣdar*: “*akramtu fulānan
  karāmatan*”, “*kallamtuhu kalāman*” (for *taklīman*), “*aʿṭāhu l-miʾata l-ritāʿā*” for *iʿṭāʾaka* — with
  the Lakhmid/Ṭāʾī verses adduced. On that model, *ism* in *bismi llāh* stands for the *maṣdar* *tasmiyah*
  (“naming”): the sense is “I begin with the naming of Allah before my act or my speech.”
* **The derivation of *Allāh*.** “*Allāh* is, in the speech of the Arabs, originally *al-Ilāh*”: the *hamzah*
  which is the *fāʾ* of the word is dropped, the *lām* which is the *ʿayn* meets the added *lām*, and the two
  are merged — “just as ”*lākin huwa llāhu rabbī*“ (18:38) is originally ”*lākin anā*“”, with the verse
  *“wa-tarmīnanī bi-l-ṭarfi ay anta mudhnibun / wa-taqḷīnanī lākinna iyyāki lā aqlī”* as the parallel. The
  **qirāʾah** of 7:127 is adduced for the root: “Ibn ʿAbbās read ”*wa-yadharaka wa-ilāhata-ka*“ — *your
  worship*” (no. 142), and “it is related that he said: *he was worshipped and did not worship*” (no. 143);
  Mujāhid likewise glossed it *wa-ʿibādata-ka*, “your worship” (no. 144). Al-Ṭabarī concludes: “*ilāhah* is
  the *maṣdar* of ”*alaha llāha fulānun ilāhatan*“, as one says ”*ʿabada llāha ʿibādatan*“”. _(Corpus
  editor's appraisal: nos. 142–143 are weak through the shaykh of al-Ṭabarī in them, Sufyān b. Wakīʿ; and
  the reading *wa-ilāhata-ka* is a shādhdh reading, the well-known reading being_ wa-ālihataka _.)_

He declines the wider question: “this is not the place to expand on whether the name is the named or other
than it, or an attribute of Him.”

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Al-Ṭabarī** (no. 140) — Ismāʿīl b. al-Faḍl → Ibrāhīm b. al-ʿAlāʾ → Ismāʿīl b. ʿAyyāsh → Ismāʿīl b.
Yaḥyā → Ibn Abī Mulaykah → one who told him → Ibn Masʿūd; and Misʿar b. Kidām → ʿAṭiyyah → Abū Saʿīd:
the Prophet ﷺ said: “*ʿĪsā ibn Maryam was handed by his mother to the *kuttāb* to teach him, and the teacher
said to him: Write *bism*. ʿĪsā said to him: And what is *bism*? The teacher said: I do not know. ʿĪsā
said: The *bāʾ* is the splendour (bahāʾ) of Allah, the *sīn* is His eminence (sanāʾuhu), and the *mīm* is
His dominion (mamlakatuhu).*” Al-Ṭabarī's own verdict: “I fear it is an error of the transmitter, and that
he meant *b-s-m* as children are taught the *abjad* letters… for it has no meaning when *bismi llāhi
l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is recited as the reciter of the Book of Allah recites it.” _(Corpus editor's appraisal:
“this is a fabricated ḥadīth with no basis”, entered through Ismāʿīl b. Yaḥyā, whom the critics declared a
liar; the same chain carries no. 145 on the word of ʿĪsā: “Allah is the God of gods”.)_

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Under “the meaning of *Allāh*”: “it is on the sense of what is related to us from ʿAbd
Allāh b. ʿAbbās — *He whom everything worships and serves* (no. 141: ”*Allāh* is the Possessor of divinity
and of being-worshipped by His creation, all of them“; chain weak, as above). And the doctrine he draws from
the name: “*al-ulūhiyyah* is not for other than Him, in any respect, neither in being-named by it nor in
meaning… and the taking of the name has been forbidden by Allah: so naught is worshipped but He.”

*(The exposition of “al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm” belongs to the same verse in this work; it is recorded under 1:3,
where al-Ṭabarī's own text treats the two names — he explains them once and refers back.)*

_Coverage: V1, V2, V5, V8, V11._

## 1:2 — الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

*[context]* All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The sense: “*al-ḥamdu li-llāh* is thanks rendered purely to Allah, without any of the things
worshipped besides Him and without any of what He created, for the blessings He has bestowed on His
servants — blessings that no number can count, in fitting the instruments for His obedience, enabling the
limbs of those under obligation to perform His ordinances, and with the provision He spread for them in
their world and the ease of living He fed them with, without their deserving it of Him, and with what He
alerted them to and called them to of the means leading to lasting immortality in the abode of enduring
bliss. So to our Lord is all praise for that, first and last.” The reports he cites for it: **Ibn ʿAbbās**
(no. 151, weak chain): “*al-ḥamdu li-llāh* is thanks to Allah, abasement before Allah, and the
acknowledgement of His blessing and His guidance and His beginning”; **the Prophet ﷺ** through al-Ḥakam b.
ʿUmayr (no. 152, chain declared by the corpus editor as not standing at all): “*When you say,* al-ḥamdu
lillāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn, *you have thanked Allah and He will grant you more*”; **Kaʿb al-Aḥbār** (no. 153,
sound chain but reaching Kaʿb): “*Whoever says* al-ḥamdu lillāh, *that is praise of Allah*”; and
**al-Aswad b. Sarīʿ** (no. 154, sound chain, and Aḥmad's *Musnad* carries it with a sound chain):
“*Nothing is more beloved to Him than praise, from Allah; and therefore He praised Himself, saying* al-ḥamdu
lillāh.” The distinction he reports: “it has been said that the speaker's words *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* are
praise of Allah by His names and His most beautiful attributes, while his words *al-shukru li-llāh* are
praise of Him by His blessings and His favours” — and Kaʿb is said to have said *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* is praise
of Allah, without making clear which of the two senses.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Four arguments belong here:

* **“Praise” and “thanks” are interchangeable (*al-ḥamd* and *al-shukr*).** The proof: all the Arabs
  accept “*al-ḥamdu li-llāh shukran*”, which would be impossible “if praise were not of the meaning of
  thanks and thanks not of the meaning of praise”.
* **The definite article.** “There is a meaning in entering the *alif* and *lām* on *al-ḥamd* that is not
  conveyed by ‘*ḥamdan*’”: the *al-* tells “all praise and complete thanks belong to Allah”; without it the
  word would indicate only the speaker's own praise of Allah. Hence “the reading of the reciters and the
  scholars of the community has followed one another in raising *al-ḥamd* — *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* — and not in
  putting it in the accusative”. And on that: “were a reciter to read it in the accusative, the sense would,
  in my view, be absurd, and he would deserve punishment for reciting it so, if he did it deliberately,
  knowing his error and the corruption of its sense.”
* **The implied “say”.** To the objection — God praised Himself, so how is the rest His speech, and how does
  *iyyāka naʿbudu* belong to Him? — al-Ṭabarī answers: “it is all the speech of Allah; but He praised
  Himself with what He is worthy of, then taught that to His servants and made its recitation obligatory on
  them, as a test and a trial, saying to them: *Say* *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*, and *Say* *iyyāka
  naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*.” The omitted word “*qūlū*” (“say”) is supplied by the same principle of
  ellipsis he established at 1:1, with the poets' lines again as evidence.
* **The meaning and scope of *al-Rabb*.** “Concerning *rabb*: in the speech of the Arabs it is applied to
  meanings — *the obeyed master* (as Labīd: *wa-ahlakna yawman rabba Kindata wa-bnahu / wa-rabba Maʿaddin
  bayna khabtin wa-ʿarʿar*), *the man who sets a thing right* (as al-Farazdaq: *kānū ka-sāliʾatin
  ḥamqāʾa idh ḥaqanat / silāʾahā fī adīmin ghayri marbūb* — “in a hide not put right”), and *the owner of a
  thing* (as ʿAlqamah: *fa-kunta mraʾan afḍat ilayka ribābatī / wa-qablaka rabbatnī fa-ḍiʿtu rubūb*).” And
  with these three, “our Lord is the Master who has no like or peer in His mastery, the One who sets the
  affair of His creation right by what He has lavished on them, and the Owner to whom belong creation and
  command”. The report of **Ibn ʿAbbās** (no. 155, weak chain, through Jibrīl): “*Say*: praise be to Allah,
  to whom belongs all creation — the heavens, all of them and all that is in them, and the earths, all of
  them and all that is in them and what is between them, of what is known and what is not known. Know, O
  Muḥammad, that this your Lord is One whom nothing resembles.*”

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** On *al-ʿālamīn*, he sets out the term and then the reports. “*Al-ʿālamīn* is the plural of
*ʿālam*; and *ʿālam* is a plural which has no singular from its own form, like *al-anām*, *al-rahṭ* and
*al-jaysh*… It is a name for the kinds of peoples: every kind of them is an *ʿālam*, and the people of every
generation of every kind is the *ʿālam* of that generation and that time. So mankind is an *ʿālam*, and the
jinn are an *ʿālam*, and likewise all the races of creation.” The reports:

* **Ibn ʿAbbās** (no. 156, weak chain): “All praise belongs to Allah, to whom belongs all creation — the
  heavens and the earths and those in them and what is between them, of what is known and what is not.”
* **ʿIkrimah from Ibn ʿAbbās** (no. 157): “the jinn and mankind”. **Saʿīd b. Jubayr** (no. 159): “the jinn
  and mankind”. **Ibn ʿAbbās** through Saʿīd b. Jubayr (no. 158): “the Lord of the jinn and mankind”.
  **Saʿīd b. Jubayr** (no. 160): “the son of Adam, and the jinn and mankind — every community of them an
  *ʿālam* on its own”. **Mujāhid** (nos. 161–162): “mankind and the jinn”. **Qatādah** (no. 163): “every
  species is an *ʿālam*”. **Ibn Jurayj** (no. 165): “the jinn and mankind”.
* **Abū al-ʿĀliyah** (no. 164): “Mankind is an *ʿālam*, and the jinn are an *ʿālam*, and besides that
  eighteen thousand *ʿālam* — or fourteen thousand, he was in doubt — of the angels on the earth; and the
  earth has four corners, in each corner three thousand five hundred *ʿālam*, created for His worship.”

Al-Ṭabarī's judgement: “this is the saying maintained; it is the saying of Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd b. Jubayr,
and it is the sense of the generality of the exegetes.”

_Coverage: V1, V2, V7, V8._

## 1:3 — الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* The Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Two matters: the two names are expounded once, in the basmalah, and referred to here — “the
explanation of *al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm* has already passed, in the explanation of *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni
l-Raḥīm*, and that suffices from repeating it in this place.” And the second, which is a *naẓm* argument
that carries his ruling: “we had no need to explain the reason for its repetition here, **because we do not
hold that *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is a verse of al-Fātiḥah** — for then a questioner could ask:
what is the reason for repeating it here, when Allah had already described Himself by it in the basmalah,
so near in place? Rather, that is for us a proof against the claim of the one who claims the basmalah is a
verse of Fātiḥat al-Kitāb: for were it so, it would be a repetition of one verse, with one wording and one
meaning, twice with no separator between them — **and there is not found in any part of the Book of Allah
two adjacent verses repeated with one wording and one meaning with no separating words.**” Then his report
of a further position: “a group of the people of explanation denied that, and said the phrase is of the
**postponed which has the sense of the placed-first** (*al-muʾakhkhar alladhī maʿnāhu l-taqdīm*), and that
the order is: *Praise be to Allah, the Compassionate, the Merciful, Lord of the worlds, Master of the Day
of Judgement*” — their argument being that *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* goes with *Malik yawm al-dīn* (the two
statements of ownership), while the two names of mercy go with the praise; and they supported it with the
Qurʾānic parallel of 18:1, *al-ḥamdu li-llāhī lladhī anzala ʿalā ʿabdihi l-kitāba wa-lam yajʿal lahu
ʿiwajan qayyiman*, where *qayyiman* belongs with *anzala* although placed at the end.

_(This is the record's clearest case of an element carried by another: al-Ṭabarī's explanation of_
al-Raḥmān _and_ al-Raḥīm _is his basmalah exposition, and is therefore filed with 1:1 below.)_

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah) — the two names, filed here by the author's own cross-reference

**Al-Ṭabarī.**

* **The patterns.** “*Al-Raḥmān* is *faʿlān* from *raḥima*, and *al-Raḥīm* is *faʿīl* from it. The Arabs very
  often build names from *faʿila yafʿalu* on *faʿlān* — as from *ghaḍiba* → *ghaḍbān*, from *sakira* →
  *sakrān*, from *ʿaṭisha* → *ʿaṭshān*. So *Raḥmān* from *raḥima*… And *Raḥīm* was said, although the *ʿayn*
  of *faʿila* is vowelled with *kasrah*, because it is praise; for the Arabs carry the patterns of names, when
  there is praise or blame in them, to *faʿīl*, as from *ʿalima* → *ʿālim* and *ʿalīm*, from *qadara* →
  *qādir* and *qadīr*.” Had the two names departed from the pattern of their verbs, “their form would have
  been *al-Rāḥim*”.
* **Which is the stronger.** “There is no disagreement among those who know the languages of the Arabs that
  the speaker's word *al-Raḥmān* — among the patterns of names from *faʿila yafʿalu* — is further removed
  from its origin (*ashaddu ʿudūlan*) than his word *al-Raḥīm*; and that every name which has an origin in
  *faʿila yafʿalu* and is then further removed from it denotes a stronger praise in its bearer than one built
  on its origin, when the naming is praise or blame. This, then, is what *al-Raḥmān* has over *al-Raḥīm* in
  the language.”
* **What each name carries.** After the reports (below), his own conclusion: “the meaning in naming Allah
  *al-Raḥmān*, beyond that in naming Him *al-Raḥīm*, is that by *al-Raḥmān* He is described with the
  **generality** of mercy to all His creation, and by *al-Raḥīm* with the **particularity** of mercy to some
  of His creation… Our Lord is therefore *Raḥmān* to all His creation in this world and the next, and *Raḥīm*
  to the believers in particular in this world and the next.” The general mercy of this world he lists as
  “the spreading of provision, the subjection of the clouds with rain, the bringing forth of plants from the
  earth, the soundness of bodies and minds, and the rest of the blessings that cannot be counted, in which
  believers and unbelievers share” — with 14:34 and 16:18, *wa-in taʿuddū niʿmata llāhi lā tuḥṣūhā*. The
  general mercy of the next world he states as “His levelling between them all in His justice and His
  judgement, so that He wrongs none of them by the weight of an atom” — with *wa-tuwaffā kullu nafsin mā
  kasabat*. The particular mercy is 33:43, *wa-kāna bi-l-muʾminīna raḥīman*.
* **The order of the three names.** “It is the habit of the Arabs, when they intend to report of one of whom
  they speak, to put his name first and then follow it with his attributes and descriptions… And since Allah
  has names He has forbidden His creation to take — namely *Allāh*, *al-Raḥmān*, *al-Khāliq* — and names He
  has permitted them to use of one another, such as *al-Raḥīm*, *al-Samīʿ*, *al-Baṣīr*, *al-Karīm* — the
  due order was that His names peculiar to Him be placed first, so that the hearer knows to whom the praise
  is directed; then the names by which others may be named follow.” The proof: 17:110, *quli dʿū llāha awi
  dʿū l-Raḥmāna ayyamā tadʿū fa-lahu l-asmāʾu l-ḥusnā*, and 25:60, the idolaters' *wa-mā l-Raḥmānu
  a-nasjudu limā taʾmurunā*.

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The transmitted explanations of the pair:

* **Al-ʿArzamī** (no. 146; the corpus editor notes him as very weak, and that Aḥmad said “his ḥadīth is
  worth nothing”): “*al-Raḥmān* to all creation, *al-Raḥīm* to the believers.”
* **The Prophet ﷺ**, through the chain of Ismāʿīl b. Yaḥyā (no. 147; editor: weak, indeed two weak chains):
  “ʿĪsā ibn Maryam said: *al-Raḥmān* is the Raḥmān of the next world and this world, and *al-Raḥīm* is the
  Raḥīm of the next world.”
* **Ibn ʿAbbās** (no. 148, weak chain): “*al-Raḥmān*, *al-Raḥīm*: the tender, the gentle to the one he loves
  to show mercy to, and the far, the severe to the one he loves to treat harshly — and so all His names.”
* **ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī** (no. 149, chain sound to ʿAṭāʾ): “He was *al-Raḥmān*; and when *al-Raḥmān* was
  cut off from His name, He was *al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*.” Al-Ṭabarī explains: “what ʿAṭāʾ meant is that
  *al-Raḥmān* was one of Allah's names by which no one of His creation named himself — and when the liar
  Musaylimah named himself by it (that is his cutting it off from His names for himself), Allah informed His
  servants that His name is *al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*, so as to distinguish His name from the name of him who had
  named himself with His names… for no one is named *Raḥmān Raḥīm*, joining the two names, but He.”
* **Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī** (no. 150, sound chain): “*al-Raḥmān* is a **forbidden name** (*ismun mamnūʿ*)” —
  that is, forbidden to creatures.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Two polemics are joined here, both of them creedal:

* **Against those who claim the Arabs did not know *al-Raḥmān*.** “Certain people of dullness claimed that
  the Arabs did not know *al-Raḥmān* and that it was not in their language, and for that the idolaters said
  to the Prophet ﷺ, *wa-mā l-Raḥmānu a-nasjudu limā taʾmurunā* (25:60), rejecting the name.” His answer
  turns on 2:146, *alladhīna ātaynāhumu l-kitāba yaʿrifūnahu kamā yaʿrifūna abnāʾahum*, and on pre-Islamic
  poetry that uses the name: *“a-lā ḍarabat tilka l-fatātu hajīnahā / a-lā qaḍaba l-Raḥmānu rabbī
  yamīnahā”*, and Salāmah b. Jandal: *“ʿajiltum ʿalaynā ʿajlataynā ʿalaykumū / wa-mā yashaʾi l-Raḥmānu
  yaʿqid wa-yuṭliq”*. _(The corpus editor adds a long note contesting the attribution of the first line.)_
* **Against the claim that *al-Raḥmān* is “of the same meaning” as *al-Raḥīm* in a loose way** — “*dhu
  l-raḥmah*” and “*al-rāḥim*” — as one says *nadmān* and *nadīm* (with Burj b. Mushir's line *wa-nadmānin
  yazīdu l-kaʾsa ṭīban*). Al-Ṭabarī: “there is no doubt that *dhū l-raḥmah* is the one for whom mercy is
  established as an attribute, while *al-rāḥim* is the one described as about to be merciful, or as having
  shown it and finished, or as in the act of it” — the two are not interchangeable, and the analogy of
  *nadmān/nadīm* does not carry.

### V13 · History, sīrah & parables

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The historical anchor of the third report above: “when the liar Musaylimah named himself the
*Raḥmān* of Yamāmah” — the incident by which ʿAṭāʾ's report is explained, and which al-Ṭabarī also uses in
1:1's creedal discussion.

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The names are opened to the servant's use as invocation: “*al-Raḥmān*” is a name no creature
may take, so that “no one is named *Raḥmān Raḥīm* — the two together — but He”; and ʿAṭāʾ's report is read
as the reason the two are joined in the basmalah, “so that His servants may know by the two together that He
is the One intended by them, apart from any other”.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V6, V8, V11, V13, V17._

## 1:4 — مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

*[context]* Master of the Day of Judgement.

### V9 · Variant readings (qirāʾāt)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** “The reciters differ in the recitation of *māliki yawmi l-dīn*: some recite *maliki yawmi
l-dīn*, some recite *māliki yawmi l-dīn*, and some recite *mālika yawmi l-dīn* with *naṣb* of the *kāf*.”
He refers the detail to his *Kitāb al-Qirāʾāt* and gives the meaning of each: “there is no disagreement
among those who know the languages of the Arabs that *malik* is derived from *al-mulk* (sovereignty) and
*mālik* is taken from *al-milk* (ownership).” The *malik* reading means “sovereignty on the Day of
Judgement belongs to Allah alone, without all His creation who had been kings and tyrants in this world,
contending with Him”; the *mālik* reading means “no one has any judgement with Him on that day as they had
in this world” (no. 166, Ibn ʿAbbās, weak chain), supported by 40:16, 78:38, 20:108, 21:28. On the *naṣb*
reading: it is read as a vocative — “*yā mālika yawmi l-dīn*, *iyyāka naʿbudu*”, the *kāf* put in the
accusative by the intention of calling, as in 12:29, *yūsufu aʿriḍ ʿan hādhā*, and as the poets address
*Jazʾ* and *Banī Shāb* with the vocative particle dropped. And his verdict: “the reading *mālika yawmi
l-dīn* is **forbidden, not permitted**, by the consensus of all the authoritative reciters and scholars of
the community upon rejecting recitation by it.”

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** “The more fitting of the two interpretations to the verse, and the sounder of the two
readings in recitation in my view, is the first — the reading of the one who reads *malik*, in the sense of
*al-mulk*. For in the acknowledgement of His exclusivity in sovereignty there is necessarily His
exclusivity in ownership, and the excellence of the addition of ownership over the owner, since it is known
that there is no king but an owner, while there may be an owner who is not a king.” And the *naẓm* argument
for it: “Allah had informed His servants in the verse before — *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn,
al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* — that He is the owner and master and reformer and caretaker of all the worlds; so the
worthiest attribute to follow that is what is not contained in *Rabb al-ʿālamīn al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*: namely
His being described as the **King**.” He then refutes at length the view that *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* speaks only
of this world — “had that been so, all the worlds of the age of the Prophet ﷺ would be included and no
other; and 45:16, *wa-faḍḍalnāhum ʿalā l-ʿālamīn*, with 3:110, *kuntum khayra ummatin ukhrijat li-l-nās*,
shows that the *ʿālam* of every age is other than the *ʿālam* of the age before and after it.”

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Besides the derivation of *malik*/*mālik* and the vocative construction (*al-naṣb bi-niyyati
l-nidāʾ*), the section contains his long treatment of the return from third-person description to direct
address at *iyyāka naʿbudu*: “had he known the explanation of the beginning of the sūrah — that *al-ḥamdu
li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* is Allah's command to His servant to say these words… and had he grasped from the
Arabs that it is their habit, when reporting or commanding the reporting of a speech that follows a saying,
to address and then report of an absent one and report of an absent one and then return to address” — its
proof the poets' lines, and the Qurʾānic *ḥattā idhā kuntum fī l-fulki wa-jarayna bihim bi-rīḥin ṭayyibah*
(10:22), “where He addressed and then returned to reporting of the absent, and did not say *wa-jarayna
bikum*”.

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** On *yawmi l-dīn*: “*al-dīn* in this place has the sense of the reckoning and the requital for
deeds” — proof from the poets: Kaʿb b. Juʿayl, *idhā mā ramawnā ramaynāhum / wa-dinnāhumū mithla mā
yuqriḍūnā*, and *wa-ʿlam bi-annaka mā tadīnu tudānu* (“as you requite, so are you requited”); and from
82:9–10, *kallā bal tukadhdhibūna bi-l-dīn… wa-inna ʿalaykum la-ḥāfiẓīn*, and 56:86, *fa-lawlā in kuntum
ghayra madīnīn*. The reports: **Ibn ʿAbbās** (no. 167, weak chain): “the day of the reckoning of creation —
and it is the Day of Resurrection, on which He requites them for their deeds: if good, then good, and if
evil, then evil, except the one He pardons”; **Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd and Companions** (no. 168, the
Suddi chain): “the Day of Reckoning”; **Qatādah** (no. 169): “the day on which Allah requites the servants
for their deeds”; **Ibn Jurayj** (no. 170): “the day on which people are dealt with in reckoning”.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The doctrinal weight of the preferred reading: in *malik* “the acknowledgement that
sovereignty on the Day of Judgement belongs to Him alone, without the kings of this world who on the Day of
Judgement pass from their sovereignty to humiliation and contempt” — the Qurʾānic statement of it being
40:16, *li-mani l-mulku l-yawma? li-llāhi l-wāḥidi l-qahhār*.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V7, V8, V9, V11._

## 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

*[context]* You alone we worship, and You alone we seek help.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** “The explanation of *iyyāka naʿbudu* is: to You, O Allah, we humble ourselves and abase
ourselves and submit, in acknowledgement to You, our Lord, of lordship — not to other than You.” His reason
for choosing this over the report: “we preferred to explain it as *we humble ourselves and abase ourselves
and submit* rather than as *we hope and we fear*… because servitude (*al-ʿubūdiyyah*), with all the Arabs,
is in origin abasement — they call the trodden road, which feet have pressed down and travellers have
levelled, *muʿabbad*,” with Ṭarafah's verse *tubārī ʿitāqan nājiyātin wa-atbaʿat / waẓīfan waẓīfan fawqa
mawrin muʿabbad*. The transmitted reports: **Ibn ʿAbbās** through Jibrīl (no. 171, weak chain): “*To You we
worship* — You we make one, You we fear and You we hope in, our Lord, not other than You”; and on the
second clause, **Ibn ʿAbbās** (no. 172, weak chain): “*wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn* — from You we seek help in
obeying You and in all our affairs.”

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The major grammatical argument of the verse is the repetition of *iyyāka*: “the *kāf* in
*iyyāka* is the *kāf* which attaches to the verb — to *naʿbudu* — were it placed after the verb; it is the
pronoun of the addressed name in the accusative by the verb, and it is buttressed by *iyyā* when placed
first, since isolated names in the speech of the Arabs are not left on a single letter.” Hence “it is the
most eloquent speech to repeat it with every verb, as in *we worship You, we seek help from You, we praise
You, we thank You* — just as repeating it with each verb after the verb is the eloquent form, and the
dropping of the repetition permissible.” He rejects the claim that it resembles *bayna* in the poets' use
(ʿAdī b. Zayd: *wa-jāʿili l-shamsi miṣran lā khafāʾa bihi / bayna l-nahāri wa-bayna l-layli qad faṣalā*;
Aʿshā Hamdān: *bayna l-ashajji wa-bayna Qaysin bādhikhun / bakh bakh li-wālidihi wa-li-l-mawlūd*): “the
likeness is from the ignorance of the one who says it; for *iyyāka*'s portion is to be repeated with every
verb… while the ruling of *bayna* is not that: it does not stand alone with one of the two names it
requires, and if it did, the speech would be impossible, as were one to say ‘the sun has separated between
the day’.”

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Two doctrinal arguments are made from this verse:

* **On the need to ask God's help in obeying Him.** To the question — if He has commanded obedience and
  granted the enablement, why ask? — he answers: “the believer who asks his Lord to help him to obey Him is
  asking for help in what remains of his life, not in what has passed of his righteous acts; and the
  servant's asking is valid because Allah's giving him that, with His enabling his limbs for what He has
  imposed on him, is a favour from Him and a subtlety He has shown him.”
* **Against the *Qadariyyah* of *tafwīḍ*.* “And in Allah's commanding His servants to say *iyyāka naʿbudu
  wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*, in the sense of asking Him for help in worship, there is the most telling proof of the
  corruption of the saying of those of *al-Qadariyyah* who hold *al-tafwīḍ*, who declare it impossible that
  Allah should command a servant with a command, or impose an obligation, without first giving him the help
  to do it and to leave it. Were it as they say, the desire of Allah in help for His obedience would be
  void, since on their saying the giving of help would be a right owing from Allah to the servant whether he
  asked or not.” His proof: “the consensus of the people of Islam in approving the words *O Allah, we seek
  help from You* and in rejecting the words *O Allah, do not be unjust to us*” — that is, no one says the
  second.

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The inner sense of *ʿubūdiyyah* is abasement before God: “servitude with all the Arabs is in
origin abasement… and from it the slave is called *ʿabd* for his abasement before his master”; and the
man's confession “*to You we humble ourselves and abase ourselves and submit*” is the disposition the verse
teaches.

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** On the order of the two clauses: to the question why worship is mentioned before the asking
of help, when worship is only by means of help, he answers that the two are inseparable and equal in rank —
“it is the same whether you say *O Allah, You alone we worship, so help us to worship You*, or *O Allah,
help us to worship You, for You alone we worship*” — with the analogy of the man who has done you a service
and done it well: “you may say *you did my need and you were kind to me* or *you were kind to me and you did
my need*, for he is not a doer of your need but a benefactor to you, nor a benefactor to you but a doer of
your need.” And he rejects the view that the verse is of the placed-first meaning the postponed, “as
Imruʾ al-Qays: *wa-law anna mā asʿā li-adnā maʿīshatin / kafānī wa-lam aṭlub qalīlun mina l-māl*” — that
pattern does not apply here, because in the poet's case the sufficiency does not entail the giving-up of the
search.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V8, V11, V14, V15._

## 1:6 — اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ

*[context]* Guide us to the straight path.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** “The sense of *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm* in this place, in our view, is: **grant us success in
steadfastness upon it** (*waffiqnā li-l-thabāti ʿalayh*)”, as reported from Ibn ʿAbbās (no. 173, weak chain,
through Jibrīl): “*Say, O Muḥammad:* ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm — that is, *inspire us with the guiding
road*”; and his inspiring it is His granting success in it. He then specifies the sense of the petition: it
is “the asking of the servant from his Lord success in steadfastness upon acting in His obedience, and in
hitting the truth and the right in what He has commanded and forbidden, in what remains of his life, not in
what has passed.” The whole verse he paraphrases: “*O Allah, You alone we worship, alone, with no partner —
devotion sincere to You apart from the gods and idols besides You; so help us to worship You, and grant us
success in what You granted success to those You blessed of Your prophets and the people of Your obedience,
of way and method.*”

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah) — the grammar of *hidāyah*

**Al-Ṭabarī.** *Hidāyah* in the speech of the Arabs means success-granting (*al-tawfīq*): “that in their
speech is more plentiful and more evident than can be counted” — with the poets' lines: *lā taḥrimannanī
hadāka llāhu masʾalatī* (“may Allah grant you success in my need”), and *wa-lā tuʿjilannī hadāka l-malīku /
fa-inna li-kulli maqāmin maqālā*. Hence “*wa-llāhu lā yahdī l-qawma l-ẓālimīn* does not mean He does not make
clear to the wrongdoers what He has imposed on them of His ordinances — for He has made the explanation
general to all those under obligation — but it means He does not grant them success, nor open their breasts
to the truth and to faith.” He also records the three Arabic constructions all used in the Qurʾān: “*hadaytu
fulānan al-ṭarīq*, *hadaytuhu li-l-ṭarīq*, and *hadaytuhu ilā l-ṭarīq*” — 7:43, *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī
hadānā li-hādhā*; 16:121, *ijtabāhu wa-hadāhu ilā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm*; and 1:6 itself.

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** 37:23, *fa-hdūhum ilā ṣirāṭi l-jaḥīm*, is adduced for the sense of “leading into”: “as the
woman is led to her husband, and as a gift is led to a man, and as the foot leads the leg”, with Ṭarafah's
verse *li-l-fatā ʿaqlun yaʿīshu bihi / ḥaythu tahdī sāqahu qadamuh* — “meaning, he brings them in”.

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** From the verse's own plain sense: “the community of the people of explanation are agreed that
*al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm* is the clear path in which there is no crookedness; and that is so in the language of
all the Arabs” — with Jarīr's line *amīru l-muʾminīna ʿalā ṣirāṭin / idhā iʿwajja l-mawāridu mustaqīm*, and
Abū Dhuʾayb's *ṣabaḥnā arḍahum bi-l-khayli ḥattā / taraknāhā adaqqa mina l-ṣirāṭ*. Then: “the Arabs borrow
*al-ṣirāṭ* and use it for every saying and act described as straight or crooked.” His preferred tafsīr:
“that it means: *grant us success in steadfastness upon what You are pleased with, and upon what You granted
success to those You blessed of Your servants, in word and deed* — and that is the straight path. For
whoever is granted success in what the blessed of Allah were granted success in, of the prophets, the
truthful, the witnesses and the righteous, has been granted success in Islam, in affirming the messengers,
in holding to the Book, in acting by what Allah commanded and refraining from what He forbade, and in
following the method of the Prophet ﷺ and the method of Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān and ʿAlī, and every
righteous servant of Allah — and all of that is of the straight path.” The reports on the identification:

* **ʿAlī from the Prophet ﷺ** (nos. 174–175, two chains): “He mentioned the Qurʾān and said: *It is the
  straight path*.” And **from ʿAlī** directly (no. 176): “*The straight path is the Book of Allah.*”
* **Ibn Masʿūd** (no. 177): “*The straight path is the Book of Allah.*”
* **Jābir b. ʿAbd Allāh** (no. 178): “It is Islam — and it is wider than what is between the heaven and the
  earth.”
* **Ibn ʿAbbās** (no. 179, weak chain): “*inspire us with the guiding road* — and it is the religion of Allah
  in which there is no crookedness”; and (no. 180): “that is Islam”; and (no. 183): “*the path*”.
* **Ibn al-Ḥanafiyyah** (no. 181): “the religion of Allah, which He accepts from the servants no other.”
* **Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and Companions** through al-Suddī (no. 182): “it is Islam.”
* **Abū al-ʿĀliyah** (no. 184): “it is the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and his two companions after him, Abū Bakr
  and ʿUmar” — and when this was mentioned to al-Ḥasan, he said: “Abū al-ʿĀliyah spoke the truth and gave
  good counsel.”
* **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Zayd** (no. 185): “Islam.”
* **Nawwās b. Samʿān** from the Prophet ﷺ (nos. 186–187, two chains through Muʿāwiyah b. Ṣāliḥ): the parable
  of the straight path surrounded by two walls with open doors and curtains, the caller at its head, and the
  caller above it — “*and the path is Islam*.”

### V10 · Juristic rulings (aḥkām)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Two rulings of interpretation, both stated as exclusions: the verse cannot mean asking for
the **explanation** of the obligations — “for Allah does not impose an ordinance on a servant except after
making it clear to him and establishing the proof against him; and to ask for explanation of what has been
imposed would be absurd, since He imposes nothing but what is made clear”; and it cannot mean “**increase
us in guidance**” in the sense of explanation, since what remains of the obligations is not yet imposed. It
must therefore mean the granting of success for what remains of life. He likewise refutes the reading which
makes the verse “*lead us into the path of Paradise*” (the *asliknā* sense), holding that “the exegetes of
the Companions and the Successors are agreed that the sense of *al-ṣirāṭ* here is other than what the
speaker of this view said.”

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V7, V8, V10._

## 1:7 — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

*[context]* The path of those whom You have blessed — not of those who incurred wrath, nor of those who are astray.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** “*Ṣirāṭa lladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim* is a clarification of the straight path — which path it
is — since every road of the roads of truth is a straight path.” And the wording given: “Say, O Muḥammad:
*Guide us, our Lord, to the straight path — the path of those You blessed by Your obedience and Your
worship, of Your angels, Your prophets, the truthful, the witnesses and the righteous*”, with 4:66–69 as the
Qurʾānic parallel naming those blessed. The reports: **Ibn ʿAbbās** (no. 188, weak chain): “the path of
those You blessed by Your obedience and worship, of the angels and the prophets and the truthful and the
witnesses and the righteous, who obeyed You and worshipped You”; **al-Rabīʿ** (no. 189): “the prophets”;
**Ibn ʿAbbās** (no. 190): “the believers”; **Wakīʿ** (no. 191): “the Muslims”; **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Zayd**
(no. 192): “the Prophet ﷺ and those with him.”

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The doctrinal point he draws from the verse itself: “in this verse there is a clear proof that
the obedience of Allah is not attained by the obedient except by Allah's blessing it upon them and His
granting them success in it. Does he not hear Him say *the path of those You blessed* — attributing all
that was theirs of guidance, obedience and worship to its being a blessing from Him upon them?” — a
rejoinder to *al-Qadariyyah*, continued at the end of the sūrah.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Three grammar-and-meaning matters:

* **The elided blessing.** To the question what blessing is meant, he answers from the ellipsis principle
  established earlier (with al-Nābighah: *ka-annaka min jimāli Banī Uqayshin / yuqaʿqiʿu khalfa rijlayhi
  bi-shann* — “a camel” dropped; and al-Farazdaq: *tarā arbāqahum mutaqallidīhā* — “them” dropped): the
  blessing meant is the upright method and the straight path itself, made clear from the preceding words.
* ***Ghayr* and the two ways of *khafḍ*.** “The reciters are agreed on reading *ghayr* with *jarr* of the
  *rāʾ*; and the *khafḍ* comes to it from two directions: either *ghayr* is a description and attribute of
  *alladhīna*, so it takes the *khafḍ* that *alladhīna* has; or *alladhīna* is taken as definite and *ghayr*
  is put in the genitive by the intention of repeating *al-ṣirāṭ* — so that you say: *the path of those You
  blessed, the path of other than those who incurred wrath*.” On the first: “it is permitted for *ghayr* to
  be an attribute of *alladhīna* though *alladhīna* is definite and *ghayr* indefinite, because *alladhīna*
  with its relative clause is not a delimited definite like Zayd and ʿAmr, but is like the unknown
  indefinites” — cf. *lā ajlisu illā ilā l-ʿālimi ghayri l-jāhili*.
* **The forbidden *naṣb* and the dispute over it.** “It is permissible to put *ghayr* in the accusative…
  though I dislike reciting by it, for its departure from the recitation of the reciters; and what departs
  from the readings on which the community has come by a clear, widespread transmission is an opinion
  contrary to the truth.” He gives the face of the accusative reading (as a description of the *hāʾ* and
  *mīm* in *ʿalayhim*, which is in the position of the accusative by *anʿamta*): *the path of those You
  guided, in blessing from You upon them, not those who incurred wrath*. Then the grammarians' dispute: the
  Baṣran took the accusative as **exception** (*istithnāʾ*) — “*except those who incurred wrath*, who were
  not blessed in their religions and not guided to the truth; so do not make us of them” — and the Kūfans
  rejected this: “had it been as the Baṣran claimed, it would be an error to say *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*; for *lā*
  is a negation and denial, and nothing is coordinated with a denial but a denial. We have not found in any
  of the speech of the Arabs an exception coordinated with it by a denial; they coordinate an exception with
  an exception, and a denial with a denial; but *the people stood except your father and not your brother*
  we have not found.” Hence “*ghayr* is in the sense of **negation**, not exception, and the one who made it
  exception is in error.”

### V9 · Variant readings (qirāʾāt)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The unanimity on *jarr* and the rejected *naṣb*, above; and, at the end of the verse, the
Baṣran claim on *lā* with which the Kūfans deal: “some of the Baṣrans claimed that the *lā* with *al-ḍāllīn*
was brought in to complete the speech and its meaning is void”, adducing al-ʿAjjāj, *fī biʾri lāḥūrin sarā
wa-mā shaʿar* (“in a pit of perdition he travelled and did not perceive”), and Abū al-Najm, *fa-mā alūmu
l-bīḍa an lā taskharā*, and al-Aḥwas, *wa-yalḥaynanī fī l-lahwi an lā uḥibbah*, and 7:12, *mā manaʿaka allā
tasjuda* — reading *an lā* as the redundant *lā*. Al-Ṭabarī sides with the Kūfans: “*the second saying is
worthier of correctness than the first*, since there is not found in the speech of the Arabs a beginning of
speech with *lā* in the sense of omission with no negation before it; nor is it permitted to coordinate by
it upon *siwā* or upon the particle of exception.” And the conclusion: “*ghayr* in the speech of the Arabs
has three senses: exception, negation, and *siwā*; and when it is established that *lā* cannot be void and
cannot be coordinated on *illā* or on *siwā*, and it is found coordinated by the *wāw* — then it is
established that *ghayr* here can only be in the sense of **negation and denial**, and *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* can
only be coordinated on *ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim*.” The resulting sense of the verse he gives as: “*Guide
us to the straight path, the path of those You blessed, not those who incurred wrath and not those who are
astray*.”

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The transmitted identifications of the two groups:

* ***Al-maghḍūb ʿalayhim*.** ʿAdī b. Ḥātim from the Prophet ﷺ (nos. 193, 194, 195 — three chains, including
  Shuʿbah → Simāk → ʿAbbād b. Ḥubaysh): “*the maghḍūb ʿalayhim* are the Jews”; and the report of the man who
  came to the Prophet ﷺ while he was besieging Wādī al-Qurā (nos. 196–199, four chains through ʿAbd Allāh b.
  Shaqīq): he asked “who are these?” and the Prophet said, “*these are those who incurred wrath — the Jews*.”
  And from the Successors and the early authorities: **Ibn ʿAbbās** (nos. 200, 204), **al-Suddī from Ibn
  ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd and Companions** (no. 201), **Mujāhid** (no. 202), **al-Rabīʿ** (no. 203), **ʿAbd
  al-Raḥmān b. Zayd** (nos. 205, 206): all “the Jews”.
* ***Al-ḍāllīn*.** ʿAdī b. Ḥātim from the Prophet ﷺ (nos. 207, 208, 209): “*al-ḍāllīn* are the Christians”;
  and the Wādī al-Qurā report (nos. 210–213): “*these are the astray — the Christians*.” **Mujāhid** (no.
  214), **Ibn ʿAbbās** (nos. 215, 216), **al-Suddī from Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and Companions** (no. 217),
  **al-Rabīʿ** (no. 218), **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Zayd** (nos. 219–220): “the Christians”. Ibn ʿAbbās's longer
  report (no. 215, weak chain): “*and not the path of the Christians whom Allah led astray by their
  fabricating against Him*… *so inspire us with Your true religion, which is* lā ilāha illā llāh *alone,
  with no partner, so that You are not angry with us as You were angry with the Jews, and do not lead us
  astray as You led the Christians astray, punishing us as You punish them; prevent that from us by Your
  gentleness, Your mercy and Your power*.”

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** On *al-ḍāll*: “every one who swerves from the aim of the road, and travels other than the
upright method, is *ḍāll* with the Arabs, for his losing the face of the road; and for that Allah named the
Christians *ḍullāl*, for their erring from the method of the road in the truth, and their taking religion in
other than the straight way.” And to the question whether that is not also a description of the Jews —
“*Yes*” — he answers: “both groups are astray and those who incurred wrath, but Allah marked each group
with the description by which His servants know it… He named neither of the two except by what is truly its
description, even if it has further descriptions of blame.”

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Three creedal matters:

* **The identification of the two groups rests on the *āthār***: his first proof for the *maghḍūb ʿalayhim*
  is 5:60, *man laʿanahu llāhu wa-ghaḍiba ʿalayhi wa-jaʿala minhumu l-qiradata wa-l-khanāzīra wa-ʿabada
  l-ṭāghūt*, and for the *ḍāllīn* 5:77, *qad ḍallū min qablu wa-aḍallū kathīran wa-ḍallū ʿan sawāʾi
  l-sabīl*.
* **The attribute of wrath.** “The people differed over the description of Allah's wrath: some said Allah's
  wrath on those of His creation with whom He is angry is His bringing down His punishment upon them, in
  this world or the next — as in 43:55, *fa-lammā āsafūnā ntaqamnā minhum*; some said it is His blame of
  them and of their acts, and His reviling them in speech; and some said the wrath from Him is a meaning
  understood, like what is known of the meanings of wrath, but — though it is so in affirmation — its
  meaning differs from the wrath of human beings which agitates and moves and harms them, because Allah's
  essence is not subject to defects; rather it is an attribute of His, as knowledge is an attribute of His
  and power is an attribute of His, **affirmed** as it is understood, though its meaning differs from the
  meanings of the knowledge of servants and their powers.”
* **Guidance and straying are from Allah.** Against those of the *Qadariyyah* who argued from the fact that
  straying is attributed to the Christians and not to Allah (*al-ḍāllīn*, not *al-muḍallīn*): “had it been as
  the dull one supposed, it would be an error to say ‘the tree moved’ when the winds moved it, and ‘the
  earth shook’ when the earthquake moved it.” And: “the Qurʾān came down in the tongue of the Arabs… it is
  their habit to attribute the act to the one it was found from, even if its cause is other than the one it
  was found from, and sometimes to its cause, even if the one from whom the act was found is other than it” —
  the proof being 10:22, *wa-jarayna bihim bi-rīḥin ṭayyibah*, the sailing attributed to the ships though
  the wind drives them; and 45:23, *a-fa-raʾayta mani ttakhadha ilāhahu hawāhu wa-aḍallahū llāhu ʿalā ʿilmin
  wa-khatama ʿalā samʿihi wa-qalbihi*, “declaring that He is the One who leads astray and guides, apart from
  any other.”

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** Under the report of Ibn ʿAbbās (no. 215, weak chain), the verse is read as a servant's
petition formed by the two examples: “*so that You are not angry with us as You were angry with the Jews,
and do not lead us astray as You led the Christians astray… prevent that from us by Your gentleness, Your
mercy and Your power*.”

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Al-Ṭabarī.** The sūrah closes with the ḥadīth of the division of the prayer, transmitted from Abū Hurayrah
in three chains (nos. 221, 222, 223, one of them *mawqūf*), and from Jābir b. ʿAbd Allāh (no. 224): the
Prophet ﷺ said: “*Allah, mighty and exalted, said: I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant into
two halves, and My servant shall have what he asks. When the servant says* al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi
l-ʿālamīn*, Allah says: My servant has praised Me. When he says* al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*, He says: My servant
has extolled Me. When he says* māliki yawmi l-dīn*, He says: My servant has magnified Me — this is Mine, and
what remains.*” (In the Jābir report the words “My servant has praised Me / has extolled Me / has magnified
Me” are given for the first three clauses, and the last portion is “this is Mine”.) Al-Ṭabarī appends:
“The end of the explanation of the sūrah Fātiḥat al-Kitāb.”

### S5 · Central theme & summary of contents (maqāṣid) — placed here as the work itself places it

**Al-Ṭabarī.** At the close of the sūrah he records the objection and answers it: “If a questioner of the
people of ilḥād, who attack the Qurʾān, asks: you have stated in the beginning of your book that the highest
degree of clarity (*al-bayān*) is the most eloquent of it… and that the worthiest of it to be so is the
speech of Allah — then what is the face of the length of the speech in a sūrah like Umm al-Qurʾān, of seven
verses, when two of them contain the meanings of all of them: *māliki yawmi l-dīn* and *iyyāka naʿbudu
wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*?” His answer: “Allah gathered for our Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ and his community, in what He
sent down to him, meanings He had not gathered in any book sent down to a prophet before him… Each book sent
down before it was sent down with some of the meanings that this Book contains: the Torah, which is
exhortations and detail; the Psalms, which are praise and magnification; the Gospel, which is exhortations
and reminder — with no miracle in any of them bearing witness for the one to whom it was sent.” And the
distinguishing meaning: “of the most honoured of those meanings by which our Book surpasses the books before
it is its wondrous composition (*naẓm*) and strange arrangement and marvellous construction, which the
orators were unable to match in the smallest sūrah of it, and the eloquent wearied of describing the shape
of part of it, and the poets were bewildered in its composition… **so that Allah intended, by that
composition which departs from the metres of poetry and the rhyming of the soothsayers and the speeches of
orators and the letters of the eloquent, to point to the prophethood of our Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ**; and by
what it contains of praise and magnification and extolling of Him, to alert the servants to His greatness
and authority and power and the greatness of His dominion, so that they remember Him by His blessings and
praise Him for His favours, and so deserve increase and the great reward; and by what it contains of
describing those He blessed with knowledge of Him and favoured with success in obeying Him, to teach His
servants that every blessing they have, in religion and world, is from Him, so that they direct their desire
to Him; and by what it contains of His mention of what He brought down upon those who disobeyed Him of His
punishments, to warn them from embarking on His disobedience.” And: “that is the face of the length of the
explanation in the sūrah Umm al-Qurʾān, and that is the transcendent wisdom and the complete proof.”
