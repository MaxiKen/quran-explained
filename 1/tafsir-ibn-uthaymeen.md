## Sūrah-level record (S1 · S3 · S4 · S5)

### S1 · Names & titles of the sūrah

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** "**This noble sūrah — *al-Fātiḥah* — comprises all the meanings of the Noble Qurʾān, and for
that it is named as the Prophet ﷺ named it — *Umm al-Qurʾān***, and **it is called *Umm al-Kitāb*; and the
*umm* of a thing is its point of reference to which it returns**" — with the poet's line: *"Upon its head a
mother for us, by which we are guided…"* — "**meaning a sign toward which we aim and which we intend**." And:
"**It is *al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī*** — the Prophet ﷺ explained the *Seven Oft-repeated* as the Fātiḥah, **and it is
seven verses**." And the sūrah is "**a great *ruqyah* for a person**" (developed at 1:1, V17).

### S3 · Verse count — and the *basmalah*

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn** (teaching the count directly from the text):

> "**Fātiḥah al-Kitāb is seven verses** — *wa-laqad ātaynāka sabʿan mina l-mathānī wa-l-qurʾāna l-ʿaẓīm*
> (15:87) — **the Prophet ﷺ explained the *Sabʿ al-Mathānī* as the Fātiḥah, and it is seven verses.** But
> perhaps one of you has a *muṣḥaf* and turns to it, and finds *ṣirāṭa lladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim ghayri
> l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* **one verse**; and if we made it one verse and began with *al-ḥamd*,
> **its verses would be six — and that is problematic.** **The answer to that is that the numbering in the
> *muṣḥaf*s printed today is built on the basmalah's being of the Fātiḥah** — and for that the basmalah is
> written 'one', and *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* 'two'. **But the truth — without doubt — is that the
> basmalah is not of the Fātiḥah, and that it is a verse with which the sūrahs are begun, except Sūrat
> al-Barāʾah.** **And the proof of that is this ḥadīth of Abū Hurayrah, established in the *Ṣaḥīḥ***:
> *'I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant into two halves; **when he says** *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi
> rabbi l-ʿālamīn*…'* — **and He did not say: when he says *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*.** **So what is the
> first of the Fātiḥah?** It is not the first of its verses; **its first is *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi
> l-ʿālamīn*.** *[ed.]*: the editor's takhrīj here gives **Muslim (395/38)** for the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh*
> ḥadīth, and **al-Bukhārī (4474)** for Abū Saʿīd b. al-Muʿallā's: *'*al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* **is
> the *Sabʿ al-Mathānī* and the *Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm*.'**"

And the corroboration by length and by the "halves": "**we find that the verse which is between Allah and His
servant, two halves, is the verse that halves the Fātiḥah exactly, for before it are three verses and after it
three verses, so it is the Fātiḥah's half** — and this middle verse is first for Allah and last for the
servant. **And also, had *ṣirāṭa lladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* been two
verses, it would be a long verse beside the preceding ones; but when the last verse is divided in two — *ṣirāṭa
lladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim* • *ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* — the verses become proportionate in
length.** **So the verbal, the semantic and the transmitted indications all show that the first of the Fātiḥah
is *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*, and that the basmalah is not of it.**"

### S4 · Faḍāʾil of the sūrah

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.**

* **The sūrah as *ruqyah*** — the full narrative of the *sariyyah* (filed at 1:1, V17 below), with the
  Prophet's word: **"*And what made you know that it is a *ruqyah*?*"** — *[ed.]* the editor cites **Muslim
  (2201/66)** from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī.
* **Given from a treasure beneath the Throne** — "**its being given from a treasure beneath the Throne**": *[ed.]* the
  transcription cites **al-Ṭabarānī, *al-Muʿjam al-Kabīr* (525)** from Maʿqil b. Yasār: *"**and I was given
  Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and the closing verses of Sūrat al-Baqarah from a treasure beneath the Throne**."*
* **Its being obligatory in every *rakʿah*** — "**and for that it is *Umm al-Kitāb*, which must be recited in
  every *rakʿah* of the prayer**" (S5 below), and "**it is a great *ruqyah* for a person.**"
* And a report he narrates in the lesson's frame: the *miʿrāj* ḥadīth — the fifty prayers made five in act
  "**and fifty in the balance**" — *[ed.]*: **al-Bukhārī (3207), Muslim (164/264)** from Mālik b. Ṣaʿṣaʿah.

### S5 · Maqāṣid — the sūrah and the whole Qurʾān

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.**

> "**This great sūrah — al-Fātiḥah — comprises all the meanings of the Noble Qurʾān; and for that Allah did
> not oblige us to recite a sūrah in the prayer except this sūrah — because it has gathered every meaning of
> the Qurʾān. In it is the mention of lordship (*rubūbiyyah*), divinity (*ulūhiyyah*), the Names and
> Attributes, requital and action, and the divisions of people.**"
> "How many divisions are people in Sūrat al-Fātiḥah? **Three**: *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm • ṣirāṭa lladhīna
> anʿamta ʿalayhim* — the first; *ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim* — the second; *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* — the third.
> **So we understand that the divisions of people are three**: those Allah has blessed — **they are the ones
> who knew the truth and acted on it**; those **wrath is upon — who knew the truth and did not act on it, at
> their head the Jews**; and the **astray — who were ignorant of the truth and acted on falsehood — the
> Christians.**"
> "**And on this sūrah revolve the meanings of the whole Qurʾān** — *rubūbiyyah*, *ulūhiyyah*, names and
> attributes, requital, and action; **and in the ḥadīth qudsī: 'I have divided the prayer between Me and My
> servant into halves'** — and the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh* assigns the first half to Allah (praise, extolling,
> magnifying), the middle verse (*iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*) between Him and His servant, and the last
> three to the servant — '**and My servant shall have what he asks**' (1:1 V5)."
> "**And whoever recites it with reflection, reviewing the words of the people of knowledge on it, will find in
> it very great meanings** — and for that it is *Umm al-Kitāb*, which must be recited in every *rakʿah*; **and
> with that it is a great *ruqyah*.**"

## 1:1 — بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* In the name of Allah, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

> The transcript never treats the basmalah as a verse *of this sūrah*; its basmalah material is exactly his
> argument against that (S3) and his rulings built on it (V10 below).

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn** — the sūrah's setting in the prayer, from the transcript's own sequence (the lecture is on
"the prayers we perform every day", and it reaches the Fātiḥah through the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh*):

* "**The prayer—'I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant into halves', and what is *al-ṣalāh*
  here? The whole prayer — but it is the Fātiḥah, and Allah named it 'the prayer' because the prayer is not
  sound except with it**" — with 17:78: *aqimi l-ṣalāta li-dulūki l-shamsi ilā ghasaqi l-layli wa-qurʾāna
  l-fajr* — "**i.e., and establish the recitation of the dawn; and the wisdom in that He says in the four
  prayers *aqimi l-ṣalāta*… and in the dawn *wa-qurʾāna l-fajr* is that the recitation of the dawn is
  witnessed (*mashhūd*) and it is lengthened in it.**"
* On hearing the servant: "'**When he says *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*, Allah says: My servant has
  praised Me**' — **who answers him? The Lord, above His Throne, above His heavens, hears him even if he is
  silent.** — But there may be a thousand worshippers in the mosque, one saying *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi
  l-ʿālamīn*, another still in the opening supplication, another finishing the Fātiḥah: how is this? **We say:
  Allah encompasses everything in mercy and knowledge; each one has his answer, even if their words differ**"
  — and the response to *al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* is "**My servant has extolled Me**"; to *māliki yawmi l-dīn*,
  "**My servant has magnified Me**"; to *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*, "**This is between Me and My
  servant**"; to *ihdinā*…, "**This is for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks.**"
* And the words of the servant under the verse: "**As for *iyyāka naʿbudu* — for Allah; and *iyyāka
  nastaʿīn* — for whom? for the creature: you seek His help in all your affairs — in worship, in eating and
  drinking, in sleep, in going out — so it is for you.**"

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The ḥadīths that frame and explain the sūrah in the lesson, as he quotes them:

> **1.** *"**I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant into halves; when he says** *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi
> rabbi l-ʿālamīn*, **Allah says: My servant has praised Me**; *al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* — **My servant has
> extolled Me**; *māliki yawmi l-dīn* — **My servant has magnified Me**; *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn* —
> **This is between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks**; *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa
> l-mustaqīm* — **This is for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks**."* — *[ed.]*: **Muslim
> (395/38)**, from Abū Hurayrah; and the Shaykh draws from it: **"the basmalah is not of the Fātiḥah"** (S3),
> and **"the middle verse is the Fātiḥah's half"** (S3).
> **2.** Abū Hurayrah on the opening supplication: *"**O Messenger of Allah, your silence between the *takbīr*
> and the recitation — what do you say?** He said: **I say: 'O Allah, distance me from my sins as You have
> distanced the East from the West; O Allah, cleanse me of my sins as the white garment is cleansed of stain;
> O Allah, wash me of my sins with water and snow and hail.'**"* — and the Shaykh's *tadabbur*: "**why the
> white garment? Because the least dirt shows in it… and why water and snow and hail, when hot water is more
> effective in cleaning? — **the scholars said: because he says 'wash me of my sins' — they are not sensory
> filth, and sins' punishment is the Fire — so is what removes these sins hot or cold? — the students: cold —
> the Shaykh: cold, cold: that is the wisdom**"** — *[ed.]*: **al-Bukhārī (744), Muslim (598/147)** (1:1, V15).
> **3.** ʿUmar's supplication taught aloud: *"**Subḥānaka llāhumma wa-bi-ḥamdika, wa-tabāraka smuka,
> wa-taʿālā jadduka, wa-lā ilāha ghayruka**"* — "**and this is sound; Abū Hurayrah's ḥadīth is sounder in
> *isnād* than this; but ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb used to say it aloud for teaching.** — **Do not combine them (that
> would contravene the sunnah), but say this one time and that another, so as to act on both sunnahs.**"
> **4.** The *takbīr*: "**when they ascended a height**" *[ed.]*: **al-Bukhārī (2994)** from Jābir — *"we used
> to say *Allāhu akbar* when we ascended and *subḥāna llāh* when we descended"* — and the Shaykh's teaching:
> "**so when you ride the plane and rise from the earth, say *Allāhu akbar*; for when man ascends he may grow
> great in his own eye, so he says *Allāhu akbar* to bound his exaltation; and when he descends he says
> *subḥāna llāh*, declaring Allah free of lowliness — lowliness being the attribute of the human being, while
> the height belongs to Allah.**"
> **5.** The *miʿrāj*: the prayers made five in act, "**fifty in the balance**", and Mūsā's repeated counsel —
> *[ed.]*: **al-Bukhārī (3207), Muslim (164/264)** from Mālik b. Ṣaṣaʿah.
> **6.** *"**Whoever recites *āyat al-Kursī* in a night, a guardian from Allah remains over him and no devil
> comes near him until morning**"* — with his warning: **"recite it with faith — not 'let me try whether it
> works', for the one who says 'let me try' has doubt"** (1:1, V14).
> **7.** On the *ruqyah* with the Fātiḥah: the *sariyyah* narrative and *"**and what made you know that it is
> a *ruqyah*?**"*, and *"**take it, and assign me a share along with you**"* — *[ed.]*: **al-Bukhārī (2276), Muslim (2201/66)** (*muttāfaq ʿalayh*)
> from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī (1:1, V17).
> **8.** Ẓāhir's report and the "**the like of it has not come down in the Torah, nor the Gospel, nor the
> Psalms, nor the Qurʾān**" — the Shaykh cites the *Ḥadīth* of the Fātiḥah's like in the lesson's course
> (cf. S4); *[ed.]*: al-Ṭabarānī (525), "given from a treasure beneath the Throne".

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** **Anas** — "**I prayed behind the Prophet ﷺ, and behind Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān, and
they did not recite *bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm***" (Muslim) — **"and its meaning is: they did not say it
aloud"** (V10); **ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb** — his saying *Subḥānaka llāhumma…* aloud for teaching; his martyrdom
in the dawn prayer ("**the dog has eaten me**" — "not the beast; **it is ʿAbū Luʾluʾa the Magian**"); his
levelling of the rows, and the report *[ed.]* **Abū Dāwūd (665)**, from al-Nuʿmān b. Bashīr: *"when we stood straight he said the takbīr"*;
**ʿUthmān** — "**the martyr of the *muṣḥaf*: the Khawārij entered upon him while he recited and killed him in
his house**", with the report that a drop of his blood fell on *fa-sa-yakfīkahumu llāhu wa-huwa l-samīʿu
l-ʿalīm* (2:137); **Abū Bakr** — the *ṣiddīq* in the Uḥud ḥadīth (V13); and **Abū Hurayrah** — his
transmission of the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh* and the *istiftāḥ* ḥadīth (V5), and the Shaykh's note that he was "of
the most eager of people for knowledge".

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirīn (aqwāl al-mufassirīn)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** He teaches the sūrah by *asking* and by the answers of "the scholars":

* "*Al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* — **what does it mean?** — **the complete description with the most
  perfect attributes, deserved by whom? by Allah, Mighty and Majestic.** And *Rabb al-ʿālamīn*: **the Lord is
  the Creator, the Owner, the Disposer**; and the *ʿālam* — **all creatures, everything other than Allah.**"
* "*Al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* — **the scholars said: *al-Raḥmān* means vast in mercy** — **because the form
  *faʿlān* indicates fullness and breadth: when we see a man whose jugulars swell and whose eyes redden, we
  say *ghaḍbān*, i.e., filled with wrath — so *al-Raḥmān* is vast in mercy; and *al-Raḥīm* means the one who
  conveys mercy to whom He wills**" — with 29:21: *yuʿadhdhibu man yashāʾu wa-yarḥamu man yashāʾ*, "**so in
  these two noble names are gathered Allah's being described with the breadth of mercy, and that this mercy
  reaches His believing and unbelieving servants: the general mercy of this world is for believers and
  unbelievers — the unbeliever is shown mercy, given provision and support — but in the next world he has no
  mercy**" — and 7:156: *wa-raḥmatī wasiʿat kulla shayʾin fa-sa-aktubuhā li-lladhīna yattaqūn*.
* "*Māliki yawmi l-dīn* — **what is the Day of *al-Dīn*? It is the Day of Resurrection, in which is the
  requital of deeds.** — **And does Allah not own this world? He owns this world and the religion — everything
  He owns; but He specified 'the Day of *al-Dīn*' because no one has ownership on that day**" — 40:16:
  *li-mani l-mulku l-yawm? li-llāhi l-wāḥidi l-qahhār*.
* "**Do you know what our saying *māliki yawmi l-dīn* entails? It entails faith in the Last Day and faith in
  the requital of deeds, and that the One who requites deeds is Allah, the Owner of the Day of *al-Dīn*.**"
* On the last verse: "**the Shaykh**" teaching through a student's answer — "**Who are those Allah has blessed?
A student: *wa-man yuṭiʿi llāha wa-l-rasūla…* (4:69). The Shaykh: yes — your brother says **those Allah has
blessed are the ones mentioned in 4:69: the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous.** How many
classes? **Four.** **The Qurʾān explains one part of it by another.**"
* And the warning he builds on it (Sufyān b. ʿUyaynah — filed at 1:7, V7).

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · ishtiqāq · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The grammatical teaching of the lessons:

* **The basmalah's place** — his argument on 15:87 and on "the verse that halves the Fātiḥah" (S3) is
  linguistically grounded: "**the verse between Allah and His servant, two halves, is the verse that halves
  the Fātiḥah exactly**"; and "**had the last *āyah* been two verses... when it is divided in two, the verses
  become proportionate in length.**"
* **The *ilā* with *h-d-y*** (1:6): "*ihdinā l-ṣirāṭ* does not say *ihdinā ilā l-ṣirāṭ*, though Allah said to
  His Prophet *wa-innaka la-tahdī ilā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm* (42:52) and also *wa-yahdiyaka ṣirāṭan mustaqīman*
  (48:2). **What is the difference between *hadāhu ilā l-ṣirāṭ* and *hadāhu l-ṣirāṭ*? The first: he directed
  him to it only; the second: he guided him to it and in it** — and for that the preposition was deleted so
  that it be general — **so the meaning is: direct us to it and grant us success in acting on it**."
* **(1:5) the fronting and the tense** — "**the words *'istaʿannā bi-llāh'* are not more eloquent than
  *iyyāka nastaʿīn*, and the difference is two-fold: **first, that in *iyyāka nastaʿīn* the object is placed
  first, and the fronting of the object indicates restriction and specification — as though you said: we seek
  help from none but You; second, that *'istaʿannā bi-llāh' is a past verb, while *nastaʿīn* is a present verb
  indicating continuity — and man is one who seeks Allah's help always and forever.**"
* **(1:7) the negation's rhetoric** — "*ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* — this is to **confirm the
  perfection** of what is affirmed (*ṣirāṭa lladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim*) — **so that there be in this path not
  even a small part of the path of those wrath is upon and of the astray**; and many students of knowledge now
  understand that **Allah's negative attributes (*ṣifāt salbiyyah*) entail the affirmation of the perfection of
  their opposite** — so when He says *wa-lā yaẓlimu rabbuka aḥadā* (18:49), **the sense is that for the
  perfection of His justice there is no wrong in His act or His judgement**; **so *ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim*
  comes for the perfection of that affirmation in *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm*, so that in this guidance there
  be nothing of straying nor anything of what brings wrath.**"
* **The word *al-ṣalāh* for the Fātiḥah** — "*qasamtu l-ṣalāh* — **what is *al-ṣalāh*? The whole prayer; but
  the Fātiḥah — and Allah named it 'the prayer' because the prayer is not sound except with it.**" And the
  *umm*'s linguistic sense: "**the *umm* of a thing is its point of return to which it returns**" — with the
  poet's line *"upon its head a mother for us, by which we are guided — meaning a sign toward which we aim and
  intend."*

### V10 · Juristic rulings & injunctions (aḥkām)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.**

* **The basmalah is not a verse of al-Fātiḥah** — the S3 argument, and the ruling he draws from it: testing
  the class, the Shaykh states the wrong conclusion — "**so he recites the basmalah aloud, right?**" — a
  student: "**No, no, Shaykh**" — "**Brothers, we said: the basmalah is not of the Fātiḥah — so if it is not
  of the Fātiḥah, is it said aloud?**" — the students: "**No.**" — "**No; it is not said aloud — just as we
  do not say the opening supplication aloud, because it is outside the Fātiḥah, so we do not say the basmalah
  aloud either; this is the correct position**" — and he had prefaced it (in the lesson's aside): "**if I
  err, correct me.**" And the ḥadīth he grounds it in: "**and for that Anas said: *I prayed behind the
  Prophet ﷺ and behind Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, and they did not recite bismi llāhi l-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* — this is in
  *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, but the meaning is that they did not say it aloud.**"
* **The printed *muṣḥaf* numbering**: "**the numbering in the printed *muṣḥaf*s today is built on the
  basmalah's being of the Fātiḥah** — so the basmalah carries 'one' and *al-ḥamdu* 'two' **— and the truth is
  other than that**" (S3; and V16 below).
* **The basmalah's status as a verse**: "**the basmalah is a verse with which the sūrahs are opened, except
  Sūrat al-Barāʾah.**"
* **The prayer's preliminaries** the lecture teaches as the Fātiḥah's setting: the *takbīr*'s meaning ("*Allāhu
  akbar* — greater than everything in greatness and majesty… **so when you ascend say *Allāhu akbar*; when you
  descend say *subḥāna llāh***"); the *rafʿ al-yadayn* (the reports of its joining the *takbīr*, preceding it
  or following it — "**and the matter here is wide**"); the opening supplication ("**do not combine Abū
  Hurayrah's and ʿUmar's — say this one time and that another**"); **the Fātiḥah's recitation in the four
  prayers and the dawn** (17:78, above).
* **The congregation behind the imām**: "**some of the common people, when the imām says *iyyāka naʿbudu
  wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*, say *istaʿanā bi-llāh* — **do not say this; nothing was reported on it; the Companions
  did not say it behind their Prophet; and the imām's recitation will make you say *āmīn* upon it — as though
  you yourself said *iyyāka nastaʿīn*; and *iyyāka nastaʿīn* is more eloquent than *istaʿannā bi-llāh***"
  (V8)."
* **And his instruction**: "**listen to your imām's recitation and do not speak; you are commanded to keep
  silent — and when he says *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*, say *āmīn***."
* **The eclipse prayer's Fātiḥah** (from the session's questions): "**how is the eclipse prayer? One says the
  *takbīr*, recites the Fātiḥah and a long sūrah — very long, like Sūrat al-Baqarah — then bows a long bowing;
  then rises and recites the Fātiḥah and a long sūrah, but shorter than the first…**"

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.**

* **The *takbīr*'s creed**: "***Allāhu akbar* — greater than everything in greatness and majesty, Mighty and
  Exalted; there is none greater than Allah in greatness and majesty**" — and the *tasbīḥ* on descent
  (V5, ḥadīth 4), with 17:44 (*wa-in min shayʾin illā yusabbiḥu bi-ḥamdihi*): "**everything glorifies Allah
  with the tongue of its state and the tongue of its speech — except the disbelievers among humans and jinn,
  who glorify Him with the tongue of their state but not of their speech.**"
* **The classification of action**: "**It is the only practical pillar whose abandoner becomes a disbeliever in
  Allah with a disbelief that takes him out of the religion**" — with his note that the last three pillars'
  abandoner (not denying their obligation) **does not disbelieve** on the *rājiḥ* position, while **the one who
  abandons the prayer is a disbeliever** by the Book, the Sunnah and what is reported of the Companions'
  consensus, **even if he affirms its obligation.**
* **The entailments of the Fātiḥah's own verses**: "*māliki yawmi l-dīn* entails **faith in the Last Day and
  faith in the requital of deeds**"; "*iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*" is the servant's worship and
  reliance confined to Allah; and the three-division reading of mankind (S5) is the creed in the sūrah's frame.
* **The `dīn` disambiguation** (V4): "*al-dīn* in 5:3 is the action; in *māliki yawmi l-dīn* and 82:17–19 it
  is the requital of actions" — and the proverb **"as you requite, so are you requited."**

### V13 · Historical narratives, sīrah & parables (qiṣaṣ · sīrah)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** (His narratives illustrate the sūrah's last verses.) "**Who are those Allah has blessed?**
the four classes of 4:69 — **the prophets (and the messengers enter among them, for every messenger is a
prophet and not the reverse), the truthful (as Abū Bakr), the martyrs (as ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, ʿAlī and many
others), and the righteous**" — and the Prophetic scene: "**the Prophet ﷺ climbed Mount Uḥud and the mountain
shook with him, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān — four of them — and the Prophet ﷺ said: *'Be still, Uḥud — upon
you is only a prophet, a truthful one and two martyrs'*: Abū Bakr the truthful; the two martyrs, ʿUmar and
ʿUthmān**" — *[ed.]* **al-Bukhārī (3699)** from Anas — and the Shaykh's commentary:

> "**The mountain shook — and it appears to me, and the knowledge is with Allah, that it shook out of joy at
> those who rode on its back, or to display a sign of Allah's signs.** — **And one may say: how does the
> Prophet ﷺ — the most sensible of people — address a stone and say *be still*? The answer: **this stone has
> understanding** — *tusabbiḥu lahu l-samāwātu l-sabʿu wa-l-arḍu wa-man fīhinna* (17:44)… — **and the Prophet
> ﷺ said: '**Uḥud is a mountain that loves us and we love it'**" — *[ed.]*: al-Bukhārī (2889), Muslim
> (1365/462) — "**so this place, had it been in the era of the late-comers, would be taken as an ill omen
> because of what befell the Muslims there of death and wounds; but the Messenger of Allah said: 'a mountain
> that loves us and we love it'** — **and he affirmed for the mountain a will — namely love.**"
> **And the story of Mūsā and the stone**: "**Mūsā — the prophet of Allah — while the Israelites bathed naked,
> used never to bathe without cover; and the Israelites said: 'Mūsā only covers himself because he has a
> defect' — **and Allah willed what was not Mūsā's choice: one day Mūsā entered to bathe and placed his
> garment on a stone, and the stone fled with his garment** — **Mūsā chased it crying 'my garment, stone, my
> garment, stone!'** — until it reached the assembly of the Israelites, who saw Mūsā — **and he was sound,
> innocent of what they said** — *yā ayyuhā lladhīna āmanū lā takūnū ka-lladhīna ādhaw mūsā fa-barraʾahu llāhu
> mimmā qālū* (33:69)" — *[ed.]*: **al-Bukhārī (3404), Muslim (339/75)** — "**and when the people saw him, the
> stone stopped, and Mūsā took his garment and beat the stone.**
> **And the *sariyyah* whose recitation cured the stung chief** (V17, and the *ruqyah* conditions at V14).
> And **the Prophetic-era lesson on differing understanding**: "**when the Prophet ﷺ returned from the battle
> of the Confederates, Jibrīl came and ordered him out to Banū Qurayẓah — a tribe of the Jews who had betrayed
> him — and the Prophet ﷺ urged his Companions, saying: '*Let none pray ʿAṣr except among Banū Qurayẓah*.'** —
> **the Companions went out; ʿAṣr came upon them and they differed into two groups: one followed the text
> literally and said 'we shall not pray ʿAṣr except in Banū Qurayẓah, even at midnight'; the others said the
> Messenger of Allah ﷺ meant: hasten, so that ʿAṣr not come upon us except as we are in Banū Qurayẓah — and
> they prayed in time. And the one who hits the mark — on the *rājiḥ* of the scholars' views — is the one who
> prayed in time.**" — and the Shaykh's parallel: "**if I send you to a town and say: 'Let not the dawn come
> before you are in such-and-such town', the meaning is: hasten.**"
> And he warns on this: "**many people err in understanding out of their supposition — even the Companions could
> err in understanding; a human being is not protected from error in understanding**" (V15).

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.**

* **The *ruqyah*'s three conditions** (from the sariyyah lesson): "**recitation profits only by three
  conditions: the receptivity of the locus, the competence of the reciter, and the validity of the recitation.
  — **Should one recite ʿazāʾim that are not valid, it profits not; and were the one recited over not
  receptive — as when he doubts, saying 'I do not know about this recitation; going to the hospital is
  better' — he profits not; and were the reciter not competent, it profits not.** — **And on the occasion I
  like to make clear that the Prophet ﷺ reported: 'Whoever recites *āyat al-Kursī* in a night, a guardian
  from Allah remains over him and no devil comes near him till morning' — so it should be recited every night,
  but with faith; not 'let me try whether it works' — for the one who says 'let me try' is doubting.**"
* **The heart purification before the Fātiḥah** — the lesson's frame: "**the prayer is preceded by
  purification: purification of the heart and purification of the body** — the body by *wuḍūʾ* from the minor
  state, *ghusl* from the major, and removal of impurities from body, clothes and place, **all out of
  magnifying it**; **and the heart's purification — [recite?] the *shahādatayn* after the *wuḍūʾ*** (with the
  ḥadīth of the supplication after *wuḍūʾ* and opening the eight gates of the Garden) — **so that a man enters
  his prayer and stands before his Lord with a pure heart, a pure body and a pure place.**"
* **The *ākhirah* orientation with *māliki yawmi l-dīn***: "**in this world we have kings; but in the next there
  is no owner but Allah**" (V4/V7) — and the reminder of the *takbīr*'s conquest of self-exaltation: "**when
  man ascends he may grow great in his own eye, so he says *Allāhu akbar* to bound his exaltation.**"
* **The Muslim's bearing in fear and hope** — the lesson's emphases in the session (the *duʿāʾ*'s preservation,
  the patience of the bereaved) are part of the same frame: the etiquette of the *musallī*'s standing between
  the two Names of mercy (1:3).

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & moral admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.**

* **"Knowledge alone without action profits nothing — and knowledge alone without wisdom may make a man exceed
  his bound, in either shortcoming or excess"** — and the role of the scholars: "**it is incumbent on the
  scholars, before this youthful awakening, to be leaders of these young people, guiding them to the truth
  and to the straight path and to following wisdom — and wisdom is knowledge and soundness.**"
* **The teaching on teaching**: from the Companions' ʿAṣr story — "**I thank the one who stays silent when he
  has no knowledge… but be careful to understand the truth as it is, and do not err in understanding; and do
  not take a matter superficially and then go and convey it**"; and "**whoever has learned one matter of
  Allah's religion has the proof established on him by this matter, and it is incumbent on him to act on it
  and to convey it — '*Convey from me, even one verse*'** — **and do not think that the scholar is only the one
  who holds a doctorate or graduates from a faculty: everyone who knows a matter is a scholar in it.**"
* **The *tadabbur* of the opening supplication** (the ḥikam of "distance me… as the East is distant from the
  West": **before the sin's commission, then cleanse after commission**; the white garment's showing of the
  least stain; the cold water's fitness for a fire-punished sin) — V5, ḥadīth 2.
* **On *dīn* and requital**: "**the *dīn* is the action by which a man is requited, and the requital is on the
  Day of *al-Dīn*: as you requite, so are you requited**" (V4/V7), with 17:78's dawn-recitation lesson: "**the
  recitation of the dawn is witnessed.**"
* **And on the whole sūrah**: "**if one of the people were enabled to recite this sūrah with attention,
  reflection and review of the people of knowledge's words on it, he would find in it very great meanings.**"

### V16 · Contemporary issues & practical application

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** (The session's questions, as far as they bear on the handling of Allah's Book and the
*muṣḥaf*.)

* **The written Qurʾān in mosques and on vessels** — from the question on the calligraphic verse-plates:
  "**a fatwa has been issued by the Permanent Committee for Scholarly Research and Fatwas forbidding such a
  thing; and uglier than that: small vessels are inscribed with Āyat al-Kursī, carved by drilling — in this
  there is no doubt a desecration of the Qurʾān: a vessel thrown on the ground, perhaps struck with the foot,
  perhaps taken by a child … and there is written on it one of the greatest verses — *rather it is the
  greatest verse in the Book of Allah*; Āyat al-Kursī. And the view I hold is that they are not to be hung at
  all; and if a man has any of them, that he burn them, and there is no harm in that — for the Companions
  burnt the copies of the Qurʾān on whose variant they agreed … and if burning is not possible, let him erase
  it by spraying paint over it until the writing goes.**"
* **The plaques with "(Allah)" and "(Muḥammad)" side by side** — "**the one looking at it would say that
  Allah and Muḥammad are the same, and that Muḥammad is a rival to Allah — Allah forbid! — and when a man said
  to the Prophet ﷺ 'what Allah wills and you will', he said: 'Have you made me a rival to Allah? Rather what
  Allah wills alone.'**" — *[ed.]*: **Aḥmad (1839)**, from Ibn ʿAbbās — "**this may not be hung at all; it
  must be removed and broken, for it plainly contains *shirk*.**"
* **And the two that bear on this sūrah** — the printed *muṣḥaf* numbering (S3) and the congregation's speech
  behind the imām (1:5, V10).

*The remaining questions of the session — organ transplantation, the ʿĪd greetings and grave-visiting, the
grain sale, the spacing of the *takbīr*s, the eclipse prayer, the beard — lie outside this chapter's elements
and are not filed here.*

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The sūrah's specific merit-material as the transcript gives it:

> **The *ruqyah* narrative** — "**the Companions came down, on a *sariyyah* the Prophet ﷺ had sent, upon a
> people; the Companions asked them for hospitality and they gave them nothing — and this is meanness and
> stinginess, for the Arabs in their *jāhiliyyah* honoured the guest. Then the Mighty, the Wise decreed that
> their chief be stung — a snake stung him — and they looked for one who could recite, and said: 'Perhaps
> these riders have someone who recites.' They sent for them and said: 'Is there one of you who recites?' They
> said: 'Yes.' They said: 'Recite over our master.' They said: 'We shall not recite — you neglected the duty
> of hospitality; **we shall not recite except for a wage.**' They said: 'We shall give you this flock of
> sheep.' So one of them went to the stung man and began to recite over him **Sūrat al-Fātiḥah** — **and the
> stung man rose as if released from a hobble — there was nothing but health, by the recitation of the
> Fātiḥah.** But the Companions, in their scrupulousness, said: 'We cannot eat of these sheep until we return
> to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and ask him.' So they asked the Prophet ﷺ and he said: '*Take it*' — or '*Eat,
> and assign me a share with you.*' **Why did he say 'assign me a share'? So that they be reassured; for when
> the muftī does what he gives fatwa for, that is the utmost reassurance for the one given fatwa.** — **Then
> he said to the one who recited over the stung man: '*And what made you know that it is a *ruqyah*?*'** —
> *[ed.]*: **al-Bukhārī (2276), Muslim (2201/66)** — **and so, the best thing we recite over our sick and over those of us
> afflicted in such matters is the Fātiḥah.**"
> **And the command of *āmīn*** — "**when he says *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*, say *āmīn*.**"
> **And the editor's takhrīj for this chapter's faḍāʾil**: al-Ṭabarānī (525), from Maʿqil b. Yasār — "**and I
> was given Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and the closing verses of Sūrat al-Baqarah from a treasure beneath the Throne**";
> al-Bukhārī (4474), from Abū Saʿīd b. al-Muʿallā — "***al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* is *al-Sabʿ
> al-Mathānī* and the *Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* which I was given**"; and **al-Bukhārī (2994)** for the *takbīr* on
> the ascent (V5).
> Beside the ruqyah: the sūrah's **recitation in every *rakʿah*** as its standing legal virtue (S5), and the
> ḥadīth's frame: "**a sūrah whose like has not come down in the Torah, nor the Gospel, nor the Psalms, nor
> the Qurʾān**" (S4).

## 1:2 — الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

*[context]* All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** "**When you say *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*, what does it mean?** — **it is the
complete description with the most perfect attributes, deserved by Allah, Mighty and Majestic**" ("*all of us
recite it, brothers*"); "***li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* — and what does *rabb al-ʿālamīn* mean? The Lord is the
Creator, the Owner, the Disposer; and the *ʿālam* — who are they?** — a student: '**other than Allah**' —
**the Shaykh: everything other than Allah is the *ʿālam*; so *rabb al-ʿālamīn* means all the created
things.**" — "**So we understand *rabb al-ʿālamīn*: that I describe Allah with the most perfect descriptions,
because He is the Lord of the worlds.**"

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** 15:87 (*sabʿan mina l-mathānī*) for the sūrah's identity (S1/S3); 17:78 (*aqimi l-ṣalāta…
wa-qurʾāna l-fajr*) — the Fātiḥah's standing in the prayer under the name *al-ṣalāh* (1:1, V2); 51:56 and 2:29
in the worship-teaching of 1:5; and the ḥadīth qudsī's answer for this verse: "*ḥamidanī ʿabdī* — **My servant
has praised Me**" (V5).

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The *qasamtu al-ṣalāh* in full, with its correspondences: "*when he says* *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi
rabbi l-ʿālamīn*, **Allah says: My servant has praised Me**" — *[ed.]*: Muslim (395/38) — **"and the response
comes from the Lord above His Throne, above His heavens, who hears him even when he is silent**"; and the
Shaykh's commentary: "**if it is said: there may be a thousand worshippers, one at *al-ḥamdu* and another
finishing — we say: Allah encompasses everything in mercy and knowledge, each one is answered**" (1:1, V2).

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** "**The scholars' saying on *al-ḥamd*: the complete description with the most perfect
attributes**"; "**the Lord is the Creator, the Owner, the Disposer**"; "**the *ʿālam*: everything other than
Allah**" — taught by question and answer in the class (above).

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The lexical teaching: *al-ḥamd* = **the complete description with the most perfect
attributes**; *rabb* = **the Creator, Owner, Disposer** (the three-fold lexical content); *al-ʿālam* =
**everything other than Allah**; and the *umm*'s linguistic sense — "**the *umm* of a thing is its point of
return**" — with the verse: *"Upon its head a mother for us by which we are guided"* — "**a sign toward which
we aim and intend**" (taught from this verse's opening).

### V16 · Contemporary issues & practical application

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The practical use he draws for this verse in the classroom: the sūrah's being obligatory in
every *rakʿah* "**because it gathered all the Qurʾān's meanings**" (S5) — so that when the believer says *al-ḥamdu
li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*, he is making the Qurʾān's whole praise his own (S5).

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V7, V8, V16._

## 1:3 — الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* The Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** "***Al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm* — what does it mean? The scholars said: *al-Raḥmān* means vast in
mercy** — **because the form *faʿlān* indicates fullness and breadth: when we see a man whose jugulars swell
and whose eyes redden, we say *ghaḍbān* — filled with wrath** — **so *al-Raḥmān* is the Owner of vast
mercy**; and ***al-Raḥīm* is the One who conveys mercy to whom He wills.**" — "**So in these two noble Names
are gathered Allah's being described with the breadth of mercy, and that this mercy has reached His servants,
believers and unbelievers — for Allah shows mercy to whom He wills and punishes whom He wills: the general
mercy, which is this world's mercy, is for believers and unbelievers — the unbeliever is shown mercy, given
provision, support and preparation — but in the next world he has no mercy.**"

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** 29:21: *yuʿadhdhibu man yashāʾu wa-yarḥamu man yashāʾ* — for the two Names' reach; and 7:156:
*wa-raḥmatī wasiʿat kulla shayʾin fa-sa-aktubuhā li-lladhīna yattaqūn* — "**My mercy encompasses all things;
I shall prescribe it for those who are mindful of Me…**" — the general mercy of this world, and the special
mercy written for the God-fearing in the next.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** In the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh* correspondence: "*when he says* *al-Raḥmāni l-Raḥīm*, **Allah
says: My servant has extolled Me**" — and his exposition: "**now he increased in description, describing Allah
as possessor of a mercy vast and reaching the one shown mercy — so in *al-Raḥmān* is a sign of mercy's breadth
and in *al-Raḥīm* a sign of its reaching the one shown mercy**" — *[ed.]*: Muslim (395/38).

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The morphological teaching: "**the form *faʿlān* indicates fullness and breadth
(*al-imtilāʾ wa-l-saʿah*)** — *ghaḍbān* for one full of wrath" — so *al-Raḥmān* is **of vast mercy**, and
*al-Raḥīm* **the conveyor of it**; and the pairing's distribution — **breadth in the first Name, conveyance in
the second.**

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V8._

## 1:4 — مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

*[context]* Master of the Day of Judgement.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** "*Māliki yawmi l-dīn* — **what is the Day of *al-Dīn*? It is the Day of Resurrection, in
which is the requital of deeds** (all of you recite it, brothers). — **And the question: does Allah not own
this world? He owns this world and the religion — everything He owns — but He specified the Day of *al-Dīn*
because on that day no one has ownership** — as Allah said: *li-mani l-mulku l-yawm? li-llāhi l-wāḥidi
l-qahhār* (40:16): **in this world we have kings whom Allah has appointed over us, but in the next world there
is no owner except Allah, Mighty and Majestic; among creation there is no owner at all — and for that we say
*māliki yawmi l-dīn*.**"

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** 40:16 (*li-mani l-mulku l-yawm…*) for the specification; 82:17–19 (*wa-mā adrāka mā yawmu
l-dīn… yawma lā tamliku nafsun li-nafsin shayʾan wa-l-amru yawmaʾidhin li-llāh*) — "**the *dīn* in these verses
is the requital of deeds**"; 5:3 (*al-yawma akmaltu lakum dīnakum* — "**the *dīn* here is the action**"); and
the proverb **"as you requite, so are you requited"** (*kamā tadīnu tudānu*) — the lexical bridge between the
verse's two senses of *dīn*.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** "*When he says* *māliki yawmi l-dīn*, **Allah says: My servant has magnified Me** (*majjadanī
ʿabdī*) — **i.e., he described Me with *al-majd*, which is greatness**" — *[ed.]*: Muslim (395/38).

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The verse's exposition as above: **the day of requital**; the *ikhtiṣāṣ* of the day's
mention; and **what the verse entails**: "**do you know what our saying *māliki yawmi l-dīn* entails? It entails
faith in the Last Day and faith in the requital of deeds, and that the One who requites deeds is Allah, the
Owner of the Day of *al-Dīn*.**"

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** *Al-dīn*'s two lexical senses deployed by the lesson — **action** (5:3) and **requital**
(82:17–19, 1:4) — with the proverb *kamā tadīnu tudānu*; and the sense of *al-majd* as **greatness** in the
Ḥadīth's answer (*majjadanī ʿabdī*).

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** "***Māliki yawmi l-dīn* entails faith in the Last Day and faith in the requital of deeds,
and that the requiter is Allah**" — the verse's creedal content; and the *tawḥīd* in *mulk*: **ownership of
the day is exclusively His** ("**there is no owner among creation at all**"), with 40:16.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V7, V8, V11._

## 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

*[context]* You alone we worship, and You alone we seek help.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn** — the lesson's most extended teaching (by question and answer):

> "**I ask: what is *al-ʿibādah* (worship)? And I do not permit the students of knowledge to answer it** — [a
> student offers] 'like such' and the Shaykh: **give it by example** — (*a student: the prayer*) — **excellent:
> the prayer is worship — and alms, fasting, pilgrimage, *wuḍūʾ* — all worship. So from these many examples we
> infer that *all that Allah has commanded, for drawing near to Him, is worship*. And the words *for drawing
> near to Him* [are added] because Allah may command us with a thing not for drawing near to Him but for our own
> benefit — *kulū wa-shrabū wa-lā tusrifū* (7:31), *wa-idhā ḥalaltum fa-ṣṭādū* (5:2) — the base of such
> commands is permissibility.**
> **Yet with that, eating and drinking may become worship.** How can you make your dinner tonight worship? —
> **when you eat in compliance with Allah's command, it is worship**; when you eat to protect yourself from
> harm, it is worship — *wa-lā taqtulū anfusakum* (4:29); **when you eat to enjoy Allah's blessing on you, is
> that worship? Yes — because Allah, when He blesses His servant with a blessing, loves to see the trace of His
> blessing on him; and because a generous man, when his generosity is eaten, loves that.** If a generous man
> sets food before you, does he love that you eat it and not leave it? Yes — **whereas a miser, if he set bread
> before you, we would say: let every two eat one loaf, so that he get back the rest.** — **So the generous one
> loves that people enjoy his generosity, and you, when you eat, [you enjoy] Allah's generosity — and this
> without doubt draws you nearer to Allah.**"
> **And *wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn* — what does *nastaʿīn* mean?** Your car is stuck in the sands — you see two or
> three men walking and you say: 'come and help me with it' — what is this called? *istiʿānah*. **So *iyyāka
> nastaʿīn* means: we ask help from You — over worship and over all our affairs.**
> — **And the ordering with the image of the caravan** (his teaching on the ḥadīth *'the strong one is not by
> wrestling…'* and *'the prayer is the *munājāh*'*): the servant's two clauses, **worship for Allah and help
> for the servant** — and in the ʿaqīdah's core, **"'there is no might nor power except with Allah' explains
> *iyyāka nastaʿīn*: 'If Allah does not strengthen you for His obedience, you cannot.'"**

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** 7:31 (*kulū wa-shrabū wa-lā tusrifū*) and 5:2 — the permissive commands that may turn to
worship by intention; 4:29 (*lā taqtulū anfusakum*); 51:56 (*wa-mā khalaqtu l-jinna wa-l-insa illā li-yaʿbudūn*)
— "**we were not created for what is in the earth; we were created for His worship**" — with 2:29 (*huwa
lladhī khalaqa lakum mā fī l-arḍi jamīʿan*): "**'He created for you what is in the earth' — and He did not say
'He created you for what is in the earth'**" — and, for *istiʿānah* and the worship's regime, 17:78 and the
*qasamtu al-ṣalāh* framework (1:1, V2).

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The instruction of the ḥadīth al-Bukhārī (the *tasḥḥīr*): "**'Take the *saḥūr*, for in the
*saḥūr* is blessing'**" — cited for the worship-character of eating with the intent of strength for obedience;
and the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh* correspondence for this verse: "*iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn* — **This is
between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks**" (1:1, V5).

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The definition: "**worship is a comprehensive name for everything Allah has commanded for
drawing near to Him**" — and the **two-clause distribution**: "***iyyāka naʿbudu* — for Allah; *iyyāka
nastaʿīn* — for the creature: you seek His help over all your affairs.**"

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The fronting of the object and the tense of the verb: — "***iyyāka nastaʿīn* is more
eloquent than 'we sought help from Allah' in two ways: first, the object *iyyāka* is placed first, and the
fronting of the object indicates restriction and specification — as though you said: we seek help from none but
You; second, that 'we sought help' is a past verb while *nastaʿīn* is a present verb indicating continuity —
and man is one who seeks Allah's help always and forever.**"

### V10 · Juristic rulings & injunctions (aḥkām)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn** (the Q&A ruling, from the session, on what is said behind the imām):

> "**And some of the common people, when the imām says *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*, say 'istaʿanā
> bi-llāh' — do not say it: nothing is reported on it, and the Companions did not say it behind their Prophet;
> then, the imām's recitation will have you say *āmīn* upon it, as though you yourself said *iyyāka nastaʿīn* —
> and *iyyāka nastaʿīn* is more eloquent than *istaʿannā bi-llāh*.**"

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The verse as the core of *tawḥīd*: worship confined to Allah (*iyyāka naʿbudu*) and help
sought from Him alone (*iyyāka nastaʿīn*) — with his teaching: "**'there is no might nor power except with
Allah' is the explanation of *iyyāka nastaʿīn*: if Allah does not strengthen you for His obedience, you are
not able**"; and the created-for-worship frame: *wa-mā khalaqtu l-jinna wa-l-insa illā li-yaʿbudūn* (51:56).

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The verse's *ādāb* as he draws them: the servant's abandonment of his own might and power
(*istaʿānah* as *tabarrī*), the ingratitude-nullifying remembrance ("**a generous one loves that his
generosity be enjoyed**"), and the practical teaching on eating, drinking and rest becoming worship by
intention — the whole as **the purification of the ordinary act into nearness.**

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V7, V8, V10, V11, V14._

## 1:6 — اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ

*[context]* Guide us to the straight path.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** "*Ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm* — **guide us**, and the Shaykh asks: **why did He say *ihdinā
l-ṣirāṭ* and not *ihdinā ilā l-ṣirāṭ*, though He said to His Prophet *wa-innaka la-tahdī ilā ṣirāṭin
mustaqīm* (42:52) and also *wa-yahdiyaka ṣirāṭan mustaqīman* (48:2)?** — **The difference between *hadāhu ilā
l-ṣirāṭ* and *hadāhu l-ṣirāṭ*: the first is that he directed him to it only; the second is that he guided him
to it and in it — and for that the preposition was deleted, so that it be general. So the meaning of *ihdinā
l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm* is: direct us to it and grant us success in acting on it — so that in this supplication
you are asking Allah for beneficial knowledge and then righteous action.**"
"**Then *al-mustaqīm* — and is there a crooked path? Yes: everything that departs from Allah's religion is a
crooked path** — *wa-anna hādhā ṣirāṭī mustaqīman fa-ttabiʿūh wa-lā tattabiʿū l-subula fa-tafarraqa bikum ʿan
sabīlihi* (6:153) — **so everything that takes you out from Allah's way is not the straight path.**"

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** 42:52 (*wa-innaka la-tahdī ilā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm*) and 48:2 (*wa-yahdiyaka ṣirāṭan
mustaqīman*) — the two constructions behind the deletion of *ilā*; 6:153 (*wa-anna hādhā ṣirāṭī
mustaqīman*) — "**every way out from Allah's way is not the straight path**"; and 15:87's *Sabʿ al-Mathānī*
for the verse's place in the count (S3).

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** In the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh* correspondence: "*ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm* — **Allah says:
This is for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks**" — *[ed.]*: Muslim (395/38).

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The two-clause exposition above (*direct us to it and in it*) as the received sense — with
the request's content: **beneficial knowledge and righteous action.**

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The grammatical crux: **the deletion of the preposition with *hadā* — "he guided him the
path" = he guided him to it *and in it*** — set against the two *Qurʾānic* constructions with *ilā* (42:52) and
without it (48:2); and *al-mustaqīm*'s sense (**the un-crooked**), with the *iʿrāb* of the verse's flow:
*ihdinā* governing the accusative *al-ṣirāṭ*, described by *al-mustaqīm*.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V7, V8._

## 1:7 — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

*[context]* The path of those whom You have blessed — not of those who incurred wrath, nor of those who are astray.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** "**Who are those Allah has blessed?** — a student answers with 4:69 — **the Shaykh: yes: the
Qurʾān explains one part of it by another; *those Allah has blessed* are the ones mentioned in *wa-man yuṭiʿi
llāha wa-l-rasūla fa-ulāʾika maʿa lladhīna anʿama llāhu ʿalayhim mina l-nabiyyīna wa-l-ṣiddīqīna wa-l-shuhadāʾi
wa-l-ṣāliḥīn* — four classes: the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, the righteous.**" — "**Then *ghayri
l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* — one may say: is not *ṣirāṭa lladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim* enough, since
those Allah has blessed are the four classes who knew the truth and acted on it? So how does *ghayri
l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* come? We say: **this is by way of confirming the perfection of what is
affirmed** — so that there be in this path not even a small part of the path of those wrath is upon and of the
astray. **And I think many students of knowledge now understand that Allah's negative attributes entail the
affirmation of the perfection of their opposite** — so *wa-lā yaẓlimu rabbuka aḥadā*: **for the perfection of
His justice there is no wrong in His act nor His judgement** — **so *ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim* comes for the
perfection of the affirmation in *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm*, so that in this guidance there be nothing of
straying nor anything of what brings wrath.**"
"**And the divisions of people are three: those Allah has blessed — who knew the truth and acted on it; those
wrath is upon — who knew the truth and did not act on it, at their head the Jews; and the astray — who were
ignorant of the truth and acted on falsehood: the Christians**" (S5).

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** 4:69 — the four classes of the blessed (with the lesson: "**the prophets — and the
messengers enter among them, for every messenger is a prophet and not the reverse; the truthful, as Abū Bakr;
the martyrs, as ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, ʿAlī and many others — and this by way of example**"); 18:49 (*wa-lā yaẓlimu
rabbuka aḥadā*) — his instance of the negative attribute entailing the perfection of its opposite; 33:69 (*lā
takūnū ka-lladhīna ādhaw mūsā*) — in the Mūsā narrative (V13); and 2:137 (*fa-sa-yakfīkahumu llāhu*) — the
verse on which ʿUthmān's blood fell (V6).

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** **The Uḥud ḥadīth**: "**the Prophet ﷺ climbed Uḥud and it shook with him, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar
and ʿUthmān — and he said: '*Be still, Uḥud, for upon you is only a prophet, a truthful one and two martyrs*'**
— *[ed.]*: al-Bukhārī (3699) from Anas"; **the Mūsā-and-the-stone ḥadīth** — *[ed.]*: al-Bukhārī (3404),
Muslim (339/75); **the ḥadīth *'Uḥud is a mountain that loves us and we love it'*** — *[ed.]*: al-Bukhārī
(2889), Muslim (1365/462); **the *Banū Qurayẓah* instruction** — "**'Let none pray ʿAṣr except among Banū
Qurayẓah'**"; **"*Convey from me, even one verse*"**; **the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh* correspondence for this verse**:
"*ihdinā… wa-lā l-ḍāllīn* — **This is for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks**" — *[ed.]*:
Muslim (395/38); and **the *āmīn* instruction** — "**when he says *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*, say *āmīn***."

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** From this verse's "those blessed": **Abū Bakr** — *al-ṣiddīq* in the Uḥud ḥadīth; **ʿUmar**
— "**the martyr of the prayer: he was stabbed while before his Lord**", and the story of his levelling the rows
and his saying *"the dog has eaten me"* — "**not the beast; it is ʿAbū Luʾluʾa the Magian… and ʿUmar is a
martyr of the prayer**"; **ʿUthmān** — "**the martyr of the *muṣḥaf*: the Khawārij entered upon him while he
recited and killed him in his house**"; and the Companions' **differing in understanding** over the ʿAṣr
instruction, with the Shaykh's verdict: **the one who hits the mark is the one who prayed in time** (V13).

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The three-class reading (above) and the authorities behind it: **Sufyān b. ʿUyaynah** —
"**whoever of our scholars is corrupt, in him is a resemblance to the Jews; and whoever of our worshippers is
corrupt, in him is a resemblance to the Christians**" — and the Shaykh's exposition:

> "**The one of the scholars who is corrupt has known the truth and opposed it — and we ask Allah to guide us
> and you to His straight path; and those of the worshippers who are corrupt are eager for good and seek
> worship but are ignorant, worshipping Allah on error — those resemble the Christians.** — **And what is your
> duty, O believer in Allah and His Messenger? Your duty is to learn in order to act — to learn to come out of
> resembling whom? The Christians; and to act to come out of resembling the Jews. — Woe to the scholars when
> they do not act on what they know! Woe to them! They are the semblances of the Jews; they are the root of
> straying — for the common people follow whom? their scholars: **if the scholars stray, they make others
> stray.**" — and the teaching: "**do not think the scholar is only the one who holds a doctorate or graduates
> from a college, or who keeps the company of scholars — everyone who knows a matter is a scholar in it; and
> for that our Imām and Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ said: '*Convey from me, even one verse*'… and whoever has learned
> one matter of Allah's religion, the proof stands against him by it, and he must act on it and convey it.**"

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The rhetorical grammar of the negation: "**this is by way of confirming the perfection of
what is affirmed**" (the **taʾkīd** effect of *ghayri… wa-lā…*), and the principle he draws: "**Allah's
negative attributes (*al-ṣifāt al-salbiyyah*) entail the affirmation of the perfection of their opposite**" —
with 18:49 as his instance; and, on *al-ḍalāl*'s lexical content, the *ḍāllīn* as "**those ignorant of the
truth who acted on falsehood**" (S5).

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The principle taught on this verse: "**Allah's negative attributes entail the affirmation of
the perfection of their opposite**" — applied to *wa-lā yaẓlimu rabbuka aḥadā* (18:49) and to *ghayri
l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*: **the exclusion is for the perfection of the guidance asked in 1:6** — and
the creedal warning against resembling the two parties' paths.

### V13 · Historical narratives, sīrah & parables (qiṣaṣ · sīrah)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** **The Uḥud scene** (with the mountain's shaking, and its gloss: "**it shook out of joy at
them, or to display a sign of Allah's signs**"); **the Mūsā and stone narrative** (filed also at 1:1, V13);
**the martyrdom of ʿUmar** (the *fajr* stabbing, ʿAbū Luʾluʾa the Magian, "**the Magians' rancour against the
religion of Islam, especially against ʿUmar, by whose hand Allah brought down Kisrā's palace**"; the narrators'
report of the drop of blood on 2:137); **the martyrdom of ʿUthmān** by the Khawārij while he recited; and **the
Banū Qurayẓah expedition** with the Companions' two-fold understanding.

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & moral admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The lesson's admonitions from this verse: "**woe to the scholars who do not act on what they
know — they are the semblances of the Jews and the root of straying, for the common people follow their
scholars**"; "**learn to act, and act so as to leave the resemblance of the Jews; and beware of ignorance in
worship, for that is the resemblance of the Christians**"; "**be careful to understand the truth as it is and
not to err in understanding — even the Companions could differ in understanding**"; "**do not take a matter
superficially and then convey it**"; "**I thank the one who stays silent when he has no knowledge**"; and
"**whoever has learned a matter must act on it and convey it**."

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Ibn ʿUthaymīn.** The verse's supplication-practice: **the *āmīn* at *wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*** — "**when he says
*wa-lā l-ḍāllīn*, say *āmīn***"; and the Fātiḥah's own rank as the *ruqyah* recited over the sick (1:1, V17),
where the verse's "**and My servant shall have what he asks**" completes the sūrah's asking.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V6, V7, V8, V11, V13, V15, V17._
