**Source work:** *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* — Ismāʿīl ibn ʿUmar Ibn Kathīr (d. 774/1373), in the English rendering of the corpus file (Abridged edition's translation).
**Corpus file:** `tafsir-ibn-kathir/001.txt` (English, with the Arabic of the ḥadīths retained).
**Extraction:** English as given; ḥadīth wording kept verbatim with its Arabic where the file carries it and its collection named; the author's argument condensed to its essential claim.

## Sūrah-level elements

### S1 · Sūrah identification, names & titles (asmāʾ al-sūrah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The sūrah is called:

* **al-Fātiḥah** — “the Opener of the Book, the Surah with which prayers are begun.”
* **Umm al-Kitāb** (the Mother of the Book) — “according to the majority of the scholars”; and **Umm al-Qurʾān**.
  Al-Bukhārī said: “It is called Umm Al-Kitab, because the Qur'an starts with it and because the prayer is
  started by reciting it.” Ibn Jarīr added that “the Arabs call every comprehensive matter that contains
  several specific areas an *Umm*” — the membrane around the brain (*Umm al-Raʾs*), the flag that gathers
  the army's ranks, and Makkah as *Umm al-Qurā*, “the grandest and the leader of all villages”.
* **al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī** (the seven oft-repeated), **al-Ḥamd** and **al-Ṣalāh** — “because the Prophet ﷺ
  said that his Lord said: *The prayer is divided into two halves between Me and My servants…*”; and
  “Al-Fatihah was called the Salah, because reciting it is a condition for the correctness of Salah — the prayer.”
* **al-Shifāʾ** (the Cure) and **al-Ruqyah** (remedy) — “since in the Sahih, there is the narration of Abū
  Saʿīd telling the story of the Companion who used Al-Fatihah as a remedy for the tribal chief who was
  poisoned”, after which the Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked: “*How did you know that it is a Ruqyah?*”

**Ḥadīth cited for the names.** At-Tirmidhī, who graded it Ṣaḥīḥ, from Abū Hurayrah: the Messenger of
Allah ﷺ said: “*Al-Ḥamdu lillāhi Rabbil-ʿālamīn* is the Mother of the Qur'an, the Mother of the Book, and
the seven repeated Ayat of the Glorious Qur'an.” Imam Ahmad recorded from Abū Hurayrah: “It is Umm
Al-Qur'an, the seven repeated (verses) and the Glorious Qur'an”; and aṭ-Ṭabarī recorded from Abū Hurayrah:
“It is Umm Al-Qur'an, Al-Fatihah of the Book and the seven repeated (verses).”

### S2 · Period & place of revelation (Makkī/Madanī)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “Al-Fatihah was revealed in Makkah as Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah and Abū al-ʿĀliyah stated” — with
the supporting verse, allāh said: “*And indeed, We have bestowed upon you the seven Mathani (seven
repeatedly recited verses)* (15:87).”

### S3 · Verse, word & letter counts (ʿadad al-āyāt)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “There is no disagreement over the view that Al-Fatihah contains seven Ayat. According to
the majority of the reciters of Al-Kufah, a group of the Companions, the Tābiʿīn, and a number of scholars
from the successive generations, the Bismillah is a separate Ayah in its beginning.” And: “The scholars
say that Al-Fatihah consists of twenty-five words, and that it contains one hundred and thirteen letters.”

### S4 · Virtues & merits of the sūrah (faḍāʾil al-sūrah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The merits are established by a series of ḥadīths:

* **The greatest sūrah.** Imam Ahmad in the *Musnad*, from Abū Saʿīd bin al-Muʿallā: “I will teach you
  the greatest Surah in the Qur'an before you leave the Masjid” — and it was *al-Ḥamdu lillāhi
  Rabbil-ʿālamīn*; “It is the seven repeated (verses) and the Glorious Qur'an that I was given.” Al-Bukhārī,
  Abū Dāwūd, an-Nasāʾī and Ibn Mājah also recorded it.
* **Nothing like it revealed.** From Ubayy bin Kaʿb: “Would you like me to teach you a Surah the likes of
  which nothing has been revealed in the Tawrah, the Injil, the Zabur or the Furqan?” — and: “By Him in
  Whose Hand is my soul! Allah has never revealed in the Tawrah, the Injil, the Zabur or the Furqan a Surah
  like it. It is the seven repeated verses that I was given.”
* **Divided between Allah and His servant.** At-Tirmidhī and an-Nasāʾī from Ubayy bin Kaʿb: “Allah has
  never revealed in the Tawrah or the Injil anything similar to Umm Al-Qur'an. It is the seven repeated
  verses and it is divided into two halves between Allah and His servant.”
* **The best sūrah.** From Ibn Jābir: “Should I inform you of the best Surah in the Qur'an? … Read, *All
  praise be to Allah, the Lord of the existence*, until you finish it.”
* **Healing.** Al-Bukhārī, in the chapter on the virtues of the Qurʾān, from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī: the
  Companion who read *Umm al-Kitāb* as a ruqyah for the poisoned chief, and the tribe's gift of thirty
  sheep. Ibn Kathīr concludes that “some scholars relied on this Hadith as evidence that some Ayat and
  Surahs have more virtues than others”.

### S5 · Central theme & summary of contents (maqāṣid)

**Ibn Kathīr.** In “The Summary of Al-Fatihah” he writes that the sūrah “contains seven Ayat including the
praise and thanks of Allah, glorifying Him and praising Him by mentioning His most Beautiful Names and
most high Attributes. It also mentions the Hereafter, which is the Day of Resurrection, and directs
Allah's servants to ask of Him, invoking Him and declaring that all power and strength comes from Him. It
also calls to the sincerity of the worship of Allah alone, singling Him out in His divinity, believing in
His perfection, being free from the need of any partners, having no rivals nor equals.”

It “directs the believers to invoke Allah to guide them to the straight path, which is the true religion,
and to help them remain on that path in this life, and to pass over the actual Sirat on the Day of
Judgment”; it “encourages performing good deeds, so that the believers will be in the company of the
good-doers on the Day of Resurrection”; and it “warns against following the paths of misguidance… including
those who have earned the anger and those who were led astray.”

## 1:1 — بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* In the Name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.

### V1 · Qurʾānic text & translation

**Ibn Kathīr.** “The Companions started the Book of Allah with Bismillah”, and “the scholars also agree
that Bismillah is a part of an Ayah in Surat An-Naml (chapter 27). They disagree over whether it is a
separate Ayah before every Surah, or if it is an Ayah, or a part of an Ayah, included in every Surah where
the Bismillah appears in its beginning.” The positions he reports: that the basmalah is an āyah of every
sūrah except al-Barāʾah — attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn az-Zubayr, Abū Hurayrah and ʿAlī, and
among the Tābiʿīn to ʿAṭāʾ, Ṭāwūs, Saʿīd bin Jubayr, Makḥūl and az-Zuhrī; the view of ʿAbdullāh bin
al-Mubārak, ash-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad bin Ḥanbal, Isḥāq bin Rāhwayh and Abū ʿUbayd al-Qāsim bin Sallām; and the
opposing view of Mālik and Abū Ḥanīfah and their followers, “that Bismillah is not an Ayah in Al-Fatihah
or any other Surah”; while Dāwūd held it “a separate Ayah in the beginning of every Surah, not part of the
Surah itself”.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The three names of the basmalah are expounded:

* **Allah** — “the Name of the Lord, the Exalted. It is said that Allah is the Greatest Name of Allah,
  because it is referred to when describing Allah by the various attributes”, as in 59:22–24: *He is Allah,
  beside Whom none has the right to be worshipped but He, the Knower of the unseen and the seen… To Him
  belong the Best Names*; and 7:180: *And all the Most Beautiful Names belong to Allah, so call on Him by
  them*; and 17:110: *Invoke Allah or invoke the Most Gracious… to Him belong the Best Names.*
* **Ar-Raḥmān, Ar-Raḥīm** — “two names derived from *Ar-Rahmah* (the mercy), but *Raḥmān* has more
  meanings that pertain to mercy than *Ar-Raḥīm*”; Ibn Jarīr reports consensus on this. Al-Qurṭubī said:
  “The proof that these names are derived (from *Ar-Rahmah*) is what At-Tirmidhī recorded — and graded
  Ṣaḥīḥ — from ʿAbdur-Raḥmān bin ʿAwf that he heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: *Allah the Exalted
  said, ‘I Am Ar-Raḥmān. I created the Raḥam (womb, i.e. family relations) and derived a name for it from
  My Name…’*” — “This is a text that indicates the derivation.” And al-Qurṭubī again: “The Arabs denied the
  name *Ar-Raḥmān*, because of their ignorance about Allah and His attributes.”
* **The scope of each name** — “He is *Ar-Raḥmān* with all creation and *Ar-Raḥīm* with the believers.”
  Ibn Kathīr gathers: “*Ar-Raḥmān* carries a broader scope of meanings pertaining to the mercy of Allah
  with His creation in both lives. Meanwhile, *Ar-Raḥīm* is exclusively for the believers”, citing
  *And He is ever Raḥīm (merciful) to the believers* (33:43), while noting the supplication “*The Raḥmān
  and the Raḥīm of this life and the Hereafter*”. He concludes: “Allah first mentioned His Name — Allah —
  that is exclusively His and described this Name by *Ar-Raḥmān*, which no one else is allowed to use.”

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** 59:22–24 and 7:180 and 17:110 for the name *Allah* and the Best Names; 33:43 for
*Raḥīm* and the believers; 25:59 and 20:5 (*Ar-Raḥmān rose over the Throne*) to show that “His mercy
encompasses all of His creation”; 43:45; and for the meaning of *al-rajīm*, 67:5, 37:6–10, 15:16–18.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “The Two Sahihs recorded that Abū Hurayrah said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: *Allah
has ninety-nine Names, one hundred minus one; whoever counts (and preserves) them will enter Paradise*.”
And, on the merit of the basmalah, Imam Ahmad recorded that a man riding behind the Prophet ﷺ said,
“The Prophet's animal tripped, so I said, ‘Cursed Shaytan.’ The Prophet said: *Do not say, ‘Cursed
Shaytan,’ for if you say these words, Satan becomes arrogant and says, ‘With my strength I made him fall.’
When you say, ‘Bismillah,’ Satan will become as small as a fly.*” An-Nasāʾī and Ibn Mardūyah add the
wording: “*Do not say these words, because then Satan becomes larger; as large as a house. Rather, say,
‘Bismillah’, because Satan then becomes as small as a fly.*”

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** On the derivation (*ishtiqāq*): “*Ar-Raḥmān* and *Ar-Raḥīm* are two names derived from
*Ar-Raḥmah* (the mercy)”, and the ḥadīth of the womb is adduced as “a text that indicates the derivation”.
Al-Qurṭubī is quoted: “It was said that both *Ar-Raḥmān* and *Ar-Raḥīm* have the same meaning, such as
the words *Nadman* and *Nadim*, as Abū ʿUbayd has stated. Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī said: ‘*Ar-Raḥmān*, which is
exclusively for Allah, is a name that encompasses every type of mercy that Allah has. *Ar-Raḥīm* is what
effects the believers’”; and Ibn ʿAbbās: “They are two soft names, one of them is softer than the other
(meaning it carries more implications of mercy).”

### V10 · Juristic rulings (aḥkām)

**Ibn Kathīr.** On reciting the basmalah aloud in the prayer, he sets out the positions and their proofs:
ash-Shāfiʿī and many of the Companions, the Tābiʿīn and the imams held that it is recited aloud with
al-Fātiḥah — with the report that Abū Hurayrah recited it aloud and said, “Among you, I perform the prayer
that is the closest to the prayer of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ”, and the ḥadīth of Umm Salamah that the
Prophet ﷺ “used to distinguish each Ayah during his recitation”. Against that, “other scholars stated that
the Bismillah should not be recited aloud in the prayer, and this is the established practice of the Four
Khalifahs… It is also the Madhhab (view) of Abū Ḥanīfah, Ath-Thawrī and Aḥmad bin Ḥanbal. Imam Mālik
stated that the Bismillah is not recited aloud or silently”, their proof being ʿĀʾishah's report that the
Prophet ﷺ “used to start the prayer by reciting the Takbir and then recite *Al-Ḥamdu lillāhi
Rabbil-ʿālamīn*”, and Anas's report that the Prophet ﷺ, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān “used to start their
prayer with *Al-Ḥamdu lillāhi Rabbil-ʿālamīn*”. He closes: “These are the opinions held by the respected
Imams, and their statements are similar in that they agree that the prayer of those who recite Al-Fatihah
aloud or in secret is correct.”

On the **istiʿādhah**: “The majority of the scholars state that reciting the *Istiʿādhah* (in the prayer
and when reciting the Qur'an) is recommended and not required, and therefore, not reciting it does not
constitute a sin. However, Ar-Rāzī recorded that ʿAṭāʾ bin Abī Rabāḥ said that the *Istiʿādhah* is required
in the prayer and when one reads the Qur'an.”

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The exclusivity of the names: “there are several of Allah's Names that are used as names
for others besides Allah. Further, some of Allah's Names are exclusive for Allah alone, such as *Allah*,
*Ar-Raḥmān*, *Al-Khāliq* (the Creator), *Ar-Rāziq* (the Sustainer), and so forth.” And the doctrinal rule
he draws: “Hence, Allah started the *Tasmiyah* with His Name, *Allah*, and described Himself as *Ar-Raḥmān*,
which is softer and more general than *Ar-Raḥīm*. The most honorable Names are mentioned first, just as
Allah did here.” On *Allāh* he notes it is said to be “the Greatest Name of Allah, because it is referred
to when describing Allah by the various attributes”. He also reports the fate of Musaylimah: “when
Musaylimah the Liar called himself the *Raḥmān* of Yamāmah, Allah made him known by the name ‘Liar’ and
exposed him… Only Musaylimah and those who followed his misguided ways described Musaylimah by
*Ar-Raḥmān*.”

### V13 · History, sīrah & parables

**Ibn Kathīr.** Two narrations are carried in this section: the report of ʿUmar bin al-Khaṭṭāb, who rode a
*Berthawn* (huge camel) “which started to proceed arrogantly… ʿUmar dismounted the animal and said, ‘By
Allah! You have carried me on a Shaytan’”; and the Qurʾānic history of the devil's enmity — Adam and
Ḥawwāʾ driven from Paradise (7:27), *Surely, Shaytan is an enemy to you, so take him as an enemy* (35:6),
18:50, and Iblīs's vow, *By Your might, then I will surely mislead them all, except Your chosen servants
among them* (38:82–83).

### V14 · Spiritual purification & etiquette (tazkiyah · ādāb)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The section on *istiʿādhah* — recorded here because it belongs to the etiquette of
recitation, not to the words of 1:1 — establishes its meaning and merits: “*Istiʿādhah* means, ‘I seek
refuge with Allah from the cursed Satan so that he is prevented from affecting my religious or worldly
affairs, or hindering me from adhering to what I was commanded, or luring me into what I was prohibited
from.’” Its virtues: “The *Istiʿādhah* cleanses the mouth from the foul speech that it has indulged in. It
also purifies the mouth and prepares it to recite the speech of Allah. Further, the *Istiʿādhah* entails
seeking Allah's help and acknowledging His ability to do everything. The *Istiʿādhah* also affirms the
servant's meekness, weakness and inability to face the enemy of his inner evil, whom Allah alone, Who
created this enemy, is able to repel and defeat.” It is said “before you recite the Qur'an”, with the
proofs of 16:98–100 and 5:6; and when one is angry, as in the two men who disputed before the Prophet ﷺ
and the words “*I know of a statement which if he said it, will make what he feels disappear: I seek
refuge with Allah from the cursed Satan.*”

The basmalah is likewise an etiquette of every act: “Basmalah is recommended before starting any action or
deed” — before the *khutbah*, before entering the place of relieving oneself, before ablution (*There is
no valid ablution for he who did not mention Allah's Name in it*), before eating (*Say Bismillah, eat with
your right hand and eat from whatever is next to you*), and before marital relations (*In the Name of
Allah. O Allah! Protect us from Satan and also protect what you grant us…*).

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The merit of saying the basmalah over any act: “This is the blessing of reciting
Bismillah.” And the supplication with which the Prophet ﷺ opened the night prayer, recorded by Imam
Aḥmad from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī — “*All praise is due to You, O Allah, and also the thanks. Blessed be
Your Name, Exalted be Your sovereignty, and there is no deity worthy of worship except You*” — followed by
“*I seek refuge with Allah, the Hearing, the Knowing, from the cursed Satan, from his coercion, lures to
arrogance and poems.*”

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V8, V10, V11, V13, V14, V17._

## 1:2 — الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

*[context]* All praise and thanks be to Allah, the Lord of all that exists.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** Abū Jaʿfar bin Jarīr said: “The meaning of *Al-Ḥamdu Lillāh* (all praise and thanks be to
Allah) is: all thanks are due purely to Allah, alone, not any of the objects that are being worshipped
instead of Him, nor any of His creation. These thanks are due to Allah's innumerable favors and bounties,
that only He knows the amount of… All thanks and praise are due to Allah for these favors from beginning
to end.” Ibn Jarīr also said it means “a praise that Allah praised Himself with, indicating to His
servants that they too should praise Him, as if Allah had said, ‘Say: All thanks and praise is due to
Allah’”, and that the statement “entails praising Allah by mentioning His most beautiful Names and most
honorable Attributes”.

On *Ar-Rabb*: “the owner who has full authority over his property… linguistically means the master or the
one who has the authority to lead. All of these meanings are correct for Allah. When it is alone, the word
*Rabb* is used only for Allah”, and “it was reported that *Ar-Rabb* is Allah's Greatest Name”. On
*al-ʿālamīn*: “*Al-ʿālamīn* is plural for *ʿĀlam*, which encompasses everything in existence except
Allah… The *ʿĀlamīn* are different creations that exist in the heavens and the earth, on land and at sea.
Every generation of creation is called an *ʿĀlam*.”

*The difference between praise and thanks:* “*Ḥamd* is more general, in that it is a statement of praise
for one's characteristics, or for what he has done. Thanks are given for what was done, not merely for
characteristics.”

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** On *al-ʿālamīn*, 26:23–24: *Firʿawn said: “And what is the Lord of the ʿālamīn?” Mūsā
said: “The Lord of the heavens and the earth, and all that is between them, if you seek to be convinced
with certainty”* — showing the scope of the term.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The virtues of *al-ḥamd*, recorded in this section:

* Imam Aḥmad from al-Aswad bin Sarīʿ: “I said, ‘O Messenger of Allah! Should I recite to you words of
  praise for My Lord that I have collected?’ He said: *Verily, your Lord likes Al-Hamd.*”
* At-Tirmidhī, an-Nasāʾī and Ibn Mājah from Jābir bin ʿAbdullāh: “*The best Dhikr is Lā ilāha illallāh
  and the best supplication is Al-Ḥamdu lillāh.*”
* Ibn Mājah from Anas bin Mālik: “*No servant is blessed by Allah and says ‘Al-Ḥamdu Lillāh’, except that
  what he was given is better than that which he has himself acquired.*”
* Ibn Mājah from Ibn ʿUmar: the servant who said, “*O Allah! Yours is the Ḥamd that is suitable for the
  grace of Your Face and the greatness of Your Supreme Authority*” — and the two angels were taught to
  “*Write it as My servant has said it, until he meets Me and then I shall reward him for it.*”

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “The Statements of the Salaf about Al-Ḥamd”: Ḥafṣ mentioned that ʿUmar said to ʿAlī, “We
know *Lā ilāha illallāh*, *Subḥān Allāh* and *Allāhu Akbar*. What about *Al-Ḥamdu Lillāh*?” ʿAlī said:
“A statement that Allah liked for Himself, was pleased with for Himself and He likes that it be repeated.”
Ibn ʿAbbās said: “*Al-Ḥamdu Lillāh* is the statement of appreciation. When the servant says *Al-Ḥamdu
Lillāh*, Allah says, ‘My servant has praised Me.’” Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded it. And on *al-ʿālamīn*, the
Successors: al-Farrāʾ and Abū ʿUbayd — “*ʿĀlam* includes all that has a mind, the Jinns, mankind, the
angels and the devils, but not the animals”; Zayd bin Aslam and Abū Muḥaysin — “all that Allah has created
with a soul”; Qatādah — “Every type of creation is an *ʿĀlam*”; az-Zajjāj — “*ʿĀlam* encompasses
everything that Allah created, in this life and in the Hereafter”; and al-Qurṭubī: “This is the correct
meaning”.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “*Al* before *Ḥamd* encompasses all Types of Thanks and Appreciation for Allah”, with the
ḥadīth: “*O Allah! All of Al-Hamd is due to You, You own all the ownership, all types of good are in Your
Hand and all affairs belong to You.*” On *ʿālam*: “*ʿĀlam* is derived from *ʿAlāmah* (sign), because it is
a sign testifying to the existence of its Creator and to His Oneness.”

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Ibn Kathīr.** *Al-Ḥamdu lillāh* is stated to be “the best supplication”, and the servant's praise is
answered by Allah's “My servant has praised Me” — the ḥamd functioning as the servant's address to his Lord.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V6, V8, V17._

## 1:3 — الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

*[context]* The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “We explained these Names in the Basmalah. Al-Qurṭubī said: ‘Allah has described Himself by
*Ar-Raḥmān, Ar-Raḥīm* after saying *the Lord of the ʿālamīn*, so His statement here includes a warning,
and then an encouragement.’” Ibn Kathīr draws the rule: “Hence, *Rabb* contains a warning while *Ar-Raḥmān
Ar-Raḥīm* encourages.”

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** 15:49–50: *Declare unto My servants, that truly, I am the Oft-Forgiving, the Most Merciful.
And that My torment is indeed the most painful torment*; and 6:165: *Surely, your Lord is swift in
retribution, and certainly He is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful.*

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn Kathīr.** Muslim recorded in his *Ṣaḥīḥ* that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: “*If the believer knew
what punishment Allah has, none would have hope in acquiring His Paradise, and if the disbeliever knew
what mercy Allah has, none will lose hope of earning His mercy.*”

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The two names are stated to have been fully treated “in the Basmalah” — the distinction of
scope between *Ar-Raḥmān* and *Ar-Raḥīm* (1:1, V8 above) is the lexical matter of this verse.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V8._

## 1:4 — مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

*[context]* The Owner of the Day of Recompense.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** On the singling out of the Day: “Allah mentioned His sovereignty of the Day of
Resurrection, but this does not negate His sovereignty over all other things… Allah only mentioned the Day
of Recompense here because on that Day, no one except Him will be able to claim ownership of anything
whatsoever. On that Day, no one will be allowed to speak without His permission.” Ad-Daḥḥāk said that Ibn
ʿAbbās commented: “Allah says, ‘On that Day, no one owns anything that they used to own in the world.’”

*The meaning of Yawm Ad-Dīn:* Ibn ʿAbbās said: “*Yawm Ad-Dīn* is the Day of Recompense for the creatures,
meaning the Day of Judgment. On that Day, Allah will reckon the creation for their deeds, evil for evil,
good for good, except for those whom He pardons.” And *al-Dīn*: “*Ad-Dīn* means the reckoning, the reward
or punishment.”

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** 78:38: *The Day that Ar-Rūḥ and the angels will stand forth in rows, they will not speak
except him whom the Most Gracious allows*; 20:108: *And all voices will be humbled for the Most Gracious*;
11:105: *On the Day when it comes, no person shall speak except by His leave*; 59:23: *He is Allah, beside
Whom none has the right to be worshipped but He, the King, the Holy*; 40:16: *Whose is the kingdom this
Day? Allah's, the One, the Irresistible*; 24:25: *On that Day Allah will pay them the recompense of their
deeds in full*; 37:53: *Shall we indeed be raised up to receive reward or punishment according to our
deeds?* — with 2:247, 18:79 and 5:20 for “king” used of others in this life “as a figure of speech”.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “The Two Sahihs recorded Abū Hurayrah saying that the Prophet ﷺ said: *The most despicable
name to Allah is a person who calls himself the king of kings, while there are no owners except Allah.*”
And: “The Two Sahihs recorded that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: *(On the Day of Judgement) Allah will
grasp the earth and fold up the heavens with His Right Hand and proclaim, ‘I Am the King! Where are the
kings of the earth? Where are the tyrants? Where are the arrogant?’*”

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Ibn Kathīr.** As above, Ibn ʿAbbās on 1:4 (“On that Day, no one owns anything…”), and his definition of
*Yawm Ad-Dīn*; “several other Companions, Tābiʿīn and scholars of the Salaf said similarly, for this
meaning is apparent and clear from the Ayah”.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** *Al-Mālik*: “Allah is the True Owner (*Malik*) of everything and everyone.” *Al-Dīn*:
“means the reckoning, the reward or punishment”, with 24:25 and 37:53 as evidence. The ḥadīth “*The wise
person is he who reckons himself and works for his life after death*” is given as the sense of “he holds
himself accountable”.

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Ibn Kathīr.** ʿUmar's saying, quoted for *ad-dīn* as self-reckoning: “Hold yourself accountable before
you are held accountable, weigh yourselves before you are weighed, and be prepared for the biggest
gathering before He Whose knowledge encompasses your deeds — *That Day shall you be brought to Judgement,
not a secret of yours will be hidden* (69:18).”

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V6, V8, V15._

## 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

*[context]* You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “*You* means, we worship You alone and none else, and rely on You alone and none else.
This is the perfect form of obedience and the entire religion is implied by these two ideas. Some of the
Salaf said, Al-Fatihah is the secret of the Qur'an, while these words are the secret of Al-Fatihah.” The
first part “is a declaration of innocence from Shirk (polytheism), while the second negates having any
power or strength, displaying the recognition that all affairs are controlled by Allah alone”. On the
order of the two: worship is mentioned before seeking help “because the objective here is the worship,
while Allah's help is the tool to implement this objective”. And on the transition of speech: “in this
Ayah, the type of speech here changes from the third person to direct speech by using the *Kāf* in the
statement *Iyyāka*… because after the servant praised and thanked Allah, he stands before Him, addressing
Him directly.” And: “Since the praise of Allah, Who is being sought for help, was mentioned, it was
appropriate that one follows the praise by asking for his need… This is the best method for seeking help,
by first praising the one whom help is sought from and then asking for His aid” — with the example of
Mūsā's *My Lord! Truly, I am in need of whatever good that You bestow on me* (28:24) and Dhun-Nūn's *Lā
ilāha illā Anta, glorified be You! Truly, I have been of the wrongdoers* (21:87).

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** 11:123: *So worship Him and put your trust in Him*; 67:29: *He is the Most Gracious, in Him
we believe, and in Him we put our trust*; 73:9: *The Lord of the east and the west; none has the right to
be worshipped but He. So take Him alone as Wakīl*; and 15:97–99, “worship your Lord until there comes unto
you the certainty” — encouraging “the performance of the acts of worship during times of distress”.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “The prayer is not valid unless one recites Al-Fatihah, if he is able. The Two Sahihs
recorded that ʿUbādah bin aṣ-Ṣāmit said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: *There is no valid prayer for
whoever does not recite Al-Fatihah of the Book.*” Also the ḥadīth qudsī of the division of the prayer,
from Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim on the authority of Abū Hurayrah: “*I divided the prayer into two halves between Myself
and My servant… When the servant says, ‘Al-Ḥamdu lillāhi Rabbil-ʿālamīn’, Allah says, ‘My servant has
praised Me’… If the servant says, ‘You we worship, and You we ask for help’, Allah says, ‘This is between
Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asked.’*”

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Ibn Kathīr.** Ad-Daḥḥāk narrated that Ibn ʿAbbās said: “*You we worship* means, ‘It is You whom we single
out, Whom we fear and Whom we hope in, You alone, our Lord, and none else’; *And You we ask for help from*,
‘to obey You and in all of our affairs.’” Qatādah said the verse “contains Allah's command to us to perform
sincere worship for Him and to seek His aid concerning all of our affairs”.

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “Linguistically, *ʿIbādah* means *subdued*. For instance, a road is described as
*Muʿabbadah*, meaning ‘paved’. In religious terminology, *ʿIbādah* implies the utmost love, humility and
fear.” On the fronting of the object (*iyyāka* before the verb) and on the change of grammatical person
from the third to the second, see V2 above.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The verse is presented under the headings **Tawḥīd al-Ulūhiyyah** — Ibn ʿAbbās's “It is You
whom we single out, Whom we fear and Whom we hope in” — and **Tawḥīd al-Rubūbiyyah** — “to obey You and in
all of our affairs”. “The first part is a declaration of innocence from Shirk.” He also notes that “Allah
called His Messenger an *ʿAbd* (servant) when He mentioned sending down His Book, the Prophet's
involvement in inviting to Him, and when mentioning the Isrāʾ… these are the Prophet's most honorable
missions”, citing 18:1, 72:19 and 17:1.

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The order of the verse teaches the method of supplication: “This method is more appropriate
and efficient in bringing about a positive answer to the pleas, and this is why Allah recommended this
better method” — praise first, then the request; and he adds the two other manners: stating one's need
(Mūsā, 28:24) and praising the one asked without mentioning the need.

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V6, V8, V11, V15._

## 1:6 — اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ

*[context]* Guide us to the straight path.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “The guidance mentioned in the Surah implies being directed and guided to success… meaning
guide, direct, lead and grant us the correct guidance.” On the straight path, at-Ṭabarī is quoted: “The
Ummah agreed that *Ṣirāṭ al-Mustaqīm* is the clear path without branches, according to the language of the
Arabs”, with Jarīr bin ʿAṭiyyah's poem: “The Leader of the faithful is on a path that will remain straight
even though the other paths are crooked”; and at-Ṭabarī again: “The Arabs use the term *Ṣirāṭ* in
reference to every deed and statement whether righteous or wicked… The straight path mentioned in the
Qur'an refers to Islam.” The answer to why the guided believer still asks: “The servant needs Allah the
Exalted every hour of his life to help him remain firm on the path of guidance and to make him even more
firm and persistent on it… Hence, *Guide us to the straight way* means, ‘Make us firm on the path of
guidance and do not allow us to deviate from it.’”

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** 90:10: *And shown him the two ways*; 16:121: *He chose him and guided him to a straight
path*; 37:23: *And lead them on to the way of flaming Fire*; 42:52: *And verily, you are indeed guiding to
the straight path*; 7:43: *All praise be to Allah, Who has guided us to this*; 4:16: *O you who believe!
Believe in Allah…* — “this command is not redundant since what is sought here is firmness and
continuity”; and 3:8: *Our Lord! Let not our hearts deviate after You have guided us.*

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn Kathīr.** Imam Aḥmad recorded in his *Musnad* that an-Nawwās bin Samʿān said that the Prophet ﷺ
said: “*Allah has set an example: a Ṣirāṭ (straight path) that is surrounded by two walls on both sides,
with several open doors within the walls covered with curtains. There is a caller on the gate of the Ṣirāṭ
who heralds, ‘O people! Stay on the path and do not deviate from it.’ Meanwhile, a caller from above the
path is also warning any person who wants to open any of these doors, ‘Woe unto you! Do not open it, for
if you open it you will pass through.’ The straight path is Islam, the two walls are Allah's set limits,
while the doors resemble what Allah has prohibited. The caller on the gate of the Ṣirāṭ is the Book of
Allah, while the caller above the Ṣirāṭ is Allah's admonishment in the heart of every Muslim.*”

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** The lexicography of *ṣirāṭ* from at-Ṭabarī: “the clear path without branches”; the noun
used by the Arabs “in reference to every deed and statement whether righteous or wicked”, so that “the
Arabs would describe the honest person as being straight and the wicked person as being crooked”.

### V15 · Wisdom, counsel & admonition (ḥikam · tadabbur)

**Ibn Kathīr.** Why the believer asks for guidance in every prayer: “The servant does not have the power to
benefit or harm himself, except by Allah's permission. Therefore, Allah directed the servant to invoke Him
constantly, so that He provides him with His aid and with firmness and success. Indeed, the happy person
is he whom Allah guides to ask of Him.”

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V8, V15._

## 1:7 — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

*[context]* The way of those on whom You have bestowed Your grace, not (the way) of those who earned Your anger, nor of those who went astray.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “*The way of those upon whom You have bestowed Your grace* defines the path”, and those
blessed are “those mentioned in Surat An-Nisāʾ (4:69): *And whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger, then
they will be in the company of those on whom Allah has bestowed His grace, the Prophets, the Siddīqīn, the
martyrs, and the righteous.*” The two wrong paths are then described: “help us to avoid the path of those
whom Allah is angry with, whose intentions are corrupt, who know the truth, yet deviate from it. Also,
help us avoid the path of those who were led astray, who lost the true knowledge and, as a result, are
wandering in misguidance, unable to find the correct path.” The distribution of the two descriptions:
“These two paths are the paths of the Christians and Jews… The path of the believers is knowledge of the
truth and abiding by it. In comparison, the Jews abandoned practicing the religion, while the Christians
lost the true knowledge. This is why ‘anger’ descended upon the Jews, while being described as ‘led
astray’ is more appropriate of the Christians.” And the warning: “both the Christians and the Jews have
earned the anger and are led astray, but the anger is one of the attributes more particular of the Jews.”

*On the omission of the agent:* “On mentioning anger, Allah said, *Not of those who earned Your anger*,
without mentioning the subject, although it is He Who has sent down the anger on them”, with 58:14; and
“Allah relates the misguidance of those who indulged in it, although they were justly misguided according
to Allah's appointed destiny” — 18:17 and 7:186.

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān)

**Ibn Kathīr.** 4:69–70 for those blessed; 5:60 for the Jews — *Those who incurred the curse of Allah and
His wrath*; 5:77 for the Christians — *Who went astray before and who misled many, and strayed themselves
from the right path*; 58:14; 18:17; 7:186; and 3:7 for the warning against following what is unclear:
*So as for those in whose hearts there is a deviation, they follow that which is not entirely clear
thereof, seeking Al-Fitnah and seeking for its hidden meanings*.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Ibn Kathīr.** Imam Aḥmad recorded that ʿAdī bin Ḥātim said: “The horsemen of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ
seized my paternal aunt…”, the account of his coming to Islam, ending with the Prophet's words: “*Those
who have earned the anger are the Jews and those who are led astray are the Christians.*” At-Tirmidhī
collected it and graded it Ḥasan Gharīb. And the ḥadīth on those who follow the unclear: “*When you see
those who follow what is not so clear in it, then they are those whom Allah has mentioned; hence, avoid
them.*”

### V6 · Companions & Successors (āthār)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “There are several Hadiths and reports from the Salaf on this subject”, including the
account of Zayd bin ʿAmr bin Nufayl before Islam, when “the Jews said to him, ‘You will not become a Jew
unless you carry a share of the anger of Allah that we have earned’”, and “the Christians said to him, ‘If
you become one of us you will carry a share in Allah's discontent’”; he remained in his pure nature,
“neither a Jew nor Christian”, and Waraqah bin Nawfal, one of his companions, believed when the Prophet ﷺ
was sent.

### V11 · Creed & theology (ʿaqīdah)

**Ibn Kathīr.** From the closing argument of the sūrah: “These and several other Ayat testify to the fact
that Allah alone is the One Who guides and misguides, contrary to the belief of the Qadariyyah sect, who
claimed that the servants choose and create their own destiny. They rely on some unclear Ayat avoiding what
is clear and contradicts their desires… Verily, no innovator in the religion could ever rely on any
authentic evidence in the Qur'an that testifies to his innovation. The Qur'an came to distinguish between
truth and falsehood, and guidance and misguidance.”

### V13 · History, sīrah & parables

**Ibn Kathīr.** The ʿAdī bin Ḥātim account (see V5) is a sīrah narrative: his aunt's capture, her release,
ʿAdī's flight and return, his observation “that he was not a king like Kisra or Caesar”, and his Islam at
the Prophet's words. Also the pre-Islamic journey of Zayd bin ʿAmr bin Nufayl to Syria in search of the
true religion (see V6).

### V17 · Specific virtues & supplications (faḍāʾil · duʿāʾ)

**Ibn Kathīr.** “Saying Āmīn”: “It is recommended to say Āmīn after finishing the recitation of
Al-Fatihah. Āmīn means, ‘O Allah! Accept our invocation.’” The evidence: Aḥmad, Abū Dāwūd and at-Tirmidhī
recorded from Wāʾil bin Ḥujr, “I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ recite, *Not of those who earned Your
anger, nor of those who went astray*, and he said ‘Āmīn’ extending it with his voice”; Abū Hurayrah
narrated that the Prophet ﷺ would say Āmīn “until those who were behind him in the first line could hear
him”; Bilāl said, “O Messenger of Allah! Do not finish saying Āmīn before I can join you.” The ruling:
“Saying Āmīn is recommended for those who are not praying and is strongly recommended for those who are
praying, whether alone or behind the Imam”, with the ḥadīth: “*When the Imam says ‘Āmīn’, then say ‘Āmīn’,
because whoever says ‘Āmīn’ with the angels, his previous sins will be forgiven.*” Its meaning: “At-Tirmidhī
said that ‘Āmīn’ means, ‘Do not disappoint our hope’, while the majority of scholars said that it means,
‘Answer our invocation.’” And the merit: “*They will not envy us for anything more than they envy us for
Friday which we have been guided to, while they were led astray from it, and for the Qiblah…, and for our
saying ‘Āmīn’ behind the Imam.*”

_Coverage: V1, V2, V4, V5, V6, V11, V13, V17._
