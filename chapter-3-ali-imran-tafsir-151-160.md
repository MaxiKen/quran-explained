# Surah Ali 'Imran (The Family of 'Imran, 3) — Verse-by-Verse Tafsir, Verses 151–160

The last batch drove Uḥud into its theological nerve: purge and proof, wish meeting encounter, Muḥammad only a messenger, death by leave, the *ribbiyyūn* who did not collapse, and God as *mawlā* and best helper. These ten verses are the autopsy proper — not rumour and not theory, but the day's anatomy named in God's own speech.

What follows is among the frankest moral x-rays in the Qur'an of a believing community's failure and recovery. God will cast horror into the hearts of the disbelievers for *shirk* He never authorised; the Fire is their home (151). God had fulfilled His promise when you were sweeping them away by His leave — then courage weakened, you disputed the command, you disobeyed after victory was brought within reach; some wanted the world, some the hereafter; He turned you from them as a test, and He has pardoned you; God is gracious to the believers (152). You were running and not looking at anyone while the Messenger called from behind; so distress upon distress — now do not grieve for what escaped or what hit you; God is aware of what you do (153). After distress He sent security as drowsiness over some of you, while others were disturbed by evil thoughts about God — the thoughts of jāhiliyyah — saying, do we have a say in the matter? Say: all matters belong to God… even if you had stayed home, those destined to be killed would have gone out to their death-beds; God tests what is in the breasts and purges what is in the hearts (154). Those who fled when the two armies met were made to slip by Satan because of some of what they had earned; God has pardoned them; He is Forgiving, Forbearing (155). Do not be like the unfaithful who say of brothers who travel or fight, if they had stayed they would not have died — God makes that a regret in their hearts; He gives life and causes death (156). If you are martyred or die in His path, forgiveness and mercy are better than what they amass (157). Whether you die or are martyred, to God you will be gathered (158). It is by God's mercy that you, O Prophet, were gentle with them; had you been hard-hearted they would have left you; so pardon them, ask forgiveness for them, consult them in matters; when you decide, trust in God; God loves those who trust (159). If God helps you, none can defeat you; if He denies you help, who else can help you? So in God let the believers trust (160).

Five cruxes govern the reading. The first is 152's full chain: promise kept → weakness → dispute about the order → disobedience after seeing what you love → world-want vs hereafter-want → test → pardon. The second is 153's image of the Prophet ﷺ calling from behind a panicking line — and distress as repayment that still ends in "do not grieve." The third is 154's split of the camp: slumber as *amanah* for a party of faith vs jāhiliyyah-thought for a party caring for themselves; *al-amr kulluhu li-llāh*; the death-bed decree; *tamḥīṣ* of hearts. The fourth is 155–158 on those who fled and on speech about the dead: Satan's slip, divine pardon, life and death as God's, martyrdom's wage better than hoarded wealth. The fifth is 159–160: the Prophet's gentleness as mercy from God; pardon, *istighfār*, *shūrā*, then *tawakkul* after decision — and help only from God restated as the trust-sentence that closes the ten.

This batch does not flinch from naming disobedience. It also does not end on condemnation. Pardon is spoken more than once. The Messenger is ordered to keep pardoning and consulting the same people who ran. That combination — exact diagnosis, open pardon, restored consultation — is the Qur'an's way of rebuilding a community after a wound it partly inflicted on itself.

## Verse 151

**"We will cast horror into the hearts of the disbelievers for associating ˹false gods˺ with Allah—a practice He has never authorized. The Fire will be their home—what an evil place for the wrongdoers to stay!"**

In simple terms: We will throw terror into the hearts of those who reject faith, because they set up partners with God that He never authorised. The Fire will be their home — a terrible place for wrongdoers to stay.

**The verse that turns the camera briefly onto the other side's interior.**

**"We will cast horror into the hearts of the disbelievers for associating false gods with Allah — a practice He has never authorized."** *Sa-nulqī fī qulūbi alladhīna kafarū al-ruʿba bi-mā ashrakū bi-llāhi mā lam yunazzil bihi sulṭānan.*

*Sa-nulqī* — We will cast / throw. *Al-ruʿb* — terror, horror, panic in the heart. *Bi-mā ashrakū* — because of / for their associating. *Mā lam yunazzil bihi sulṭānan* — that for which He sent down no authority. The half-bracketed "false gods" correctly names the object of *shirk*. *Shirk* is not only a metaphysical error. In this verse it is the cause of a terror God casts into hearts. The battlefield is not only swords. It is also the unseen work God does inside the chests of those who reject Him.

**Why here, after 150's "best Helper."** The believers were told God is their *mawlā* and best *nāṣir*. Verse 151 shows one form that help can take: not only strength in your line, but instability in theirs. Classical readers connect this to moments in the wider sīrah when enemy hearts failed without a simple material account. The verse's own claim is enough: God casts *ruʿb* for *shirk* done without any warrant from Him.

**"The Fire will be their home — what an evil place for the wrongdoers to stay!"** *Wa-ma'wāhumu al-nār; wa-bi'sa mathwā al-ẓālimīn.*

*Ma'wā* — refuge, lodging, the place they end. *Mathwā* — abode, stay. The wrongdoers' final stay is named so that Uḥud's temporary swagger on the other side is not misread as final success. Continuous with 131's prepared Fire and 140's God who does not love *ẓālimīn*.

**A pastoral caution.** This verse is not a licence for believers to become sadists of the imagination. It is information about God's justice and about one means of help. The believers' job remains the job of 134–136 and 146–148: spend, restrain anger, stay, pray, trust. The terror in enemy hearts is God's act to announce, not a mood for the believer to cultivate as hatred for its own sake.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When you face a power that looks immovable, verse 151 blocks the atheism of appearances. Hearts are places God can act. That does not replace your duty. It places your duty inside a larger field than the one you can see. *Shirk* — including the modern *shirk* of treating power, nation, or self as ultimate — is not stable ground. God authorises no such ultimacy.

**Shirk without sulṭān.** The phrase *mā lam yunazzil bihi sulṭānan* runs through the Qur'an's indictment of *shirk*: no warrant, no authority, no revealed basis. Terror is cast not for having a culture or a tribe, but for seating partners on a throne God never shared. The precision matters in a surah that refuses to indict peoples as lineages and insists on conduct.

**Connection to Badr's ruʿb and the wider war-vocabulary.** The Qur'an elsewhere names God sending terror as a means of help. Verse 151 places that means inside Uḥud's aftermath and inside the moral cause of *shirk*. The believers who have just been told to trust (150) are shown that the unseen field includes enemy hearts.

## Verse 152

**"Indeed, Allah fulfilled His promise to you when you ˹initially˺ swept them away by His Will, then your courage weakened and you disputed about the command and disobeyed, after Allah had brought victory within your reach. Some of you were after worldly gain while others desired a heavenly reward. He denied you victory over them as a test, yet He has pardoned you. And Allah is Gracious to the believers."**

In simple terms: God kept His promise when at first you were overwhelming them by His will. Then you lost courage, argued about the order, and disobeyed after He had shown you the victory you wanted. Some of you wanted this world; some wanted the hereafter. He withheld victory from you as a test — and He has pardoned you. God is gracious to the believers.

**The anatomy of Uḥud in one verse — among the densest moral diagnostics in the Qur'an.**

**"Indeed, Allah fulfilled His promise to you when you initially swept them away by His Will."** *Wa-la-qad ṣadaqakumu Allāhu waʿdahu idh taḥussūnahum bi-idhnihi.*

*Ṣadaqakum waʿdahu* — He was true to you in His promise / He fulfilled His promise to you. The promise of help under the conditions already named (124–126) was not a lie. *Idh taḥussūnahum bi-idhnihi* — when you were cutting them down / overwhelming them by His leave. The half-bracketed "initially" correctly marks that this is the first phase of the day. The classical reports agree: the Muslims had the early upper hand; the enemy was driven; then the archers' post was left.

**"Then your courage weakened and you disputed about the command and disobeyed, after Allah had brought victory within your reach."** *Ḥattā idhā fashiltum wa-tanāzaʿtum fī al-amri wa-ʿaṣaytum min baʿdi mā arākum mā tuḥibbūn.*

Three verbs of failure, in sequence the tradition reads as the archers' story:

1. ***Fashiltum*** — you lost heart / weakened / failed in courage. The same root as 122's near-cowering, now completed in a party.
2. ***Tanāzaʿtum fī al-amr*** — you disputed about the command / the matter. The order was clear: hold the post whether we seem to win or lose. Dispute opened when victory appeared.
3. ***ʿAṣaytum*** — you disobeyed. Dispute became leaving. The commander of the archers, ʿAbdullāh ibn Jubayr, is remembered in the reports as holding and being killed with those who stayed; others went for the spoils.

*Min baʿdi mā arākum mā tuḥibbūn* — after He showed you what you love. What they loved: victory's shape, and the booty that victory makes available. The verse is surgically precise. Disobedience came *after* the loved thing appeared, not only under pure fear. Success can be as spiritually dangerous as threat.

**"Some of you were after worldly gain while others desired a heavenly reward."** *Minkum man yurīdu al-dunyā wa-minkum man yurīdu al-ākhirah.*

The fork of 145 returns as a census of the field. Some wanted *al-dunyā* — this world: the spoils, the immediate gain. Some wanted *al-ākhirah* — the hereafter: those who held the post under orders. Ibn ʿAbbās and the tafsir tradition commonly map the world-want to those who left for booty and the hereafter-want to Ibn Jubayr and those who stayed until they were killed. The verse itself does not name them. It names the split. Uḥud was not only Muslims vs Quraysh. Inside the Muslim line there were two wants.

**"He denied you victory over them as a test, yet He has pardoned you. And Allah is Gracious to the believers."** *Thumma ṣarafakum ʿanhum li-yabtaliyakum; wa-la-qad ʿafā ʿankum; wa-Allāhu dhū faḍlin ʿalā al-mu'minīn.*

*Ṣarafakum ʿanhum* — He turned you from them / diverted you. The supplied "denied you victory" is dynamic and fair. *Li-yabtaliyakum* — that He might test you. The test is not information God lacked; it is the making-evident already threaded through 140–142. *Wa-la-qad ʿafā ʿankum* — and He has certainly pardoned you. After the densest diagnosis of failure in the batch, the next clause is pardon. Not "and so you are finished." Pardon. *Dhū faḍlin ʿalā al-mu'minīn* — possessor of bounty/grace upon the believers. Grace is named over the same people whose disobedience was just named.

**Why the pardon matters theologically.** Without it, the autopsy would become a closed damnation of the archers and the panicking. With it, the community can hear the truth without being destroyed by the truth. Pardon here is not the claim that disobedience was fine. It is the claim that God did not write the community off. Continuous with 155's second pardon of those who fled.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** The sequence is transferable far beyond a hill in the third year. Promise kept and early success → heart weakens when the loved prize appears → dispute about a clear order → disobedience → a split between those who want the visible return and those who want the end with God → a turning that tests → a pardon that must not be cheapened into "orders never mattered." The spiritual discipline is to notice which want is driving you *after* success appears, not only after fear appears. Many people hold a post under threat and leave it under spoil.

**And the spiritual centre.** God can be true to His promise and still turn you from the enemy for a test when you break the conditions. Those two are not a contradiction. They are the difference between God as vending machine and God as *Ḥakīm*.

## Verse 153

**"˹Remember˺ when you were running far away ˹in panic˺—not looking at anyone—while the Messenger was calling to you from behind! So Allah rewarded your disobedience with distress upon distress. Now, do not grieve over the victory you were denied or the injury you suffered. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do."**

In simple terms: remember when you were running in panic, not looking at anyone, while the Messenger was calling you from behind. So God repaid you with distress on top of distress. Now do not grieve for the victory you missed or the injury you took. God knows everything you do.

**The image that should never leave a reader of Uḥud.**

**"Remember when you were running far away in panic — not looking at anyone — while the Messenger was calling to you from behind!"** *Idh tuṣʿidūna wa-lā talwūna ʿalā aḥadin wa-al-rasūlu yadʿūkum fī ukhrākum.*

*Tuṣʿidūna* — you were going up / climbing away / fleeing upward (the terrain of Uḥud's slopes lives in the verb for many classical readers). *Lā talwūna ʿalā aḥadin* — not turning aside for anyone, not looking back at anyone. Tunnel flight. *Wa-al-rasūlu yadʿūkum fī ukhrākum* — and the Messenger was calling you in your rear, from behind you. The half-brackets "Remember" and "in panic" are interpretive aids; the Arabic scene is plain enough. The man whose face would be wounded, whose tooth would break, whose body was the centre of the rumour, was behind them calling them back — and the line was not looking.

**What this does to every later claim about "what the Prophet meant to us."** Love of the Prophet ﷺ is measured partly here. When he called from behind, did the feet stop? The verse records a day when many did not. It records it so that love can be repentant and re-trained, not so that the day becomes the only day.

**"So Allah rewarded your disobedience with distress upon distress."** *Fa-athābakum ghamman bi-ghammin.*

*Athāba* here is repaid / requited — the root of "reward" used for a bitter repayment. *Ghamman bi-ghammin* — distress upon distress, grief with grief. Classical readings of the double distress include: the distress of defeat layered on the distress of disobedience; the distress of the rumour that the Prophet had been killed layered on the distress of the rout; the distress of loss layered on the distress of shame. The verse does not require choosing only one pairing. It requires feeling the stack.

**"Now, do not grieve over the victory you were denied or the injury you suffered."** *Li-kaylā taḥzanū ʿalā mā fātakum wa-lā mā aṣābakum.*

Purpose after the double distress: so that you would not go on grieving for what escaped you and what hit you. The pedagogy of stacked distress is not sadism. It is re-proportioning. What escaped (the completion of victory, the spoils) and what hit (wounds, deaths, shock) are real. They are not to become the permanent occupation of the heart. Continuous with 139's *lā taḥzanū* — grief as collapse is forbidden; grief as love of the dead will still be human and later regulated by 156–158.

**"And Allah is All-Aware of what you do."** *Wa-Allāhu khabīrun bi-mā taʿmalūn.*

*Khabīr* — fully aware, the One who knows the inner of acts. The flight, the not-looking, the call unheard — all known. So is the recovery that will follow.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** There is a version of this scene in ordinary panic: the leader, the parent, the friend calling from behind while you run toward exit, image-management, or numbness, not looking at anyone. Verse 153 asks whether you can still hear a call from behind when fear has narrowed the field of vision. And when distress stacks, it asks whether you will let the stack teach proportion or only teach bitterness about what escaped and what hit.

**The Messenger in your rear.** Leadership in this image is not only at the front in glory. It is behind in recovery, calling people who are not looking. The Qur'an freezes that posture so that later generations know what prophethood looked like under rout: not abandonment of the people, not harshness that scatters (159 will forbid that), but a call from behind.

**Li-kaylā taḥzanū — the purpose clause.** Stacked distress is unto a end: that you not grieve over the missed and the hit as if those two were the whole of the real. The verse does not order amnesia. It orders the end of grief as the mind's permanent government.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 154

**"Then after distress, He sent down serenity in the form of drowsiness overcoming some of you, while others were disturbed by evil thoughts about Allah—the thoughts of ˹pre-Islamic˺ ignorance. They ask, “Do we have a say in the matter?” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “All matters are destined by Allah.” They conceal in their hearts what they do not reveal to you. They say ˹to themselves˺, “If we had any say in the matter, none of us would have come to die here.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Even if you were to remain in your homes, those among you who were destined to be killed would have met the same fate.” Through this, Allah tests what is within you and purifies what is in your hearts. And Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart."**

In simple terms: then after the distress He sent down calm as a drowsiness that covered some of you, while others were shaken by ugly thoughts about God — the old pagan kind of thinking. They say, "Do we have any say in this?" Tell them: the whole matter belongs to God. They hide in their hearts what they do not show you. They say, "If we had had any say, we would not have been killed here." Tell them: even if you had stayed home, those marked for death would have gone out to where they were to die. God tests what is in your chests and cleans what is in your hearts. God knows best what the heart holds.

**The longest verse in the batch — security and suspicion in one camp.**

**"Then after distress, He sent down serenity in the form of drowsiness overcoming some of you."** *Thumma anzala ʿalaykum min baʿdi al-ghammi amanatan nuʿāsan yaghshā ṭā'ifatan minkum.*

*Amanah* — security, safety, calm. *Nuʿās* — drowsiness, slumber. The same gift that fell at Badr (8:11) falls here after Uḥud's distress: sleep as mercy on a battlefield, reported in Bukhārī through Abū Ṭalḥah's sword falling from his hand more than once as sleep took him. *Yaghshā ṭā'ifatan minkum* — covering a party of you. Not everyone. A party — the party of faith and reliance in the classical mapping.

**"While others were disturbed by evil thoughts about Allah — the thoughts of pre-Islamic ignorance."** *Wa-ṭā'ifatun qad ahammathum anfusuhum yaẓunnūna bi-llāhi ghayra al-ḥaqqi ẓanna al-jāhiliyyah.*

A second *ṭā'ifah*. *Ahammathum anfusuhum* — their own selves concerned them, occupied them, distressed them about themselves. Self as the centre of worry. *Yaẓunnūna bi-llāhi ghayra al-ḥaqq* — thinking of God other than the truth. *Ẓanna al-jāhiliyyah* — the thought of jāhiliyyah: the half-bracketed "pre-Islamic" correctly names the era-word; the moral content is pagan suspicion of God's wisdom and support — God will not help His Messenger; we are left; the matter is chaos or human mismanagement alone. Hypocrites are commonly identified in the tafsir for this party (e.g. names like Muʿattib ibn Qushayr appear in reports); the verse's own category is the thought, not a closed list of names.

**"They ask, 'Do we have a say in the matter?' Say, 'All matters are destined by Allah.'"** *Yaqūlūna hal lanā min al-amri min shay'; qul inna al-amra kullahu li-llāh.*

*Hal lanā min al-amri min shay'* — do we have any portion of the matter? The complaint under the question: if we had been properly consulted / if the plan had been ours, this would not have happened. *Qul inna al-amra kullahu li-llāh* — say: the matter belongs entirely to God. Continuous with 128's *laysa laka min al-amri shay'* spoken to the Prophet himself. If the Prophet has no private share of the final *amr*, the grumbling party has none either. *Kullahu* — entirely — shuts the door on the jāhiliyyah thought that human rearrangement would have rewritten the day's decree.

**"They conceal in their hearts what they do not reveal to you. They say to themselves, 'If we had any say in the matter, none of us would have come to die here.'"** Hidden speech: the public may still show a Muslim face; the heart says the death here proves the plan was wrong. The verse exposes the concealment without denying that consultation (*shūrā*) is a real value — 159 will order the Prophet to keep consulting them. The issue is not that people wanted a say. The issue is the theology under the want: a theology that makes human say the condition of God's wisdom.

**"Say, 'Even if you were to remain in your homes, those among you who were destined to be killed would have met the same fate.'"** *Qul law kuntum fī buyūtikum labaraza alladhīna kutiba ʿalayhimu al-qatlu ilā maḍājiʿihim.*

*Kutiba ʿalayhimu al-qatl* — killing was written upon them. *Ilā maḍājiʿihim* — to their lying-places, their death-beds, the places they would fall. Decree of death is not cancelled by staying home. This is not an argument against taking precautions or against the Prophet's tactical choices. It is an argument against the counterfactual that funds despair and accusation: "if only we had not come out." Those written for death would have gone out to meet it even from their houses. Continuous with 145's *kitāban mu'ajjalan*.

**"Through this, Allah tests what is within you and purifies what is in your hearts. And Allah knows best what is hidden in the heart."** *Wa-li-yabtaliya Allāhu mā fī ṣudūrikum wa-li-yumaḥḥiṣa mā fī qulūbikum; wa-Allāhu ʿalīmun bi-dhāti al-ṣudūr.*

*Ibtilā'* of what is in the breasts; *tamḥīṣ* of what is in the hearts — the purge-vocabulary of 141 returns at full strength. Uḥud is laboratory and surgery. God already knows *dhāt al-ṣudūr* — the inmost of the chests. The day makes the inmost walk in the open.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** After a collective failure, camps split the same way: some receive a strange calm and can rest even under unfinished threat; others cannot sleep because the self has become the only project, and suspicion of God dresses itself as "just asking whether we had a say." Verse 154 trains you to notice which camp you are joining. Consultation is still commanded later. Accusation that treats decree as mismanagement, and hiding that accusation while smiling, is jāhiliyyah-thought — even when it uses modern vocabulary.

## Verse 155

**"Indeed, those ˹believers˺ who fled on the day when the two armies met were made to slip by Satan because of their misdeeds. But Allah has pardoned them. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing."**

In simple terms: those believers who turned back on the day the two armies met were made to slip by Satan because of some of what they had earned. But God has pardoned them. God is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.

**The second pardon — now for those who fled.**

**"Indeed, those believers who fled on the day when the two armies met were made to slip by Satan because of their misdeeds."** *Inna alladhīna tawallaw minkum yawma l-taqā al-jamʿāni innamā istazallahumu al-shayṭānu bi-baʿḍi mā kasabū.*

*Tawallaw* — turned away, turned their backs, fled. *Yawma l-taqā al-jamʿān* — the day the two hosts met: Uḥud. *Istazallahumu al-shayṭān* — Satan made them slip / sought to make them slide. *Bi-baʿḍi mā kasabū* — because of some of what they had earned: some prior misdeed, some opening in the soul that the slip could use. The half-bracketed "believers" correctly keeps them inside the faith-community; the verse is not reclassifying them as disbelievers for the flight. Flight was a slip, not a completed apostasy in this naming.

**Satan's slip and human earning.** The verse holds both agents without letting either erase the other. Satan slips people. People earn openings. Blaming only Satan becomes irresponsibility. Blaming only the self without the unseen enemy becomes a flattened psychology the Qur'an will not endorse. *Bi-baʿḍi mā kasabū* is the hinge.

**"But Allah has pardoned them. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing."** *Wa-la-qad ʿafā Allāhu ʿanhum; inna Allāha ghafūrun ḥalīm.*

*ʿAfā* again — the same pardon-verb as 152. *Ghafūr* — All-Forgiving. *Ḥalīm* — Most Forbearing, the One who does not rush to punish, who holds back while the servant still has room. ʿUthmān and others are mentioned in some reports among those affected by the rout's confusion; the verse's pardon is wider than any one name. God pardoned people who ran on the day the hosts met. That sentence alone rebuilds the possibility of return.

**Connection to 152's pardon.** Two pardons in four verses: one after the archers' disobedience-chain, one after the flight. The community is not being managed by permanent shame. It is being told the truth and then told *ʿafā*.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When you have fled a post — a duty, a truth-telling, a stand you knew was yours — two lies arrive. One: it was only Satan, I am fine. Two: I am finished, God is done with me. Verse 155 refuses both. There was a slip; Satan was in it; something you earned opened the door; God has a pardon large enough for people who ran on the day two armies met. *Ḥalīm* means you still have time to walk back toward the call you did not answer.

**Minkum — from among you.** The fled are still *minkum* — from you, of you. The community boundary is not erased by the slip. That grammatical mercy matches the pardon that follows. A community that expels everyone who ran on a bad day will have no one left to rebuild with. God pardons and keeps the "you" intact.

**Ghafūr Ḥalīm after the slip.** Forgiving and forbearing: not only cancelling a debt, but giving time and not hastening ruin. The people who heard this verse still had to live with memory, with the dead, with the Prophet's face. *Ḥilm* is what makes that living possible without the community consuming itself in permanent trial.

## Verse 156

**"O believers! Do not be like the unfaithful who say about their brothers who travel throughout the land or engage in battle, “If they had stayed with us, they would not have died or been killed.” Allah makes such thinking a cause of agony in their hearts. It is Allah who gives life and causes death. And Allah is All-Seeing of what you do."**

In simple terms: O you who believe — do not be like those who reject faith and say of their brothers who travel or go out to fight, "If they had stayed with us they would not have died or been killed." God makes that thought a source of agony in their hearts. God gives life and causes death. God sees everything you do.

**The speech-rule about the dead and the absent.**

**"O believers! Do not be like the unfaithful who say about their brothers who travel throughout the land or engage in battle, 'If they had stayed with us, they would not have died or been killed.'"** *Yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā takūnū ka-alladhīna kafarū wa-qālū li-ikhwānihim idhā ḍarabū fī al-arḍi aw kānū ghuzzan law kānū ʿindanā mā mātū wa-mā qutilū.*

*Lā takūnū ka-alladhīna kafarū* — do not be like those who disbelieved. The model to refuse is a speech-habit of the rejecters (and hypocrites in the classical application): counterfactual blame about brothers who went out — travel for trade or journey (*ḍarabū fī al-arḍ*) or were raiders/fighters (*ghuzzan*). "If they had stayed with us, they would not have died or been killed." The sentence sounds like care. Under it is a theology that treats staying in the house as the real protector and going out in God's path as the real cause of death. Verse 154 already broke that counterfactual. Verse 156 forbids wearing it as communal speech.

**"Allah makes such thinking a cause of agony in their hearts."** *Li-yajʿala Allāhu dhālika ḥasratan fī qulūbihim.*

*Ḥasrah* — regret, burning sorrow, agony of the "if only." God makes that thought a regret in their hearts. The causal reading in classical tafsir: this speech does not protect the dead; it wounds the living who keep rehearsing it. The believer is ordered not to import that wound.

**"It is Allah who gives life and causes death. And Allah is All-Seeing of what you do."** *Wa-Allāhu yuḥyī wa-yumīt; wa-Allāhu bi-mā taʿmalūna baṣīr.*

Life and death are God's acts. Not the house's. Not the mother's wish. Not the hypocrite's counterfactual. *Baṣīr* — All-Seeing of what you do: including the speech you produce about the dead, which is also a deed.

**Connection to 3:145 and 3:154.** Written term; death-beds that would have been reached from home; now a ban on the social form of the jāhiliyyah counterfactual. The doctrine becomes *adab*.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** After a death in a path of duty — a soldier, a relief worker, a believer who traveled for a need — the sentence "if only they had stayed" arrives dressed as love. Verse 156 says: do not be like those who reject in that speech. Grieve. Bury. Pray. Do not baptise regret as theology. Life and death are God's. Your job is the next obedience, not the endless rewrite of a decree you did not hold.

**Brothers — ikhwān — even in the rejecters' mouth.** The dead and the traveled are still called brothers in the speech being condemned. The problem is not that kinship feeling exists. The problem is the theology hung on kinship feeling: staying as salvation, going out as the cause of death. Correct care for brothers sounds like 157–158, not like 156's counterfactual.

**Travel and battle paired.** *Ḍarabū fī al-arḍ* and *ghuzzan* cover ordinary risk and combat risk. The ban on counterfactual speech is not only about martyrs. It is about any death that the jāhiliyyah-mind wants to rewrite as proof that obedience was the mistake.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 157

**"Should you be martyred or die in the cause of Allah, then His forgiveness and mercy are far better than whatever ˹wealth˺ those ˹who stay behind˺ accumulate."**

In simple terms: if you are killed in God's path or you die, forgiveness and mercy from God are better than all the wealth those who stay behind pile up.

**The wage that outranks accumulation.**

**"Should you be martyred or die in the cause of Allah, then His forgiveness and mercy are far better than whatever wealth those who stay behind accumulate."** *Wa-la-in qutiltum fī sabīli Allāhi aw muttum la-maghfiratun min Allāhi wa-raḥmatun khayrun mimmā yajmaʿūn.*

*Qutiltum fī sabīli Allāh* — if you are killed in God's path. *Aw muttum* — or you die (not only by killing — death in the path more broadly, or death as such in the paired reading; the path-context is strong). *La-maghfiratun… wa-raḥmatun* — forgiveness and mercy. *Khayrun mimmā yajmaʿūn* — better than what they amass/gather. The half-bracketed "wealth" and "who stay behind" are interpretive completions of *mā yajmaʿūn* — what the stay-behind accumulators gather. The comparison is deliberate: the hypocrite's and world-seeker's pile vs. God's forgiveness and mercy for the one who dies in His path.

**Why this follows 156.** If the forbidden speech treats staying as the smart protection of life, 157 names what dying in the path actually purchases in God's economy: *maghfirah* and *raḥmah* better than the pile. The dead are not the losers of the comparison. The accumulators are.

**Connection to 3:136 and 3:157.** Forgiveness as first wage of the *muttaqīn*; forgiveness and mercy as the wage of the martyred and the dead in the path. The surah keeps placing *maghfirah* at the centre of what is worth wanting.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** The modern pile is not only coin. It is safety-stock, reputation-stock, the endless securing of a life that must not be risked for anything. Verse 157 does not order recklessness. It orders a true comparison when risk in God's path is real: what God gives the one who dies there is better than what the safe accumulator can stack. Let that comparison quiet the panic that treats every risk as irrational.

**Maghfirah and raḥmah as the pair better than the pile.** Not fame. Not a documentary. Forgiveness and mercy. The martyred and the dead in the path are framed first as people who receive God's pardon and mercy — continuous with the surah's refusal to make the end only spectacle. What God gives them is better than what the accumulators can show on a balance sheet.

**Aw muttum — or you die.** The path's wage is not locked to the single glamour of a battlefield killing. Death in the cause, and death more broadly under the verse's reach in paired readings, still meets forgiveness and mercy that outrank the pile. The point is the comparison of ends, not the engineering of a particular death.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 158

**"Whether you die or are martyred—all of you will be gathered before Allah."**

In simple terms: whether you die or are killed, it is to God that you will all be gathered.

**The short eschatological seal on the death-discussion.**

**"Whether you die or are martyred — all of you will be gathered before Allah."** *Wa-la-in muttum aw qutiltum la-ilā Allāhi tuḥsharūn.*

*Muttum aw qutiltum* — if you die or are killed: the pair reversed from 157's order, covering both ends. *La-ilā Allāhi tuḥsharūn* — to God you will be gathered. Gathering (*ḥashr*) is the resurrection-assembly. Every path of death ends at the same Lord. The stay-behind and the go-out, the one who fled and the one who held, the one who wanted the world and the one who wanted the hereafter — all are gathered. That does not erase moral distinction in the gathering. It erases the illusion that some death-routes escape God.

**Why this verse is only one sentence long in force.** After the long diagnostics, the short seal is mercy. You do not need another paragraph of argument. You need the end named: God is where all of you arrive. Live and die accordingly.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When fear of a particular kind of death dominates — the death away from home, the death in a headline, the death that leaves a counterfactual for relatives to weaponise — verse 158 widens the frame. All death is a road to the gathering. The question is not only how you die. It is toward whom you were facing when you did.

**Tuḥsharūn — you will be gathered — as equaliser and as warning.** Equaliser: no death-route escapes the meeting. Warning: the meeting is not a vague reunion; it is gathering before God, where the wants of 152 and the flights of 155 and the held posts of the *ribbiyyūn* are not the same weight. Short verses in this surah often carry the heaviest seals.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 159

**"It is out of Allah’s mercy that you ˹O Prophet˺ have been lenient with them. Had you been cruel or hard-hearted, they would have certainly abandoned you. So pardon them, ask Allah’s forgiveness for them, and consult with them in ˹conducting˺ matters. Once you make a decision, put your trust in Allah. Surely Allah loves those who trust in Him."**

In simple terms: it is by God's mercy that you, O Prophet, were gentle with them. If you had been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have scattered from around you. So pardon them, ask God to forgive them, and consult them in affairs. Then when you have decided, put your trust in God. God loves those who trust Him.

**One of the great leadership-verses of the Qur'an — revealed over a community that had just run.**

**"It is out of Allah's mercy that you O Prophet have been lenient with them."** *Fa-bi-mā raḥmatin min Allāhi linta lahum.*

*Bi-mā raḥmatin min Allāh* — by a mercy from God. The Prophet's gentleness is not merely temperament celebrated. It is mercy God placed in him for them. *Linta lahum* — you were soft, gentle, lenient with them. The half-bracketed "O Prophet" correctly marks the addressee. After 152–155's diagnosis of disobedience and flight, the next address to the Messenger is not "be done with them." It is: your gentleness with them is God's mercy.

**"Had you been cruel or hard-hearted, they would have certainly abandoned you."** *Wa-law kunta faẓẓan ghalīẓa al-qalbi la-nfaḍḍū min ḥawlik.*

*Faẓẓ* — harsh, rough in manner. *Ghalīẓ al-qalb* — thick-hearted, hard-hearted. *La-nfaḍḍū min ḥawlik* — they would have scattered from around you. The sociological truth is stated without embarrassment: harshness at this moment would have finished the community's cohesion. Gentleness held the circle. This is not flattery of weakness. It is God's naming of how He kept them around His Messenger after a day that could have ended in dispersal.

**"So pardon them, ask Allah's forgiveness for them, and consult with them in conducting matters."** *Fa-uʿfu ʿanhum wa-staghfir lahum wa-shāwirhum fī al-amr.*

Three commands in sequence — a curriculum of restored relationship:

1. ***Uʿfu ʿanhum*** — pardon them yourself. Drop the claim of the personal offence. The Prophet ﷺ had the most claim: they left him calling from behind. He is ordered to pardon.
2. ***Wa-staghfir lahum*** — and ask forgiveness for them. Take their case to God. Vertical intercession after horizontal pardon.
3. ***Wa-shāwirhum fī al-amr*** — and consult them in the matter. *Shūrā* — consultation. After failure, after flight, after the jāhiliyyah-thought about "having a say," the Prophet is ordered to keep giving them a say in the proper form. Consultation is not cancelled by their mistake. It is restored under his leadership. This is among the Qur'an's strongest endorsements of consultative leadership — and it is placed on the far side of a disaster, not only in ease.

**"Once you make a decision, put your trust in Allah. Surely Allah loves those who trust in Him."** *Fa-idhā ʿazamta fa-tawakkal ʿalā Allāh; inna Allāha yuḥibbu al-mutawakkilīn.*

*Idhā ʿazamta* — when you have resolved / decided. Consultation is not endless deferral. There is a point of *ʿazm* — firm decision. After that: *tawakkal ʿalā Allāh* — trust in God. Not trust in the consultation as such, not trust in the majority that once pressed to go out to Uḥud, not trust in the gentleness alone. Trust in God. *Yuḥibbu al-mutawakkilīn* — He loves those who trust. Love again — continuous with *muḥsinīn* and *ṣābirīn*. The loved posture here is reliance after decision.

**The order is the doctrine of leadership under revelation.** Mercy-given gentleness → pardon → *istighfār* for them → consult them → decide → trust God. Skip the gentleness and you scatter them. Skip the pardon and consultation becomes theatre. Skip the decision and consultation becomes paralysis. Skip the trust and decision becomes self-reliance dressed as resolve.

**Connection to the night before Uḥud.** The Prophet ﷺ had consulted; the younger men pressed to go out; he agreed with the majority against an initial preference to fight from the city. Verse 159 does not say "never consult again." It says keep consulting — then, when you decide, trust God. The lesson of a consultation that preceded a hard day is not "abolish *shūrā*." It is "do not let *shūrā* replace *tawakkul*, and do not let failure cancel *shūrā*."

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Parents, teachers, imams, managers, organisers — anyone who leads people who have failed them — meet this verse as a mirror. Harshness that would feel justified would scatter. Pardon without asking God to forgive them stays horizontal only. Consultation without ever deciding breeds contempt. Decision without trust becomes anxiety in charge. The verse is a full sequence. Practise it in order.

**And the spiritual centre.** The gentleness is *from God's mercy*. Leaders who think their softness is only their personality can become proud of softness. Leaders who think softness is weakness can become hard and empty the room. The verse relocates gentleness to mercy received, then orders it into pardon, prayer, consultation, decision, and trust.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 160

**"If Allah helps you, none can defeat you. But if He denies you help, then who else can help you? So in Allah let the believers put their trust."**

In simple terms: if God helps you, no one can defeat you. If He withholds His help, who is there that can help you after that? So let the believers put their trust in God.

**The trust-sentence that closes the autopsy's first major movement.**

**"If Allah helps you, none can defeat you. But if He denies you help, then who else can help you?"** *In yanṣurkumu Allāhu fa-lā ghāliba lakum; wa-in yakhdhulkum fa-man dhā alladhī yanṣurukum min baʿdihi.*

*In yanṣurkum Allāh* — if God helps you. *Fa-lā ghāliba lakum* — then there is no one who can overcome you. *Wa-in yakhdhulkum* — and if He forsakes you / withholds help from you. *Fa-man dhā alladhī yanṣurukum min baʿdihi* — then who is it that can help you after Him? The double conditional is the whole theology of Uḥud in compressed form. Early in the day, help was present and they swept the enemy (152). When conditions broke and the test came, the sense of forsaking was tasted. Neither phase was random. Both teach the same dependence.

**"So in Allah let the believers put their trust."** *Wa-ʿalā Allāhi fa-l-yatawakkali al-mu'minūn.*

The same conclusion-formula as 122 after God was named *walī* of the nearly-broken. Here it follows the help/forsake conditional and 159's *tawakkul* after decision. *Tawakkul* is the batch's last word because it is the only sane posture once you have seen both the promise fulfilled and the victory turned for a test.

**Connection to 3:126 and 3:160.** Help only from God; God as best helper (150); if He helps, none overcomes; if He withholds, none can supply what He withheld. The *n-ṣ-r* thread closes this ten on the same nail it has been striking since the disciples asked to be God's helpers.

**Why this is not quietism.** Trust is commanded after consultation and decision (159), after posts and plans and means. *Tawakkul* is not the refusal to string the bow. It is the refusal to believe the bow is God.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Before the next hard day — medical, financial, communal, public — run the double conditional honestly. If God helps, the opposition's size is not decisive. If God withholds the help you wanted, no alternate patron will replace Him — so do not invent one out of panic. Then trust. Then do the next right action under that trust.

**And the spiritual centre of the ten verses.** Terror cast into hearts of *shirk*. A promise kept, then weakness, dispute, disobedience after the loved victory appeared; world-want and hereafter-want split the line; God turned you for a test and pardoned you. Running without looking while the Messenger called from behind; distress upon distress; do not make grief for the missed and the hit your permanent home. Slumber as security on one party; jāhiliyyah-thought on another; the matter entirely God's; death-beds that find you even from home; purge of hearts. Those who fled slipped by Satan through what they earned; God pardoned them. Do not speak the rejecters' counterfactuals over your dead. Martyrdom's forgiveness and mercy outrank the pile. All of you are gathered to God. The Prophet's gentleness is mercy from God; pardon them; ask forgiveness for them; consult them; decide; trust. If God helps you, none beats you; if He withholds, who can help you after Him? In God let the believers trust.

Uḥud has been told as God tells it: exact, pardoning, rebuilding. The next batches will keep walking the same ground — booty, the dead who are alive with God, and the long work of not turning the wound into a new religion of despair or pride.

**Khadhala — forsake / withhold help — without divine injustice.** When God *yakhdhul*, it is not because He failed. In Uḥud's light it is bound to test, to broken conditions, to pedagogy (152). The conditional still stands for every later day: help is not a right created by our need; it is God's act. Trust is what you do with that fact.

**Fa-l-yatawakkali al-mu'minūn as plural recovery.** The command is to the believers as a body. Trust is not only the Prophet's *tawakkul* after *ʿazm* (159). It is the community's shared posture after autopsy. They have heard the truth about themselves. Now they trust.


===VERSE-END===

## Synthesis of the Ten Verses

These ten verses are the first full autopsy-panel of Uḥud: causes, panic, split hearts, speech about the dead, and the leadership that rebuilds.

**151 sets the other side's interior.** God casts *ruʿb* into disbelievers' hearts for unauthorised *shirk*; the Fire is their home. Help can take the form of instability in the enemy, not only strength in the self.

**152–153 name the failure and the flight.** Promise fulfilled in the early sweep; then *fashal*, dispute about the order, disobedience after being shown what you love; world-want vs hereafter-want; turned for a test; pardoned; God gracious to believers (152). Running without looking; the Messenger calling from behind; distress upon distress; do not grieve on as occupation for what escaped and what hit (153).

**154–155 split the camp and pardon the fled.** After distress, *amanah* as *nuʿās* on a party; another party self-occupied with jāhiliyyah-thought about God and about "our say"; the matter entirely God's; concealment; the death-bed decree; *ibtilā'* and *tamḥīṣ* of breasts and hearts (154). Those who turned back slipped by Satan through some of what they earned; God pardoned them; *Ghafūr Ḥalīm* (155).

**156–158 regulate speech and desire about death.** Do not be like those who reject, saying if they had stayed they would not have died; that thought is *ḥasrah* in hearts; God gives life and death (156). If killed or dead in His path, forgiveness and mercy beat what they amass (157). Whether you die or are killed, to God you are gathered (158).

**159–160 rebuild leadership and trust.** Gentleness from God's mercy; harshness would have scattered them; pardon, *istighfār*, *shūrā*; when you decide, *tawakkul*; God loves the *mutawakkilīn* (159). If God helps, none overcomes; if He withholds, who helps after Him? In God let believers trust (160).

**Threads carried and extended.**

- ***ʿAfw / pardon*** — 152 and 155: diagnosis without permanent damnation of the community.
- ***World / hereafter want*** — 145's fork becomes 152's field-census (spoils vs held post).
- ***Tamḥīṣ*** — 141 → 154's purge of what is in the hearts.
- ***Amr belongs to God*** — 128 → 154's *al-amru kulluhu li-llāh*; even the Prophet ordered to gentleness and consultation under that sovereignty.
- ***Tawakkul*** — 122 → 159 after decision → 160 as batch seal; continuous with *mawlā* and best *nāṣir* (150).
- ***Naṣr*** — help conditional in experience (160) without cancelling "help only from God" (126).
- ***Shūrā*** — not abolished by Uḥud; restored and bounded by *ʿazm* + *tawakkul* (159).
- **Speech about the dead** — 156–158 against counterfactual theology; life/death God's; gathering to God.

**Where the supplied translation has chosen, it has been flagged.** Half-brackets throughout ("false gods," "initially," "Remember," "in panic," "pre-Islamic," "O Prophet," "believers," "wealth," "who stay behind," "conducting") are interpretive aids. "Swept them away," "denied you victory," "rewarded your disobedience with distress," "serenity"/"drowsiness," "lenient," "abandoned you" are dynamic and fair. "All matters are destined by Allah" for *al-amru kulluhu li-llāh* leans destinarian; the Arabic is "the matter belongs entirely to God" — sovereignty first, decree implied. "Horror" for *ruʿb* is strong and fair. None is distortion; each is a choice.

**The verses to leave with are 152 and 159 together.** God tells the truth about disobedience after the loved victory appeared — and pardons. God tells the Messenger to stay gentle, pardon, seek forgiveness for them, keep consulting them, decide, and trust. A religion that can autopsy its own battlefield this way, and then order its Prophet to keep consulting the people who ran, is a religion serious about both justice and mercy. Uḥud is not only a wound. It is a rebuild-protocol.

**A note on honesty in citation.** No page numbers, volumes or publication details are given, because this is a synthesized commentary rather than a citation apparatus, and inventing precise references would be worse than omitting them.

Five places in this batch required particular care.

**The first is 152's chain.** Promise kept is not cancelled by later turning for a test. *Fashal*, dispute, disobedience after seeing what is loved maps to the archers without reducing the whole army to one sin. World/hereafter split has strong classical mapping to leavers vs holders; the verse itself names wants, not names. Pardon is real and must not be read as "disobedience was fine."

**The second is 153.** The Prophet calling from behind is the image held without melodrama. Double distress has more than one classical pairing; the pedagogy toward not grieving as occupation is the point.

**The third is 154.** Slumber as mercy (with Badr parallel and Abū Ṭalḥah's report). Second party as self-occupied jāhiliyyah-thought — hypocrites commonly identified, thought-category primary. *Al-amru kulluhu li-llāh* is sovereignty; "destined" in the translation is a lean. Death-bed decree does not cancel tactics or blame for abandoned posts. *Tamḥīṣ* continues 141.

**The fourth is 155–158.** Satan's slip via some of what was earned — dual agency. Pardon of those who fled does not reclassify flight as obedience. Speech-ban in 156 is about counterfactual theology, not about banning grief. 157–158 set martyrdom and gathering without promising recklessness.

**The fifth is 159–160.** Gentleness as mercy from God, not mere personality. Pardon + *istighfār* + *shūrā* after failure is deliberate rebuild. *Shūrā* then *ʿazm* then *tawakkul* is the order; Uḥud's prior consultation does not abolish consultation. Help/forsake conditional restates dependence without quietism.

**On the translation.** Choices noted above; verse openings preserved byte-for-byte. The translation is not wrong; it has chosen, and the reader is entitled to know what was chosen.

Two boundaries observed throughout. No Companion covered by the pardons of 152 and 155 has been treated as a permanent villain — the text's own *ʿafw* is normative. And no reading of decree has been allowed to erase the moral specificity of abandoned posts and unheard calls — secondary causes remain nameable under the written term. The batch stops with trust restored as the command. Waiting for authorisation to continue.
