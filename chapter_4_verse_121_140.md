## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:121

> It is they who will have Hell as their home, and they will find no escape from it!

**Commentary:**

### The End of Satanic Delusion

After describing Satan’s promises and false hopes, Allah states their consequence. Those who persist in rejecting truth and following Satan will have Hell as their home. The verse exposes the final emptiness of every promise that made disobedience appear safe or attractive.

### “Home” as a Lasting Destination

Calling Hell a home reverses the comfort promised by false gods and worldly delusion. What was presented as freedom becomes captivity, and what appeared to be security becomes loss. The phrase does not describe a temporary inconvenience but a final destination for those who meet Allah in persistent rebellion and unbelief.

### No Escape from Divine Justice

In the world, wealth, influence, concealment, or migration may allow a person to evade human consequences. No such escape exists from Allah’s judgement. His knowledge reaches the hidden act, the motive, the harm, and the opportunities for repentance that a person knowingly rejected.

### Read with Mercy and Repentance

This warning must be read with the preceding promise that Allah forgives sins when a person sincerely repents. The threatened permanence concerns those who persist in shirk and rebellion until death without returning to Allah. Muslims should fear the end while never declaring that a living person has been denied the possibility of repentance.

### Not a Licence for Human Condemnation

The verse describes Allah’s final judgement, not permission for individuals to persecute, insult, or attack people of another faith. Human beings do not control the Hereafter. Their duty is to convey truth with wisdom, act justly, protect rights, and leave final accountability to Allah.

### The Meaning of a Good Home

The Qur’an contrasts Hell with the good home of the obedient and repentant. A person builds their final destination through repeated choices: worship, truthfulness, mercy, justice, or their opposites. The believer therefore asks what habits are making the heart more at home with obedience or with Satan’s path.

### Hope for Those Who Turn Back

Fear of Hell should lead to correction rather than paralysis. The door of repentance remains open during life. Leaving idolatry, abandoning injustice, restoring people’s rights, and returning to Allah can transform a person’s direction before the final journey begins.

### A Warning against False Security

Satanic delusion often tells people that consequences are remote or that status will protect them. This verse restores seriousness to the future. True security is not the absence of present discomfort; it is meeting Allah with faith, repentance, and a record of good deeds.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:185 and 18:49 on the certainty of death, judgement, and the complete record of deeds.
- Qur’an 66:8 and 25:70–71 on sincere repentance before the final accounting.
- Qur’an 2:256 and 60:8–9 on no compulsion in religion and justice toward peaceful people.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on the seriousness of dying upon shirk, the reality of the Hereafter, and Allah’s acceptance of sincere repentance.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:122

> And those who believe and do good, We will soon admit them into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there for ever and ever. Allah’s promise is ˹always˺ true. And whose word is more truthful than Allah’s?

**Commentary:**

### Faith Joined to Action

The verse pairs belief with righteous deeds. *Īmān* is not a claim on the tongue detached from conduct; it shapes worship, honesty, justice, mercy, and service. Good actions are not a substitute for faith, and faith that produces no obedience is not the complete response the Qur’an praises.

### The Garden as a Real Promise

Allah promises admission into Gardens with flowing rivers and lasting residence. The Qur’anic description communicates life, beauty, refreshment, security, and abundance beyond the limitations of this world. Paradise is not earned through human entitlement; it is entered by Allah’s grace for those who believe and act righteously.

### Eternal Security

The contrast with the hopeless end of Satan’s followers is complete. The righteous do not merely receive a temporary relief followed by another loss. Their home is secure, and their joy is not threatened by death, injustice, illness, or the instability that marks worldly blessings.

### Allah’s Promise Is True

Allah’s promise, *waʿd Allāh ḥaqq*, cannot fail. Human promises may be made sincerely but be defeated by weakness, forgetfulness, or circumstances. Allah has perfect knowledge and power, so His word is the most truthful word. Trust in this promise gives believers courage to sacrifice immediate advantage for obedience.

### Hope and Responsible Effort

Confidence in Paradise should not produce passivity. The promised destination is pursued through prayer, repentance, lawful work, care for creation, and perseverance in good. Hope strengthens action because the believer knows that no sincere deed is lost, even when people do not recognise it.

### The Role of Grace

Righteous deeds matter, yet the believer does not boast as though Paradise were a wage forced upon Allah. The Prophet ﷺ taught that no one enters Paradise solely by their deeds but by Allah’s mercy, while also teaching that deeds are the means through which mercy is sought. This produces both effort and humility.

### A Community Shaped by the Promise

A community that believes Allah’s promise should be trustworthy in its own promises. It should protect the weak, return trusts, honour agreements, and resist corruption. The future home of believers is connected to the moral quality of their present lives.

### No Despair after Failure

The description is not limited to people who never make mistakes. Believers can sin, repent, and resume good action. Faith grows through returning to Allah. The important contrast is between sincere belief that seeks righteousness and persistent rejection that follows Satan’s delusion.

### Evidence and references

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:123

> ˹Divine grace is˺ neither by your wishes nor those of the People of the Book! Whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly, and they will find no protector or helper besides Allah.

**Commentary:**

### Salvation Is Not Wishful Thinking

Allah rejects the idea that divine favour is secured by claims, ancestry, slogans, or untested wishes. Neither the wishes of Muslims nor those of the People of the Book determine the standard of acceptance. A person must respond to truth with faith, repentance, and righteous action.

### Beyond Religious Boasting

The verse addresses communities that argued over which group possessed superior status. It does not erase the distinctions between revelation and disbelief, nor does it condemn every individual from another religious community. It rejects collective boasting when it becomes an excuse for disobedience and injustice.

### Responsibility for Evil

Whoever commits evil will meet its consequence. This establishes personal accountability and prevents a person from using group membership as immunity. A Muslim cannot rely on identity while committing oppression, and a person cannot be condemned merely by belonging to a group. Allah judges the individual’s belief, knowledge, intention, conduct, and response to guidance.

### “Rewarded Accordingly” and Divine Mercy

The consequence of evil is not a denial of repentance or forgiveness. Allah forgives whom He wills, accepts sincere repentance, and may turn suffering or lawful expiation into purification. The wording warns against presumption, not against hope. A believer responds to sin with immediate repentance rather than assuming that good deeds or labels will cancel deliberate harm.

### No Protector besides Allah

When accountability arrives, no created protector can overrule Allah. Wealth, relatives, leaders, institutions, and religious claims cannot rescue a person from truth. In this life, people should still seek lawful help and defend the oppressed; the verse concerns ultimate dependence and final judgement.

### The Prophet’s Comfort

When this warning caused the Companions distress, the Prophet ﷺ taught that the hardships and pains experienced by a Muslim can be an expiation when met with patience. This does not mean every hardship is a direct punishment, nor does it excuse seeking treatment or justice. It places suffering within Allah’s knowledge and mercy.

### Grace and Effort

Divine grace is not earned as a right, but it is sought through faith and action. The servant works sincerely, avoids arrogance, and asks Allah to accept imperfect deeds. This balance protects from both entitlement and despair.

### Justice across Communities

The verse establishes a standard that is morally serious and universal. No community should claim that its members are automatically exempt from honesty, justice, or accountability. Religious conviction should produce humility and ethical conduct, not contempt for people outside one’s group.

### A Call to Self-Examination

The believer should ask whether they rely on worship, family, nationality, scholarship, or public reputation while neglecting private obedience. Allah’s favour is received through sincere submission, and sins should be met with repentance, restitution, and renewed effort.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:123–126 on accountability, righteous deeds, the People of the Book, and sincere submission to Allah.
- Qur’an 2:111–112 on rejecting claims based on wishes and defining good through submission and righteous action.
- Qur’an 3:85 and 5:18 on religious claims, divine ownership, and accountability.
- Qur’an 6:164, 17:15, and 35:18 on each soul bearing its own burden.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 and 25:70–71 on repentance, forgiveness, and hope after sin.
- Qur’an 49:13 and 5:8 on dignity, justice, and the rejection of group arrogance.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2574, the report of Abū Hurayrah that the verse caused concern and the Prophet ﷺ explained that trials and hardships can expiate sins; Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim on repentance and personal accountability.
- For *amānī*, *ahl al-kitāb*, *yujza*, *sūʾ*, *walī*, *naṣīr*, and the balance between accountability and divine mercy, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:123.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:124

> But those who do good—whether male or female—and have faith will enter Paradise and will never be wronged ˹even as much as˺ the speck on a date stone.

**Commentary:**

### Equal Access to Divine Reward

Allah explicitly includes both male and female believers. Gender does not reduce a person’s access to faith, righteous action, forgiveness, or Paradise. Every believing woman and man is addressed as a morally responsible servant and can rise through sincerity and good deeds.

### Faith and Righteous Deeds

The verse joins faith with good action. Belief gives deeds their direction and purpose, while deeds demonstrate that faith has influenced the life. The good deed may be worship, honesty, service, patience, justice, care for parents, protection of the vulnerable, or any lawful act done with a sound intention.

### No Injustice, Even in Small Matters

The expression about the speck or groove of a date stone, *naqīr*, emphasises perfect fairness. Allah does not overlook a sincere good deed, reduce a person’s reward because of gender or social status, or punish someone for another person’s sin. Human systems may miss quiet contributions; Allah does not.

### Correcting Community Bias

A community that believes this verse must honour women’s spiritual and moral agency. It should not reserve religious seriousness, learning, leadership in appropriate forms, or charitable recognition for men while treating women’s deeds as secondary. At the same time, equality of spiritual worth does not mean that every earthly role or circumstance is identical; it means no believer is lesser before Allah because of sex.

### The Measure Is Not Appearance

People may evaluate worth through wealth, beauty, ancestry, education, or public influence. Allah evaluates faith and deeds with knowledge of intention and circumstance. A hidden act of mercy may outweigh a public performance, while a celebrated deed may be empty if it was done for reputation.

### Paradise Is a Gift and a Promise

The believer acts with effort but enters Paradise by Allah’s mercy. The promise creates hope for those whose good works are small in people’s eyes and humility for those given greater opportunity. No one may demand reward from Allah as a right, yet no sincere servant will be treated unjustly.

### Comfort for the Overlooked

Many people serve without applause: caregivers, parents, honest workers, peacemakers, teachers, and those who resist injustice quietly. The verse assures them that Allah’s record is more complete than human recognition. Their good is not lost because society failed to notice it.

### A Standard for Human Justice

The divine refusal to wrong even slightly should shape human conduct. Employers, parents, judges, teachers, and leaders must not diminish a person’s rights because of gender, poverty, disability, ethnicity, or social position. Perfect divine justice is a standard toward which human institutions must strive.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:123–126 on rejecting wishful claims, personal accountability, and equal reward for believing men and women.
- Qur’an 33:35 on the equal spiritual qualities and reward of believing men and women.
- Qur’an 16:97 on a good life and reward for any believing male or female who does good.
- Qur’an 9:71–72 on believing men and women supporting one another and receiving Allah’s mercy.
- Qur’an 40:40 on the reward of good deeds without gender discrimination.
- Qur’an 18:30 and 21:47 on Allah not wasting good deeds and weighing deeds with perfect justice.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on Allah not wronging anyone, the value of sincere deeds, and the reward of believing women and men.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:125

> And who is better in faith than those who ˹fully˺ submit themselves to Allah, do good, and follow the Way of Abraham, the upright? Allah chose Abraham as a close friend.

**Commentary:**

### The Best Way of Faith

Allah presents a complete pattern of faith: surrendering oneself to Allah, doing good, and following the upright way of Abraham. The question is rhetorical praise for a life in which inner belief, outward conduct, and pure devotion are joined together.

### Submission and Trust

To submit one’s face to Allah, *aslama wajhahu lillāh*, means to direct the whole self toward Him—belief, worship, intention, ethics, and choices. Islam is not merely a label inherited from a family. It is willing surrender to the One who created and sustains the person.

### Doing Good with Excellence

The verse combines surrender with *iḥsān*, doing what is beautiful and excellent. Worship should be sincere, and dealings with people should show honesty, mercy, fairness, patience, and care. A claim of devotion that produces cruelty or betrayal contradicts the pattern Allah praises.

### The Upright Way of Abraham

Abraham’s way, *millat Ibrāhīm*, is *ḥanīf*: turning away from all false gods and directing worship wholly to Allah. Abraham did not define faith by tribal ownership or inherited idols. He questioned false worship, accepted truth even when it challenged his society, and submitted to Allah with courage.

### Abraham as a Common Prophetic Model

The People of the Book and the Muslims all recognise Abraham’s importance, but no community can claim him as a justification for division or pride. His legacy is tawḥīd, trust, sacrifice, hospitality, justice, and obedience. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was sent in continuity with this same devotion to Allah alone.

### Allah’s Close Friendship

Allah chose Abraham as a *khalīl*, a close friend. *Khullah* indicates an intimate, profound love and closeness. This honour was connected to Abraham’s complete devotion and testing, not worldly status. It teaches that nearness to Allah is cultivated through sincerity, steadfastness, and faithful response to trials.

### A Life of Integrated Faith

The verse resists separating worship from ethics. Prayer, charity, family conduct, public justice, and treatment of strangers belong to one submission. A believer asks in every area: What would it mean here to surrender to Allah and act with excellence?

### No Room for Arrogance

Calling Abraham a model should make believers humble, because his path required sacrifice and obedience. Faith is not a certificate of superiority over others. It is a trust that should produce gratitude, good character, invitation with wisdom, and concern for justice.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:130–135 and 3:67–68 on Abraham’s upright submission and his relation to later communities.
- Qur’an 6:74–83 on Abraham’s rejection of idolatry and search for truth.
- Qur’an 16:120–123 on Abraham as an upright nation and the command to follow his way.
- Qur’an 60:4 on Abraham’s devotion to Allah alone, with wisdom about the historical context of his disassociation from idolatry.
- Qur’an 2:177 and 16:90 on faith expressed through righteousness, justice, and excellence.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the ḥadīth of Jibrīl on Islam, īmān, and iḥsān; reports on Abraham as Allah’s *khalīl* and the Prophets’ shared call to tawḥīd.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:126

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is Fully Aware of everything.

**Commentary:**

### Universal Ownership

Everything in the heavens and the earth belongs to Allah. No community owns revelation as a private possession, no ruler owns people as property, and no human being possesses creation independently of its Creator. Allah’s ownership is absolute, while human possession is temporary stewardship and a trust.

### The Context of Religious Claims

The verse concludes the discussion of competing wishes and claims to divine favour. Since all creation belongs to Allah, no group can compel Him through ancestry, slogans, or pride. The appropriate response to His ownership is submission, righteous action, and humility rather than boasting.

### Ownership Creates Responsibility

Human beings may own and use property through lawful means, but ownership never permits oppression, waste, exploitation, or harm. Land, wealth, animals, natural resources, and institutions are trusts. The believer asks whether their use respects Allah’s limits and the rights of people and future generations.

### Allah’s Complete Awareness

Allah is fully aware of everything, *muḥīṭ*, encompassing every visible and hidden reality. He knows the sincere deed that receives no applause, the private injustice concealed from courts, the hardship behind a person’s choice, and the motive beneath public piety. Nothing falls outside His knowledge.

### Justice and Comfort

Divine awareness is both a warning and a comfort. It warns the oppressor that concealment cannot defeat judgement. It comforts the oppressed because forgotten pain, unseen service, and suppressed truth remain known to Allah. The believer pursues lawful justice while trusting that no detail is lost.

### Knowledge beyond Human Limits

Human knowledge is partial and dependent. Even the most careful judge cannot know every intention or circumstance. This is why people must investigate responsibly without claiming omniscience. Allah’s complete knowledge supports humility, due process, and caution before condemning others.

### Stewardship of the Earth

Because the earth belongs to Allah, environmental care is part of faith. Waste, corruption, pollution, and reckless exploitation violate the trust of stewardship. Beneficial development is not prohibited, but it must be balanced with justice, sustainability, and care for the vulnerable.

### The Spiritual Effect of Ownership

Remembering Allah’s ownership loosens the heart’s attachment to status and possessions. Wealth becomes a means to fulfil trusts and help people, not a substitute for worship. Loss becomes painful but does not destroy the believer’s identity, because the Creator remains the true owner of all things.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:127

> They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ regarding women. Say, “It is Allah Who instructs you regarding them. Instruction has ˹already˺ been revealed in the Book[223] concerning the orphan women you deprive of their due rights[224] but still wish to marry, also helpless children, as well as standing up for orphans’ rights. And whatever good you do is certainly well known to Allah.”

**Commentary:**

### Allah Is the Source of Their Rights

The Prophet ﷺ is asked about women, and he is directed to say that Allah gives the instruction. Women’s rights are therefore not gifts granted by male guardians, tribal customs, or social permission. They are established by the Creator, who knows human vulnerability and will hold those with power accountable.

### Orphan Women and Withheld Rights

The verse returns to the issue of orphan girls under guardianship. Some guardians wanted to marry them because of their beauty or wealth while withholding a fair *mahr* and treating them unjustly. If a guardian cannot uphold justice, the earlier guidance directs him to marry elsewhere and protect the orphan’s property and dignity.

A guardian must never turn care into access, ownership, or exploitation.

### Marriage Requires Justice

Marriage is not a mechanism for acquiring an orphan’s wealth or controlling a vulnerable person. It requires lawful capacity, genuine consent, an appropriate dowry, safety, and the ability to fulfil rights. Guardians must not pressure, manipulate, or silence a girl who depends on them. The command to marry only with justice is a protection, not a lower standard for the vulnerable.

### Helpless Children

Allah also directs attention to children who lack power to protect themselves. Their rights include life, safety, nourishment, education, inheritance, identity, affection, and protection from abuse and exploitation. The mention of helpless children broadens the passage beyond marriage law to the full responsibility of society toward dependants.

### Standing Up for Orphans

The phrase “standing up for orphans’ rights” calls for active advocacy. It includes safeguarding their property, ensuring proper care, challenging abuse, arranging trustworthy guardianship, and giving them a voice in matters affecting them. Silence in the face of exploitation is not neutral when one has the ability to help.

### A Fiduciary Trust

Guardianship, *wilāyah*, is an *amānah*, a trust. A guardian manages another person’s affairs for that person’s benefit, not for personal gain. Financial records, independent oversight, transparent decisions, and safe reporting channels can help prevent the powerful from hiding exploitation behind family authority.

### The Sunnah Clarifies the Context

ʿĀʾishah, may Allah be pleased with her, explained the context of this passage in the report recorded by al-Bukhārī: a guardian might desire to marry an orphan girl while denying her a fair dowry, so the guardians were told either to treat her justly or to marry other women. The Prophetic explanation places the vulnerable person’s right at the centre.

### Every Good Act Is Known

Allah closes by assuring those who protect the vulnerable that no good is overlooked. Caregiving, advocacy, honest administration, and quiet generosity may be hidden from society but are fully known to Allah. This also warns guardians that hidden exploitation is known even when no human authority sees it.

- Qur’an 4:19 and 4:21 on dignified treatment, consent, and the seriousness of marital covenant.
- Qur’an 4:36 and 93:9–10 on caring for orphans and not oppressing or repelling them.
- Qur’an 6:152 and 17:34 on protecting the property of orphans until maturity.
- Qur’an 24:33 and 2:220 on helping the vulnerable and improving their welfare.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:128

> If a woman fears indifference or neglect from her husband, there is no blame on either of them if they seek ˹fair˺ settlement, which is best. Humans are ever inclined to selfishness.[225] But if you are gracious and mindful ˹of Allah˺, surely Allah is All-Aware of what you do.

**Commentary:**

### Addressing Marital Neglect

The verse recognises a painful marital reality: a wife may fear indifference, desertion, or neglect from her husband. Islam does not tell her to pretend that the problem is not real. It gives the couple a path of honest discussion and fair settlement before resentment becomes deeper and the marriage collapses.

### Settlement Is Better

*Al-ṣulḥ khayr*, settlement is best, means that a mutually acceptable agreement may preserve dignity and reduce harm. It can involve clarifying responsibilities, arranging time and support, obtaining counselling, or agreeing to a voluntary adjustment of certain rights. A settlement is only genuinely fair when it is free from coercion, threats, financial captivity, or fear of violence.

### No Blame for a Lawful Agreement

The verse removes blame from both spouses when they seek a just arrangement. This recognises that a marriage may require practical compromise and that rigidly insisting on every preference can destroy a relationship. Compromise, however, must not turn an obligatory right into something a vulnerable spouse is pressured to surrender.

### Selfishness and Shuhḥ

Human beings are inclined toward *shuhḥ*, a grasping selfishness that wants to keep every benefit while giving as little as possible. A husband may cling to comfort while neglecting his wife, and either spouse may refuse generosity or fairness. Allah calls both to overcome self-interest through *iḥsān* and *taqwā*.

### The Wife’s Dignity and Agency

The verse treats the woman as a participant in resolving her marriage, not as property to be transferred or silently endured. She may express fear, seek advice, involve trusted mediators, and pursue lawful remedies. Her safety and dignity remain essential even when reconciliation is attempted.

### Settlement Is Not Concealment of Abuse

Reconciliation must not be confused with covering domestic violence, coercive control, sexual abuse, or serious neglect. In such cases, protection, safe housing, professional support, and lawful intervention may be necessary. Asking a victim to “settle” while leaving the perpetrator unaccountable is not the justice of this verse.

### Generosity beyond Minimum Rights

The closing command to be gracious points beyond legal minimums. Spouses should show patience, gratitude, emotional care, and generosity. A marriage survives not through constant calculation of entitlement but through mutual mercy, while rights remain safeguards against exploitation.

### Allah Knows the Hidden Situation

Allah is fully aware of what each spouse does, including sacrifices that are never acknowledged and injuries hidden from relatives. This awareness restrains the negligent and comforts the person who tries sincerely to repair the marriage. It also means that apparent peace cannot make hidden oppression acceptable.

### Mediation and Communication

A couple should speak clearly, listen without humiliation, and seek wise mediation when needed. Qur’an 4:35 provides for arbitrators from both families when serious discord arises. The purpose is reform and justice, not a family tribunal that pressures the weaker spouse to endure harm.

- Qur’an 4:35 on mediation and arbitration in serious marital discord.
- Qur’an 2:228–232 on mutual rights, dignity, reconciliation, and respectful separation.
- Qur’an 30:21 on affection and mercy as purposes of marriage.
- Qur’an 65:6–7 on housing, provision, and avoiding harm during separation and family difficulty.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:129

> You will never be able to maintain ˹emotional˺ justice between your wives—no matter how keen you are. So do not totally incline towards one leaving the other in suspense.[226] And if you do what is right and are mindful ˹of Allah˺, surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Commentary:**

### The Difference between Feeling and Conduct

Allah acknowledges that a person cannot control every emotion equally. Affection, inclination, and emotional attachment are not fully subject to command. The verse therefore distinguishes inward feeling from outward justice. A husband may not be able to make his heart feel identical toward multiple wives, but he remains responsible for how he treats them.

### Do Not Leave a Wife Suspended

The prohibited imbalance is to incline completely toward one wife while leaving another *muʿallaqah*, suspended between marriage and separation. Such a wife is neither treated as a full spouse nor released to pursue a dignified life. Neglecting maintenance, time, housing, communication, or basic marital rights under the excuse of preference is injustice.

### Material Fairness Remains Required

The verse does not cancel the practical justice required in 4:3. Where plural marriage exists within a lawful system, the husband must deal fairly in the matters he can control: financial support, accommodation, scheduled time, public treatment, and protection from humiliation. Emotional limitation is not a licence for deliberate unequal conduct.

### A High Responsibility, Not a Privilege without Limits

The Qur’an’s discussion of plural marriage is framed by justice, guardianship, and accountability. It is not a permission to collect relationships while abandoning obligations. If a man fears that he cannot fulfil the rights of wives, the guidance of 4:3 directs him toward one marriage rather than exposing multiple women to neglect.

### Honesty in Marriage

The verse calls spouses to acknowledge realities rather than make impossible promises. A husband should not hide a serious intention to abandon a wife while continuing to receive her loyalty and labour. Honest discussion, mediation, counselling, a fair arrangement, or respectful separation is better than prolonged uncertainty.

### The Wife’s Rights and Agency

A wife who is neglected may seek advice, mediation, financial protection, and lawful dissolution where appropriate. She is not required to accept indefinite emotional or material abandonment simply because the husband claims that feelings cannot be controlled. The law protects her from being held in a state of harm.

### Taqwā and Restraint

Mindfulness of Allah, *taqwā*, restrains desire from becoming oppression. A person may feel attachment, but must still act with truthfulness, generosity, and responsibility. The stronger the power imbalance, the more important it is to establish transparent arrangements and listen to the vulnerable spouse.

### Forgiveness and Mercy

Allah’s forgiveness at the end gives hope to those who recognise their failures and correct them. Mercy is shown through apology, restored rights, changed conduct, and fair decisions—not through repeating neglect while asking the injured person to be patient.

### The Purpose of the Guidance

The verse is a protection against romanticising an arrangement that can easily produce harm. Its practical message is to fulfil controllable rights, avoid suspension and abandonment, and choose separation when justice cannot be maintained. Allah knows the heart’s limits and judges the choices made within those limits.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:130

> But if they choose to separate, Allah will enrich both of them from His bounties. And Allah is Ever-Bountiful, All-Wise.

**Commentary:**

### Separation Can Be a Just Outcome

When a marriage cannot be maintained with dignity and justice, separation may be better than prolonged harm. Islam does not require two people to remain trapped in a relationship that has become a place of abuse, persistent neglect, or irreparable hostility. Ending a marriage lawfully is not automatically a spiritual failure.

### Allah’s Enrichment

Allah promises to enrich both spouses from His bounty. Enrichment may include lawful provision, emotional healing, supportive relationships, spiritual growth, safety, and a new capacity to live responsibly. The promise is not a guarantee of immediate wealth or an invitation to abandon practical planning; it is reassurance that a person’s future is not limited to one painful relationship.

### Dignified Release

Separation should be handled with *maʿrūf*: truthful communication, respect, fair financial arrangements, protection from humiliation, and fulfilment of legal obligations. Former spouses should not use children, secrets, social media, or community gossip as weapons against one another.

### No Coercion to Stay or Leave

The verse recognises the couple’s choice while broader Islamic principles protect against coercion. Neither spouse should be forced to remain in danger, and neither should be pushed into separation merely to settle family pride or financial disputes. Wise mediation can help when reconciliation remains possible; safe exit is necessary when it does not.

### Children and Continuing Duties

Separation ends a marital contract but does not erase parental responsibility. Children retain rights to safety, care, education, affection, and appropriate financial support. Parents must distinguish the end of their relationship from the child’s need for stable, loving and safe contact.

### Trust after Loss

Fear often tells a person that leaving means losing everything. Allah’s name *Wāsiʿ*, Ever-Bountiful, opens a wider horizon. A believer may grieve sincerely while trusting that Allah can provide a future better than the present arrangement. Grief and reliance upon Allah can coexist.

### Wisdom in Decision-Making

Allah is *Ḥakīm*, All-Wise. A wise separation considers safety, housing, income, legal rights, children, counselling, and the emotional consequences of transition. Trust does not replace planning, and planning does not replace prayer. Both should serve a just outcome.

### The End of Ownership Thinking

A spouse is not property that must be retained at any cost. Marriage is a covenant of tranquillity, affection, mercy, and mutual rights. When those purposes cannot be restored, releasing the other person with dignity may be closer to obedience than using the marriage as a tool of control.

### Hope for a New Beginning

The verse follows difficult guidance with hope. A person who leaves an unjust or destructive marriage can rebuild through repentance, healing, community support, lawful work, and reliance on Allah. The community should help both parties rather than treating divorce as a reason to abandon or shame them.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:131

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Indeed, We have commanded those given the Scripture before you, as well as you, to be mindful of Allah. But if you disobey, then ˹know that˺ to Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth. And Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy.

**Commentary:**

### A Universal Command

Allah reminds the People of the Scripture and the Muslim community that the command to be mindful of Him is not new. *Taqwā*—living with awareness of Allah, reverence, and responsible obedience—was central to the message of earlier revelation and remains central to Islam. Prophetic guidance shares a moral foundation even when laws differ in detail.

### Ownership and Authority

The heavens and earth belong to Allah, so His command is not an external burden imposed by a powerless ruler. He owns the whole order of existence and knows what protects it. Human beings may debate how a principle applies, but they do not become its ultimate source or judge above Allah.

### Taqwā in Family Life

The verse follows detailed guidance about marriage, guardianship, settlement, and separation. These rules are not technical procedures detached from spirituality. Taqwā appears in how spouses handle conflict, how guardians protect orphans, how property is managed, and how people release one another without cruelty.

### Disobedience Does Not Harm Allah

If people disobey, Allah remains the owner of the heavens and earth. Our obedience does not increase His kingdom, and our disobedience does not diminish it. The consequences return to us: disobedience damages individuals, families, communities, and the soul, while obedience cultivates justice and flourishing.

### Allah Is Self-Sufficient

Allah is *Ghanī*, Self-Sufficient and free of need. He does not need worship to complete Himself; we need worship to become rightly ordered. This protects believers from imagining that religious obligations are for Allah’s benefit rather than for the guidance and welfare of His creation.

### Allah Is Praiseworthy

Allah is *Ḥamīd*, Praiseworthy in Himself and in what He commands and decrees. Praise remains due even when people do not understand every wisdom immediately. The believer studies, asks sound questions, and submits with humility rather than measuring revelation solely by personal preference.

### Respect for Earlier Revelation

Mentioning those given Scripture before Muslims teaches that accountability extends across communities. It also encourages respectful dialogue: Muslims affirm the original prophetic call while recognising the Qur’an as the final criterion. Religious disagreement must not justify injustice, mockery, or harm toward peaceful neighbours.

### Taqwā and Human Rights

Mindfulness of Allah must be visible in rights. A person who performs rituals but cheats a spouse, exploits an orphan, withholds inheritance, or humiliates a former partner has not understood the connection between worship and justice. Taqwā restrains private wrongdoing because Allah’s ownership and knowledge encompass every act.

### A Broader Moral Horizon

The family laws in this passage belong to a complete ethic of stewardship, justice, mercy, and accountability. The believer applies the same awareness to work, money, public office, the environment, and relationships. Every trust is held under Allah’s ownership.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:132

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs.

**Commentary:**

### The Repeated Reminder of Ownership

The repetition of Allah’s ownership reinforces the previous command to remain mindful of Him. Human relationships, property, families, and institutions are all within His dominion. No person or community can claim absolute independence from the One who sustains every part of creation.

### Allah as Wakīl

Allah is sufficient as *wakīl*, the Trustee and Disposer of affairs. A *wakīl* is relied upon to manage a matter with knowledge and care. Allah’s sufficiency means that the believer’s ultimate security does not depend on human approval, wealth, or control over every outcome.

### Tawakkul after Responsible Effort

Reliance upon Allah, *tawakkul*, is not abandonment of means. A person plans, consults, works, seeks treatment, protects dependants, and pursues justice; then the heart entrusts the result to Allah. The Prophet ﷺ took precautions while relying completely upon his Lord.

### A Protection against Anxiety

Human beings often try to control what cannot be controlled: another person’s feelings, the future of a marriage, public opinion, or the outcome of a legal dispute. Remembering Allah as *wakīl* does not remove grief, but it prevents anxiety from becoming worship of control. The believer acts within their responsibility and leaves what exceeds it to Allah.

### A Protection against Arrogance

The same belief also restrains pride. A leader, guardian, employer, or wealthy person is not the ultimate owner of those under their care. They are trustees who will answer for how they used authority. Allah’s ownership makes every human claim to power conditional and accountable.

### Trust in Family Difficulty

In the surrounding passage, spouses may face neglect, separation, or uncertainty. They should pursue fair settlement and lawful remedies, but neither spouse controls the whole future. Allah can provide, heal, and open a path for each person. Trust in Him supports dignified decisions rather than clinging to a harmful arrangement from fear.

### Trust and Justice

Entrusting affairs to Allah does not mean tolerating injustice. A person who says “Allah is my wakīl” should still testify truthfully, protect an orphan, pay a debt, and seek lawful accountability. Trust is expressed by obeying Allah’s commands concerning the means as well as the result.

### All Creation under One Lord

The universal wording connects human law with the wider cosmos. Justice is not a private preference; it is part of living rightly within Allah’s created order. Respect for people, animals, land, and resources follows from recognising that nothing belongs to us absolutely.

### Gratitude and Contentment

Allah’s sufficiency cultivates gratitude for what is received and contentment with what is beyond one’s control. Contentment does not mean refusing improvement or accepting oppression. It means seeking good without allowing greed, fear, or status to become the heart’s master.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:133

> If it is His Will, He can remove you altogether, O humanity, and replace you with others. And Allah is Most Capable to do so.

**Commentary:**

### Humanity Is Not Indispensable

Allah reminds humanity that He can remove one people and bring another in their place. Creation does not depend on any nation, class, generation, or religious community. This breaks the illusion that a person or society may disobey indefinitely without consequence.

### A Warning against Arrogance

The verse follows repeated reminders of Allah’s ownership and human responsibility. People who become wealthy, powerful, or numerous may imagine that their position proves divine approval. Allah’s power to replace them exposes that assumption. Blessing is a trust, not a certificate of immunity.

### Replacement through History

Communities may be replaced when corruption, oppression, and persistent rejection destroy their moral foundations. The replacement may occur through the rise of another generation, the loss of political power, social decline, or the spread of guidance through people previously overlooked. History is a field of warning, not a licence to celebrate another community’s downfall.

### Allah’s Power Is Not Human Permission

The verse describes Allah’s sovereignty; it does not authorise people to remove, expel, or harm groups they dislike. No individual or faction may use it to justify ethnic cleansing, forced displacement, persecution, or violence. Human beings remain bound by justice, treaties, due process, and the sanctity of life.

### Renewal through Obedience

Replacement can also be a spiritual warning. If one person refuses a good responsibility, Allah can raise another who fulfils it. If a community neglects justice, another community may become a vehicle for reform. The believer asks not whether they are indispensable but whether they are worthy of being entrusted.

### Human Contingency

Each generation receives the world temporarily. Bodies age, institutions change, and nations rise and fall. This awareness encourages humility about political power and urgency in using time well. A person should do good now rather than assume that opportunity will remain forever.

### Capability and Wisdom

Allah is *Qadīr*, Most Capable, and His power is joined to wisdom. He does not need creation, while creation needs Him. The warning is therefore not a threat from a rival power but a reminder that the One who gave existence can withdraw worldly privilege whenever He wills.

### A Call to Gratitude and Reform

The appropriate response is repentance, gratitude, justice, and service. Leaders should welcome correction, communities should protect the vulnerable, and individuals should use their influence for good. Continuity is preserved not by entitlement but by obedience and responsible stewardship.

### Hope in Renewal

The possibility of replacement also carries hope. A corrupt situation is not necessarily permanent. New believers, younger generations, overlooked servants, and repentant people can become sources of justice and mercy. Allah’s ability to change circumstances means that reform is always worth pursuing.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:134

> Whoever desires the reward of this world, then ˹let them know that˺ with Allah are the rewards of this world and the Hereafter. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Seeing.

**Commentary:**

### Two Horizons of Reward

Allah reminds people that both worldly and everlasting rewards are with Him. Whoever seeks only the world should know that worldly benefit is also under Allah’s control, while the Hereafter is the greater and lasting horizon. No one needs to abandon lawful worldly responsibilities in order to seek Allah, but no temporary gain should replace the final goal.

### Lawful Worldly Aspirations

Islam does not condemn work, education, health, family stability, financial security, or legitimate enjoyment. These can become worship when pursued lawfully and used with gratitude. The danger is treating the world as the only measure of success, or seeking it through injustice, neglect of prayer, exploitation, or betrayal.

### The Balance of the Believer

The Qur’an teaches believers to ask for good in this world and the Hereafter. A balanced person plans for employment, savings, children, and community welfare while preparing for death and meeting Allah. Worldly means are held in the hand, not enthroned in the heart.

### Allah Controls Both Rewards

People often assume that success is produced solely by talent, networks, or relentless effort. Effort matters, but provision and opportunity are ultimately from Allah. This recognition produces gratitude in success, patience in delay, and ethical conduct when competition becomes intense.

### Allah Hears Desires

Allah is *Samīʿ*, All-Hearing. He hears spoken prayers, silent hopes, anxious thoughts expressed to Him, and the requests people conceal from others. The believer can ask for lawful worldly good without shame while remembering that Allah knows which request will truly serve them.

### Allah Sees Actions

Allah is *Baṣīr*, All-Seeing. He sees whether a person pursued wealth honestly, whether a leader distributed opportunity fairly, and whether a seemingly religious deed was motivated by sincerity or reputation. His awareness prevents the believer from measuring success only by visible results.

### Wealth as a Trust

Worldly reward is not proof of Allah’s approval, and poverty is not proof of His rejection. Wealth can be a test of gratitude and generosity; hardship can test patience and dignity. The believer uses whatever Allah provides to fulfil trusts, support dependants, and serve the common good.

### Do Not Postpone the Hereafter

A common delusion is to promise oneself that worship, repentance, or ethical reform will begin after obtaining a degree, promotion, marriage, or financial comfort. The verse places both rewards with Allah now. Worldly striving should be integrated with obedience rather than used to postpone it.

### A Prayerful Perspective

The believer asks Allah for beneficial knowledge, lawful provision, health, family wellbeing, forgiveness, and Paradise. If a worldly request is not granted, the servant trusts Allah’s wisdom without abandoning responsible effort. If it is granted, gratitude prevents the blessing from becoming an idol.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:135

> O believers! Stand firm for justice as witnesses for Allah even if it is against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives. Be they rich or poor, Allah is best to ensure their interests. So do not let your desires cause you to deviate ˹from justice˺. If you distort the testimony or refuse to give it, then ˹know that˺ Allah is certainly All-Aware of what you do.

**Commentary:**

### Justice as a Religious Duty

Believers are commanded to stand firmly for justice, *qisṭ*, as witnesses for Allah. Justice is not merely a social preference or a tool used against enemies. It is an act of worship that remains binding when the truth damages one’s reputation, family interests, political alliance, or financial position.

### Against Oneself

The hardest part of testimony may be admitting one’s own error. This verse demands honesty about an unpaid debt, a broken promise, a conflict of interest, or harm one caused. Repentance begins with truthful recognition; a person cannot repair what they insist never happened.

### Against Family Loyalty

Parents and close relatives deserve kindness, but affection cannot turn falsehood into truth. Protecting a relative from every consequence may expose another person to injustice and encourage the relative to continue wrongdoing. Genuine loyalty calls family members toward honesty, restitution, and reform.

### Rich and Poor before the Law

The rich must not receive favourable treatment because of influence, and the poor must not be romanticised or condemned because of their poverty. Allah knows the interests of both better than human beings do. Justice examines evidence and rights rather than allowing wealth or hardship to become a substitute for facts.

### Desire as a Source of Bias

Personal desire, *hawā*, can bend judgement through love, resentment, fear, greed, tribalism, class prejudice, or the desire to appear compassionate. A person may distort the truth to reward a friend or punish an enemy. The verse requires believers to identify these pressures before they speak or decide.

### Distorting or Withholding Testimony

Testimony can be distorted through lying, selective omission, ambiguous wording, exaggeration, or presenting rumour as observation. It can also be withheld when a person knows that the truth would inconvenience them. Both forms obstruct justice. A witness should distinguish what they directly know from what they assume or heard from others.

### Due Process and Evidence

The command to testify for Allah supports fair investigation, a genuine opportunity to respond, protection from intimidation, and judgement by reliable evidence. It does not encourage mobs, public shaming, or private punishment. Authorities must assess testimony carefully and protect witnesses and accused people from retaliation.

### Justice in Modern Life

The verse applies in courts, family disputes, workplaces, schools, elections, academic research, journalism, and online discussions. A Muslim should not manipulate data, hide a relevant fact, or share a misleading clip simply because it benefits their side. Truth remains a trust when no formal court is present.

### Compassion within Justice

Justice is not cruelty. A truthful decision can include mercy, rehabilitation, payment plans, safeguarding, and attention to circumstances without changing the facts. The poor person’s need may shape a compassionate remedy, but it must not cause the innocent person to bear a false guilt.

### Allah’s Awareness

Allah is fully aware of every testimony, hesitation, omission, and motive. This awareness strengthens the person who speaks truth under pressure and warns the person who believes a distorted account will remain hidden. The final witness is Allah Himself.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:136

> O believers! Have faith in Allah, His Messenger, the Book He has revealed to His Messenger, and the Scriptures He revealed before. Indeed, whoever denies Allah, His angels, His Books, His messengers, and the Last Day has clearly gone far astray.

**Commentary:**

### “Believers, Believe”

The call to believers to believe does not mean that faith is a meaningless repetition. It calls for renewal, completion, and steadfastness. Faith can weaken through neglect, confusion, sin, or attachment to competing loyalties, so the believer continually renews conviction and aligns conduct with what they profess.

### The Pillars of Faith

The verse gathers essential matters of *īmān*: Allah, His Messenger, the Qur’an, earlier revealed Scriptures, angels, all messengers, and the Last Day. Faith is not a self-designed selection from revelation. It accepts the realities Allah has made known and submits to their implications.

### Allah and His Messenger

Faith in Allah means affirming His oneness, lordship, names, and right to worship. Faith in the Messenger Muhammad ﷺ means accepting his truthfulness, following his guidance, and taking his Sunnah as the practical explanation of revelation. Loving the Messenger does not mean worshipping him; it means obeying the one sent by Allah.

### The Qur’an and Earlier Scriptures

Muslims believe that Allah revealed the Torah, Psalms, Gospel, and other scriptures to His messengers in their original divine source. The Qur’an confirms their truth while serving as the final criterion and preserved revelation. Respect for the original prophetic messages does not require treating every later claim or textual interpretation as equally authentic.

### One Source of Prophetic Guidance

The sequence unites the messages of the Prophets. Allah is not the God of one ethnicity or age. From Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad, peace be upon them all, the central call was worship Allah, live righteously, and prepare to meet Him. Differences in law and circumstance do not erase this shared foundation.

### Angels and the Unseen

Belief in angels affirms that reality is wider than what human senses measure. Angels carry Allah’s commands, record deeds, bring revelation, and worship Him. This belief encourages humility and moral awareness without turning angels into objects of worship or superstition.

### The Last Day

Belief in the Last Day gives justice an ultimate horizon. Some wrongs are never corrected fully in this world, and some good deeds remain unseen. Resurrection, judgement, Paradise, and Hell assure believers that Allah’s wisdom and justice will ultimately be manifest.

### Denial as Going Astray

The verse calls denial of these foundations clear misguidance because it rejects the framework Allah provided for knowing Him and living responsibly. It should not be used to mock people, declare the final fate of individuals, or excuse coercion. Faith is conveyed through evidence, wisdom, good character, and freedom from compulsion.

### Faith Is More than Information

Belief should produce prayer, honesty, patience, mercy, justice, and trust. A person may recite a creed while allowing wealth, nationalism, celebrity, or desire to become their practical master. Renewed *īmān* means allowing revelation to govern priorities and choices.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:137

> Indeed, those who believed then disbelieved, then believed and again disbelieved—˹only˺ increasing in disbelief—Allah will neither forgive them nor guide them to the ˹Right˺ Way.

**Commentary:**

### A Pattern of Deliberate Vacillation

The verse describes repeated movement between belief and disbelief that ends in increasing rejection. The repetition suggests more than ordinary weakness, questions, or a believer’s struggle with sin. It points to a settled pattern of treating faith as a tool: entering it for advantage, leaving it in defiance, and repeatedly deepening the rejection of truth.

### Not Every Spiritual Struggle Is This Pattern

A person may experience doubts, intrusive thoughts, fear, or a temporary weakening of faith while sincerely seeking understanding and wanting to remain faithful. Such struggle should be met with knowledge, patient counsel, prayer, and mercy. The verse addresses persistent, wilful rejection—not every question or emotional crisis.

### Disbelief That Increases

Disbelief can increase when a person knowingly suppresses evidence, mocks truth, manipulates religious identity, encourages others to reject guidance, or builds a life around opposition. Each choice makes the next refusal easier. The heart can become attached to denial until it no longer seeks guidance honestly.

### Allah’s Forgiveness and the End of Life

The warning concerns those who persist in disbelief and die upon it. While a person is alive, repentance remains possible, even after serious rejection. If they return sincerely, affirm faith, abandon the falsehood, and repair harm, Allah’s mercy is open. No one should declare a living person beyond the possibility of guidance.

### Guidance Is Not Forced

Allah’s not guiding such people is a just consequence of their persistent choice, not an arbitrary prevention of repentance. Guidance is sought through humility, truthfulness, and willingness to follow truth. A person who continually rejects every reminder may be left to the path they insist upon.

### Do Not Police Hearts

The verse does not give individuals permission to investigate hidden faith, harass those who struggle, or pronounce final judgements on people. Human beings judge outward conduct through proper authority and evidence; Allah knows the inner state and the final end. Faith cannot be produced by coercion.

### Religious Identity as Manipulation

Repeatedly adopting and abandoning identity for political, financial, or social advantage damages trust. Religious affiliation should not be used as a costume for access or a weapon against others. Communities should teach with clarity while refusing exploitation and protecting people from coercive pressure.

### Returning to Stability

The antidote to vacillation is sincere learning, consistent worship, trustworthy companionship, and honest repentance. Faith is strengthened by acting upon what one knows, asking questions responsibly, and seeking Allah’s help. A person need not wait until every difficulty disappears before taking the next faithful step.

### A Warning for Believers

Believers should ask whether they approach religion only when it serves an interest. Faith that disappears whenever obedience becomes costly is vulnerable to the pattern warned against. Steadfastness means returning to Allah after weakness and refusing to make rejection a settled identity.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:138

> Give good news of a painful punishment to hypocrites,

**Commentary:**

### The Irony of “Good News”

The command to give “good news” of punishment is an expression of severe irony. Hypocrites often use pleasant words and religious appearances to conceal betrayal. The verse reverses their language: behind the attractive presentation awaits a painful consequence known to Allah.

### What Is Hypocrisy?

*Nifāq* is the division between outward profession and inward rejection or betrayal. The passage addresses people who claim faith while secretly plotting against the community, manipulating religious identity, and repeatedly choosing disbelief. It is not a label for every believer who commits a sin or experiences weakness.

### Hypocrisy as a Public Danger

Private sin harms the sinner, but hypocrisy can weaponise trust. A hypocrite may obtain access, influence, or protection through a public claim while using that position to damage others. The warning therefore protects communities from deception and calls leaders to combine good assumptions with responsible safeguards.

### Painful Punishment

The punishment is painful because hypocrisy combines falsehood with betrayal. It injures the victims who trusted the person, corrupts the hypocrite’s own heart, and presents an external religious image while rejecting its moral demands. Allah’s judgement accounts for both the inner denial and the outward harm.

### Do Not Rush to Accuse

Only Allah knows the full inner reality of a person. Muslims may identify hypocritical behaviour when evidence is clear and may protect a community from betrayal, but they must not casually declare a named person a *munāfiq* or claim certainty about their final fate. Advice, investigation, and due process are safer than public accusation.

### Recognising the Traits

The Prophet ﷺ described signs of hypocrisy such as lying, breaking promises, and betraying trusts. These are grave traits even when they do not establish the theological hypocrisy described in the verse. A believer who recognises such traits should repent and reform rather than despair or accuse everyone else.

### The Responsibility of Leaders

Leaders should not reward flattery while ignoring betrayal, nor should they demand blind loyalty from critics. Trust must be based on reliable conduct, clear accountability, and consultation. A community can protect itself from deception without turning suspicion into a culture of surveillance.

### Warning and Opportunity

The warning is an opportunity to abandon duplicity. A person can make the public and private self truthful through faith, repentance, honest speech, and fulfilled trusts. Allah’s severe description should awaken the heart before hypocrisy becomes a settled character.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:139

> who choose disbelievers as allies instead of the believers. Do they seek honour and power through that company? Surely all honour and power belongs to Allah.

**Commentary:**

### Allegiance and the Hypocrites

The verse continues the description of hypocrites who sought influence through hostile alliances while claiming membership among the believers. The issue is not ordinary friendship, trade, neighbourliness, citizenship, or cooperation in a just cause with non-Muslims. It concerns choosing a hostile power as a source of protection and status while betraying one’s own moral community.

### The Meaning of Awliyāʾ

The word *awliyāʾ* can carry meanings of protectors, patrons, allies, or intimate authorities. Its meaning is determined by context. The Qur’an prohibits alliances that assist aggression, treachery, or persecution, while it permits justice and kindness toward those who do not fight Muslims. Religious difference alone is not a command to sever every human relationship.

### False Sources of Honour

The hypocrites sought *ʿizzah*, honour and power, through association with an influential hostile group. People may similarly seek dignity through wealth, political patrons, celebrity, ethnicity, or proximity to power. The verse asks whether status has become dependent on people whose approval requires abandoning truth.

### Honour Belongs to Allah

Allah is the source of true honour. Human recognition rises and falls, but dignity before Allah is established through faith, justice, courage, and moral integrity. A believer does not need to humiliate others or imitate oppression to feel valuable. Nor does reliance on Allah prohibit lawful cooperation, diplomacy, or learning from others.

### Loyalty and Justice

Loyalty to believers means supporting justice and protecting the community from betrayal, not excusing every mistake made by Muslims. A Muslim must not lie for a group, conceal its crimes, or oppress outsiders to prove allegiance. Allah’s honour is never gained by injustice.

### Peaceful Relations Remain Permitted

The verse must be read with Qur’an 60:8–9, which permits kindness and justice toward people who do not fight or expel Muslims, and with the treaties and protections discussed earlier in this sūrah. Peaceful non-Muslims are not automatically hostile, and ordinary cooperation in good is not the alliance condemned here.

### Dependency and Moral Compromise

A person who makes a powerful patron their ultimate protector may gradually accept that patron’s falsehoods and injustices. Independence of conscience requires gratitude without surrender, cooperation without moral captivity, and refusal to trade faith for access or social elevation.

### Humility in Community

The verse also warns believers not to confuse belonging with superiority. Allah grants honour to whom He wills, and the measure is not race, nationality, wealth, or political strength. A community becomes worthy of honour by justice, trust, service, and obedience—not by slogans.

### Practical Application

Muslims should evaluate alliances by their purpose and consequences: Do they protect people and promote justice, or do they enable aggression and betrayal? They should build relationships based on truth, keep agreements, resist oppression regardless of its source, and seek honour through service to Allah.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:140

> He has already revealed to you in the Book that when you hear Allah’s revelations being denied or ridiculed, then do not sit in that company unless they engage in a different topic, or else you will be like them.[227] Surely Allah will gather the hypocrites and disbelievers all together in Hell.

**Commentary:**

### Do Not Normalise Religious Mockery

Allah instructs believers not to remain in a gathering where His revelations are being denied or ridiculed. Remaining silently for social comfort can appear to approve the mockery, especially when one has the ability to leave or object respectfully. Faith includes moral boundaries around what the heart accepts and what one’s presence seems to endorse.

### The Earlier Instruction

The verse refers to guidance already given in Qur’an 6:68: turn away from those engaged in mockery until they move to another subject. The instruction is therefore a consistent principle, not a call to seek confrontation. When the topic changes, a person may rejoin ordinary conversation and maintain lawful relationships.

### “You Will Be Like Them”

The likeness concerns participation, approval, or passive acceptance of the mockery—not that every person in the room shares every belief of the speakers. A believer should not laugh along, amplify the insult, provide a platform for it, or remain when their presence meaningfully strengthens the abuse.

### Wisdom in Real Circumstances

People may encounter ridicule at work, school, online, or in a family setting where immediate departure is difficult or unsafe. They should use the safest responsible response: express respectful disapproval, change the topic, disengage from the conversation, report targeted harassment when appropriate, or leave when possible. Allah does not demand a person expose themselves or dependants to avoidable danger.

### No Private Vigilantism

The command to withdraw is not permission to threaten, attack, vandalise, or punish people privately. Religious mockery may be answered with knowledge, dignified speech, lawful complaint, and patient character. Islam protects freedom from compulsion while asking believers not to voluntarily participate in contempt for their faith.

### Choosing Environments

Companionship shapes the heart. Repeated exposure to mockery can make what once felt shocking appear normal, weaken reverence, and encourage the believer to mock others in return. Choosing friends, media, and online spaces carefully is a form of spiritual safeguarding.

### Protecting Vulnerable People

Leaders and institutions should distinguish debate from targeted humiliation. A person may ask hard questions or critique an interpretation without ridiculing revelation or dehumanising believers. Families, schools, and platforms should address harassment through fair policies and protect people from abuse while allowing responsible discussion.

### Hypocrisy and Final Accountability

The verse closes by warning hypocrites and persistent disbelievers of their final gathering before Allah. Hypocrisy is condemned because it presents loyalty outwardly while accepting or advancing rejection inwardly. This final judgement belongs to Allah; human beings must not use the verse to declare individuals doomed or to harm peaceful people.

### Faith with Good Character

A believer’s refusal to join mockery should be calm, principled, and humane. The Prophet ﷺ endured insults without abandoning truth or justice. The best response may be a clear boundary, a thoughtful explanation, or dignified departure, depending on what the circumstance permits.
