## Verse 282

Verse 282 says: *"O believers! When you contract a loan for a fixed period of time, commit it to writing. Let the scribe maintain justice between the parties. The scribe should not refuse to write as Allah has taught them to write. They will write what the debtor dictates, bearing Allah in mind and not defrauding the debt. If the debtor is incompetent, weak, or unable to dictate, let their guardian dictate for them with justice. Call upon two of your men to witness. You must not be against writing ˹contracts˺ for a fixed period—whether the sum is small or great. This is more just ˹for you˺ in the sight of Allah, and more convenient to establish evidence and remove doubts. However, if you conduct an immediate transaction among yourselves, then there is no need for you to record it, but call upon witnesses when a deal is finalized. Let no harm come to the scribe or witnesses. If you do, then you have gravely exceeded ˹your limits˺. Be mindful of Allah, for Allah ˹is the One Who˺ teaches you. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things."*

"O believers! When you contract a loan for a fixed period of time, commit it to writing" — the verse's command: the tadayantum — the debt contract — the ila ajal musamma — the fixed term — the faktubuhu — write it down. Ibn Kathir (the verse's exposition): the command to the believers to record the debt with the specified term — the protection of both parties: the writing is the debt's memory. The verse's frame: the writing as the command: not the recommendation but the prescribed safeguard: the fixed term is the written term.

"Let the scribe maintain justice between the parties. The scribe should not refuse to write as Allah has taught them to write" — the verse's scribe: the katib — the writer — the bi al-'adl — with justice — the la ya'ba — the no refusal. Ibn Kathir (Mujahid and 'Ata'): if asked to do so, the scribe is required to record. The verse's frame: the scribe's duty: the writing is the service of the justice; the taught lesson — "as Allah has taught them" — the writing is the divine instruction's instrument: the refusal is the denial of the learning.

"They will write what the debtor dictates, bearing Allah in mind and not defrauding the debt" — the verse's dictation: the yumlil — the debtor dictates — the taqwa — the bearing in mind — the la yabkhas — the not reducing. Ibn Kathir: "Let him (the debtor) who incurs the liability dictate, and he must have taqwa of Allah, his Lord" — indicates that the debtor should dictate to the scribe what he owes — and not defraud the debt. The verse's frame: the debtor's honesty: the one who owes writes what he owes: the taqwa is the not-reducing: the fraud of the terms is the fear's absence.

"If the debtor is incompetent, weak, or unable to dictate, let their guardian dictate for them with justice" — the verse's guardian: the safih — the incompetent — the da'if — the weak — the wali — the guardian — the waliyumil — the dictates for them — the bi al-'adl — with justice. The verse's frame: the protection of the incapable: the guardian is the voice of the one who cannot speak: the dictation of the justice for the incompetent and the weak.

"Call upon two of your men to witness. If two men cannot be found, then one man and two women of your choice will witness—so if one of the women forgets the other may remind her" — the verse's witnesses: the shahidayn — two witnesses — the men — the one man and two women — the ta'dhill — the errs — the tudhakkira — the reminds. Ibn Kathir: "And get two witnesses out of your own men. And if there are not two men (available), then a man and two women, such as you agree for witnesses, so that if one of them (two women) errs, the other can remind her" — the reference to the two women witnesses: whenever one of them forgets a part of the testimony, the other woman's testimony mends the shortcoming of forgetfulness in the first woman. Al-Jalalayn: the number of women is because of the fact that if one of the two women errs — forgets the testimony — the other will remind her — the "reminding" clause is the reason for the choice of two women. The verse's frame: the verse's own stated wisdom: the two women's mutual corroboration: the memory's supplement — the two witness where one might forget: the reminder is built into the pair. The classical notes on the context: the domain of the financial transactions where women were less familiar (Almuntakhab: memory lapse, lack of experience; ar-Razi and Ibn Taymiyyah per the classical discussion): the measure is the experiential familiarity — the safety of the reminder — not the ranking of the persons. The verse's frame: the testimony's reliability: the two for the one: the safeguard of the forgetfulness.

"The witnesses must not refuse when they are summoned" — the verse's summons: the la ya'ba — the no refusal — the idha ma du'u — when they are called. The verse's frame: the witness's duty: the summoning is the obligation: the testimony is the right's support: the refusal is the justice's deprivation.

"You must not be against writing ˹contracts˺ for a fixed period—whether the sum is small or great" — the verse's weariness: the la tas'am — the do not be weary — the saghir aw kabir — the small or great — the ila ajalahu — to its term. The verse's frame: the smallness's command: the small debt deserves the same writing: the weariness of the small is the doubt's door — the great and the small are both the contract's justice.

"This is more just ˹for you˺ in the sight of Allah, and more convenient to establish evidence and remove doubts" — the verse's wisdom: the aqsat — the more just — the aqwam — the more upright, the more established — the adna alla tartabu — the least of the doubt. The verse's frame: the three fruits: the justice, the evidence's strength, the doubt's removal — the writing's own wisdom: the contract is the proof and the proof is the peace.

"However, if you conduct an immediate transaction among yourselves, then there is no need for you to record it, but call upon witnesses when a deal is finalized" — the verse's exception: the tijarah hadirah — the immediate trade — the la janah — the no blame — the la taktubuhu — the not writing — the ash-hidu — the take witnesses — the idha tabaya'tum — when you transact. The verse's frame: the present transaction's exception: the hand-to-hand: the writing's need is the deferred: the witnessed deal remains: the immediate trade is not the written memory's requirement.

"Let no harm come to the scribe or witnesses. If you do, then you have gravely exceeded ˹your limits˺" — the verse's protection: the la yudarra — the no harm — the katib — the scribe — the shahid — the witness — the fusoq — the wickedness, the exceeding. Ibn Kathir: "Let neither scribe nor witness suffer (or cause) any harm" — the scribe and the witness must not cause any harm — such as when the scribe writes other than what he is being dictated, or the witness testifies to other than what he heard or conceals his testimony. Ma'arif: when the verse began, the scribes were instructed not to refuse writing or testifying — and it was possible that people would give them more trouble than necessary — so the verse said: neither scribe nor witness should be harmed — the Muslim jurists have said that the scribe who demands his wages while writing, or the witness who claims his legitimate conveyance allowance, are entitled to get it as a matter of right — not paying these dues is included under the scope of harming them. The verse's frame: the two harms: the harm from them (the false writing, the false testimony) and the harm to them (the burdens, the unpaid dues): the exceeding of the limits is the fusoq.

"Be mindful of Allah, for Allah ˹is the One Who˺ teaches you. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things" — the verse's close: the taqwa — the mindfulness — the yu'allimukum — the teaches you — the 'alim — the All-Knowing. The verse's frame: the teaching: the law of the writing is the lesson of the Lord: the One who taught the writing teaches the justice: and the 'alim knows the contracts' hidden — the small and the great.

The verse's place: the longest verse of the Qur'an — the verse of the debt (Ayat al-Dayn) — the capstone of the financial legislation: after the charity (2:261–274), the riba (2:275–280) — the law of the lawful debt — the writing, the witnessing, the protecting: the credit's justice. The verse's frame: the contract's holiness: the written word, the honest witness, the just scribe: the believer's economy is the recorded justice.

The verse's lesson: the debt is the trust's memory: write it, witness it, protect the writer and the witness — the small and the great. And the reader's part: the fixed term is the written term; the scribe is the justice's hand; the witnesses are the evidence's eyes; and the harm to them is the exceeding of the limits.

## Verse 283

Verse 283 says: *"If you are on a journey and a scribe cannot be found, then a security can be taken. If you trust one another, then ˹there is no need for a security, but˺ the debtor should honour this trust ˹by repaying the debt˺—and let them fear Allah, their Lord. And do not conceal the testimony, for whoever conceals it, their hearts are indeed sinful. And Allah ˹fully˺ knows what you do."*

In simple terms, the verse gives the journey's law: if the scribe cannot be found, take the security (rahn); and if one trusts the other, the debtor must honor the trust and fear Allah; and the testimony must never be concealed — the concealing heart is sinful — and Allah knows all.

"If you are on a journey and a scribe cannot be found, then a security can be taken" — the verse's journey: the safar — the travel — the la tajidu katiban — the cannot find a scribe. Ibn Kathir: "And if you are on a journey and cannot find a scribe" — traveling, and some of you borrowed some money to be paid at a later date — "and cannot find a scribe" who would record the debt for you. Ibn 'Abbas: "And even if they find a scribe, but did not find paper, ink or pen" — the pledge is taken. The verse's frame: the journey's substitute: the writing is not possible — the rahn — the pledge — the maqbuda — the taken in hand — the security in the creditor's possession: the writing's absence is the pledge's presence.

"If you trust one another, then ˹there is no need for a security, but˺ the debtor should honour this trust ˹by repaying the debt˺—and let them fear Allah, their Lord" — the verse's trust: the fa-in amina ba'dukum ba'dan — if one of you trusts another — the falyu'add alladhi u'tumina amanatahu — the one trusted discharges the trust — the waliyattaqi Allaha rabbahu — and let them fear Allah, their Lord. Ibn Kathir: if one of you (the creditor) trusts another (the debtor) over the debt and does not require a pledge — let him who is trusted (the debtor) deliver his trust (the debt) — and let him fear Allah, his Lord — when delivering it. Al-Jalalayn: the trust — the debtor — the pledge's alternative: the trust is the faith's contract and the repayment is the trust's discharge. The verse's frame: the two securities: the pledge for the untrusted, the trust for the trusted: the fear of the Lord is the guarantee's root: the faith is the collateral.

"And do not conceal the testimony, for whoever conceals it, their hearts are indeed sinful" — the verse's testimony: the la taktumu al-shahadah — the do not conceal the testimony — the man yaktumha — the whoever conceals it — the athim qalbuhu — the sinful heart. Ibn Kathir: the false testimony is one of the worst of the major sins, and such is the case with hiding the true testimony — "for he who hides it, surely, his heart is sinful" — As-Suddi: "meaning he is a sinner in his heart" — the cross-reference (5:106): "We shall not hide the testimony of Allah, for then indeed we should be of the sinful." Ma'arif: the person who knows the correct situation in a disputed matter should not withhold his testimony — the heart has been identified as the sinner because somebody might think his sin was restricted to the tongue (which failed to speak up) — but it was in the heart that he first decided to do what he did — therefore the sin of his heart comes first. The verse's frame: the concealing is the sin before the sin: the heart's decision to hide the truth is the sin's root: the testimony's concealment is the justice's murder.

"And Allah ˹fully˺ knows what you do" — the verse's close: the 'alim — the All-Knowing. The verse's frame: the known: the concealment of the witness and the keeping of the pledge: the All-Knowing of the deeds.

The verse's place: the journey's law within the debt's law (2:282–283): the writing at home, the pledge on the road, the trust's honor everywhere: the testimony's duty always. The verse's frame: the raising of the opposite of the pledge: the trust's honor: the debtor who is trusted honors the trust — the higher law: the creditor who trusts is the believer's trust.

The verse's lesson: the writing is the protection, the pledge is the journey's substitution, the trust is the faith's height — and the concealed testimony is the sinning heart. And the reader's part: honor the trust and fear the Lord; never hide the truth you witnessed — the heart that hides it is sinful, and the All-Knowing knows.

## Verse 284

Verse 284 says: *"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Whether you reveal what is in your hearts or conceal it, Allah will call you to account for it. He forgives whoever He wills, and punishes whoever He wills. And Allah is Most Capable of everything."*

In simple terms, the verse states the divine ownership and the accounting: all in the heavens and the earth is Allah's; whether you reveal what is in the hearts or conceal it, Allah calls you to account; He forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills; and He is Most Capable of everything.

"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth" — the verse's ownership: the mulk — the kingdom — the heavens and the earth. Ibn Kathir: Allah states that His is the kingship of the heavens and earth and of what and whoever is on or between them — that He has perfect watch over them. The verse's frame: the ownership of all: the realm of the accounting: the One who owns the heavens and the earth owns the hearts and their hidden: the kingdom is the jurisdiction of the reckoning.

"Whether you reveal what is in your hearts or conceal it, Allah will call you to account for it" — the verse's accounting: the tubdu — the reveal — the tukhfu — the conceal — the yuhasibkum — the calls you to account. Ibn Kathir: no apparent matter or secret that the heart conceals is ever a secret to Him, however minor it is — Allah also states that He will hold His servants accountable for what they do and what they conceal in their hearts. The verse's frame: the heart's reckoning: the inner intention is the accountable: the revealed deed and the hidden intention: the accounting of the interior — the realm beyond the witnesses and the scribes (2:282–283): the One who knows the hearts.

"He forgives whoever He wills, and punishes whoever He wills" — the verse's judgment: the yaghfir — the forgives — the yu'addhib — the punishes. The verse's frame: the judgment's will: the forgiveness and the punishment are the divine will's justice: the will is not the caprice: the justice is the wisdom's measure: the accounting's end.

"And Allah is Most Capable of everything" — the verse's close: the qadir — the Most Capable. The verse's frame: the capability of the accounting: the One who knows the hearts is the One who is capable of the reckoning: the power over all.

The verse's occasion and the classical interpretation: Ibn Kathir: when this ayah was revealed, it was hard on the Companions — since out of their strong faith and conviction, they were afraid that such reckoning would diminish their good deeds — then Allah abrogated the ayah and sent down 2:286 — per the hadith (Muslim and the Group): when they did that, Allah abrogated it and sent down "Allah burdens not a person beyond his scope" — therefore they were pardoned what happens in their hearts, and were held accountable only for their actions. The classical note (Hizb ut-Tahrir's exposition): the verse is not about matters of belief alone but about the rulings — and 2:286 clarifies: the accountability is based on what appears through the limbs in actions and words, not on what remains hidden within the hearts — the hidden intention that becomes deed is accounted; the passing whisper that does not manifest is the pardoned. The verse's frame: the two verses together: the accounting of the hearts (2:284) and the mercy of the capacity (2:286): the believer holds the heart's account, and the Lord's mercy balances the whispered thoughts.

The verse's lesson: all is Allah's and the hearts are His: the accountable interior: the knowing of the hidden: the forgiveness's will and the mercy's balance. And the reader's part: keep the heart: the One who owns the heavens and the earth calls the hearts to account — and the Most Capable forgives whom He wills.

## Verse 286

Verse 286 says: *"Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford. All good will be for its own benefit, and all evil will be to its own loss. ˹The believers pray,˺ "Our Lord! "All good will be for its own benefit, and all evil will be to its own loss" — the verse's earning: the laha ma kasabat — the good for it — the 'alayha ma iktasabat — the evil against it. The verse's frame: the two accounts: the good's gain and the evil's burden: the earned: the personal responsibility.

"˹The believers pray,˺ "Our Lord! Do not punish us if we forget or make a mistake"" — the verse's first plea: the la tu'akhidhna — do not punish us — the in nasina — if we forget — the aw akhta'na — or made a mistake. "Our Lord! The verse's frame: the previous burdens: the communities before had the heavier loads: the believers' plea: the mercy of the ummah: the easy of this legislation.

"Our Lord! Do not burden us with what we cannot bear" — the verse's third plea: the la tuhammilna — do not burden us — the ma la taqata lana bihi — what we cannot bear. Ibn Kathir: the believers asked — and Allah answered — "I have done so" — the mercy: the not-burdening beyond the capacity: the answer of the Lord. The verse's frame: the answered capacity: the verse of the capacity is the answer to the plea: the recitation of the prayer is the answer's reception.

"Pardon us, forgive us, and have mercy on us" — the verse's triad: the u'fu — the pardon — the ighfir — the forgive — the irham — the have mercy. Ibn Kathir: the three pleas answered — the pardon, the forgiveness, the mercy — the believers' triple request and the Lord's triple grant. The verse's frame: the mercy's completeness: the pardon of the errors, the forgiveness of the sins, the mercy of the hearts: the three mercies.

"You are our ˹only˺ Guardian" — the verse's guardianship: the anta mawlana — You are our Guardian. The verse's frame: the singular reliance: the Guardian of the believers (2:257): the one Protector: the helper and the watcher: the believers' wali.
