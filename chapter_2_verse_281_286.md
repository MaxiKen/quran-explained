## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:281

> Fear a Day when you will be returned to Allah. Then every soul will be paid in full for what it earned, and none will be wronged.

**Commentary:**

### The Final Return

This āyah is the last command in the Qur’an to be revealed according to a well-known report from Ibn ʿAbbās, and it stands as a fitting conclusion to the economic and social laws of the preceding passage. After discussing charity, ribā, debt, documentation, and mercy toward the debtor, Allah turns the heart toward the final account. Every financial decision and every human right will return to Him.

“Returned to Allah”—*turjaʿūna ilā Allāh*—does not mean that Allah was absent from the servants’ lives. It means that they will be gathered before Him for judgement. The world is a temporary trust; the Hereafter is the place where the full meaning of each choice becomes visible.

### Every Soul Receives Its Due

Every soul will be paid in full—*tuwafā kullu nafsin mā kasabat*. No good deed is lost, and no wrong is forgotten. A creditor who forgave a poor debtor, a scribe who documented a contract honestly, a witness who told the truth, and a person who refused ribā all have their deeds recorded. Likewise, hidden fraud, withheld wages, false testimony, and economic exploitation cannot disappear because a court or audience failed to notice them.

The word *kasb* refers to what a person acquires through intention and action. Wealth itself is not the whole account. Allah records why it was earned, how it was obtained, how it was spent, and whose rights were affected. A small act may be great through sincerity, while a large public gift may be empty through showing off.

### None Will Be Wronged

“None will be wronged”—*lā yuẓlamūn*—is a universal guarantee of divine justice. Allah will not reduce the reward of a good deed, add a sin to an innocent person, or punish a person for what lay beyond his ability and knowledge. He knows coercion, ignorance, intention, capacity, and the effect of every circumstance.

This promise comforts people whose rights were taken. A worker may not recover unpaid wages in a human court, a widow may be defrauded of inheritance, or a debtor may be humiliated despite genuine hardship. Allah sees the full truth. It also warns the powerful that escaping human accountability does not mean escaping the final account.

### Fear as Moral Protection

“Fear a Day” translates *ittaqū yawman*. Taqwā is not panic; it is protecting oneself from wrongdoing by remembering the meeting with Allah. The preceding laws become meaningful when a person understands that contracts, oaths, charity, and debt are acts of worship and matters of judgement.

A person who remembers the final return is less likely to take a bribe, hide a debt, pressure a widow, or charge a desperate borrower an unlawful increase. He may not receive immediate worldly benefit from justice, but he knows that Allah’s account is certain.

### Hope within Accountability

The verse is serious, but it is not a message of despair. Allah’s mercy remains open through repentance, charity, restitution, and forgiveness. The next verses close the sūrah with faith, obedience, and a prayer for pardon. Fear directs the servant away from injustice, while hope motivates him to return when he falls.

Repentance for financial wrongdoing must include restoring people’s rights where possible. Saying “Allah forgive me” while keeping stolen property is incomplete. The person returns the money, corrects the record, apologizes where appropriate, and seeks Allah’s mercy.

### A Conclusion to Economic Ethics

The sequence from 2:261 to 2:281 contrasts charity with ribā and generosity with exploitation. The final return explains why the contrast matters. A person may appear to gain through interest, fraud, or manipulation, but the final account reverses the appearance. The one who gives may seem to lose, but Allah pays him in full and multiplies the reward.

The verse therefore unites private worship and public justice. Faith is not only prayer and recitation; it is how a person handles another’s money, records an agreement, and responds to someone in hardship.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:275–281 on ribā, debt, mercy, documentation, and the final account.
- Qur’an 3:25, 18:49, and 99:7–8 on the gathering, complete records, and the presentation of every deed.
- Qur’an 4:40 and 16:90 on Allah’s perfect justice and command to act with justice and excellence.
- Qur’an 39:53 and 66:8 on repentance, forgiveness, and returning to Allah.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on the final return, the repayment of rights, and the value of hidden deeds.
- For *turjaʿūna*, *kasb*, *tawfiyah*, *ẓulm*, and the relation between economic conduct and the Hereafter, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 2:281.
## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:282

> O believers, when you contract a debt for a specified term, write it down. Let a scribe write it between you with justice. Let no scribe refuse to write as Allah has taught him; so let him write. Let the debtor dictate, and let him fear Allah, his Lord, and not leave anything out. If the debtor is mentally deficient, weak, or unable to dictate, then let his guardian dictate with justice. Call two men from among you as witnesses; if two men are not available, then one man and two women from those acceptable to you as witnesses, so that if one of the women makes an error, the other can remind her. Witnesses should not refuse when they are called. Do not be reluctant to write the debt, whether it is small or large, together with its specified term. That is more just in the sight of Allah, stronger as evidence, and more likely to prevent doubt between you, except for an immediate transaction conducted directly among yourselves, for which there is no blame if you do not write it. Take witnesses when you conclude a commercial contract. Let no scribe or witness be harmed. If you do so, it is disobedience on your part. Be mindful of Allah; Allah teaches you, and Allah knows all things.

**Commentary:**

### The Longest Verse and the Ethics of Documentation

This is the longest āyah in the Qur’an, and its length reflects the importance of preserving rights. Debt can create gratitude, tension, suspicion, and family conflict. A written record protects the lender from denial and the borrower from exaggerated claims. It also protects heirs, witnesses, and communities when the original parties die or memories differ.

The command to write—*faktubūhu*—is a strong instruction toward documentation. Classical jurists discussed whether it is an obligation in every debt or a recommendation reinforced by the surrounding commands. The moral principle is clear: important financial commitments should be recorded accurately, especially when repayment is delayed or the amount is significant.

### A Scribe with Justice

The scribe must write between the parties with justice—*bi-l-ʿadl*. He should record the actual amount, currency, goods, due date, instalments, collateral, conditions, and any lawful fees without favouring the powerful party. He must not hide a penalty, change a number, or use technical language that the other party cannot understand.

The command not to refuse writing “as Allah has taught him” honours useful skill as a trust. A qualified accountant, lawyer, translator, notary, or digital record keeper serves the public when he documents accurately. He may charge a fair fee, but he must not use his expertise to exploit ignorance.

Modern electronic contracts, bank records, signed messages, and secure digital signatures can serve the purpose of written documentation when their authenticity and terms are clear. The medium changes; the obligation of accuracy and access to the record remains.

### The Debtor Dictates

The debtor is told to dictate the terms because he is the one who owes the obligation and knows what he has undertaken. He must fear Allah and leave nothing out. He cannot conceal a condition, deny receiving the money, or exploit the creditor’s trust. Honesty is required even when the lender is powerful.

Having the debtor dictate also protects against a scribe or creditor imposing terms that were not agreed. The parties should read or explain the document in a language they understand before signing. Consent is meaningful only when the material terms are known and the person is free from coercion.

### Guardianship of the Vulnerable

If the debtor is *safīh*, mentally deficient, weak, or unable to dictate, his guardian dictates with justice. The provision protects minors, people with impaired judgement, the illiterate, and those temporarily unable to speak or understand the transaction. A guardian is not an owner; he is a trustee whose duty is to protect the person’s rights.

The guardian must not exploit disability, age, illness, or lack of education. Independent witnesses, legal oversight, accessible explanations, and supported decision-making can help preserve genuine consent. The verse’s purpose is protection, not exclusion from financial life.

### Witnesses and Financial Evidence

The verse instructs the parties to call witnesses, establishing a strong evidentiary practice for deferred debts. It describes two men, or one man and two women from those acceptable as witnesses, with the stated purpose that one may remind the other if she errs. Classical jurists discussed the conditions, scope, and legal effect of this arrangement in the specific context of documented financial transactions.

The wording should not be turned into a declaration that women are universally deficient in intelligence, memory, or moral worth. The Qur’an recognizes women as moral agents, property owners, teachers, witnesses in other legal contexts according to the relevant evidence, and equal recipients of spiritual accountability (33:35). The verse gives a procedure for a particular kind of financial documentation in a particular social setting, and its application requires knowledge of the legal tradition and contemporary evidentiary systems.

Witnesses must be competent, impartial, and truthful. They should not accept payment that corrupts testimony, conceal a conflict of interest, or sign a document they do not understand. Where modern courts and secure records establish reliable evidence, qualified scholars can explain how the underlying objective is fulfilled.

### Do Not Refuse Testimony

“Witnesses should not refuse when they are called” means that a person who is needed to protect a right should not evade the responsibility out of inconvenience, fear, or loyalty to a powerful party. At the same time, a witness should not be summoned to harassment, public humiliation, or an unlawful investigation. The request must be legitimate, and the witness must speak only to what he knows.

The Qur’an repeatedly commands truthful testimony even when it is against oneself or relatives (4:135). A witness who remains silent while a vulnerable person’s property is stolen may share in the injustice.

### Small or Large Debts

The command applies whether the debt is small or large. Small debts can produce enormous conflict because people assume that memory is enough or that recording a modest amount is insulting. Writing protects friendship and kinship by removing uncertainty. It also prevents a lender from later adding an amount that was never agreed.

The specified term must be recorded. “I will pay you later” is a source of avoidable dispute. A clear date, schedule, or condition gives both parties a standard for communication and lawful extension.

### Immediate Transactions

The verse gives an exception for a transaction completed immediately—*tijārah ḥāḍirah tudīrunahā baynakum*. There is no blame if the parties do not write every cash exchange on the spot. Even here, receipts, invoices, and records can be wise, especially in business. The exception removes a burden; it does not forbid documentation.

The next command to take witnesses when concluding a commercial contract shows that caution remains valuable. The parties choose the level of documentation according to the risk, while debts and delayed obligations deserve particular care.

### Do Not Harm the Scribe or Witness

A scribe or witness must not be harmed. Harm includes threats, bribery, coercion, defamation, withholding payment for professional work, or punishing someone for truthful testimony. A creditor and borrower may not pressure the scribe to alter the record, and an employer or official may not retaliate against a witness.

The prohibition also protects the parties from a scribe or witness who uses his position to extort them. Everyone involved remains under justice. Documentation is meant to resolve conflict, not create a new opportunity for abuse.

### Taqwā and Knowledge

The verse ends by saying that Allah teaches and knows all things. Financial law is therefore a field of worship. A person learns how contracts work, writes clearly, seeks expert advice, and remembers that Allah sees the hidden agreement. Taqwā prevents both careless informality and manipulative legalism.

The āyah gives a model for a trustworthy economy: clear terms, informed consent, protection of vulnerable people, reliable evidence, and accountability. These principles preserve friendship and prevent disputes before they grow.

- Qur’an 4:58 and 4:135 on returning trusts and standing firmly for justice.
- Qur’an 5:1 and 16:91 on fulfilling contracts and covenants.
- Qur’an 33:35 on equal spiritual accountability of men and women, relevant to reading the witness provision without degrading women.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on truthful testimony, returning trusts, and the prohibition of consuming another’s wealth; the Prophet ﷺ also encouraged documenting financial obligations.
- For *dayn*, *ajal musammā*, *kātib*, *shahādah*, *walī*, the witness arrangement, and the protection against harm, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 2:282.
## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:283

> If you are on a journey and cannot find a scribe, then a pledge held in hand may serve as security. If you trust one another, let the one entrusted discharge his trust, and let him fear Allah, his Lord. Do not conceal testimony; whoever conceals it has a sinful heart. Allah knows all that you do.

**Commentary:**

### A Practical Alternative when Travelling

The preceding verse established writing and witnesses for deferred debts. This verse addresses travel or circumstances in which a scribe cannot be found. A pledged security—*rahn maqbūḍah*—can protect the creditor until repayment. The pledge may be property held by the creditor, but it remains a security for a defined debt, not an automatic transfer of ownership.

The provision reflects practical wisdom. Travellers may be far from courts, notaries, or reliable records. Allah gives a method for preserving rights without making the absence of a scribe an excuse for fraud. The pledge should be valued fairly, its condition recorded where possible, and returned when the debt is paid.

### Trust Can Replace Security

“If you trust one another,” the verse permits the relationship to proceed without a pledge. Trust—*amānah*—is not foolishness; it is a moral relationship supported by faith and character. A trusted borrower must discharge what he was entrusted with, and the creditor must not abuse the trust by inventing extra claims.

The verse does not condemn all unsecured lending. It recognizes that families, friends, businesses, and communities may lend on trust. But trust should be informed. A person may request documentation or collateral without insulting the borrower, and a person may offer a guarantee without promising what he cannot fulfil.

### The Entrusted Person’s Duty

The one entrusted—*alladhī uʾtumina amānatahu*—must fear Allah and return the trust. He records the obligation, communicates honestly when repayment is delayed, protects any pledged property, and does not treat a friend’s confidence as permission to disappear. The Prophet ﷺ taught that signs of hypocrisy include betraying a trust and breaking a promise.

Repayment is a moral act even when there is no court, witness, or public pressure. A person who can repay but refuses is wronging the creditor. A person who genuinely cannot repay communicates, seeks a revised schedule, and avoids further deception.

### The Pledge Is Not a Licence for Exploitation

A creditor holding collateral must not use it as an opportunity to seize more than the debt. If the collateral’s value exceeds the amount owed, the surplus belongs to the owner. The creditor must care for the property and return it after settlement. Any benefit or condition attached to a loan must be examined for ribā and exploitation.

Modern collateral can include vehicles, land, equipment, or financial assets. Written records, independent valuations, and lawful enforcement protect both parties. The Qur’an’s aim is security without oppression.

### Concealing Testimony

The verse ends with the duty not to conceal testimony. A witness who knows a debt or right and hides what he knows can cause property to be stolen and trust to collapse. Concealing testimony is described as a sin of the heart because it reflects a settled willingness to hide truth, even if the person’s tongue remains silent.

The command does not require a person to testify to what he did not see, expose private matters unnecessarily, or participate in an unlawful proceeding. It requires truthful testimony when a legitimate right depends upon it. A person may say, “I do not know,” rather than inventing certainty.

### The Heart and Outward Conduct

The verse connects testimony to the heart because financial dishonesty often begins inwardly. A person decides that a friend’s property matters less than his own need, or that loyalty to a powerful family outweighs truth. The hidden decision then appears as silence, altered records, or evasive speech.

Allah knows the inner motive and the outward act. Human beings may not discover that a witness concealed a fact, but Allah does. This protects victims and warns those who think silence is morally neutral.

### Trust in Community Life

A healthy community needs both formal safeguards and personal trust. Documentation protects when memory fails; trust allows relationships to function without turning every interaction into litigation. Islam teaches people to use each appropriately. Wealthy institutions especially should not demand unreasonable guarantees from the poor, while vulnerable people should not be exposed to informal agreements that powerful lenders can manipulate.

Charitable loan funds can combine written records, fair collateral, and mercy for hardship. Trust is strengthened by transparency, not by abandoning evidence.

### Repentance and Restoration

Someone who concealed testimony or misused a pledge must repent and restore the harmed person’s right. If a witness’s silence caused a false judgement, he should correct the record. If collateral was taken unlawfully, it must be returned or compensated. Allah’s forgiveness is sought through repair, not only words.

- Qur’an 4:58 and 5:1 on returning trusts and fulfilling contracts.
- Qur’an 4:135 and 5:8 on truthful testimony even against oneself or one’s group.
- Qur’an 2:280 and 2:275–279 on hardship, delay, and the prohibition of exploitative increase.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on returning trusts, repaying debts, and the signs of hypocrisy; the Prophet ﷺ also used pledges in lawful transactions.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:284

> To Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Whether you reveal what is within yourselves or conceal it, Allah will call you to account for it. He forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills, and Allah is over all things capable.

**Commentary:**

### Universal Ownership

The verse begins by returning every visible and hidden matter to Allah: whatever is in the heavens and the earth belongs to Him. This statement follows the detailed guidance on debts, witnesses, pledges, and testimony. Human beings may own property in a legal and temporary sense, but absolute ownership belongs to Allah. Therefore wealth, contracts, and rights must be handled according to His limits.

Divine ownership also means that no private agreement is outside moral accountability. A lender and borrower may meet in secret, an executor may hide an asset, or a witness may conceal the truth, but Allah owns the circumstances and knows the act. Human privacy is respected; hidden injustice is not invisible to Him.

### The Inner and Outer Life

Allah knows what people reveal and conceal within themselves. This includes settled intentions, deliberate plans, hidden hypocrisy, and the motives behind outward actions. A charitable gift may look generous while being intended to buy influence; a legal document may look fair while concealing exploitation. Allah knows the inward reality.

The verse should not be misunderstood as making believers sinful for every involuntary thought, passing temptation, or intrusive image. The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah has pardoned his community for what they think within themselves unless they speak or act upon it, in the authentic reports of Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Accountability concerns what the heart deliberately adopts and what a person brings into speech or action.

### The Companions’ Concern

The Companions were troubled by the verse because they understood the seriousness of hidden accountability. The Prophet ﷺ taught them to respond with submission, and the following verses brought reassurance and described faith, obedience, and the limits of capacity. The passage therefore moves from fear to a balanced understanding of Allah’s judgement.

A believer should take the inner life seriously without falling into despair or obsessive self-accusation. He notices envy, anger, and temptation, seeks purification, and refuses to nourish them. A passing feeling is not the same as a chosen plan.

### Forgiveness and Punishment

Allah forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills. His forgiveness is not arbitrary and His punishment is not unjust. He knows the evidence received, the intention formed, the harm caused, repentance made, and capacity possessed. He forgives through faith, repentance, good deeds, and mercy, while deliberate injustice and rejection have consequences.

The statement teaches humility. No person can demand forgiveness as though Allah were obligated to him, and no sinner should declare Allah’s mercy impossible. The servant works, repents, restores rights, and hopes in Allah’s generosity.

### Hidden Financial Conduct

The immediate application is to financial dealings. A person may not be caught when he hides a debt, alters a contract, gives false testimony, or misuses a pledge. Allah’s knowledge reaches the concealed deed. This gives protection to people with little power and restrains those who think legal appearances can shield corruption.

The verse also comforts the honest. A borrower may be unable to pay but truthful about his hardship; a witness may tell the truth despite pressure; a scribe may refuse to alter a record. Allah knows the sacrifice even when worldly systems reward the dishonest.

### Capacity and Accountability

The next verse will declare that Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity. This provides an essential qualification. Allah knows not only what is hidden but what a person could actually control. A person is not judged for involuntary thoughts, unavoidable ignorance, or an impossible burden. He is judged for deliberate choice within ability.

This balance is important in spiritual life. Believers do not use hidden intention as an excuse to ignore conduct, and they do not treat every unwanted thought as a major sin. They cultivate a truthful heart and seek help when intrusive thoughts or mental illness cause distress.

### Allah’s Complete Power

Allah is capable of everything—*qadīr*. He can preserve a hidden good deed, expose concealed fraud, forgive a repentant sinner, and bring every matter to a final account. No complexity in the human heart or social economy exceeds His knowledge and power.

The verse prepares the community to receive the final passage of the sūrah with humility, submission, and hope.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:285

> The Messenger believes in what has been sent down to him from his Lord, and so do the believers. They all believe in Allah, His angels, His Books, and His messengers. They say, “We make no distinction between any of His messengers,” and they say, “We hear and obey. Our Lord, we seek Your forgiveness, and to You is the return.”

**Commentary:**

### The Messenger Believes First

The verse begins with the Messenger ﷺ believing in what was sent down to him. He is not merely a transmitter who stands outside the message. He receives it with complete certainty, obeys it, and embodies it. His personal submission gives the believers a model: revelation is not only information to be delivered to others but a trust to be accepted by the one who teaches it.

The believers follow his example. Their faith is comprehensive: they believe in Allah, angels, revealed Books, and all the messengers. This is the foundation of Islamic belief and a protection against selective religion.

### Belief in the Messengers

“We make no distinction between any of His messengers” means that Muslims do not believe in some prophets while rejecting others. They honour Ādam, Nūḥ, Ibrāhīm, Mūsā, ʿĪsā, Muhammad, and every messenger named or unnamed in revelation. The messengers differ in rank and mission, as 2:253 explains, but none may be rejected as a false prophet.

The statement does not deny that Allah favoured some messengers with particular degrees. It rejects the partisan method of accepting one and insulting another. Muslims honour Jesus as a mighty messenger and the Messiah while rejecting his deification, and they honour Moses while accepting the final revelation sent through Muhammad ﷺ.

### Allah’s Books

Belief in the Books means affirming the original revelations Allah sent to His messengers. The Qur’an confirms truth from earlier scripture and serves as the final criterion. Muslims do not claim that every later text or interpretation attributed to previous revelation has been preserved without alteration; they follow what the Qur’an confirms and what reliable revelation establishes.

The Qur’an itself is believed to be Allah’s final preserved speech. It guides the community in creed, worship, law, and character. Belief in previous Books should produce respect for their prophets and caution against careless claims about their texts.

### “We Hear and Obey”

The response of the believers is *samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā*: we hear and we obey. This is not a rejection of reason or a demand to obey every human claim. It is the posture of a servant before clear revelation. The believer listens with understanding, asks sincere questions when needed, and then accepts Allah’s command rather than making personal desire the final judge.

Obedience is not instantaneous perfection in every detail. A believer may struggle, fall, and repent. The statement establishes the direction of the heart: he does not make resistance to Allah a permanent principle. He seeks knowledge and tries to bring conduct into harmony with faith.

### Seeking Forgiveness

Even after affirming faith and obedience, the believers ask forgiveness. This teaches that obedience does not produce self-righteousness. Human beings have weaknesses in intention, attention, and performance. They thank Allah for guidance and ask Him to cover their shortcomings.

The request for forgiveness also follows the warning about hidden thoughts in 2:284. Believers do not claim that they are accountable only for public deeds while ignoring the heart. They ask Allah to purify both inner motives and outward conduct.

### Return to Allah

“To You is the return”—*ilayka al-maṣīr*. The final destination gives weight to every statement of faith and act of obedience. Believers will return to the Lord who revealed, guided, and taught them. Their hope is not in their own perfection but in Allah’s mercy, while their deeds testify to sincere submission.

The return also unites the messengers’ message. Humanity has one destination and one Lord, even though communities received revelation at different times. The believer prepares through faith, worship, reconciliation, and justice.

### The Prophet’s Sunnah and the Community

The Prophet ﷺ lived this verse through prayer, charity, patience, justice, and forgiveness. He accepted revelation even when it corrected his preferences and taught the Companions to respond with submission. The community’s claim to follow him is measured by the same qualities.

The final two verses of al-Baqarah are especially beloved in the Sunnah. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever recites them at night, they suffice him, in the authentic reports of Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Their benefit is realized through recitation, reflection, and the obedience they teach.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:286

> Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity. It will have the reward of what good it earns, and it will bear the consequence of what evil it earns. “Our Lord, do not take us to task if we forget or make a mistake. Our Lord, do not place upon us a burden like the one You placed upon those before us. Our Lord, do not burden us with what we have no strength to bear. Pardon us, forgive us, and have mercy on us. You are our Protector, so help us against the disbelieving people.”

**Commentary:**

### The Final Principle of Capacity

The sūrah closes with the promise that Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity—*lā yukallifu Allāhu nafsan illā wusʿahā*. This is a foundational principle of Islamic law and spiritual life. Allah knows the body, mind, resources, circumstances, knowledge, and genuine ability of every servant. He does not demand the impossible or punish a person for what lies completely beyond control.

The principle does not mean that every obligation will feel easy. Prayer, fasting, charity, patience, truthfulness, and resisting sin can require effort. Capacity includes the ability to persevere through reasonable difficulty, while genuine illness, disability, coercion, danger, and incapacity bring concessions explained throughout the Sharīʿah.

### Each Soul Earns and Bears

The verse immediately joins capacity to personal accountability. Each soul receives the benefit of what it earns—*lahā mā kasabat*—and bears the consequence of what it earns—*ʿalayhā mā iktasabat*. No one can claim another person’s reward as his own, and no one is punished for another person’s sin. The principle of mercy does not erase responsibility.

Good deeds are attributed as a gift and benefit to the servant, while wrongdoing is described as something he acquires against himself. Allah’s justice recognizes intention, knowledge, coercion, and ability. A person who makes an honest mistake is not judged like one who deliberately chooses betrayal after repeated clarification.

### Forgetting and Mistake

The supplication asks Allah not to take believers to task for forgetfulness or error. The Prophet ﷺ reported that Allah answered this request, as recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. This does not make negligence harmless. A person who forgets a prayer makes it up when he remembers; a person who makes a financial mistake corrects the record; a person who harms another apologizes and repairs what he can.

The distinction is between an unintentional error and deliberate neglect disguised as forgetfulness. Allah knows whether a person truly forgot, failed despite reasonable care, or avoided responsibility and then claimed an excuse. The servant takes precautions while trusting Allah’s mercy for what remains beyond him.

### The Burdens of Earlier Communities

The believers ask not to be placed under a burden like those placed upon earlier communities. This can refer to severe legal burdens, difficult tests, or the consequences that followed earlier peoples’ disobedience. The prayer is not a complaint against Allah’s justice; it is a request for mercy and ease in the final community’s law.

The Qur’an describes the Prophet ﷺ as lifting burdens and shackles from people through the final revelation (7:157). The law contains clear obligations but also purification, repentance, concessions, and the principle of capacity. Muslims should not invent extra burdens and then call them greater piety.

### Do Not Burden Us beyond Strength

The believers ask not to be burdened with what they cannot bear—*mā lā ṭāqata lanā bih*. This prayer is relevant to illness, poverty, persecution, family conflict, debt, trauma, and spiritual weakness. It invites people to ask Allah for relief while using lawful means: medical care, counselling, legal protection, financial assistance, and supportive community.

A person should not be shamed for using a concession Allah granted. A sick person may break a fast and make it up or give fidyah according to the ruling; a traveller may shorten prayer; someone in danger may pray according to ability. The form changes, but the servant remains connected to Allah.

### Pardon, Forgiveness, and Mercy

The supplication then asks for *ʿafw*, pardon; *maghfirah*, forgiveness; and *raḥmah*, mercy. Pardon can mean Allah overlooking a fault, forgiveness can mean covering the sin and its consequence, and mercy can mean granting guidance and good in this world and the Hereafter. The sequence shows how completely the servant depends upon Allah.

The believer should also embody these qualities. He pardons where justice and safety allow, forgives sincere repentance, and shows mercy to those facing weakness. He does not use the capacity principle to excuse oppressors, nor does he use accountability to crush people who are genuinely unable.

### Allah as Protector

“You are our Protector”—*anta mawlānā*. *Mawlā* can mean guardian, master, supporter, and intimate ally. The servant belongs to Allah and seeks His protection from sin, fear, despair, and hostile forces. This allegiance does not require hatred of every person outside the faith. It places ultimate loyalty with Allah while commanding justice toward peaceful people.

A believer asks Allah for help against those who disbelieve and actively oppose the truth or attack the community, in the context of the earlier verses. He must not use the prayer as permission to harm innocent people or to turn religious difference into private violence.

### Help against Hostility

The final request for help against disbelieving people completes the sūrah’s discussions of persecution, defence, leadership, charity, and patience. Help may come through victory, protection, truthful evidence, reconciliation, or the strength to remain faithful. It is sought within the limits of 2:190–193: no transgression, acceptance of cessation, and justice toward those who do not fight.

The Prophet ﷺ taught believers to seek Allah’s help, remain patient, and use lawful means. The final victory is not only defeating an external opponent; it is preserving faith and justice without becoming oppressive.

### The Sūrah’s Closing Balance

Sūrah al-Baqarah closes with faith, obedience, forgiveness, capacity, and supplication. It has moved through worship, family law, charity, contracts, ribā, leadership, conflict, and revelation. The final prayer gathers all these subjects. People need Allah’s guidance in public law and private weakness, in wealth and poverty, in victory and fear.

The Prophet ﷺ said that the last two verses of this sūrah suffice the one who recites them at night. Their sufficiency is not a substitute for action; it is a source of protection, remembrance, and faith. The servant recites them, understands them, and lives by their submission and mercy.
