## Sūrah-level record (S1–S6)

### S1 · Names & titles of the sūrah (*asmāʾ al-sūrah*)

**Al-Alūsī.** "*Al-Baqarah* — **this is the well-known name**. And in the *Ṣaḥīḥ*, from Ibn Masʿūd — may
Allah be pleased with him: **'this is the station of the one on whom Sūrat al-Baqarah was revealed'** — and
this **contradicts what has been reported of the prohibition of that** [i.e. of naming sūrahs after things
in them], **and it became necessary to say 'the sūrah in which the cow is mentioned' — and likewise in the
sūrahs of the Qurʾān altogether.** **Hence the majority permitted that without dislike**; **and it may be
reconciled by saying that it was disliked at the beginning of Islam because of the idolaters' mockery, then,
after its light rose, the prohibition was abrogated, so it became widespread without objection — and a
ḥadīth came showing its permissibility.**"

**Its further names as al-Alūsī gives them:**

* **Fusṭāṭ al-Qurʾān** (the encampment of the Qurʾān) — "**Khālid b. Maʿdān used to name it** so."
* **Sanām al-Qurʾān** (the sūrah's hump/apex) — "**and it came in a *marfūʿ* ḥadīth in *Musnad
  al-Firdaws***" — al-Alūsī's gloss: "**the *sanām* of a thing is its highest part**."
* **And the reason for its greatness**: "**because of its greatness and what has been gathered in it of the
  rulings not mentioned in other sūrahs — so much so that one of the shaykhs said: in it are a thousand
  commands, a thousand prohibitions and a thousand reports; it was said: and in it are fifteen parables;
  and for this Ibn ʿUmar — may Allah be pleased with him — spent eight years on learning it.**"

### S2 · Period & place of revelation (*Makkī* / *Madanī*)

**Al-Alūsī.** "**It is Madinan (*madaniyyah*)**, and **in it is the last verse revealed, His saying
*wattaqū yawman turjaʿūna fīhi ilā llāh* (2:281); it came down at the Farewell Pilgrimage, on the Day of
Sacrifice — and it does not leave its being Madinan, as is not hidden.**"

### S3 · Verse, word & letter counts (*ʿadad al-āyāt*)

**Al-Alūsī.** "**And its verses are two hundred and eighty-seven (*miʾatāni wa-sabʿun wa-thamānūn*) on the
well-known count — and it was said: two hundred and eighty-six (*sittun wa-thamānūn*).**" — and in the same
passage: "**and in it are a thousand commands, a thousand prohibitions and a thousand reports; and it was
said: in it are fifteen parables.**"

### S4 · Virtues & merits of the sūrah (*faḍāʾil al-sūrah*)

**Al-Alūsī.**

* **The Prophetic report of its rank**: "**it is reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: 'Which of the
  Qurʾān is best (*afḍal*)?' — they said: 'Allah and His Messenger know best.' — He said: 'Sūrat
  al-Baqarah.' — Then he said: 'And which of it is best?' — they said: 'Allah and His Messenger know best.'
  — He said: 'Āyat al-Kursī.'**" — al-Alūsī introduces it with "it is reported (*ruwiya*)".
* **Sanām al-Qurʾān** — "**and it came in the ḥadīth of *al-Mustadrak* [that] it is named the *sanām* of the
  Qurʾān — and the *sanām* of everything is its highest part**, and it is as though [it is named so] for
  that too."
* **Fusṭāṭ al-Qurʾān** on the tongue of **Khālid b. Maʿdān** (S1).
* **And the reason for its merit**: the rulings gathered in it that are in no other sūrah — with the
  shaykh's count of a thousand commands, a thousand prohibitions and a thousand reports, and fifteen
  parables; and **Ibn ʿUmar's eight years spent learning it** (S1).

### S5 · Central theme & summary of contents (*maqāṣid*)

**Al-Alūsī** (from the *munāsabah* passage, S6 below): the sūrah **comprises knowledge of the Lord first —
as in *yuʾminūna bi-l-ghayb* and its like — then the acts of worship and what pertains to them, then the
request of what is needed in the present and the next world**; and its greatness lies in what it gathers of
the rulings (S1), "**so much so that one of the shaykhs said: in it are a thousand commands, a thousand
prohibitions and a thousand reports.**"

### S6 · Thematic coherence & connection (*munāsabah · naẓm*)

**Al-Alūsī** — "**the face of its *munāsabah* with Sūrat al-Fātiḥah**: **the Fātiḥah comprises the
statement of lordship (*rubūbiyyah*) first, servitude (*ʿubūdiyyah*) second, and the request for guidance in
the religious aims and certainties third — and likewise Sūrat al-Baqarah comprises the statement of
knowledge of the Lord first, as in *yuʾminūna bi-l-ghayb* and its like, and the acts of worship and what
pertains to them second, and the request of what is needed in the present and the next world last. And also:
at the end of the Fātiḥah is the request for guidance, and at the beginning of al-Baqarah is an allusion to
that in His saying *hudan li-l-muttaqīn*.**"

And the second face: "**when He — glorified be He — opened the Fātiḥah with the manifest (*al-ẓāhir*) — and
behind every manifest is a hidden — He opened this sūrah with what is hidden of secret and concealed except
from whom Allah wills.**" — Hence in the Fātiḥah the opening points to His manifest name and in al-Baqarah
to His hidden name; **He is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden; and the precedence of the
First indicates that the manifest is put first, and by it the universality of the mission** — "**we judge by
the manifest, and Allah takes charge of the secrets**"; and in the first is an allusion to the station of
gathering and in the second a symbol of the separation after the gathering; **and the opening of this sūrah
with the hidden and its following with the clear has the most perfect suitableness to the story of the cow
by which the sūrah is named** — *wa-idh qataltum nafsan fa-ddāraʾtum fīhā wa-llāhu mukhrijun mā kuntum
taktumūn* (2:72).

## 2:1 — الم

*[context]* Alif Lām Mīm.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Alūsī.** "*Alif Lām Mīm* — **it and the rest of the letters with which one spells (like *bā tā thā*) are
names (*asmāʾ*), whose named objects (*musammayāt*) are the drawn-out letters (*al-ḥurūf al-mabsūṭah*) from
which the word is composed** — by the due of the definition of the name on which there is agreement, and the
occurrence of its properties on each of them. **And it is related of al-Khalīl that he asked his companions:
'How do you pronounce the *bāʾ* in *ḍaraba* and the *kāf* in *laka*?' They said: '*bāʾ*, *kāf*.' He said:
'**You have given the name, not the letter** — and I say: *bah* – *kah*.'**"

**The dominant view of the letters' status:**

> "**Much speech has been made concerning the *fawātiḥ* of the sūrahs; and that on which the majority
> settled — and it is the *madhhab* of Sībawayh and others of the early ones — is that they are names of
> [the sūrahs], and [the sūrahs] were named with them as a notification that they are words of known
> composition — so that, were they not revelation from Allah, their capacity would not fall short of
> producing their like; and that is as they named a man with *Lām*, the father of Ḥārithah b. Lām
> al-Ṭāʾī, and with *Ṣād* the coppersmith, and with *Qāf* the mountain.**"
> "**And the proof of it is that, were they not [intended] to be understood, the address with them would be
> like addressing with the meaningless and like speaking with the Abyssinian tongue to the Arab; and the
> Qurʾān would not be, in its entirety, exposition and guidance, and the challenge (*taḥaddī*) with it
> would not be possible** — whereas if they are understood — either as meaning the sūrahs whose openings
> they are, as their titles — **or otherwise, and the second is false, because either the intended is what
> they were coined for in the Arabic language, and it is plain that it is not so, or something else, and it
> is false, because the Qurʾān came down in a clear Arabic tongue and is not to be carried to what is not in
> their language.**"

**The five objections and al-Alūsī's answers** (his own exposition, condensed):

1. **Many sūrahs share the same opening** (*Alif Lām Mīm*, *Ḥā Mīm*) while the purpose of a proper name is
   the removal of ambiguity. — **Answer**: this is answered as with shared proper names: the repeated name
   is not one single act of positing; and the fame of one of two proper names does not harm the other's
   being a proper name.
2. **Had they been names they would have been transmitted and famous, whereas the sūrahs are famous by
   other names** (al-Baqarah, Āl ʿImrān). — **Answer**: it is established from the Prophet ﷺ: "**Yāsīn is
   the heart of the Qurʾān**", and "**whoever recites Ḥā Mīm is kept safe until morning**", and in *al-Sunan*
   that "**the Prophet ﷺ prostrated in *Ṣād***"; and if it is established for some, it is established for
   all, since there is no distinction.
3. **The Arabs never named with a combination of more than two names** (like *Baʿlabakk*), and none of them
   was named with three, four or five names together — **so calling them names of the sūrahs departs from
   their language.** — **Answer**: naming with three names, for example, is only impossible if they are
   compounded into one name; but if they are scattered as the names of numbers are scattered, no —
   **and naming with three words did occur**: *shāba qarnāhā*, *surra man raʾā*, *Dārabjard* — and Sībawayh
   himself affirms naming with a sentence and a verse of poetry, and with a set of letter-names.
4. **It entails the unity of name and named.** — **Answer**: it is a naming of the composed with a simple
   [name]; and the simple is other than the composed — as in *Ṣād*, the composed of it and of something
   else — so there is no unity.
5. **These letters are part of the sūrahs, and the part precedes the thing by rank while the name of a
   thing follows it — so it would have to be both prior and posterior, which is impossible.** — **Answer**:
   the posteriority of what is prior in one respect is not impossible; **the part is prior in its essence,
   posterior in its attribute — which is its being a name** — and there is no impossibility.

**Al-Alūsī's own verdict — the concealment is the point:**

> "**And behind these two views there are other views which I fear would weary you by their transmission.
> And what most weighs on the conjecture is that the truth of that is a knowledge veiled and a secret
> screened, which the scholars were unable to grasp** — as **Ibn ʿAbbās** said of its attainment — **and the
> horses of imagination fell short of catching it.** And for that **Abū Bakr — may Allah be pleased with him
> — said: 'Every book has a secret, and the secret of the Qurʾān is the openings of the sūrahs (*awāʾil
> al-suwar*)'; and al-Shaʿbī said: 'It is Allah's secret — so do not seek it.'**"
> "**And the servant's obeying in what he does not know is the most indicative of the perfection of
> submission and the utmost of surrender — as in the stoning of the *jamarāt*, the walking between
> al-Ṣafā and al-Marwah, the *raml* and the *iḍṭibāʿ*** — **so why should it not be allowed that the One who
> is not questioned about what He does, majestic is His affair, command us with what we have not come upon
> the meaning of, the purpose being the appearance of the perfection of the commanded one's submission to
> the Commander and the utmost of surrender to the All-Wise, the All-Powerful?**" — with the verse:
> "**Were He to say, in perplexity: 'Halt at the embers of al-ghaḍā!' — I would halt obediently and not
> hesitate.**" (*law qāla tīhan qif ʿalā jamri l-ghaḍā • lawaqaftu mumtathilan wa-lam atawaqqaf*)
> And he adds: "**in it there is another benefit — that when a man comes upon the meaning and encompasses
> it, its impact falls away from the heart; whereas when he does not come upon the intended from it, with
> certainty that its Speaker is All-Wise, his heart stays always toward it, turning to it, reflecting on
> it** — **and the door of the charged duty is the secret's occupation with the mention of the Beloved and
> reflection upon it and upon His speech**" — with the verse:
> "**Between the lovers there is a secret which neither speech nor pen discloses to mankind.**" (*bayna
> l-muḥibbīna sirrun laysa yufshīhi • qawlun wa-lā qalamun li-l-khalqi yaḥkīhi*) — "**so no one knows it
> after the Messenger of Allah ﷺ but the friends [of Allah], the inheritors.**"

**The ʿārifūn's readings of the letters (recorded by al-Alūsī as theirs, with his own lāṭāʾif):**

* **Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq**: every book has a secret and the Qurʾān's secret is the sūrah-openings (above);
  **al-Shaʿbī**: "Allah's secret, do not seek it" (above).
* **Al-Shaykh al-Akbar** (Ibn ʿArabī) in the *Futūḥāt* — condensed: "**know that the beginnings of the
  sūrahs, whose reality none knows but the people of the intelligible forms**: He made them **twenty-nine
  sūrahs — and that is the perfection of the form** (*wa-l-qamara qaddarnāhu manāzil*, 36:39) — the
  twenty-ninth being the pole by which the sphere stands, and it is **Sūrat Āl ʿImrān**; **and their total,
  with the letters repeated, is seventy-eight letters — and the eighth is the reality of the *biḍʿ*
  [odd number]: 'Faith is seventy-odd branches' — and these letters are seventy-eight**; **so the servant
  does not complete the secrets of faith until he knows the realities of these letters in their sūrahs.**"
  And on the fourteen single letters, the eight for knowing the Essence, the seven attributes, and the four
  composed natures; and the **alif** for the existence of the Essence in its perfection, the **nūn** for the
  world of composition — with the long meditation on the written alif and mīm and the line: "**the alif of
  what we are in is an allusion to *tawḥīd*, the mīm to the unperishing *mulk*, and the lām between them a
  mediator**"; his book *al-Isrā ilā l-maqām al-asrā* on the 3,532 letters; and "**whoever recites it with
  this reality is present with the all for the all with the all**".
* **Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq** — "**in the *alif* there are six of Allah's attributes: the beginning — and Allah is
  the First; the *istiwāʾ* — and Allah is the Just who does not wrong; the individuality — and Allah is the
  Unique; the not-being-joined to a letter — and He is separate from His creation; the letters' need of it
  while it needs not — 'and you are the poor toward Allah, and Allah is the Rich' (*wa-antumu l-fuqarāʾu
  ilā llāh, wa-llāhu l-ghaniyy*, 35:15); and its meaning is *ulfah* (familiarity) — and by Allah is
  familiarity**" — and al-Alūsī adds (his own point): "**the alif points to the *sharīʿah*, the lām to the
  *ṭarīqah*, the mīm to the *ḥaqīqah*; and the alif comes from the farthest of the throat, the lām from the
  edge of the tongue — the middle of the points of articulation — and the mīm from the lip, which is the
  last of them: so it points to the fact that the first of the servant's mention, its middle and its last
  belong to none but Allah; and in it is an allusion to the secret of the triad — the alif to Allah, the lām
  to Jibrīl, the mīm to Muḥammad ﷺ.**"
* **The letter-counting of the conquest of Bayt al-Maqdis** — "**among the pleasures (*ṭarāʾif*) related**:
  '**al-ʿIzz b. ʿAbd al-Salām related that ʿAlī — may Allah be pleased with him — extracted the battle of
  Muʿāwiyah from *ḥ-m-s-ʿ-q*; and Abū l-Ḥakam ʿAbd al-Salām b. Barrājān extracted, in his tafsīr, the
  conquest of Bayt al-Maqdis in the year 583 from His saying *Alif Lām Mīm • ghulibati l-Rūm* (30:1–2)**' —
  **and the Shaykh [Ibn ʿArabī] mentioned how that extraction is done by a different route: taking the
  number of *Alif Lām Mīm* in the small *jummal* (8) and adding it to the eight of *biḍʿ* in the verse,
  making sixteen; then subtracting the one belonging to the alif as root, leaving fifteen; then the great
  *jummal*: eight times seventy-one — the spelled-out value of *biḍʿ* — as years, giving 568, to which the
  fifteen kept in hand are added: 583 — the year of the conquest of Bayt al-Maqdis, **on the reading
  *ghalabat* — with the *ghayn* and *lām* vowelled — and *sa-yughlabūn* — with the *yāʾ* vowelled and the
  *lām* vowelled with fatḥah.**"
* **And the Shīʿī claim al-Alūsī records and rebuts** — "**and among the entertaining things: some of the
  Shīʿah sought in these letters a proof for the caliphate of the Commander ʿAlī — for if one removes the
  repeated letters, there remains what can yield '*ṣirāṭu ʿAliyyin ḥaqqun numiskuhu*' [ʿAlī's path is the
  truth, we hold it]; but you, O Sunnī, may use it for what suits you, for after removing [the repeated]
  there remains what can yield an address to the Shīʿī and a reminder of what came concerning the Companions
  — namely '*ṭaraqa samʿaka l-naṣīḥah*' [the counsel has struck your hearing] — and this is like what they
  mentioned letter by letter; and if you wish you may say '*ṣaḥḥa ṭarīquka maʿa l-sunnah*' [your road is
  sound with the Sunnah], and it is perhaps more fitting and subtle.**" — **and his closing word on the
  openings**: "**in sum, the wonders of these openings do not end, and counting does not encompass them.**"

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (*tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān*)

**Al-Alūsī.** The muqaṭṭaʿāt of this sūrah are set beside their counterparts: *Qāf • wa-l-Qurʾāni
l-majīd* (50:1), *Ṣād • wa-l-Qurʾān* (38:1), *Kāf Hā Yā ʿAyn Ṣād* (19:1), and *Alif Lām Mīm • ghulibati
l-Rūm* (30:1–2) — the last carrying the prophecy al-Alūsī reports (V2). And for the hidden-then-clear
sequence: *Dhālika l-Kitābu lā rayba fīhi hudan li-l-muttaqīn* (2:2) — with his citation: "**as in
*al-Ibānah* of Abū Naṣr — Ibn ʿAbbās said: the last with which Jibrīl reviewed (*ʿāraḍa*) the Messenger of
Allah ﷺ [were] *Alif Lām Mīm • Dhālika l-Kitāb…***" — and the closing of the *munāsabah*: *wa-idh qataltum
nafsan fa-ddāraʾtum fīhā wa-llāhu mukhrijun mā kuntum taktumūn* (2:72), **the story of the hidden made
manifest, by which the sūrah is named** (S6).

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Al-Alūsī.**

> **1.** From **Ibn Masʿūd** — "**I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: 'Whoever recites a letter from the
> Book of Allah has by it a good deed, and the good deed tenfold. I do not say *Alif Lām Mīm* is a letter,
> but *alif* is a letter, *lām* is a letter and *mīm* is a letter.'**"* — and al-Alūsī's analysis of
> the wording: "**what is intended by 'a letter' is not the technical term, for that is a new convention —
> rather the lexical meaning, which is one of the letters of the alphabet; so the meaning of '*alif* is a
> letter' is [the letter] named by the name *alif*, and so on. And it may be that he ﷺ called that [spelled
> letter] 'a letter' after the name of its object, so it is a true meaning. And what was said — that he
> called it 'a letter' figuratively because it is the name of the letter, [by the familiar trope of] one of
> two correlates for the other — is nothing.**"
> And his accounting: "**if the intended is like what is here, then the intended is the object named itself,
> and the number of good deeds then is ninety** [*alif*, *lām*, *mīm* spelled out = nine letters]; **and if
> what is meant by *alam* is the opening of Sūrat al-Fīl (*a-lam tara*), the intended is likewise what it
> names, and the good deeds are thirty.**"
> **2.** "**And it came from him ﷺ: '*Yāsīn is the heart of the Qurʾān*'; and '*whoever recites Ḥā Mīm is
> kept safe until morning*'; and in the *Sunan* that the Prophet ﷺ prostrated in *Ṣād* — and if it is
> established in some, it is established in all, since there is no distinction.**"
> **3.** And on the sūrah's merit from the same section: the Prophetic report "**Which of the Qurʾān is
> best? — Sūrat al-Baqarah; and which of it is best? — Āyat al-Kursī**" (S4).
> **4.** And **al-Bukhārī**'s report on the naming of the sūrahs: from **Ibn Masʿūd**, "**this is the station
> of the one on whom Sūrat al-Baqarah was revealed**" (S1).

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (*aqwāl al-mufassirīn*)

**Al-Alūsī.**

* **Sībawayh and the early majority** — the muqaṭṭaʿāt are **the names of the sūrahs**, as they named a man
  with *Lām*, *Ṣād* and *Qāf* (V2), and the proof is the necessity of understanding for the challenge.
* **Al-Khalīl b. Aḥmad** — the exchange with his companions on the naming of letters, "**you have given the
  name, not the letter**" (V2).
* **Ibn ʿAbbās** — "**the last with which Jibrīl reviewed the Messenger of Allah ﷺ**" (V4), and his inability
  to attain the openings' knowledge (V2).
* **Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq** — "**every book has a secret, and the secret of the Qurʾān is the openings of the
  sūrahs**"; **al-Shaʿbī** — "**Allah's secret, so do not seek it**" (V2).
* **Some of the verifiers** — "**that these are the names of the cut-off letters is nearer to verification,
  because of its clarity and its not involving trope, and its soundness against what is objected to
  others** — and **more fitting to the subtleties of the revelation**, for its indication of the inimitability
  by intent" — and that **Sībawayh's words are not a text on the matter**, since he may have meant only that
  they run in the manner of names (like "I recited: *Bānat Suʿād*"), becoming as it were dominant proper
  names — a position al-Alūsī reports from **al-Sayyid al-Sanad**, and notes the objection against it from
  the third objection above (the Arabs' non-naming with three names), which he says cannot be escaped.
* **Al-Shaykh al-Akbar** (Ibn ʿArabī), **Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq**, **Ibn Barrājān** and **al-ʿIzz b. ʿAbd al-Salām**
  — the letter-readings and the letter-counting related above (V2).
* **And on the received view of the *fawātiḥ*'s place in the sūrah's own flow** — the *munāsabah* material
  (S6): the hidden opening then the clear, suiting **the story of the cow**: *wa-idh qataltum nafsan* (2:72).

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (*lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah*)

**Al-Alūsī.**

* **The name and its object** — "*Alif Lām Mīm* and the like are **names whose named objects are the
  drawn-out letters** (Sībawayh's and al-Khalīl's discussion, V2); **and they observed in this naming a
  subtlety, in that they made the *musammā* the first of each name** — as **Ibn Jinnī** said — **so that its
  utterance would strike the hearing first**: you say *jīm*, and the first of its letters is *jīm*; you say
  *alif*, and the first of its letters is *alif*, which you uttered as *hamzah*; and since the positor could
  not begin with the alif that is a silent prolongation, it is propped by the lām before it, vowelled, to
  allow beginning with it — hence they said *lā* — not *alif lām* as the teachers say, for that is an
  error; and the lām was singled out for the prop because its sister is in the definite article, as if a
  kind of exchange were intended. **And the alif is the first letter of the alphabet — the form of the
  hamzah in truth** — and this is like the deposit of meaning in the words *basmalah*, *ḥamālah*,
  *ḥawqalah*; and the grammarians call it *naḥt*."
* **The iʿrāb of the openings** — "**and people differed on their inflection according to their differences
  on what they are: if they are made names of the sūrahs, they have a share of iʿrāb — *rafʿ*, *naṣb* and
  *jarr***": **rafʿ** as the predicate of a deleted subject or a subject whose predicate is deleted;
  **naṣb** by supposing the verb of an oath or a verb suiting the place; **jarr** by the implied particle of
  oath — and his refutation of **Ibn Hishām**'s calling the latter a delusion ("that is specific, among the
  Baṣrans, to the name of Allah"; and there is no answer to the oath in al-Baqarah and its like; and the
  deletion of the *lām* as in the verse of poetry *wa-rabbi l-samāwāti l-ʿulā wa-burūjihā…*): "**his calling
  it a delusion is not hidden from al-Walīd** — *wa-madhhabunā kūfiyyun* — **our madhhab is Kūfan, and
  following the Baṣran is not an obligation**" — and the much-repeated sufficing with what the context
  indicates, the content of what follows being the *muqsam ʿalayh* — as declared in *al-Tashīl* and its
  commentaries; and that the report of "elongation" of the oath is not binding but is the preponderant norm,
  as Ibn Mālik declared.
* **The single vs the weighed** — "**then what is single of these openings, like *Ṣād*, or balanced
  [against a word] like *Ḥā Mīm* in the weight of *Qābīl*, inflection comes to it in wording or in place —
  by its being made vowelless as a quotation of its prior state and its iʿrāb supposed, and it is
  uninflected (*ghayr munṣarif*) for the proper-name-ness and femininity; and what differs from these two,
  like *Kāf Hā Yā ʿAyn Ṣād*, is quoted only.**" And the permission of *ḥikāyah* (quotation) in these names
  though quotation is specific to proper names transferred from sentences — for the sake of their forms —
  with the discussion of *Mīm*, *Ay*, and the saying "leave us from *tamratāni*" (two dates), and **if they
  remain on their meanings, strung out in the manner of enumeration, they are not inflected for lack of
  cause and operator** — and likewise if they are made parts [of words] on the correct view, or added for
  separation.
* **Whether the openings are themselves a verse** — "**and there is disagreement on whether these openings
  are a verse: the Kūfans said *Alif Lām Mīm* is a verse wherever it occurs, and likewise *Alif Lām Mīm
  Ṣād* and *Ṭā Sīn Mīm* and their sisters, and *Ṭā Hā* and *Yā Sīn* and *Ḥā Mīm* and their sisters, and *Kāf
  Hā Yā ʿAyn Ṣād* is a verse, and *Ḥā Mīm • ʿAyn Sīn Qāf* are two verses; but *Alif Lām Mīm Rā* and its
  five sisters are not a verse, and likewise *Ṭā Sīn*, *Ṣād*, *Qāf* and *Nūn*; and the Baṣrans said: none
  of that is a verse.**" — "**and in *al-Murshid*: the openings in all the sūrahs are verses according to
  the Kūfans without distinction — and it is nothing** — like the saying of some that *Alif Lām Mīm* in Āl
  ʿImrān is not a verse."
* **And the rhetorical purpose** (his own list, V2): "**if you wish, say: they came thus to be like an
  awakening and a striking of the staff for those who challenged the Qurʾān; or: so that the opening of what
  is recited to them be independent, bearing a kind of strangeness, a model of what is in the rest of the
  kinds of inimitability … or: to draw the minds' attention and to bridle every one of the unbelievers who
  babbled at the Qurʾān's coming down — for when they heard what they did not understand of this strange
  pattern they left off their clamour and their motives were multiplied to look into the matter**" — and the
  rest of his list (a mercy to the believers; the letters' indicating a name of Allah; the ordering to
  submission).

### V9 · Variant readings (*qirāʾāt*)

**Al-Alūsī.** The one reported reading-item that the verse's exposition carries is the reading on which the
letter-counting of the conquest (V2) turns — *ghalabat* (with the *ghayn* and *lām* vowelled) and
*sa-yughlabūn* (with the *yāʾ* vowelled and the *lām* vowelled with fatḥah) — **i.e. the familiar ʿUthmānic
reading of 30:2–4**, which the computation treats as the given; the computation's other materials are the
small and great *jummal* of *Alif Lām Mīm* (V2). And in the same passage the *alif* is stated to be
**"the form of the *hamzah* in truth"** (V8), the point on which the letter-naming rests.

### V11 · Creed & theology (*ʿaqīdah*)

**Al-Alūsī** (the doctrine he draws from the verse and its concealment):

> "**What most weighs on the conjecture is that the truth of that is a knowledge veiled and a secret
> screened** … **and the servant's obeying in what he does not know is the most indicative of the perfection
> of submission**" — and: "**why should it not be allowed that the One who is not questioned about what He
> does command us with what we have not come upon the meaning of, the purpose being the appearance of the
> perfection of the commanded one's submission to the Commander, and the utmost of surrender to the
> All-Wise, the All-Powerful?**" — with the analogy of the rites whose rationale we do not know (the
> *jamarāt*, al-Ṣafā and al-Marwah, the *raml* and *iḍṭibāʿ*).
> And the creed's balance: "**He is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden**" (S6) — and by the
> precedence of the First: "**that the manifest is put first, and by it the universality of the mission:
> *we judge by the manifest, and Allah takes charge of the secrets***"; and by the hidden opening: "**the
> station of gathering**, and in the clear [thereafter] **the separation after the gathering**" (S6).
> And on the secret's custodians: "**so no one knows it after the Messenger of Allah ﷺ but the friends [of
> Allah], the inheritors** — **they know it from that presence**" (V2) — with his reservation that the
> openings' wonders **"do not end, and counting does not encompass them."**

## 2:2 — ذَٰلِكَ الْكِتَابُ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ هُدًى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ

*[context]* That Book — no doubt in it — is guidance for the God-fearing.

### V2 · Running exposition (bayān)

**Al-Alūsī.** "*Dhālika l-kitābu lā rayba fīhi hudan li-l-muttaqīn* — **a resumed sentence (*jumla
mustaʾnafa*), the beginning of a speech, or connected to what precedes it; and in it are possibilities over
which they have been lengthy. And the Book of Allah is carried on the best of constructions (*aḥsan
al-maḥāmil*), the farthest of them from contrivance and the smoothest in the Arabs' tongue.**"

**What *dhālika* points to** — al-Alūsī's survey:

* "***Dhālika* is an indication (*ishārah*) to the Book promised in His saying *innā sa-nulqī ʿalayka
  qawlan thaqīlā* (73:5)** — as **al-Wāḥidī** said";
* "**or to what was on the tongue of Mūsā and ʿĪsā — peace be upon them — because of His saying *wa-kānū min
  qablu yastaftiḥūn ʿalā lladhīna kafarū* (2:89)**, **and it is supported by what is related from Kaʿb:
  *'Cling to the Qurʾān, for it is the understanding of the mind and the light of wisdom, the springs of
  knowledge, and the most recent of the books in covenant with Allah'***; and **he said in the Torah: *'O
  Muḥammad, I am sending down to you a new Torah, by which you will open blind eyes, deaf ears and sealed
  hearts'*** — as more than one has said";
* "**or to what is between our hands** *[i.e. present]* — **and the indication by *dhālika* is for
  magnification, with the distance of rank made as the distance of reality, as in His saying *fa-dhālikunna
  lladhī lumtunnanī fīhi* (12:32)** — as chosen in *al-Miftāḥ*; **or because, when it came down from the
  presence of lordship and came to be in our presence, it was distant** — and one who gives another a thing,
  or conveys it to him, or considers its conveyance, may express it so, since by its separation from him it
  is distant, or in its ruling; **and it has been said: everything that is not in your hands is distant.**"

And on **al-Suhaylī's comparison** with the sister verse *wa-hādhā kitābun anzalnāhu* (6:92): "**since that
[the *hādhā* verse] is an indication to what is with Him — glorified be He — the *dhālika* of distance was
not brought with it, though the farness of degree [applies]; and this mention — the spelling letters here
and their absence there — is a difference of the two stations and the parting of the two indications, as
al-Suhaylī said** — and with a group it is a verification; **and he guides you to what in it, in my view, is
a subtle objection.**" **And the strangest he saw**: "**the most unusual we have seen in the directing of the
indication is that it points to the *ṣirāṭ mustaqīm* of the Fātiḥah — as though, when they asked for
guidance to it, it was said to them: *that path you asked to be guided to is the Book*; and if you accept
this, the face of al-Baqarah's connection with Sūrat al-Ḥamd is shown in the most perfect way, and the
indication is to what was mentioned before.**" — **And his own view**: "**that to which the ears open is that
it is an indication to the Qurʾān, with the face of farness that the author of *al-Miftāḥ* mentioned, while
the light of nearness shines upon it.**" And on the demonstrative's nature: "**the *ism al-ishārah* is
considered with the sensory indication, whose attachment is only conceivable to something sensed and seen;
so if it indicates what cannot be sensed — like *dhālikumu llāhu rabbukum* (22:5) — or a sensed thing not
seen — like *tilka l-jannah* — it is to make it as though seen; the intellectual indication being placed in
the station of the sensory, as in al-Raḍī** — so the indication here is not free of subtlety." — and
**Ibn ʿUṣfūr**'s rule: "**every *lām* that occurs after a demonstrative, and after *ayy* in the vocative,
and after the sudden *idhā*, is for present covenant (*al-ʿahd al-ḥuḍūrī*)**" — and there is a reading
***tanzīlu l-kitāb***.

**_Al-kitāb_ lexically** — "***al-kitāb* is like *al-katb*, the *maṣdar* of *kataba*, and it is applied to
the written thing, like *al-libās* in the sense of *al-malbūs*; and *al-katb*, as al-Rāghib said, is the
joining of one hide to another by stitching, and conventionally the joining of letters one to another; the
origin in writing is the ordering by the line, and it is said of what is joined by speech; hence each is
borrowed for the other — and for that Allah's Book is called *kitāb* even if it is not a written thing.**"
**The Book here** — "**either remains on the *maṣdariyyah* and the object is named by it for hyperbole; or it
is in the sense of the object, and it is applied to the ordered expression before its letters are set down,
naming by what it will come to, with the suitability.**" — **and his rebuke of al-Rāzī's etymology**: "**the
Imām's saying that *kitāb* is derived from *katabtu l-shayʾ* when you gathered it, and the *katībah* (army)
is named for its gathering, so the *kitāb* is so named because it is like the *katībah* against the armies
of doubts, or because all the sciences are gathered in it, or because Allah bound the obligations on
creation in it — is patchwork speech whose flaw is not hidden.**" **And the *lām* of *al-kitāb*** — "**is for
the reality (*li-l-ḥaqīqah*), like the *lām* in 'you are the man'; and the meaning is: that is the perfect
Book, worthy that the name of the Book be singled out for it — for the utmost of its excelling the rest of
the individuals in possessing the perfections of the genus, as though the other heavenly books were outside
it in relation to it.**"

**_Al-rayb_** — "**is doubt (*al-shakk*); its origin is the *maṣdar rābanī l-shayʾ* — when agitation
(*al-rībah*) occurs in you, which is the soul's disquiet; and from it is *rayb al-zamān* for its
calamities** — it is among what is transferred from disquiet to what resembles it; **it is used also for
what stirs in the heart of causes of wrath.**" — And: "**the Imām al-Rāzī said these two may return to the
meaning of doubt, since what is feared of events is being possible, so it is as though doubted**" — and
**Abū Zayd's distinction**: "**it is said *rābanī min fulānin amrun* — when you are certain of him with
doubt; and when you think ill of him without being certain, you say *arābanī* — and on it is Bashshār's
saying: *your brother is he who, if you cause him doubt, says merely 'perhaps' — and if you reproach him,
his side softens***". And: "**some differentiate between *al-rayb* and *al-shakk*: that *al-rayb* is doubt
with accusation (*tuhmah*) — and al-Rāghib said: *al-shakk* is the soul's standing between two opposites
such that neither outweighs the other by a sign; *al-miryah* is wavering between opposites and seeking a
sign (from *marā l-ḍarʿ*, i.e. wiped it for milking); *al-rayb* is to suppose something of a thing and then
to be disclosed from what was supposed**"; and **al-Jawlī**: "**shakk is said of what the two beliefs are
equal in, or are not equal but neither has reached the degree of manifestation on which matters are built;
rayb is of what has not reached the degree of certainty, even if some manifestation shows — and for that
*lā rayba fīhi* is fine here: to indicate that no doubt occurs in it, let alone hesitation.**"

**The negation despite the many doubters** — "**He negated doubt of it while the doubters are many — may
Allah not multiply them — on the meaning that it is of such high rank and shining proof that the rational
one does not doubt, after reflection, that it is revelation from Allah — not that no one doubts of it so
that it be invalid and need the existence of doubt in some to be placed in the station of non-existence
because of what removes it.**" — **and it is said**: "**it is on ellipsis — as though He said: no cause of
doubt is in it, because the causes that bring doubt in speech are confusion, obscurity, contradiction and
claims bare of proof, and all of that is absent from Allah's Book**"; **and it is said**: "**its meaning is
the prohibition, though its wording is a report: 'do not doubt it', on the pattern of *fa-lā rafatha wa-lā
fusūqa* (2:197)**"; **and it is said**: "**its meaning is: no doubt in it for the God-fearing — the
adverbial being then a description and *li-l-muttaqīn* the predicate; and *hudan* a ḥāl from the pronoun:
no doubt occurring in it for the God-fearing, while it is guiding**" — a **permanent (*lāzimah*) ḥāl**, "**so
it yields the absence of doubt in all times and states, and the restriction is as the proof of the absence
of doubt**"; and "*lā* is for negating the name's being described by the predicate, not for negating the
name's restriction, so it is not directed at it so that the meaning be disturbed**" — though al-Alūsī notes
it is "**a view of little benefit, since the preponderant for the adverbial after this *lā* is to be the
predicate.**"

**Why not *lā fīhi rayb*?** — "**He did not say *lā fīhi rayb*, on the pattern of *lā fīhā ghawl* (37:47),
because the fronting would suggest what is far from the intended — namely that another book has doubt in it
— as was intended in that verse's preferring the wine of the Garden over the wines of this world: that it
does not steal the minds as they do, so there is not in it what is in others of defect** — as
**al-Zamakhsharī** said **— and some did not differentiate between 'there is not in the house a man' and
'there is no man in the house', to the point that Abū Ḥayyān denied the fronting of the predicate here
yielding restriction — and it is of what is not attended to.**"

**The waqf and the readings** — "**the waqf on *fīhi* is the well-known one, and on it the Book itself is
guidance, and that has recurred in the revelation; and from Nāfiʿ and ʿĀṣim the waqf on *lā rayba*, and
there is no harm in the deletion of the predicate; and al-Zajjāj held that *lā rayba* is in the sense of
'truly', so the waqf on it is complete — except that it too is beneath the first.**"

**_Hudan_** — "***al-hudā* in origin is the *maṣdar* of *hadā*, or a substitute for the *maṣdar* — and both
are in Sībawayh's speech; and from the *maṣādir* only a little has come in this form (*fuʿalā*), like
*al-tuqā*, *al-surā* and *al-bukā* with shortening in a dialect, and *luqan*, as al-Shāṭibī said and
recited: *wa-qad zaʿamū ḥilman luqāka fa-lam azid • bi-ḥamdi lladhī aʿṭāka ḥilman wa-lā ʿaqlā***; **and what
is intended here is the active participle by one of the known faces in its like; it is a feminine word with
Ibn ʿAṭiyyah, masculine with al-Liḥyānī, and the Banū Asad feminize it, as al-Farrāʾ said** — so it is like
*al-hidāyah*."

**The meaning of guidance (al-Kashshāf and its rebuttal)** — "**in *al-Kashshāf* it is the guidance that
conveys to the goal (*al-dalālah al-mūṣilah*), and he inferred it by three faces: (1) that error (*al-ḍalāl*)
occurs opposite to it, as in *la-ʿalā hudan aw fī ḍalālin* (34:50? 41:44), and error is the failure to
reach the goal — so if arrival were not considered in the concept of guidance they would not be opposite,
since their conjunction would be possible; (2) that *mahdī* is said in the place of praise as *muhtadī*,
and one who has the guidance without attaining it is not called so — so arrival is considered in its
concept; (3) that *ihtadā* is the *muṭāwiʿ* of *hadā*, and the *muṭāwiʿ* is never contrary to its
origin's meaning — do you not see *kasarahū fa-nkasara*?**" — **and al-Alūsī's rebuttal in five faces**:
"(1) *al-hudā* set against *al-ḍalālah* is the intransitive *hudā* in the sense of *ihtidā*, by trope or by
sharing — and our speech is of the transitive, whose opposite is *iḍlāl*; (2) we do not concede that
*al-ḍalālah* is the failure to reach the goal — **rather it is the turning away from the road that leads to
the goal**, so *al-hudā* is the guidance to the road that leads; (3) it does not follow from *mahdī*'s
being applied only to the one who attains that attainment is considered in the concept — the derived word
may dominate in one individual; (4) we do not concede that *ihtadā* is the *muṭāwiʿ* of *hadā* — **rather
it is of the kind of *amarahū fa-tamara*, from a verb's following that differs from the first**; (5) what he
mentioned is contradicted by the many places in which guidance occurs without arrival to the goal" — and the
evidence: "**it is said *hadāhu fa-lam yahtadī***" — and: "**a group whose consideration is counted said:
the existence of a verb does not necessarily entail the existence of its *muṭāwiʿ* at all — in *al-Mukhtār*:
it is not necessary that the *muṭāwiʿ* agree with its origin, while it is necessary in other than it — and
it is supported by His saying *wa-mā nursilu bi-l-āyāti illā takhwīfan* (17:59) with *wa-nukhawwifuhum fa-mā
yazīduhum illā ṭughyānan* (17:41? 17:60?): the warning existed without the fear.**"

**Whose doctrine it is** — "**for this they differed: is it literal (*ḥaqīqah*) in absolute guidance and
tropical in other than it, or the reverse, or is it shared between the two, or coined for a common amount?
— and to each a group went. It is said: what is mentioned in the Ashʿarīs' speech is that the chosen with
them is what *al-Kashshāf* mentions, and with the Muʿtazilah what we have mentioned, and the famous is the
reverse — and the reconciliation: that the Ashʿarīs' speech is of the legal meaning and the famous is built
on the lexical or customary meaning**; **but the author of *al-Kashshāf*'s choosing — with his rigidity in
secession — what he chose scratches [the reconciliation], though the apparent in the Qurʾān is the legal
meaning; so the more apparent for the reconciler is the reverse of this reconciliation. And the truth with
the people of truth is that *al-hidāyah* is shared between the two meanings mentioned and the non-destruction
(*ʿadam al-ihlāk*) — and by it many a 'it is said' is repelled.**"

**_Al-muttaqīn_** — "**the plural of *muttaqī*, the active participle from *waqāhu* → *ittaqā*, so its *fāʾ*
is *wāw* not *tāʾ*; *al-wiqāyah* lexically is preservation absolutely, and in the law the preservation of
the person from what harms in the Hereafter; and the ranks are many for the manyness of the ranks of harm:
the first is preservation from *shirk*; the second from the major sins — and persistence in the small ones
is among them; the third is what the **Tirmidhī** reported from him ﷺ: ***'the servant does not attain
being of the God-fearing until he abandons what is no harm, out of caution for what is harmful'*** — and in
this rank the abandoning of the small ones is considered; **and hence it is said: *'Leave the sins, the
great of them and the small — for that is piety; and act as one walking over the ground of thorns, wary of
what he sees; do not despise a small thing — for the mountains are of pebbles'***." And: "**in this rank the
expressions of the great differ**: it is said **'that Allah not see you where He forbade you, and not miss
you where He commanded you'**; and **'the disavowal of might and power'** (*al-tabarri ʿan al-ḥawl
wa-l-quwwah*); and **'the being free of everything that occupies the secret from the Truth'** — **and in
this field the spirits of the lovers ran, and the forms of the wayfarers perished, until their speaker
said: *Had there come to me, in other than You, a will — upon my thought in inadvertence — I would judge
my own apostasy***."

**The ḥaqīqah question, and the Book's guidance** — "**and the guidance of the manifest Book comprises the
people of all these ranks together. If what is intended by its being guidance for the God-fearing is its
directing them to obtaining the first rank, then what is meant by them is those on the verge of it
(*al-mushārifūn*), by trope — because obtaining what is already obtained is impossible — and preferring it
over the expression that would state that is for brevity; and the sūrah's opening with the mention of His
friends, the magnification of their rank, and the consideration of nearness, is by the time of the
guidance's being related, so it does not contradict the beauty of following with *alladhīna yuʾminūn*
(2:3), because that is — as is said — by the time of that relation's being established.**" And: "**and this
is realized by Mawlānā, Muftī of the lands of Rūm — and from it is known the repelling of what is said:
that if *al-hidāyah* is interpreted as the guidance that conveys, it makes *hudan li-l-muttaqīn* indicate
the obtaining of what is obtained — as though it were said: a guidance conveying to the goal for those who
have reached it**" — and: "**some verifiers verified that the more apparent is that there is no need for
trope here: when it is said 'the weapon is protection for the one protected' and 'wealth is wealth for the
rich', meaning the cause of his protection and his wealth, it does not follow that weapon and wealth are
causes of protection and wealth newly arising other than what they are [now]; so what we are in is in no
need of interpretation, and is not of trope in anything — the God-fearing one is guided by this guidance in
truth**" — with the discussion of the derived adjective (is it literal for the present or the future? the
time of the relation or of the speaking? al-Subkī and al-Kirmānī on *'whoever kills a killed one, his
spoils are his'* being literal), concluding: "**hence the meaning in what we are in was made: there is no
guidance for the God-fearing except by the Book of Allah, whose guidance-light glitters and whose proof of
indication shines.**"

**And its containing the obscure does not harm** — "**then it does not impugn its being guidance that it
contains the concise (*mujmal*) and the ambiguous (*mutashābih*), because its being guidance does not entail
its guiding by every part of it — so it may mention what is a trial for the possessors of minds, or what is
not free of clarification of the intended, as the Shāfiʿīs held, in which case after clarification it is
guidance; and its guidance's depending on a thing does not harm it — as on a view it depends on the prior
faith in Allah and His Messenger. The Imām [al-Rāzī] stated that whatever the Qurʾān's being a proof depends
on for its soundness, the Qurʾān is not guidance in it — like knowing the Essence, the Attributes and the
prophethoods — lest circularity follow (unless it be guidance in confirming what is in the intellects and
relying on it); and some verified that the Qurʾān in itself is guidance in everything, even the knowledge
of Allah for one who reflects on its rational proofs and certain arguments, as the apparent of *shahru
ramaḍāna lladhī unzila fīhi l-qurʾānu hudan li-l-nās* (2:185) suggests.**" — "**and the restriction to the
God-fearing here is, on our interpretation of guidance, praise of them, so that He show that they are the
ones who took guidance and profited by it — as in *innamā anta mundhiru man yakhshāhā* (79:45) with the
universality of his ﷺ warning; and as for others, no: *wa-idhā qaraʾta l-qurʾāna jaʿalnā baynaka
wa-bayna lladhīna lā yuʾminūna bi-l-ākhirati ḥijāban mastūran* (17:45) • *wa-lā yazīdu l-ẓālimīna illā
khasāran* (17:82). And the saying that the construction is: guidance for the God-fearing *and the
unbelievers*, with the mention of the God-fearing doing duty for it — on the pattern of *sarābīlu taqīkumu
l-ḥarr* (16:81) — **is of what is not attended to.**"

**The verses' mutual coherence** — "**and the coherence of these sentences is not hidden: *Alif Lām Mīm*
pointed to what it pointed to; *dhālika l-kitāb* established some of its indication — that it is the perfect
Book that none other deserves to be named a book in its genus, i.e. in the chapter of challenge and
guidance; *lā rayba fīhi* is as the confirmation of one of the two pillars; *hudan li-l-muttaqīn* as the
confirmation of the other pillar** — **and its summary: it is the one worthy that the challenge be made by
it, for the perfection of its composition in the chapter of eloquence, and its perfection in itself and in
what is intended from it.**" — "**and it is said: [the construction is] the resumed speech — as though it
were asked: what is it that made it inimitable? — and it was answered: it is perfect, having reached the
utmost perfection in wording and meaning, which is the sense of *dhālika l-kitāb*; then the cause of its
being singled out as the perfect Book was asked, and it was answered that no doubt hovers around it; then
when proof of that was demanded, He inferred it by its being *hudan li-l-muttaqīn* — for the appearance of
its comprising the religious and worldly benefits and the interests of livelihood and the Hereafter, such
that none denies it but one who vies with himself and opposes his mind and his sense. And it may be said:
inimitability entails the utmost perfection; the utmost perfection of eloquent speech is by its freedom from
doubt and obscurities, because of the appearance of its truth; and that entails its guiding and directing —
so if the identity of the meanings in outcome is considered, the second confirms the first, and hence the
conjunction was left; if the first's entailing what follows it is considered, and its being in its power
makes it as though put in the station of the *badal al-ishtimāl* for the suitability and inseparability
between them, its analogy is the analogy of *aʿjabatnī l-jāriyatu ḥusnuhā* (`it pleased me — the slave-girl
— her beauty`) — and the conjunction was left for the strength of the connection between these sentences.
And in them also of the brilliant subtleties and surpassing excellences what is not hidden for one who has
passed what we mentioned over his thought.**"

### V4 · Qurʾān cross-references (*tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān*)

**Al-Alūsī.** Within the record above: 73:5 (*innā sa-nulqī ʿalayka qawlan thaqīlā* — the promised Book);
2:89 (*wa-kānū min qablu yastaftiḥūn* — the Book on Mūsā's and ʿĪsā's tongues); 6:92 (*wa-hādhā kitābun
anzalnāhu*) — the sister indication whose difference from *dhālika* al-Suhaylī explains; 12:32 (*fa-dhālikunna
lladhī lumtunnanī fīhi*) — the far of rank as the far of reality; 22:5 (*dhālikumu llāhu rabbukum*) and
*tilka l-jannah* — the indication to the unsensed and the unseen-sensed; 37:47 (*lā fīhā ghawl*) — the
fronting question (al-Zamakhsharī); 2:197 (*fa-lā rafatha wa-lā fusūqa*) — the report with the sense of
prohibition; 34:50 / 41:44 (*la-ʿalā hudan aw fī ḍalālin*) — the *hudā*/*ḍalāl* opposition; 17:59 and 17:41?

mention.

### V5 · Prophetic traditions (ḥadīth)

**Al-Alūsī.** **Al-Tirmidhī**'s report from the Prophet ﷺ, cited for the *taqwā*'s higher rank:
"***The servant does not attain being of the God-fearing until he abandons what is no harm, out of caution
for what is harmful***." And in the same section: the Prophet ﷺ's warning's universality against the
restriction of guidance (*innamā anta mundhiru man yakhshāhā*, 79:45, cited as the pattern) — and the
Ḥadīth that the Qurʾān's guidance profits only its people (with 17:45, 17:82).

### V7 · Exegesis of the classical mufassirūn (*aqwāl al-mufassirūn*)

**Al-Alūsī** — named in the record as he cites them: **al-Wāḥidī** (the promised Book), **Kaʿb** (the
*mufradāt* of the Qurʾān, and the Torah's address to Muḥammad ﷺ), **al-Suhaylī** (the *dhālika* / *hādhā*
difference), the author of ***al-Miftāḥ*** (far of rank as far of reality), **ʿIkrimah** (the indication to
the Torah and the Gospel — "**and if a sound ḥadīth has come in it we accept it and take trouble for it;
otherwise we strike it against the wall — and not every possibility is fitting**"), **Ibn ʿUṣfūr** (the
*lām* after a demonstrative), **al-Rāghib** (*katb*, *shakk*/*rayb*), **al-Rāzī** (his *kitāb* etymology
rebuked; the *rayb*/*shakk* return; the circularity in guidance), **Abū Zayd** (*rābanī*/*arābanī*),
**Bashshār b. Burd** (the verse), **al-Jawlī** (*shakk*/*rayb*), **al-Zamakhsharī** (guidance that conveys;
the fronting), **Abū Ḥayyān** (denying the restriction — "not attended to"), **al-Zajjāj** (*lā rayba* =
truly), **Ibn ʿAṭiyyah**, **al-Liḥyānī**, **al-Farrāʾ** (the feminizing of *hudā*), **al-Shāṭibī** (the
*luqan* verse), **al-Subkī** and **al-Kirmānī** (the derived adjective), **Mawlānā the Muftī of Rūm**
(the defence against the "obtaining what is obtained" objection) — and the view he calls the strangest: that
*dhālika* points to the Fātiḥah's *ṣirāṭ mustaqīm*, "**and if you accept it, the connection of al-Baqarah
with Sūrat al-Ḥamd is shown in the most perfect way.**"

### V8 · Language, lexicon & grammar (*lughah · iʿrāb · balāghah*)

**Al-Alūsī.** Everything marshalled in the record above: the sentence's parsing (*jumla mustaʾnafa* /
connected); the *ism al-ishārah*'s sensory basis and its use for the unsensed (22:5) and the unseen-sensed
(*tilka l-jannah*); **Ibn ʿUṣfūr**'s *lām* rule; the reading *tanzīlu l-kitāb*; *al-kitāb*'s *maṣdar*/*ism
al-mafʿūl* question; the *lām* of reality (as in *anta l-rajul*); *al-rayb*'s lexicon (*al-rībah* = the
soul's disquiet; *rayb al-zamān*; the *rābanī*/*arābanī* distinction with **Abū Zayd** and **Bashshār**'s
verse; **al-Rāghib**'s *shakk* / *miryah* / *rayb* definitions; **al-Jawlī**'s distinction); the *ḥāl*
construction and the permanent *ḥāl*; the *ḥaṣr* and the fronting question with **al-Zamakhsharī** and
**Abū Ḥayyān**; the *waqf* places (with **Nāfiʿ**, **ʿĀṣim**, **al-Zajjāj**); **Ibn Kathīr**'s *
*fīhī* pronunciation rule for the pronominal *hāʾ* after a *yāʾ*, and **al-Zuhrī**'s and **Ibn Jundub**'s
ḍamm of the pronouns; *hudā*'s form (*fuʿalā*, with *al-tuqā*, *al-surā*, *al-bukā*, *luqā*, **al-Shāṭibī**'s
verse) and its gender (**Ibn ʿAṭiyyah**, **al-Liḥyānī**, **al-Farrāʾ**); *al-hidāyah*'s meaning dispute
(**al-Kashshāf**'s three proofs and al-Alūsī's five-fold rebuttal); *al-muttaqī*'s morphology (*waqā* →
*ittaqā*, the *fāʾ* a *wāw*); and the *badal al-ishtimāl* analogy (*aʿjabatnī l-jāriyatu ḥusnuhā*) for the
conjunction-less sequence.

### V9 · Variant readings (*qirāʾāt*)

**Al-Alūsī.** **Salīm, Abū l-Shaʿthāʾ**, read ***lā raybun fīhi*** with *rafʿ* — "**and it is because it is
the contrary of *raybun fīhi*; and it admits of being the affirmation of an individual, whose negation
yields its absence but does not yield universality [as does] the well-known reading — and for this 'there is
no man in the house, but two men' is allowed, unlike 'no man in it, but two men'**" — and the *waqf*: the
well-known on *fīhi* (**Nāfiʿ**'s and **ʿĀṣim**'s on *lā rayba*, with **al-Zajjāj**'s view making it
complete but inferior); the reading ***tanzīlu l-kitāb***; **Ibn Kathīr**'s ***fīhī*** (joining the
pronominal *hāʾ* as a *yāʾ* in pronunciation, and likewise every pronominal *hāʾ* preceded by a silent
*yāʾ* — joined with *wāw* if preceded by another silent; **Ḥafṣ** agreeing with him in *fīhi muhānan*,
*mu-lāqīhi*, *sa-uṣlīhi*, others not saturating; and when what precedes the *hāʾ* is vowelled they saturate
it); and **al-Zuhrī**'s and **Ibn Jundub**'s reading with *ḍamm* of the pronominal *hāʾ*s throughout the
Qurʾān "**on the original**".

### V11 · Creed & theology (*ʿaqīdah*)

**Al-Alūsī.** The doctrinal load of the verse as he presents it: the **Ashʿarī–Muʿtazilī** dispute over
whether *al-hidāyah* is by nature the conveying guidance or the indicating guidance — "**and the truth with
the people of truth is that *al-hidāyah* is shared between the two meanings mentioned and the non-destruction
(*ʿadam al-ihlāk*) — and by it many a 'it is said' is repelled**"; the Qurʾān as guidance for the God-fearing
with the general *hudan li-l-nās* (2:185); the **al-Rāzī** circularity discussion (what the Qurʾān's being a
proof depends on cannot be taken from the Qurʾān — unless it be in confirming the intellect's data, which
some rejected, holding the Qurʾān self-sufficiently guidance in everything); and the rank of *taqwā* as the
locus of benefit — "**as in *innamā anta mundhiru man yakhshāhā* with the universality of his warning**" —
and the fate of the rest: *wa-lā yazīdu l-ẓālimīna illā khasāran* (17:82).
