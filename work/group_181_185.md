## Verse 181

Verse 181 says: *Indeed, Allah has heard those ˹among the Jews˺ who said, “Allah is poor; we are rich!” We have certainly recorded their slurs and their killing of prophets unjustly. Then We will say, “Taste the torment of burning!*

In simple terms, the verse threatens those among the Jews who said, "Allah is poor; we are rich": Allah has heard them, recorded their words and their unjust killing of prophets, and will say to them, "Taste the torment of burning."

The verse is the record of the audacity and the threat of the burning. Ibn Kathir: "We shall record what they have said and their killing of the Prophets unjustly, and We shall say: 'Taste you the torment of the burning (Fire).'" The verse's *laqad sami'a Allahu qawla alladhina qalu inna Allaha faqirun wa nahnu aghniya'* — Allah has certainly heard the saying of those who said: Allah is poor and we are rich — is the hearing; the *sanaktubu ma qalu wa qatlahumu al-anbiya'a bighayri haqq* — We shall record what they said and their killing of the prophets without right — is the record; and the *wanaqulu dhuqu 'adhaba al-hariq* — and We shall say: taste the punishment of the burning — is the punishment.

"Allah has heard those ˹among the Jews˺ who said, 'Allah is poor; we are rich!'" — *laqad sami'a Allahu qawla alladhina qalu inna Allaha faqirun wa nahnu aghniya'* — Allah has certainly heard the statement of those who said: Allah is poor and we are rich. The *sam'* — the hearing — is the divine: the *sami'a Allah* — Allah has heard — the *qawl* — the saying — the audacity — the *faqir* — poor — the *aghniya'* — rich — the claim of the poor and the rich. Ibn Kathir's context: Sa'id bin Jubayr said that Ibn 'Abbas said — "When Allah's statement, 'Who is he that will lend to Allah a goodly loan so that He may multiply it to him many times?' (2:245) was revealed, the Jews said, 'O Muhammad! Has your Lord become poor so that He asks His servants to give Him a loan?' Allah sent down: 'Indeed, Allah has heard...'" (collected by Ibn Marduwyah and Ibn Abi Hatim). Al-Jalalayn: they said this when 2:245 was revealed — "If God were truly rich He would not be asking us for loans." The verse's *sami'a* — He has heard — is the note: the *qawl* — the saying — heard — the divine hearing — of the audacity — the *faqir* — the poor — and the *aghniya'* — the rich — the claim of the Jews.

"We have certainly recorded their slurs and their killing of prophets unjustly" — *sanaktubu ma qalu wa qatlahumu al-anbiya'a bighayri haqq* — We shall write what they said and their killing of the prophets without right. The *kitabah* — the recording, the writing — is the record: the *sanaktub* — We shall record — the *ma qalu* — what they said — and the *qatl* — the killing — of the *anbiya'* — the prophets — the *bighayr haqq* — without right — the record of the saying and the slaying. Al-Jalalayn: "We shall order that it be written... in the scrolls containing their deeds so that they will be requited for it" — with the variant readings (*naktubu* active / *yuktabu* passive; *qatlahum* accusative / *qatluhum* nominative). Ibn Kathir: "'We shall record what they have said' contains a threat and a warning that Allah followed with... 'and their killing of the Prophets unjustly'" — "This is what they say about Allah and this is how they treat His Messengers." The verse's *qatl* — the killing — is the deed: the *anbiya'* — the prophets — slain — the *bighayr haqq* — without right — the recorded — the *sanaktub* — the writing — of the two: the *qawl* — the saying — and the *qatl* — the slaying — the record of the audacity.

"Then We will say, 'Taste the torment of burning!" — *wanaqulu dhuqu 'adhaba al-hariq* — and We shall say: taste the punishment of the burning. The *qawl* — the saying — is the divine: the *naqul* — We shall say — the *dhuqu* — taste — the *'adhab al-hariq* — the punishment of the burning — the taste of the fire. Ibn Kathir: "Allah will punish them for these deeds in the worst manner" — "They will be addressed like this as a way of chastising, criticism, disgrace and humiliation." Al-Jalalayn: *(naqulu)* — also read *yaqulu* — "God shall say to them by the tongue of the angels in the Hereafter: 'Taste the chastisement of the Burning.'" The verse's *dhuqu* — taste — is the command: the *'adhab* — the punishment — the *hariq* — the burning — the taste — of the fire.

The verse's context: the rebuke of the audacity (181–182) — the *sami'a* — the hearing — the *sanaktub* — the record — the *dhuqu* — the taste — the threat against the claim. Ibn Kathir's frame: "Allah Warns the Idolators" — the *qawl* of the *faqir* and the *aghniya'* — the *qatl* of the *anbiya'* — the *'adhab* of the *hariq* — the warning of the audacity.

For the reader, the verse is the hearing of the audacity and the record of the deeds. The believer is taught: the *sami'a Allah* — Allah has heard — the claim: the *faqir* and the *aghniya'* — the poor and the rich — the audacity of the Jews at 2:245 — "Has your Lord become poor?" — the divine hearing of the saying; and the *sanaktub* — the record — the *qawl* and the *qatl* — the saying and the slaying — the *bighayr haqq* — the unjust — the record of the two. And the *dhuqu 'adhab al-hariq* — the taste of the burning — the recompense: the *naqul* — the divine saying — the *dhuq* — the taste — the *'adhab* — the punishment — the *hariq* — the burning. The believer's program: the *qawl* — the saying — about Allah — not the audacity of the *faqir* and the *aghniya'*; the *sanaktub* — the record — the deeds — the *qatl* — the slaying — of the prophets — the unjust — and the *dhuq* — the taste — avoided — the *'adhab al-hariq* — the punishment of the burning.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 182

Verse 182 says: *This is ˹the reward˺ for what your hands have done. And Allah is never unjust to ˹His˺ creation.”*

In simple terms, the verse explains the recompense: this is the reward for what your hands have done — and Allah is never unjust to His creation.

The verse is the justice of the recompense. Ibn Kathir: "This is because of that which your hands have sent before you. And certainly, Allah is never unjust to (His) servants." The verse's *dhalika bima qaddamat aydikum* — that is for what your hands sent forth — is the cause; and the *wa anna Allaha laysa bizallamin lil-'abid* — and that Allah is not unjust to (His) servants — is the justice.

"This is ˹the reward˺ for what your hands have done" — *dhalika bima qaddamat aydikum* — that is for what your hands have sent forth. The *dhalika* — that — is the recompense: the *bima qaddamat* — for what sent forth — the *aydikum* — your hands — the *qaddam* — the sending on ahead — the deeds — the cause of the 'adhab. Ibn Kathir: "This is because of that which your hands have sent before you." The verse's *aydikum* — your hands — is the agent: the *qaddamat* — sent forth — the deeds — the *dhalika* — that — the burning — the *bima* — for what — the cause of the recompense — the *qaddam* — the forward — the deeds of the hands.

"And Allah is never unjust to ˹His˺ creation" — *wa anna Allaha laysa bizallamin lil-'abid* — and that Allah is not one to wrong (His) servants. The *zulm* — the injustice — is the negation: the *laysa bizallam* — is not unjust — the *lil-'abid* — to the servants — the justice of the divine. Ibn Kathir: "And certainly, Allah is never unjust to (His) servants." The verse's *zallam* — the unjust — is the denial: the *laysa* — is not — the *zallam* — the wronger — the *'abid* — the servants — the justice — of the recompense — the *zulm* — the injustice — negated.

The verse's context: the justice of the record (182) — the *dhalika* — the recompense — the *bima qaddamat* — the deeds — the *laysa bizallam* — the not-unjust — the justice of the divine. Ibn Kathir: they will be addressed as chastisement, criticism, disgrace and humiliation — the *dhalika* — the cause — and the *laysa bizallam* — the justice.

The verse's doctrine aligns with the surah's sustained teaching on justice: the same *lazallam* — the not-unjust — appears at 3:182 as the seal of the threat, and elsewhere the Qur'an insists that Allah does not wrong anyone — "Whoever does a wrong, he is repaid for it" and "whoever does a righteous deed, it is for his own self" — because the accounting belongs to the Divine who is never a wronger of servants. The *bima qaddamat aydikum* — for what your hands have sent ahead — is the exacting justice of the recompense: not the arbitrary but the earned. Al-Jalalayn's framing of 181–182 as one sentence — the hearing, the record, the taste, the cause, and the justice — shows the sequence: the *qawl* (the saying) is heard, the *qatl* (the slaying) is recorded, the *'adhab* (the punishment) is tasted, and the *dhalika* (that) is the effect of the *qaddamat* (the deeds sent ahead) — the justice of the record.

The reader should note the precision of *aydikum* — "your hands" — rather than a generic "you": the hands are the instrument of the *qaddam* — the sending ahead — the deeds committed and the deeds prepared. The threat of 181–182 therefore addresses not merely belief but the *'amal* — the action — and the *yashtarun* — the trading — of the desert: the record is of deeds, and the recompense is of the deeds, and Allah is never unjust to the servants. The *dhalika* — that — pointed forward at the burning and backward at the doing: the *guruf* — the branches of the recompense — and the *'amal* — the deed — the *dhalika* of the justice.

For the reader, the verse is the justice of the recompense and the doctrine of the divine justice. The believer is taught: the *dhalika* — that — the recompense — the *bima qaddamat aydikum* — for what the hands sent forth — the deeds — the cause of the punishment — the *qaddam* — the forward — the hands' works; and the *laysa bizallam* — the not-unjust — the *'abid* — the servants — the justice — the *zulm* — negated — the divine justice. The verse's *dhalika* — that — is the pointer: the burning — the cause — the *qaddam* — the sent forth — the record — the *'adhab* — the punishment — the justice. The believer's program: the *qaddam* — the sending forward — of the deeds — the *aydi* — the hands — the *'amal* — the work — the *zulm* — the injustice — avoided — the *'adl* — the justice — of the divine — the *laysa bizallam* — the not-unjust — the recompense of the deeds.

## Verse 183

Verse 183 says: *Those ˹are the same people˺ who say, “Allah has commanded us not to believe in any messenger unless he brings us an offering to be consumed by fire ˹from the sky˺.” Say, ˹O Prophet˺, “Other prophets did in fact come to you before me with clear proofs and ˹even˺ what you demanded—why then did you kill them, if what you say is true?”*

In simple terms, the verse refutes the Jews' pretext: they say Allah commanded them not to believe any messenger unless he brings an offering consumed by fire from the sky — but prophets before came with clear proofs and even that sign, and they killed them — so why, if they are truthful?

The verse is the refutation of the pretext. Ibn Kathir: "Allah refuted their claim that in their Books, Allah took a covenant from them to only believe in the Messenger whose miracles include fire coming down from the sky that consumes the charity offered by a member of the Messenger's nation, as Ibn 'Abbas and Al-Hasan stated." The verse's *alladhina qalu inna Allaha 'ahida ilayna alla nu'mina lirasulin hatta ya'tiyana biqurbanin ta'kuluhu al-nar* — those who said: Allah has taken our promise not to believe in any Messenger until he brings us an offering that the fire consumes — is the claim; the *qul qad ja'akum rusulun min qabli bil-bayyinati wabil-ladhi qultum* — say: messengers have come to you before me with clear proofs and with what you said — is the proof; and the *falima qataltumuhum in kuntum sadiqin* — why then did you kill them, if you are truthful — is the challenge.

"Those ˹are the same people˺ who say, 'Allah has commanded us not to believe in any messenger unless he brings us an offering to be consumed by fire ˹from the sky˺'" — *alladhina qalu inna Allaha 'ahida ilayna alla nu'mina lirasulin hatta ya'tiyana biqurbanin ta'kuluhu al-nar* — those who said: Allah has made a covenant with us not to believe in any Messenger until he brings us an offering which the fire consumes. The *'ahd* — the covenant, the command — is the claim: the *'ahida ilayna* — He made covenant with us — the *alla nu'mina* — that we not believe — the *li-rasul* — in a Messenger — the *hatta* — until — the *qurban* — the offering — the *ta'kuluhu al-nar* — the fire consumes it — the claim of the covenant and the sign. Al-Jalalayn: "God has already made covenant with us in the Torah that we should not believe in any messenger accepting his truthfulness until he bring us an offering to be devoured by fire" — the offering of grazing livestock or another kind of animal; if accepted, a white fire comes down from heaven and consumes it, otherwise it remains — "such a covenant was made with the Children of Israel but not in the case of Jesus and Muhammad." Ibn Kathir (Ibn 'Abbas and Al-Hasan): the claim of the fire consuming the charity. The verse's *qurban* — the offering — is the sign: the *ta'kuluhu al-nar* — the fire consumes it — the *qurban* — the sacrifice — the *'ahd* — the covenant — the claim of the Jews — the sign of the fire.

"Other prophets did in fact come to you before me with clear proofs and ˹even˺ what you demanded" — *qul qad ja'akum rusulun min qabli bil-bayyinati wabil-ladhi qultum* — say: messengers have come to you before me with clear proofs and with what you said. The *bayyinat* — the clear proofs — are the signs: the *ja'akum* — came to you — the *rusul* — the messengers — the *min qabli* — before me — the *bil-bayyinat* — with the clear proofs — and the *bil-ladhi qultum* — with what you said — the fire that consumes the accepted charity. Ibn Kathir: "with proofs and evidence... a fire that consumes the accepted charity, as you asked." Al-Jalalayn: "messengers such as Zachariah and John" — with the *bayyinat* — the miracles — and with what they said. Maududi: the Bible mentions the token of divine acceptance of a sacrificial offering — the mysterious fire consuming it (Judges 6:20-1 and 13:19-20; 2 Chronicles 7:1-2) — but does not state it was an indispensable token of prophethood; Elijah challenged the worshippers of Ba'l, his sacrifice was consumed by fire (1 Kings 18-19). The verse's *bil-ladhi qultum* — with what you said — is the proof: the *qurban* — the offering — the *nar* — the fire — the *ja'at* — came — the *rusul* — the messengers — the *bayyinat* — the proofs — the answer to the claim.

"why then did you kill them, if what you say is true?" — *falima qataltumuhum in kuntum sadiqin* — why then did you kill them, if you are truthful? The *qatl* — the killing — is the accusation: the *qataltumuhum* — you killed them — the *lima* — why — the *in kuntum sadiqin* — if you are truthful — the challenge. Ibn Kathir: "Why did you meet these Prophets with denial, defiance, stubbornness and even murder, if you are truthful — if you follow the truth and obey the Messengers." Al-Jalalayn: "the address here is for those living at the time of our Prophet although the deed was their forefathers', for they, their descendants, are content with it." Maududi: "How dare you ask for the miracle of the consuming fire when in the past you have not even refrained from murdering Prophets who performed that miracle?" — Elijah was forced to flee to Sinai. The verse's *sadiqin* — the truthful — is the condition: the *qatl* — the killing — the *lima* — why — the *qataltum* — you slew — the claim — disproved by the *qatl* — the murder — the *sadiq* — the truth — the challenge.

The verse's context: the refutation of the claim (183) — the *'ahd* — the covenant — the *qurban* — the offering — the *nar* — the fire — the *bayyinat* — the proofs — the *qatl* — the killing — the *sadiqin* — the truthful. Ibn Kathir's frame: the claim of the fire and the answer of the proofs — the *rusul* — the messengers — the *qatlu* — the slaying — the refutation of the pretext.

For the reader, the verse is the refutation of the pretext and the challenge of the truth. The believer is taught: the *'ahd* — the claimed covenant — the *qurban* — the offering — the *ta'kuluhu al-nar* — the fire consuming — the pretext of the Jews: the sign demanded; and the *ja'akum rusul* — the messengers came — the *bil-bayyinat* — with the clear proofs — and the *bil-ladhi qultum* — with what they demanded — the fire of the accepted offering — the sign — given — the *falima qataltum* — the why-killed — the challenge: the *qatl* — the murder — of the prophets — the *in kuntum sadiqin* — the truthful — the test. And the *qurban* — the offering — the *nar* — the fire — not the indispensable sign — the *bayyinat* — the proofs — the *sadq* — the truth — the *qatl* — the murder — the challenge. The believer's program: the *bayyinat* — the clear proofs — not the *qurban* — the demanded sign — the *'ahd* — the covenant — the *haqq* — the truth — the *qatl* — the murder — the refutation of the pretext — the *sadq* — the truth — of the messengers.

## Verse 184

Verse 184 says: *If you are rejected by them, so too were messengers before you who came with clear proofs, divine Books, and enlightening Scriptures.*

In simple terms, the verse consoles the Prophet: if they reject you, so were messengers before you rejected, who came with clear proofs, divine Books, and enlightening Scriptures.

The verse is the consolation of the rejection. Ibn Kathir: "Then if they reject you, so were Messengers rejected before you, who came with Al-Bayyinah and the Scriptures and the Book of Enlightenment" — "do not be sad because they deny you, for you have an example in the Messengers who came before you." Al-Jalalayn: "so be patient as they were." The verse's *fa-in kadhthabuka faqad kudhdhiba rusulun min qablike ja'u bil-bayyinati waz-zuburi wal-kitabi al-munir* — and if they deny you, then messengers before you were denied, who came with clear proofs, the Zubur, and the enlightening Book — is the consolation; the *bil-bayyinat* — with the clear proofs — is the evidence; the *az-zubur* — the divine Books — is the revelation; and the *al-kitab al-munir* — the enlightening Book — is the illumination.

"If you are rejected by them, so too were messengers before you" — *fa-in kadhthabuka faqad kudhdhiba rusulun min qablik* — then if they deny you, so were messengers before you denied. The *takdhib* — the denial, the rejection — is the shared: the *kadhdhabuka* — they deny you — the *kudhdhiba* — were denied — the *rusul* — the messengers — the *min qablik* — before you — the pattern of the denial. Ibn Kathir: "do not be sad because they deny you, for you have an example in the Messengers who came before you." The verse's *kudhdhiba* — were denied — is the precedent: the *rusul* — the messengers — the *min qablik* — before you — the *takdhib* — the denial — the pattern — the example — of the *rasul* — the comfort — the consolation.

"who came with clear proofs, divine Books, and enlightening Scriptures" — *ja'u bil-bayyinati waz-zuburi wal-kitabi al-munir* — who came with clear proofs, the Zubur, and the enlightening Book. The *bayyinah* — the clear proof — is the first: the *ja'u* — they came — the *bayyinat* — the clear proofs — the miracles; the *zubur* — the divine Books — is the second: Al-Jalalayn — the scrolls of Abraham; the *kitab al-munir* — the enlightening Book — is the third: Al-Jalalayn — "the lucid Book, the Torah and the Gospel"; Ibn Kathir — "the divinely revealed Books that were sent down to the Messengers" and "the clarification and best explanation." The verse's *munir* — the enlightening — is the illumination: the *kitab* — the Book — the *munir* — the luminous, the enlightening — the Torah and the Gospel — the *zubur* — the scrolls — the *bayyinat* — the proofs — the evidence of the messengers.

The verse's context: the consolation (184) after the refutation (183) — the *takdhib* — the denial — the *rusul* — the messengers — the *bayyinat* — the proofs — the *zubur* — the Books — the *kitab munir* — the enlightening Book — the pattern and the example. Ibn Kathir: "These Messengers were rejected although they brought clear proofs, plain evidence and unequivocal signs" — the *rusul* — the messengers — the *takdhib* — the denial — the *sabr* — the patience — "so be patient as they were."

For the reader, the verse is the consolation of the rejection and the doctrine of the precedent. The believer is taught: the *kadhdhabuka* — they deny you — the *kudhdhiba rusul* — the messengers denied — the *min qablik* — before you — the pattern — the precedent — the *takdhib* — the rejection — shared with the prophets — the comfort of the example. And the *bayyinat* — the clear proofs — the *zubur* — the divine Books — the *kitab al-munir* — the enlightening Book — the credentials of the messengers: the *bayyinah* — the miracle — the *zubur* — the scrolls — the *munir* — the luminous — the Torah and the Gospel — the evidence. The verse's *munir* — the enlightening — is the illumination: the *kitab* — the Book — that clarifies — the *zubur* — the Books — that guide — the *bayyinat* — the proofs — that prove. The believer's program: the *takdhib* — the denial — not the grief — the *rusul* — the messengers — the precedent — the *bayyinat* — the proofs — the *zubur* — the Books — the *kitab munir* — the illumination — the *sabr* — the patience — of the prophets — the consolation of the rejection.

## Verse 185

Verse 185 says: *Every soul will taste death. And you will only receive your full reward on the Day of Judgment. Whoever is spared from the Fire and is admitted into Paradise will ˹indeed˺ triumph, whereas the life of this world is no more than the delusion of enjoyment.*

In simple terms, the verse establishes the final truth: every soul will taste death; the full reward comes only on the Day of Judgment; whoever is spared from the Fire and admitted to Paradise has truly triumphed — and this worldly life is nothing but the delusion of enjoyment.

The verse is the truth of the death and the triumph. Ibn Kathir: "Everyone shall taste death. And only on the Day of Resurrection shall you be paid your wages in full. And whoever is moved away from the Fire and admitted to Paradise, he indeed is successful. The life of this world is only the enjoyment of deception." The verse's *kullu nafsin dha'iqatu al-mawt* — every soul is tasting death — is the universal; the *wa innama tuwaffawna ujurakum yawma al-qiyamah* — and you will be paid your rewards in full on the Day of Resurrection — is the payment; the *faman zuhziha 'ani al-nari wa udkhila al-jannata faqad faza* — whoever is removed from the Fire and admitted to Paradise has succeeded — is the triumph; and the *wa ma al-hayatu al-dunya illa mata'u al-ghurur* — and the life of this world is not but the enjoyment of delusion — is the belittlement.

"Every soul will taste death" — *kullu nafsin dha'iqatu al-mawt* — every soul will taste death. The *dhawq* — the tasting — is the universal: the *kull nafs* — every soul — the *dha'iqah* — the tasting — the *mawt* — the death — the universal of the taste. Ibn Kathir: "Allah issues a general and encompassing statement that every living soul shall taste death" — 55:26–27 — "Whatsoever is on it (the earth) will perish. And the Face of your Lord full of majesty and honor will remain forever" — "Allah Alone is the Ever-Living Who never dies, while the Jinn, mankind and angels, including those who carry Allah's Throne, shall die." The verse's *kull nafs* — every soul — is the universal: the *jinn* — the *ins* — the *mala'ikah* — the *dhawq* — the tasting — the *mawt* — the death — the universal of the creation.

"And you will only receive your full reward on the Day of Judgment" — *wa innama tuwaffawna ujurakum yawma al-qiyamah* — and you will only be paid in full your rewards on the Day of Resurrection. The *tawfi'* — the full payment — is the Day: the *innama* — only — the *tuwaffawn* — you will be paid in full — the *ujur* — the rewards — the *yawm al-qiyamah* — the Day of Resurrection — the fullness of the payment. Ibn Kathir: "Allah will then recompense the creation for their deeds, whether minor or major, many or few, big or small. Surely, Allah will not deal unjustly with anyone, even the weight of an atom." The verse's *innama* — only — is the restriction: the *ujur* — the rewards — the *tawfi'* — the full — the *yawm al-qiyamah* — the Day — the fullness — of the payment — and the *yawm* — the Day — the appointed — of the accounting.

"Whoever is spared from the Fire and is admitted into Paradise will ˹indeed˺ triumph" — *faman zuhziha 'ani al-nari wa udkhila al-jannata faqad faza* — whoever is removed from the Fire and admitted to Paradise has succeeded. The *fawz* — the success, the triumph — is the goal: the *zuhzih* — removed, drawn away — the *'an al-nar* — from the Fire — the *udkhil* — admitted — the *al-jannah* — Paradise — the *faza* — succeeded — the triumph. Ibn Kathir: "whoever is kept away from the Fire, saved from it and entered into Paradise, will have achieved the ultimate success" — Ibn Abi Hatim/Abu Hurayrah — "A place in Paradise as small as that which is occupied by a whip is better than the world and whatever is on its surface" (in the Two Sahihs via another chain; also Ibn Hibban and Al-Hakim). Al-Jalalayn: "he will have attained his ultimate wish." The verse's *faza* — succeeded — is the achievement: the *zuhzih* — the removal — the *udkhil* — the admission — the *fawz* — the success — the ultimate wish — the triumph of the spared.

"whereas the life of this world is no more than the delusion of enjoyment" — *wa ma al-hayatu al-dunya illa mata'u al-ghurur* — and the life of this world is not but the enjoyment of delusion. The *mata'* — the enjoyment, the provision — is the delusion: the *illa* — not but — the *mata'* — the enjoyment — the *ghurur* — the delusion, the deception — the life — the enjoyment of the delusion. Ibn Kathir: "belittling the value of this life and degrading its importance. This life is short, little and finite" — 87:16-17 ("Nay, you prefer the life of this world. Although the Hereafter is better and more lasting") — 28:60 — and the Hadith — "By Allah, the world is not more in the Hereafter than one of you dipping his finger in the ocean, then see what it brings back." Al-Jalalayn: "the comfort of delusion; of inanity enjoyed for a little while then perishing." The verse's *ghurur* — the delusion — is the deception: the *mata'* — the enjoyment — the *dunya* — the world — the *ghurur* — the deception — the enjoyment — of the delusion.

The verse's context: the death and the triumph (185) — the *dhawq* — the tasting — the *tawfi'* — the payment — the *fawz* — the success — the *mata' ghurur* — the delusion — the truth of the two ends.
