- Ibn Kathir and Al-Jalalayn on 3:121 — "The Battle of Uhud": the majority view (Ibn 'Abbas, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi) — the verses describe Uhud, Saturday, Shawwal, year 3 of Hijrah ('Ikrimah: mid-Shawwal); Al-Jalalayn: the Prophet set out with 1000 or 950 men while the idolaters numbered 3000, camped at the ravine with Uhud behind him, stationed the archers under 'Abdullah bin Jubayr ("Defend us with your arrows... remain here whether we are defeated or victorious"); the reason for the battle — the caravan of Abu Sufyan returned safely to Makkah, the Makkan leaders and the children of the Badr dead spent the caravan money on warfare, mobilized 3000 including the Ahabish; Ibn Kathir: the Prophet consulted (Ibn Ubayy advising to stay in Madinah; those who missed Badr urging to go out), "It is not for a Prophet to wear his shield then lay down arms before Allah decides in his favor"; 1000 marched, Ibn Ubayy withdrew a third at Shawt; 700 remained; archers = 50 under Ibn Jubayr. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-121-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir and Al-Jalalayn on 3:122 — the two groups about to lose heart = Banu Salimah and Banu Harithah (the two flanks), influenced by Ibn Ubayy's withdrawal ("Why should we get ourselves and our children killed?"; "If we knew how to fight we would follow you"); "Allah was their Wali" — He made them steadfast and they did not abandon the field; the command "upon Allah the believers rely." https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-122-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir on 3:123–125 — "The Support of the Angels": scholars differ over whether the 3,000/5,000 promise refers to Badr or Uhud; view one (Al-Hasan, Ash-Sha'bi, Ar-Rabi', Ibn Jarir): Badr — the Muslims were distressed by news that Kurz bin Jabir was aiding the idolaters, so Allah revealed the promise; Kurz heard of the defeat and did not reinforce, so the 5,000 were not sent; Ar-Rabi': Allah supported with 1,000 (8:9), then 3,000, then 5,000 — the 8:9 "each behind the other in succession" indicates thousands following; the angels did fight at Badr; view two: Uhud — the angels did not come because the condition of patience and Taqwa was not met; the Muslims fled and received no angel; 'Ali: the angels at Badr were distinguished by white wool and marked horses; Al-Hasan/Qatadah/Ar-Rabi'/As-Suddi: "they come rushing at you" = instantaneously, all at once (Al-'Awfi/Ibn 'Abbas), before their anger subsides. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-124-sora-3.html
