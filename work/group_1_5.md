## Verse 1

Verse 1 says: *Alif-Lãm-Mĩm.*

In simple terms, the verse opens the surah with the detached letters — Alif, Lam, Mim — the Qur'an's known openings whose true meaning is with Allah.

"Alif-Lãm-Mĩm" — the verse's letters: the huruf muqatta'at — the detached, the disjointed letters at the openers of twenty-nine surahs of the Qur'an. Ibn Kathir: we already explained the tafsir of Alif-Lam-Mim at the beginning of Surat Al-Baqarah — the letters are among the surah's openers; their reality is known to Allah, and their recitation is the faith's test: the Arabs' own letters, the same ones they used for their speech, and the Qur'an of these same letters is the challenge that they could not meet. The verse's frame: the letters — the Qur'an's alphabet — the challenge in the very tongue of the objectors: the speech of these letters is the inimitable.

The verse's place: the opening of Surat Al 'Imran — revealed in Al-Madinah (Ibn Kathir: as evident from the fact that the first eighty-three ayat relate to the delegation from Najran that arrived in Al-Madinah in the ninth year of the Hijrah). The verse's frame: the surah's opening with the letters and the divine names (3:2) — the same pattern as Al-Baqarah: the challenge and the creed.

The verse's lesson: the letters are the sign — the revelation's miracle in the people's own alphabet. And the reader's part: recite them as revealed — their full meaning is with the One who revealed them.

## Verse 2

Verse 2 says: *Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him —the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining.*

In simple terms, the verse declares the divine Oneness: no god except Allah — the Ever-Living, the All-Sustaining.

"Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him" — the verse's shahada: la ilaha illa Huwa — the negation and the affirmation: no god, except Him. Ibn Kathir: he explained the meaning of "Allah! La ilaha illa Huwa, Al-Hayyul-Qayyum" in the tafsir of Ayat al-Kursi (2:255) — the same formula that opens the greatest verse of the Qur'an. The verse's frame: the surah's foundation: the Oneness before the revelation's story and the battles' lessons: the creed of the whole surah.

"—the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining" — the verse's two names: al-Hayy — the Ever-Living — al-Qayyum — the All-Sustaining — the Self-Subsisting, the One by Whom all subsists. The verse's frame: the same names of 2:255: the Life that never dies and the Sustenance that never fails: the One who lives and keeps all living.

The verse's lesson: the Oneness is the surah's key — the Living and the Sustaining are the first knowledge. And the reader's part: begin with the Oneness — every verse and every story of Al 'Imran is the unfolding of the no-god-except-Him.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 3

Verse 3 says: *He has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book in truth, confirming what came before it, as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel*

In simple terms, the verse speaks of the revelation: the Book was sent down in truth, confirming what came before it — as the Torah and the Gospel were revealed.

"He has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book in truth" — the verse's revelation: the nazzala — the sent down — the Kitab — the Book — the bi al-haqq — in truth. Ibn Kathir: "It is He Who has sent down the Book to you with truth" — means, revealed the Qur'an to you, O Muhammad, in truth — there is no doubt or suspicion that it is revealed from Allah — Allah revealed the Qur'an with His knowledge, and the angels testify to this fact — Allah is sufficient as a Witness. Al-Jalalayn: the Qur'an, enveloped by the truth, with veracity in what it announces. The verse's frame: the truth of the descent: the Book is not the report of a poet or the invention of a sage: the truth is the Book's badge.

"confirming what came before it" — the verse's confirmation: the musaddiqan lima bayna yadayhi — confirming what came before it. Ibn Kathir: from the previous divinely revealed Books, sent to the servants and Prophets of Allah — these Books testify to the truth of the Qur'an, and the Qur'an also testifies to the truth these Books contained — including the news and glad tidings of Muhammad's prophethood and the revelation of the Glorious Qur'an. The verse's frame: the two-way confirmation: the previous Books announced him; the Qur'an confirms their true content: the message is one — the confirmers and the confirmed meet in the single truth.

"as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel" — the verse's earlier revelations: the Tawrah — the Torah — to Musa — the Injil — the Gospel — to 'Isa. Ibn Kathir: He revealed the Torah and the Gospel — the previous scriptures of the People of the Book. The verse's frame: the three revelations: the Torah, the Gospel, the Qur'an — the chain: each confirming and confirmed: the People of the Book's own scriptures are the witnesses of this Book.

The verse's lesson: the Book in truth, confirming the Books before — the Qur'an is the confirmation's crown. And the reader's part: the revelation is one line from the Torah to the Gospel to the Qur'an — the truth that confirms is the truth that is confirmed.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 4

Verse 4 says: *previously, as a guide for people, and ˹also˺ revealed the Standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺. Surely those who reject Allah’s revelations will suffer a severe torment. For Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment.*

In simple terms, the verse continues: the Torah and the Gospel came previously as guidance for people; and He revealed the Standard (al-Furqan); and those who reject the revelations will suffer a severe torment — Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment.

"previously, as a guide for people" — the verse's guidance: the min qablu — previously — the hudan li al-nas — a guidance for people. Ibn Kathir: the Torah and the Gospel revealed before — as a guidance to mankind. The verse's frame: the scriptures' purpose: the guidance of the people — the Torah for the Children of Israel, the Gospel for its followers: the guidance's continuity.

"and ˹also˺ revealed the Standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺" — the verse's Furqan: the al-Furqan — the Criterion, the Standard — the separator between the truth and the falsehood. Ibn Kathir (and the classical notes): the Furqan — the Qur'an, the decisive authority — the distinguisher between the right and the wrong, the guidance and the misguidance (the tafsir notes: "as a guidance to people, and sent down the Criterion" — the decisive authority; some: the miracles of the Prophet). The verse's frame: the revealed Standard: the judgment's scale: in the Qur'an the truth is separated from the falsehood — the decisive book for the disputes.

"Surely those who reject Allah's revelations will suffer a severe torment" — the verse's warning: the kafaru bi ayat Allah — those who reject the revelations — the 'adhab shadid — the severe torment. Ibn Kathir: the warning against those who reject His ayat and defy His honorable Messengers and great Prophets — the severe punishment. The verse's frame: the rejection's wage: the signs are clear — the denial after the clarity is the severe torment's cause.

"For Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment" — the verse's close: the 'Aziz — the Almighty — the dhi intiqam — the capable of punishment, the possessor of retribution. The verse's frame: the Almighty's power and the punishment's capability: the One who revealed the Standard is the One who enforces it.

The verse's lesson: the revelation's history is the guidance's chain — the standard is the distinctor, and the rejection of the clear signs is the severe torment. And the reader's part: take the Standard — the truth and the falsehood are separated in it — and fear the Almighty's capability.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 5

Verse 5 says: *Surely nothing on earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah.*

In simple terms, the verse declares: nothing on the earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah — His knowledge encompasses all.

"Surely nothing on earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah" — the verse's encompassing: the la yakhfa — nothing is hidden — the 'ala Allah — from Allah — the fi al-ard — on the earth — the wa la fi al-sama' — nor in the heavens. Ibn Kathir: the knowledge of Allah encompasses all that is in the heavens and the earth — nothing is hidden from Him — the smallest and the greatest. The verse's frame: the divine knowledge: the hidden's domain — the secret of the wombs, the paths of the night, the intentions of the hearts: the not-hidden from the Knower.

The verse's place: between the revelation (3:1–4) and the womb's shaping (3:6) — the knowledge's frame: the One who revealed the Standard knows the hidden — the revelation's truth is the Knower's speech. The verse's frame: the connection: nothing is hidden from the One whose Book is in truth.

The verse's lesson: the hidden has no hiding — the earth's depths and the heavens' heights are the opened to His knowledge. And the reader's part: live in the open of His knowledge — the secret is His — and the revealed Standard is His speech.

===VERSE-END===
