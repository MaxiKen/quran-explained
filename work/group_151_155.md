## Verse 151

Verse 151 says: *We will cast horror into the hearts of the disbelievers for associating ˹false gods˺ with Allah—a practice He has never authorized. The Fire will be their home—what an evil place for the wrongdoers to stay!*

In simple terms, the verse announces the divine punishment of the disbelievers: We will cast horror into their hearts for associating partners with Allah without any authority — the Fire will be their home, an evil place for the wrongdoers.

The verse is the terror of the disbelievers and the recompense of the shirk. Ibn Kathir: "We shall cast terror into the hearts of those who disbelieve, because they joined others in worship with Allah, for which He sent no authority; their abode will be the Fire and how evil is the abode of the wrongdoers." The verse's *sanu'lqi fi qulubi alladhina kafaru al-ru'ba* — We will cast terror into the hearts of those who disbelieve — is the terror; the *bima ashraku billahi ma lam yunazzil bihi sultana* — because they associated with Allah what He did not send down authority for — is the cause; and the *wa ma'wahumu al-naru wa bi'sa mathwa al-zalimin* — and their abode is the Fire, and evil is the abode of the wrongdoers — is the recompense.

"We will cast horror into the hearts of the disbelievers" — *sanu'lqi fi qulubi alladhina kafaru al-ru'ba* — We will cast terror into the hearts of those who disbelieve. The *ilqa'* — the casting, the throwing — is the divine act: the *ru'b* — the terror, the horror — the *ilqa'* — the casting — into the hearts of the disbelievers. Ibn Kathir: "Allah next conveys the good news that He will put fear of the Muslims, and feelings of subordination to the Muslims in the hearts of their disbelieving enemies." The verse's *sanu'lqi* — We will cast — is the future of the divine: the terror of the enemy — the *ru'b* — the horror — the fear of the Muslims in the hearts of the disbelievers. The Two Sahihs record Jabir: "I was given five things that no other Prophet before me was given: I was aided with fear the distance of one month, the earth was made a Masjid and clean place for me, I was allowed war booty, I was given the Intercession, and Prophets used to be sent to their people, but I was sent to all mankind." The verse's *ru'b* — the terror — is the divine aid of the Prophet: the fear of the distance of a month — the horror cast into the disbelievers' hearts.

"for associating ˹false gods˺ with Allah—a practice He has never authorized" — *bima ashraku billahi ma lam yunazzil bihi sultana* — because they associated with Allah that for which He sent down no authority. The *shirk* — the association — is the cause: the *ashraku* — they associated — the false gods with Allah; the *ma lam yunazzil bihi sultana* — what He did not authorize — the authority not sent down: the *shirk* without the proof, the association without the permission. Ibn Kathir: "because of their Kufr and Shirk." The verse's *lam yunazzil* — He did not send down — is the absence of the authority: the *shirk* — the association — has no divine authorization; the *sultan* — the authority — the proof — was not sent — the association is the unauthorized, the unsupported.

"The Fire will be their home—what an evil place for the wrongdoers to stay!" — *wa ma'wahumu al-naru wa bi'sa mathwa al-zalimin* — and their abode is the Fire, and evil is the abode of the wrongdoers. The *ma'wa* — the abode, the home — is the Fire: the *ma'wahum* — their abode — the Fire — the dwelling of the wrongdoers; the *bi'sa* — how evil — is the condemnation: the *mathwa* — the abode, the resting place — the evil — of the wrongdoers. Ibn Kathir: "their abode will be the Fire and how evil is the abode of the wrongdoers." The verse's *ma'wa* — the abode — is the dwelling: the Fire is the home — the *mathwa* — the resting place — of the *zalimin* — the wrongdoers — the evil place of the home.

The verse's place: the terror of the disbelievers and the recompense of the shirk — the verse follows the believers' protection (150) — the *mawla* and the *khayru al-nasirin* — with the horror of the disbelievers: the believers' Guardian casts the horror into the hearts of the enemy; the believers' best Helper recompenses the shirk with the Fire. Ibn Kathir: "And Allah has prepared torment and punishment for them in the Hereafter."

For the reader, the verse is the terror of the enemy and the recompense of the association. The believer is taught: the *ilqa'* — the casting — of the *ru'b* — the horror — into the disbelievers' hearts is the divine: the fear of the Muslims, the subordination to the believers — the terror of the enemy; and the cause — the *shirk* — the association without the authority — the unauthorized — the recompense: the *ma'wa* — the Fire — the home of the wrongdoers. The verse's *bi'sa mathwa* — the evil abode — is the condemnation: the Fire is the evil home of the *zalimin* — the wrongdoers of the association. The believer's confidence: the horror of the enemy and the Fire of the wrongdoers — the divine recompense of the *shirk* — the believers' cause is the *ta'ah* (149–150) and the enemy's end is the *ru'b* and the *nar* (151).

## Verse 152

Verse 152 says: *Indeed, Allah fulfilled His promise to you when you ˹initially˺ swept them away by His Will, then your courage weakened and you disputed about the command and disobeyed, after Allah had brought victory within your reach. Some of you were after worldly gain while others desired a heavenly reward. He denied you victory over them as a test, yet He has pardoned you. And Allah is Gracious to the believers.*

In simple terms, the verse recounts the decisive moment: Allah fulfilled His promise — you swept them away by His will — then your courage weakened, you disputed about the command and disobeyed after the victory was within reach; some wanted the world's gain, others the Hereafter's reward; He denied you the victory as a test, yet He has pardoned you — and Allah is Gracious to the believers.

The verse is the account of the turning of Uhud. Ibn Kathir: "Allah did indeed fulfill His promise to you — in the beginning of the day of Uhud — when you were killing them, slaying your enemies, with His permission, for He allowed you to do that against them — until when you Fashiltum — and fell to disputing about the order, and disobeyed — such as the mistake made by the archers — after He showed you what you love, that is, victory over the disbelievers." The verse's *wa laqad sadaqakumu Allahu wa'dahu* — and Allah certainly fulfilled His promise to you — is the fulfillment; the *idh tahussunahum bi-idhnihi* — when you were sweeping them away by His permission — is the defeat; the *hatta idha fashiltum wa tanaza'tum fi al-amri wa 'asaytum* — until you weakened and disputed about the command and disobeyed — is the failure; the *min ba'di ma arakum ma tuhibbun* — after He showed you what you love — is the victory's reach; the *minkum man yuridu al-dunya wa minkum man yuridu al-akhirah* — among you who desires the world and who desires the Hereafter — is the division; the *thumma sarafakum 'anhum liyabtaliyakum* — then He turned you away from them to test you — is the test; and the *wa laqad 'afa 'ankum* — and He has pardoned you — is the pardon.

"Allah fulfilled His promise to you when you ˹initially˺ swept them away by His Will" — *wa laqad sadaqakumu Allahu wa'dahu idh tahussunahum bi-idhnihi* — and Allah certainly fulfilled His promise to you when you were sweeping them away with His permission. The *sadaq* — the truth, the fulfillment — is the divine: the promise of 3:122–126 — the victory — fulfilled: the *tahs* — the sweeping, the slaying — the *hass* — the cutting down — of the enemy with His permission. Ibn Kathir: "when you were killing them, slaying your enemies, with His permission, for He allowed you to do that against them." The verse's *bi-idhnihi* — with His permission — is the divine leave: the slaying was the permitted — the *idhn* — the allowing — of the divine — the fulfillment of the promise — the beginning of the day of Uhud — the victory's start.

"then your courage weakened and you disputed about the command and disobeyed" — *hatta idha fashiltum wa tanaza'tum fi al-amri wa 'asaytum* — until when you lost courage and disputed about the command and disobeyed. The *fashal* — the losing courage — is the first: Ibn Jurayj from Ibn 'Abbas: "lost courage"; the *tanazu'* — the disputing — is the second: the dispute about the command — the archers' order — the *tanazu'* — the disagreement — the "Let us depart for our comrades have been given victory" and "We should not disobey the command of the Prophet" (Al-Jalalayn); the *'isyan* — the disobedience — is the third: the command of the Prophet disobeyed — the station abandoned. Ibn Kathir: "such as the mistake made by the archers." The verse's *fashiltum* — you lost courage — and the *tanaza'tum* — you disputed — and the *'asaytum* — you disobeyed — the three failures of the turn: the courage, the command, the obedience.

"after Allah had brought victory within your reach" — *min ba'di ma arakum ma tuhibbun* — after He showed you what you love. The *ara* — He showed — is the display: the victory — what you love — shown: the defeat of the enemy, the flight, the rout — the *ma tuhibbun* — what you love — the victory over the disbelievers. Ibn Kathir: "after He showed you what you love, that is, victory over the disbelievers." The verse's *min ba'd* — after — is the sequence: the victory shown, then the failure — the *ara* — the showing — and the *fashal* — the weakening — the victory in reach and the courage lost.

"Some of you were after worldly gain while others desired a heavenly reward" — *minkum man yuridu al-dunya wa minkum man yuridu al-akhirah* — among you the one who desires the world and among you the one who desires the Hereafter. The *yurid al-dunya* — desires the world — is the booty: Ibn Kathir — "referring to those who sought to collect the booty when they saw the enemy being defeated"; the *yurid al-akhirah* — desires the Hereafter — is the station: Al-Jalalayn — "holding to it until he was slain, such as 'Abdullah bin Jubayr and his companions." The verse's *minkum... wa minkum* — among you... and among you — is the division: the two desires within the ranks — the world's booty and the Hereafter's station — the two orientations of the command's abandonment and the command's holding.

"He denied you victory over them as a test, yet He has pardoned you" — *thumma sarafakum 'anhum liyabtaliyakum wa laqad 'afa 'ankum* — then He turned you away from them to test you, and He has surely forgiven you. The *sarf* — the turning away — is the defeat: He made you flee from them — the *sarf* — the turning — "He turned you back in defeat" (Al-Jalalayn); the *liyabtaliyakum* — to test you — is the purpose; the *'afa* — He forgave — is the pardon: "He forgave the error you committed, because, and Allah knows best, the idolators were many and well supplied, while Muslims had few men and few supplies" (Ibn Kathir). The verse's *sadaqakum* — the fulfillment — and the *sarafakum* — the turning — the promise fulfilled and the defeat sent — the test of the two; the *'afa* — the pardon — the grace after the failure — the *fadl* — the bounty — "Allah is Most Gracious to the believers."

"And Allah is Gracious to the believers" — *wa Allahu dhu fadlin 'ala al-mu'minin* — and Allah is the possessor of bounty over the believers. The *fadl* — the bounty, the grace — is the divine: the *dhu fadl* — the possessor of the bounty — over the believers; the *'afa* of the pardon and the *fadl* of the bounty — the grace after the failure. Ibn Kathir: "He forgave the error you committed... Allah is Most Gracious to the believers."

The hadiths of the account, per Ibn Kathir: Al-Bukhari/Al-Bara' — the archers' instruction ("Retain your position, and if you see that we have defeated them, do not abandon your positions..."); the enemy fled and their women fled up the mountain; the archers: "The booty, the booty!"; Ibn Jubayr: "Allah's Messenger commanded me not to allow you to abandon your position"; they refused, left, and the Muslims were defeated — seventy killed; Abu Sufyan's shouts — "Is Muhammad present...? Is the son of Abu Quhafah...? Is the son of Al-Khattab...?" — "Do not answer him"; 'Umar: "You lie, O enemy of Allah!"; Abu Sufyan: "O Hubal, be high!" — "Say, Allah is Higher and more Sublime"; "We have the (idol) Al-'Uzza and you have no 'Uzza" — "Say, Allah is our protector and you have no protector"; "Our victory today is vengeance for yours in the battle of Badr, and in war the victory is always undecided and is shared in turns by the belligerents." Muhammad bin Ishaq/Zubayr — the archers went down when the enemy fled, "they uncovered our back lines to the horsemen," who attacked from behind; "Muhammad has been killed" was shouted; the enemy's flag fell until 'Amrah bint 'Alqamah picked it up. Al-Bukhari/Anas — Anas bin An-Nadr missed Badr ("if Allah gives me a chance to fight with the Messenger of Allah, then Allah will see how bravely I will fight"), at Uhud when the Muslims fled: "O Allah! I apologize to You for what these have done, and I denounce what these pagans have done" — to Sa'd bin Mu'adh: "Where are you! Paradise! I am smelling its aroma coming from before Uhud" — more than eighty wounds, mutilated, recognized only by his sister.

For the reader, the verse is the account of the turn and the lessons of the failure. The believer is taught: the promise was fulfilled — the *sadaq* — the victory at the start; the failures — the *fashal* (the lost courage), the *tanazu'* (the dispute), the *'isyan* (the disobedience) — the archers' mistake — the station abandoned for the booty; the *min ba'di ma arakum* — after the victory shown — the reach and the loss. The verse's *dunya* and *akhirah* — the world's gain and the Hereafter's reward — the two desires of the ranks: the booty-seekers and the station-holders — Ibn Jubayr and his companions holding till slain. And the *sarf* and the *'afa* — the turning away and the pardon — the test and the grace: the defeat tested the believers and the *fadl* — the bounty — pardoned the faithful. The believer's lesson: the victory is the divine (the *idhn* — the permission), the failure is the human (the *'isyan* — the disobedience), and the pardon is the divine (the *'afa* — the grace). And the believer's program: the *akhirah* over the *dunya* — the Hereafter's reward over the world's booty — the station held to the end — the *thabat* of Ibn Jubayr and the *sabr* of Anas bin An-Nadr.

## Verse 153

Verse 153 says: *˹Remember˺ when you were running far away ˹in panic˺—not looking at anyone—while the Messenger was calling to you from behind! So Allah rewarded your disobedience with distress upon distress. Now, do not grieve over the victory you were denied or the injury you suffered. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do.*

In simple terms, the verse recalls the flight: you were running away in panic without looking at anyone while the Messenger called you from behind; Allah repaid your disobedience with distress upon distress — so do not grieve over the victory denied or the injury suffered; Allah is All-Aware of what you do.

The verse is the rebuke and the lesson of the flight. Ibn Kathir: "when you ran away without even casting a side glance at anyone, and the Messenger was in your rear calling you back. There did Allah give you one distress after another by way of requital, to teach you not to grieve for that which had escaped you, nor for what struck you." The verse's *idh tus'iduna wa la talwuna 'ala ahad* — when you were going up (the mountain) and not turning to anyone — is the flight; the *wa al-Rasulu yad'ukum fi ukhrakum* — and the Messenger was calling you in your rear — is the call; the *fa'athabakum ghamman bi-ghammin* — so He rewarded you with distress upon distress — is the requital; and the *la ta'sa 'ala ma fatakum wa la ma asabakum* — do not grieve over what escaped you nor what struck you — is the lesson; and the *wa Allahu khabirun bima ta'malun* — and Allah is All-Aware of what you do — is the knowledge.

"when you were running far away ˹in panic˺—not looking at anyone" — *idh tus'iduna wa la talwuna 'ala ahad* — when you went up (the mountain) and did not turn to anyone. The *su'ud* — the going up — is the ascent: Ibn Kathir — Al-Hasan and Qatadah — "go up the mountain"; the *la talwuna* — not turning — the panic: "without even casting a side glance at anyone — you did not glance at anyone else due to shock, fear and fright." As-Suddi: some Muslims ran to Madinah, some went up Mount Uhud to a rock and stood on it. The verse's *tus'iduna* — you went up — is the flight of the panic: the ascent of the mountain, the running from the battle — the *la talwuna* — the not-turning — the panic's blindness.

"while the Messenger was calling to you from behind!" — *wa al-Rasulu yad'ukum fi ukhrakum* — and the Messenger was calling you in your rear. The *yad'u* — he was calling — is the call: the Messenger called from behind the fleeing — "to stop fleeing from the enemy and to return and fight" (Ibn Kathir). As-Suddi: "the Messenger of Allah kept heralding, 'Come to me, O servants of Allah! Come to me, O servants of Allah!'" The verse's *fi ukhrakum* — in your rear — is the position: the Messenger behind the fleeing — the *ukhra* — the rear — the call from behind — the believers fleeing and the Messenger calling — the contrast of the flight and the call.

"So Allah rewarded your disobedience with distress upon distress" — *fa'athabakum ghamman bi-ghammin* — so He requited you with distress upon distress. The *'adhab* — the requital, the reward — is the correspondence: the *ghamm* — the distress — upon the *ghamm* — the distress — the *ghamman bi-ghammin* — the distress upon distress — the requital of the disobedience. Ibn Kathir: "There did Allah give you one distress after another by way of requital." The verse's *'athabakum* — He requited you — is the divine: the disobedience — the flight, the abandon of the command — requited with the *ghamm* — the distress — the grief of the defeat, the grief of the loss — the *ghamm* upon the *ghamm* — the requital of the command's breach.

"Now, do not grieve over the victory you were denied or the injury you suffered" — *la ta'sawa 'ala ma fatakum wa la ma asabakum* — so that you do not grieve over what escaped you nor what struck you. The *ta'sawa* — grieve, be sad — the negative: the *fatakum* — what escaped you — the victory denied; the *asabakum* — what struck you — the injury suffered. Ibn Kathir: "to teach you not to grieve for that which had escaped you, nor for what struck you." The verse's *la ta'sawa* — do not grieve — is the lesson: the distress was the requital to teach the non-grief: the victory escaped (Badr's victory lost at the turn), the injury struck (the wounds, the seventy dead) — and the *ghamm* taught: do not grieve — the *ghamman bi-ghammin* — the requital — and the *la ta'sawa* — the lesson — the non-grief of the escaped and the struck.

"And Allah is All-Aware of what you do" — *wa Allahu khabirun bima ta'malun* — and Allah is All-Aware of what you do. The *khabir* — the All-Aware — is the knowledge: the *bima ta'malun* — of what you do — the deeds of the flight and the holding, the command's abandon and the station's defense — all known. The verse's *khabir* — the All-Aware — is the close: the requital's correspondence and the knowledge's comprehensiveness — the *ghamm* requited and the deeds known — the divine awareness of the flight and the call.

For the reader, the verse is the rebuke and the lesson of the flight. The believer is taught: the *su'ud* — the ascent — the panic's flight — the *la talwuna* — the not-turning — the shock and the fright; and the *yad'u* — the call — the Messenger calling from the rear — "Come to me, O servants of Allah!" — the flight and the call. And the *ghamman bi-ghammin* — the distress upon distress — the requital: the *'adhab* of the disobedience — the grief of the defeat — and the *la ta'sawa* — the non-grief — the lesson of the requital: the victory escaped and the injury struck — the *ghamm* taught the *ta'sa* — the grief's correction. The believer's lesson from Uhud: the flight of the panic and the call of the Messenger — the command's breach and the requital's distress — and the *la ta'sawa* — the non-grief — the trust of the *fata* and the *asaba* — the escaped and the struck — all in the *khabir* — the All-Aware — the divine knowledge of the deeds.

## Verse 154

Verse 154 says: *Then after distress, He sent down serenity in the form of drowsiness overcoming some of you, while others were disturbed by evil thoughts about Allah—the thoughts of ˹pre-Islamic˺ ignorance. They ask, “Do we have a say in the matter?” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “All matters are destined by Allah.” They conceal in their hearts what they do not reveal to you. They say ˹to themselves˺, “If we had any say in the matter, none of us would have come to die here.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Even if you were to remain in your homes, those among you who were destined to be killed would have met the same fate.” Through this, Allah tests what is within you and purifies what is in your hearts. The verse is the contrast of the two groups after the distress and the doctrine of the decree. Ibn Kathir: "Allah reminds His servants of His favor when He sent down on them tranquillity and slumber that overcame them while they were carrying their weapons and feeling distress and grief." The verse's *thumma anzala 'alaykum min ba'di al-ghammi amanatan nu'asan* — then He sent down upon you, after the distress, security as slumber — is the serenity; the *yaghsha ta'ifatan minkum* — overcoming a party of you — is the believers; the *wa ta'ifatun qad ahammathum anfusuhum* — and a party worried about themselves — is the hypocrites; the *yazunnuna billahi ghayra al-haqqi zanna al-jahiliyyah* — thinking of Allah other than the truth, the thought of ignorance — is the evil thought; and the *yaquluna hal lana mina al-amri min shay'* — saying, do we have any part in the matter — is the question; and the *qul inna al-amra kullahu lillah* — say, the matter belongs wholly to Allah — is the answer; and the *yu'addibuna...liyabtaliya* — to test and purify — is the purpose.

"Then after distress, He sent down serenity in the form of drowsiness overcoming some of you" — *thumma anzala 'alaykum min ba'di al-ghammi amanatan nu'asan yaghsha ta'ifatan minkum* — then He sent down upon you, after the distress, security — slumber covering a party of you. The *amana* — the security — is the *nu'as* — the slumber — the divine: the serenity of the slumber — the *aman* — the safety — after the *ghamm* — the distress. Ibn Kathir: "slumber is a favor and carries meanings of calmness and safety" (8:11 — "when He covered you with a slumber as a security from Him"). Al-Bukhari/Anas from Abu Talhah: "I was among those who were overcome by slumber during the battle of Uhud. My sword fell from my hand several times and I would pick it up, then it would fall and I would pick it up again"; at-Tirmidhi/An-Nasa'i/Al-Hakim — "everyone's head was nodding from slumber" (at-Tirmidhi: hasan sahih). Al-Jalalayn: the believers "would become dizzy under their shields and their swords would fall from their hands." The verse's *yaghsha* — covering — is the slumber's cover: the *amanah* — the security — the drowsiness — the calm of the believers — the *ta'ifah* — the party — the people of faith, certainty, firmness and reliance (Ibn Kathir) — certain that Allah shall give victory to His Messenger.

"while others were disturbed by evil thoughts about Allah—the thoughts of ˹pre-Islamic˺ ignorance" — *wa ta'ifatun qad ahammathum anfusuhum yazunnuna billahi ghayra al-haqqi zanna al-jahiliyyah* — and a party whose own souls distressed them, thinking of Allah other than the truth — the thought of ignorance. The *hamma* — the worrying — is the self-concern: the *ta'ifah* — the party — the hypocrites — "who only thought about themselves" — "the most cowardly people and those least likely to support the truth" (Ibn Kathir); the *yazunnuna billahi ghayra al-haqq* — the thinking — other than the truth — the *zann al-jahiliyyah* — the thought of ignorance: they thought the idolaters achieved ultimate victory and that Islam and its people would perish (48:12). The verse's *ahammathum anfusuhum* — their souls worried them — the self-absorbed: the distress of the hypocrites — the *zann* — the wrong thinking — of Allah — the doubt of the victory, the evil thought of the ignorance. Al-Jalalayn: "they caused them grief so that their only wish was their deliverance regardless of the Prophet and his Companions and they were unable to fall asleep — these were the hypocrites."

"They ask, 'Do we have a say in the matter?'" — *yaquluna hal lana mina al-amri min shay'* — they say, do we have any part in the matter? The *hal lana* — do we have — is the question of the *amr* — the matter: the part of the affair — the say of the matter. Al-Jalalayn: read interrogative *hal* as negative *ma* — "we have no part in the assistance which we were promised." Ibn Kathir: the hypocrites asked whether they had any part — the question of the matter's control. The verse's *mina al-amri* — of the matter — is the affair: the *amr* of the battle, the affair of the success — the hypocrites' question of the part.

The verse's *illah* — to Allah — is the ownership: the matter — the victory, the defeat, the decree — the whole belongs to the divine — the answer to the question of the part: no part of the matter is theirs — the *kulluhu* — the whole — is Allah's.

"They conceal in their hearts what they do not reveal to you" — *yukhfuna fi anfusihim ma la yubduna laka* — they conceal within themselves what they do not disclose to you. Al-Jalalayn: "they conceal within their hearts what they do not manifest to you" — the *zann* — the doubt, the wrong thought — hidden from the Prophet. "They say ˹to themselves˺, 'If we had any say in the matter, none of us would have come to die here.'" — *yaquluna law kana lana mina al-amri shay'un ma qutilna hahuna* — they say: had we any part in the matter, we would not have been killed here. Al-Jalalayn: "Had the choice been ours we would not have set out and thus been slain; but we were forced to set out." The verse's *qutilna hahuna* — killed here — is the grievance: the death at Uhud — the killed here — the mortal of the matter — the hypocrites' logic of the part: if they had the say they would not have been killed — the counterfactual of the decree's denial.

"Say, 'Even if you were to remain in your homes, those among you who were destined to be killed would have met the same fate.'" — *qul law kuntum fi buyutikum labaraza alladhina kutiba 'alayhimu al-qatlu ila madaji'ihim* — say: if you were in your houses, those for whom the killing was written would have emerged to their death beds. The *kutiba* — it was written — is the decree: the *qatl* — the killing — written — the appointed; the *madaji'* — the death beds, the resting places — the places of the fall. Al-Jalalayn: "those of you for whom it had been appointed that they be slain would have sallied forth to the places where they were to lie — the battleground where they were to fall — and they would have been slain; and their staying put at home would not have saved them, for God's decree will be inevitably." The verse's *labaraza* — they would have emerged — is the inevitability: the written killing emerges to its place — the homes do not save — the *madaji'* — the death beds — the appointed — the decree's inevitability.

"Through this, Allah tests what is within you and purifies what is in your hearts" — *wa liyabtaliya Allahu ma fi sudurikum wa liyumahhisa ma fi qulubikum* — and so that Allah might test what is in your breasts and purify what is in your hearts. The *ibtilah* — the testing — is the *sudur* — the breasts: the *ma fi sudurikum* — what is in your breasts — of sincerity or hypocrisy; the *tamhis* — the purification — is the *qulub* — the hearts: the *ma fi qulubikum* — what is in your hearts. Al-Jalalayn: "that God might try that He might test what was in your breasts — your hearts — of sincerity or hypocrisy — and that He might prove that He might distinguish what was in your hearts." The verse's *liyabtaliya* — to test — and the *liyumahhisa* — to purify — are the purposes: the test of the breasts and the purification of the hearts — the *zann* of the hypocrites and the *iman* of the believers — known and purified.

"And Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart" — *wa Allahu 'alimun bi dhati al-sudur* — and Allah is Knowing of what is in the breasts. The *'alim* — the Knowing — is the divine: the *dhat al-sudur* — the contents of the breasts — known. Al-Jalalayn: "God knows what is in the breasts — what is in the hearts. Nothing can be hidden from Him, and He tries people only to make matters manifest for them." The verse's *'alimun bi dhati al-sudur* — the Knowing of the breasts' contents — is the close: the hidden thoughts — the concealed *zann* — known to the divine; the test and the purification — the manifestation of the known.

For the reader, the verse is the contrast of the two groups and the doctrine of the decree. The believer is taught: the *amana* — the security — the slumber — the divine over the distressed: the believers' calm — the swords falling, the heads nodding — the *ta'ifah* of the faith; and the *zann* — the evil thought — of the hypocrites: the doubt of the victory, the self-absorption, the *zann al-jahiliyyah* — the thought of ignorance. The verse's *kullahu lillah* — the whole belongs to Allah — is the doctrine: the matter — all of it — the divine's; the *kutiba* — the written — the decree: the killing written, the death beds appointed — the homes do not save — the decree inevitable. And the *liyabtaliya* and the *liyumahhisa* — the test and the purification — the purposes: the breasts tested, the hearts purified — the *zann* and the *iman* — manifest; the *'alim bi dhat al-sudur* — the Knowing of the breasts' contents — the divine knowledge of the hidden. The believer's confidence: the *kulluhu lillah* — the matter wholly Allah's — the decree's inevitability — the *Qadar* of the divine; and the *liyumahhisa* — the purification — the *tamhis* of the faithful — the test manifesting and purifying.

## Verse 155

Verse 155 says: *Indeed, those ˹believers˺ who fled on the day when the two armies met were made to slip by Satan because of their misdeeds. But Allah has pardoned them. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.*

In simple terms, the verse gives the verdict on those who fled: they were made to slip by Satan because of their misdeeds — but Allah has pardoned them; He is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.

The verse is the pardon of the fleeing believers. Ibn Kathir: "Those of you who turned back on the day the two hosts met, Shaytan only caused them to err because of some of what they had earned. But Allah, indeed, has forgiven them. Surely, Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Forbearing." The verse's *inna alladhina tawallaw minkum yawma iltaqa al-jam'an* — those of you who turned back on the day the two hosts met — is the fugitives; the *innama istazallahumu al-shaytanu bi-ba'di ma kasabu* — Shaytan only caused them to slip because of some of what they earned — is the cause; the *wa laqad 'afa Allahu 'anhum* — and Allah has certainly pardoned them — is the pardon; and the *inna Allaha ghafurun halim* — and Allah is Forgiving, Forbearing — is the names.

"those ˹believers˺ who fled on the day when the two armies met" — *inna alladhina tawallaw minkum yawma iltaqa al-jam'an* — those of you who turned back on the day the two hosts met. The *tawalli* — the turning back, the fleeing — is the retreat: the *yawm iltaqa al-jam'an* — the day the two hosts met — Uhud — the two hosts of the Muslims and the disbelievers. Al-Jalalayn: "the Muslims with the exception of twelve men" — the turners — the fugitives of the day. The verse's *minkum* — of you — is the address: the turners — the believers of the day — the *tawallaw* — the ones who turned — the day of the meeting of the two hosts.

"were made to slip by Satan because of their misdeeds" — *innama istazallahumu al-shaytanu bi-ba'di ma kasabu* — only Shaytan caused them to slip because of some of what they earned. The *istizlal* — the causing to slip — is the Satanic: the *shaytan* — the tempter — the *istazallahum* — caused them to slip — the *ba'di ma kasabu* — some of what they earned — the misdeeds: the disobedience of the Prophet's command. Al-Jalalayn: "through some of what they had earned of sins — namely when they disobeyed the Prophet's command." The verse's *innama* — only — is the limitation: the slip — the *istizlal* — the Satanic — through their earning: the *kasb* — the earning — the disobedience — the cause of the slip — the *shaytan* used the earned misdeeds to trip them.

"But Allah has pardoned them" — *wa laqad 'afa Allahu 'anhum* — and Allah has certainly pardoned them. The *'afw* — the pardon — is the divine: the *'afa 'anhum* — He pardoned them — the fugitives of the day — the *'afw* of the slip. The verse's *laqad* — certainly — is the emphasis: the pardon is the certain — the *'afa* — the divine over the *tawalli* — the slip forgiven. Ibn Kathir: "But Allah, indeed, has forgiven them" — the pardon of the turners — the believers' flight — the *'afw* of the divine.

"Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing" — *inna Allaha ghafurun halim* — indeed, Allah is Forgiving, Forbearing. The *Ghafur* — the All-Forgiving — and the *Halim* — the Forbearing — are the names: the forgiveness of the slip and the forbearance of the punishment — the *hilm* — the restraint — "hastening not against the disobedient with punishment" (Al-Jalalayn). The verse's *Ghafur* and *Halim* — the forgiving and the forbearing — are the divine: the *'afw* of the fugitives — the *hilm* — the forbearance — the reward after the slip — the names of the pardon.

The verse's place: the pardon of the fugitives of Uhud — the *tawallaw* — the ones who turned — were the *istazallahum* — the slipped — by the *shaytan* — through the *kasb* — the misdeeds — and the *'afa* — the pardon — the *Ghafur* and the *Halim* — the divine. Ibn Kathir's section: "Slumber Overcame the Believers; the Fear that the Hypocrites Suffered" — the *ta'ifah* of the faith (the slumber) and the *ta'ifah* of the hypocrites (the fear) — and 155: the *tawallaw* — the fugitives — the *istazallahum* — the slipped — the *'afa* — the pardoned — the verse's mercy to the slipping believers.

For the reader, the verse is the mercy of the slip and the doctrine of the pardon. The believer is taught: the *tawallaw* — the ones who turned — the day of the two hosts — were *istazallahum* — caused to slip — by the *shaytan* — the tempter — through *ba'di ma kasabu* — some of what they earned — the misdeeds of the disobedience; and the *'afa* — the pardon — the divine: the *'afw* of the slip — the *Ghafur* — the All-Forgiving — the *Halim* — the Forbearing — the names of the pardon. The verse's *innama* — only — is the limitation: the slip was the Satanic through the earning — the *kasb* — the misdeeds — the cause; and the *laqad 'afa* — certainly pardoned — is the emphasis: the pardon is the certain. The believer's confidence: the flight of the believers at Uhud — the *tawalli* — was the *istizlal* — the Satanic slip — and the *'afw* — the pardon — the divine mercy: the *Ghafur* — the forgiveness — and the *Halim* — the forbearance — the mercy of the believers' failure.
