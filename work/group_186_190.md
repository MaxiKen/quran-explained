## Verse 186

Verse 186 says: *You ˹believers˺ will surely be tested in your wealth and yourselves, and you will certainly hear many hurtful words from those who were given the Scripture before you and ˹from˺ the polytheists. But if you are patient and mindful ˹of Allah˺—surely this is a resolve to aspire to.*

In simple terms, the verse foretells the test: you will surely be tried in your wealth and selves, and will hear much hurtful speech from the People of the Book and the polytheists — but if you endure patiently and fear Allah, that is true resolve.

The verse's *'azm* — the resolve — is the steadfast: the *sabr* — the patience — and the *taqwa* — the fear — the *'azm* — the determination — the *umur* — the affairs — the resolve — to aspire to.

## Verse 187

Verse 187 says: *˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when Allah took the covenant of those who were given the Scripture to make it known to people and not hide it, yet they cast it behind their backs and traded it for a fleeting gain. What a miserable profit!*

In simple terms, the verse censures the concealment: Allah took the covenant of the People of the Book to make it known and not hide it, but they cast it behind their backs and traded it for a small price — what a miserable profit!

The verse is the censure of the covenant-breaking and the concealment. Ibn Kathir: "Allah chastises the People of the Scriptures, from whom Allah took the covenant by the words of their Prophets, that they would believe in Muhammad and describe him to the people, so that they would recognize and follow him when Allah sent him. However, they hid this truth and preferred the small amounts and the material gains instead of the rewards of this life and the Hereafter that they were promised. This is a losing deal and a failing trade, indeed." The verse's *wa idh akhadha Allahu mithaqa alladhina utu al-kitaba latubayyinunnahu lil-nasi wala taktumunahu* — and when Allah took the covenant of those given the Scripture: you shall make it clear to the people and not conceal it — is the covenant; the *fanabadhuhu wara'a zuhurihim* — but they threw it behind their backs — is the casting; the *washtaraw bihi thamanan qalilan* — and bought with it a small price — is the trade; and the *fab'isa ma yashtarun* — and evil is what they buy — is the condemnation.

"when Allah took the covenant of those who were given the Scripture to make it known to people and not hide it" — *wa idh akhadha Allahu mithaqa alladhina utu al-kitaba latubayyinunnahu lil-nasi wala taktumunahu* — and when Allah took the covenant of those given the Scripture: you shall make it clear to the people and not conceal it. The *mithaq* — the covenant, the solemn pledge — is the divine: the *akhadha* — He took — the *mithaq* — the covenant — the *latubayyinunnahu* — you shall clarify it — the *lil-nas* — to the people — the *wala taktumunahu* — and not conceal it — the covenant of the disclosure. Ibn Kathir: the covenant by the words of their prophets that they would believe in Muhammad and describe him. Al-Jalalayn: "the pledge taken from them in the Torah — 'You shall expound it (the Book) to people and not conceal it'" — with variant readings (tubayyinunnahu/yubayyinunnahu; taktumunahu/yaktumunahu). The verse's *latubayyinunnahu* — you shall clarify — is the obligation: the *tabyin* — the explication — the *lil-nas* — to the people — the *kutm* — the concealment — negated — the *mithaq* — the covenant — of the disclosure.

"yet they cast it behind their backs" — *fanabadhuhu wara'a zuhurihim* — then they threw it behind their backs. The *nabdh* — the casting, the throwing — is the rejection: the *nabadhuhu* — they cast it — the *wara'a zuhurihim* — behind their backs — the *mithaq* — the covenant — discarded. Ibn Kathir: "they threw it away behind their backs." Al-Jalalayn: "they rejected it — they discarded the covenant behind their backs and so they did not act in accordance with it." The verse's *wara'a zuhurihim* — behind their backs — is the picture: the *nabdh* — the throw — the *zuhur* — the backs — the *wara'* — behind — the casting — of the covenant — the concealment — the *mithaq* — set aside.

"and traded it for a fleeting gain" — *washtaraw bihi thamanan qalilan* — and bought with it a small price. The *shira'* — the trading, the buying — is the exchange: the *ishtaraw* — they bought — the *bihi* — with it — the *thaman qalil* — a small price — the *mithaq* — the covenant — sold. Al-Jalalayn: "they took in its place a small price of this world from the debased among them, enjoying supremacy over them in knowledge and they concealed it lest the supremacy escape them." The verse's *thaman qalil* — a small price — is the worth: the *shira'* — the purchase — the *qalil* — the little — the *thaman* — the price — the *dunya* — the world's — the small gain — the *mithaq* — the covenant — traded.

"What a miserable profit!" — *fab'isa ma yashtarun* — and evil is what they buy. The *bi'sa* — how evil — is the condemnation: the *yashtarun* — they buy — the *bi'sa* — the evil — of the purchase — the *shira'* — the trade — condemned. Ibn Kathir: "This is a losing deal and a failing trade, indeed." Al-Jalalayn: "how evil is this purchase of theirs!" The verse's *bi'sa* — how evil — is the verdict: the *ma yashtarun* — what they buy — the *thaman qalil* — the small price — the *bi'sa* — the evil — of the trade.

The verse's context: the covenant-breaking (187) and the warning to the scholars — the *mithaq* — the covenant — the *tabyin* — the disclosure — the *kutman* — the concealment — the *nabdh* — the casting — the *shira'* — the trade — the *bi'sa* — the condemnation. ## Verse 188

Verse 188 says: *Do not let those who rejoice in their misdeeds and love to take credit for what they have not done think they will escape torment. They will suffer a painful punishment.*

In simple terms, the verse warns the pretenders: do not think those who rejoice in their misdeeds and love to be praised for what they did not do will escape torment — for them is a painful punishment.

The verse is the warning against the self-praise and the claim. Ibn Kathir: "Think not that those who rejoice in what they have done, and love to be praised for what they have not done" — "refers to those who show off, rejoice in what they do and claim to do what they have not done." Al-Jalalayn: "those who rejoice in what they have brought — in what they have done by leading people astray — and who love to be praised for what they have not done in the way of adherence to the truth, being themselves misguided." The verse's *la tahsabanna alladhina yafrahuna bima ataw wa yuhibbuna an yuhmadu bima lam yaf'alu* — do not think those who rejoice in what they brought and love to be praised for what they did not do — is the pretenders; the *fala tahsabannahum bimafazatin mina al-'adhab* — do not think them in safety from the punishment — is the negation; and the *wa lahum 'adhabun alim* — and for them is a painful punishment — is the punishment.

"Do not let those who rejoice in their misdeeds and love to take credit for what they have not done think they will escape torment" — *la tahsabanna alladhina yafrahuna bima ataw wa yuhibbuna an yuhmadu bima lam yaf'alu fala tahsabannahum bimafazatin mina al-'adhab* — do not think those who rejoice in what they brought, and love to be praised for what they did not do — do not think them in safety from the punishment. The *farah* — the rejoicing — is the first: the *yafrahun* — they rejoice — the *bima ataw* — in what they brought — the deeds of the misdeeds; the *hubb* — the love — is the second: the *yuhibbun* — they love — the *an yuhmadu* — to be praised — the *bima lam yaf'alu* — for what they did not do — the claim; the *hasb* — the thinking — is the negation: the *la tahsabannahum* — do not think them — the *bimafazah* — in safety, in escape — the *mina al-'adhab* — from the punishment — the negation of the escape. Ibn Kathir: the Two Sahihs — "Whoever issues a false claim to acquire some type of gain, then Allah will only grant him decrease" and "He who claims to do what he has not done is just like a person who wears two robes made of falsehood." The verse's *bimafazah* — in safety — is the negation: the *fawz* — the escape, the safety — negated — the *'adhab* — the punishment — the *la* — the not — the escape — denied.

"They will suffer a painful punishment" — *wa lahum 'adhabun alim* — and for them is a painful punishment. The verse's *alim* — the painful — is the quality: the *'adhab* — the punishment — the *alim* — the painful — the *farah* — the rejoicing — and the *hubb* — the love — the *'adhab* — the recompense — of the pretenders.

Ibn Kathir: Imam Ahmad recorded that Marwan told his guard Rafi' to go to Ibn 'Abbas and proclaim — "If every person among us who rejoices with what he has done and loves to be praised for what he has not done will be tormented, we all will be tormented" — Ibn 'Abbas said, "This Ayah was revealed about the People of the Scriptures" — then recited 187. The verse's *bima ataw* — what they did — and the *bima lam yaf'al* — what they did not — the rejoicing and the claim — the *'adhab alim* — the painful — the warning.

For the reader, the verse is the warning against the self-praise and the doctrine of the claim.
