## Verse 221

Verse 221 says: *"Do not marry polytheistic women until they believe; for a believing slave-woman is better than a free polytheist, even though she may look pleasant to you. And do not marry your women to polytheistic men until they believe, for a believing slave-man is better than a free polytheist, even though he may look pleasant to you. They invite ˹you˺ to the Fire while Allah invites ˹you˺ to Paradise and forgiveness by His grace. He makes His revelations clear to the people so perhaps they will be mindful."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on marriage with the polytheists: believers may not marry idolatrous women, nor give their daughters to idolatrous men, until the idolaters believe; the faith of a believing servant outweighs the status and charm of a free idolater; the idolaters call towards the Fire while Allah calls towards Paradise and forgiveness.

The verse's first command: the prohibition of marrying the polytheist (the mushrik) women until they believe. Ibn Kathir: Allah forbade the marriage of the idolatresses until they believe — and notes that this ruling excludes the women of the People of the Scripture, who are permitted by the later verse (5:5) — the explanation of Mujahid, 'Ikrimah, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Makhul, al-Hasan, ad-Dahhak, Zayd ibn Aslam and ar-Rabi' ibn Anas and others. The verse's *mushrikat* — the polytheist women, the idolatresses — the marriage is barred until the faith: the marriage is not only the union of the bodies; the marriage is the union of the worships, the creed, the path. The verse's second: the marriage of the believing women to the polytheist men — do not give your daughters to the mushrik men until they believe. The prohibition is symmetrical: the wife's faith and the husband's faith — the marriage of the believer and the idolater is the contradiction of the worship.

"A believing slave-woman is better than a free polytheist, even though she may look pleasant to you" — the verse's scale: not the beauty, not the lineage, not the freedom — the faith. A believing slave-woman is better than a free polytheist though the polytheist pleases you, Ibn Kathir: this is the scale of the marriage: the faith outweighs the beauty, the wealth, the lineage. The verse's context: the companions at the beginning — some years Muslims were still looking forward to marry the idolatresses and marry their daughters to the idolaters out of the desire for their noble lineages; and the story of the believer who married a slave-woman and was reproached — and the verse was revealed about them. The verse's point: the pleasure of the appearance is not the standard; the standard is the faith, and the believing slave-woman is better than the free polytheist.

"They invite you to the Fire while Allah invites you to Paradise and forgiveness by His grace" — the verse's comparison: the spouses are invitations. The polytheist spouse invites to the Fire — the way of life, the creed, the worship, the house of the disbelief; and Allah invites to the Paradise and the forgiveness by His grace. Ibn Kathir quotes the hadith (in the Two Sahihs, from Abu Hurayrah): the life of this world is but a delight, and the best of the delights of this earthly life is the righteous wife. The verse's frame: the marriage is the invitation, and the believer chooses the invitation of the Paradise over the invitation of the Fire.

"In modern life" — the verse's message: the marriage is the companionship of the faith. The modern person is tempted by the appearance, the wealth, the charm, the status — and the verse says: the faith is the first measure; the believing partner, even of the humbler status, is better than the attractive non-believer — because the marriage is the invitation: the spouses invite each other to the path, the values, the worship, the life. The believer's marriage is the companion of the hereafter: not only the beauty of the wedding, but the invitation of the path. The verse's close: the revelations are clear so the people may be mindful: the choice of the spouse is the choice of the invitation — and the believer chooses the invitation of the Paradise by His grace.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 222

Verse 222 says: *"They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about menstruation. Say, "Beware of its harm! So keep away, and do not have intercourse with your wives during their monthly cycles until they are purified. When they purify themselves, then you may approach them in the manner specified by Allah. Surely Allah loves those who always turn to Him in repentance and those who purify themselves.""*

In simple terms, the verse answers the question of menstruation: it is a state of harm; husbands must abstain from intercourse during the monthly cycle, may resume when the wife is purified, and Allah loves the repentant and the purifiers.

"They ask you, O Prophet, about menstruation" — the verse's context: the companions asked about the monthly cycles, and the answer came. Ibn Kathir: Imam Ahmad recorded that Anas said the Jews used to avoid their menstruating women — they would not eat or even mingle with them in the house — and the companions asked about this matter and Allah revealed the verse. The verse's *mahid* — menstruation, the monthly cycle — and the answer: the *adha* — the harm, the ailment, the state of hurt — the verse's description of the menses: it is the harm, the harm for the marital intercourse.

"Beware of its harm! So keep away, and do not have intercourse with your wives during their monthly cycles until they are purified" — the verse's *fa'tazilu al-nisa'* — keep away from the women during the menses — the verse's command: the abstinence from the sexual intercourse during the cycle. Ibn Kathir: "keep away from women during menses" means avoid the sexual organ; the Prophet said, "Do everything you wish, except having sexual intercourse" (Muslim) — and this is why most of the scholars said it is allowed to fondle the wife except for the intercourse (during the menses). Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah reported from 'Abdullah ibn Sa'd al-Ansari: he asked the Prophet, "What am I allowed of my wife while she is having her menses?" — "What is above the Izar." The verse's *hatta yat-hurna* — until they are purified — the end of the cycle; and *fa-idha tat-hahrana* — when they have purified — the bath; the purification is the end of the menses and the bath — and then the approach.

"Then you may approach them in the manner specified by Allah" — the verse's *min haythu amarakum Allah* — from where Allah has commanded you: the approach is in the manner of the lawful — the verse: the normal marital relations, in the place of the lawful, not the forbidden. Ibn Kathir: Abu Razin, 'Ikrimah and ad-Dahhak and others said "when they are pure, and not during the menses." And the verse's close: "Surely Allah loves those who always turn to Him in repentance and those who purify themselves" — the verse's *tawwabin* — the constantly repentant; *muttahhirin* — the purifiers — the love of Allah for the repentant even for the repeated sin, and for the purified from the impurity. Ibn Kathir: those who purify themselves from the impurity and the filth that include having sexual intercourse with the wife during the menses and the anal sex.

In modern life, the verse is the divine law of the marital gentleness. The modern person treats the body as the object of the convenience, and the verse says: the marriage has the divine limits; the monthly cycle has the divine command — the abstinence in the period of the harm, the purification, the return. The verse's mercy: the Jews of the time avoided the woman completely — no eating, no mingling — and the verse gives the middle: the companionship continues, the love continues, only the intercourse is withheld; "do everything you wish, except the intercourse." The verse's message: the marital law is not the law of the harshness or the taboo; it is the law of the divine purity — the repentance and the purification — and the Lord loves the repentant and the purifiers.

## Verse 223

Verse 223 says: *"Your wives are like farmland for you, so approach them ˹consensually˺ as you please. And send forth something good for yourselves. Be mindful of Allah, and know that you will meet Him. And give good news to the believers."*

In simple terms, the verse gives the law of the marital approach: the wives are the tillage of the husbands, who may approach in the lawful manner they wish; the believers are to send ahead good deeds, be mindful of Allah, and receive the good news.

"Your wives are like farmland for you" — the verse's *harth* — the farmland, the tillage, the field — the image of the marriage: the wife is the field, the husband is the one who approaches the field — the marital relation as the sowing: the marriage is not the play; the marriage is the field — the seed, the harvest, the offspring. The verse's frame: the marriage's purpose — the lawful pleasure within the divine limits, and the harvest of the offspring.

"So approach them consensually as you please" — the verse's *fa'tu harthakum anna shi'tum* — so go to your tillage as you will, in the manner you wish. The verse's point: the approach is permitted in the usual manner — the front, in the lawful place; Ibn Kathir (from Ibn Jurayj's report): the Prophet said "in the front or the back" — and the commentators: the approach is in the law's limits — the front (the vaginal), not the forbidden (the anal, the menses) — per the understanding of Ibn Abbas (in Tanwir al-Miqbas: "fear Allah regarding penetrating your wives in their anus or having sex with them during menstruation"). The verse's *anna shi'tum* — as you will — the manner of the lawful: the freedom within the divine bounds.

"And send forth something good for yourselves" — the verse's *qaddimu li-anfusikum* — send forward something for yourselves: the good deed — the children (the righteous offspring), the kindness, the remembrance — the marriage's fare for the hereafter. Ibn Kathir (Tanwir): send forth good — righteous children. The verse's frame: the marriage is not only the present; the marriage is the future — the soul's provision: the righteous child, the mutual kindness, the obedience — the send-forth.

"Be mindful of Allah, and know that you will meet Him. And give good news to the believers" — the verse's close: the taqwa and the meeting — the marriage is under the mindfulness, and the meeting with the Lord is the horizon; and the good news to the believers — the glad tidings of the Paradise for the ones who observe the limits. Ibn Kathir (with Ibn Abbas, Tanwir): the good news — the believers who avoid the anal and the menses — theirs is the Paradise.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the marital tenderness and the marital purpose. The modern marriage is often the law of the individual pleasure and the power; and the verse's image: the wife is the field — the partnership of the sowing and the harvest, not the object of the consumption; and the husband's approach is the lawful tenderness, within the limits, in the consensuality. The verse's message: the marriage is the field: the kind approach, the good send-forth — the children, the kindness, the faith — the marriage's harvest is the soul's provision. And the close: the mindfulness and the meeting — the believers' marriage is the marriage under the Lord, and the good news of the believers.

## Verse 224

Verse 224 says: *"Do not use Allah's Name in your oaths as an excuse for not doing good, not guarding against evil, or not making peace between people. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the oaths: Allah's Name must not be turned into a pretext for avoiding the good, the guarding against evil, or the reconciliation of people — for Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

"Use Allah's Name in your oaths as an excuse" — the verse's *la taj'alu Allah 'urdatan li-aymanikum* — do not make Allah a hindrance, an excuse, an object of your oaths: the oath by Allah turned into the barrier — "I swore by Allah, so I will not do it." Ibn Kathir: Allah commands — do not implement your vows in Allah's Name to refrain from pious acts and severing the relations with the relatives, if you swear to abandon such causes. Ibn Abbas (via 'Ali ibn Abi Talhah): the meaning — do not vow to refrain from doing good works; pay the kaffarah and perform the better deed. The verse's *'urda* — the shield, the pretext, the obstacle: the oath becomes the shield against the good — "I have sworn" as the excuse for the neglect of the good.

"Doing good, not guarding against evil, or not making peace between people" — the verse's three: the *birr* — the good, the piety, the kindness; the *taqwa* — the guarding, the fear; the *islah* — the reconciliation of the people. The verse's context: Tanwir al-Miqbas — the verse was revealed about 'Abdullah ibn Rawahah who swore not to show kindness to his sister and her husband, not to speak with them or make peace between them — and Allah warned him: do not make Allah the reason. The verse's sin: the oath against the good, the oath against the reconciliation, the oath against the peace — the oath is not the license of the harm; the oath is the breach of the divine command.

The verse's remedy: the oath that blocks the good is broken — the kaffarah and the better deed. Ibn Kathir: the hadith (Muslim, Ahmad): "Whoever makes a vow and then finds what is better than his vow should break his vow, pay the kaffarah and perform the better deed"; and "It is more sinful to Allah that one of you implements his vow regarding (severing the relations with) his relatives than (breaking his promise and) paying the kaffarah that Allah has required in such cases." And the hadith (Abu Dawud): "No vow (is valid) to disobey the Lord, cut the relations of the womb or dispose of what you do not own."

"And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing" — the verse's close: the Heard and the Knowing — the oath heard by the Lord; the intention known by the Lord. The verse's frame: the oath is not the escape; the Lord heard the oath and knows the motive — and the oath against the good is the oath against the command of the One who hears and knows.

In modern life, the verse is the warning of the oath-as-excuse. The modern person makes the promises, the vows, the "I swear" — and the verse says: the oath is not the shield of the neglect. The modern forms: "I swore I would never speak to him" — and the reconciliation is blocked; "I promised myself I would never give" — and the charity is blocked; "I gave my word" — and the good is abandoned. The verse's message: the oath cannot be the excuse for the harm; the good, the taqwa, the reconciliation are above the oath; and the oath that blocks the good is broken — with the kaffarah and the better deed. The believer's oath is the servant of the good, not the barrier: the Lord who hears the oath and knows the intention is the One who accepts the better deed.

## Verse 225

Verse 225 says: *"Allah will not hold you accountable for unintentional oaths, but for what you intended in your hearts. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing."*

In simple terms, the verse distinguishes the oaths: Allah will not hold the believers for the idle and unintentional oaths, but will hold them for the oaths deliberately intended in their hearts — He is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.

"Allah will not hold you accountable for unintentional oaths" — the verse's *laghw* — the unintentional, the inadvertent, the idle: the oaths of the tongue's habit, the careless utterance, the "No, by Allah" and "Yes, by Allah" of the everyday talk. Ibn Kathir: Allah does not punish or hold you accountable for the laghw vows that you make — the unintentional vows are just like the habitual statements that the tongue repeats without really intending them — for instance (in the Two Sahihs, from Abu Hurayrah): whoever swore and unintentionally mentioned al-Lat and al-'Uzza in his vow, should then say "There is no deity worthy of worship except Allah"; and the laghw includes what the man says in his house, such as "No, by Allah" and "Yes, by Allah"; Ibn Abi Hatim from Ibn Abbas — the laghw vow includes vowing while angry.

"But for what you intended in your hearts" — the verse's *wa lakin yu'akhidhukum bima kasabat qulubukum* — but He will hold you accountable for what your hearts have earned: the deliberate oath. Ibn Kathir: Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and several others said this means swearing about a matter while knowing that he is lying — the deliberate lie; and the parallel of 5:89 — "He will punish you for your deliberate oaths." The verse's line: the unintentional is forgiven; the intended is accounted. The verse's *kasaba* — earned — the heart's earning: the intention, the deliberation, the knowing falsehood — the heart earns the accountability.

The verse's *kasabat qulubukum* — the hearts have earned — the verse's principle: the accountability follows the intention. Ibn Kathir (with 2:286): although Allah will question His servants and judge them, He will only punish for what one is able to protect himself from; as for what one cannot protect himself from — the passing thoughts, the inner suggestions — they will not be punished; to dislike the evil thoughts that cross one's mind is part of the faith.

"And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing" — the verse's close: the *Ghafur* — the All-Forgiving — the forgiveness of the laghw, the unintentional, the careless; the *Halim* — the Most Forbearing, the Clement — the forbearance toward the servants who slip. Ibn Kathir: He is Oft-Forgiving to His servants and Most Forbearing. The verse's frame: the accidental oath is forgiven; the intended is judged; and the Lord's character is the forgiveness and the forbearance — the believer trusts the mercy of the Lord of the oaths.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the tongue and the heart. The modern person speaks: the swear words, the casual oaths, the "I swear to God" of the routine — and the verse says: the Lord does not hold you for the careless tongue, but He holds you for the heart's intent — the deliberate oath, the knowing lie, the heart consumed. The verse's mercy: the everyday slips of the tongue are under the Lord's forbearance; the verse's seriousness: the deliberate oath is under the Lord's accountability. The believer's discipline: the tongue is guarded, the oath is rare, and the heart is pure; and the believer's trust: the All-Forgiving, the Most Forbearing — the Lord who forgives the careless and accounts the intended, and who is the mercy of the repentant.

===VERSE-END===
