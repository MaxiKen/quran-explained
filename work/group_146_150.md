## Verse 146

Verse 146 says: *˹Imagine˺ how many devotees fought along with their prophets and never faltered despite whatever ˹losses˺ they suffered in the cause of Allah, nor did they weaken or give in! Allah loves those who persevere.*

In simple terms, the verse holds up the example of the past: how many devotees fought alongside their prophets and never faltered whatever losses they suffered in Allah's cause — they did not weaken or give in — and Allah loves those who persevere.

The verse is the comfort of the example: the losses of Uhud are the patterns of the past. Ibn Kathir: "And many a Prophet fought and along with him many Ribbiyyun. But they never lost heart for that which befell them in Allah's way, nor did they weaken nor degrade themselves. And Allah loves the patient." The verse's *ka'ayyin min nabiyyin qatala ma'ahu ribbiyyuna kathir* — how many a Prophet fought, with him many Ribbiyyun — is the example; the *fama wahana lima asabahum fi sabil Allah* — and they did not weaken for what befell them in the way of Allah — is the firmness; the *wa ma da'ufu wa ma istakanu* — nor did they weaken or degrade themselves — is the endurance; and the *wa Allahu yuhibbu al-sabirin* — and Allah loves the patient — is the love.

"how many devotees fought along with their prophets" — *ka'ayyin min nabiyyin qatala ma'ahu ribbiyyuna kathir* — how many a Prophet fought, with him many Ribbiyyun. The *ribbiyyun* — the devotees, the lordly ones, the large bands — is the key word. Ibn Kathir: Ibn Jarir — many Prophets and their companions were killed in earlier times; Ibn Ishaq (in his Sirah; defended by As-Suhayli) — many a Prophet was killed and had many companions whose resolve did not weaken after their Prophet died, not feeble before the enemy, not losing heart at what they suffered in Jihad — "This is patience, and Allah loves the patient"; Ibn Mas'ud (Ath-Thawri) — Ribbiyyun means thousands; Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, 'Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Ar-Rabi', 'Ata' Al-Khurasani — large bands; Al-Hasan (Ma'mmar) — many scholars, patient and pious scholars. The verse's *ma'ahu* — with him — is the accompaniment: the devotees fought with their prophets — the *ribbiyyun* — the large bands — the companions of the prophets, the scholars, the devotees — the followers who fought alongside the prophets.

"and never faltered despite whatever ˹losses˺ they suffered in the cause of Allah" — *fama wahana lima asabahum fi sabil Allahi* — and they did not lose heart for what befell them in the way of Allah. The *wahn* — the weakness, the faltering — is the negative: they did not weaken; the *asabahum* — what befell them — is the losses: the deaths of the prophets, the wounds, the defeats; the *fi sabil Allah* — in the way of Allah — is the cause. Ibn Kathir: the *ribbiyyun* of the past "never lost heart for that which befell them in Allah's way" — the deaths of the prophets, the losses of the cause — the *wahn* — the weakening — was not theirs. The verse's *mā asabahum* — what befell them — is the test: the losses of the cause — the killing of the prophets — the *sabil Allah* — the way — the cause.

"nor did they weaken or give in!" — *wa ma da'ufu wa ma istakanu* — nor did they weaken nor degrade themselves. The *du'f* — the weakness — and the *istikana* — the submission, the humiliation, the giving in — are the two negatives: they did not weaken and did not give in. Ibn Kathir: Qatadah and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas — "nor did they weaken" — after their Prophet was killed; "nor did they degrade themselves" — by reverting from the true guidance and religion; they fought on the path that Allah's Prophet fought on until they met Allah; Ibn 'Abbas — nor became humiliated; As-Suddi and Ibn Zayd — did not give in to the enemy. The verse's *istakanu* — the giving in — is the submission: the *ribbiyyun* did not give in — not to the enemy, not to the despair, not to the retreat.

"Allah loves those who persevere" — *wa Allahu yuhibbu al-sabirin* — and Allah loves the patient. The *hubb* — the love — is the divine: the *sabirin* — the patient — the loving; the *sabr* — the patience — the perseverance — the quality of the *ribbiyyun* of the past. The verse's *yuhibbu* — He loves — is the verdict: the patient — the ones who did not weaken and did not give in — are the loved; the *sabr* — the perseverance — the firmness of the cause.

The verse's application to Uhud: the losses of the believers at Uhud — the seventy dead, the flight, the wound of the Prophet — are the patterns of the past: the *ribbiyyun* of the earlier prophets suffered the losses and did not weaken — the deaths of the prophets, the defeats, the wounds — and the believer of Uhud is taught: the patience of the past is the pattern; the losses are the *asabahum* of the cause. Ibn Kathir: the *ribbiyyun* "did not lose heart... and Allah loves the patient."

For the reader, the verse is the example of the past and the doctrine of the patience. The believer is taught: the *ribbiyyun* — the devotees — fought with the prophets — the large bands, the scholars, the followers; and the *wahn* — the weakening — and the *istikana* — the giving in — were not theirs: the deaths of the prophets and the losses of the cause met with the patience. The verse's *yuhibbu al-sabirin* — the love of the patient — is the verdict: the perseverance — the *sabr* of the cause — is the loved quality. The believer's lesson from Uhud: the losses are the patterns — the *asabahum fi sabil Allah* — what befell them in the way; the *wahn* and the *du'f* and the *istikana* — the weakening and the giving in — are the negatives of the *ribbiyyun*; and the *sabr* — the perseverance — is the quality of the pattern — the patience of the past and the love of the divine.

## Verse 147

Verse 147 says: *And all they said was, “Our Lord! Forgive our sins and excesses, make our steps firm, and grant us victory over the disbelieving people.”*

In simple terms, the verse records the response of the devotees: all they said was the prayer — "Our Lord! Forgive our sins and our excesses, make our steps firm, and grant us victory over the disbelieving people."

The verse gives the speech of the devotees: their response to the losses was the prayer. Ibn Kathir: "And they said nothing but: 'Our Lord! Forgive us our sins and our transgressions, establish our feet firmly, and give us victory over the disbelieving folk.' And this was the statement that they kept repeating." The verse's *wa ma kana qawlahum illa an qalu* — and their statement was not except that they said — is the limitation; the *rabbana ighfir lana dhunubana wa israfana fi amrina* — Our Lord, forgive us our sins and our excesses in our affair — is the first; the *wa thabbit aqdamana* — and make firm our steps — is the second; and the *wansurna 'ala al-qawmi al-kafirin* — and grant us victory over the disbelieving people — is the third.

"And all they said was" — *wa ma kana qawlahum illa an qalu* — and their saying was not but that they said. The *qawl* — the saying, the statement — is the limitation: the *illa* — except — the only statement; the *kana qawlahum* — their saying was — the divine record of their speech. Ibn Kathir: "this was the statement that they kept repeating." The verse's *illa* — except — is the exclusivity: the sayings of the devotees were nothing but this — the *qawl* of the *ribbiyyun* — the prayer, not the complaint, not the grief of the retreat — the single statement of the prayer.

"Our Lord! Forgive our sins and excesses" — *rabbana ighfir lana dhunubana wa israfana fi amrina* — Our Lord! Forgive us our sins and our excesses in our affair. The *maghfirah* — the forgiveness — is the first: the *dhunub* — the sins — and the *israf* — the excesses, the extravagance — the sins and the oversteps of the affair. The *rab* — the Lord — is the address: the *rabbana* — Our Lord — the believers' address to their Lord; the *israf* — the excess — is the excess of the affairs — the overstepping, the transgression — the *israf* forgiven by the *ghafir*. The verse's *dhunubana* — our sins — and the *israfana* — our excesses — are the confession: the *ribbiyyun* confessed the sins and the excesses; the *maghfirah* — the forgiveness — was the petition.

"make our steps firm" — *wa thabbit aqdamana* — and make firm our feet/steps. The *tathbit* — the making firm — is the second: the *aqdam* — the feet, the steps — the firmness of the standing; the *thabbit* — make firm — the divine the firmness: the steps of the battle, the standing of the cause. Ibn Kathir: "establish our feet firmly" — the firmness of the *ribbiyyun* — the *thabat* — the constancy — of the steps of the cause. The verse's *thabbit* — make firm — is the petition: the firmness of the steps in the battle, the standing of the ranks, the constancy of the cause — the *ribbiyyun* petition for the firmness.

"and grant us victory over the disbelieving people" — *wansurna 'ala al-qawmi al-kafirin* — and grant us victory over the disbelieving people. The *nusrah* — the victory, the help — is the third: the *nusrah* over the *qawm al-kafirin* — the disbelieving people; the *ansurna* — grant us victory — the petition of the help. The verse's *al-qawm al-kafirin* — the disbelieving people — is the enemy — the people of the disbelief — the *nusrah* — the victory — the divine over the enemy of the cause.

The verse's place: the *qawl* of the *ribbiyyun* — the speech of the devotees of the past — is the model of the believers' response to the losses: the prayer — the confession and the petition — the *maghfirah* of the sins, the *tathbit* of the steps, the *nusrah* over the enemy. Ibn Kathir: the statement they kept repeating — the *qawl* of the *ribbiyyun* — the *istighfar* and the *thabat* and the *nusrah*. And the verse's *illa* — except — is the limitation of the speech: they did not say the complaint, the grief, the regress — they said the prayer — the *rabbana* — the address, the confession, the petition, the victory.

For the reader, the verse is the model of the believers' response and the content of the prayer. The believer is taught: the *qawl* of the devotees — the *illa* — the only — was the prayer: the *maghfirah* of the sins and the excesses, the *tathbit* of the steps, the *nusrah* over the disbelieving people. The verse's *rabbana* — Our Lord — is the address: the believers' address to their Lord — the confession of the sins and the excesses, the petition of the firmness and the victory. And the *illa* — except — is the model: the devotees did not say the complaint or the regret — they said the prayer — the *istighfar*, the *thabat*, the *nusrah* — the trilogy of the believer's speech at the losses. The believer's program: the *maghfirah* of the sins, the *thabat* of the steps, the *nusrah* over the enemy — the prayer of the *ribbiyyun* — the model of the response to the losses.

## Verse 148

Verse 148 says: *So Allah gave them the reward of this world and the excellent reward of the Hereafter. For Allah loves the good-doers.*

In simple terms, the verse gives the result: Allah gave them the reward of this world and the excellent reward of the Hereafter — for Allah loves those who do good.

The verse is the reward of the devotees. Ibn Kathir: "So Allah gave them the reward of this world — victory, triumph and the good end — and the excellent reward of the Hereafter, added to the gains in this life; And Allah loves the good-doers." The verse's *fa'atahumu Allahu thawaba al-dunya* — so Allah gave them the reward of this world — is the first; the *wa husna thawabi al-akhirah* — and the excellent reward of the Hereafter — is the second; and the *wa Allahu yuhibbu al-muhsinin* — and Allah loves the good-doers — is the love.

"So Allah gave them the reward of this world" — *fa'atahumu Allahu thawaba al-dunya* — so Allah gave them the reward of this world. The *'ata'* — the giving — is the divine: the *thawab al-dunya* — the reward of this world — is the *'ata'* of the world: the victory, the triumph, the good end. Ibn Kathir: "victory, triumph and the good end." The verse's *fa'atahum* — so He gave them — is the result: the devotees' prayer (147) — the confession, the firmness, the victory — and the divine giving: the *thawab* of the world — the victory of the cause, the triumph of the history, the good end of the believers.

"and the excellent reward of the Hereafter" — *wa husna thawabi al-akhirah* — and the best/excellent reward of the Hereafter. The *husn* — the excellence, the best — is the quality: the *thawab al-akhirah* — the reward of the Hereafter — is the excellent — added to the gains of this life. Ibn Kathir: "the excellent reward of the Hereafter, added to the gains in this life." The verse's *husna* — the excellent — is the superlative: the Hereafter's reward is the excellent — the reward beyond the world's — the *jannah* and the *maghfirah* — the excellent of the Hereafter.

"For Allah loves the good-doers" — *wa Allahu yuhibbu al-muhsinin* — and Allah loves the good-doers, the Muhsinin. The *hubb* — the love — is the divine: the *muhsinin* — the good-doers — the loved; the *ihsan* — the good doing, the excellence — is the quality of the devotees — the *ribbiyyun* — the ones who prayed, who fought, who endured. The verse's *yuhibbu* — He loves — is the close: the *muhsinin* — the good-doers — the ones of the *ihsan* — are the loved — the same *hubb* of 3:134 — the love of the good-doers — the devotees of the past and the believers of the present.

The verse's parallel with 3:134: the *muhsinin* of 148 — the devotees of the past who fought with the prophets — and the *muhsinin* of 134 — the believers who spend and control and pardon — the same love of the divine, the same designation of the *ihsan*. Ibn Kathir: the *ribbiyyun* of 146–147 — the patience and the prayer — and the reward of 148 — the world's and the Hereafter's — the same pattern of the believers of every time: the *sabr* and the *du'a'* — the patience and the prayer — the *thawab* of the two lives — the *hubb* of the *muhsinin*.

For the reader, the verse is the reward of the devotees and the doctrine of the good-doers. The believer is taught: the *ribbiyyun* — the devotees of the past — prayed (147) and Allah gave them the reward of the world and the excellent of the Hereafter (148): the *thawab al-dunya* — the victory, the triumph, the good end — and the *husna thawabi al-akhirah* — the excellent reward of the Hereafter. And the *hubb* of the *muhsinin* — the love of the good-doers — is the close: the *ihsan* — the good doing — the devotion — the quality of the *ribbiyyun* and the believers. The believer's confidence: the prayer of the devotees (147) is the model and the reward of 148 is the result — the world's and the Hereafter's — the *thawab* of the two lives — and the *hubb* of the *muhsinin* — the love of the good-doers.

## Verse 149

Verse 149 says: *O believers! If you yield to the disbelievers, they will drag you back to disbelief—and you will become losers.*

In simple terms, the verse warns: if you obey the disbelievers, they will drag you back to disbelief — and you will end up among the losers.

The verse turns from the example of the past to the warning of the present. Ibn Kathir: "O you who believe! If you obey those who disbelieve, they will send you back on your heels, and you will turn back as losers." The verse's *ya ayyuha alladhina amanu* — O you who believe — is the address; the *in tuti'u alladhina kafaru* — if you obey those who disbelieve — is the condition; the *yaruddukum 'ala a'qabikum* — they will turn you back on your heels — is the return; and the *fatanqalibu khasirin* — and you will turn back as losers — is the verdict.

"If you yield to the disbelievers" — *in tuti'u alladhina kafaru* — if you obey those who disbelieve. The *ta'ah* — the obedience — is the compliance: the obedience of the disbelievers; the *tuti'u* — you obey — the yielding — in what they command. Al-Jalalayn: "if you obey the disbelievers in what they command you." Ibn Kathir: "Allah warns His believing servants against obeying the disbelievers and hypocrites, because such obedience leads to utter destruction in this life and the Hereafter." The verse's *in* — if — is the condition: the obedience of the disbelievers — the compliance with their counsel, their commands — the yielding of the believers to the enemy's guidance.

"they will drag you back to disbelief" — *yaruddukum 'ala a'qabikum* — they will turn you back on your heels. The *radd* — the turning back, the returning — is the dragging: the *a'qab* — the heels — the return of the retreat; the *yaruddukum* — they will turn you back — the disbelievers' work: the return to the disbelief, the regression of the faith. Ibn Kathir: "they will send you back on your heels, and you will turn back (from faith) as losers." The verse's *'ala a'qabikum* — on your heels — is the image of the retreat: the ones who followed the disbelievers' counsel are turned back — from the faith, from the cause, from the march — the *radd* — the return — the regression of the obedience.

"and you will become losers" — *fatanqalibu khasirin* — and you will turn back as losers. The *inqilab* — the turning back — and the *khusran* — the loss, the ruin — is the verdict: the *khasirin* — the losers — the ones of the loss. Ibn Kathir: "such obedience leads to utter destruction in this life and the Hereafter." The verse's *khasirin* — the losers — is the designation: the believers who obeyed the disbelievers are the losers — the *khusr* — the loss — of the two lives — the destruction of the obedience.

The verse's place: the warning of the *ta'ah* of the disbelievers follows the reward of the devotees (148) — the *ribbiyyun* obeyed Allah (147) and were rewarded (148); the believers who obey the disbelievers (149) are the losers — the contrast of the two obediences. Ibn Kathir's frame: "Allah warns His believing servants against obeying the disbelievers and hypocrites, because such obedience leads to utter destruction in this life and the Hereafter" — and the command: "Allah also commands the believers to obey Him, take Him as their protector, seek His aid and trust in Him" (150). The verse's *tuti'u* — you obey — and the 150's *bali Allahu mawlakum* — Nay, Allah is your protector — the two obediences: the disbelievers' and the divine.

For the reader, the verse is the warning of the obedience's regression. The believer is taught: the *ta'ah* of the disbelievers — the obedience of their counsel, their commands — is the condition of the *radd* — the turning back — the regression of the faith; the *yaruddukum* — they will drag you back — the work of the disbelievers: the return to the disbelief, the retreat of the heels. And the *khasirin* — the losers — is the verdict: the obedience leads to the *khusran* — the loss of the two lives — the destruction of the yielding. The believer's program: the *ta'ah* of Allah — the obedience of the divine (147) — and the *ta'ah* of the disbelievers — the yielding to the enemy — the two obediences, the two ends: the *thawab* of the devotees (148) and the *khusr* of the yielders (149); the believer obeys his Lord and not the enemy's counsel.

## Verse 150

Verse 150 says: *But no! Allah is your Guardian, and He is the best Helper.*

In simple terms, the verse corrects the direction: no! Allah is your Guardian — and He is the best Helper.

The verse is the correction of the obedience's direction. Ibn Kathir: "Nay, Allah is your protector, and He is the best of helpers." The verse's *bali Allahu mawlakum* — Nay, Allah is your Protector — is the denial and the affirmation; the *wa huwa khayru al-nasirin* — and He is the best of helpers — is the superiority.

"But no!" — *bali* — nay, but rather. The *bali* — the denial's correction — is the turn: the *illa* of the conditions — not the disbelievers, not the obedience of their counsel — but the *bali* — the yes — of the divine. Ibn Kathir: "Allah also commands the believers to obey Him, take Him as their protector, seek His aid and trust in Him." The verse's *bali* — nay — is the refutation: the *khasirin* of 149 — the losers of the disbelievers' obedience — are corrected by the *bali* — the divine — the protector and the helper.

"Allah is your Guardian" — *Allahu mawlakum* — Allah is your Protector, your Mawla. The *mawla* — the protector, the master, the guardian — is the divine: the *mawla* of the believers — the protector who guards, the master who owns, the guardian who defends. The verse's *mawlakum* — your Protector — is the believers' guard: the *mawla* of 149's obedience — not the disbelievers' but the divine's — the *wila'ah* — the protection — of Allah. Ibn Kathir: the command — obey Him, take Him as protector, seek His aid and trust in Him.

"and He is the best Helper" — *wa huwa khayru al-nasirin* — and He is the best of helpers. The *khayr* — the best — is the superlative: the *nasirin* — the helpers — the best is Allah; the *nusrah* — the help — the divine — the best of the helpers. Ibn Kathir's frame: the *nusr* — the help — and the *tawakkul* — the trust — the believers' protection is the divine — the *mawla* and the *nasir* — the guardian and the best helper.

The verse's pair with 149: the *tuti'u* (149) — the obedience of the disbelievers — and the *bali Allah* (150) — the divine: the believers' obedience is not the disbelievers' but the divine's; the *mawla* — the guardian — and the *khayru al-nasirin* — the best helper — the two names of the believers' protection. Ibn Kathir's command: obey Him, take Him as protector, seek His aid, trust in Him — the *bali* — the yes — of the divine over the disbelievers' counsel.

For the reader, the verse is the correction of the direction and the doctrine of the protection. The believer is taught: the *bali* — nay — is the correction: not the disbelievers' obedience (149) but the divine's; the *mawla* — the Guardian — is the believers' protection: Allah is the Protector, the Master, the Guardian of the believers; the *khayru al-nasirin* — the best of helpers — is the superiority: the helpers of the world and the helper of the divine — Allah the best. The verse's *bali* — the turn — is the believer's direction: the obedience of the divine over the counsel of the enemy; the *mawla* — the protection — the believers' guard; and the *khayru al-nasirin* — the best helper — the believers' confidence: the help of Allah is the best — the *nusrah* of the divine over the obedience of the disbelievers. And the verse's program: obey Him, take Him as the protector, seek His aid, trust in Him — the *bali* — the yes — of the divine direction.
