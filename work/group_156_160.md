## Verse 156

Verse 156 says: *O believers! Do not be like the unfaithful who say about their brothers who travel throughout the land or engage in battle, “If they had stayed with us, they would not have died or been killed.” Allah makes such thinking a cause of agony in their hearts. It is Allah who gives life and causes death. And Allah is All-Seeing of what you do.*

In simple terms, the verse warns against the disbelievers' thinking: do not be like those who say of their brothers who travel or fight, "if they had stayed with us, they would not have died or been killed" — Allah makes such thinking a source of agony in their hearts; it is Allah who gives life and causes death — and He is All-Seeing of what you do.

The verse is the prohibition of the fatalistic regret. Ibn Kathir: "O you who believe! Be not like those who disbelieve (hypocrites) and who say to their brethren, about their dead brethren, when they travel through the earth for the purpose of trading and otherwise, or go out to fight, participating in battles: 'If they had stayed with us, in our area, they would not have died or been killed.'" The verse's *la takunu kalladhina kafaru* — do not be like those who disbelieve — is the prohibition; the *wa qalu li-ikhwanihim* — and said of their brothers — is the saying; the *law kanu 'indana ma matu wa ma qutilu* — if they had been with us, they would not have died or been killed — is the regret; the *liyaj'ala Allahu dhalika hasratan fi qulubihim* — so that Allah makes it a cause of regret in their hearts — is the agony; the *wa Allahu yuhyi wa yumit* — and Allah gives life and causes death — is the refutation; and the *wa Allahu bima ta'malun basir* — and Allah is All-Seer of what you do — is the seeing.

"Do not be like the unfaithful" — *la takunu kalladhina kafaru* — do not be like those who disbelieve. The *kufr* — the disbelief — is the model: the *kalladhina kafaru* — like those who disbelieve — the *ka* — the likeness — the prohibition: the believers are not to be like the disbelievers — in their creed of the death and the predestination. Ibn Kathir: "Allah forbids His believing servants from the disbelievers' false creed, seen in their statement about those who died in battle and during travel." The verse's *la takunu* — do not be — is the prohibition of the likeness: the *kafaru* — the disbelievers (the hypocrites of the verse, per Ibn Kathir's parenthetical) — the creed of the regret — the believers not like them.

"who say about their brothers who travel throughout the land or engage in battle" — *wa qalu li-ikhwanihim idha darabu fi al-ardi aw kanu ghuzzan* — and said of their brothers, when they traveled in the land or were fighters. The *ikhwan* — the brothers — is the kinship: the dead brothers — the travelers, the fighters; the *darabu fi al-ard* — traveled in the land — the trading and the other travel; the *kanu ghuzzan* — were fighters — the participants of the battles. Ibn Kathir: "they travel through the earth for the purpose of trading and otherwise, or go out to fight, participating in battles." The verse's *darabu* — they traveled — and the *ghuzzan* — the fighters — the two categories: the traders of the land and the fighters of the battle — the brothers of the sayers.

"'If they had stayed with us, they would not have died or been killed.'" — *law kanu 'indana ma matu wa ma qutilu* — if they had been with us, they would not have died nor been killed. The *law kanu* — if they had been — is the counterfactual: the *'indana* — with us — the staying; the *ma matu* — they would not have died — the death's denial; the *ma qutilu* — they would not have been killed — the killing's denial. Ibn Kathir: "'If they had stayed with us,' in our area, 'they would not have died or been killed' — they would not have died while traveling or been killed in battle." The verse's *law* — if — is the false conditional: the *'indana* — with us — the staying — the *ma matu* — the non-death — the counterfactual of the regret: the if-then of the disbelievers' creed — the death avoided by the staying.

"Allah makes such thinking a cause of agony in their hearts" — *liyaj'ala Allahu dhalika hasratan fi qulubihim* — so that Allah makes that a cause of regret in their hearts. The *hasrah* — the regret, the agony, the grief — is the cause: the *dhalik* — that — the thinking — the *hasrah* — the regret — in the hearts. Ibn Kathir: "Allah creates this evil thought in their hearts so that their sadness and the grief they feel for their loss would increase." The verse's *liyaj'ala* — so that He makes — is the divine: the *hasrah* — the regret — the cause — the thinking of the disbelievers — the regret in the heart — the *hasrah* of the loss's thought. The verse's *dhalika* — that — is the thought: the *law kanu* — the if-they-had-stayed — the regret's thinking — the *hasrah* — the agony — the divine cause of the false thought's grief.

"It is Allah who gives life and causes death" — *wa Allahu yuhyi wa yumit* — and Allah gives life and causes death. The *yuhyi* — gives life — and the *yumit* — causes death — are the divine: the *ihya'* — the life — and the *imatah* — the death — the power; the *Allah* — the subject — the divine. Ibn Kathir: "the creation is under Allah's power, and the decision is His Alone. No one lives or dies except by Allah's leave, and no one's life is increased or decreased except by His decree." The verse's *yuhyi wa yumit* — gives life and causes death — is the refutation: the death is the divine — not the travel, not the battle, not the staying — the *yuhyi* and the *yumit* — the divine power of the life and the death — the refutation of the *law kanu* — the counterfactual.

"And Allah is All-Seeing of what you do" — *wa Allahu bima ta'malun basir* — and Allah is All-Seer of what you do. The *basir* — the All-Seeing — is the vision: the *bima ta'malun* — of what you do — the deeds of the sayers and the travelers and the fighters. Ibn Kathir: "His knowledge and vision encompasses all His creation and none of their affairs ever escapes Him." The verse's *basir* — the All-Seer — is the close: the deeds — the travels, the battles, the sayings, the regrets — all seen; the divine vision of the life and the death and the deeds.

For the reader, the verse is the prohibition of the fatalistic regret and the doctrine of the death. The believer is taught: the *kalladhina kafaru* — the likeness of the disbelievers — the creed of the *law kanu* — the if-they-had-stayed — is the prohibited: the death denied as the travel's or the battle's consequence; the *hasrah* — the regret — the agony — the divine cause of the false thought's grief. And the *yuhyi wa yumit* — the life and the death — the divine power: the death is the divine — not the travel, not the battle — the *ihya'* and the *imatah*; and the *basir* — the All-Seer — the vision of the deeds. The believer's program: the death of the *ghazw* and the *safar* — the battle and the travel — the divine appointed; the *hasrah* of the *law kanu* — the regret — the false thought of the disbelievers — avoided by the believer; and the *yuhyi wa yumit* — the divine life and death — the trust of the decree — the *basir* — the seeing.

## Verse 157

Verse 157 says: *Should you be martyred or die in the cause of Allah, then His forgiveness and mercy are far better than whatever ˹wealth˺ those ˹who stay behind˺ accumulate.*

In simple terms, the verse weighs the two goods: if you are killed or die in Allah's cause, then His forgiveness and mercy are far better than whatever the ones who stay behind accumulate.

The verse is the weighing of the martyrdom and the wealth. Ibn Kathir: "And if you are killed or die in the way of Allah, forgiveness and mercy from Allah are far better than all that they amass." The verse's *wa la'in qutiltum fi sabil Allahi aw muttum* — and if you are killed in the way of Allah or die — is the cause's death; the *lamaghfiratun mina Allahi wa rahmah* — then forgiveness from Allah and mercy — is the reward; the *khayrun mimma yajma'un* — better than what they accumulate — is the comparison.

"Should you be martyred or die in the cause of Allah" — *wa la'in qutiltum fi sabil Allahi aw muttum* — and if you are slain in the way of Allah or die. The *qatl* — the killing — is the martyrdom: the *fi sabil Allah* — in the way of Allah — the cause's slaying; the *mawt* — the death — is the natural death: the *aw muttum* — or you die — the death in the cause — the two: the killed and the died. Al-Jalalayn: "if death comes to you thereat — forgiveness from God for your sins and mercy therefor from Him for you." The verse's *la'in* — and if — is the condition: the *qutil* — the slain — and the *mut* — the died — the cause's two ends — the martyrdom of the sword and the death of the way.

"then His forgiveness and mercy are far better" — *lamaghfiratun mina Allahi wa rahmah* — then forgiveness from Allah and mercy. The *maghfirah* — the forgiveness — and the *rahmah* — the mercy — are the reward: the forgiveness of the sins and the mercy of the divine — the *lam* — the oath — the emphasis of the reward. Ibn Kathir: "death and martyrdom in Allah's cause are a means of earning Allah's mercy, forgiveness and pleasure." The verse's *min Allah* — from Allah — is the source: the *maghfirah* — the forgiveness — and the *rahmah* — the mercy — the divine reward of the cause's death.

"than whatever ˹wealth˺ those ˹who stay behind˺ accumulate" — *khayrun mimma yajma'un* — better than what they amass. The *khayr* — the better — is the comparison: the *maghfirah* and the *rahmah* — the better — than the *yam'a'* — the accumulation — the *mimma yajma'un* — what they amass — the wealth of the stayers. Al-Jalalayn: read *tajma'un* (you amass) or *yajma'un* (they amass). Ibn Kathir: "This, indeed, is better than remaining in this life with its short-lived delights." The verse's *yajma'un* — they accumulate — is the stayers' wealth: the accumulation of the world — the *jama'* — the gathering — and the *maghfirah* and the *rahmah* — the better — the forgiveness and the mercy over the wealth.

The verse's place: the weighing of the martyrdom — the *qutil* and the *mut* in the cause — the *maghfirah* and the *rahmah* — the better than the *jama'* of the stayers. Ibn Kathir's frame (156–158): "Prohibiting the Ideas of the Disbelievers about Death and Predestination" — the *law kanu* of 156 — the refrain of the disbelievers — and the weighing of 157: the death in the cause is the *maghfirah* and the *rahmah* — the better than the stayers' accumulation; and the *hashr* of 158 — the gathering to Allah. The verse's comparison: the *ghazw* — the martyrdom — and the *jama'* — the accumulation — the two ends: the forgiveness and the mercy over the wealth.

For the reader, the verse is the weighing of the cause and the wealth. The believer is taught: the *qutil* and the *mut* — the slain and the died — in the *sabil Allah* — the cause — are the *maghfirah* — the forgiveness — and the *rahmah* — the mercy — the reward: the *lam* — the oath — the emphasis; and the *khayr* — the better — than the *jama'* — the accumulation — the wealth of the stayers: the forgiveness and the mercy over the gathering of the world. The verse's *fi sabil Allah* — in the way of Allah — is the cause: the death in the cause is the *maghfirah* and the *rahmah* — the mercy and the pleasure of the divine; and the *yajma'un* — the accumulation — the stayers' gathering — the short-lived delights — the lesser. The believer's confidence: the martyrdom of the cause — the *qutil* — and the death in the way — the *mut* — are the *maghfirah* and the *rahmah* — the better than the wealth — the weighing of the divine: the mercy over the accumulation.

## Verse 158

Verse 158 says: *Whether you die or are martyred—all of you will be gathered before Allah.*

In simple terms, the verse states the destination: whether you die or are martyred, all of you will be gathered before Allah.

The verse is the gathering of the two ends. Ibn Kathir: "And whether you die or are killed, verily, unto Allah you shall be gathered." The verse's *wa la'in muttum aw qutiltum la'ila Allahi tuhsharun* — and if you die or are killed, to Allah you will be gathered — is the gathering.

"Whether you die or are martyred" — *wa la'in muttum aw qutiltum* — and if you die or are killed. The *mawt* — the death — and the *qatl* — the killing — the two: the *muttum* — you die — and the *qutiltum* — you are killed — the two ends of 157 — the died and the slain; the *la'in* — the if — the oath's condition. Ibn Kathir: "whoever dies or is killed will return to Allah, the Exalted and Most Honored, and He will reward him if he has done good deeds, or will punish him for his evil deeds." The verse's *aw* — or — is the two: the *muttum* and the *qutiltum* — the natural and the martyred — the two ends of the cause.

"all of you will be gathered before Allah" — *la'ila Allahi tuhsharun* — then to Allah you will be gathered. The *hashr* — the gathering — is the destination: the *tuhsharun* — you will be gathered — the *ila Allah* — to Allah — the gathering of the two ends. Ibn Kathir: "will return to Allah... He will reward him if he has done good deeds, or will punish him for his evil deeds." The verse's *ila Allah* — to Allah — is the destiny: the *hashr* — the gathering — to the divine — the reward or the punishment; the *tuhsharun* — you will be gathered — the passive — the gathering of the divine. The verse's *la'* — the oath — the emphasis: the gathering is the certain — the *ila Allah* — the destination — the divine gathering.

The verse's pair with 157: the *maghfirah* and the *rahmah* of 157 — the reward of the cause's death — and the *hashr* of 158 — the gathering: the two ends — the died and the killed — gathered to Allah — the reward of the good deeds or the punishment of the evil. Ibn Kathir: "whoever dies or is killed will return to Allah, the Exalted and Most Honored, and He will reward him if he has done good deeds, or will punish him for his evil deeds." The verse's *tuhsharun* — you will be gathered — is the completion: the death in the cause — the *maghfirah* and the *rahmah* (157) — and the gathering to Allah (158) — the reward's destiny.

For the reader, the verse is the gathering of the ends. The believer is taught: the *muttum* and the *qutiltum* — the died and the killed — the two ends — are the *hashr* — the gathering — to Allah: the *ila Allah* — the destination — the divine; and the *tuhsharun* — you will be gathered — the reward or the punishment — the good deeds rewarded, the evil punished. The verse's *la'ila Allah* — to Allah — is the destiny: the gathering of the two ends to the divine — the *hashr* of the natural and the martyred; the reward of the good or the punishment of the evil. The believer's confidence: the death and the martyrdom — the two ends — gathered to Allah — the *maghfirah* and the *rahmah* — the mercy (157) — and the gathering (158) — the destiny of the divine — the reward of the cause's death.

## Verse 159

Verse 159 says: *It is out of Allah’s mercy that you ˹O Prophet˺ have been lenient with them. Had you been cruel or hard-hearted, they would have certainly abandoned you. So pardon them, ask Allah’s forgiveness for them, and consult with them in ˹conducting˺ matters. Once you make a decision, put your trust in Allah. Surely Allah loves those who trust in Him.*

In simple terms, the verse states the mercy behind the Prophet's conduct: it was out of Allah's mercy that he was lenient with them; had he been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have dispersed from around him. So he should pardon them, seek forgiveness for them, and consult them in matters — and once he decides, put his trust in Allah; Allah loves those who trust in Him.

The verse is the mercy of the Prophet and the command of the consultation. Ibn Kathir: "And by the mercy of Allah, you dealt with them gently. And had you been severe and harsh-hearted, they would have broken away from about you; so pardon them, and ask forgiveness for them; and consult them in the affairs. Then when you have taken a decision, put your trust in Allah, certainly, Allah loves those who put their trust (in Him)." The verse's *fabima rahmatin mina Allahi linta lahum* — and by the mercy of Allah you were lenient with them — is the mercy; the *wa law kunta fazza ghaliza al-qalbi lanfaddu min hawlik* — and had you been severe and harsh-hearted, they would have scattered from around you — is the contrast; the *fa'fu 'anhum wastaghfir lahum wa shawirhum fi al-amr* — so pardon them, seek forgiveness for them, and consult them in the matter — is the command; the *fa idha 'azamta fatawakkal 'ala Allah* — and when you have resolved, put your trust in Allah — is the trust; and the *inna Allaha yuhibbu al-mutawakkilin* — and Allah loves those who trust — is the love.

"It is out of Allah's mercy that you ˹O Prophet˺ have been lenient with them" — *fabima rahmatin mina Allahi linta lahum* — and by the mercy from Allah you were lenient with them. The *rahmah* — the mercy — is the source: the *linta* — you were gentle, lenient — the *lin* — the gentleness — the mercy's work. Ibn Kathir: "meaning, who would have made you this kind, if it was not Allah's mercy for you and them"; Qatadah: "With Allah's mercy you became this kind"; Al-Hasan Al-Basri: "this, indeed, is the description of the behavior that Allah sent Muhammad with"; parallel 9:128. The verse's *rabima rahmatin* — by the mercy — is the cause: the Prophet's gentleness — the *lin* — the leniency — was the divine mercy — the *rahmah* of Allah for the Prophet and the Ummah.

"Had you been cruel or hard-hearted, they would have certainly abandoned you" — *wa law kunta fazzan ghaliza al-qalbi lanfaddu min hawlik* — and had you been severe (rude) and harsh-hearted, they would have dispersed from around you. The *fazz* — the severe, the rude — is the harsher: "The severe person is he who utters harsh words"; the *ghaliz al-qalb* — the harsh-hearted — "the person whose heart is hard"; the *lanfaddu* — they would have scattered, broken away — the *min hawlik* — from around you — the dispersal. Ibn Kathir: "Had this been the Prophet's behavior, they would have scattered from around you. However, Allah gathered them and made you kind and soft with them, so that their hearts congregate around you"; 'Abdullah bin 'Amr: the description in the previous Books — "He is not severe, harsh, obscene in the marketplace or dealing evil for evil. Rather, he forgives and pardons." The verse's *law* — if — is the contrast: the hypotherical harshness — the *fazz* and the *ghaliz* — the dispersal — the *rahmah* — the gentleness — the gathering.

"So pardon them, ask Allah's forgiveness for them, and consult with them in ˹conducting˺ matters" — *fa'fu 'anhum wastaghfir lahum wa shawirhum fi al-amr* — so pardon them and seek forgiveness for them and consult them in the matter. The *'afw* — the pardon — is the first: the pass over of what they did — the *istighfar* — the seeking of the forgiveness — the *shawir* — the consultation — the third: the *amr* — the matter — the affairs. Al-Jalalayn: "pardon them — pass over what they have done — and ask forgiveness for them — for their sins — until I forgive them — and consult them — find out their opinions — in the matter — your affair in the battle and otherwise — in order to win their hearts over and so that you may be emulated in this respect; and indeed the Prophet would frequently consult them." Ibn Kathir's examples of the consultation: Badr (the caravan — "if you wish to cross the sea, we would follow you... we would never say what the Children of Israel said to Musa"; the camping — Al-Mundhir bin 'Amr near the enemy for martyrdom), Uhud (fortify or go out — the majority's request), Khandaq (the one-third of the fruits offer to the Ahzab tribes — Sa'd bin 'Ubadah and Sa'd bin Mu'adh rejected and the Prophet followed their advice), Hudaybiyyah (Abu Bakr: "We did not come here to fight anyone. Rather, we came to perform 'Umrah"), Ifk (the consultation with 'Ali and Usamah). Ibn Majah/Abu Hurayrah: "The one whom advice is sought from is to be trusted" (Abu Dawud, at-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i: hasan).

"Once you make a decision, put your trust in Allah" — *fa idha 'azamta fatawakkal 'ala Allah* — and when you have resolved, then put your trust in Allah. The *'azm* — the resolution, the decision — is after the consultation: the *idha 'azamta* — when you have decided — the *tawakkul* — the trust — the *fatawakkal* — then trust — in Allah. Ibn Kathir: "if you conduct the required consultation and you then make a decision, trust in Allah over your decision." The verse's *'azamta* — you have resolved — is the decision's moment: the consultation done and the decision made — the *tawakkul* — the trust — the *fatawakkal* — the reliance — over the decision — the trust in Allah after the counsel.

"Surely Allah loves those who trust in Him" — *inna Allaha yuhibbu al-mutawakkilin* — certainly, Allah loves those who trust (in Him). The *hubb* — the love — is the divine: the *mutawakkilin* — the trusting ones — the loved; the *tawakkul* — the trust — the reliance — the quality of the loved. The verse's *yuhibbu al-mutawakkilin* — the love of the trusting — is the close: the *tawakkul* — the trust — after the *'azm* — the decision — the love of the divine for the trusters.

For the reader, the verse is the mercy of the Prophet and the method of the leadership. The believer is taught: the Prophet's *lin* — the gentleness — was the *rahmah* — the mercy — not the nature but the divine gift — Qatadah's "With Allah's mercy you became this kind" — and the *fazz* and the *ghaliz* — the harshness — would have dispersed the community. The verse's *'afw* and *istighfar* and *shawir* — the pardon, the forgiveness-petition, and the consultation — are the method: the pardon of the offenders, the prayer for the sinners, and the counsel of the affairs — the *shawir* — the consultation — the Prophet's frequent practice (Badr, Uhud, Khandaq, Hudaybiyyah, Ifk) — the *amr* — the matters — consulted. And the *'azm* and the *tawakkul* — the resolution and the trust — are the leadership: the consultation done and the decision made — the *tawakkul* — the trust over the decision; the *yuhibbu al-mutawakkilin* — the love of the trusters — the divine love of the reliance. The believer's program: the *lin* of the mercy (the gentleness), the *'afw* and the *istighfar* (the pardon and the prayer), the *shawir* (the consultation), the *'azm* and the *tawakkul* (the decision and the trust) — the method of the Prophet's leadership.

## Verse 160

Verse 160 says: *If Allah helps you, none can defeat you. But if He denies you help, then who else can help you? So in Allah let the believers put their trust.*

In simple terms, the verse states the exclusivity of the divine help: if Allah helps you, none can defeat you; but if He withholds His help, who could help you then? So in Allah let the believers put their trust.

The verse is the exclusivity of the help and the command of the trust. Ibn Kathir: "If Allah helps you, none can overcome you; and if He forsakes you, who is there after Him that can help you? And in Allah (Alone) let believers put their trust." The verse's *in yansurkumu Allahu fala ghaliba lakum* — if Allah helps you, none can overcome you — is the first; the *wa in yakhdhulkum faman dha alladhi yansurukum min ba'dihi* — and if He forsakes you, who after Him can help you — is the second; and the *wa 'ala Allahi falyatawakkal al-mu'minun* — and upon Allah let the believers rely — is the trust.

"If Allah helps you, none can defeat you" — *in yansurkumu Allahu fala ghaliba lakum* — if Allah helps you, then no victor/overcomer is for you. The *nusrah* — the help — is the divine: the *yansurkum* — He helps you — the *fala ghalib* — then none overcomes — the *ghalib* — the victor, the overcomer — the negation: the divine help — the unovercome. Ibn Kathir: "If Allah helps you, none can overcome you." The verse's *in* — if — is the condition: the *nusrah* of Allah — the *fala ghalib* — the no-victor — the help's exclusivity: the helped by Allah are not overcome — the *ghalib* — the overcomer — none.

"But if He denies you help, then who else can help you?" — *wa in yakhdhulkum faman dha alladhi yansurukum min ba'dihi* — and if He forsakes you, then who is the one who helps you after Him? The *khidhlan* — the forsaking, the denial of the help — is the second: the *yakhdhulkum* — He forsakes you — the *faman dha* — then who — the rhetorical question: the *yansurukum min ba'dihi* — helps you after Him — the answer: no one. Ibn Kathir: "and if He forsakes you, who is there after Him that can help you?" Al-Jalalayn's parallel (3:126): the victory is only from Allah. The verse's *min ba'dihi* — after Him — is the exclusivity: after Allah — no helper — the *faman dha* — who is the one — the rhetorical — the *yansurukum* — the helper — none after Him — the *khidhlan* — the forsaking — the denial — and the no-helper.

"So in Allah let the believers put their trust" — *wa 'ala Allahi falyatawakkal al-mu'minun* — and upon Allah let the believers rely. The *tawakkul* — the trust — is the command: the *falyatawakkal* — let them rely — the *'ala Allah* — upon Allah — the believers' reliance. Ibn Kathir: "Allah next commands the believers to trust in Him" — the *tawakkul* — the trust — after the exclusivity — the *'ala Allah* — upon Allah — the believers' reliance — the only helper — the trusted. The verse's *falyatawakkal* — let them rely — is the imperative: the believers' trust — the *'ala Allah* — the divine — the only — the trust of the *nusrah* — the help — the exclusive.

The verse's place: the completion of the section — the *nusrah* of 3:126 — "And there is no victory except from Allah, the All-Mighty, the All-Wise" — and the *tawakkul* of 160 — the exclusivity and the trust. Ibn Kathir: the parallel "is similar to His statement" — 3:126 — the victory only from Allah — the *'Aziz* and the *Hakim* — and 160 — the help's exclusivity and the trust. The verse's *in* and *faman* — the if and the who — the two questions: the help's exclusivity — the *nusrah* — and the *tawakkul* — the trust — the believers' reliance on the only helper.

For the reader, the verse is the exclusivity of the help and the command of the trust. The believer is taught: the *nusrah* of Allah — the *fala ghalib* — the no-overcomer — the helped are not overcome; and the *khidhlan* — the forsaking — the *faman dha* — the who — the rhetorical: no one after Him — the *yansurukum min ba'dihi* — the helper after Him — none. And the *tawakkul* — the trust — the command: the *'ala Allah* — upon Allah — the believers' reliance — the exclusive helper — the trusted. The verse's *falyatawakkal* — let them rely — is the believer's program: the trust of the divine — the *nusrah* and the *khidhlan* — the help and the forsaking — the exclusive — and the *tawakkul* — the reliance — the believer's answer. The believer's confidence: the help of Allah — the unovercome; the forsaking of Allah — the helpless; and the *tawakkul* — the trust — the believers' reliance on the only helper — the *'ala Allah* — the divine — the trust of the believers.
