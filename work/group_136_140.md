## Verse 136

Verse 136 says: *Their reward is forgiveness from their Lord and Gardens under which rivers flow, staying there forever. How excellent is the reward for those who work ˹righteousness˺!*

In simple terms, the verse gives the reward of the described believers: forgiveness from their Lord and Gardens with rivers flowing beneath, staying there forever — and how excellent is the reward of the workers.

The verse closes the description of the believers. Ibn Kathir: "For such, the reward is forgiveness from their Lord, and Gardens with rivers flowing underneath (Paradise), wherein they shall abide forever. How excellent is this reward for the doers (of good)." The verse's *ula'ika jazau'uhum maghfiratun min rabbihim* — those are — for them, forgiveness from their Lord — is the reward; the *wa jannatun tajri min tahtiha al-anhar* — and Gardens under which rivers flow — is the Paradise; the *khalidina fiha* — abiding therein forever — is the permanence; and the *wa ni'ma ajru al-'amilin* — how excellent is the reward of the workers — is the praise.

"Their reward is forgiveness from their Lord" — *ula'ika jazau'uhum maghfiratun min rabbihim* — for such, their reward is forgiveness from their Lord. The *jaza'* — the reward — is the recompense: the *maghfirah* — the forgiveness — from the Lord. Ibn Kathir: "as a reward for these qualities." The verse's *min rabbihim* — from their Lord — is the source: the forgiveness from the Lord of the believers — the reward of the qualities: the giving (134), the control of the anger (134), the pardon (134), the remembrance and the forgiveness (135) — the reward of the *maghfirah* — the forgiveness from the Lord.

"and Gardens under which rivers flow, staying there forever" — *wa jannatun tajri min tahtiha al-anharu khalidina fiha* — and Gardens under which rivers flow, abiding therein forever. The *jannat* — the Gardens — is the Paradise: the rivers flowing beneath — "carrying all kinds of drinks" (Ibn Kathir); the *khulud* — the abiding forever — is the permanence: the same *khulud* of the earlier verdicts — the abiding of the believers in the Gardens, the abiding of the deniers in the Fire. The verse's *tajri min tahtiha al-anhar* — the rivers flowing beneath — is the description of the Paradise of the surah (2:25, 3:15, 3:198) — the Gardens of the believers; the *khalidina* — abiding — is the permanence of the reward.

"How excellent is the reward for those who work ˹righteousness˺!" — *wa ni'ma ajru al-'amilin* — and how excellent is the reward of the workers. The *ni'ma* — how excellent, how good — is the praise of the reward; the *ajr* — the reward — is the recompense; the *'amilin* — the workers, the doers — are the ones who work — the believers who do the works of 134–135. Ibn Kathir: "Allah praises Paradise in this part of the Ayah." The verse's *ni'ma* — how excellent — is the divine commendation: the reward is excellent — the forgiveness and the Gardens and the permanence — for the workers.

The verse's place: the description of the *muttaqin* (133–136) — the *jannah* prepared for the *muttaqin* (133), the qualities of the *muttaqin* (134–135), and the reward of the *muttaqin* (136): the *maghfirah* and the *jannat* and the *khulud* — the three parts of the reward; and the *ni'ma* — the praise — the excellence of the reward of the workers. The section is the surah's portrait of the mindful: the giving, the control of the anger, the pardon, the repentance — and the reward: the forgiveness, the Gardens, the eternity.

For the reader, the verse is the reward of the workers. The believer is taught: the reward of the believers — the *maghfirah* from the Lord, the *jannat* with the rivers, the *khulud* forever — is the recompense of the qualities: the giving, the control, the pardon, the repentance. The verse's *ni'ma ajru al-'amilin* — how excellent is the reward of the workers — is the divine praise: the reward is excellent; the *'amilin* — the workers — are the ones who work the righteousness; the *ajr* — the reward — the forgiveness and the Gardens and the eternity. The believer's program: the *'amal* — the work — is the path of the reward: the *infaq* and the *kazm* and the *'afw* and the *istighfar* — the qualities of the *muttaqin* (134–135) — and the reward of the *maghfirah* and the *jannat* and the *khulud* — the excellence of the reward of the workers.

## Verse 137

Verse 137 says: *Similar situations came to pass before you, so travel throughout the land and see the fate of the deniers.*

In simple terms, the verse turns the believers to history: similar situations have passed before them — so travel through the land and see what happened to the deniers.

The verse turns from the believers' qualities to the lessons of history. Ibn Kathir: "Many similar ways (and mishaps of life) were faced (by nations (believers and disbelievers) that have passed away) before you, so travel through the earth, and see what was the end of those who denied." The verse's *qad khalat min qablikum sunan* — similar situations have passed before you — is the history; the *fasiru fi al-ard* — so travel in the earth — is the command; and the *fanzuru kayfa kana 'aqibatu al-mukadhdhibin* — and see how was the end of the deniers — is the survey.

"Similar situations came to pass before you" — *qad khalat min qablikum sunan* — ways/situations have passed before you. The *sunan* — the ways, the patterns, the situations — is the history: the ways of Allah with the previous nations. Ibn Kathir: "the previous nations who followed their Prophets before you, they too suffered losses. However, the good end was theirs, and the ultimate defeat was for the disbelievers." Al-Jalalayn: "Ways of life have passed away before you — all manner of unbelief has preceded where they have been given respite but are then seized with punishment." The verse's *khalat* — have passed — is the past: the *sunan* — the ways — of the previous nations; the *qablikum* — before you — is the precedence: the believers' situation is not the first of the history; the losses and the victories were the patterns before.

"so travel throughout the land and see the fate of the deniers" — *fasiru fi al-ardi fanzuru kayfa kana 'aqibatu al-mukadhdhibin* — so travel in the earth and see how was the end of the deniers. The *sayr* — the travel — is the survey: the believers travel the earth; the *nazar* — the seeing — is the contemplation: see the *'aqibah* — the end — of the *mukadhdhibin* — the deniers. Ibn Kathir: "see what was the end of those who denied." Al-Jalalayn: "travel in the land, O believers, and behold how was the end of those who denied the messengers — that is how their affair ended in destruction. So do not grieve on account of their victory; I am only giving them respite until their appointed time." The verse's *'aqibah* — the end — is the lesson: the end of the deniers is the destruction; the travel and the seeing — the historical survey — is the instruction: the believers who look at the history of the deniers see the end — the *'aqibah* — the destruction of the deniers.

The verse's composition: the believers who suffered at Uhud (the seventy dead) are taught the *sunan* — the ways of the past: the previous nations who followed their prophets also suffered losses; the good end was theirs and the ultimate defeat was the disbelievers'. Ibn Kathir: "Allah states to His believing servants who suffered losses in the battle of Uhud, including seventy dead: 'Many similar ways (and mishaps of life) were faced before you' — for the previous nations who followed their Prophets before you, they too suffered losses. However, the good end was theirs, and the ultimate defeat was for the disbelievers." The verse's *sunan* and *'aqibah* — the ways and the end — are the two: the *sunan* of the past and the *'aqibah* of the deniers — the history is the instruction, and the end is the lesson.

For the reader, the verse is the command of the historical instruction. The believer is taught: the *sunan* — the ways — of Allah with the previous nations have passed; the losses of the believers were the patterns before; and the *'aqibah* — the end — of the deniers is the destruction — the travel and the seeing of the history is the instruction. The verse's *fasiru* — so travel — is the command: the believers survey the earth and see the end of the deniers; the *fanzuru* — and see — is the contemplation: the historical sight is the lesson of the end. The believer's lesson from Uhud: the loss was not the novelty — the previous nations suffered losses and the good end was theirs; the *'aqibah* of the deniers is the destruction — the respite of the world, the seizure of the end. The verse's *sunan* — the ways — and the *'aqibah* — the end — are the believer's historical confidence: the ways of Allah are the constant and the end of the deniers is the destruction.

## Verse 138

Verse 138 says: *This is an insight to humanity —a guide and a lesson to the God-fearing.*

In simple terms, the verse states the value of the Qur'an: it is an insight for humanity, a guidance and an instruction for the God-fearing.

The verse states the nature of the recitation. Ibn Kathir: "This is a plain statement for mankind, a guidance and instruction for the Muttaqin." The verse's *hadha bayanun lil-nas* — this is a plain statement for mankind — is the declaration; the *wa hudan wa maw'izatun lil-muttaqin* — and a guidance and an instruction for the God-fearing — is the double function.

"This is an insight to humanity" — *hadha bayanun lil-nas* — this is a plain statement, an explanation for mankind. The *bayan* — the statement, the explanation, the insight — is the Qur'an: the true reality of things. Ibn Kathir: "the Qur'an explains the true reality of things and narrates how the previous nations suffered by the hands of their enemies." The verse's *lil-nas* — for mankind — is the universal address: the *bayan* is for all humanity — not the exclusive of the believers, not the private of the community — the declaration of the truth for the people. The *bayan* of the verse — the statement — is the Qur'an's explanation: the *sunan* of the past (137), the *'aqibah* of the deniers (137), the realities of the truth — the explanation of things as they are.

"a guide and a lesson to the God-fearing" — *wa hudan wa maw'izatun lil-muttaqin* — and guidance and instruction for the God-fearing. The *huda* — the guidance — and the *maw'izah* — the instruction, the admonition — are the double: the **huda** — the guidance — for the hearts; the *maw'izah* — the instruction — for the *muttaqin* — "to discourage committing the prohibited and forbidden matters" (Ibn Kathir). The verse's *lil-muttaqin* — for the God-fearing — is the function's address: the *bayan* is for all humanity, and the *huda* and the *maw'izah* are for the *muttaqin* — the ones who fear, the ones who are mindful — the guidance of the hearts and the instruction of the conduct. Ibn Kathir: "the Qur'an contains the news of the past, and guidance for your hearts, and instruction for the Muttaqin to discourage committing the prohibited and forbidden matters."

The verse's place: the *bayan* of the Qur'an — after the *sunan* of the past (137) — is the insight for humanity, the guide for the hearts, the lesson for the God-fearing. The Qur'an of the surah — the *bayan* — is the true reality: the history of the deniers (137), the guidance of the believers (138), the instruction of the *muttaqin* (138) — the three functions: the *bayan* for the people, the *huda* for the hearts, the *maw'izah* for the God-fearing.

For the reader, the verse is the Qur'an's statement of its own value. The believer is taught: the *bayan* — the insight, the statement — is for humanity: the truth explained, the realities declared; the *huda* — the guidance — is for the hearts: the way of the truth; the *maw'izah* — the instruction — is for the *muttaqin*: the lesson of the conduct, the discouragement of the forbidden. The verse's *lil-nas* — for mankind — is the universal: the Qur'an is not the private book but the public statement; the *lil-muttaqin* — for the God-fearing — is the function: the guidance and the instruction are for the ones who fear — the *bayan* for all, the *huda* and the *maw'izah* for the mindful. The believer's response: the *bayan* — the acceptance of the statement; the *huda* — the guidance of the heart; the *maw'izah* — the lesson of the conduct — the Qur'an as the insight, the guide, and the instruction.

## Verse 139

Verse 139 says: *Do not falter or grieve, for you will have the upper hand, if you are ˹true˺ believers.*

In simple terms, the verse commands the believers: do not lose heart or grieve — you are the upper hand, if you are true believers.

The verse is the comfort of the believers after the loss of Uhud. Ibn Kathir: "So do not become weak, because of what you suffered, nor be sad, and you will be triumphant if you are indeed believers — for surely, the ultimate victory and triumph will be yours, O believers." The verse's *wa la tahinu* — and do not become weak — is the first negative; the *wa la tahzanu* — and do not grieve — is the second; and the *wa antum al-a'lawna in kuntum mu'minin* — and you are the upper hand, if you are believers — is the verdict.

"Do not falter or grieve" — *wa la tahinu wa la tahzanu* — and do not weaken, and do not grieve. The *wahn* — the weakness, the faltering — is the loss of the strength: the believers do not become weak because of what they suffered. Ibn Kathir: "because of what you suffered." The *huzn* — the grief, the sadness — is the loss of the hope: the believers do not grieve — the loss of Uhud is not the end of the hope; the *wahn* and the *huzn* — the weakness and the grief — are the two negatives: the believers do not falter and do not grieve — the *la* of the two commands is the prohibition of the despair.

"for you will have the upper hand" — *wa antum al-a'lawna* — and you are the upper, the supreme, the triumphant. The *a'la* — the upper, the highest, the supreme — is the position: the believers are the upper hand; the *a'lawn* — the supreme — is the believers' position: the ultimate victory and triumph is the believers', not the disbelievers'. Ibn Kathir: "for surely, the ultimate victory and triumph will be yours, O believers." Al-Jalalayn: "the final good end will be yours" — the *a'lawn* — the upper — is the verdict of the believers' position: the superiority is the believers' — the *a'lawn* is not the temporary of Uhud but the ultimate of the history.

"if you are ˹true˺ believers" — *in kuntum mu'minin* — if you are believers. The *in* — if — is the condition: the believers' upper position is conditioned on the faith; the *mu'minin* — the believers — are the ones of the *iman* — the true believers. The verse's *in* — if — is the conditional: the *a'lawn* — the upper — is the believers' position *if they are believers* — the *iman* is the condition of the upper hand; the *mu'minin* — the true believers — are the ones who hold the condition. Ibn Kathir: "you will be triumphant if you are indeed believers."

The verse's context: the comfort after the loss of Uhud (the seventy dead, the wound of the Prophet). The believers who suffered the loss are taught: do not weaken, do not grieve; the *a'lawn* — the upper — is your position, if you are believers. The loss of Uhud is not the verdict of the history; the *sunan* of the past (137) — the previous nations suffered losses and the good end was theirs; the *a'lawn* of the believers — the ultimate victory and triumph — is the believers'.

For the reader, the verse is the command against the despair. The believer is taught: the *wahn* — the weakness — and the *huzn* — the grief — are the prohibited: the believers do not falter and do not grieve — the loss is not the end, the defeat is not the verdict. And the *a'lawn* — the upper — is the believers' position: the ultimate victory and triumph is theirs, *if they are believers* — the *in* of the condition — the *iman* is the condition of the upper hand. The believer's lesson from Uhud: the weakness and the grief are the enemies of the *iman*; the *a'lawn* — the superiority — is the believers' — the ultimate, not the temporary; and the *in kuntum mu'minin* — if you are believers — is the condition: the faith is the condition of the upper hand — the believers who hold the *iman* are the ones who are the upper.

## Verse 140

Verse 140 says: *If you have suffered injuries ˹at Uḥud˺, they suffered similarly ˹at Badr˺. We alternate these days ˹of victory and defeat˺ among people so that Allah may reveal the ˹true˺ believers, choose martyrs from among you—and Allah does not like the wrongdoers—*

In simple terms, the verse states the divine wisdom of the alternation: if you have suffered injuries at Uhud, they suffered similarly at Badr — We alternate these days among people so that Allah may reveal the true believers and choose martyrs from among you — and Allah does not like the wrongdoers.

The verse states the wisdom of the alternation. Ibn Kathir: "If a wound has touched you, be sure a similar wound has touched the others... And so are the days, that We give to men by turns, that Allah may know (test) those who believe, and that He may take martyrs from among you. And Allah likes not the wrongdoers." The verse's *in yamsaskum qarhun faqad massa al-qawma qarhun mithluhu* — if a wound touches you, a similar wound has touched the people — is the parallel; the *wa tilka al-ayyamu nudawiluha bayna al-nas* — and these days We alternate among people — is the alternation; the *wa liya'lama Allahu alladhina amanu* — so that Allah may know those who believe — is the revelation; the *wa yattakhidha minkum shuhada'* — and take martyrs from among you — is the selection; and the *wa Allahu la yuhibbu al-zalimin* — and Allah does not like the wrongdoers — is the verdict.

"If you have suffered injuries ˹at Uḥud˺, they suffered similarly ˹at Badr˺" — *in yamsaskum qarhun faqad massa al-qawma qarhun mithluhu* — if a wound touches you, a similar wound has touched the people, the enemy. The *qarh* — the wound, the injury — is the suffering: at Uhud the believers suffered the injuries; at Badr the disbelievers suffered the similar wound. Al-Jalalayn: "If a wound touches you, befalls you at Uhud, a like wound already at Badr has touched the other people, the disbelievers." Ibn Kathir: "if you suffered injuries and some of you were killed, then your enemies also suffered injuries and fatalities." The verse's *mithluhu* — similar to it — is the parallel: the wound of the believers and the wound of the disbelievers — the injury of Uhud and the injury of Badr — the two wounds of the two battles.

"We alternate these days ˹of victory and defeat˺ among people" — *wa tilka al-ayyamu nudawiluha bayna al-nas* — and these days We alternate among people, We deal them out in turn. The *dawlah* — the alternation, the turn — is the distribution: the days of the victory and the defeat are alternated among the people. Al-Jalalayn: "We dispense them among mankind, one day for one group, the next day for another." Ibn Kathir: "at times — out of wisdom — We allow the enemy to overcome you, although the final good end will be yours." The verse's *nudawiluha* — We alternate — is the divine: the *ayyam* — the days — of the victory and the defeat — the alternation among the people; the *bayna al-nas* — among the people — is the distribution: the days are not the constant of one group; the victory and the defeat are the alternation.

"so that Allah may reveal the ˹true˺ believers" — *wa liya'lama Allahu alladhina amanu* — and so that Allah may know those who believe. The *'ilm* — the knowledge — is the knowing: the *liya'lama* — so that He may know — is the revelation of the believers: Ibn Kathir — "'So that We find out who would be patient while fighting the enemies,' according to Ibn 'Abbas"; Al-Jalalayn — "that God may know through knowledge manifested outwardly those who believe who are sincere in their faith from others." The verse's *alladhina amanu* — those who believe — is the revealed: the alternation reveals the true believers — the patient, the sincere, the steadfast — the ones who are known by the test of the days.

"choose martyrs from among you" — *wa yattakhidha minkum shuhada'* — and that He may take witnesses, martyrs from among you. The *shuhada'* — the martyrs, the witnesses — are the ones killed in the cause: Ibn Kathir — "those who would be killed in Allah's cause and gladly offer their lives seeking His pleasure"; Al-Jalalayn — "honouring them with martyrdom." The verse's *yattakhidha* — He takes — is the selection: the martyrs are taken from among the believers — the ones who are chosen, honored, killed in the cause — the *shuhada'* of the alternation.

"and Allah does not like the wrongdoers" — *wa Allahu la yuhibbu al-zalimin* — and Allah does not like the wrongdoers. The *hubb* — the liking, the love — is the negative: Allah does not like the *zalimin* — the wrongdoers. Al-Jalalayn: "the evildoers, the disbelievers — that is, He will chastise them and the blessings He bestows upon them are only a means of drawing out their punishment." The verse's *la yuhibbu* — does not like — is the verdict: the wrongdoers are not the liked; the *zalimin* — the wrongdoers — the disbelievers — are the ones of the punishment — the blessings of the world are the drawing out of their punishment.

For the reader, the verse is the wisdom of the alternation. The believer is taught: the wound of Uhud — the suffering of the believers — is the parallel of the wound of Badr — the suffering of the disbelievers: the *qarh* of the two, the injuries of the two — the similar wounds. And the days — the victory and the defeat — are the alternation: the *dawlah* of the divine — the days dealt out in turn among the people; the *liya'lama* — so that He may know — the revelation of the true believers: the patients, the sincere, the steadfast — the known by the test; the *shuhada'* — the martyrs — the selected, the honored — the ones of the cause. And the *la yuhibbu al-zalimin* — the wrongdoers not liked — is the verdict: the wrongdoers are the ones of the punishment; the blessings of their world draw out the punishment. The believer's confidence: the alternation is the divine wisdom — the revelation of the believers, the selection of the martyrs, and the verdict against the wrongdoers — the days of the world are the alternation, and the good end is the believers'.
