- Ibn Kathir on 2:282 — the Verse of the Debt (Ayat al-Dayn), the longest verse of the Qur'an: the command to record the fixed-term loan; the just scribe required to record (Mujahid and 'Ata'); the debtor's dictated terms with taqwa and no defrauding; the guardian's dictation for the incompetent and the weak; two men or one man and two women — "so that if one of them errs, the other can remind her" (the other woman's testimony mends the first's forgetfulness); the witnesses must not refuse; the writing small or great; "more just, stronger as evidence, prevents doubt"; the immediate transaction's exception; "Let neither scribe nor witness suffer (or cause) any harm" — writing other than dictated, testifying other than heard, concealing testimony. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-282-sora-2.html
- Al-Jalalayn on 2:282 — the two women witnesses and the "reminding" clause as the reason for the choice of two women; the witnesses' acceptability by piety and probity. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-282-sora-2.html
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an on 2:282 — the scribe's wages and the witness's legitimate conveyance allowance as rights; not paying them falls under harming them; the jurists' position. https://islamicstudies.info/quran/maarif.php?sura=2&verse=282&to=283
