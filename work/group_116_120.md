## Verse 116

Verse 116 says: *Indeed, neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah. It is they who will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever.*

In simple terms, the verse states the futility of the disbelievers' worldly strength: neither their wealth nor their children will benefit them against Allah — they will be the residents of the Fire, there forever.

The verse returns to the disbelievers' condition after the portrait of the believing People of the Book (113–115). Ibn Kathir: "Beware, the wealth and children of the disbelievers do not avail them in the least — this is such that they will not save them from the punishment of Allah." The verse's *lan tughni* — will not avail — is the futility; *min Allahi shay'an* — against Allah, in the least — is the absolute; and *ashab al-nar* — the companions of the Fire — is the verdict.

"neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah" — *lan tughni 'anhum amwaluhum wa la awladuhum min Allahi shay'an* — their wealth and children will not avail them against Allah in the least. The *amwal* — the wealth — and the *awlad* — the children — are the two worldly strengths: the resources and the lineage, the property and the family. Al-Jalalayn: "the wealth with which they may ransom themselves, or the children who they may seek help from" — neither the payment of the ransom nor the assistance of the children. Ibn Kathir: "their wealth and children will not benefit them — they will not save them from the punishment of Allah." The verse's *min Allahi shay'an* — against Allah, anything — is the absolute: in the presence of the divine justice, the worldly strengths are nothing; the *tughni* — the avail — is the denial: the wealth of the disbelievers cannot redeem, the children of the disbelievers cannot save.

"It is they who will be the residents of the Fire" — *ula'ika ashabu al-nar* — those are the companions of the Fire. The *ashab al-nar* — the companions, the people of the Fire — is the same verdict of 2:217, 3:10, and the *hum fiha khalidun* — they will abide in it forever: the companions of the Fire are the permanent. The verse's *hum* — they — is the identity: the disbelievers — the ones of the *kufr* — are the companions of the Fire; and the *ashab* — the companions — is the dwelling: the Fire is the abode, not the visitation.

"They will be there forever" — *wa hum fiha khalidun* — and they will abide in it forever. The *khulud* — the permanence, the abiding — is the same word of 3:107: the abiding of the believers in the mercy and the abiding of the deniers in the Fire — the two permanences of the surah's parallel verdicts. Ibn Kathir: "they will abide in the Fire forever." The verse's *fiha* — in it — is the dwelling of the Fire; and the *khalidun* — abiding — is the permanence: the residents of the Fire are not the visitors; they are the dwellers.

The verse's parallels: 3:10 — "Indeed, neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah—and they will be the fuel for the Fire" — the same statement in the opening of the surah's section of the disbelievers; and the same in 2:217, 58:17, 4:18. The *amwal* and the *awlad* of the disbelievers are the repeated futility: the strengths of the world do not avail against Allah. The *wud* — the fuel — of the Fire (3:10) is the same *ashab* — the companions — of 116: the disbelievers are not the visitors but the fuel — the *waqid* — the firewood of the Fire.

For the reader, the verse is the Qur'an's critique of the worldly security. The believer is taught: the wealth and the children — the two human strengths — do not avail the disbeliever against Allah; the *amwal* of the world and the *awlad* of the family are not the currency of the accounting. The verse's *min Allahi shay'an* — against Allah in the least — is the absolute: no ransom, no help, no resource — the *tughni* of the world's strengths is the denial. And the verse's *ashab al-nar* — the companions of the Fire — with the *khalidun* — abiding forever — is the verdict: the disbelievers are the people of the Fire. The believer's lesson: the trust of the world's strengths is the trust of the disbelievers' — the *amwal* and the *awlad* are the means of the world, and the *iman* and the *'amal* — the faith and the deed — are the means of the Hereafter.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 117

Verse 117 says: *The good they do in this worldly life is like the harvest of an evil people struck by a bitter wind, destroying it ˹completely˺. Allah never wronged them, but they wronged themselves.*

In simple terms, the verse gives the parable: the good the disbelievers do in this worldly life is like the harvest of an evil people struck by a bitter wind and destroyed completely — Allah never wronged them; they wronged themselves.

The verse is the parable of the futile expenditure. Ibn Kathir: "'The example of what they spend in this life' — the disbelievers' spending in this life — 'is like the example of a freezing wind that strikes the crops of a people who wronged themselves and destroyed them' — meaning, their expenditure in the worldly life is not built on firm foundations; it will not benefit them in the Hereafter." The verse's *mathal* — the example, the parable — is the picture: the *sirr* — the bitter, freezing wind — and the *harath* — the harvest — and the *zulm* — the wrong — of the self.

"The good they do in this worldly life" — *mathalu ma yunfiquna fi hadhihi al-hayati al-dunya* — the example of what they spend in this worldly life. The *yunfiqun* — they spend — is the expenditure: the charity, the generosity, the good of the disbelievers — the *sadaqat* of the world; and the *fi hadhihi al-hayat al-dunya* — in this worldly life — is the sphere: the good of the world, not the good of the Hereafter. Ibn Kathir: "their expenditure in the worldly life is not built on firm foundations; it will not benefit them in the Hereafter." The verse's *ma yunfiqun* — what they spend — is the comprehensive of the disbelievers' giving: the charity of the world, the good of the life — the deeds without the foundation.

"is like the harvest of an evil people struck by a bitter wind, destroying it ˹completely˺" — *ka mathali rihin fiha sirrun asabat haratha qawmin zalamu anfusahum fa'ahlakat'hu* — like the example of a wind in which is *sirr* that struck the harvest of a people who wronged themselves and destroyed it. The *sirr* — the freezing cold, the frigid, the severe cold — is the key word of the verse. Ibn Kathir: "'sirr' — a frigid, freezing wind" — from Ibn 'Abbas, 'Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas: the *sirr* is the severe cold — the wind that freezes and burns; also from Ibn 'Abbas and Mujahid: "a fire" — the *sirr* is the fire/wind that burns — and the two interpretations agree: the extreme cold burns like the fire, destroying the harvest. The *harath* — the harvest, the crops — is the fruits of the effort: the produce that was struck; and *zalamu anfusahum* — they wronged themselves — is the state: the *zulm* — the wrong — of the owners of the harvest. The *fahlakat* — destroyed completely — is the ruin: the expediture of the disbelievers is the ruin; the good of the world is the wasted.

"It is not Allah who wrongs them, but it is they who wrong themselves" — *wa ma zalamahum Allahu wa lakin anfusahum yazlimun* — and Allah did not wrong them, but they wrong themselves. The verse's *ma zalamahum Allah* — Allah did not wrong them — is the exoneration of the divine; and *lakin anfusahum yazlimun* — but they wrong themselves — is the identification of the wrongdoer. Ibn Kathir: "Allah did not wrong them — the wind, the destruction, the loss — but they wronged themselves: their *zulm* — their disbelief, their evil, their rebellion — is the cause of the ruin." The verse's *zalamu anfusahum* — they wronged themselves — is the same of 3:117: the *zulm* of the disbelievers is the self-wrong; and the *ma zalamahum Allah* is the divine justice: the Lord did not strike the harvest of the innocent; the *zulm* of the self is the cause of the destruction.

The verse's parable, in the interpretation: the *sirr* — the bitter wind — is the calamity that strikes the *harath* — the harvest — of the people who wronged themselves: the *sadaqah* of the disbelievers — their charity, their generosity, their good — is destroyed by the *zulm* of their state: the *kufr* and the sin that strip the deeds' reward. The wind of the *sirr* is the divine permission; the *zulm* of the self is the cause; and the destruction of the harvest is the conclusion. The extremes — the cold and the fire — are the same: the *sirr* that burns by the cold is the *sirr* that burns by the fire — the destruction of the harvest in both.

For the reader, the verse is the parable of the futility without the foundation. The believer is taught: the *sadaqah* and the good of the disbeliever are like the harvest struck by the bitter wind — they are destroyed, not because Allah is unjust but because the *zulm* of the self — the disbelief, the rebellion — strips the reward. The verse's *sirr* — the bitter — is the divine permission; the *zalamu anfusahum* — they wronged themselves — is the cause. And the verse's *ma zalamahum Allah* — Allah never wronged them — is the justice: the Lord of the harvest is not the destroyer; the self's wrong is the destruction. The believer's lesson: the good of the faith and the *taqwa* — the foundation — is the harvest that stands; the good of the world without the foundation is the harvest struck by the wind. The *zulm* — the wrong — is the self's, and the justice is the divine.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 118

Verse 118 says: *O believers! Do not associate closely with others who would not miss a chance to harm you. Their only desire is to see you suffer. Their prejudice has become evident from what they say—and what their hearts hide is far worse. We have made Our revelations clear to you, if only you understood.*

In simple terms, the verse warns the believers: do not associate closely with those outside the faith — they would not miss a chance to harm you, their only desire is to see you suffer, their prejudice shows in what they say, and what their hearts hide is far worse; the revelations have been made clear if you understood.

The verse turns from the condition of the People of the Book (110–117) to the believers' relations with them. Ibn Kathir: "'O you who believe! Take not as your bitanah (advisors, helpers, and intimate friends) those outside your religion' — this is one of the greatest issues; Allah orders the believers not to take the supporters and the companions from among the disbelievers and the hypocrites, for they will not cease their assistance in the disbelievers' favor and will cooperate with them against the believers." The verse's *bitanah* — the intimates, the inner circle — is the key: the trusted advisors of the secrets, the allies of the heart; and the *min dunikum* — outside your circle — is the boundary.

"Do not associate closely with others who would not miss a chance to harm you" — *la tattakhidhu bitanatan min dunikum la ya'lunakum khabala* — do not take intimates from outside yourselves; they will not cease [to cause] you corruption. The *bitanah* — the inner, the intimate, the confidant — is the one of the secrets: the advisor, the companion of the trust; the *min dunikum* — from besides you, outside your circle — is the boundary: the one outside the community of the believers. Al-Jalalayn: "Jews, Christians, and the hypocrites — do not take them as counselors or intimate friends, and do not share your secret affairs with them." The *khabal* — the corruption, the harm, the ruin — is the continuous effort: the *la ya'lun* — they do not cease — is the continuous. Ibn Kathir: "they will not cease their assistance in the disbelievers' favor and will cooperate with them against the believers." Al-Jalalayn: "they are keen on your ruin and corruption."

"Their only desire is to see you suffer" — *waddu ma 'anittum* — they love what troubles you, what distresses you. The *wudd* — the love — of the *'anat* — the hardship, the distress — is the second: the outsiders love the believers' hardship; the *'anat* — toils, troubles — is the suffering, and the *wadd* — the love — is the desire. Ibn Kathir: "they love your hardship and distress." The verse's *'anittum* — you are distressed — is the believers' trial; the *waddu* — they love — is the outsiders' desire: the enemies love to see the believers harmed.

"Their prejudice has become evident from what they say—and what their hearts hide is far worse" — *qad badati al-baghda'u min afwahihim wa ma tukhfi suduruhum akbar* — hatred has appeared from their mouths, and what their breasts hide is greater. The *baghda'* — the hatred, the enmity, the prejudice — is the third: *badat* — appeared — from the mouths: the hatred of the speech. Ibn Kathir: "hatred has appeared from their mouths — their words, their insults, their mockery of your religion." The *sudur* — the breasts, the hearts — is the depth: the *tukhfi* — they conceal — is the hidden; and the *akbar* — greater, far worse — is the comparison: "what their breasts hide is greater — the hatred, the enmity, the deception — than what their mouths show." The verse's *akbar* — the greater — is the warning: the surface of the speech is the lesser; the depth of the hearts is the greater; the *baghda'* of the mouths is the sign, and the *baghda'* of the breasts is the reality.

"We have made Our revelations clear to you, if only you understood" — *qad bayyanna lakum al-ayati in kuntum ta'qilun* — We have made clear to you the signs, if you are reasonable, if you understand. The *tabyin* — the clarification — is the method: the signs of the *bitanah* — the prohibition, the harms, the comparison — are the clarified; and the *ta'qil* — the understanding — is the condition: the signs are clear to the ones who reason. Ibn Kathir: "We have explained the signs to you, if you understand." The verse's *in kuntum ta'qilun* — if you are reasonable — is the appeal: the warning is the addressed; the *'aql* — the reason — is the response.

The hadith and report of Ibn Kathir: Al-Bukhari and An-Nasa'i record Abu Sa'id — "Every Prophet and Khalifah has two types of *bitanah* — an advisor commanding him good and an advisor commanding him evil." The hadith is the illustration: the *bitanah* of the good and the *bitanah* of the evil — the believers are to take the *bitanah* of the good. And Ibn Abi Hatim records from Ibn Abi Ad-Dahqanah: 'Umar refused the Christian scribe of Hirah — "I would then be taking advisors from among the disbelievers" — after he heard this verse. The report is the application: the *bitanah* of the believer — the scribe, the advisor, the confidant — is the believer; and the *khabal* of the outsider — the corruption, the harm — is the avoided.

For the reader, the verse is the Qur'an's most direct command of the community's boundaries. The believer is taught: the *bitanah* — the inner circle, the trusted advisors, the confidants of the secrets — are to be of the believers; the outsider — the one outside the circle — is not the confident of the community. The reasons are the three harms: the *khabal* — the never-ceasing corruption; the *wadd* — the love of the distress; the *baghda'* — the prejudice of the mouths and the greater of the breasts. And the verse's *bayyanna* — the clarification — with the *ta'qilun* — the understanding — is the believer's responsibility: the warning is clear, and the reasoning is the response. The verse's practical: the believers' advisors, the community's secrets, the *bitanah* of the faith — the *bitanah* of the good, per Abu Sa'id's hadith — and the avoidance of the *bitanah* of the evil — per 'Umar's report: the believer's inner circle is the believer's.

## Verse 119

Verse 119 says: *Here you are! You love them but they do not love you, and you believe in all Scriptures. When they meet you they say, “We believe.” But when alone, they bite their fingertips in rage. Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “˹May you˺ die of your rage!” Surely Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart.*

In simple terms, the verse exposes the imbalance: here you are — you love them but they do not love you, and you believe in all the Scriptures; when they meet you they say, "We believe," but alone they bite their fingertips in rage; say, "May you die of your rage!" — Allah knows best what is hidden in the heart.

The verse is the exposure of the relationship's imbalance. Ibn Kathir: "Allah states that the believers love the People of the Book because of their kinship and their pretended friendship, and in their times the believers treated them with friendliness. But the People of the Book do not love the believers, because they oppose their religion." The verse's *ha antum* — here you are — is the rhetorical introduction; the *tuhibbunahum* — you love them — and the *la yuhibbunakum* — they do not love you — is the imbalance; the *tu'minun bil-kitab kullihi* — you believe in all the Books — is the believers' openness; the *idha khalaw 'addu al-anamil* — when alone they bite the fingertips — is the rage's display; and the *mutu bi ghayzikum* — may you die in your rage — is the return.

"Here you are! You love them but they do not love you" — *ha antum ula'i tuhibbunahum wa la yuhibbunakum* — here you are, you love them and they do not love you. The *ha antum* — here you are, behold — is the rhetorical presentation of the imbalance: the verse's *tuhibbun* — you love — is the believers' affection — the *mawaddah* — of the kinship and the friendship; and the *la yuhibbunakum* — they do not love you — is the denial. Ibn Kathir: "the believers love them because of kinship and pretended friendship, but they do not love the believers because they oppose the religion." The imbalance of 119a is the exposure: the *mawaddah* of the believers is the unrequited; the affection that is not returned.

"and you believe in all Scriptures" — *wa tu'minuna bil-kitabi kullihi* — and you believe in all the Scripture. The *tu'minun bil-kitab kullihi* — you believe in all the Books — is the believers' comprehensive faith: the *iman* in the books of Allah — the Torah of Moses, the Gospel of Jesus, the Psalms of Dawud, the Qur'an of Muhammad — the whole of the revelation. Ibn Kathir: "you believe in the Book that was sent down to you and to them, and you believe in the Torah, the Gospel, and the Qur'an." And the context of the imbalance: the believers believe in the whole revelation, and the outsiders do not return the faith — they do not believe in the believers' Book. Al-Jalalayn: "you believe in the whole Scripture — or all the Books — and they do not believe in yours." The imbalance of 119b: the believers' openness to the whole revelation and the outsiders' closure to the believers' — the comprehensiveness and the rejection.

"When they meet you they say, "We believe."" — *wa idha laqukum qalu amanna* — and when they meet you, they say "we believe." The *qalu amanna* — they say "we believe" — is the hypocrisy's surface: the profession of the faith in the meeting, the *aman* — the belief — of the tongue. Ibn Kathir: "when they meet you, they say 'we believe' — they pretend and lie." The verse's *idha laqukum* — when they meet you — is the occasion: the meeting, the encounter; and the *qalu amanna* — they say "we believe" — is the display: the profession of the hypocrites — the *aman* of the meeting, not the *aman* of the heart.

"But when alone, they bite their fingertips in rage" — *wa idha khalaw 'addu 'alaykum al-anamila mina al-ghayz* — and when they are alone, they bite at you the fingertips from the rage. The *khalaw* — they are alone — is the occasion: the privacy, the absence of the believers; and the *'addu al-anamil* — they bite the fingertips — is the display of the *ghayz* — the rage, the fury: the gesture of the extreme anger. Ibn Kathir: "when they are alone, they bite their fingers because of the rage and the fury against you." Al-Jalalayn: "they bite their fingertips in extreme rage." The verse's *min al-ghayz* — from the rage — is the cause: the *ghayz* — the fury — is the rage of the *baghda'* — the hatred, the prejudice — of the hearts (118).

"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ "˹May you˺ die of your rage!"" — *qul mutu bi ghayzikum* — say: may you die in your rage! The *qul* — say — is the address to the Prophet; the *mutu* — die — is the command: the *mut* — the die — of the rage; and the *ghayz* — the rage — is the thing. Al-Jalalayn: "remain in this state until you die — for you will never see what will please you; they will not be able to defeat you." The verse's *mutu* — die — is not the curse of the death but the command of the permanence: the rage that is the state — the *ghayz* of the enemies — is the rage they might die in; and the believers' response is the divine command: the *mutu bi ghayzikum* — the dismissal of the rage, the return of the fury.

"Surely Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart" — *inna Allaha 'alimun bi dhati al-sudur* — indeed, Allah is knowing of what is in the breasts. The *'alimun bi dhati al-sudur* — the knower of the breasts' contents — is the divine knowledge: the *sudur* — the breasts, the hearts — and the *dhat* — the contents, the inner — are known. Ibn Kathir: "Allah knows what is in the hearts — the enmity, the hatred, the hypocrisy." The verse's *'alim* — the knower — is the assurance: the *baghda'* of the breasts (118) is not hidden from the divine; the *ghayz* of the hearts is the known — and the known is the recompensed.

For the reader, the verse is the exposure of the imbalance and the believers' instruction. The believer is taught: the love of the believers is the unrequited; the faith of the believers is the comprehensive — the *iman* of the whole revelation — and the outsiders' is the rejection; the "we believe" of the meeting is the hypocrisy's surface, and the rage of the alone is the reality. And the verse's "May you die of your rage" — is the believers' confidence: the rage of the enemies does not prevail; the *ghayz* of the fury is the enemies' own. And the "Allah knows best what is hidden in the heart" — the knowledge of the hearts — is the believers' security: the *baghda'* that is hidden is known; the known is the recompensed. The believer's program: the love of the faith — not the unrequited love of the enemies — and the *iman* of the whole revelation — not the partial — and the trust in the divine knowledge.

## Verse 120

Verse 120 says: *When you ˹believers˺ are touched with good, they grieve; but when you are afflicted with evil, they rejoice. ˹Yet,˺ if you are patient and mindful ˹of Allah˺, their schemes will not harm you in the least. Surely Allah is Fully Aware of what they do.*

In simple terms, the verse describes the enemies' reactions: they grieve when good touches you and rejoice when evil afflicts you — but if you are patient and mindful of Allah, their schemes will not harm you in the least; Allah is fully aware of what they do.

The verse concludes the section of the relations with the People of the Book. Ibn Kathir: "'If a good befalls you, it grieves them' — the enemies are hurt when good comes to the believers; 'but if some evil overtakes you, they rejoice at it' — the enemies rejoice. 'Yet if you are patient and have Taqwa, their plotting will not harm you at all' — the believers are promised the protection of the divine." The verse's *in tamsaskum hasanah* — if good touches you — is the first; *tasu'hum* — it grieves them — is the reaction; *wa in tusibkum sayyi'ah* — if evil strikes you — is the second; *yafrahu biha* — they rejoice — is the reaction; and the *tasbiru wa tattaqu* — the patience and the taqwa — with the *kayd* — the scheming, the plotting — and the *ihatah* — the encompassing awareness — is the resolution.

"When you ˹believers˺ are touched with good, they grieve" — *in tamsaskum hasanatun tasu'hum* — if good touches you, it grieves them. The *hasanah* — the good — is the good of the believers: the success, the victory, the blessing; and the *tasu'* — it grieves, it harms — is the enemies' grief: the *su'* — the distress — of the good's arrival. Ibn Kathir: "the enemies are hurt when the good comes to the believers." The verse's *tamsaskum* — touches you — is the contact: the *hasanah* — the good — of the believers' affairs; and the *saw'* — the grief — of the enemies is the reverse: the good of the believers is the distress of the enemies.

"but when you are afflicted with evil, they rejoice" — *wa in tusibkum sayyi'atun yafrahu biha* — and if evil strikes you, they rejoice at it. The *sayyi'ah* — the evil, the bad — is the misfortune: the harm, the loss, the defeat; and the *yafrahu* — they rejoice — is the celebration: the enemies' joy at the believers' distress. Ibn Kathir: "the enemies rejoice when evil touches the believers." The verse's pair — the *hasanah* and the *sayyi'ah* — is the mirror: the good of the believers and the evil of the believers — the grief and the rejoicing of the enemies — the *saw'* and the *fara'a* — the distress and the joy — of the outsiders.

"˹Yet,˺ if you are patient and mindful ˹of Allah˺, their schemes will not harm you in the least" — *wa in tasbiru wa tattaqu la yadurrukum kayduhum shay'an* — and if you are patient and have Taqwa, their scheming will not harm you in the least. The *sabr* — the patience — and the *taqwa* — the consciousness — are the two protections; the *kayd* — the scheming, the craft, the cunning — is the enemies' effort; and the *la yadurrukum shay'an* — will not harm you in the least — is the protection. Ibn Kathir: "the believers are promised: if they are patient and have Taqwa, the plotting of the enemies will not harm them." Al-Jalalayn: "their cunning and their plot will not harm you in the least." The verse's *sabr* — the patience — is the endurance: the good and the evil — the *hasanah* and the *sayyi'ah* — are met with the patience; and the *taqwa* — the consciousness of Allah — is the trust: the *kayd* of the enemies is the *kayd* of the created, and the protection is the divine.

"Surely Allah is Fully Aware of what they do" — *inna Allaha bima ya'maluna muhit* — indeed, Allah encompasses what they do. The *ihatah* — the encompassing — is the divine: the *muhit* — the surrounding, the full awareness — of their deeds. Ibn Kathir: "Allah encompasses their deeds — He knows them and He is able to recompense them." The verse's *bima ya'malun* — with what they do — is the deeds of the enemies: the *kayd* — the scheming, the *baghda'* — the prejudice, the *ghayz* — the rage — all are known; and the *muhit* — the encompassed — is the divine: the deeds are not beyond the awareness and the recompense.

For the reader, the verse is the conclusion of the believers' protection and the enemies' futility. The believer is taught: the enemies' reactions are the mirror — the grief at the good and the joy at the evil — and the believers' two protections are the *sabr* and the *taqwa* — the patience and the consciousness — and the *kayd* — the scheming — will not harm them. The verse's *la yadurrukum shay'an* — will not harm them in the least — is the absolute: the *kayd* of the enemies is the *kayd* of the created, and the *ihatah* of the divine is the full awareness. The believer's program: the *sabr* of the good and the bad, the *taqwa* of the conscious, and the trust in the *muhit* — the encompassed — the known and the recompensed. The verse's resolution: the believers' affairs — the good, the evil, the scheming — are all in the divine awareness; and the *sabr* and the *taqwa* are the believers' keys — the patience and the consciousness that make the enemies' scheming the harmless.
