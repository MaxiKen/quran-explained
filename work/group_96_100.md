## Verse 96

Verse 96 says: *Surely the first House ˹of worship˺ established for humanity is the one at Bakkah—a blessed sanctuary and a guide for ˹all˺ people.*

In simple terms, the verse states that the first House of worship established for people is the one at Bakkah — a blessed place and a source of guidance for all people.

The verse answers the second Jewish objection: the change of the qibla from Jerusalem to the Ka'bah. Al-Jalalayn records the occasion: "When they said, 'Our direction of prayer (qibla) came before yours,' the following was revealed." The answer is the priority of the Ka'bah: not the priority of the claim but the priority of the history — the first House of worship established for humanity was at Bakkah. Ibn Kathir: "Allah said, 'Verily, the first House appointed for mankind' — for all people, for their acts of worship and religious rituals. They go around the House in Tawaf, pray in its vicinity and remain in its area in I'tikaf. 'was that at Bakkah' — meaning, the Ka'bah that was built by Ibrahim Al-Khalil, whose religion the Jews and Christians claim they follow. However, they do not perform Hajj to the house that Ibrahim built by Allah's command, and to which he invited the people to perform Hajj."

"the first House ˹of worship˺ established for humanity" — *inna awwala baytin wudi'a lil-nas* — indeed the first house placed for the people. The verse's *awwal* — first — is the claim of the priority: the first house of the worship of the One God. Al-Jalalayn's tradition: "it was built by the angels before the creation of Adam, and after it the Aqsa in Jerusalem was built, a period of forty years separating them, as reported in the hadith of the Two Sahihs" — and in the hadith: "The first thing to appear on the surface of the water at the creation of the heavens and the earth was a white foam underneath which the earth was unrolled." The verse's *bayt* — house — is the first establishment of the worship, the house of the tawhid in the earth.

"is the one at Bakkah" — *lalladhi bi Bakkata* — is that at Bakkah. Bakkah is one of the names of Makkah. Ibn Kathir: "Bakkah means 'it brings Buka' (crying, weeping) to the tyrants and arrogant — meaning they cry and become humble in its vicinity. It was also said that Makkah was called Bakkah because people do Buka next to it, meaning they gather around it. There are many names for Makkah, such as Bakkah, Al-Bayt Al-'Atiq (the Ancient House), Al-Bayt Al-Haram (the Sacred House), Al-Balad Al-Amin (the City of Safety) and Al-Ma'mun (Security). Makkah's names include Umm Rahm (Mother of Mercy), Umm Al-Qura (Mother of the Towns), Salah, as well as others." Al-Jalalayn: "Bakka, a variant of Makka, so called because it 'crushes' (tabukku) the necks of tyrants."

"a blessed sanctuary and a guide for ˹all˺ people" — *mubarakan wa hudan lil-'alamin* — blessed and a guidance for the worlds. The *barakah* — the blessing — is the presence of the divine grace at the house; the *huda* — the guidance — is the orientation of the worship: the qibla of the believers, the direction of the *salat*, the center of the pilgrimage. Ibn Kathir cites the hadith of Abu Dharr (Imam Ahmad, the Two Sahihs): "Which Masjid was the first to be built on the surface of the earth?" — "Al-Masjid Al-Haram (in Makkah)." — "Which was built next?" — "Al-Masjid Al-Aqsa (in Jerusalem)." — "What was the period of time between building the two?" — "Forty years." — "Wherever you may be, and the prayer becomes due, perform the prayer there, for the whole earth was made a Masjid."

Maududi's note: the second objection was the change of the qibla — answered in Surah 2 (142 ff.). The Bible itself testifies that Jerusalem was built by Solomon more than four and a half centuries after Moses (1 Kings 6:1), and that it was during his time that the worshippers of the One God began to pray towards it (1 Kings 8:29–30). It is established by traditions from numerous sources, undisputed throughout Arabia, that the Ka'bah was constructed by Abraham, who lived some eight or nine centuries before Moses. The Ka'bah was older than the Temple of Jerusalem beyond dispute.

The theological and historical point of the verse: the qibla is not the arbitrary choice of the new religion but the return to the origin. The first house of the worship of the One, the house of Adam's descendants, the house of Abraham and Ishmael — the *bayt* of the primordial monotheism — is the direction of the believers of the final revelation. The verse's answer to the Jews: your house — Jerusalem — was built centuries after the Ka'bah; the priority is ours, and the guidance of the house is the guidance of all the worlds — the *hudan lil-'alamin*.

For the reader, the verse is the Qur'an's statement of the cosmic and historical centrality of the Ka'bah. The believer is taught: the direction of the prayer is the direction of the history of the worship; the first house is the house of the tawhid; the qibla is the orientation of the one direction — the worship of Allah alone, from the first house to the last. The verse's *mubarakan* — blessed — is the believer's sense of the sanctity: the house is not a monument of the past but the living center of the worship — the *tawaf*, the *salat*, the *i'tikaf* of every generation. And the verse's *hudan lil-'alamin* — guidance for all people — is the universality of the house: not the sanctuary of a tribe but the first house of humanity, the qibla of the worlds.

## Verse 97

Verse 97 says: *In it are clear signs and the standing-place of Abraham. Whoever enters it should be safe. Pilgrimage to this House is an obligation by Allah upon whoever is able among the people. And whoever disbelieves, then surely Allah is not in need of ˹any of His˺ creation.*

In simple terms, the verse details the marks of the House: it contains clear signs, including the standing-place of Abraham; whoever enters is safe; Hajj to it is a duty owed to Allah by whoever is able; and whoever refuses is in want of nothing from Allah — He needs nothing.

The verse completes the account of the Ka'bah with the signs, the security, the obligation, and the divine independence. Ibn Kathir's treatment covers the four elements in sequence: the visible signs (the Maqam), the sanctuary's security, the obligation of the Hajj, and the kufr of the denier.

"In it are clear signs" — *fihi ayatun bayyinat* — in it are manifest signs. Ibn Kathir: "clear signs that Ibrahim built the Ka'bah and that Allah has honored and blessed it." Al-'Awfi reports Ibn 'Abbas: "Such as the Maqam and Al-Mash'ar Al-Haram." Mujahid: "The impression of Ibrahim's feet remains on the Maqam as a clear sign" — reported similarly by 'Umar bin 'Abdul-'Aziz, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Muqatil bin Hayyan, and others. The signs of the House are the visible proofs of the divine history: the sanctuary of the primordial worship, the traces of the patriarch, the place of the *tawaf* — the *ayat* for the believers.

"and the standing-place of Abraham" — *maqamu Ibrahima* — the station of Ibrahim. Ibn Kathir: "When the building (the Ka'bah) was raised, Ibrahim stood on the Maqam so that he could raise the walls higher, while his son Isma'il was handing the stones to him. We should mention that the Maqam used to be situated right next to the House. Later, and during his reign, 'Umar bin Al-Khattab moved the Maqam farther to the east, so that those who go around the House in Tawaf are able to perform it easily, without disturbing those who pray next to the Maqam after finishing their Tawaf. Allah commanded us to pray next to the Maqam: 'And take you (people) the Maqam (station) of Ibrahim as a place of prayer' (2:125)." The *maqam* is the visible trace of the prophet's act: the stone of the standing, the imprint of the feet, the station of the prayer.

"Whoever enters it should be safe" — *man dakhalahu kana aminan* — whoever enters it is secure. Ibn Kathir: "the Haram of Makkah is a safe refuge for those in a state of fear. There in its vicinity, they will be safe, just as was the case during the time of Jahiliyyah." Al-Hasan Al-Basri: "A man would commit murder, then wear a piece of wool around his neck and enter the Haram. And even when the son of the murdered person would meet him, he would not make a move against him, until he left the sanctuary." The parallels: 29:67 — "Have they not seen that We have made (Makkah) a secure sanctuary, while men are being snatched away from all around them?"; 106:3–4 — "So let them worship the Lord of this House, Who has fed them against hunger and has made them safe from fear." The sanctuary is the divine protection: the *aman* of the house is the sign of the protection of Allah.

The prohibitions of the Haram, per Ibn Kathir: it is not allowed to hunt in the Haram, drive game out of its den, cut the trees, or pick the grass — as the hadiths testify. The Two Sahihs record the hadith of the conquest: "Beware! Allah made this town (Makkah) a sanctuary when He created the heavens and earth, and it is sacred by Allah's decree until the Day of Resurrection. Fighting in Makkah was not permitted for anyone before me, and it was made legal for me for only a few hours or so on that day. It is not allowed to uproot its thorny shrubs, hunt its game, pick up its lost objects except by announcing it, or to uproot its trees" — with the exception of lemongrass. Abu Shurayh's hadith: "Allah, not the people, made Makkah a sanctuary. Therefore, anybody who has belief in Allah and the Last Day should neither shed blood in it nor cut down its trees… Allah allowed me only for a few hours on that day, and today its sanctity is as valid as it was before." Jabir: "None of you is allowed to carry a weapon in Makkah" (Muslim). And the hadith of 'Abdullah bin 'Adi: "By Allah! You are the best of Allah's land and the most beloved land to Allah. Had it not been for the fact that I was driven out of you, I would not have left you" (Ahmad; at-Tirmidhi hasan sahih).

"Pilgrimage to this House is an obligation by Allah upon whoever is able among the people" — *wa lillahi 'ala al-nasi hijju al-bayti mani istata'a ilayhi sabila* — and Hajj to the House is a duty upon the people for Allah, whoever is able to find a way. The verse establishes the obligation of the Hajj. Ibn Kathir: "This Ayah established the obligation of performing Hajj. There are many hadiths that mention it as one of the pillars and fundamentals of Islam, and this is agreed upon by the Muslims. According to texts and the consensus of the scholars, it is only obligatory for the adult Muslim to perform it once during his lifetime." The hadith of Abu Hurayrah: "O people! Hajj has been enjoined on you, therefore, perform Hajj." A man asked, "Is it every year, O Allah's Messenger?" The Prophet remained silent until the man repeated three times, then said: "Had I said yes, it would have become an obligation and you would not have been able to fulfill it. Leave me as I leave you, those before you were destroyed because of their many questions and disputing with their Prophets. If I command you with something, perform it as much as you can. If I forbid something for you, then refrain from it" (Muslim). The meaning of the ability: Ibn 'Umar — "Having provision and a means of transportation"; Al-Hakim's Anas — "Having sufficient provision and a means of transportation"; Ibn 'Abbas — "Whoever intends to perform Hajj, let him rush to perform it" (Ahmad; Abu Dawud).

"And whoever disbelieves, then surely Allah is not in need of ˹any of His˺ creation" — *wa man kafara fa inna Allaha ghaniyyun 'an al-'alamin* — and whoever disbelieves, then indeed Allah is independent of the worlds. Ibn Kathir: "Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid and several others commented on this Ayah: 'Whoever denies the necessity of Hajj becomes a disbeliever, and Allah is far richer than to need him.'" Al-Isma'ili records 'Umar: "Whoever can afford Hajj but did not perform it, there is no difference in his case if he dies while a Jew or Christian" (authentic chain). The verse's *ghaniyy* — the rich, the self-sufficient, the independent — is the divine attribute answering the refusal: the one who denies the obligation denies the right of the One who needs nothing — and the *ghina* of Allah is the measure of the loss of the denier.

For the reader, the verse is the foundation of the Hajj teaching and the doctrine of the sanctuary. The believer is taught: the House carries the signs of the divine history — the *maqam* of the patriarch, the security of the sanctuary, the obligation of the pilgrimage; the Hajj is the once-in-a-lifetime duty of the capable, and the denial of it is the *kufr* of the denial of the right of Allah. The verse's *ghaniyy* — Allah is in need of nothing — is the believer's deepest orientation: the worship is not the service of a needy deity but the response to the self-sufficient One; the obligation is the honor of the believer, not the need of the Lord.

## Verse 98

Verse 98 says: *Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O People of the Book! Why do you deny the revelations of Allah, when Allah is a Witness to what you do?”*

In simple terms, the verse challenges the People of the Book again: why do you reject Allah's revelations while Allah Himself witnesses everything you do?

The verse returns to the address of the People of the Book and is the first of the two questions of the closing appeal. Ibn Kathir's section on 3:98–100 frames them within the warning "Warning Muslims Against Imitating People of the Scriptures" — but the direct address at 3:98 is the question to the People of the Book themselves. The question is the same challenge of 3:70 — *lima takfuruna bi ayat Allah* — why do you disbelieve in the Ayat of Allah — now with the added witness: *wa Allahu shahidun 'ala ma ta'malun* — while Allah is a Witness to what you do.

"O People of the Book! Why do you deny the revelations of Allah" — *ya ahl al-kitabi lima takfuruna bi ayat Allah* — O People of the Book, why do you disbelieve in the revelations of Allah. The *ayat* — the signs, the verses, the revelations — are the ones they had confirmed: the signs of the earlier prophets, the descriptions of the final Messenger in their Books, the clear proofs of the Qur'an. The *kufr* of the verse is the rejection of the known: the *ayat* of Allah were not foreign to the People of the Book; they were the content of their own witness, the inheritance of their own tradition.

"when Allah is a Witness to what you do" — *wa Allahu shahidun 'ala ma ta'malun* — and Allah is a Witness over what you do. The witness of Allah is the third register of the verse: the deeds are not hidden, the denials are not private, the rejections are not unobserved — the Witness is present. Ibn Kathir's framing: the *shahadah* of Allah — the witnessing — is the guarantee of the account: the one who denies the Ayat while Allah witnesses the denial is the one whose denial is recorded. The verse's *shahid* is the counterpart of 3:81's *shahidin* — the witness: Allah witnessed the covenant of the prophets; Allah witnesses the deeds of the People of the Book; and the witness is the same — the divine attention to the human choice.

The verse's place: 3:98–99 are the closing appeal to the People of the Book before the final warning to the believers (3:100–101). The two questions are the surah's last word to the community of the earlier revelations: why do you reject (98)? why do you obstruct (99)? Both are questions of the known truth — the *kufr* and the *sadd* — and both are under the divine witness.

For the reader, the verse is the reminder of the presence of the witness. The believer is taught: the deeds are done before the Witness; the denial of the truth is not the private act of the conscience but the recorded act before the divine attention. The verse's *shahid* — the Witness — is the believer's security and the denier's record: the one who acts in the knowledge of the witness acts in the truth; the one who acts in the denial of the witness acts in the denial of the record's reality. And the verse is the apologist's method in miniature: the question of the inconsistency — you who claim the Book, why do you reject its Ayat? — the question that exposes the contradiction of the witness and the denial.

## Verse 99

Verse 99 says: *Say, “O People of the Book! Why do you turn the believers away from the Way of Allah—striving to make it ˹appear˺ crooked, while you are witnesses ˹to its truth˺? And Allah is never unaware of what you do.”*

In simple terms, the verse asks the second question: why do you block the believers from Allah's way, trying to make it look crooked — while you themselves are witnesses to its truth? Allah is never unaware of what you do.

The verse escalates from the denial of 3:98 to the obstruction. Ibn Kathir: the People of the Book are accused of turning the believers away from the Way of Allah — the *sadd* — the blocking, the barring — and of trying to make the Way appear crooked — the *bugh* of the deviation — while they were the very witnesses of the truth. The accusation combines the two wrongs: the rejection of the truth (98) and the obstruction of the others from it (99).

"why do you turn the believers away from the Way of Allah" — *lima tasudduna 'an sabili Allahi man amana* — why do you bar from the way of Allah those who believe. The *sadd* — the barring, the blocking, the turning away — is the act of the obstruction: the prevention of the believers from the Way, the hindrance of the seekers. Ibn Kathir's parallels: the same charge in 7:86, 11:19, 16:88, 22:25 — the *sadd* of the way of Allah and the *zulm* of the barrers. The act is the obverse of the *da'wah*: the one who cannot convince the believers attempts to divert them.

"striving to make it ˹appear˺ crooked" — *tabghunaha 'iwajan* — seeking to make it crooked. The *'iwaj* — the crookedness, the deviation — is the distortion of the straight path: the attempt to make the *sirat al-mustaqim* appear as the path of the error, the religion of the truth as the path of the crooked. The People of the Book did not merely reject; they sought to render the path crooked in the eyes of others — the *bugh* of the distortion. Ibn Kathir: the description of the Prophet in their Books was hidden; the sign of his coming was concealed; and the straight path was presented as the crooked.

"while you are witnesses ˹to its truth˺" — *wa antum shuhada'* — while you are witnesses. The clause is the crux: the witnesses of the truth are the ones who know the straightness of the path; and the witnesses who know the truth are the ones who seek to make it appear crooked — the contradiction of the knowledge and the act. Ibn Kathir: the *shuhada'* — the witnesses — are the ones who testified to the truth of the path, who found the description of the final Messenger in their Books, who acknowledged the way in their hearts, and who then obstructed it. The verse's accusation is the same shape as 3:70-71: the rejection and the concealment of the known truth.

"And Allah is never unaware of what you do" — *wa ma Allahu bighafilin 'amma ta'malun* — and Allah is not heedless of what you do. The verse's close is the denial of the divine negligence: the *ghaflah* — the heedlessness, the unawareness — is not an attribute of Allah; the works are not lost, the obstructions are not unregistered, the crooked-making is not unseen. Ibn Kathir: the same assurance to the believers — the wrongs of the People of the Book are known to Allah, and the account is with Him. The verse's *la ghafilin* is the guarantee of the believers' security: the ones who are obstructed, diverted, and slandered are not left without the record — the Witness and the Knower sees.

For the reader, the verse is the warning against the obstruction of the way and the comfort of the faithful. The believer is taught: the Way of Allah is straight; the obstructionists of every age have sought to make it appear crooked — the distortion, the slander, the misrepresentation — and the witnesses of the truth are the ones who know better. The verse's *wa antum shuhada'* is the exposure: the ones who know the truth and distort it are the ones whose knowledge is the witness against their act. And the verse's *la ghafilin* is the believer's rest: the wrongs are seen, the deceptions are known, and the account is with the One who is never heedless. The believers' task is the steadfastness in the straight path; the obstructionists' charge is the crookedness they themselves attempt.

## Verse 100

Verse 100 says: *O believers! If you were to yield to a group of those who were given the Scripture, they would turn you back from belief to disbelief.*

In simple terms, the verse warns the believers: if you obey a group of the People of the Book, they will turn you back from belief to disbelief.

The verse is the first of the surah's direct warnings to the believers at the close of the second section. Ibn Kathir titles the passage "Warning Muslims Against Imitating People of the Scriptures": "Allah warns His believing servants against obeying the People of the Book, who envy the believers for the favor that Allah gave them by sending His Messenger." Al-Jalalayn records the occasion: "The following was revealed when the Jews passed by the Aws and the Khazraj and were infuriated by their comradeship and set about reminding them of their mutual hostility in the days before Islam, such that they caused them to quarrel and the two tribes were on the verge of fighting one another."

"O believers!" — *ya ayyuha alladhina amanu* — O you who believe. The address is the believers — the ones who have the faith, who have submitted, who are being warned. The verse's addressees are the strong in the religion of the community: the ones who received the revelation, the ones who had the Messenger among them — and the warning is for them: the faith is not immune to the influence of the others.

"if you were to yield to a group of those who were given the Scripture" — *in tuti'u farigan mina alladhina utu al-kitaba* — if you obey a group of those who were given the Book. The *ita'ah* — the obedience — is the key: not the mere contact or the discussion but the *ita'ah* — the yielding, the heeding, the following of the instruction. The *fariq* — the group, the party — is the specific faction among the People of the Book: the envious, the conspiring, the obstructing group of 3:69–74 and 3:99. Ibn Kathir: the warning is against the ones "who envy the believers for the favor that Allah gave them by sending His Messenger" — the envy of 2:109: "Many of the People of the Scripture wish that they could turn you away as disbelievers after you have believed, out of their own envy."

"they would turn you back from belief to disbelief" — *yaruddukum ba'da imanikum kafirin* — they would turn you back, after your belief, to disbelievers. The *radd* — the returning, the turning back — is the reversal: the believers turned back to the *kufr* after the *iman*. The verse's power is the conditional: *in* — if — the possibility is not the inevitability but the danger: the believers are not to be turned if they do not yield; the warning is the condition of the obedience. Ibn 'Abbas (Tanwir): "they will make you disbelievers in Allah and Muhammad after your belief."

The verse's context — the occasion of Aws and Khazraj — is the historical depth of the warning: the ancient hostility of the two tribes, simmering beneath the brotherhood of Islam, was the instrument the Jews used — the reminder of the pre-Islam enmity, the stirring of the old quarrels, the verge of the fight. The *ita'ah* — the yielding to the promptings of the envious — would have undone the *ukhhuwwah* — the brotherhood — of the believers. The verse's warning is the warning against the *fitnah* — the discord, the stirring of the old divisions by the external agents.

The hadith of the most amazing faith, recorded by Ibn Kathir on 3:101: the Prophet asked, "Who among the faithful believers do you consider has the most amazing faith?" They said, "The angels." He said, "Why would they not believe, since they are with their Lord?" They mentioned the Prophets, and he said, "Why would they not believe while the revelation is sent down to them?" They said, "Then, we are." He said, "Why would not you believe when I am among you?" They asked, "Who has the most amazing faith?" He said, "A people who will come after you and who will find only books that they will believe in." The hadith is the surah's answer to the warning: the faith of the later believers — without the Prophet among them, without the direct revelation — is the most amazing faith; and the warning of 3:100 is the caution to those who have the Prophet among them: the ones who see the evidence should be the most secure in the faith, and the yielding to the envious is the risk of the faith that has the best conditions.

For the reader, the verse is the lasting warning of the surah on the influence of the external agents. The believer is taught: the faith is not the automatic security of the community; the ones who have the best conditions — the revelation, the Messenger, the witness — can be turned if they yield to the envious of the truth. The warning is the *ita'ah* — the obedience: not the contact, not the dialogue, not the learning, but the subservience to the instruction of the envious. The believer's defense is the discernment: the knowledge of the truth, the vigilance against the stirring of the old divisions, and the independence of the faith from the promptings of the external. And the verse's answer is the hadith of the later people: the faith that persists through the books alone is the most amazing; and the faith that has the new revelation among it should be the strongest against the reversal — the *iman* of the ones who see and the *iman* of the ones who believe — both anchored in the truth, both guarded against the yield.

===VERSE-END===
