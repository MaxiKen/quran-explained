## Verse 101

Verse 101 says: *How can you disbelieve when Allah’s revelations are recited to you and His Messenger is in your midst? Whoever holds firmly to Allah is surely guided to the Straight Path.*

In simple terms, the verse asks the believers the impossible question: how could you fall into disbelief while the verses of Allah are being recited to you and the Messenger is among you? And it gives the way out: whoever holds firmly to Allah will be guided to the Straight Path.

The verse answers the warning of 3:100 with the counter-question. Ibn Kathir: "'And how would you disbelieve, while unto you are recited the verses of Allah, and among you is His Messenger?' — meaning, disbelief is far from touching you, since the Ayat of Allah are being sent down on His Messenger day and night, and he recites and conveys them to you." The believers of the Prophet's own time lived in the most privileged conditions of the whole history: the revelation descending, the Messenger present, the proofs in their hands. The question of the verse is not the accusation of the inevitable but the rebuke of the absurd: the disbelief of the one who has the evidence in his own assembly is the unreason of the evidence's self-contradiction.

"How can you disbelieve" — *wa kayfa takfurun* — and how could you disbelieve? Al-Jalalayn: "an interrogative of amazement and rebuke." The *kayfa* — how — is the question of the impossibility of the condition, not of the method: the believer who has the recitation and the Messenger has the means of the faith, and the disbelief of the one who has the means is the unjustified departure. The verse's parallel in 57:8: "And what is the matter with you that you believe not in Allah while the Messenger invites you to believe in your Lord, and He has indeed taken your covenant?" — the same combination of the invitation, the covenant, and the question of the absurd departure.

"when Allah's revelations are recited to you" — *wa antum tutla 'alaykum ayat Allah* — while the Ayat of Allah are recited to you. The *tilawah* — the recitation — is the continuous presence of the revelation: the verses descending day and night, read in the prayer, heard in the assembly. Ibn Kathir: "the Ayat of Allah are being sent down on His Messenger day and night, and he recites and conveys them to you." The believers of the early community did not need to seek the revelation; it came to them. The verse's argument is the argument of the presence: the revelation is not a distant tradition but the living word in the assembly.

"and His Messenger is in your midst" — *wa fikum rasuluhu* — and His Messenger is among you. The second source is the living presence of the Prophet: the one who received the revelation, who taught it, who embodied it, who was the witness of the command. The Believers of the first generation saw the Qur'an in action: the Messenger among them, the model of the faith, the interpreter of the word. The verse's double condition — the recitation and the messenger — is the perfection of the evidence: the word and its embodiment, the message and its messenger, the proof and its example.

The hadith of the most amazing faith, recorded by Ibn Kathir, is the verse's profound commentary: the Prophet asked his Companions, "Who among the faithful believers do you consider has the most amazing faith?" They said, "The angels." He said, "Why would they not believe, since they are with their Lord?" They mentioned the Prophets, and he said, "Why would they not believe while the revelation is sent down to them?" They said, "Then, we are." He said, "Why would not you believe when I am among you?" They asked, "Who has the most amazing faith?" He said, "A people who will come after you and who will find only books that they will believe in." The hadith is the mirror of the verse: the faith of the ones who have the Messenger among them is the faith of the evidence; the faith of the ones who come after with the books alone is the faith of the heart. The verse's question to the first generation is the honor of their condition; the hadith's answer is the honor of the later generations. And both are the faith: the believers of every time hold the rope.

"Whoever holds firmly to Allah is surely guided to the Straight Path" — *wa man ya'tasim billahi faqad hudiya ila siratin mustaqim* — and whoever holds firmly to Allah, then he has been guided to a straight path. The *i'tisam* — the holding, the clinging, the taking of the firm hold — is the verse's way out: the believers who face the risk of the departure are given the countermeasure — the clinging to Allah. The *sirat al-mustaqim* — the Straight Path — is the same path of the prayer of the Fatihah (1:6–7): the path of the prophets and the blessed, not of those who earned anger or went astray. Ibn Kathir: "trusting and relying on Allah are the basis of achieving the right guidance and staying away from the path of wickedness. They also represent the tool to acquiring guidance and truth and achieving the righteous aims."

For the reader, the verse is the surah's answer to the faith of the evidence and the faith of the absence. The believers are taught: the faith is not the passive reception of the proofs; it is the firm hold on the One who gives the proofs. The one who has the recitation and the Messenger and departs has no excuse; and the one who has only the books and the absence of the Messenger and believes has the firmest hold — the *i'tisam* of the heart. The verse's standard is the hold: the *i'tisam* — the clinging that does not let go, the dependence that does not waver — is the guidance to the Straight Path. And the believer of every generation is given the method: the recitation of the revelation, the emulation of the Messenger, and the firm hold on Allah — the three that make the disbelief of the impossible the disbelief of the abandoned.

## Verse 102

Verse 102 says: *O believers! Be mindful of Allah in the way He deserves, and do not die except in ˹a state of full˺ submission ˹to Him˺.*

In simple terms, the verse commands the believers to have true consciousness of Allah — to fear Him as He truly deserves — and to die only in a state of full submission to Him.

The verse is the surah's supreme command of the *taqwa* at the height of its address. Ibn Kathir titles the passage "Meaning of 'Taqwa of Allah'": the verse's *ittaqu Allah haqqa tuqatihi* — have Taqwa of Allah as is His due — is the fullest form of the divine consciousness. The command is the foundation of the whole section: the believers, warned against the disbelief of 3:100–101, are given the *taqwa* and the *islam* as the orientation of the life and the death.

"Be mindful of Allah in the way He deserves" — *ittaqu Allah haqqa tuqatihi* — fear Allah, be conscious of Allah with the true consciousness. Ibn Abi Hatim records Ibn Mas'ud: "That He is obeyed and not defied, remembered and not forgotten, and appreciated and not unappreciated" (authentic chain; Al-Hakim's Mustadrak). The definition is the completeness of the *taqwa*: the obedience that does not defy, the remembrance that does not forget, the gratitude that does not deny. Anas's report: "The servant will not have Taqwa of Allah as is His due until he keeps his tongue idle" — the *taqwa* of the heart is the *taqwa* of the tongue: the one who controls the speech controls the fire. The *haqqa tuqatihi* — the due of His taqwa — is the command of the absolute: the consciousness of Allah that is not partial, not occasional, not convenient, but the whole of the life's orientation.

"and do not die except in ˹a state of full˺ submission ˹to Him˺" — *wa la tamutunna illa wa antum muslimun* — and do not die except while you are Muslims, submitters. The command of the life is the command of the death: the believer is to live in the submission so that the death finds him in it. Ibn Kathir: "preserve your Islam while you are well and safe, so that you die as a Muslim. The Most Generous Allah has made it His decision that whatever state one lives in, that is what he dies upon and is resurrected upon. We seek refuge from dying on other than Islam." The verse's *illa* — except — is the condition of the life: the believer lives as a Muslim so that he dies as a Muslim; the submission is not the final act but the continuous state that the final act finds.

The Mujahid-Ibn 'Abbas hadith, recorded by Ibn Kathir (at-Tirmidhi, an-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah, Ibn Hibban, Al-Hakim — hasan sahih): the Prophet recited this verse and then said, "Verily, if a drop of Zaqqum falls, it will spoil life for the people of the earth. What about those whose food is only from Zaqqum?" The hadith is the teaching of the *targhib wa al-tarhib* — the promise and the warning: the *taqwa* is the consciousness of the reality, and the reality of the default is the severity the *taqwa* avoids. The hadith's question — what about those whose food is only Zaqqum? — is the rhetorical teaching of the stakes of the departure.

Imam Ahmad records Jabir: three nights before the Messenger's death, he was heard saying, "None of you should die except while having sincere trust in Allah, the Exalted and Most Honorable" (also Muslim). The hadith is the verse's completion: the *islam* of the death is the *husn al-zann* — the good opinion, the trust in Allah. The Two Sahihs record Abu Hurayrah: Allah said, "I am as My servant thinks of Me." The dying believer's trust is the reflection of the living believer's *taqwa*: the one who lived in the consciousness of Allah dies in the trust of Allah; the one who lived in the obedience dies in the hope; and the one who lived in the submission dies in the submission.

For the reader, the verse is the Qur'an's definition of the faithful life and the faithful death. The believer is taught: the *taqwa* is not the abstract reverence but the concrete obedience — the obeying that does not defy, the remembering that does not forget, the appreciating that does not deny; and the *islam* is not the label but the state — the submission that finds the death. The verse's command of the death is the command of the life: the one who lives as a Muslim dies as a Muslim; the death is the snapshot of the life, and the resurrection is the snapshot of the death. The believer's practical program is in the verse: the continuous obedience, remembrance, and gratitude — the *taqwa* that fills the life and shapes the death — and the trust in Allah that the death meets.

## Verse 103

Verse 103 says: *And hold firmly to the rope of Allah and do not be divided. Remember Allah’s favour upon you when you were enemies, then He united your hearts, so you—by His grace—became brothers. And you were at the brink of a fiery pit and He saved you from it. This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so that you may be ˹rightly˺ guided.*

In simple terms, the verse commands the believers to hold firmly to Allah's rope together and not to divide; it reminds them of the favor of unity — they had been enemies, He joined their hearts, and by His grace they became brothers; they were on the edge of the fire and He saved them.

The verse is the surah's most beloved command of the unity and one of the most recited verses of the Qur'an. Ibn Kathir titles the section "The Necessity of Holding to the Path of Allah"; Al-Jalalayn identifies the *habl Allah* — the rope of Allah — as "His religion," with the command "together" and the warning "do not scatter after submitting to Islam." The verse's occasion per Al-Jalalayn: the Aws and the Khazraj — the two tribes of Madinah who were enemies before Islam, until Allah joined their hearts through Islam so that by His grace they became brothers in religion and comradeship; and they were upon the brink of the pit of fire, such that to fall into it they only had to die in disbelief, but He delivered them through belief.

"hold firmly to the rope of Allah" — *wa i'tasimu bi habli Allahi jami'an* — and hold firmly to the rope of Allah, all of you. The *habl* — the rope — is the bond, the covenant, the religion — the same word used of the covenant of protection in 3:112 (*hablun min Allah* — a covenant from Allah). The *i'tisam* — the holding — is the same verb of 3:101's *i'tasam*: the firm grasp. The believers are commanded to hold the rope *jami'an* — all together — the unity as the form of the holding: the rope is held by the community, not the individual; the division is the release of the rope. Ibn Kathir: the rope of Allah — the Qur'an and His religion — is the single bond; the community's grasp of it is the community's preservation.

"and do not be divided" — *wa la tafarraqu* — and do not scatter, do not divide. The *tafarruq* — the splitting, the scattering — is the opposite of the *i'tisam*: the release of the rope, the sectarian division, the loss of the brotherhood. The command's *la* is the prohibition of the division: not the harmless difference of the scholars but the shattering of the community's unity. Ibn Kathir: the division of the *firqah* — the partisanship — is the release of the single rope; the *sunnah and jama'ah* — the way and the community — is the holding.

"Remember Allah's favour upon you when you were enemies, then He united your hearts, so you—by His grace—became brothers" — *wa udhkur nif'mata Allahi 'alaykum idh kuntum a'da'an fa'allafa bayna qulubikum fa'asbahum bi ni'matihi ikhwan* — the memory is the basis of the command: the believers were enemies — the *Aws* and the *Khazraj*, the tribes of the pre-Islamic blood-feuds — and Allah united their hearts; by His grace they became *ikhwan* — brothers. The *ta'lif* — the joining, the uniting — is the divine act: not the diplomacy, not the treaty, not the common interest, but the joining of the hearts. The brotherhood of the faith is the brotherhood of the heart: the *ukhuwwah* of the Muslims is the work of Allah, and the memory of it is the preservation of it.

"And you were at the brink of a fiery pit and He saved you from it" — *wa kuntum 'ala shafa hufratin mina al-nari fa anqadhakum minha* — and you were on the edge of a pit of the Fire and He rescued you from it. The *hufrah* — the pit — is the grave of the disbelief: the pre-Islamic state was the edge of the abyss, and the Islam was the rescue. Al-Jalalayn: "to fall into it you only had to die disbelieving; but He delivered you from it through belief." The *anqadh* — He rescued — is the divine deliverance: the believers of the early community were not the authors of their salvation; they were the rescued ones. The verse's picture is the picture of the mercy: the edge of the pit and the hand of the rescue; the abyss of the condition and the deliverance of the faith.

"This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so that you may be ˹rightly˺ guided" — *kadhalika yubayyinu Allahu lakum ayatihi la'allakum tahtadun* — thus Allah makes clear to you His signs so that you might be guided. The *tabyin* — the making clear — is the method of the Qur'an: the signs of the unity, the memory, and the rescue are the evidences of the guidance. Ibn Kathir: the clear signs are the evidences of the divine favor; the *la'allakum* — so that — is the purpose: the guidance, the *huda*.

For the reader, the verse is the charter of the Muslim community's unity and the most powerful rebuke of its division. The believer is taught: the unity is not the political convenience but the divine gift; the brotherhood is not the common interest but the joined hearts; the community is not the tribal confederation but the *ikhwan* of the grace. The verse's command is the *i'tisam* — the holding of the rope together — and the memory is the preservation: the believers who remember the enemies they were and the brothers they became are the believers who hold the rope. The verse's warning is the *tafarruq* — the division — and the verse's rescue is the *anqadh* — the deliverance from the edge of the pit. The believer's practice: the holding of the Qur'an and the Sunnah together, the brotherhood of the hearts, and the memory of the rescue — the three that are the *habl* of Allah and the preservation from the pit.

## Verse 104

Verse 104 says: *Let there be a group among you who call ˹others˺ to goodness, encourage what is good, and forbid what is evil—it is they who will be successful.*

In simple terms, the verse commands that a group among the believers should arise to call people to goodness, to command the right, and to forbid the wrong — and it is they who will be successful.

The verse is the Qur'an's foundational command of the *da'wah* and the *hisbah* — the call and the accountability. Ibn Kathir titles the passage "The Command to Establish the Invitation to Allah": "Let there arise out of you a group of people that calls to righteousness, enjoins all that is good and forbids evil in the manner Allah commanded," and "it is they who are the successful."

"Let there be a group among you" — *waltakun minkum ummatun* — and let there be from you a community, a group. The *ummah* here is not the whole community but the dedicated segment: the *ummah* within the *ummah*, the group of the callers. Ad-Dahhak: "They are a special group of the Companions and a special group of those after them, that is those who perform Jihad and the scholars." Ibn Kathir: "The objective of this Ayah is that there should be a segment of this Muslim Ummah fulfilling this task, even though it is also an obligation on every member of this Ummah, each according to his ability." The verse's structure is the Qur'anic balance: the collective obligation of the whole (*fard kifayah* — the communal duty) and the dedicated group that carries it.

"who call ˹others˺ to goodness" — *yad'una ila al-khayr* — calling to the good. The *da'wah* — the call — is the first function: the invitation to the good, the summoning to the faith, the call of the truth. The *khayr* — the good — is the comprehensive: the good of the faith, of the worship, of the conduct, of the community — the good that the religion identifies and the callers summon to.

"encourage what is good, and forbid what is evil" — *wa ya'muruna bil-ma'ruf wa yanhawna 'an al-munkar* — and they enjoin the ma'ruf and forbid the munkar. The *ma'ruf* — the known good, the recognized right — and the *munkar* — the denied evil, the repudiated wrong — are the Qur'an's categories of the moral command. The *amr bil-ma'ruf* and the *nahy 'an al-munkar* — the commanding of the good and the forbidding of the evil — are the defining functions of the Muslim community (3:110, 9:71, 22:41, 31:17). Ibn Kathir's hadith collection on the verse: Muslim records Abu Hurayrah — "Whoever among you witnesses an evil, let him change it with his hand. If he is unable, then let him change it with his tongue. If he is unable, then let him change it with his heart, and this is the weakest faith" (and in another narration, "there is no faith beyond that, not even the weight of a mustard seed"). Imam Ahmad records Hudhayfah — "By He in Whose Hand is my soul! You will enjoin righteousness and forbid evil, or Allah shall send down a punishment from Him to you. Then, you will supplicate to Him, but He will not accept your supplication" (at-Tirmidhi: hasan). The hadiths are the verse's practical method: the gradation of the hand, the tongue, and the heart — and the warning of the abandonment.

"it is they who will be successful" — *wa ula'ika humu al-muflihun* — and those are the successful ones. The *falah* — the success, the prosperity, the salvation — is the Qur'an's highest verdict (2:5, 23:1, 62:10): the success of the callers is the success of the *falah* — the salvation and the prosperity. The verse's *hum* — they — is the identity: the callers of the good, the commanders of the right, the forbidders of the wrong are the *muflihun*. The success is the success of the function: the community that calls and commands and forbids is the community that succeeds.

For the reader, the verse is the Qur'an's command of the community's mission. The believer is taught: the faith is not the private piety; it is the communal mission — the call to the good, the commanding of the right, the forbidding of the wrong. The verse's structure is the balance of the collective and the specific: the group of the callers within the community of the believers, each member carrying his share according to his ability. The method is the hadith's gradation: the hand, the tongue, and the heart — the action of the power, the word of the speech, and the conviction of the heart; and the abandonment is the warning of the withheld prayer. And the verdict is the *falah*: the callers are the successful — not the successful of the world's measure but the successful of the divine verdict, the *muflihun* of the Qur'an.

## Verse 105

Verse 105 says: *And do not be like those who split ˹into sects˺ and differed after clear proofs had come to them. It is they who will suffer a tremendous punishment.*

In simple terms, the verse warns the believers: do not follow the pattern of those who split into sects and disagreed after the clear proofs had reached them — they will face a tremendous punishment.

The verse is the negative counterpart of 3:103–104. Ibn Kathir: "In this Ayah, Allah forbids this Ummah from imitating the division and discord of the nations that came before them. These nations also abandoned enjoining righteousness and forbidding evil, although they had proof of its necessity." The verse's *la takunu* — do not be — is the prohibition of the imitation; the *kalladhina tafarruqu* — like those who split — is the negative model; and the *ba'da ma ja'ahum al-bayyinat* — after the clear proofs came to them — is the forfeiture of the excuse.

"do not be like those who split ˹into sects˺ and differed" — *wa la takunu kalladhina tafarruqu wa ikhtalafu* — and do not be like those who split and differed. The *tafarruq* and the *ikhtilaf* — the splitting and the differing — are the two movements of the sectarian collapse: the splitting into the *firq* — the parties, the factions — and the differing in the religion. Ibn Kathir's context: the *ahl al-kitabayn* — the People of the Two Scriptures — divided; the difference is the difference after the proofs, not the legitimate *ikhtilaf* of the scholars but the sectarian *ikhtilaf* of the factions.

"after clear proofs had come to them" — *min ba'di ma ja'ahum al-bayyinat* — after the clear proofs came to them. The *bayyinat* — the clear proofs, the manifest evidences — are the same word of 3:86: the proofs the rejecters had and rejected. The qualification is the verdict's crux: the division of the ignorant is the error; the division of the proven is the rebellion. Ibn Kathir: the nations before "had proof of its necessity" and abandoned it — the division after the proofs is the division of the knowing. The *bayyinat* also includes the clear proofs of the unity: the message of the prophets, the evidence of the oneness, the proofs of the Qur'an. The sects of the two scriptures divided after the proofs: the truth was clear, and they divided about it.

Imam Ahmad records the hadith of Abu 'Amir 'Abdullah bin Luhay: "We performed Hajj with Mu'awiyah bin Abi Sufyan. When we arrived at Makkah, he stood up after praying Zuhr and said, 'The Messenger of Allah said: The People of the Two Scriptures divided in their religion into seventy-two sects, and this Ummah will divide into seventy-three sects — meaning the desires — all of them in the Fire except one — and it is the Jama'ah. And some of my Ummah will be guided by desire, like one who is infected by rabies; no vein or joint will be saved from these desires.' Then Mu'awiyah said, 'By Allah, O Arabs! If you do not adhere to what came to you from your Prophet, then other people are even more prone not to adhere to it'" (Abu Dawud records it from Ahmad bin Hanbal and Muhammad bin Yahya). The hadith is the verse's commentary: the division of the seventy-three, the one saved — the *jama'ah* — and the desires that run through the veins like rabies.

"It is they who will suffer a tremendous punishment" — *ula'ika lahum 'adhabun 'azim* — those are for them a great punishment. The *'adhab 'azim* — the tremendous punishment — is the verdict of the split after the proofs. The verse's *hum* — they — is the identity: the dividers, the differers, the sectarians of the proven truth are the ones of the great punishment. Ibn Kathir's treatment of the next verse: the faces of the followers of the Sunnah and the Jama'ah radiate with whiteness, and the faces of the followers of the innovation and the division are darkened — the punishment of the division is the darkness of the face at the gathering.

For the reader, the verse is the Qur'an's warning against the sectarianism and the command of the unity's preservation. The believer is taught: the difference of the scholars is the mercy (the *ikhtilaf* of the *rukhsah*), but the division of the community after the proofs is the *'adhab 'azim* — the great punishment. The verse's standard is the *bayyinat*: the proofs were clear, and the division after them is the rebellion; the sectarianism of the proven is the sin of the knowing. The hadith of the seventy-three is the model: the one saved is the *jama'ah* — the community, the congregation of the truth — and the desires that infect like rabies are the desires of the faction. The believer's program is in the verse: the unity of the community, the avoidance of the sectarian division, the adherence to the *jama'ah* — and the preservation of the faith from the desires that would split it. The verse's warning is the warning of the face's darkness at the gathering — the recompense of the division after the proofs.
