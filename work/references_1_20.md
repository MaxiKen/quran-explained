- Al-Jalalayn on 3:1–4 — the Qur'an enveloped by the truth, confirming the previous Books; the Torah and the Gospel revealed in order. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-3-sora-3.html
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an (Mufti Muhammad Shafi) on 3:1–4 — the revelation in truth with no doubt or suspicion; the Ever-Living, the One Who sustains all that exists. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-3-sora-3.html
- Tanwir al-Miqbas (Ibn 'Abbas) on 3:3–4 — Gabriel brought the Scripture with the truth, confirming the divine Oneness; the Torah to Moses, the Gospel to Jesus. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-3-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir on 3:5–6 — perfect knowledge of the heavens and earth; the shaping in the wombs as He wills (male or female, handsome or otherwise, happy or miserable); the argument that 'Isa was a created servant shaped in the womb, cf. 39:6; Al-Jalalayn on the forms in the womb; Tanwir al-Miqbas (Ibn 'Abbas) on short/tall, pretty/ugly, male/female, felicitous/damned; Maududi, *Tafhim al-Qur'an* (note 4) — no being knows human nature as well as God; the One who provides from conception will not fail to provide guidance. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-6-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir on 3:7 — "The Mutashabihat and Muhkamat Ayat": the muhkamat are the entirely clear foundations (the rulings, the allowed, prohibited, limits, obligations); the mutashabihat are not entirely clear (abrogated ayat, parables, oaths); the deviant-hearted follow the mutashabih to alter its meanings; Muhammad bin Ishaq's comment on the muhkam as proof of the Lord and the mutashabih as a test; the example of Christians citing "Ruhullah" against the clear verses 43:59 and 3:59; the hadith (al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, from 'A'ishah): "When you see those who seek the ambiguous verses in it, they are those whom Allah has named. Beware of them!"; the four types of tafsir (Ibn 'Abbas); "all of it is from our Lord" — both kinds are true and each testifies to the truth of the other. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-8-sora-3.html (verse 7–9 tafsir section)
- Ibn Kathir on 3:8–9 — "Do not deviate our hearts from the guidance after You allowed them to acquire it. Do not make us like those who have wickedness in their hearts, those who follow the Mutashabih…"; Al-Jalalayn on the deviation from the truth; "grant us mercy from You"; "Our Lord, Verily, it is You Who will gather mankind together on the Day about which there is no doubt"; the believers' conviction in the unbreakable promise. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-8-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir on 3:10–11 — "On the Day of Resurrection, No Wealth or Offspring Shall Avail": the disbelievers as the fuel of the Fire (cf. 21:98, 9:55, 40:52); *ka-da'b* as "like the behavior of the people of Fir'awn" (Ad-Dahhak from Ibn 'Abbas; 'Ikrimah, Mujahid, Abu Malik); the same end the people of Firawn and previous nations met; Al-Jalalayn on 'Ad and Thamud; "Allah is severe in punishment" — none can escape His grasp. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-10-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir on 3:12 — "Threatening the Jews With Defeat and Encouraging Them to Learn a Lesson From the Battle of Badr": Muhammad bin Ishaq on the Prophet gathering the Jews in the marketplace of Bani Qaynuqa' after Badr; Al-Jalalayn on the Jews' reply ("Do not fool yourself…") and the fulfillment of the verse in this world and the Hereafter; *bi'sa al-mihad* — the evil resting place (cf. 3:197). https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-12-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir on 3:13 — the two camps at Badr; Muslims in the cause, Quraysh idolaters in disbelief; the two explanations of "twice their number" (the idolaters' illusion vs. the Muslims' sight), with Ibn Mas'ud's report and Ibn 'Abbas (Tanwir): 313 believers against Abu Sufyan's 950; Al-Jalalayn on the Muslims' numbers; the divine management of perception (8:44, 8:42); "a lesson for those who have intelligence and sound comprehension." https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-12-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir on 3:14–15 — "The True Value of This Earthly Life": the delights Allah placed in this life, women first because the test with them is more tempting (hadith: "I did not leave behind me a test more tempting to men than women"); the hadiths on marriage, righteous wives, and the prayer as the comfort of the eye; the purpose of children and wealth (pride vs. worship and kindness); the qintar (Abu Hurayrah: twelve thousand uqiyah, recorded by Ibn Jarir); horses and their three purposes; "Shall I inform you of something far better than those?" — Gardens with the Lord, rivers beneath, eternity, purified spouses, and Allah's pleasure above all; Al-Jalalayn on the affirmative interrogative, the purified spouses, and the readings ridwan/rudwan; Allah is Seer of the servants, requiting each according to his deeds. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-15-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir on 3:16–17 — "The Supplication and Description of Al-Muttaqin": "We have indeed believed — in You, Your Book, and Your Messenger"; the five marks — the patient in obedience and against prohibitions, the truthful concerning the proclamation of faith, the obedient, the spenders (kith and kin, the needy, the destitute), and the seekers of forgiveness in the last hours of the night; Ya'qub's waiting for the latter part of the night (12:98); the hadith of the descent in the last third of the night ("Is there anyone seeking My forgiveness, so that I may forgive him?") recorded in the Two Sahihs, Musnad, and Sunan; 'A'ishah on Witr; Ibn 'Umar's practice (collected by Ibn Abi Hatim); Al-Jalalayn on the last part of the night as the time of unawareness and the joy of sleep; Maududi (note 13) on steadfastness in the cause of Truth. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-17-sora-3.html
- Ibn Kathir on 3:18–20 — "The Testimony of Tawhid": Allah's testimony through proofs and signs; the angels and those having knowledge bear witness; "this ayah emphasizes the great virtue of those who have knowledge"; "(He) always maintains His creation in justice"; Al-Jalalayn on the testimony of the prophets and the believers through conviction and words; "Truly, the religion with Allah is Islam" (cf. 3:85); the People of the Scripture did not differ except out of rivalry after knowledge came; "say: I have submitted myself to Allah, and (so have) those who follow me"; the call to the People of the Book and the illiterates (Arab pagans); "if they turn away, your duty is only to convey the Message" — their reckoning is with Allah. https://www.alim.org/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/surah/3/19/
