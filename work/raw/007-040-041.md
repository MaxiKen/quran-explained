<<<VERSE 7:40>>>
The earlier verses spoke of messengers coming to every people. They are understood as a covenant that each person made before birth, in the world of spirits. When Allah's prophets came with guidance, people would listen in earnest and act on it. Those who kept the pledge would be saved from sorrow and earn lasting peace, and those who denied the prophets would face the endless punishment of Jahannam. Now the surah shows what happened when people came into the world. Some threw the pledge behind their backs, and others held to it and did good. Verses 40 and 41 describe the deniers, and verses 42 and 43 the believers who kept faith: "Surely those who receive our revelations with denial and arrogance, the gates of heaven will not be opened for them, nor will they enter Paradise until a camel passes through the eye of a needle. This is how We reward the wicked".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Surely those who receive our revelations with denial and arrogance"**: those who reject Allah's clear signs and verses, Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an. They are too proud to believe in them or follow them, and so they have lost everything.

**"the gates of heaven will not be opened for them"**: this has two explanations, given in the sections below. Their deeds and their supplication will not rise through those gates, and their souls will not be let in.

**"nor will they enter Paradise until a camel passes through the eye of a needle."** The verb yalij comes from wulūj, entering a narrow opening. Jamal is a camel, and samm the eye of a needle. Ibn Masʿūd said the word means a male camel born of a she-camel, and another narration calls it the she-camel's mate. The camel is one of the largest animals and the eye of a needle one of the smallest openings. Its passing through is impossible in practice, and so is their entry into Paradise. The point is that their punishment in Hell is everlasting.

Mujāhid and ʿIkrimah reported that Ibn ʿAbbās used to recite the word as jummal, a thick rope. On that reading, Paradise stays closed to them until a rope can be threaded through a needle.

**"This is how We reward the wicked"**: so Allah repays those who sin through disbelief, the idolaters, and all who stubbornly do wrong.

### What does "the gates of heaven will not be opened" mean?

One explanation is that their good deeds and supplication will not rise through those gates. Mujāhid and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said this, as did Ibn ʿAbbās in the reports of al-ʿAwfī and ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah. ʿAṭāʾ also reported it from Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ records from Ibn ʿAbbās that the gates will be opened neither for them nor for their prayers. Their prayers will not be answered. Their deeds will be kept from reaching the place where the deeds of Allah's righteous servants are stored, which the Qur'an calls ʿIlliyyīn (83:18-21, in Sūrat al-Muṭaffifīn). Another verse touches on the same theme: good words rise to Allah, and good deeds lift them. A person's good deeds are what carry his good words up to Allah.

The second explanation is that the gates of heaven will not be opened for the disbelievers' souls, which will be thrown down. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported this from Ibn ʿAbbās, and it is reported from other Companions too. As-Suddī and several others gave the same meaning.

Ibn Jurayj joined the two. The gates, he said, will not be opened for their deeds or for their souls. That combines both meanings, and Allah knows best.

### What happens to the soul at death?

A hadith of al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib supports the second explanation. Ibn Jarīr reported it, and Abū Dāwūd, an-Nasāʾī, Ibn Mājah and Imam Aḥmad recorded it in full. In summary it runs as follows.

The Prophet ﷺ went to the funeral of a man from the Anṣār. The grave was not yet ready, so he sat down in an open spot, and the Companions sat silently around him. Then he raised his head and spoke.

When death comes to a believing servant, angels with white, shining faces come down from heaven. They bring a shroud and perfume from Paradise and sit before the dying man. Then the angel of death, ʿIzrāʾīl, comes and addresses his rūḥ, his soul: "O soul at peace, come out to the forgiveness and pleasure of your Lord." The soul leaves the body smoothly, like water flowing from the mouth of an opened water-skin. The angel of death takes it in his hands and gives it to the waiting angels, who set off with it.

Each group of angels they pass asks, "Whose pure soul is this?" They answer with the most honourable names he was known by in the world: so-and-so, son of so-and-so. At the first heaven they ask for the gate to be opened, and it is opened. More angels join them at each heaven until they reach the seventh. Then Allah says, "Write My servant's record in ʿIlliyyīn, and send him back." The soul returns to the grave.

There the angels of account sit him up and ask, "Who is your Lord?" and "What is your religion?" He answers, "Allah is my Lord, and Islam is my religion." Then they ask, "Who is this man who was sent to you?" He says, "He is the Messenger of Allah." A call comes from heaven: "My servant has spoken the truth. Spread for him a carpet from Jannah, dress him in the clothing of Paradise, and open for him a door toward Jannah." Its fragrance and breeze reach him through that door. His good deeds come to him in a handsome form, to keep him company and put him at ease.

When death comes to a disbeliever and denier, angels with dark, terrifying faces come down from heaven. They carry a coarse, ragged cloth and sit before him. The angel of death pulls out his soul as a thorny branch is dragged out through wet wool wrapped around it. When the soul comes out, its stench is worse than that of a rotting carcass. The angels set off with it.

Each group of angels they meet asks, "Whose foul soul is this?" They answer with the worst names he was known by in the world: so-and-so, son of so-and-so. At the first heaven they ask for the gate to be opened, but it is not opened for him. Then the Prophet ﷺ recited, "the gates of heaven will not be opened for them". The command comes to place his record in Sijjīn, where the records of the disobedient are kept. His soul is flung down and returns to the grave.

The angels of account sit him up and ask him the same questions. To every one he can only say, "I do not know." He is given a bed from Jahannam and clothing from Jahannam. A door is opened in his grave toward Jahannam, and its heat and flames keep reaching him. His grave is made to close tight around him. We seek refuge with Allah from it.

In short, the souls of deniers and disbelievers are carried up to the gate of heaven. When it does not open, they are thrown back down. Instead of rising, they are taken down to Sijjīn. The believer's soul, by contrast, has the gates opened for it and is carried up to the seventh heaven. So the verse may also mean that the gates of heaven are shut to them at the moment of death.

### Why is arrogance so hard to cure?

Why does the ego of those addressed stop them from accepting the call to truth? The one who calls has nothing but argument to lean on, while his hearers stand on material glories. He stands on reasons, and they stand on something solid and visible. The force of argument cannot be touched or seen. That difference breeds arrogance. People come to see the caller as beneath them, and that gives them a "reason" to ignore him.

For such people, entering Allah's mercy is as impossible as a camel passing through the eye of a needle. They ignore Allah, so Allah ignores them. He showed them glimpses of Himself through the caller, appearing before them in the form of arguments. They gave those signs no weight and refused to bow before them. How can such people have any share in His bounty?

### In short

- Those who deny Allah's revelations out of arrogance will find the gates of heaven shut to their deeds, their prayers and their souls.
- At death a believer's soul is welcomed through every heaven, while a disbeliever's soul is refused at the first gate and thrown down to Sijjīn.
- They will no more enter Paradise than a camel, or a thick rope, can pass through a needle's eye, so their punishment is everlasting.
- Arrogance grows when people value visible wealth over unseen arguments, and so they dismiss the one who calls them to the truth.

<<<VERSE 7:41>>>
After shutting them out of Paradise, the surah describes the added severity of their punishment: "Hell will be their bed; flames will be their cover. This is how We reward the wrongdoers". Those who rejected the truth out of pride will be surrounded by Hell from below and from above.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Hell will be their bed"**: the word is mihād, a place of rest, a bed or a floor. For them it is a bed of fire. Muhammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī explained mihād as beds.

**"flames will be their cover."** The word is ghawāsh, plural of ghāshiyah, something that covers. In the Arabic form ghawāshin, the ending makes up for a dropped letter yāʾ. Muhammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī said it means coverings, and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk ibn Muzāḥim and as-Suddī said the same. Above them will be a covering of torment, the coverings of Hell. Even the bed they rest on and the sheet over them will come from Hell. This is an everyday image used to express how extreme the punishment is.

**"This is how We reward the wrongdoers"**: so Allah repays the idolaters, who overstepped His limits by disbelieving in Him and turning away from Him.

### Why does the closing phrase change?

The previous verse, about being kept out of Paradise, ends with "This is how We reward the wicked". This verse, describing the punishment of Hell, ends with "This is how We reward the wrongdoers". The change is deliberate, because the punishment here is more severe than the deprivation described before.

### In short

- The deniers will have Hell for their bed and its fire for their covering, enclosed in punishment from below and above.
- The image of bed and sheet expresses in plain terms how complete and severe their torment will be.
- Being denied Paradise is matched by the harsher word "wrongdoers" when the punishment of Hell itself is described.
