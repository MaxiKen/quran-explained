<<<VERSE 7:40>>>
The passage brings the two groups to their destinations. "Surely those who receive our revelations with denial and arrogance, the gates of heaven will not be opened for them, nor will they enter Paradise until a camel passes through the eye of a needle. This is how We reward the wicked". The verse names an impossibility and uses it to measure another one. A camel cannot go through the eye of a needle, and the people described here cannot enter Paradise.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Surely those who receive our revelations with denial and arrogance"** names the people. They deny the signs and they scorn them, which is to say that they refuse to believe in them and they refuse to be ruled by them.

**"the gates of heaven will not be opened for them"** has been read in two ways, and both readings are carried. Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās said that their good deeds and their supplication will not rise through those gates. The report reaches that reading through al-ʿAwfī and through ʿAlī bin Abī Ṭalḥah, and ath-Thawrī narrated it from Layth, who had it from ʿAṭāʾ, who had it from Ibn ʿAbbās. The second reading is that the gates will not be opened for the souls of the disbelievers, which is what aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported from Ibn ʿAbbās, and what as-Suddī and several others said as well. Ibn Jurayj combined the two, saying that the gates will not be opened for their deeds or for their souls. And that combined reading is the strongest, and Allah knows best.

**"nor will they enter Paradise until a camel passes through the eye of a needle"** puts the two impossibilities side by side. The word used is the word for a camel, and Ibn Masʿūd said it means the male of the species. A second narration gives it as the mate of the she camel instead. There is also a variant recitation reported from Ibn ʿAbbās by way of Mujāhid and ʿIkrimah, in which the word is not the word for a camel at all but a word meaning a thick rope, so that the sentence reads: until a thick rope goes through a needle's eye. A rope and a needle, or a camel and a needle, make the same point. The opening the camel would have to pass through is one of the smallest openings there is, and the camel is one of the largest animals. Their entry into Paradise is exactly as possible as that. And since the entry is impossible, what the verse proves is that the punishment of the Fire for these people is never going to end.

**"This is how We reward the wicked"** is the sentence passed at the end of the verse, and the wicked here are the idolaters, the ones who were sinful through disbelief.

### The report of the soul at death

The second reading of the gates is supported by a report from al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib, which Abū Dāwūd, an-Nasāʾī, Ibn Mājah and Aḥmad all recorded in detail. A summary follows.

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ attended the funeral of one of the Anṣār, and while the grave was still being prepared he sat down on an open patch of ground, and the Companions sat around him in silence. He raised his head and spoke of the death of the wicked, and then of the soul of such a person being carried up to the heaven. He said:

> So they carry it up, and it does not pass by a gathering of the angels without one of them asking whose wicked soul this is. They answer with the name of such and such, using the worst of the names he was called by in his life. When they reach the lowest heaven, they ask that its door be opened for the soul, and it is not opened for it.

And then the Prophet ﷺ recited this verse, about the gates of heaven not being opened for them. That portion belongs to a long hadith, and it is not the whole of it.

In the fuller narration the Prophet ﷺ described both journeys side by side. For a believing servant of Allah, angels come down from the heaven with radiant white faces, carrying a shroud and a fragrance from Paradise, and they sit in front of the dying man. Then the angel of death comes and says to the rūḥ, the soul: O soul at peace, come out to the forgiveness and the pleasure of your Lord. The soul comes out of the body as easily as water pours from a skin bag when its mouth is opened, and the angel of death takes it into his hands and hands it to the angels waiting there. Wherever the group meets other angels on the way, the question is asked whose soul this is, and the answer gives the man's name and the title he was known by, as an honour done to him. At the first heaven the gate is opened for him, and more angels join the company until the seventh heaven is reached. Then Allah says that the record of this servant's deeds is to be written in ʿIlliyyīn, and the soul is sent back to the grave.

The verses of Sūrat al-Muṭaffifīn give the name of that place, for it says that the record of the righteous is in ʿIlliyyīn, and asks what will make a person realise what ʿIlliyyīn is, and answers that it is a written record witnessed by those brought near (83:18-21). And there is another verse of the Qur'an on the same subject, which says that good words are taken up to Allah, and that good deeds raise them.

The account of the disbeliever runs the other way. Angels come down with dark and terrifying faces, carrying a coarse ragged piece of cloth, and they sit in front of him. Then the angel of death draws his soul out the way a thorny branch is pulled out of wet wool wrapped around it, and the stench of that soul is worse than the stench of a dead animal gone bad. As the angels carry it up, other angels ask whose evil soul this is, and the answer gives the worst of the names he was known by. At the first heaven they ask for the gate to be opened, and it is not opened. The command comes that his record is to be kept in Sijjīn, which is where the record of the disobedient is kept. And his soul is thrown down.

The soul returns to the grave, and the angels who keep the account make him sit up and ask him the same questions the believer was asked. His answer to every one of them is nothing but that he does not know. His bed there is made of the Fire, and so is what he is dressed in, and a door is opened in his grave that faces the Fire, so that the heat and the flames reach him without stopping.

So the spirits of the deniers are carried all the way to the gate of the heaven, and there the door stays shut, and they are thrown back down. That is another way to take the words about the gates: they name the hour of death, and not the Day of Resurrection.

### Where these verses sit in the passage

A pledge was taken from every human being back in the world of spirits, before any of them was born into this world of bodies. What the pledge required was that when the prophets come with guidance and with instructions, a person should listen to them seriously and then do what he has heard. They were told that those who keep the pledge and carry out what it involves will be saved from every sorrow and will deserve lasting peace and comfort, and that those who deny the prophets or rebel against the instructions they bring will have the lasting punishment of Jahannam waiting for them.

The verses that follow present what actually happened once different groups of people came into the world and behaved differently. Some of them threw the pledge away behind their backs and set themselves against it, and others held to it and acted rightly. The end of the two groups, with their reward and their punishment, is set out in the four verses that run from verse 40 to verse 43. Verses 40 and 41 describe the deniers and the criminals, and verses 42 and 43 describe the believers and the mindful who fulfilled the pledge. So this verse is the opening of a two-part conclusion, and the word "wicked" at its close is answered by the word "wrongdoers" at the close of the next.

### Why the arrogance was there in the first place

The question goes deeper than the sentence. Why is it that when God's messengers give the call to the truth, the egotism of the people they address stops them from accepting it? The reason lies in what each side has to stand on. What the caller has is argument. What the person he is addressing has is material glory. The caller stands on the strength of what he can prove, and the other man stands on something he can see and touch. The force of an argument cannot be seen or touched.

That difference is what breeds the egotism. It makes a man judge the one who calls him to be lower in rank than himself, and it hands him what looks like a reason to ignore him. So that person's admission to the mercy of God is as hopeless as the camel at the needle's eye. They set God at nothing, and God sets them at nothing. God gave them glimpses of Himself through the one who called them, and He presented Himself to them in the form of arguments, and they treated none of it as important and refused to bow to it. How should people like that have any share in the bounty of God?

### In short

- The gates of heaven are closed to their deeds and to their souls, on the two readings, and a third reading combines the two.
- Paradise is as closed to them as the eye of a needle is to a camel, and the point of the image is that their punishment does not end.
- The report of the soul at death describes the gate that opens for the believer and the gate that stays shut for the disbeliever, with the record kept in ʿIlliyyīn for one and in Sijjīn for the other.
- The verse opens the conclusion of the passage, and the two verses about the deniers are answered by the two verses about the believers that follow.
- The arrogance of the address came from what each side had to stand on, since argument cannot be seen and material glory can.

<<<VERSE 7:41>>>
The second verse describes what is waiting inside. "Hell will be their bed; flames will be their cover. This is how We reward the wrongdoers". The verse gives them a bed and a covering, and both of them are made of the same thing.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Hell will be their bed"** translates a word that means a place of rest, a bed, or a floor. What is meant is that the bed they lie on comes from the Fire. This is how Muhammad bin Kaʿb al-Quraẓī explained it, and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk bin Muzāḥim and as-Suddī said the same.

**"flames will be their cover"** translates a plural word for something that covers, and the singular of it means a covering. The top sheet over them comes from the Fire as well. Another reading of it is simply that above them hangs a covering of torment.

**"This is how We reward the wrongdoers"** closes the verse, and the wrongdoers here are the idolaters, the people who went past the limits set for them and turned away from their Lord.

### Why the two verses close differently

The two verses end in almost the same way, and they are not quite the same. The verse before this one ends by naming the wicked, and this one ends by naming the wrongdoers. The difference is deliberate, and the reason given for it is that the second description is the more severe of the two. So the word moves from one fault to a heavier one as the punishment moves from being shut out to being shut in.

The picture itself is put in the plainest everyday idiom. A bed and a covering are the two things a person needs to rest. The verse takes both of them and makes both of them out of the Fire, so that rest itself becomes the punishment. For these people there is nothing to lie on and nothing to lie under that is not burning. It is the most eloquent way there is of saying, in ordinary words, that the punishment is extreme.

### In short

- The bed and the covering are both from the Fire, so that rest itself is taken away from them.
- The words for the bed and the covering are explained by al-Quraẓī, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk and as-Suddī.
- The verse closes on the wrongdoers where the one before it closed on the wicked, and the change of word marks the more severe of the two.
- The idiom is an ordinary one, and it is used to carry an extreme meaning.
