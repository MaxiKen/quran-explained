<<<VERSE 4:82>>>
The challenge to look closely: "Do they not then reflect on the Quran? Had it been from anyone other than Allah, they would have certainly found in it many inconsistencies" (4:82). The verse is an open invitation to deliberate in the Qur'an, and it hides a proof inside the invitation, for a book that survives deep reflection unchanged can only be from the One who sees all.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do they not then reflect on the Quran?"** uses the word for pondering and deliberation, tadabbur, not the word for plain recitation. The choice carries a delicate hint. Looked at deeply, the Qur'an shows no discrepancy in its words and meanings, while reciting without deliberating, without thinking it through, a person may start seeing contradictions that were never there. The verse also shows that the Qur'an itself demands that every human being ponder its meanings, so supposing that pondering is the exclusive domain of its highest and ablest exponents is not correct. The levels of deliberation differ with the levels of knowledge. The deliberation of the great exponents deduces the solution of thousands of problems from a single verse. A common scholar's pondering extends only to grasping what those problems and their solutions are. When ordinary people recite the Book and think through its meanings via a translation and explanation in their own tongue, the greatness of Allah settles on their hearts, love for Him grows there, and concern for the life-to-come is planted. That is the master key to success. To stay safe from doubts along the way, the better course is to sit with an ʿālim and study the Qur'an lesson by lesson. If that cannot be done, take up some authentic and reliable tafsir instead. Should a doubt arise during such study, the wiser course is not to force a solution from personal opinion, but to consult expert scholars.

**"Had it been from anyone other than Allah"** faces the alternative squarely. The verse means: had it been fraudulent and made up, as the ignorant idolaters and the hypocrites assert in their hearts.

**"they would have certainly found in it many inconsistencies"** draws the conclusion. Nothing in the Qur'an wobbles. No inconsistency, no contradiction, no statements that conflict, no discrepancy, because the Book is revelation from the Most-Wise, Worthy of all praise, the truth that comes from the Truth. The Qur'an asks the same elsewhere: "Do they not then think deeply in the Qur'an, or are their hearts locked up from understanding it?" (47:24). One contradiction in one subject would have multiplied across many subjects into a great deal of contradiction, as the Bayān al-Qur'ān puts it, but the truth is that there is just no contradiction anywhere in it. This perfect uniformity cannot be found in the word of man. Its eloquence never tapers. It talks about the Oneness of Allah and about the disbelief of men, it lays down rules for the lawful and the unlawful, and no inconsistency enters the process. It offers information about the unseen, and not one bit of that information fails to match reality. Its order retains the quality of its diction all along, never touching a pitch that is low, while the speech and writing of man shifts with peace and distraction, happiness and sorrow. That is a clear proof of its being the word of Allah.

The sections of the Book belong together in this proof. Those firmly grounded in knowledge say of the clear and the unclear alike: "We believe in it, all of it" (3:7). Both kinds of section, the muhkam that lies entirely clear and the mutashabih that does not, are true. The grounded understand the unclear from the clear, and thus gain guidance. Those in whose hearts is the disease of hypocrisy do the reverse, understanding the muhkam from the mutashabih, and gain only misguidance. Allah praised those who have knowledge and criticised the wicked.

### Two gatherings that went wrong

The danger of mishandling the Book has a face in the reports. ʿAmr bin Shuʿayb relates from his father, from his grandfather, in the record of Imām Aḥmad:

> I and my brother were present in a gathering more precious to me than red camels. We came and found some of the leaders of the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ sitting close to one of his doors. Not liking to be separate from them, we sat near the room. They then mentioned an āyah and began disputing until they raised their voices. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ came out so angry that his face was red. He threw sand on them and said: Behold, O people! This is how the nations before you were destroyed, because of their disputing with their Prophets and their contradicting parts of the Books with other parts. The Qur'an does not contradict itself. Rather, it testifies to the truth of itself. Whatever of it you have knowledge in, implement it, and whatever you do not know of it, refer it to those who have knowledge in it.

ʿAbdullāh bin ʿAmr reported the like: he went to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ one day, and while they sat, a pair of men fell to disputing over an āyah until their voices rose. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> Verily, the nations before you were destroyed because of their disagreements over the Book.

Muslim and an-Nasāʾī recorded this hadith.

### Who may draw from the Book

No group or individual holds a monopoly on the exegesis of Qur'an and Sunnah, yet there are conditions for it, and the verse proves a rule on the way. When no explicit statement in the Qur'an and Sunnah covers a certain problem, the best of efforts must be made to find a solution by serious deliberation within them, a method technically known as qiyās, analogical deduction, as al-Qurṭubī notes. But the serious deliberation of a master mujtahid, which deduces solutions at the highest level, requires that the one pondering first acquaint himself with the basic rules of such deduction, so that his results are correct and sound. Without the basic postulates, with faulty education and training, without the qualifications that must be found in a mujtahid exponent, the results come out wrong, and better-equipped scholars may rightfully challenge and contradict them. Think of the man who never walked the corridors of a medical college, objecting that trained doctors hold a monopoly of medical treatment. Or the one who gags his reason and asks why canals, bridges and dams go only to expert engineers, when he as a citizen could render the service. Or the person devoid of reason who stands up asking why legal experts alone interpret the law of the land. To each it must be said that the right exists, but so do the years of education, training and experience, the enormous body of arts and sciences assimilated under expert teachers. Yet the moment the same analogy is laid on the reading and application of Qur'an and Sunnah, a task intricate and delicate beyond these, a barrage of taunts arrives claiming that the ʿulamā keep the job locked up for themselves. Does this work require no ability, no qualification? The claim is terrible.

### The outer proof

None of the statements of the Qur'an contradicts any established truth. It contains nothing against human nature. It conflicts with no fact known through the earlier divine books. No part of its content contradicts any fact verified by the body of knowledge established through observation and experimentation. That it conforms to factual reality at every point is the definite proof of its being Allah's revealed message. This freedom from contradiction appears to the one who ponders, for truth appears as truth to the sincerity with which it is sought, while one who does not ponder will ceaselessly raise baseless objections against the Book, and may do so until Doomsday ends the period of human trial.

### The inner reading

Pondering, tadabbur, alludes to meanings acquired through immersing oneself deeply in contemplation.

### In short

- The word is reflect, not merely recite. Deep looking finds no discrepancy, and careless looking invents them.
- Pondering the Qur'an is every human's right, at the level each can reach, best pursued under a guide, a reliable tafsir, or expert scholars when doubt strikes.
- Had the Book been man's invention, one contradiction would have bred many. Instead its law, reports of the unseen and diction hold one quality throughout.
- Disputing over the āyahs destroyed earlier nations, and the Prophet ﷺ forbade it with anger, sand thrown upon the disputers.
- Qiyās is licensed here: where no explicit text exists, deliberate within the texts, with the training of a mujtahid.

<<<VERSE 4:83>>>
The instruction about news: "And when they hear news of security or fear, they publicize it. Had they referred it to the Messenger or their authorities, those with sound judgment among them would have validated it" (4:83). The verse settles how rumour is to be handled in a community whose safety depends on truth, and it ends with a warning about how few would have stood upright without Allah's grace.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And when they hear news of security or fear"** speaks of tidings about the raiding parties sent by the Prophet ﷺ and what happened to them, security through victory or booty, fear through defeat and killing. According to Ibn ʿAbbās, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk and Abū Muʿādh, the verse was revealed about the hypocrites, and according to Ḥasan and many others, about weak Muslims, as Rūḥ al-Maʿānī carries the two reports. Out of resentful envy, the hypocrites insisted on divulging such news, and the broadcasting sapped the courage of the believers and distressed the Prophet ﷺ.

**"they publicize it"** is the charge. They make it widely known before being sure of its truth, disclosing and spreading news that might not be true at all. Hearsay is not to be repeated without prior investigation into its credibility. In the introduction to his Ṣaḥīḥ, Imām Muslim recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> Narrating everything one hears is sufficient to make a person a liar.

The same narration was collected by Abū Dāwūd in the section on manners in his Sunan. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from al-Mughīrah ibn Shuʿbah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ prohibited "It was said," and, "So-and-so said." The Ṣaḥīḥ also records:

> Whoever narrates a hadith while knowing it is false, then he is one of the two liars, the one who invents and the one who spreads the lie.

**"Had they referred it to the Messenger or their authorities"** gives the correct course. The word for the investigation that follows literally names the drawing out of water from the depth of a well, the mustambat water that oozes out first, and here it means getting to the bottom of something and finding out the truth of the matter, as al-Qurṭubī explains. As for those in authority, positions vary. Ḥasan, Qatādah and Ibn Abī Laylā say the expression refers to Muslim scholars and jurists. As-Suddī says it means rulers and officials. After reporting both statements, Abū Bakr al-Jassas takes the general view that the expression means both, which is the correct approach. Some scholars doubt that the jurists could be meant, since the expression in its literal sense names people whose orders are carried out administratively. The reality of the thing answers the doubt. The implementation of authority takes two forms. One works by force, coercion and oppression, and belongs to office holders. The other works by reliance and trust, and that stands reposed in the revered jurists alone, as the whole body of Muslims have shown in every age, taking the decisions of the religious scholars as the mandatory course in all matters of religion by their own free will. Under the precepts of the Sharīʿah, obedience to the rules the scholars set is obligatory, wājib, so the application of the term to them is correct, as the Aḥkām al-Qur'ān of al-Jaṣṣāṣ argues. Details of the subject appeared under the verse that says: "Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you" (4:59). One gloss reads the authorities as those possessed of reason and understanding, Abū Bakr and his companions. The sense of referring is keeping quiet until fully informed.

**"those with sound judgment among them would have validated it"** names the people who follow the matter up and seek knowledge of it. They would have known from the Prophet ﷺ and the authorities whether the news ought to be broadcast or not. The sentence also proves something larger about prophethood itself. The command turns to two sources, first the Prophet ﷺ and then those in authority, and the injunction to investigate covers both parties alike, which shows that the Holy Prophet ﷺ too was obligated with the function of extracting, formulating and reasoning out injunctions with proofs, as the Aḥkām al-Qur'ān of al-Jasas demonstrates.

**"Had it not been for Allah's grace and mercy, you would have followed Satan—except for a few"** closes the warning, and the excepted few are the believers, as ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported from Ibn ʿAbbās. The bounty is Islam and the mercy is the Qur'ān, and but for them all would have followed Shayṭān in the abominations he commands. The truly Islamic society is one whose members have done enough self-analysis to acknowledge their shortcomings, ready to place their affairs in the charge of members more competent than themselves and to accept their leadership. That quality of self-assessment is what deters people from following the path of Satan. A person devoid of it jumps into the fray without the capacity to handle complicated issues, and brings destruction upon himself and his own people. In matters of collective application, silence matters more than speech, and a rumour spread on hearsay is help handed to Satan.

### The divorce rumour

The hadith of ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, collected in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs, is the one to weigh when considering the background of the verse. When ʿUmar was informed that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had divorced his wives, he left his house for the Masjid and found the people at its doorsteps already talking about the news. He said to himself that this was something which must first be investigated, went in to the Prophet ﷺ and asked him whether he had divorced his wives. He said: no. Having found out the truth, ʿUmar returned and, standing at the door of the Masjid, made an announcement in the loudest voice that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had not divorced his wives, and that whatever the people were saying was wrong. Thereupon the verse was revealed.

### Qiyās and ijtihād in new problems

From this verse comes the rule for problems not specifically mentioned in the authoritative texts, the nass, of the Qur'an or Sunnah. Their rulings are deduced in the light of the Qur'an through ijtihād and qiyās, because the verse commands turning to the Prophet ﷺ for the resolution of new problems when he is present, and, when he is not, to the scholars and jurists of the community, who have the ability to extract and formulate injunctions from the texts. Points of guidance follow. In the absence of explicit textual authority, the course is to turn to the jurists and scholars. The injunctions given by Allah are of two kinds, some explicit textual imperatives, others carrying meanings hidden in the depths of the verses as Allah willed in His infinite wisdom. It is the duty of the scholars to extract and formulate those meanings through the established methodology of ijtihād and qiyās. And for the great masses of Muslims, it is necessary to follow the guidance the scholars give in such problems, as the Aḥkām al-Qur'ān sets out. If anyone suspects that the verse has no relation with deducing injunctions, and only instructs people not to indulge in rumour-mongering in situations of peace and fear before an enemy, the answer is that the verse makes no mention of an enemy. The state of peace and fear is universal, and the way it relates to the enemy, so it relates to day-to-day problems, for a new problem throws the commoner into a fix where both sides hold probability of gain and loss, and the smartest way out devised by the Sharīʿah is to turn to those capable of extracting injunctions. The outcome of ijtihād, lastly, stays humble. A ruling extracted through investigation cannot categorically be declared the absolute truth in the sight of Allah, and the probability always exists that it might turn out otherwise. What one gains from it is the positive, overwhelming and weightier opinion of its being true, and that is the thing to act upon.

### The inner reading

Istinbāṭ, precise extraction, is the mining of the jewels of meanings. The heedless have no confidant for their secrets, so they pour them out to one another. The believers have the opposite estate: the One who knows their secrets is also their Friend and Protector. Whatever reaches them, they raise it in speech with Him, needing no created being to broadcast it to. Allah hears their whispered confidence, and Allah knows their hidden affairs. Had such people carried their secrets to the respected ones, the people of the story, the doubts would have been dispelled and the light of guidance and spiritual instruction would have carried them through. And were it not for Allah's bounty toward His friends, they would stray down every valley of parting that the moment opens, exactly as their outward look-alikes do.

### In short

- News of security or fear is not to be broadcast before verification, for repeating all one hears is enough to make a liar.
- The reference runs to the Prophet ﷺ and the authorities, which covers rulers and, by trust and wājib obedience, the scholars and jurists.
- ʿUmar's handling of the divorce rumour, ask first, then announce the truth from the Masjid door, is the pattern the verse demands.
- Where the texts are silent, the qualified investigate and deduce through ijtihād and qiyās, and the masses follow, for following Satan needs no help from anyone but a few.
