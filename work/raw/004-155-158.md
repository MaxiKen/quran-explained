<<<VERSE 4:155>>>
The record of the crimes: "˹They were condemned˺ for breaking their covenant, rejecting Allah’s signs, killing the prophets unjustly, and for saying, “Our hearts are unreceptive!”—it is Allah Who has sealed their hearts for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few—" (4:155). The verse opens the charge sheet that runs to the end of the passage.

### Phrase by phrase

**"for breaking their covenant"** names the first crime. The Jews broke the promises and vows Allah took from them, and history recorded one consequence of it: their disbelief in Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'ān ended with the capitation tax laid upon them.

**"rejecting Allah's signs"** means His āyāt, His signs and proofs and the miracles they witnessed at the hands of their own prophets.

**"killing the prophets unjustly"** tallies the long record of offenses against the prophets of Allah. Many of them they killed, may Allah's peace be upon them all, and never with any right.

**"and for saying, “Our hearts are unreceptive!”"** records their excuse. Their word "Ghulf" means wrapped with covering, as Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ʿIkrimah, as-Suddī and Qatādah explained. They claimed their hearts could absorb any kind of knowledge yet could not grasp what the Prophet ﷺ said, as if the hearts were wrapped and the message simply could not enter. The idolaters had said the like before them: "And they say: Our hearts are under coverings from that to which you invite us" (41:5). The Jews would say it ironically, showing not inability but conviction that whatever their misconduct, their salvation stood safe.

**"it is Allah Who has sealed their hearts for their disbelief"** overturns the excuse. It is not as they say. Allah set the seal upon their hearts because of their disbelief, as explained before in sūrat al-Baqarah, and so they cannot heed any admonition.

**"so they do not believe except for a few"** means hearts grown used to kufr, transgression and weak faith. The little faith left to them will be of no use. The few are ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his companions and followers, who did believe.

### The birthright they presumed

Revelation had asked of the Jews a surrender to the will of God in this world, holding out Paradise in the next. The first half of that bargain they dropped, the second they treated as a birthright. Rot spread through the community, and their trust that salvation came by lineage alone grew so firm that acknowledging the new Prophet seemed simply unnecessary. Their hearts were not sealed by nature. The seal answered the misconduct.

The inward reading traces the descent step by step. Because they committed these prohibited acts, and were marked by acts that deserve punishment, they were made to alight at the dwellings of disgrace, and punishments of every kind were sent down upon them. The misfortune of their oppositional acts gathered force as it went. Leave the disobedient to himself and he drifts into acts still more prohibited. The covenant they violated without repentance, and so were pulled toward disbelief in the signs. That misfortune in turn set them loose upon the prophets, whom they slew wrongfully. Then came the audacity to claim a keen ability to comprehend, saying their hearts were vessels for every kind of knowledge. The reply came that Allah sealed them, hiding from them the place of deeper knowledge, ʿirfān, and they wandered on in their errors.

### In short

- Covenant broken, signs rejected, prophets killed: the three crimes stand recorded, and the jizyah followed in history.
- The claim of wrapped hearts was an ironic excuse, answered by the seal their disbelief earned.
- The few who believed, like ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām, show the door was never closed by anything but their own doing.

<<<VERSE 4:156>>>
The calumny: "and for their denial and outrageous accusation against Mary" (4:156). The charge sheet turns from the community's crimes to the slander of a woman.

### Phrase by phrase

**"for their denial"** is a second disbelief, in ʿĪsā (Jesus) and the Gospel, added to all that came before.

**"and outrageous accusation against Mary"** brings the report of Ibn ʿAbbās, given by ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah, that the Jews accused Maryam of fornication. As-Suddī, Juwaybir, Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq and several others said the same. It is apparent in the verse itself, for they accused Maryam and her son of grave charges, claiming he was an illegitimate son. Some of them even claimed she was menstruating while fornicating. One report adds that the calumny was the cause of their being turned into swine.

### The two errors

Error has two directions. A person may run past the limit or fall short of the truth, and both miss. Some slandered Maryam with the charge of fornication. Others ran past the limit differently, naming her son the son of God. Each group erred in its own way. Maryam, it is said, was a friend of God at whose hands two parties met ruin: the party of excess and the party of shortfall. So stands the rule for all His friends. Disrespect ruins the rejecters, and adulation beyond what may be said of anyone ruins the overpraisers. This is something most of the great ones have had to endure.

### In short

- The denial was doubled: the messenger rejected and his mother slandered.
- Slander from one side and excess from the other both miss the truth about Maryam and her son.

<<<VERSE 4:157>>>
The boast answered: "and for boasting, “We killed the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, the messenger of Allah.” But they neither killed nor crucified him—it was only made to appear so. Even those who argue for this ˹crucifixion˺ are in doubt. They have no knowledge whatsoever—only making assumptions. They certainly did not kill him" (4:157). The verse gives the truth of the most famous false claim in their history.

### Phrase by phrase

**"and for boasting, “We killed the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, the messenger of Allah.”"** records words uttered only in jest and mockery, saying in effect: we killed the man who styled himself the messenger of Allah. The polytheists had mocked in the same tone: "O you to whom the Dhikr has been sent down! Verily, you are a mad man!" (15:6).

**"But they neither killed nor crucified him—it was only made to appear so"** refers to the person whom the Jews thought was ʿĪsā. The one slain and crucified was an associate of theirs, and Allah cast the likeness of ʿĪsā over him, so they thought it was him. In sūrat Āl ʿImrān, five promises had already been given about foiling the Jewish plans against ʿĪsā and shielding him from their hostile intentions: "O ʿĪsā! I will take you and raise you to Myself" (3:55). Among them was the pledge that the Jewish design on his life would come to nothing. The present verse makes it explicit: they did not kill him nor crucify him. What actually happened was that they were deluded by resemblance.

**"Even those who argue for this ˹crucifixion˺ are in doubt"** — those who differ therein are full of doubts, the Jews who claimed to kill him and the ignorant Christians who believed them out of ignorance and lack of reason. When some of them saw the slain man, some said the face is that of ʿĪsā but the body is not his, so it cannot be he. Others answered: no, it is he. They are all in confusion, misguidance and bewilderment.

**"They have no knowledge whatsoever—only making assumptions"** — they follow nothing but conjecture. They have no certitude based on any true knowledge. The diverse claims they advanced only landed them in mutual differences.

**"They certainly did not kill him"** closes the verse on their own uncertainty. Which man died they never knew for sure, and doubt and confusion covered the whole matter.

### How they were deluded by resemblance

Ad-Daḥḥāk tells it this way. When the Jews resolved to kill ʿĪsā, his disciples assembled at a given place and ʿĪsā joined them there. Iblīs it was who pointed the execution squad to the house, and four thousand men closed around the place. ʿĪsā said to his disciples that one of them could go out and be killed and then be with him in Paradise. One of them offered, and ʿĪsā gave him his shirt and head-cover. The likeness of ʿĪsā settled on him, and the moment he stepped outside the Jews, sure they had their man, seized and crucified him. ʿĪsā meanwhile was lifted away.

Another report runs differently. The Jews had sent a man known to the Arabs as Teetlanoos to kill ʿĪsā. He found no one, for ʿĪsā had already been lifted by Allah toward Himself. Coming back out, the man found himself wearing the looks of ʿĪsā. To the Jews he was ʿĪsā, and so they carried off and killed one of their own.

There is room for whichever of the situations came to pass, for the Holy Qur'ān has not determined any particular situation. Allah alone knows what really happened. Seen with the other reports, however, the verse yields one common factor: a compelling delusion was cast over Jews and Christians alike. What actually took shape stayed hidden from them, though conjecture bred claim after claim. Reports add that when some stirred to what had happened, they said that they seemed to have killed their own man, for the one put to death resembled the Messiah in face only, not in the rest of the body. If this man is the Messiah, they asked, where is our own man? And if this is our man, where is the Messiah?

### The story behind the boast

When Allah sent ʿĪsā with proofs and guidance, the Jews envied him for his prophethood and his obvious miracles: curing the blind and the leprous, bringing the dead back to life by Allah's leave, shaping a bird from clay, breathing into it so that it became a bird by Allah's leave and flew. He performed other miracles besides, yet they defied and belied him and tried their best to harm him. He could not live long in any one city and had to travel often with his mother, peace upon them both. The Jews remained unsatisfied. They carried their complaint to the king of Damascus, in those days a Greek polytheist who worshipped the stars, telling him that a man in Jerusalem, in Bayt al-Maqdis, was misleading the people, splitting them and stirring unrest among the king's subjects. Angered, the king wrote to his deputy in the city: arrest this rebel leader, put a stop to him, crucify him, and set a crown of thorns upon his head. On a Friday evening the deputy went with some Jews to the house where ʿĪsā stayed with twelve, thirteen or seventeen of his companions. They surrounded it, and when ʿĪsā sensed that they would soon enter, he asked who would volunteer to be made to look like him and be his companion in Paradise. A young man volunteered, was judged too young, kept volunteering, and at last heard: well then, you will be that man. Over the young man settled the exact likeness of ʿĪsā. A hole opened in the roof, and ʿĪsā, made to sleep, rose through it to heaven. When those in the house came out, the watchers took the man who looked like him, crucified him at night and crowned him with thorns. The Jews boasted of a killing, and some Christians accepted the false claim. Those in the house had witnessed the ascension, while the rest thought the crucified man was ʿĪsā. They even told of Maryam sitting under the corpse weeping, and of the dead man speaking to her. All of it stood as a test from Allah, wise in its design, for His servants.

Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded a longer account from Ibn ʿAbbās. Just before the raising, ʿĪsā came to his companions, twelve inside the house, his hair dripping water, and told them that some among them would disbelieve in him twelve times after having believed. Then he asked who would volunteer that his image appear as his own and be killed in his place, and be with him in Paradise. The youngest kept volunteering until ʿĪsā said: you will be that man. The resemblance was cast upon him, and ʿĪsā ascended through a hole in the house while the Jews took the young man and crucified him. The followers then divided into three groups. One, the Yaʿqūbiyyah, said: Allah stayed among us as long as He willed, then rose to heaven. Another, the Nasṭūriyyah, said: Allah's son stayed among us as long as he willed, then Allah took him. The third, the Muslims, said: Allah's servant and messenger stayed among us as long as Allah willed, and then Allah took him to Himself. The two disbelieving groups joined hands against the Muslim group and killed them, and Islam was then veiled until Allah sent Muḥammad ﷺ. Its chain of narration up to Ibn ʿAbbās is authentic, and an-Nasāʾī carries it as well, through Abū Kurayb reporting from Abū Muʿāwiyah. Many among the Salaf likewise stated that ʿĪsā asked for a volunteer to carry his appearance and be killed in his stead, and to be his companion in Paradise.

### The inward reading

One report of the inward school has the slanderer himself, the betrayer, clothed in that likeness and killed upon the cross in the other's place. A proverb of the age already knew the pattern: who digs a grave for his brother falls into it. It is also said that ʿĪsā said whoever agreed to have his resemblance cast upon him, so that he would be killed in his place, would have the Garden, and one of his companions agreed. Because that man patiently bore the injury, he was not deprived of the recompense from Allah, for "Indeed We do not leave the reward of those of good deeds to go to waste" (18:30).

False conviction emboldens every crime. The covenant sits light on such people, clear arguments bounce off them, and against whoever calls them to truth they stop at no aggression, not even calumny to dishonour the caller. Yet every plot of theirs must fail. Allah's power and His immutable system of nature stand behind His envoys, and through all plots and all opposition arrives every kind of divine succour, right up to the mission's completion.

### In short

- The boast was mockery, and the truth is the reverse: they killed neither the messenger nor anyone certain, only a likeness they mistook for him.
- The reports differ in detail, the common factor is the delusion, and the Qur'ān fixes only that.
- The volunteer who took the likeness chose the Garden, and the reward of his good deed was not left to waste.

<<<VERSE 4:158>>>
The raising: "Rather, Allah raised him up to Himself. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise" (4:158). Where the plot failed, the divine promise prevailed, as verses 157 and 158 together declare.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Rather, Allah raised him up to Himself"** means into heaven, in body and spirit together, saved whole from their plot. The planning of the Jews to kill him proved insignificant against the power of Allah, who had taken it upon Himself to protect him, lifting him toward Himself as the promise of sūrat Āl ʿImrān had said.

**"And Allah is Almighty"** names the One never weak, whose will nothing resists. Might and mastery are His domain, and His vengeance reaches His enemies.

**"All-Wise"** covers all He decides and ordains for His creatures, His actions wise, His triumph granted to His friends, every deed of His fitted by wisdom to its end. Should the materialists, boxed into a single track, find the lifting beyond their understanding, the limit is theirs, not the deed's.

### The return before the end

The raised one comes back. Al-Bukhārī recorded that Abū Hurayrah said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> By Him in Whose Hands my soul is, the son of Maryam will shortly descend among you as a just ruler. He will break the cross, kill the pig and abolish the jizyah. Then wealth will abound and no one will accept charitable gifts any more. At that time one prostration will be better for them than this life and all that is in it.

From Abū Hurayrah, Imām Aḥmad transmits the call of Ihlāl that ʿĪsā will raise on the mountain highway of ar-Rawḥāʾ, for ḥajj, for ʿumrah, or for both, and Muslim transmits it as well. Then there is the question al-Bukhārī records from the Prophet ﷺ: what will your state be when al-Masīḥ, son of Maryam, comes down among you, and your imam is one of yourselves? Aḥmad and Muslim record that one too. The power that raised him is the power that guards the mission until it is complete.

### The rank of the ransomed one

The inward reading does not forget the volunteer. Because that companion of ʿĪsā was without any imperfection in his lower self, his nafs, he became his friend in spirit, in rūḥ. When ʿĪsā went up to the place of intimacy, maḥall al-zulfa, the spirit of him who ransomed ʿĪsā with his own lower self went up to the place of nearness, maḥall al-qurba. The one who was killed in the likeness received the recompense of his good deed, for Allah does not let that reward go to waste.

### In short

- The raising was body and spirit, the failure of the plot and the keeping of the promise.
- Almighty answers their plotting, All-Wise answers every question raised against the manner of it.
- The one who wore the likeness was not forgotten: his spirit was raised to nearness as ʿĪsā was raised to intimacy.
