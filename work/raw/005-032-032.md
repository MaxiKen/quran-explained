<<<VERSE 5:32>>>
"That is why We ordained for the Children of Israel that whoever takes a life—unless as a punishment for murder or mischief in the land—it will be as if they killed all of humanity; and whoever saves a life, it will be as if they saved all of humanity. ˹Although˺ Our messengers already came to them with clear proofs, many of them still transgressed afterwards through the land"

This verse draws a rule from the story of the two sons of Adam in 5:27 to 5:31. Because of the crime of Qabil, Allah prescribed for the Children of Israel that taking a life is as if one had killed all of mankind, unless it is a punishment for murder or for corruption in the land. Saving a life is as if one had saved all of mankind. The verse then says that messengers came to them with clear proofs, but many of them still transgressed in the land. The material reads the verse as a general principle of the sanctity of human life, and as a warning against the disorder that unjust killing brings. It is the central legal rule of this part of the chapter.

### Phrase by phrase

**"That is why We ordained for the Children of Israel."** The material says the phrase "that is why" links the rule to the crime of Qabil. The material also notes that the Qur'an tells such stories with the word truth, as it does for the people of the cave in 18:13 and for 'Isa, son of Maryam, in 19:34, so that the account of Qabil is given with the same care. The killing of Habil was the first unjust killing, and the rule that follows is given to the Children of Israel as a law. The material notes that Allah legislated for them and informed them of it. It also notes that the rule was not peculiar to them, since the same principle applies to all people.

**"Whoever takes a life, unless as a punishment for murder or mischief in the land, it will be as if they killed all of humanity."** The material explains that a life may be taken only in two cases: as retribution for murder, or as the punishment for spreading mischief in the land. Any other killing is treated as the killing of all mankind, because one life is not worth less than another in its sanctity. The material gives several explanations of this comparison. Mujahid said that whoever kills a believer intentionally will be cursed, and that his punishment is the same as if he had killed all people. Ibn 'Abbas explained that whoever kills one soul whose killing Allah has forbidden is like one who kills all mankind. Sa'id bin Jubayr said that whoever allows the blood of one Muslim to be shed is like one who allows the blood of all people to be shed.

The material cites a report from Abu Hurayrah, who went to 'Uthman while he was besieged, to offer his support. 'Uthman asked him whether he would like to kill all the people, including him. Abu Hurayrah said no. 'Uthman said that killing one man is the same as killing all, and he told him to go back without fighting.

**"Whoever saves a life, it will be as if they saved all of humanity."** The material explains that saving a life means refraining from killing a soul that Allah has forbidden to kill. Whoever forbids the killing of one soul without justice protects the lives of all people from himself. Mujahid is reported to have said that whoever kills no one, the lives of people are safe from him. Sa'id bin Jubayr is also reported to have said that whoever forbids the shedding of the blood of one Muslim is like one who forbids the shedding of the blood of all people.

**"Although Our messengers already came to them with clear proofs."** The material explains that the clear proofs are the signs and evidence brought by the messengers. The verse criticises those who transgress after knowing that a thing is forbidden. The material mentions the case of the Jews of Madinah, including the tribes of Qurayzah, an-Nadir and Qaynuqa', who used to fight alongside Khazraj or Aws in the days before Islam. When the war ended, they would ransom the captives of the other side, even though they had themselves killed. Allah criticised this in Surat al-Baqarah, in 2:84-85, which says that they shed one another's blood and drove out their own people, and then ransomed the captives, although their expulsion was forbidden to them.

**"Many of them still transgressed afterwards through the land."** The material explains that the transgression is the spread of mischief and disorder. The word translated transgress covers unbelief, killing and similar crimes, and the material says that the mischief in the land covers many kinds of evil.

### The punishment for mischief in the land

The material explains the verses that come after this one, since the rule about mischief in the land has its own punishment. It cites 5:33, which says that those who wage war against Allah and His messenger and spread corruption in the land shall be killed, crucified, have their hands and feet cut off on opposite sides, or be banished from the land. The material says that the choice indicated by "or" is a choice for the ruler, as it is in the other places where the Qur'an uses the same word, including the penalty for killing game in the sacred state in 5:95. The material reports that Ibn 'Abbas took the verse to mean that a person who takes up arms in the land of Islam and spreads fear on the roads, if he is caught, may be killed, crucified, or have his hands and feet cut off. It reports that Sa'id bin al-Musayyib, Mujahid, 'Ata', al-Hasan al-Basri, Ibrahim an-Nakha'i and ad-Dahhak said something similar.

The material notes that the verses were read by some as referring to the idolaters, and by others as general. It says that the more reliable view is that the verse is general. It reports the account of a group from the tribe of 'Ukl, who killed a herdsman, drove away the camels, and were punished severely after they had been healed by drinking the milk and urine of the camels. The material says this is the basis of the punishment in the verse.

The material also states that the punishment does not apply to a person who repents before he is caught. Ibn Jarir, reporting from 'Ikrimah and al-Hasan al-Basri, and the material on Ibn 'Abbas and Abu Dawud, says the early reports of the verse were read as applying to the idolaters who repented before capture, and that later views held the same for Muslims. The material gives the case of 'Ali al-Asadi, who waged war and shed blood, then repented after hearing a verse that tells the believers not to despair of the mercy of Allah. He went to Madinah, sat with Abu Hurayrah, and Marwan was told that he had come in repentance and could not be punished. He went to sea in jihad, and the account says that the ship he was on drowned with the enemy.

### The reports on the sanctity of life

The material cites 'Ubadah bin as-Samit, who reported that the Prophet ﷺ took the same pledge from the men as he did from the women, that they would not associate anything with Allah, steal, commit adultery, kill their children, or spread lies. Whoever keeps the pledge has his reward with Allah. Whoever falls short and is punished has his punishment as expiation. Whoever is covered by Allah's forgiveness is in Allah's hands. The material also cites a report from 'Ali, recorded by Ahmad, Ibn Majah and at-Tirmidhi, that Allah is far more just than to punish a servant twice for one sin, and far more generous than to punish him for a fault He has pardoned. Ad-Daraqutni said that the report is sound in its attribution to the Prophet ﷺ.

### In short

- Unjust killing is as if one had killed all mankind, and saving a life is as if one had saved all mankind.
- Killing is allowed only as retribution for murder or as punishment for corruption in the land, and the punishment for corruption is set out in 5:33.
- The verse is also a warning against transgression after clear proofs, and it gives the example of those who repent before capture.
