<<<VERSE 3:7>>>
This verse, "He is the One Who has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book, of which some verses are precise—they are the foundation of the Book—while others are elusive", gives a rule for reading the whole Qur'an. Some verses settle meaning, and some do not. The clear ones form "the foundation of the Book", and the elusive ones must be read under their control. The verse then divides readers by how they handle that difference, and it closes with the answer of "those well-grounded in knowledge, they say, “We believe in this ˹Quran˺—it is all from our Lord.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"He is the One Who has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book"**: Allah sent Gabriel with the Scripture to Muhammad ﷺ. The verse begins with the sending down because the dispute that follows is a dispute about how the Book is to be read.

**"of which some verses are precise"**: these are the muḥkam verses, entirely clear and plain, and they are the basis of the Qur'an. They state what is allowed and what is forbidden, the laws, limits, obligations and rulings that must be believed in and put into practice. They are clear to anyone who knows the rules of Arabic grammar well, and most of the Book belongs to this kind.

**"they are the foundation of the Book"**: they are called Umm al-Kitāb, mother of the Book, because they are the root and essence of all its teaching. Whoever refers the unclear verses to them reaches correct guidance, and whoever does the opposite goes astray. When people disagree, they are the reference point.

**"while others are elusive"**: these are the mutāshabih verses, which carry more than one possible meaning. Some of their meanings agree with the clear verses, and some carry literal signals that are not the intended sense. Abrogated verses, parables, oaths, and things that must be believed but not put into practice also fall into this kind.

**"Those with deviant hearts follow the elusive verses seeking ˹to spread˺ doubt through their ˹false˺ interpretations"**: people misguided and turned from truth to falsehood pick out the unclear verses because their wording covers such a wide area that it can be bent to serve a false reading. The clear verses cannot be bent, so they stand as unequivocal proof against such people. Seeking doubt, al-fitnah, means seeking to misguide followers by pretending to prove an innovation from the Qur'an, so the conduct is marked as dangerous by the Prophet's own warning, given in the hadith that follows.

**"but none grasps their ˹full˺ meaning except Allah"**: the word used here is taʾwīl. What taʾwīl means, and where the sentence should pause, is taken up in the section below on the pause in this phrase.

**"As for those well-grounded in knowledge, they say, “We believe in this ˹Quran˺—it is all from our Lord.”"**: the grounded believe in both kinds. Both the clear and the elusive are true, and each testifies to the other, because both come from Allah and nothing that comes from Allah contains contradiction. Allah states the test Himself: "Do they not then consider the Qur'an carefully? Had it been from other than Allah, they would surely have found therein many a contradiction" (4:82).

**"But none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason"**: only those with good minds and sound comprehension take admonition here. The word translated as being mindful is yadhdhakkar, in which an initial tāʾ has been assimilated into the dhāl, and the people named are those with pith, the possessors of intellect.

### Why this verse came down when it did

The first five verses of the surah set out the Oneness of Allah, and the text now answers doubts raised against it. A group of Christians came to the Prophet ﷺ and discussed religion with him. He refuted their doctrine of the Trinity in detail and proved the Oneness of Allah from His eternal existence, perfect power, all-encompassing knowledge and unique creative act, and it was agreed that once the Oneness stood, the Trinity could not stand with it.

The Christians then turned to the Qur'an itself. ʿĪsā (Jesus) is called in it Rūḥullāh, the Spirit of Allah, and Kalimatullāh, the Word of Allah. In their reading, those titles gave him a share in divinity. This verse settles that question. Titles of that kind belong to the elusive verses, so they cannot be read against the clear ones, and the clear ones speak plainly. "He ['Isa] was not more than a servant. We granted Our favor to him" (43:59). And, "Verily, the likeness of 'Isa before Allah is the likeness of Adam. He created him from dust, then (He) said to him: 'Be!' and he was" (3:59). Anyone who shuts his eyes to those verses and pulls at the elusive titles until they yield a godhood that the clear verses deny is simply going astray and being obstinate.

### Two kinds of verse, in plain terms

There are two kinds of subject in the Qur'an, and the style follows the subject. Some things belong to the known human world, such as historical events, signs in the universe and commandments for this life. They are put in a direct style, and they can be understood precisely. Other things belong to the unseen matters that no one can grasp in this life, such as the attributes of Allah and the states of Heaven and Hell. Everyday language cannot carry them, so they are framed in an allegorical style. If one says "man's hand", that is a plain statement of fact. If one says "God's hand", that is an allegory. A reader who misses this falls into taking allegorical verses literally, which leads away from the truth and never arrives at it.

Muḥammad ibn Ishaq bin Yasar described the clear verses as carrying the Lord's proof, protecting servants, and answering opponents and falsehood. Their intended sense is fixed. The unclear verses, he said, admit tampering with words even though tampering is forbidden. Allah tests servants through them just as He tests them through what is permitted and what is banned, so no one may twist them toward a false sense.

Another way of putting the difference is to say that the clear verses are rules that are acted upon and not abrogated, and that they are the leading theme in every Book. The example given is "Say: Come, I will recite unto you that which your Lord hath made a sacred duty for you" (6:151). The elusive verses are then described in two ways. In one, they are vague in meaning, like the letters of the alphabet used by their numerical value, ḥisāb al-Jumal, in Alif Lām Mīm and the other openings of surahs. In the other, they are the verses that were abrogated and are no longer acted upon. The whole Qur'an can also be called clear in one sense and allegorical in another. It is called clear where Allah says, "A Book whose verses have been made clear", meaning that it contains no imperfections. And it is called allegorical where Allah says, "A Book consimilar", meaning that its parts resemble each other in beauty and veracity.

### Who pursues the elusive verses, and what they want

The deviating pictured here were given names. Kaʿb Ibn al-Ashraf, Ḥuyayy Ibn Akhtab and Judayy Ibn Akhtab are named, men whose hearts held scepticism, opposition and turning away from guidance. They pursued the allegorical part of the Qur'an in order to hold fast to unbelief, to ascribe partners to Allah, and to keep their own error, and they did so by trying to work out the future of this community from it, hoping that dominion would come back to them. Allah's answer is that the explanation of that belongs to Him alone, and at that point He interrupts His own speech and begins again with the grounded in knowledge. Those of sound instruction, who have a sound grasp of the Torah, are then described as ʿAbdullāh Ibn Salām and his companions, sound-minded men who accept the simile of the Qur'an and say that both kinds of verse are from their Lord.

The warning was given in a hadith too. Imām Aḥmad recorded that ʿĀʾishah said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ recited this verse and then said, "When you see those who argue in it, using the parts that are not clear, then they are those whom Allah meant. Therefore, beware of them." Al-Bukhārī recorded a similar hadith from ʿĀʾishah in his explanation of this verse, Muslim recorded it in his book on the Divine Will in his Ṣaḥīḥ, and Abū Dāwūd recorded it in the section on the Sunnah in his Sunan, with the wording, "When you see those who follow what is not so clear of the Qur'an, then they are those whom Allah described, so beware of them." ʿĀʾishah is also quoted in more modest words: when people are seen busy investigating the elusive verses, they should be kept at a distance, because they are the same people Allah has talked about.

A second hadith names three fears for the community, the ummah. The first is that people grow wealthy and begin to envy one another, and that envy opens rifts and bloodshed. The second is that the Book of Allah is trifled with, with even the uninitiated and the ignorant claiming a perfect understanding through translations, and with the very things that are not meant to be understood becoming the great object of study, although Allah alone knows their meaning. The third is that people let their knowledge go to waste once it has grown, by stopping the work of increasing it.

### Where the sentence pauses, and what taʾwīl means

Scholars of Qur'an recitation differ over where to pause in the words "none knows its taʾwīl except Allah", and the differing answers depend on what taʾwīl means. The pause after the name of Allah was reported from ʿĀʾishah, ʿUrwah, Abū ash-Shaʿthāʾ and Abū Nahīk. Others pause after "those who are firmly grounded in knowledge" and make them sharers in the knowing, arguing that the Qur'an does not address people with what they cannot understand at all. Ibn ʿAbbās was quoted by Mujāhid, through Ibn Abī Najīḥ, as saying, "I am among those who are firmly grounded in its taʾwīl." The Messenger of Allah ﷺ prayed for Ibn ʿAbbās in these words: "O Allah! Bestow on him knowledge in the religion and teach him the Ta'wil (interpretation)."

Taʾwīl has two meanings in the Qur'an. In the first, it is the true reality of a thing and what it will turn out to be. That is the sense in Yūsuf's words, "O my father! This is the Ta'wil of my dream aforetime!" (12:100). And it is the sense in the waiting for the reality of the Resurrection in "Await they just for its Ta'wil?" (7:53), where the reality they were told about is what is awaited. With that meaning, the pause must come after the name of Allah, because only Allah knows the true reality of things, and "those who are firmly grounded in knowledge" then begin a new sentence describing their conduct.

In the second, taʾwīl means explanation and description. That is the sense in "Inform us of the Ta'wil of this" (12:36), where people ask for an explanation. With that meaning, the pause comes after "those who are firmly grounded in knowledge", because the learned do have general knowledge of what they were addressed with and can understand it, even though they do not know the true reality of things. The verse about the coming of the Lord is explained on the same footing: "And your Lord comes, and the angels, in rows" (89:22) means that your Lord will come and the angels will come in ranks, and that much can be stated without claiming to know the reality of it. Ibn Jarīr transmitted part of this discussion in his reports on the verse.

### Who the grounded in knowledge are

Nāfiʿ bin Yazīd said, in a report recorded by Ibn al-Mundhir, that those firmly grounded in knowledge are the people who are modest for the sake of Allah, seek His pleasure with humility, and neither exaggerate the standing of those above them nor belittle those below them. The weightier description given is that they are the main body of Muslims, ahl al-sunnah wa'l-jamāʿah, who accept only the interpretation of the Qur'an and the Sunnah that has been authentically reported from the Companions and the righteous generations after them and that carries the agreement of the community. They hold that the clear verses are the axis and centre of the Qur'an. What lies beyond their understanding in the elusive verses they leave with Allah, confessing the limits of their knowledge and insight. They are never proud of their knowledge, however complete it may seem, and never proud of the strength of their faith. Instead they pray constantly for steadfastness, grace and mercy. They have no appetite for mischief, so they do not make the elusive verses the object of their intellectual pursuit. The clear verses they needed for life, and those meanings Allah did not keep secret. The elusive ones He chose not to disclose, so believing in them as they stand is enough.

### The inner reading of the opening words

There is also an inward reading of the verse, and it begins with a single word, Huwa, "He". The word points to being itself rather than naming or describing. It declares that our Lord exists, rightly exists, and has always existed, without place and raised above attributes. Shurayḥ ʿĀbid recalled a dervish praying inside the Sanctuary mosque: "O He who is He! Beside You there is no he. Forgive me." From the Unseen came an answer that angels would keep writing the reward of the first utterance until resurrection.

The letters teach return. The ḥ comes from the throat's far end while the w begins at its near opening, suggesting that speech starts with Him and finally comes back to Him. Created beings follow the same arc, issued by His power at the beginning and recalled by His decree at the end. A rapt dervish answered every inquiry (name, origin, desire) with "He". When the others suggested that God was meant, he heard that name and surrendered his spirit, leaving the world. People picture Your name in a hundred senses, surrender their lives while remembering it, and pour out life for the fragrance of union while possessing only incapacity to describe You.

The two divisions then mirror two faces of religion: outward law's majesty in clear texts, and inner reality's beauty in difficult texts. Common people need the outward side first, perceiving and practising toward blessings and joy.

Ibn ʿAbbās set the same boundary in a shorter form. Explanation is of four types: explanation that the Arabs know from their language, explanation that no one is excused for not knowing, explanation that the scholars know, and explanation that only Allah knows. The elusive verses are left open by that fourth type, and the answer of the grounded is belief first, and then the supplication of the next two verses.

### In short

- The Qur'an has clear verses, the root of all its teaching, and elusive verses that must be read under their control.
- People with deviating hearts chase the elusive verses to spread doubt, often to defend error or to claim knowledge of the future, and the Prophet ﷺ warned against them by name and description.
- Only Allah knows the true reality of the elusive verses, though the learned can explain what can be explained. The grounded believe in both kinds, because all of it is from their Lord.

<<<VERSE 3:8>>>
The eighth verse gives the prayer of the people described in the verse before it: "˹They say,˺ “Our Lord! Do not let our hearts deviate after you have guided us. Grant us Your mercy. You are indeed the Giver ˹of all bounties˺.”" Men and women of real knowledge do not trust their own knowledge to hold them. They ask Allah to hold their hearts, because guidance and going astray are both finally in His hand.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Our Lord! Do not let our hearts deviate after you have guided us"**: do not let our hearts incline away from the guidance after You let them acquire it. The petition carries a fear inside it, that a heart might slip toward the kind of reading the last verse condemned, trying to interpret the unclear in ways that are not appropriate for us, as happened to the hearts of others.

**"Grant us Your mercy"**: give us mercy from You, ladunka, with which You make our hearts firm and increase our faith and certainty. As a strengthening, it is mercy that steadies the heart rather than knowledge that flatters it.

**"You are indeed the Giver ˹of all bounties˺"**: You alone are the Bestower. This has also been explained as the Bestower of prophethood and Islam to Muhammad ﷺ, and more simply as the One who gave to the believers before us.

### Why the grounded in knowledge pray this prayer

The earlier verse says that the grounded were not proud of their excellence. This verse adds another of their marks: they pray for steadfastness on the right path, and they pray for it with salvation in the next life in view, not for any worldly gain. That choice teaches something. Whether a heart is led or left belongs to Allah alone. A guided heart leans toward goodness, while a heart abandoned to error bends away from straightness. Since His power is absolute and His will effective, anyone anxious to remain steady goes directly to Him and begs for firmness.

A hadith states the same dependence in an image: "There is no heart which is not there in between the two of Allah's fingers. He makes it firm on the truth as long as He wills, and turns it away from the truth when He wills." The practical conclusion is not despair. It is prayer.

### The prayer the Prophet ﷺ kept on his tongue

Ibn Abī Ḥātim and Ibn Jarīr recorded that Umm Salamah said the Prophet ﷺ used to supplicate, "O You Who changes the hearts, make my heart firm on Your religion", and then recite, "Our Lord! Let not our hearts deviate (from the truth) after You have guided us" (3:8). In another wording the prayer runs, "O reverser of hearts, make our hearts firm on the faith chosen by You." He did not trust even his own standing to an untended heart. He asked for firmness, and he went on asking for it.

### The right posture before the unclear part of the Book

The correct academic and intellectual stand toward the allegorical part of the Qur'an is for a person to admit his limits. He should be content with an abstract understanding of things that cannot be grasped by the senses exactly. When even the mind cannot hold a reality whole, the practical course is not to force a precise meaning, but to pray for protection from drowning in fruitless discussion. He should pray to Allah for the wisdom that lets him recognise his place and rest his faith in those realities without knowing all their details. A Day is coming when they will come into full view, but that cannot happen while a person is still under test in this life. The intellect's path can be slippery. From one angle reality is assessed properly, and from another it is misunderstood. Greater wisdom means learning which angle to trust.

### What this supplication means when it is answered

When these people were truthful in asking for help, Allah assisted them with the lights of sufficiency. When the heart is limpid, the present moment is empty, and the tongue is flowing with the remembrance of the Real, the arrow of supplication inevitably reaches the target of response. The hard part is the coming together of that limpidity, loyalty and supplication. The sense of their words is, "O God, keep our hearts far from confusion and straying and make us firm on the carpet of service with the stipulation of the Sunnah."

"Give us mercy from Thee" asks for a gift rather than wages. Human obedience cannot purchase the Presence of His Majesty, so deeds should be forgotten and bounty awaited. A pīr of the Ṭarīqah said that travellers and obedient servants burst with fear at the words "We shall advance upon what deeds they have done and make them scattered dust" (25:23), yet he called that verse the sweetest in the Qur'an. Asked why, he explained that release from displeasing and unworthy obedience lets the heart cling wholly to grace and mercy.

### In short

- Knowledge does not secure the heart. The grounded in knowledge pray not to deviate after being guided, and they pray for mercy, not for rewards in this world.
- Guidance and straying are in Allah's hand, so steadfastness is asked for from Him, as the Prophet ﷺ asked when he prayed for a firm heart.
- Before the unclear part of the Book the sane posture is humility: believe, do not force a meaning, and wait for the Day when these realities come into view.

<<<VERSE 3:9>>>
The ninth verse completes the prayer: "Our Lord! You will certainly gather all humanity for the ˹promised˺ Day—about which there is no doubt. Surely Allah does not break His promise." The grounded in knowledge pray with the end of time in front of them. They ask not to deviate now because they know they will be gathered then, judged by what they did, and answered for what they disputed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Our Lord! You will certainly gather all humanity for the ˹promised˺ Day"**: You will gather Your creation together on the Day of Return, after they die, to judge between them and decide over what they disputed. That day gathers resurrection following death, an audit of deeds, the Bridge over Hell, the Scale, the Garden and the Fire.

**"about which there is no doubt"**: the Day of Resurrection, when Allah will requite people for their deeds as He promised. There is no uncertainty in it.

**"Surely Allah does not break His promise"**: He never misses the appointed promise of raising the dead. There is a shift of address in these last words, from the second person to the third, and they may also be read as Allah's own speech confirming what His servants say. All people are going to be called back to Him, to be held to account on that Day, and He does not break promises.

### Why this prayer is made in this form

The purpose of shaping the supplication this way is to show where its makers' concern sits. Their concern is with the matter of the Hereafter. That is why they ask for adherence to the path of guidance, and they ask for it so that they may reach its reward. A person who settles the gathering in his mind reads the verses about guidance and error differently, because the reading itself will be answered for.

The same fear sits behind two reports that speak of the earlier verse. ʿĀʾishah reported, in a hadith recorded by al-Bukhārī and Muslim, that the Prophet ﷺ recited the verse of the clear and the unclear and said, "When you see those pursuing the allegorical parts know that these are the ones God refers to in this verse so beware of them." And in al-Kabīr, aṭ-Ṭabarānī transmits that Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī heard the Prophet ﷺ say, "I fear nothing for my community except three faults", among them the day the Book would be opened in front of a believer who desires to interpret it, though none knows its interpretation except God, while those firmly rooted in knowledge say, "We believe in it, all is from our Lord." A short hadith with the same warning, reported at Q. 37 in the chain of that report, is left without a number here because the reference in the material is damaged.

### Today's gathering and tomorrow's

There is a gathering already happening and another still to come. Tomorrow everyone gathers where reward, thawāb, and punishment, ʿiqāb, will be assigned. Today the lovers already gather at approach, iqtirāb. Inner selves, asrār, meet now for the unveiling of Majesty, jalāl, and Beauty, jamāl. Bodies, abshār, will meet later amid the terrors, ahwāl, and the states, aḥwāl, foretold for them. The prayer of this verse belongs to people who live in the first gathering with their eyes on the second.

Allah will reward or punish each person according to the deeds he did in this life. That is the whole burden of the verse, and it is the reason the grounded in knowledge say their earlier words at all. They believe, they refuse to chase what can only be settled at the gathering, and they ask to be held to the path until they reach it.

### In short

- All humanity will be gathered on a Day with no doubt in it, and Allah does not break His promise.
- On that Day disputes are decided and every person is rewarded or punished for what he did here.
- The prayer names the gathering in order to fix concern on the Hereafter, since that concern is what makes a reader hold to the path now.

<<<VERSE 3:10>>>
The tenth verse turns from prayer to threat: "Indeed, neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah—and they will be the fuel for the Fire". Wealth and children are the two things people most often trust to stand between them and harm. For the disbelievers neither will avail, and they themselves will be fed to the Fire.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah"**: on the Day of Resurrection, what they were granted in this life will not avail them with Allah or save them from His punishment or severe torment. The disbelievers meant are those who disbelieved in the signs of Allah, denied His messengers, defied His Books and took no benefit from His revelation to His prophets. In this context the verse was read of Kaʿb Ibn al-Ashraf and his companions, and also of Abū Jahl and his companions, people rich in possessions and rich in children.

**"and they will be the fuel for the Fire"**: they are the wood with which the Fire is kindled and fed, the firewood of Hell. Their wealth and children will not protect them from Allah's punishment in this world or in the Afterlife, and they themselves will be the fuel with which Hell is lit on the Day of Resurrection.

### What the Qur'an says elsewhere about this wealth

Allah sets the same emptiness out in several places. "The Day when their excuses will be of no profit to wrongdoers. Theirs will be the curse, and theirs will be the evil abode (i.e. painful torment in Hell-fire)" (40:52). The wealth and children that impress onlookers are themselves part of the punishment: "So let not their wealth nor their children amaze you; in reality Allah's plan is to punish them with these things in the life of this world, and that their souls shall depart (die) while they are disbelievers" (9:55). Nor should the free disposal and affluence of the disbelievers across the land deceive anyone: "Let not the free disposal (and affluence) of the disbelievers throughout the land deceive you. A brief enjoyment; then, their ultimate abode is Hell; and worst indeed is that place for rest" (3:196-197). And the fire they go to is fed by more than their bodies: "Certainly you (disbelievers) and that which you are worshipping now besides Allah, are (but) fuel for Hell!" (21:98).

### A warning that carries a kindness inside it

There is a gentleness hidden in the threat, for the God who warns unbelievers in these words is majestic, generous and lovingly kind. He promises the faithful in the very middle of warning the unbelievers, and He caresses some while blaming others. His point stands in two directions. At the resurrection unbelievers gain nothing from property or offspring. Believers, by contrast, may benefit when dues are paid, religion is held firmly through what they own, and endless happiness is sought by means of it.

Muṣṭafā ﷺ said, "How excellent are wholesome possessions for the wholesome man! What excellent assistance are possessions for being wary of God!" And the Lord of the Worlds says, "And, with what God has given thee..." (28:77), calling a person to bring the next world to hand with what this world has given him, and to seek the felicity of the next world through it. Begin at the table. Food, drink and clothing strengthen the soul. A strengthened soul performs bodily obedience. Obedience supplies the lamp's material while divine bestowal is the lamp's root. The lamp of tawḥīd lights the heart, and a lit heart recognizes God, where the felicity of the next world lies. Possessions, traced backward through those steps, can therefore serve endless happiness.

### The measure that keeps wealth from turning against its owner

The steps only work up to a measure. A person must not pass beyond the measure of sufficiency, because then possessions turn into a cause of rebellion, as He says: "No indeed; surely man is rebellious because..." (96:6-7). That is why the Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked, "Lord God, make the food of Muhammad's family in the measure of sufficiency!" When sufficiency frees time for worship, it shares worship's worth, because provisions for a journey belong to the journey itself. A lawful landed estate supplied Shaykh Abū'l-Qāsim al-Kurragānī with the sufficiency he lived on.

### In short

- Wealth and children will not avail disbelievers against Allah, and the disbelievers themselves will be the fuel of the Fire.
- The same wealth that amazes people in this world is, for those who reject, a brief enjoyment that ends in Hell.
- For the faithful, wealth handled rightly and kept to the measure of sufficiency becomes road-supplies toward recognition of God, whose root is the lamp of tawḥīd.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:11>>>
The eleventh verse sets the present disbelievers inside an old pattern: "Their fate will be like that of the people of Pharaoh and those before them—they all rejected Our signs, so Allah seized them for their sins. And Allah is severe in punishment". Nothing new is being invented for them. The same conduct met the same end before, and their wealth and children will no more save them than it saved anyone earlier.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Their fate will be like that of the people of Pharaoh and those before them"**: their case is like the case of the people of Pharaoh and of those before them who disbelieved in Allah and denied His signs. The word translated "fate" here is daʾb, which means a people's practice, behaviour, tradition and habit. Ad-Daḥḥāk said that Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as "like the behaviour of the people of Firʿawn", and ʿIkrimah, Mujāhid, Abū Mālik, ad-Daḥḥāk and others read it the same way. Others took it as practice, conduct and likeness, and these meanings are all possible, because the daʾb of a people is the way they habitually behave.

**"they all rejected Our signs"**: those before them rejected the messengers, the verses and the proofs that Allah sent with them. The text's history lesson is short. His own people lied to Muhammad ﷺ and mistreated him, just as Moses' people lied to Mūsā (Moses) and mistreated him.

**"so Allah seized them for their sins"**: He seized and destroyed them on account of their denial. The earlier end has a present tense inside it. Allah says to His Messenger that He will do with these people on the day of Badr what He did with the people of Moses on the day of the drowning, and that seizure, like the drowning, will be for the sin of disbelieving the Scripture and the Messenger sent to them.

**"And Allah is severe in punishment"**: His punishment is severe and His torment is painful, when He does punish. Nothing escapes His grasp and nothing escapes His knowledge. He does what He wills and prevails over all things. Everything is humbled before Him, and there is no deity worthy of worship and no Lord except Him.

### The peoples named as parallels

Pharaoh's folk stand first, and after them the communities that came before, such as ʿĀd and Thamūd, who denied the signs of Allah. The verse compresses them into one picture: signs offered and refused, a prophet given the lie and mistreated, and then seizure. The sense of the warning is that this is the end the disbelievers of this generation will meet, and that neither wealth nor children changes the result, just as neither saved the peoples before them.

### Why defeat is a pattern, not an accident

An inward reading hears two ways persisting side by side. They kept to insolence as their sunna, and Allah answered with retribution as His sunna. They refused to abandon persistence and never aspired to good, wa-lā fī l-mabārri Ṭamiʿū. Regret and distress overtook them for what they sent ahead, only to find the door shut, wajadū l-bāba masdūdan, and their regret turned back on them, wa-l-nadama ʿalayhim mardūdan. The door they found shut was the one they had refused to enter while it stood open.

### In short

- These disbelievers repeat the daʾb, the settled behaviour, of Pharaoh's people and those before them, from ʿĀd and Thamūd onward: the lie given to prophets, and then seizure for sin.
- Their wealth and children will avail them no more than it availed those nations, and Badr is named as the drowning's counterpart.
- Allah is severe in punishment, and nothing escapes His grasp or His knowledge.
