<<<VERSE 4:176>>>
The closing ruling: "They ask you ˹for a ruling, O Prophet˺. Say, “Allah gives you a ruling regarding those who die without children or parents.” If a man dies childless and leaves behind a sister, she will inherit one-half of his estate, whereas her brother will inherit all of her estate if she dies childless. If this person leaves behind two sisters, they together will inherit two-thirds of the estate. But if the deceased leaves male and female siblings, a male’s share will be equal to that of two females. Allah makes ˹this˺ clear to you so you do not go astray. And Allah has ˹perfect˞ knowledge of all things" (4:176). The sūrah ends where much of it began, with the shares, and the last word on inheritance is also the last word of the whole chapter.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They ask you ˹for a ruling, O Prophet˺"** introduces a question from a Companion. Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh al-Anṣārī said: I had seven sisters, and I fell so ill that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ came visiting me, found me unconscious, and spat on my face, and I woke. I asked him: O Messenger of Allah, may I bequest two thirds of my wealth to my sister? He said: hold back. I asked: what about half? He said: hold back, and went out and left me. Then he came back and said: O Jābir, you are not going to die of this illness. Allah has revealed verses explaining the share your sisters should have, and He has apportioned two thirds for them. Jābir used to say that this verse was revealed about him.

**"Say, “Allah gives you a ruling regarding those who die without children or parents.”"** names the kalālah. The word, in the Arabs' usage, means the crown that surrounds the head from all sides, and the scholars took it for one who dies leaving behind neither descendants nor ascendants, anyone other than the father and the son. Abū Bakr used to say exactly that, and ʿUmar bin al-Khaṭṭāb felt shy to change a ruling Abū Bakr had issued. His saying is what the majority of the Companions, their followers and the earlier and later imams agree with, and it is the ruling the Qur'ān indicates.

**"If a man dies childless and leaves behind a sister, she will inherit one-half of his estate"** counts a full sister or a half sister from the father's side, taking her half after the preferential rights are settled, the debts, the wills, the burial expenses. The remaining half goes to the heirs of the aṣbah category, and if none is alive, that half also returns to her. As for a half sister from the mother's side, her share was already fixed in the earlier verse: one sixth if she is alone, and a third shared if there are two or more (4:12). The daughter-and-sister case divided the early authorities. Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn az-Zubayr judged that a man who dies leaving a daughter and a sister leaves the sister nothing, for the verse gives her half only when the deceased leaves no child. The majority disagreed: the daughter takes one half and the sister the other. Muʿādh bin Jabal had so judged in the Prophet's ﷺ own time, as Sulaymān reported from Ibrāhīm, who reported it from al-Aswad. And al-Bukhārī records the fuller case: Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī was asked about a daughter, a grand-daughter and a sister, and ruled the daughter one half and the sister one half, sending the questioners on to Ibn Masʿūd. Ibn Masʿūd answered that he would otherwise have deviated from the rightly guided: the daughter one half, the grand-daughter one sixth, and what remains, a third, to the sister. Confronted with Abū Mūsā's answer, he said: do not ask me for legal verdicts as long as this scholar is still among you.

**"whereas her brother will inherit all of her estate if she dies childless"** gives the brother the whole of what his sister leaves if she has no surviving offspring or parent. If a surviving parent exists, he inherits nothing, and where a fixed-share heir stands, such as a husband or a half brother from the mother's side, that share is taken first and the remainder goes to the brother. The rule of the farā'iḍ runs beneath it, as the Prophet ﷺ said, in the report of Ibn ʿAbbās: give the fixed shares to their people, and whatever is left is the share of the nearest male relative.

**"If this person leaves behind two sisters, they together will inherit two-thirds of the estate"** adds that more than two share in the two thirds. From this the scholars took the ruling on two daughters or more, just as the sisters' share had been taken from the verse about the daughters: "if there are only daughters, two or more, their share is two thirds of the inheritance" (4:11).

**"a male's share will be equal to that of two females"** states the agnation principle, applied to sons, grandsons and brothers: the male regularly receives twice the female's share.

**"Allah makes ˹this˺ clear to you so you do not go astray"** means His law and His set limits made clear, so that no one errs in the division after this explanation. Inheritance is not a simple matter, and this is one of the areas where disobeying the divine injunction leads astray from the straight path of guidance.

**"And Allah has ˹perfect˞ knowledge of all things"** declares perfect knowledge of the consequences of everything and of the benefit each matter carries for His servants, and of what each relative deserves, by the degree of relation to the deceased.

### The last verse revealed

Al-Bukhārī recorded that al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib said that the last sūrah to be revealed was sūrat Barāʾah, chapter 9, and the last verse to be revealed was this one. The two Shaykhs, Bukhārī and Muslim, report it. Imām Aḥmad recorded the illness behind it from Jābir bin ʿAbdullāh: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ came visiting him when he was so ill he fell unconscious, performed ablution, and poured, or had poured, the remaining water on him. Regaining consciousness, Jābir said he would leave inheritance only through kalālah, and asked what would come of it, and Allah revealed the verse of the farā'iḍ (4:11). The Two Ṣaḥīḥs and the Group recorded it. The chains run from Jābir through the companions of the Prophet ﷺ who transmitted it, through Hishām ibn Abi Abd Allāh and the line of Abū ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Abī Ḥāmid.

### ʿUmar's three wishes

The meaning and ruling of the kalālah was somewhat confusing even to the Leader of the Faithful. In the Two Ṣaḥīḥs, ʿUmar said that there were three matters he wished the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had explained, so that they could abide by his explanation: the share of the grandfather, the kalālah, and a certain type of ribā. Imām Aḥmad recorded that he said he asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about nothing more than the kalālah, until the Prophet ﷺ stabbed him with his finger in the chest and said: the verse at the end of sūrat an-Nisāʾ should suffice for you. Muslim recorded a longer form of it. Ibn Jarīr recorded that Ṭāriq bin Shihāb said that ʿUmar once gathered the Companions and said he would give a ruling on the kalālah that women would talk about in their bedrooms. A snake then appeared in the house and the gathering dispersed, and ʿUmar said: had Allah willed this verdict to happen, it would have happened. Al-Ḥākim Abū ʿAbdullāh an-Naysābūrī recorded that ʿUmar said that had he asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about three things, it would have been better for him than red camels: who should be the khalīfah after him, whether to fight a people who agreed to pay zakah but not to the khalīfah, and the kalālah. Its chain is ṣaḥīḥ, as al-Ḥākim noted, though the two Shaykhs did not record it.

### The two communities compared

The inheritance rulings stand scattered at three places in this sūrah, at the beginning, in the middle, and now at the end. The wisdom may lie in the injustice that prevailed in inheritance before Islam: taken up gradually, the subject would alert the addressees step by step to the need of justice in this area. Around the closing ruling, the comparison of verse 170 and verse 175 completes itself. There, the condition of the disbelievers, to whom belongs what is in the heavens and the earth whether they believe or not, and here, the model of the noble Companions, who believed in Allah and held fast to Him. The People of the Book made the abomination of suggesting a partner and a son in the purest conceivable divinity an article of their faith, and took a blatant position against the revelation. The Companions, without saying much of their fundamentals of faith and their devoted worship, were equally inquisitive about obligations subsidiary and commonplace, those of inheritance and marriage. They waited for wahy, the command of Allah through revelation, and sought the Prophet's ﷺ guidance in everything, though it was easier to do their own bidding under reason or desire. Men and women of faith, they turned to Allah in remembrance and were honored by being addressed by their most exalted Creator, a grace never granted to any other community. What they did not understand, they rechecked with him until satisfied. Here are two sets of people, so different and so apart.

The verse also shows that the Prophet ﷺ gave no decision of his own without the guiding command of revelation. Where no standing guidance existed, he held his decision and waited for wahy to come, and then ruled. In this lies a hint at the wisdom of gradual revelation: had the whole Book come down at one fixed time, as the People of the Book demanded, it would not have carried the benefits of a revelation arriving as needed and when appropriate. This modality accommodated questioners who asked out of necessity and were answered through the recited revelation, al-Wahy al-Matluww. Any verse revealed in favor of a Companion, or answering his question, is treasured as a testament of his virtues, keeping his name and merit alive until the Day of Doom. Thus Jābir b. ʿAbd Allāh is remembered in the Book itself. So sūrat an-Nisāʾ ends, and praise belongs to Allah.

### The inward reading

The ruling cuts disputes short. Wealth is an object of desire for humankind, and the lower selves are prone to greed. Had the appropriate amounts not been decisively determined, doubts would arise from the use of independent judgment, ijtihād, and these would lead to contention and mutual attacks. The fixed portions settle the disputants' claims definitively. The designation of inheritance rights for women, even when their closest relatives do not defend them, shows consideration for their weaker position, while the preference given to males answers the burdens laid on them of providing, seeking income and supporting.

### In short

- The kalālah is the one who dies leaving neither child nor parent, and the sisters, brothers and combinations receive their measured halves, two thirds, and the two-to-one rule.
- This is the last verse revealed, reported by al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib, answered for Jābir and his seven sisters, and it sufficed ʿUmar, who yet wished he had asked three things more.
- The verse displays the Companions' habit of asking and waiting for revelation, and the Book's habit of answering when the need arose.
- Fixed shares cut the greed of the lower selves short, protect the weaker, and weigh the burdens of the stronger.
