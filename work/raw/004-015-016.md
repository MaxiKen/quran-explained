<<<VERSE 4:15>>>
The earlier verses set right the wrongs of the days of ignorance toward orphans and in matters of inheritance. The people of that time also treated women cruelly and kept other evil customs, such as marrying women who were forbidden to them. This verse and the next deal with a woman's proven offence and how it is handled: "˹As for˺ those of your women who commit illegal intercourse—call four witnesses from among yourselves. If they testify, confine the offenders to their homes until they die or Allah ordains a ˹different˺ way for them." The correction of such customs continues through the next two or three passages.

The verse speaks of zinā, which covers both adultery and fornication. The penalty described here was the first one given, in the early days of Islam. It was later replaced, as the sections below explain.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹As for˺ those of your women who commit illegal intercourse"**. The Arabic word is fāḥishah, a lewd act, and here it means zinā. Explanations differ on which women are meant. One says free, married women. Another says women whether married or unmarried. A third says simply women who commit adultery.

**"call four witnesses from among yourselves"**. Four Muslim men are to be called as witnesses. One short explanation adds that they must be trustworthy, and another that they must have caught the pair in the act. The authority before which the case comes must call four qualified witnesses to prove the offence. All of them must be men, and a woman's testimony is not valid here.

**"If they testify, confine the offenders to their homes until they die"**. At the beginning of Islam, when a woman committed adultery and it was established by sufficient proof, she was confined to her home, with no leave to go out, until she died. Other explanations say that she was kept from mixing with people, or even held in prison until death. The order of revelation is also noted. The command to punish the offenders came first, and the order to confine the women came after. The Qur'an gave two limits for the confinement, death, or a way that Allah would appoint. If Allah prescribed another punishment while she was alive, it would replace the confinement.

**"or Allah ordains a ˹different˺ way for them"**. The "other way" is the later ruling that replaced this one. Ibn ʿAbbās said that confinement was the first ruling, until Allah revealed Sūrat an-Nūr, which put flogging for fornication and stoning for adultery in its place. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, Zayd ibn Aslam, Qatādah, Abū Ṣāliḥ, ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī, al-Ḥasan, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and ʿIkrimah all gave a similar account, and the matter is agreed upon. Short explanations state it in plain terms. Imprisoning a free, married woman until death was replaced by stoning, or the virgin receives a hundred lashes and a year's banishment while the married woman is stoned.

### Why four witnesses, and all of them men

Zinā is an extremely grave offence. It violates honour and modesty and brings shame on a family. For that reason, Islam set a very strict standard of proof for it. First, the witnesses must be men, and women's evidence is not accepted. Second, the number is raised to four. That is a hard condition, and it can very seldom be met. The strictness is there so that the woman's husband, his mother, another wife or a sister cannot bring false charges against her out of personal spite. It also stops other ill-wishers from using the charge to settle a private score.

If fewer than four people testify, their evidence is not valid. The accuser and all the witnesses can be treated as liars, and the penalty for false accusation, the ḥadd of qadhf, then applies to them for wrongly accusing a Muslim. Sūrat an-Nūr says clearly that those who cannot produce four witnesses are liars. Some of the elders give a reason for the number four. The case involves two persons, a man and a woman, so it falls under one rule for two cases. Each case needs two witnesses, and so this one needs four.

### The veil over sins

There is also a reading from the allusive side. Allah lowered a veil of generosity over the sins of His servants, and He did it by requiring an excessive number of witnesses to prove this lewd act. Following the witnessing procedure in the law makes it almost impossible to establish such a case. When Māʿiz ibn Mālik came to the Prophet ﷺ and said, "O Messenger of Allah, I have committed fornication," the Prophet ﷺ said, "Maybe you only kissed." On another occasion he said, "Smell his breath." In this is the strongest proof of the veil that is drawn over ugly acts.

### The "way" that Allah made

The "way" was later set out in the clear words of the Prophet ﷺ, which describe the married and the unmarried separately. ʿUbādah ibn aṣ-Ṣāmit said that when revelation came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, it weighed on him and his face showed the strain. One day Allah sent down a revelation, and when the strain had passed, the Prophet ﷺ said, as Aḥmad recorded:

> "Take it from me: Allah has made a way for them. The married with the married, the unmarried with the unmarried. The married receives a hundred lashes and stoning to death, and the unmarried a hundred lashes and banishment for a year."

Muslim and the compilers of the Sunan recorded another wording from ʿUbādah, in which the Prophet ﷺ says "Take from me, take from me", and at-Tirmidhī called it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. Muslim's version is also given with the words "Come and listen to me! Come and listen to me! Allah has now made a way for them."

The Prophet ﷺ gave the ḥadd of zinā in the case of Māʿiz ibn Mālik and in the case of a woman from the tribe of Azd. Both were married, so both were stoned. A couple from the Jews of Madīnah was also stoned for zinā, and that judgment was based on a ruling in the Torah.

The ruling for the unmarried offender is in Sūrat an-Nūr itself: "As for female and male fornicators, give each of them one hundred lashes" (24:2). A verse about rajm, stoning to death, was also revealed earlier, but its recitation was later abrogated. The ruling itself stayed in force. ʿUmar said that Allah sent Muḥammad ﷺ with the truth and sent down the Book to him, and that the verse of rajm was part of what was revealed. The Prophet ﷺ stoned, he said, and we stoned after him, and the revealed ruling of rajm stands against everyone who commits zinā while married, man or woman. Al-Bukhārī and Muslim recorded it, as cited in Mishkāt.

So the commands to "torture" and to "confine to homes" in these verses were replaced when the legal ḥadd was revealed, which is one hundred lashes or rajm. Further detail will come with the discussion of Sūrat an-Nūr.

### What the verse did not fix

These two verses do not give a definite ḥadd. They say only "punish them" and "confine the women to their homes". They name no particular method for the punishment, so that was left to the authorities. Ibn ʿAbbās said it means that the offenders are rebuked in words and shamed, and also punished physically, for example by being struck with hands and sandals. This sounds like an illustration, and the whole matter was left to the judgment of those in authority.

### Justice in punishment

Anyone who commits an act that Islamic teaching counts as a sin is to be dealt with under the rule of law and not at will. It is unjust to call a person a criminal without meeting the legal conditions, and there is no permission to go ahead hastily even against a proven criminal. Punishment exists to uphold justice, and tyranny or injustice can never serve that goal.

### In short

- The verse gave the first ruling for a woman who commits zinā: four male Muslim witnesses, then confinement to her home until death, or until Allah appointed another way.
- The four-witness rule is deliberately hard to meet, to stop spite and false charges, and false accusers are themselves punished.
- Sūrat an-Nūr and the Prophet's ﷺ words replaced confinement with a hundred lashes and a year's banishment for the unmarried and stoning for the married, as in the cases of Māʿiz ibn Mālik and the Azd woman.
- Punishment must follow the rule of law, and the strictness of proof is read as a veil of mercy over people's sins.

<<<VERSE 4:16>>>
This verse gives a second rule and then opens the door of repentance: "And the two among you who commit this sin—discipline them. If they repent and mend their ways, relieve them. Surely Allah is ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful." The two verses together (4:15 and 4:16) were first read as dealing with illegal relations between a man and a woman. Some scholars, however, think this verse refers to two men.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And the two among you who commit this sin"**. The Arabic uses a dual form, "the two". Scholars understood it in two ways. One view takes it to mean the unmarried man and woman who commit zinā. Another takes it to mean two men, whether married or unmarried. Mujāhid said that the verse was revealed about two men who do it, as if he were pointing to the act of the people of Lūṭ.

Qāḍī Thanāʾullāh Pānīpatī and some other commentators hold that the verse is about homosexual acts between two males. The Arabic pronoun for "those two of you" is masculine and presumes males on both sides. Those who read both verses as being about zinā answer that masculine wording in general usage includes the feminine. Whichever side is right, the possibility that this verse refers to homosexual acts cannot be ruled out. One explanation judges the reference to two men to be the more obvious reading. It argues that the particle "of you" is attached to a masculine pronoun, that both parties suffer the same punishment, that both can repent, and that both are told to be left alone afterward. All of this fits men, since detention was stipulated for women in the previous verse. Ash-Shāfiʿī, however, was of the opinion that it refers to an adulterer and an adulteress.

**"discipline them"**. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās said that this punishment includes cursing the pair, shaming them and striking them with sandals. Other explanations speak of insults and beating with sandals, of maligning and upbraiding, or of words and blows to humiliate and discipline them. No particular method is given, and the matter was left to the authorities. This ruling stood until Allah replaced it with flogging or stoning. A short explanation says that the unmarried were to receive a hundred lashes in its place, and another says that the unmarried are whipped a hundred times and banished for a year, and the married are stoned. If a lewd act means adultery, this verse is replaced by the prescribed punishment. If it means homosexual intercourse, ash-Shāfiʿī held that it is replaced as well. In his view, though, the person on the receiving end is not stoned even if he is married. He is flogged and banished.

**"If they repent and mend their ways, relieve them"**. The repentance meant here is to stop the evil act, after which the offenders' actions become righteous. "Relieve them" means do not abuse them verbally after that, since a person who truly repents is just like one who has no sin. It does not mean that repentance cancels the punishment. The repentance is mentioned after the punishment, as the Arabic connecting particle shows. So if the offenders have repented after the punishment, they must not be disgraced or punished any further. If they have not repented, reproach is still in order after the punishment.

The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record a related ruling: when the slave-girl of one of you commits zinā, let him flog her and not chastise her afterwards. The lashes she receives erase the sin she committed.

**"Surely Allah is ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful"**. Allah overlooks sins. He turns, with relenting, to those who repent and is compassionate toward them. A person who turns away from a sin and asks forgiveness is like a person who has not sinned.

### The act of the people of Lūṭ in the reports

Reports about this act are severe. The collectors of the Sunan recorded that Ibn ʿAbbās said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "Whoever you find doing the act of the people of Lūṭ, kill both the one who does it and the one it is done to."

Abū Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that Allah has cursed seven kinds of His creatures from above the seven heavens. He cursed one of them three times, saying, "Cursed is the one who does what the people of Lūṭ did", three times, and He cursed the others once, which is enough for them. This is cited in At-Targhīb wa-t-Tarhīb. Abū Hurayrah also reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that four kinds of people spend their mornings under Allah's wrath and their evenings under His rage. The Companions asked who they were, and he said, "Men who imitate women, women who imitate men, one who goes to animals, and one who goes to men."

According to Ḥāfiẓ Zakī ad-Dīn in the same work, four caliphs, Abū Bakr, ʿAlī, ʿAbdullāh ibn az-Zubayr and Hishām ibn ʿAbd al-Malik, condemned those guilty of sodomy to burning. He also relates, from Muḥammad ibn al-Munkadir, that Khālid ibn al-Walīd wrote to Abū Bakr about a man in a certain place in Arabia with whom people did what is done with women. Abū Bakr called the Companions together, ʿAlī among them. He said that no group had committed a sin like this except one, and that they all knew how Allah dealt with that group. He proposed that the man be burned. The other Companions agreed, and Abū Bakr ordered it so.

The people of Lūṭ were the people to whom the prophet Lūṭ was sent. Besides rejecting the message and the messenger and ascribing partners to Allah, they were addicted to this unnatural practice. When Lūṭ's teaching had no effect on them, the angels acted as Allah commanded. They lifted the towns off the ground, turned them upside down and threw them back down. The details will come in the discussion of Sūrat al-Aʿrāf.

Jurists differ on the fixed punishment for this act, and the books of fiqh give the details. The punishments reported are as severe as any: burning in fire, crushing under a wall, throwing from a height, stoning and beheading with the sword.

### Other acts the reports warn against

Other narrations give very severe warnings against anal intercourse with women. Ibn ʿAbbās reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that Allah does not look with mercy on a person who does it with a man or a woman. Khuzaymah ibn Thābit reported that he said three times that Allah is not embarrassed by the truth, and then said, "Do not go to women anally." Abū Hurayrah reported that he said, "Cursed is the one who goes to women anally." Abū Hurayrah also reported that whoever goes to a woman in her menstruation, or in her anus, or goes to a soothsayer, a kāhin, and believes his claims about the unknown, has rejected what was revealed to Muḥammad ﷺ.

### Forgiveness for those who repent

If a sinner admits his fault and reforms, it becomes essential to treat him with sympathy and forgiveness. It is not right to judge anyone by his past. Allah Himself pardons those who sincerely repent and turns in kindness to those who mend their ways. Human beings therefore have no right to sneer at former offenders or make fun of them. A person who taunts them may yet prove to be a wrongdoer himself.

### The inner reading

The command to use different kinds of punishment is the strongest way of gently communicating the warning to stay away from the act. A servant is meant to be on guard and not become deserving of the greater punishment.

### In short

- The "two" in the verse are understood either as an unmarried man and woman who commit zinā or as two men. The possibility of a reference to homosexual acts cannot be ruled out.
- The early punishment was discipline by words and blows. It was replaced by flogging and banishment for the unmarried and stoning for the married, though ash-Shāfiʿī gave a different treatment for the receiving partner.
- Repentance and reform end the reproach. It does not cancel the punishment, but afterward the offender is not to be shamed or abused.
- The reports on the act of the people of Lūṭ are very severe, jurists differ on its fixed punishment, and similar warnings are given about anal intercourse.
- Allah accepts repentance and is Most Merciful, and no one has the right to taunt a person who has repented.
