The enemies of Islam had objected to the change of qiblah in the hope of planting doubts about the religion itself. The preceding verses answered them, but some simply ignored the answers and persisted in their hostility, and that was likely to dishearten the Muslims. Verse 153 forestalls such a reaction by prescribing the way to overcome grief and anxiety. "O believers! Seek comfort in patience and prayer. Allah is truly with those who are patient". Having commanded that He be thanked (verse 152), Allah now ordains patience and prayer, for a servant is always either enjoying a bounty he should be thankful for or suffering a hardship he should meet with patience. A hadith describes the believer's condition as remarkable: every divine decree brings him good. Receiving a blessing, he responds gratefully and benefits through that gratitude. Facing affliction, he remains patient and benefits through that patience.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Seek comfort in patience and prayer."**. You who have faith in Allah and follow His Messenger ﷺ, ask Allah's help in everything, for this world and the Hereafter, through the beauty of patience and by establishing the prayer as He has instructed. Patience here means steadfastness in fulfilling Allah's obligations and in abstaining from violations and shameful deeds, and patience under affliction. Prayer is singled out for mention because of how often it is performed and how great it is, and it includes abundant supererogatory prayers by night and day, which cleanse a person of sin. Patience and prayer are the best means of easing the effects of affliction, as the earlier verse said: "And seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed, it is a burden except for the humble—" (2:45). The Qur'an states the remedy in a deliberately general form, "seek help", without specifying the situation, which shows that it works for all the ills that are part of human existence, whether wants and needs or anxiety and suffering. The promise applies above all to those who pray, whether the obligatory (farḍ) or the voluntary (nafl) prayer, since prayer is the supreme form of worship.

**"Allah is truly with those who are patient"**. He helps them, protects them from shameful deeds and gives them success. This phrase holds the secret of how patience saves a person from every kind of trouble. The reward of patience is the honour of Allah's company, and once the might of the Lord of the Worlds has come to a person's aid, what pain can overwhelm him, and who can stop his affairs from prospering?

### What patience (ṣabr) means

The Arabic ṣabr is far wider than the English "patience". Its root meaning is restraining oneself, keeping oneself under control. In the usage of the Qur'an and hadith it has three modes. The first is holding back from whatever the Sharīʿah has declared unlawful (ḥarām). The second is forcing oneself to be regular in the forms of worship and steadfast in obeying Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, and this kind brings more reward than the first. The third is enduring every kind of trouble and pain, understanding that it is Allah's will that one should suffer and hoping for a reward for it, and this kind is mandatory, just as repentance is. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr is cited for the point that a word of grief or a sigh of pain that one cannot hold back does not contradict ṣabr or nullify it. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam divided ṣabr into two parts: patience for Allah's sake in what pleases Him, even if it is hard on heart and body, and patience in avoiding what He dislikes, even if it is desired. Whoever acquires both will, Allah willing, be among the patient whom Allah greets when they meet Him, as Sūrat al-Aḥzāb says: "Their greeting on the Day they meet Him will be, 'Peace!' And He has prepared for them an honourable reward" (33:44).

People commonly identify ṣabr with the third mode alone and overlook the first two, which are in fact the more basic. All three are equally obligatory, and every Muslim is required to practise them all. In the language of the Qur'an and hadith, al-Ṣābirūn is the title of those who are steadfast in all three with equal rigour. A hadith says that on the Day of Judgement a call will go out, "Where are the Ṣābirūn?", and those who kept to the three forms of patience will rise and be admitted to Paradise without having to render an account of their deeds. The Qur'an itself corroborates this: "Only those who endure patiently will be given their reward without limit" (39:10).

### Why prayer is named separately

Ṣabr, as just defined, already covers every form of worship, prayer included, since all of them are its branches. Ṣalāh is nevertheless mentioned on its own because it is the most perfect model of ṣabr. In prayer a person binds himself to obedience and worship and restrains himself not only from everything sinful but even from what is otherwise permissible, such as eating, drinking and talking. Prayer is thus a visible demonstration of what ṣabr means: keeping oneself under control, shunning everything sinful and submitting wholly to obedience.

Prayer also has a special power to release a person from every kind of trouble and to fulfil his needs. This may not be explicable rationally, but the efficacy is a characteristic quality in the very nature of prayer, as it is in certain medicines. It shows itself, however, only when prayer is offered properly, with the physical and spiritual etiquette the Sharīʿah lays down. If our prayers seem fruitless, it is because we have been careless of that etiquette and have not turned to Allah with single-minded devotion and total submission. According to hadith, whenever the Prophet ﷺ faced a grave problem of any kind he hastened to offer nafl prayers, and through the blessing (barakah) of prayer Allah came to his aid and resolved it.

### The world as a testing ground

Whoever has adopted religion in the real sense has truly discovered Allah. He lives in permanent thanksgiving and remembrance of his Lord, and that life alone is a source of peace and happiness. Peace and happiness, however, will materialise completely only in the eternal world to come. This world was not created for reward but for the trial of humankind. Because it is a testing ground, Allah's servants meet obstacles on their path, so that the earnest can be told apart from those who are not serious. The obstacles come in ordinary situations: human temptations, family demands, worldly interests, social pressure. These are the real tests of life. The person must recognise the test being faced. Remembering Allah and giving thanks for life's blessings then helps avoid its pitfalls. There is only one way to succeed, and that is patience and prayer, which means attaching oneself to Allah and sticking conscientiously to the path of truth while enduring every setback. Those who do not waver from the path despite its difficulties, who stay with Allah even when no worldly benefit seems to come of it, are the ones who will finally emerge successful. Allah has reserved the eternal blessings of the next life for those willing to sacrifice this life for it.

### The blessing that comes through patience

In the devotional reading, "seek help through patience and prayer" is also read as: seek Allah's blessing (ṣalāt) through patience, for one becomes worthy of the Lord's blessing by bearing patiently the flow of His decrees. That is why this passage says "give good tidings to the patient" (verse 155) and then "upon those rest blessings and mercy from their Lord" (verse 157). One explanation connects the patient's highest ranks and ultimate treasures with divine company, maʿiyyah. Allah says, "Allah is truly with those who are patient".

### In short

- When opponents persist despite every answer, the believer's remedy for grief is patience and prayer (cf. 2:45), a remedy stated generally because it suits every trouble.
- Ṣabr has three obligatory modes: abstaining from the ḥarām, steadfastness in worship and obedience, and enduring hardship. The Ṣābirūn who keep all three enter Paradise without reckoning (39:10, 33:44).
- Prayer is named separately as the most perfect form of self-restraint and because of its special efficacy when performed with proper etiquette, as the Prophet ﷺ did in every crisis.
- "Allah is with the patient" is the secret: His company brings help, protection and success, and the world's trials are the means by which the earnest are distinguished.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Never say that those martyred in the cause of Allah are dead"**. Do not say, O believers, of those killed in obedience to Allah, striving in His path at Badr or in any other battle, that they are dead like other people who die. One who dies in the cause of Allah is a shahīd, a martyr, and although it is correct and even permitted to call him dead, we are forbidden to regard his death like ordinary deaths.

**"in fact, they are alive!"**. They are alive with their Lord, living as those in Paradise live, provided with comfort and bounties and receiving their sustenance. Muslim reports in his Ṣaḥīḥ that the souls of the martyrs are inside green birds that move about in Paradise wherever they wish and then take refuge in lamps hanging beneath the Throne. Their Lord looked at them and asked what they wished for, and they replied: what more could we wish for, when You have favoured us as You have favoured none of Your creation? The question was repeated until they understood that an answer was required. They asked to return to earthly life for another opportunity to fight for Allah and be killed for Him again. The Lord said: I have written that they shall not be returned to it. Imām Aḥmad reports that ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Kaʿb ibn Mālik related from his father that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that the believer's soul is a bird feeding on the trees of Paradise until Allah returns it to its body on the day of resurrection. That hadith includes all believers in its general sense, so the Qur'an's singling out of the martyrs serves to honour and favour them, even though other believers share in the rewards they enjoy.

**"But you do not perceive it"**. You are not aware of the honour bestowed on them or of their real state, because the manner of their life can be known only through revelation from Allah. With these words the Qur'an asserts that life in the barzakh transcends all sensory perception.

### Life in the barzakh

According to the teachings of Islam, the dead are given a kind of new life in the barzakh, the intermediate stage that begins with death and lasts until the Day of Resurrection, and this life develops in them a sensitivity to punishment and reward. Believer or disbeliever, virtuous or vicious, everyone receives this taste of life in the barzakh. It has many grades, however: one for people in general, and others for prophets and the righteous, which differ in degree of excellence. Many scholars have gathered material on the subject, and the balanced view of Maulānā Ashraf ʿAlī of Thānā Bhawan, which agrees closely with the Qur'an and the teaching of the Prophet ﷺ, can be summarised as follows.

Life in the barzakh is given to everyone and gives perception of reward and punishment, but the martyr's life there is qualitatively different. His perception is keener and deeper, as life is present both in the fingertips and in the heels but the fingertips are far more sensitive. The finer life of the martyr in the barzakh even reaches his physical body, whereas ordinary bodies are unaffected. Consequently a martyr's body does not waste away, decay or mingle with the dust, but keeps its freshness and a semblance of life, as hadiths and observation confirm. The martyrs are therefore described as living, and we are forbidden to call them dead. For all worldly purposes, however, they are treated like the dead: their property is divided and their widows may remarry. The prophets' life in the barzakh has a further distinction. Their life-sensitivity is finer still, their bodies retain the quality of life, and in some ways it extends into this world, so that their property is not divided and their widows may not remarry. The strongest in retaining this quality of life are the prophets, then the martyrs, then ordinary people. Some hadiths indicate that certain friends of Allah and righteous people share this excellence with the martyrs, apparently those who die while exercising severe discipline over their lower selves. So although the verse speaks specifically of martyrs, it does not exclude the virtuous and the truthful.

If a martyr's body does return to dust as ordinary bodies do, the chances are that the person did not actually die in the cause of Allah, which is the sole criterion of martyrdom (shahādah). Where someone fulfilled all the requirements of martyrdom beyond any doubt, a hadith establishes that his body must not return to dust. If it nevertheless mingles with the earth, which is what made the author of Rūḥ al-Maʿānī wonder how a martyr's body could be eaten away despite the hadiths, the explanation is that the hadith denies the earth's consuming the body but not decomposition from other causes such as chemical reaction, enzymes and bacteria. Weapons, medicines, food and the elements of water, fire and air undoubtedly affected the bodies of the prophets in this world, and the martyr's life in the barzakh is not superior to the prophets' life in this world, so if such agents act on martyrs' bodies it does not contradict the hadiths that say their bodies are forbidden to the earth. Another answer is that the martyrs' distinction appears in their bodies remaining unspoiled for a very long time, even if disintegration in the long run remains possible, for preservation over a long period is in itself a departure from the normal behaviour of dead bodies, just as eternal preservation would be.

### Alive in the Friend

In the devotional reading, the life of this world has left the martyrs behind, but they have reached endless life. Release from this world's abasement is no loss to them. They have gained union with their exalted Patron. The lover asks that death be described as receiving life in the Friend who took him. True life depends on Him rather than on the spirit alone. The life received in the Friend does not end. The Pīr of the Path prayed: O Lord, if someone is occupied with You, how could his occupation end? When someone lives through You, how could he ever die? A spirit in a body that is deprived of You is like an imprisoned corpse. Truly alive is he who lives with You. Praise be to Allah for the slain of whom the King says that they are alive. Awe is pictured as their cloak. Beneath the Tremendous Throne they find intimate rest, while their spirits repose in the Presence of the Real's majesty, "at the Seat of Honour in the presence of the Most Powerful Sovereign" (54:55).

### In short

- The martyrs of Badr, Uḥud and every battle are not to be called dead as others are dead. They live with their Lord and are provided for, their souls in green birds in Paradise (Muslim), and their one wish is to return and be killed again.
- All the dead have some life in the barzakh, but the martyr's is keener, so that even his body resists decay, and the prophets' is higher still. For worldly rulings, however, martyrs are treated as dead.
- Decay of a body indicates either that the person was not truly a martyr or that natural agents, not the earth's consuming, were at work. In any case long preservation is itself extraordinary.
- "You do not perceive it": this life is beyond the senses and known only by revelation.

### The price of the Path

Calling people to truth requires the greatest sacrifices for Allah. Suffering is his lot, because his hearers respond badly to the criticism and admonition that are inseparable from such preaching, and few people in this world are willing to be reproved and warned. The sincere preacher also earns the hostility of mercenary people who do brisk business in the name of religion, since his sincerity threatens the standing of hypocrites. Taking up the task of preaching Allah's word is like putting one's hand into a fire. It exposes a person to prejudice, economic ruin and even expulsion from his own land, and places his life and property in the utmost peril. Persecution in this world is inevitable for one who follows Allah's path. Yet losing everything for Allah is pictured as true finding. Those surrendering their lives to Him receive life without end. For those who do not try to build their paradise in this fleeting world, Allah has prepared an eternal paradise in the next.

In the devotional reading, it is the custom of the Lord that whenever He threatens the servant or shows severity in a verse, He caresses him and gives him hope right after it or just before it. Bearing grief for Him requires genuine strength.

### Death as a reminder

The death of a loved one puts a person in a state of prayerfulness, deep thought and contemplation. It reminds him that very little time is at his disposal and that death can come calling at any moment. Yet we are so absorbed in worldly things that we never pause to think of the day that is fast approaching. The Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ said that people are asleep and will wake up only when they die. Death suddenly brings a person before Allah for an account of every deed. The difference between the life actually lived and the life that should have been lived then becomes clear. The Prophet ﷺ also said that on the Day of Judgement no one's feet will move until four questions are answered: where his money came from, the way he spent it, the use he made of his youth, and what he did with his knowledge. The Creator has divided human life into two parts, before death and after it. The first is very short, like the tip of an iceberg, compared with the second, which is eternal, and it is the preparatory phase in which one becomes eligible to enter Paradise. This worldly life is a test for everyone, poor or rich, powerful or powerless, strong or weak, and it is passed by living a need-based life rather than one driven by desire and greed, so that in the Hereafter Allah admits a person to Paradise to live for ever close to his Creator.

On the death of a loved one, then, one should not sink into mourning. The Qur'an gives great hope in moments of grief and loss, saying that Allah will reunite the righteous members of a family in Paradise, and that if one member has reached a higher level, the other righteous members will be raised to it, so that all of them enjoy eternal bliss and nearness to Allah. This idea gives great solace, and it is an incentive to good work and a pious life. The Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ described three things continuing to benefit a person after death: ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge and righteous children praying for him. Other connections are cut off.

The patient are those who do not oppose His decree in what He brings to pass. The demands of the unseen (ghayb) come either through wealth, through the self or through near relations. Whoever devotes wealth to Allah will have salvation. Refusing to withhold the spirit from Allah brings intimate communication without end. Those struck by affliction meet it with patience, even with gratitude, and some with exultation. Whoever sees things as the property of the Real sees himself as a stranger standing between Him and His decree. The Creator, after all, has a greater right over the things He made than they have over themselves.

The mercy promised here has a long history among the family of the Kaʿbah. Ibrāhīm (Abraham), a native of Iraq, left his wife Hājar and his infant son Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) at Allah's command in the uninhabited, waterless place that is now Makkah. Thirst drove Hājar to run back and forth between the hills of Ṣafā and Marwah, and after seven unsuccessful attempts she returned to find the spring of Zamzam flowing from the ground. The event shows how Allah looks after His servants. Whoever persists in His service until there is nothing beneath his feet but burning sand will find that Allah in His mercy causes a refreshing spring to gush from the dryness of the desert, and the pilgrims to Makkah repeat her quest between the hills to commemorate what it means for everyone who struggles in Allah's path. The Jewish religious leaders of Madinah, by contrast, denied the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ although Allah's signs were displayed clearly in his life and teaching. They feared that accepting an Arab prophet would end their religious hegemony, that people would stop looking to them as leaders, and that the old traditions and the interests bound up with them, of which they were the main beneficiaries, would be destroyed. They thought it wise to conceal the truth lest they lose their worldly status, when it would have been far wiser to proclaim it. They forgot that Allah's servants are required to put truth before worldly interest. Whoever becomes destitute for the sake of the Lord draws succour from the inexhaustible treasures of divine grace. Allah keeps the way to mercy open for His servants. Whoever recognises his error, reforms his conduct and declares the truth as commanded can receive forgiveness. Only those who persist in disbelief until the day they die are deprived of His mercy.

### In short

- The patient who say the istirjāʿ receive Allah's praise and forgiveness in this world and His mercy, meaning safety from punishment, in the next (Saʿīd ibn Jubayr).
- ʿUmar called the blessings and mercy "two righteous things" and the guidance "the heights", that is, reward and more.
- Their patience itself was a gift of Allah's prior mercy: blessed at the beginning, they were guided at the end.
- Hājar's spring shows that Allah's mercy meets those who persist for Him, and its doors stay open to all who turn back, closing only on those who die in disbelief.
