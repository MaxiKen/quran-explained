<<<VERSE 3:86>>>
The verse asks, “How will Allah guide a people who chose to disbelieve after they had believed” (3:86), even though they had acknowledged the Messenger and received clear proofs? They have chosen wrongdoing after the truth became clear. Allah does not grant them special guidance while they persist in rejecting it.

### Phrase by phrase

**“How will Allah guide a people who chose to disbelieve after they had believed”** describes people who first accepted Islam and then returned to disbelief. Their prior faith means that they knew what they were leaving. One report concerns a man from the Anṣār who embraced Islam, became an idolater again, and later regretted his decision.

The man sent word through his people asking whether the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would accept his repentance. The verses describing the apostasy and later repentance were revealed. When his people recited them to him, he said they were truthful and returned to Islam. The Prophet accepted his repentance. Ibn Jarir, An-Nasaʾī, al-Ḥākim, and Ibn Ḥibbān record the report. Al-Ḥākim says its chain is ṣaḥīḥ, sound.

Another report describes al-Ḥārith bin Suwayd, who had been a Muslim and fought with the Prophet ﷺ, then left Islam and joined his people. When someone read the verses to him, he acknowledged that the reader was truthful, that the Messenger was more truthful, and that Allah is more truthful than all. He returned to Islam and became a good Muslim.

**“acknowledged the Messenger to be true”** means they had already testified that Muhammad ﷺ brought the truth. Their later rejection is not simply a lack of familiarity with his message.

**“and received clear proofs”** means that evidence had come to them. The Prophet’s truth was made clear through revelation and signs. They knowingly turned back from what they had recognized.

**“For Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people”** means that Allah does not grant special guidance to those who persist in wrongdoing. It does not mean that repentance is impossible. The next verses explicitly allow repentance and amendment. The warning describes their condition while they continue in rejection, not an unchangeable barrier against returning to faith.

A reflective explanation says that those who have turned away from sincere service cannot expect closeness while remaining in their chosen error. Yet the report of the man from the Anṣār shows that when he regretted his apostasy and returned, Allah accepted him.

### In short

- Some people accepted Islam and later apostatized after recognizing the Messenger’s truth.
- The report of the man from the Anṣār shows that sincere repentance can be accepted.
- Allah does not guide those who persist in wrongdoing, but the next verse makes room for return.

<<<VERSE 3:87>>>
The verse describes the consequence of knowingly turning away: “Their reward is that they will be condemned by Allah, the angels, and all of humanity” (3:87). The punishment is expressed as a curse, meaning distance from Allah’s mercy and condemnation by His creation.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Their reward is”** introduces the consequence for people who reject the truth after clear evidence and remain in that state. It is not a reward of honor but a fitting recompense for their choice.

**“they will be condemned by Allah”** means they are distanced from His mercy while they persist in disbelief. One inward reading says their final state follows from the rejection they chose at the beginning and the turning away from service that followed.

**“the angels”** are also described as condemning them. The verse presents their rejection as a matter known beyond human society.

**“and all of humanity”** means that the condemnation is not limited to the believers who witnessed the apostasy. It extends to people who recognize the truth and to the broader community of humankind.

In the reported case of the man from the Anṣār, the verses were revealed after he asked whether he could repent. The story shows that the warning concerns those who continue in rejection. When the man returned sincerely, he was accepted. The curse is not placed upon everyone who once committed apostasy and later repents.

### In short

- Persistent rejection after clear evidence brings condemnation.
- The curse describes distance from Allah’s mercy.
- The person who sincerely repents is not excluded from forgiveness.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:88>>>
The verse says, “They will be in Hell forever” (3:88). Those who persist in rejection will not have their punishment lightened or delayed. The warning describes the final outcome of those who die without repentance.

### Phrase by phrase

**“They will be in Hell forever”** refers to the lasting punishment of those who remain in disbelief. Another explanation connects the pronoun with the curse itself, which is the distance from Allah’s mercy that the previous verse described.

**“Their punishment will not be lightened”** means the suffering will not be reduced, even for a short time. They will not be granted relief from the consequence of knowingly rejecting the truth.

**“nor will they be delayed ˹from it˺”** means they will receive no reprieve or postponement once the punishment is due. The verse emphasizes the seriousness of the choice to persist in disbelief.

This warning follows the mention of condemnation by Allah, the angels, and humanity. It describes what happens to those who remain in that state, while the next verse explains that repentance and reform change a person’s outcome.

### In short

- Those who die persisting in disbelief face lasting punishment.
- Their punishment will not be reduced or postponed.
- The next verse distinguishes them from those who repent and amend their conduct.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:89>>>
The verse makes clear that the door remains open: “As for those who repent afterwards and mend their ways” (3:89), Allah is Forgiving and Merciful. A person who returns from disbelief and follows repentance with righteous action may be forgiven.

### Phrase by phrase

**“As for those who repent afterwards”** refers to people who turn back to Allah after apostasy or wrongdoing. The man from the Anṣār in the report asked whether Allah would accept his return, and he became Muslim again when the verses reached him.

**“and mend their ways”** means they correct their conduct and sincerely return to Allah’s oneness. Repentance is more than regret in words. It includes leaving disbelief and living according to the faith one has accepted.

**“then surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful”** means Allah forgives those who sincerely repent and shows them mercy. The report about al-Ḥārith bin Suwayd also describes a man who returned to Islam and became a good Muslim.

An inward reading says that the people reached by mercy were not in the final group of those who remain in rejection, even if others thought they would be. Allah knows who sincerely returns.

### In short

- Repentance is possible after disbelief.
- Returning to Allah must be joined with correcting one’s actions.
- Allah forgives and shows mercy to those who sincerely return.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:90>>>
The verse warns about people “who disbelieve after having believed then increase in disbelief” (3:90). Their repentance is not accepted if they keep increasing in rejection until death. They have strayed from the path of truth.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Indeed, those who disbelieve after having believed”** refers to people who first accepted Islam and later rejected it. A report from Ibn ʿAbbās describes people who accepted Islam, apostatized, returned, and then apostatized again. Their situation is not the same as a person who regrets and makes a sincere return.

**“then increase in disbelief”** means they persist in rejection and become more committed to it. Some explanations apply this to Jews who rejected ʿĪsā (Jesus), the Injīl (Gospel), and then Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur’an. Abū al-ʿĀliyah says it concerns Jews and Christians who knew the descriptions of Muhammad but continued to disbelieve in him. ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī and al-Ḥasan also explain the verse in connection with rejection of Jesus and the Gospel followed by rejection of the final messenger.

**“their repentance will never be accepted”** means that a last-minute claim of repentance at the point of death does not undo a life of increasing rejection. Another verse says repentance is not accepted from those who continue evil until death is facing them (4:18). This does not contradict the previous verse, which accepts sincere repentance made before that point.

A hadith recorded by al-Bazzār from Ibn ʿAbbās describes people who repeatedly entered Islam and then left it. Their question about repentance led to the revelation of these verses. The account is a warning not to treat repeated rejection as harmless or assume that a person can postpone return until the last moment.

An inward reading applies the verse to people who leave the path of spiritual striving and return to habitual desires. They become more isolated in their darkness and no longer feel regret for the moments of sincere service they have lost. It says that a person who turns away from the Ṭarīq, the path, must feel remorse and return before the heart grows accustomed to estrangement.

The Qur’an says that Allah may turn hearts and eyes away when people refuse to believe at the first opportunity (6:110). The warning is not that Allah rejects a sincere person who turns back. It is that repeatedly choosing disbelief can harden the heart until the person no longer seeks repentance.

### When is repentance no longer accepted?

The verse concerns people who remain in disbelief and continue increasing in it, especially those who wait until death to claim repentance. The previous verse offers forgiveness to those who turn back and amend their conduct while they can still do so.

### In short

- Persistent disbelief after faith leads a person farther from the truth.
- Repentance at the point of death is not the same as sincere return before then.
- The verse urges people to repent while they can still change their course.

<<<VERSE 3:91>>>
The verse declares that “if each of those who disbelieve then die as disbelievers were to offer a ransom of enough gold to fill the whole world, it would never be accepted from them” (3:91). After death, wealth cannot purchase release from punishment, and no helper can rescue them.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Indeed, if each of those who disbelieve then die as disbelievers”** describes people who persist in rejection until death. Their final state is different from that of those who repent and return before death.

**“were to offer a ransom of enough gold to fill the whole world”** means that even the greatest imaginable fortune would not pay for forgiveness. The Qur’an says that no compensation will be accepted on that Day (2:123), that a ransom cannot rescue those who die in disbelief (5:36), and that there will be no bargaining or friendship that saves them (14:31). Their wealth and influence cannot change Allah’s judgment.

The same warning appears in a hadith about a person from the Fire. Allah asks whether he would ransom himself with the earth full of gold. He says yes, but Allah tells him that he had been asked in worldly life to do something easier and did not do it. Aḥmad records the report from Anas. The contrast is between an impossible ransom after death and the opportunity to obey Allah while alive.

A report also describes ʿAbd Allāh bin Judʿān, who was generous to guests, helped people in debt, and fed the poor. The Prophet ﷺ was asked whether those good deeds would benefit him. He said they would not, because the man had not believed or asked Allah to forgive him. Good conduct has value, but it cannot replace faith in Allah.

Another report from Aḥmad speaks of a man from Paradise who asks to return to the world and be killed in Allah’s cause ten times because of the honor of martyrdom. A man from the Fire, when offered the whole earth in gold as ransom, accepts, but Allah says he had refused an easier command during his life. The report shows that the Hereafter is not a place where people can buy back the chances they rejected.

**“it would never be accepted from them”** means that the ransom cannot cancel the punishment. The rejected offer is not a matter of its size. It is rejected because the person died without faith and repentance.

**“It is they who will suffer a painful punishment”** means that their outcome is severe and real. It is not a temporary inconvenience that can be exchanged for wealth.

**“and they will have no helpers”** means no one can shield them from the punishment or intercede without Allah’s permission. Wealth, family, and former status cannot rescue them.

One report says these verses were revealed about ten hypocrites who left al-Madīnah for Makkah as apostates. Some died in that state, some were killed, and some later returned to Islam. The warning applies to those who die in disbelief, not those who repent before death.

### Why can’t wealth serve as a ransom?

In this life, a person can use wealth to repair harm, return property, or help others. After death, no one can purchase a new opportunity to believe. The verses call people to repent and obey while they still have time.

### In short

- A person who dies in disbelief cannot ransom themselves with any amount of wealth.
- Good deeds do not replace faith in Allah and repentance.
- No helper can remove the punishment after a person dies rejecting the truth.
