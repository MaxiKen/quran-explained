<<<VERSE 3:86>>>
The question is asked of people who had everything needed to stay: "How will Allah guide a people who chose to disbelieve after they had believed, acknowledged the Messenger to be true, and received clear proofs? For Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people". Proofs and evidences were established, the truth was explained, and they reverted anyway into the darkness of polytheism. Guidance is withheld not from ignorance, but from a willing leap into blindness.

### Phrase by phrase

**"How will Allah guide a people who chose to disbelieve after they had believed"**: How shall Allah guide to His religion, or grant faith in Him and His messengers, to people who disbelieved after their belief, after having faith in Allah and witnessing that what the Prophet Muhammad brought was truth? He shall not guide them.

**"acknowledged the Messenger to be true, and received clear proofs?"**: and after they bore witness that the Messenger, Muhammad, is true, and after clear proofs of Allah's sovereignty had come to them, the exposition of the Truth and the Book. The proofs testified to the truth of what the Messenger was sent with.

**"For Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people"**: Allah guides not wrongdoing folk, the idolaters and those not fit for His religion, people who are wrongdoers and choose error instead of guidance.

### The Ansari who came back

Ibn Jarir records Ibn ʿAbbās on an Ansari who embraced Islam, reverted to the polytheists, regretted it, and asked his people to consult the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about repenting. Revelation descended from "How shall Allah guide a people who disbelieved after their belief" through "Verily, Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful". Word reached him through his people, and Islam was re-embraced.

This is the wording recorded by An-Nasa'i, Al-Hakim and Ibn Hibban. Al-Hakim said, "Its chain is Sahih and they did not record it."

The man is named in another report. Al-Harith ibn Suwayd was a Muslim who had been with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He later renounced Islam and joined his own people. A man from among his people took these verses and read them to him. Al-Harith said, "By Allah, I know that you are truthful, and I know that the Messenger of Allah, Allah bless him and give him peace, is more truthful than you, and Allah, exalted is He, is more truthful than all." He went back, declared his Islam and became a good Muslim. The same report came through Dawud ibn Abi Hind from ʿIkrimah from Ibn ʿAbbās: a certain man from the Helpers renounced Islam and joined idolatry, later regretted it and sent to ask whether his repentance would be accepted, and when the verses were sent he came back and became Muslim.

### A doubt removed: refusal, not impossibility

A superficial reading of "How shall Allah give guidance" raises the doubt that an apostate receives no guidance afterward, whereas many apostates do re-embrace faith and become recipients of guidance. The answer is that withholding here follows common usage. A punished criminal might say, "I have been given special treatment by the ruler with his own hands." The reply comes: "Why are we going to give special treatment to such a criminal?" That is, no special treatment is called for in this case. It does not mean the person cannot become deserving of special treatment if he behaves well. So the verses can both deny guidance to wrongdoers as they stand and open repentance to the one who returns.

Inwardly, the direction is stronger. Those rendered unsuitable in His prior decree for the communion of lovers cannot now expect nearness on service's carpet through favor. Remoteness decreed pre-eternally raises the same question about later sincerity in actions. God prevails over His affair.

### In short

- Guidance is denied to people who believed, witnessed the Messenger's truth, received clear proofs, and reverted willingly into blindness.
- An Ansari apostate, named as Al-Harith ibn Suwayd, heard these verses read to him, declared all three speakers truthful, and came back.
- "How shall Allah guide" refuses the claim of the obstinate, without making return impossible for the repentant.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:87>>>
The sentence is stated by its three witnesses: "Their reward is that they will be condemned by Allah, the angels, and all of humanity". Their recompense is a curse that comes from every direction at once. Allah curses them, and His creation also curses them, and they are excluded and removed from His mercy.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Their reward is that they will be condemned by Allah"**: their requital is that there shall rest on them the curse of God, the punishment of Allah. The word "reward" carries the irony of the crime: this is what their choice earns.

**"the angels, and all of humanity"**: and of angels, the curse of the angels, and of men combined, the curse of the believers. Their condemnation is not a private decree. It is pronounced by Allah, the angels and humankind altogether.

### Rejection at the beginning, banishment at the end

End and beginning correspond in His decree. Fate's rejection, radd al-qisma, opens their affair. Being turned away from service, al-ṣaddu ʿan al-khidma, fills the middle. Banishment and abasement, al-Ṭard wa-l-madhalla, close the outcome.

### In short

- The apostates' requital is the curse of Allah, the angels and all humanity, and removal from mercy.
- Read inwardly, their arc runs from fate's rejection through being turned away from service to banishment and abasement.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:88>>>
The curse is made permanent: "They will be in Hell forever. Their punishment will not be lightened, nor will they be delayed ˹from it˺". They abide in the curse, or in the Fire implied by it, without lessening, postponement or respite. No hour of relief interrupts it, and no extra time is given to repent and ask forgiveness once it begins.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They will be in Hell forever"**: they will abide therein, in the curse, or in the Fire implied by the curse. These disbelievers will live eternally in the fire of Hell, never to leave.

**"Their punishment will not be lightened"**: neither will their torment be lightened. The chastisement shall not be lightened for them, not even for an hour, and not even for a moment.

**"nor will they be delayed ˹from it˺"**: nor will it be delayed or postponed. They shall not be reprieved, and they shall not be given any respite, nor more time to repent.

### No pause in the separation

Inside that abasement, no moment brings abatement: lā yaftur ʿanhum al-ʿadhāb laḥẓatan. Nor does an hour lighten separation: lā yukhaffafu dūnihim al-firāq sāʿatan. The two units of time are minimal on purpose. There is no smallest gap in which the punishment breathes.

### In short

- They abide in the curse and its Fire forever, with no lightening, no postponement and no respite.
- Even a moment's abatement and an hour's easing of separation are denied.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:89>>>
The exception is opened in the same breath: "As for those who repent afterwards and mend their ways, then surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful". After apostasy, repentance and righteous deeds still meet forgiveness. This verse shows Allah's kindness, graciousness, compassion, mercy and favour on creatures who repent to Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who repent afterwards and mend their ways"**: except those who afterward, after their apostasy, repent from disbelief and hypocrisy and do right. They make amends in their actions and sincerely declare Allah's divine Oneness.

**"then surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"**: God is Forgiving to them, Forgiving of those who repent among them, and Merciful toward those who die in a state of repentance.

### The mercy that identifies its people

Repentance identifies the people mercy reaches. They were not among the group distanced by the prior decree, although people imagined them to be part of it.

### In short

- Repentance after apostasy, joined to amended deeds, is met by Allah's forgiveness and mercy.
- The returning Ansari belongs to this verse, not to the group permanently distanced.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:90>>>
The second apostasy passage is narrower and harder: "Indeed, those who disbelieve after having believed then increase in disbelief, their repentance will never be accepted. It is they who are astray." Allah threatens those who revert after believing and then insist on disbelief until death. In that terminal state, repentance is not accepted, because the time for acceptance has expired.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, those who disbelieve after having believed then increase in disbelief"**: those who disbelieve after their profession of belief and afterward grow violent in disbelief, who do not waver in their persistence. The Jews are specified in one explanation: those who disbelieve in Jesus after they have believed in Moses and then increase in unbelief in Muhammad ﷺ.

**"their repentance will never be accepted"**: their repentance shall not be accepted when they are drawing their last gasps of life, or when they have died as disbelievers, as long as they remain in that state. "And of no effect is the repentance of those who continue to do evil deeds until death faces one of them" (4:18).

**"It is they who are astray"**: they have lost their way from the straight path which leads to Allah, abandoning the path of truth for the path of wickedness.

### Repeated returns, and the reports that narrow the case

Al-Bazzar records Ibn ʿAbbās on people cycling through Islam, disbelief, Islam again, then disbelief. Their people inquired and asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. This verse was revealed on that.

Al-Hasan, Qatadah and Ata al-Khurasani apply revelation to Jews whose disbelief in Jesus and the Gospel grew by disbelief in Muhammad and the Qur'an. Abu'l-Aliyah widens it to Jews and Christians who rejected Muhammad, Allah bless him and give him peace, despite prior belief in his attributes and description, then added disbelief by remaining disbelievers.

### Apostasy from the path itself

This alludes to those who return to the states of the people of habit, ahl al-ʿāda, after having travelled the path of spiritual desire, Ṭarīq al-irāda. World preference and submission to whims replace seeking the Real. People of the path, ahl al-Ṭarīqa, are renounced, leaving greater solitude in inner darknesses. Trust, amāna, cannot be regained once treachery, khiyāna, has been displayed. Punishment arrives as day-by-day estrangement of the heart, without distress over lost felicity. Perseverance would have meant accepted repentance. Listless reversion to habitual states, aḥwāl al-ʿāda, without regret for vanished moments, nevertheless meets the Real's way, sunna. "And We shall confound their hearts and eyes; just as they did not believe in it the first time" (6:110).

An apostate from Islam makes a more powerful enemy to Muslims than an original disbeliever. Likewise, reverting from the path produces a stronger rejection and opposition against its people than lifelong alienation ever did.

### In short

- Repentance is refused only where disbelief is increased in and persisted in to the deathbed (4:18).
- Reports narrow the verse to repeated apostates, and to Jews and Christians who accepted the Prophet's description and then rejected him.
- Inwardly, the habit-bound revert after spiritual desire, whose hearts grow estranged without regretting what slipped away (6:110).
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:91>>>
No treasury can purchase an exit: "Indeed, if each of those who disbelieve then die as disbelievers were to offer a ransom of enough gold to fill the whole world, it would never be accepted from them. It is they who will suffer a painful punishment, and they will have no helpers". Dying in disbelief closes both doors at once: the ransom is refused, and there is no helper to take the punishment away.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, if each of those who disbelieve then die as disbelievers were to offer a ransom of enough gold to fill the whole world"**: those who disbelieved, and died while they were disbelievers, would not have the whole earth full of gold accepted from anyone of them if they offered it as ransom. If they gave the equivalent of the earth's weight in gold to ransom themselves, it would not be accepted. The earth's fill includes all of it: its mountains, hills, sand, dust, valleys, forests, land and sea. The fāʾ in fa-lan yuqbala, "it shall not be accepted", enters the predicate because the statement about "those who disbelieve" resembles a conditional.

**"it would never be accepted from them"**: no good deed shall ever be accepted from them, even if they spent the earth's fill of gold in what was perceived to be an act of obedience. "...nor shall compensation be accepted from him, nor shall intercession be of use to him" (2:123). A day comes "on which there will be neither mutual bargaining nor befriending" (14:31). "Verily, those who disbelieve, if they had all that is in the earth, and as much again therewith to ransom themselves thereby from the torment on the Day of Resurrection, it would never be accepted of them, and theirs would be a painful torment" (5:36).

**"It is they who will suffer a painful punishment, and they will have no helpers"**: for them is a painful torment, alīm, that is muʾlim, painful, which will extend to their hearts. They shall have no helpers to protect them, no one to save them from Allah's punishment or rescue them from His painful punishment on the Day of Judgement.

### Generosity without one sentence of faith

A question reached the Prophet ﷺ concerning ʿAbdullah bin Jud'an: would generosity to guests, help to the indebted and feeding the poor benefit him? The answer was: "No, for not even one day during his life did he pronounce, 'O my Lord! Forgive my sins on the Day of Judgment.'" 

The same exchange is pictured from the other side. Imam Ahmad recorded that Anas said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "A man from among the people of Paradise will be brought and Allah will ask him, 'O son of Adam! How did you find your dwelling?' He will say, 'O Lord, it is the best dwelling.' Allah will say, 'Ask and wish.' The man will say, 'I only ask and wish that You send me back to the world so that I am killed ten times in Your cause,' because of the honor of martyrdom he would experience. A man from among the people of the Fire will be brought, and Allah will say to him, 'O son of Adam! How do you find your dwelling?' He will say, 'It is the worst dwelling, O Lord.' Allah will ask him, 'Would you ransom yourself from Me with the earth's fill of gold?' He will say, 'Yes, O Lord.' Allah will say, 'You have lied. I asked you to do what is less and easier than that, but you did not do it,' and he will be sent back to the Fire."

Ten hypocrites occasion verses 90 and 91: Tu'mah and his companions, who left Medina to Mecca as apostates from their religion, Islam. Some died as apostates, some were killed, and some later returned to the fold of Islam.

### A beautiful beginning that does not gather its owner

To die after becoming listless is the inward case. A beautiful beginning does not guarantee, in the hereafter, a gathering alongside the people of this story, ahl hādhi al-qiṣṣa, even if a thousand of those with deeper knowledge were to intercede for him. Perfection of the ruse casts his image onto someone else in the hereafter, so acquaintances among the people of deeper knowledge, ahl al-maʿrifa, mistake the image for him. No thought of interceding for him occurs to anyone.

### In short

- Dying in disbelief means no ransom is accepted, even earth's fill in gold, and no intercession or helper avails (2:123, 14:31, 5:36).
- ʿAbdullah bin Jud'an's generosity gained him nothing without a single day of asking forgiveness.
- The ten hypocrites around Tu'mah who left for Mecca are the occasion: some died, some were killed, some returned.
