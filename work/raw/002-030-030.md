The verses before this one listed Allah's blessings and asked human beings to recognise their Benefactor rather than deny Him. Now the surah opens a story that runs for ten verses, the story of Ādam (Adam), the father of mankind. It continues the same theme and also illustrates it. Blessings are of two kinds, tangible and intangible. Food, water, wealth, houses and land are tangible. Honour, happiness and knowledge are intangible. The earlier verses dealt with the first kind, and these verses deal with the second: how Allah gave Ādam the gift of knowledge, made the angels bow to him in respect, and gave human beings the honour of being his children. The story begins before Ādam existed. "Remember when your Lord said to the angels, 'I am going to place a successive human authority on earth.'" The angels replied with a question: "Will You place in it someone who will spread corruption there and shed blood while we glorify Your praises and proclaim Your holiness?" And Allah answered, "I know what you do not know."

### Phrase by phrase

**"Remember when your Lord said to the angels"** is addressed first to the Prophet ﷺ: mention to your people what Allah said to the angels. In doing so Allah reminds the children of Ādam of a favour that came before any other, for He spoke of them in the highest assembly before He had created them. The angels addressed were, on one view, angels who were already on the earth.

**"I am going to place a successive human authority on earth"** translates the Arabic khalīfah. Literally it means a successor, someone who steps into another's place. Under hereditary rule the word was commonly applied to a monarch succeeding his predecessor, and so it came to denote a person invested with authority. The verse uses the word in several overlapping senses. It means people who reproduce generation after generation and century after century, each replacing the one before, as Allah says, "He is the One Who has placed you as successors on earth and elevated some of you in rank over others, so He may test you with what He has given you" (6:165), and, "Who makes you successors in the earth? Is it another god besides Allah?" (27:62), and, "Had We willed, We could have easily replaced you all with angels, succeeding one another on earth" (43:60), and again, "Then they were succeeded by other generations who inherited the Scripture" (7:169). It also means a deputy, one who acts on behalf of Allah by implementing His rulings in the earth, and in this sense the vicegerent announced here was Ādam himself. Al-Qurṭubī noted a further shade of meaning: a khalīfah is one who judges the disputes that arise among people, restraining them from injustice and sin. Some scholars think that Allah was not referring to Ādam alone, since the angels' reply about corruption and bloodshed would not fit a single prophet, but to the human race of which he was the father. Others add that the word recalls those who lived on the earth before. The offspring of the jinn used to inhabit it, and when they became corrupt Allah sent the angels against them and they were driven away to the islands and into the mountains. Ādam's race would take their place.

**"They asked, 'Will You place in it someone who will spread corruption there and shed blood'"** raises the obvious question: how did the angels know what human beings would do? Several explanations are given, and they do not exclude one another. Most authoritative scholars hold, on the basis of reports from the Companions, that Allah Himself told the angels on this occasion how human beings would behave on earth. Qatādah said that when Allah informed them that He would create a creation on the earth, they already knew that it would commit corruption there. Others say they inferred it from human nature, since Allah had told them that He would create man from clay and such a creature is prone to the acts they mentioned. Others say they understood it from the word khalīfah itself, since a judge and restrainer is needed only where there is wrongdoing to restrain. Others point to the precedent of the jinn, who had corrupted the earth and shed blood before. Behind the question lay an assumption the angels had formed. Ibn Jarīr cited a report from Ibn ʿAbbās that before man appeared the angels had taken it for granted that Allah would never create a being superior to them or greater in knowledge, since they had been created before him and had seen what he had not seen.

It is important to be clear about what the angels were not doing. They were not disputing with Allah, and they were not envious of the children of Ādam, as some have mistakenly supposed. Allah describes the angels as those who never speak before He speaks and never act except by His command, so they do not ask Him anything without His permission. Their words were a question asked in order to learn the wisdom behind the decision, as if they had said: Our Lord, what is the wisdom in creating such beings when they will cause trouble on the earth and spill blood? If the purpose is that You be worshipped, we already praise and glorify You and never cause corruption, so why create other creatures? Angels are innocent of objection and of pride. Their words were a humble submission and an offer of their own services. They were being curious, not critical.

**"while we glorify Your praises and proclaim Your holiness"** describes what the angels were already doing. To glorify with praise is to say continually, "Glory and praise be to You." To proclaim His holiness is to declare Him far above anything that does not befit Him, to pray as He commands, and to mention Him in a state of purity. On the grammar, the lām in laka ("for You") is an extra particle, and the clause "we sanctify You" is a circumstantial qualifier. The implied sense is: since we already do all this, we are surely more entitled to be Your vicegerents. The angels thought that their nature, wholly good, incapable of evil and perfectly obedient, made them better suited to manage the affairs of the world.

**"Allah responded, 'I know what you do not know'"** is the first answer, given in the mode of authority. The angels did not understand the nature and the requirements of vicegerency on the earth, and Allah alone knew them fully. Scholars have unfolded the content of this answer in several ways, and all of them are true.

First, Allah knew that the benefit of creating this kind of being far outweighs the harm the angels mentioned, and this the angels did not know. Among the children of Ādam He would raise prophets and send messengers, and He would create the truthful, the martyrs, the righteous, the worshippers, the modest, the pious, scholars who practise what they know, the humble, and lovers of Allah who follow His messengers. Among Ādam's progeny there would be the obedient as well as the transgressor, and justice would prevail between them. The Ṣaḥīḥ records that when the angels ascend to Allah with the records of His servants' deeds, Allah asks them, though He knows better than they, "How did you leave My servants?" and they answer, "We came to them while they were praying and we left them while they were praying." This is because the angels who watch over people work in shifts, changing over at the Fajr and ʿAṣr prayers, so that the angels who have just descended stay with us while those who have been with us ascend with our deeds. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that the deeds of the night are raised to Allah before the morning and the deeds of the day before the night falls. The angels' report, "We came to them praying and left them praying," is a living commentary on "I know what you do not know."

Second, some understood the answer as: I have a specific wisdom in creating them that you have no knowledge of. Third, some read it as a reply to the angels' words "we glorify Your praises and proclaim Your holiness," meaning: I know that Iblīs is not as you are, even though he stands among you. Fourth, some read the angels' words as a request to be allowed to inhabit the earth themselves instead of the children of Ādam, and Allah's answer as: I know whether your living in the heavens is better or worse for you. Ar-Rāzī and others mentioned this reading. And Allah knows best.

### Why did Allah tell the angels at all?

A question naturally arises. Why did Allah announce His decision to the angels? Was it simply to inform them, to seek their advice, or to draw out their opinion?

It cannot have been to seek advice. One turns to wise and trustworthy people for advice when one cannot see every side of a problem and does not wish to rely on one's own understanding alone, or when others have rights equal to one's own and must be consulted, as happens in the councils of the world. Neither situation applies here. Allah created the universe and knows the smallest particle of dust in it. He sees and hears everything, open or hidden. How could He need anyone's advice? Nor does He govern the universe on a parliamentary model in which all have equal rights and everyone must be consulted. He is the Lord and Master, and all His creatures, angels and human beings alike, are His slaves. None of them has the right to question His actions or to ask Him why He did this and not that: "He cannot be questioned about what He does, but they will all be questioned" (21:23).

Two reasons for the form of the announcement are given. The first is that Allah, in His wisdom, gave a plain statement the form of a consultation in order to teach human beings the value of consulting one another. The Prophet ﷺ was Allah's Messenger and could have received through revelation everything he needed to manage the affairs of the world, yet the Qur'an instructs him to consult his Companions, so that the Muslim community would learn the lesson from him and the practice of mutual consultation would be established through him. The second reason is suggested by the verses themselves. The angels had assumed, as the report of Ibn ʿAbbās shows, that Allah would never create a being superior to them in knowledge. Allah knew that He would create a being superior to all other creatures and greater in knowledge, one who would receive the gift of vicegerency. So He raised the matter in the assembly of the angels so that they would voice what they had been thinking, and then He could show them, in a vivid demonstration, where they had gone wrong.

In the reading of the Sufis the point is sharper still. The announcement was not a consultation with the angels at all. It was the laying of the foundation of Ādam's honour. Allah needed no help. He was unrolling the carpet of Ādam's dignity before Ādam existed.

### What the angels would learn

The verses that follow describe how Allah answered the angels a second time, in the mode of wisdom, by teaching Ādam the names of all things and asking the angels to name them. That demonstration belongs to the next verses, but its lesson belongs to the understanding of this one. Ādam was preferred over the angels because of his superiority in the station of knowledge. A deputy on the earth must know the names, properties and characteristics of the things found there, and the angels had no aptitude for that kind of knowledge. An angel cannot truly experience the pain of hunger and thirst, the turmoil of the passions, the agony of a scorpion's sting or a snake's bite, or the elation an intoxicant produces. Such things could be learned only by a being with Ādam's capacity, and he was taught them. Nothing in the Qur'an indicates that he was taught in private, apart from the angels. The teaching may have been open to both, with his nature able to receive it and theirs not. Or the teaching may have taken no outward form at all, the Ādamic nature being made to carry this knowledge within itself without formal instruction, just as an infant does not need to be taught to suck its mother's milk or a duckling to swim. Should someone ask why Allah, being all-powerful, did not simply change the angels' nature so that they could learn these things, the answer is that the question amounts to asking why He did not turn the angels into human beings, for with a different nature they would no longer have been angels.

Through this demonstration the angels came to see that purity and innocence are not the criterion for choosing a vicegerent. The criterion is knowledge of the things found on the earth, of how to use them, and of the consequences that follow from using them. A general principle can be drawn from this: a ruler must thoroughly know the nature, temperament and peculiarities of the people he governs, or he cannot establish justice and order. If a man has never known the pain of hunger, how can he do justice to someone who has been wrongfully kept hungry? When the angels found that another being was, by his special kind of knowledge, better suited to the task, they acknowledged it as humbly as they had spoken, and withdrew their opinion, declaring that all purity belongs to Allah and that they had no knowledge except what He had taught them. In this context "To You belongs all purity" carries a further implication: Allah is free of any charge of having withheld from the angels the knowledge He gave to Ādam, because, being All-Knowing and All-Wise, He gives each creature the kind and degree of knowledge and understanding that He alone knows to suit that creature's nature.

### Why a creature capable of evil?

Besides demonstrating Ādam's superiority in knowledge, Allah dispelled the angels' concern about the evil tendencies in human beings with the short answer, "I know what you do not know." There is a subtle suggestion in it. The very peculiarity that made man look unfit for vicegerency in the angels' eyes is exactly what makes him fit for it. A deputy is needed on the earth precisely to prevent bloodshed and disorder. Where there is no possibility of disorder, what need is there for an administrator? Allah's will and wisdom decreed that, just as He had created beings as innocent as the angels, beings as wholly evil as Satan and his progeny, and beings like the jinn in whom evil outweighs good, He would also create beings in whom good and evil are mixed in equal measure, who would strive to conquer the evil in themselves and grow in goodness in order to win the pleasure of their Creator.

The same thought can be put in terms of freedom. When God created man to live on the earth, He decreed that man should have freedom of will. The angels feared that man would be corrupted by this grant of power and freedom and would spread evil and bloodshed in the world, and history has shown that their fear was well founded. God was fully aware of the possibility. But He had a particular purpose in giving man power and freedom. Even if many human beings were corrupted by their power, a substantial number would still, with all their power and freedom, acknowledge God's greatness and their own helplessness, and who would freely choose the path of submission and obedience. Such people would be few by comparison, but they would be prized above all others. They are like the grain at harvest time, far outweighed in bulk by the chaff and straw, yet the only part of the crop that is really valued. If the chaff and straw are allowed to grow at all, it is only for the sake of the grain.

### Man as Allah's vicegerent: the principles of governance

Since these verses tell of a vicegerent appointed to keep order on the earth and to establish divine law, they yield the basic principles of human governance.

The ultimate sovereignty in the universe belongs to Allah alone. The Qur'an states this explicitly in many places: "It is only Allah Who decides" (6:57), "Indeed, to Allah alone belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth" (9:116), and, "The creation and the command belong to Him alone" (7:54).

In His wisdom, however, He chose to send His vicegerents to the earth to maintain spiritual and temporal order. Their function is to announce and establish His commandments, to teach people how to live by them, and sometimes to wield temporal power together with spiritual authority, under divine guidance. Allah makes the appointment Himself. It is in no sense a reward for a person's good deeds or spiritual striving. All authentic scholars of the Muslim Ummah agree that prophethood cannot be attained through personal effort or earned by merit. Allah, in His supreme knowledge and wisdom, chooses certain individuals as His messengers, prophets and vicegerents. The Qur'an declares this plainly: "Allah selects messengers from both angels and people" (22:75), and, "Allah knows best where to place His message" (6:124). These vicegerents receive His commandments directly and establish them in the world. The chain began with Ādam and continued unbroken to the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ.

### The Prophet ﷺ as the last vicegerent

The Prophet ﷺ came as the last vicegerent (khalīfah), the last Messenger (rasūl) and the last Prophet (nabī) of Allah, with special qualities that no other prophet shares. Some of them are these.

First, each earlier prophet was sent to guide a particular country or people, and his authority was limited to them. Mūsā (Moses) and ʿĪsā (Jesus) were sent to Banī Isrāʾīl, the Israelites. The Prophet ﷺ was sent to guide all human beings and all jinn, and his authority extends to every member of both species. The Qur'an declares the universality of his mission: "Say, O Prophet, 'O humanity! I am Allah's Messenger to you all'" (7:158). A hadith in the Ṣaḥīḥ of Muslim reports him saying that he was given superiority over the other prophets in six things, and the first of them is the universality of his prophethood.

Second, just as the earlier prophets' vicegerency was limited to particular peoples and lands, it was also limited to particular periods. When one prophet's age ended, another came as the new vicegerent. The Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ was sent as the last of all prophets, and his prophethood is not confined to a period but lasts until the end of time.

Third, the teachings and the Sharīʿah of each earlier prophet remained intact for a while, after which people gradually drifted from them and distorted them beyond recognition, and Allah would then send a new prophet with a new law. The Sharīʿah of the Prophet ﷺ is to remain alive in its complete form until the end of the universe. Allah has taken upon Himself to protect the words and the meanings of the Qur'an: "It is certainly We Who have revealed the Reminder, and it is certainly We Who will preserve it" (15:9). He has likewise made special provision for preserving the hadith, which contain the Prophet's ﷺ teachings. Through every upheaval of history there will remain, until the Day of Judgment, a group of people who preserve these teachings and transmit them accurately, aided and protected by Allah. Since the survival of the Qur'an and the hadith has been decreed, there is no need for a new prophet, messenger or vicegerent, and no room for a new law.

Fourth, because the vicegerency of the last Prophet ﷺ continues to the end of time, those who succeed him in preserving spiritual and temporal order are not vicegerents of Allah but vicegerents and deputies of the Prophet ﷺ. A hadith reported by both al-Bukhārī and Muslim says that the Israelites were governed by their prophets, and whenever a prophet died another took his place, but that no prophet will come after him, and instead there will be his deputies (khulafāʾ), and they will be many.

Fifth, Allah has ordained that after the Prophet ﷺ his Ummah as a body should enjoy a privilege that had belonged to the prophets. The community as a whole has been declared protected from error and placed under Allah's special care, so that it will never unanimously agree on a doctrinal error or deviation. A decision reached in religious matters by the consensus of the Ummah is therefore a manifestation of divine command, and consensus is accepted as the third foundation of the Sharīʿah after the Qur'an and the hadith. The Prophet ﷺ himself said that his Ummah will never collectively agree upon error. Another hadith, already mentioned, promises that however much the world changes and however indifferent people become to the truth, a group within the Ummah will always defend and preserve it and will finally prevail.

### The caliphate after the Prophet ﷺ

Since the Ummah as a body cannot go astray, the responsibility of choosing a deputy to the Prophet ﷺ has been entrusted to it. The legitimate way of governing the earth is for the Ummah to select a khalīfah who, once chosen, bears sole responsibility for maintaining spiritual and temporal order. One khalīfah for the entire world is also possible.

The first to succeed the Prophet ﷺ as his deputies were the four great caliphs known as al-Khulafāʾ ar-Rāshidīn, the Rightly-Guided Caliphs, and the order of the caliphate functioned according to its proper principles up to the end of their time. Their decisions are therefore not merely temporary judgments but carry permanent legislative value and an authority of their own degree, for the Prophet ﷺ said to hold fast to his way and to the way of the rightly-guided caliphs.

After their age, different rulers appeared in different regions, and none of them can properly be called the khalīfah of the whole Muslim community, though each may be called the amīr of his region. Once agreement by all the world's Muslims on a single man became practically impossible, and separate rulers for separate regions became customary, people accepted the principle that whoever was chosen or acknowledged by the majority of the Muslims of a country should be its amīr. The basis for this is given by the Qur'an itself in its description of the believers as those "who respond to their Lord, establish prayer, conduct their affairs by mutual consultation, and donate from what We have provided for them" (42:38). Modern legislative assemblies are a form of mutual consultation too, but with a crucial difference. They are free to make whatever laws they please according to their own opinion, whereas an Islamic assembly, its members and their amīr are all bound by the law Allah sent through the Prophet ﷺ. There are specific conditions for membership in such an assembly and for the choice of an amīr, and above all its laws must stay within the basic principles laid down by the Qur'an and the Sunnah, whose authority the assembly has no right to question.

The whole discussion can be summarised in five principles. Sovereignty over the heavens and the earth belongs to Allah alone. The task of establishing His commandments on the earth is carried out by a vicegerent who is at the same time His messenger and prophet. The chain of such vicegerents ended with the Prophet ﷺ, the last Messenger and Prophet. The function of vicegerency is now carried out by the deputies of the Prophet ﷺ. And such a deputy, the khalīfah, is to be chosen by the Muslim community.

### Rulings on appointing a leader

Al-Qurṭubī and other scholars said that this verse proves the obligation of appointing a khalīfah to judge disputes among people, to support the oppressed against the oppressor, to apply the penal law and to forbid evil. Many other duties can be fulfilled only by appointing an imām, and whatever is necessary for performing an obligation is itself obligatory.

The leadership can come about in several ways. A group among the scholars of Ahl as-Sunnah held that the Prophet ﷺ named Abū Bakr as his successor, or at least indicated him. A sitting khalīfah may name his successor, as Abū Bakr did with ʿUmar. He may leave the choice to a consultative council of righteous men, as ʿUmar did. Or the people of authority may gather around one man and give him their pledge of allegiance, or appoint one of their number to select the candidate, which is the view of the majority.

The khalīfah must be a responsible adult Muslim male, capable of ijtihād, independent legal reasoning, physically able, upright, and knowledgeable in warfare and politics. According to the correct view he must be from Quraysh, but he need not be from Banū Hāshim, nor need he be protected from error, as the Rāfiḍah falsely claim.

Should a khalīfah who becomes immoral, a fāsiq, be removed? Scholars disagree, but the correct view is that he is not to be deposed, because of the Prophet's ﷺ words that obedience is owed unless you witness clear disbelief for which you have clear proof from Allah. May a khalīfah resign? Here too there is a difference. It is a fact that al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī gave up the position and surrendered it to Muʿāwiyah, but he did so out of necessity, and he was praised for it.

It is not permissible to appoint two or more imāms over the world at the same time, because the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that whoever comes to you while you are united and tries to divide you should be put to death, whoever he may be. This is the position of the majority. Imām al-Ḥaramayn related that Abū Isḥāq allowed two or more imāms when the provinces are far apart, but Imām al-Ḥaramayn himself was undecided about that view.

### Two modern misreadings

Two distortions of this verse have gained currency in modern times and need to be answered.

The first is the habit of some modernists of reading these verses as if man's vicegerency meant a duty to advance in science, meaning the empirical study of the physical world. One self-described Muslim translator of the Qur'an even went so far as to translate the name Ādam by the English word "Man," thereby denying the existence and the prophethood of Ādam. Against such errors it must be said that the names Allah taught Ādam do not refer merely to the chemical, biological or psychological properties of things and people, but to their essential qualities and aptitudes, in the technical, metaphysical sense of "essential" that the word originally had in the West as well. Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānavī adds in his Bayān al-Qurʾān that knowledge of the names even includes knowledge of the rulings of the Sharīʿah that distinguish the lawful from the unlawful. Many great Sufis hold that Ādam was given knowledge of the names of Allah, not all the divine names in detail, since a created being cannot comprehend the Infinite, but the divine names in summary form, and this interpretation was advanced by so reliable a scholar as Qāḍī Thanāʾullāh of Pānīpat in his Tafsīr al-Maẓharī. The subtle point behind it is that everything that exists reflects some divine attribute, which in turn manifests a divine name. The divine names are thus the essential principles and roots of all things, and whoever knows them knows things in their inner natures.

The second distortion is more serious. Under the influence of Western humanism, and in their general indifference to doctrine, modernists have come to identify the prophet Ādam, the father of mankind, entirely with the biological species "man," and to claim that every individual of the species, unconditionally and without qualification, is born a vicegerent of Allah. The error has been fed by a careless misreading of Sufi metaphysical texts and poetry. What these writers have never troubled to learn is that there are degrees, and that the degrees differ. The Sufis do often speak of "man" as Allah's vicegerent, but what they mean is no biological organism or species. They mean al-Insān al-Kāmil, the Universal Man, a term the orientalists wrongly rendered "the perfect man," importing ethical connotations into a sphere of pure metaphysics. In Sufi prose and poetry "Man" also stands for the total and essential reality of man (al-Ḥaqīqah al-Jāmiʿah al-Insāniyyah). The Universal Man par excellence is the Prophet ﷺ. This is the first degree of "manhood," and to it belong the awliyāʾ, the great friends of Allah, and those rulers who dispensed justice according to the Sharīʿah. Lower degrees follow: the pious and virtuous Muslims, and at the bottom those who are sinful but, as Muslims, may still hope for salvation. Allah alone knows who belongs to which degree, and below the degree of the Companions one can never speak with certainty. If vicegerency is attributed to an ordinary Muslim at all, it is vicegerency by reflection, just as the īmān of every Muslim is a reflection of the īmān of the Prophet ﷺ. The necessary condition for receiving even a faint reflection of vicegerency and "manhood" is that one be a Muslim, for the Qur'an declares that Allah will now accept no faith except Islam. As for attributing vicegerency to the ordinary human being as such, it can at best be vicegerency in potency, to use Aristotle's terms, not in act, and it cannot become effective without total submission to the Sharīʿah, strenuous spiritual effort and patient waiting upon the grace of Allah. In truth the highest excellence open to a human being now is to be, in word, deed and thought, a perfect follower of the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.

### A reading for the inner life

The Sufis retell this verse as a drama of love. Picture a world at rest, with no heart yet burning in passion and no breast yet deluded by wild fervour. Then the ocean of mercy started to boil. The treasuries of obedient deeds stood full, and the foreheads of the obedient carried no dust of weariness. The banner of their proud claim, "We glorify Your praise," was raised as high as the star Capella. Every subtle being in the cosmos began to crave the honour for itself. The majestic Throne looked at its own vastness and said, "Perhaps these words are written for me." The Footstool looked at its own breadth: "Perhaps this sermon is read in my name." The eight paradises gazed at their own beauty: "Maybe this rulership will be given to us." None of them wanted anything to do with dust. Each fell into its own delusion.

Suddenly, from the Presence of Exaltation and Majesty, the report reached the world of the angels: "I am going to place a vicegerent on earth." This was no consultation with the angels. Allah was laying the foundation of Ādam's exaltation and spreading the carpet of his dignity. It was as if He said: My severity has done its work. I have ordered the pen of generosity to write one inscription across the world's ledger from first page to last, binding on everyone in both worlds, from the Throne's summit down to the Carpet, and it reads: Ādam of the dust is made chief over all the kingdoms, his breast will shine with the light of recognition, and in him the subtleties of My generosity and the artefacts of My bounty will be put on display.

This declaration made the hearts of the angels nearest to Allah quake with awe. They said, "What is this about? He has not even been created yet." Before he had entered the bonds of creation, the drum of his vicegerency was already sounding at the threshold of his beauty. From the hidden affairs of the Unseen the majesty of predetermination announced: keep away from the field of Ādam's good fortune, because the secret of his innate disposition is beyond you. On the branch of that good fortune no falcon of any mind has perched, and the beauty of his clear sun no eye of insight has taken in.

Where did this eminence come from? It came from Ādam being the oyster shell that held the mysteries of lordship, the treasury that held the jewels of the kingdom. Countless precious pearls and royal gems were laid in that shell. Yet next to every pearl a black bead was strung, and every prophet's pearl was given its dark counterpart. Ādam the chosen had Satan the wretched. Ibrāhīm (Abraham) the intimate friend had Nimrod the rebel. Mūsā son of ʿImrān had Pharaoh the forsaken. ʿĪsā son of Maryam had a tribe steeped in misguidance and transgression. And the Arab Muṣṭafā ﷺ had Abū Jahl, the man full of ignorance.

This terrifying declaration drove calm and repose out of the angels, and their intellects and their patience lost all composure. They cried out together: "Will You place in it someone who will spread corruption there and shed blood?" O Lord, they said, O King, O Magnanimous, O Creator, this Ādam of the dust will stain the embroidered robe of nearness with disobedience. He will pull his head out of the collar of obedience. You created us from holiness and the declaring of holiness, You adorned our breasts with reciting the formula of tawḥīd and with glorification, You made all of this our means!" It is said that a fire came forth from the hidden affairs of the Unseen and consumed a tribe of the angels.

Then came the declaration in the attribute of exaltedness: "I know what you do not know." You who are gazers, keep on gazing. The secret treasuries of divinity are no business of yours, and the hidden affairs of Our unseen lordship are not yours to meddle in. We alone know what Our divinity has prepared and what mysteries Our lordship conceals. Puny minds, the sciences and intellects of anyone but Us, defective understandings, insights that arrived only yesterday: how could any of these find a path to the mysteries of Our divinity? "With Him are the keys of the unseen, no one knows them except Him" (6:59). Before all beginnings We decreed that the lamp of the realities of recognition would be lit in the breast of Ādam of the dust, that the edict of rulership would pass into his hand, and that the flag of the earthly kingdoms would be planted in his soldierly heart.

And the angels were given their orders. You who are the nearest angels of the kingdom, be serving-boys, lay the carpet before the throne of his good fortune, and fall down in prostration before him. You who circle Our Throne, ask forgiveness for the sins of Ādam's progeny that have not yet been committed by people who do not yet exist, and ask safety for their going forth, saying, "Peace be upon them, peace be upon them," so that once they exist their feet will not slip on servanthood's carpet. You who keep the veils, weep for the heedless among Ādam's progeny, so that because of your weeping We may cover their disobedience with Our forgiveness. You who are the folk of the cushions, fill water-bags of light from this pure water whose waves lap around Our Throne, and when they lift their thirsty heads from the earth on the Day of Resurrection, give them to drink. You who are the sinless ones of the Lote Tree of the Furthest Boundary, wait until the Greatest Terror appears at the Resurrection, when awe and the seizing and grasping of punishment come forth, and then grant the faithful among them safety from that terror and carry to them Our greeting of peace. Every one of these commands is given so that you angels will learn the eminence of these dust-dwellers and raise no protest against Our decree.

There is a sound report that the Supreme Assembly, the angels nearest the Exalted Threshold, said, "O Lord, You have given the dust-dwellers the low world. Give us the high world, for we are the birds of the Presence and the peacocks of the Exalted Threshold." The answer came: the wholesome progeny of the one whom My own two hands created will never be made like those who came to be when I merely said "Be!" I am the intimate of passion, and you are merely passing by, unaware of the tale and the state of the passionate. Why do you grieve over the ugliness of My beloved? Come into My eyes and look at him.

### In short

- Allah announced to the angels, before creating Ādam, that He would place a khalīfah on the earth: a successor to those before, a deputy who applies His rulings, and the father of generations that replace one another (6:165, 27:62, 43:60).
- The angels asked, not in objection but to learn the wisdom, why a creature capable of corruption and bloodshed should be preferred to beings who glorify and sanctify Allah without pause. Allah answered that He knows what they do not: among Ādam's children would be prophets, martyrs, the righteous and lovers of Allah, and the capacity for evil is exactly why a restraining deputy is needed.
- The announcement in the form of a consultation teaches the value of mutual consultation and exposed the angels' assumption that nothing more knowledgeable than they would ever be created.
- From the verse scholars derive the principles of governance: sovereignty is Allah's alone, vicegerents are chosen by Him, the chain ended with the Prophet ﷺ, and after him the Ummah chooses his deputies, with the obligation, methods and conditions of the caliphate spelled out by the jurists.
- Vicegerency belongs to the Prophet ﷺ, the awliyāʾ and just rulers in the fullest sense, and to the ordinary Muslim only by reflection. It is not a licence for every human being as such, nor a charter for scientific progress.
