<<<VERSE 2:164>>>
The idolaters had asked for proof of the claim in verse 163 that God is one, and this verse answers with a series of signs that the learned and the unlettered can grasp alike. "Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth; the alternation of the day and the night; the ships that sail the sea for the benefit of humanity;" then "the rain sent down by Allah from the skies, reviving the earth after its death; the scattering of all kinds of creatures throughout;" and finally "the shifting of the winds; and the clouds drifting between the heavens and the earth—˹in all of this˺ are surely signs for people of understanding". The message is simple and clear: the origination and continuance of all these things is the work of His perfect power alone, and no being other than He can take the credit.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth;"**. The sky with its height, its intricate design, its vastness and the heavenly bodies in their orbits, and the earth with its density, its lowlands, mountains, seas, deserts and valleys and all the useful things it holds. The sign lies in their making, or in the making of the marvels they contain. Who can overlook the marvel of earth and heavens being created?

**"the alternation of the day and the night;"**. One comes and goes and the other follows without delaying an instant, as Allah says: "It is not for the sun to catch up with the moon, nor does the night outrun the day. Each is travelling in an orbit of their own" (36:40). Sometimes the day shortens and the night lengthens, sometimes the reverse, each taking from the length of the other: "He merges the night into day and the day into night" (57:6). The alternation is understood by some to include the succession of poverty and wealth, death and life, sorrow and happiness.

**"the ships that sail the sea for the benefit of humanity;"**. Allah shaped the sea so that it can carry ships from one shore to another, and the ships run on it without cracking and sinking, so that people enjoy what other regions have and export their own goods in return, food, clothing and merchandise of every kind. It is a formidable sign of His power that water, so liquid and fluid, lifts on its back ships carrying tons of weight from East to West. Had water not been given the state in which its particles move freely while remaining one mass, no maritime activity would exist, and even with that fluidity the help of the winds was needed to cross thousands of miles of sea: "If He wills, He can calm the wind, leaving the ships motionless on the water. Surely in this are signs for whoever is steadfast, grateful" (42:33). "For the benefit of humanity" points to the countless benefits people gain from trading their goods by sea, benefits that vary from country to country and from age to age.

**"the rain sent down by Allah from the skies, reviving the earth after its death;"**. He revives the earth with vegetation after drought has dried it out: "There is a sign for them in the dead earth: We give it life, producing grain from it for them to eat" (36:33), down to "Glory be to the One Who created all ˹things in˺ pairs—˹be it˺ what the earth produces, their genders, or what they do not know" (36:36). The water descends drop by drop, in a measure and manner that harms nothing, for if it always came as a flood nothing would survive. Nor is the raining the whole marvel. The storage of water on earth baffles human ingenuity. If everyone were asked to store a six-month quota of water for personal use, how would anyone manage it, and how could it be kept drinkable and clean all that time? Allah in His mercy took care of this too: "We send down rain from the sky in perfect measure, causing it to soak into the earth. And We are surely able to take it away" (23:18). Water is stored openly in ponds and lakes for people and animals, lowered under mountains and valleys into a hidden network accessible to anyone who digs, and kept as a vast sea of ice on the mountain ranges, safe from spoiling and melting slowly to reach the world through nature's own water lines.

**"the scattering of all kinds of creatures throughout;"**. He creates and spreads living things of every shape, colour, use and size, male and female, dividing them across the earth where the vegetation gives them fertile pasture. Allah knows them all, sustains them, and nothing is hidden from Him: "There is no moving creature on earth whose provision is not guaranteed by Allah. And He knows where it lives and where it is laid to rest. All is ˹written˺ in a perfect Record" (11:6), that is, in the Preserved Tablet (al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ).

**"the shifting of the winds;"**. Allah harnesses the winds to serve humanity and in His wisdom keeps changing their direction, to right and to left, from south to north and from cold to warm. Sometimes the wind brings mercy and sometimes torment. Sometimes it announces the clouds that follow it, sometimes it leads them, herding, scattering or directing them. It blows from the north, from the south, from the east striking the front of the Kaʿbah, and from the west striking its back. Many books have been written about winds, rain, stars and the rules concerning them, but this is not the place to elaborate.

**"and the clouds drifting between the heavens and the earth"**. The clouds are compelled and subjected by Allah's command, running between sky and earth to whatever lands He wills without being attached to either.

**"˹in all of this˺ are surely signs for people of understanding"**. All these things are clear signs testifying to Allah's Oneness for those who comprehend and contemplate, who understand evidence and proof and who believe that these things come from Him. Allah says likewise: "Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of the day and night there are signs for people of reason" (3:190), "˹They are˺ those who remember Allah while standing, sitting, and lying on their sides, and reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth" (3:191).

### The universe as an introduction to its Maker

The vast universe spread out before us is a magnificent introduction to Allah. A limitless world of earth and heavens proves that a Creator stands behind it. Its apparently different and contrasting elements work together in complete harmony, which shows that their Creator and Sustainer is one and only one God. Every single thing benefits the rest of the universe in some way, which shows that all of it is designed on a definite and fully conscious plan. Seemingly lifeless things are given life through natural processes, showing that death here is only a temporary phase always followed by new life. Every kind of living creature flourishes in huge numbers, all sustained by varied forms of the same food and drink, a token of Allah's unfathomable power. The atmosphere wrapping the whole earth shows that human life lies entirely in the Creator's hands, and the fact that everything in the universe has been tamed to serve human needs, with every requirement provided before a person is even born, shows that the Creator is deeply compassionate. These signs are reflections of the Creator in His creation. The universe displays His existence and His oneness on so vast a scale, combining every aspect of perfection, that no one with any vision or a grain of intelligence can fail to discover Him in it. Yet the signs serve as proofs only for the one who reflects on them sincerely. To reach the truth one must search for it sincerely, rise above worldly considerations before drawing conclusions, and be absorbed not in externals but in the inner reality beneath the surface of things. Allah's Oneness is thus proved by citing some manifestations of His perfect power, a subject that al-Jaṣṣāṣ, together with al-Qurṭubī and others treat in great detail.

### Three gazes

In the devotional reading, the Lord of the universe here shows people in general the road to Himself, so that they may gaze on the wonders of the dominion of heaven and earth and the artisanry of land and sea, recognise the Artisan and attest to His oneness. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ said that He made Himself known to the common people through His creation, through His attributes to the elect, and through His Essence to prophets and the highest elect. The gaze of ordinary believers rests on the artisanry, and recognising the artisanry leads them to its Artisan. Elect believers recognise the attributes, and from the attributes reach the One described, from the name the Named. So when the Children of Israel were told, "Allah commands you to sacrifice a cow" (2:67), they did not recognise it until the cow had been described to them, and then they sacrificed it. The prophets and the sincerely truthful recognise Him through Him and not through anything else. Their gaze begins and ends with Him rather than passing from another thing to Him. Allah alludes to this state in the words, "Have you not seen how your Lord extends the shade" (25:45). The instruction was not to look at the shadow in order to see His artisanry. It was to look at Him, and so see His artisanry. The paragon of the world is told not to fix on Gabriel's coming but on the One who sends him, looking from Allah to the angel rather than from the angel to Allah. Consider the women who were the companions of Joseph. When the very being of Joseph was unveiled to them, they lost awareness of themselves and even of Joseph’s attributes.

### In short

- As proof that God is one, the verse points to seven signs: the heavens and earth, the alternation of night and day (36:40, 57:6), ships on the sea (42:33), rain reviving the dead earth and stored in the ground (36:33, 23:18), the spread of living creatures (11:6), the shifting winds and the suspended clouds.
- Each sign shows a single power, knowledge and wisdom at work, for only one Creator could make contrasting elements serve one harmonious plan centred on human need.
- The signs convince only those who reflect sincerely, rising above worldly motives (3:190-191).
- Inwardly, the common people know Allah through His works, the elect through His attributes, and the prophets through Himself (Ibn ʿAṭāʾ, cf. 2:67, 25:45).
