<<<VERSE 2:135>>>
The earlier verses defined the religion of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and established that its present form is Islam. Now the surah records what the two communities who claimed him actually said. "The Jews and Christians each say, “Follow our faith to be ˹rightly˺ guided.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “No! We follow the faith of Abraham, the upright—who was not a polytheist.”" For all their pretension to be his followers, the Jews and the Christians did not in fact follow his religion. Instead of accepting Islam, each group kept asking the Muslims to accept its own religion as the way to true guidance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Jews and Christians each say, “Follow our faith to be ˹rightly˺ guided.”"**. The Arabic literally says, "Be Jews or Christians, then you will be guided." The "or" spells out the two claims. The first is what the Jews of Madinah said, the second what the Christians of Najrān said. Ibn ʿAbbās reports that the verse came down about four Jewish leaders in Madinah, Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf, Mālik ibn al-Ṣayf, Wahb ibn Yahūdhā and Yāsir ibn Akhṭab, as well as about the Christians of Najrān. When they argued with the Muslims about religion, each party claimed a better right to Allah's religion than anyone else. The Jews said: our Prophet Mūsā (Moses) is the best of prophets, our Scripture the best of Scriptures, our religion the best of religions, and they rejected ʿĪsā (Jesus), the Gospel, Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'an. The Christians said: our Prophet ʿĪsā is the best of prophets, the Gospel the best of Scriptures, and our religion the best, and they rejected Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'an. Both parties told the believers that there was no true religion except theirs and invited them to join it. In another report, also from Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAbdullāh ibn Ṣūriyā al-Aʿwar said to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ: guidance is only what we Jews follow, so follow us, Muḥammad, and you will be rightly guided. The Christians said the same kind of thing, and Allah sent down this verse.

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “No!"**. Tell them: it is not as you claim. We have no need of the Judaism or the Christianity you call us to. In its own day, and for its own day, each of these two religions was genuine. But both had since been distorted, and both had been abrogated by Allah. So Allah tells the Prophet ﷺ to declare, for himself and for his followers, that they are and will remain firm on the religion of Ibrāhīm.

**"We follow the faith of Abraham, the upright"**. We follow the creed of Ibrāhīm the ḥanīf. The word describes one who inclines away from every other religion towards the upright religion, one who turned from false ways of life to the way of truth. Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī and ʿĪsā ibn Jāriyah explained it as being on the straight path. Abū Qilābah said that the ḥanīf is what the messengers, from the first to the last, believed in. One gloss reads: follow the religion of Ibrāhīm, the upright, the Muslim, the sincere, and only then will you be guided. Ibrāhīm shunned every form of shirk (associating partners with Allah), adored the One God alone and obeyed no one else. His religion therefore did not carry a trace of distortion.

**"who was not a polytheist.”"**. He did not worship others alongside Allah as some do, and he did not follow the religion of the Jews or of the Christians.

### Why they turned against the Prophet ﷺ

The religion the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ taught was the religion of Ibrāhīm, the very religion the Jews and Christians claimed as their own. Why, then, did they turn away from him? Because the religion he brought taught people to take on the colour of Allah and to devote themselves to Him entirely. For the People of the Book religion had come to mean something else. It had shrunk to a badge of national pride. The Prophet's ﷺ message wounded that pride, so they turned against him. People who hold their own race superior cannot accept the truth when it appears in a nation other than theirs. They will believe in prophets who come from their own stock, but not in those raised among other peoples. The only figures they acknowledged were those of their own race. Those who see religion as the worship of Allah, by contrast, recognise the truth of every piece of wisdom Allah sends down, whoever teaches it. For the learned men of these communities, realising that the Prophet ﷺ was Allah's final messenger was no insuperable difficulty. Nothing stopped them seeing that his religion was true. They should have declared what, deep in their hearts, they knew. They refused to accept him as the final prophet, as Allah had commanded, for the simple reason that their own position and prestige mattered more to them.

In the devotional reading, the verse is addressed to the Prophet ﷺ: when the parties contended with you, they demanded that you fall in with them. Stand firm against what they call you to, and turn your attention more and more to Us. Follow the way of the Friend, who withdrew from the crowd, whether it was his own father or anyone else out of step with his Protector. As he told his father, "As I distance myself from ˹all of˺ you and from whatever you invoke besides Allah, I will ˹continue to˺ call upon my Lord ˹alone˺" (19:48). The ḥanīf, on this reading, is the one who leans towards the straight truth on the path of truth, free from any defect of created things, in agreement with the truth through the Truth Himself.

### In short

- The Jews of Madinah and the Christians of Najrān each told the Muslims that their own religion was the only road to guidance, each claiming the best prophet, the best Scripture and the best religion.
- The Prophet ﷺ is told to answer that the Muslims need neither, since both had been distorted and abrogated, and that they follow Ibrāhīm, the ḥanīf, who turned from every false way and never associated anything with Allah.
- The People of the Book rejected a message identical to Ibrāhīm's because religion had become national pride for them, and the truth had appeared outside their race.
- Inwardly, the believer is told to stand firm against the pull of the crowd, as Ibrāhīm withdrew even from his father (19:48).

<<<VERSE 2:136>>>
Having rejected the Jewish and Christian invitations, the Muslims are now told what to declare instead. "Say, ˹O believers,˺ “We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us; and what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants", the verse begins, "and what was given to Moses, Jesus, and other prophets from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them. And to Allah we all submit.”" Allah directs His believing servants to have faith in what came down to them through His Messenger Muḥammad ﷺ, and in what the earlier prophets received in general. Some prophets He names, and many others He leaves unnamed. He tells the believers to believe in all of them and not to differentiate between them. In this way He teaches them the path of Allah's oneness, so that the Jews and Christians could see what that oneness means.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O believers,˺"**. The address is to the believers, and the answer is for the Jews and Christians who make these baseless claims.

**"“We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us"**. In Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'an.

**"and what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants"**. In Ibrāhīm and his Scripture, the ten scrolls, in Ismāʿīl, Isḥāq and Yaʿqūb (Jacob) and their Scriptures, and in the sons of Yaʿqūb and theirs. The Arabic calls Yaʿqūb's descendants al-Asbāṭ, the Tribes. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, al-Rabīʿ and Qatādah said that al-Asbāṭ are the twelve sons of Yaʿqūb, each of whom had a community of people descended from him, which is why they bear this name. Al-Khalīl ibn Aḥmad and others said that the Asbāṭ among the Children of Israel are what the tribes are among the children of Ismāʿīl. So the word means the different tribes of Israel, to whom Allah sent a number of prophets. Mūsā (Moses) told the Children of Israel to remember Allah's favour "when He raised prophets from among you, made you sovereign" (5:20), and Allah says, "We divided them into twelve tribes—each as a community" (7:160). Al-Qurṭubī said that a sibṭ is a group of people, or a tribe, all descended from the same ancestors. Yaʿqūb had twelve sons, and the offspring of each formed a tribe. Allah blessed his seed so richly that Yūsuf (Joseph) and his brothers entered Egypt as a group of twelve men, yet their line flourished, and when the Israelites left Egypt with Mūsā they numbered in the thousands. Another form of this blessing was the large number of prophets Yaʿqūb's progeny produced.

**"and what was given to Moses, Jesus, and other prophets from their Lord"**. In Mūsā and the Torah, in ʿĪsā (Jesus) and the Gospel, and in all the prophets and the Books and signs they received. Qatādah said that Allah ordered the believers to believe in Him, in every Book of His and in every messenger. Sulaymān ibn Ḥabīb said that they were commanded to believe in the Torah and the Injīl as originally revealed, not to put them into practice.

**"We make no distinction between any of them."**. We do not believe in some and reject others, as the Jews and Christians do. Allah sent every one of them as a prophet to teach His oneness. The believers must not imitate those whom Allah describes as wishing "to make a distinction between Allah and His messengers, saying, “We believe in some and disbelieve in others,” desiring to forge a compromise", of whom He says, "they are indeed the true disbelievers" (4:150-151).

**"And to Allah we all submit.”"**. We acknowledge that we worship Him, declaring that there is only one true God, and we humbly surrender to Him alone.

### The creed in five points

This verse sets out the basic tenets of the religion of Ibrāhīm in its present form. First, Muslims believe in Allah and in the guidance He sent them through the Prophet ﷺ. Second, they believe in all the prophets Allah sent from age to age, some of whom are named here. Third, some prophets may in certain respects be superior to others, but a Muslim must believe in every one of them without discriminating between them. Fourth, Muslims hold that the Sharīʿahs of all the prophets named here were instituted by Allah Himself, but have now been abrogated, so they follow the Sharīʿah of the Prophet ﷺ, which alone remains valid. Fifth, Muslims obey Allah alone in the end and submit themselves totally to Him.

Two hadiths show how the Prophet ﷺ himself used this verse. Al-Bukhārī relates from Abū Hurayrah that the People of the Book would read the Torah in Hebrew and then explain it to the Muslims in Arabic. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: neither believe the People of the Book nor call them liars, but say, "We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us." And Muslim, Abū Dāwūd and an-Nasāʾī record from Ibn ʿAbbās that in the two voluntary rakʿahs before Fajr the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would mostly recite this verse, "We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us", and the words of the disciples of ʿĪsā, "We believe in Allah, so bear witness that we have submitted" (3:52).

### Truth is personal, not ancestral

Just as the people of old received their due as individuals, so later generations will receive what they themselves deserve, for truth is an individual matter, not an ancestral one. The mistake of the People of the Book was to imagine that the good deeds of their ancestors would earn a reward for the present generation and those to come. The Christian idea of original sin implied the reverse, that sins pass from one generation to the next. Neither belief has any basis in truth. Allah rewards each person according to his own actions, and no one answers for the deeds of others.

In the devotional reading, because our Prophet ﷺ believed in everything revealed before him, he was honoured with everything Allah had conferred in honour on the prophets before him. When Allah proclaimed the harmony of all the prophets' messages, He placed everything in existence under his banner, and he said that Ādam (Adam) and all others would be under his banner on the Day of Resurrection. When his community believed in all that Allah had revealed to His messengers, dividing none of them from the rest, it drew the highest share of honour and took precedence over every other community.

### In short

- The believers are told to declare faith in the Qur'an and in everything revealed to Ibrāhīm, Ismāʿīl, Isḥāq, Yaʿqūb, the twelve Tribes, Mūsā, ʿĪsā and all the prophets, without picking and choosing (4:150-151).
- The Tribes are the communities descended from Yaʿqūb's twelve sons (5:20, 7:160), a line that grew from twelve men in Egypt to thousands and produced many prophets.
- The creed has five points: faith in Allah and His final guidance, in all prophets, without distinction, with only the last Sharīʿah in force, and total submission to Allah alone.
- The Prophet ﷺ taught Muslims to answer the People of the Book with this verse and recited it, with 3:52, in the two rakʿahs before Fajr.
- Reward is personal, and the community that accepted all the messengers took precedence over all others.
