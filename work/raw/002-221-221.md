<<<VERSE 2:221>>>
From wealth and life the surah passes to marriage. This verse gives the ruling on marriage with those who associate partners with Allah. "Do not marry polytheistic women until they believe; for a believing slave-woman is better than a free polytheist, even though she may look pleasant to you. And do not marry your women to polytheistic men until they believe, for a believing slave-man is better than a free polytheist, even though he may look pleasant to you." It concludes: "They invite ˹you˺ to the Fire while Allah invites ˹you˺ to Paradise and forgiveness by His grace. He makes His revelations clear to the people so perhaps they will be mindful".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not marry polytheistic women until they believe;"**. Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān said the verse was revealed about Marthad ibn Abī Marthad al-Ghanawī, an ally of Banū Hāshim, whom the Prophet ﷺ had sent to Makkah to bring out some Muslim prisoners held there. He sought the Prophet's ﷺ permission to marry ʿAnāq, a poor and fairly beautiful woman of Quraysh who was an idolatress while he was a Muslim, telling him that he liked her. Allah revealed this verse. Allah forbade the believers to marry al-mushrikāt, polytheistic women who worship idols, women who give partners to Allah, until they believe in Him alone and enter Islam. Though the wording is general enough to cover every mushrik woman, idolater or of the People of the Scripture, Allah excluded the women of the People of the Scripture by the verse, "˹permissible for you in marriage˺ are chaste believing women as well as chaste women of those given the Scripture before you—as long as you pay them their dowries in wedlock" (5:5). ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah relates from Ibn ʿAbbās that Allah excepted the women of the People of the Scripture. This is the explanation of Mujāhid, together with ʿIkrimah, together with Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, together with Makḥūl, together with al-Ḥasan, together with aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, together with Zayd ibn Aslam, together with al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and others. Some scholars said the verse speaks only of idol worshippers and not of the People of the Scripture at all, which comes to the same result. Allah knows best. If mushrik is taken to mean all non-Muslims, the women of the People of the Book must be exempted because of 5:5. If it is taken to mean those non-Muslims who believe in no prophet and no scripture, no exception is needed. Abū Jaʿfar ibn Jarīr, after noting the consensus (ijmāʿ) that marriage to women of the People of the Scripture is allowed, said that ʿUmar disliked it, lest Muslims turn away from marrying Muslim women or for similar reasons. An authentic chain has Shaqīq relate that when Ḥudhayfah married a Jewish woman ʿUmar wrote to him to divorce her, and when he wrote back asking whether ʿUmar claimed she was unlawful for him, ʿUmar replied that he did not, but feared the Muslims would marry the prostitutes among them. Ibn Jarīr relates from Zayd ibn Wahb, with a stronger chain, that ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb said that the Muslim man may marry the Christian woman but the Christian man may not marry the Muslim woman. Ibn Abī Ḥātim said that Ibn ʿUmar disliked marrying women of the People of the Scripture on the strength of his own reading of this verse. Al-Bukhārī records him saying that he knew of no greater shirk than a woman's saying that Jesus is her Lord. In the devotional reading, holding religion’s rope and seeking Muslim protection is preferable rather than settling for an outsider to faith on a road towards disbelief. A concession exists in the law for marriage with the women of the Book. Yet the inward suggestion is to abstain and forgo that entitlement, while marriage with polytheists is absolutely forbidden and cutting off relations with them is an irreversible decree.

**"for a believing slave-woman is better than a free polytheist, even though she may look pleasant to you."**. Al-Suddī relates through Abū Mālik from Ibn ʿAbbās that these words were revealed about ʿAbdullāh ibn Rawāḥah, who owned a black slave-woman and struck her in anger, then, troubled by what he had done, hurried to tell the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. Asked what really worried him, he said that she fasted, prayed, performed ablution properly and testified that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad ﷺ is His Messenger. The Prophet ﷺ told him that she was a believer. ʿAbdullāh swore by Him who had sent him with the truth that he would free her and marry her, and did so, and some Muslims reproached him for marrying a slave. This is because they still hoped to marry idolatresses and to marry their daughters to idolaters out of desire for noble lineage. The verse was revealed as a rebuttal of the idea that marrying a slave-girl was shameful and marrying a free idolatress better. Faith in Allah and His Messenger makes an enslaved woman better than a free idol-worshipping woman, however much her beauty and wealth may please you. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record a hadith from Abū Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ said a woman is married for four things, her wealth, her lineage, her beauty and her religion. Therefore, take the one with religion and may your hands be filled with dust. Muslim records this hadith also from Jābir. Muslim also records from Ibn ʿAmr that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said this world is a passing delight. The best of its delights is a righteous wife.

**"And do not marry your women to polytheistic men until they believe,"**. Do not give believing women in marriage to al-mushrikīn, polytheistic men, as Allah says of the believing women who emigrated, "These ˹women˺ are not lawful ˹wives˺ for the disbelievers, nor are the disbelievers lawful ˹husbands˺ for them" (60:10). Here no exception is made. Since the difference of the People of the Book is lighter, one might argue that if Muslim men may marry their women, Muslim women should be able to marry their men. A little reflection shows the basic difference between the two cases. Women are somewhat weaker by nature, and the husband holds the controlling and caretaking role. Therefore, the likelihood of a wife being won over to his beliefs is real, and a Muslim woman married to a Jew or Christian runs a strong risk of having her faith spoiled. By contrast, a Jewish or Christian woman married to a Muslim is unlikely in principle to sway her husband, and if he falls to his wife's instigation against the normal order of things, the fault is his own. For the guardians of a girl it follows that they must investigate the beliefs of a suitor before accepting him. This is because it is not correct to give a Muslim woman to a man who is outwardly counted a Muslim but whose beliefs are those of a non-Muslim, and if such corruption of belief arises after the marriage, the contract (nikāḥ) breaks of itself. Many Muslims, ignorant of their religion and under the influence of so-called modern thinking, have ruined their beliefs in this way.

**"for a believing slave-man is better than a free polytheist, even though he may look pleasant to you."**. A believing man, even an Abyssinian slave, is better than a mushrik, even a rich master, however his body, strength, wealth and good looks may impress you.

### Faith and the household

**"They invite ˹you˺ to the Fire"**. Men and women who commit shirk call, by their words and actions, to disbelief and to the works that lead to Hell. This is the reason for the ruling. Marriage demands mutual love and harmony, without which its real purpose is not achieved. If such bonds of love are formed with mushriks or disbelievers the inevitable effect is that Muslims tilt emotionally towards kufr and shirk, or at the least lose their abhorrence of them. They may therefore become involved in them and end in Hell. Mingling closely with disbelievers makes one love this life and prefer it to the Hereafter, with the severest consequences. Why, then, are the women of the People of the Book excepted, when the danger seems to apply to all non-Muslims? Because their difference from Islam is relatively less. Islamic belief rests on three poles, tawḥīd, the Hereafter and prophethood. The Jews and Christians agree with the Muslims on the Hereafter according to their original faith, in which associating partners with God is likewise kufr, the Christians' slide into shirk through excessive love and veneration of ʿĪsā being another matter. The remaining fundamental disagreement concerns disbelief in Muhammad ﷺ as Allah’s Messenger, a cardinal belief without which no one is a Muslim, yet their difference is lighter than that of other non-Muslims and the danger of corruption correspondingly smaller. Another doubt is that marriage influences both parties equally, so that if a Muslim spouse may be swayed, the non-Muslim spouse may equally be won to Islam. The two possibilities should cancel out. The wisdom of the prohibition is that where the danger of harm and the hope of benefit are equal, the reasonable course is to give more weight to preventing the harm than to seeking the benefit, as the Persian proverb says that a wise man does not take the antidote on certainty and the poison on doubt. So the hope of the non-Muslim spouse's conversion is set aside and the fear for the Muslim spouse's faith is provided against. The permission for Muslim men to marry Jewish and Christian women means only that such a marriage, if contracted, is valid and its children legitimate, for hadiths show that even this marriage is not viewed with favour. The Prophet ﷺ taught that a Muslim should seek a wife who fully observes the faith, so that she strengthens him in their joint practice of religion and their children grow up as practising Muslims under their care. If marriage to a non-practising Muslim woman was not favoured, how could marriage to a non-Muslim be? For this reason ʿUmar al-Fārūq, on learning that such marriages were spreading alarmingly in Iraq and Syria, stopped them by an executive order noting that they cause religious and political corruption in Muslim families, as Imam Muhammad's Kitāb al-Āthār records, and in modern times the use of marriage for political ends, for entry into Muslim families and for spying has been admitted by Christian writers themselves. There is a further reason today. If the lives of most contemporary Europeans and Americans registered as Jews and Christians were examined, it would be found that a great many have nothing to do with Christianity or Judaism: they believe neither in ʿĪsā nor in the Bible, neither in Mūsā nor in the Torah, nor even in God or a Day of Judgment. Such people do not fall under the Qur'anic permission, and marriage with their women, as with other non-Muslims, is absolutely ḥarām. Ashraf ʿAlī Thānavī notes that it is not correct to marry women from a group counted among the People of the Book by name and identity when scrutiny of their beliefs shows otherwise, as when white Westerners are generally assumed to be Christians although many hold wholly atheistic beliefs, for how can those who deny God, the prophethood of Jesus and the Bible as revealed scripture be Christians? Marrying Western women without investigating these matters is described as a serious mistake.

**"while Allah invites ˹you˺ to Paradise and forgiveness by His grace."**. Allah calls, through His law, His commands and prohibitions, and through the voice of His prophets, to the deeds that merit Paradise and pardon, to Paradise through His Oneness and to forgiveness through repentance, by His leave and will, so that His call may be heeded by marrying among His friends.

**"He makes His revelations clear to the people so perhaps they will be mindful"**. He expounds His commands and prohibitions regarding marriage so that people may take admonition, refrain from unlawful marriage and act obediently. The relationship of husband and wife may be likened to that of a farmer and his field: a couple does not take the marriage vow for selfish enjoyment alone. Planting a field is serious work. Likewise, the marital relationship is serious and must follow certain guidelines. The first consideration in choosing a spouse is belief. This is because the bond between husband and wife is extremely delicate, balanced within a network of social, domestic and psychological factors, and for so close a relationship to flourish their beliefs must harmonise. Marriage of a Muslim to someone of another faith may have to compromise his beliefs, in which case his faith suffers, or refuse to, in which case his marriage founders. Secondly, husband and wife should live together according to the pattern of creation, for nature, like revelation, expresses Allah's will and follows His commands to the letter. Therefore, natural laws are to be followed as revealed laws are obeyed. And one should fear Allah at all times, mindful that everyone returns before Him and that He knows both inward and outward states.

### In short

- Marriage with idolatresses is forbidden until they believe, revealed when Marthad al-Ghanawī wished to marry ʿAnāq (Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān). A believing slave-woman is better than a free idolatress, revealed when ʿAbdullāh ibn Rawāḥah freed and married his believing slave (Ibn ʿAbbās). Religion, not wealth, lineage or beauty, is the ground of choice (Abū Hurayrah, together with Jābir, together with Ibn ʿAmr).
- Women of the People of the Scripture are excepted by 5:5 (Ibn ʿAbbās, together with Mujāhid and others, consensus per Ibn Jarīr), though ʿUmar and Ibn ʿUmar disliked such marriages and ʿUmar stopped them in Iraq and Syria. The exception does not cover nominal Christians and Jews who deny God, Jesus, Moses and the Scriptures (Thānavī).
- Believing women may never be married to mushrik men (60:10), nor to nominal Muslims of corrupt belief. This is because the wife is the more likely to be swayed and averting harm outweighs the hope of benefit.
- Mushriks call to the Fire by drawing their spouses towards kufr and love of this world, while Allah calls through His law and prophets to Paradise and forgiveness.
- Marriage is a serious bond like a farmer's work in his field, requiring harmony of belief and the fear of Allah.
