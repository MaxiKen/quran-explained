<<<VERSE 2:208>>>
The previous verse praised the sincere. Sincerity can sometimes slip inadvertently into excess, when a person means to add to his obedience but the addition turns out to exceed the limits of the Sharīʿah and the Sunnah, which is called bidʿah. ### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Enter into Islam wholeheartedly"**. The occasion, related by ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās and in the earliest reports, concerns ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and his companions, who had been learned scholars among the Jews before their Islam. Saturday was the sacred day of rest in Judaism and camel meat was unlawful, so once in Islam they thought to combine the two faiths: they would go on honouring the Sabbath, which the law of Moses required and Islam did not forbid, and avoid camel meat and milk in practice while believing them lawful, since Islam did not oblige anyone to eat them. In this way, they thought, they could stay in touch with the law of Moses without opposing the Sharīʿah of Muhammad ﷺ, which struck them as a stronger show of obedience and a closer approach to faith. When some Muslims reproached them they defended themselves by saying they could observe both, and they told the Prophet ﷺ that the Torah was the Book of Allah and asked to be allowed to follow its teachings. Allah then revealed this verse, correcting the idea by establishing that Islam is an obligation in its totality and is total and perfect only when what is not necessary in it is not counted as part of it. To treat such a thought or practice as part of the faith is a satanic slip that may bring a far severer punishment than ordinary sins. Al-silm, also read al-salm, has two senses, peace and Islam, and here by the consensus of the Companions and their successors it means Islam, as al-ʿAwfī relates from Ibn ʿAbbās and as Mujāhid, Ṭāwūs, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, ʿIkrimah, Qatādah, al-Suddī and Ibn Zayd said. Kāffah means totally and universally, and is a circumstantial qualifier (ḥāl) with two possible references. It may qualify the pronoun of "enter", describing the condition of the believers as they enter, so that their entire person, hands and feet, eyes and ears, feeling and thinking, must lie within the parameters of Islam and obedience to Allah. This warns against physically carrying out the dictates of Islam while heart and mind are not fully satisfied, or being satisfied in heart and mind while one's actions stay outside Islam. Or it may qualify Islam itself, describing the complete state of Islam the believers must enter, so that one accepts all its injunctions rather than accepting some and hesitating over others. Since Islam is the name of the way of life given through the Qur'an and Sunnah, it does not matter which facet of life is concerned, beliefs and worship, social dealings, business, government and politics, trade and industry or anything else: what matters is entry into Islam as a complete system, an organic whole, unified and indivisible. On both readings, no Muslim deserves the name unless he sincerely accepts all the injunctions of Islam from the depths of his heart, whatever department of life they belong to and whether they concern the body or the heart and mind. Allah commands His servants who believe in Him and His Messenger ﷺ to implement all of Islam's legislation, to hold to all its commandments as far as they can and to refrain from all its prohibitions. That "wholeheartedly" means "in its entirety" is the explanation of Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ʿIkrimah, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, al-Suddī, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, Qatādah and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk. Mujāhid said it means performing all good works and pious deeds, addressed especially to the People of the Scripture who had embraced the faith, and Ibn Abī Ḥātim relates from Ibn ʿAbbās that it refers to the believers among them who, having believed in Allah, still kept parts of the Torah and the earlier revelations, and were commanded to embrace the religion of Muhammad ﷺ in its entirety and abandon none of it, no longer adhering to the Torah. The believers are not to leave out any part of Islam as the People of the Scripture believed in parts of their book and disbelieved in others. The occasion shows that one must keep nothing but the teachings of Islam in view and practise them in full, which makes the Muslims independent of all other religions and nations. The verse is a stern warning to those who have tied Islam up with the mosque and acts of worship while neglecting the injunctions on social life, business and personal dealings as if they were no part of religion, a negligence widespread among the technically religious, who seem not to regard these injunctions as Islam's, make no effort to learn them in an orderly way and do not think of acting on them. In the devotional reading, the believer is charged to make peace with everyone except his lower self, which makes no move but to oppose its master, for whoever makes peace with himself grows listless in his spiritual struggles, which is why a seeker falls short and an aspirant loses interest.

**"and do not follow Satan's footsteps."**. Do not follow the ways of Satan, his temptations by way of creating divisions, or his embellishment of the supposed unlawfulness of the Sabbath, camel meat and the like. Perform the acts of worship and avoid what Satan commands, for "He only incites you to commit evil and indecency, and to claim against Allah what you do not know" (2:169), and "He only invites his followers to become inmates of the Blaze" (35:6). **"Surely he is your sworn enemy"**. His enmity is open and obvious, which is why no step of his may be followed.

In the devotional reading, the veil of the unseen is lifted and the clarity of the divine preordination exposed. Those who put their own interests first and Islam second, and those who enter Islam unconditionally, are people of very different mettle. It is usually the former who gather the splendours of this life while the latter go without, and the possession of material resources makes the former feel entitled to an air of superiority and to look down on the true believers. But this state of affairs is short-lived. The world will end and give way to a new and finer order in which the great will be cut down to size and those once thought lowly will occupy the high places of the heavenly world.

### In short

- The hesitant are waiting, in effect, for Allah to come in the shadows of clouds with the angels on the Day of Resurrection (Abū al-ʿĀliyah, compare 25:25, 89:21, 6:158), when belief will no longer help.
- Allah's coming is among the mutashābihāt, affirmed without asking how, and the people of tawḥīd need no taʾwīl, knowing He is beyond movement and place.
- Faith is meant to rest on proofs and on the signs of the universe, since the test is to believe without seeing.
- When the matter is settled all affairs return to Allah, and the worldly superiority of the half-hearted will be reversed in the next order.
