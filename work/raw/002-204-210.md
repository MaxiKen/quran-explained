<<<VERSE 2:204>>>
The previous verses distinguished two kinds of people who pray, those who want everything here and those who ask for the good of both worlds. The same two kinds now appear as the hypocrite and the sincere believer. "There are some ˹hypocrites˺ who impress you with their views regarding worldly affairs and openly call upon Allah to witness what is in their hearts, yet they are your worst adversaries".

### Phrase by phrase

**"There are some ˹hypocrites˺ who impress you with their views regarding worldly affairs"**. Al-Suddī said that these verses were revealed about al-Akhnas ibn Sharīq al-Thaqafī, an ally of Banū Zuhrah, who came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in al-Madīnah and announced his Islam while his heart concealed otherwise. He was eloquent and good-looking, and the Prophet ﷺ liked his speech. He claimed to have embraced Islam sincerely, invoking Allah as witness. He professed love for the Prophet ﷺ and support both privately and publicly. Frequent visits and oaths reinforced that profession. Away from the Prophet ﷺ, however, he promptly turned to mischief against the Muslims. Ibn ʿAbbās said the verses were revealed about some hypocrites who criticised Khubayb and his companions killed at al-Rajīʿ, after which Allah condemned the hypocrites and praised Khubayb and his companions. It was also said that they describe the hypocrites and the believers in general, the view of Qatādah, Mujāhid, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and several others, and this is correct. Such a man's speech, words and outward appearance please you in this life, though they would not please you in the Hereafter, since they contradict what he really believes. Ibn Jarīr conveys al-Quraẓī's report of Nawf al-Bikālī, a reader of the earlier Books. Nawf found a description there of some members of this ummah who exploit religion for profit. Their words surpass honey in sweetness, but their hearts surpass aloes in bitterness. They look like sheep while concealing wolves' viciousness. The reported divine address asks how they dare challenge Him while being deceived by Him. It includes an oath by Himself to bring a fitnah upon them. That trial will bewilder even the wise. Nawf said that he then found these people described in the Qur'an in this very verse, and al-Quraẓī's statement is ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ.

**"and openly call upon Allah to witness what is in their hearts,"**. He swears by Allah that he loves and follows you. Such people pretend to be Muslims while defying Allah through the disbelief and hypocrisy hidden in their hearts, as He says of them, "They try to hide ˹their deception˺ from people, but they can never hide it from Allah" (4:108), an explanation Ibn Isḥāq relates from Ibn ʿAbbās. It was also said that when such people announce their Islam they swear that what is in their hearts matches what their tongues say, which is also correct, the choice of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam and of Ibn Jarīr, who relates it from Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid, and Allah knows best. The hypocrite uses professions of faith to protect himself and his finances. He falsely invokes Allah as witness to inward faith and goodness. His actual disposition is hostility and hatred towards the Muslims.

**"yet they are your worst adversaries"**. The word aladd literally means wicked, and here most quarrelsome, from the same root as the "contentious" people of "warn those who are contentious" (19:97). The hypocrite lies, twists the truth when he argues and cares nothing for it, deviating, deceiving and disputing stubbornly on account of his enmity towards the Prophet ﷺ and his followers, arguing for falsehood as a staunch opponent. The Ṣaḥīḥ records that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said the signs of a hypocrite are three: when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it, and when he disputes he is most quarrelsome. Al-Bukhārī records from ʿĀʾishah that the Prophet ﷺ said the most hateful of people to Allah is the one who is aladd and khaṣim, the bitterly quarrelsome.

### Words and deeds

Those who have made opportunism their religion can always impress people with words, because it serves their interest to do so. Right and wrong do not matter to them and no fixed standard guides them, only what is calculated to make a good impression on the listener. They have no difficulty painting a glowing picture outside while their hearts lack the warmth of sincerity, for they owe loyalty to nothing but their own interests. His speech suggests a great reformer, but his actions spread corruption. The contradiction lies within him. He professes love of truth while pursuing personal advantage at others' expense. Promises of peace and reconciliation give way to actions breeding conflict. He manipulates emotions and seeks popularity without regard for the actual situation. Such people have sold themselves for this world. Even when the truth is made plain they do not follow it, seeing no gain in it, and they uphold it only when there is enough profit to be had. Behind an obsequious exterior they hide a proud ego, which is why they can never acknowledge a truth that comes from a preacher they consider beneath them. The devotional reading contrasts skill in speech with hearts deprived of divine attention. Their hearts are pictured as bound by deprivation and covered in ignorance. Nothing inward supports their outward appearance. Their words lack weight, and their fidelity cannot be trusted. The allusion is to the people of externals who have not been helped by the lights of insight and have no faith in this kind of discourse, from whom secrets must be kept since they receive them only with disapproval. Peaceable ordinary people who respect and fully believe in this path are nearer to it. Those claiming to be elect while lacking faith in it are farther away.

### In short

- The verse describes the hypocrite, revealed about al-Akhnas ibn Sharīq (al-Suddī), about the hypocrites who mocked Khubayb's companions (Ibn ʿAbbās), or hypocrites in general (Qatādah, Mujāhid, al-Rabīʿ).
- Sweet speech and oaths of sincerity mask disbelief, as 4:108 says, and the earlier Books described such men as sheep outwardly and wolves within (Nawf al-Bikālī).
- Aladd means bitterly quarrelsome (compare 19:97), and the most hateful person to Allah is the stubborn disputant (ʿĀʾishah), quarrelsomeness being one of the three signs of a hypocrite.
- Opportunists impress with words but corrupt with deeds, follow truth only when it pays, and hide a proud ego behind flattery.

<<<VERSE 2:205>>>
Fine words are tested by what follows them: "And when they leave ˹you˺, they strive throughout the land to spread mischief in it and destroy crops and cattle. Allah does not like mischief." The person's conduct away from the Prophet ﷺ exposes the corruption hidden beneath the speech.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And when they leave ˹you˺, they strive throughout the land to spread mischief in it"** The explanation understands the departure as turning away, and another gloss connects it with anger. The person then acts through transgression. False words, corrupt belief and evil deeds belong to the same portrait.

**"and destroy crops and cattle."** The harm reaches cultivated land, animals and what people depend upon for livelihood. Burning grain and fields or killing livestock damages more than the owner's immediate property.

**"Allah does not like mischief"** He rejects such corruption and the conduct of those practising it. Religious and worldly affairs alike can be ruined by the actions described.

### What is reported about al-Akhnas?

Al-Suddi says al-Akhnas left the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and passed a Muslim field and some donkeys. He burned the field and hamstrung the animals. The verse is connected with those acts.

Another account says he saw the crops and livestock, then returned at night to burn and slaughter. The alternatives differ in timing, while both illustrate destruction concealed behind plausible speech.

Mujahid gives another aspect: corruption brings the withholding of rain, so crops and offspring perish. The explanation thus includes harm directly committed and the wider consequence of the corrupt conduct.

### Does striving mean running?

Sa'a means striving or intending. The same word describes Pharaoh turning away in (79:22). In (62:9), believers are called towards Friday remembrance with related language.

The latter command means setting out for prayer, not running to the mosque. The sunnah instead teaches walking calmly and with dignity. The same verb therefore takes its moral character from the purpose towards which the person strives.

### What inward corruption is pictured?

The devotional reading describes ties to religion weakening while attachment to worldly gain grows stronger. Desire becomes woven around forbidden acquisitions. Private planning then follows worldly advantage rather than sound judgment.

Such people enlist helpers whose inner sight has been removed. The account joins this loss of discernment with outward damage: both religious affairs and the proper order of worldly life suffer.

The warning therefore looks beyond a single destructive incident. It asks what the person's ambitions and private choices are doing to the bonds of obedience.

### In short

- The person's actions expose what fine speech concealed.
- Reports connect al-Akhnas with burning crops and harming livestock.
- Striving takes its meaning from its aim, and the Friday command does not require running.
- The inward reading contrasts weakened devotion with tightening attachment to worldly gain.

<<<VERSE 2:206>>>
Advice reveals the pride behind the corruption: "When it is said to them, "Fear Allah," pride carries them off to sin." Rather than turn back, the hypocrite takes offence and continues the wrong. The verse names Hell as the outcome of that refusal.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When it is said to them, "Fear Allah," pride carries them off to sin."** The advice asks for restraint and adherence to truth. Pride answers with anger. The person makes the very correction an occasion for further sin instead of repentance.

**"Hell will be their proper place. What an evil place to rest"** The Fire suffices as punishment. Its description as a resting place or cradle makes the destination terrible rather than comforting. Corruption joined to refusal of correction receives this warning.

### Why does advice provoke more wrongdoing?

Habitual evil makes the hypocrite resent being corrected. Rage and self-importance carry him further into what he was asked to avoid. He protects his pride instead of examining his conduct.

The explanation compares (22:72), where those rejecting revelation show rage when it is recited. They appear ready to attack the people reciting it. Here too, the response to a truthful warning exposes the refusal to receive it.

### How does the inward reading describe the adviser?

The proud person asks how anyone dares address someone like him. He then reverses the advice, accusing the adviser of needing correction instead. Hostility can remain in the heart for years.

Divine mercy would have helped him recognise his faults and receive the warning humbly. That recognition is itself a favour. Without it, pride makes fair criticism seem an insult.

### What punishment is already felt inwardly?

The devotional reading sees estrangement, darkness in the lower self and narrowed choice as an earlier chastisement. Desire repeatedly drives the person before the greater punishment arrives.

It connects this with (32:21), which describes a lesser torment before the major one. The inward application does not replace the outward warning of Hell. It describes a present foretaste alongside the final consequence.

One early report alleges that al-Akhnas ibn Shurayq burned another person's crops and killed a donkey. It connects the three verses with that conduct. The account's reported character is retained rather than raised to certainty.

### In short

- Pride turns a call to fear Allah into an occasion for more sin.
- The hypocrite attacks the advice instead of examining the fault.
- Recognising one's errors and accepting correction is a divine favour.
- Inward estrangement is pictured as a foretaste of the greater punishment.

<<<VERSE 2:207>>>
After exposing hypocrisy, the passage honours sincere sacrifice: "And there are those who would dedicate their lives to Allah's pleasure." Such people prefer obedience to personal gain. The closing words connect their devotion with Allah's graciousness towards His servants.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And there are those who would dedicate their lives to Allah's pleasure."** The image is of someone selling himself, spending his life in obedience for Allah's approval. Ṣuhayb ibn Sinān al-Rūmī is central to accounts of revelation, but the meaning also embraces other forms of costly faithfulness.

**"And Allah is Ever Gracious to ˹His˺ servants"** Allah gently guides His servants towards what pleases Him. His mercy is vast. Their ability to surrender themselves is a gift of that gentleness, not a claim that obliges Him to favour them.

### What did Ṣuhayb surrender?

The connection with Ṣuhayb is given by Ibn ʿAbbās and Anas, together with Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab and Abū ʿUthmān al-Nahdī. ʿIkrimah and others also give it. Al-Ḥākim, Ibn Jarīr and Ibn Abī Ḥātim report it with sound chains.

Ṣuhayb embraced Islam in Makkah and sought to emigrate. Quraysh would not let him take his wealth away. He chose to leave the wealth rather than give up migration.

Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab relates that pursuers caught him after he left Makkah. Ṣuhayb dismounted and prepared his bow and arrows. He reminded them of his skill as an archer and swore to resist with arrows, then his sword, while able. Alternatively, he would disclose where his money was if they promised to let him go.

They accepted that bargain, and he gave them the information. On reaching al-Madīnah safely, he told the Prophet ﷺ what had happened. The Prophet ﷺ twice congratulated Abū Yaḥyā, Ṣuhayb's kunyah, on the profitable exchange. The verse confirmed that praise.

Other accounts describe capture and torture. In them, Ṣuhayb argued that he was an old man who would not harm the Makkans by staying or leaving. He offered his money in return for being left with his religion. They accepted and even supplied a mount and provisions.

ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, Abū Bakr and other Companions met him at al-Ḥarrah, the black stony ground outside al-Madīnah. They congratulated him on his trade. He wished that theirs too would prosper and asked what they meant. They answered by reciting the verse revealed about him.

### Who else falls within the meaning?

Similar events involving other Companions are also cited. One early account joins Ṣuhayb with his companions and connects the closing words with Yāsir and Sumayyah. They were ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir's parents and were among those killed by the Makkan idolaters.

The meaning extends to fighters who give themselves for Allah. The exchange of believers' lives and wealth for Paradise is also described in (9:111). When Hishām ibn ʿĀmir advanced into the enemy's ranks, some criticised him. ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb and Abū Hurayrah answered them with this verse.

Al-Ḥasan gives another application. A Muslim invites a disbeliever to affirm Allah's oneness, offering safety for his blood and property. When the invitation is refused, the Muslim pledges his own life for Allah and fights until killed.

Another explanation concerns commanding good and forbidding evil. Abū al-Khalīl relates that ʿUmar heard the verse recited and uttered the words of return to Allah. He identified a man killed while carrying out that duty.

### What is the inward meaning of the exchange?

Sincerity places Allah's command ahead of the demands of the self. It may require relinquishing wealth, popularity or inherited religious custom to follow the truth. Hostility does not change that standard.

The devotional reading describes servants favoured by mercy and raised through divine predetermination. They prefer the Real's pleasure to themselves and surrender to their mawlā, their Protector. His gentleness brings them to those states. The states do not first earn that gentleness as a debt.

### In short

- Ṣuhayb's surrender of wealth for emigration illustrates the profitable exchange of personal advantage for Allah's pleasure.
- The accounts also include martyrs, fighters and those killed while commanding good.
- Sincerity obeys Allah despite the cost in possessions, status or life.
- Divine graciousness enables devotion rather than becoming something devotion can demand.

<<<VERSE 2:208>>>
Sincere intention does not make every religious addition right. The verse calls for complete acceptance of Islam: "O believers! Enter into Islam wholeheartedly and do not follow Satan's footsteps." It warns against both omitting its commands and adding practices as religion beyond the Sharīʿah and Sunnah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Enter into Islam wholeheartedly"** Al-silm, also read al-salm, can mean peace or Islam. Here the Companions and their successors understood Islam. Kāffah expresses completeness: the whole person is to submit, and all of Islam is to be accepted.

**"and do not follow Satan's footsteps."** The warning rejects Satan's temptations, divisive suggestions and apparently pious additions that contradict guidance. He may also whisper that sound conduct and abandoning harmful inclinations are beyond one's ability.

**"Surely he is your sworn enemy"** Satan's hostility is evident. His suggestions must not be followed simply because they appear to offer greater devotion.

### Why did keeping earlier practices become a problem?

ʿAṭāʾ relates from Ibn ʿAbbās an occasion involving ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and his companions, formerly learned Jews. They wished to continue honouring the Sabbath and avoiding camel meat and milk after becoming Muslims.

Their reasoning was that Moses' law required the Sabbath, while Islam did not command its dishonouring. They could believe camel meat lawful without eating it, since eating it was not obligatory. Combining the practices seemed to them a stronger obedience to divine law without opposing Muhammad's ﷺ Sharīʿah.

When reproached, they defended the possibility of observing both. They also asked the Prophet ﷺ for permission to follow the Torah, recognising it as Allah's Book. The verse corrected their proposed combination.

The distinction concerns counting an unnecessary practice as part of the faith. Islam's completeness does not depend on adding requirements from the earlier law. Sincerity that exceeds the limits of Sharīʿah and Sunnah becomes bidʿah. Such a satanic lapse can merit punishment more severe than ordinary wrongdoing.

Ibn Abī Ḥātim relates Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation concerning believers who still kept parts of the Torah and earlier revelation. They were told to accept Muhammad's ﷺ religion completely, omit none of it and cease that adherence to the Torah.

### What does completeness include?

Al-ʿAwfī conveys Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation of al-silm. The same sense is given by Mujāhid and Ṭāwūs, by aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk and ʿIkrimah, and by Qatādah, al-Suddī and Ibn Zayd.

Kāffah functions as a ḥāl, a qualification of state. It may qualify the people entering Islam. On that reading, hands, feet, eyes and ears must agree with the heart, thought and feeling in obedience. Outward observance without inward acceptance is incomplete. So is inward approval that does not govern action.

Alternatively, it qualifies Islam itself. Then the command requires acceptance of every injunction rather than selection among them. The two readings converge on sincere and comprehensive obedience.

Abū al-ʿĀliyah and al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas explain the completeness alongside Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid. Others include Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, ʿIkrimah, al-Suddī, Qatādah and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk. Mujāhid includes the performance of good and pious deeds, especially addressing converts from the People of the Scripture.

Obedience includes all commands within one's ability and restraint from prohibitions. Its scope includes belief and worship, social dealings and personal rights, business and trade, industry, government and politics. The Qur'an and Sunnah provide a unified way of life, not disconnected permissions to choose.

### Why is ritual alone not enough?

The warning especially challenges those who confine religion to the mosque and prescribed worship. Neglecting social and commercial duties does not remove them from Islam. The discussion censures religious people who neither learn those duties systematically nor intend to act on them.

The occasion also calls Muslims to keep Islam's teaching as their religious standard rather than depend on other religions and nations. Earlier recipients of Scripture accepted some parts and denied others. Believers must not reproduce that partial acceptance.

### What are Satan's inward footsteps?

The commands to avoid Satan also connect with his incitement to evil and unsupported claims against Allah in (2:169). His invitation to the Blaze appears in (35:6). A suggestion may resemble faith while leading away from it.

The devotional reading asks the believer to make peace with others but not with the opposing lower self. Making peace with that self weakens spiritual effort and lets the seeker's interest fade.

Satan whispers that obedience and the abandonment of unwise inclinations cannot be maintained. The contrasting image is Moses' mother in (28:7). She is told to place him in the river when afraid, and Allah returns him safely. Trust in His direction is set against the whisper of inability.

### In short

- The occasion concerns converts wishing to retain Sabbath observance and avoidance of camel meat as religion.
- Completeness requires inward and outward obedience, and acceptance of Islam's whole teaching.
- Social rights and commercial duties belong with worship, not outside religion.
- Satan can disguise deviation as added piety or discourage obedience through whispers of inability.

<<<VERSE 2:209>>>
Clear proofs leave no excuse for deliberately turning away. The verse warns: "If you falter after receiving the clear proofs, then know that Allah is indeed Almighty, All-Wise". Accepting Islam means following its commands, not preserving only the parts that suit one's interests.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you falter after receiving the clear proofs,"** Slipping here means turning from the precepts of Islam after their truth has been established. The proofs include the Prophet ﷺ, the Qur'an and Islam itself. For the former Jews addressed in an account of revelation, they also include the explanations in their own Scripture.

**"then know that Allah is indeed Almighty, All-Wise"** Allah cannot be defeated, nor can anyone escape His punishment. His decisions, actions and laws are governed by wisdom. A delay in punishment therefore does not mean that wrongdoing has been overlooked.

### What does wholehearted acceptance require?

One early report connects the verse with ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and his companions. They retained attachment to the Sabbath and an aversion to camel meat and other things. The warning concerns resistance after guidance has become clear.

The contrast is between two ways of accepting Islam. One person follows its commands and prohibitions without making personal advantage the condition of obedience. Another accepts it only where it fits ordinary life and protects existing interests. Habits, cherished beliefs, desired profits, honour and leadership then become barriers to change.

Initial enthusiasm may fade when obedience requires a change of thinking or the surrender of a preference. The person keeps the Muslim name but prevents Islam from directing life. The warning calls for more than such conditional acceptance.

### Why is a later slip more serious?

Turning away after proof is a graver matter than making an ordinary mistake. The inward reading contrasts a single lapse after disclosure with many lapses before it. Once treachery has become known, trust cannot simply be assumed. It also pictures the punishment for the worst sins as uprooting those who persist in them.

This is not a reason to treat divine patience as permission. Allah's might means that nothing can block His judgment against rejection of His Messenger ﷺ. His wisdom governs when that judgment arrives.

Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Qatādah and al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas connect His might with retribution and His wisdom with decree. That wisdom also governs the sacred laws He establishes and the replacement of earlier nations' laws. Believers should fear Him and honour His greatness.

### In short

- Clear proofs make deliberate withdrawal from guidance especially serious.
- Wholehearted Islam allows its commands to change one's habits and interests.
- Allah's power cannot be resisted, and His wisdom determines the timing of judgment.

<<<VERSE 2:210>>>
The verse challenges those who postpone acceptance of clear guidance: "Are they waiting for Allah ˹Himself˺ to come to them in the shade of clouds, along with the angels?" At the final judgment, a sudden profession of belief cannot reopen a life whose test has ended.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Are they waiting for Allah ˹Himself˺ to come to them in the shade of clouds, along with the angels?"** The question concerns those who do not enter Islam completely, those following Satan and, in another explanation, the people of Makkah. The scene belongs to the Day of Resurrection, when Allah judges the earliest and latest generations and angels surround them.

**"˹If He did˺, then the matter would be settled ˹at once˺."** Judgment would have been completed. Another explanation emphasises the completion of their destruction. The people of Paradise and Hell would already have their appointed destinations.

**"And to Allah ˹all˺ matters will be returned ˹for judgment˺"** Every outcome returns to Him in the Hereafter. Reward and punishment rest with Him alone. No competing power remains to alter His judgment.

### How is the coming described?

Ẓulal is the plural of ẓullah, meaning a shade or canopy. Abū Jaʿfar al-Rāzī conveys Abū al-ʿĀliyah's explanation: angels descend in the clouds' shadows, and Allah comes as He wills. A reading places both Allah and the angels within that description. The descent of angels with the clouds is also described in (25:25).

Another explanation takes the coming as the arrival of Allah's command or chastisement. The account also describes it as befitting His majesty and without modality, bilā kayf.

The discussion places this coming among the mutashābihāt, whose reality exceeds human understanding. The majority of the Companions, their successors and the revered elders of the ummah affirmed the statement without attempting to determine how it occurs. Human reason cannot establish the nature of Allah's Being and attributes.

### What does the inward reading emphasise?

People may imagine the Hour far away. Its terrible events disclose the gravity of Allah's affair and the working of His power. In this reading, the described acts express those events rather than a change in His locality.

Hearts illumined by insight into divine oneness do not need to seek taʾwīl here. They recognise the Real as beyond fading, movement, a particular place or time, and appearing before human beings. When the matter is determined, the veil of the unseen is removed and divine preordination becomes clear.

### Why is waiting until then futile?

Arguments for belief have already been given. For someone seeking signs, the universe itself lies before him. Refusing clear arguments does not become reasonable merely by demanding further miracles.

The purpose of this world's test is faith without seeing Allah, on the strength of His proofs. Seeing the final judgment and only then believing does not fulfil that test. The passage beginning in (89:21) describes the crushed earth, the angels in ranks and Hell brought near. Its warning asks what use remembrance will have once that moment arrives. The same loss of opportunity for belated belief appears in (6:158).

### What returns to Allah?

Al-umūr means the matters or affairs whose consequences return to Allah. The verb has a passive reading, describing their being returned, and an active reading, describing their return. Both place the final decision with Him and connect it with each person's deeds.

The contrast also reaches those who put personal interests ahead of Islam. Their material advantage may encourage contempt for wholehearted believers who lack it. But that advantage lasts only through the present order. In the next, those apparently great are lowered and those considered lowly receive high places.

### In short

- The final judgment is not a further chance to postpone faith until Allah is seen.
- The accounts preserve differing explanations of the coming and affirm the limit of human knowledge about its manner.
- The inward reading denies movement or confinement in place to the Real.
- All affairs return to Allah, and temporary worldly superiority does not determine the final ranks.
