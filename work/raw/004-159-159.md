The finale of the passage: "Every one of the People of the Book will definitely believe in him before his death. And on the Day of Judgment Jesus will be a witness against them" (4:159). The verse answers the boast of the killing with two certainties: a universal belief still to come, and a testimony that will stand against them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Every one of the People of the Book"** gathers the Jews and the Christians, every last one of them, into a single verdict.

**"will definitely believe in him"** means a belief in ʿĪsā as he truly is: not a sorcerer, as the Jews said, and not Allah, nor His son, nor His partner, as the Christians said. Blinded at present by malice and envy, these people cannot see reality as it is. The Jews reject him as a prophet and call him a liar and an impostor. Some Christians, in ignorance like the Jews, accepted that he was crucified and killed. Others, in an excess of belief, went past every limit and took him for God and son of God. Yet the verse promises a time when their eyes will open and they will know that all their notions about ʿĪsā, and about Muḥammad al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ, were false.

**"before his death"** carries two readings, and both are recorded. In one, the pronoun of "his death" returns to the Person of the Book. At the moment of death, when the angels come and the veil of the barzakh lifts before the dying man's eyes, he will believe, but that belief will not profit him, just as the belief of Pharaoh did not profit him while he drowned. The other reading, adopted by a large number of Companions and their Successors and supported by authentic hadith, returns the pronoun to ʿĪsā himself: before the death of ʿĪsā, after he descends at the approach of the Hour. Abū Mālik commented that this occurs after ʿĪsā returns and before he dies, when all of the People of the Scripture will believe in him. So the People of the Book of that time, Jews and Christians, will believe in him genuinely. The Christians of that day will enter Islam with beliefs held true. Of the Jews, the hostile will be killed and the remainder will submit. Then kufr will be banished from the world with all its forms, and Islam will finally reign supreme on the earth. And he will die afterwards on this very earth, after every last Jew of that time has passed.

**"And on the Day of Judgment Jesus will be a witness against them"** means, as Qatādah said, that he will testify before them: the message from Allah he truly delivered, and a servant of Allah he truly was. His witness will stand against their conduct when he was sent to them, against actions that agreed with the sacred law or not. The like of it stands at the end of sūrat al-Māʾidah: "O ʿĪsā, son of Maryam! Did you say unto men" (5:116), a passage that runs until "Almighty, the All-Wise" (5:118).

### The report that fixes the reading

The reading that ties the verse to the descent rests on strong ground. From Abū Hurairah it is reported that the Holy Prophet ﷺ said:

> The son of Maryam shall definitely reappear as a just ruler. He will kill the Anti-Christ and the swine. He will break the Cross, and worship shall be for Allah alone, the one Lord of all the worlds.

Abū Hurairah then said to read, if one wished, the verse of the Qur'ān, verse 159, which mentions that not one person of the People of the Book will remain without having believed in ʿĪsā before his death, and he said with stress, before the death of ʿĪsā, repeating it three times. The report comes from a highly venerated Companion on the added strength of a sound chain of narrators, and it determines that the verse relates to the coming of ʿĪsā close to the qiyāmah.

Support comes from sūrat az-Zukhruf: "he is a sign of the Hour" (43:61). A larger number of the early authorities said the pronoun there returns to ʿĪsā, so that he himself is the sign. Another reading of the word, la-ʿalamun with the vowel opened, makes it plainer still, for ʿalam in that form means a sign or symbol. Ibn ʿAbbās gave this added support, reporting that the verse refers to ʿĪsā, who will come before the qiyāmah.

Taken together, the statement "before his death" and the authentic hadith of Abū Hurairah prove conclusively that ʿĪsā is alive, that he will reappear close to the Day of Doom, and that the Jews will be overcome. The tidings of his coming as a just ruler have been conveyed through an uninterrupted succession of narrations, more than a hundred of them, collected by the teacher Anwar Shāh al-Kashmīrī, a great servant of Islam. The collection was compiled in Arabic and published long ago, and more recently the major Syrian scholar al-ʿAllāmah ʿAbd al-Fattah Abū Ghuddah published from Beirut a fine edition of the work, enlarged with explanations and notes. Absolute and unanimous among the Muslims is the belief in the coming of ʿĪsā toward the later times, and whoever denies it goes out of the pale of Islam.

The inward reading draws its own lesson from the dying-hour belief. Because He determined that no amān, no protection, will be granted to them at the time of despair, their īmān at that time will not benefit them. What is taken into consideration comes from the protection granted by the Real, not from the servant's faith.

### The descent and the work he will do

Al-Bukhārī recorded, in the chapter about the prophets under the descent of ʿĪsā son of Maryam, that Abū Hurayrah said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> By Him in Whose Hands my soul is, the son of Maryam will shortly descend among you as a just ruler. He will break the cross, kill the pig and abolish the jizyah. Then there will be an abundance of wealth and nobody will accept charitable gifts any more. At that time one prostration will be better for them than this life and all that is in it.

Imām Aḥmad recorded from Abū Hurayrah that ʿĪsā will call the Ihlāl from the mountain highway of ar-Rawḥāʾ, for ḥajj, ʿumrah or both, and Muslim recorded it too. Aḥmad recorded another hadith of the same cast: he descends, slays the pig, smashes the cross, leads the congregation in prayer, showers wealth until none will take it, ends the jizyah, and makes for ar-Rawḥāʾ to perform ḥajj or ʿumrah, or both together. Ḥanzalah noted that Abū Hurayrah added, reciting the verse, that they will believe in ʿĪsā before ʿĪsā dies, though he could not say whether that belonged to the Prophet's hadith or was Abū Hurayrah's own word. Ibn Abī Ḥātim also recorded it. Al-Bukhārī recorded the question the Prophet ﷺ put: what will your state be when al-Masīḥ, son of Maryam, comes down among you, and the imam leading you is one of you? Imām Aḥmad and Muslim recorded it too.

Imām Aḥmad recorded the fuller description:

> The Prophets are paternal brothers. Their mothers are different, but their religion is one. I, more than any of mankind, have the right to ʿĪsā, son of Maryam, for there was no prophet between him and me. He will descend, and if you see him, know him. He is a well-built man, the color of his skin between red and white. He will descend wearing two long, light yellow garments. His head appears to be dripping water even though no moisture touched it. He will break the cross, kill the pig, banish the jizyah and call the people to Islam. Allah will destroy all religions except Islam, and Allah will destroy al-Masīḥ ad-Dajjāl, the False Messiah. Safety will fill the earth, so much so that lions will mingle with camels, tigers with cattle and wolves with sheep, and children will play with snakes, which will not harm them. ʿĪsā will remain for forty years and then die, and the Muslims will offer the funeral prayer for him.

Abū Dāwud also recorded it.

### The great confrontation

In his Ṣaḥīḥ, Muslim recorded from Abū Hurayrah, on the authority of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, that the Romans must first occupy al-Aʿmāq or Dābiq before the Hour can start. An army of the best people on earth at that time will come from al-Madīnah and challenge them. Camped face to face, the Romans will ask to fight those who captured some of their men, and the Muslims will refuse to abandon their brothers. A third of the army will flee, and those are the ones Allah will never forgive. Another third will be killed, the best martyrs before Allah. The last third will be victorious, never stricken with fitnah, and they will capture Constantinople. While dividing the spoils, swords hung on olive trees, Shayṭān will shout that al-Masīḥ, meaning ad-Dajjāl, has cornered their people. They will set out for ash-Shām, and there ad-Dajjāl will appear. As the Muslims arrange their lines and the prayer is called, ʿĪsā, son of Maryam, will descend and lead them in prayer. At the sight of him the enemy of Allah will dissolve just as salt dissolves in water, and Allah will kill him by the hand of ʿĪsā, showing the Muslims his blood on the spear.

Muslim recorded that ʿAbdullāh bin ʿAmr said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> You will fight the Jews and will kill them, until the stone says: O Muslim, there is a Jew here, so come and kill him.

And Muslim recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the Hour will not start until the Muslims fight the Jews and kill them. The Jew will hide behind a stone or a tree, and it will say: O Muslim, O servant of Allah, there is a Jew behind me, come and kill him. Except al-Gharqad, for it is a tree of the Jews.

### The hadith of an-Nawwās bin Sāmʿān

In his Ṣaḥīḥ, Muslim bin al-Ḥajjāj has the account of an-Nawwās bin Sāmʿān: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ mentioned ad-Dajjāl one day, belittling him, blind as he is yet claiming to be Allah, and speaking of him in grave terms, until the hearers thought he was hiding in the date gardens of al-Madīnah. Sensing their anxiety, he asked what was the matter, and on being told, he said:

> I fear other than ad-Dajjāl for you. If he appears while I am still among you, I will be his adversary on your behalf. If he appears while I am not among you, each one will depend on himself, and Allah will be the Helper of every Muslim after me. He is young, with very curly hair, and his eye is smashed. I thought that he looked like ʿAbd al-ʿUzza bin Qaṭan. Whoever lives long and meets him, let him recite the beginnings of sūrat al-Kahf. He will appear on a pass between ash-Shām and al-ʿIrāq, and will wreak havoc to the right and the left. O servants of Allah, hold fast.

Asked how long he would stay, he said forty days: one day like a year, one like a month, one like a week, and the rest like ordinary days. Asked whether the prayers of one day would suffice for the year-long day, he said no, but to count for their due measure. His speed on earth will be like a storm driven by the wind. He will come to a people, call them, and be believed, ordering the sky to rain and the land to grow, so their cattle return with the longest hair, the fullest udders and the fattest stomachs. He will come to another people and be rejected, leaving them to wake destitute, stripped of every possession. He will pass a deserted land and tell it to bring out its treasures, and they will follow him like swarms of bees. He will summon a man in his youth, strike him once with the sword and cut him in two, then call the dead man, who will come back radiant with pleasure and laughter. While all this is happening, Allah will send down al-Masīḥ, ʿĪsā, son of Maryam. His descent will be by the white minaret east of Damascus, dressed in garments lightly touched with saffron, both hands resting on the wings of two angels. Droplets scatter as his head dips. When he lifts it, precious stones like pearls scatter. No disbeliever can survive his breath, which reaches as far as his sight. Ad-Dajjāl he will pursue to the gates of Ludd in Palestine, and there he will kill him. A people who resisted and survived will pass by him, and he will anoint their faces and tell them their grades in Paradise.

While this happens with ʿĪsā, Allah will reveal to him that He has raised a people of His creation that no one can fight, and that he should gather His servants at at-Ṭūr, the mountain of Mūsā in Sinai. Then Gog and Magog will be raised, swarming swiftly from every mound, their front forces drinking all of Lake Tabariyyah, the last of them saying as they pass that the lake once had water. ʿĪsā and his companions will be cornered until the head of a bull is more precious to them than a hundred dinars today. Their supplication will go up, and Allah will loosen an-Naghaf, a worm, upon the necks of Gog and Magog, so that morning finds every one of them dead, like the death of a single soul. Coming down from at-Ṭūr, ʿĪsā and his companions will find no hand-span of earth free of their fat and rot, and they will supplicate again. Allah will send birds as large as the necks of camels to carry the corpses away wherever He wills, then a rain from which no house of mud or animal hair is spared, cleansing the earth until it is as clean as a mirror. Commanded to produce its fruits and regain its blessing, the earth will feed the group from a pomegranate whose skin shelters them, and the milk of one camel will suffice a large group. Then a pure wind will take the soul of every believer, and only the evildoers will remain, indulging in shameless public vice, and on them the Hour will begin.

Imām Aḥmad and the collectors of the Sunan also recorded this hadith, which explains the verse of sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ: "Until, when Yaʾjūj and Maʾjūj are let loose from their barrier" (21:96). In the year seven hundred and forty-one, a white minaret of stone was built in the Umayyad mosque of Damascus in place of one destroyed by a fire the Christians were suspected of starting, and there is a strong feeling that this is the minaret of the descent.

### The account of ʿAbdullāh bin ʿAmr

In his Ṣaḥīḥ, Muslim transmits through Yaʿqūb bin ʿĀṣim bin ʿUrwah bin Masʿūd ath-Thaqafī that ʿAbdullāh bin ʿAmr once answered a man asking about a hadith that dated the Hour. He declared the glory of Allah at the very idea, saying only that tremendous incidents would soon be witnessed, the House, the Kaʿbah, destroyed by fire, and other things besides. Then he related that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that ad-Dajjāl would appear in his nation and remain for forty, the narrator doubting whether days, months or years. Then Allah would send down ʿĪsā, son of Maryam, looking just like ʿUrwah bin Masʿūd, who would seek ad-Dajjāl and kill him. People would remain seven years with no enmity between any two. Then a cool wind from ash-Shām would take the soul of every person with even an atom's weight of good or faith, finding him even in the middle of a mountain. The evildoers left behind would be light as birds with the comprehension of beasts, knowing neither righteousness nor evil. Shayṭān would appear to them and command the worship of idols, while provision came to them in abundance and life was good. Then the Trumpet would be blown, and everyone who hears it will tilt his head to catch the distant sound. The first to hear it will be a man busy at the water pool for his camels, and he and all the people will swoon. Heavy rain will follow, and with it the bodies of the people swell and grow. The Trumpet will sound again and the people will be resurrected, staring about. It will be said: O people, come to your Lord, "but stop them, verily, they are to be questioned" (37:24). The share of the Fire will be brought forth, from every thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine. That is the day when "the children will turn grey-headed" (73:17), the day "when the Shin shall be laid bare" (68:42).

### His form described

From ʿAbdur-Raḥmān bin Adam it is narrated, on the authority of Abū Hurayrah, that the Prophet ﷺ described him for his people: whoever sees ʿĪsā should know him. a well-built man, his skin between red and white, descending in light yellow garments, his head looking as if dripping water no moisture touched. Al-Bukhārī and Muslim recorded from Abū Hurayrah the account of the night of Ascension: the Prophet ﷺ met Mūsā, tall, with hair like one of the men of the tribe of Shanuʾah, and he met ʿĪsā, of moderate height and red-faced as if he had just come out of a bath, and he saw Ibrāhīm, whom he resembled more than any of the prophet's own children did. Then al-Bukhārī has, through Mujāhid, the report of Ibn ʿUmar that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> I saw Mūsā, ʿĪsā and Ibrāhīm. ʿĪsā was of red complexion, with curly hair and a broad chest. Mūsā was of brown complexion, with straight hair and a tall stature, as if he was from the people of az-Zutt.

Al-Bukhārī and Muslim recorded that Ibrāhīm said that ʿAbdullāh bin ʿUmar related that the Prophet ﷺ once mentioned the False Messiah before the people, saying that Allah is not blind in His eye, but al-Masīḥ ad-Dajjāl is blind in his right eye, his eye like a protruding grape. Muslim recorded the dream at the Kaʿbah: a light-colored man of beautiful color, hair combed to his shoulders and dripping water, circling the House leaning on two men, and he was told: this is al-Masīḥ, son of Maryam. Behind him, a man with very curly hair, blind in the right eye, looking exactly like Ibn Qaṭan, and he was told: he is al-Masīḥ ad-Dajjāl. Al-Bukhārī recorded that Sālim reported his father as swearing that the Prophet ﷺ never called ʿĪsā red. What he related was a dream of circling the Kaʿbah: a brown man, ample-haired, pacing between two others, water dripping from his head, and he was told this was the son of Maryam. Looking behind, he saw a fat man, red in complexion, hair curled hard, the right eye blind and bulging like a grape, and was told this was ad-Dajjāl, the closest likeness being Ibn Qaṭan. Ibn Qaṭan, az-Zuhrī noted, belonged to the tribe of Khuzaʿah and had died in the days of Jāhiliyyah. So runs the wording of al-Bukhārī.

### In short

- Every soul of the People of the Book will believe in ʿĪsā as he is, whether at the useless hour of death or, as the strong reading holds, before his own death after the descent.
- The descent is fixed by an uninterrupted succession of reports: he comes as a just ruler, breaks the cross, kills the pig and ad-Dajjāl, abolishes the jizyah, and dies after forty years.
- The end-time sequence, the Romans at al-Aʿmāq, the wars with the Jews, ad-Dajjāl, Gog and Magog and the Trumpet, is mapped in the hadiths of Abū Hurayrah, ʿAbdullāh bin ʿAmr and an-Nawwās bin Sāmʿān.
- On the Day of Judgment he is a witness, not an accused: he delivered the message, and he is but a servant of Allah.
