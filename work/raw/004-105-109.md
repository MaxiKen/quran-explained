<<<VERSE 4:105>>>
The judge and the Book: "Indeed, We have sent down the Book to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth to judge between people by means of what Allah has shown you. So do not be an advocate for the deceitful" (4:105). The verse sets the standard of judgement and then guards the judge against the pull of loyalty. Behind it stands a case of theft that nearly went the wrong way.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We have sent down the Book to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth"** means that the Book truly came from Allah, every narration and every commandment of it. Gabriel, in the idiom of one rendering, brought it down to show the true from the false. The passage revealed over this incident runs all the way to the word "Whoso ascribeth partners unto Allah hath wandered far astray" (4:116).

**"to judge between people by means of what Allah has shown you"** means judging by what Allah taught and showed in the Qur'ān, not according to whims and fancy. The inward reading hears the precision. The command is not to judge between them blindly, but by what He unveiled from the lights of insight, until the matter is understood by His teaching and His directing. The same holds for those of the community who judge with truth.

The office is human, and the Prophet ﷺ said so himself. In the Two Ṣaḥīḥs, Zaynab bint Umm Salamah relates from Umm Salamah the day the noise of disputing people rose close to the door of his room. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ went out to them saying:

> Verily, I am only human and I judge based on what I hear. Some of you might be more eloquent in presenting his case than others, so that I judge in his favour. If I judge in one's favour concerning the right of another Muslim, then it is a piece of the Fire. So let one take it or leave it.

Imām Aḥmad recorded from Umm Salamah the case of two men of the Anṣār who came with a dispute regarding some old inheritance, without evidence. He told them that they brought their disputes to a man who was only human. Some are more persuasive than others, and he judges only by what he hears:

> Whomever I judge in his favour and give him a part of his brother's right, let him not take it, for it is a part of the Fire that I am giving him, and it will be tied around his neck on the Day of Resurrection.

The two men wept, and each forfeited his right to his brother. He then told them to go, divide the inheritance, try to be just in the division, draw lots, and each forgive his brother thereafter.

**"So do not be an advocate for the deceitful"** names the case. Tuʿmah ibn ʿUbayriq, of Banū Ẓafar ibn al-Ḥārith, one of the Helpers, stole a coat of mail from his neighbour Qatādah ibn an-Nuʿmān. The mail lay in a sack of ground wheat, and as he took it, wheat spilled from a hole and left traces in his house. He hid the mail with a Jew named Zayd ibn al-Samin. Searched, his house yielded nothing, and he swore by Allah that he had not taken it and knew nothing of it. The owners followed the trail of ground wheat to the Jew's house. The Jew said that it was Tuʿmah who had left the armour with him, and Jewish witnesses testified to it. The clan then came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and asked him to argue for their man. Otherwise, they said, their man was doomed and exposed, and the Jew would come out innocent. He was on the verge of doing as requested, for his heart was with them, and of punishing the Jew, when Allah revealed the verse.

### The pull of the clan

The warning reaches everyone who judges. The context here is the hypocrites. While the passage dealt with the open disbelievers, the Qur'ān turned to the hypocrites, whose kufr was the common denominator with them, and a particular episode of theirs now begins. Behind the episode lies the standing temptation of communal bias, the mentality that says my community right or wrong. A man of one's own circle is supported even in error, while the outsider receives nothing though right. The command of Allah is the standard instead, and whoever errs is checked without prejudice, and whoever stands for truth is supported whatever his group.

The inward reading turns the warning inward. Do not defend the masters of worldly concerns, but be with the children of Allah's rights. The worshipper of whim betrays the taqwā his soul was given in trust. The one who banks on the pull of the lower desires betrays the shame owed before the eyes of the Protector.

### In short

- The Book came in truth, and judgement runs by what Allah shows, never by eloquence or partiality.
- The Prophet ﷺ judged by what he heard, and a wrongly awarded right is a piece of Fire hung around the neck.
- Tuʿmah ibn ʿUbayriq's theft of the mail, hidden with Zayd ibn al-Samin, nearly took the Jew's hand, until revelation set the case upright.
- Community loyalty is no standard of right, and the master of worldly concerns has no defence.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:106>>>
The word to the judge: "And seek Allah's forgiveness" (4:106), "indeed, Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful". The verse stands between the standard and the prohibition, and it holds the story that gives all five verses their setting.

### The episode of Banū ʿUbayriq

The nine verses from 105 to 113 belong to one episode, and in the characteristic style of the Qur'ān the instructions are not peculiar to it. They form a set of basic principles beneficial to Muslims of every time. The episode centres on the clan of Banū ʿUbayriq in Madinah. A man of the clan broke into the house of Rifāʿah, the uncle of Qatādah ibn an-Nuʿmān, and committed a theft. His name prints as Bashīr in the narration of Tirmidhī and Ḥākim, and as Tuʿmah in that of al-Bāghawī and Ibn Jarīr. The Tirmidhī narration adds that the man was in reality a hypocrite. Living in Madinah, he was yet audacious enough to compose poetic satires insulting the noble Companions, publicising them in the names of others with his own identity concealed.

The theft belonged to the early period of the hijrah, when the Muslims lived straitened, facing need, hunger and deprivation. Their common food was barley bread or dates. Wheat flour was scarce, hardly available in Madinah, coming only occasionally from Syria, whereupon some would buy it for guests or a special need. Rifāʿah bought some wheat flour, put it in a bag, laid arms on top and stashed it in a small room of the house. Ibn ʿUbayriq came to know, broke in and made off with the bag. Morning brought the discovery, and Rifāʿah went with his nephew Qatādah to investigate in the locality. The light of a fire had been seen at the Banū ʿUbayriq house in the night, people said, probably for cooking the stolen goods. When Banū ʿUbayriq learned their secret was out, they came charging that the thief was Labīd ibn Sahl, a man well known as a sincere and pious Muslim. Labīd came out of his house with his sword in hand. The blade, he declared, would not return to its sheath until the truth of the theft became clear. The clan quietly assured him that nobody was blaming him and that this was not the sort of thing he would do. The narration of al-Bāghawī and Ibn Jarīr then has the clan charging a Jew with the theft. Their clever work was a trail of seemingly falling flour, trickling from a slit in the bag, from Rifāʿah's house all the way to the Jew's, and the stolen arms and armours were carried to the same man for safe keeping. The goods were recovered from his house in the investigation, and the Jew declared on oath that they had been given to him by Ibn ʿUbayriq. To harmonise the two narrations, the clan likely charged Labīd first, and when the ruse failed, targeted the Jew.

The circumstantial evidence convinced Qatādah and Rifāʿah overwhelmingly that this was the work of Banū ʿUbayriq, and Qatādah carried the theft and his strong suspicion to the Prophet ﷺ. Banū ʿUbayriq came too, complaining that Qatādah and Rifāʿah blamed them without valid legal proof, despite the property found in the Jew's house. Their appeal to the Prophet ﷺ was to restrain the two, failing which the suit should go to the Jew. The outside appearance inclined even the Prophet ﷺ toward the probability that the theft was the Jew's work. So far, according to al-Bāghawī, that he intended to inflict on the Jew the punishment of theft and sever his hand. Matters worsened when Qatādah appeared before him and was admonished for accusing a Muslim family without proof. Qatādah was grieved, wishing he had never spoken even at the loss of his property. Rifāʿah, hearing what had been said, exercised patience with the words that from Allah alone is all help sought.

Not much time passed before a whole section of the Qur'ān came down about the episode. The reality was shown to the Prophet ﷺ, general instructions were given for such cases, the theft of Banū ʿUbayriq stood exposed and the Jew acquitted. They had no choice but to return the stolen property, which the Prophet ﷺ had restored to Rifāʿah, and Rifāʿah finally endowed all those arms for use in jihād. When the exposure came, Bashīr the son of ʿUbayriq ran from Madinah and landed in Makkah among the disbelievers. A hypocrite before, he became an acknowledged disbeliever, and if he had been a Muslim, an apostate now. The curse of his antagonism to Allah and His Messenger gave him no peace even there. The woman at whose house he had taken residence, learning of the episode, threw him out. He came to loitering in the streets until the day he broke into yet another house, where the wall he went in by collapsed and killed him, as al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ records.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And seek Allah's forgiveness"** asks the Prophet ﷺ to seek forgiveness, and the reason lies in the station. The obvious circumstances and evidence were such that a tilt toward the Jew's probable guilt was no sin, yet it stood contrary to the actual fact. The station of prophets is very high, and from them Allah does not favour the issuance of even something so insignificant. One rendering reads it as repentance for being on the verge of striking the Jew, Zayd ibn al-Samin. The gloss adds that Allah is Forgiving of the transgression about to be committed, Merciful toward him.

**"indeed, Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"** keeps the door open behind the correction. The inward reading widens it: pray for forgiveness for your community, for the word already suffices about the forgiveness of what is past, "that Allah may forgive you what is past of your sin" (48:2).

### In short

- One theft, a bag of Syrian wheat, arms and a forged trail of flour, ran through nine verses of the Book.
- Labīd ibn Sahl was the first false target, then the Jew, and the Prophet ﷺ nearly ruled by appearances before revelation corrected the case.
- The clan's own exposure cost them the property, and Bashīr died under a collapsing wall in Makkah, an apostate among disbelievers.
- Even an inclination short of sin, in a prophet's station, calls for seeking forgiveness, for Allah does not favour the least departure from truth in them.

<<<VERSE 4:107>>>
The prohibition repeated: "Do not advocate for those who wrong themselves. Surely Allah does not like those who are deceitful, sinful" (4:107). The emphatic assertion returns so that the lesson of the episode becomes a standing law.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not advocate for those who wrong themselves"** forbids pleading for people who have betrayed themselves by disobedience, whose betrayal falls back on their own heads. The rule for every judge follows: do not argue for a person who commits a sin and denies it, until more is known of the matter.

**"Surely Allah does not like those who are deceitful, sinful"** defines the pair. Treacherous through the stealing itself, sinful through the lying oath and the false accusation of an innocent. The one Allah does not love, that is to say, is the one He will punish..

The teaching beneath runs to the conscience. Abandoning the truth is like abandoning oneself, for a man is dishonest to himself before he is dishonest to anyone else. In every heart Allah has implanted a conscience as His representative, and whoever intends to go against the truth is stopped by this invisible representative, so that the inner voice must first be suppressed before justice is traded for injustice. All too often he supports some erring person because of the importance attached to him, and social relations and expediencies lead him to turn a blind eye. Refusing to keep apart from a follower of the path of untruth costs a person his nearness to Allah, and whoever supports untruth for the esteem of men in this world loses Allah's company in the Hereafter.

The inward reading gives the same verdict in its own terms. Those who prefer their worldly concerns over His rights, and are content in the familiar territories of whim without moving to the waystations of contentment, are not loved by Allah. He will humble them and most assuredly will not honour them.

### In short

- The prohibition stands: no defence for those who wrong themselves, and no argument for a sinner who denies.
- Deceit and sin pair as the theft and the lying oath paired.
- The conscience must first be silenced before injustice is done, and that first silencing is the real betrayal.
- Worldly concerns preferred over rights end in humiliation, not honour.

<<<VERSE 4:108>>>
The strange shyness: "They try to hide ˹their deception˺ from people, but they can never hide it from Allah" (4:108). The verse studies the psychology of the deceitful and finds it upside down.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They try to hide ˹their deception˺ from people"** chastises the hypocrites for hiding their evil works from the people so as not to be criticised. They feel shy before other people like themselves and conceal the theft from them.

**"but they can never hide it from Allah"** exposes the inversion. Before Allah the evil lies disclosed, under His perfect watch over their secrets and His knowledge of their hearts, yet before Him they feel no shame at all.

**"in Whose presence they plot by night what is displeasing to Him"** means His knowledge attends their night plotting and its words, the secret plan to say the things He is not pleased with, their resolve being to swear by Allah, deny the theft and accuse the Jew.. The shyness is strange: shy before men, and never before Allah, who is with them always and watches everything they do, above all when they conspired together to fix the blame on the Jew, then came to the Prophet ﷺ complaining against Rifāʿah and Qatādah, charging false accusation, and appealing for support against the Jew.

**"And Allah is Fully Aware of what they do"** closes the file. He encompasses what they do, secretly and openly, nothing hidden from Him, and He will repay them for their actions.

The inward reading marks the divide. What created beings can see or not see dominates such hearts, and the ones branded with separation have no awareness that the Real looks down upon their hearts.

### In short

- Shyness before people and none before Allah is the deceitful heart turned inside out.
- The night plotting, the oath, and the framing of the Jew all stood in Allah's presence.
- He is Fully Aware and encompassing, and the repayment is certain.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:109>>>
The advocate out of office: "Here you are! You ˹believers˺ are advocating for them in this life, but who will ˹dare to˺ advocate for them before Allah on the Day of Judgment? Or who will come to their defence" (4:109). The verse moves the courtroom to the Hereafter.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Here you are! You ˹believers˺ are advocating for them in this life"** addresses the clan of Tuʿmah, the Banū Ẓufr in one gloss, who contested the case, that is to say, on behalf of Tuʿmah and his men, in the life of this world.. Suppose such people gain the verdict from the rulers in their favour, since rulers judge according to what is apparent to them.

**"but who will ˹dare to˺ advocate for them before Allah on the Day of Judgment?"** asks the question no one can answer. Before Allah, who knows the secret and what is yet more hidden, what will their condition be? Who will stand as their advocate on that Day? None will support them then.

**"Or who will come to their defence"** asks for a guardian to take charge of their affair, and the silence answers: no one.

The verse works twice over, admonishing them and holding the hazards of the Hereafter before their eyes, so as to persuade them to repent and turn to Allah. The inward reading hears the address to the Prophet ﷺ in it: out of respect for you, Muḥammad, they are defended, since you are among them. But how will they stand on the Day of Resurrection, once your blessing, O believers, has been taken from them?

The wider frame is the trial of this world. Everyone may commit mistakes here, toward Allah and toward His servants, and when someone errs, the right course is sincere repentance, seeking Allah's guidance, begging forgiveness, and praying for assistance to do only virtuous deeds. One who seeks Allah's shelter so sincerely will certainly receive it. But the one who does wrong and will not admit it, justifying himself, gathering supporters and fighting those who merely informed him of the mistake, stands with his supporters among the worst criminals in the eyes of Allah, whose eloquence will become meaningless and whose host of supporters will be of no avail in the Hereafter.

### In short

- Verdicts won in this world rest on appearances, and the Day of Judgement rests on the secret and what is more hidden.
- No advocate, no guardian, no defender stands for the deceitful before Allah.
- The verse admonishes and invites repentance, for the advocate of this life is withdrawn in the next.
