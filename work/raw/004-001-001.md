<<<VERSE 4:1>>>
"O humanity! Be mindful of your Lord Who created you from a single soul, and from it He created its mate, and through both He spread countless men and women. And be mindful of Allah—in Whose Name you appeal to one another—and ˹honour˺ family ties. Surely Allah is ever Watchful over you." This verse opens Surah An-Nisa and sets its whole tone. It commands all human beings, men and women, those alive then and those to come until the Day of Judgment, to be mindful of their Lord, and it grounds that command in creation: every human being comes from one soul, Adam, and from Adam's mate, Hawwa (Eve), and from the two of them the whole of humanity spread. Because they share one origin, people are brothers and sisters, and no distinction of race, colour, language or caste may be made the measure of worth. The verse then turns the same command inward: people demand their rights from one another in Allah's name, so the one who expects others to fear Allah must fear Him himself, and the closing words warn against cutting family ties. It ends with the reminder that Allah is always watching.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O humanity!"** addresses all human beings without distinction, men and women, those present at the revelation and those to be born until the Day of Judgment.

**"Be mindful of your Lord"** is the command ittaqu, to fear acting against your Lord. "Lord" (Rabb) was chosen from among Allah's names because He is the One who nurtures and sustains, the One whose lordship a person witnesses at every moment. The thought of rising in antagonism against Him is terribly dangerous. The fear of Allah so commanded has its own justification and wisdom.

**"Who created you from a single soul"** means from Adam alone, Eve's soul being included in his. Allah created all human beings from one person, Adam, peace be unto him, in His wisdom and mercy. It was possible to create them in more than one way, but He chose this one way, tying all of them in a strong bond of brotherhood.

**"And from it He created its mate"** refers to Hawwa (Eve), created from Adam's left rib, from his back while he slept. When Adam woke and saw her he was drawn to her and she to him.

**"And through both He spread countless men and women"** means Allah produced many men and women from Adam and Hawwa and scattered them across the world in varied shapes, colours and languages. In the end their gathering and return is to Allah.

**"And be mindful of Allah"** repeats the command, now with the name Allah, and shows that the surah's laws are not given in cold print like the laws of governments but combined with the training of minds and hearts.

**"In Whose Name you appeal to one another"** refers to the way people demand their rights from others by saying "I ask you by Allah." One who expects others to fear Allah must fear Him himself.

**"And honour family ties"** translates al-arham, the plural of rahm, "womb." The womb is where blood relationship comes from, so maintaining that bond is ilat ar-rahm and severing it is qat ar-rahm. The command is not to cut relations with one's kin but to keep and honour them. Ibrahim, Mujahid and Al-Hasan took the mutual-rights phrase in this sense, while Ad-Dahhak said it means fearing Allah when conducting transactions and contracts, and that Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Ad-Dahhak, Ar-Rabi and others took the command to honour the womb as a command not to cut family ties.

**"Surely Allah is ever Watchful over you"** means He watches all your deeds and sees every circumstance, including the intentions and motives of the heart. Doing things half-heartedly, or only for fear of embarrassment, is not accepted. Allah's watchfulness is echoed in "And Allah is Witness over all things" (58:6). The people of Makkah were addressed here, and they used to implore one another by ties of kinship, asking "I ask you by Allah" or "For God's sake."

### Why taqwa opens the surah

The last verse of Surah Al-Imran ended with an exhortation to taqwa, the fear of Allah. Here taqwa appears as a command. The previous surah had spoken of battles, of the treatment of opponents, and of misappropriation of spoils. This surah turns to the rights a person owes to those closest to him: orphans, relatives, wives. Some of these rights can be enforced by law, like buying and selling, lease and wages. Others, the rights of children, parents, husband and wife, and of relatives toward one another, depend on civilised behaviour, respect, love and care, and above all on a genuine desire to do good. Such things cannot be weighed or settled by agreement, so the only way to secure them is taqwa, the fear of Allah and of what comes in the life to come. Taqwa, on this view, is more effective than government and law together. The verse serves as an introduction to the injunctions that follow: it dissuades people from usurping divine rights while, by telling them they are children of the same father, it gives love, mutual sympathy and concern as the working basis of common life, so that the rights of relatives, orphans and married couples may be fulfilled from the heart.

### The bond of brotherhood

All human beings are one by birth and can trace their origin to the same man and woman as father and mother. This unity grows tighter in families, and decent behaviour among kin matters more. Good behaviour between fellow human beings is important not only morally but as a matter of personal concern, because everyone is governed by Allah, who as Reckoner for all will decide the eternal future of everyone in the Hereafter, requiting them according to their actions. A person should not treat his dealings with others as merely human business but as a matter between himself and God. He should fear the grip of God and adhere to the bounds set by God. A report says God proclaimed, "I shall associate Myself with one who strengthens the ties of kinship and detach Myself from one who severs them." This shows that attachment to God is tested through dealings with others: fearing God while honouring others' rights is true veneration, and loving people is loving their Creator. Fair dealing and compassion are general social duties, yet so vital to family life that they rank second only to God. Allah watches over a person and nothing he does escapes Him. Rather, He takes it into account and repays accordingly.

### The hadiths about kinship

Several hadiths develop the command. The Prophet said, "Whoever likes to have his livelihood made plentiful and his age extended for him should maintain good relations with his near of kin." Abdullah ibn Salam reported that when the Prophet came to Madinah al-Tayyibah the first words he heard were: "Make a habit of greeting one another with salam, feeding people for Allah's pleasure, treating near relations well, and praying by night while others sleep, and you will enter Paradise in peace." Maymunah, the Mother of the Faithful, freed a bondwoman and was told that giving her to her maternal uncle would have brought a greater reward, and the Prophet said that charity to the needy is charity while charity to a near relative is twofold: charity and kinship. He also warned: "A breaker of blood relationships shall not enter Paradise," and "Mercy shall not descend upon a people among whom there is a breaker of blood relationships." A hadith about the rib says that woman was created from a rib, and that the most curved part of the rib is its upper part, so trying to straighten it breaks it while leaving it keeps it crooked. Another authentic hadith says, "Worship Allah as if you see Him, for even though you cannot see Him, He sees you."

### The marriage sermon and charity

The word ittaqu, translated here as "fear," means "fear acting against your Lord," and this is perhaps why the Prophet used to recite this verse as part of his address when solemnising a marriage. Reciting it in the khutbah of nikah (the marriage sermon) is a perennial revival of the sunnah (the Prophet's practice). The verse was also recited to a delegation from Mudar who came wearing striped woollen clothes out of poverty: after the Zuhr prayer the Prophet stood and gave a speech in which he recited "O mankind! Have taqwa of your Lord, Who created you from a single person" to the end of the verse, and also "O you who believe! Have taqwa of Allah. And let every person look to what he has sent forth for the tomorrow" (59:18), and encouraged them to give sadaqah (charity), saying, "A man gave sadaqah from his dinar, from his dirham, from his sa of wheat, from his sa of dates." Muslim recorded this, and Ahmad and the collectors of the sunan recorded it from Ibn Masud. Jarir bin Abdullah Al-Bajali reported it.

### The inner meaning of godwariness

The verse calls for making godwariness (taqwa) one's shelter. Godwariness means taking the commands of the shariah as a shield, so that the arrows of prohibition do not reach the servant. It has three levels: first, taking refuge in the sentence of tawhid (the oneness of Allah) and avoiding every associationism. Second, taking refuge in obedience and stepping away from the path of disobedience. Third, taking refuge in caution and fleeing from ambiguity. Whoever climbs these way stations of godwariness truthfully will reach deliverance without fail, for the Qur'an says, "God will deliver those who were godwary" (39:61), and "Whosoever is wary of God, He will appoint a way out for him and He will provide for him from whence he never reckoned" (65:2-3). Whenever someone takes hold of godwariness, God will ease his deliverance from every suffering and send provision from where he has no hope.

The story is told of the sister of Bishr Hafi, who went to see Ahmad Hanbal and asked whether it was permissible to work her spindle on the roof by the light of the Tahirids' torch. Ahmad asked whether she could tolerate such godwariness, and when she said she was the sister of Bishr Hafi he wept and said that godwariness like that was permissible only in the household of Bishr Hafi, and that she should emulate her brother. A report is quoted in which MuṣṬafā, narrating from his Lord, says that when He knows that what dominates over His servant's heart is being occupied with Him, He puts the servant's appetite into supplicating Him and conversing with Him, and that when the servant desires to be negligent of Him, He comes between him and his negligence. "Those are my friends in truth. Those are the champions." He is saying that when the servant wants only Him, knows only Him and belongs only to Him, He turns the face of His heart toward him, shuts the door of all desires and appetites, and gives him ease on the carpet of passion, holding the sword of beginningless jealousy over his head so that he may not look at another. "Today I will entrust him to the policeman of godwariness so that I may keep him under the protection of My shariah." Tomorrow he will rest in the seat of truthfulness (54:55) before At-ness, for "the godwary will be in the midst of gardens and a river, in a seat of truthfulness, at an Omnipotent King" (54:54-55).

The verse also speaks of creation as a pair. The Lord who created everything as a pair made a spouse appear for everyone, joining each with a likeness, for unity and solitariness are an attribute specific to Him. It has been narrated in one of the books, "I have given the things mates so that My unity will be inferred." And from the offspring of one upright individual God brought forth many thousands of creatures with diverse natures and colours, forms and conducts: each has another colour, nature, character, state and aspiration, and no two people are alike in nature and visage. "So glory be to Him whose predetermined things have no end and whose objects of knowledge have no limit!" The watcher is the One who hears hearts without asking, who knows deeds without inquiry, and who never tires in striving. This is an admonishment of the servant: since he knows that God gives ear to hearts and keeps His eyes on deeds and words, he should put watchfulness to work and carry out God's rightful due. Watchfulness means the servant keeps his heart turned toward the Real and the Real's gaze before his eyes. Hence MuṣṬafā said, "When you do not like people seeing something from you, do not do it while you are alone." In this meaning they have sung, "When you are alone with yourself one day, do not say, 'I'm alone.' Say, 'Over me is a Watcher.'"

### The virtues of the surah

Ibn Abbas said, "Ask me about Surah An-Nisa, for I learned the Qur'an when I was still young," and Al-Hakim graded it sahih by the standards of the two Sahihs, though neither collected it. Al-Hakim recorded in his Mustadrak that Ibn Masud said five verses in Surah An-Nisa outweigh the world and all it contains: "Surely, Allah wrongs not even the weight of an atom" (4:40). "If you avoid the great sins which you are forbidden to do" (4:31). "Verily, Allah forgives not that partners should be set up with Him, but He forgives except that to whom He wills" (4:48). "If they, when they had been unjust to themselves, had come to you" (4:64), and "And whoever does evil or wrongs himself, but afterwards seeks Allah's forgiveness, he will find Allah Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful" (4:110). Al-Awfi reported Ibn Abbas as saying the surah was revealed in Al-Madinah, and Ibn Marduwyah recorded like statements from Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr and Zayd bin Thabit. The surah is Madinan.

### In short

- The verse commands all humanity to be mindful of their Lord and grounds the command in creation: all come from Adam and Hawwa, so all are brothers and sisters.
- It warns against cutting family ties and reminds that people who invoke Allah's name in their dealings must fear Him themselves.
- It closes with the reminder that Allah is always watching, which is the reason to fear Him.
- The material adds a long spiritual reading of godwariness, its three levels, and the watchfulness of God over the servant.
- The surah's virtues include five verses preferred by Ibn Masud and Ibn Abbas's claim to know it well.
