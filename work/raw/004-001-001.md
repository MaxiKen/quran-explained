<<<VERSE 4:1>>>
The surah opens with a call to every human being: "O humanity! Be mindful of your Lord Who created you from a single soul, and from it He created its mate, and through both He spread countless men and women." The call then turns to what people owe to Allah and to one another: "And be mindful of Allah—in Whose Name you appeal to one another—and ˹honour˺ family ties. Surely Allah is ever Watchful over you."

This one verse is the doorway to the whole surah. It does two things at once. It warns people against seizing for themselves what belongs to Allah alone, and it reminds them that they are all children of one father and one mother. That second fact gives life together a warm footing of love, sympathy and concern, so that the rights of relatives, orphans and married couples are met from the heart and not only on paper. The verse that follows turns straight to orphans and the protection of their property.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O humanity!"** Ibn ʿAbbās explained that this wording is general, although it can also be meant for one particular group. One explanation takes it as a call to the people of Makkah, who were the first to hear it. The wider reading is that it takes in every human being, men and women alike, whether alive when the Qur'an came down or born at any time until the Day of Judgment. It is addressed to the human race, not only to the believers.

**"Be mindful of your Lord"** translates ittaqū rabbakum, a command built on taqwā (being mindful of Allah), the word that runs through this verse and through the whole surah. It is often rendered "fear", and it means to fear acting against your Lord. Allah commands His creatures to be mindful of Him by worshipping Him alone, with no partner. Some scholars put it in a few words: obey your Lord. Others said it means to be mindful of His punishment by being obedient to Him. This may be why the Prophet ﷺ used to recite this verse in the address he gave at a wedding. Reciting it in the khuṭbah (sermon) of the nikāḥ (marriage) therefore keeps his Sunnah, his way, alive.

The verse could have used any of Allah's most beautiful names. It chose Rabb, Lord, the One who takes full responsibility for nurturing human beings. People see the marks of that lordship at every moment of their lives. A Being who looks after them so completely deserves all the awe they are able to feel. So the command carries its own wisdom and reason, and the very thought that anyone could rise against such a Lord in defiance is terribly dangerous.

**"Who created you from a single soul"** reminds people of Allah's ability: He made all of them from one person, Ādam (Adam). He could have brought them into being in more than one way. He chose one particular way, a single man as the origin of everyone, and by doing so tied all people together in a strong bond of brotherhood. Ibn ʿAbbās added that people were created through the union of a man and a woman, and that the "single soul" is Ādam's alone, since Ḥawwāʾ's soul was included in his.

**"and from it He created its mate"**. The mate is Ḥawwāʾ (Eve), Ādam's wife. She was created from his left rib, taken from his back while he slept. When Ādam woke and saw her, he was pleased with her and grew fond of her, and the feeling was mutual. An authentic hadith says:

> "Woman was created from a rib. The most curved part of a rib is its upper part. If you try to straighten it, you will break it, and if you leave it alone, it will stay crooked."

A short explanation calls her the mother of all people, as Ādam is their father.

**"and through both He spread countless men and women"**. From Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ, Allah created many men and women and scattered them across the world. He made them different in shape, character, colour and language. In the end they will all be gathered, and their return will be to Allah. Other explanations use the words "separated", "spread" and "all over the earth", and speak of scores of human beings, male and female.

**"And be mindful of Allah—in Whose Name you appeal to one another—"**. The command is repeated, this time with the name Allah, and the next words say how people use that name. The Arabic is tasāʾalūna bihi. Ibrāhīm, Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan said it refers to the habit of saying to someone, "I ask you by Allah," and then, "and by the tie of the womb between us." Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk said it means: be mindful of Allah, the One you call on when you make deals and contracts. Another explanation says people claim their goods and their rights from one another by appealing to Allah's right. To be mindful of Allah here means to protect yourself from Him by obeying Him.

On grammar, the verb is written tassāʾalūna. It stands for tatasāʾalūna, with one tā' merged into the sīn. A variant reading is tasāʾalūna, in which the sīn is not doubled. People also need to hear the point behind the wording. They demand their rights from others in Allah's name and press for what they want. Whoever expects others to fear Allah must therefore fear Him himself.

**"and ˹honour˺ family ties"** translates wal-arḥām, literally "and the wombs". Arḥām is the plural of raḥm, the womb. A mother's womb is the child's home until birth, and it is where blood relationship begins. Keeping up the relationship that comes through it is called ṣilat ar-raḥm, which literally means an umbilical link or bond. Being careless and indifferent toward one's blood relations is qaṭʿ ar-raḥm, cutting that bond, which means cutting off one's kin.

Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrimah, Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, ar-Rabīʿ and others said that the command is to revere the womb by not cutting its ties, but to keep them and honour them. Shorter explanations say the same: be mindful of breaking relations with your blood relatives, or keep the ties of kinship and do not sever them. There is also a second sense, that people claim their rights from one another by the right of kinship and descent.

Another reading puts a different vowel on the last word and joins it to the pronoun in "by Him". The sense is then: be mindful of Allah, in whose name you appeal to one another, and in the name of the wombs. This fits the habit of the time, since people used to implore one another by ties of kinship. The last word of the verse is a warning in any case. It says that any failure to look after close relatives, on the father's side or the mother's, must be avoided.

**"Surely Allah is ever Watchful over you"**. The Arabic word is raqīb, one who watches. Allah watches everything you do and sees how you stand in every circumstance. Another verse says: "For Allah is a Witness over all things" (58:6). An authentic hadith says:

> "Worship Allah as if you see Him, for even though you cannot see Him, He sees you."

This part of the verse encourages a firm certainty that Allah is watching, completely and perfectly. Other explanations call Him a Protector who will question you about what He commanded, in the way of obedience and keeping the ties of kinship. Others say He is heedful of your deeds and will repay you for them, and that nothing you do escapes Him. The words "is ever" show that this is an attribute He always has.

### One father, one mother, and no high or low

All human beings are one by birth. Every person traces back to the same man and the same woman, as father and mother. So people should feel close to one another and deal with each other fairly and kindly, as one large family would. Because of this shared origin, there must be no one high and no one low in race or caste, in colour or language. None of these differences may be made the measure of good or bad, of noble or mean.

Allah also gave a reason for making mankind from a single father and a single mother. It was so that they would feel compassion for one another and be kind to the weaker among them.

This unity of descent grows tighter inside the family. For that reason decent behaviour among relatives matters even more. The verse is therefore not only about faith in the abstract. It asks a person to carry out the rights of humanity, which are mutual sympathy, well-being and the good of everyone, and it asks this on top of the fear of Allah and the fear of the Hereafter.

### Dealings with people are dealings with Allah

Good behaviour toward others is not only a moral point. It is also a matter of personal concern, because everyone is governed by the Almighty and Reckoner, who will decide the eternal future of every human being in the Hereafter and will repay people for what they did in this world. A person should not treat his dealings with others as business between one human being and another. They are business between him and Allah. He should fear Allah's grip and keep inside the limits Allah has set, so as to be saved from His anger.

A hadith reports that Allah said:

> "I will join Myself to the one who strengthens the ties of kinship, and I will separate Myself from the one who severs them."

This shows that a person's attachment to Allah is tested by his relations with other people. Whoever is in fear of Allah when he deals with the rights of others truly reveres Him, and whoever loves other human beings truly loves his Creator. Fair dealing and compassion are basic requirements of any human society. They matter so much to family relations that they rank next to the duty owed to Allah Himself.

### How the Prophet ﷺ used this verse

Muslim recorded in his Ṣaḥīḥ that Jarīr ibn ʿAbdullāh al-Bajalī said a delegation from Muḍar came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He saw how they were dressed, in striped woollen clothes, a sign of their poverty. After the Zuhr prayer the Prophet ﷺ stood and gave a speech, in which he recited this verse to its end. He also recited: "O believers! Be mindful of Allah and let every soul look to what ˹deeds˺ it has sent forth for tomorrow" (59:18). Then he urged them to give charity. He spoke of a man who gave ṣadaqah (charity) from his dīnār, from his dirham, from his ṣāʿ (a measure) of wheat and from his ṣāʿ of dates, and so on to the end of the hadith. Aḥmad and the Sunan compilers also collected this narration, from Ibn Masʿūd.

### Keeping the ties of kinship

The hadiths put great weight on the bonds of kinship. The Mishkāt collection records several. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Whoever wants his provision to be made wide and his life to be lengthened should keep good relations with his relatives."

This hadith names two benefits. Fair treatment of one's kin brings a reward in the Hereafter, and it also helps in this life, by easing the narrowness of one's provision and by blessing one's years with more good.

ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām said that when the Prophet ﷺ came to Madīnah al-Ṭayyibah and he presented himself, the first words he heard from him were:

> "O people, greet one another with salām, feed others, treat your relatives well, and pray at night while people sleep. You will enter Paradise in peace."

Maymūnah, Mother of the Believers, once freed a bondwoman and told the Prophet ﷺ. He said, "Had you given her to your maternal uncle, your reward would have been greater." Islam urges people to free slaves and counts it among the finest of good deeds. Even so, the standing of kinship was rated higher.

He also said:

> "Charity to a needy person is only charity, but charity to a relative is twofold: it is charity and it is kinship."

A simple change in where a gift goes thus earns two kinds of merit.

The opposite attitude is cold-shouldering and severing blood relations. The warnings against it are stern. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "One who breaks the ties of blood will not enter Paradise."

and:

> "Mercy does not descend on a people among whom there is someone who breaks the ties of blood."

### What Allah's watchfulness asks of a person

The closing words move hearts to give people their rights when they fall due. Allah's watchfulness means He knows whatever is in a person's heart: his intentions, his hesitations and his motives. Doing things half-heartedly, as a formality, or from fear of embarrassment, with no real wish to serve, remains unacceptable to Allah. Here too we find why Allah should be feared: because He watches over everyone, always.

This is how the Qur'an usually teaches. It does not set out its laws in sheer cold print, as the governments of this world do. It gives them as education, training and affection, joining the rule to the shaping of minds and hearts.

### The inner reading: mindfulness as a shield

A call comes to the one who stands at the centre of human nature and carries the mark of mortality: take mindfulness as your shelter and cling to it. The life of the servants is in it, and the safe arrival of the travellers.

Mindfulness here means that the servant makes the commands of the Sharīʿah (the revealed law) his shield, so that the arrows of prohibition cannot reach him. There are three levels. At the first, he shelters in the declaration of tawḥīd, the oneness of Allah, and avoids every kind of associating partners with Him. At the second, he shelters in obedience and leaves the path of disobedience behind. At the third, he shelters in caution and runs away from whatever is doubtful. Anyone who climbs these stations truthfully will certainly reach deliverance. The Qur'an says so: "And Allah will deliver those who were mindful ˹of Him˺ to their place of ˹ultimate˺ triumph" (39:61). Elsewhere it says: "And whoever is mindful of Allah, He will make a way out for them and provide for them from sources they could never imagine" (65:2-3). To whoever takes hold of mindfulness, Allah makes easy the road out of every suffering and sends provision from where he had no hope.

### The sister of Bishr Ḥāfī and the spindle

A story shows how far this carefulness can go. The sister of Bishr Ḥāfī went to see Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal. She said, "O Imam of the Muslims! I spin thread on the roof of my house. When the torch of the Ṭāhirids passes by, it may happen that I join threads by the light of that torch. Is this permissible or not?"

Aḥmad asked her first to say who she was. He wanted to know whether she was able to bear such mindfulness. She told him she was the sister of Bishr Ḥāfī. Aḥmad wept. He said this kind of scrupulousness was permissible only in the household of Bishr Ḥāfī, and that it was not fitting for her to do it, so she should be careful not to. Bishr would be happy with her. She should follow her brother's example, in the hope of becoming like him. Then, if she wanted to spin by the glow of the Ṭāhirids' torch, her hand would not obey her. Her brother had reached a degree where his hand refused to obey him whenever it moved toward food that was doubtful in any way.

### When the servant wants only Allah

Allah says that when the servant wants to be negligent of Him, He comes between the servant and his negligence. This is in the report in which Muṣṭafā, the Prophet ﷺ, narrates from his Lord:

> "When I know that what rules my servant's heart is being occupied with Me, I put his appetite into asking Me and conversing with Me. When My servant is like that, he has passion for Me, and I have passion for him. When he is like that and wants to be negligent of Me, I come between him and his negligence. These are My friends in truth. They are the champions. For their sake I remove from the earth those whom they wish to see punished."

On this reading, when a servant wants only Allah, knows only Him and belongs only to Him, Allah turns the face of His own heart toward him. He shuts every door of desire, appetite and want for that servant and clears every other thing out of his heart. Passion for speaking to Allah and listening to Him comes to rule his spirit and heart, and Allah gives him ease on the carpet of passion. He holds the sword of beginningless jealousy over the servant's head. If the servant wished to look at another, crave another or do business with another, Allah would not allow it. Night is turned into day and day into night in the servant's work, and his business with the world's creatures is ruined.

Allah knows how to plunder a servant He wants. Today He entrusts him to the policeman of mindfulness, to keep him under the protection of the Sharīʿah, and mindfulness gives all his movements and his stillness the character of courtesy. Tomorrow He will give him his place in the seat of truthfulness (54:55). That place lies in what the text calls the Presence of At-ness, which means nearness to Him.

On the Day of Resurrection, mindfulness itself will be told to come, because today is the day of its bazaar. Whoever has a portion of it will be settled in a dwelling that matches his portion. Those who knew it well will be seated in the Presence of At-ness, for in the beginningless past Allah decreed: "Indeed, the righteous will be amid Gardens and rivers, at the Seat of Honour in the presence of the Most Powerful Sovereign" (54:54-55).

### The inner reading: pairs, many forms and the Watcher

Allah created everything as a pair, and so every creature was given a spouse. Each was joined to its like and bound to one that resembles it, because being one and being alone belong to Allah alone. They are His attribute, His right and worthy of Him. It has been narrated in one of the books that Allah said, "I have given the things mates so that My unity will be inferred."

The words "spread countless men and women" show His perfect power and the majesty of His lordship. From the offspring of one upright person He brought out many thousands of creatures with different natures and colours, different forms and conduct. Each has its own colour, nature, character, state and aspiration. You will never meet two people who are the same in nature and face, or in form and manner. Glory be to Him, whose decrees never run out and whose knowledge has no limit.

At the end of the verse Allah says that He is watchful over you. A watcher is one who listens to hearts without investigating, who knows deeds without asking, and who has no need to rest in his striving. This is a warning to the servant and eloquent advice for the traveller on the path. It means: you know that I listen to hearts and watch deeds and words, so practise watchfulness and give Me what I am owed.

Watchfulness is that the servant keeps looking at the Real with his heart and keeps the gaze of the Real in front of his eyes. He knows that Allah is not heedless of him, so he is always on guard. Hence Muṣṭafā said, "When you do not like people to see something from you, do not do it while you are alone." In this sense they have sung:

> When you are alone with yourself one day, do not say, "I am alone." Say, "Over me is a Watcher."

### In short

- The verse speaks to all humanity: be mindful of Allah, who made everyone from one soul and made its mate from it, then spread countless men and women from the two of them.
- Being from one father and one mother means no one is high or low by race, caste, colour or language, and it calls for compassion toward the weak and fairness toward everyone.
- People appeal to one another in Allah's name, and they are commanded to honour the ties of the womb. The hadiths promise wide provision and a long life for keeping them, and say that one who breaks them will not enter Paradise.
- Because Allah is always watching, every right must be fulfilled sincerely and from the heart, not as a formality.
- Its inner reading treats mindfulness as a shield with three levels, oneness, obedience and caution, and treats watchfulness as always keeping the gaze of the Real before one's eyes.
