<<<VERSE 4:1>>>
The surah opens with an address to the entire human race: "O humanity! Be mindful of your Lord Who created you from a single soul". No tribe and no era is left out. The command is taqwa (being mindful of Allah), and it is the foundation stone of everything that follows, because this surah is a book of rights: the rights of orphans, wives, children and relatives, the rights that no court can enforce. The verse gives two reasons for the command in the same breath. Allah is the Lord who made every person from one soul, and He is the One whose name people invoke when they claim something from one another. Both truths ask for the same answer. The God who made you, and the God you call upon over your neighbour's conscience, deserves to be obeyed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O humanity!"** The address is general, and it can also be read as particular. In its wide sense it takes in all human beings, men and women, those alive when the Qur'an came down and everyone still to be born until the Day of Judgement. In its narrower sense it was spoken to the people of Makkah. Either way, the verse is not addressed to believers only. It speaks to people as people.

**"Be mindful of your Lord"** translates the command ittaqū, guard yourselves against acting against your Lord. To have taqwa of Allah is, first of all, to worship Him alone, with no partner. To fear Allah is to fear His punishment, and the way to fear it is to obey. The name chosen here is Rabb, Lord, picked from the fairest of Allah's names, and the choice carries a lesson. The One totally responsible for a person's nurture, whose care he witnesses every moment of his life, deserves all the awe a person can feel. The mere thought of rising against Him in defiance is dangerous.

**"Who created you from a single soul"** identifies the soul as Ādam (Adam), the father of all human beings. Allah could have created people in more than one way. He chose one way and one form: every human being from one man, so that the whole race is tied together in a strong bond of brotherhood. One reported reading adds that Ādam's soul alone is meant, with the soul of Ḥawwā (Eve) already contained in it, her creation being the drawing out of what was in him.

**"and from it He created its mate"** names Ḥawwā (Eve), the mother of humankind. She was created from Ādam, from a rib on his left side, taken from his back while he slept. When he woke and saw her, he was drawn to her and loved her, and her feeling matched his. An authentic hadith draws a lesson for married life from the manner of her creation:

> Woman was created from a rib. The most curved part of the rib is its upper part. If you try to straighten it, you will break it. If you leave it as it is, it will remain crooked.

**"and through both He spread countless men and women"** means that from Ādam and Ḥawwā came generation after generation, spread throughout the world in different shapes, characteristics, colours and languages. In the end their gathering and their return will be to Allah. Read inwardly, the phrase is a display of perfect power and lordhood. From the offspring of one upright individual came thousands upon thousands of creatures, each with its own colour, nature, character and state, its own form and its own aspiration. No two people are alike in nature and face. Glory to the One whose decrees have no end and whose knowledge reaches beyond all limits.

**"And be mindful of Allah—in Whose Name you appeal to one another"** points to a habit of speech that was current then and is still current now. A person pressing a claim says, "I ask you by Allah", or "for the sake of Allah", or "I ask you by Allah, and then by the womb that joins us", a manner of appeal reported from Ibrāhīm, Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan. Ad-Daḥḥāk applied it to trade: fear the One you invoke when you conduct your transactions and contracts. The grammar was weighed closely. The verb can be read tassā'alūna, where the first letter of the doubled form has merged into the next, or tasā'alūna, you ask one another. Both readings give the same scene of two people, one saying to the other, "I ask you by Allah." The command makes the appeal a mirror. Whoever expects others to fear Allah when they owe him something must fear Allah himself in what he owes them.

**"and ˹honour˺ family ties"** turns to the wombs, al-arḥām. They are to be revered by not cutting the ties of kinship but keeping and honouring them. This is how Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿIkrimah explained it, and al-Ḥasan, ad-Daḥḥāk and ar-Rabīʿ said the same. On one variant reading the word joins the name of Allah that precedes it, as though the verse said: fear Allah, and fear the wombs. People did indeed implore one another by ties of kinship, as they implored by Allah's name. A third rendering takes the phrase as a plain command: keep the ties of kinship, and do not sever them.

**"Surely Allah is ever Watchful over you"** means He watches all your deeds and sees your every circumstance. The Qur'an says the same elsewhere: "And Allah is Witness over all things" (58:6). An authentic hadith turns the doctrine into a way of standing before Him:

> Worship Allah as if you see Him, for even though you cannot see Him, He sees you.

Nothing escapes Him. He takes every act into account and repays it. A Watcher, in the language of this reading, is One who gives ear to hearts without having to investigate, who is aware of deeds without asking, and who never needs to rest from striving. He is also a Protector: He will ask about the acts of obedience He commanded, and about the kinship He told people to keep.

### One family from one soul

All human beings are one and the same by birth. Every person alive can trace his origin to the same man and the same woman, Ādam and Ḥawwā. That single origin makes the human race one extended family, and it ought to live with fairness and goodwill, the members of one household. No one may treat race, caste, colour or language as the measure of good or bad, nobility or meanness. Those differences are real, but they are never the standard by which people are judged. What the shared origin tightens in the human family it tightens further in the family itself: the closer the blood, the higher the duty of decent behaviour among kin.

The verse also states, in passing, why dealings with other people are never only human business. Everyone stands under the Great Almighty God, who is the Reckoner for all, who will decide the eternal future of every human being in the Hereafter and repay each according to what he did in this world. A person should therefore not treat his dealings with others as a matter between man and man. They are a matter between man and God. He should fear God's grip and keep within the bounds God has set, to save himself from His wrath.

There is a further sign in the pairing. The Lord who created everything created it as a pair, and He made a spouse appear for everyone, joining each with a likeness and attaching each to what resembles it. Unity and solitariness belong to Him alone. It is His due and worthy of Him. It is narrated in one of the books that Allah said: "I have given the things mates so that My unity will be inferred." Even the division of creation into pairs points back to the One who has no partner.

### The blessing of kinship, and the warning against cutting it

The word al-arḥām is the plural of raḥm, the womb, the child's home until it is born. Since the womb is where blood relationship begins, keeping that relationship alive is called silatur-raḥm in Arabic, literally the umbilical bond. Its opposite, indifference toward the ties of blood, is qaṭʿur-raḥm, cutting the bond. The hadiths on this subject are many and strong.

The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever wants his livelihood made plentiful and his term extended should keep good relations with his near of kin. The reward of the Hereafter is promised, and this life sees a share of the benefit too: provision widens, and the years are blessed. ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām said that when the Prophet ﷺ came to Madinah aṭ-Ṭayyibah, the very first words he heard from him were these:

> O men, make a practice of greeting each other with salām, feed people for the pleasure of Allah, treat your near relations well, and pray by night while people sleep. You will enter Paradise in peace.

Maymūna, the Mother of the Faithful, once freed a bondwoman and told the Prophet ﷺ about it. He told her that had she given her to her maternal uncle, her reward would have been greater. Freeing a slave is one of the finest acts of merit in Islam, and still the fair treatment of relatives was put above it. In the same spirit he said that charity to a needy person is charity, while to a near relative it is twofold: charity and kinship. A simple change in where the charity goes earns two rewards at once.

The warnings on the other side are stern. The Prophet ﷺ said that a breaker of blood relationships shall not enter Paradise, and that mercy does not come down upon any people among whom lives someone who has severed the bonds of kinship. According to a hadith in which Allah Himself speaks, He proclaimed: "I shall associate Myself with one who strengthens the ties of kinship, and detach Myself from one who severs them."

These reports show what the verse is testing. A person's attachment to Allah is measured by his relations with other human beings. Whoever fears Allah while dealing with the rights of others is the one who truly venerates Him, and whoever loves other human beings is the one who really loves his Creator. Fair dealing and compassion are general requirements of any society, but for family relations they are so vital that they stand second in importance only to Allah Himself.

### Three degrees of godwariness

Read inwardly, the opening command is an address to the centre of the human being: "O centre of human nature, make godwariness your shelter and cling to it, for the life of the servants is through it and the deliverance of the travellers is in it." Godwariness means that the servant makes the commands of the sharīʿah, the law Allah has laid down, into his shield, so that the arrows of prohibition cannot reach him. It has three levels. First, take refuge in the sentence of tawḥīd, the oneness of Allah, and avoid every kind of associationism. Second, take refuge in obedience and step away from the path of disobedience. Third, take refuge in caution and flee from what is ambiguous. Whoever climbs these way stations with truthfulness will without fail reach deliverance, for the Qur'an itself gives the report: "God will deliver those who were godwary" (39:61). And it promises: "Whosoever is wary of God, He will appoint a way out for him and He will provide for him from whence he never reckoned" (65:2-3). Whoever takes hold of godwariness finds the road of deliverance made easy from every suffering, and provision sent from where he had no hope of it.

Scruple of this fineness has its own story. The sister of Bishr al-Ḥāfī went to see Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal and said: "O Imam of the Muslims, I spin thread on the roof of my house. When the torch of the Ṭāhirids passes by, I may join my threads by its rays. Is that permissible or not?" Aḥmad asked her first who she was, and whether she belonged among people able to carry such godwariness. When she said she was the sister of Bishr al-Ḥāfī, he wept and said that godwariness like this is permitted only in the household of Bishr al-Ḥāfī, that it was not worthy of her, and that she should emulate her brother so that perhaps she might become like him. Then, if she wanted to spin by the light of the Ṭāhirids' torch, her hand would not obey her. Her brother had reached a degree where his hand refused to obey him whenever he reached for food touched by the slightest suspicion.

For those drawn all the way in, the verse opens onto Allah's own care. Muṣṭafā ﷺ narrated from his Lord that when Allah knows that what dominates His servant's heart is being occupied with Him, He turns the servant's appetite toward supplicating Him and conversing with Him. The servant gains passion for Allah, and Allah for him. And when such a servant then desires to be negligent, Allah comes between him and his negligence. These are His friends in truth, His champions, the ones for whose sake He removes from the earth those they desire to be punished. For the servant who wants only Him, knows only Him and belongs only to Him, Allah turns the face of His heart toward him. He shuts against him the doors of desires, appetites and wants, removes all others from his heart, and lets passion for speaking with Him and listening to Him rule his spirit. He gives him ease on the carpet of passion and holds over his head the sword of a jealousy that predates creation, so that looking at another, craving another, doing business with another, is not allowed. "I will make night into day and day into night in your work. I will ruin your business with the world's creatures." When Allah wants such a servant, He knows how he must be plundered. Today He entrusts him to the policeman of godwariness, keeping him under the protection of His sharīʿah, which gives his movements and his stillnesses the condition of courtesy. Tomorrow He gives him a place in the seat of truthfulness (54:55), within the Presence of At-ness, the state of being with Him alone.

The end of the road is pictured as a market day. At the resurrection it will be said to godwariness: "Come, for today is the day of your bazaar. Whoever has a portion of you, settle him in a dwelling in the measure of his portion." Those familiar with godwariness are settled in the Presence of At-ness, for it was decreed from before time: "Surely the godwary will be in the midst of gardens and a river, in a seat of truthfulness, at an Omnipotent King" (54:54-55).

### The Prophet's ﷺ use of this verse

In his Ṣaḥīḥ, Muslim records the account of Jarīr ibn ʿAbdullāh al-Bajalī. A delegation of Mudar came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and he saw the state they were in: striped woollen cloaks, worn thin by poverty. After the Zuhr prayer the Prophet ﷺ stood up and gave an address in which he recited this verse to its end. He also recited: "O you who believe! Have taqwa of Allah. And let every person look to what he has sent forth for the tomorrow" (59:18). Then he called on them to give charity, saying that a man gives ṣadaqah from his dinar or his dirham, from a saʿ of wheat or a saʿ of dates, and so on through the report. Aḥmad and the compilers of the Sunan collected the same narration from Ibn Masʿūd. The choice of this verse for that day is itself a reading of it. The Prophet ﷺ reached for it when he wanted to stir the conscience of the poor toward giving: first the fear of the Lord who made one family of them all, then the spending that such fear produces. He also used to recite it in the khutbah when a marriage was solemnized, which is why it is still recited at weddings, and that use suits it equally, for a marriage is where two families' mutual rights begin.

### How the verse opens the surah

The verse is the doorstep of everything the surah commands. Its purpose is to stop people from becoming usurpers of the rights of Allah and of His servants, and to do it by reminding them that they are all children of one father. That reminder sets love, mutual sympathy and concern as the working basis of common life, so that the rights of relatives, orphans and married couples are fulfilled from the heart, at the root, where no law can reach. The last sentence of the verse, that Allah is watchful, is what keeps the fulfilment honest. Divine watchfulness reaches intentions, scruples and motives, everything hidden in human hearts. Acts done halfheartedly, or for form's sake, or out of fear of embarrassment, without any genuine desire to serve, remain unacceptable to Allah. The Qur'an does not bring its laws in cold print the way governments publish statutes. It teaches them the way a teacher or a parent teaches, joining each rule to the training of minds and hearts.

### In short

- The verse commands taqwa of all humanity: be mindful of the Lord who made every person from one soul, Ādam (Adam), and created from him his mate Ḥawwā (Eve), then spread the two of them into countless men and women.
- Since people appeal to Allah's name when claiming their rights from one another, whoever invokes that name must fear Allah in the rights he owes.
- Family ties are never to be cut. Keeping them brings plentiful livelihood and blessed years in this world and Paradise in the next. Cutting them keeps a person out of Paradise and withholds mercy from his whole people.
- Godwariness has three degrees: tawḥīd without any associationism, obedience instead of the path of disobedience, and caution against the ambiguous. Its promise is deliverance and a seat of truthfulness with an Omnipotent King.
- Allah is Watcher over everything. He sees deeds, hears what hearts contain, and repays every act. Worship Him as if you saw Him, for He sees you.
- The Prophet ﷺ recited this verse to move a poor delegation to charity and in the marriage address. It opens the surah because the laws that follow stand on the fear of the One who watches.
