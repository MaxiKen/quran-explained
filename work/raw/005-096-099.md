<<<VERSE 5:96>>>
The prohibition of land game is followed by a permission: "It is lawful for you to hunt and eat seafood, as a provision for you and for travellers." What the sea yields is not caught by the restriction on hunting. The verse then returns to the ban on land game during pilgrimage and closes with a call to mindfulness of the One to whom all will be gathered.

### Phrase by phrase

**"It is lawful for you to hunt and eat seafood"** permits the pursuit of water game and its use as food. Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib, Sa'id bin Jubayr and others explain the first part as what is eaten fresh from it and the second as what is eaten dried and salted.

Ibn 'Abbas describes water game as what is taken from the water while still alive, and its use for food as what the water throws ashore dead. Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i and Abu Salamah bin 'Abdur-Rahman are reported in the same sense, as are 'Ikrimah, Abu Ayyub Al-Ansari, 'Abdullah bin 'Amr, Zayd bin Thabit and Abu Bakr As-Siddiq.

One gloss limits sea game to creatures that can live only in the sea, such as fish, and excludes what can live both in water and on land, such as crabs. Another permits catching aquatic animals and whatever the sea throws out, dead or alive.

**"as a provision for you and for travellers."** is explained as food and provision for you and for those at sea and travelling along it, according to 'Ikrimah. Other scholars say water game is allowed both for those who fish it from the sea and, once salted, as food for travellers inland. A similar statement is reported from Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, As-Suddi and others.

Another gloss describes it as a benefit for a resident and nourishment for a wayfarer taking provisions, and a third as revealed concerning people of Banu Mudlij who lived off the sea and asked the Prophet ﷺ about its fish and about what the sea washes out.

**"But hunting on land is forbidden to you while on pilgrimage."** prohibits land game during ihram and, in one gloss, in the sacred precinct. Land game is described as the edible beasts living on land.

**"Be mindful of Allah to Whom you all will be gathered"** calls for fear of Allah and awareness that people will be gathered to Him and questioned about the game made forbidden during consecration and in the sacred precinct. Mindfulness is explained as carrying out His instructions and avoiding His prohibitions, with return to Him on the Day of Rising and repayment for actions.

### What are the rules for a pilgrim and game?

Hunting land game in the state of ihram entered for Hajj or 'Umrah is not allowed. Whoever does it must pay the compensation set out for the offender (5:95), together with the sin he earns if he acted intentionally. If he hunted by mistake he still pays expiation and may not eat from it, because such game is like a dead animal, whether the person is in ihram or not.

If someone not in ihram hunts and gives the meat to a person in ihram, the latter may not eat it if the animal was killed specifically for him. As-Sa'b bin Jaththamah presented a zebra to the Prophet ﷺ while at Waddan or Abwa', and it was handed back to him.

Seeing the effect of the refusal on As-Sa'b's face, he explained that they had returned it only because they were in a state of ihram. The hadith is in the Two Sahihs. The explanation given is that the Prophet ﷺ supposed As-Sa'b had hunted the zebra for him, which is why he declined it.

Otherwise a person in ihram may eat game hunted by someone who is not. When Abu Qatadah hunted a zebra while not in ihram and offered it to those who were, they hesitated and asked the Messenger ﷺ. He asked whether any of them had pointed at it or helped kill it. When they said no, he told them to eat, and he ate from it himself. This is also in the Two Sahihs, in various wordings.

One gloss states the same rule from the Sunnah, that game caught by a person outside consecration may be eaten by one inside it.

### What report illustrates the provision of the sea?

Malik bin Anas records Jabir bin 'Abdullah describing an army sent by the Messenger ﷺ toward the eastern coast under the command of Abu 'Ubaydah bin Al-Jarrah, three hundred men strong, with Jabir among them.

They marched until their food was nearly gone. Abu 'Ubaydah ordered all the journey's provisions gathered, and they filled two bags of dates. He rationed them in small daily amounts until the supply was exhausted, and each man's share came down to a single date.

Asked how one date could suffice, Jabir answered that they knew its value only when even that was gone. When they reached the shore they found an enormous fish like a small mountain, and the army ate from it for eighteen days.

Abu 'Ubaydah then had two of its ribs fixed upright in the ground and ordered a she camel to be ridden underneath. It passed through the arch without touching. The hadith is also collected in the Two Sahihs.

### What about seawater itself?

Malik records Abu Hurayrah describing a man who asked the Messenger ﷺ about going to sea with little water carried along. If they used it for wudu, ablution, they would be thirsty, so could they perform ablution with seawater? The Messenger ﷺ answered that its water is pure and its dead are lawful.

Ash-Shafi'i and Ahmad bin Hanbal record the hadith together with the compilers of the four Sunan collections. Al-Bukhari, At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Hibban graded it sahih, authentic, and it is also recorded from the Prophet ﷺ by several other Companions.

### Why is hunting forbidden in this state?

The prohibition was ordained not because of anything inherent in the animal but as a matter of trial for human beings. To test people, Allah chose to ban certain things symbolically.

Where the Lawgiver saw that the trial might cause needless hardship, the law was relaxed. Pilgrims travelling by sea or river toward the House of Allah may legitimately fish and eat the catch if nothing else is available to them on the voyage. They have no alternative if their lives are to be saved.

That is the shape of the ruling here. A symbolic restriction is imposed for the sake of obedience, and provision is opened where necessity would otherwise press.

### How does the verse gather the surrounding commands?

The closing call to mindfulness is explained as a command to fear Allah and beware of His might by obeying what He commands and avoiding what He prohibits in these verses revealed to the Prophet ﷺ.

The verses gathered here, running on from verse 93 through verse 96, forbid intoxicants, gambling, al-ansab and al-azlam, together with al-sayd, the hunting and killing of land game while in ihram. To Allah is the return and the destination, and He will punish disobedience and reward obedience.

The explanation of this verse and of verses 97 to 99 is summarised from Ibn Jarir At-Tabari, since the fuller treatment of them is not given in the account that precedes it. The summary distinguishes what is halal and what is haram, states what is wajib upon the offender and keeps the emphasis on the gathering to Allah.

### In short

- Water game is lawful, whether caught alive or cast ashore dead, fresh or preserved, for residents and travellers alike.
- Land game is forbidden during ihram and in the sacred precinct, with expiation due and a distinction between animals killed for a pilgrim and those not.
- The expedition of Abu 'Ubaydah and the ruling on seawater show how the provision of the sea meets necessity.
- The prohibition is a symbolic trial, relaxed where hardship would be undue.
- The closing command gathers the surrounding prohibitions and points to the return to Allah.

<<<VERSE 5:97>>>
Four things are named as a support for human life: "Allah has made the Ka’bah—the Sacred House—a sanctuary of well-being for all people, along with the sacred months, the sacrificial animals, and the ˹offerings decorated with˺ garlands." The verse then draws a conclusion about knowledge. All of this is so that people may know Allah knows everything in the heavens and the earth.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah has made the Ka’bah—the Sacred House"** names the House of Allah. In Arabic the word denotes a square place, and another house built by the Arab tribe of Khath'am carried the same name, and was known precisely as al-Ka'bah al-Yamaniyah. The addition of the words Al-Baytul-Haram, the Sacred House, therefore distinguishes the House of Allah from that other building.

The Ka'bah is described as including the whole sacred boundary. It is termed the Haram because Allah prohibited hunting its game and cutting its trees or grass.

**"a sanctuary of well-being for all people"** is explained as an asylum of safety for people who had no chief to keep the strong from transgressing against the weak, the wicked from the good, and oppressors from the oppressed. The words for standing are explained as that on which the stability and survival of a thing depends.

The word for people is general. In this context it may mean the people of Makkah, the people of Arabia or the peoples of the world, and it certainly includes humanity as a whole, while the people of Makkah and Arabia hold a particular place.

One account describes an enduring institution by which people's religious life is sustained through pilgrimage to it and their worldly life through the security guaranteed to whoever enters, the absence of interference and the fact that fruits of every kind are brought to it. Another describes it as a standard, sanctuary and emblem for humanity for the purpose of worship.

**"along with the sacred months"** is the second of the four. The singular form leads most scholars to read it as the month of Dhu'l-Hijjah, in which the rites of Hajj are performed, while others hold that the singular signifies the category and so includes all the sacred months.

One account names those months as Dhū’l-Qa‘da, Dhū’l-Hijja, Muḥarram and Rajab, instituted so that people would be secure from fighting during them. Another describes the sacred month as a time of security.

**"the sacrificial animals"** is the third. The word hady refers to an animal sacrificed in the Haram. It was common Arab practice not to stop or obstruct anyone driving such animals, who could travel in peace and accomplish his intention, and so the sacrificial animals also became a means of establishing peace.

One gloss describes the offerings as presented to the House, a gesture of security to the company of people present where the offering is made. Another describes them as instituted so that their owner suffers no interference.

**"and the ˹offerings decorated with˺ garlands."** is the fourth. The word al-qala'id is the plural of qiladah, a garland. In the age of ignorance a person setting out for Hajj would put a garland around the neck of his sacrificial animal as a sign, so that people would know he was bound for Hajj and should not be molested. Garlands were also placed on the animals themselves.

One gloss describes these garlands as made from the bark of trees of the sacred precinct, again as a gesture of security to the people there.

**"All this so you may know that Allah knows whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth"** draws the conclusion. Allah says He made these symbols an asylum so that people would know that the One who made symbols benefiting their life and providing their security also knows everything in the heavens and the earth that brings about their immediate or eventual benefit.

**"and that He has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of everything"** adds that no deed or affair of theirs escapes His observation. He counts them, rewarding the good with good and the evil in kind. One gloss describes His knowledge as covering the benefits of all things and what is good for the people of the heavens and the earth.

The appointment of these things to secure benefit and ward off harm before they occurred is itself offered as evidence of that knowledge, since it testifies to His awareness of all that is and all that will be.

### How did the House function as a government in the age of ignorance?

Peace is normally maintained through the laws of a government and their enforcement, which is why robbers, thieves, killers and plunderers do not dare. During the Arab period of ignorance there was no formal government and no general law keeping public peace.

Whatever political arrangement existed rested on tribal considerations. One tribe could attack the life, property and honour of another whenever it chose, so no tribe ever enjoyed settled peace.

Allah therefore made the House in Makkah al-Mukarramah stand, by His perfect power, as a regular government ushering in peace and security. Just as no sane person today would dare break the law of a strong government, so Allah impressed reverence for the House on the hearts of ordinary people in that age, so that they cast personal desire and emotion aside when its honour and standing were concerned.

That age was proverbial for tribal prejudice and long wars. Yet such was the reverence Allah placed in their hearts that a man would say nothing even to a sworn enemy, despite anger and resentment, if that enemy had entered the Haram. A son who met his father's killer inside the Haram would lower his gaze and walk away.

The same respect was given to whoever had set out for Hajj or 'Umrah, or who was driving sacrificial animals in the Haram. Even the worst of people would not harm him. They would say nothing to a sworn enemy whose garments of ihram or garlands made his pilgrimage plain.

### What example is given?

In the sixth year of the Hijrah the Prophet ﷺ entered the ihram of 'Umrah with a group of his Companions and set out for the House. He halted at Hudaybiyah, near the limits of the Haram, and sent 'Uthman al-Ghani to Makkah with some companions to tell its chiefs that the Muslims had come to perform 'Umrah, not to fight, and should not be obstructed.

After much debate the Makkans sent a representative of their own to the Prophet ﷺ. On seeing him, the Prophet ﷺ said that this man upheld the sanctities of the House, so the animals marked for sacrifice should be brought before him.

On seeing the animals destined for sacrifice, the representative agreed that people like these must never be hindered from reaching the House. The awe of the venerated Haram, present even in the age of ignorance, made it a cause and a wellspring of peace.

### Who benefited from that peace?

As a direct result of this reverence, those who went in and out of the Haram, or who came for Hajj or 'Umrah bearing some sign of the intended pilgrimage, were the ones protected. Outsiders did not share that peace.

Within Arabia, however, the site of the House and its surroundings were honoured universally, and the months of Hajj received the same respect. They were called the sacred months, and some included Rajab among them. During those months fighting and killing, even outside the Haram, was treated by the whole of Arabia as forbidden.

The sacred month, the sacrificial animal and the garlands are therefore all adjuncts of the House, and reverence for them is part of reverence for it. Allah made their combination a source of stability, constancy and resilience in everything bearing on the worldly and otherworldly life of the people of Arabia, and of Makkah in particular, as well as for humanity at large.

### What does stability for people mean?

Several explanations are given. Some say the House and the sacred precinct around it were made a place of peace for everyone. Others say the phrase refers to the widened means of sustenance for the people of Makkah, since what is neither grown nor made there reaches them from all over the world by Allah's grace.

Still others say that the people of Makkah, as custodians of the House, were held in high esteem as a special people serving it, and that this distinction of theirs is what the phrase identifies.

Al-Razi says there is no contradiction among these statements, and that all fall within the sense of the phrase. Allah made the House a source of betterment, prosperity and success, materially and spiritually, for the stay, survival, stability, sustenance and return of all peoples, both return to it and, through it, return to the final destination of the Hereafter. The people of Arabia, and Makkah above all, were blessed with its outward and inward favours.

### Is the House the mainstay of the world?

The master of Qur'anic commentary 'Ata' is quoted in al-Bahr al-Muhit on this point. In terms of significance, the House is the pillar of the whole world. As long as people keep turning toward it and Hajj keeps being performed, the world remains. Should that reverence ever end, the world would end with it.

The sense given is that as long as people from every country, region and direction keep facing the House for their prayer, and as long as Hajj continues to be performed by those on whom it is fard, obligatory, the world will go on and stay safe. If there ever came a fatal year in which nobody performed Hajj and nobody turned toward the House for prayer, the whole world would be overtaken by mass punishment.

The question of how the universal system is connected with the House is then addressed. Grasping how it works is not required of anyone. Who understands what binds a magnet to iron, or lightning to straw? Yet both are realities proved by observation and cannot be denied.

The comprehension of the link between the House and the order of the universe is not within mortal reach. It can be known only if the Creator of the universe tells us. That the House causes the survival of the universe is a matter of the spirit, beyond physical insight or formal research. That it is a cause of peace and security for Arabia and the people of Makkah, however, stands proved by long experience and observation.

### What do the symbolic rites teach?

The Ka'bah is the eternal centre of Islam and of the Muslim community. Making it obligatory to face it in prayer links every Muslim with its centrality, and Hajj makes it the international gathering place of Islam.

The symbolic rites performed in connection with the pilgrimage are of great importance, not because of any holiness inherent in them, but because they are signs of the trial of human beings. When a servant of Allah carries out His commands in these symbolic rites, he reminds himself that although Allah is not visibly present, He is actively so. He gives commands, He watches over His servants and He is aware of all their actions.

Those thoughts cultivate the fear of Allah and enable a person to remain a true servant through the changes of his life. It is a human weakness to attach supreme importance to gathering crowds and accumulating showy worldly goods. In Allah's eyes importance belongs to quality alone, and quantity has no value with Him.

Those who rush toward abundance and set no value on truth consider themselves clever, but are in fact unwise. Whoever orders his life by the fear of Allah rather than by material considerations and worldly cares is the one who truly succeeds.

### In short

- The House, the sacred months, the sacrificial animals and the garlands are named together as supports of human security and stability.
- Their function in the age of ignorance is described in detail, including the example of Hudaybiyah in the sixth year of the Hijrah.
- Explanations of stability for people include peace, provision and the standing of Makkah's custodians, and Al-Razi holds them all within one sense.
- The House is described as the pillar of the world, with the connection between it and the universe known only to its Creator.
- The symbolic rites train servants in the awareness that Allah commands, watches and knows, which is what the verse's conclusion states.

<<<VERSE 5:98>>>
Two attributes are placed side by side: "Know that Allah is severe in punishment and that He is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful." The verse begins with knowledge, and it holds severity and mercy together rather than choosing between them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Know that Allah is severe in punishment"** addresses those who disobey and defy Him. One gloss specifies the person who declares lawful what Allah has made unlawful. Another describes the severity as directed at His enemies.

**"and that He is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"** turns to the other side. Allah pardons the sins of those who obey and repent, and is more merciful than to punish them for sins they have turned from. He overlooks mistakes and is merciful toward whoever repents, and forgiving and compassionate toward His friends.

### Why are the two joined in one verse?

Severity and gentleness are combined so that the servant lives between fear and hope. Looking at severity he fears, and seeing gentleness he hopes.

Fear is described as the fortress of faith, the remedy against caprice and the weapon of the faithful. Hope is described as the mount of service, the provision for striving and the supply of worship.

Faith and certainty are said to have two wings, fear and hope, and no bird flies on a single wing. So the faithful cannot cover the road of religion on fear with no hope, nor on hope with no fear.

### What does the balance illustrate?

True faith is compared to a balance. One pan is fear and the other is hope, while the beam is love. The pans hang from knowledge.

Just as a balance must have pans, hope and fear must have knowledge, and that is why the verse opens with know. Fear without knowledge is described as the fear of the Khawarij, hope without knowledge as the hope of the Murji'ah, and love without knowledge as the love of libertines.

### How does this relate to the surrounding rulings?

The verse follows a long series of commands and prohibitions running from verse 96 to verse 99. The prescribed rulings of what is halal and what is haram rest on perfect wisdom and consideration. As long as they are obeyed they bring nothing but good to whoever keeps them, and choosing the contrary is to embrace the worst of curse and punishment.

Along with the warning comes an assurance. If someone sins forgetfully or heedlessly, Allah does not punish at once. The doors of His forgiveness remain open for those who repent and feel shame at what they did.

### In short

- Severity belongs to disobedience and defiance, and forgiveness to whoever repents.
- The two are joined so that a servant lives between fear and hope, neither wing alone being enough.
- Faith is compared to a balance whose pans hang from knowledge, which is why the verse begins with know.
- Knowledgeless fear, hope and love are each described as the mark of a deviating group.
- The verse confirms that the rulings rest on wisdom, that obedience brings good and that forgiveness remains open after failure.

<<<VERSE 5:99>>>
The passage ends by fixing responsibility: "The Messenger’s duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺." What follows belongs to Allah, who knows what people disclose and what they hide. The verse is both a comfort to the Messenger ﷺ and a warning to those who hear him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Messenger’s duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺."** The Messenger ﷺ sent to people has only to convey Allah's message, after which the reward for obedience and the punishment for disobedience rest with Allah. His duty is to convey the injunctions to the people Allah created, and it is not his duty to make them accept guidance, which lies in Allah's hands alone.

**"And Allah ˹fully˺ knows what you reveal and what you conceal"** describes a knowledge that nothing escapes. Allah knows what a person does, displays physically, announces and utters with his tongue, and what he hides in his heart, whether faith, disbelief, certainty, doubt or hypocrisy.

One gloss describes what is proclaimed as the good and evil a person displays publicly and what is hidden as the good or evil he keeps back. Another adds that He knows what people show among themselves and what they conceal from one another concerning a particular sum of money taken.

### Why is this a warning?

The obedience of those who accept the message never escapes Allah's knowledge, and neither does the disobedience and defiance of those who refuse it. Whoever is capable of that knowledge cannot be deceived, since He knows everything anyone does, openly or in secret.

Nothing the hearts conceal, and no outward act of souls in the heavens and the earth, escapes Him. Reward and punishment are in His hand alone, and He is worthy to be feared, obeyed and never disobeyed.

### What does it mean for the one who hears?

Whether people accept and believe or refuse and disbelieve, the profit and the loss reach only them. Their disbelief brings no loss on the Messenger ﷺ.

That is the point of stating the duty so narrowly. Delivery is complete on his side, and accountability is complete on Allah's side. What remains for the hearer is the choice, made in full view of a knowledge that reaches the hidden as well as the open.

Allah will requite people for what they manifest and for what they conceal, and He will repay their actions, whether guidance or misguidance.

### In short

- The Messenger's ﷺ duty is delivery alone, and acceptance is not his to produce.
- Refusal harms the one who refuses, not the one who conveyed.
- Allah knows the open and the hidden, including faith, disbelief, certainty, doubt and hypocrisy.
- Reward and punishment are in His hand, and He will requite what people manifest and conceal.
