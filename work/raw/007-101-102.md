<<<VERSE 7:101>>>
After telling the stories of the peoples of Nūḥ (Noah), Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lūṭ (Lot) and Shuʿayb, Allah sums them up for His Messenger ﷺ: "We have narrated to you ˹O Prophet˺ some of the stories of those societies. Surely, their messengers came to them with clear proofs, but still they would not believe in what they had already denied. This is how Allah seals the hearts of the disbelievers". He destroyed the disbelievers, saved the believers, and warned these nations by making the truth plain through the words of His messengers. They are told so that the thoughtful may take them as a model and the heedful as a warning.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have narrated to you ˹O Prophet˺ some of the stories of those societies."** These are the towns whose people Allah destroyed. "Narrated" here means that Allah sent Jibrīl (Gabriel) to the Prophet ﷺ with the story of their destruction. The Arabic word for "stories", anbāʾ, is used for weighty news. The word min, "some of", shows that these events are only a few of many that carry the same lesson. Another verse says:

> "These are accounts, We relate to you ˹O Prophet˺, of the ˹destroyed˺ cities. Some are still standing ˹barren˺, while others have been mowed down. We did not wrong them, rather they wronged themselves" (11:100-101).

**"Surely, their messengers came to them with clear proofs"**: the messengers brought commands, prohibitions and signs, plain miracles and evidence of the truth of their message. Allah punishes no one until He has sent a messenger to warn them (17:15).

**"but still they would not believe in what they had already denied."** They would not believe in the scriptures and messengers later, because they had denied the truth when it first came to them, even though they recognised it. This is how Ibn ʿAṭiyyah explained it, and the explanation is sound. A verse supports it:

> "What will make you ˹believers˺ realize that even if a sign were to come to them, they still would not believe? We turn their hearts and eyes away ˹from the truth˺ as they refused to believe at first" (6:109-110).

One explanation says they had denied it on the Day of the Covenant. Another says the last nations did not believe in what the first nations had disbelieved. So when the signs came to them they persisted in disbelief. Allah did not guide them to the truth, a just repayment for rejecting it the first time despite seeing clear signs and proofs.

**"This is how Allah seals the hearts of the disbelievers"**: Allah seals the hearts of those who disbelieve in Him and reject the truth, as is in His knowledge, so that they cannot accept good as good. In the same way that He closed the hearts of the peoples of these towns who rejected their prophets, He closes the hearts of those who reject Muhammad ﷺ, so that they are not guided to faith.

### Did every prophet have miracles?

This verse shows that all the prophets were given miracles. The Qur'an mentions the miracles of some prophets and not of most. That does not allow anyone to conclude that the prophets whose miracles are not mentioned had none. As for the claim of Hūd's people in Sūrah Hūd that he had brought them no clear sign, this verse shows it was sheer stubbornness. Or perhaps they thought his miracles of little account.

### Why do people cling to what they once said?

The verse describes a trait of the disbelievers: they held rigidly to ignorance simply to prove that what they had once said was right, ignoring every clear sign and proof of the truth. Many Muslims, and even some scholars, show the same habit, defending their mistaken statements in the face of clear proofs. This is a common cause of incurring Allah's anger.

### How does a heart become insensitive?

Allah has given human beings the powers of seeing, hearing and thinking, so that they may learn lessons and understand His signs. When a person does not use these powers for their purpose, the sensitivity of his heart slowly fades until his feelings are dulled. Then the seal of insensitivity is fixed on his heart and mind. He becomes like someone who does not see though he has eyes, does not hear though he has ears and does not understand though he has a mind. Though a human being, he has lost his humanity. Such a heart is kept off the path of guidance.

### In short

- These five stories are only some of the many that teach the same lesson, told to the Prophet ﷺ through Jibrīl.
- Every people received messengers with clear proofs, and every prophet was given miracles, mentioned or not.
- Those who reject the truth when it first comes are left unable to believe later, for Allah seals their hearts.
- Defending one's earlier word against clear proof, a habit even some Muslims share, invites Allah's anger.

<<<VERSE 7:102>>>
The stories end with a verdict on most of these peoples: "We did not find most of them true to their covenant. Rather, We found most of them truly rebellious". Most of the earlier nations did not keep their covenant. They were rebellious, turning away from obedience and compliance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We did not find most of them true to their covenant."** Allah found no loyalty in most people to the covenant made on the day of the pledge, the first covenant. Grammatically, the following "Rather, We found" uses a softened in, meaning "indeed".

**"Rather, We found most of them truly rebellious"**: most of them, or as one explanation puts it, all of them, were wrongdoers who broke their covenants and rebelled against Allah, not standing firm on what He commanded.

### Which covenant is meant?

The covenant mentioned here is the fiṭrah, the natural disposition Allah placed in people while they were still in their fathers' loins. He took their covenant that He is their Lord and King, and that none but He deserves worship. They affirmed it and testified to it against themselves. Then they broke it, threw it behind their backs and worshipped others besides Allah, with no proof or argument, and no support from reason or divine law. The pure fiṭrah rejects such acts, and every honourable messenger, from first to last, forbade them.

Two reports given under 7:30 make the same point. One hadith, in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs, says that every child is born on the fiṭrah until his parents change his religion. In another, recorded by Muslim, Allah says He created His servants pure in faith (ḥunafāʾ), and then the devils led them away and forbade them what He had allowed.

ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās said this covenant is the covenant Allah made with the spirits of all creatures before creating them, when He asked, "Am I not your Lord?" Every human spirit answered "Yes" and so entered the covenant (7:172). After coming to earth most people forgot it and took to worshipping false gods. So the verse says that Allah did not find most people true to it.

ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd said the covenant here is the covenant of īmān, faith, which another verse mentions when it speaks of one who has taken a covenant with the Most Merciful. On this reading, the verse means that most people turned away from their covenant with Allah.

### Why do people forget promises made in distress?

Nearly everyone, however sinful, turns to Allah when caught in some trouble. Often he promises Allah, aloud or in his heart, that he will be faithful, obey Him and avoid disobedience if only he is rescued. But once he is out of it, he goes back to his worldly desires with no thought for his covenant with Allah. The Qur'an mentions many such people.

The verse says "most of them", not "all of them". Some people are so perverse that they do not turn to Allah even in distress and never think of making Him any promise. Others keep their promise and stay obedient to Him. The verse ends by saying that most people were rebellious, turning from their covenant to stay obedient to Allah.

### How has history repeated itself?

The human race began with the followers of Ādam (Adam). When they fell into wrong ways, Allah's prophets came to warn them. Those who reformed themselves when the prophets came were saved, and those who refused were destroyed. Then the following generations forgot the vows of submission they had made to their prophets, and they suffered the same fate as the later generations of Ādam's followers. This has happened again and again, until the history of most of the human race has become a history of disobedience and broken pledges.

### What comes next?

These verses have described five events among earlier peoples, so that people today may learn from them and not follow the road that led their forerunners to ruin. The next account, of the Prophet Mūsā (Moses), is told in some detail. His miracles were more numerous and more striking than those of other prophets. His people, the Israelites, were also more stubborn and ignorant than other peoples. The verses that follow (7:103-110) also contain several rulings and points for discussion.

### The inner reading: loyalty to the covenant

Junayd said, "The finest of servants in state is the one who stands with Allah in guarding the limits and in loyalty to the covenants."

### In short

- Allah found most of the earlier peoples untrue to their covenant and rebellious against Him.
- The covenant is the fiṭrah and the pledge "Am I not your Lord?", or, in Ibn Masʿūd's view, the covenant of faith.
- People often promise Allah obedience in distress and forget it once relieved.
- After these five stories, the surah turns to Mūsā, whose miracles were greatest and whose people were most stubborn.
