The surah gathers the five stories it has told and draws the meaning out of them. "We have narrated to you ˹O Prophet˺ some of the stories of those societies. Surely, their messengers came to them with clear proofs, but still they would not believe in what they had already denied. This is how Allah seals the hearts of the disbelievers"

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have narrated to you ˹O Prophet˺ some of the stories of those societies"** points back to what has been told. The word used for a story or tiding carries the sense of great news. The small word meaning from or of, together with it, makes plain that what these verses describe is only a part of a far larger number of events carrying the same lesson. The Book has given a handful of them, and the handful is enough to make the point.

The stories are those of the peoples of Nuh, of Hud, of Salih, of Lut and of Shu'ayb. The account of each ended with the destruction of those who disbelieved, the deliverance of those who believed, and the warning given to the nations by the truth set out with the evidence that the messengers brought.

**"Surely, their messengers came to them with clear proofs"** is the first half of the charge against them. The signs and miracles that the messengers came with were a definite means of deciding between right and wrong. The tiding was brought down with Gabriel as the angel of revelation, and the proofs were sent in the words of the messengers.

A further point belongs here. It is known from this verse that miracles were given to every prophet. The miracles of some have been told in the Book and the miracles of most have not been mentioned in it, and the silence about them is no ground for saying that those prophets had none. The people of Hud said to him that he had not brought them any clear sign. This verse makes plain that their statement came out of plain obstinacy, and it may also be that they thought his miracles were of little weight compared with what they wanted to see.

**"but still they would not believe in what they had already denied"** is the second half, and the hardest of it. The sense given by Ibn 'Atiyyah is that they were not the kind of people to believe afterwards in what the messengers brought them, since they had already denied the truth when it first came to them, even though they recognized it. That reading is sound, and another verse supports it, where Allah says that when the signs come they will not believe, and their hearts and their eyes will be turned away from the guidance as they refused to believe in it the first time (6:109-110).

So the denial recorded here is not a single act that a man might take back. It is a settled refusal that a person grows into, and once it is settled the door of belief is closed from the inside.

### The justice of the warning before the punishment

The Book states elsewhere that Allah never punished a people until He had sent a messenger to warn them (17:15). The same principle stands in the words about the towns of which the story is related, where it is said that some of them are still standing and some have been reaped, and that they were not wronged, but they wronged themselves (11:100-101). The proof had come to each people in its own language and its own way, and the warning had been delivered by a man from among them. What came afterwards was the fruit of what they had chosen.

### The habit that this verse warns against

There is a trait of the disbelieving peoples that the verse lays bare, namely that a people will hold stubbornly to the path of ignorance with no regard for clear signs and proofs, only to prove that what they once said was true. That trait is not confined to the peoples of the past. Many Muslims, and even some of the religious scholars, are seen to hold to a wrong position in the face of clear proofs of the truth, and that habit is a usual cause of a person's falling under the anger of Allah.

### How the hearts come to be sealed

**"This is how Allah seals the hearts of the disbelievers"** states the law of it. The sealing is put on those who disbelieve and reject the truth, and the result is that they can no longer accept the good as good.

The way it comes about is plain. Man has been given the powers of observation, of hearing and of thinking, so that he may learn lessons and come to understand the signs of God through them. When a person does not use those powers for the purpose they were given for, the sensitivity of his heart keeps fading until his feelings grow blunt, and the seal of insensitivity is fixed on his heart and his mind. His state then is that of a man who does not see though he has eyes, does not hear though he has ears, and does not understand though he has a mind, and for all that he is counted among human beings, he has lost the humanity that was meant.

The record of the human race bears the same pattern. It began with the followers of Adam. When those followers went astray, the prophets of God came to remind them. Those who put themselves right at the coming of a prophet were saved, and those who refused to be put right were destroyed. The generations after them forgot the vows of submission they had made, and they met what the earlier generations had met. The same state of affairs has come round again and again, until for the greater part of the human race the record has become one of disobedience and broken pledges.

### What a covenant implies

The last part of the charge against the peoples was the breaking of the covenant, and the verse that follows takes it up on its own. The hearts of these peoples were sealed because they denied the truth when they saw it, and no fresh sign was going to help them after that. The mercy of the warning had been spent, and the sealing was the settled consequence.

What the verse establishes for the reader is a line between the one who turns and the one who will not. The towns whose stories were told are a warning to the people who came after, and the same words hold for a people who reject the messenger in front of them. The hearts of those who reject are sealed in the same way, and they are not guided to the faith, and the justice of it lies in this, that the truth had reached them with its proofs and they turned away.

### In short

- The verse gathers the stories of the five peoples already told, and the word used for them means great tidings, of which only a part has been related.
- The messengers came to each people with clear proofs, and miracles were given to every prophet, whether the Book has mentioned them or not.
- Their denial was a settled refusal, and the sense of the words is that they would not believe later in what they had already denied.
- The warning came before the punishment, and no people was destroyed until a messenger had been sent to them.
- The sealing of the heart follows the refusal, and it leaves a person unable to see, hear or understand what the signs are for.

<<<VERSE 7:102>>>
The subject of the covenant is taken up in the same breath. "We did not find most of them true to their covenant. Rather, We found most of them truly rebellious"

### Phrase by phrase

**"We did not find most of them true to their covenant"** is the opening of verse 102, and it states the failure. It was not the warning that was lacking, and it was not the proofs. What was lacking was loyalty to a commitment they had already made.

Which commitment it was has been answered in more than one way. Ibn Abbas said that the covenant meant here is the one that Allah made with the spirits of all creatures before they were created, when the question was put to them, that He was not their Lord, and all the human spirits entered into the covenant by answering that He was. Most of the people forgot that covenant after they came to the earth, and they fell to worshipping false gods instead of Allah.

Abdullah ibn Masud said that the covenant meant here is the covenant of iman, the faith itself, and the Book has spoken of a man who entered into a covenant with the Most Merciful. On this reading the verse says that most people turned away from their covenant with Allah. The everyday shape of that is easy to see. Take what happens to nearly every person. When distress catches him, he turns to Allah. He makes a promise, in words or in his heart, that if he is relieved of what has come on him he will be faithful, obedient, and will keep away from disobedience. Once he is out of it, he goes back to what he desires, with no regard for the covenant he made.

Another answer takes the covenant of the covenant of the human being's first nature. It is the fitrah that Allah placed in them while they were still in the loins of their fathers, together with the taking of the covenant that He is their Lord and King and that no deity but He is worthy of worship. All of them affirmed this, and they testified against themselves to it. Then they defied it, threw it behind their backs, and worshipped others besides Allah, with no proof and no argument, and with nothing from reason or from the sacred law to support them. The pure fitrah refuses such a thing, and every one of the honoured messengers, from the first of them to the last, forbade it. A hadith collected by Muslim carries the point. It says that Allah created His servants as hunafa, turned to Him alone, and that the devils came to them and turned them from their religion and forbade them what He had allowed them. It is recorded in the Two Sahihs that every child is born on the fitrah, and the parents are the ones who turn him into a Jew or a Christian or a Zoroastrian.

**"Rather, We found most of them truly rebellious"** is the second half of the finding. In this reading the words mean that most of the nations of the past were found disobedient, turning away from obedience and from compliance.

### The word most and what it allows

It is worth noticing that the verse says most of them and not all of them. The reason is that there are people so perverted that they do not turn to Allah even in their distress. They never think of making any promise to Him at all. There are others who fulfil what they promised and stay obedient to Him. So the exception in the wording keeps the door open, and it keeps the description honest.

Junayd said that the most beautiful of the servants in state is the one who halts with God in preserving the limits and in being loyal to what he has covenanted. The whole of the verse is that standard applied to the nations that have gone before.

### Where the surah goes next

The five accounts of the earlier peoples have now been closed so that those who read may take the lesson from them, and the next event is the account of Musa, which is told at length in the verses that follow. His miracles were greater in number and more striking in their nature than what was given to the prophets before him, and his people, the Children of Israel, were more stubborn and more ignorant than the other peoples of the world. The verses from 103 to 110 carry that account, and they carry some rulings and points of discussion along with it.

### In short

- The failure of those peoples was not a want of warning, but a want of loyalty to a covenant they had already made.
- One answer takes the covenant as the pact made with all human spirits before creation, when they affirmed that Allah was their Lord.
- Another takes it as the covenant of faith, which most people break as soon as the distress that made them promise is lifted.
- A third takes it as the fitrah that was placed in them, and the affirmation that went with it, which they threw behind their backs to worship others.
- The wording says most and not all, since some people turn in their distress and some keep what they promised, and the account of Musa follows next with more of its signs.
