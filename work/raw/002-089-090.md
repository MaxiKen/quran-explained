<<<VERSE 2:89>>>
Verse 89 describes the bitterest irony of all. The Jews had been waiting for this very Prophet and praying for his arrival. "Although they used to pray for victory ˹by means of the Prophet˺ over the polytheists, when there came to them a Book from Allah which they recognized, confirming the Scripture they had ˹in their hands˺, they rejected it. So may Allah’s condemnation be upon the disbelievers".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Although they used to pray for victory ˹by means of the Prophet˺ over the polytheists"**. Before this Messenger came, the Jews used to ask Allah to aid them through his arrival against their polytheist enemies in war, saying, "O Allah, give us help against them through the Prophet who is to be sent at the end of time." They would tell the polytheists that a Prophet was to be sent shortly before the end of the world, and that together with him they would wipe them out as the nations of ʿĀd and Iram were wiped out. One early gloss names the enemies as the tribes of Asad, Ghaṭafān, Muzaynah and Juhaynah. Ibn ʿAbbās said that the Jews of Khaybar were at war with Ghaṭafān and lost every encounter, so they devised this supplication: "O Allah, we beseech You by the truth of the unlettered Prophet whom You promised to send to us at the end of time, give us victory over them." Whenever they said it, Ghaṭafān was defeated. As-Suddī told it differently: the Arabs used to pass by the Jews and do them harm, and the Jews, who knew Muḥammad's description from the Torah, asked Allah to send him so that they could fight the Arabs alongside him.

**"when there came to them a Book from Allah"**. That is, the Qur'an, which Allah sent down to Muḥammad ﷺ.

**"which they recognized"**. They knew it to be the truth, and they knew the Prophet's traits and description from their own Book. The mission of the Prophet ﷺ was exactly what they had been told to expect.

**"confirming the Scripture they had ˹in their hands˺"**. The Qur'an agrees with the Torah and the Gospel in their universal true principles. To say that the Qur'an confirms the Torah means that the Qur'an is concrete evidence of the truth of the Torah's prophecies about the coming of the Prophet ﷺ and of the Qur'an itself. Whoever believes in the Torah cannot consistently deny the Qur'an and the Prophet ﷺ, because that denial would be a denial of the Torah too. The Torah had foretold his coming in several places, and the Jews themselves used to tell the Arabs that a new prophet and a new divine Book were soon to appear.

**"they rejected it"**. They denied that he was the one. They disbelieved out of envy and for fear of losing their leadership. Even after recognising its authenticity, the Jews denied the Qur'an out of sheer spite. The Arabic has two "when" clauses, and the answer to the first is supplied by the answer to the second.

**"So may Allah’s condemnation be upon the disbelievers"**. His wrath and torment are upon the Jews who disbelieved in Him and His Prophet.

### The occasion of revelation

Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq related from Ibn ʿAbbās that the Jews used to invoke Allah for the coming of Muḥammad ﷺ in order to gain victory over the Aws and the Khazraj, before he was sent. When Allah sent him to the Arabs, they rejected him and went back on everything they had said about him. Muʿādh ibn Jabal and Bishr ibn al-Barāʾ ibn Maʿrūr, of Banū Salamah, said to them: "O Jews, fear Allah and embrace Islam. You used to invoke Allah for the coming of Muḥammad when we were still disbelievers, and you used to tell us that he would come and describe him to us." Salām ibn Mishkam of Banū an-Naḍīr replied: "He has brought nothing we recognise. He is not the Prophet we told you about." Allah then revealed this verse about their statement. Abū al-ʿĀliyah said that the Jews used to ask Allah to send Muḥammad ﷺ so that they could gain victory over the Arab disbelievers, praying, "O Allah, send the Prophet we read about in the Torah, so that we can torment and kill the disbelievers alongside him." When Allah sent him and they saw that he was not one of them, they rejected him and envied the Arabs, though they knew he was the Messenger of Allah. As-Suddī adds their objection in their own words: all the messengers were descendants of Yaʿqūb (Jacob), so how can this one be a descendant of Ismāʿīl (Ishmael)?

### Knowledge is not faith

Why does the Qur'an call the Jews unbelievers when they recognised the truth as truth, which might seem to qualify them as believers? Because faith (īmān) does not mean merely knowing the truth. It means accepting the truth and affirming it in deed. Otherwise Satan would have to be called a believer, for he knows perfectly well what the truth is, and in his case that knowledge only intensifies the gravity of his unbelief (kufr). The next verse traces the Jews' unbelief to its source in malice.

### Empty words

A modern reading sees the Jews of Arabia repeating the old pattern. They had seen prophecies of a final prophet in their scriptures and eagerly awaited him. "When he comes," they used to say, "we will join him in a grand alliance against the pagans and idolaters." These proved to be empty words, a pretence aimed at consolidating their status as undisputed guardians of the divine faith. When the Prophet ﷺ came, their real position was exposed. They were steeped in bigotry, unwilling to believe in a prophet who did not come from the Jewish race. They could not dispute the authentic indications the Qur'an presented in support of his prophethood. All they could say was that it sounded very impressive, but their ancestral religion was good enough for them, their hearts were sealed, and they would accept nothing besides the faith inherited from their forefathers.

### The soldier who breaks at the muster

The devotional reading finds here an allusion to the person who resolves on purity and clarity (ṣafāʾ), promises complete loyalty (wafāʾ) and raises the banners of his zeal to march out to battle, but who, when the call comes to line up and advance boldly, breaks down at the rallying of the ranks and cuts himself off from the company, afraid of dangerous attacks. Allah says of such people that when the matter was resolved upon, it would have been better for them had they been true to Allah (47:21).

### In short

- The Jews had prayed for victory through the coming Prophet, at Khaybar against Ghaṭafān, and in Madinah against the Aws and the Khazraj, and had described him to the Arabs.
- When the Qur'an came confirming their Scripture and they recognised the Prophet ﷺ, they rejected him, and Salām ibn Mishkam of Banū an-Naḍīr told the Muslims he was not the one.
- Their motives were envy, fear of losing leadership, and racial pride that a prophet should come from Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) rather than Yaʿqūb (Jacob).
- Recognising the truth is not īmān, which demands acceptance and action, and he who deserts when the call to advance comes has not kept his promise (47:21).

<<<VERSE 2:90>>>
Verse 90 passes judgement on the bargain the Jews made. "Miserable is the price they have sold their souls for", it says, which was "denying Allah’s revelation and resenting Allah for granting His grace to whoever He wills of His servants! They have earned wrath upon wrath. And such disbelievers will suffer a humiliating punishment".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Miserable is the price they have sold their souls for"**. Mujāhid said that the Jews sold the truth for falsehood and hid the truth about Muḥammad ﷺ. As-Suddī said simply that the Jews sold themselves: what they chose for themselves, disbelief in what Allah revealed to Muḥammad ﷺ instead of believing in him, aiding him and supporting him, was an evil choice. What they gave away was their share of reward in the Hereafter, their portion of faith in Allah and His messengers. The Arabic construction singles out the very thing being condemned, the price.

**"denying Allah’s revelation"**. They disbelieved in the Qur'an that Allah sent down, in the Book and the Messenger together. This conduct was the fruit of their injustice, envy and hatred.

**"and resenting Allah for granting His grace to whoever He wills of His servants"**. The word for resentment, baghyan, gives the reason for their disbelief: envy that Allah should send down of His bounty, that is, revelation, upon whomever He chooses to carry His message, meaning Muḥammad ﷺ. Out of His bounty Allah sent Jibrīl (Gabriel) to him with the Book and with prophethood, and they were resentfully envious of it. There is no envy worse than this. Prophethood cannot be earned by one's own effort or merit. It is a special grace that Allah bestows on whomever He likes, and the Jews, in denying the Prophet ﷺ and the Qur'an out of spite, were questioning the will of Allah Himself.

**"They have earned wrath upon wrath"**. They came back laden with Allah's anger. The indefinite form of the word stresses how awesome that anger is. Ibn ʿAbbās said that Allah was angry with them because they neglected part of the Torah, and angry again because they disbelieved in the Prophet He sent to them. Abū al-ʿĀliyah said that Allah was angry with them for their disbelief in the Injīl (Gospel) and in ʿĪsā (Jesus), and angry a second time for their disbelief in Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'an. ʿIkrimah and Qatādah said the same. Another explanation is that the phrase means they deserved and acquired multiplied anger, or curse upon curse. The Jews earned divine wrath in two ways, for denying a true prophet, and for their envy and malice in doing so.

**"And such disbelievers will suffer a humiliating punishment"**. A disgraceful torment through which they will be humiliated, or, as some say, a severe punishment. Because their disbelief grew out of transgression and envy, which were themselves born of arrogance, they are punished with disgrace and humiliation in this world and the next. Allah says likewise that those too proud to worship Him will enter Hell humbled (40:60), meaning disgraced, degraded and humiliated. Imām Aḥmad narrated from ʿAmr ibn Shuʿayb, from his father, from his grandfather, that the Prophet ﷺ said that the arrogant will be gathered on the Day of Resurrection the size of ants but in the shape of men, with everything towering over them because of the humiliation laid upon them, until they enter a prison in Jahannam called Bawlas, where the fire will close over them from above and they will drink the pus of the people of the Fire.

### A punishment of disgrace, not of purification

The punishment announced here involves not only pain but disgrace and abasement. That added condition suggests that this kind of punishment belongs to unbelievers alone and is not meant for a true Muslim at all. A Muslim, however great his sins, is punished in order to be purified, not in order to be disgraced. For disbelieving in Muḥammad ﷺ and for distorting the Torah beforehand they deserved anger upon anger, and a humiliating torment on the Day of Judgement awaits those who denied his prophethood. The verse that follows reports what the Jews used to say about the Qur'an, and it shows their envy and malice as clearly as their unbelief (kufr).

### From nobility to ignominy

The devotional reading sums it up. Envy brought them down from the abode of nobility to the lowland of ignominy. When they would not be content with what the decree brought, they were reduced to a state of abasement, and they called down upon themselves what brings wrath upon wrath.

### In short

- The Jews sold their souls, their share in the Hereafter, for the wretched price of rejecting the Qur'an, as Mujāhid and as-Suddī explained.
- The motive was envy that Allah's grace of prophethood went to whomever He willed, meaning Muḥammad ﷺ, and no envy is worse than resenting Allah's own choice.
- "Wrath upon wrath" is the anger earned for neglecting the Torah and rejecting ʿĪsā (Jesus), compounded by the anger earned for rejecting Muḥammad ﷺ, as Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ʿIkrimah and Qatādah explained.
- Their punishment is humiliating as well as painful, the fate of the arrogant (40:60), who will be raised the size of ants, whereas a believer's punishment purifies rather than disgraces.
