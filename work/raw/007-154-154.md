<<<VERSE 7:154>>>
The anger passes and the Tablets are taken up again. "When Moses’ anger subsided, he took up the Tablets whose text contained guidance and mercy for those who stand in awe of their Lord"

### Phrase by phrase

**"When Moses’ anger subsided"** marks the end of the state he had come down the mountain in. He had thrown the Tablets down out of zeal for Allah and out of anger for His sake, and the cause of it was the worship of the calf by his people. Verse 154 opens with the anger going quiet, and one reading has the calm coming over him with his people.

**"he took up the Tablets"** is what he did once he was himself again. What he had cast down he now lifted up. Some of those who explained the Book said that when the Tablets were thrown on the ground they were shattered, and that Musa gathered up the pieces afterwards. The reports add that two of them had broken, and one account says that Allah then gave him the Torah written on other material. The word rendered here as the text of them carries the sense of a copy, that is a writing taken from a book or from other writings.

**"whose text contained guidance and mercy"** describes what was in them. Guidance from error and mercy that holds back torment are the two things named. Musa found in what was written there a guide for the people and a mercy laid over them, and the loss of the physical Tablets did not take either of those things away. One view holds that what remained after the breaking was enough, and another that the guidance and the mercy were in what was given back to him.

**"for those who stand in awe of their Lord"** names the people the Book works for. What is written there is guidance and mercy, and it reaches the one who holds his Lord in awe and stands in fear of Him. The Book of God is the best guide in this life and a certain cause of grace in the life to come, but it benefits only the person who fears God and keeps before his mind the account he will one day give. Such people are the real seekers after the truth, and when the truth is set in front of them they take it, without the twistings that come of wanting a different answer.

### What the two sentences say about the reader

There is a description of the man who is changed by the Book in the verse, and it is worth drawing out. His fear of God does not sit still in him. It wakes up his awareness, it clears the barriers from his eyes, and it keeps him ready to recognise the signs of God whenever they appear before him. God becomes the centre of his hopes and his fears together, and his life and his possessions are given over to his Lord. He does not settle into ease and think himself safe with what he holds. He lives in fear, and not in comfort with himself.

### Where the passage has come from

The book in his hands is the same one that had been laid down in anger a few verses earlier, and that is the point of the sentence being placed here. The law of the people is in the hands of a messenger who has just been angry, and the verses before this one have recorded the sin of the people, the taking of the brother to task, and the supplication of the two of them for forgiveness. After all of that, the Tablets are taken up again, and what is in them is described as guidance and mercy for the people who fear their Lord. The warning and the mercy stand in the same writing, and the passage closes its account of the calf with both.

### In short

- When the anger of Musa went quiet he took up the Tablets he had thrown down in zeal for Allah and in anger for His sake over the worship of the calf.
- The reports say the Tablets were shattered when they were thrown, that he gathered up the pieces afterwards, and that the Torah was then given to him written on other material, with knowledge of the details resting with Allah.
- What their text carried was guidance from error and mercy that holds back torment, and one reading places both in what was given back to him.
- The guidance and the mercy reach those who stand in awe of their Lord, and the verse describes how such a person is woken by his fear of God rather than settled into ease.
