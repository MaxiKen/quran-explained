<<<VERSE 5:20>>>
"And ˹remember˺ when Moses said to his people, “O my people! Remember Allah’s favours upon you when He raised prophets from among you, made you sovereign, and gave you what He had never given anyone in the world"

This verse begins the story of Musa and the Children of Israel. Musa reminds his people of three favours from Allah: prophets were raised up among them, they were made sovereign, and they were given what no one else in the world had been given. The material explains that the reminder was the preface to a command to enter the Holy Land. It places the verse in the chapter's wider call to remember the blessings of Allah and to answer them with obedience. The earlier verses had spoken of the covenant made with the twelve leaders, and this story shows how the covenant was tested.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And remember when Moses said to his people."** The material explains that Musa, to whom Allah spoke directly, reminded his people of what Allah had given them. He does this before he gives the command, which is the wise method of a teacher who first recalls the favours and then asks for the duty.

**"O my people! Remember Allah's favours upon you when He raised prophets from among you."** The material notes that there were many prophets among the Children of Israel, and that when one died another rose in his place, from the time of their forefather Ibrahim onward. The line ended with 'Isa, and the final prophet came from the offspring of Isma'il. The material calls this the most honoured of gifts, because it is a spiritual distinction that is hard to match. It cites a report from A'mash, recorded by Ibn Abi Hatim, that in one period alone a thousand prophets were sent to the Children of Israel between Musa and 'Isa.

**"Made you sovereign."** The material explains the word translated as sovereign. It reports that 'Abdur-Razzaq recorded Ibn 'Abbas saying that the favour was a servant, a wife and a house. Al-Hakim recorded that Ibn 'Abbas said it was a wife and a servant, and Qatadah said they were the first people to keep servants. The material says the word "kings" is applied to the whole people, in the sense that every one of them owned a home and household, or in the sense that a people who have a ruler are known as rulers by proxy. One reading takes this as a hint that an Islamic state is run by the people, who elect their leader and can remove him by their collective will.

**"And gave you what He had never given anyone in the world."** The material explains that the phrase means what was given in their own time, and that they were the most honoured people of their age. It cites 45:16, which says that Allah gave the Children of Israel the Scripture, the wisdom and the prophethood, and preferred them above the nations. It also cites 7:138-140, where Musa told the people that he would not seek a god for them other than Allah, who had preferred them over the nations.

### The favour and the test

The material says the favour carried a test. The people who had been slaves of Pharaoh had seen Allah destroy their enemy and make them masters of Egypt. They had seen the sea divided and the drowning of Pharaoh and his army. Yet the verses that follow show that they still refused the command. The material says this shows that favour without gratitude does not protect a people from disobedience.

The material also notes that the earlier community was given many favours, and that the Muslim ummah has its own status. It cites 2:143, which describes the Muslim community as a moderate nation, and it notes that the reports about the honour of this community are many. The material answers the apparent contradiction by saying that the blessing spoken of in this verse is limited to the time of Musa.

### In short

- Musa reminded his people of three favours: the prophets among them, their sovereignty, and what no one else had been given.
- The favours were a preface to a command, and they were given so that the people would obey.
- The verse teaches that favour from Allah must be answered with gratitude and obedience.

<<<VERSE 5:21>>>
"O my people! Enter the Holy Land which Allah has destined for you ˹to enter˺. And do not turn back or else you will become losers.”"

This verse gives the command. Musa tells his people to enter the Holy Land that Allah has destined for them, and not to turn back, because turning back would make them losers. The material identifies the Holy Land with Syria, and it explains that the command came with good news of victory, since the land had been promised to them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O my people! Enter the Holy Land which Allah has destined for you."** The material explains that the Holy Land is Syria, and it cites Ka'b al-Ahbar, who said that he had read in the Torah that Syria is a special treasure of Allah on earth, with His dear servants in it. The material explains that the Holy Land is Syria, and that some reports identify it with Jerusalem, Palestine, Jordan or Damascus. Qatadah is reported to have said that the whole of Syria is the Holy Land. The material says the land is holy because it was the home of many prophets. It also notes a report that Ibrahim, looking from a mountain in Lebanon, was told that the land he could see was the Holy Land. The material says that the different reports do not really conflict, since the reports describe different parts of one land.

**"And do not turn back or else you will become losers."** The material explains that turning back means retreating from the jihad. The loss is the loss of the promised land and of the favour that goes with it.

### The promise attached to the command

The material says that the command came with a promise, and that the jihad it called for was the way the Children of Israel were to take what was theirs. The land had been given to the believers among them through their forefather Isra'il, and Musa had received the assurance of victory. The verse therefore asks the people to act on a promise that Allah had already given. The material says that Musa's wise method was to recall the favours first, then to give the command, and then to remind them of the promise.

### In short

- Allah had destined the Holy Land for the Children of Israel, and Musa commanded them to enter it.
- They were warned not to turn back, because turning back would make them losers.
- The command came with a promise, so the people had no reason to doubt the outcome.

<<<VERSE 5:22>>>
"They replied, “O Moses! There is an enormously powerful people there, so we will never ˹be able to˺ enter it until they leave. If they do, then we will enter!”"

This verse gives the reply of the people. They say that the land is held by an enormously powerful people, and that they will never enter until those people leave. The material explains that the people were afraid of the Amalekites who held the land, and it notes that the reply was a refusal in the form of a condition.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They replied, ‘O Moses! There is an enormously powerful people there.’"** The material explains that the enemy were the Amalekites, who were said to be of the people of 'Ad. The material reports that Ibn 'Abbas, 'Ikrimah and Ibn Abi Talhah said that they were unusually tall and strong. The material notes that many stories were told about their size, but the scholars it follows hold that these tales have no basis in reason or in the law, and that the strength of the Amalekites was the subject of the common report. The Amalekites were the remnant of the people of 'Ad, and they held Syria and Jerusalem at the time of the Children of Israel, who were the Bani Isra'il of this story and the Pharaoh had been their oppressor earlier.

**"So we will never be able to enter it until they leave."** The material says that the people refused the command outright. They presented their fear as the reason, and they set a condition that they could never meet on their own.

**"If they do, then we will enter!"** The material explains that the people did not intend to fight at all. They wanted the enemy to leave the land, so that they could enter it without a battle. This was a refusal to obey Musa.

### The weakness of the reply

The material says that the reply shows a people who had seen clear signs of Allah's power but still lacked trust in Him. It contrasts their reply with the response of Abu Bakr, 'Umar and the Ansar at Badr, who were ready to fight. The material says that a reply of this kind is what breaks faith with a prophet, even when it is not a formal rejection.

### In short

- The people said the enemy was too powerful to face, and they set a condition that would never be met.
- The reply was a refusal to obey, and it showed a lack of trust in Allah's promise.
- The material contrasts it with the readiness of the believers at Badr.

<<<VERSE 5:23>>>
"Two God-fearing men—who had been blessed by Allah—said, “Surprise them through the gate. If you do, you will certainly prevail. Put your trust in Allah if you are ˹truly˺ believers.”"

This verse gives the counsel of two men who feared Allah. They tell the people to surprise the enemy through the gate, and they promise that victory will follow. They end by telling the people to put their trust in Allah, if they are truly believers. The material identifies the two men as Yusha' bin Nun and Kalib bin Yufanna, who had kept the secret of the reconnaissance mission, while the others had disclosed it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Two God-fearing men, who had been blessed by Allah. Their oneness of trust in Allah is the point of the verse, as is the oneness of the believer's reliance."** The material explains that the two men are described in two ways. The first is that they feared Allah, and the material says this means they feared Allah alone, because no one else can bring benefit or harm without His will. The second is that Allah had blessed them. The material says that any excellence a person has is a blessing from Allah. Twelve chiefs had been sent to spy on the enemy, and only these two kept faith with Musa's instruction not to reveal what they had seen.

**"Surprise them through the gate. If you do, you will certainly prevail."** The material explains that the advice was to advance boldly on the city gate. The two men may have judged, from their own assessment of the Amalekites, that they were strong in body but weak in heart, and that a surprise attack would break them. The material also says the men may have spoken from their faith in the promise of Allah that Musa had told them.

**"Put your trust in Allah if you are truly believers."** The material says that trust in Allah is what the believer relies on after taking the means, and that Allah's custom is to give victory after faith and preparation. Allah gives the reward to those who act, and the trust is the condition of the action.

### The story of the spies

The material gives an account of the spies. The twelve chiefs went ahead to Jerusalem and were captured by the Amalekites, who then freed them. When they reported back, most of them spoke of the strength of the enemy, and the people were frightened. The material says that the two men were Caleb and Yusha', and that the people of the Bani Isra'il were afraid to go to the Syrian land. Musa had asked the spies to keep their findings secret, but ten of them let it out. Only Yusha' and Kalib, whom the material also calls Caleb, kept the secret and reported faithfully to the ummah. The material says that these two men were the ones who spoke in the verse.

### In short

- Two men who feared Allah urged the people to advance through the gate, with trust in Allah.
- They had kept faith with Musa, while the others had spread fear.
- The verse teaches that victory follows faith joined with action.

<<<VERSE 5:24>>>
"˹Yet˺ they said, “O Moses! ˹Still˺ we will never enter as long as they remain there. So go—both you and your Lord—and fight; we are staying right here!”"

This verse gives the final refusal of the people. Even after the counsel of the two men, they say that they will never enter while the enemy is in the land. They tell Musa to go with his Lord and fight, while they stay where they are. The material says this reply was a grave refusal, and it became proverbial.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Yet they said, ‘O Moses! Still we will never enter as long as they remain there.’"** The material explains that the word "still" shows that they had heard the advice and rejected it. Their repeated refusal shows that they had no intention of obeying.

**"So go, both you and your Lord, and fight; we are staying right here."** The material explains that the reply could be read as mockery, which would be disbelief. The material says that the more likely meaning is that they meant "you go and fight, your Lord will help you, but we will not help you." Even so, the response was rude and grotesque. The material notes that this statement became a proverb about those who refuse to act.

### The companions at Badr

The material places this reply beside the response of the Companions at Badr. The Prophet ﷺ prayed to Allah before the battle, and Miqdad bin al-Aswad said that they would never say to the Prophet what the Children of Israel had said to Musa. He said they would defend him from the right, the left, the front and the back. The Prophet ﷺ was pleased, and Ibn Mas'ud is reported to have said that he envied Miqdad's words. The material also records Sa'd bin Mu'adh's reply at Badr, which was part of the jihad of the early ummah,, and the report of the Ansar, who said that they would follow the Prophet even if he led them to the camels at Bark al-Ghimad near Makkah. Imam Ahmad, an-Nasa'i and Ibn Hibban recorded this report, and al-Bukhari recorded Miqdad's words.

### In short

- The people refused a third time, and their refusal was a breach of the pledge they had given.
- The material says the reply was grave but not open blasphemy, that it became a proverb, and that Musa and Harun remained among them, so that the people could not have meant it as mockery. The material says the one who refused to join the jihad broke the pledge, as the Pharaoh had broken his promise before.
- The Companions at Badr gave the opposite reply, pledging to stand with the Prophet.
