<<<VERSE 4:80>>>
The right that follows the affirmation: "Whoever obeys the Messenger has truly obeyed Allah. But whoever turns away, then ˹know that˺ We have not sent you ˹O Prophet˺ as a keeper over them" (4:80). An earlier passage of this surah had affirmed the prophethood. This verse establishes its right, which is compulsory obedience, and then absolves the Prophet ﷺ of all responsibility for those who refuse it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever obeys the Messenger has truly obeyed Allah"** carries a weighty principle bearing on the authority of Allah's Messenger. The opening statement of the verse equates obedience to the Prophet ﷺ with obedience to Allah. It follows that turning away from him is turning away from the command of Allah. And since obeying Allah is a duty even by reason, obeying His Messenger stands on the same footing of duty. Whatever the Messenger ﷺ utters is not of his own desire. It is revelation inspired to him, so his commands carry the command of Allah itself. Abū Hurayrah narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, in the record of Ibn Abī Ḥātim:

> Whoever obeys me, obeys Allah, and whoever disobeys me, disobeys Allah. Whoever obeys the amīr I set over him has obeyed me, and whoever disobeys that amīr has disobeyed me.

The hadith also stands in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs.

**"But whoever turns away"** is answered with comfort for the one refused. Do not worry about him. The job of the Prophet ﷺ is only to convey. Whoever obeys him acquires happiness and success, and a reward similar to the one he earns accrues to the Prophet ﷺ himself. As for the one who turns away, he gains failure and loss, and no burden of what he does falls upon the one he turned from. Another hadith states:

> Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger acquires guidance, and whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger will only harm himself.

**"then ˹know that˺ We have not sent you ˹O Prophet˺ as a keeper over them"** releases him from guard duty over hearts. He was sent as a warner, not as a watcher set over deeds, for their affair is Allah's to deal with and He will requite them. The statement belongs to the period before the command to fight them was revealed. ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy had drawn a crooked lesson from the earlier verse about messengers being sent to be obeyed by Allah's leave, objecting that Muḥammad ﷺ commands obedience to himself instead of obedience to Allah, and this verse came down as the answer. As Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānāvī explains in the Bayān al-Qur'ān, the Prophet of Allah is not duty-bound to see that people do not indulge in disbelief. He is not charged with stopping anyone from disbelieving. His mission is to deliver the message, and once that is done his duty as a prophet stands fulfilled. If they still go about disbelieving, that is their business, for which he is in no way accountable.

### The caller accepted or refused

To acknowledge Allah's caller is to accept a human being like oneself, and that is the hard part of faith. People may accept Allah yet never extend their acceptance to Allah's caller. The real test is to acknowledge him and stand at his side, for unless one takes him to be truly a messenger of Allah, one cannot be serious toward him. There is no point in agreeing to lend support to his face, then going one's own way afterwards, or spreading irresponsible rumours about him. No plea of ignorance will win exoneration in the Hereafter for people who treat him with such carelessness. His veracity, when pondered, is self-evident in his utterances, which are inspired by Allah.

### The inner reading

The verse also alludes to the gathering, the jamʿ, of the Messenger's own state, for the divine word runs:

> Obedience to him is obedience to Us. Whoever draws near to him draws near to Us. Whoever is accepted by him is accepted by Us, and whoever is rejected by him is rejected by Us.

### In short

- Obedience to the Prophet ﷺ is obedience to Allah, for he speaks only what is revealed to him, and so his obedience is as binding as Allah's own.
- His job is conveyance, not custody. The one who obeys gains success and earns him a like reward, and the one who turns away carries his own loss.
- Ibn Ubayy's objection, that the Prophet ﷺ sought obedience to himself, is answered here: the mission is delivery of the message, and disbelief after that is the disbeliever's own business.
- Nearness to him is nearness to Allah, and acceptance by him is acceptance by Allah.

<<<VERSE 4:81>>>
The two faces: "And they say, 'We obey,' but when they leave you, a group of them would spend the night contradicting what they said" (4:81). The verse condemns a settled policy of double-dealing. Some hypocrites would say one thing with their tongues and keep another hidden in their hearts. Verse 80 had made obedience obligatory and comforted the Prophet ﷺ for those who refuse. This verse shows the sort of people his mission now had to pass through.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And they say, 'We obey'"** catches them mid-performance. The hypocrites, ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy and his followers among them, would come face to face with the Holy Prophet ﷺ and declare themselves quite obedient, accepting whatever he had commanded them with. Their speech amounted to saying that his command stood obeyed, O Muḥammad, that he should command them with whatever he willed and they would comply.

**"but when they leave you, a group of them would spend the night contradicting what they said"** turns the coin over. Once departed from his company, a section of them would gather and conspire together through the night, planning other than what they pretended in his presence and working out how they could disobey him. This duplicity of theirs caused him great pain.

**"Allah records all their schemes"** holds the threat inside the comfort. Allah has full knowledge of the plots they try to hide, and He records them through His command to His scribes, the angels responsible for recording the actions of the servants, so that they will be requited for what they harbour, written in their scrolls of deeds. A verse of like import shows the same habit of speech: "We have believed in Allah and in the Messenger, and we obey" (22:47).

**"So turn away from them"** sets the instructed attitude. They are to be pardoned and borne with, neither punished nor exposed before the people, nor feared. The command is to turn aside in forgiveness, to ignore them and pay no attention, for they will not harm him at all.

**"and put your trust in Allah. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs"** hands the whole matter over. For those who rely on Him and return to Him, He is enough as Protector, enough as Supporter and Helper, and He it is who grants triumph and sovereignty over such people. The Prophet ﷺ was told to surrender his affair to Allah and rely on Him alone, who would watch over his concerns and take care of him.

### The instruction for a leader

From here comes a lesson for anyone who leads people. Hardships of every sort come with leading people. Absurd allegations get levelled at a leader, and enemies sit near him dressed as friends and confidants. Through all of it he must stay firm and unshaken, giving himself to his mission in complete reliance on Allah. If his objective and orientation are right, he will, Allah willing, succeed.

### The inner reading

In the idiom of the inward path, their coming into his presence was a surrender to his witnessing, the mushādaha, but once they left, the light of drawing near, the iqbāl, was cut off and they returned to their darknesses. The saying of this path about their kind: each time he repents, he returns to his ignorance, just as the feeble one suffers a relapse of his illness.

### In short

- The hypocrites, Ibn Ubayy among them, pledged obedience to the face of the Prophet ﷺ and spent their nights plotting against his commands.
- Allah records the nightly schemes through His scribes, and requital for what is harboured is certain.
- The instructed response is pardon, forbearance, no exposure, no fear, and no punishment.
- The leader who keeps his orientation right outlasts allegations and false friends, for Allah is sufficient as Trustee of Affairs.
