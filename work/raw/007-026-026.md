<<<VERSE 7:26>>>
The story of Ādam and Shayṭān has just been told. The first result of Shayṭān's prompting was that the heavenly clothing of Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ (Eve) fell away, leaving them to hide themselves with leaves. Now Allah addresses all their descendants: "O children of Adam! We have provided for you clothing to cover your nakedness and as an adornment. However, the best clothing is righteousness. This is one of Allah’s bounties, so perhaps you will be mindful". The clothes people wear are a great blessing, and they should know its worth. Three kinds of clothing are named, and the last is the best.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O children of Adam!"** The address is not to Muslims alone but to every child of Ādam. This is a sharp pointer to a fact: covering the body and wearing clothes is both a human need and a natural desire. Everyone keeps to it, whatever their religion or community.

**"We have provided for you clothing"**: the Arabic word is anzalnā, "We have sent down". Here it means "We have bestowed, We have blessed you with", or "We have created it and given it to you". The clothing need not come down from the sky ready to wear. Another verse uses the same word for iron: "And We sent down iron with its great might" (57:25), though iron is dug out of the earth, as anyone can see.

In both places, "sent down" points to something. No human planning plays any part in what descends from the heavens. In the same way, the basic stuff of clothing, such as cotton, wool and fur, owes nothing to human cleverness. It is a pure gift of Allah's creative power. Human skill does come in when these materials are made into garments that suit a person's taste and temperament and keep out heat and cold. Yet even the way to that skill is shown by Allah. So eyes that see reality see all of it as a gift sent down from the heavens.

**"to cover your nakedness"**: the verb yuwārī comes from muwārāt, which means to cover, hide or conceal. The word sawʾāt is the plural of sawʾah. It is used for the parts of the body that every human being, by nature, feels it bad and shameful to uncover. Allah has sent down clothing with which people can cover those parts. This first kind of clothing is essential.

**"and as an adornment"**: the word is rīsh, literally "feathers". It means everything one adorns oneself with, the outer garments worn for beauty, and this second kind is a complement to the first. Ibn Jarīr said rīsh includes furnishings and outer clothes. Another explanation takes it as property and goods, the furniture of a house. The meaning is that a simple set of clothes would be enough to cover what must be covered. But Allah has given far more than that. He has blessed people with clothes that make them look handsome, decent, neat and civilised.

**"However, the best clothing is righteousness."** This is libās at-taqwā, "the clothing of taqwā", meaning mindfulness and fear of Allah. In some readings (qirāʾāt) the word libās ends in -a, joining it to the earlier "clothing". Read with "We have sent down", it then means: We have sent down a third clothing, the clothing of taqwā. In the well-known reading it ends in -u and begins a new sentence: everyone knows the two kinds of clothing just named, but there is a third, the clothing of taqwā, and that is the best of all.

Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿUrwah ibn az-Zubayr explained the clothing of taqwā as right conduct, good deeds and fear of Allah. Others describe it as righteous deeds and virtuous traits. Others say it is the clothing of declaring Allah's Oneness and of self-restraint, which is better than clothes of cotton. Others again say it means following what Allah commands and keeping away from what He has not allowed, which is better than any physical clothing. ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said, "When someone fears Allah, Allah covers his faults. Hence 'the clothing of taqwā'."

**"This is one of Allah’s bounties"**: the clothing mentioned here is among the signs of Allah. It is one of His marvels and a proof of His power over everything.

**"so perhaps you will be mindful"**: so that people may take heed, remember Allah's blessings to them, give thanks for them and believe. In the Arabic, the address here shifts from "you" to "they".

### What is clothing for?

Two uses of clothing are named here. The first is to cover the parts of the body that must be covered (satr). The second is to protect against heat and cold and to adorn the body. Covering is mentioned first. This shows that the real purpose of human clothing is to cover the ʿawrah, the private parts (satr al-ʿawrah).

Covering is also what marks humans off from animals. An animal's clothing is made by nature a part of its body, and it serves to protect it from heat and cold or to make it beautiful. No elaborate arrangement covers its private parts. Even so, the parts of an animal's body are placed so that they do not stay fully exposed. Some have the screen of a tail, and many others have other things that block the view.

This mention of clothing comes straight after the story of how Shayṭān led Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ astray. That placing shows that for human beings to be naked, or to expose their shame before others, is a sign of utter disgrace and the worst indecency. It also opens the way to every kind of evil and disorder.

### The tempter of Ādam and Eve still stalks

Shayṭān's first attack on humankind came through this opening, when their clothing fell from where it belonged. Even today, when Shayṭān wants to confuse and waylay people through his accomplices, he puts on a stylish front. He dresses it up as trendy, hip, hot or cool. In the end he pulls people out of their homes and into the streets and alleys, naked or nearly so. It seems that what Shayṭān calls modern progress cannot happen until women are robbed of their sense of shame and modesty and made to parade about almost nude.

### Why is covering the first duty after faith?

Shayṭān sensed this weakness in human beings and aimed his first assault at their covering. The Sharīʿah of Islam is charged with planting, protecting and bringing to flower every good in its people. So it took the matter very seriously and made covering the body the first duty after īmān, faith. Ṣalāh, ṣawm (fasting) and duties like them come after it.

Al-Fārūq al-Aʿẓam reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever puts on a new garment should say a supplication as he does so. In it he praises Allah, who gave him clothes to cover what must be covered and to look well in throughout his life.

The Prophet ﷺ also said that whoever puts on a new garment and gives the old one in ṣadaqah, charity, to someone poor and needy, comes under Allah's protection and care in every state of his life and death. This is recorded in the Musnad of Aḥmad. This hadith too recalls the two purposes for which Allah created human clothing.

### Was the first man naked?

The story of Ādam and the words of the Qur'an make one thing clear. Covering the body and wearing clothes is a natural desire and an inborn need of human beings, and it has been with them since the very beginning. Some claim that the first man went about naked and invented clothing only after passing through stages of evolution. That claim is plainly false.

### What is the clothing of taqwā?

Physical clothing covers what must be covered, protects from heat and cold, and gives beauty. In just the same way there is a spiritual clothing: right and fitting conduct, good deeds and fear of Allah. This clothing of taqwā hides human weaknesses and moral failings. It saves a person from hardship now and from loss at the end. That is why it is the best clothing anyone can wear.

Allah has made the world so that its outward things are signs of an inner reality. By thinking about what is outward, a person can reach hidden truths. Clothing is one such sign. Allah gave people clothes that protect them and also improve their looks and standing. This shows that the human spirit needs a clothing too. That clothing is taqwā, righteousness born of the fear of Allah, and it is a person's inner self. It shields him from Shayṭān's attacks. It also refines his inner self and makes him fit to live in the fine and beautiful world of Paradise.

What, then, is taqwā? It includes:

- Fearing Allah and acknowledging the truth.
- Applying the same standard to oneself as to others.
- Seeing oneself as Allah's subject.
- Behaving with modesty and humility.
- Living for the Hereafter rather than wandering lost in the world.

When a person grows these virtues, he clothes his inner self. The outer body is covered with a garment of cloth, and the soul is clothed in taqwā. If he behaves in the opposite way, it is as if he were stripping his inner self bare.

### Can a hidden deed stay hidden?

The verse also hints that an evildoer who has no fear of Allah and no care for right conduct will be disgraced in the end, however well he covers himself. Ibn Jarīr reported from ʿUthmān al-Ghanī that the Prophet ﷺ swore by Allah, in whose hand is Muhammad's life. Whatever a person does in secret, he said, Allah wraps around him like a sheet and makes known. If the deed is good its good shows, and if it is evil its evil shows. Being wrapped in it like a sheet means that everyone can see it. However secretly something is done, Allah makes its effects show on the face and the body. To support his words, the Prophet ﷺ then recited this verse, from its mention of adornment and the clothing of taqwā to "This is one of Allah’s bounties".

### How should taqwā show in dress?

The phrase "clothing of taqwā" shows that even physical clothing, which covers and beautifies the body, is really meant as a way to taqwā. That aim should show in the way a person dresses.

- The parts of the body that must be covered should be fully covered, so that one's modesty stays hidden.
- Clothes should not be so tight that the body looks naked through them.
- Dress should carry no air of pride and arrogance. It should reflect modesty and humility.
- It should not be extravagant. The cloth used should be cut to need.
- Women's dress should not be masculine and men's dress should not be feminine, for this is hateful in Allah's sight.
- One should not copy others' dress in a way that signals a departure from the settled ways of one's own community.

Along with all this comes the vital need to set one's morals and deeds right, and that is the real aim of clothing. That is why the verse ends with an invitation to understand, so that people may take the advice to heart.

### In short

- Clothing is a blessing to every child of Ādam. Covering the body is a human need and a natural desire, not a late invention.
- Allah "sent down" clothing: its raw materials are His gift, and even the skill to make garments comes from His guidance.
- Clothing covers and adorns. Covering comes first, and after faith it is the first duty.
- The best clothing is taqwā: right conduct, good deeds and fear of Allah, which hides faults and guards against Shayṭān.
- Dress should be modest, not tight, proud or extravagant, and should not cross between the sexes or imitate others.
