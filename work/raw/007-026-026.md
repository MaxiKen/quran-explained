<<<VERSE 7:26>>>
The story of the garden ends and the address changes hands. "O children of Adam! We have provided for you clothing to cover your nakedness and as an adornment. However, the best clothing is righteousness. This is one of Allah's bounties, so perhaps you will be mindful". Everything before this verse was told about two people. This verse is spoken to everyone who comes after them, and it turns the story they have just read into something they can wear.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O children of Adam!"** is the address, and the important thing about it is who is being addressed. This is not a message to Muslims alone. It goes to every child of Adam, and the width of the address is a pointer in itself. Covering the parts of the body that need covering, and wearing clothes at all, is a human need and a natural desire. Every community and every religion practises it, and none of them had to be taught it.

**"We have provided for you clothing to cover your nakedness"** names the first use of dress. The Arabic word behind "cover" comes from a root meaning to hide or conceal, and the word for nakedness is the plural of a word for a part of the body which, by nature, all human beings consider it shameful to uncover. So the sense of the words is that Allah has sent down for people a dress with which they can cover the parts of the body whose exposure brings shame.

**"and as an adornment"** names the second use. The word here covers whatever is worn for beauty and for dignity, and Ibn Jarīr said it takes in furniture and outer clothes as well. The point of naming it second is that a plain garment is enough to do the covering. Allah gave people more than that. What they were given includes things that make a person look well, and decent, neat and civilised.

**"However, the best clothing is righteousness"** names the third, and puts it above the other two. ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam commented on these words that when a person fears Allah, Allah covers his faults, and that this is what the garment of taqwā means. In some of the readings (qirāʾāt) the word for garment is vowelised differently, so that the sentence reads as a further gift: We have sent down a third dress, that of taqwā. On the reading that is well known, the meaning is that everyone knows the first two garments, and that there is a third kind, the garment of taqwā, and that this is the best of all of them. Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿUrwah ibn az-Zubayr explained the garment of taqwā as right conduct, good deeds and the fear of Allah, and they placed the oneness of God at the head of it.

**"This is one of Allah's bounties, so perhaps you will be mindful"** closes the verse. The invitation is to understand what has been said and to take the instruction from it.

### How dress was sent down

The word used for what Allah did with clothing is that He sent it down, and this is worth a moment. The expression does not require clothes to come out of the sky ready to wear. The same word is used elsewhere of iron (57:25), which is dug out of the ground for everyone to see. What the word points to in both places is the way no human planning is the active agent in it. Cotton and wool and the like are the mother material of dress, and human ingenuity had no part in bringing them into being. They are simply a gift of the great creative power of Allah.

Human skill does begin to work after that, in making the materials up into clothes to fit a person's taste, his temperament and his need to be safe from heat and cold. And even the road to that skill was shown by Allah. So an eye that sees reality sees the whole of it as nothing but a gift sent down.

### The two uses, and which one comes first

Two uses of dress are identified in the verse, and the order they are given in is not an accident. The first is to cover the parts of the body that need covering. The second is protection from heat and cold, and the adornment of the body.

Placing the covering first tells the reader what the real purpose of human dress is. It also marks the line between human beings and the animals. The dress of an animal has been built into its body. What it does is protect the animal from heat and cold, or make it beautiful. No elaborate arrangement has been made for it to cover what a person would call his nakedness, though the shapes of particular parts of an animal's body have been placed so that they are not left wholly exposed. Some have a tail that acts as a screen, and others have something else in the way of a view.

The connection with the story that has just been told is direct. The first result of the satanic whispering was that the heavenly apparel of Adam and Hawwāʾ came off and left them uncovered, and they began hiding themselves with leaves. So the mention of dress here tells the reader what nakedness is. For a human being, to be naked, or to expose his shameful parts in front of others, is a sign of utter disgrace and of indecency at its worst. It is also the doorway to every kind of evil and disorder.

### The first attack, and the one still being made

It is worth noticing where the first assault of Satan landed. It came at this opening, and it came against human beings at the exact point where their dress had fallen from where it belonged. And the same thing is still going on. When Satan works through his accomplices to confuse people and waylay them, he picks a fashionable front for it. He calls it the trend, he calls it cool, and in the end he pulls people out of their houses into the streets naked or almost naked. What Satan has classified as modern advancement does not come about unless women are first stripped of their sense of shame and modesty and made to parade about nearly uncovered.

So the assault was answered by legislation, and it was answered at the earliest point available. The Sharīʿah took the matter with the seriousness it deserves, because it is responsible for planting, protecting and bringing to flower every good thing in its people. It therefore made the covering of the body the first duty after faith itself. The prayer, the fast and the duties like them all come after that.

### What the Prophet ﷺ taught about clothing

ʿUthman al-Ghanī reported a hadith of the Holy Prophet ﷺ. When a person puts on a new garment, he should say this as he puts it on:

> Praise be to Allah who has given me clothing with which I cover the parts of my body that need covering, and with which I look well in my life.

The same Companion reported another instruction. When a person puts on something new and gives the old one away as ṣadaqah, charity, to someone poor and in need, he comes under the protection and patronage of Allah in every state of his life and of his death. This is from the Musnad of Ahmad, and Ibn Jarīr is the one who reports the first of the two. In both of them the two considerations behind the creation of human dress are brought back to mind.

### Covering the body is natural

The story of Adam and the words of the Qur'an settle a further question. Wanting to cover the body and wanting to dress in something is built into a human being, and it has been there from the very beginning. There is a claim that the first man went about naked, and that the making of clothes came only after humanity had passed through a series of stages. That claim is plainly false.

### The garment that covers what the body cannot

The first two garments are visible things. The third is not, and it does more. A physical garment covers the parts of the body, protects against heat and cold, and gives beauty and embellishment. The garment of taqwā works in the same way for a person's inner life. It conceals his weaknesses and his moral shortcomings, and it delivers him from hardship here and from loss at the end. That is why it is the best of the garments anyone can put on.

There is a warning attached to it. A person who does evil and has no fear of Allah, and who does not care whether he acts rightly, will be disgraced in the end, however well he covers himself up. Ibn Jarīr reports from ʿUthman al-Ghanī, and the Holy Prophet ﷺ swore an oath on it. By Allah in whose hands rests the life of Muhammad, no deed a person does in secret stays private. Allah wraps it around him the way a sheet is wrapped, and then He announces it. If the deed was good, the announcement is good. If the deed was evil, the announcement is evil. A deed wrapped around a person like a sheet is a deed everyone can see. However quietly a man may act, the marks of it are made to show on his face and in his body. The Prophet ﷺ recited this verse to confirm it, with its mention of the dress that adorns and of the dress of taqwā that is the best.

### The purpose that a physical garment serves

The expression "the garment of taqwā" carries a further indication, and the book of tafsīr known as Rūḥ al-Maʿānī is where these readings of it are gathered. It means that the physical dress, which covers the body properly and also makes it beautiful, is really a route to the ability to be mindful of Allah. And once a person has that ability, it ought to show in the way he dresses. The parts of the body that must be covered should be properly covered, and a person should make sure that his modesty stays concealed. The body should not be left uncovered, and the garment should not be so tight that the parts look as though they were bare.

From there the requirements continue. The dress should carry no flavour of pride and arrogance. It should show modesty and humility instead. It should not be extravagant, and the material used should be cut to the need. The clothing of women should not be a man's clothing, and the clothing of men should not be a woman's, which is hateful and repugnant in the sight of Allah. And there should be no imitation in dress that copies other people, since that is a sign of drifting away from the settled norms of one's own community.

All of this runs alongside something more important, which is the need to put a person's character and his conduct right. That is the real purpose of dress, and the verse ends by inviting the reader to see it, since the whole matter is one of the signs of Allah and the point of a sign is to make a person willing to be told.

### The outward sign and the inward reality

God made the world on a pattern in which outward things are signs of something inward, and a person who thinks about what he sees outside can arrive at truths that are hidden. Human attire is one of those outer signs. The clothes a person has protect him, and they also improve his looks and his standing. That points to something about his inner life, which also needs a covering.

That covering is the righteousness that is born of the fear of God, and it stands for a person's inner self. On one side it protects a man from the attacks of Satan, and on the other it improves what is inside him and fits him for a place in the fine and beautiful world of Paradise.

What is this righteousness? It is fear of God. It is acknowledging the truth. It is holding to the same standard for oneself as for other people. It is treating oneself as a subject of God. It is behaving with modesty and humility. It is living for the Hereafter rather than wandering off after the world. A person who has built these qualities into himself has dressed his inner self, and while his body is under a garment of cloth, his soul is under the garment of taqwā. When his conduct is the opposite of all this, he is in effect laying his inner self bare.

### In short

- The address is to all the children of Adam and not to Muslims alone, because covering the body is a human need and a natural desire shared by everyone.
- Three garments are named: the one that covers, the one that adorns, and the garment of taqwā, and the third is the best of them.
- The word "sent down" does not require clothes to fall from the sky. It points to the mother materials of dress as a gift, not the product of human planning.
- Covering comes first among the uses of dress, and that is what marks human beings off from the animals and what the Sharīʿah made the first duty after faith.
- The garment of taqwā is right conduct, good deeds and the fear of Allah. It hides a person's weaknesses, and it is carried in the way he dresses, which must be modest, unostentatious and his own.
