<<<VERSE 4:75>>>
The question that shames the hesitant: "And what is it with you? You do not fight in the cause of Allah and for oppressed men, women, and children who cry out, 'Our Lord! Deliver us from this land of oppressors! Appoint for us a saviour; appoint for us a helper—all by Your grace.'". Rather than a straight command, the Qur'an elected the interrogative of rebuke: what is wrong with you that you do not fight?

### Phrase by phrase

**"You do not fight in the cause of Allah"** speaks of fighting in His path, in obedience of Allah, against the people of Makkah, and the hint of the rebuke is that jihād under such conditions is a natural duty, not doing which is far from a reasonable man.

**"and for oppressed men, women, and children who cry out"** names the weak, ill-treated and oppressed left behind in Makkah, restless because of having to remain there, prevented by the disbelievers from emigrating. Ibn ʿAbbās and his father said: my mother and I were among them, as al-Bukhārī records.

**"'Our Lord! Deliver us from this land of oppressors!'"** identifies the town as Makkah, whose people are oppressors through unbelief, idolaters all. The like stands in the verse: "Many a town, stronger than your town which has driven you out" (47:13).

**"'Appoint for us a saviour; appoint for us a helper'"** asks for protectors and helpers, one to take charge of their affair and one to defend them against the oppressors, all by His grace.

### The left behind

The Muslims unable to migrate, for physical weakness or insufficiency of resources, were later stopped by the disbelievers themselves and hurt and harassed in every way to turn them from their faith. Their names are preserved in the exegetic works: Ibn ʿAbbās and his mother, Ṣuhaym ibn Hishām, al-Walīd ibn al-Walīd and Abū Jandal ibn Sahl, as al-Qurṭubī's record carries them. They faced tyranny and torture with unflinching faith, showing no sign of surrender, and kept praying to Allah Almighty for deliverance, which was finally accepted when He commanded the Muslims to wage jihād and rescue the oppressed from coercion and persecution.

### The two prayers answered

The supplication asked two things: rescue from the town, and a supporter and helper. Both were granted. Some were given the opportunity to move out, which set them on the path out, fulfilling the first wish, and some remained until the Conquest of Makkah, when the Holy Prophet ﷺ appointed ʿAttāb ibn Asīd, spelled Ibn Usayd in one reading, as their caretaker, who had the oppressed delivered from their oppressors and sought justice for the wronged from those who had wronged them. Allah answered their prayers and made the Prophet ﷺ their defender and ʿItāb their protecting friend. The prayer of the weak was thus one of the very reasons the command to fight came down, a lesson in itself: praying to Allah is the panacea for all hardships.

### In short

- The rebuke, not the command, opens the verse, for fighting for the oppressed needs no persuading of a reasonable man.
- The trapped believers of Makkah, from Ibn ʿAbbās and his mother to Abū Jandal ibn Sahl, prayed the two-part prayer under torture.
- Both parts were answered, the exit for some and ʿAttāb ibn Asīd's justice at the Conquest for the rest.

<<<VERSE 4:76>>>
The two banners named: "Believers fight for the cause of Allah, whereas disbelievers fight for the cause of the Devil. So fight against Satan's ˹evil˺ forces. Indeed, Satan's schemes are ever weak". Verse 75 asked why they do not fight. Verse 76 shows what each side is actually fighting for.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Believers fight for the cause of Allah"** means fighting in obedience to Allah and for His pleasure. The purpose of the believer's striving is that the Divine Law becomes operative in this world and the authority of Allah reigns supreme, for He is the master of all, the Creator of the universe in His infinite wisdom, whose Law is based on pure justice. When the rule of justice is established, there is peace which sustains.

**"whereas disbelievers fight for the cause of the Devil"** means obedience to Shayṭān, the Ṭāghūt of the earlier verses, Abū Sufyān and his companions battling for the idols. The disbelievers seek to spread the message of kufr and work for its ascendency, eager that satanic powers rule the world, so that kufr and shirk are enforced through the universal order, dressed with the sheen of the seemingly positive. Since kufr and shirk are the ways of Satan, the disbelievers end up helping Satan in doing his job.

**"So fight against Satan's ˹evil˺ forces"** names his friends, the supporters of his religion and the soldiers of the devil, and the believer defeats them with the strength drawn from God.

**"Indeed, Satan's schemes are ever weak"** — his deceptive plans are insubstantial and cannot harm true Muslims, for their helper is Allah Himself, and Satan's plotting cannot stand up to God's plotting against the disbelievers.

### Badr and the fleeing helper

This is what happened at the battle of Badr. Satan stood by the disbelievers, dishing out tall claims: nobody would overpower them that day, he was the helper at their side, and his armies would be there in support. When the actual fighting began, he did advance with his army, but at the first sight of the angels arriving to help the Muslims his whole design collapsed, and he turned in flight. The word he flung back at his accomplices severed the alliance: no responsibility rested on him for them, he saw what they could not see, and he feared Allah, whose punishment is severe, as al-Mazharī relates.

The feebleness is not unconditional. Two conditions emerge from verse 76 itself. The person against whom Satan tries his guile must be a believer, from the words "those who believe", and the effort he makes must be for the pleasure of Allah alone, with no selfish motive behind it, from "fight in the way of Allah". If either condition fails, the feebleness of Satan's guile is no longer guaranteed. Ibn ʿAbbās said: when you see Satan, do not think twice, attack him, and then recited this very verse of the feeble guile, in the record of al-Qurṭubī.

The servants of God know the pattern from within: a preacher who warns people of the perils of Hell and guides them toward Paradise, refraining from quarrels on materialistic or political issues, is still opposed and challenged by evil-doers, for his utterances assail their egos, and the spread of his message threatens them with economic and political danger. Finding no valid argument against his arguments, they resort to violence, the followers of the path of Satan fighting a servant of God for no crime but his truthfulness.

### The inner reading

Those sincerely devoted to God prefer nothing to God and hold nothing back from God, ever against their lower selves for His sake. The states of the disbelievers are the reverse. Therefore He strengthened and encouraged the believers with the command to fight the friends of Satan without fear of them, His own undertaking being the care of their affairs and their sufficiency against enemies.

### In short

- The believer fights for the reign of the Divine Law and lasting peace, the disbeliever for the ascendency of kufr dressed as progress.
- At Badr, Satan promised armies and fled at the sight of the angels, the proof of his feeble guile.
- The feebleness has two conditions, faith and sincerity of purpose, and without either the guarantee lifts, for the jihād of verse 76 is what the promise rides on.
- Ibn ʿAbbās's counsel, attack him on sight, rests on this verse, and the inward reading rests on God's own sufficiency.
