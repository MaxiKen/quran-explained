<<<VERSE 5:48>>>
"We have revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ this Book with the truth, as a confirmation of previous Scriptures and a supreme authority on them. So judge between them by what Allah has revealed, and do not follow their desires over the truth that has come to you. To each of you We have ordained a code of law and a way of life. If Allah had willed, He would have made you one community, but His Will is to test you with what He has given ˹each of˺ you. So compete with one another in doing good. To Allah you will all return, then He will inform you ˹of the truth˺ regarding your differences"

This verse turns from the earlier books to the Qur'an. It says that the Qur'an came with the truth, confirms what was before it, and watches over it. It then commands the Prophet ﷺ to judge by what Allah has revealed and not by the desires of the People of the Book. The verse ends with a teaching about the differing laws of the prophets and a call to good deeds.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have revealed to you this Book with the truth."** The material explains that the Qur'an is sent down with the truth, which comes from Allah and is beyond doubt. The word "with the truth" links the revelation to its source and to its content.

**"As a confirmation of previous Scriptures and a supreme authority on them."** The material gives several readings of the Arabic word translated as supreme authority. Ibn 'Abbas said it means trustworthy. Mujahid, Qatadah and As-Suddi read it as witness. Al-Awfi reported Ibn 'Abbas as saying it means dominant. The material says these meanings are close, and that together they show the Qur'an is trustworthy, a witness and dominant over every Scripture before it. Whatever in the earlier books agrees with the Qur'an is true, and whatever disagrees with it is false. The Qur'an alone preserves the original truth of those books, since the earlier bearers could not keep them intact. Allah promised to guard the Qur'an, as the verse of 15:9 states.

**"So judge between them by what Allah has revealed."** The material explains that the Prophet ﷺ was given the choice to judge or turn away when the Jews came to him, but the command in this verse is to judge by the Qur'an and by the law of the earlier prophets that the Qur'an approves. Ibn 'Abbas reported that the choice was replaced by this command.

**"And do not follow their desires over the truth that has come to you."** The material says that the desires in question are the ideas that turned the Jews away from what Allah had revealed. The verse warns the Prophet ﷺ not to move from the truth to suit them.

**"To each of you We have ordained a code of law and a way of life."** The material explains that the word for code is a clear path. Every prophet had a distinct law, and that law was partly or fully abrogated by the law of a later prophet. The law of Muhammad ﷺ is the last, and it abrogated all those before it.

**"If Allah had willed, He would have made you one community."** The material says this is a general statement of the power of Allah. He could have made all mankind follow one religion and one law that would never change. He chose different laws in order to test His servants in their obedience.

**"But His Will is to test you with what He has given each of you."** The material says the trial is in what has been given, which for the believers is the Book. The test is to see who obeys the command given to them at each time.

**"So compete with one another in doing good."** The material explains that the believers should race to good deeds, which are obedience to Allah and following the law of the final prophet. Ad-Dahhak read the address as directed to the community of Muhammad ﷺ, though the material says the wider sense is clearer.

**"To Allah you will all return, then He will inform you regarding your differences."** The material says the return is to Allah on the Day of Resurrection. Those who were sincere will be rewarded, and those who rejected the truth and turned to other paths without proof will be punished.

### The wisdom of the differing laws

The material gives a full answer to the question of why the laws of the prophets differ. The differences are in the subsidiary rules, while the principle of worship and obedience is the same. The material says that the true worship is in being a servant who obeys, and that prayer, fasting, pilgrimage, zakah, remembrance and recitation are means to that end and not ends in themselves. The material gives examples. Prayer is forbidden at certain times, and praying then is a sin. Fasting on the two Eids is forbidden, and it is a sin to fast on them. Gathering on the plain of Arafat is rewarded only on the ninth of Dhul-Hijjah. The same principle applies to every act of worship. When it is done as commanded, it is worship. When it is stopped at a certain limit, it becomes forbidden.

The material says that when people turn a habit or a custom into worship, they begin to ignore the open commands of Allah and His Messenger. This is how innovations enter the religion over time, and it was the main cause of the changes that happened to the earlier books.

The material gives a second reason for the difference. Human beings differ in temper and taste, and the times differ too, so the laws of the subsidiary rules were changed to suit the condition of the people. The material explains that abrogation is not a mistake or a change of mind by the Lawgiver. It is like the medicine a physician prescribes in stages, since the physician knows in advance what the patient will need.

The material also explains that the change of the qiblah shows this, as the verse of 2:143 makes clear. The Children of Israel were commanded to face Jerusalem in prayer, and they came to think that prayer meant facing Jerusalem. The qiblah was then changed to the Kabah, and the change showed who worshipped the building and who worshipped Allah. The material says that no prophet will come after Muhammad ﷺ, so such a change will not happen again. The true aim of worship remains the same, which is to turn to Allah directly with a mind free of outward ceremony.

### Musa, the Injil and the abrogated rules

The material says that Allah praised the Torah he sent down to Musa, the one He spoke to directly, and commanded its people to follow it until its law was abrogated. He then praised the Injil and commanded its people to follow it. The rules that were abrogated were not evil in their time. They were haram or halal according to the need of that time, and the change to a new law was a test of obedience. The material gives the example of the rule of qisas, which the Torah set out and the Qur'an kept. Ibn Zayd and As-Suddi are reported to have read the earlier laws in the same way, as stages in one religion. The material says that the rule of mansukh in the Qur'an means a rule that was replaced, and that replacement is itself the wisdom of the lawgiver.

### The Jews and the offer of conversion

The material explains that some Jewish leaders came to the Prophet ﷺ with an offer. They would become Muslims if he judged their case in their favour. The verse was revealed to warn him against deciding in such a way, and to command him to judge by the law of Allah with equity and justice. The material says the offer was not his concern.

### The summary of the lessons

The material lists five lessons from the verse. First, the rulings of an earlier law remain in force until the Qur'an abrogates them, as in the Torah's rule of equal retaliation and the stoning for adultery. Second, the rules of the earlier scriptures that the Qur'an did not abrogate are still binding. Third, judging against the law of Allah is kufr when a person believes it is false, and it is injustice or sin when he accepts it as true and still acts against it in practice. Fourth, taking a bribe is forbidden, and in a court case it is especially grave. Fifth, the prophets and their laws agree in principle, and differ in details for reasons of wisdom.

### In short

- The Qur'an came with the truth, confirms the earlier books, and watches over them.
- The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to judge by the law of Allah and not by the desires of the People of the Book.
- The different laws of the prophets test the people, and the true aim of worship is obedience to Allah.

<<<VERSE 5:49>>>
"And judge between them ˹O Prophet˺ by what Allah has revealed, and do not follow their desires. And beware, so they do not lure you away from some of what Allah has revealed to you. If they turn away ˹from Allah's judgment˺, then know that it is Allah's Will to repay them for some of their sins, and that many people are indeed rebellious"

This verse repeats the command to judge by the revelation and warns the Prophet ﷺ against being led away from it. It says that if the Jews turn away from the judgement, Allah will punish them for some of their sins, and that most people are rebellious.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And judge between them by what Allah has revealed, and do not follow their desires."** The material says this is a repeated command. The Prophet ﷺ is to judge by the law of Allah, and not by the wishes of the people who came to him. The material links this to the report that Ibn 'Abbas gave of the choice being replaced.

**"And beware, so they do not lure you away from some of what Allah has revealed to you."** The material explains that the Jews were enemies, and the warning is to beware of their plan. They wanted to lead the Prophet ﷺ away from part of the revelation, for example by setting aside the stoning for adultery. The material says that they were liars, treacherous and disbelievers.

**"If they turn away, then know that it is Allah's Will to repay them for some of their sins."** The material explains that their turning away was itself a sin, and that Allah's decree included punishment for their earlier sins. The material says the punishment in the world is for some of their sins, and the greater punishment is in the Hereafter.

**"And that many people are indeed rebellious."** The material says most people disobey their Lord and turn from the truth. It cites 12:103, which says that most people will not believe even if you desire it eagerly, and 6:116, which warns that following most of those on earth will lead one away from the path of Allah.

### The report of the four leaders

The material gives an account from Ibn Ishaq and Ibn 'Abbas. Ibn Zayd and Abu al-Yaman are also named in the reports on the trial of the Prophet ﷺ in this matter. Ka'b bin Asad, Ibn Suluba, Abdullah ibn Suriya and Shas bin Qays said to each other that they should go to Muhammad and try to lead him from his religion. They said that if he would rule in favour of them against their people, they would follow him, and the Jews would follow them. The Prophet ﷺ refused, and this verse was revealed. Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim recorded the report.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to judge by the revelation and not to follow the desires of the People of the Book.
- Their turning away is a sin, and Allah will punish them for some of their sins in this world and all of them in the Hereafter.
- Most people are rebellious, and the verse cites 12:103 and 6:116 in support.

<<<VERSE 5:50>>>
"Is it the judgment of ˹pre-Islamic˺ ignorance they seek? Who could be a better judge than Allah for people of sure faith"

This verse ends the passage with a question. It asks whether the people seek the judgement of the age of ignorance, and it says that no one is a better judge than Allah for those who are certain in their faith.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Is it the judgment of pre-Islamic ignorance they seek?"** The material explains that jahiliyyah is the age before Islam, when people ruled by opinion, desire and custom. The question is one of rebuke. The material says that those who turn away from the law of Allah are seeking this kind of judgement.

**"Who could be a better judge than Allah for people of sure faith?"** The material says that no one can judge better than Allah, who knows everything, can do all things, and is just. The people of sure faith are those who understand the law of Allah and are certain that He is the best judge, and that He is more merciful to His creation than a mother to her child.

### The rulings of custom

The material gives an example from later times. The Tatars followed the law that their king, Genghis Khan, wrote, called Al-Yasiq. It drew on rulings from several religions and from his own opinion and desires. The material says that whoever follows such laws over the law of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger is a disbeliever, and deserves to be fought until he returns to the judgement of Allah and His Messenger. The material also gives a hadith from al-Tabarani, which Abu al-Yaman narrated in a similar form in the collection of Al-Bukhari. Its warning is against the Muslim who goes after the customs of the age of ignorance, and against the one who spills blood without cause.

The material says the unseen matters of faith, which the people of sure faith accept, are the reason the verse speaks of them. Ibn Zayd read the verse as a warning to those who choose to reject the unseen.

### In short

- The question rebukes those who seek the judgement of the age of ignorance.
- Allah is the best judge for those who are certain of their faith, since He knows all and is more merciful than a mother to her child.
- Preferring a law of opinion or custom over the law of Allah is a grave error, and the material records a hadith against seeking the ways of ignorance.
