<<<VERSE 5:48>>>
After honouring the Torah and the Gospel, the passage turns to the Book given to Muhammad ﷺ: "We have revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ this Book with the truth, as a confirmation of previous Scriptures and a supreme authority on them." The Qur'an confirms what those books truly contain and judges what has been changed in them.

The verse then gives the Messenger ﷺ his rule for judgment, explains why revealed laws have differed, and turns the reader toward the response that matters. Differences are not a reason for indifference. They are the setting of a test, and the appropriate answer is to race toward good.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ this Book with the truth"** describes revelation from Allah, beyond doubt, brought by Jibra'il (Gabriel). It distinguishes truth from falsehood rather than leaving the matter to preference.

**"as a confirmation of previous Scriptures and a supreme authority on them."** The Qur'an confirms the earlier books and stands over them as a guardian. Whatever in them agrees with it is true, and whatever disagrees is false, as Ibn Jarir explains.

**"So judge between them by what Allah has revealed"** commands the Messenger ﷺ to decide among the People of the Scripture according to revelation. Ibn Jarir explains this as judging by what Allah revealed and by what He approved of the earlier prophetic law.

**"and do not follow their desires over the truth that has come to you."** Their wishes, including the substitution of flogging for stoning in the reported case, are not a standard for judgment. Desire is set against truth that has already been made clear.

**"To each of you We have ordained a code of law and a way of life."** Each community received a shir'ah, a revealed law, and a minhaj, a clear path of obligations and practices. Ibn Abi Hatim records Ibn 'Abbas explaining the first word as a clear path.

**"If Allah had willed, He would have made you one community"** affirms that unity under one unchanging law was entirely within Allah's power. Its absence is not a limitation upon Him.

**"but His Will is to test you with what He has given ˹each of˺ you."** The given thing is explained through the Book itself in one reading, and through scripture, obligations and practices generally. The test concerns obedience to the command actually in force.

**"So compete with one another in doing good."** The response to difference is not argument for its own sake but eagerness in obedience. Ad-Dahhak reads this as addressed particularly to the ummah of Muhammad ﷺ, while the broader reading addressed to all communities is described as more apparent.

**"To Allah you will all return, then He will inform you ˹of the truth˺ regarding your differences"** places every dispute before the Resurrection. Allah will disclose the truth and requite each according to deeds, rewarding sincerity and punishing those who left clear proof for their own preferences.

### What does the Qur'an's authority over earlier books mean?

Muhaymin is explained as trustworthy, entrusted, witness and dominant. Through Abu Ishaq and At-Tamimi, Sufyan Ath-Thawri reports Ibn 'Abbas giving the sense of being entrusted over the earlier books. 'Ali bin Abi Talhah reports him calling the Qur'an trustworthy over every preceding divine book.

As-Suddi, Ibn Zayd, Mujahid and Qatadah are named with that explanation, along with 'Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Muhammad bin Ka'b, 'Atiyyah, Al-Hasan and 'Ata' Al-Khurasani. Al-Walibi reports Ibn 'Abbas saying witness, with Mujahid, Qatadah and As-Suddi agreeing. Al-'Awfi reports him saying dominant.

The treatment regards these meanings as closely related rather than contradictory. The Qur'an is trustworthy, a witness and dominant over what came before. As the final book, it gathers the good of earlier scriptures and contains what they did not.

A further explanation describes the Qur'an as the touchstone that shows which parts of earlier books kept their original form. The different scriptures are treated as editions of one divine Book, called al-kitab, sharing the same content. Later communities could not preserve theirs unaltered, so the Qur'an serves as the authentic expression of Allah's will against which their contents are tested.

This guardianship is not a claim that the earlier revelations were worthless. Their truth is honoured precisely because the Qur'an confirms it. It is alteration, not the original giving of the Torah to Musa (Moses) or the Gospel to 'Isa (Jesus), that the Qur'an exposes.

Allah also guarantees the protection of this book. He calls it dhikr, remembrance, and swears by His own honour that He will guard it from corruption (15:9). The confirmation of Allah's oneness and of the shared laws is part of what it preserves.

### Why was an earlier option to decline judgment removed?

Ibn Abi Hatim reports Ibn 'Abbas saying that the Prophet ﷺ previously had a choice between judging these parties and referring them to their own law. This verse then commanded him to judge between them by the Qur'an.

That reading relates to the sequence begun earlier in the chapter, where declining a case was permitted and justice was obligatory if judgment was undertaken. The legal discussion distinguishes protected citizens from parties under a narrower agreement. Here the emphasis is that the standard of judgment is revelation, never the litigants' preference.

### Why did revealed laws differ at all?

All prophets came from Allah, and all scriptures and laws come from Him. Their agreement in principle is therefore expected. The differences concern subsidiary rulings, and the verse explains them as an appointed test rather than a defect.

Had Allah willed, humanity could have been a single community under one law that was never replaced. He instead gave each prophet a law appropriate to his people and time. Later laws replaced earlier ones in part or wholly, until the law sent with Muhammad ﷺ abrogated what preceded it.

The test is whether a person recognises the reality of servitude. A servant obeys the command in force, even when it requires leaving a practice dear to him and long treated as ancestral religion. Someone who fails that test treats one particular code as an end in itself and stops attending to the divine call.

### Does replacing a ruling mean the earlier one was wrong?

No. Nasikh and mansukh, the replacing and replaced ruling, do not imply that the Lawgiver first made an error and then corrected it. The earlier command was right and necessary for its period, and the later command is right and necessary for its own.

The comparison given is a physician's prescription. A doctor who knows that a symptom will appear after three days changes the medicine when the condition changes. The first prescription was not a mistake, and the second is not a correction of ignorance. Each is correct for its stage.

A related explanation gives a different emphasis. The essential teachings of religion have been one throughout, given to every prophet with changes in language and arrangement of commands. The outward framework changed because, over time, ritual form tends to take the place of inward reality and become treated as holy in itself.

On that account, the change of qiblah, the direction faced in prayer, illustrates the point. The Children of Israel were commanded to face Jerusalem, and gradually came to identify prayer with facing that direction. The Ka'bah was then appointed, separating those attached to the earlier custom from those following the command (2:143).

The explanation draws the conclusion that the change exposed who worships walls and doors and who worships Allah. Since no further prophet will come, no such change remains open. The inward purpose continues: follow the appointed form without granting it the status of the real objective, and direct the prayer itself to Allah.

### How does this understanding of worship affect ordinary practice?

The reality of 'ibadah, worship, is obedience rather than the performance of particular acts as ends. Salah, prayer, sawm, fasting, Hajj, pilgrimage, zakah, almsgiving, dhikr, remembrance, and recitation all serve that obedience.

That is why the same act can be reward or sin depending on the command. Prayer during times when it is forbidden brings no reward but sin. Fasting on the days of 'Id al-Fitr and 'Id al-Adha is unlawful. Gathering on the plain of 'Arafat is the greatest act of worship on the ninth of Dhul-Hijjah, but is not a rewarded act of worship on another day appointed in its place.

Ignorance of this principle lets habits and inherited customs acquire the standing of worship. Once that happens, people may ignore plain commands from Allah and His Messenger ﷺ. The explanation identifies this as a principal source of innovations and deviations that later become attached to religion as though part of it, and as a major cause of alteration in earlier books and laws.

### What practical rules does the discussion derive?

It records five conclusions from this passage and the verses around it. First, an injunction of an earlier revealed law remains in force until abrogated by the Qur'an or revelation. The reported cases of equal qisas and of stoning were in the Torah and retained.

Second, retaliation for wounds stated with reference to the Torah was enforced by the Prophet ﷺ. On that basis the majority of scholars treat unreplaced rulings of earlier laws as valid and binding in Islamic law.

Third, judging contrary to what Allah revealed is disbelief under certain conditions, particularly when joined with a belief that the revealed ruling is not true. Where a person accepts it as true but acts against it, the conduct is injustice and sinful disobedience.

Fourth, taking a bribe is absolutely unlawful, and taking one in a judicial case is described as far graver.

Fifth, the prophets and their laws agree in principle while differing in subsidiary rulings, and that difference rests on wisdom rather than contradiction.

### What does competing in good look like?

The competition is in obedience to the law now in force and belief in the final Book. One account directs the Muslim community to vie with other nations in obligations, practices and righteous works, and another reads it as hastening to acts of obedience.

An inward explanation distinguishes the shariah, the revealed law given to everyone, from haqiqah, the inner reality granted to some. The avenue is what Muhammad al-Mustafa ﷺ brought, and the method is the lamp Allah holds beside the heart. The avenue is the message heard from the Messenger ﷺ, and the method is the light found in the innermost self.

That distinction does not separate the two as rival religions. The inward light is access to what the outward law commands, not permission to abandon it. The explanation then describes different people's striving: renunciants compete by letting go of this world, worshippers by cutting off caprice, those who recognise Allah by negating their own wishes, and those who speak of His oneness by leaving people behind and forgetting both this world and the next.

### In short

- The Qur'an confirms earlier revelation and stands as the trustworthy measure of what remains true in it.
- Judgment must follow revealed truth, never the litigants' desires.
- Different laws test obedience, and replacing a ruling does not make the earlier command a mistake.
- Worship is obedience, so the same act can be reward or sin according to the command in force.
- Differences will be settled when all return to Allah. The proper response now is to strive in good.

<<<VERSE 5:49>>>
The command is repeated with a warning attached: "And judge between them ˹O Prophet˺ by what Allah has revealed, and do not follow their desires." Repetition is not redundant. It marks how persistently the parties tried to turn judgment into a bargaining device, and how firmly the standard must remain revelation.

The verse also tells the Messenger ﷺ what to expect if they refuse his decision. Their turning away does not defeat the truth. It exposes them to a consequence Allah has already willed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And judge between them ˹O Prophet˺ by what Allah has revealed"** emphasises the duty just stated. The parties described include the two tribes in the blood-money dispute and the people of Khaybar in the reported adultery case.

**"and do not follow their desires."** Their preference in that case was flogging and public humiliation in place of stoning. Desire is again set against revelation as a competing standard of judgment.

**"And beware, so they do not lure you away from some of what Allah has revealed to you."** warns against trusting them or being drawn by their presentation. The explanation describes them as liars, treacherous and disbelieving, capable of distorting what they convey.

**"If they turn away ˹from Allah’s judgment˺, then know that it is Allah’s Will to repay them for some of their sins"** describes punishment in this world for some of their offences, with the fuller reckoning of all their sins in the Hereafter. Their turning away is itself counted among those sins.

**"and that many people are indeed rebellious"** states a general fact about most of humanity. Related verses record that most people will not believe however earnestly the Messenger ﷺ desires it (12:103), and that obeying most of the people on earth would lead away from Allah's path (6:116).

### What offer did they bring?

Ibn 'Abbas reports that Shas bin Qays, 'Abdullah bin Surya, Ka'b bin Asad and Ibn Saluba agreed to go to the Prophet ﷺ to try to turn him from his religion. Muhammad bin Ishaq reports this through Ibn 'Abbas, and Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim record the account.

They told him that they were the scholars, nobles and chiefs of the Jews, and that if they followed him the rest would follow without contradiction. The condition was that he decide a dispute in their favour against their opponents, after which they would believe in him and confirm his message.

Another report names Ka'b ibn Asad, 'Abd Allah ibn Suriya and Shas ibn Qays and describes their purpose as seducing him from his religion. The lists differ slightly in the names included, and both are preserved as reported.

The Prophet ﷺ refused. The warning in this verse was revealed about them. The account draws out the point that the offer was not for him to arrange: gaining their followers was not a reason to bend a judgment.

A related explanation notes the temptation in more general terms. A judge might reason that deciding for such people would bring a whole community into Islam. The verse removes that calculation from the weighing of a case. Justice is not traded for a hoped-for outcome, however attractive.

### Why warn specifically about being turned away from part of the revelation?

The wording says some of what Allah revealed, not all of it. Distortion often begins with a single ruling that is inconvenient, while the rest of the book is professed. In the reported case, the concealed passage concerned one particular penalty.

The warning therefore addresses partial compromise. Someone may keep the whole framework of a judgment while quietly removing the part that offends a powerful litigant. That is still departure from what Allah revealed.

It also explains why the parties are described as seeking to seduce rather than merely to disagree. They were not asking for an interpretation of the text. They wanted a decision produced to order, with belief offered as the price.

### What does turning away bring upon them?

The verse describes Allah's will to punish them for some of their sins. One explanation treats this as worldly punishment for particular offences, with the complete accounting of every sin belonging to the Hereafter. Another says the punishment in this life concerns some sins while all are requited later.

The mention of some sins does not reduce the seriousness of the rest. It describes the wisdom of a consequence that begins before the final judgment. Their refusal of a just decision is not a neutral difference of opinion.

The closing statement that many people are rebellious prevents surprise at such refusals. Most of the People of the Book are described in one gloss as evil doers who break covenants. The majority of humanity, in the wider statement, is disobedient and turns away from truth.

### What should a person take from that?

The lesson is not despair about people, nor a licence to follow the majority. It is a correction of expectation. Truth is not established by counting adherents, and a judge or teacher should not measure his success by how many accept him.

The duty remains to decide by revelation and to guard against being moved from it. Those who refuse bear their own consequence. Steadfastness in a just decision is itself part of the obedience the preceding verse commands.

### In short

- The repeated command fixes judgment to revelation and forbids deciding according to the parties' desires.
- The reported offer of belief in exchange for a favourable ruling was refused, and the warning was revealed about it.
- Being turned away from part of what Allah revealed is as much a departure as abandoning the whole.
- Refusal of a just decision brings a willed consequence, beginning in this world and completed in the Hereafter.
- Widespread rebellion means truth is not measured by how many people accept it.

<<<VERSE 5:50>>>
The question sharpens the contrast: "Is it the judgment of ˹pre-Islamic˺ ignorance they seek?" The alternative to Allah's judgment is not another equally valid system. It is the rule of inherited custom, opinion and desire that characterised the period before Islam.

The verse then asks who could be a better judge for people of sure faith. The answer is contained in the question. Certainty about Allah changes what a person expects from a court.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Is it the judgment of ˹pre-Islamic˺ ignorance they seek?"** is an interrogative expressing rejection rather than a request for information. One reading addresses them directly as "do you seek", and another reads "do they seek". Both carry the same rebuke.

The judgment of jahiliyyah, the pre-Islamic period of ignorance, is explained as decision by opinion, desire and invented custom, without basis in Allah's religion. The gloss describes it as the judgment of the idolaters, reached according to their desires.

**"Who could be a better judge than Allah for people of sure faith"** sets Allah's judgment above every human alternative. He knows all things, is able over all things and is just in every matter. One gloss describes Him as more merciful to His creation than a mother to her child.

The people of certainty are singled out because they are the ones who reflect. Their conviction in Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an is what makes the answer obvious to them. The question is not posed to those whose desires decide in advance what they will accept.

### What did the earlier passage show about this alternative?

The Jews in the account had mixed their own innovations with the religion of Allah and had interests tied to the resulting arrangement. They were unwilling to give it up for the plain religion brought by the Prophet ﷺ.

Rather than submit to truth, they pressed the one who carried it, hoping he would yield to them. Allah could have restrained them at once. He allowed them respite so that their conduct would expose what they really served.

That allowance was a hard test for the one conveying the truth. It was also the means by which it became clear who deserved Paradise and who deserved the Fire. The claimants of righteousness were shown to be devoted to self-interest rather than to Allah.

The weakness described is general. A person wants his desires fulfilled and finds being governed by divine command intolerable. He may then produce interpretations of religion shaped to fit those desires. Pure religion is acceptable to someone who does not measure everything by preference.

### Why is certainty necessary to see Allah's judgment as best?

In this world of trial, truth stands behind a curtain of doubt. Allah's word is the truest word, yet its recognition requires an act of the person. The test is whether he can tear that curtain aside and make the unseen real to himself.

Someone captured by outward doubts fails that test. Someone who pushes past apparent doubt and reaches the truth succeeds. Certainty is therefore not a gift that removes responsibility. It is the achieved clarity of a person who has refused to let preference decide the matter for him.

The verse's mention of people of sure faith explains why others cannot appreciate the comparison. A person whose desires govern him will always find some human judgment more attractive than a divine one that crosses him.

### What historical example illustrates preferring a human code?

The explanation compares the judgment of ignorance with the code followed by the Tatar, the Mongols, inherited from their king Genghis Khan and written as Al-Yasiq. It describes that book as containing rulings drawn from Judaism, Christianity and Islam together with much that came from his own opinion and desire.

His descendants are described as preferring those rulings to the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger ﷺ. The supplied legal comment calls such preference disbelief and states that the person was to be fought until he returned to the judgment of Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, so that no matter, small or great, would be referred to any other law.

That is the historical legal statement recorded about rule by a foreign code in a specific situation. It is not a permission for an individual to declare other Muslims unbelievers or to take up arms on his own judgment. The point retained here is the principle: revelation is not one option among competing systems of preference.

### How does the warning apply to a believer today?

A prophetic report recorded by At-Tabarani from Ibn 'Abbas describes the person most hated by Allah as a Muslim who seeks the ways of the days of ignorance, together with one who seeks to shed a person's blood without justification. Al-Bukhari records a similar hadith through Abu Al-Yaman with some addition.

Seeking the ways of ignorance is not limited to adopting a pagan code. It includes preferring custom, tribal advantage or personal interest over a revealed ruling, and treating inherited practice as though it carried religious authority.

The verse therefore returns judgment to its proper source. Every type of good is included in Allah's command and every type of evil forbidden by it. Opinions, desires and customs invented by people have no independent standing beside that.

### In short

- The judgment of ignorance is decision by desire and inherited custom rather than by revelation.
- Allah's judgment is best, and certainty is what enables a person to see it.
- The historical account of the parties' pressure and of Al-Yasiq illustrates preferring a human code over divine law.
- Truth is veiled by doubt in this world. The test is whether a person reaches it rather than settling for what suits him.
- Seeking the ways of ignorance and shedding blood without justification are described as especially hateful to Allah.
