<<<VERSE 6:114>>>
˹Say, O Prophet,˺ “Should I seek a judge other than Allah while He is the One Who has revealed for you the Book ˹with the truth˺ perfectly explained?” Those who were given the Scripture know that it has been revealed ˹to you˺ from your Lord in truth. So do not be one of those who doubt

The case is closed, and the verse asks whether anyone should now go looking for another judge.

### The case and its judgment

What came before was the stubbornness of the disbelievers of Makkah. They had seen signs and miracles proving the truth of the Qur'an and of the Prophet, and they went on demanding miracles of the kind they chose. The answer was that such a miracle would be easy for Allah, but the people asking were obstinate, and they would see it and stay in their wickedness, at which point the divine law would take over and they would be swept away by punishment. That is why the Prophet, in his universal mercy, refused to oblige them, and asked them instead to think about the signs and proofs they had already seen. These two verses now carry the evidence that settles the matter.

The substance is a statement of the case. The dispute between us is this: am I his prophet and his messenger? That is my claim and you reject it. The case has already been decided by the judge of all judges in my favour, in a way that leaves the miracle of the Qur'an itself more than enough proof. It is the same Qur'an that challenged the nations of the world, if they doubt its being the Word of Allah, to produce a small surah or a single verse to match it. Even the eloquent Arabs could not do it. Whole nations set themselves against Islam, against the Qur'an and against the Prophet, at the cost of their lives, their property and their honour, and not one of them rose with a single verse that matched the words of this Qur'an. Was that open miracle not enough to make a person accept that a man nobody had ever taught was reciting what left Arabia, and the world beyond it, wondering?

### Phrase by phrase

**"Should I seek a judge other than Allah"** The verse came down when the people asked the Prophet to appoint an arbiter between himself and them, and the answer refuses the premise. Say to these idolaters who worship others along with him: does it make sense that I should accept anyone other than Allah as a judge between us, or that I should take a lord other than him to worship? It is he who revealed to you the Book. Some read the sentence as: while it is he who sent down to you the Scripture, fully explained, the lawful in it and the unlawful. It came through Gabriel, and it is also said that it was revealed in instalments, a few verses at a time. Truth is distinguished from falsehood in it.

**"Those who were given the Scripture know that it has been revealed from your Lord in truth"** The Jews and the Christians know it, since the Torah was given to one group and the Gospel to the other, and the prophets before them conveyed to them the good news that he would come. The proof of it stands in their own scriptures. Those given knowledge of the Torah are meant here, people like ʿAbd Allāh b. Salām and his followers, who are certain about it on the basis of what they have. The recitation allows either form of the verb for "revealed".

**"So do not be one of those who doubt"** This stands beside the other verse: "if you are in doubt about what we have revealed to you, then ask those who read the Book before you. The truth has come to you from your Lord, so do not be one of those who doubt" (10:94). The condition in that verse does not mean that doubt could ever occur to the Prophet. In the presence of proofs this clear there is no room for doubt at all, and his own saying is on record: I never doubted, and I never asked. The words are addressed to him, but the hearing is meant for others, and the effect is heightened by that way of putting it, as if to say: if this has been said to the Prophet, who would have the audacity to entertain a doubt?

### The four qualities named in the verse

The sentence describes four perfections of the Qur'an, and each is a proof on its own. It has been sent down from Allah. It is perfect, miraculous, unchallenged and inimitable. Every matter of principle in it has been given in clear detail. And before it came, the People of the Book, Jews and Christians, knew for certain that this Qur'an is the true Word of Allah. Those who cared for truth said so out loud. Those who were hostile would not say it, and they were sure of it all the same.

### What keeps a person from seeing it

Only a mind working under some other influence than revelation will have doubts here. There have always been only a few in any age who can see a matter in its true perspective, and many who lose their way in a verbal labyrinth, taking what is only an appearance for a reality because it has been described in beautiful words. A person who brings his thinking in line with divine realities has no possibility of deviance in it. When such explanation has been given and a man still goes astray, Allah knows his condition. He knows the one who, to keep his own standing, attaches no significance to anything beyond himself. He knows the one whose bias has left him no capacity to understand godly matters, the one who is so taken with cheap display that he does not listen to the voice of truth, and the one who is prey to jealousy and therefore stays a stranger to it.

### The judge who cannot be replaced

The inner reading takes the question further than a court case. Shall I take other than Allah as an object of worship? Certainly not. Shall I know a god other than him? Never. The object of worship has no peer, because he is one and unique. In activity and in overwhelming power he has no equal, and in carrying out his work and mastering it he has no likeness. He is known for the way he is gentle with his servants, and kindness and love are his description. The Pir of the Tariqah said: "You are found by those who recognize you, and you are the wish of the hearts of those who yearn for you, and you are mentioned on the tongues of those who praise you." And: "how should I not want you, when you hear the voice of those who call on you? How should I not praise you, when you make the hearts of your servants happy?" And again: "how should I not know you, when you are the adornment of the universe? How should I not love you, when you are the delight of the spirit?"

### In short

- The verse is read as the closing of a case already decided, and the demand for another judge is refused as unreasonable rather than merely wrong.
- Those who were given the Scripture are named as witnesses from their own books, with ʿAbd Allāh b. Salām and his followers given as the example.
- The command not to doubt is addressed in form to the Prophet and in purpose to everyone else, and his own words are cited on it.
- The four qualities in the sentence are listed, since the material counts them and rests the argument on them.
- Doubt is traced to standing, bias, display and jealousy, which is what the verse's own audience had in abundance.

<<<VERSE 6:115>>>
The Word of your Lord has been perfected in truth and justice. None can change His Words. And He is the All-Hearing, All- Knowing

Two more qualities, and then a clause that shuts the door on any revision.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Word of your Lord has been perfected in truth and justice"** The word rendered as perfected denotes a state of completion, and the Word of your Lord here is the Qur'an, in the reading Qatadah gave and Al-Bahr Al-Muhit reports. Its subject matter is of two kinds, and the two qualities belong one to each. In one kind the Qur'an sets out events and conditions from the history of this world that carry a lesson, reward for good deeds and warning of punishment for evil ones. In the other it lays down injunctions about what will better human life and make it prosper. Truth belongs to the first kind, so that every event, condition, promise and warning in it is correct, with no room in it for fault, defect or error. Justice belongs to the second, so that every injunction rests on justice. Qatadah put the pair in these words: in truth concerning what he states, and in justice concerning what he decides. Whatever Allah says is true, and he is most just in what he commands. His commandments are pure justice, and outside them there is no justice, and everything he forbade is evil, since he forbids only what brings evil results. "He commands them with good and forbids them from evil" (7:157).

**"None can change His Words"** The sixth quality is that there is no one to change the words of Allah. A change could come about in one of three ways. Someone could prove a mistake in it, so that it had to be revised. An enemy could change it by force. Or Allah himself could change it by abrogation, that is, by naskh. The first two are impossible for a word that is pure in the way this one is. On the third, he has made a promise himself: "we have revealed the Dhikr, and we are its protector" (15:9). Who could break through the protection of Allah and introduce a change or an alteration? Over fourteen hundred years have passed. In every century and every age there have been more people against this Book than for it, in numbers and in power, and not one of them had the courage to introduce the smallest variance in even a vowel point of the Qur'an. Ibn Abbas drew the conclusion from the verse itself. It tells us that the noble Prophet is the final prophet and this Qur'an the final book, so that after it no abrogation is possible, which he said is made clear in other verses of the Qur'an.

The clause is also read in other ways. It means that the Qur'an itself cannot be changed. It means that the word of your Lord has prescribed that his allies will triumph, that he is truthful in his speech and just in what is to come, and that nothing can alter what he has said about helping those who side with him. It is also taken to mean that the religion of your Lord has now appeared openly, that people believe in truth that it is Allah's religion, and that his command is just and nothing will ever change it. In another reading none can change his words by contravening his rulings or by slipping past the terms he has appointed, and the pair "in truth and in justice" is there to specify what the perfecting consists in.

**"And He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing"** The two names that end the verse are then unpacked. He is the hearing one, who takes in what these people say, and the knowing one, who knows them and what they do, in the open and in secret. He will give a return for every deed of theirs, and nothing is hidden from him.

### Truth in what he says, justice in what he orders

Justice here carries two senses. It means a state in which no injustice is done to anyone, and no right is compromised or taken away. It also means moderation, so that a command is not made to serve human desire, and is not something that a person's inborn feelings and natural make-up could never carry. Taken together: no injunction inflicts injustice on anyone, and no injunction carries hardship that a human being cannot bear, and "Allah does not burden any soul beyond its capacity" (2:286). The word for perfected adds something further, since the point is not only that the Qur'an has these two qualities, but that it is complete in them in every respect.

Think for a moment about what that claim is. For the injunctions of one book to be full of justice and moderation for every people, every coming generation and every changed condition, from now until the last day of qiyamah, is something no legislature can produce. No assembly or congress in the world can foresee all the conditions that will arrive, and none can write a law that accommodates them all. Every country frames its laws for the conditions that usually prevail there, and even then those laws are amended when experience shows that some of their provisions have fallen short of justice and moderation. To make a master law for other nations and peoples, with complete regard for everything that will happen, is beyond human thought and perception. This is why the quality of truth and justice in the Qur'an is impeccable, and why it is in itself the proof that the Book is the Word of Allah.

### The touchstone

This is a world in which Allah has made himself clear about every basic reality, and going astray on the plea of ignorance is not something that will be pardoned. The objection that came to be about the law of sacrifice shows how a verbal trick is mistaken for an argument. Rules were given in the Qur'an about sacrificing animals, and it was specified where the meat of a sacrificed animal may be eaten, and animals that die of themselves or from another cause were forbidden. On that some people said: the religion of these Muslims is strange. They treat as permitted an animal a man has killed with his own hands, and they treat as forbidden an animal killed by Allah. There is no argument in that statement, only a play with words, and still many were taken in by it and began to look at Islam with suspicion. This is what always happens. People become lost in a verbal labyrinth and take an illusion to be real because it has been elegantly put. Allah's discourse is the touchstone by which every person can test every claim and find out whether it is a trick of words or something grounded in fact.

### In short

- Truth is assigned to what the Book reports and promises, and justice to what it commands, which is how the pair in the verse is divided.
- Justice is given a second sense, moderation, and it is what makes the claim about unbearable hardship impossible.
- The argument from human legislation is reported as a test of the claim, since no assembly writes a law for every future condition.
- Change is excluded in its three possible forms, and abrogation is answered with the verse on protection and with Ibn Abbas's conclusion about the last book.
- The verse ends on hearing and knowing, which turns the perfection of the word into a reckoning for the people who argue about it.
