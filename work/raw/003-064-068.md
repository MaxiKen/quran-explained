<<<VERSE 3:64>>>
After the ordeal was refused, a fairer door is opened: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O People of the Book! Let us come to common terms: that we will worship none but Allah, associate none with Him, nor take one another as lords instead of Allah.” But if they turn away, then say, “Bear witness that we have submitted ˹to Allah alone˺.”" The call begins at the one point both sides already profess: the Oneness of God. Jews and Christians are addressed, and those who follow their ways, and the invitation is repeated in the Prophet's letter to the Roman emperor.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O People of the Book! Let us come to common terms"**: "Word" here is a complete sentence, although Allah calls it one word. It is described as a word that is the same between us and you, mustawin amruhā, a matter upright and fair to both parties. Jews and Christians are invited to unite on this fair word in which all are equal.

**"that we will worship none but Allah, associate none with Him"**: we worship neither a statue, cross, idol, Taghut, false god, fire or anything else. Rather, we worship Allah alone without partners. This is the message of all Allah's messengers: "And We did not send any Messenger before you but We revealed to him (saying): 'None has the right to be worshipped but I (Allah), so worship Me (Alone and none else)'" (21:25), and "And verily, We have sent among every Ummah a Messenger (proclaiming): 'Worship Allah (Alone), and avoid (or keep away from) Taghut (all false deities)'" (16:36).

**"nor take one another as lords instead of Allah.”"**: Ibn Jurayj commented, "We do not obey each other in disobedience to Allah." Nobody is to obey another person in what involves a transgression against Allah, and nobody is to take rabbis and monks as lords, to be worshipped and followed besides Allah, no matter the person's rank or status.

**"But if they turn away, then say, “Bear witness that we have submitted ˹to Allah alone˺.”"**: if they abandon this fair call and reject God's Oneness, say to them, "Bear witness that we are Muslims", that we have surrendered to Allah and are of those who affirm His Oneness and obey Him. You will remain in Islam as Allah legislated for you.

### The letter to Heraclius and a problem of dates

The Prophet ﷺ wrote the same invitation to the Byzantine ruler. In al-Bukhari's version it runs:

> "I begin with the name of Allah who is All-Merciful, Very-Merciful. From Muhammad, servant of Allah, and His messenger: To Heraclius, the Byzantine emperor. Peace be on him who takes the right path. After that, I invite you to the call of Islam. Embrace Islam and be in peace. Allah will bestow upon you a twofold reward, but should you turn away, then, on you shall be the sin of your subjects."

Then the verse itself is quoted. In the other version the sender is "Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah", the greeting is "peace be upon those who follow the true guidance", the promise is that Allah will grant you a double reward, and the warning is that you will carry the burden of the peasants.

This raises a difficulty. Muhammad bin Ishaq and other scholars said the beginning of Surah Āl-ʿImrān, and more than eighty verses after it, were revealed concerning the Najran delegation. Az-Zuhri placed Najran first among peoples paying Jizyah, the tax paid to the Muslim state. Yet the Jizyah verse (9:29) belongs after Makkah's conquest, later than Najran's arrival in Al-Madinah. How could 3:64 enter a letter to Heraclius before the victory at Makkah, and how are these statements harmonised?

The proposed solution dates Najran before Al-Hudaybiyyah and treats its payment as Mubahalah's substitute rather than Jizyah. Later revelation supported what had occurred with Najran. Law can confirm prior practice: ʿAbdullah bin Jahsh divided raid booty into one fifth for the Prophet ﷺ and four-fifths for fighters before Badr, and later verses upheld the division. The Prophet ﷺ may therefore have written "Say, 'O People of the Scripture...'" before the Qur'anic verse descended, with revelation then matching his words exactly. Revelation also agreed with ʿUmar on Badr's prisoners, the Hijab, not praying for hypocrites, and his statements at (2:125) and (66:5).

### A principle of calling, and the close of an argument

Tabligh, preaching, and Daʿwah, calling to Islam, have a method. A person carrying his call to a group holding different beliefs should induce that group to unite only on what both can agree to. When the Prophet ﷺ called Heraclius he picked the one agreed point, the Oneness of Allah Almighty. And the closing words of the verse teach a second lesson. If someone refuses the truth after it has been proved clearly, the proper course is to restate your own belief and conclude the conversation. Further debate and verbal altercation are not appropriate.

Judged by this criterion, monotheism is not only a basic teaching of the Prophet Muhammad. It stands enshrined even in the interpolated versions of the Torah and the Bible. Monotheism implies belief in one God, worship of Him alone, no associates, and no human being accorded the place that is the sole prerogative of the Lord of the Universe. That concept is preserved in pure form in Islam in the Qur'an. Other religions, while accepting monotheism ideologically, adopted almost everything that ran counter to it: believing in God as Sustainer, in practice they accorded that status to their saints.

### The inward form of the common word

Realisation turns the address toward people of tawḥīd seeking ultimate reality. Tomorrow's dwellers in the seat of truthfulness (54:55) should guard today's walkway from customs' debris, clear mortal nature's opacities from the present moment's carpet and purify aspiration's drinking place from others' dust. Singleness of heart, desire and aspiration is prescribed: "When someone comes to have one concern, God will suffice him against the concerns of this world and the next."

"Some of us do not take others of us as lords apart from God" also forbids settling in every street with a scattered heart. Refuse obedience to the commanding soul. Do not make blameworthy caprice an object of worship: "take not two gods; surely He is but One God" (16:51). As long as some defiled fellow's talk grabs you, you serve your own fancy, not God.

### In short

- The common word is worship of Allah alone, no partners, and no human lords, the message of every messenger (21:25, 16:36).
- The same words went to Heraclius, and their presence before the Jizyah verse is explained by Najran's payment being in lieu of Mubahalah (9:29).
- When the fair call is refused, the method is to witness to your own submission and end the argument.

<<<VERSE 3:65>>>
The dispute now runs backward in time and collapses on its own chronology: "O People of the Book! Why do you argue about Abraham, while the Torah and the Gospel were not revealed until long after him? Do you not understand". Jews and Christians each claimed Ibrāhīm (Abraham) as one of their own. Neither Judaism nor Christianity existed when he lived, because both came after the books that founded them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O People of the Book! Why do you argue about Abraham"**: Muhammad bin Ishaq bin Yasar reports through Ibn ʿAbbās that Najran Christians and Jewish rabbis disputed before the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. "Ibrahim was certainly Jewish," said the rabbis. "Certainly, Ibrahim was Christian," said the Christians. This verse answered them. They also claimed surrender and adherence to Abraham's religion from the Torah.

**"while the Torah and the Gospel were not revealed until long after him?"**: how can you, Jews, claim Ibrāhīm was a Jew, when he lived before Allah sent down the Tawrah to Mūsā (Moses)? How can you, Christians, claim he was a Christian, when Christianity came after his time? The Torah was not revealed, and neither was the Gospel, until a very long time after him. Only after those two were revealed did Jewry and Christianity come into being. Neither book says that Abraham was Jew or Christian.

**"Do you not understand"**: have you then no sense? Do you not comprehend the falsehood of what you say?

### A cloak over the friend of God

Inwardly, possessiveness was cloaked with a niqāb and jealousy with a ḥijāb over His friend. After their accusation, his connection to every later claimant was severed and confused arguments decisively contradicted. No later religion can retroactively own Abraham.

### In short

- Ibrāhīm lived before the Torah and the Gospel, so he cannot have belonged to Judaism or Christianity.
- Both claims were made face to face before the Prophet ﷺ by Najran Christians and Jewish rabbis, and refuted by chronology.
- The friend of God is veiled from later claimants, not delivered to them.

<<<VERSE 3:66>>>
The rebuke sharpens from chronology to method: "Here you are! You disputed about what you have ˹little˺ knowledge of, but why do you now argue about what you have no knowledge of? Allah knows and you do not know". Disputing inside one's own scripture is one thing. Disputing about a man who predates it is another, and the verse orders unknown matters to be referred to the One who knows the seen and the unseen.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Here you are! You disputed about what you have ˹little˺ knowledge of"**: hā! You disputed concerning what you know: the affairs of Moses and Jesus, your claimed adherence to their religions, and the Law legislated for you until Muhammad ﷺ. Scripture itself had clear evidence, bayān, that could be proof, burhān: Muhammad is a prophet sent by Allah, and Abraham was neither Jew nor Christian. You rejected it.

**"but why do you now argue about what you have no knowledge of?"**: Abraham's circumstances are absent from your book and were not discussed by your prophets. Without definitive evidence, dalīl, and no way of knowing, maʿrifa, judgment and comprehension cannot be claimed.

**"Allah knows and you do not know"**: God knows his circumstances, and you know not that he was Jew or Christian, because he was neither. Allah knows the true reality of all things.

### Arguing without knowledge

This verse criticises everyone who argues and disputes without knowledge, as the Jews and Christians did over Ibrāhīm. Had they confined themselves to disputing about their religions, which they had knowledge of, it would have been better for them. Instead they argued about what they had no knowledge of, and Allah commanded them to refer such matters to Him Who knows the seen and unseen matters. Advocates in every age use the names of prophets and saints to justify additions and inventions, and with time people stop distinguishing the original religion from its later version, until the later version is taken as the real one. When the call to return to the true and pure religion is issued, opponents discredit it as an attack on what their saints handed down, although their yardstick is a body of later alterations, not the saints' own religion. This is how a veil of falsehood is drawn over the truth, through pronouncements invalid in religion and logic that the public cannot analyse.

### In short

- Dispute inside known revelation is bad enough, while dispute about Abraham, outside both books, has no evidence at all.
- Scripture itself contained the proof rejected: Muhammad's prophethood and Abraham's freedom from both labels.
- Unknown things must be referred to Allah, who knows reality, rather than claimed by inheritance.

<<<VERSE 3:67>>>
The answer is stated plainly: "Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian; he submitted in all uprightness and was not a polytheist". Ibrāhīm (Abraham) was a Ḥanīf and a Muslim, shunning shirk and living in īmān, faith. The Makkan polytheists made the same false claim in the other direction, calling their religion the faith of Abraham.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian"**: God dissociated Abraham from their claims. No, in truth he was not a Jew following the religion of the Jews, nor a Christian following the religion of the Christians. He was not a Jew or Christian in belief.

**"he submitted in all uprightness"**: he was a true Muslim Ḥanīfa, professing God's Oneness and leaving all other religions for the upright one. Treading monotheism's straight path, he opposed false religions and obeyed Allah alone.

**"and was not a polytheist"**: he was not of the Mushrikīn, and not one of those who ascribe partners to Allah. This cuts against the idolaters of the Arabs, who claimed to follow his belief while practising the opposite.

### What a Ḥanīf is

The Ḥanīf stands straight, mustaqīm, in truth. An aḥnaf may stand straight with curved legs, leaning on a twisted foot, so calling him mustaqīm is optimistic language. Abraham did not bend from truth, swerve from law or stumble after personal gain. He surrendered wealth, self and son, everything altogether, to God's decree, standing ready for His command.

Jews and Christians bracketed their religious history with Abraham's, and the Makkan polytheists did the same. In every age people use the names of prophets and saints to justify additions and inventions, until the later version passes for the original religion. Secondary features can be so emphasised by interpretation that they appear to be the main focus, while the fundamentals lose their significance. People stray from monotheism's thoroughfare into byways of unimportant issues. A Ḥanīf is the one who does not stray.

### The same answer in Surah Al-Baqarah

This verse is paired with the answer given in Surah Al-Baqarah: "And they say, 'Be Jews or Christians, then you will be guided...'" (2:135). The alternatives offered there are the same two labels claimed here, and both are answered by the Ḥanīf who precedes them.

### In short

- Ibrāhīm was a Muslim and a Ḥanīf, inclined away from every false religion toward the upright one, and no polytheist.
- Arab idolaters who claimed his faith are refuted by the same sentence that refutes Jews and Christians.
- A Ḥanīf stands straight in truth and surrenders wealth, self and son to God's decree.

<<<VERSE 3:68>>>
The right to Abraham is awarded: "Indeed, those who have the best claim to Abraham are his followers, this Prophet, and the believers." And "Allah is the Guardian of those who believe". Claims to Abraham are weighed by practice rather than argument volume. His own era's followers, Muhammad ﷺ, and those who believe in him carry the right.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, those who have the best claim to Abraham are his followers"**: the strongest right to follow Ibrahim belongs to those who followed his religion during his time. They, rather than you, deserve to say, "We follow his religion." 

**"this Prophet, and the believers"**: and this Prophet, Muhammad ﷺ, and his Companions from the Muhājirīn, the Anṣār and those who followed their lead. Muhammad follows Abraham's religion and accords with him in most of the rulings of his Law, and those who believe in Muhammad and in the Qur'an follow Abraham's religion too.

**"And Allah is the Guardian of those who believe"**: Allah is the Walī, the Protector and Helper of the believers, their Helper and Preserver. He is the Protector of all those who believe in His messengers.

### The occasion, and every prophet's guardian among prophets

Ibn ʿAbbās said the leaders of the Jews declared, "By Allah, O Muhammad, you know that we have the better claim to Abraham's religion than you or anybody else, and you know that he was a Jew. You are only resentfully envious!" Then Allah revealed this verse.

Saʿīd bin Mansur recorded that Ibn Masʿud said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, "Every Prophet had a Wali (supporter, best friend) from among the Prophets. My Wali among them is my father Ibrahim, the Khalil (intimate friend) of my Lord, the Exalted and Most Honored." The Prophet ﷺ then recited this verse.

The community is identified in the same terms: the closest people to Ibrāhīm now are the Prophet of Islam, Muhammad al-Mustafa ﷺ, and the believers in him and in past prophets. These believers are Muhammad's community, guarded by Allah, who rewards their belief.

### One party across the ages

Whims, ahwāʾ, and blameworthy innovations, bidaʿ, break a group into factions, each ending in error. The people of truth, ahl al-ḥaqq, in every era, time and moment, hold to the best proof and form a single party. Abraham, truth's companion, ṣāḥib al-ḥaqq, gives that party its claim over others. Professing his religion, in the manner of our Messenger ﷺ and his community, places a person inside Abraham's religion, whose core is declaring God's unity, tawḥīd.

### A second report behind the claim

ʿAbd al-Rahman ibn Ghanm reported the same saying from the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, with a longer setting also mentioned by Muhammad ibn Ishaq bin Yasar. After Jaʿfar ibn Abi Talib and his companions migrated to Abyssinia and settled there, and after the Messenger of Allah ﷺ migrated to Madinah and the Battle of Badr followed, the Quraysh met in Dar al-Nadwah. They resolved to exact revenge for their dead at Badr from Muhammad's Companions who were with the Negus. Money and gifts, including tanned skins, were collected for the Negus, and two men of good judgement were selected for the mission: ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ and ʿUmārah ibn Abi Muʿayt. They sailed across the sea to Abyssinia, prostrated themselves before the Negus and greeted him, and warned him that the emigrants represented a liar who had emerged among them claiming to be Allah's Messenger, followed only by fools. They claimed the Quraysh had made life very hard on them, driven them to the canyons, cut off all access, and left them destroyed by hunger and thirst. The report as it stands breaks off at that appeal.

### In short

- The best claim to Abraham belongs to his followers in his own time, to Muhammad ﷺ, and to the believers, not to late argument.
- The Prophet ﷺ named his own Walī among the prophets as his father Ibrahim, the Khalil of his Lord.
- Allah guards the believers with aid, help, selection and nearness, in every age one party holding the best proof.
