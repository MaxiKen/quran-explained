<<<VERSE 6:109>>>
They swear by Allah their most solemn oaths that if a sign were to come to them, they would certainly believe in it. Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Signs are only with Allah.” What will make you ˹believers˺ realize that even if a sign were to come to them, they still would not believe

The demand for a miracle is put in its own words, and the answer to it is given in three parts.

### The bargain over the mountain

The account comes down through Abu Ma'shar, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, and Ibn Jarir gives it as well. The chiefs of Quraysh spoke to the Messenger of Allah and said: you tell us that a stone struck by the staff of Moses gave twelve springs, that Jesus brought the dead back to life, and that the people of Thamud had a camel. They asked why nothing of that kind was brought to them, so that they could believe. He asked which sign they wanted, and they named one: turn the mount of al-Safa into gold. He put a condition to them, and they gave their word that every one of them would follow him if the mountain was turned. He told them to bind it with a firm agreement. They took the solemn oaths the verse now recites.

Then he stood up and began to supplicate. Gabriel arrived and put a choice in front of him. The mountain could be turned into gold, if he wanted it. But no sign was ever sent to a people who then disbelieved in it without punishment following it. Or he could leave them, so that some of them might turn back in repentance. The Messenger of Allah chose the second course, and asked that they be left to themselves for a while. And so these verses came down, from "they swear by Allah their most solemn oaths" down to "they would not believe, unless Allah so willed" (6:111). The disbelievers of Makkah had sworn that if a sign of the kind they asked for came to them, they would believe in it, and the mercy that refused the miracle is the same mercy that had already been shown in refusing to destroy them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They swear by Allah their most solemn oaths"** A solemn oath is an utmost one, for when a person swears by Allah, the oath he swears is solemn. Their strongest oaths are given as a guarantee that they would affirm the truth of whatever sign arrived.

**"Say: Signs are only with Allah"** Say this to those who ask for a sign as an act of defiance and disbelief, and not because they want guidance or knowledge. The matter of sending signs belongs to Allah. He sends them when he wills, and he turns the request away when he wills. Signs come from Allah and he sends them down when he wills. I am but a warner. On this point the earlier verse is the answer: the proofs and evidences already given are from him too, and the other party has the right to test those proofs and show them false. What it has no right to do is leave the evidence untouched and then say that it would prefer to see a miracle of this kind or that kind before it believes. That is like a man in a court who refuses to cross-examine the witnesses brought against his case, and then pleads that he will only accept the statement of a particular person he chooses. No court would hear such a plea, and the claim of a prophet is settled by what stands in front of the people who doubt it.

**"What will make you realize that even if a sign were to come, they still would not believe"** Mujahid took the "you" here to be the idolaters, so that the sentence asks them: what makes you think you are truthful in the vows you have sworn? On that reading the verse means that they still will not believe if the sign they asked for comes. Others took the "you" to be the believers, so that the sense is: what will let you know, O believers, that the idolaters will not believe even when the signs come. The reciters differ here as well. One reads it as "you will not believe", addressing the disbelievers directly, and another takes the following word as a word of hope rather than of certainty.

### What Allah tells before it is said

The clause assumes a knowledge of hearts that no sign would change. What his servants will say, and what they will do, is mentioned by Allah before any of it happens, and "none can inform you like Him who is the All-Knower" (35:14). He also quotes the regret of the people of the Fire before it happens. A soul cries out: "alas, my grief for what I neglected in the sight of Allah" (39:56), and the passage ends with a wish: "if only I had another chance, so that I should be among the doers of good" (39:58). Their case is the same as the verse which says of them, when they are thrown back into the world: "if they were returned, they would certainly go back to what they were forbidden, and they are liars" (6:28). The two readings of the middle clause are also supported by two other passages. One asks Iblis why he did not prostrate when he was commanded (7:12), and the other states that a town Allah has destroyed cannot go back to this world (21:95). With those in view, the sense of this verse becomes: what lets you know, you believers who want so badly for them to believe, that they would believe if the signs came?

### In short

- The oaths are reported with the demand behind them, and the demand is named as gold turned out of a mountain.
- The answer "signs are only with Allah" is read as a statement about who owns the timing of a miracle, not as an escape from the question.
- The Prophet's refusal of the offer is reported as an act of mercy, since a denied sign brings punishment with it.
- The second half of the verse is given in both readings, one addressed to the idolaters and one to the believers.
- Knowledge of what people would do if they were given what they asked is presented as one of the attributes of Allah, not as a guess.

<<<VERSE 6:110>>>
We turn their hearts and eyes away ˹from the truth˺ as they refused to believe at first, leaving them to wander blindly in their defiance

The consequence is described as an act done to them, and it is measured against what they had already chosen.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We turn their hearts and eyes away"** He confounds their hearts and their eyes at the moment the sign is revealed, so that they do not believe in it. Their hearts are turned aside from the truth so that they cannot take it in, and their eyes with them, so that they do not see it. The intervention is described as a preventing of guidance: he puts himself between them and faith, just as he put himself between them and belief in the Qur'an the first time, and he does it because of their stubbornness. The comparison is exact and it carries the weight of the verse: if they were sent back to this life, they would be kept from accepting guidance just as they were kept from it the first time, when they were in the world.

**"As they refused to believe at first"** Just as they did not believe in what the Prophet told them about the sign before this, so they will not believe when it arrives. Ibn Abbas, in al-Awfi's report from him, adds that when the idolaters rejected what Allah sent down, their hearts lost all steadiness, and they turned aside from every matter of benefit. Mujahid took the clause to mean that he prevents them from faith, so that even if every sign came they would not believe, exactly as he prevented them from faith the first time, and ʿIkrimah and ʿAbdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam said the like. Their denial was not built on a misunderstanding or on a lack of information. It came from hostility and obstinacy, and that is a disease which miracles do not treat.

**"Leaving them to wander blindly in their defiance"** He leaves them in it and abandons them. Ibn Abbas and As-Suddi took their trespass to mean their disbelief, while Abu Al-'Aliyah, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas and Qatadah took it to mean their misguidance. To wander blindly is to be left hesitating and perplexed, and Al-A'mash also gave it the sense of doing it playfully. Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Abu Al-'Aliyah, Ar-Rabi', Abu Malik and others said: they wander in their disbelief.

### The temper that cannot be reached

Truth may be laid in front of a man with all the arguments that support it, and he will still reject it, for one reason only. He looks at things perversely instead of looking at them as they are. An argument can be as rational and as sound as it likes. A person with no wish to accept it will find the words to refuse it. Instead of treating what a preacher says as settled, he starts asking whether all the great men before him were wrong and strangers to the truth. Then he raises other points of the same kind. Anyone who has developed that temperament will find it extremely difficult to reach the right path. He can give any subject the wrong colour and then use it as his excuse. Against a theoretical argument he pleads that it cuts across the creed of his forefathers, and against something he is being shown he says it is an optical illusion or a conjuring trick. The same attitude that blocks the argument also blocks the fact that is supposed to confirm it, and a man determined not to receive the truth will go without guidance, as he always has.

Such deniers are arrogant by nature, and they want to see themselves set above other people at any cost. When a preacher appears among them with a message of truth, he is often a stranger in that place, with none of the fine trappings of the age. To be connected with such a man would, they feel, lower their own standing. People with a superiority complex are therefore unable to accept him, and they reject him with a variety of excuses.

### In short

- The turning of hearts and eyes is reported as God's act, and it is dated to the moment the sign appears.
- The comparison "as they refused to believe at first" is what makes the verse an answer to the demand for a miracle rather than a threat.
- Trespass is given two readings, disbelief and misguidance, and both are attributed.
- Wandering blindly is glossed as hesitation and perplexity, with one reading that takes the word as playing.
- The longest passage here is about temperament, since the verse's real subject is a way of looking, not a missing proof.

<<<VERSE 6:111>>>
"Even if We had sent them the angels, made the dead speak to them, and assembled before their own eyes every sign ˹they demanded˺, they still would not have believed—unless Allah so willed. But most of them are ignorant ˹of this˺"

Three impossible suppositions, in increasing scale, and one clause at the end that undoes the whole demand.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Even if We had sent them the angels"** Had he answered what they asked for, angels would have come down to them, and they would have testified to the truth of what they had been denying. The demand is stated elsewhere in their own words: "or you bring Allah and the angels before us face to face" (17:92), and: "we shall not believe until we are given the like of what the messengers of Allah were given" (6:124), and: "why are not angels sent down to us, or why do we not see our Lord? They think too highly of themselves, and they are scornful with great pride" (25:21).

**"Made the dead speak to them"** The dead would speak to them from their graves. They would tell them that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, and that the Qur'an is his speech. That is what they had asked for.

**"Assembled before their own eyes every sign"** All things, of birds and beasts, would be gathered against them in array, face to face, testifying that what is said is the truth and confirming what they deny. To gather them "before them" is explained by ʿAli bin Abi Talhah and al-Awfi, who report it from Ibn Abbas as meaning before their eyes. This is also the view of Qatadah and of ʿAbdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam. The word is read in two ways, one taking it as throng upon throng, and one as happening before their very eyes. The sense is that if all the nations were gathered in front of them, one after another, and each of them testified to the truth of what the messengers had come with.

**"They still would not have believed"** They would not have believed in him and in the Qur'an, because guidance is with Allah and not with them. Allah guides whom he wills and leaves whom he wills to go astray, and he does what he wills, and this is the meaning of the clause "unless Allah so willed". No one is asked about what he does, while they will all be asked (21:23), and this follows from his knowledge, his wisdom, his power and his authority, which nothing slips past. The same rule is stated about those against whom the word of your Lord has been justified: they will not believe, even if every sign comes to them, until they see the painful torment (10:96-97).

**"But most of them are ignorant"** Most of them do not know that this is the truth from Allah, and they do not turn to him to be guided.

### Recognition is a light that is lit

The inner reading takes the three suppositions and stretches them further. Suppose the angels of heaven were sent down. Not only those nearest, but the cherubim, the messengers and the devout, the watchers over what is decreed and measured out, and the trustees of the exalted threshold, calling the abandoned toward him and reporting about him. Suppose the dead were mustered in the earth to direct them to his threshold. Suppose eloquence were given to every animal and to every lifeless thing, to the shapes and bodies of created things, to the form and the substance of what has been measured out, and to every instance of all that is known. Suppose all of them were sent to hold up the marks of his divinity and the signposts of his lordhood, so that they saw every report and knew it. Even then, as long as he does not will it and does not show them the road, they would have no faith, and they would not find the way to recognition. What is a handful of dust, that it should speak about the eternal, if not for the eternal care and the wish of the Generous? What is a heart, that it should scatter pearls without him? What is a body, that it should run a kingdom without him? By Allah, intelligence cannot find the road without him, and the spirit has no boldness to stay without him.

That is the position of the people of the sunnah. The Exalted Lord has to make himself known to the heart of a servant, and fix in it the marks that witness to the eternal attributes. Until he does, the servant will not find the road to recognition of him. For that reason the scholars of the sunnah and the leaders people follow have said that recognition becomes necessary once it is transmitted, is required once it is conveyed, and is acquired once it is made known. It is a candle, but when will he set it alight? It is a pearl, but where will he place it? He says that it is one of his secrets, which he deposits in the hearts of those he loves among his servants.

### What wisdom asks of a person

Wisdom lies in a person accepting the plan of God and shaping his mind to fit it. It is the reverse of wisdom for a man to keep a standard he made for himself in place of that plan, and to declare that he will accept whatever fits his standard and refuse whatever does not. For a man like that there is nothing in this world except ruin. In this world of his a person reaches his goal only by taking the roads he has been given, not by walking away from them. He cannot say that he will accept only what fits a standard of his own, and reject the rest.

### In short

- The three demands are answered one by one, and each is tied to the verses in which the same demands are made.
- "Before their eyes" is explained with a grammar note and with the named early interpreters who took it that way.
- The final clause places guidance with Allah, and the entry reports it as a statement about will and knowledge, not about unwillingness to help.
- The Sufi layer turns the verse into an argument that recognition has to be given, since no amount of witnessing produces faith by itself.
- The closing passage restates the same lesson as a question of what a person is willing to submit to.

<<<VERSE 6:112>>>
"And so We have made for every prophet enemies—devilish humans and jinn—whispering to one another with elegant words of deception. Had it been your Lord's Will, they would not have done such a thing. So leave them and their deceit"

A verse of consolation, and it is built on a pattern that has run through every prophethood.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And so We have made for every prophet enemies"** Just as Allah made enemies for the Prophet, people who oppose him, rebel against him and take up hostility to him, so he made enemies for every prophet who came before him. Do not be saddened by it. Two other verses put the same thing: "messengers were denied before you, but they bore their denial and the harm done to them with patience" (6:34), and "nothing is said to you except what was said to the messengers before you" (41:43). And: "in this way We made for every prophet an enemy among the criminals" (25:31). Waraqah bin Nawfal said to Allah's Messenger that no one came with what he came with without becoming the target of enmity.

**"Devilish humans and jinn"** The devils of mankind and the devils of the jinn are the enemies of the prophets. The word for devil describes a being that is unlike its kind because of its wickedness, and it is only these devils, from among humans and jinn, who oppose the messengers. Qatadah, in the report Ma'mar took from him, commented on the pair of them: there are devils among the jinn and devils among mankind, and they inspire one another. One gloss brings the same point with the names of the people in front of the Prophet. Just as Abu Jahl and those who sneered were appointed as your enemies, so an adversary and a tyrant was appointed for every prophet.

**"Whispering to one another with elegant words of deception"** They dictate to one another speech that has been made beautiful, and they do it as a delusion, so that the words deceive the ignorant people who hear them. The fine speech is falsehood dressed up, and the aim of dressing it up is to lead the son of Adam astray.

**"Had it been your Lord's Will, they would not have done such a thing"** If your Lord had willed it, they would not have done this, and they would not have whispered such words to each other. All of it happens by his decree, his will and his decision, including the fact that every prophet has enemies from among these devils.

**"So leave them and their deceit"** Leave them to what they invent, and pay no attention to it. The verse orders patience in the face of the harm done by wicked people, and trust in Allah against their enmity, since Allah is enough for you and will give you victory over them. This was revealed before the command to fight them.

### The satans among human beings

Ibn Jarir brings a report from Abu Dharr al-Ghifari. He said: I sat in a gathering of the Prophet, and it was a long one. He asked me whether I had said my salah, and I said no. He told me to get up and offer the salah of two rak'ahs. I did it, and came back to the gathering. Then he asked: did you seek protection against the satans among human beings and jinn? I said: O Prophet of Allah, are there satans among human beings as well? He said: yes, and those satans are worse than the ones among the jinn.

The satans among human beings, on this reading, are people who have reached respect and fame through a religion of their own making, and who will do anything to prove the call of truth false. When that call is delivered in its purest form, they feel that every protection is being stripped off them. The straight way for them would have been to accept the truth once it was made clear. But their own position is dearer to them than acceptance is, so they set out to prove that the preacher and his message are doubtful. Their allegations are baseless, and because they are put elegantly, many people are moved by them and filled with doubt.

### Rank, and the size of the trial it brings

The enemies are counted among the trials that come with rank. When a person's rank is higher, his trial is more complete, and when he is nearer to the Real and his heart is clearer, his soul is more in the grip of the enemy's hand. The story of love cannot be told unless tribulation brings grief, and the honey of friendship is not found unless the trial has venom in it.

Look then at what Adam the Chosen, that sapling honored and nurtured by holiness, suffered from his enemy Iblis, when Satan made him slip from where he was (2:36). Look at that other elder of the prophets, the father of the world's people. Noah called his people for nine hundred years and some, and every day they beat him until he lost consciousness, and they told his own children to be his enemies. He was patient through it, and went on hoping that they would have faith, until it was said to him that none of his people would believe except those already committed to it (11:36). He said: Lord, my hope in them is cut off, and there is no way to make them sound. Their life in this world will only add to corruption and bring ruin. So leave no disbeliever walking on the earth (71:26). After him came Abraham, the prophet who was the tree of tawhid. He fell on his knees night and day with his white old age held in his own hands, and asked that he and his sons be kept away from idolatry (14:35). The rebellious Nimrod tried him with arrogance and obstinacy, and the hardship he bore from it is stated in the passage. So it went with the prophets one by one. Hud, Salih, Lot, Zachariah, John, Jesus and Moses came to lamentation at the hands of tyrants, the arrogant and the stubborn, weeping to the Real. After all of them, the trial of MuḤammad Muṣṭafā was more complete and the torment he took from his enemies greater, and he said that no prophet whatsoever has been tormented as he was tormented. Those people who had no respect for him did not know the measure of the man they were treating that way, and they had no eyes with which to recognize him. They set out to kill him, and they tightened their belts in cruelty against him.

### In short

- The verse is read first as a comfort: the same pattern was run through every prophet before this one.
- Devil is explained from the root for being unlike one's kind, which is why men can be devils as well as jinn.
- Abu Dharr's report is given with its question and its answer, since it is what puts human devils inside the verse.
- Elegance is treated as the real instrument of deception, and the human satans as the more dangerous of the two kinds.
- The list of prophets is reported as a single argument about rank and trial, with each prophet given only what the passage gives him.

<<<VERSE 6:113>>>
so that the hearts of those who disbelieve in the Hereafter may be receptive to it, be pleased with it, and be persistent in their evil pursuits

Three clauses that look like purposes, and they say who is moved by elegant speech.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So that the hearts of those who disbelieve in the Hereafter may be receptive to it"** The hearts of those who do not believe in resurrection after death incline to that fine speech and to the deception wrapped in it. Ibn Abbas explained the verb, taşghā, as meaning to incline to it, and As-Suddi said that the verse is about the hearts of the disbelievers. The hearts named here are their minds and their hearing as well, and one reading takes the clause as attached to the phrase before it, to the delusion, so that what the hearts incline to is the embellished speech itself.

**"And be pleased with it"** They like it and adore it, and they accept that embellishment and deception from the satans. Only those who disbelieve in the Hereafter take this evil speech, since they are the enemies of the prophets. Two other passages are cited at this point. "You and what you worship other than Allah cannot lead anyone astray, except whoever is destined to burn in the Blaze" (37:161-163). And: "you are holding different opinions, and whoever is turned away from it is turned away" (51:8-9).

**"And be persistent in their evil pursuits"** Let them earn what they are earning, which is what ʿAli bin Abi Talhah reported from Ibn Abbas. As-Suddi and Ibn Zayd gave the same sense in another form: let them do whatever they will do. The one gloss adds what the other clauses leave out, that they earn sins and are punished for them.

### Why the test is left open

If a preacher of truth can state his case in the language of rational argument, then the defenders of falsehood can put up equally beautiful words against the truth, words that look like valid arguments against it. Once liars and truth-tellers command the same range of plausible expression, a person is put to the test. He has to pass the test of telling a true argument from a false one, and then accept the true one after rejecting what has no basis. That state of affairs will go on until the last day. The satans among human beings use their intelligence to deceive, but the only people they win over are those who have no fear of the Hereafter. Worry about the Hereafter makes a person serious, and the reality of things never stays hidden from someone who serves God sincerely. A person with no care for the Hereafter is not serious about truth, and for that reason he cannot tell a solid argument from a trivial one.

### In short

- Inclining, being pleased and earning are three steps, and the entry keeps them separate because the material does.
- The clause is reported as a purpose with a warning inside it, since the hearts that incline are named by what they do not believe.
- The two citations in this verse are given as proof that guidance is not taken away from anyone who was steady.
- Seriousness is described as the fruit of fear of the Hereafter, which is the verse's own standard for who is taken in.
- The three clauses end the passage on demands for signs by turning to the people who make them.
