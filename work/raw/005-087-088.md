<<<VERSE 5:87>>>
"O believers! Do not forbid the good things which Allah has made lawful for you, and do not transgress. Indeed, Allah does not like transgressors"

This verse addresses the believers and forbids them to make unlawful the good things that Allah has made lawful for them. It also forbids transgression, and it says that Allah does not love those who transgress.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Do not forbid the good things which Allah has made lawful for you."** The material explains that the verse was revealed about a group of Companions who wanted to turn away from the world completely. The material names several of them, among them Abu Bakr, Ali ibn Abi Talib, Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, Abu Dharr al-Ghifari, Salman al-Farisi and Ammar ibn Yasir. They gathered at the house of Uthman ibn Maz'un and agreed to fast by day, pray by night, abstain from meat, fat, perfume and women, and in one report to cut off their male organs. The material reports that they resolved to live as monks.

**"And do not transgress."** The material says that transgression here means making the lawful unlawful without a valid reason. Ibn 'Abbas and others report that the Prophet ﷺ said that he fasts and breaks his fast, prays and sleeps, and marries women, and that whoever turns away from his Sunnah is not of him. The material cites 7:31, which says to eat and drink without wasteful excess, and 25:67, which describes those who are neither extravagant nor miserly.

**"Indeed, Allah does not like transgressors."** The material says the limits are set by Allah, and that the middle way is the path between excess and neglect. Allah does not love those who cross the limits.

### The Sunnah and the rule of the Prophet

The material reports that a man came to the Prophet ﷺ and said that he had forbidden meat to himself because it stirred his desire. The Prophet ﷺ rebuked the Companions who had made such vows, and the material cites the saying of the Prophet ﷺ, recorded in the two sahihs, that those who are not pleased with his Sunnah are not of him. The material says the Companions had the same question in mind when they asked the wives of the Prophet ﷺ about his worship in private.

### Lawful and unlawful by belief, speech and practice

The material explains that a lawful thing may be made unlawful in three ways. The first is by belief, which is a disbelief if the lawfulness of the thing is proven. The second is by speech, such as an oath that one will never eat a certain food, which must be broken and expiated. The third is by practice, which is permanent abstention from a lawful thing as a means of merit. The material says that such abstention as an act of worship is a bid'ah and a monasticism that is forbidden. Abstention for a real illness or a harm to the body or the heart is not forbidden.

### In short

- The believers are told not to forbid the good things that Allah has made lawful.
- Some Companions wanted to turn to monasticism, and the Prophet ﷺ taught them the Sunnah instead.
- Transgression is crossing the limits of Allah, including making the lawful unlawful as an act of merit.

<<<VERSE 5:88>>>
"Eat of the good, lawful things provided to you by Allah. And be mindful of Allah in Whom you believe"

This verse tells the believers to eat of the good and lawful things Allah has provided, and to be mindful of Allah in whom they believe.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Eat of the good, lawful things provided to you by Allah."** The material explains that the words lawful and good are both stated together. The food must be pure, permitted, and of good quality. The material says that the good things are not to be refused as a means of seeking reward.

**"And be mindful of Allah in Whom you believe."** The material explains that taqwa means keeping away from disobedience, and that it is the fear of Allah in all affairs. Refusing the good things as an act of devotion is not taqwa, which is shown by using them as a gift of Allah and giving Him thanks for them.

### The gift and the gratitude

The material says that the verse answers the monastic impulse with the proper attitude to the blessings of Allah. The believer receives them, uses them within their limits, and gives thanks. He does not refuse them as a way of being close to Allah. The material says that the Companions were taught this lesson and that the verse keeps the balance between the two extremes.

### In short

- The believers are to eat the good and lawful food that Allah provides.
- Taqwa is found in using the blessings with gratitude, not in refusing them.
- The verse makes the limits clear, without excess or neglect.
