<<<VERSE 4:19>>>
After a series of rulings about women, the surah turns to the excesses women suffered at the hands of husbands and even of heirs: "O believers! It is not permissible for you to inherit women against their will or mistreat them to make them return some of the dowry ˹as a ransom for divorce˺—unless they are found guilty of adultery. Treat them fairly. If you happen to dislike them, you may hate something which Allah turns into a great blessing." The aim is to correct disorders in society. The husbands are addressed in particular in the words "treat them fairly", and 4:20 and 4:21 conclude the subject.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! It is not permissible for you to inherit women against their will"**. Ibn ʿAbbās described the old practice in a report recorded by al-Bukhārī. When a man died, his male relatives had the right to do as they wished with his wife. One of them might marry her, give her in marriage, or stop her from marrying, since they had more right to her than her own family. This verse was revealed about that practice. In pre-Islamic times they could also marry the woman without a dowry, or marry her off and keep the dowry themselves. They could stop her from marrying until she surrendered whatever she had inherited, or until her death left them free to take her estate. One explanation specifies that the women meant here were those of one's fathers. Another explanation gives the cases that led to the verse. It was said to be about Muḥsin ibn Abī Qays al-Anṣārī and Kabshah bint Maʿan al-Anṣāriyyah, since before Islam they used to inherit the women of their fathers. If a widow was rich and beautiful, the eldest son married her without any dowry. If she was neither rich, young nor beautiful, he did not marry her. He left her untouched until she bought her freedom with her own money.

The word "against their will" is read in two ways by reciters, as karhan or kurhan, with the same sense. It is not meant as a condition, as if inheriting women would be fine with their consent. It states the fact. To take over the life and property of women as a self-appointed owner, with no legal or rational basis, could only ever be by force. No woman in her normal senses would agree to it, as al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ points out. The Sharīʿah does not treat her approval as valid here. If a woman out of her mind agreed to be owned by someone, Islamic law would not allow it.

**"or mistreat them to make them return some of the dowry ˹as a ransom for divorce˺"**. Allah forbids harshness toward a wife that is meant to force her to hand back all or part of her dowry, or to surrender one of her rights under pressure and oppression. The Arabic says "do not prevent them", and this includes stopping a divorced woman from marrying again in order to take back what you or a relative gave her. Some husbands, though they had divorced her, would not let the woman marry again, so that she would break down and give back the dowry or forgo what was still owed. Both returning a dowry already paid and giving up an unpaid one are forbidden (ḥarām) when forced. Money or property taken this way is not lawful for the man, and no right is forgiven by it.

The same applies to gifts. What was given to the wife as a gift, and made hers, cannot be taken back by her husband or his heirs. This rule covers only what was handed over to her as an owner. If something was lent for temporary use, such as jewellery, without making her the owner, it does not become part of her ownership, and asking for it back is not forbidden.

**"unless they are found guilty of adultery"**. The Arabic word is fāḥishah, an open lewd act. Saʿīd ibn Abī Hilāl, Zayd ibn Aslam, as-Suddī, Abū Ṣāliḥ, Abū Qilābah, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī, ʿIkrimah, Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Muḥammad ibn Sīrīn, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, ash-Shaʿbī, Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib, Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd said that it means illicit sex. So if the wife commits adultery, the husband may take back her dowry. He may also make things hard for her until she gives it back in return for a khulʿ, a divorce she buys. In Sūrat al-Baqarah Allah says: "It is not lawful for husbands to take back anything of the dowry given to their wives, unless the couple fears not being able to keep within the limits of Allah" (2:229).

Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, ʿIkrimah and Ibn ʿAbbās took fāḥishah to mean disobedience and defiance. ʿĀʾishah is also reported to have understood it this way, as insolence and indecent abuse toward the husband. Abū Qilābah and al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī took it here to mean immodesty and adultery. Ibn Jarīr chose the view that the word is general and covers all these things: adultery, disobedience, defiance, rudeness and the like. According to him, the husband may make things hard for a wife who does any of these until she gives up all or part of her rights and he separates from her, and this view is good. Other explanations say the same. If she does something clearly shameful that forces the husband to think of divorce, it is her fault, and he has the right to keep her in the marriage until he gets back what he gave her or she gives up the unpaid dower. Where fornication is established by witnesses, one explanation adds, the old penalty was imprisonment, and that was later replaced by the verse on stoning. If a woman does this, the husband may force her to buy her release or to give up her dowry.

**"Treat them fairly"**. This is to speak kindly to them, treat them well and make yourself pleasing to them as far as you can, as you would like them to do the same for you. Allah said in another verse: "Women have rights similar to those of men equitably" (2:228). Others explain it as being decent in speaking to them and in providing for them and housing them. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "The best of you is the one who is best to his family, and I am the best of you to my family."

### The Prophet's ﷺ way with his wives

It was the Messenger's ﷺ habit to be kind and cheerful with his wives, to joke with them, to be gentle, to spend on them and to laugh with them. He raced ʿĀʾishah, the Mother of the Faithful, out of kindness toward her. ʿĀʾishah said that he raced her and she won, before she put on weight. Later she raced him again, and he won, and he said, "This one is for that one."

Sometimes, while he was in one wife's house, all of his wives would gather there and eat together before returning to their own homes. He and his wife slept in the same bed, and he took off his upper garment and slept in his lower garment. After the ʿIshāʾ prayer and before sleeping, he would talk with the wife whose night it was. Allah says: "Indeed, in the Messenger of Allah you have an excellent example for whoever has hope in Allah and the Last Day, and remembers Allah often" (33:21).

### What lay behind the old ways

The most glaring injustice before Islam was that men acted as owners of the life and property of women. A woman taken in marriage was passed on like a piece of property to her husband's heirs after his death. They became her new owners and the inheritors of her property too. They could marry her, or give her in marriage to someone else for a payment. A son of the husband by another wife could marry her himself after his father's death. When a living human being is treated as property, what happens to ordinary property is easy to imagine. This one basic social ill caused hundreds of other unjust practices, some of which follow.

If a woman received a share of an inheritance or a gift from her parents, she stayed deprived of it. Everything given in her name was devoured by the men of her husband's house. If she somehow came into possession of her share, the men stopped her from remarrying, so that she could not take it out of the house. They wanted her to die there and leave her property for them to take.

In some places a husband disliked his wife for reasons of whim and not because of any fault in her. He no longer treated her as a wife, but he did not release her by divorce. His aim was to wear her down until she returned the jewellery and dower money he had handed over, or, if he had not yet paid them, dropped her claim before she could hope to be released. After the husband died, his heirs would not allow his widow to remarry, either to protect an inflated sense of honour or to release her only after taking something for themselves.

All of this rested on one assumption, that a man owns the woman's property and her very life. The Qur'an struck at the root of the evil. It declared that it is not lawful to take women as inheritance by force.

The Qur'an did not use an ordinary prohibitive command here. It said that this is not lawful for you. Along with stressing how serious the sin is, this may point to a ruling. If a man marries an adult woman without her consent, the marriage is not lawful. In fact it is void. It does not create a husband-and-wife relationship, and the rules of inheritance and lineage do not follow from it.

### A call, an admonishment, an allusion

The words of the verse can be read as five things. "O" is the call, "you" is the admonishment, "who" is the allusion, "have faith" is the witness, and the rest is the ruling. The ruling means that women are weak and are held beneath your strictness. Do not torment them, and do not dominate them through scheming and deceit. Do not be harsh, and do not demand of them what the Sharīʿah does not approve. Live with them honourably, with the teachings of the religion, and model your manners on the character of those who submit to Allah. Show them the road of religion and teach them the manners of a Muslim and the Sharīʿah. Protect them from the Fire, as Allah says in another place: "O believers! Protect yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is people and stones" (66:6). Keep the courtesy of companionship and put up with some hurt from them, but do not load them with the burden of serving you.

Outwardly the verse speaks of women, but by allusion it speaks of all Muslims. It means: watch out that you never see yourself as having a due from any other Muslim or a superiority over him. Do not expect people above you to wait on you, and do not impose yourself on people below you. Do not bully the weak, but be considerate to them, comfort them and stay close to them. A revelation came to Dāwūd (David): "O Dāwūd, if you see someone broken on My road, or someone whose heart is lost in My work, take care to serve him." The passage breaks off there.

### "You may hate something which Allah turns into a great blessing"

Your patience in keeping wives whom you dislike has good rewards for you in this life and in the Hereafter. Ibn ʿAbbās said that it means the husband may come to feel compassion for his wife and Allah gives him a child through her, and that child carries enormous good. Others say the same: Allah may give you a righteous child from her. An authentic hadith says:

> "No believing man should hate a believing woman. If he dislikes one trait in her, he will surely like another."

The advice is meant for husbands and wives, but it is general guidance too. The Qur'an often gives guidance on one specific matter and sets down advice of a general nature that concerns a person's whole life. Living together is an essential condition of human life, because no one can survive alone. Since temperaments differ, irritations will inevitably arise. The only workable approach is to overlook complaints, avoid friction and work to keep the peace. A person often notices a fault in his companion and becomes annoyed. If he reflects, he will see that few situations lack an unfavourable side. Hardships can be a test of patience. Sometimes they push a person to turn to Allah in repentance, and sometimes their very vexation teaches an important lesson.

### Property and people are different

The heirs of a dead man may inherit his property. But his widow is not part of the estate, to be handled however they decide. Property has no feelings and is a proper thing to inherit. Human beings have their own feelings and independent existence, and they have the right to decide their own future. If a woman has some physical or temperamental flaw, it should be tolerated, so that she has the chance to use her other gifts to play her part in building the household. A person should overlook her unpleasant aspects and try to get along. The secret of the progress and strength of any family or society is that its members overlook each other's faults and give everyone the chance to use his or her abilities. Those who take the way of patience and tolerance in this world for Allah's sake are the ones who will be admitted to Paradise.

When someone does not like his life partner and decides to separate instead of putting up with her, he often exaggerates the other's faults to justify his decision. Charges are made up so that the weaker person becomes anxious and leaves. In the same way, false reasons are put forward while cutting the marriage bond. All of this goes against the covenant. A covenant is sacred to Allah, and whether it is written or unwritten, it is binding in its essence. It applies equally to both sides and leaves them no choice.

### In short

- It is not lawful to inherit women as property. A widow is not part of an estate, and no one may marry her, marry her off or stop her marrying against her will.
- A husband may not make life hard for a wife to make her give back her dowry or give up what he owes. The exception is a clear fāḥishah, understood as adultery, or as disobedience and abuse.
- Husbands are told to live with their wives honourably. The Prophet ﷺ was the best example, with kindness, play and racing ʿĀʾishah.
- A dislike may hide a blessing, such as a righteous child. The general lesson is to be patient with the people we live with.
- Inwardly, the verse tells every Muslim never to see himself as owed service or as above another.

<<<VERSE 4:20>>>
This verse goes on to the dowry: "If you desire to replace a wife with another and you have given the former ˹even˺ a stack of gold ˹as a dowry˺, do not take any of it back. Would you ˹still˺ take it unjustly and very sinfully?" If the woman has shown no defiance or immodesty, and the husband simply wants to marry another woman, he may not claim anything back from her in return for the divorce, even though he gave her a lot. He may not force her to give up the dowry still owed either. She has done nothing wrong, and the cause that makes the dower due has already taken place, since they married and were alone together.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you desire to replace a wife with another"**. One explanation says: if you wish to marry a woman and divorce the wife you already have, or to marry another woman in addition to her. Another says that a man may divorce one woman and replace her, but he may not take anything back. The Arabic phrase also fits the case of exchanging one wife for another.

**"and you have given the former ˹even˺ a stack of gold ˹as a dowry˺"**. The Arabic word is qinṭār, a very large sum. One explanation calls it a hundredweight. The meaning of qinṭār was explained in the discussion of Sūrat Āl ʿImrān. The verse clearly shows that the dowry may be large. ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb used to discourage large dowries, but later changed his view.

Aḥmad recorded that Abū al-ʿAjfāʾ as-Sulamī heard ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb say:

> "Do not make dowries for women too large. If that had been an honour in this world or a part of taqwa, the Prophet ﷺ would have had more right to it than you. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ never gave any of his wives, and none of his daughters received, more than twelve ūqiyah. A man used to pay a large dowry and so hide hostility toward his wife."

Aḥmad and the collectors of the Sunan collected this hadith through various chains, and at-Tirmidhī said it is ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ.

Al-Ḥāfiẓ Abū Yaʿlā recorded that Masrūq said that ʿUmar stood on the minbar of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and asked, "O people, why do you go to extremes with women's dowries?" He reminded them that the Messenger ﷺ and his Companions paid four hundred dirhams or less, and that if paying more had been part of taqwa or an honour, the people would not have outdone them. "So I do not want to hear of a man paying more than four hundred dirhams," he said. He came down from the minbar, and a woman of Quraysh said to him, "O Leader of the Faithful, you have forbidden people to pay more than four hundred dirhams in a dowry?" He said, "Yes." She said, "Have you not heard what Allah revealed in the Qur'an?" He said, "Which part?" She said, "Have you not heard Allah's words, 'and you have given the former a stack of gold'?" He said, "O Allah, forgive me." He went back and stood on the minbar and said, "I had forbidden you to pay more than four hundred dirhams in dowries. So let each man pay from his money what he likes." The chain of this report is strong.

**"do not take any of it back"**. This forbids taking back any part of what was given. Taking it back would be an injustice and a clear sin. Another explanation says that taking back what was given would be an act of wrong and a plain transgression.

**"Would you ˹still˺ take it unjustly and very sinfully?"** The wrongdoing is described in stages. The first stage is in these words, which ask whether you want to take it by false charge and open sin. This looks back to 4:19, which laid down that a husband has no right to take back the dowry unless his wife has done a shameful act. By that rule, if you take back the dowry from your wife, you are implying that she did a shameful act, because that is the only case where your claim would be right. Since she did not, your claim is a false accusation, and that is an open sin. In Arabic the two words for "unjustly" and "sinfully" are put in the accusative, as a description of how the taking is done. The question is asked as a rebuke and a rejection of such a claim.

### The inner reading

This verse is the full realisation of generosity in the religion of friendship, and it smooths the foundation of chivalry. It is as though Allah says, "Do not add to the cruelty of separation by taking back a woman's livelihood. That is no act of the generous, and the chivalrous would be ashamed of it. You have already left the scar of parting on a poor woman's heart, so do not also cut her hand off from her means of support. Taking back what you gave would put a scar on a scar."

Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī once divorced a wife and afterward sent her a great deal of money. He said of her, "The trial of our separation is enough. I must not put the suffering of poverty on her as well." They say the money was 40,000 dirhams. The woman threw the money on the ground and said, "Paltry goods from a departed lover!"

### In short

- A husband who wants to replace a wife may not take back any part of the dowry he gave her, however large, because she has done no wrong and the marriage has been consummated.
- Taking it back would amount to accusing her of a shameful act, and that is why the verse calls it an open sin.
- The verse shows that a dowry may be large. ʿUmar first tried to limit it to four hundred dirhams, and gave way when a woman of Quraysh reminded him of this verse.
- Inwardly, a generous person does not add to the pain of parting by taking back what he gave, as Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī showed.

<<<VERSE 4:21>>>
The verse gives a second reason why the dowry cannot be taken back. It follows 4:19, which allowed the husband to claim it back only when his wife had done a clearly shameful act, and 4:20, which called any other claim an open sin: "And how could you take it back after having enjoyed each other intimately and she has taken from you a firm commitment?"

### Phrase by phrase

**"And how could you take it back after having enjoyed each other intimately"**. The question means: how can you take back the dowry from a woman you have been with, and who has been with you? As-Suddī, Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās and several others took it to mean sexual intercourse, which validates the dowry. Other explanations speak of the love, enjoyment and sharing of secrets that has passed between the couple, and say that greed for wealth that is rightfully hers after all of this is forbidden and shameful. If the dowry was given against the marriage, she certainly deserves it, and it is hers now because she gave herself to her husband. To think it could be returned makes no sense. If the money was given as a gift, it cannot be returned either. What spouses give each other as gifts cannot be taken back, and such a claim is neither permitted by the Sharīʿah nor enforced by law. The marriage bond itself prevents the taking back of what was given as a gift.

**"and she has taken from you a firm commitment"**. This is the covenant of marriage, according to Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr. It is the marriage tie, which is solemnly witnessed in the name of Allah before a gathering of people after a khuṭbah. One explanation says that Allah took from men, when they married women, a firm pledge that they would either keep them in goodness or release them in kindness. Another explanation says that through this word of Allah and His sacred law, the women became lawful for the men. The earlier companionship has a firm sanctity, so out of respect for what is due to it, a man should stop and keep to what the covenant requires.

### The couple who swore oaths of mulāʿanah

A report in the two Ṣaḥīḥs shows the principle at work. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said three times to a couple who were swearing oaths of mulāʿanah, "Allah knows that one of you is a liar. Will either of you repent?" The man said, "O Messenger of Allah, my money," meaning the dowry he had given his wife. The Messenger ﷺ said:

> "You have no money. If you spoke the truth, the dowry is in return for what you enjoyed of her. If you lied, then the money is even further from you."

### The covenant of marriage

The Prophet ﷺ spoke of this covenant in the speech he gave at the Farewell Ḥajj. Muslim recorded from Jābir that he said:

> "Be kind to women. You took them by Allah's covenant, and you made them lawful to you by Allah's word."

To sum up this verse (4:21), once the covenant has been made and closeness in private has brought the couple together, to force the woman to give back what was given to her is plain injustice and tyranny. All Muslims must stay away from it.

### In short

- A husband cannot take back the dowry after intimacy, because she earned it and became its owner.
- Gifts between spouses are not recoverable either, since the marriage bond prevents it.
- The "firm commitment" is the covenant of marriage, sworn in Allah's name. The Prophet ﷺ told men at the Farewell Ḥajj to be kind to women because they took them by Allah's covenant.
- Forcing a wife to give back what she was given is open injustice.

<<<VERSE 4:22>>>
The verse now goes back to another practice from the days of ignorance: "Do not marry former wives of your fathers—except what was done previously. It was indeed a shameful, despicable, and evil practice." These verses give the rules about women who are forbidden in marriage. Some are forbidden forever and never become lawful under any condition. The others are not permanently forbidden, and they become lawful in certain circumstances. The permanently forbidden are of three kinds: by blood, by fosterage and by marriage. The kind mentioned at the end stays forbidden only until the woman is married to someone else.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not marry former wives of your fathers"**. In the days of ignorance, people saw nothing wrong in marrying their father's wife after he died. Allah forbade this shameful practice, an evil that brings His anger. How can a man go on calling a woman "mother" for years and then sink so low as to make her his wife after his father's death? The prohibition is made out of honour and respect for fathers, so their sons may not have relations with their wives after they die. A woman becomes forbidden to her husband's son as soon as the marriage contract is made, and there is a consensus on this.

The ruling is that marrying a father's lawfully wedded wife is unlawful. The verse does not require that the father had consummated the marriage. The fact that he married her is enough to forbid her to the son, and she will never be lawful for him. In the same way, it is not correct for a father to marry his son's wife, even if the son's marriage was only a formal nikāḥ and she has not yet come to live with him.

Ibn ʿAbbās described the situation in a report recorded by Ibn Jarīr. In the days of ignorance, people forbade what Allah forbids in marriage, except for marrying a stepmother and having two sisters as rival wives. Allah revealed "Do not marry former wives of your fathers" and "nor two sisters together at the same time" (4:23). ʿAṭāʾ and Qatādah are reported to have said the same. So this practice is forbidden for this ummah, and it is disgraced as an awful sin.

**"except what was done previously"**. The past is past, and what was done in the age of ignorance is forgiven. One explanation says that you will not be held to account for it. The saying that what has passed has passed is not just about marriage. It is a general rule of wisdom. Whenever a change takes place in life, in a family or in a nation, many past affairs look wrong by the new standards. Orders based on things that no longer matter cause endless problems. The right approach is to forget the past and put one's effort into reforming the present and the future.

**"It was indeed a shameful, despicable, and evil practice"**. Similarly Allah says in other verses: "Do not come near indecencies, openly or secretly" (6:151), the warning against al-fawāḥish, and "Do not go near adultery. It is truly a shameful deed and an evil way" (17:32). In this verse He adds a further word, maqtan, which means offensive. It is a sin in itself, and it makes a son hate his father after he marries his wife. People usually dislike those who married a woman before them. This is one reason the Mothers of the Faithful were not allowed to marry anyone after the Messenger ﷺ. They are the Mothers of the Faithful because they married the Messenger, who is like a father to the believers. His claim is greater than any father's claim, and love for him comes before each person's love for himself. Another explanation says that maqtan means it results in severe hatred from Allah.

ʿAṭāʾ ibn Abī Rabāḥ said that maqtan means that Allah will hate the person, and "an evil way" is for those who take this way. Whoever does this has turned away from the religion. He deserves the death penalty and the seizure of his property for the Muslim treasury. Aḥmad and the compilers of the Sunan record from al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib that his uncle Abū Burdah was dispatched by the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to deal with a man who had married his stepmother, with orders to put him to death and take his property.

### The people it was revealed about

The verse is said to have been revealed about Muḥsin ibn Abī Qays al-Anṣārī, and Allah then showed them which women are forbidden to marry. Another account names several men. Ḥiṣn ibn Abī al-Qays married the wife of his father, Kubayshah bint Maʿn. Al-Aswad ibn Khalaf married his father's wife. Ṣafwān ibn Umayyah married his father's wife Fākhitah bint al-Aswad ibn al-Muṭṭalib. Manẓūr ibn Zabbān married his father's wife Mulaykah bint Khārijah.

Ashʿath ibn Sawwār said that when Abū Qays, one of the righteous men of Madīnah, died, his son Qays proposed to his father's wife. She said to him, "I consider you my son. I will consult the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and see what he commands." She went and told him, and Allah revealed this verse.

### The inner reading

The verse points to the protection of honour and keeping within the limits of respect. If a man naturally hates that another man should take his wife to bed, then sons are all the more forbidden to disregard their fathers' rights and seek to bed their fathers' wives.

### In short

- A man may not marry a woman who was lawfully married to his father, whether or not the father consummated the marriage, and a father may not marry his son's wife.
- What was done before the revelation was forgiven. The past is past, and it is wise to put one's effort into the present and the future.
- Allah calls this practice shameful, offensive and an evil way, and one explanation holds that a man who does it has turned away from the religion.
- The verse is linked to several men of Madīnah who had married their fathers' widows, among them Muḥsin ibn Abī Qays.
- Inwardly, it protects honour and respect, as no son should do to his father what no man would want done to himself.
