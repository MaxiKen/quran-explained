<<<VERSE 4:37>>>
The last verse ended by saying that Allah does not like the arrogant and boastful. This one describes who they are: "those who are stingy, promote stinginess among people, and withhold Allah’s bounties. We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment." These are people who hold back rights they are obliged to give, fail to understand what they owe, and go to the other extreme by urging others, in word and in deed, to do the same.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who are stingy"**. The word for stinginess, bukhl, generally means falling short in giving money that is owed. But the background of the verse shows that it is used here in a wider sense that includes holding back both money and knowledge. Allah chides those who will not spend their money on what He has ordered, such as kindness to parents, compassion for relatives, orphans, the poor, the neighbour who is a relative, the companion on a journey, the needy traveller, and slaves and servants. They do not give Allah's right out of their wealth.

The Prophet ﷺ said, "What disease is worse than stinginess?" He also said, "Beware of stinginess, for it destroyed those before you. It urged them to cut their family ties, and they did, and it urged them to sin, and they did." The Prophet ﷺ said that every morning two angels come down. One says, "O Allah, bless the one who spends in the way of good." The other says, "O Allah, bring ruin on the miser's wealth." Al-Bukhārī and Muslim recorded it from Abū Hurairah. He also said to Asmāʾ, "Give, and do not count, for Allah will then count against you. And do not withhold, for Allah will then withhold from you. Give what you can." Abū Hurairah reported that he said, "A generous person is near to Allah, near to Paradise and near to people. The miser is far from Allah, far from Paradise and far from people, and close to the Fire. A generous ignorant person is dearer to Allah than a miserly worshipper." At-Tirmidhī recorded it. Abū Saʿīd reported that he said, "Two traits cannot be joined in a true Muslim, stinginess and bad character," and at-Tirmidhī recorded that too.

**"promote stinginess among people"**. They spread it to others by their words and deeds. Short explanations say that they tell others to hide wealth, knowledge and other gifts of Allah, and do not show people the truth but hide it and show falsehood. Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Zayd said that the verse was revealed about a group of Jews who went to some of the Helpers, the Anṣār, mixed with them and gave them advice. They said, "Do not spend your wealth, for we fear that you will end up poor." Allah then revealed this verse.

**"and withhold Allah’s bounties"**. A miser is ungrateful for Allah's favour, because the effect of it does not show on him, in his food, his clothes or what he gives. Allah says the same elsewhere: "Surely humankind is ungrateful to their Lord—and they certainly attest to this—and they are truly extreme in their love of ˹worldly˺ gains" (100:6 to 100:8). Their manners and conduct bear witness to it. The word kufr means to cover something. The miser covers up the favours Allah has granted him by not spreading them, so he is called a kāfir, ungrateful, in respect of those favours. A hadith says, "When Allah gives a servant a blessing, He likes its effect to be seen on him."

Some of the Salaf said that this verse describes the Jews who hid what they knew about the description of Muḥammad ﷺ. The general sense certainly includes this. They were also convinced of the prophecies in their Scriptures about his coming and the signs of his appearance, but they would not act on this knowledge and would not pass it on to others who might. Ibn ʿAbbās reported that the verse was about the Jews of Madīnah, who were proud and tight-fisted in the extreme when it came to money. In the same way, Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and his companions hid the description of Muḥammad ﷺ given in their Scripture. The words of the verse suggest stinginess with money, though stinginess with knowledge is included. Like the next verse, it speaks of spending on relatives and the weak.

Those who are stingy with what Allah has given them, in knowledge and in faith, are ungrateful to Allah and do not acknowledge His blessings.

**"We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment"**. This is why He threatens them. Allah has prepared a humiliating punishment for those who disbelieve in this and other matters, a punishment they cannot escape. Short explanations say that hiding the truth is one of the marks of disbelief.

### Why a person who owes everything to Allah is generous

Everything a person owns is given by Allah. That fact calls on him to surrender to Allah and become His worshipper. Such a person is naturally humble, and his humility shows in his dealings with others. He is gracious toward his mother and father. He feels Allah's watchful presence with everyone he meets. He pays each person his due according to the relationship and the need. To overlook any person, in whatever role he has to deal with him, would feel to him like risking being overlooked by Allah.

A person who does not surrender to Allah develops an overbearing pride. He thinks that all he owns is the product of his own work and intelligence, and so he believes his earnings belong to him alone. To be associated with poor relatives and destitute people seems a disgrace to him. He spends lavishly to satisfy his desires and serve his own interests, and he becomes tight-fisted about any expense that does not please his ego. He is very generous where he gains publicity and a miser when it comes to religious causes that nobody sees.

### The inner reading

Servanthood is to cling closely to the command and to part from what is forbidden. To "associate nothing with Him" has an outward form and a hidden form. The outward form of shirk is to believe in objects of worship other than Allah, iʿtiqād maʿbūdin siwāhi. Its hidden form is to look at anything that exists other than Him. To declare His unity is to know that everything contingent is brought into being by Allah and lasts through Him. He makes things happen, brings them forth and keeps them in being. There is not an atom, a splinter or the slightest trace that belongs to anyone else and has the power to bring things into existence or originate them.

The subtle kinds of showing off are hidden idolatry, shirk khafī. They include secret tricks and the hidden corners of pride, acting for the eyes of creatures, seeking the pleasure of their praise and wilting when they reject or blame.

"Be kind to parents": iḥsān, acting with kindness, follows degrees of closeness. You were commanded to keep your parents' rights first because they are your kin, they raised you, and you reached full growth and knowledge through them. When your closeness and company with your kin, the poor, the needy, orphans and others of that kind is sound and good, you will be raised through that to be worthy of His company. Among your neighbours are your two angels. Do not annoy them with your disobedience, and respect their right in what you give them to record from your good deeds.

### In short

- The stingy refuse to give what they owe, urge others to be stingy and hide what Allah has given them, whether wealth or knowledge. Their ingratitude shows in their lives.
- The verse is applied to the Jews who hid the description of Muḥammad ﷺ in their Scriptures, though its general meaning covers every miser, and Allah has prepared a humiliating punishment for those who disbelieve.
- The Prophet ﷺ praised the generous as close to Allah, to Paradise and to people, and said that stinginess and bad character cannot go together in a true Muslim.
- A person who knows everything comes from Allah is humble and gives each person his due, while a proud person thinks his wealth is his own.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:38>>>
The verse that follows names another group, who are not stingy with money in every case but spend for the wrong reason: "Likewise for those who spend their wealth to show off and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day. And whoever takes Satan as an associate—what an evil associate they have." Allah first named the punished misers who do not spend. He now names those who do spend, to show off and win a reputation for generosity, and not for the sake of Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Likewise for those who spend their wealth to show off"**. These people usually hold back from spending in Allah's way and tell others to do the same. When they want to impress others, they do spend. In one explanation, they are the Jewish leaders who spend to be seen by people, so that people say they follow the way of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and give and spend their money. Another explanation says that the likes of the hypocrites and the Meccans are meant. It is as disgraceful to be niggardly and slow in giving what is owed as it is to spend to show off or to spend on aimless things. A good deed done for the applause of others, and not for Allah alone, is not accepted. In the hadith, it has been called shirk.

Abū Hurairah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that Allah says, "I am free of any need for partners. Whoever does a good deed and joins someone else to Me in it, I leave him to his deed, with the partner he made." Shaddād ibn Aws said that he heard the Prophet ﷺ say, "Whoever prays to show off has committed shirk, whoever fasts to show off has committed shirk, and whoever gives charity to show off has committed shirk." Aḥmad recorded it, as cited in Mishkāt. Maḥmūd ibn Labīd said that the Prophet ﷺ said, "What I fear most for you is the lesser shirk." The Companions asked, "O Messenger of Allah, what is the lesser shirk?" He said, "Riyāʾ, doing a good deed just to be seen." This too is Aḥmad's. Al-Baihaqī adds a report that on the Day of Judgment, when the rewards of good deeds are given out, Allah will say to those who did good only for show, "Go to those you wanted to see you do good deeds in the world, and see for yourselves whether they have any reward for your deeds."

A hadith says that the first three persons the Fire will feed on are a scholar, a fighter and a spender who showed off through their deeds. The wealthy man will say, "I did not leave a single cause that You love to be spent on without spending on it for You." Allah will say, "You lie. You only did it so people would say, 'He is generous,' and it was said." That is, he got his reward in the life of this world, which is what he sought by his action.

**"and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day"**. Since they have no faith in Allah and the Last Day, spending for Allah's pleasure and for a reward in the next life never even arises. They are accomplices of Shayṭān, and so their end will be no different from his. It was Shayṭān who lured them into this wrong, instead of doing the good deed as it should be done. He excited and tempted them, and made the evil look good.

**"And whoever takes Satan as an associate—what an evil associate they have"**. The one who takes Satan as a companion follows his orders, as these people do, and an evil companion he has. Satan is a helper in this world, and in the Fire he becomes a bad companion.

### The inner reading

These people are included in Allah's words that He does not love the conceited and boastful, the fakhūr (4:36). Their punishment now is that they are not among those whom He loves. That is trial enough. The conceited look at themselves, and those who show off look at others like them. Both bear the brand of hidden shirk, and Allah does not love those who join partners with Him.

In a herd of camels, a fakhūr is a she-camel whose teats are blocked, so that milk collects in her udder in large amounts. A buyer thinks it is normal for her, but it is not. In the same way, anyone who looks only at himself and his standing at this moment is making false claims. He is a fakhūr, and Allah does not love him. This also holds for the person who spends to show off.

### The fellow travellers of Satan

People who turn proud instead of humble after receiving Allah's bounties, and people who spend what Allah gave them on selfish investments and not on causes Allah approves, are fellow travellers of Satan. Lured by the passing gains of this life, they lose interest in the lasting gain Allah has promised. For them there will be nothing but severe punishment in the Hereafter. A person calls unimportant whatever he does not practise. This is self-centred theorising and an attempt at self-justification, and all such efforts are futile and worthless in Allah's eyes.

### In short

- Some people spend, but only to be seen and praised. They do not believe in Allah or the Last Day, and Allah does not accept what is done to show off.
- The Prophet ﷺ called showing off the lesser shirk and said that Allah leaves such a person to his deed. On the Day, those who did good for show will be told to ask those they wanted to impress for their reward.
- Satan is their companion in this world and an evil one, and the end of his friends will be like his.
- Inwardly, they are like the she-camel with blocked teats, who looks full but is not, and they belong to those Allah does not love.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:39>>>
This verse turns from blame to persuasion: "What harm could have come to them if they had believed in Allah and the Last Day and donated from what Allah has provided for them? And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of them." The earlier verses condemned those who refused to believe in Allah and the Hereafter and who were miserly. These verses invite people to believe and to engage in the profitable business of spending in Allah's way. The later verses, 4:41 and 4:42, describe the hard scenes on the plains of Resurrection as a warning for those who refuse to believe and to do good.

### Phrase by phrase

**"What harm could have come to them if they had believed in Allah and the Last Day and donated from what Allah has provided for them?"** The question is meant to deny that there is any harm. What was so hard, difficult or frightening about it? All of it is easy. Believing, following and acting on these things hurt no one and cause no inconvenience. The harm lies in what they follow instead. Why would someone choose disobedience, when its certain result is disaster in the Hereafter? What harm would it do them to believe in Allah, to walk the righteous path, to put sincerity in the place of showing off, and to wait for the reward He has promised in the Hereafter to people who do good and spend His gifts on what He loves and is pleased with? According to one explanation, "them" refers to the Jews. It asks what they have to fear if they believe in Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, and in the resurrection after death and the bliss of Paradise, and if they spend in Allah's way from what He has given them. The question could also be put to anyone who spends only to show off. A short explanation says that they would have got everything good by being sincere.

**"And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of them"**. He knows their intentions, whether good or evil. He knows who deserves success, and He grants it and guides them to the deeds that earn His pleasure. He also knows who deserves failure and exclusion from His great mercy, which means total loss in this life and the next. We seek refuge in Allah from such an end. Another explanation says that none of their affairs is hidden from Him, and each will be repaid for what he does. It adds that this includes those among the Jews who believe and those who do not.

### The inner reading

For these people there is no difficulty in placing their faith and trust in Allah. Had they believed, they would have gained great honour in this world and in the next. What keeps them from turning to Him is only their lack of commitment and respect.

### In short

- The verse asks what harm believing in Allah and the Last Day and spending from His gifts could possibly do. There is no harm in it, and the harm is in the path they chose.
- Allah knows their intentions and knows who deserves guidance and who deserves failure.
- Inwardly, nothing keeps them from turning to Him except a lack of commitment and respect.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:40>>>
After the warnings, the surah gives a promise about Allah's justice: "Indeed, Allah never wrongs ˹anyone˺—even by an atom’s weight. And if it is a good deed, He will multiply it many times over and will give a great reward out of His grace." Allah does not cheat any of His servants on the Day of Resurrection, whether the matter is as small as a mustard seed or a speck of dust.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, Allah never wrongs ˹anyone˺—even by an atom’s weight"**. The word dharrah has generally been translated as an atom or particle. Some commentators point out that it names the tiniest kind of red ant, which the Arabs used as a picture of something weightless and slight. Two short explanations say that Allah will not leave the unbeliever's works the weight of an ant in the Hereafter, or add anything to a person's evil deeds. Allah says elsewhere: "We will set up the scales of justice on the Day of Judgment, so no soul will be wronged in the least. And ˹even˺ if a deed is the weight of a mustard seed, We will bring it forth" (21:47). Luqmān said to his son, "˹Even˺ if a deed were the weight of a mustard seed—be it ˹hidden˺ in a rock or in the heavens or the earth—Allah will bring it forth" (31:16). The Qur'an also says that on that Day people will proceed in separate groups to be shown the results of their deeds, and whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it (99:6 to 99:8).

The two Ṣaḥīḥs record the long hadith of intercession, narrated by Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that Allah will say, "Go back and take out of the Fire everyone in whose heart you find the weight of a mustard seed of faith." In another version, Allah says, "Whoever had the least, least, least speck of faith, take him out of the Fire," and they bring out many people. Abū Saʿīd then said, "Read, if you wish: Surely Allah wrongs not even the weight of a speck of dust."

**"And if it is a good deed, He will multiply it many times over"**. Allah does not let the reward of good deeds be lessened in any way or in any amount. He adds extra reward, in multiples, as special favours, and the life to come will show this great mercy. By Allah's lowest measure of reward, one good deed is recorded as ten. It does not stop there. Through His mercy and grace He adds more and more to this treasure of good deeds. A short explanation says that He doubles it from one to ten times, and another says from ten times up to more than seven hundred, in the case of a believer. Some hadiths say that there are deeds whose reward rises as high as two million times. Allah is al-Karīm, the Most Noble and Most Generous, and in His limitless mercy He increases His blessing on His servants so far that it cannot be counted or measured in human terms. The extent of His generosity is beyond human imagination, and no one can calculate what cannot be calculated.

Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded the account of Abū ʿUthmān an-Nahdī, who spent more time with Abū Hurayrah than anyone. One year Abū Hurayrah left for Ḥajj ahead of him, and Abū ʿUthmān heard people in al-Baṣrah saying that he reported a saying of the Messenger ﷺ that Allah repays a good deed with a million deeds. Abū ʿUthmān was shocked, since he had never heard this from him. He set out to meet him and, finding that he had already gone to Ḥajj, he followed him there to ask about the hadith. In another version through Abū ʿUthmān, Abū Hurayrah said, "By Allah, I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say, 'Allah rewards a good deed with two million deeds.'" Then he recited, "The enjoyment of this worldly life is insignificant compared to that of the Hereafter" (9:38).

**"and will give a great reward out of His grace"**. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, Qatādah, al-Ḥasan, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ʿIkrimah and Abū Hurayrah said that the great reward is Paradise. We ask Allah for His pleasure and for Paradise. Another explanation says that it is an immense reward from Him, in addition to the multiplying, which no one can estimate.

### Is a disbeliever's punishment made lighter?

Saʿīd ibn Jubayr commented on "if it is a good deed, He will multiply it". He said that the disbeliever's punishment will be lightened on the Day of Resurrection, but he will never leave the Fire. For proof he used an authentic hadith in which al-ʿAbbās said, "O Messenger of Allah, your uncle Abū Ṭālib used to protect and support you. Did you do him any good?" The Messenger ﷺ said, "Yes. He is in a shallow place in the Fire, and were it not for me, he would be in its deepest depths." This hadith, however, applies only to Abū Ṭālib, not to other disbelievers. In support, Abū Dāwūd aṭ-Ṭayālisī has in his Musnad a saying that Anas heard from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ: "Allah does not wrong a believer in even one good deed. He is rewarded for it with provision in this life and rewarded for it in the Hereafter. As for the disbeliever, he is given provision in this life for his good deeds, and on the Day of Resurrection he will have no good deed left."

### The inner reading

Allah does not deprive them of anything in their reward. He is first with His bounty toward them, though they have not deserved it, and then He doubles their wages for their works. It is impossible to call anything He decrees wrongdoing, since all creation and all kingship are His. A wrongdoer is someone who passes a limit that was set for him, and the glory of Allah's sublime measure cannot be described in such a way.

### In short

- Allah never wrongs anyone, not even by the weight of an atom or the smallest ant. He will bring out every deed, and He takes out of the Fire anyone with the slightest faith.
- A good deed is at least ten times rewarded, and some hadiths speak of a million or two million times. The "great reward" is Paradise.
- For the disbeliever, one view is that the punishment may be lightened but he never leaves the Fire, and a hadith says that his good deeds are repaid in this life only.
- Inwardly, Allah is first with His bounty, and nothing He does can be called wrong, since all is His.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:41>>>
The next verse brings the scene of the Day of Judgment before the reader: "So how will it be when We bring a witness from every faith-community and bring you ˹O Prophet˺ as a witness against yours?" The words are an invitation to picture the weight of what will happen, and a warning to the disbelievers of Quraysh.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So how will it be"**. Allah is describing the horrors and hardships of the Day of Resurrection. A short explanation asks how the unbelievers will react. Their anxiety will have no limit.

**"when We bring a witness from every faith-community"**. The witnesses are the prophets. Every prophet of every community will appear as a witness to the good and bad deeds of his own people. A short explanation says that each prophet will testify that he delivered the message. Allah says elsewhere that on that Day the earth will be lit by its Lord's light, the book will be set down, and the prophets and the witnesses will be summoned (39:69). He also says that He will call against every faith-community a witness of their own (16:89).

**"and bring you ˹O Prophet˺ as a witness against yours"**. Muḥammad ﷺ, the last of the prophets, will appear as a witness against his community. He will testify in particular about the disbelievers and idolaters among them. He will say that they saw clear signs and miracles, yet denied and rejected them, and in the end refused to believe in the oneness of Allah and in his prophethood. Another explanation says that Allah brings him as a witness in favour of his community, testifying that it is reliable and upright. His community, in turn, will witness for the other prophets against their peoples when those peoples claim that no message reached them. Another puts it as bringing the Prophet ﷺ as a witness for all the communities and messengers.

**Whom the word "yours" refers to**. Some commentators say that it points to the disbelievers and hypocrites of the Prophet's own time. Others say that it covers the whole community until the Last Day. Some hadiths say that the deeds of his community are being shown to him continuously. From this we also learn that the prophets of earlier communities will be witnesses over their communities and that the Prophet ﷺ will testify to the deeds of his own. The way the Qur'an puts it also tells us that no prophet will come after him who could be a witness over a community of his own. If there were one, the Qur'an would have mentioned him and the witness he would give. In that light, this verse is also a proof of the finality of prophethood.

### The Prophet ﷺ's tears

Al-Bukhārī recorded ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd's account. The Prophet ﷺ asked him to recite, and Ibn Masʿūd answered that it had been revealed to the Prophet himself. The Prophet ﷺ replied that he liked to hear it from others. Ibn Masʿūd recited Sūrat an-Nisāʾ until he reached this verse, and the Prophet ﷺ said, "Enough for now." Ibn Masʿūd looked at him and saw that his eyes were full of tears. Al-Qasṭallānī writes that the verse put the whole scene of the Hereafter before his eyes, and he thought of those in his community who were short in good deeds, and that is why the tears came.

### The ordinary man who is made a witness

A messenger of the truth is just an ordinary person at the start of his mission. No pomp or show surrounds him. So the prominent leaders and intellectuals of society reject him. They cannot understand that a man with no outward splendour could be so important that Allah would choose him to bring His message to humanity.

But when the Last Day comes near and the divine court is ready to give its verdict, those leaders will be astonished to see that the very ordinary man whom they rejected as worthless has been honoured as Allah's witness and raised above the nations. Those who rejected him will stand among the accused in that court, while the man they rejected sits in the place of Allah's spokesman. It will be so terrible and hard a moment for them that they will wish the earth would split open and swallow them. But their regret and their sense of humiliation will serve no purpose then. The whole record of what they said, did and thought will be with Allah. They will be told that their rejection of the truth did not come from ignorance but from pride. They thought themselves better than the one who called to the truth, and accepting reality, even when it was laid out before them, seemed to them like setting their own greatness at nothing.

### The inner reading

Since the Messenger ﷺ will be a witness against his community and also its intercessor, he will bear witness only to what can be the object of his intercession.

### In short

- On the Day of Judgment, a prophet from every community will testify about it, and Muḥammad ﷺ will testify against his own community, and for it.
- The disbelievers of Quraysh are warned that clear signs came to them and they refused. Some say the verse is also a proof that there is no prophet after Muḥammad ﷺ.
- When Ibn Masʿūd recited this verse to him, the Prophet ﷺ said "Enough" and wept, thinking of those short in good deeds.
- The ordinary man the proud rejected will stand as Allah's witness, and the proud will see that their rejection was pride and not ignorance.

<<<VERSE 4:42>>>
The final verse of this group shows the disbelievers' utter despair: "On that Day, those who denied ˹Allah˺ and disobeyed the Messenger will wish they were reduced to dust. And they will never be able to hide anything from Allah." On that Day they would rather cease to exist than face it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"On that Day, those who denied ˹Allah˺ and disobeyed the Messenger"**. The day is the Day of Resurrection. The people meant are those who disbelieved in Allah and refused the Messenger by rejecting his message.

**"will wish they were reduced to dust"**. They will wish to go out of existence rather than face the ordeal, to become a patch of the earth beneath them, or that the earth would crack open and swallow them, leaving only dust mixed with dust on top, so that they would be spared the scrutiny and the punishment of that fateful time. Because of the horror of the gathering place and the disgrace, shame and humiliation that they will suffer, they will wish that the earth would open and swallow them. The wording is "they will wish that the earth were levelled with them", and it has been translated literally to convey some of its force. The Arabic verb may be read in the passive or the active, with a doubled consonant or not. Another explanation says that they will wish to be dust like the animals. Allah says in another verse that the day will come when every person sees what his hands have sent forward and the disbeliever says, "I wish I were dust" (78:40). The same wish appears in that surah, where on the plains of Resurrection the disbelievers will see all animals turned to dust after settling accounts for each other's wrongs, and they too will long to become dust.

**"And they will never be able to hide anything from Allah"**. It means that these disbelievers will be unable to keep any of their beliefs or deeds hidden from Allah. They will own up to all they did and will hide none of it. Their own hands and feet will confess, the prophets will give evidence, and everything will be there in the books of deeds. Allah will seal their mouths so they cannot speak, and He will give their limbs permission to bear witness to what they did. According to one explanation, they will not say, "O Allah, our Lord, we did not associate any partner with You."

### An apparent contradiction

Another verse says that their only argument on that Day will be, "By Allah, our Lord! We were never polytheists" (6:23). One explanation notes that they hide no talk at this stage, although at another stage they do hide it and say, "By God, our Lord, we never associated anything with You." So the two verses seem to disagree. ʿAbd ar-Razzāq recorded that Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said that a man came to Ibn ʿAbbās and said, "There are things in the Qur'an that confuse me." Ibn ʿAbbās asked, "What do you doubt?" The man said, "Not doubts, but things that confuse me." Ibn ʿAbbās said, "Tell me what confused you." The man said, "I hear Allah say that they will have no argument except 'By Allah, our Lord, we were not polytheists,' but He also says that they will never hide a single fact from Allah. They did hide something."

Ibn ʿAbbās replied that when the polytheists see on the Day of Resurrection that Allah does not forgive anyone except the people of Islam, and that He forgives sins however great except shirk, they will lie. They will say, "By Allah, our Lord, we were not polytheists," hoping that Allah will forgive them. But Allah will seal their mouths, and their hands and feet will tell what they did. Then they will not be able to hide anything from Allah. The disbelievers, when they see that no one but true Muslims is being admitted to Paradise, will decide to deny flatly that they ever committed shirk or any other wrong, hoping that their plan will work and save them. But right after this denial, the parts of their bodies will give evidence against them, and that will ruin their plan of hiding the truth. They will then confess everything.

### The inner reading

The disbelievers will become remorseful, but it will not help them. They will bite their fingertips, but their anguish will not ease. They will be wrapped in the veil of disgrace and moved to the homes of trial and hardship.

### In short

- On the Day of Resurrection, the disbelievers who rejected the Messenger will wish the earth would swallow them or that they were dust.
- They will not be able to hide anything from Allah. Their hands and feet will confess, the prophets will testify, and the books of deeds will be there.
- Ibn ʿAbbās explained that when they first lie that they were never polytheists, it is a final attempt to be saved, and their limbs will then speak against them.
