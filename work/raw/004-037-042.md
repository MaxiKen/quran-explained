<<<VERSE 4:37>>>
The portrait of the withholding disposition, now in full colour: "those who are stingy, promote stinginess among people, and withhold Allah's bounties. We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment". The previous verse ended by naming the arrogant and the boastful. This one shows what they hoard and how they recruit.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who are stingy"** describes those who refuse to spend their money on what Allah ordered: kindness to parents and compassion to relatives, orphans, the poor, the relative who is also a neighbour, the companion during travel, the needy wayfarer, the slaves and servants. They do not give Allah's right from their wealth. The word bukhl, niggardliness, is generally used for shortcoming in financial rights, but the background of the revelation gives it its general sense, covering close-fistedness in money and in knowledge both.

**"promote stinginess among people"** adds the negative extreme: through word and deed they persuade others to take up the habit. Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Zayd said the verse was revealed about a group of Jews who used to mix with certain men of the Helpers and advise them not to spend their wealth, saying they feared poverty for them. Most exegetes said the verse came about the Jews who concealed the description of Muḥammad ﷺ written in their Scriptures, and al-Kalbī said the like, while one report names Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and his companions, and Mujāhid read the three verses together as revealed about the Jews. The general meaning includes all of it, for the miserly conduct with knowledge is as real as the miserly conduct with money.

**"and withhold Allah's bounties"** means the miser covers the favours Allah blessed him with and does not spread them, so their effect does not appear on him, in his food, his clothes or what he gives. Kufr means to cover something, and so the miser is described by the term kāfir regarding the favours granted him, ungrateful for them, as the Qur'an says of man that he is ungrateful to his Lord and bears witness to it by his manners and conduct (100:6-7), and violent in the love of wealth (100:8). A hadith states: "When Allah grants a servant a favour, He likes that its effect appears on him." Some of the Salaf held the whole of this reading.

**"We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment"** is the threatened end, disgraceful torment for the arrogance and the hiding both.

### The disease and its company

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked: "What disease is more serious than being stingy?" And he said: "Beware of being stingy, for it destroyed those who were before you, as it encouraged them to cut their relations and they did, and it encouraged them to commit sin and they did." Every morning two angels descend, one saying, "O Allah, bless him who spends in the way of good", and the other, "O Allah, send destruction on the wealth of the miser", in a report al-Bukhārī and Muslim carry from Abū Hurairah. From Sayyidah Asmāʾ comes the command: "Give, and do not calculate, for Allah will then calculate in your case. Do not withhold, for Allah will then withhold in your case, and give whatever you can." Abū Hurairah narrates that the generous sit near Allah, near Paradise and near people, while the miser keeps his distance from Allah, from Paradise and from people and sits near the Fire. The generous ignoramus, he adds, is dearer to Allah than a devotee grown miserly. Abū Saʿīd narrates the pair that cannot combine in a true believer: miserliness and bad morals.

### The inner reading

Read inwardly, the passage turns the two verses into one scale of hiddenness. Shirk has an open face and a hidden one. Openly it is belief in objects of worship other than God, what the masters call iʿtiqād of a maʿbūdin siwāhi. Hiddenly it is the glance that slides toward any existing thing besides Him, the mulāḥaẓa of a mawjūdin siwāhi. Under the hidden face gather the fine disguises of showing off and guile, the secret lairs of pride, performance staged for created eyes, the craving of their praise and the wilting under their censure. All of it is concealed idolatry. No atom, splinter or particle belongs to any but Him with power to originate anything. And iḥsān follows degrees of association: the parents' rights come first because they are the kin who raised a person to maturity, and when companionship has become sound with the near kindred, the poor, the needy and the orphans, a person is raised up from that to become worthy of His companionship. Among the neighbours are the two angels, who must not be annoyed with disobedience, and whose right observed in acts of kindness is what they are given to record.

### In short

- The stingy withhold both money and knowledge, and recruit others to the habit, as the Jews of Madinah withheld the description of the Prophet ﷺ and counselled the Helpers against spending.
- The miser is a coverer of favours, which is the literal sense of kufr, and the effect of Allah's favour never shows on him.
- The destruction stingy conduct brought on earlier peoples is held up as the disease more serious than any other.
- Read inwardly, the hidden shirk of showing off and pride stands behind the open hoarding, and kindness begun at home rises toward the companionship of God.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:38>>>
The second face of the arrogant spender: "Likewise for those who spend their wealth to show off and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day. And whoever takes Satan as an associate—what an evil associate they have". Not the miser, but his twin, who gives everything for the eye of the onlooker. The verse repeats the fate of verse 37's ending for them, for they are of the conceited and boastful the verse before (4:36) had already named.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Likewise for those who spend their wealth to show off"** announces the same humiliating punishment for them. They spend only when wishing to show off before people, to gain the reputation of generosity and not for the Face of Allah. One reading of the first audience names the Jewish leaders who gave so people would say they followed the practice of Abraham, and the hypocrites and the Meccans are named in the same breath.

**"and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day"** adds their creed to the charge. With no faith in Allah or in resurrection, the question of spending for His pleasure and the reward of the life to come simply does not arise.

**"whoever takes Satan as an associate"** names the prompter. It was Shayṭān who lured them into the evil act instead of performing the good deed as it should be performed, encouraging and exciting them by making the evil appear good. The end of it all will be no different from the end of their comrade.

### The minor shirk of the eye

A good deed aimed at drawing applause is not acceptable in the sight of Allah, and in hadith it is declared shirk, the associating of others with Allah's divinity. Allah Almighty says: "I am absolutely free of any need to have partners. Whoever does a good deed and associates in it someone else with Me, I leave him with this deed of his." Shaddād ibn Aws heard the Prophet ﷺ say: "Whoever performed ṣalāh to show off, committed shirk. Whoever fasted to show off, committed shirk. And whoever gave in charity to show off, committed shirk." Maḥmūd ibn Labīd narrates: "What fills me with grave apprehensions about you is the minor shirk." The Companions asked what that was, and he said: riyāʿ, doing a good deed just to show off. A narration of al-Baihaqī adds the settlement of the Day of Doom, when Allah will say to those who did good for applause: "Go to those you wanted to see you doing good deeds in the mortal world, and find out for yourself if they have the reward for your deeds." A hadith tells of the first three persons on whom the Fire will feed: a scholar, a fighter and a spender who showed off with their actions. In the report of the two angels that descends every morning, the one praying for the spender and the other for the miser's ruin is carried from Abū Hurairah. The wealthy will say he did not leave any area Allah likes to be spent on but spent on it, and Allah will answer: "You lie. You only did that so that it would be said, 'He is generous.' And it was said." The reward was collected in the life, and nothing else is owed.

### The she-camel

In the camel flock, the fakhūr is the she-camel with blocked teats, so that milk gathers in the udder beyond her real state. The buyer reads the swelling as her plenty, and is deceived. So too the one whose eye rests on himself and his present standing: his claim is false, and God does not love him. Conceit is the eye turned on oneself, and showing off the eye turned on others, and hidden idolatry brands them both, for God loves not the associaters. Their immediate punishment is that they stand outside the company of those whom He loves, and that alone is tribulation enough.

### In short

- Spending for the eye of people is the twin of hoarding, and its reward is paid out in full in this world.
- Riyāʿ is the minor shirk, and the ṣalāh, the fast and the charity done for show all fall under it.
- On the Day of Doom the show-offs are sent to collect their wage from those they impressed.
- The fakhūr she-camel, swollen with withheld milk, is the image of the one full of false claims.

<<<VERSE 4:39>>>
The question that closes the trap: "What harm could have come to them if they had believed in Allah and the Last Day and donated from what Allah has provided for them? And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of them". What, in fact, would it have cost them?

### Phrase by phrase

**"What harm could have come to them"** is the interrogative of disavowal: there is no harm in it at all. Nothing about believing in Allah, going on the righteous path and replacing showing off with sincerity is tough, difficult or frightening. Taking to these does not hurt, harm or inconvenience anyone. The harm lies in what they follow instead.

**"if they had believed in Allah and the Last Day and donated from what Allah has provided for them"** holds the two acts. Belief and spending, the two things the misers withheld and the show-offs counterfeited, are as easy as they come, and had they done them they would have received everything that is good, attaining glorious honour in this world and the hereafter.

**"And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of them"** reaches the intentions, good or evil. He knows who deserves success, and grants success and guidance, and who deserves expulsion from His great mercy, which is to fail in both lives.

### The nothing they were guarding

The verse exposes the arithmetic. The kāfir, as the earlier verse established, is a coverer, for kufr means covering, and what these people covered was the favour of the One who provided for them, as the Salaf read in the hadith that when Allah grants a favour He likes its effect to appear. Man stands witness against himself by his manners and conduct, ungrateful to his Lord (100:6-7) and violent in the love of wealth (100:8). Nothing prompts the turn away from God but a lack of commitment and respect, and the certain outcome of that disobedience is nothing but disaster in the Akhirah. Had they held the belief and made the donation, the loss would have been nothing, and the gain would have been the doubled reward the next verse holds out.

### In short

- Belief and spending cost nothing and inconvenience no one, and the question hangs the refusal on nothing but pride.
- What they guarded was not theirs, and covering the Giver's favour is the literal sense of kufr.
- The verse's close rests on Allah's perfect knowledge of intentions, the successful and the failed alike.

<<<VERSE 4:40>>>
The justice that makes the wish for dust comprehensible: "Indeed, Allah never wrongs ˹anyone˺—even by an atom's weight. And if it is a good deed, He will multiply it many times over and will give a great reward out of His grace". After the hoarders and the show-offs, the scale itself is described.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah never wrongs ˹anyone˺—even by an atom's weight"** states that on the Day of Resurrection no servant receives injustice from Him, not the weight of a mustard seed nor a speck of dust. The wrongdoer is one who transgresses a limit ordained for him, and the creation is His creation and the kingdom His kingdom, so it is impossible to describe what He has decreed as wrongdoing.

**"And if it is a good deed, He will multiply it many times over"** — with Allah the least measure is that one good deed is recorded as ten, and the multiplication runs up to seven hundred times and beyond, and He is first with His bounty for His servants before they have deserved anything.

**"and will give a great reward out of His grace"** refers, in the reading of Abū Hurayrah, ʿIkrimah, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah and Ad-Daḥḥāk, to Paradise itself.

### The balances of the Book

The verse stands with the whole company of the reckoning: "We shall set up balances of justice" (21:47), Luqman's counsel to his son that a mustard seed's weight, though it hide in rock or heavens or earth, is brought forth by Allah (31:16), and the Day mankind walks in scattered groups to be shown its deeds, where a speck of dust's worth of good or evil is seen (99:6-8). The two Ṣaḥīḥs recorded the long hadith of the intercession narrated by Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, in which Allah says: "Go back, and take out of the Fire everyone in whose heart you find the weight of a mustard seed of faith", and in another narration: "Whoever had the least, least, least speck of faith, take him out of the Fire", and many people are taken out. Abū Saʿīd then said: "Read, if you will, 'Surely, Allah wrongs not even of the weight of a speck of dust.'"

### The least of the disbeliever's ease

Saʿīd ibn Jubayr commented on the doubling that the disbeliever's punishment is eased on the Day of Resurrection, yet the Fire never releases him. His evidence is the authentic hadith in which al-ʿAbbās said: "O Messenger of Allah, your uncle Abū Ṭālib used to protect and support you, did you benefit him at all?" The Messenger ﷺ said: "Yes. He is in a shallow area in Hell-fire, and were it not for me, he would have been in the deepest depths of the Fire." The lessening applies to Abū Ṭālib alone, not to the rest of the disbelievers. Supporting this, the Musnad of Abu Dāwūd at-Tayālisī carries from Anas the word of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ:

> Allah does not wrong the faithful even concerning one good action, for he will be rewarded for it by provision in this life and awarded for it in the Hereafter. As for the disbeliever, he will be provided provision in this life for his good action, and on the Day of Resurrection he will not have any good deed.

### The million and the two million

Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded Abū ʿUthmān an-Nahdī's pursuit of this report. No one, he said, had kept Abū Hurayrah's company longer than he. One year the older man went on Ḥajj before him, and at al-Baṣrah he found people claiming Abū Hurayrah narrated that he heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say, "Allah rewards the good deed with a million deeds." He protested that he had never once heard it from him. Setting out to meet Abū Hurayrah, he learned he had left for another Ḥajj, and followed him there to ask. In Ibn Abī Ḥātim's second chain, Abū ʿUthmān put the Baṣrah claim to Abū Hurayrah's face, and the answer came back sworn: by Allah, what he heard from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was that Allah rewards the good deed with two million deeds. He then recited: "But little is the enjoyment of the life of this world as compared to the Hereafter" (9:38). In truth Allah is al-Karīm, the most-noble, the most-generous, and He magnifies the blessing past all count and human measure. As for the dharrah of the verse, rendered here as particle, some commentators say it names the smallest of red ants, which the Arabs cited as the very type of the light and the little.

### In short

- No soul is wronged by so much as a speck, and the balances, the mustard seed and the scattered groups all carry the same guarantee.
- A speck of faith pulls a person out of the Fire, and the least of the disbeliever's easing, like Abū Ṭālib's shallow place, is exceptional, not a general hope.
- The faithful are repaid twice, here and hereafter, while the disbeliever's good is paid out in this life only.
- The good deed starts at tenfold, rises through seven hundred to the two million Abū Hurayrah swore to, and ends in Paradise, for the Giver is al-Karīm.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:41>>>
The scene of the witnesses: "So how will it be when We bring a witness from every faith-community and bring you ˹O Prophet˺ as a witness against yours". The question is addressed to imagination: picture it, and then measure what your disobedience will feel like.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So how will it be"** invites visualization of the gravity of the Last Day's site. How shall the predicament of the disbelievers be, when every community's witness is brought forward?

**"when We bring a witness from every faith-community"** means the prophets, each testifying that he delivered the message to his people, as the Qur'an says: "The earth will shine with the light of its Lord, and the Book will be placed open, and the Prophets and the witnesses will be brought forward" (39:69), and "the Day when We shall raise up from every nation a witness against them from amongst themselves" (16:89).

**"and bring you ˹O Prophet˺ as a witness against yours"** sets Muḥammad ﷺ, the last of the prophets, as witness over his community. In the Divine Court he declares that these people saw open signs and miracles, yet belied and rejected them and refused to believe in the Divinity and his prophethood. The admonition lands first on the disbelievers of the tribe of Quraysh. It is also said the witness is in favour of his community, vouching for its reliability and uprightness, for that community is itself scheduled to testify for the other prophets against nations denying ever receiving a message.

### The recital and the tears

ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd is the narrator, in al-Bukhārī's record, of the recital. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked him to recite to him, and he marvelled: recite it to you, when it was revealed to you? The Prophet ﷺ said yes, for he liked to hear it from other people. So he recited Surat an-Nisāʾ until he reached this verse, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "Stop now", and Ibn Masʿūd found his eyes tearful. Allāma al-Qastalanī writes that the verse set the whole Akhirah before his eyes, and his community with it, short on good deeds, and that is why the tears started flowing.

Some commentators hold the "yours" of the verse to be the disbelievers and hypocrites present during the age of prophethood, and others the entire community right through the Day of Doom, since, as hadith narrations indicate, the deeds of his community are continuously presented before him. Either way, the prophets of past communities appear as witnesses over their communities, and the Prophet ﷺ testifies over his own, and no prophet comes after him to witness over a community of his, or the Qur'an would have mentioned him. Read from this angle, the verse is a proof of the finality of prophethood as well.

### The company of the verses

The whole run of reports around this passage stands behind the Day it describes: the balances of justice (21:47), Luqman's mustard seed hidden in rock or heavens or earth (31:16), the scattered groups shown their deeds (99:6-8), the shining earth and the brought-forward witnesses (39:69), the Day man sees what his hands have sent forth (78:40), the associaters' plea of innocence (6:23), and the little enjoyment of this world beside the Hereafter (9:38). The intercession hadith of Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs, al-ʿAbbās's question about Abū Ṭālib, Anas in the Musnad of at-Tayālisī, the two-million reward of Abū Hurayrah traced by Abū ʿUthmān through the Ḥajj and the people of al-Baṣrah in the records of Ibn Abī Ḥātim, and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's readings with Ad-Daḥḥāk, all gathering around the ummah's standing before its witness.

### In short

- Every prophet is brought forward as a witness over his own community, and Muḥammad ﷺ over his, which is a proof that no prophet follows him.
- He wept when Ibn Masʿūd recited this verse to him, seeing his community short on good deeds.
- The community he witnesses against also stands as witness for the other prophets against their deniers.
- The Day gathers every balance and every record the Book has named, from the mustard seed to the scattered groups.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:42>>>
The wish of the cornered: "On that Day, those who denied ˹Allah˺ and disobeyed the Messenger will wish they were reduced to dust. And they will never be able to hide anything from Allah". The verse before showed the witness. This one shows what the witnessed feel.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who denied ˹Allah˺ and disobeyed the Messenger"** are the disbelievers of the gathering place, the same who rejected the ordinary man devoid of outward splendour, failing to see that God might choose him to carry His message.

**"will wish they were reduced to dust"** gives the shape of the wish. They will want the earth to open and swallow them, or to level itself with them so that they become dust with the beasts, because of the horror of the gathering place and the disgrace, dishonour and humiliation they suffer. This is like the Day man will see the deeds his hands have sent forth (78:40), and like the disbeliever's cry "O would that I were dust!" On the plains of Resurrection they will see the animals turned into dust after exchanging the retribution of each other's excesses, and they too will pine for an end like that, wishing to have become all dust.

**"And they will never be able to hide anything from Allah"** means they will admit everything they did and hide none of it. Their own hands and feet confess, the prophets testify, and everything is present in the Books of Deeds.

### The sealed mouths

The apparent puzzle of the Qur'an was put to Ibn ʿAbbās: at one place the Holy Qur'an says they shall not conceal anything from Allah (4:42), and at another they say "By Allah, our Lord, we were not those who joined others in worship with Allah" (6:23). His answer, in the record ʿAbdur-Razzāq carries from Saʿīd ibn Jubayr: when the disbelievers see that none but true Muslims are admitted to Paradise, and that Allah forgives the sins, no matter how big, except shirk, they will lie, hoping for salvation. Then Allah seals their mouths, and the hands and feet speak out what their owners used to do, frustrating every plan of concealment, and at that they confess everything. The whole apparatus of the Day surrounds them: the balances of justice (21:47), Luqman's mustard seed brought forth from rock or heavens or earth (31:16), the scattered groups shown their deeds (99:6-8), the shining earth and the brought-forward witnesses (39:69), the witness raised from every nation (16:89), and the little enjoyment of this world against the Hereafter (9:38). The two Ṣaḥīḥs hold Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī's intercession, at-Tayālisī's Musnad holds Anas, Ibn Abī Ḥātim holds the two-million reward that Abū Hurayrah confirmed to Abū ʿUthmān over the Ḥajj from al-Baṣrah, al-ʿAbbās asked after Abū Ṭālib, and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr with Ad-Daḥḥāk read the great reward as Paradise.

Read inwardly, the day closes on them with its own sequence: they become remorseful, and it does not benefit them. They bite their fingertips, and their anguish does not subside. Ignominy is wound about them like a veil, and they are carried off to the homesteads of tribulation and adversity. Since the Messenger ﷺ is the witness against his community as well as their intercessor, he bears witness only to what can be the object of his intercession, and what remains outside it is the part the disbelievers keep for themselves: the record of their speech, actions and thoughts, showing that their rejection of truth came not from ignorance but from pride, and that the acceptance of reality looked to them like setting their own greatness at naught.

### In short

- The terror of the gathering turns the denial into a wish to be dust with the beasts.
- Nothing can be hidden: mouths are sealed, limbs speak, prophets testify, and the Books of Deeds stand open.
- The associaters' false oath of innocence is answered by the confession of their own bodies, and that resolves the puzzle the questioner brought to Ibn ʿAbbās.
- The tears of the witness, the intercession, and the mustard seed of faith all stand on one side of the balance, and the proud on the other.
