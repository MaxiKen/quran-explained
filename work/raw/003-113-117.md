<<<VERSE 3:113>>>
> “Yet they are not all alike: there are some among the People of the Book who are upright, who recite Allah’s revelations throughout the night, prostrating ˹in prayer˺” (3:113)

The verse corrects any broad judgment about the People of the Book. Some of them were upright, recited Allah’s revelations at night, and worshipped Him in prostration. Their response to guidance was different from those who rejected it.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Yet they are not all alike”** means that the People of the Book cannot be treated as one undivided group. The previous verses criticized those who rejected revelation, but this verse recognizes people among them who believed and acted sincerely. Religious identity alone does not tell us what every individual believes or how they behave.

### Who were the upright among them?

Reports connect the verse with people such as Abdullah ibn Salam, Thaʿlabah ibn Saʿyah, Usayd ibn Saʿyah, and Asad ibn Ubayd, who accepted Islam after recognizing the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Some former leaders criticized them for leaving the religion of their ancestors. The verse answers that accusation by saying that those who follow clear guidance are not equal to those who knowingly reject it. It is a distinction based on response to truth, not ancestry.

Ibn Abbas and Muqatil are among those who relate this setting. Other reports from Ibn Masʿud connect the passage with people who waited for the late-night prayer when the Prophet ﷺ came out to lead it. The report praises their devotion at a time when others were resting. It does not say that only Muslims pray at night, but it describes a particular community’s worship and the sincerity of those who came to follow the Prophet. As-Suddi also explains the verse through the devotion of those who accepted guidance. Verse 110 had already praised the ummah for faith and service, and verse 199 later describes believers among the People of the Book who accept what was revealed to Muhammad and to them. Their worship reflects belief in Allah’s oneness, shared by the prophets from Moses to Sulayman.

**“there are some among the People of the Book who are upright”** describes people who stand by what is right and follow Allah’s guidance. Their uprightness is shown in their response to revelation and their conduct. They did not cling to a former identity when the truth became clear. Their example rebuts the claim that every person who changes religious commitment must be corrupt or disloyal.

**“who recite Allah’s revelations throughout the night”** points to sustained worship and reflection. The Qur’an is recited not only for public display but also in the quiet hours when a person turns to Allah privately. Night recitation nourishes faith and steadies the heart. It is part of a life oriented toward Allah, not a substitute for justice and good conduct during the day.

**“prostrating ˹in prayer˺”** describes humility before Allah. Prostration is a sign of worship, and the verse praises those who give Allah their devotion rather than seeking status among people. This practice continues in tahajjud, voluntary prayer during the night, for Muslims who are able to rise and worship.

### In short

- The People of the Book are not all alike in belief or conduct.
- Some accepted the Prophet’s message and worshipped Allah sincerely.
- Night recitation and prostration show a life of devotion and humility.

<<<VERSE 3:114>>>
> “They believe in Allah and the Last Day, encourage good and forbid evil, and race with one another in doing good. They are ˹truly˺ among the righteous” (3:114)

The verse continues describing the upright group through belief, moral responsibility, and eagerness to do good. Their faith is active: it shapes their choices and moves them to serve others.

### Phrase by phrase

**“They believe in Allah and the Last Day”** means that they affirm Allah’s oneness and trust His promise of resurrection and judgment. Faith in the Last Day gives their choices weight. They know that deeds are not forgotten and that their lives are accountable to Allah.

**“encourage good and forbid evil”** shows that their devotion is not confined to private worship. They encourage maʿruf, recognized good, and resist munkar, conduct rejected by Allah’s guidance. They do this with knowledge and care, not by imposing personal preferences or condemning sincere disagreements. The same obligation was given to the Muslim community in the preceding verses.

**“and race with one another in doing good”** describes their eagerness to act when an opportunity for good appears. They do not delay kindness or wait for public recognition. Their competition is not for wealth, ancestry, or social rank. It is a race in generosity, worship, and service.

A report from Abu Bakr and other companions connects righteous conduct with truthfulness, charity, and care for others. The verse’s description includes people who worship Allah and use their ability to do good. They may perform the prescribed acts of worship, support the vulnerable, and respond to the needs of their neighbors. Their deeds show the benefit of faith in ordinary life.

**“They are ˹truly˺ among the righteous”** confirms that these people belong to the muttaqin, those mindful of Allah. Their righteousness is recognized through belief and action together. The verse praises them without claiming that one community owns every virtue. Anyone who believes and acts sincerely can be among the righteous by Allah’s mercy.

The description continues the distinction first made in verse 110 between those who respond to revelation and those who turn away. As-Suddi and other early interpreters understand the righteous group to include those who accepted Muhammad ﷺ after following earlier prophets such as Moses. Their belief in the oneness of Allah and the Last Day gives them a reason to encourage good and hasten to it. The later description in 3:199 similarly recognizes believers among the People of the Book.

### In short

- Faith includes belief in Allah and accountability on the Last Day.
- The upright encourage good, resist clear wrong, and hasten to serve.
- Their righteous conduct is a result of faith and mindfulness of Allah.

<<<VERSE 3:115>>>
> “They will never be denied the reward for any good they have done. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of those mindful ˹of Him˺” (3:115)

The verse reassures the upright among the People of the Book that their good deeds will not be lost. Allah knows who is mindful of Him and will reward what they have done.

### Phrase by phrase

**“They will never be denied the reward”** means that Allah does not overlook sincere good. People may fail to notice a kind act or may not thank the person who performed it, but Allah knows it. Their acceptance of the Prophet’s message does not erase the good they did before. Their faith and righteous deeds are known to Allah and rewarded by Him.

**“for any good they have done”** includes acts of worship, kindness, generosity, and support for what is right. The reward is not lost because a deed seemed small or because other people did not see it. A person should not assume that good done in private is forgotten, nor should they rely on public reputation as proof of righteousness.

**“And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of those mindful ˹of Him˺”** means that Allah knows both the act and the intention behind it. Taqwa is not merely a visible appearance. Allah knows who obeys Him sincerely, who turns toward Him when the truth becomes clear, and who acts from a desire for His approval. Nothing in the life of a muttaqi is hidden from Him.

The verse therefore gives hope to those who accept guidance after belonging to another community. Their past efforts are not dismissed. Allah knows the full course of their lives, and His reward is not limited by what people remember or recognize. Abdullah ibn Salam is one of the named examples of a person who believed after knowing an earlier scripture. The promise here accords with the wider description in verse 110 and the believers from the People of the Book mentioned in 3:199. As-Suddi explains that the muttaqin are recognized by their faith and obedience, not simply by their former community.

### In short

- Allah does not lose or deny the reward of sincere good deeds.
- He knows the intention and conduct of those who are mindful of Him.
- People who accept guidance can hope in Allah’s generous reward.

<<<VERSE 3:116>>>
> “Indeed, neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah. It is they who will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever” (3:116)

The verse warns that wealth and children cannot protect a person from Allah’s judgment if they persist in disbelief. It is a warning about the Hereafter, not a judgment that every wealthy person or every parent is condemned.

### Phrase by phrase

**“neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah”** means that material resources and family ties cannot buy release from the consequences of rejecting faith. People often rely on wealth to solve problems and on their children to defend their interests. Neither can replace belief or act as a ransom before Allah.

The warning is not that wealth or children are evil. They can be blessings and responsibilities. The danger is treating them as guarantees of safety or as more important than Allah’s guidance. A person should use wealth for good and raise children with care, while remembering that each person is accountable for their own choices.

**“It is they who will be the residents of the Fire”** describes the outcome of those who die persisting in disbelief. The verse warns people to turn back while they are alive and able to repent. It should not be used to make careless judgments about a particular individual’s final state. Allah alone knows what guidance reached them and how they responded.

**“They will be there forever”** emphasizes the lasting nature of the punishment for those described. The Qur’an repeatedly contrasts temporary worldly advantage with the final outcome of the Hereafter. Wealth, children, and status pass away, while the consequences of faith and disbelief remain.

Some reports connect the warning with leaders who opposed Muhammad ﷺ and believed their position or resources would protect them. A later explanation identifies Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and his followers as among those in view. Such reports illustrate the principle without limiting the verse to one individual or group. At the Day of Qiyamah, worldly rank will not function as a ransom. As-Suddi and other commentators say that wealth and children cannot shield a person from Allah’s judgment. Verse 110’s praise of the believing community and the promise to believers in 3:199 contrast with this warning to those who persist in disbelief.

### In short

- Wealth and family cannot serve as a ransom against Allah’s judgment.
- The verse warns those who persist in disbelief until death.
- Worldly blessings are responsibilities, not guarantees of salvation.

<<<VERSE 3:117>>>
> “The good they do in this worldly life is like the harvest of an evil people struck by a bitter wind, destroying it ˹completely˺. Allah never wronged them, but they wronged themselves” (3:117)

The verse compares the disbelievers’ worldly spending to a harvest destroyed by a harsh wind. Their good deeds may have effects in this life, but without faith they do not secure the reward they expect in the Hereafter. Allah does not wrong them. Their own choices lead to loss.

### Phrase by phrase

**“The good they do in this worldly life”** includes acts such as generosity and other efforts that appear beneficial. The verse does not deny that people may receive worldly results from good conduct. A gift may help someone, and a just act may bring real relief. The parable addresses the lasting reward that people hope their deeds will bring before Allah.

**“is like the harvest of an evil people”** compares the people’s work with crops belonging to those who wronged themselves. A harvest represents the result of long labor and hope. The farmer expects food and benefit from it, but the comparison shows that the result can be lost when the work is not grounded in faith and obedience to Allah.

### What does the bitter wind represent?

**“struck by a bitter wind, destroying it ˹completely˺”** describes a sudden force that ruins the crop just as it is ready. The Arabic word for the wind has been explained in different ways, including a cold, icy wind or a burning blast. Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Ar-Rabi, and As-Suddi are among those who explain its destructive quality. Extreme cold can burn plants as fire does, so the descriptions point to the same outcome: the harvest is wiped away when its owner most needs it.

The image teaches that wealth spent without faith cannot purchase the Hereafter. It is not saying that a charitable act has no value to the person who received it. Rather, the deed does not become a means of salvation when the giver rejects Allah and the faith that makes worship meaningful. Believers should therefore join generosity with faith, sincerity, and obedience.

**“Allah never wronged them”** means that Allah does not deprive people of a reward they were entitled to or punish them without cause. He gives guidance and makes the truth clear. His judgment reflects full knowledge of each person’s actions and choices.

**“but they wronged themselves”** explains that the loss comes from their own rejection of guidance. They chose disbelief and failed to build their deeds on faith. The verse invites people to correct their course before the harvest of life is complete and no new work can be done. Some say the bitter wind is a cold blast, while others describe it as fire-like in its effect. Both interpretations underline the image of a harvest destroyed at the moment of need. This teaching follows the warning in verse 116 and the praise of the faithful in verse 110. The wider passage later recognizes believers among the People of the Book (3:199). At the Day of Qiyamah, the difference between a deed’s worldly benefit and its lasting reward becomes clear.

### In short

- Good deeds may benefit people in this world, but faith is essential to the reward of the Hereafter.
- The wind and ruined harvest picture the sudden loss of deeds that were not grounded in faith.
- Allah is not unjust. The people in the parable bring loss upon themselves through their choices.
