<<<VERSE 7:8>>>
After the questioning of nations and messengers comes the Balance: "The weighing on that Day will be just. As for those whose scale will be heavy ˹with good deeds˺, ˹only˺ they will be successful". The verse makes two statements. The weighing of deeds on the Day of Resurrection is real and fair, so no one will be wronged. And those whose good deeds weigh heavy are the ones who win.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The weighing on that Day will be just."** "That Day" is the day of the questioning just mentioned, the Day of Resurrection. What is weighed is people's deeds, or the scrolls in which the deeds are written. A hadith describes the balance as having a tongue and two pans. The word rendered "just" is al-ḥaqq, "the true", and grammatically it describes the weighing itself: a true and fair weighing. It is certain and due, with no room for doubt, and Allah will not wrong anyone in the least. Two verses say the same:

> "We will set up the scales of justice on the Day of Judgment, so no soul will be wronged in the least. And ˹even˺ if a deed is the weight of a mustard seed, We will bring it forth. And sufficient are We as a ˹vigilant˺ Reckoner" (21:47).

> "Indeed, Allah never wrongs ˹anyone˺—even by an atom's weight. And if it is a good deed, He will multiply it many times over and will give a great reward out of His grace" (4:40).

**"As for those whose scale will be heavy ˹with good deeds˺"** means those whose good actions weigh more than their bad ones.

**"˹only˺ they will be successful"**: they are the winners. They reach their goal, enter Paradise, and are saved from what they feared, escaping Allah's anger and His torment. Sūrat al-Qāriʿah says that those whose scale is heavy "will be in a life of bliss" (101:6-7), and another verse adds, "it is they who will be successful" (23:102).

### How can deeds be weighed at all?

The verse stresses that the weighing is definite, and there is a hint in that. Nobody should be misled by the idea that only things with bulk and heaviness can be weighed. Human deeds, good or bad, have no body or mass. How, then, can they be weighed? Several answers are given.

1. Allah's authority is absolute and He has power over everything. Why should something we cannot weigh be beyond His weighing?
2. Even in our own time there are countless new instruments that weigh or measure things without the old balance, scales, rod or pointer. They measure what nobody once thought measurable: air, electric current, heat, cold and much else. A meter is all that is needed. If Allah, the Maker of all makers, weighs human deeds by His perfect power, with or without a device, what is hard to believe about that?
3. The Creator can also turn our deeds, at some point, into a material presence and give them a shape. People happily accept something like this in a Spielberg film, yet squirm at the religious account of what will really happen.

Many hadiths of the Prophet ﷺ confirm that deeds will appear in particular forms in Barzakh, the state between death and resurrection, and at the Maḥshar, the great gathering of the Resurrection. A person's good deeds, made beautiful, will keep him company in the grave, while evil deeds will crawl over him as snakes and scorpions. One hadith says that whoever did not pay the zakāh due on his wealth will find that wealth come to his grave as a poisonous snake. It will bite him and say, "I am your wealth, I am your treasure." Countless authentic narrations, reliably reported, show that once our deeds leave this passing world they take on particular shapes and are present in material form on the plain of Resurrection.

Verses of the Qur'an confirm it too. "They will find whatever they did present ˹before them˺" (18:49). A verse of Sūrat az-Zalzalah says that whoever does good, even the weight of a particle, will see it on the Day of Qiyāmah, and whoever does evil of that weight will see it as well. Such statements open the possibility that human deeds will exist in some nuclear form. No further interpretation is needed, because what matters is that the result of each deed will exist and be perceived.

Seen this way, weighing deeds is neither difficult nor far-fetched. Human beings, though, reason within a narrow frame. They judge everything by comparison with their present state of being, and they are so used to it that they cannot do otherwise. Sūrat ar-Rūm describes them: "They ˹only˺ know the worldly affairs of this life, but are ˹totally˺ oblivious to the Hereafter" (30:7). They know one obvious side of this life, and not even all of that. In their rush after what can be seen and touched they shake the earth and space to discover the undiscovered. Yet they know nothing of the vast field of realities that will unfold in the Hereafter.

That is why the verse takes such care to say the weighing that Day is definite. Man, so taken with the obvious, should have no room to deny the weighing of deeds in the Hereafter. It is proved by the Qur'an and is the collective belief of the Muslim Ummah. Many verses return to it from different angles, and hadith gives it in abundant detail.

### What exactly will be placed on the scale?

Three answers are reported.

**The deeds themselves.** Although deeds are not physical things, Allah will give them weight on the Day of Resurrection. Al-Baghawī said this was reported from Ibn ʿAbbās. It is supported by reports in the Ṣaḥīḥ. Al-Baqarah and Āl ʿImrān will come on the Day of Resurrection as two dense clouds, two canopies, or two rows of birds, and will cast their shade over those who used to recite them. The Qur'an will come to the person who recited and kept it in the form of a pale young man. Asked who he is, he will say, "I am the Qur'an, who kept you awake at night and thirsty by day." In the hadith of al-Barāʾ about the questioning in the grave, a young man with a fair complexion and a pleasant scent comes to the believer. When the believer asks who he is, he answers, "I am your good deeds." The Prophet ﷺ described the opposite for the disbeliever and the hypocrite.

**The record of deeds.** In a hadith reported from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar, a man of the Prophet's ﷺ Ummah will be brought before all creation. Ninety-nine scrolls of his deeds are spread out, each reaching as far as the eye can see, and every one is full of sins. He is asked whether anything in them is wrong, or whether the recording angels did him any injustice. He admits, "My Lord, all of it is correct," while inwardly he dreads how he can ever be saved. Allah says that today no one will be wronged, and that He holds one good testimony for him: a card bearing the declaration, "I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and His Messenger." The man asks what that little card could weigh against such a vast black record, and is told, "You will not be wronged." The scrolls go into one pan and the card into the other. The scrolls fly up and the card weighs heavier. The Prophet ﷺ then said that nothing outweighs the name of Allah. At-Tirmidhī, Ibn Mājah, Ibn Ḥibbān, al-Baihaqī and al-Ḥākim reported it, and at-Tirmidhī said of his wording that it is authentic.

**The doers themselves.** Abū Hurairah reported, in a hadith in al-Bukhārī and Muslim, that on the Day of Qiyāmah a big, heavy man will be brought who weighs less with Allah than a mosquito's wing. The Prophet ﷺ then recited, "so We will not give their deeds any weight on Judgment Day" (18:105). In a hadith on the virtues of ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd, the Prophet ﷺ said of him:

> "Do you wonder at how thin his legs are? By the One in whose hand is my soul, on the Balance they are heavier than Mount Uḥud."

These reports seem to describe different ways of weighing. They can be brought together by saying that all of it will truly happen. Sometimes the deeds will be weighed, sometimes the scrolls that record them, and sometimes the people who did them. After gathering these narrations, one scholar concluded that the weighing may be repeated several times in different ways. Allah knows best. The full reality is known to Him alone, and nobody needs to know it in order to live rightly. It is enough to know that our deeds will be weighed.

### Which deeds weigh heavy?

Many hadiths speak of particular deeds and their weight on the scale. They show what certain deeds are worth.

- Abū Hurairah reported the hadith with which al-Bukhārī closes his Ṣaḥīḥ. Two phrases are light on the tongue, heavy on the scale and dear to Allah: "Subḥān Allāhi wa bi-ḥamdihi" (Glory be to Allah, and praise be to Him) and "Subḥān Allāhi al-ʿAẓīm" (Glory be to Allah, the Most Great).
- ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar reported that the Prophet ﷺ used to say that "Subḥān Allāh" fills half the pan of the scale and "al-ḥamdu lillāh" fills the other half.
- Abū ad-Dardāʾ reported that the Prophet ﷺ said nothing will be heavier on the scale than good character. Abū Dāwūd, at-Tirmidhī and Ibn Ḥibbān reported it with a sound chain.
- The Prophet ﷺ told Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī of two things that are easy for anyone yet heaviest on the scale: good character, and keeping silent as a habit, speaking only when it is needed.
- In his Kitāb az-Zuhd, Imam Aḥmad reported from Ḥāzim that Jibrīl al-Amīn (Gabriel, the Trustworthy) came to the Prophet ﷺ while someone nearby was weeping out of fear of Allah. Jibrīl said that every human deed will be weighed except tears shed from fear of Allah and of the Hereafter. A single such tear, he said, puts out the fiercest fire of Jahannam (Hell).
- A hadith, reported from Ibn al-Mubārak, tells of a man on the plain of Resurrection who sees very few good deeds in his record and grows anxious. Suddenly something like a cloud rises and falls into the pan of his good deeds. He is told it is the reward for the faith he taught. It grew as people acted on it, and a share was kept for him with every single person who followed his teaching.
- Aṭ-Ṭabarānī reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that whoever goes with a funeral (janāzah) to the graveyard will have two qīrāṭs placed on his scale. Other narrations say each qīrāṭ will weigh as much as Mount Uḥud.
- Aṭ-Ṭabarānī also reported from Jābir that the first deed placed on a person's scale will be what he spent on his family and on caring for their needs.
- Al-Dhahabī reported from ʿImrān ibn Ḥuṣayn that on the Day of Qiyāmah the ink scholars (ʿulamāʾ) spent writing on the faith and its laws will be set on the scale against the martyrs' (shuhadāʾ) blood, and the ink will prove heavier.

There are many more hadiths on the weighing of deeds. These few are given for the light they throw on the worth of particular deeds.

### The inner reading: many scales

The weighing of deeds on the scale of self-purification is true, and the weighing of inner states on the scale of truthfulness is just. Wretched and deprived is the person whose deeds are stained by showing off and whose states are mixed with self-admiration. At the scales those states will count for nothing and those deeds will have no weight, as in the verse quoted above, "We will not give their deeds any weight" (18:105).

Among the sayings of ʿUmar is this: "Take an account of yourselves before you are brought to account. Weigh your deeds before they are weighed, and prepare for the Greatest Exposure." In other words, weigh your own deeds before they are weighed against you. Look at your works and ask what you have made ready for the Greatest Exposure and for the gathering of the resurrection. That is why the Lord of the Worlds said, "let every soul look to what ˹deeds˺ it has sent forth for tomorrow" (59:18).

According to a report, a sensible person has four hours to pursue his happiness and beautify his days. In one he calls himself to account and weighs his own deeds and states. In another he speaks secretly with the Real and shows Him his need. In a third he sees to his livelihood. The fourth is for rest, finding ease in private prayer and in the worldly things he has been granted.

The masters of the spiritual path (ṭarīqah) and the people of recognition say there are different scales.

- The soul and the spirit have a scale whose measure is commands and prohibitions. Its two pans are the Book and the Sunnah.
- The heart and the intellect have a scale whose measure is reward. Its two pans are threats and promises.
- Recognition and the secret core have a scale whose measure is Allah's approval. Its two pans are fleeing and seeking. Fleeing is escaping this world and clinging to the next. Seeking is leaving even the next world aside and seeking the Patron Himself.

### In short

- The weighing of deeds on the Day of Resurrection is real and perfectly just. No one will be wronged by so much as an atom's weight.
- Deeds have no mass, yet Allah can weigh them, and hadiths say they will take shape in the grave and at the Resurrection.
- Reports speak of the deeds, the record scrolls and the doers themselves being weighed, and all three may happen.
- Remembrance of Allah, good character, silence, tears of fear, teaching the faith, following funerals, spending on one's family and the ink of scholars all weigh heavily.
- Inwardly, a person should weigh his own deeds before they are weighed, as ʿUmar said.

<<<VERSE 7:9>>>
This is the other side of the Balance: "But those whose scale is light, they have doomed themselves for wrongfully denying Our signs". Those whose good deeds weigh little, because their evil deeds outweigh them, have lost their own selves. The verse names the cause: they denied Allah's signs, knowingly and unjustly.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But those whose scale is light"**: light in good deeds, because of their evil deeds. Their bad actions weigh more than their good ones. Sūrat al-Qāriʿah describes where they end up: "And as for those whose scale is light, their home will be the abyss. And what will make you realize what that is? ˹It is˺ a scorching Fire" (101:8-11). The abyss is Hāwiyah, a pit of Hell.

**"they have doomed themselves"**: they have lost their own souls. They lose them by being punished, steering them onto the road to the Fire. On the Day of Judgement they earn their own destruction and are shut out of eternal bliss. Another passage shows the same moment. When the Trumpet is blown, kinship will count for nothing and no one will even ask about another, and "those whose scale is light, they will have doomed themselves, staying in Hell forever" (23:101-103).

**"for wrongfully denying Our signs"**: they treated Allah's signs unjustly, denying them while they knew. The signs meant are Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, and all of Allah's signs and verses.

### If faith outweighs everything, how can a believer's scale be light?

Several narrations suggest that the declaration of faith outweighs any amount of sin. There is the hadith of the card, told under 7:8. Ibn ʿUmar also reported, in a narration in the Musnad of al-Bazzār and the Mustadrak of al-Ḥākim, that at the time of his death Nūḥ (Noah) pointed to the words "Lā ilāha illā Allāh" (there is no god but Allah). If the heavens and the earth were placed in one pan of the scale and these words in the other, the pan with the words would always be heavier. Other narrations on the same theme come from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, Ibn ʿAbbās and Abū ad-Dardāʾ. Their chains are reliable, and they are spread through various hadith collections.

On their face, these narrations mean that a believer who says the Kalimah, the declaration of faith, will always find his scale heavier, however many sins he carries. Yet many other verses and hadiths show that a Muslim's good and bad deeds will themselves be weighed. For some the pan of good deeds will be heavier, and for others the pan of sins. The first will be saved and the second will be punished.

- Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ promises that every deed, good or bad, will be brought to the scales of justice, even if it weighs as little as a mustard seed (21:47, quoted in full under 7:8).
- Sūrat al-Qāriʿah says that whoever has a heavy scale of good deeds will have a blissful life, and whoever has a light one will have Hell for his home (101:6-9).
- Explaining these verses, Ibn ʿAbbās said that the believer whose good deeds are heavier on the scale will go to Jannah (Paradise) with his deeds, and the one whose evil deeds are heavier will be sent to Jahannam (Hell) with his deeds. Al-Baihaqī reported this in Shuʿab al-Īmān.
- Abū Hurairah reported, in a narration in Abū Dāwūd, that if a servant's obligatory acts (farāʾiḍ) fall short, Allah will ask whether he has any voluntary acts (nawāfil). If he has, the shortfall in the obligatory acts will be made up from the voluntary ones.

All this means the scale will sometimes come out heavier for a believer and sometimes lighter. Scholars who explain the Qur'an therefore say that the weighing at the Gathering will happen twice.

1. The first weighing sets belief against disbelief, to separate believers from disbelievers. Anyone whose record holds even the bare declaration of faith will find his scale heavier, and he will be taken out of the ranks of the disbelievers.
2. The second weighing sets good deeds against bad among the believers. One believer will find his good deeds heavier, another his evil deeds, and each will be punished or rewarded accordingly.

In this way the verses and the narrations fit together.

### And if a believer's deeds weigh light?

It is enough for us to know that our deeds will be weighed. If they prove lighter on the scale, we deserve punishment. It is a separate matter that Allah, in His grace, may forgive our sins, either directly or through the intercession of a prophet (nabī) or of one of His friends (walī), so that we are spared the punishment. Some narrations say that certain people will be saved simply for having said and believed the declaration "Lā ilāha illā Allāh", with all their sins forgiven for its sake. That belongs to the exception just described. It lies outside the general rule and is a special display of Allah's grace and mercy.

### Where the real weight lies

In this world a person's importance is measured by the wealth and splendour around him, by his power to move people with impressive words, and by the size of his following. The one who calls to the truth seldom has these advantages. So worldly people think his words worthless, while his opponents, basking in material grandeur, make the greater impression. When the Last Day uncovers all the facts, it will be the other way round. All the weight will be on the side of the truth. Falsehood will be stripped of every argument and left without value.

### In short

- Those whose evil deeds outweigh their good have lost themselves: they are punished, driven toward the Fire and shut out of eternal bliss.
- The cause is that they knowingly and unjustly denied Allah's signs, the Prophet ﷺ and the Qur'an.
- The declaration of faith outweighs everything in a first weighing that separates believers from disbelievers. A second weighing sets each believer's good deeds against his bad.
- A believer whose deeds weigh light deserves punishment, unless Allah forgives him by His grace or through intercession.
- On that Day all the weight lies with the truth, however little the world valued it.

<<<VERSE 7:10>>>
The two verses before this warned sinners of disgrace on the Day of Resurrection and the punishment that follows. This verse turns to Allah's blessings, to draw people toward the truth and toward acting on it: "We have indeed established you on earth and provided you with a means of livelihood. ˹Yet˺ you seldom give any thanks". Allah has given human beings the earth to live on and everything they need to live by. The verse ends on a note of complaint about how little thanks He receives.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have indeed established you on earth"** is addressed to the children of Ādam. Allah has given them power on the earth, with full ownership and control of it. He made it a settled place to live. He set firm mountains and rivers on it, gave people homes, and let them use its benefits.

**"and provided you with a means of livelihood"**: the word is maʿāyish, the plural of maʿīshah, whatever a person lives by. It covers what you eat, what you drink and what you wear. Allah made the clouds work for people, bringing rain so that they can grow their food. He also created the means of earning a living: trade, commerce and every kind of profession.

**"˹Yet˺ you seldom give any thanks"**: you give thanks neither for the great things nor for the small. It is also said to mean that you give little thanks for all that is done for you. As in verse 3, the small word mā in qalīlan mā only stresses how little it is. Most people give little thanks for these gifts. Another verse says: "If you tried to count Allah's blessings, you would never be able to number them. Indeed humankind is truly unfair, ˹totally˺ ungrateful" (14:34). The close of the verse is a complaint, which has also been rendered, "Little you are receptive to advice."

### The earth as a storehouse

It is as if the Master of the universe has made the whole earth, with all it holds, into one vast storehouse of human needs and comforts, with a complete inventory inside. Thousands of paths to comfort lie open. What remains for people is to learn how to go in, take out what they need, and use it as they choose. Science and technology are, at bottom, a trained way of drawing out what the Master of the universe has stored there, properly and carefully, and putting it to sound and useful work. Someone who lacks the sense and the manners to take things out, or does not understand how to use what he has taken, stays deprived of their benefits. An intelligent person gains from both.

### Why are people so ungrateful?

Allah has placed on and in the earth everything human beings need, and that calls for gratitude to Him at all times and in every state. But people are prone to heedlessness. It does not take them long to forget the favours of their Creator and Master and go back to their world of things.

Everything Allah gives a person in this world is given to draw out his gratitude, yet that is the very thing he does not offer his Lord. The reason is that Shayṭān (Satan) twists his feelings and steers him away from any expression of thanks.

### In short

- Allah gave human beings control of the earth, a home on it, and every means of livelihood: food, drink, clothing, rain, trade and crafts.
- The earth is like a storehouse of human needs, and science and technology are ways of drawing out what Allah stored there.
- Such gifts call for constant thanks, yet people give little, because they are heedless and Shayṭān steers them away from gratitude.

<<<VERSE 7:11>>>
Here begins the story of Ādam and Iblīs: "Surely We created you, then shaped you, then said to the angels, “Prostrate before Adam,” so they all did—but not Iblîs, who refused to prostrate with the others". Allah tells the children of Ādam of the honour He gave their father, and of the enmity of Shayṭān, who still envies them as he envied Ādam. The point is that they should beware of him and not follow in his footsteps.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Surely We created you"**: the "you" is plural, but it refers to Ādam, and Ādam was made from dust. The plural is used because Ādam is the father of all humankind. In the same way Allah said to the Children of Israel who lived in the Prophet's ﷺ time, "We shaded you with clouds and sent down to you manna and quails" (2:57). That favour was given to their forefathers in the time of Mūsā (Moses). But since their forefathers are the root they come from, the descendants share in the favour too.

The verse "And indeed, We created humankind from an extract of clay" (23:12) works differently. It means only that Ādam was created from clay. His children were created from a nuṭfah, the mingled fluid of male and female. That verse speaks of the origin of humankind. It does not mean that every human being was created from clay. Allah knows best.

**"then shaped you"**: He fashioned you in the wombs. Another explanation is that He shaped Ādam with all his descendants placed within his back. He made Ādam in the finest form and design. It is said that Ādam was fashioned between Makkah and aṭ-Ṭāʾif.

**"then said to the angels, “Prostrate before Adam,”"**: one explanation says these were the angels who lived on the earth. The prostration was a bow of greeting, to honour Ādam. Another passage describes the command: "when your Lord said to the angels, “I am going to create a human being from sounding clay moulded from black mud. So when I have fashioned him and had a spirit of My Own ˹creation˺ breathed into him, fall down in prostration to him.”" (15:28-29). Allah made Ādam with His Hands out of dried, altered mud, formed him as a human being and breathed life into him. Then He commanded the angels to bow before him, a bow that honoured Allah's own glory and majesty.

**"so they all did"**: the angels all heard, obeyed and prostrated.

**"but not Iblîs, who refused to prostrate with the others"**: Iblīs, called Shayṭān, did not bow down. He refused out of pride and stubbornness. One explanation calls him the leader of the angels. Another calls him the father of the jinn, who was among the angels. This subject was explained at the start of Sūrat al-Baqarah.

### The real test: jealousy and pride

The story of Ādam and Shayṭān shows the true standard by which a person's case is judged. A person is tested at the point where jealousy and pride begin to grow in the heart. This world is a place of trial, and in it one person keeps rising above another, or gains more wealth and honour than those around him. In a dealing between two people, one may feel it beneath him to give the other what is rightly his. On every such occasion a person is being judged. Does he respond well, or does he react badly?

### The inner reading: why Ādam was honoured

In this verse the wise Lord, renowned and mighty, the All-Compeller who acts and knowingly keeps His servants, reminds the children of Ādam of His favours. He teaches them how good He is as their God, and how faithfully He holds to His Covenant. It is as though He says:

"It was I who created you and shaped the beauty of your faces. I drew your upright stature. I gave you two eyes that see, two ears that hear and a tongue that speaks. I am the Lord who turns non-being into being and draws out what exists from what did not exist. I originate things from their first beginning. I am the painter of faces and the adorner of beauty. I pair each thing with its companion and make every being in a fitting way. I created heaven, earth and lifeless things to show My power. I created the angels, the satans and the jinn to show My awe. I created Ādam and his children to show My forgiveness and mercy."

"For 700,000 years Jibrīl (Gabriel), Mīkāʾīl (Michael), Seraphiel, the Cherubim, the Circlers and the Row-keepers went around the Kaʿbah of My all-compelling might, saying, 'The Glorified! The Holy!' Yet they never reached My names of love, loving kindness and friendship, or came to know them. They never dared to claim My friendship. But I claimed friendship with the dwellers in dust: 'We are your friends' (41:31), 'He loves them' (5:54). Several of My names come from My friendship and kindness toward them: the Loving, the Ever-Merciful, the Forgiving and the Clement. The angels saw nothing from Me but severity and might, and I kept them behind veils of awe. To the dwellers in dust I showed only gentleness and mercy, and kept them on the carpet of expansiveness."

Among the angels, Jibrīl was honoured and foremost, singled out for the special favours of nearness. His name was Servant of the All-Merciful. He stood always on the carpet of justice, in awe, and had never seen the carpet of bounty and expansiveness. Before Ādam the Chosen came there was no parting and no union, no rejection and no acceptance. Nobody spoke of the heart, of the beloved or of friendship. All these wonders and treasures belong to the register of passionate love.

### In short

- "We created you" refers to Ādam, the father of all people, because a favour given to forefathers counts for their descendants.
- Allah made Ādam from clay, shaped him, breathed life into him and ordered the angels to bow to him in greeting and honour. All did except Iblīs, out of pride and stubbornness.
- The verse warns Ādam's children of Shayṭān's lasting envy, and shows that people are tested where jealousy and pride arise.
- Inwardly, human beings were made to show Allah's forgiveness, mercy and love, which even the highest angels had not known.
