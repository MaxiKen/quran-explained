The surah turns from the destroyed towns to the weighing itself. "The weighing on that Day will be just. As for those whose scale will be heavy ˹with good deeds˺, ˹only˺ they will be successful". Two sentences, and between them a whole future is decided by the position of a balance. The verse also answers the obvious objection before it can be raised. Human deeds have no bulk and no mass, so how can they be weighed at all? The answer is that the weighing is fixed and certain, and the wording is deliberate so that nobody can afterwards claim they did not know.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The weighing on that Day will be just"** means that the weighing of deeds on the Day of Resurrection is the true weighing, and the reason given is that Allah will not wrong anyone. The wording is exact. Al-haqq, "the true", qualifies al-wazn, "the weighing", as an adjective does. The day in question is the Day of Resurrection, and the balance itself has been described in a report as one with a tongue and two palms like a pair of scales.

**"As for those whose scale will be heavy ˹with good deeds˺"** means those whose good deeds outweigh their bad. They are the successful, the triumphant, the ones who escape the wrath and the torment of Allah.

**"˹only˺ they will be successful"** closes the matter. A gloss on the app's rendering is that the successful are those who have reached the goal and been saved from what they feared.

### The weighing will be exact, and no one will be wronged

The whole of the Qur'an's treatment of the weighing is built on one assurance. The scales of justice will be set up on the Day of Judgment, so that no soul will be wronged in the least, and even a deed of the weight of a mustard seed will be brought forth, and Allah is sufficient as a vigilant Reckoner (21:47). Allah never wrongs anyone, even by an atom's weight, and if a deed is good He multiplies it many times over and gives a great reward out of His grace (4:40). The same is said in Surah Al-Anbiyaʾ and in Surah Al-Qariʿah: whoever has a heavy scale will have a life of bliss, and whoever has a light scale will have the abyss for a home, and the abyss is a scorching fire (101:6-11).

Another passage gives the whole day in three verses. When the Trumpet sounds, all kinship between them is gone for that Day, and not one of them will even think of asking after another. Those whose scale is heavy are the successful, and those whose scale is light have doomed themselves and will stay in the Fire forever (23:101-103).

### What exactly is put on the scale

Three answers are given in the material, and all three are held together rather than fought over.

The first is the deeds themselves. Al-Baghawi reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that the deeds will be placed on the balance even though they are not material objects, and that these deeds will be given a real weight on the Day of Resurrection. The second is the Book of Records in which the deeds are written. A report describes a man being brought forth with ninety-nine scrolls of errors and sins, each of them as long as the sight can reach, laid on one side of the balance. Then he is brought a card on which "La ilaha illallah" is written, and he will say to his Lord: what weight can this small card possibly have against all those scrolls? The answer is that he will not be wronged, and the card is placed in the other pan. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said of it: the ninety-nine scrolls will rise and the card will sink. A wording close to this is recorded by at-Tirmidhī, who calls it authentic.

The third is the person who did the deeds. A report says that a man will be brought forth whose weight in the sight of Allah is no heavier than the wing of a mosquito, and the verse that goes with it is that "on the Day of Resurrection We will not give their deeds any weight" (18:105). The Prophet ﷺ also spoke of ʿAbdullah ibn Masʿūd in this way: his legs look so thin, yet by Him in Whose Hand is my soul they will weigh more on the Balance than the mountain of Uhud.

The three can be reconciled without strain. Sometimes the deeds are weighed, sometimes the scrolls in which they are written, and sometimes the one who did them. Allah knows best.

### What the good deeds will look like when they arrive

Two further reports describe the deeds as things that can be seen and met. It is recorded in the Sahih that Al-Baqarah and Āl ʿImrān will come on the Day of Resurrection as two dense clouds that cast their shade over those who recited them, or as two objects that give shade, or as two lined groups of birds. The Qur'an comes to the one who recited and kept it as a pale-faced young man. He will ask, "Who are you?" and the reply is, "I am the Qur'an, who made you stay up sleeplessly at night and caused you thirst in the day."

The report of al-Baraʾ about the questioning in the grave goes the same way for the good deeds. A young man with fair colour and a good scent will come to the believer, who will ask, "Who are you?" and he will reply, "I am your good deeds." The Prophet ﷺ described the reverse for the disbeliever and the hypocrite.

### How deeds that have no weight can be weighed

The objection is a sensible one, and it is met directly. Do not be deceived by the idea that things which are normally weighed or measured have some heaviness to them. Human deeds, good or bad, have no body and no mass. So how could they be weighed? The first answer is authority. The power of Allah is absolute, and there is nothing that He cannot do. Why should it be impossible for Him to weigh or measure human deeds, with or without a device?

The second answer is that the Creator can give a deed a material presence. People find it easier to believe in a film than in the religious account of what will come to pass, yet the Creator of the universe is in fact able to bring things into a shape at some point. There are many reports confirming that human deeds take particular shapes and forms during the barzakh, the state between death and resurrection, and on the plains of the Resurrection. Good deeds, in forms made fair, become the companions of the grave, and evil deeds crawl about in the shape of snakes and scorpions. One report says that a person who has not paid the zakah due on his wealth will have that wealth come to his grave as a venomous serpent, which bites him and tells him: I am your wealth, I am your treasure.

The third answer is that people have always been able to measure what cannot be weighed in the ordinary way. There are instruments now that weigh air, electric current, heat and cold, and a meter is all that is needed. If the Maker of makers can weigh or measure human deeds, what is there in that to call far-fetched?

There are verses for it. The record of deeds will be laid open, and the wicked will be seen in fear of what is written in it (18:49). In Surah Az-Zalzalah it is said that whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see that too. What matters is that the deeds of every person will be there on the plain of the Resurrection in a form that is perceptible.

### The wording was chosen on purpose

Because human beings judge everything by analogy from the conditions of their own life in this world, they reason about the Hereafter from what they can see and touch. That is the state the Qur'an describes in Surah Ar-Rum: they only know the worldly affairs of this life, but are totally oblivious to the Hereafter (30:7). They will shake the earth and the skies to find out what cannot be found, while the great field of reality that has to unfold in the life to come is something they are entirely unaware of.

So the verse says, in this exact form, that the weighing on that day is definite. The point of the careful wording is that a person caught up in the obvious and the perceptible may not afterwards be able to deny the weighing of deeds in the life to come, which is proved from the Qur'an and is the collective belief of the Muslim Ummah.

### The weighing is done twice

One set of scholars holds that the weighing on the plains of the Resurrection happens twice. First, belief and disbelief are weighed, and that is the weighing by which a believer is separated from a disbeliever. Whoever has even the declaration of faith written in the Book of Deeds has that scale become heavier, and he is set apart from the group of the disbelievers. Then comes a second weighing, of good deeds against bad deeds. In it the good deeds of one believer are heavy on the scale while the scale of another is heavy with evil, and each receives the punishment or the reward that follows from it. Once this is understood, the verses and the reports about the weighing are all in step with one another.

The reports that seem to pull the other way are the ones about the declaration of faith. At-Tirmidhī, Ibn Mājah, Ibn Ḥibbān, al-Bayhaqī and al-Ḥākim all record from ʿAbdullah ibn ʿUmar that a man of the community will be brought before the whole of creation on the plain of the Resurrection, and ninety-nine Books of Deeds will be brought in, each scroll as long as the sight can reach, every one of them full of sins and evils. He is asked whether everything written there is correct, and he admits that it is, and in his heart he is nervous, wondering how he will find his deliverance. Then Allah says: today there is no injustice for anyone, and against all his sins there is set a testimonial of his being good, written where the declaration of faith is: "I testify that there is no god but Allah and I testify that Muhammad is His servant and His Messenger." The man asks what weight this small note can carry against such a large black Book of Deeds, and he is told that no injustice will be done to him. The pan holding the note then becomes the heavier, and the pan full of sins tips up. After relating this the Prophet ﷺ said: nothing can weigh more than the name of Allah.

From the Musnad of al-Bazzār and the Mustadrak of al-Ḥākim, also from Ibn ʿUmar: at the time of his death Nūḥ (Noah) said "La ilaha illallah", because if the heavens and the earth were placed in one pan and the declaration of faith in the other, the pan containing the declaration would invariably remain the heavier. Narrations to the same effect come from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, Ibn ʿAbbās and Abū ad-Dardāʾ, carried by reliable transmitters and spread across many collections of reports.

Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported in the collection on faith by al-Bayhaqī, put the two together: a believer whose good deeds outweigh on the scale goes to Paradise carrying his deeds with him, and the one whose evil deeds are heavier is sent to Hell with his deeds. A believing Muslim who recites the declaration of faith therefore finds the scale heavier in his favour, however many sins he carries.

But many verses and many reports also prove that good and bad deeds are weighed, so that for one the pan of good deeds is heavier and for another the pan of sins. And there is a further report from Abū Hurayrah in Abū Dāwūd: if a shortage is found in the obligatory duties of a servant, Allah will ask whether he has any voluntary acts to his credit, and if they are there the shortage of the obligatory is made up by the voluntary.

### What weighs heavy

There are reports that single out particular deeds because of how much they weigh.

One, narrated by Abū Hurayrah and appearing in al-Bukhārī and Muslim, says that on the Day of Resurrection some people will be brought forward who are heavy in appearance, yet in the sight of Allah they are worth no more than a mosquito's wing, and it is supported by the verse that on the Day of Resurrection no weight will be assigned to those who reject the signs of their Lord (18:105).

One of the virtues of ʿAbdullah ibn Masʿūd is a report that his legs look so thin, yet on the balance of justice for the Day of Resurrection their weight is more than the weight of Mount Uhud.

There is the report of Abū Hurayrah with which al-Bukhārī closed his collection, in which two words are said to be very light when spoken and very heavy when weighed, and dear to Allah: Subḥānallāhi wa biḥamdihī, and Subḥānallāhi al-ʿaẓīm. From ʿAbdullah ibn ʿUmar it is reported that the Prophet ﷺ used to say that saying Subḥān Allah fills half the pan, and that saying al-ḥamdu lillāh fills the other pan.

The three of them, Abū Dāwūd, at-Tirmidhī and Ibn Ḥibbān, report on sound authority from Abū ad-Dardāʾ that no deed will be heavier than good character on the scale. To Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī the Prophet ﷺ said: I tell you of two things that are not difficult for anyone, yet on the scale of deeds they will be the heaviest. Good character, and keeping to silence, that is, not talking unless it is necessary.

In his book on asceticism Aḥmad reports from Ḥāzim that the angel Jibraʿīl al-Amīn came to the Prophet ﷺ while someone there was weeping from the fear of Allah. Jibraʿīl said: all human deeds will be weighed, but the tears that flow from the fear of Allah and the Hereafter are a deed that is not weighed. A single such tear would hold back the fiercest fire of Hell.

From Ibn al-Mubarak comes the account of a man on the plain of the Resurrection who looks at his Book of Deeds and sees very few good deeds there, and is nervous. Suddenly something rises like a cloud and falls into the pan of his good deeds, and he is told that the reason is the teaching of the faith, which went on multiplying as people acted on it, with a share set aside for him for every single follower of his teaching.

Aṭ-Ṭabarānī reports from Ibn ʿAbbās that a person who goes to the graveyard with the funeral will have two karats placed on his scale, and other narrations give the weight of that karat as equal to Mount Uhud. He reports from Jābir that the first deed to be set on the scale is the good deed of providing for his family and looking after what they need. Al-Dhahabī reports from ʿImrān ibn Ḥusayn that on the Day of Resurrection the ink used by the scholars to set down the religion and its rulings will be weighed against the blood of the martyrs, and the ink will turn out to be the heavier.

### How the weighing is put in practice

One authority has said that the weighing may be repeated several times in different ways, and that the full reality of these matters is known to Allah alone. For a person going about his ordinary life, it is not necessary to know that reality. It is quite enough to know that the deeds will be weighed. If they turn out to be light, punishment is what is deserved, and then it is a separate matter whether Allah forgives in His grace, by Himself, or through the intercession of a prophet or a saint, and the person stands delivered. The claim that some people are saved solely because they said and believed the declaration of faith, and that all their sins are forgiven on that account, belongs to the exception, and is a special gift of the grace and mercy of Allah.

### The inner reading

Deeds weighed on the scale of self-purification, and states weighed on the scale of truthfulness. Wretched is the man whose deeds are stained by showing off and whose states are mingled with self-admiration, for at the station of the scales such states are worth nothing and such deeds have no weight. The verse is quoted to say that "on the Day of Resurrection We will not give their deeds any weight" (18:105).

Among the traditions of ʿUmar is this: "Take an accounting of yourselves before you are brought to account, weigh your deeds before they are weighed, and prepare for the Greatest Exposure." The sense of it is that you should take your own inventory and look at what you have actually done, and then ask yourself what you have stored up for the Greatest Exposure and for the day of the gathering. That is why the Lord of the Worlds said, "be mindful of Allah and let every soul look to what deeds it has sent forth for tomorrow, and fear Allah, for certainly Allah is All-Aware of what you do" (59:18).

It is reported that a man of intelligence has four hours set aside for seeking felicity and adorning his life. The first is the hour in which he takes an account of himself and weighs his own deeds and states. The second is the hour of private conversation with the Real, when he lays his need before Him. The third is the hour in which he takes care of his own livelihood. The fourth is the hour of rest in quiet prayer and in whatever this world has given him.

Those who walk the Tariqah and the lords of recognition hold that the scales are many. There is a scale for the soul and the spirit, a scale for the heart and the intellect, and a scale for recognition and the secret core. The first measures commands against prohibitions, and the two pans that meet are the Book and the Sunnah. The second measures reward, and its two pans are the threat and the promise. The third measures approval, and its two pans are fleeing and seeking. Fleeing means escaping this world to cling to the world to come. Seeking means leaving the world to come aside to look for the Patron.

### In short

- The weighing is the true weighing, because Allah wrongs no one. Not a mustard seed, not an atom, is overlooked.
- Three things are described as being weighed: the deeds, the scrolls in which they are written, and the person who did them. All three are held together, and Allah knows best.
- Deeds have no mass, and the answer is that the power of Allah is absolute, that He can give deeds a material presence, and that the record of deeds will be laid open.
- The declaration of faith outweighs any quantity of sins, so the weighing is done twice: once for belief, once for deeds.
- Nothing is what it seems in weight. Good character, silence, tears of fear, teaching the faith, a funeral, and spending on one's family all weigh more than their size suggests.

The other half of the pair. The previous verse described the scale that sinks down. This one describes the scale that tips up, and it says what is on it and what the loss actually amounts to. "But those whose scale is light, they have doomed themselves for wrongfully denying Our signs". The loss is self-inflicted. Nobody is taken away from Paradise, and nobody fails to arrive. They weigh themselves down, and the verse says so in the plainest terms.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But those whose scale is light"** means those whose bad deeds outweigh their good ones. The reason is given in the same clause: they are light because of the evil they did.

**"they have doomed themselves"** means they have lost their own souls, and the loss is a punishment. One gloss puts it as the soul travelling towards the Fire. Another says that they gained their destruction on the Day of Judgment and were cut off from the eternal bliss, and the word for "doomed" in the Arabic carries the sense of destroying oneself. The parallel passage in the Qur'an says the same in a single line: those whose scale is light have doomed themselves and will stay in the Fire forever (23:103).

**"for wrongfully denying Our signs"** gives the cause and the aggravating circumstance. They knew. They denied the signs knowingly, and they denied them wrongfully, and the denial that condemned them was of the revelations of Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an.

### Where the light scale leads

The account of the light scale in Surah Al-Qariʿah is short and terrible. "And as for those whose scale is light, their home will be the abyss" (101:8-9). The word behind "abyss" is the pit, and the gloss given for it is Hell itself, and the question that follows has no comfortable answer: "And what will make you realize what that is? It is a scorching Fire" (101:10-11).

Two things are being said at once. The home is already settled, and the home is the Fire. And the verdict cannot be reopened, because the scale is not weighed twice. The parallel passage at 23:103 puts the outcome in the plainest possible words, and it is worth noticing what it does not say. It does not say that the fire will be lifted, or that the light scale will be weighed again, or that a late good deed might turn it. Those whose scale is light stay in the Fire forever.

The loss is also described as exclusion rather than merely as suffering. Those who are cut off from the bliss, cut off from it, and the cutting off is the punishment.

### The knowing that made it worse

The final clause is what turns the verse from a warning into an indictment. The denial was not a mistake, and it was not a failure to understand. It was a denial of signs that had been shown to them, and one gloss puts it as their mistreating of what they had already been given.

The signs they denied are named in another reading as the revelations of Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an itself. So nothing about the scale was hidden from them. They knew what the signs were, they knew what they were saying about them, and they said it anyway. A person who has seen a thing and then set it aside has not been short of information. He has been short of something else.

### What it means to lose yourself

The phrase is stronger than it looks. To lose yourself here is not to be disappointed, and not to fail in some hope. It is to be the cause of your own ruin, so that the losing is done by you and not done to you. A person is left with nothing, and what is left is the loss of the self, which is the one thing that cannot be replaced.

That is why the verse pairs the light scale with the deliberate refusal. The verse before it promised success to those whose scale was heavy. This one closes the other door. The scale does not tip by accident, and it is not tipped by an outside power. It tips because of what was put on it.

### The weight changes hands at the last day

In this world the importance of a person is judged by the material splendour around him and by his ability to move people with impressive words, and so to have a large following. The preacher of truth is generally not in that happy position. What he says carries no weight with the people of the world, while the words of his rivals, who are decked in worldly splendour, carry a great deal.

But when the Day of Judgment uncovers all the facts, the position is exactly reversed. All the weight then lies on the side of the truth. Untruth is stripped of all its arguments and rendered valueless, and the things that were counted for nothing are the things that carry the day.

That reversal is the point of the two verses together. The scale in verse 8 and the scale in verse 9 are the same scale, and the difference between the two kinds of people is not the scale but what was put on it. The world measures the wrong things. The Last Day measures the right ones.

### In short

- A light scale means the bad deeds outweigh the good, and the cause given is the knowing and wrongful denial of the signs.
- To doom yourself is the strongest phrase available: the ruin is self-inflicted, and the loss is of the self itself.
- The outcome is the abyss as a home and the Fire forever, and the same word is used in the parallel passage at 23:103.
- The world measures the wrong things, and the Last Day reverses every weighing.

The earth is described as a storehouse. Science and technology are a trained method of taking out what the Master of the Universe has stored there and using it soundly. An intelligent person benefits from both.

The demand that follows is plain. Everything people need has been put into the earth by Allah, and that is a standing demand on them to be grateful to Him at every moment and in every circumstance. Human beings are prone to heedlessness, and they do not take long to forget the favours of their Creator and Master. That is why the verse ends in a complaint: you are so little receptive to advice.

### The reason the thanks are withheld

Whatever has been bestowed on human beings in this world was given to draw out their gratitude, and gratitude is the one thing they do not offer to their Lord. The reason is that the devil distorts their feelings and steers them away from every expression of gratitude.

The story of Adam and the devil shows the real criterion of a person's case. Human beings are judged at the point where jealousy and pride form in the heart. In this testing world the same situation keeps arising, in which one person ends up above another. Sometimes one person receives a greater share of wealth and honour than his fellow men. Sometimes, in a deal between two people, one of them treats the discharge of the other's legitimate rights as degrading to himself. On every such occasion a person is judged, and the question is whether he responds positively or reacts negatively. The pride that turns even a fair transaction into a wound is what blocks the response.

### The verse in the Qur'an's own summary

The same point is made in another verse: "He has granted you all that you asked Him for, and if you tried to count Allah's blessings you would never be able to number them. Indeed humankind is truly unfair and totally ungrateful" (14:34).

### In short

- The earth was made a place of dwelling, with mountains, rivers, homes and every trade and profession.
- The favour is universal and the requirement is gratitude at all times and in all conditions.
- Human beings forget quickly. The verse ends in a complaint, not in a blessing: you are so little receptive to advice.
- Gratitude is withheld because the feelings are distorted, and judgement falls where jealousy and pride begin.

Allah is informing the children of Adam of the honour of their father and of the enmity of the devil, who still carries jealousy towards them and towards their father Adam. They should therefore beware of him and not follow in his footsteps.

The scene is described more fully elsewhere. "When your Lord said to the angels, I am going to create a human being from sounding clay moulded from black mud" (15:28), and "So when I have fashioned him and had a spirit of My own creation breathed into him, fall down in prostration to him" (15:29). Once Allah had made Adam out of dried clay of altered mud and shaped him into a human form, and had breathed the soul into him, He gave the angels the order to prostrate to him, in honour of Allah's glory and His majesty. ### The inner reading

The wise Lord, the renowned and tremendous One who compels all, the knower of His servants, is reminding the children of Adam of His favours and teaching them about His goodness and His holding to the covenant. He is saying: I made you, and I shaped the beauty of your faces. I set your heights, and gave you two eyes that see, two ears that hear and a tongue that speaks. I am the Lord who brings being out of nothing, who produces what was not there, and who renews things from the very beginning.

It is I who paint the faces. It is I who adorn the beauties. It is I who pair each thing with its counterpart. It is I who make every being in a fitting way. It is I who made the heavens, the earth and the lifeless things, to show forth power. It is I who made the angels, the satans and the jinn, to show forth awe. It is I who made Adam and the Adamites, to show forth forgiveness and mercy. And for 700,000 years the one called Gabriel, and Michael, and Seraphiel, and the Cherubim, and the Circlers, and the Row-keepers made their circuits round the Kaʿbah of the One who compels all, crying "The Glorified, the Holy". They never gained access to the names of love, of loving kindness and of friendship, and they never had the presumption to claim friendship with Me. I, on the other hand, chose friendship with the people of dust. "We are your friends" (41:31). "He loves them" (5:54). Out of that friendship and that kindness towards them I took several of My names: the Forgiving, the Loving, the Clement, the Ever-Merciful. As for the angels, I showed them nothing but severity and the power that compels all, and I kept them behind the veils of awe. To the people of dust I showed nothing but gentleness and mercy, and I kept them on the carpet of bounty and expansiveness.

Among the angels, Gabriel was honoured and foremost, and he was singled out for the special favours of proximity. His name was the servant of the All-Merciful. He stood constantly on the carpet of justice with the attribute of awe, and he had never seen the carpet of bounty and expansiveness. Before Adam the chosen one came, there was no separation and no union, no rejection and no acceptance, and no talk of heart, of sweetheart and of friendship. All of these wonders and storehouses belong to the register of passion.

### In short

- The verse is about Adam, and it uses the plural because Adam is the father of all mankind. The honour and the enmity are his and are inherited by his children.
- "We created you" is Adam, who is from dust. "Then shaped you" covers the wombs, and the command to the angels came only after he was complete.
- The prostration was a greeting. Every angel obeyed. Iblīs, the leader of the jinn who was among the angels, refused out of pride and stubbornness.
- The parallel with 2:57 shows the same rule for the Children of Israel, and 23:12 is the one verse that does not follow it, since Adam's children are made from the nutfah.
