<<<VERSE 7:93>>>
The messenger is left looking at the ruin of his people. "He turned away from them, saying, “O my people! Indeed, I have delivered to you the messages of my Lord and gave you ˹sincere˺ advice. How can I then grieve for those who chose to disbelieve?”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"He turned away from them"** describes his leaving, and it happened before the punishment came down. He went apart from them with the sentence of his Lord in sight. The greater number of the early authorities hold that he and those who had believed with him left that country for Makkah, and that they stayed there until their lives came to their end.

The turning away was also the moment he spoke over them. With the torment, the punishment and the destruction having struck them, he addressed them once more in the way of an admonition and a rebuke, and that is the sense of his words here.

**"O my people! Indeed, I have delivered to you the messages of my Lord and gave you ˹sincere˺ advice"** is the summary he gives of his own work among them. He had conveyed what he was sent with. He had warned them of the torment of Allah, called them to repent, and called them to believe, and he had wanted good for them in all of it.

**"How can I then grieve for those who chose to disbelieve?"** is put as a question that expects no answer. It was the depth of his disappointment with a people who were sunk in disobedience and stubbornness that had moved him to ask his Lord to chasten them. When the punishment then came, and it came as the answer to his prayer, his heart was distressed for them, as the heart of a prophet who carries affection for his people is distressed. The words here settled his own heart. He had done what a messenger does, and there was no room left for grief over the state they had chosen for themselves.

### The place this leaves the story

The prayer of Shu'ayb is answered and the community of Madyan is finished. The account closes with the messenger leaving, with the testimony of a task fully done, and with the refusal of the people standing as the reason for everything that came on them.

### In short

- Shu'ayb left the country before the punishment fell, and the greater number of the early authorities hold that he went to Makkah and stayed there.
- He spoke over his people one last time, as an admonition and a rebuke.
- He had delivered the messages of his Lord, warned them, invited them to repent, and wished them well.
- His prayer against them had been answered, yet his heart was pained for them, since a prophet carries affection for his people.
- The grief is set aside in these words, since a people who refused belief after the message had reached them left him nothing to grieve over.

<<<VERSE 7:94>>>
The story of Madyan closes and the surah draws the general rule out of it. "Whenever We sent a prophet to a society, We afflicted its ˹disbelieving˺ people with suffering and adversity, so perhaps they would be humbled".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whenever We sent a prophet to a society"** widens the frame. The lesson is not tied to the people of Nuh, or of Ad, or of Thamud, or of Shu'ayb on their own. The way described here is the usual way of Allah with the peoples He has sent messengers to, and the verse states it as a rule.

**"We afflicted its ˹disbelieving˺ people with suffering and adversity"** names the first treatment. The affliction is said in the words Basa and Darra. Basa is taken to mean hunger and poverty, and Darra to mean illness, and the two expressions have these senses in other places in the Book as well. Abdullah ibn Masud confirmed these meanings for the two words. Some of those who study the language said that the first word refers to hardship in wealth and the second to the loss of health, and one account has the pair the other way round, with the physical sicknesses and ailments of the people under the first word and the poverty and humiliation they went through under the second.

**"so perhaps they would be humbled"** gives the purpose. The affliction was sent so that they would call on Him, humble themselves before Him, and ask Him to lift what had come on them. The turning of a man to his Lord when he is pressed is a human thing, and the distress was therefore a mercy in disguise, sent for the good of the people it fell on. So the verse carries its own warning for the people who heard it. The fate of suffering that the earlier verses had described was not a thing set apart for those who had gone before, and the people of Quraysh and every other people who refuse the way of Allah were being told plainly what treatment attends a message that is denied.

The complaint of a man against his own time is put in verse by that meaning, that people are made to behave badly towards you so that you turn back to your Lord in tears. Verse 94 ends on that note, and the verse that follows it, verse 95, takes the second half of the same treatment.

**Where the surah is going.** The stories of five messengers and their peoples have now been told. The sixth account, that of Musa and his people, is taken up after a few more verses, and the warning given in these verses covers the ground between the two.

### On the way the Book tells a story

The usual way of the Book with historical events differs from the way of the books of history. It does not set out to give an event in full or in its order of time. It chooses the part of it that carries the purpose in hand, and then it puts the weight on the lesson or the moral that part contains. What the reader is meant to carry away from the stories of the peoples is the manner in which Allah deals with those who deny what He sends, and that manner does not change from one generation to the next.

### In short

- The verse widens the account, since what happened to the earlier peoples was not reserved for them alone.
- The messengers came to their societies first with a call, and the affliction came to those who denied them.
- Basa and Darra name the two sides of that affliction, hunger or want on one side and illness or loss of health on the other.
- The point of the affliction was that the people would humble themselves and turn back to their Lord.
- The people of Quraysh were warned in the same words that this way of Allah still holds.
- The Book tells such events by selecting the part that carries the lesson, not by setting down the whole of the history in order.

<<<VERSE 7:95>>>
The second half of the treatment is put in front of them. "Then We changed their adversity to prosperity until they flourished and argued ˹falsely˺, “Our forefathers ˹too˺ had been visited by adversity and prosperity.” So We seized them by surprise, while they were unaware".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then We changed their adversity to prosperity"** turns the condition round. In this verse the word for the evil condition refers to the hardship, and the word for the good condition means prosperity, and the word used for the increase means growth. The hardship was changed into ease, the illness and sickness into health and well-being, and the poverty into richness of provision. So the drought and the want of fruit were replaced with fertility, abundance and comfort.

**"until they flourished"** gives the result of that turn in their fortunes. They increased in number, in wealth and in children.

**"and argued ˹falsely˺, “Our forefathers ˹too˺ had been visited by adversity and prosperity.”"** is how they read what had happened. The intended end of the whole turn was that they would be thankful to their Lord and turn back to Him, and instead they said that hardship and ease had come to their fathers before them just as they had come to these people. On their reading there was nothing to learn in either, since it was only the habit of time, a matter of the usual ups and downs that people pass through, and no consequence of anything they had done. They held firmly to what they were on and took their fathers as their example.

The effect was that they were shut off from learning anything from either state. Trouble came, and they said that it was bound to happen. When it lifted and they were given good days instead, they settled deeper into their misunderstanding. So the first set of circumstances taught them nothing, and neither did the second.

The words of the verse have nothing more to say about them after that point. **"So We seized them by surprise, while they were unaware"** gives the end of it. The punishment took them suddenly, while they were not expecting it, and before they perceived that its time had come.

### Why the second test is the heavier one

The two states were set as tests, and the people failed both. Neither the affliction nor the ease moved them to change their ways, and the failure in the second is the more dangerous of the two. To be given progress even after a person has been guilty of arrogance is a serious thing, since it is a sign that God has left the culprit to be taken at the point where he has become most fearless of the punishment of God.

Understanding of wisdom and of the testing itself is what the two conditions call for. A believer gives thanks to Allah in good times and practices patience in hard times. A report in the Sahih says that the affair of the believer is a wonder, because nothing that Allah decrees for him is anything but good for him. If harm strikes him he is patient, and that is good for him, and if prosperity is given to him he gives thanks, and that is good for him.

Other reports carry the same lesson. A believer goes on being tried by afflictions until he comes out purified of sin, and the likeness given for the one who plays a part is that of a donkey that does not know why its owners tied it up or why they let it go. Another report says that death coming suddenly is a mercy for the believer and a painful punishment for the one who disbelieves.

The reward of a life lived in awareness of God is in the Hereafter. If God wishes, He gives a part of it in this world as well, in well-being and in respect, and both are from His favour and not from the merit of the one who receives them.

### In short

- When the hardship did not humble them, their condition was turned round, and the hardship was replaced with ease, health and wealth.
- They increased in numbers and in what they owned, and the turn should have brought gratitude and a return to their Lord.
- They reasoned that hardship and ease had visited their fathers too, and took the whole of it for the ordinary movement of time.
- They were caught in that state, and the punishment took them suddenly while they were unaware of its coming.
- Being given ease after arrogance is the more dangerous of the two tests, since it can leave a person fearless of the punishment of God.
- The believer is the one who thanks in prosperity and is patient in hardship, and the reward of that life is with Allah.
