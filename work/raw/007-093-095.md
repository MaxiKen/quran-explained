<<<VERSE 7:93>>>
After the punishment fell, the Prophet Shuʿayb turned from his people with words of reproach: "He turned away from them, saying, “O my people! Indeed, I have delivered to you the messages of my Lord and gave you ˹sincere˺ advice. How can I then grieve for those who chose to disbelieve?”" He had delivered everything he was sent with. Since they disbelieved in what he brought, he would not grieve for them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He turned away from them"**: when the punishment was about to fall on his people, Shuʿayb and his believing companions moved away from that place. One explanation says he left them before they were destroyed. Others say he turned from them once they had been destroyed. Most commentators say these blessed people went to Makkah and stayed there until the end of their lives.

**"saying, “O my people! Indeed, I have delivered to you the messages of my Lord"**: I conveyed to you what my Lord told me to convey, explaining His commands and prohibitions.

**"and gave you ˹sincere˺ advice."** I warned you of Allah's punishment and invited you to repent and believe. But you did not take my advice or follow my guidance.

**"How can I then grieve for those who chose to disbelieve?”"** Why should I grieve over a people who disbelieved in Allah, insisted on their disbelief and were destroyed for it? The question is rhetorical and means: I will not grieve.

### Why did a prophet speak like this?

Shuʿayb had prayed that his people be chastened, out of utter disappointment with people sunk in disobedience and stubbornness. But when the punishment actually came as a result, his heart ached, out of a prophet's care and love for his people. So it was to calm his own heart that he spoke these words to them. He had done all that was required of him, and the loss was theirs.

### In short

- After the punishment, Shuʿayb and the believers left, most say for Makkah, where they lived out their days.
- He reminded his destroyed people that he had delivered his Lord's messages and advised them sincerely.
- His words calmed his own grieving heart, since those who chose disbelief had brought their end on themselves.

<<<VERSE 7:94>>>
After the stories of earlier peoples comes a general rule, a warning for people now, so that they may escape the fate of those before them: "Whenever We sent a prophet to a society, We afflicted its ˹disbelieving˺ people with suffering and adversity, so perhaps they would be humbled". The ruin described so far was not limited to the peoples of Nūḥ (Noah), ʿĀd and Thamūd. It is Allah's usual way to send prophets to guide people to lasting success. When a people ignores their counsel and rejects their call, Allah sends hardship upon it, hoping it will come back to Him in repentance. This verse (7:94) states the first half of the rule, and the next (7:95) gives the second.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whenever We sent a prophet to a society"**: whenever Allah sent a messenger to a town whose people denied him, and whose people He later destroyed.

**"We afflicted its ˹disbelieving˺ people with suffering and adversity"**: the words are baʾsāʾ and ḍarrāʾ. One explanation says baʾsāʾ means the physical illnesses and ailments they suffered, and ḍarrāʾ the poverty and humiliation they went through. Another says baʾsāʾ means hunger or poverty and ḍarrāʾ means illness. The Qur'an uses the words in these senses elsewhere too, and ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd confirmed these meanings. Some linguists say baʾsāʾ is financial hardship and ḍarrāʾ is loss of health. Others list fear, affliction and hardship, and illness, pain and hunger. All this came before their destruction.

**"so perhaps they would be humbled"**: so that they might call on Allah, humble themselves before Him and ask Him to remove their afflictions. Then they might lay aside their pride and disbelief and come to believe. But they did not do what He commanded. This was a warning for Quraysh and for every people who disbelieve and reject Allah's way.

### Why does the Qur'an tell history this way?

So far the surah has told the stories of five earlier prophets and their peoples. The sixth, the story of Mūsā (Moses) and his people, comes a few verses later. The Qur'an's way with history differs from that of history books. It does not try to give an event in full or in strict order. It picks out the part that matters and stresses the lesson it holds.

### Why does Allah send hardship?

It is human nature to turn to the Creator in distress. So this suffering is really a blessing from Allah in disguise, meant for people's good. The great spiritual guide Maulānā Rūmī put it in verse: people are made to treat you badly so that you will turn to your Lord in tears.

Allah sends all kinds of difficulty on people so that their hearts may soften and they may stop relying on anything other than Him. Their arrogance, which keeps them from accepting truth from outside themselves, may then shrink. In this way, by Allah's arrangement, a sense of one's own shortcomings and helplessness is planted in a person, so that he gives the voice of truth his full attention. Both the general public and those to whom a prophet is sent go through this treatment.

But by Allah's usual way, all of this comes wrapped in doubt. When trouble strikes someone, it comes through the ordinary chain of cause and effect. That is what makes it a test for so many people. They dismiss it, saying it was bound to happen, and it did.

### In short

- Whenever a people rejected their prophet, Allah first struck them with hardship, illness and poverty, so that they might humble themselves.
- Such hardship is a mercy in disguise, meant to soften hearts and break the pride that blocks the truth.
- Because hardship comes through ordinary causes, many dismiss it as chance and miss its lesson.
- The Qur'an tells history selectively, for its lessons, and this rule is a warning to Quraysh and every later people.

<<<VERSE 7:95>>>
When the first test failed, Allah tested them in the opposite way: "Then We changed their adversity to prosperity until they flourished and argued ˹falsely˺, “Our forefathers ˹too˺ had been visited by adversity and prosperity.” So We seized them by surprise, while they were unaware". They had been put through hardship so that they would repent and turn to Allah. When they learnt nothing from it, they were given the test of ease.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then We changed their adversity to prosperity"**: the word sayyiʾah here means distress, and ḥasanah means prosperity. Allah replaced hardship with ease, sickness with health and well-being, and poverty with plentiful provision. Drought and failed fruit gave way to fertile land, abundance and comfort, so that they might thank Allah for it. But they did none of this.

**"until they flourished"**: the word ʿafaw means they increased and grew in numbers, wealth and children, and in strength.

**"and argued ˹falsely˺, “Our forefathers ˹too˺ had been visited by adversity and prosperity.”"** Out of ingratitude for this grace, they said: hardship and happiness came to our fathers before us, just as they have come to us. This is only the way of time, not something from Allah, so hold firm to what you follow. In effect: our fathers suffered hardship and distress as we have, yet they held to their religion, and we are like them and will follow their example. They meant to say that their suffering and their prosperity had nothing to do with their deeds. It was simply the natural turn of events, a cycle in which sometimes we suffer hardship and sometimes we enjoy plenty.

**"So We seized them by surprise, while they were unaware"**: Allah struck them with punishment all at once, while they had no idea it was coming and did not expect it. That came after they stubbornly persisted in their evil and ignorance.

### Why did they fail both tests?

Allah tested them with hardship and then with ease and plenty, so that they might humble themselves and turn to Him. But they failed both tests. Neither one moved them to change their ways. They did not grasp Allah's wisdom, or the fact that He was testing them in both states.

When hardship does not teach people, Allah changes their conditions for the better. Then they fall into a worse misunderstanding. They feel sure that all this is just the way of the times, the usual ups and downs people go through. Otherwise, they reason, good days would not have followed bad ones. So they learn nothing from either set of circumstances. Prosperity granted to people who have behaved arrogantly is a grave danger sign. It is a sign that Allah has decided to seize the guilty at the very point where he feels most secure from His punishment.

The real reward of a life of fearing Allah comes in the Hereafter. Yet if Allah wills, He gives a first instalment of it in this world too, in the form of well-being and honour.

### How does a believer face good and bad times?

Believers, by contrast, thank Allah in good times and are patient in hard times. A hadith in the Ṣaḥīḥ says the believer's affair is amazing, for whatever Allah decrees for him is good for him. If harm (ḍarrāʾ) strikes him, he is patient, and that is good for him. If he is given ease, he thanks Allah, and that too is good for him. So the believer understands that both prosperity and adversity are tests from Allah.

Another hadith says that the believer will go on being tested with afflictions until he comes out pure of sin. The hypocrite, by contrast, is like a donkey that does not know why its owners tied it up or why they let it go.

### What does sudden seizure mean?

"So We seized them by surprise" describes punishment coming all at once. A hadith about sudden death says it is a mercy for the believer, but a punishment that brings regret for the disbeliever.

### In short

- When hardship did not humble them, Allah gave them health, wealth and growing numbers as a second test.
- They called both hardship and ease mere cycles of fate, like those their forefathers had known, and so learnt from neither.
- Prosperity after arrogance is a warning sign, and Allah seized them suddenly when they felt most secure.
- A believer is patient in hardship and grateful in ease, and so gains from both.
