The court turns its attention from the magicians to the nation, and the king answers with a policy. "The chiefs of Pharaoh’s people protested, “Are you going to leave Moses and his people free to spread corruption in the land and abandon you and your gods?” He responded, “We will kill their sons and keep their women. We will completely dominate them.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"The chiefs of Pharaoh’s people protested"** records the pressure that came on the king from his own court. They put the question to him in front of one another, since something about his conduct had begun to look strange to them. His fury and his rage had fallen on the magicians alone, and against Musa and Harun, who were the real opponents, he had not dared to say a word. His own leading men noticed it, and they came to him with the question the verse reports.

**"Are you going to leave Moses and his people free to spread corruption in the land"** is the charge. What they meant by corruption was that Musa and his people were spreading unrest among the subjects and calling them to worship their Lord instead of the king. The wonder of it is that these very men were the corrupters of the land, and they did not see it in themselves.

**"and abandon you and your gods?"** is the second half of the charge, and it gives away something about the king himself. He had set small idols for his people to worship, and he had told them that he was their lord and the lord of their fathers. So a man who claimed to be a god for his people was being asked to protect the gods he kept beside himself. The complaint also lets it be seen that the king had claimed lordship while worshipping others, and that is the contradiction his own chiefs were pressing on him.

**"He responded, “We will kill their sons and keep their women. We will completely dominate them.”"** is his answer, and it is a policy rather than a reply. He had no convincing word to the question, so he returned to the measure he had used before. By killing the males and leaving the women alive he meant to bring the whole people to an end within a period of time, and the women were to be spared to serve his people as servants. His words about power over them were a claim of complete dominance and supremacy.

**"your gods", and whose calf it was.** The idols the chiefs spoke of were, in the report that As-Suddi gave from Ibn Abbas, cows. Whenever the king saw a beautiful cow, he would command the people to worship it. That is the same calf that As-Samiri later made for the Children of Israel, a statue that seemed to moo, and the report shows where his idea came from.

**What the king was afraid of.** The whole answer was the speech of a frightened man. He had seen the staff turned into a serpent and the hand come out shining, and after that he could threaten the whole tribe of the Israelites while keeping silent about the two men who stood in front of him. Maulana Rumi put the law of it in a line. The one who takes up taqwa and holds Allah in awe is himself held in awe by every human being, and by the spirits as well.

### What the policy shows about the king

The order to kill the males and spare the women was not new. It had been put into force once before, in the years before the birth of Musa, so that he would never be born, and the king was looking at the wreck of his own plan while he spoke. The crowd of the Israelites standing in front of him at that moment was the evidence that the first version of the policy had failed. The one he was sent to destroy was raised in the king's own house and stood before his throne as the messenger of the Lord of all worlds.

There is a rule in that. When Allah intends to bring disgrace on a people, everything they contrive turns into nothing but disaster for them. The law that was meant to end the people of Israel served to bring their deliverer into the world, and the second attempt at the same law was answered by the destruction of the king and his army in the sea.

### In short

- The chiefs of the court pressed the king, since his anger had gone no further than the magicians and he had not dared to threaten Musa and Harun.
- The charge they brought was that Musa was spreading corruption and leading the people away from the king and his gods.
- The ones who were really corrupting the land were the men bringing the charge.
- His answer had no argument in it, and he returned to the old law of killing the sons and sparing the women, to end the people in time and keep the women as servants.
- The gods the chiefs named were the cows the king set up for worship, and the same calf was later set up for the Children of Israel by As-Samiri.
- The first round of that law had already failed, since the one it was meant to prevent was standing in front of the king, and a people whose ruin is intended finds every plan of theirs turning to disaster.

<<<VERSE 7:128>>>
The messenger turns to his own people with the answer that holds for every generation under a tyrant. "Moses reassured his people, “Seek Allah’s help and be patient. Indeed, the earth belongs to Allah ˹alone˺. He grants it to whoever He chooses of His servants. The ultimate outcome belongs ˹only˺ to the righteous.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Moses reassured his people"** is the setting of the instruction. The law had just been laid on them once more, and they were badly alarmed by what they had gone through before the coming of Musa and by what was now beginning again.

**"Seek Allah’s help and be patient"** gives the two wise remedies he placed in front of them. The first was to ask Allah alone for help to remove the hardship and to bring good, and the second was to hold themselves steady under the trial until matters turned in their favour. The two together are the sovereign cure that does not fail against any difficulty.

Seeking help is the essence of the remedy, and the reason is plain. If the Creator of the universe comes to a person's help, then who is there to stop Him? Maulana Rumi put the same truth in a couplet, that the earth, the air, the water and the fire are all servants of Allah, and that they are dead to us while they are full of life to Him. There is a tradition that says when Allah wills a thing to be done, everything turns in favour of that purpose. So nothing is more powerful against an enemy than to seek the help of Allah with a sincere heart, and the mere uttering of a formula is not what is meant.

Patience is the second ingredient, and the word used for it, sabr, means to keep oneself under control when things go against a person. Everybody knows that nothing significant is achieved without difficulty, and the one who prepares himself ahead of time to meet hardship is the one who reaches what he aims at. The Prophet ﷺ said in a tradition that no greater blessing has been given to anyone other than patience, and the report is carried by Abu Dawud.

**"Indeed, the earth belongs to Allah, He grants it to whoever He chooses of His servants"** is the ground of the whole instruction. The earth spoken of here is the land of Egypt, and the point is that it belongs to Allah alone. It does not belong to Pharaoh or to any other person to do with as he pleases. It is Allah who turns it over to one people and then to another as He wills. So a people who keep taqwa and act on the two instructions would come in the end to rule the whole country.

**"The ultimate outcome belongs only to the righteous"** names the end of the matter. The good end, and in one reading the Garden itself, is for those who keep their duty to Him and turn away from disbelief, from the worship of partners, and from shameful deeds.

### The difference between a prophet and a politician

The Israelites had brought the problem of the rule of Pharaoh to their prophet and asked him to solve it for them, and the solution he gave was that they should turn to God. That is the difference between the way of the worldly leader and the way of the prophet in the face of a communal problem. The worldly leader looks for the answer at the level of the government, either by making terms with it or by coming to blows with it. What the prophet offered here was that whatever was happening should be borne, and that the help of God be sought, without expecting anything from the powers of the day.

What the people said back to him is recorded in the verse that follows, and the answer he gave to that is the plainest statement of where power comes from and what it is for.

### In short

- The instruction came at a moment when the old law had been renewed and the people were frightened.
- The two remedies are seeking the help of Allah and holding steady under the trial, and the two together do not fail against any difficulty.
- The tradition carried by Abu Dawud says that no greater blessing has been given to a person than patience.
- The earth belongs to Allah alone, and He gives the rule of it to whichever of His servants He chooses.
- The good end belongs to those who keep their duty to Him, and in one reading the good end means the Garden.
- A prophet meets a communal problem by turning the people to God, while the worldly leader looks for the answer in a bargain or a fight with the government.

<<<VERSE 7:129>>>
The people answer with a complaint about their whole history. "They complained, “We have always been oppressed—before and after you came to us ˹with the message˺.” He replied, “Perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy and make you successors in the land to see what you will do.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"They complained, “We have always been oppressed—before and after you came to us ˹with the message˺.”"** gives the objection that the Children of Israel raised with Moses. They had been tormented by the killing of their sons, by the seizure of their women, and by the labour laid on them, and they said that the persecution had gone on both before the messenger came and since he came. The words carry the difficulty they had with the instruction they had just been given, since they could not see how patience by itself was going to free them from the punishment of the king or bring them success against him.

**"He replied,"** opens the answer of Musa. The word rendered as perhaps sounds open, and on the tongue of a man it would carry uncertainty. Spoken of Allah it conveys certainty. The promise given there was that the adversary of the Israelites would be destroyed.

**"and make you successors in the land"** is the second part, and it was fulfilled in the plainest way. Their enemy was destroyed, and they were made the ones in authority in the land of Egypt, and in the broader sense they were given the earth as an inheritance.

**"to see what you will do"** is where the promise turns into something weightier than a promise. The words encourage them to value what Allah gives when affliction is lifted and replaced with bounty, and then they put the question of conduct in front of them. That is the point the whole account is making. Power and rule are a test for those who hold them, given so that it may be seen how far they fulfil the duty of establishing peace and justice and make the good prevail over the evil.

### The report about the verse

Abu Hayyan included the following in his commentary, Al-Bahr Al-Muhit. Amr ibn Ubaid visited Mansur, the second of the Abbasid caliphs, before he came to the caliphate, and recited this verse, that it may be that Allah will destroy your enemy and make you a successor in the earth. The reciting was taken as a prediction that the man before him would come to the throne. Soon afterwards Mansur did succeed and became the caliph, and Amr ibn Ubaid came to him again. The caliph reminded him of what he had said, and Amr ibn Ubaid answered at once that the first part had come true and the man had become caliph, while the second part remained, since the verse goes on to say that He will see how you act. He meant that gaining power is no ground for pride by itself, since the acts of those in authority are judged afterwards, and the way they use what was put in their trust is seen.

### Where power comes from

Moses explained to the Israelites why he was offering them a solution that went against the ordinary way of thinking. The troubles had come from the ruling authority, and by any ordinary reckoning that authority was the only thing that could resolve them. What the people had not weighed was how power comes to be held in the first place. Nobody holds power merely by the working of strategies, since power is conferred and taken away directly by God, and if that is so then the resolution of the problem lies with God as well. Another verse covers the same ground, that Allah gives dominion to whom He wills (3:26), and the Israelites were being taught to look past Pharaoh to the One who had set him over them.

### What power is for

Power in the hands of any people is a test, and being powerless is a test of its own kind. If a person has been given power, it is because he is being tried on whether he turns haughty and tyrannical or behaves with justice and modesty. If the empowerment goes to somebody else, the purpose is still to test. When a group loses power because of its own incompetence and another group takes it, the second group is put to the same examination, and if it proves incompetent in turn the power will be taken from it and given to a third. The prosperity and the standing that a person craves in this world are things that can only be held in the real sense in the Hereafter. In this world they are given out for the test, and in the Hereafter they will be given to the righteous as their reward.

### In short

- The people complained that the oppression had gone on both before the messenger came and after, and they could not see how patience would change it.
- The promise given back to them was certain, that their enemy would be destroyed and that they would be made the ones in authority in the land.
- The last words of the verse make conduct the point of the whole promise, since power is given so that it may be seen how those who hold it behave.
- Abu Hayyan records the report of Amr ibn Ubaid before Mansur, who was told that the first half of the verse had come true and that the second half still stood over him.
- Power is conferred and removed by God alone, and for that reason the answer to the problem of a ruling authority also lies with Him.
- Rule is a test for whoever holds it, and the prosperity people crave in this world is only held in the real sense in the Hereafter.
