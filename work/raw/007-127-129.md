<<<VERSE 7:127>>>
Allah describes the plotting of Pharaoh and his people, their evil intentions and their hatred for Mūsā (Moses) and his people: "The chiefs of Pharaoh’s people protested, “Are you going to leave Moses and his people free to spread corruption in the land and abandon you and your gods?” He responded, “We will kill their sons and keep their women. We will completely dominate them.”" With clever falsehoods, Pharaoh had managed to keep his ignorant people in their old ignorance. Yet they noticed something odd. All his fury fell on the magicians alone. He did not dare say a word against Mūsā and Hārūn (Aaron), his real opponents.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The chiefs of Pharaoh’s people protested"**: the leaders and notables of Pharaoh's people urged him on, provoking him to act against Mūsā and the believers with him.

**"“Are you going to leave Moses and his people free"**: will you not kill them? Will you let them go free?

**"to spread corruption in the land"**: spreading unrest among your subjects, changing their religion and worship, and calling them to worship their Lord, Allah alone, instead of you. How strange that these people worried about Mūsā and his people causing corruption! In truth, Pharaoh and his people were the ones spreading corruption, though they did not see it.

**"and abandon you and your gods?”"** and to leave you and the worship of your gods. In another reading: and to give up worshipping you as a god. Ibn ʿAbbās, as as-Suddī narrated from him, said their gods were cows. Whenever they saw a beautiful cow, Pharaoh would order them to worship it. That is why as-Sāmirī made for the children of Israel the statue of a calf that seemed to low. One explanation says Pharaoh had made small idols for them to worship, while telling them he was their lord and the lord of those idols. Their words also show that Pharaoh himself worshipped other gods, even while claiming to be his people's god.

**"He responded, “We will kill their sons"**: Pharaoh accepted his people's advice. We will slaughter their newborn sons, as we did the first time. The verb can be read nuqattilu, "we will kill in great numbers", or naqtulu, "we will kill".

**"and keep their women."** We will let their grown women live, to serve us as maidservants.

**"We will completely dominate them.”"** We have overwhelming power and supremacy over them. So they did this to them, and the children of Israel were filled with grief.

### Why did Pharaoh not threaten Mūsā himself?

Pharaoh had no convincing answer to his chiefs' question, and could only say he would kill the Israelites' sons and spare their women. It is explained that he tried to reassure his people this way. By killing the males and sparing the women, he would wipe out the Israelites over time, while the women would serve his people. In fact Pharaoh was so frightened by Mūsā's miracle that even while threatening to kill every Israelite man, he could not bring himself to utter one word against the two brothers. As Maulānā Rūmī put it, all humans and spirits stand in awe of the one who fears Allah and holds to taqwā.

### Did the old law work?

This was the second time the law of killing the Israelites' sons and sparing their women was proclaimed. The first time was before Mūsā's birth, so that Mūsā would not survive. The failure of that law was plain to see that very day, in the huge crowd of Israelites present. The opposite of what Pharaoh intended came about. The fate he planned for the children of Israel, to crush and humiliate them, fell on him instead. Allah gave the children of Israel victory, disgraced Pharaoh and drowned him with his soldiers. When Allah means to humble a people, everything they devise leads only to disaster, and this tyranny led Pharaoh and his people to their shameful end.

### In short

- Pharaoh's chiefs goaded him to act against Mūsā, calling him a spreader of corruption, when they themselves were the corrupters.
- Their "gods" included the cows they worshipped, which explains as-Sāmirī's later calf.
- Pharaoh renewed his old law of killing the Israelites' sons, yet was too afraid to threaten Mūsā and Hārūn themselves.
- The first law had already failed, and this one too would turn back on him.

<<<VERSE 7:128>>>
When Pharaoh pressed on with his evil plan, Mūsā (Moses) gave his people a remedy: "Moses reassured his people, “Seek Allah’s help and be patient. Indeed, the earth belongs to Allah ˹alone˺. He grants it to whoever He chooses of His servants. The ultimate outcome belongs ˹only˺ to the righteous.”" After his defeat, Pharaoh had enforced the law of killing the Israelites' sons, and they were terrified, remembering what they had suffered before Mūsā came. Mūsā was aware of their fear. Out of kindness he offered them two wise steps, and promised that the good end would be theirs and that they would prevail.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Moses reassured his people, “Seek Allah’s help"**: ask Allah alone for help, to remove hardship from you and bring you good.

**"and be patient."** Endure the trial you have been given, and their persecution, until things turn in your favour.

**"Indeed, the earth belongs to Allah ˹alone˺."** The land, the land of Egypt, belongs to Allah, not to Pharaoh or anyone else to do with as they please.

**"He grants it to whoever He chooses of His servants."** He bequeaths it as an inheritance to whom He wills. By His will, He gives authority in the land to different peoples in turn.

**"The ultimate outcome belongs ˹only˺ to the righteous.”"** The praiseworthy end belongs to those who are mindful of Allah, who guard against disbelief, idolatry and indecency. One explanation says the end meant is Paradise. Those who believe, follow their Lord's commands and avoid what He forbids will reach the good end despite their tests and trials. So if they kept taqwā by acting on these two instructions, they would in the end rule the whole country.

### What is the remedy that never fails?

A little reflection on Mūsā's two teachings shows that together they are a sure remedy for every difficulty. The first ingredient, the heart of the remedy, is seeking Allah's help. If the Creator of the universe comes to someone's aid, who can stop Him? Maulānā Rūmī put it in a couplet: earth, air, water and fire are all Allah's servants, lifeless to you and me, but fully alive to Allah. A hadith says that when Allah wills something, everything turns to serve that purpose. No weapon against an enemy is stronger than turning to Allah for help with a sincere heart. Merely saying some formula is not enough.

The second ingredient is patience. The Arabic word ṣabr literally means holding oneself in check when unwelcome things happen. Everyone knows that nothing important is achieved without hardship. A person who readily prepares to face difficulty succeeds in most of his aims. The Prophet ﷺ said that no one has been given a greater blessing than patience. Abū Dāwūd recorded this.

### How does a prophet solve a people's problem?

Pharaoh's rule had created a problem for the children of Israel, and they asked their prophet to solve it. The solution he offered was to turn to Allah. This shows the difference between how worldly leaders and prophets approach a community's problems. Worldly leaders look for the answer at the level of government, either by compromising with the authorities or by clashing with them. The Prophet's solution was to bear what was happening patiently, seek Allah's help, and turn to Allah without expecting anything from the powers that be.

He also explained why his solution went against the usual thinking of his people. Their problems seemed to come from the ruling power and seemed solvable only by that power. But the more important question was how power comes to be held at all. No one holds power merely through clever strategy. Power is given or taken away directly by Allah. So the solution to the problem also lies with Allah.

### In short

- Facing Pharaoh's renewed persecution, Mūsā told his people to seek Allah's help and be patient.
- The earth belongs to Allah, who gives it to whom He wills, and the good end belongs to the mindful.
- Sincere reliance on Allah and patient endurance together are a remedy that never fails. Patience is among the greatest of blessings.
- Prophets solve a people's problems by turning them to Allah, the true giver and taker of power.

<<<VERSE 7:129>>>
The children of Israel could not see how patience alone would free them from Pharaoh's oppression, and they complained to Mūsā (Moses): "They complained, “We have always been oppressed—before and after you came to us ˹with the message˺.”" His answer held out hope: "He replied, “Perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy and make you successors in the land to see what you will do.”" Mūsā answered by reminding them of their present situation and how it would change.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They complained, “We have always been oppressed"**: we suffered at Pharaoh's hands. Pharaoh and his people humiliated and disgraced us, killing our sons, using our women and forcing us to labour, and you have seen some of it.

**"before and after you came to us ˹with the message˺.”"** Perhaps they meant that they had been waiting for a prophet to save them from Pharaoh's oppression, yet their lot was unchanged even after he came.

**"He replied, “Perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy"**: when Allah says "perhaps" (ʿasā), it carries certainty. Your Lord will destroy your enemy, Pharaoh and his people, through drought and hunger. Mūsā gave them hope of release from their trial.

**"and make you successors in the land"**: Allah will establish you in the land after them and make you its inhabitants, the land of Egypt.

**"to see what you will do.”"** So that He may see how you act in it, whether you obey Him, and whether you are thankful or ungrateful. Mūsā was encouraging them to value Allah's favour when the afflictions were lifted and replaced by blessings.

### Is power a reward or a test?

The last phrase of the verse holds a wise lesson. Sovereignty and rule are not goals in themselves. They are a means to make peace and justice prevail in the land, a sacred trust given by Allah so that good may overcome evil. The verse warned the Israelites that, if they were given this trust, they should not forget the fate of those before them.

The verse speaks directly to the Israelites, but indirectly it warns everyone entrusted with rule. Sovereignty belongs to Allah alone. He has made human beings His deputies on earth, giving them power and taking it away when He wills:

> "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O Allah! Lord over all authorities! You give authority to whoever You please and remove it from who You please; You honour whoever You please and disgrace who You please" (3:26).

So power is a test of how far rulers fulfil their duty to establish peace and justice and make good prevail over evil.

In this world, being powerful is as much a test as being powerless. Whoever has power today is being tested on whether he will grow arrogant and tyrannical, or act with justice and humility. If power is given to others instead, that too is to test them. If one group loses power through its own incompetence and it passes to another group, which also proves incompetent, power will be taken from it too and given to yet another. The prosperity and power people long for in this world can only be truly had in the Hereafter. In this world they are given only as a test, and in the Hereafter they will be given to the righteous as a reward.

### What did ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd tell the caliph?

Abū Ḥayyān records an event under this verse in his tafsīr, Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ. ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd once visited al-Manṣūr, later the second caliph of the Abbasid dynasty, before he came to power. He recited this verse to him, "Perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy and make you successors in the land", as a kind of prediction that al-Manṣūr would come to the throne. Soon afterwards al-Manṣūr became caliph. When ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd came to him again, al-Manṣūr reminded him of his prediction. ʿAmr replied at once that the first part had come true, since he was now caliph. But the second part still remained: "to see what you will do". Gaining power, he meant, is nothing to be proud of, because afterwards Allah judges how those in authority use that trust.

### In short

- The Israelites complained that their suffering had not changed since Mūsā came.
- Mūsā assured them that Allah would destroy their enemy and make them successors in the land, to see how they would act.
- Power is a trust and a test from Allah, who gives and takes authority as He wills, and the powerless are tested too.
- ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd reminded a new caliph that the verse's warning, "to see what you will do", still lay ahead of him.
