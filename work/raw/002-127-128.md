<<<VERSE 2:127>>>
Verse 127 shows Ibrāhīm (Abraham) at work on the House itself, and shows what was in his heart as he worked. "And ˹remember˺ when Abraham raised the foundation of the House with Ishmael, ˹both praying,˺ “Our Lord! Accept ˹this˺ from us. You are indeed the All-Hearing, All-Knowing". Allah tells the Prophet ﷺ: remind your people how Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) built the House and raised its foundations while saying these words. In obedience to Allah, together with Ibrāhīm had left the fertile land of Syria and settled his wife and child in a barren desert, and now he took up the labour of building the House of Allah. A man who had borne such hardships might be expected to feel satisfied with himself and relax in self-congratulation. But the Friend of Allah recognised the Majesty of Allah and knew that no creature can worship or obey his Creator as is His due, only within his own limited powers. So he knew that in performing the hardest and greatest tasks a person should not be proud of his achievement but should humbly pray that Allah accept the little effort he has been able to make, an effort made with Allah's own grace. That is exactly what he did as he began, with his young son, to build the Kaʿbah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember˺ when Abraham raised the foundation of the House with Ishmael"**. He raised up the supports or walls of the House, building it, and Ismāʿīl was with him. In the Arabic "and Ishmael" is joined to "Abraham" as a second subject, and "of the House" attaches to the verb "raises". Ibrāhīm built while Ismāʿīl helped, which is the order the names themselves suggest: the builder of the Kaʿbah is Ibrāhīm and his son's role is that of a helper. When they started digging, the earlier foundations became visible, and it was on these that they raised the walls.

**"˹both praying,˺ “Our Lord! Accept ˹this˺ from us"**. Receive from us this building of Your House. Al-Qurṭubī mentioned that Ubayy and Ibn Masʿūd used to recite the verse with the word "saying" added, and what follows, "Our Lord! Make us both submit", confirms that reading, since it continues the same prayer. The two prophets were performing a good deed, and yet they asked Allah to accept it from them. Ibn Abī Ḥātim narrated that Wuhayb ibn al-Ward recited this verse and wept, saying: You, the Khalīl of ar-Raḥmān, build His House’s foundations and still fear that He will not accept it from you? This is the way of sincere believers, whom Allah described as "those who give whatever they give" (23:60), that is, who give charity and perform acts of worship, "with their hearts fearful" (23:60), afraid that their good deeds may not be accepted. There is an authentic hadith of ʿĀʾishah on this point. In the devotional reading, the success of a request lies in the sincerity of the supplication: when the two of them humbly sought help in prayer, He helped them and granted what they asked.

**"You are indeed the All-Hearing, All-Knowing"**. The Hearer of words and of our prayer, the Knower of deeds and of the answer to this prayer, or, as it is also explained, the Knower of our intention in building Your House. Allah hears what people say and knows their intentions and all that they do.

### How the House was built, in the Prophet's ﷺ own account

Al-Bukhārī recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās the story of how all this came about. Ibrāhīm took Ismāʿīl and his mother and travelled with them until he reached the site of the House, where he left them under a tree above the place of Zamzam, in the upper part of the Mosque. She was still nursing the child. Makkah was then uninhabited and had no water, and he left them with a bag of dates and a skin of water. As he turned to go, Ismāʿīl's mother followed him and asked to whom he was leaving them in a valley with no people in it. She repeated the question several times and he did not answer. Then she asked whether Allah had commanded him to do this. He said yes, and she said: then He will not abandon us. Ibrāhīm went on until, out of their sight near Thaniyyah, he faced the House, raised his hands and prayed that he had settled some of his offspring in a barren valley by the Sacred House, down to the words about giving thanks (14:37).

She went back, drank from the skin and nursed the boy, and when the water ran out both of them grew thirsty. Unable to bear the sight of him suffering, she went to the nearest hill, aṣ-Ṣafā, climbed it and looked in vain for anyone. Coming down into the valley she lifted her garment and ran like an exhausted person until she reached al-Marwah, where again she saw no one, and she went back and forth seven times. Ibn ʿAbbās reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said this is why people make the trip between aṣ-Ṣafā and al-Marwah. On al-Marwah she heard a voice, said "hush" to herself, listened, heard it again and called out, "I have heard you. Do you have relief?" She found the angel digging with his heel, or his wing, at the place of Zamzam until water gushed out. Astonished, she began scooping it with her hands into the water-skin. The Prophet ﷺ said: may Allah have mercy on the mother of Ismāʿīl. Had she left the water alone, it would have run as a stream over the surface of the earth. She drank, her milk returned for the child, and the angel, Jibrīl (Gabriel), told her not to fear abandonment, for a House of Allah would be built here by this boy and his father, and Allah does not abandon His people. At that time the site of the House stood higher than the ground around it, and floods passed to its right and left.

Later some people of the tribe of Jurhum, passing through Kadāʾ, camped at the bottom of the valley. They noticed birds circling and said that birds are only found where there is water, though they had never known water in that valley. They sent one or two scouts, who found the spring and reported back, and they all came to Ismāʿīl's mother and asked leave to settle beside her. She agreed, on condition that they would have no exclusive right to the water, and they accepted. The Prophet ﷺ remarked that she welcomed human company. They settled and sent for their relatives. The boy grew up among them, learned Arabic from them, and because they admired the way he had been raised they gave him one of their women in marriage. Then his mother died.

Some time afterwards Ibrāhīm came to visit the family he had left. He did not find Ismāʿīl, who had gone hunting, and asked his wife how they lived. She complained of misery and poverty. He told her to greet her husband and tell him to change the threshold of his door. When Ismāʿīl returned he sensed a visitor had come, heard what had been said, and told her: that was my father, and you are the threshold. He was telling me to part with you. He divorced her and married another woman. When Ibrāhīm came again he found Ismāʿīl out hunting once more, and this wife praised Allah and said they were well. Asked what they ate and drank, she said meat and water, and he prayed: O Allah, bless their meat and their drink. The Prophet ﷺ explained that they had no grain then, or Ibrāhīm would have prayed for a blessing on that too, and that no one outside Makkah can live on meat and water alone. Ibrāhīm told her to greet her husband and tell him to keep the threshold of his door, and Ismāʿīl understood: that was my father, and he commanded me to keep you.

On a third visit Ibrāhīm found Ismāʿīl near the well of Zamzam, under a tree, mending his arrows. They greeted each other as father and son do, and Ibrāhīm said that his Lord had commanded him to do something and asked whether Ismāʿīl would help. Ismāʿīl said: obey your Lord, and I will help you. Ibrāhīm said that Allah had commanded him to build a house for Him there, pointing to a mound that stood above the ground. The two of them rose and raised the foundations of the House. Ibrāhīm built while Ismāʿīl passed him the stones, and both kept saying, "Our Lord! Accept ˹this˺ from us. You are indeed the All-Hearing, All-Knowing". So they built, part by part, going round the House and repeating that prayer.

### The Quraysh rebuild the Kaʿbah

Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq ibn Yasār relates in his Sīrah that when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was thirty-five years old the Quraysh gathered to rebuild the Kaʿbah and give it a roof, though they were afraid to demolish it. At that time its walls were barely higher than a man's shoulder, and they wanted to raise them and build a ceiling. Its treasure, which used to be kept in a well in the middle of the Kaʿbah, had been stolen beforehand. It was later found with a man called Duwayk, a freed slave of Banū Mulayḥ ibn ʿAmr of Khuzāʿah, and the Quraysh cut off his hand, though some said the real thieves had left it with him. Then a ship belonging to a Roman merchant was wrecked at Jeddah, and they took its timber for the ceiling, a Coptic carpenter in Makkah preparing what they needed. When they decided to begin the demolition, Abū Wahb, descended through ʿAmr, ʿĀʾidh, ʿAbd and ʿImrān from Makhzūm took hold of a stone of the Kaʿbah, and it slipped from his hand and returned to its place. He said: people of Quraysh, spend on rebuilding the House only what has been earned from pure sources. Let no money from a prostitute, from usury or from injustice go into it. Ibn Isḥāq notes that this saying is also ascribed to al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah ibn ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn Makhzūm.

The Quraysh organised the work so that each subtribe was responsible for a particular part, but they still hesitated to pull the building down. Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah said he would begin. He took an axe, standing beside the Kaʿbah, he addressed Allah, no harm is meant, we seek only to do good. Then he began to break away the stones. The people waited through the night, saying that if something struck him they would stop and rebuild it as it had been, and if nothing happened then Allah approved of what they were doing. Next morning al-Walīd went back to work and the others joined him. When they reached the foundations laid by Ibrāhīm they uncovered green stones lying one upon another like a pile of spears. Ibn Isḥāq was told that a man of Quraysh put his shovel between two of these stones to lever them up, and when one of them moved the whole of Makkah shook, so they left those stones undisturbed.

Each tribe collected stones separately and the building rose until it reached the point where the Black Stone had to be set in place. Then a dispute broke out, every tribe wanting the honour for itself, and it nearly came to fighting among the leaders of Quraysh beside the Sacred House. Banū ʿAbd ad-Dār and Banū ʿAdī ibn Kaʿb ibn Luʾayy pledged to each other to fight to the death. After four or five days Abū Umayyah, whose lineage runs through al-Mughīrah, ʿAbdullāh and ʿAmr to Makhzūm, the oldest man of Quraysh, intervened and proposed that the first man to enter through the door of the Mosque should judge between them. They agreed, and the first to enter was Muḥammad ﷺ. When they saw who it was they all said: this is al-Amīn, the trustworthy one. We accept him. This is Muḥammad. When he reached them and heard the dispute, he asked for a cloak and spread it on the ground, placed the Black Stone on it, and had the leader of each tribe hold an edge so that they all lifted it together to its place. Then he took the Stone himself, set it in position and built around it. The Quraysh had called him al-Amīn even before revelation came to him.

### Ibn az-Zubayr, al-Ḥajjāj, and what the Prophet ﷺ had wished

Ibn Isḥāq says that in the Prophet's ﷺ time the Kaʿbah’s height was eighteen cubits, with Egyptian linen as its covering, and later with a striped cloth, and that al-Ḥajjāj ibn Yūsuf was the first to cover it with silk. Quraysh’s building remained until fire damaged it under ʿAbdullāh ibn az-Zubayr, after the year 60 of the Hijrah, at the end of the reign of Yazīd ibn Muʿāwiyah, when Ibn az-Zubayr was besieged in Makkah. He then pulled it down and rebuilt it on the foundations of Ibrāhīm, bringing al-Ḥijr inside it, and gave it an eastern door and a western door at ground level, because he had heard his aunt ʿĀʾishah, the Mother of the Believers, relate that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had wished for this. It remained so until al-Ḥajjāj killed Ibn az-Zubayr and, on the orders of ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Marwān, rebuilt it as it had been before.

Muslim recorded ʿAṭāʾ's account. The House was burned in Yazīd's reign when the people of ash-Shām (Syria) attacked Makkah. Ibn az-Zubayr left it untouched until the pilgrims came, wishing to stir them against the Syrians, and asked the people whether he should pull it down and rebuild it or only repair the damage. Ibn ʿAbbās advised him to leave the stones that had stood when the people became Muslims and the Prophet ﷺ was sent, and to rebuild it as it then was. Ibn az-Zubayr answered that if one of their own houses burned down its owner would not rest until he rebuilt it, so how much more the House of Allah, and said he would seek his Lord's guidance for three days and then act. After three days he resolved to demolish it. People held back, fearing that the first to climb onto it would be struck down, until one man went up and threw down some stones and came to no harm, and then the rest joined in and brought it down to the ground. Ibn az-Zubayr hung curtains from pillars round the site so that the House stayed screened until the walls were up. He then said he had heard ʿĀʾishah relate that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: were it not that your people have only recently left unbelief, and that I lack the money to spend on it, I would have brought five cubits of al-Ḥijr into the House and given it one door for entering and another for leaving. Ibn az-Zubayr said he had the money and did not fear the people. So he added five cubits from al-Ḥijr, which showed clearly as a rear part of the House, raised the walls to eighteen cubits, and thinking that still too low added ten cubits at the front, and made two doors, one to go in by and one to come out by. When he was killed, al-Ḥajjāj wrote to ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Marwān about it. ʿAbd al-Malik replied that he did not approve of what Ibn az-Zubayr had done: the height could stay, but what he had added from al-Ḥijr was to be pulled down and the House rebuilt as before, and the extra door closed. Al-Ḥajjāj did so. An-Nasāʾī in his Sunan collected the Prophet's ﷺ words from ʿĀʾishah, without the rest of the story.

The correct sunnah agreed with what Ibn az-Zubayr did, since it was what the Prophet ﷺ had wished but had held back from, fearing that the hearts of the newly converted could not bear it. This was not clear to ʿAbd al-Malik, and when he learned that ʿĀʾishah had narrated it he said: I wish we had left it as Ibn az-Zubayr made it. Muslim recorded that al-Ḥārith ibn ʿAbdullāh came to ʿAbd al-Malik during his reign, and ʿAbd al-Malik said he had not believed that Abū Khubayb, meaning Ibn az-Zubayr, had really heard from ʿĀʾishah what he claimed. Al-Ḥārith said that he had heard it from her himself: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had told her that her people had rebuilt the House smaller, that had they not been so close to the time of shirk he would have restored what they left out, and that if her people ever thought of rebuilding it he would show her what they had left out, and he showed her about seven cubits. One of the narrators, al-Walīd ibn ʿAṭāʾ, added that the Prophet ﷺ said he would have made two doors at ground level, one east and one west, and asked her whether she knew why her people had raised the door. When she said no, he explained: so that only those they wished could enter, and when a man they did not want climbed up to the door they would push him down. ʿAbd al-Malik asked al-Ḥārith whether he had really heard ʿĀʾishah say this, and when he said yes, ʿAbd al-Malik said: I wish I had left it as it was.

### The Kaʿbah will be destroyed before the Last Hour

The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ foretold Dhū as-Suwayqatayn’s destruction of the Kaʿbah, a man with two thin legs, from Ethiopia. Al-Bukhārī recorded that Ibn ʿAbbās reported the Prophet ﷺ saying: it is as if I can see him now, a thin-legged black man removing the Kaʿbah’s stones individually. The Musnad of Imam Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal records that ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say that Dhū as-Suwayqatayn from Ethiopia will destroy the Kaʿbah, loot its ornaments and strip its covering, and that he could almost see him, bald and thin-legged, striking it with his axe. This will happen after the coming of Gog and Magog, for al-Bukhārī recorded from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said there will still be Hajj and ʿUmrah to the House after Gog and Magog appear.

### What the prayer meant for the world

Allah ordained that a universal centre for the call to Islam be established in the Ḥijāz, and chose Ibrāhīm and his family to found it and provide for its working. The words the two of them spoke while building were prayers, but they were also a sign of their unwavering loyalty to Allah's plan, and prayer of that kind pleases Him most. He accepted it, and Arabia’s barren desert became an enduring fountain of spiritual life for the world. Its name was Islam.

### In short

- Ibrāhīm, helped by Ismāʿīl, raised the walls of the Kaʿbah on its ancient foundations, and the two of them kept praying that Allah accept the work, the mark of sincere believers who fear their deeds may be refused (23:60).
- Al-Bukhārī's account from Ibn ʿAbbās tells how Hājar and the infant were left at the site, how Zamzam sprang up, how Jurhum settled there, how Ismāʿīl married, and how father and son built the House together.
- The Quraysh rebuilt the Kaʿbah when the Prophet ﷺ was thirty-five, and he settled their quarrel over the Black Stone by having every tribe lift it on a cloak and then placing it himself.
- Ibn az-Zubayr later rebuilt it as the Prophet ﷺ had wished, including al-Ḥijr and two doors at ground level, and al-Ḥajjāj restored the Quraysh form, which ʿAbd al-Malik later regretted.
- Near the end of time a thin-legged man from Ethiopia will demolish the Kaʿbah, after the coming of Gog and Magog.

<<<VERSE 2:128>>>
Verse 128 continues the prayer of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) as they built the House. "Our Lord! Make us both ˹fully˺ submit to You and from our descendants a nation that will submit to you. Show us our rituals, and turn to us in grace. You are truly the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful". The prayer springs from the same fear and awe, and the same knowledge, as the request that the building be accepted. Ibrāhīm had spent his whole life in exemplary obedience, yet he prays to be made obedient, because the more a person grows in knowledge of the Divine Majesty, the more he realises that he is not as faithful and obedient as he should be.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Our Lord! Make us both ˹fully˺ submit to You"**. Make us compliant and obedient to You, sincere in not ascribing any partner to You and in worshipping You alone. Ibn Jarīr said that they meant: make us submit to Your command and obedience, associating no one with You in obedience or worship. ʿIkrimah related that Allah answered, "I shall do that." In the devotional reading, "submitting" means yielding to Your decree so fully that every pulse in our veins accords with Your good pleasure.

**"and from our descendants a nation that will submit to you"**. Make of our progeny a people submissive to You. The Arabic "from our descendants" is partitive, some of them, in keeping with Allah's earlier words that His covenant does not reach the wrongdoers. Again ʿIkrimah related the answer, "I shall do that." It is significant that Ibrāhīm included his descendants. The men of Allah, who never hesitate to sacrifice themselves and their children in His way, still love their children deeply, but they know what parental love really requires. Ordinary people suppose that their children's well-being lies in physical health and comfort alone and spend all their care on providing that. Those favoured by Allah are far more anxious about their children's spiritual welfare and about what becomes of them in the next world. There is a further benefit: experience shows that when respected people and their descendants keep to the right path, they are held in esteem and their conduct moves others to reform. Allah heard this prayer too, and among the descendants of Ibrāhīm there has always been a group obedient to Allah and firm on the Straight Path. Even in the Jāhiliyyah, when the whole world and Arabia in particular lay in the darkness of idolatry and shirk, there remained men of his line who believed in the One God and the next world and obeyed Him, such as Zayd ibn ʿAmr ibn Nufayl and Quss ibn Sāʿidah, and it is reported that ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib ibn Hāshim, the Prophet's ﷺ grandfather, shunned idol-worship. This prayer is like the prayer Allah reports from His believing servants who ask for spouses and children who will be a comfort to their eyes and to be made leaders of the righteous (25:74). Such a prayer is permitted, because wanting offspring who worship Allah alone is a sign of complete love for Allah. That is why, when Allah told Ibrāhīm He would make him a leader, he asked at once for his offspring, and the answer that the covenant excludes wrongdoers is explained by his own words elsewhere, "keep me and my children away from the worship of idols" (14:35). Muslim narrated in his Ṣaḥīḥ from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that when a person dies his deeds come to an end except for three: a continuing charity, knowledge that goes on being of benefit, and a righteous child who prays for him. The devotional reading contrasts the one who seeks an heir for his wealth with the one who seeks a deputy to carry on acts of obedience after him. Ibrāhīm asked for successors who would take his place in upholding Allah's rights.

**"Show us our rituals"**. Teach us our ceremonies of worship, the rites of our pilgrimage. Manāsik, the plural of mansik, means the different actions of the Hajj and also the places where they are performed, such as ʿArafah, together with Muzdalifah and Minā, and both meanings are intended. Ibrāhīm wanted the rites explained and their locations pointed out. The verb is arinā, "show us", and seeing is done through the eyes and through the heart, so the sites were shown to him by the archangel Jibrāʾīl (Gabriel) and the rulings of Hajj were explained in detail. Inwardly, the request means that there is no way to know what conforms to Allah's will except by the path He grants, through His giving of success and His communication.

**"and turn to us in grace"**. Relent towards us, overlook our shortcomings and forgive our faults in carrying out what You commanded. They asked Allah to turn to them despite their moral impeccability, out of humility and in order to teach their descendants. In the devotional reading, they asked for this after carrying out all they were commanded so that they would not look at what they had done or left undone, but would turn back to Him from witnessing their own actions, leaving no room for the hidden shirk of imagining that anything came from them or through them.

**"You are truly the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful"**. The One who overlooks shortcomings and turns to those who ask forgiveness, and the One who is merciful towards the believers and compassionate to His creation.

### How Jibrīl showed Ibrāhīm the rites

Saʿīd ibn Manṣūr related, through ʿAttāb ibn Bashīr and Khaṣīf, that Mujāhid said: when Ibrāhīm prayed "show us our rituals", Jibrīl came down, took him to the House and told him to raise its foundations, and Ibrāhīm raised them and completed the building. Then Jibrīl took him by the hand to aṣ-Ṣafā and said that this was among the rituals of Allah, and to al-Marwah and said the same. He took him on to Minā, and at al-ʿAqabah they found Iblīs standing by a tree. Jibrīl told him to say the takbīr, "Allah is the Greatest", and throw pebbles at him, and he did. Iblīs moved to the middle jamrah, and as they passed Jibrīl told him again to say the takbīr and throw, and he threw. The devious Iblīs was trying to add some evil to the rites of Hajj, but he failed. Jibrīl then led him to al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām and to ʿArafāt and asked him three times, "Have you learned (ʿarafta) what I showed you?", and Ibrāhīm said that he had. Similar reports come from Abū Mijlaz and Qatādah.

### The fruit of the prayer

Allah made the people of that desert, the offspring of Ismāʿīl, enthusiastic champions of the cause of Islam and enabled them to carry His word far and wide. Through them He taught mankind how to please their Lord so that He would turn to them in mercy. The final Prophet ﷺ came to Ismāʿīl’s descendants, and for the first time in history the whole prophetic pattern was recorded for posterity, so that later generations could follow it.

### In short

- Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl asked to be made fully submissive and for a submissive nation among their descendants, and Allah answered "I shall do that" to both.
- Praying for righteous offspring is a sign of complete love for Allah (25:74, 14:35), and a righteous child who prays for a parent is one of the three deeds that outlive a person.
- The manāsik are the actions and the places of Hajj. Jibrīl showed them to Ibrāhīm one by one, from aṣ-Ṣafā and al-Marwah to the stoning at Minā and ʿArafāt.
- Asking Allah to turn to them after perfect obedience taught humility and guarded against the hidden shirk of crediting oneself.
