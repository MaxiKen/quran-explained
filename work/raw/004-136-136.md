<<<VERSE 4:136>>>
The call upward: "O believers! Have faith in Allah, His Messenger, the Book He has revealed to His Messenger, and the Scriptures He revealed before" (4:136). After the subsidiary injunctions, with issues of faith, īmān, and of disbelief, kufr, appearing as a corollary to the dealings with antagonists, the sūrah now takes up these issues in detail, almost to its end. The first word commands faith to those who already have it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Have faith"** is no redundancy. Allah commands His faithful servants to hold fast to every element of faith, branch, pillar and cornerstone alike. The command serves the completing of faith and its continual maintenance. The believer already proclaims in every prayer: "Guide us to the straight way" (1:6), which means make us aware of the straight path, increase us in guidance and strengthen us on it. The command to believe in Him and His Messenger stands likewise elsewhere: "O you who believe! Have Taqwa of Allah, and believe in His Messenger" (57:28). One school reads the address with perseverance built in: believe and keep believing. The teaching beneath runs the same: calling oneself a Muslim does not suffice to be held one in the eyes of Allah. A true Muslim is one who has made Allah the centre of his life, with his trust reposed in Him alone, whose belief in the Prophet ﷺ and his guidance excludes all else, whose hold on the revealed Scripture reaches the point that thought and feeling are subordinated to it, to whom belief in the angels means being surrounded by Allah's guardians constantly watching, and whose belief in the Hereafter is so profound that he examines all his words and deeds in its balance.

**"the Book He has revealed to His Messenger"** is the Qur'ān, and **"the Scriptures He revealed before"** are the previously revealed divine Books given to the messengers before him.

**"whoever denies Allah, His angels, His Books, His messengers, and the Last Day has clearly gone far astray"** gives the reverse: deviation from the correct guidance and distance from its path. Whoever falls short of that belief stands astray, whatever his own reckoning of himself as Muslim and believer, while whoever reaches it stands on the straight path to success in the Hereafter.

### The occasion of the call

One record reads the address over the people of an earlier covenant. The verse, it says, came down over a company of Torah believers: ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, Asad and Usayd the two sons of Kaʿb, Thaʿlabah ibn Qays, Salam and Salāmah his nephews, and Yāmīn ibn Yāmīn. Their call ran: believers in Moses and the Torah, now believe in Allah and in His Messenger Muḥammad, in the Scripture revealed to him, and in every Scripture given to the prophets before. When the verse came down, all the people named embraced Islam.

### The two faiths

The inward reading hears two faiths inside the call, the one built on proof and the other on face-to-face vision. The road of inference and the employment of intellectual evidence make the first, while the second is the finding of the day of union and the arrival at its degrees. Read by allusion, the command runs: your faith by proof acquired, now strive for the faith of vision. And what is that? Gazing with the eye of response on the Responder, with the eye of solitariness on the Solitary, with the eye of presence on the Present, being near to Allah's nearness by being far from self, present with His generosity by being absent from self. He is not far from the strivers nor absent from the desirers, for He says: "We are nearer to him than the jugular vein" (50:16). The Pīr of the Ṭarīqah prayed it out:

> O Lord! You are found by the souls of the chevaliers, present to the hearts of the rememberers. They give marks of You up close, but You are far beyond that. They fancy You from afar, but You are closer than the spirit. Lovely idol! Are you then my beloved? Now that I look close, You are my spirit.

Another reading runs the verse as: the assent given, now give the realization. The Sharīʿah accepted, now accept the Ḥaqīqah, the reality. Then come the contrasts of the pair, in the record's own parallel:

> What is the Sharīʿah? What the Ḥaqīqah? The Sharīʿah is a lamp, the Ḥaqīqah a burn. The Sharīʿah is a bond, the Ḥaqīqah advice. The Sharīʿah is need, the Ḥaqīqah joy. The Sharīʿah is the outer pillars, the Ḥaqīqah the inner pillars. The Sharīʿah is not having evil, the Ḥaqīqah is not having self. The Sharīʿah is service based on conditions, the Ḥaqīqah exile based on witnessing. The Sharīʿah is by intermediaries, the Ḥaqīqah by unveiling.

Obedience kept and disobedience left aside is the way of the Sharīʿah's folk. The folk of the Ḥaqīqah flee themselves and take joy in oneness. Everlastingness and subsistent bliss are the hope of the former, while the latter boldly busy themselves with the Cupbearer.

### In short

- Faith is commanded to believers for its completion and maintenance, as guidance is sought in every prayer already received.
- The Qur'ān and the previous Scriptures stand together in the one assent, and denial of any pillar of faith is far straying whatever the self-naming.
- The Torah believers who answered the call, headed by ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, are the occasion.
- Faith by proof is the road, faith by vision the arrival, and the Sharīʿah accepted must lead to the Ḥaqīqah realized, the lamp to the burn.
