<<<VERSE 4:136>>>
The call upward: "O believers! Have faith in Allah, His Messenger, the Book He has revealed to His Messenger, and the Scriptures He revealed before" (4:136). After the subsidiary injunctions, with issues of faith, īmān, and of disbelief, kufr, appearing as a corollary to the dealings with antagonists, the sūrah now takes up these issues in detail, almost to its end. The first word commands faith to those who already have it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Have faith"** is no redundancy. Allah commands His faithful servants to adhere to all the elements of faith, its branches, pillars and cornerstones, and the command is stated for the completing of faith and its continual maintenance. The believer already proclaims in every prayer: "Guide us to the straight way" (1:6), which means make us aware of the straight path, increase us in guidance and strengthen us on it. The command to believe in Him and His Messenger stands likewise elsewhere: "O you who believe! Have Taqwa of Allah, and believe in His Messenger" (57:28). One school reads the address with perseverance built in: believe and keep believing. The teaching beneath runs the same: calling oneself a Muslim does not suffice to be held one in the eyes of Allah. A true Muslim is one who has made Allah the centre of his life, with his trust reposed in Him alone, who believes in the Prophet ﷺ and his guidance to the exclusion of all else, who adheres to the revealed Scripture until his thinking and feeling are subordinated to it, to whom belief in the angels means being surrounded by Allah's guardians constantly watching, and whose belief in the Hereafter is so profound that he examines all his words and deeds in its balance.

**"the Book He has revealed to His Messenger"** is the Qur'ān, and **"the Scriptures He revealed before"** are the previously revealed divine Books given to the messengers before him.

**"whoever denies Allah, His angels, His Books, His messengers, and the Last Day has clearly gone far astray"** gives the reverse: deviation from the correct guidance and distance from its path. One who does not become a believer in the full sense has gone astray, however much he may consider himself a Muslim and a believer, while the one who does is on the straight path to success in the Hereafter.

### The occasion of the call

One record reads the address over the people of an earlier covenant. The verse, it says, was revealed regarding ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, Asad and Usayd the sons of Kaʿb, Thaʿlabah ibn Qays, Salam and Salāmah the nephews of ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, and Yāmīn ibn Yāmīn, believers from among the people of the Torah. To them it was said: O you who believe in Moses and the Torah, believe in Allah and His Messenger Muḥammad, and in the Scripture revealed to him, and in the Scripture revealed aforetime to all the past prophets. When the verse came down, all the people named embraced Islam.

### The two faiths

The inward reading hears two sorts of faith in the call, one based on proof and the other on face-to-face vision. Faith by proof is the road of inference, the employing of the evidence of intellects, while faith by vision is the finding of the day of union and the reaching of the degrees of arrival. Read by allusion, the verse says: O you who have acquired faith by proof, strive to acquire faith by face-to-face vision. And what is that? Gazing with the eye of response on the Responder, with the eye of solitariness on the Solitary, with the eye of presence on the Present, being near to Allah's nearness by being far from self, present with His generosity by being absent from self. He is not far from the strivers nor absent from the desirers, for He says: "We are nearer to him than the jugular vein" (50:16). The Pīr of the Ṭarīqah prayed it out:

> O Lord! You are found by the souls of the chevaliers, present to the hearts of the rememberers. They give marks of You up close, but You are far beyond that. They fancy You from afar, but You are closer than the spirit. Lovely idol! Are you then my beloved? Now that I look close, You are my spirit.

Another reading of the verse runs: O you who have faith by assenting, have faith by realizing. You have accepted the Sharīʿah, now accept the Ḥaqīqah, the reality. Then come the contrasts of the pair, in the record's own parallel:

> What is the Sharīʿah? What the Ḥaqīqah? The Sharīʿah is a lamp, the Ḥaqīqah a burn. The Sharīʿah is a bond, the Ḥaqīqah advice. The Sharīʿah is need, the Ḥaqīqah joy. The Sharīʿah is the outer pillars, the Ḥaqīqah the inner pillars. The Sharīʿah is not having evil, the Ḥaqīqah is not having self. The Sharīʿah is service based on conditions, the Ḥaqīqah exile based on witnessing. The Sharīʿah is by intermediaries, the Ḥaqīqah by unveiling.

Obedience kept and disobedience left aside is the way of the Sharīʿah's folk. The folk of the Ḥaqīqah flee themselves and take joy in oneness. Everlastingness and subsistent bliss are the hope of the former, while the latter boldly busy themselves with the Cupbearer.

### In short

- Faith is commanded to believers for its completion and maintenance, as guidance is sought in every prayer already received.
- The Qur'ān and the previous Scriptures stand together in the one assent, and denial of any pillar of faith is far straying whatever the self-naming.
- The Torah believers who answered the call, headed by ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, are the occasion.
- Faith by proof is the road, faith by vision the arrival, and the Sharīʿah accepted must lead to the Ḥaqīqah realized, the lamp to the burn.
