<<<VERSE 2:146>>>
Verses 144 and 145 contrast inward knowledge with public denial. The People of the Book knew that Allah had commanded the Muslims to face His House. Verse 146 shows that their conduct towards the Prophet ﷺ himself was just as dishonest. "Those We have given the Scripture recognize this ˹Prophet˺ as they recognize their own children. Yet a group of them hides the truth knowingly". The Torah and the Injīl (Gospel) had foretold his coming and set down the signs by which people would know him. Their Sacred Books provided decisive evidence about the promised Last Prophet ﷺ. Jews and Christians recognised him from that evidence, yet many stubbornly refused acknowledgment.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those We have given the Scripture"**. The scholars of the Jews and the Christians, those given knowledge of the Torah. ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his companions are the believing recipients connected with its revelation. They belonged to the People of the Book and knew the Messenger of Allah ﷺ through the description, traits and mission set out in their Scripture.

**"recognize this ˹Prophet˺ as they recognize their own children."**. They know Muḥammad ﷺ by his description and traits as surely as a man knows his own son among other children. The pronoun may also refer to the Kaʿbah at Makkah as the true qiblah. The scholars recognised the truth of the Messenger's ﷺ mission with the same ease as their own children. Arabs use this comparison for something unmistakable. In a hadith, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked a man who had a boy with him whether the boy was his son. The man said yes, and testified to it. The Prophet ﷺ remarked that he would not wrong the boy, nor would the boy wrong him. ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām said that he recognised the Prophet ﷺ the moment he saw him as he would his own son, and that his recognition of Muḥammad ﷺ was the more intense of the two. In al-Qurṭubī's account, ʿUmar asked him whether he knew Muḥammad ﷺ as he knew his own son. He replied: yes, and more, for the Trustworthy One came down from heaven to the Trustworthy One on earth with his description, and by it I recognised him, whereas I know nothing of his mother's story. In another version, ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb asked him how that could be. He answered that he bore witness truly and with certainty that Muḥammad ﷺ is the Messenger of Allah, but could not bear witness with the same certainty that his son was his, since he had no knowledge of what women do. ʿUmar said: may Allah give you success, O son of Salām!

A word on the simile. When a comparison brings two things together it does not involve every aspect of both, only those relevant to the occasion. One should not let the imagination wander into illegitimate cases. The point is simple and obvious: a son grows from infancy to manhood under his parents' eyes, so his face and general appearance are enough for his father to know him without doubt or hesitation. The Jews and the Christians enjoyed the same ease, or even certainty, in recognising the Last Prophet ﷺ. Persisting in denial was therefore as dishonest as a man refusing to recognise his own son.

**"Yet a group of them hides the truth knowingly"**. A party of the People of the Book conceal his description and traits, knowing them to be in their Book, and conceal from people what they find there about him, which is the truth that you, Muḥammad ﷺ, follow. They do it out of envy, fully aware that it is the truth. This was gross dishonesty. Some of them denied the truth themselves and also tried to keep it hidden from others, knowing perfectly well that it had been established by revelation from Allah Himself, including the new ruling on the qiblah.

### Why clear truth is still denied

Whenever Allah reveals a truth to the world it comes supported by clear arguments, so that no one can fail to recognise it as the truth. In this argument, refusing that truth reveals a failure to know Allah. Genuine knowledge of Him would bring recognition of His word. Those who find words to reject the truth imagine they have discovered strong arguments and are standing on firm logical ground. Soon enough they find that those words were imaginary props, devised by themselves to give their egos a false satisfaction. In the devotional reading, their hidden envy breeds haughtiness about what they necessarily know. Whoever has been defeated in the darkness of his lower self throws off the cloak of bashfulness. Censuring him is useless, for words will not rein in his abandon.

### In short

- The scholars of the Jews and Christians knew the Prophet ﷺ from the description in their own Scriptures as surely as a father knows his son among other boys, which is why ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām said his certainty about the Prophet ﷺ exceeded his certainty about his own son.
- The simile makes one point only: recognition without doubt, so denial was deliberate dishonesty.
- A party of them hid his description, and the truth about the qiblah, out of envy, knowing full well it was true.

<<<VERSE 2:147>>>
Verse 147 turns from the deniers to the Prophet ﷺ and his followers. "˹This is˺ the truth from your Lord, so do not ever be one of those who doubt". After exposing the concealment of the People of the Book, Allah strengthens the resolve of His Prophet ﷺ and the believers and affirms that what he came with is the truth beyond any doubt.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹This is˺ the truth from your Lord"**. That you are a Prophet sent by Allah, and that the Kaʿbah at Makkah is the qiblah, is the truth from your Lord. The phrase can also be read in a general sense as a definition of truth itself: only that is truth which comes from Allah. Whoever grasps this can never allow himself to doubt this particular truth, or any other truth Allah has revealed to a prophet.

**"so do not ever be one of those who doubt"**. Do not be among those who waver, who doubt whether they really know. The Arabic says "be not of the doubters", that is, be not of such a disposition, which is more forceful than simply saying "do not waver". Make absolutely certain, O Messenger, that you are not among those who doubt its truth. The address is to the Prophet ﷺ, but the community is intended by it. In the devotional reading: after the suns of certainty have risen for you, do not give way to the results of mere guessing.

### In short

- What the Prophet ﷺ brought, including the qiblah, is the truth from his Lord, and truth by definition is what comes from Allah.
- The command not to be "of the doubters" forbids even the disposition to waver. It is addressed to the Prophet ﷺ and meant for his Ummah.
