The verse turns the charge of madness back on the people who made it. "Have they not ever given it a thought? Their fellow man is not insane. He is only sent with a clear warning"

### Phrase by phrase

Verse 184 answers the false notion of the disbelievers that the Prophet ﷺ was a man of unsound mind or was possessed by a jinn. It puts the answer as a question, and the question is enough.

**"Have they not ever given it a thought?"** is addressed to those who deny the signs. The verse asks a little reflection of them, and says that a little reflection is all that the matter needs.

**"Their fellow man is not insane."** names the companion they knew. There is no madness in Muhammad ﷺ. He is not a sorcerer, a soothsayer or a madman. He is truly the Messenger of Allah, and he calls to the truth. Another passage says the same of him, that the companion of the people is not a madman (81:22).

**"He is only sent with a clear warning."** states what he is. He is a plain warner and a Messenger who explains to them in a language they understand. His warning is clear for those who have a mind and a heart to understand it with, and he stands as a warner to them in the face of a severe torment.

One passage carries the whole appeal, and it is quoted here. The Prophet is told to say that he asks the people for one thing only, that they stand up for the sake of Allah in pairs and singly, and reflect. There is no madness in their companion. He is only a warner to them in the face of a severe punishment (34:46). The standing for Allah is asked in sincerity, without stubbornness and without bias. The pairs and the single ones are individuals and groups, and both are meant. The reflection is asked about the man who brought the message from Allah, whether he is mad. If the people do that, it becomes clear to them that he is the Messenger of Allah in truth and in justice.

The reports draw the point out in the same way. A little reflection on the part of the people is enough to show that the Prophet ﷺ was neither of unsound mind nor under the influence of a jinn. His matchless wisdom and knowledge are an obvious feature of his person. Those with a claim to wisdom and knowledge are left astounded at the charge. They ask how a man whose insight and sagacity are so extraordinary could be accused of madness. To blame him with madness is itself madness. He is only an explainer of realities and a warner against the punishment of Allah.

One account puts the matter as a plain question about the mind. The same point comes as a question to the people who reject the signs of Allah and of His Messenger. Do they never think about things at all? Their own minds, if they used them, would show them plainly that there is no madness in Muhammad. Allah sent him as His messenger, and the warning he gives is clear, and it concerns the punishment of Allah.

### The call on the mountain

The report of Qatadah bin Di'amah gives the setting of the verse. People informed him that the Prophet of Allah stood one day on the mountain of As-Safa and called the Quraysh, subtribe by subtribe. He called out to the children of one family and then to the children of another, and he warned them against the might of Allah and against what He has done with His enemies in the way of punishment.

Some of the people who heard him found a word for the call. They said that this companion of theirs was mad, and that he had kept shouting until the morning. Then Allah sent down this verse.

### The man with a mission

The refusal of the charge has a wider reason behind it. A man with a mission is a man of principle, and his thinking is not conditioned by the things around him. He has risen above the interests of this world, and the truth becomes his only concern. He gives up his life and his property for a goal which appears to yield nothing in this world at all.

That is why the man with a mission is called a madman. The greatest man with a mission among a people was always a prophet of God, and so the prophets of God have been called fanatics in every age. To call a man who carries the religion of God a madman is the greatest of all transgressions, because the message he brings is one that the whole of heaven and earth bears witness to. He calls human beings towards God, who is supremely manifest in the creation of the universe, and he informs them of the Hereafter, a matter which on the earth and in the heaven is as heavy as a full pregnancy in the womb of a woman.

People are not serious about the truth. The man who gives his life for the sake of truth therefore looks to them like a madman. Had they known the real value of the truth, they would never have used that word of him.

### In short

- Verse 184 answers the notion that the Prophet ﷺ was of unsound mind or possessed by a jinn, and it answers it with a question, asking the deniers whether they have ever given the matter thought.
- The companion they knew was Muhammad ﷺ, and there is no madness in him. He is not a sorcerer, a soothsayer or a madman, and another passage says the same, that the people's companion is not a madman (81:22).
- He is a plain warner and a Messenger who explains what he brings in a language the people understand, and he warns them in the face of a severe punishment, in the way the passage from Surah Saba' sets out, where the people are asked to stand up for the sake of Allah in pairs and singly, and to reflect about the man who brought the message (34:46).
- The setting of the verse comes from the report of Qatadah bin Di'amah, in which the Prophet ﷺ stood on As-Safa and called the Quraysh to hear him, subtribe by subtribe, until some of them said that their companion was mad and had gone on shouting until the morning. Then the verse came down.
- The reflection asked for is enough to show that his wisdom and knowledge belong to no man of unsound mind, and the reason the charge is made at all is that a man with a mission, who rises above the interests of this world and gives his life for the truth, appears mad to people who are not serious about it. To call him mad is the greatest of transgressions.
