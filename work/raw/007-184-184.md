<<<VERSE 7:184>>>
Verse 184 answers the deniers' charge that the Prophet ﷺ was mad, or possessed by a jinn: "Have they not ever given it a thought? Their fellow man is not insane. He is only sent with a clear warning". A little reflection would show them that he was neither of unsound mind nor under the influence of jinn.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Have they not ever given it a thought?"** Do those who deny Allah's signs and His Messenger never think among themselves and realise the truth? Had they put their minds to work, they would have seen plainly that Muhammad ﷺ was no madman, soothsayer or sorcerer.

**"Their fellow man is not insane."** There is no trace of madness in their companion, their Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. He is not mad. He is truly the Messenger of Allah, calling them to the truth. Allah says the same elsewhere: "And your fellow man is not insane" (81:22).

**"He is only sent with a clear warning"**: he is a Messenger from Allah, sent to warn plainly of Allah's punishment, explaining things to them in a language they understand. His warning is evident to anyone with a mind and a heart that can understand and grasp it. He is only one who makes realities clear and warns of the punishment of Allah.

### How would reflection clear him?

Allah says in another verse: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “I advise you to do ˹only˺ one thing: stand up for ˹the sake of˺ Allah—individually or in pairs—then reflect. Your fellow man is not insane. He is only a warner to you before ˹the coming of˺ a severe punishment.”" (34:46). The meaning is: I ask you to stand before Allah sincerely, without stubbornness or bias, alone or in groups. Then consider the man who brings you Allah's Message, and ask whether he is mad. Do that, and you will see that he is Allah's Messenger ﷺ, truly and rightly.

His matchless wisdom and knowledge are plain features of his character. Those who claim wisdom and learning are astonished that a man of such extraordinary insight and judgement could be accused of madness. To call him mad is in fact madness itself.

### When was the charge made?

Qatādah ibn Diʿāmah said it was reported that the Prophet of Allah ﷺ once stood on the hill of aṣ-Ṣafā and called out to the Quraysh, clan by clan: "O children of so-and-so, O children of so-and-so!" He warned them of Allah's might and of what He had done to His enemies. Some of them said that this companion of theirs must be mad, for he had kept shouting until morning. Then Allah sent down this verse.

### Why are men with a mission called mad?

Someone devoted to a mission lives by principle. His thinking is free of conditioning. He has risen above every kind of worldly interest, and truth becomes his only concern. He gives his life and property for a goal that seems to yield nothing in this world. That is why such a man is called crazy and out of his mind. A prophet of Allah was the greatest man with a mission of his time, so in every age people have called the prophets fanatics.

To call a missionary of Allah's religion a madman is the greatest of all transgressions. The message he brings is confirmed by all the heavens and the earth. He calls humankind to Allah, who is supremely evident in His creation throughout the universe. He tells people of the Hereafter, which weighs on the heavens and the earth like a pregnancy come to term in a woman's womb. But people do not take the truth seriously, so a man who gives his life for it seems mad to them. Had they known the real worth of truth, they would never have used the word "mad".

### In short

- The deniers called the Prophet ﷺ mad, as some of the Quraysh did after he warned them from aṣ-Ṣafā, but a little honest reflection would clear him.
- He was not insane or possessed but a clear warner, whose wisdom made the charge itself a kind of madness.
- Men devoted to a mission are often called mad by those who do not value truth, and so it was with every prophet.
