<<<VERSE 5:68>>>
"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O People of the Book! You have nothing to stand on unless you observe the Torah, the Gospel, and what has been revealed to you from your Lord.” And your Lord's revelation to you ˹O Prophet˺ will only cause many of them to increase in wickedness and disbelief. So do not grieve for the people who disbelieve"

This verse tells the People of the Book that they have no real footing with God unless they keep the Torah, the Gospel and what has been revealed to the Prophet ﷺ from his Lord. It then warns the Prophet ﷺ that the revelation will increase the wickedness of many of them, and tells him not to grieve over them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You have nothing to stand on unless you observe the Torah, the Gospel."** The material explains that the phrase means that there is no true religion for them unless they act on their books. The Torah and the Injil command the people to follow the Prophet ﷺ and believe in his prophecy, and so keeping them means following him.

**"And what has been revealed to you from your Lord."** The material notes that the verse does not simply name the Qur'an, but speaks of what has been revealed to the Prophet ﷺ. The Companions and the Successors read this as the Qur'an, which was sent to the whole community of the faith, including the Jews and Christians. The material also reads the wording as covering the other revelation the Prophet ﷺ received, which is the explanation of the Qur'an. A hadith recorded by Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah says that the Prophet ﷺ was given the Qur'an and something like it, and that a man will one day say the Qur'an is enough, and will take as halal only what the Qur'an calls halal and as haram only what it calls haram, though what the Messenger declares haram is like what Allah declares haram.

**"Will only cause many of them to increase in wickedness and disbelief."** The material explains that the revelation is a blessing for the believers, and for the disbelievers it increases their envy and denial. The Prophet ﷺ is told not to be sad at this.

**"So do not grieve for the people who disbelieve."** The material says this is a comfort to the Prophet ﷺ and the believers. Their disbelief is not their fault and does not reduce his mission.

### Three kinds of rulings

The material explains that the rulings of the Prophet ﷺ come in three kinds. The first are those stated in the Qur'an. The second are those revealed to him through a separate revelation that is not recited. The third are those he gave by ijtihad and qiyas, when no revelation came against them. All three are binding, and they are all included in what has been revealed to him. The material says that the rulings of the earlier books were replaced in part by the later ones, so following the replacing ruling is itself obedience to both books.

### The lesson on the path

The material draws a lesson for the believers. No lineage, no intellect, no spiritual exertion and no inner illumination can bring salvation without adherence to the Shari'ah. The spiritual paths and the discipline of the heart are of no benefit unless they stand within the law of Allah.

### In short

- The People of the Book have no true footing unless they keep the Torah, the Gospel and the revelation to the Prophet ﷺ.
- The revelation is a blessing that increases the faith of the believers and the envy of the disbelievers.
- The Prophet ﷺ is told not to grieve over those who reject the message.

<<<VERSE 5:69>>>
"Indeed, the believers, Jews, Sabians and Christians—whoever ˹truly˺ believes in Allah and the Last Day and does good, there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve"

This verse names four communities and promises that whoever among them truly believes in Allah and the Last Day and does good will have no fear and no grief. The material reads the promise as one that depends on faith in the Prophet ﷺ and his law, not on the name of the community.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, the believers, Jews, Sabians and Christians."** The material explains that the believers are the Muslims. The Jews are those who were given the Torah. The Christians are those who were given the Injil. The Sabians are a sect that is hard to identify. The material reports from Mujahid that they were a sect of Christians and Magians with no fixed religion, and from Qatadah that they worshipped angels, prayed facing away from the qiblah, and recited the Zabur of Dawud. The material notes that the four Scriptures named in the Qur'an are the Torah, the Zabur, the Injil and the Qur'an, and that the four communities are linked to these books.

**"Whoever truly believes in Allah and the Last Day and does good."** The material says the three parts of faith are belief in Allah, belief in the Last Day, and righteous deeds. The verse does not list every article of faith, since it is not the place to do so. It points to the basic beliefs and invites people to the rest.

**"There will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve."** The material says that whoever takes to obedience, faith and good deeds, whatever he was before, will be acceptable to Allah. The sins and shortcomings of the past are forgiven, and in the Hereafter there is no fear or grief.

### The belief in the Messenger

The material explains that the verse does not mention belief in the Prophet ﷺ. This does not mean it is not required. It cites 2:285, which says that the believers have faith in Allah, His angels, His books and His messengers, and that they make no difference between the messengers. It cites 4:150–151, which condemns those who say they believe in some messengers and reject others. The material reports the saying of the Prophet ﷺ that if Musa were alive, he would have had no choice but to follow him.

The material also argues that the earlier verse about the Companions shows that true belief in Allah includes belief in the messenger. It cites 2:137, which says that if people believe in the same as the prophets believe in, they are on the right path. The material says that the faith of the Companions had belief in the Messenger as a great pillar, and so belief in Allah includes belief in him.

The material says that if any religion were enough for salvation without the Prophet ﷺ, then the sending of the last prophet and the Qur'an would have been unnecessary. It also argues that the Prophet ﷺ fought against the Jews and Christians who rejected him, which would be unjustified otherwise. It cites 38:79, in which Iblis confirms his faith in the Last Day, and asks why he is cursed if belief in Allah and the Last Day were sufficient. The material says the error comes from thinking religion can be given as a gift, and that relations with non-Muslims should be based on tolerance, mercy and justice, while the limits of the religion are kept.

### The earlier verse of 2:62

The material cites 2:62, which has almost the same words. The only difference is that the place of certain words is changed. The message is the same in both places, and it shows that the promise is open to every one of these communities if they believe and do good.

### In short

- Four communities are named, and the promise is open to whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day and does good.
- True faith includes belief in the Prophet ﷺ, as 2:285, 4:150-151 and 2:137 show.
- Anyone who turns to the faith, whatever he was before, has no fear and no grief in the Hereafter.
