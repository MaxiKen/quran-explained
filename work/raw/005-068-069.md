<<<VERSE 5:68>>>
The People of the Book are told what their position actually rests on: “You have nothing to stand on unless you observe the Torah, the Gospel, and what has been revealed to you from your Lord.” The claim to a religion is not established by ancestry, by possessing books or by spiritual reputation. It is established by acting on what those books require.

The verse then consoles the Messenger ﷺ. Revelation increases many of them in wickedness and disbelief, and that is not a reason for grief.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O People of the Book!"** addresses Jews and Christians through the Messenger ﷺ. The instruction to say shows that the statement is to be delivered openly, not merely held.

**"You have nothing to stand on unless you observe the Torah, the Gospel"** means they have no real religion until they adhere to and implement those books. One gloss explains it as truly believing in what they contain, and another as having no basis in religion to rely upon.

**"and what has been revealed to you from your Lord.”"** is understood by the great majority, including the Companions and their successors, as the Qur'an. It was sent to the whole community invited to faith, including Jews and Christians, through the Prophet ﷺ.

The books they already hold command following Muhammad ﷺ and believing in his prophethood while adhering to his law. Observing the Torah and the Gospel therefore leads to the Qur'an rather than away from it. One gloss adds that what was revealed to him concerns all the scriptures given to all the messengers.

**"And your Lord’s revelation to you ˹O Prophet˺ will only cause many of them to increase in wickedness and disbelief."** repeats the effect described earlier in this chapter. Their disbelief and envy turn the blessing given to him into an increase of rebellion in them.

**"So do not grieve for the people who disbelieve"** consoles him. He is not to be saddened or taken aback by their disbelief, nor concerned with them if they refuse. One explanation adds that in the believers who follow him there is goodness enough.

### What would be wasted without obedience to the law?

They had been given high privileges. They were the descendants of prophets. They possessed the intellectual treasure of the Torah and the Injil. Many men of Allah among them had undergone severe spiritual exertion in searching for truth.

All of it depends on one binding condition, which is following the divine shariah. Without that, no superiority of lineage works, and neither intellectual excellence nor spiritual striving becomes a means of salvation. If they do not adhere, their achievements stand wasted.

### Does the same rule apply to Muslims?

The statement is read as crucial guidance for Muslims too. No way of the dervishes, no path of spiritual realisation, no repeated striving toward spiritual goals, no extra-sensory illumination and no visitation of inspiration becomes a source of spiritual achievement or salvation in Allah's sight without full adherence to the shariah.

Inward attainment is therefore not an alternative to the revealed law. It is a fruit of it. The verse's demand for implementation applies to whoever claims a religion, and the claim of inner experience does not excuse neglect of the commands.

### Why is the third item named at length?

The Torah and the Injil are named briefly, while the third is described as what has been revealed from your Lord. One explanation takes the longer wording as pointing to the hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ said that he had been given the Qur'an and, along with it, other fields of knowledge like it.

He warned that in later times a sated man reclining on his couch would say that the Qur'an alone is sufficient, taking as lawful what it makes lawful and as unlawful what it makes unlawful. The answer given is that what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ has declared unlawful is like what Allah has declared unlawful. Abu Dawud, Ibn Majah and Ad-Darimi record the hadith.

On this reading the longer phrase indicates that following every injunction given in the Qur'an or given by the Prophet ﷺ is equally mandatory.

### How many kinds of ruling are included?

The discussion divides the rulings given to the ummah into three. First are those stated clearly in the Qur'an. Second are those not stated clearly in it but revealed to the Prophet ﷺ through a separate revelation that is not recited as scripture.

Third are commands he gave on the basis of his own ijtihad, fully deliberated judgment, and qiyas, analogical deduction, where no contrary guidance was subsequently revealed. Those also carry the force of a revealed injunction.

The Qur'an itself testifies to this arrangement by stating that he does not speak from himself, and that what he says is revealed to him. That passage is referred to without a verse number in the account, so no citation is added here.

All three kinds are obligatory to follow, and all fall within the phrase about what has been revealed from your Lord. The distinction matters because it explains why the verse does not simply name the Qur'an and stop there.

### How can three books be followed when some replace others?

The objection is obvious. The Injil replaced some injunctions of the Torah, and the Qur'an replaced many injunctions of both. How then can the sum of the three be acted upon?

The answer is that each incoming book changed certain rulings of the one before it, so following the later ruling is itself acting in accordance with both books. Both require that the replacement be followed. Acting on an abrogated ruling goes against the requirement of both.

Obedience to the earlier books is therefore not preserved by refusing the Qur'an. It is completed by it, since they themselves direct their readers to the messenger whose coming they announce.

### What does wishful thinking do to a community?

The Jewish community is described as thriving on stories of its own excellence and the supposed holiness of its elders, while not subjecting itself and its personal affairs to Allah's will. Wishful thinking convinced them of salvation before Allah, but that conviction had no value with Him because they were not ruled by His religion.

What carries weight with Allah is scrupulous obedience to His commandments and founding one's life on His religion. People given to false hopes react strongly when told that intentions and deeds carry weight rather than wishes. In that call they see the demolition of their castles in the air, and it becomes a trial for them.

Their self-centredness, hidden under showy devotion, then comes into the open. Instead of drawing divine nourishment from the call to truth, they are driven by it toward disbelief and arrogance. That is the mechanism behind the increase mentioned in this verse.

### In short

- Their standing depends on implementing the Torah, the Gospel and the revelation given to the Prophet ﷺ, not on lineage, learning or spiritual claims.
- The third item is understood as the Qur'an, and the longer wording is connected with the obligation of following what the Messenger ﷺ declared.
- Rulings stated in the Qur'an, revealed to him without recitation, and given through his deliberated judgment all bind the community.
- Following a later revealed ruling is obedience to the earlier books too, since they announce it.
- The Messenger ﷺ is consoled, because revelation increases the resentful in rejection while wishful thinking hardens them.

<<<VERSE 5:69>>>
Four communities are named together and one condition is stated: "whoever ˹truly˺ believes in Allah and the Last Day and does good, there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve." The promise is wide, and its condition is precise. Lineage, homeland and communal name carry no weight beside it.

The explanation of this verse therefore has two parts. It establishes that distinctions of community do not save anyone by themselves, and it explains what belief in Allah actually requires after the final revelation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, the believers, Jews, Sabians and Christians"** addresses four traditional religious communities. The believers are the Muslims, the Jews were entrusted with the Torah, and the Christians were entrusted with the Injil and are known.

The Sabians are less clearly identified, since no community of that name exists today and scholarly opinion varies. Quoting Qatadah, one report describes them as people who worshipped angels, prayed in a direction other than the qiblah and recited the Zabur revealed to Dawud (David). The Qur'anic context is said to support this, because the four scriptures it names are the Torah, the Zabur, the Injil and the Qur'an, so the four groups here are the believers in those books.

Another gloss describes them as a Christian sect milder than other Christians, and a third as a sect among the Jews. These differing identifications are preserved rather than settled by assertion. The Christians named include those of Najran as well as others.

**"whoever ˹truly˺ believes in Allah and the Last Day and does good"** states the condition for each of them. Belief in the Last Day means belief in resurrection after death and in the Day of Judgment and reckoning.

Good action is not left undefined. The explanation says that deeds count as good only if they conform to the law of Muhammad ﷺ, after his being sent to all mankind and the jinn. Another gloss adds repentance from the previous path and doing right sincerely between a person and his Lord.

**"there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve"** promises freedom from fear of what is coming and from sorrow over what was lost. One account describes believers in Musa (Moses) and in all the prophets and scriptures who die in that state. Other glosses say they will not fear when others fear, or will not grieve when death is slain and the Fire is closed.

### Where else does the Qur'an say this?

An almost identical statement appears in Al-Baqarah, promising such people their reward with their Lord, with no fear and no grief (2:62). Apart from a transposition of some words according to place, there is no difference between the two.

The repetition shows that this is not an isolated remark. It is a settled principle about who is acceptable to Allah.

### What does the principle establish?

Distinctions of lineage, homeland and nationality do not matter in Allah's sight. Anyone who takes complete obedience, faith and good deeds as his way of life, whatever he was before, will find himself acceptable to Allah, and his devotion will bring the best appreciation from his Creator.

That is why the verse can address four communities with one condition. It removes salvation from group membership and places it in belief and conduct.

### What does complete obedience mean after the Qur'an?

The explanation is explicit. Complete obedience after the revelation of the Qur'an depends on being a Muslim, because the past scriptures themselves contain instructions to that effect and the Qur'an was revealed for this purpose.

After the Qur'an and the appearance of the last of the prophets, it is not correct to follow the Torah, the Injil or the Zabur without believing in the Qur'an and having faith in the Prophet ﷺ. The verse therefore means that whoever from among these communities becomes a Muslim becomes deserving of salvation and reward in the Hereafter.

The doubt that naturally follows is answered at once. What happens to their past sins, disobedience, disbelief and plotting against Islam and Muslims? All past sins and shortcomings stand forgiven, and in the life to come they will have no fear and no grief.

### Why are Muslims mentioned when the aim is to invite others?

Someone might object that the occasion calls for mentioning only those who need persuading to enter Islam, since Muslims already stand where the verse points. The answer given is that this is a special mode of eloquence in the Qur'an.

It is compared with a law issued by the highest authority stating that it applies to everyone, whether in favour of it or opposed to it, bringing reward for the obedient and punishment for the disobedient. Those already in favour are obedient, and the purpose of naming them is to make the opposing camp hear.

The particular wisdom is that the obedient are told why they are appreciated. It is not because of any personal or group excellence of theirs, but simply because of their obedience to their Creator. If the dissenter takes the same path of obedience, he deserves the same grace and reward.

### What three things does the address require?

The instruction is divided into belief in Allah, belief in the Last Day and good deeds. The verse does not intend to give the entire detail of the articles of faith, and there is no occasion for it here.

By mentioning some basic beliefs, the aim is to point to all of them and invite people to them. It is not necessary that every verse mentioning faith should list its details at that point.

### Why does the absence of an explicit mention of prophethood not create a loophole?

The absence of an emphasised mention of belief in the Messenger ﷺ at this place should not have given anyone room for doubt. The whole Qur'an, and hundreds of its verses, are full of clear statements about belief in the message.

There are plain assertions that no salvation exists without believing in the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and in what he said, completely, and that no belief or action is acceptable or valid without it. A group insisting on pushing its own ideas into the Qur'an has nevertheless produced a theory from the silence of this single verse.

That theory holds that any person, whatever his religion, whether Jew, Christian or even an idol worshipper, could deserve salvation in the Hereafter if he believes only in Allah and the Last Day and does good deeds, and that entry into Islam is not necessary for final salvation. The discussion answers it with a plea for Allah's protection from such a claim, and then with the Qur'an itself.

### What do the other verses say?

The place where the articles of faith are detailed is at the end of Al-Baqarah, where all are described as believing in Allah, His angels, His Books and His Messengers, making no distinction between any of His Messengers (2:285). Believing in only one or some of the messengers is therefore insufficient. Belief in all of them is a binding condition, and whoever disbelieves in even a single messenger has no valid or acceptable faith.

Another passage describes those who disbelieve in Allah and His messengers and want to separate Allah from His messengers, saying that they believe in some and disbelieve in some and want a way between the two. Those are the disbelievers in truth (4:150-151).

The Prophet ﷺ is also reported as saying that had Musa been alive, he would have had no option but to follow him. That statement removes the possibility of an independent continuing path for an earlier law after his coming.

### What would the theory make meaningless?

If the followers of every religion could keep acting according to their own religions and find Paradise without believing in the Prophet ﷺ and without becoming Muslims, the verses just cited would be disregarded outright.

More follows. If acting on any religion in any age were sufficient for ultimate success, the coming of the last of the prophets and the revelation of the Qur'an would become pointless, and sending one shariah after another would be redundant. The first messenger's book and law would have been enough.

At most it would have been necessary for people to preserve that law and book, practise them and persuade others to do the same, which has been the duty of the scholars of every community. In that situation the statement that Allah made a way and a method for each of them (5:48) would lose its meaning.

The discussion adds a further argument from history. The Prophet ﷺ carried out jihad against Jews, Christians and others who did not believe in him and in the Book revealed to him, and fought them on battlefields. What justification would remain for that if their own path were already sufficient for salvation?

### Why is Satan not saved by believing in Allah and the Last Day?

The argument is put as a question. If faith in Allah and the Last Day alone made a person a true believer acceptable to Allah, why is Iblis, Satan, cursed? Did he not believe in Allah? Was he a denier of the Last Day?

He was not. Even in his anger he confirmed the Last Day by asking for respite until the Day when the dead are raised (38:79). His ruin therefore cannot be explained by absence of those two beliefs. What he lacked was submission to the command.

That is the point the theory misses. Belief is not a list of propositions held while obedience is refused. It is acceptance of what Allah has said, including what He has said through His Messenger ﷺ.

### Does this verse itself point to belief in the Messenger ﷺ?

The discussion answers that it does. In Qur'anic usage, only that belief in Allah is valid which includes belief in everything Allah has told. The Qur'an makes its terminology clear where it says that if they believe in the like of what you believe in, they have certainly found the right path (2:137).

That means the faith of the Companions is the only faith deserving the name of belief in Allah. Belief in the Messenger ﷺ was a great pillar of their faith. The words whoever believes in Allah therefore include belief in His Messenger ﷺ.

### Where does the error come from?

The discussion traces it to a notion that religion can be handed over as a gift on a silver plate, as gifts are given at weddings. In a modern international setting, religion can be used to build bonds with other nations.

The Qur'an is described as saying openly that relations with non-Muslims should be based on tolerance, sympathy, favour, charity and mercy. That must be done while ensuring that the limits of the religion are not crossed and its frontiers remain guarded.

Courtesy and goodwill are therefore required. They are not a reason to redefine what salvation requires. The verse's own promise stands, and its condition is belief in Allah, belief in the Last Day and good action conforming to the law now in force.

### What does upholding the Book of Allah mean in practice?

Upholding Allah's Book is described as having full faith in Him, being overwhelmed by fear of the Hereafter and leading a life of righteous character among other human beings. This is the true religion, and everybody is required to adopt it in his way of living.

A community blessed with the Book of Allah has great merit in the world so long as its members adopt that true religion. On deviating from it they become wholly undeserving before Allah, described as even less meritorious than open deniers and polytheists.

The reason given is that generations succeeding the early followers of the ancient prophets gradually took the shape of a regular group. They no longer followed the examples set by the prophets but perpetuated legends praising past glories and supremacy. Each group came to regard itself as the best, certain of salvation and highest in status before Allah.

Such group religions have no value in Allah's eyes. Every individual's case will be presented separately, and the decision about his future will rest wholly on his own deeds and on no other basis. That is the sentence this verse pronounces, and it applies to whoever hears it.

### In short

- Four communities are addressed with one condition: belief in Allah and the Last Day, and good action.
- The Sabians are identified differently in the reports, and the differing views are preserved.
- After the Qur'an, complete obedience means becoming Muslim, and past sins are forgiven for those who do.
- Muslims are named so that the obedient learn why they are accepted and the opposed hear the same law.
- Other verses, the hadith about Musa, the meaning of successive laws, the record of jihad and the case of Satan all show that belief in Allah includes belief in His Messenger ﷺ.
- Every individual's case is judged on his own deeds, not on group membership or inherited legends.
