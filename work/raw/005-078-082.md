<<<VERSE 5:78>>>
A long record is summarised in one sentence: "The disbelievers among the Children of Israel were condemned in the revelations of David and Jesus, son of Mary." The condemnation is not new. It was pronounced through two prophets and is repeated here so that later people will not imitate the conduct that earned it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The disbelievers among the Children of Israel were condemned"** describes a curse pronounced long ago and revealed through Dawud (David) and 'Isa (Jesus), son of Maryam. In the report 'Ali bin Abi Talhah gives from Ibn 'Abbas, they were cursed in the Torah, the Injil, the Zabur, that is the Psalms, and the Furqan, that is the Qur'an.

Another explanation says Allah distanced the disbelievers among them from His mercy in the scripture He gave Dawud, the Psalms, and in the scripture He gave 'Isa, the Gospel. The condemnation is therefore attested in more than one revelation, not only in this chapter.

**"in the revelations of David and Jesus, son of Mary."** is explained through their tongues and their supplications. One account says that through Dawud's supplication some became apes and through the supplication of 'Isa some became swine. Another identifies those transformed into apes as the people of Aylah and those turned into pigs as the people who ate at the table sent down from heaven.

A third account reverses the pairing, describing the curse through Dawud as producing swine and that through 'Isa as producing monkeys. These differing reports are preserved as they stand, since the material does not settle which prophet's supplication is connected with which transformation.

**"That was for their disobedience and violations"** gives the reason. They disobeyed Allah and transgressed against His creatures. Specific acts named include rebellion concerning the Sabbath and what followed eating from the table sent from heaven, killing the prophets, and declaring their own indulgence in sin lawful.

### Which prophets pronounced this condemnation?

Two are named here. Some scholars observe that the mention of two prophets follows from the context, while the fact is that the pronouncement began with Musa (Moses) and ended with the last of the prophets ﷺ. On that account four prophets in succession spoke this condemnation against those hostile to the prophets or guilty of making them sharers in divine attributes.

The naming of two therefore does not limit the history. It identifies the two revelations whose words are being recalled at this point.

### Why is the condemnation recalled here?

It describes the sad end of those among Bani Isra'il who fell into the excess and deficiency discussed in the preceding verses. Their conduct is recorded so that it will not be imitated.

The central failure named in the next verse is that they did not stop one another from evil. Many hadith command enjoining righteousness and forbidding evil, recorded by Ahmad, Muslim, At-Tirmidhi, Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah, and the principal ones are given with the following verse.

### In short

- The condemnation was pronounced through Dawud and 'Isa and is attested in the Torah, the Injil, the Psalms and the Qur'an.
- Reports differ over which supplication produced which transformation, and the differences are preserved.
- The causes named are disobedience, transgression, violation of the Sabbath, killing prophets and making sin lawful.
- The pronouncement began with Musa and continued to the last of the prophets ﷺ, even though two are named here.
- The record is given so that the conduct will not be imitated.

<<<VERSE 5:79>>>
The specific failure is named: "They did not forbid one another from doing evil." Sins were committed openly because nobody objected. The verse then passes judgment on that silence: "Evil indeed was what they did".

### Phrase by phrase

**"They did not forbid one another from doing evil."** They did not restrain each other from sins and prohibited acts. One gloss explains it as not restraining one another and not repenting from the wickedness they did. Another says one person would not forbid another from repeating an indecency already committed.

**"Evil indeed was what they did"** condemns both the sins and the silence. One explanation singles out the failure to forbid as itself the evil deed, since offenders acted openly because no one disapproved.

Allah chastised them for this behaviour so that it would not be imitated. The condemnation is therefore directed at a habit of a community, not only at individual crimes.

### What do the hadith say about forbidding evil?

Ahmad records Hudhayfah bin Al-Yaman reporting the Prophet ﷺ as swearing by the One in whose hand is his soul that they must enjoin righteousness and forbid evil, or Allah will send a punishment upon them from Himself. They would then supplicate to Him, and their supplication would not be accepted. At-Tirmidhi also records it and calls it hasan, good.

Muslim records Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri reporting the Messenger ﷺ as saying that whoever witnesses an evil should change it with his hand, and if he cannot, then with his tongue, and if he cannot even that, then with his heart, which is the weakest faith.

Abu Dawud records Al-'Urs, meaning Ibn 'Amirah, reporting that when sin is committed on the earth, whoever witnesses it and hates it, and in one wording forbids it, will be like one who did not witness it, and whoever was away from it yet approved of it stands with those who saw it. Only Abu Dawud records this hadith.

Abu Dawud also records a Companion reporting the Prophet ﷺ as saying that people will not perish until they leave, or have, no excuse for themselves.

Ibn Majah records Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri describing the Messenger ﷺ addressing the people and saying that fear of people must not prevent anyone from stating the truth he knows. Abu Sa'id then wept and said that by Allah they had seen errors and had been afraid of people.

Another report from Abu Sa'id gives the Messenger ﷺ as saying that the best jihad is a word of truth spoken before an unjust ruler. Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah record it, and At-Tirmidhi calls it hasan gharib, good and singly transmitted, through this route.

Ahmad records Hudhayfah reporting that a Muslim is not required to humiliate himself. Asked how a person humiliates himself, he said it is by taking on trials he is not capable of enduring. At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah record it, and At-Tirmidhi calls it hasan sahih gharib.

Taken together these reports set a graded duty, from action to speech to the heart, and at the same time forbid a person from loading himself with trials he cannot bear. The two limits belong to the same instruction.

### How does a community come to this silence?

One account explains deviation as arising when a people are impressed by the thinking of communities that have gone astray and try to imitate them. Accepting Allah's religion, they interpret it so that it appears consistent with current thinking, and in the name of Allah's religion they adopt an ungodly one.

Christians are described as adapting their religion to the ideas of polytheistic communities and presenting it as the religion Allah favours. A second type of deviation occurs when religion is cast in the mould of national aspirations, and the Jews are given as the example, interpreting Allah's religion so that it legitimised their worldliness.

Muslims have no scope for inserting such interpretations into the text of Allah's Book. Outside the text, however, they remain free to follow the example of earlier communities. The warning is therefore not only historical.

### What does faith do to a person's sensitivity?

Faith makes a person sensitive to transgression and evil. When he sees someone committing it, he is disturbed and immediately wants to stop him. He relates to evil people through separation rather than friendship.

When faith weakens, his sensitivity narrows to himself. Only what damages his own interests appears evil to him, and he stays neutral about evils directed at others. That narrowing is the beginning of the condition this verse condemns.

### In short

- The evil named is both the sins committed and the failure to forbid them.
- The hadith set a graded duty of hand, tongue and heart, and warn that silence can bring a punishment after which supplication is not accepted.
- Speaking truth before an unjust ruler is called the best jihad, while a Muslim must not take on trials he cannot bear.
- Deviation comes from reshaping religion to fit current thinking or national ambition.
- Weakening faith narrows a person's sense of evil to what touches his own interests.

<<<VERSE 5:80>>>
The next charge concerns alliances: "You see many of them taking the disbelievers as allies. Truly wicked are their misdeeds". The verse identifies what they sent ahead for themselves, the wrath it earned and the lasting torment that follows.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You see many of them taking the disbelievers as allies."** Mujahid says the verse refers to the hypocrites. One gloss describes many hypocrites seeking assistance and support from disbelievers, naming Ka'b and his followers, while another reading describes many Jews such as Ka'b and his followers befriending the disbelievers of Makkah, Abu Sufyan and his followers.

Another explanation says they affiliated with the disbelievers among the Makkans out of spite for the Prophet ﷺ. A further account describes disbelieving Jews allying with idolaters and opposing the Prophet ﷺ and those who believe in Allah's oneness.

**"Truly wicked are their misdeeds"** describes what their souls sent forward for the day of their inevitable return. The evil is that they gave loyalty and support to disbelievers instead of believers.

**"which have earned them Allah’s wrath."** That act produced hypocrisy in their hearts and brought Allah's anger, which remains with them until the Day of Return.

**"And they will be in everlasting torment"** states the end. They will abide in torment on the Day of Resurrection, described in one gloss as never dying and never being removed.

### Why is this alliance so damaging?

The cultivation of deep friendship with disbelievers is prohibited because its outcome is disastrous. The discussion suggests it may also indicate that the deviation of Bani Isra'il resulted from close friendly collaboration with disbelievers, which harmed their conduct and in the end brought them down into the abyss.

Alliance is therefore not treated as a neutral social arrangement. It shapes belief over time, and the shaping is what the verse warns against.

### What did their own prophets say about them?

The Children of Israel faced this downfall, yet they continued to make self-righteous statements, and their chosen speakers went on making fine speeches. They were not serious about rushing to intervene when they saw tyranny and evil, or about preventing it.

Dawud said of the Children of Israel of his own period that not one of them was righteous. From his discourses it is clear that they used to speak of peace with their neighbours, describing Allah's law and reaffirming their pledge to Him, while their hearts were full of ill intent.

'Isa likewise denounced the Jews of his time, calling them hypocritical scholars who had taken over the dwellings of widows while prolonging their prayers. He lamented that they paid tithes of mint, anise and cumin while neglecting the weighty provisions of the revealed law, namely justice, mercy and faith.

He named them blind guides, straining out a gnat and swallowing a camel, and scorned them for appearing righteous while being wholly hypocritical and faithless within. This is reported as recorded in the Gospel of Matthew.

They did what 'Isa described. They recounted Allah's law, offered long prayers and paid tithes from their crops, but all of it was lip service. They performed harmless commands with a show of piety, and when the question was treating others justly, showing mercy to a downtrodden person or crushing self-interest to carry out Allah's command, they turned back.

If anyone pointed out their mistakes, they became his enemy. It was such behaviour that brought upon them divine wrath and curses.

### In short

- The alliance described is with disbelievers, whether the hypocrites seeking support or Jews allying with the Makkans.
- What they sent forward earned wrath that lasts until the Day of Return and torment that does not end.
- Deep friendship with opponents of the faith is prohibited because it reshapes belief and led, in this account, to the fall of Bani Isra'il.
- Their own prophets described fine speech without intervention, tithes without justice, and enmity toward whoever corrected them.

<<<VERSE 5:81>>>
The verse states what sincere faith would have prevented: "Had they believed in Allah, the Prophet, and what has been revealed to him, they would have never taken those ˹pagans˺ as allies." Belief and this alliance are presented as incompatible, not as two separate matters.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Had they believed in Allah, the Prophet, and what has been revealed to him"** describes sincere belief in Allah, His Messenger ﷺ and the Qur'an. One gloss explains it as genuine belief in Allah and in His oneness, in the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and in what was revealed to him.

**"they would have never taken those ˹pagans˺ as allies."** Such belief would have prevented the wrong of backing disbelievers in secret and treating as enemies the people who hold faith in Allah, in His Messenger ﷺ and in what came down to him. One reading describes the hypocrites not choosing the Jews as friends for assistance, and another describes the Jews not taking Abu Sufyan and his followers as friends.

**"But most of them are rebellious"** describes disobedience to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ and defiance of the revealed verses. Some versions of the material read many rather than most, and many among the People of the Book are described as disbelievers or hypocrites, wicked and rebellious against faith. Another account says most of them do not obey Allah and do not side with Him and the believers.

### What does the conditional form show?

It connects inward belief with outward alignment. The verse does not treat alliance as a practical matter separate from faith. If the belief were genuine, the alliance would not exist.

That is why the preceding verses move from their misdeeds to their alliances. The alliance is the visible expression of the belief they actually hold, and the hypothetical statement exposes the contradiction they would rather not face.

### In short

- Sincere belief in Allah, the Prophet ﷺ and the revelation would have made this alliance impossible.
- The glosses differ over whether the hypocrites or the Jews are meant, and both readings are preserved.
- Most of them are described as rebellious, disobedient and defiant of the revealed verses.
- The conditional form ties inward faith to outward alignment rather than treating them separately.

<<<VERSE 5:82>>>
The verse compares two attitudes toward the believers. It names "the most gracious to be those who call themselves Christian" and sets that against the bitterness of others. The reason it gives is not ancestry or numbers. It is the presence of priests and monks, and the absence of arrogance.

That comparison is precise. It praises a described quality in a particular group. It does not turn praise into a general rule about alliances.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You will surely find the most bitter towards the believers to be the Jews and polytheists"** names the strongest enmity. One gloss adds that their hostility is the most vehement and their speech the vilest toward those who believe in Muhammad ﷺ and his Companions.

The Jews meant here include Banu Qurayzah and Banu An-Nadir, together with Fadak and Khaybar. The most vehement of the idolaters are the idolaters of Makkah.

Their disbelief is described as rebellion and defiance. It opposes the truth, belittles people and degrades scholars. Another account traces it to the intensity of their disbelief, to ignorance and to complete preoccupation with whims. A third names malice, jealousy and pride.

The history cited is grave. They killed many of their prophets. They tried several times to kill the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. They worked magic against him and poisoned him. They stirred up their like among the polytheists against him.

**"and the most gracious to be those who call themselves Christian."** refers to people who claim that name and follow the religion of the Messiah and the teaching of his Injil. They are described as more tolerant toward Islam and Muslims than the others.

The reason given is mercy and kindness. Their hearts acquired it through part of the Messiah's religion. Another verse recalls that Allah ordained compassion and mercy in the hearts of those who followed him, along with monasticism (57:27).

Their book contains the saying that whoever strikes the right cheek should be turned the left. Fighting was prohibited in their creed. One account describes them as nearest in affection and most lenient in speech.

That account identifies them as the Negus and his followers, thirty two men. Another count gives forty in all. On this reckoning thirty two came from Abyssinia and eight were monks from historic Syria. The Syrian group is named as the monk Bahira with Abrahah, Ashraf, Idris, Tamim, Tammam, Durayd and Ayman.

**"That is because there are priests and monks among them"** explains the cause. The word for priests is given in the text, and monks are described as people dedicated to worship.

One gloss describes the priests as ascetics whose heads are shaved in the middle. It describes the monks as secluded in monasteries and as the learned men of the group. Another calls the priests scholars and the monks devout worshippers.

**"and because they are not arrogant"** adds humility to knowledge and worship. They are described as following truth and fairness. They are not disdainful of the truth in the way the Jews and the people of Makkah were.

Pride is the obstacle named. One account states plainly that good does not enter a proud heart. Another says their lack of pride appeared in believing in Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an.

### What was the occasion of revelation?

Sa'id bin Jubayr, As-Suddi and others connect these verses with a delegation. An-Najashi, king of Ethiopia, sent it to the Prophet ﷺ to hear his words and observe his qualities.

The delegation met him and he recited the Qur'an. They embraced Islam, wept and were humbled. They then returned and told An-Najashi what had happened.

'Ata' bin Abi Rabah says they were Ethiopians. They embraced Islam while the Muslims who had migrated to Ethiopia lived among them. Qatadah says they followed the religion of 'Isa son of Maryam. On seeing Muslims and hearing the Qur'an they accepted Islam without hesitation.

Ibn Jarir says the verses concern people matching that description, whether from Ethiopia or elsewhere.

Ibn 'Abbas gives a longer account. While the Messenger ﷺ was in Makkah he feared persecution of his Companions by the idolaters. He therefore sent 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud and Ja'far ibn Abi Talib to the Negus at the head of a group.

He described the Negus as a righteous man. He oppressed no one, and no one was oppressed in his dominion. The group was told to head there and remain until Allah granted the Muslims relief.

The Negus honoured them. He asked whether they had memorised anything of what had been revealed to them. They recited while monks and priests stood around him. At every verse tears ran down their cheeks, because of the truth they recognised.

Another report comes through Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and 'Urwah ibn al-Zubayr, then Ibn Shihab, Yunus and al-Layth. The Messenger ﷺ sent 'Amr ibn Umayyah al-Damri to the Negus with a message. The Negus read it.

He then summoned Ja'far ibn Abi Talib and all the emigrants with him. He also invited priests and monks to the assembly. He ordered Ja'far to recite from the Qur'an, and Ja'far recited Surah Maryam. They believed, and their eyes flowed with tears.

### What happened during the migration to Abyssinia?

Among the People of the Book there were people free of malice toward Muslims. Their godliness and inclination toward truth explained it. 'Abdullah ibn Salam is given as an example.

Such people were very rare among the Jews, almost nonexistent. There were comparatively more among the Christians. Ethiopia held a large number of them in the Prophet's ﷺ time, including King Najashi and his officials.

The Muslims of Makkah al-Mukarramah were exhausted by the oppression of Quraysh. The Prophet ﷺ advised them to migrate to Ethiopia. He had heard that its king oppressed nobody and allowed no oppression, so they should stay there for a while.

A first group of eleven set out. It included 'Uthman ibn 'Affan and his wife Ruqayyah, the daughter of the Prophet ﷺ. A larger caravan followed, with eighty two men besides women, under the leadership of Ja'far ibn Abi Talib.

The king and his people received them gently, and they lived in peace. Quraysh could not bear that Muslims they had persecuted should be at peace elsewhere. They sent a delegation with the customary gifts, asking the king to expel the Muslims.

The king decided to investigate first. He spoke with Ja'far and his companions about Islam and its Prophet ﷺ. He heard of the Prophet's ﷺ life and of the teachings of Islam.

He found them true to the prophecy of 'Isa and to the Injil. That book carried glad tidings of the last of the prophets, Muhammad al-Mustafa ﷺ. It gave an outline of his teaching, his physical features and the features of his Companions for ready identification.

Convinced, he returned the gifts brought by the Quraysh delegation. He told them plainly that he could never order such people out of his country.

Ja'far's presentation was brief and comprehensive. The Muslims' conduct as role models also worked on the king, his officials and the common people. Genuine feeling for Islam and its Prophet ﷺ grew among them.

The Prophet ﷺ then migrated to Madinah al-Tayyibah. News reached Ethiopia that the Muslims were comfortable and at peace. The emigrants decided to go to Madinah.

Najashi sent a delegation with them. It was made up of leading scholars and missionaries from his own co-religionists, to present his compliments to the Prophet ﷺ. The group numbered seventy. Sixty were from Ethiopia and eight from Syria.

They wore the coarse robes of monks. The Prophet ﷺ recited Surah Yasin to them. Tears flowed as they listened, and they remarked how close it was to what had been revealed to 'Isa. All of them embraced Islam.

After their return, Najashi also embraced Islam. He sent his son to lead another delegation, carrying a letter. The boat that carried them sank at sea. The king, his officials and his people had treated Islam and Muslims gently and justly, and in the end they embraced it.

### Is the praise general or particular?

The majority of scholars say these verses were revealed about those honoured people. The wording is nevertheless general. Its legal force therefore reaches every Christian devoted to truth and justice as the people of Ethiopia were.

They followed the Injil before Islam and followed Islam after its coming. Any Christian who resembles them is counted with them.

Such people are described elsewhere in the Qur'an. They are people of the Scripture who hold faith in Allah together with what came to you and what came to them, and who humble themselves before Allah (3:199). They are those given the Scripture earlier who believe in this Book, and who say when it is recited to them that they were already Muslims before it (28:52-53). The description continues to the statement that they seek not the ignorant (28:55).

There were also Jews of this kind. They had adhered to the Torah since the time of Musa and entered Islam when it came. Their number was too small to be significant among communities and nations. The rest were foremost in hostility toward Muslims.

### What reading does the verse not support?

A particular group of Christians is praised here. They feared Allah and were upright in matters of truth. The group includes Najashi, his officials and his people, and other Christians who shared those qualities or will share them later.

It does not mean that Christians must be taken as friends however far they stray. Nor does it require friendship toward people aggressively hostile to Islam. That reading is described as false and contrary to the facts.

Abu Bakr Al-Jassas addresses it in his Ahkam al-Qur'an. He says some people take the praise of Christians here in an absolute sense. They then consider Christians better than the Jews in an absolute sense as well. He calls that open ignorance.

Comparing the beliefs of the two groups shows the Christians more pronounced in shirk, which is ascribing partners in Allah's divinity. In their dealings with Muslims, Christians of the modern age have not lagged behind Jews in activity against Islam.

What is correct is that many God-fearing and truth-loving people existed among them. That is why they were granted the ability to accept Islam and become Muslims. These verses were revealed to show that particular difference between the two groups.

The Qur'an gives the reason at the end of this verse. Among them are priests and monks, and they are not arrogant. Arrogance is what could make them unwilling to listen and think.

The contrast makes the position of the Jews at that time clear. They were not God-fearing and truth-loving. Their scholars and rabbis were far from giving up worldly pleasure for knowledge and religion.

They harnessed learning as a means of gathering material benefit. Greed for the worldly gripped them so tightly that they stopped distinguishing truth from falsehood. They no longer separated the lawful from the unlawful.

### What does this say about a community's inner life?

The verse reveals something crucial about a believing community. Its scholars and spiritual guides are its real movers and makers. They are the spirit of its order.

Genuine scholars and guides must be present in a society. They should not follow worldly desires in matters of religion or in the public good of believers. Their motive should be the fear of Allah and answerability before Him alone.

Where such people remain, the community is never deprived of real good and real blessing. The difference drawn in this verse is therefore traced to the character of its learned people.

Where learning is joined to humility and worship, truth can still be recognised. Where learning is harnessed to material gain, a community loses the capacity to hear.

### In short

- The Jews and the idolaters of Makkah are named as most bitter toward the believers, for reasons of rebellion, defiance, ignorance, malice and pride.
- Those who call themselves Christians are named as nearest in affection, because of mercy in their religion, their priests and monks, and their lack of arrogance.
- The occasion is the delegation of Najashi and the earlier migration to Abyssinia, preserved with differing counts and lists.
- The praise describes a quality and extends to whoever shares it, and it is not a warrant for alliance with hostility toward Islam.
- Scholars and guides motivated by the fear of Allah are the moving spirit of a community, and their character explains the difference drawn here.
