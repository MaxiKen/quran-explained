<<<VERSE 5:78>>>
"The disbelievers among the Children of Israel were condemned in the revelations of David and Jesus, son of Mary. That was for their disobedience and violations"

This verse says that the disbelievers among the Children of Israel were cursed in the revelations given to David and to Jesus, son of Mary. It gives the reason for the curse as their disobedience and their violations.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The disbelievers among the Children of Israel were condemned in the revelations of David and Jesus, son of Mary."** The material explains that the curse was pronounced through the tongues of David and Isa. The material reports that David invoked a curse on them, and they were turned into apes, and that Isa invoked a curse on them, and they were turned into swine. The material notes that Ibn 'Abbas said they were cursed in the Torah, the Injil, the Zabur and the Furqan, which is the Qur'an. The material says the curse began with Musa and ended with the last of the prophets, and the verse names two only because the context requires it.

**"That was for their disobedience and violations."** The material says the disobedience was the breaking of the sabbath and the eating from the table sent from heaven, the killing of prophets, and the making lawful of sin.

### The duty to forbid evil

The material explains that the Children of Israel used not to forbid one another from the evil they committed, and that this was the reason for their curse. The material gives a number of reports from the collections of Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah. Hudhayfah bin al-Yaman reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that the believers must enjoin good and forbid evil, or else punishment would come, and their prayers would not be answered. Abu Sa'id al-Khudri reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever sees an evil should change it with his hand, or with his tongue, or with his heart, and that the last is the weakest of faith. Abu Dawud reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that the one who witnesses sin and hates it is like the one who does not witness it. Abu Sa'id also reported that the best jihad is a word of truth spoken before an unjust ruler.

### In short

- David and Isa cursed the disbelievers among the Children of Israel, and the curse was for their disobedience and violations.
- The disbelievers did not forbid one another from evil, which was a central part of their fall.
- The material draws from the hadiths that the duty to speak against evil is a duty for the believers too.

<<<VERSE 5:79>>>
"They did not forbid one another from doing evil. Evil indeed was what they did"

This verse says that they did not forbid one another from doing evil, and that what they did was indeed evil.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They did not forbid one another from doing evil."** The material explains that the failure was not only in their own sins, but also in their silence about the sins of others. They allowed evil to continue among them without anyone speaking against it.

**"Evil indeed was what they did."** The material says that the verse is a plain judgement on their conduct. Their silence was as bad as the evil they committed, and God condemned both.

### The lesson of the silence

The material explains that a believer who has faith becomes sensitive to evil. When he sees it, he wants to prevent it, and he keeps his distance from the wrongdoer. When faith weakens, a person cares only for his own interests, and he is neutral about the evils that harm others. The material cites the experience of the Children of Israel as a warning.

### In short

- The people did not forbid one another from evil, and they were condemned for their silence.
- The judgement of the verse is that what they did was evil.
- A believer who loses his concern for others becomes neutral about evil that harms them.

<<<VERSE 5:80>>>
"You see many of them taking the disbelievers as allies. Truly wicked are their misdeeds, which have earned them Allah's wrath. And they will be in everlasting torment"

This verse describes the alliances that many of them made with the disbelievers. It says their misdeeds are wicked, that they have earned the wrath of Allah, and that they will be in everlasting torment.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You see many of them taking the disbelievers as allies."** The material explains that the alliances were with the disbelievers of Makkah, and that the material reads the verse as an attack on those who sided with the enemies of the Prophet ﷺ. The material notes that the alliances were made out of spite and hostility to the believers.

**"Truly wicked are their misdeeds, which have earned them Allah's wrath."** The material says that the wrath of Allah is the result of what they have sent forward before themselves, which is their hypocrisy and their support for the disbelievers. The wrath lasts until the Day of Return.

**"And they will be in everlasting torment."** The material says the torment will be on the Day of Resurrection, and that they will remain in it.

### The alliances in the material

The material says that some reports apply the verse to the hypocrites, and that others apply it to the Jews who supported the Quraysh. The material says it is the same act in each case: taking the enemies of the believers as friends.

### In short

- Many of them took the disbelievers as allies, out of spite for the believers.
- Their misdeeds have brought Allah's wrath, and it will remain on them until the Day of Return.
- They will be in everlasting torment.

<<<VERSE 5:81>>>
"Had they believed in Allah, the Prophet, and what has been revealed to him, they would have never taken those ˹pagans˺ as allies. But most of them are rebellious"

This verse says that had they truly believed in Allah, His Prophet and what has been revealed to him, they would never have taken the pagans as allies. It then says that most of them are rebellious.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Had they believed in Allah, the Prophet, and what has been revealed to him."** The material explains that true belief includes belief in the Prophet ﷺ and in the Qur'an. A faith that rejects the Prophet ﷺ is not the faith the verse means.

**"They would have never taken those pagans as allies."** The material says that faith in Allah and in the Prophet would have stopped them from taking the enemies of the believers as friends.

**"But most of them are rebellious."** The material says that most of them rebelled against the commands of Allah and His Messenger, and against the signs of His revelation.

### In short

- True belief in Allah and in the Prophet would have stopped them from allying with the pagans.
- Most of them chose rebellion.

<<<VERSE 5:82>>>
"You will surely find the most bitter towards the believers to be the Jews and polytheists and the most gracious to be those who call themselves Christian. That is because there are priests and monks among them and because they are not arrogant"

This verse says that the most hostile people towards the believers are the Jews and the polytheists, and that the people nearest to the believers in affection are those who call themselves Christians. It gives the reason as the presence of priests and monks among them, and their lack of arrogance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You will surely find the most bitter towards the believers to be the Jews and polytheists."** The material explains that the Jews are the most hostile in rebellion, defiance and the opposing of the truth. The material says the Jews killed many of their prophets and tried to kill the Prophet ﷺ several times. It also says that they worked against him, and incited the polytheists to fight him. The material reads the polytheists as the idolators of Makkah.

**"And the most gracious to be those who call themselves Christian."** The material says the praise applies to a particular group of Christians who did not have the hostility of the Jews. The material points to the Christians of Abyssinia, who received the Muslims who migrated there and gave them peace.

**"That is because there are priests and monks among them."** The material explains that the priests and monks are men of knowledge and worship, and that their humility and fairness made them accept the truth. The material cites 57:27, which describes the compassion, mercy and monasticism that God placed in the hearts of the followers of Isa.

**"And because they are not arrogant."** The material says that humility allows a person to listen and to accept the truth. The material contrasts this with the arrogance that the Jews showed.

### The Negus and the delegation

The material explains the cause of the revelation. The Prophet ﷺ advised some of his Companions to migrate to Abyssinia, because its king did not allow anyone to be oppressed in his land. Ja'far ibn Abi Talib led a group, and the Quraysh sent a delegation asking the king to return them. The king heard Ja'far recite about Isa and the Prophet, and he refused to hand them over. When the Muslims later reached Madinah, the Negus sent a delegation of about forty men, with monks among them, to the Prophet ﷺ. They heard the Qur'an, wept, and embraced Islam. The material says that the Negus himself later became a Muslim, and that his son was sent with a letter, though the boat carrying him was lost at sea.

The material cites 3:199, which speaks of the People of the Book who believe in what has been revealed to the Prophet ﷺ and to them, and 28:52-53, which speaks of those given the Scripture who believe in it. It cites 28:55, in which they say they do not seek the ignorant. The material says that the verses describe a group who accepted the truth, and their reward is given in the next verses.

### The limits of this praise

The material says that the praise is not a rule that the Christians are friends of the believers in general, and it cites the view of Abu Bakr al-Jassas that the Christians are closer to shirk in their beliefs. It says that the verse is a difference between the two groups in this one respect, and that the Jews as a group were not God-fearing in the same way.

### In short

- The Jews and the polytheists were the most hostile to the believers, and the Christians who did not share that hostility were the nearest in affection.
- The reason given is the presence of priests and monks who were humble and not arrogant.
- The praise applies to a particular group, as the delegation of the Negus shows, and not to every Christian.
