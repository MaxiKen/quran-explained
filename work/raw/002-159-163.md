<<<VERSE 2:159>>>
Several verses earlier, in the debate over the qiblah, verse 146 pointed out that the People of the Book recognise the Prophet ﷺ as they recognise their own sons, and that a group of them conceals the truth knowingly. Verse 159 concludes that subject with a stern warning to those who not only hide the truth but persist obstinately in doing so. "Those who hide the clear proofs and guidance that We have revealed—after We made it clear for humanity in the Book—will be condemned by Allah and ˹all˺ those who condemn". Abū al-ʿĀliyah said these verses were revealed about the People of the Scripture who hid the description of Muḥammad ﷺ, and another report says they concern the scholars of the People of the Book and their concealment of the verse of stoning for married adulterers as well as the matter of Muḥammad ﷺ.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who hide the clear proofs and guidance that We have revealed"**. The clear proofs are the signs the messengers were sent with, which guide to the correct path, together with the commands and prohibitions mentioned in the Torah. The guidance is what benefits hearts, and in particular the description of Muḥammad ﷺ and his features given in the Torah. The Jews and Christians who hid the proofs showing the truth of the Prophet ﷺ and what he brought are the first people meant.

**"after We made it clear for humanity in the Book"**. Allah had made all this plain to His servants through the Books He revealed to His messengers, and to the Children of Israel in the Torah, so there was no excuse of ignorance.

**"will be condemned by Allah and ˹all˺ those who condemn"**. Allah removes them far from His mercy, and one reading adds that He torments them in the grave. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and Qatādah said that "those who condemn" are the angels and the believers. Others include the prophets, or every single thing, which curses them when it prays that they be cursed, or all created beings other than humans and jinn, who curse them when they hear their screams from the grave. Just as everything asks forgiveness for the scholar, even the fish in the sea and the bird in the air, so those who hide knowledge are cursed by Allah and by the cursers. Mujāhid said that when drought strikes the earth the animals say that this is because of the sinners among the children of Adam, and ask Allah to curse them. Mujāhid and ʿIkrimah noted that the verse leaves the cursers unspecified, which suggests that everything and every living being curses such people, animals and insects included, because their misdeeds harm all created life. A hadith from al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib, which al-Qurṭubī cites from Ibn Mājah, has the Prophet ﷺ say that "those who curse" are all the creatures that move on the earth. In sum, those who hide knowledge are cursed in this life and on the Day of Resurrection by Allah, the angels, all humanity and all who curse, each in its own way. Allah knows best.

### The duty to pass on religious knowledge

Concealing the clear signs and guidance Allah has revealed is a terrible crime that earns the curse of Allah and of His whole creation. Several rulings follow from this. First, it is forbidden to conceal knowledge that must be disclosed and spread. The Musnad records, through several chains that strengthen one another, that Abū Hurayrah related the Prophet's ﷺ saying that whoever is asked about knowledge he possesses and hides it will have a bridle of fire fastened on his mouth on the Day of Resurrection. Al-Qurṭubī gives it from Abū Hurayrah and ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ as reported by Ibn Mājah. The jurists say this warning applies to a person who is the only knowledgeable one available in a given situation. If others are present, he may suggest that the question be put to a scholar who knows, as al-Qurṭubī and al-Jaṣṣāṣ explain. Second, one who lacks sound knowledge of religious rulings should not try to explain them. Third, answers to intricate religious questions beyond the grasp of ordinary people should not be set before them, lest they fall into misunderstanding. This is not concealment of knowledge, since what is forbidden is hiding the clear guidance of the Qur'an and Sunnah, which must be spread widely, and the verse's phrase "clear proofs and guidance" strongly suggests this limit. It was of such questions that the Companion ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd said that reciting hadiths which people do not fully understand throws them into a nest of discord. Al-Bukhārī reports ʿAlī as saying: disclose to people only as much knowledge as they have the capacity to understand, or do you want them to deny Allah and His Messenger ﷺ? Anything beyond their comprehension breeds doubt and may lead them to reject it.

A scholar or guide is therefore responsible for assessing people's ability to receive what he communicates, and sensitive questions should not be raised before someone likely to fall into error. This is why jurists, when discussing such questions in writing, close with a standard warning that the matter is delicate and should be kept to the scholar's own understanding rather than broadcast. The Prophet ﷺ is reported to have said: do not withhold wisdom from those who deserve it, for then you wrong them, and do not place it before those who do not deserve it, for then you wrong it. From this al-Qurṭubī deduced that a disbeliever who engages in polemics against Muslims, or a heresiarch who invites people to his misleading ideas, should not be initiated into the Islamic disciplines unless it is certain that the teaching will correct his thinking. Likewise, rulers should not be given rulings they could misuse to terrorise their citizens, and the concessions (rukhṣah) and legal stratagems (ḥiyal) should not be aired in public unnecessarily, lest people grow used to seeking excuses in practising the faith.

### Hadith falls under the same warning

Al-Bukhārī records that Abū Hurayrah said: were it not for a verse in the Book of Allah, I would not have narrated a single hadith to anyone, and he then recited this verse. Other Companions are reported to have said similar things while narrating hadith. These reports show that in the Companions' view the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ shares some of the privileges stated for the Qur'an. The verse itself warns those who conceal what was revealed in the Qur'an and does not mention hadith, but the Companions ruled that the Prophet's ﷺ hadith was covered by the reference, so that concealing hadith would bring the same warning upon them.

### Stinginess with the knowledge of the Path

In the devotional reading, the verse alludes to those to whom the Real has unveiled knowledge of the good manners of travelling the Path, but who have become stingy in sharing it with aspirants seeking advice and guidance. At that moment they become deserving of wrath, and one fears that the blessing will be removed from their knowledge, since they fell short in it just as they held back from imparting it to the deserving.

### In short

- The verse condemns the People of the Book who hid the Torah's description of the Prophet ﷺ and the stoning verse, concluding the theme of verse 146.
- They are cursed by Allah and by all who curse: angels, believers, prophets and, as Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah and the hadith of al-Barāʾ indicate, every creature harmed by their sin.
- Hiding needed religious knowledge brings a bridle of fire (Abū Hurayrah, Ibn Mājah), yet knowledge must be matched to the hearer's capacity (ʿAlī in al-Bukhārī, Ibn Masʿūd), and al-Qurṭubī draws further limits for polemicists, rulers and concessions.
- Abū Hurayrah narrated hadith because of this verse, showing that the Companions held hadith to fall under the same duty of disclosure.

<<<VERSE 2:160>>>
The fate of those who persist in concealment is obvious, yet Allah still holds out His mercy. "As for those who repent, mend their ways, and let the truth be known, they are the ones to whom I will turn ˹in forgiveness˺, for I am the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful". Allah excludes from the curse everyone who repents to Him, regrets what he has been doing, corrects his conduct and makes known to people what he had hidden.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who repent"**. Those who turn back from such deeds and ask forgiveness, regretting that they hid the clear signs. For the People of the Book, the explanation here means turning from Judaism and accepting Islam. Belief in Allah and His Prophet ﷺ then demonstrates an honest change of heart.

**"mend their ways"**. They make amends in their actions, correcting what they do outwardly and inwardly, and worship Allah alone without ascribing partners to Him.

**"and let the truth be known"**. They show clearly what they used to conceal, the truth and guidance they had hidden, and in particular the traits and description of Muḥammad ﷺ. To demonstrate their changed stand they come forward and state the truth clearly and publicly.

**"they are the ones to whom I will turn ˹in forgiveness˺"**. Allah accepts their repentance and overlooks their past transgressions. He turns to those who turn to Him asking forgiveness and regretting what they did. The verse also shows that those who used to call people to innovation, or even to disbelief, are forgiven by Allah if they repent to Him.

**"for I am the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful"**. He is the Relenting towards those who repent and the Merciful towards the believers, and towards whoever dies in a state of repentance.

### Making amends by guiding others

In the devotional reading, those excepted are the ones who make up for their past negligence by graciously turning back and taking on the task of advising the aspirants. They show them clearly the goodness of keeping to certain modes of behaviour, explaining and demonstrating it beautifully. The clearest proof of the sincerity of your actions, and the truest testimony to the message by which you call people to Allah, is that your behaviour does not contradict what you allude to in your speech. As the prophet Shuʿayb said to his people: "I do not want to do what I am forbidding you from" (11:88).

### In short

- The curse of verse 159 is lifted from whoever repents, reforms his conduct and openly declares the truth he concealed, which for the People of the Book means embracing Islam.
- Even those who once preached innovation or disbelief are forgiven on sincere repentance, for Allah is the Accepter of Repentance and Most Merciful.
- Inwardly, amends for withholding guidance are made by guiding others, and one's conduct must match one's words (11:88).

<<<VERSE 2:161>>>
After the exception for those who repent, Allah states the fate of those who do not. "Surely those who disbelieve and die as disbelievers are condemned by Allah, the angels, and all of humanity". Those who disbelieve in Allah and His Messenger ﷺ and remain in that state until they die, without repenting, deserve this curse in this life and the next.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Surely those who disbelieve and die as disbelievers"**. The phrase "while they are disbelievers" is a circumstantial clause: the curse falls on those whose disbelief lasts until death.

**"are condemned by Allah, the angels, and all of humanity"**. The curse of Allah is His torment and His removing them from His mercy. The angels curse them, and so do people, whether this means people in general or the believers, all of whom call for them to be excluded from Allah's mercy. One gloss adds that the curses believers direct at one another end up falling on these disbelievers.

### Cursing in general and cursing by name

There is no disagreement that cursing the disbelievers as a class is lawful. During qunūt in prayer, and at other times, ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb invoked a curse on the disbelievers. Later imams continued that practice. Cursing a specific disbeliever is another matter. Some scholars held it impermissible, because we do not know how Allah will bring about his end. Others allowed it, citing the story of the man repeatedly brought for punishment for drinking: when someone said "may Allah curse him, how often he is brought", the Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade that curse because the man loved Allah and His Messenger. They understood the reply to permit cursing someone who lacked that love. Allah knows best. From the words "and die as disbelievers", al-Jaṣṣāṣ, al-Qurṭubī and others concluded that it is not permissible to curse a disbeliever about whom it is not certain that he will die in disbelief. Since we have no way of knowing in what state a person will die, we are bound by the ruling that cursing a disbeliever by name is not permitted. That the Prophet ﷺ cursed some disbelievers by name is explained by the fact that their death in disbelief was revealed to him. Cursing the disbelievers as a whole, including tyrants and the unjust, without specifying anyone, is technically correct.

It follows that cursing is so grave that it is disallowed even against a disbeliever whose end is uncertain. How then could it be permissible against a Muslim, or even against an animal? Ordinary people in Muslim societies neglect this aspect of proper conduct, and women in particular are prone to very hard language about acquaintances they dislike. Cursing is not confined to the word "curse": every synonym falls under the same ruling. Laʿnah means being driven away from the mercy of Allah, so all damning language, whatever its shade, circumstance or tongue, counts as laʿnah.

### In short

- Those who die in disbelief without repenting are cursed by Allah, the angels and all people, in this world and the next.
- Cursing disbelievers in general, as ʿUmar did in the qunūt, is lawful. Cursing a named person is not, since no one knows how he will die (al-Jaṣṣāṣ, al-Qurṭubī), and the Prophet's ﷺ naming of some was by revelation.
- Cursing is grave enough that it is forbidden even against an uncertain disbeliever, let alone a Muslim or an animal, and every form of damning speech counts.

<<<VERSE 2:162>>>
Verse 162 describes how long the curse lasts. "They will be in Hell forever. Their punishment will not be lightened, nor will they be delayed ˹from it˺". Those who die in disbelief suffer the eternal curse until the Day of Resurrection and after it in the fire of Jahannam.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They will be in Hell forever."**. They abide for ever in the curse and in the Fire, which the next words indicate.

**"Their punishment will not be lightened"**. It will not be lifted, decreased or reduced, not even for a day or for the blink of an eye.

**"nor will they be delayed ˹from it˺"**. Their torment will not be deferred, changed or tempered even for an hour. They are given no respite in which to repent or excuse themselves, and no extra time on the Day of Judgement. It is continuous and eternal. We seek refuge with Allah from such an end.

### Those who turn back from the Path

In the devotional reading, verses 161 and 162 allude to those who travel the path of spiritual desire but then choose a return to habitual life. They die while caught in that estrangement. They are the companions of separation. There is no drawing near for their spirits, no setting right of their calamity, and no one to ask mercy for them. They have lost in this world and the Hereafter, cursed by the gnats in the air and the stagnation in the water. "Abiding" means that they remain for ever in their contemptible position, insignificant, with no lessening of it and no relief, without kindness or favour.

### In short

- The curse on those who die disbelieving is eternal: in Hell their punishment is neither reduced nor postponed, and no respite is given for repentance or excuse.
- Inwardly, the warning is for those who abandon the Path for the life of habit and die in that estrangement, the companions of separation.

<<<VERSE 2:163>>>
After the warning to those who die in disbelief, Allah declares the truth they denied. "Your God is ˹only˺ One God. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Most Compassionate, Most Merciful". When the idolaters (mushrikīn) of Arabia heard "your God is one God", so contrary to their belief, they were puzzled how there could be a single object of worship for the whole wide world, and felt that so serious a claim needed proof. That proof is given tersely in this verse and the next. One report says the verse was revealed when they asked Muḥammad ﷺ to describe his Lord.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Your God is ˹only˺ One God."**. The One deserving of your worship is one God, without son or partner, without equal in essence or attributes, unique in both. He is the One and Only, the Sustainer, with no partners or equals.

**"There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him"**. No one else has the right to be worshipped. Allah declares His Oneness here because the disbelievers denied it.

**"the Most Compassionate, Most Merciful"**. He is al-Raḥmān, the Most Gracious, the Tender, whose mercy is vast, and al-Raḥīm, the Most Merciful, compassionate with His creation and surrounding them with blessings. These two Names were explained at the beginning of Sūrat al-Fātiḥah. Shahr ibn Ḥawshab reported from Asmāʾ bint Yazīd ibn al-Sakan that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that Allah's Greatest Name is contained in two verses: this one, and the opening of Sūrat Āl ʿImrān, "Alif-Lām-Mīm" (3:1) followed by "Allah! There is no god except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of all". Allah then goes on, in the next verse, to mention some of the proofs that He alone is God, for He created the heavens and the earth and all the creatures between them, and all of them testify to His Oneness.

### Tawḥīd in its wider sense

Tawḥīd, the cardinal principle of the faith, is proved repeatedly in the Qur'an, so a summary suffices here. First, He is One in His being: nothing in the universe of His creation is like Him, He has no duplicate, replica, equal or parallel, and so unshared is His station that He alone deserves to be called al-Wāḥid, the One. Second, He is One in the right to be worshipped: given the nature of His Being, the completeness of His perfect attributes and the wonder of His creation and nurture of it, all human obedience and all worship (ʿibādah) must be for Him alone. Third, He is One in being free of any composition: He has no parts, segments, organs, substances, elements, atoms or particles, and cannot be analysed, divided or resolved. Fourth, He is One in being the first and the last: He existed when nothing else did and will exist when nothing else does. Who then, if not He, should be called the only One? This summary is al-Jaṣṣāṣ's.

Humanity has only one God, and He is the only worthy focus of a person's attention. Our very existence on earth and all the benefits we draw from the world around us are manifestations of His boundless grace and mercy. In return we should become His devoted servants, living for Him, dying for Him and setting all our hopes on His eternal favour. A person owes everything to his Creator. Once he becomes conscious of that debt, his Lord comes to mean everything to him, the way a mother is everything to her infant.

### One in everything

In the devotional reading, the verse describes the one Lord, who is God and King. His oneness shows in magnanimity and in governance, in forbearance and in beautiful doing, in generosity and in peerlessness, in loving kindness and in the caressing of His servants. Every magnificence is the mantle of His majesty and every tremendousness the shawl of His lordship, and in these He is one. He is one in Essence, in attributes, in deed and mark, in loyalty and covenant, in gentleness, in love and friendship. On the day the portions were apportioned, who was there but He, the One? Before it, who was there? That same One. After it, who handed over the portions, who shows, who adorns? That same One. He is more apparent than anything in the world of appearance and more hidden than anything in the world of hiddenness, and in both He is one. O You who are more face to face than anything in the world and more hidden than its most hidden thing, more distant than all the servants suppose and closer to them than the vein of the spirit! How disloyal is the child of Adam who does not know the worth of this declaration and the exaltedness of this ascription. The wonder is not that He ascribed the servants to Himself and said, "My servants" (15:42). The wonder is that He ascribed Himself to the servants and joined His name to theirs, saying, "Your God". This is not because His lordship must be joined to their servanthood, or because they deserve it, but because in generosity and loving kindness He is unique and one. In the presence of our Heart-taker's beauty we are not suited for Him. He is suited for us. There was no world and no Adam, no tracks and no traces, no one in the house, and your God was one God.

### In short

- Against the idolaters' astonishment, the verse declares that God is one, without partner, son or equal, and alone has the right to worship. The next verse gives the proof from creation.
- The Greatest Name of Allah lies in this verse and the opening of Āl ʿImrān (3:1), according to the hadith of Asmāʾ bint Yazīd.
- Tawḥīd means He is one in being, in the right to worship, in freedom from composition, and in being first and last (al-Jaṣṣāṣ).
- Inwardly, the wonder is that the One joined His name to His servants' name, "your God", out of pure generosity (cf. 15:42).
