<<<VERSE 6:27>>>
"If only you could see when they will be detained before the Fire!" The scene is the Day of Rising, and the ones held in it are the wrongdoers who denied the signs. They are stopped at the edge of the Fire and shown what is there: the chains, the restraints, the punishment, all of it with their own eyes, and all of it past anything they had pictured. Then the wish comes out of them, and it asks for one thing only, to be sent back.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If only you could see"** is addressed to the Prophet ﷺ. Whatever the sentence leaves unsaid is understood: see it, and you will be looking at an awesome sight, a remarkable thing in their trouble. **"when they will be detained before the Fire"** means being brought there, exposed to it and shut up in it with no opening left. **"Oh! If only we could be sent back"** is the cry of people who have lost every other way out. They want the world given back so that they can act differently in it. **"we would never deny the signs of our Lord"** promises a change about the Scriptures and the messengers they had thrown aside. **"and we would ˹surely˺ be of the believers"** completes it, and the wish is to belong to the believers outwardly and inwardly, the way they belong.

The two verbs of that promise, nukadhdhibu and nakūna, are read in more than one way. One reading takes them as a fresh sentence, so that the verbs of denying and of being believers stand on their own. Another reads them as the answer drawn after the wish, and then the forms say something slightly different: on the first reading they say, in effect, we will not deny and we will be believers, and on the second they carry the hope itself, that we would not deny and would be believers. The point is not the grammar. The promise itself is worthless, and the next verse says why.

### Why the hereafter weighs so heavily

Three principles carry the rest of faith: the oneness of Allah, prophethood, and belief in the hereafter. Every other article of faith falls under one of them. They tell a human being what he is and what his life is for, and that knowledge turns a life around, because it shows him the straight path. Of the three, the hereafter has the widest effect in practice. Belief in a reckoning to come, with good rewarded and evil punished, aims every deed in one direction. That is why the subjects of the Qur'an move around these three, and why the verses here press on the reckoning, its rewards and punishments, and the true nature of this mortal world.

### In short

- The verse shows wrongdoers at the edge of the Fire, seeing with their own eyes what they had denied.
- Their only wish is to be sent back, with a promise to stop denying the signs and to join the believers.
- The address "if only you could see" leaves its answer to be understood, and the answer is a terrible sight.
- Belief in the reckoning is singled out because it channels every deed, next to oneness and prophethood.
- The promise they make will be refused, because it is forced out of them by what they now see.

<<<VERSE 6:28>>>
"But no! ˹They only say this˺ because the truth they used to hide will become all too clear to them." This verse tears the promise of verse 27 apart. The wish to go back is not faith arriving, it is pain speaking. What they now say about believing was never the real condition of their hearts, and the proof is that the condition has just become visible to everybody.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But no!"** is not a plain refusal of the words. It rejects the desire to believe that the wish behind those words carried. **"˹They only say this˺ because the truth they used to hide will become all too clear to them"** means that the disbelief, the denial and the rebellion they kept folded inside have now been uncovered. They tried to keep them hidden in this world and they tried to keep them hidden on that Day, and both attempts failed. What they used to conceal was the oath they swore in front of the believers in this life: by Allah their Lord, they were never people who joined others in worship with Him. That claim has now become evident for what it was, because their own limbs had already testified against them in the scene just before this verse. **"Even if they were to be sent back, they would certainly revert to what they were forbidden"** answers the wish directly. Grant them the return they beg for, and they will forget every resolution the second they are standing in the world again, and go back to the disbelief and the association of partners with Allah that they had been stopped from. **"Indeed they are liars"** closes it: the promise that they would believe if only they were returned is a lie.

### What kind of lie is it

The wording leaves the charge of lying open in three directions, and all three fit. It can mean that the promise would not come true, since a people who lie their way out of the Day would go on lying once they were back in the world. It can mean that the words do not come from a real intention at all, since they are buying time and trying to step out of the punishment they are watching while they still have no wish to do what they promise. And it can mean that they are lying about what they had hidden, since they used to swear by Allah that they were not idolaters.

On the reading that sits closest to the sequence of the verses, the whole exchange runs like this. Their All-Knowing, All-Aware Master knows what they would do if He gave them what they asked for, and so He refuses it. They are not asking to go back because they want faith, they are asking because they are frightened of the torment standing in front of them. Fear of punishment is not belief. Their disease was never a shortage of evidence. They did not relish the realities the prophets of Allah had brought them, they knew the prophets were true, and they raised dust to keep those realities unmanifest, out of chronic obstinacy or out of worldly greed. On that Day everything comes into the open. They see the power of Allah with their own eyes, they know the truth of what was claimed, and the life after death they had denied is before them, real and physical. They are direct witnesses to reward, to punishment and to the Fire, and no argument is left to them. Their wish to be returned is then nothing but the absence of a choice.

### The Day that sorts out reputations

One reading takes this unveiling further, to the Day when states are uncovered and secrets brought out, the day when hidden cores are tested (86:9). On it many who were counted among the renunciants in this world, who wore the color of the close friends of Allah and dressed in their dress, will find the burning mark of wretchedness on their own foreheads as they are taken down to the dwelling place of strangers. And many whom everyone knew as shameless, whom they expected to see in chains, who owned nothing and had no name at all, will be the ones in whose name the treasuries of the unseen and the robes of generosity are carried in. The holy ones of the Higher Plenum and the dwellers of the Gardens of Shelter will stand astonished at what He does, and it will be as a poet said of the reversal: many a praying master of the path left without a mount, and many a tavern haunter riding off on a roaring lion. The same reading then adds that the people of punishment, if they were sent back to this world, would return to their refusal and denial, and the people of clear loyalty, if they were sent back, would return to the beauty of their deeds. Each of them goes back to what he already is.

### In short

- Their wish to return is forced by the torment in front of them, not by belief.
- The truth they hid has become clear, and their old oath of innocence is exposed as a lie.
- Sent back, they would revert to the disbelief and the shirk they were forbidden, so the promise proves false three ways over.
- They were never short of evidence. Obstinacy and greed kept the truth unmanifest.
- That Day uncovers hearts, tests secret cores (86:9) and reverses the reputations people built for themselves.

<<<VERSE 6:29>>>
"They insisted, 'There is nothing beyond this worldly life and we will never be resurrected.'" This is the sentence that explains everything before it. It is not a report about the Fire. It is the creed that put them there, and it is what they would say again if they were returned, which is exactly what verse 28 promised.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They insisted"** carries the sense of going back to something and sticking to it, and the wording ties this verse to the "they would certainly revert" of the verse before it. Read it as the answer to that promise and the verse says: if they were sent back and reached the world, they would say the very thing they are saying now. **"There is nothing beyond this worldly life"** is the claim that this life, and nothing else, is real. **"and we will never be resurrected"** denies being raised after death to give an account. The ones who say it here are the idolaters of Makkah, and their denial is not a doubt about what comes after, it is a settled refusal.

### How can a witness deny

A question sits on the surface of these verses. They have seen the return to life, the Day of Qiyamah, the reckoning, the taking of account, the reward and the punishment. Once a person has seen all that, how can he go back to denying it? The answer is that denial does not require real uncertainty. A person can be certain and still deny. Look at the unbelievers and wrongdoers of our own time: in spite of their knowledge of Islamic realities they keep to denial and falsification, simply out of hostility. The Qur'an describes precisely that temper in the words about people who kept denying the signs while their own hearts were sure of them, and did it only out of wrongdoing and pride (27:14). Musa put the same thing plainly to Firʿawn, whose people were not uncertain about the signs but unwilling to accept them: "Verily, you know that these signs have clearly been sent down by none but the Lord of the heavens and the earth as eye-opening evidence" (17:102). It is also what was said about some of the People of the Book, who recognized the Last Prophet ﷺ the way they recognized their own sons and remained bent on opposing him.

So to sum up: the Creator knows with His eternal knowledge that their claim, that they would become ideal believers if only they were sent back, is a lie and a trick. If a world torn down were built up again and they were released into it, their conduct would be the conduct they had already shown in the lives they first lived.

### The scale and the offer

A reported saying of the Prophet ﷺ sets the same test at the center of the Day. At the time of the reckoning, Allah will ask Adam to stand near the Scale of Justice, to look at the deeds of his children, and to let into Paradise anyone whose good deeds outweigh his sins. Then He will say: the one I admit to the punishment of Hell is the one about whom I know that, if he were sent back into the world, he would do the same thing he had been doing. The verdict rests not on what a person promises under pressure but on what he would actually do.

### In short

- The verse quotes the creed behind all the rest: nothing beyond this life, and no resurrection.
- It continues verse 28, since reverting means saying this same thing again in the world.
- Denial can coexist with certainty, as 27:14 shows about those who rejected the signs out of pride.
- Allah's eternal knowledge already knows that their promise would not be kept.
- The saying about Adam and the Scale makes the sentence about what a person would really do again.

<<<VERSE 6:30>>>
"But if only you could see when they will be detained before their Lord!" The address now repeats the opening of verse 27 and moves the scene one step further. There they were stopped before the Fire. Here they are brought before their Lord, held there, constrained, and questioned, and the questioning is the kind that convicts rather than informs.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But if only you could see"** is again said to Muhammad ﷺ, and again its answer is left to be understood: you would certainly see an awesome thing. **"when they will be detained before their Lord"** means being set before Him and hemmed in, with no way to step aside from the address. **"He will ask ˹them˺, 'Is this ˹Hereafter˺ not the truth?'"** is a rebuke, not a request for news. The sense is: is not this resurrection and reckoning real, the torment and the rising after death, contrary to what you reckoned? One reading takes the question as coming from Allah to them, and another explains it as the angels asking it in rebuke on His tongue. **"They will cry, 'Absolutely, by our Lord!'"** is an oath by the Lord who created them: it is the truth, and there is no doubt about it, exactly as the messengers reported it. **"He will say, 'Then taste the punishment for your disbelief'"** is the sentence, and its point is that the retribution is the result of their denial of this day, which they went on rejecting through the whole of the life of the world.

### Why the admission arrives too late

The confession is honest, and worthless. On this Day they will affirm with an oath what they had denied in the world, because nothing is left but affirming it. Belief that arrives when the unseen has become seen does not count, and the verse before this one had already shown why their acceptance cannot be trusted: it is produced by what is in front of them, not by conviction. That is the same rule that made their wish to be sent back a lie. A thing believed only when it cannot be escaped is not believed in the way that was asked of them.

### A question asked in rebuke

Other verses put the same rebuke in other words, and one of them sharpens it: "Is this magic, or do you not see?" (52:15). The questioners know the answer before they ask. The point of asking is to make the denyers say it out loud in front of everyone, so that the sentence falls on a mouth that has just admitted the truth. Their Lord asks whether it is real, they swear that it is, and the punishment is then named as theirs by their own account, because it is the punishment for disbelief, not for bad luck.

### In short

- The scene of 6:27 is repeated at a higher place: before the Fire, and then before their Lord.
- "Is this not the truth?" is a rebuke about resurrection, the reckoning and the torment, not a question seeking information.
- Their answer is an oath that it is the truth, exactly as the messengers said.
- The sentence "taste the punishment for your disbelief" ties the torment to their denial of this day in the world.
- Admitting the truth when it can no longer be escaped does not change the verdict.
