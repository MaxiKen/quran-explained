<<<VERSE 5:100>>>
"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Good and evil are not equal, though you may be dazzled by the abundance of evil. So be mindful of Allah, O people of reason, so you may be successful.”"

This verse tells the Prophet ﷺ to say that good and evil are not equal, even when evil is abundant and seems to dazzle. It calls people of reason to fear Allah, so that they may succeed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, O Prophet, Good and evil are not equal."** The material explains that the words tayyib and khabith are opposites. Tayyib is what is best and pure, and khabith is what is condemned and impure. The majority of the people of knowledge read the words in this verse as the lawful and the unlawful, and in the wider sense the pure and the impure of wealth, of people, and of deeds. The material says that the verse is a natural law, that what is lawful and what is unlawful are not equal before Allah.

**"Though you may be dazzled by the abundance of evil."** The material says that onlookers are often impressed by evil because it is widespread and seems to be winning. The material says this is a flaw in human judgement, and that abundance does not make a thing good.

**"So be mindful of Allah, O people of reason, so you may be successful."** The material says that the people of reason are the ones who avoid the unlawful and let the lawful be sufficient for them. The success is in this life and in the Hereafter.

### The example of the dealer

The material reports that a dealer in wine who had savings from the trade asked the Prophet ﷺ whether he could spend them in good causes, such as jihad or hajj. The Prophet ﷺ answered that Allah accepts nothing except what is pure and lawful, and that such wealth would not weigh even the wing of a mosquito. The material says that this shows the lack of value in unlawful wealth in the sight of the Hereafter, and that lawful wealth brings a true comfort that unlawful wealth cannot.

The material also reports that Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, when he stopped the unlawful taxes and returned the wealth taken illegally, found that the treasury was empty. A governor wrote to him to complain, and he replied by quoting this verse, saying that the people before had used the wealth of the state unlawfully, and that the lawful is enough.

### In short

- Good and evil, the lawful and the unlawful, are not equal before Allah.
- The abundance of evil does not make it good, and it can dazzle the careless.
- The people of reason fear Allah, and let the lawful be enough for them.

<<<VERSE 5:101>>>
"O believers! Do not ask about any matter which, if made clear to you, may disturb you. But if you inquire about what is being revealed in the Quran, it will be made clear to you. Allah has forgiven what was done ˹in the past˺. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing"

This verse tells the believers not to ask about matters which, if made clear, would trouble them. It says that if they ask about what is being revealed in the Qur'an, it will be made clear to them. It notes that Allah has forgiven what was done in the past, and that He is All-Forgiving and Most Forbearing.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Do not ask about any matter which, if made clear to you, may disturb you."** The material explains that the believers are forbidden to ask questions that would bring difficulty if answered. Anas ibn Malik reported in the sahih of Al-Bukhari that the Prophet ﷺ gave a speech that moved the people to tears, and that a man asked who his father was, and this verse was revealed. Muslim, Ahmad, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i also recorded the report. Ibn Abbas reported that some of the people asked the Prophet ﷺ to mock him, one asking who his father was and another asking where his camel was.

**"But if you inquire about what is being revealed in the Quran, it will be made clear to you."** The material explains that if the believers ask about matters that they are prohibited from asking, and the revelation comes while the Prophet ﷺ is alive, the answer will be made plain to them. The material says this is easy for Allah. The material also reports from Ali that when the verse on the hajj came, the people asked whether it was obligatory every year, and the Prophet ﷺ said that if he had said yes, it would have become obligatory, and they would not have been able to bear it. Al-Aqra ibn Habis is reported to have asked the question in this form.

**"Allah has forgiven what was done in the past."** The material says that this refers to the questions the believers had asked before the revelation of the verse. The forgiveness is for what has passed.

**"And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing."** The material explains that the verse is a reminder that Allah forgives the questions asked in ignorance, and is patient with those who ask.

### The silence of the law

The material gives the saying of the Prophet ﷺ that the worst of the Muslims is the one who asks about a matter that is then made unlawful because of his asking. It also gives the saying that those before them were destroyed by their many questions and their disputes with their prophets. The material says that Allah has set obligations, limits and prohibitions, and that He has left some things without rulings out of mercy, and so the believers should not ask about them.

The material says that after the revelation of the Qur'an is complete, asking about the unnecessary is still blameworthy, since it wastes time. It says that the Muslim jurists' work on the questions of religion is not waste, because later generations needed it.

### In short

- Believers are forbidden to ask questions which would bring trouble when answered.
- Questions asked while the revelation is coming would be answered, and Allah forgives the questions that were asked before.
- The silence of the law is a mercy, and asking about what is not necessary is blameworthy.

<<<VERSE 5:102>>>
"Some people before you asked such questions then denied their answers"

This verse says that some people before the believers asked such questions, and then denied the answers that were given to them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Some people before you asked such questions."** The material explains that the people before the believers asked their prophets about matters that were not yet clear to them. The material says they asked not in order to be guided, but to mock and to defy.

**"Then denied their answers."** The material says that the answers were revealed, and the people did not accept them, and so they became disbelievers. The material says that after the answers came, they neglected to implement them.

### The lesson

The material says that the verse warns the believers not to make the same mistake. The questions that bring no benefit, and that are asked only to test the Prophet ﷺ, lead to disbelief. The material cites the saying of the Prophet ﷺ that the good Muslim leaves what does not concern him.

### In short

- Earlier people asked their prophets questions and then denied the answers.
- Their questions were asked to mock and defy, not to be guided.
- The believers are warned not to do the same.
