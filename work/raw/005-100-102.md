<<<VERSE 5:100>>>
A distinction is stated flatly: “Good and evil are not equal, though you may be dazzled by the abundance of evil.” Quantity does not decide value. The verse then addresses people of reason and ties mindfulness of Allah to success.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Good and evil are not equal"** sets two opposites against each other. In Arabic the two words are antonyms. One denotes what is best and purest in a thing, and the other what is most condemned and evil.

Most scholars take the evil here to mean what is unlawful or impure and the good to mean what is lawful and pure. With Allah, and with everyone of sound reason and good taste, the pure and the impure cannot stand as equals.

**"though you may be dazzled by the abundance of evil."** acknowledges that people are sometimes awed by how much evil and impurity there is. They may even begin to call those things good because they have spread everywhere and appear to be winning.

That is described as a disease of human knowledge and awareness, and as a defect in the natural feeling for what is right. The little that is permitted is better than an abundance of what is evil.

**"So be mindful of Allah, O people of reason"** addresses those with sound minds. They are told to avoid and abandon the impermissible and to let the permissible be enough for them. One gloss describes it as fearing Allah concerning taking what is unlawful, and the addressees as people possessed of mind and reason.

**"so you may be successful.”"** promises success in this life and the next. One explanation describes it as being saved from Allah's wrath and torment, another as prospering and triumphing, and a third as attaining Paradise and being saved from the Fire by leaving the bad and doing the good.

### How wide is the comparison?

Because the two words are general, the comparison covers three things at once. It includes wealth and property, whether lawful or unlawful. It includes human beings, whether good or bad. It includes deeds and character, whether virtuous or vicious.

The natural law stated is that the lawful and the unlawful are not equal in Allah's sight, and in the same way good and bad deeds and morals are not equal, nor are virtuous and vicious people. A large amount of something does not make it the best.

### What is reported as the occasion?

Some narrations say that when liquor and its buying and selling were prohibited, a dealer who had savings from that trade asked the Prophet ﷺ whether spending that money on a good and pious cause would benefit him.

He was told that if he spent it on jihad, Hajj or something similar, it would not be worth the weight of a mosquito's feather, because Allah accepts nothing except what is pure and lawful.

The depreciation described belongs to the Hereafter. Looking deeper and keeping the end in view, lawful and unlawful wealth are not equal even in the business of this world. The benefits, favourable outcomes and genuine comfort that come through the lawful never come, and can never come, through the unlawful.

### What example is given of acting on this?

Citing Ibn Abi Hatim, Tafsir al-Durr al-Manthur reports that when 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz, the rightly guided caliph of the age of the successors, abolished the impermissible taxes imposed by the powerful before him and restored money and property taken unlawfully to their owners, the treasury emptied and ordinary revenue fell sharply.

A provincial governor wrote to complain of the low income and to ask how the affairs of government could run in such circumstances. 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz answered him by quoting this verse.

He also wrote that those before them had filled the treasury through injustice and oppression, and that they should now match it by establishing equity and justice, letting the treasury's holdings fall and not caring, since the expenditures of government would be met within the reduced income.

### What is the general lesson about numbers?

Although the occasion is a particular event, the basic message is that high and low numbers mean little. Matters cannot be measured by majority and minority, and a count of fifty one against forty nine cannot become the test of what is right and true.

A casual look at society shows less good and more evil in every comparison. Set belief against disbelief, godliness and honesty and trust against sin and debauchery, justice against oppression, knowledge against ignorance, and reason against unreason, and the second is always found in greater quantity.

Numerical abundance is therefore not absolute proof that a thing, group or party is good or true. Its goodness depends on its relevant particulars and states. If those are good it is good, and if they are bad it is bad.

### Does Islam ever treat a majority as decisive?

Yes, in a limited way. On some occasions the numerical majority is treated as decisive, namely where there is no ruling authority competent to decide by the force of argument and by assessing intrinsic qualities. To settle disputes among the people, a majority vote is then used.

The example given is appointing a leader when no imam or amir is present to decide. Majority opinion is preferred on such occasions to ward off dispute and settle matters peacefully. It never means that whatever a majority adopts becomes lawful, permissible and true.

That is why the verse addresses people of understanding. It is not the manner of wise people to be drawn to something by its quantity, or to treat the majority as the test of truth. Intelligent people do not act that way, and they are therefore commanded to fear Allah.

### What does the inward reading add?

In the language of the Shariah the vile is the forbidden and the goodly the permitted. In the language of inward reality, every occupation empty of mention and remembrance of the Real is vile, and every occupation that begins with Allah's name, holds His witnessing in the middle and ends in praise and gratitude is goodly.

'A'ishah, the sincerely truthful, once ordered a shirt to be sewn. The person sewing appears to have been heedless of remembering Allah, and his heedlessness became known to her. She told him to undo the sewing, saying that this was vile and the vile was not fitting for her.

It is also said that wealth from which Allah's due is taken and the alms tax paid is goodly, while wealth from which it is withheld is vile and stands on the edge of ruin. Muhammad al-Mustafa ﷺ is quoted saying that no property is wasted on land or sea except through withholding its alms tax.

The vile is further described as what a person accumulates and stores in this world, holding it back from expenditure and good. The goodly is what he sends ahead for himself, spends on good and stores for the next world. That is the sense of the saying that what was sent forward was gain and what was left behind was loss.

### In short

- Good and evil are not equal, and abundance does not make the evil good.
- The comparison covers wealth, people and deeds, since the lawful and unlawful cannot stand as equals in any of them.
- The reported occasion concerns savings from the liquor trade, which were worth nothing in a pious cause.
- 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz answered a governor's complaint about reduced revenue by quoting this verse.
- Majorities may settle practical disputes where no competent authority exists, but they never determine truth, and inward reality makes remembrance the mark of the goodly.

<<<VERSE 5:101>>>
A discipline of speech is taught: "Do not ask about any matter which, if made clear to you, may disturb you." The verse forbids prying into matters that carry no ruling, because an answer may create an obligation people cannot bear. It also promises that matters asked about during revelation will be made plain.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Do not ask about any matter which, if made clear to you, may disturb you."** teaches good conduct by forbidding useless questions. If people received the answers they sought, those answers might trouble them and be hard on their ears.

One gloss explains it as not asking the Prophet ﷺ about things Allah has relieved them of, which would trouble them if they were made obligatory. Another describes the trouble as the hardship that would follow disclosure.

**"But if you inquire about what is being revealed in the Quran, it will be made clear to you."** means that if they ask about matters they were forbidden to ask about, then when revelation concerning them comes to the Messenger ﷺ they will be made plain, and that is easy for Allah.

One gloss describes the timing as the period when Jibra'il brings down the Qur'an, and says that matters asked about then will be made known, that is, made obligatory. Another explains that if they ask during the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime the Qur'an will disclose those matters, and once disclosed the matters will grieve them, so they should not ask.

**"Allah has forgiven what was done ˹in the past˺."** covers what they did before this instruction. One gloss describes it as pardoning that questioning, and another as God having pardoned the things asked about so that they should not ask again.

**"And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing"** describes forgiveness toward whoever repents and forbearance toward their ignorance.

### Which reports explain the occasion?

Al-Bukhari records Anas bin Malik saying the Messenger ﷺ once delivered a speech unlike any he had heard before, in which he said that if they knew what he knew they would laugh little and weep much. The Companions covered their faces and the sound of weeping came from their chests.

A man then asked who his father was, and the Prophet ﷺ named someone. This verse was revealed afterward. Muslim, Ahmad, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i also record the hadith, and Ibn Jarir records Qatadah on the verse.

Anas also narrates that people questioned the Messenger ﷺ until they angered him. He climbed the minbar and said that they would not ask him about anything that day but he would explain it.

The Companions feared that a momentous event was beginning. Anas looked to his right and left and saw only people covering their faces and weeping. An argumentative man, said to be the son of someone other than his real father, asked who his father was, and was told that his father was Hudhafah.

Seeing anger on the Prophet's ﷺ face, 'Umar stood and declared that they were content with Allah as Lord, Islam as religion and Muhammad ﷺ as Messenger, and sought refuge with Allah from the evil of trials. The Messenger ﷺ then said that he had never witnessed good and evil as he had that day, and that Paradise and the Fire had been shown to him and he had seen them before that wall. The hadith is in the Two Sahihs, from Sa'id.

Al-Bukhari records Ibn 'Abbas saying that some people questioned the Messenger ﷺ in order to mock him. One would ask who his father was, and another would ask where his lost camel was. This verse was revealed about them.

Ahmad records 'Ali saying that when the verse making pilgrimage to the House a duty owed to Allah by those able to undertake the journey was revealed (3:97), people asked whether it was required every year. The Prophet ﷺ did not answer. They asked again and he remained silent. On the third asking he said no, and added that had he said yes it would have become obligatory, and had it become obligatory they would not have been able to bear it. This verse was then revealed. At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah also record it.

Another account in Muslim gives the questioner as Al-Aqra' ibn Habis, and one gloss names Harith ibn Yazid. A tradition records a man of Banu Asad standing during a sermon in which the Prophet ﷺ announced that Hajj had been made compulsory, and asking whether it applied every year. The Prophet ﷺ became very angry and swore by the Lord in whose hand his life is that had he said yes it would have become compulsory annually, and they would have failed to perform it every year and so fallen into denying the truth. He told them to leave what he leaves, to do what he orders and not to do what he prohibits.

These differing identifications of the questioner are preserved as reported rather than reduced to one name.

### What general rule do the reports give?

The wording indicates a prohibition on asking about matters that a person would regret knowing. It is better to avoid such questions. Do not ask about things that have no ruling yet, because a question may cause a difficult ruling to be ordained.

A hadith states that the worst offender among the Muslims is the one who asks whether a matter is unlawful, and it becomes unlawful because of his asking. In the Sahih the Messenger ﷺ tells people to leave him as he has left them, and says that those before them were destroyed by excessive questioning and by disputing with their prophets.

An authentic hadith describes Allah as having ordained obligations that must not be neglected, set limits that must not be crossed and prohibited things that must not be committed, and as having left other matters without ruling out of mercy rather than forgetfulness, so they must not be asked about.

The instruction given to the community is to do what is ordered to the best of its ability and to leave what is forbidden, without digging into matters for which no injunction exists.

### Does the prohibition end with revelation?

The wording restricts disclosure to the period while the Qur'an is being revealed, which indicates that prophethood and revelation would be discontinued after its completion.

After that discontinuation, the consequences once feared, namely a new injunction, an optional matter becoming obligatory or a secret being disclosed through revelation, are no longer likely. Minting unnecessary questions and prying into them remains prohibited all the same.

The reason is simple. It wastes one's own time and the time of others. The Prophet ﷺ said that part of a person's being a good Muslim is leaving what does not concern him.

Many Muslims busy themselves with subjects that have no bearing on conduct, such as the name of the mother of Musa (Moses) or the exact length and breadth of the ark of Nuh (Noah). Asking such questions is blameworthy, particularly since those given to them are often unaware of the basics of their religion. Absorbing what is wasteful usually leaves a person deprived of what is necessary.

The great body of work by Muslim jurists answering hypothetical religious problems is not included in this blame. Later events showed that future generations needed those answers, so they do not fall under wasteful questioning. It is also part of Islamic teaching that a person should not occupy himself with any activity, whether intellectual or practical, or with any task or conversation, that wastes precious time without benefit in this life or the next.

### What is the inward warning about asking?

It tells a person not to circle the stations of the great ones, not to seek access to their states and not to ask about their stages. Doing so shows him that his own level falls short, and he despairs, and despair is the seed of remorse and the foundation of idleness.

A merchant from the bazaar came to Junayd and asked what hope remained for ordinary people if servanthood was what the pir possessed, calling it a place for despair. The pir answered that a commander's army is not made up entirely of intimate companions. There are also dog keepers and herdsmen, and in a kingdom all are of use, each living in his own place and measure.

Though you are beautiful, do not disdain the ugly, since in this realm flies are as useful as peacocks. The dervish is held up as exalted who prayed to be accepted as a lover, or failing that as a servant, or failing that as a dog, asking that if he is not given the foremost place of honour he may at least be kept outside the door like the dogs.

The inward reading therefore turns the prohibition around. Prying into the states of others breeds despair, and the remedy is to hold one's own place with contentment rather than to measure oneself against stations not given to one.

### In short

- Questions that may produce an unbearable obligation are forbidden, and matters asked about during revelation will be disclosed.
- The reports give several occasions, including a speech that moved the Companions to tears, mocking questions about fathers and lost camels, and the question about annual Hajj.
- The rule continues after revelation ended, because needless questioning wastes time and deprives a person of what is necessary.
- Juridical work answering hypothetical cases is distinguished from wasteful prying.
- The inward warning adds that asking about the stations of others breeds despair, while contentment with one's own place is the cure.

<<<VERSE 5:102>>>
The warning is given a precedent: "Some people before you asked such questions then denied their answers." Earlier communities asked and were answered, and the answers did them no good. The verse explains why, and it is the reason the preceding prohibition matters.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Some people before you asked such questions"** describes communities who put such questions to their prophets. They were given answers in the form of revealed explanations of the rules concerned.

**"then denied their answers"** states the outcome. They did not believe the answers and so became disbelievers on that account. One gloss describes their disbelief as neglecting to implement what had been explained, and another as failing to carry out an order once it was given.

### Why did the answers not help them?

The rulings were made plain to them, yet they gained nothing from them, because they had not asked in order to be guided. They asked in order to mock and to defy.

That is the difference between a question asked to act on the answer and a question asked to test the one who answers. The first is answered and obeyed. The second is answered and abandoned, and the abandonment becomes disbelief.

### What does this add to the prohibition?

It shows that the danger of excessive questioning is not only that a burden may be imposed. The deeper danger is that a person may receive an answer he then refuses, and the refusal hardens into rejection of the message itself.

Earlier communities are described as ruined through the multiplication of questions, asking repeatedly about matters Allah and His Messenger ﷺ had not made obligatory. Optional matters became obligatory in consequence, and they then fell into disobeying them.

The precedent therefore explains why the community is told to leave what has been left, to do what is commanded to the best of its ability and to avoid what is forbidden.

### In short

- Communities before this one asked such questions and then disbelieved the answers given.
- Their disbelief took the form of neglecting to implement what had been explained.
- They asked to mock and defy rather than to be guided, which is why the answers did them no good.
- The precedent shows that repeated questioning can turn optional matters into obligations that are then disobeyed.
