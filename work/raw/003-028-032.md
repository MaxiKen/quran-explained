<<<VERSE 3:28>>>
The verse orders a boundary of loyalty: "Believers should not take disbelievers as guardians instead of the believers—and whoever does so will have nothing to hope for from Allah—unless it is a precaution against their tyranny. And Allah warns you about Himself. And to Allah is the final return". What is forbidden is not every dealing with non-Muslims. It is taking them as intimate guardians in place of believers, and the material separates that act carefully from kindness, courtesy and trade.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Believers should not take disbelievers as guardians instead of the believers"**: believers are prohibited from becoming supporters of disbelievers or taking them as comrades with whom they build friendship, rather than the believers. In the situation pictured, the people sought as friends were the hypocrites, ʿAbdullāh Ibn Ubayy and his companions, together with the Jews, taken in a search for might and honour in preference to sincere believers.

**"and whoever does so will have nothing to hope for from Allah"**: whoever commits what Allah prohibited in this will be discarded by Him. He has no connection with Allah, no honour, mercy or protection from Him, and does not belong to the religion of God in any way.

**"unless it is a precaution against their tyranny"**: except believers who in some places or times fear for their safety from disbelievers. Such people may save themselves by speaking toward disbelievers in a friendly way while their hearts dislike it. The word for the safeguard, tuqātan, is the verbal noun of taqiyyah, and the allowance is outward only, never inward. It was stated before the hegemony of Islam, and it applies to a person living in a land where he has no say.

**"And Allah warns you about Himself. And to Allah is the final return"**: anger and severe torment await supporters of His enemies and enemies of His friends. Warning plants fear of wrath for patronage. After death the journey ends with Him, where deeds receive reward or punishment.

### The same prohibition across the Qur'an

Allah states the ban in a series of forms across the Holy Qur'an, including Surah al-Mujadalah and Surah al-Mumtahinah. "O you who believe! Take not My enemies and your enemies as friends, showing affection towards them", ending with the words that whoever of you does that has gone astray from the straight path (60:1). "O you who believe! Take not for friends disbelievers instead of believers. Do you wish to offer Allah a manifest proof against yourselves?" (4:144). "O you who believe! Take not the Jews and the Christians as friends, they are but friends of each other. And whoever befriends them, then surely, he is one of them" (5:51). After the faithful believers are shown supporting the faithful among the Muhājirīn, the Anṣār and the Bedouins, the other side is matched against them: "And those who disbelieve are allies of one another, (and) if you do not behave the same, there will be Fitnah and oppression on the earth, and a great mischief and corruption" (8:73).

Al-Bukhārī recorded that Abū ad-Dardāʾ said, "We smile in the face of some people although our hearts curse them." Al-Bukhārī also transmitted that al-Ḥasan said, "The Tuqyah is allowed until the Day of Resurrection." The warning that follows the exception is then explained in concrete terms as a warning about unlawful killing, unlawful sex, unlawful property, intoxicants, false testimony and associating partners with Allah, the whole field of disobedience that patronage could drag a person into.

### Four degrees of relation with non-Muslims

The verses on this subject come in many places with varying shades, so the discussion collects them and distinguishes degrees. Any emotionally involved friendship from the heart is absolutely forbidden, ḥarām, while formal friendship at the level of mutual dealings is permissible, though not favoured when unnecessary.

1. **Muwālāt, close friendship.** The first degree comes from the heart: affection and love with intense emotional commitment. This kind is restricted to true Muslims, and a Muslim is not permitted it with a non-Muslim.

2. **Muwāsāt, sympathetic concern.** The second degree is sympathy, kindness and concern: charitable help and support, condolence and consolation, and any well-meaning wish for another's good. Except for disbelievers at war with Muslims, this is permissible with all other non-Muslims. Its detailed rule is given in Sūrat al-Mumtaḥinah: "Allah does not forbid you from treating those who do not fight you on your faith, nor have they driven you out of your homes, with benevolence and equity" (60:8).

3. **Mudārāt, cordial conduct.** Level three covers ordinary courtesy: pleasant manners, friendly behaviour and polite adequacy. Non-Muslims may receive it broadly, particularly to communicate faith, host guests or avert possible harm. Because cordiality can resemble close friendship, the verse's guard-yourself exception concerns this level and exempts it from prohibited intimacy.

4. **Muʿāmalāt, dealings.** The fourth degree is transactions in business, employment, wages, industry and technology. These are permissible with non-Muslims except when they harm the general body of Muslims. The continuing practice of the Prophet ﷺ, the rightly-guided caliphs and other Companions proves it. Muslim jurists accordingly prohibited selling arms to disbelievers at war with Muslims, while trade and allied activities are permitted, as is employing non-Muslims or being employed in their institutions.

The conduct of the Prophet ﷺ fills the permitted degrees with examples. When Makkah was gripped by famine he personally went out to help enemies who had driven him from his home town. At the conquest of Makkah, with all those enemies under his power, he set them free, saying there would be amnesty that day and not even censure for past tyranny. Non-Muslim prisoners of war were treated with a tenderness many people cannot claim even toward their children. Disbelievers inflicted injuries and pain on him, yet he never raised his hand in revenge and did not even wish them ill. A delegation of Banū Thaqīf, who had not yet embraced Islam, was honoured with lodging in the Prophet's mosque, the most honourable place for Muslims. ʿUmar gave stipends and allowances to needy non-Muslim dhimmīs, and the accounts of the rightly-guided caliphs and Companions are full of similar conduct. Another verse states the limit from the other side: "You shall not find those who believe in Allah and in the Hereafter having friendship with those who have enmity with Allah and His messenger, even though they may be their fathers or sons or brothers or members of their tribe" (58:22). All of the merciful treatment falls under concern, cordiality or dealings. None of it is the close and intimate friendship that is forbidden.

### Why the close bond is blocked

Life has a purpose, unlike the purposeless cycle of an animal or jungle tree that sprouts, flourishes and dies. A central aim orders every phase: eating, drinking, standing, sitting, sleeping, waking, living and dying. Conformity makes action sound, while opposition corrupts it. For Rūmī, remembering and serving the Creator defines life, and devotion's absence makes it shameful. Lose the aim and crowds resemble humans only as empty shells. Believers declare the aim solemnly: "My prayer and my sacrifice and my life and my death are all for Allah, the Lord of the Worlds" (6:162).

Everything else then follows the purpose, including business, government, politics and personal and social relations. Those who oppose the purpose are man's worst enemies, and Satan stands foremost: "Surely, Satan is your enemy, so take him as enemy" (35:6). People who follow Satan's allurements and oppose Allah's injunctions cannot be owed deep love and intimate friendship, because a person with a definite purpose makes all friendships and enmities, agreements and disagreements, serve that purpose. The Prophet ﷺ said in a hadith in al-Bukhārī and Muslim, "Whoever loves for the sake of Allah, and hates for the sake of Allah alone, has perfected his faith." Faith, īmān, stays incomplete until love, friendship, hatred and enmity are subordinated to Allah. Deep emotional commitment therefore belongs to whoever pursues the same purpose and obeys the same Lord.

### The friendship of Allah and its reliable handhold

Faith completed sends the traveller on tawḥīd's path back into God's friendship. Friendship means alignment: loving His friends and opposing His enemies. The master of the sharīʿah called love in God and hate in God faith's most reliable handhold. One report says Allah revealed to an earlier prophet a searching question for servants who renounced the world for comfort and worshipped for reputation:

> "In this world you have taken up renunciation in order to hurry to your own comfort and be relieved of this world's suffering. With your obedience and worship you have sought your own exaltation and your own good name. Now look: What have you done for Me? Have you ever loved My friends? Have you ever taken My enemies as your enemies?"

A parallel address to Jesus says:

> "O Jesus, if the worship of all the inhabitants of heaven and earth accompanies you in the path of the religion, but you have no love for My friends and no enmity for My enemies, then your worship has been useless and without profit."

Abū Idrīs al-Khawlānī told Muʿādh of loving him in God's path. Muʿādh answered with a joyful report he had heard about Resurrection Day: seats encircle the Splendorous Throne for faces shining like full moons. Terror surrounds everyone else, while these people remain secure and still. They are simply those who love one another in God. Another saying makes Allah's love obligatory for people who love, gather, visit and spend for one another in Him. Mujāhid added that when God's friends exchange smiles, sins drop like leaves from a tree.

### In short

- Believers may not take disbelievers as intimate guardians in place of believers, though outward friendliness under real fear is allowed while the heart refuses (60:1, 4:144, 5:51, 8:73).
- Kindness, courtesy and trade with non-Muslims are degrees below close friendship and are permitted, as the Prophet's famine relief, amnesty at Makkah and stipends for dhimmīs show.
- The blocked bond is the heart's, because life has one purpose (6:162), and perfected faith loves and hates for Allah's sake alone.

For He knows whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is Most Capable of everything.”" Close friendship belongs to the heart, and no one knows the affairs of the heart but Allah, so a person who loves disbelievers inwardly while denying it verbally has no workable denial before Him.

### Phrase by phrase

The verse addressed hypocrites and Jews, including the forbidden friendship they concealed.

### Why an all-knowing God makes servants careful

Allah tells His servants that He knows the secret and apparent matters and that nothing about them escapes His observation. His knowledge encompasses them in all conditions, times, days and instances, and it encompasses everything in heaven and earth, leaving not even an atom's weight, or anything smaller, in earth, seas or mountains unobserved. Since He has perfect knowledge of everything they do and is able to punish promptly, the verse alerts servants to fear Him enough not to commit what He prohibits and dislikes. He gives respite to some people, and then He punishes them, swift and mighty in taking account.

Meanwhile a believer still owes justice and fairness to every human being, Muslim or non-Muslim. The unlawful friendship is alliance with non-Muslims at war with Islam. In God's eyes intention carries the issue. His scale catches every wrongdoer, and nothing human escapes His sight in public or in private. Yet sincere intention receives consideration, while God-conscious behaviour matters everywhere because account awaits everything.

Knowledge also consoles. Because nothing escapes Him, grief over calamity can soften. Succour and response approach, trials disappear, and assistance and protection are hastened. What finally matters is whether Islam lives in the heart. Faith means love for God springing from the heart's core. Such lovers merit His attention. A bond thus formed brings forgiveness for failings. Arrogance meets hardness, modesty meets gentleness.

### In short

- Concealed friendship and revealed hostility are equally known to Allah, who knows heaven and earth and can punish without delay.
- The heart's inward state is the ground of accountability, so no verbal denial can cover it.
- The same knowledge comforts the afflicted: assistance and protection are hastened by the One from whom nothing is hidden.

<<<VERSE 3:30>>>
The verse sets the meeting at which all of it appears: "˹Watch for˺ the Day when every soul will be presented with whatever good it has done. And it will wish that its misdeeds were far off. And Allah warns you about Himself. And Allah is Ever Gracious to ˹His˺ servants". Good deeds arrive as joy and evil deeds as grief, and the wish to be far from one's own sins is the human picture of that Day.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Watch for˺ the Day when every soul will be presented with whatever good it has done"**: Resurrection Day displays both good and evil actions before each servant. "On that Day man will be informed of what he sent forward, and what he left behind" (75:13). Registers contain the good without deficiency, present for its owner to see.

**"And it will wish that its misdeeds were far off"**: good deeds bring happiness and delight, but visible evil causes sadness and anger. A person would rather disown that work or place an immense interval (in one image, as long as sunrise-to-sunset) between himself and it so it could never reach him. Even his former devil-companion, who encouraged evil through life, receives the wish: "Would that between me and you were the distance of the two easts, a horrible companion (indeed)!" (43:38).

**"And Allah warns you about Himself. And Allah is Ever Gracious to ˹His˺ servants"**: He warns you against His punishment, and the warning is repeated here for emphasis. Then He brings hope to His servants so they do not despair of His mercy or lose heart at His kindness. He is full of pity for the believers.

### Kindness inside the warning

Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī found kindness within the threat: Allah warns people against Himself precisely because He cares for them. Other readings stress mercy toward creation. He wants servants on the straight path and chosen religion, following His honoured Messenger, while repeated warning prevents despair by promising kindness after fear.

The sight of that Day will be dreadful. When the veil of the divine test is lifted, the world of the Hereafter appears and the fruits of a person's actions stand before him. The things of this world that had looked so pleasing become the very things he wants far away. Whoever is proved wrong on that scale cannot escape.

### Fear and hope by turns

The pairing teaches changing states. Servants move between fear and hope, contraction and expansion, harshness and generosity. "God warns you about Himself" first bewilders a person until selfhood loosens. Then clemency seats him in gentleness's ship and carries him from confoundedness's whirlpool toward intimacy's shore.

An elder of the religion wondered whether self's whirlpool could be escaped aboard deliverance, whether compassion's hand could catch his among wishing waves, whether unity's proof could draw dispersion's veil from his eyes, and whether his heart could rest inside the body. Avarice has emptied a hundred thousand merchants' purses of pure gold seeking just such an alchemy.

### In short

- On the Day of Resurrection every soul meets its good with delight and its evil with grief, wishing for an immense distance from its sins (75:13, 43:38).
- The repeated warning is itself kindness, meant to keep servants on the straight path and stop them despairing of mercy.
- Inwardly the verse alternates fear and hope until confoundedness gives way to intimacy.

<<<VERSE 3:31>>>
Here is the test for every claim of love: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “If you ˹sincerely˺ love Allah, then follow me; Allah will love you and forgive your sins. For Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.”" Love is a secret with no yardstick of its own, so Allah sets the touchstone outside the claimant. Follow the Messenger, and the claim stands. Refuse, and it falls, whatever the words were.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “If you ˹sincerely˺ love Allah, then follow me"**: say to mankind that whoever really loves Allah must follow Muhammad ﷺ, following what he brought on the inside and the outside. Love leaves traces and signs, and the educated guess about another's love is made from visible conditions and dealings. Here the visible test is exact: match the claim against where, how often and how well a person follows him.

**"Allah will love you and forgive your sins"**: what you earn is much more than what you sought in loving Him, because Allah will love you back, and by following the Messenger ﷺ you earn His love and forgiveness together, with the blessing of his mission. He will reward you and forgive your sins, the sins committed previously by the one who now follows, and He is Merciful to him.

**"For Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.”"**: Forgiving to whoever repents, and Merciful toward whoever dies in a state of repentance.

### A verse that judges claims

This honourable verse judges against everyone who claims to love Allah without following the way of Muhammad ﷺ. The claim stays unproven unless Muhammad's law and religion govern his speech, behaviour and circumstances. The Ṣaḥīḥ preserves the Messenger's ruling: "Whoever commits an act that does not conform with our matter (religion), then it will be rejected of him." Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and several scholars among the Salaf commented, "Some people claimed that they love Allah. So Allah tested them with this Ayah." The genuine and the false in a claim become visible in the same proportion as the following. The stronger the following, the truer the claim, and the weaker the following, the weaker the claim. The Prophet's own words sharpen it into an equation: "He who obeyed Muhammad ﷺ obeyed Allah and he who disobeyed Muhammad ﷺ disobeyed Allah."

### The disputes that surrounded the command

Several settings frame the command. Idol-worshippers said their worship sought closeness to God through love, prompting the answer. Jews also claimed divine sonship and beloved status. After revelation, ʿAbdullāh Ibn Ubayy accused Muhammad ﷺ of demanding Christian-style love for Jesus from Muslims. Jews echoed that Islam wanted him taken as a compassionate Lord like Jesus. The next verse (3:32) answers those objections. Claimed love here never means adoration of Muhammad ﷺ. It means heartfelt obedience, modelled perfectly by a Prophet whose own obedience God attested. A believer nominal in sentiment but disobedient in life aligns with denial, because nobody loves a beloved while embracing the beloved's enemy.

### Intellect as watchman, not as leader

At reality's level, fervour hears another summons: step into the Prophet's tracks, because the task is already prepared in his footsteps. Intellect should watch the gate, not hold the reins. It can carry rulings' saddle-cloth, but religion's magnificence exceeds intellectual scales and categories of substance and accident. Seek the object through prophethood, not autonomous reason.

This faith is shared with the 124,000 prophets and messengers. Scripture says: "He has set down for you as the religion that with which He counseled Noah" (42:13). Only two supports uphold it: "God said" and "God's Messenger said." Remove the theologians' substances, accidents, distinctions and intellectual determinations from creation entirely, and neither religion's threshold nor the Sunnah's gate would suffer an iota. Folk of the Sunnah heard perfection announced: "Today I have perfected your religion for..." (5:3). Kalām's route is shadowy, its shadows among the worst. Follow instead the Hadith people's road, where Muṣṭafā's imamate suffices. Confusion is discarded in the simple religion of servants and old women.

### A covenant made before the world

Before Adam's dust and the world itself existed, created spirits gathered and the prophets' spirits covenanted: "Say: If you love God, follow me." Serving the empire's chieftain and centre of fortune required a tightened belt and willing assistance. The divine retelling says: "They said, 'We assent.' He said, 'So bear witness'" (3:81). Spirits then vanished into nonexistence's concealment. Across breaths in the field of power and decree, entrances into the world came singly. Adam, Ibrāhīm (Abraham), Mūsā (Moses) and ʿĪsā (Jesus) passed through and entered dust, as did thousands of prophets. Eventually the field emptied and Muḥammad was called to his moment.

His single imperial step toppled fourteen battlements from Caesar's castle. All 360 Kaabah idols fell face down. Truth's arrival and falsehood's departure sounded across the world's four corners: "The truth has come, and falsehood has..." (17:81). Prophethood's pearl rested upon exaltation's carpet while messengerhood's pavilion stretched ropes east to west. Noble traits spread like gems as beauty's mask lifted, matching his saying: "I was sent out with the all-comprehensive words to complete the noble character traits." Eyes that saw his sanctified spirit let spirit detach from heart. One shell opened in love's ocean and pearls filled the water. In the language of the Folk of the Ṭarīqah, "You love God" names dispersion, whereas "God will love you" names togetherness.

### In short

- The truth of loving Allah is measured by following the Messenger in statement, action and condition, and the reward is greater than the claim: Allah's love and forgiveness.
- Occasions of the verse include idol-worship for closeness, Jewish claims of sonship, and Ibn Ubayy's charge that Muslims were being made to love Muhammad ﷺ as Christians loved Jesus.
- Read inwardly, intellect serves as watchman while prophethood leads, and the command echoes a covenant taken from the prophets before the world (42:13, 5:3, 3:81, 17:81).

<<<VERSE 3:32>>>
The sequence closes with a blunt order: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Obey Allah and His Messenger.” If they still turn away, then truly Allah does not like the disbelievers". The obedience asked for is the belief in the Oneness of Allah that the Messenger enjoins, together with what Allah obligated and the Messenger practised. Turning away after that is named by its final name.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Obey Allah and His Messenger.”"**: obey Allah, who has obligated His commands, and His Messenger in his practices and instructions. Fulfilling His instructions and avoiding His prohibitions, following His sacred law and the Messenger's commands, is what obedience means here.

**"If they still turn away, then truly Allah does not like the disbelievers"**: if they object to obedience and turn their backs, Allah will chastise them. The shift to the open noun, "the disbelievers", in place of the pronoun "they", makes the judgement explicit. For the Jews and hypocrites addressed, turning away from obeying them became the disbelief named here.

### Why refusal is called disbelief

Defying the Messenger's path is kufr. Claiming to love Allah or draw near to Him does not change its status until the claimant follows the unlettered Prophet, the final messenger to humans and jinn. Earlier prophets and mighty messengers, had they lived in his era, could only follow, obey and remain under his law. The prophets' covenant verse will make that explicit (3:81).

Belief in prophethood and the necessity of following the Messenger stand here beside the Oneness of Allah, and rejecting either is counted the same kind of act. The psychological fact behind it is plain. A person cannot truly love someone and love that person's enemy, and when the beloved is the Creator and Sustainer, love for Him necessarily awakens the desire to obey Him in all matters. Sentiment that produces no obedience is false. The example of total obedience has been testified by God Himself in His Prophet, and the authentic model of a life of God-worship is the one his messenger handed down.

### A mercy hidden in the naming

He commanded obedience, then said, "But if they turn their backs." He did not say, then Allah loves not the disobedient, ʿāṣūn. He said the disbelievers, kāfirūn. The address therefore indicates that He loves the believers even when they are disobedient. The threat of this verse lands on rejection, not on sin inside faith. Obedience remains commanded, and the previous verse remains the proof of love, but the person who believes and falls short is still inside the circle of those He loves.

### In short

- Obeying Allah and His Messenger in belief, command and prohibition is the form love must take, and refusal is named disbelief.
- Every earlier prophet, had he lived in Muhammad's time, would have followed him (3:81).
- The verse calls the turners-away disbelievers rather than disobedient, which indicates that Allah loves believers even when they disobey.
