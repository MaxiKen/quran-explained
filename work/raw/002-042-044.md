<<<VERSE 2:42>>>
The address to the Children of Israel continues with a prohibition aimed especially at their scholars. "Do not mix truth with falsehood or hide the truth knowingly." Two things are forbidden here, and two things are commanded by implication. They are forbidden to blend truth with falsehood and to conceal the truth, and they are therefore required to make the truth known and to explain it plainly. The verse speaks to people who possessed real knowledge, who had the description of the Prophet ﷺ in their own Books, and who were tempted by fear and greed to blur it or bury it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not mix truth with falsehood"** forbids them to confuse the truth that Allah revealed with falsehood of their own making. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported that Ibn ʿAbbās explained it simply: do not mix truth with falsehood and facts with lies. Qatādah said it means do not mix Judaism and Christianity with Islam, when you know that Islam is Allah's religion and that Judaism and Christianity as now practised are innovations with no origin in Him, and al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī is reported to have said the same. Another explanation is that the Jewish scholars would mix the description of the false messiah with the description of Muḥammad ﷺ, so that people could no longer tell which was which. The verse makes clear that it is not permissible to combine truth and falsehood in such a way that the listener is left confused about what the truth is.

**"or hide the truth knowingly"** forbids the second offence. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq reported that Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as: do not hide the knowledge you have of My Messenger and of what he was sent with, for his description, which you know well, is written in the Books in your possession. They knew it to be the truth and were convinced of it, and yet they concealed it. "Knowingly" may also mean: while you know the immense harm this evil will do to people, who will be misled and brought to the Fire by following the falsehood you have blended into the truth. Concealing the truth out of fear or greed is forbidden, and it is this particular temptation, the fear of losing followers and income, that the verse confronts.

### The scholar and the caliph

Al-Qurṭubī related in his commentary a story that illustrates the duty this verse imposes. It comes through a chain of reliable reporters and is taken from the Musnad of ad-Dārimī.

During a visit to Madīnah, the Umayyad caliph Sulaymān ibn ʿAbd al-Malik asked whether anyone was still alive who had kept company with a Companion of the Prophet ﷺ. He was told that only Abū Ḥāzim remained, and he sent for him. The caliph opened by accusing him of discourtesy and disloyalty, since every person of standing in Madīnah had come to pay respects except him. Abū Ḥāzim answered: may Allah protect the leader of the Muslims from saying what is not true. You did not know my name before today and I had never set eyes on you, so how could I have come to meet you, and how is that disloyalty? The caliph looked around for support, and Imām az-Zuhrī spoke up to say that Abū Ḥāzim was right and the caliph wrong.

The caliph changed the subject. Why, he asked, do I dislike the thought of dying? Because, said Abū Ḥāzim, you have made your world flourish and let your dwelling in the next world fall into ruin, and no one likes leaving a thriving city for a wasteland. The caliph admitted it and asked what it would be like to stand before Allah tomorrow. Abū Ḥāzim said that the one who has done good will come to Allah like a traveller returning to the people he loves, while the one who has done evil will come like a runaway slave dragged back to his master. The caliph wept and sighed that he wished he knew how Allah would deal with him. Weigh your deeds against the Book of Allah, said Abū Ḥāzim, and you will know. Which verse, asked the caliph? Abū Ḥāzim recited: "Indeed, the virtuous will be in bliss, and the wicked will be in Hell" (82:13-14). The caliph observed that Allah's mercy is vast and may reach even wrongdoers, and Abū Ḥāzim answered with another verse: "Indeed, Allah's mercy is always close to the good-doers" (7:56).

The questions went on. Who is the most honourable of Allah's servants? Those who are considerate of their fellow human beings and have the understanding to recognise the truth. Which is the best of deeds? Carrying out what Allah has made obligatory and keeping away from what He has forbidden. Which prayer is most likely to be answered? The prayer of a person for someone who has done him good. Which is the best charity? Giving a person in distress as much as you can despite your own need, without making him feel indebted and without hurting him by putting him off. Which is the best speech? Telling the truth plainly and without reservation to someone who can harm you or from whom you hope for a favour. Who is the wisest of the Muslims? The one whose actions are governed by obedience to Allah and who calls others to it. And who is the most foolish? The one who helps another person commit an injustice, for he has sold his religion to serve that person's worldly interests.

Then the caliph asked pointedly what Abū Ḥāzim thought of him. Abū Ḥāzim tried to be excused, but the caliph insisted on a word of counsel, so he said: leader of the Muslims, your forefathers seized power over people by the sword and against their will, after killing hundreds of men, and then they left this world. I wish you could know what they are saying now, after their death, and what people are saying about them. One of the courtiers, afraid of the caliph's displeasure, rebuked Abū Ḥāzim for speaking so rudely. He replied that he had said nothing rude, only what Allah commands, for Allah has bound the scholars to speak the truth before people and not to hide it, and he recited the verse about those who were given the Scripture, that they must "make it known to people and not hide it" (3:187).

How can we put ourselves right now, asked the caliph? Give up your pride, Abū Ḥāzim said, acquire fellow feeling for people, and give them justly what is due to them. Would he come and live at court? May Allah protect me from it, he said, for I fear that if I lived with you I would come to love your wealth and splendour and suffer a grievous punishment for it in the next world. Was there anything he needed? Yes, he said: help me escape the Fire and enter Paradise. That is not in my power, said the caliph. Then there is nothing you can do for me, he replied. Asked to pray for him, Abū Ḥāzim prayed: O Allah, if Sulaymān is pleasing to You, make the good of this world and the next easy for him, and if he is Your enemy, drag him by the hair toward the deeds You approve of. Asked for one piece of special advice, he said: keep it short. Fear your Lord and revere Him to the point that He never finds you where He has forbidden you to be and never misses you from where He has commanded you to be.

Later the caliph sent him a hundred gold dinars. Abū Ḥāzim returned them with a letter: if these dinars are payment for my words, then blood and pork are cleaner in my eyes than this money. If you consider it my due from the public treasury, then Islam has hundreds of scholars and servants. Send the same to each of them and I can accept it. Otherwise I have no need of it. His refusal shows that no wage may be taken for worship or for an act of obedience to Allah.

### Why they did it

As the recognised representatives of revealed religion in Arabia, the Israelites were the people others asked about the Arab Prophet. Feigning innocence, they would raise points designed to turn people away from him and his mission. They urged others to follow the truth and live by it, yet when their own turn came they failed to accept it. When answering the call of God means giving up positions of honour and prestige, those who owe their religious rank to worldly elevation find it extremely hard. For the truly devout these considerations are no obstacle. Where others find their satisfaction in worldly pleasures, the true believer finds it in remembering God, spending for His cause, obeying His commands and walking steadfastly on His path. He knows that what deserves fear is God's punishment, not worldly loss.

### A reading for the inner life

Take care not to mix truth and falsehood, truthfulness with lying, the approved with the unapproved. This does not mean refusing to recognise falsehood. You must recognise falsehood in order to avoid it, and recognise truth in order to pursue it. The Prophet ﷺ, al-Muṣṭafā, prayed: O God, show us truth as truth and grant us to choose it, and show us falsehood as falsehood and grant us to avoid it. The masters of the realities read the verse as a warning not to mix the share of the lower self with the food of the heart, for the two cannot coexist. The owner of a heart rejoices in the rightful claim of truth, while the servant of the self is tied to the self's portion, and these two cannot meet. This world is trifling and the next world precious, and these cannot agree. Friendship with the Creator is felicity without beginning or end, while friendship with created things is a ready affliction. "Allah does not place two hearts in any person's chest" (33:4). Worship of God and worship of self are opposites, and they cannot be housed in one nature. Love for the self and love for the loving Friend will not both reach you. Choose one or the other, for you will not have both.

### In short

- The Jewish scholars are forbidden to blend truth with falsehood, for instance by confusing the description of the Prophet ﷺ with that of the false messiah, and forbidden to conceal the truth they know, above all his description in their Books.
- By implication the verse commands scholars to declare the truth plainly, as Abū Ḥāzim did before the caliph Sulaymān ibn ʿAbd al-Malik, citing 3:187, and to refuse payment for doing so.
- Concealing truth from fear or greed is the besetting temptation of those whose religious rank depends on worldly standing.
- Inwardly, truth and falsehood cannot share one heart (33:4): a person must choose between the self's portion and the heart's food.

<<<VERSE 2:43>>>
After three verses about faith and doctrine, the address to the Children of Israel turns to deeds, naming only the most important of them. "Establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and bow down with those who bow down." These are commands to people who have accepted the truth, telling them what the law now requires. They are also, read closely, an invitation to join the Muslim community in its worship. This is because the bowing of the Islamic prayer is something the Israelites' own rituals did not include.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Establish prayer"** means perform the five prescribed prayers fully, with their obligatory and recommended parts. Muqātil said that this command to the People of the Book means praying behind the Prophet ﷺ. Whenever the Qur'an insists on prayer it uses, with one or two exceptions, the word iqāmah, "establishing." Literally the word means making something straight or keeping it firmly in place, and since what stands straight and firm usually lasts, it also means establishing a thing and making it permanent. Joined to the word ṣalāh in the Qur'an and the hadith, it signifies not merely saying prayers but performing the five prayers steadfastly, in the prescribed form, at the prescribed hours, and with all their necessary conditions. The great rewards and blessings promised for prayer are all tied to iqāmah in this sense. The Qur'an says, for example, that "genuine prayer should deter one from indecency and wickedness" (29:45). Prayer bears this fruit only when it is performed in the full sense of iqāmah. If one meets people who are regular in their prayers yet indulge in indecent or evil conduct, one should not doubt the verse. Such people have been praying. But they have not been observing the conditions of iqāmah.

**"pay alms-tax"** commands zakāh from one's wealth. Muqātil said that for the People of the Book this meant paying it to the Prophet ﷺ. The Arabic word zakāh has two root meanings, to purify and to grow. Zakāh is not a tax imposed by the state or by society. In the language of the Sharīʿah it is the portion of one's possessions that is set apart and spent in full accordance with the injunctions of the Sharīʿah, out of wealth that Allah placed in one's hands. The verse by itself does not show that prayer and alms were obligatory on the Israelites before Islam, but another verse does: "Allah made a covenant with the Children of Israel and appointed twelve leaders from among them and then said, 'I am truly with you. If you establish prayer, pay alms-tax…'" (5:12). The two duties bound them too, even if their outward forms were different.

**"and bow down with those who bow down"** means pray with those who pray, that is, with the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions, and be humble before Allah together with the humble of his community. Muqātil said that Allah here commands the People of the Book to be with the Ummah of Muḥammad ﷺ and among them, and the phrase also means: be among the believers in doing the best of what they do, and foremost among their deeds is prayer. This passage was revealed concerning the Jewish scholars who used to tell their relatives among the Muslims to stay firm on the religion of Muḥammad ﷺ because it was the truth.

Lexically, rukūʿ means bowing, and could even cover prostration, which is bowing in its fullest form, but in the terminology of the Sharīʿah it means the particular bowing prescribed in prayer. Why is this one gesture singled out from all the movements of prayer? One answer is that it is a figure of speech in which a part stands for the whole, just as "the recitation of the Qur'an at dawn" stands for the dawn prayer in another verse, and just as the word sajdah in some hadiths stands for a whole unit of prayer or even the whole prayer. On this reading the verse simply means: pray along with those who pray. A fuller answer is that the ritual prayer of the Israelites and others included prostration but not bowing. This particular bowing, rukūʿ, is peculiar to the Islamic prayer, so "those who bow down" can only be the Muslim community. The verse thus calls the Israelites to embrace Islam and join Muslim prayer.

### Patience and prayer as a cure

It was usually love of money and love of power that made it hard for the Jews, and especially their scholars, to accept Islam. These verses prescribe the remedy for both diseases: fortify yourself with ṣabr and ṣalāh, patience and prayer. "Patience" is a weak translation of ṣabr, which has three senses: bearing pain and misfortune patiently, restraining oneself from sin, and remaining steadfast in obedience to Allah. In this wide sense patience is the perfect cure for love of money, because money is never an end in itself but only a means of satisfying appetites, and once a person has firmly resolved not to follow his appetites like a slave, he no longer needs much money, and love of it no longer blinds him. Prayer is the cure for ambition and love of power, because inwardly and outwardly it is an exercise in humility, and the more carefully it is performed, the more it purifies a person of love of money and power, of ambition and pride. These being the real substance of every spiritual disorder, once they are brought under control it becomes easy to accept Islam and to stay firm in faith.

There is a difference between the two remedies. Patience requires only the restraint or abandonment of excessive appetites and needless desires. Prayer requires, in addition, the performance of prescribed actions and the temporary renunciation of perfectly lawful desires and needs that the Sharīʿah otherwise permits, such as eating, drinking, speaking and walking. It requires this five times a day and night at fixed hours. Once a person decides to give up needless desires, the urge weakens within days, so patience is not so difficult after all. But prayer, with its conditions, its fixed hours and its suspension of basic human activities, is demanding for human nature. One might object, then, that the Qur'an prescribes a remedy that is itself hard to take. The Qur'an admits that regular, steadfast prayer is exacting, and the verses that follow show the way through: prayer is not a burden to the humble in heart. The human heart loves to roam freely through the vast spaces of thought and fancy, and since every organ of the body serves the heart, it wants them free as well. Prayer demands the surrender of that freedom. This annoys the heart and pains the organs it governs. The disease is restless motion, and its cure is the opposite, restfulness. This is what khushūʿ, usually rendered "humility," really signifies: the restfulness of the heart. A person cannot empty the heart of thoughts by trying. The only way is to absorb the mind in one thought so completely that all others fall away on their own. The verses that follow supply that thought, and once the heart is at rest, prayer becomes easy, regular prayer gradually cures pride and ambition, and the way to perfect faith becomes smooth. Such is the well-ordered art of spiritual medicine the Qur'an teaches, as against the fashionable systems of thought-concentration and meditation, dressed in a pseudo-mystical or Eastern style but born of the anxieties of the modern West, which only further disorder an already disordered mind.

### Prayer in congregation

"Establish prayer" shows that prayer is obligatory. "Bow down with those who bow down" shows that it is to be performed in the company of other Muslims, in congregation (jamāʿah). Many scholars cite this verse as proof that congregational prayer is obligatory for men. The crucial question is the degree of the obligation. The jurists differ.

A large body of Companions, their successors and jurists of the Ummah hold that praying in congregation is necessary (wājib) and that abandoning it is a sin. Some Companions went so far as to hold that praying alone without a valid excuse is not permissible at all. The literal wording of the verse supports this view, and so do several hadiths. Abū Dāwūd reported a hadith to the effect that a man who lives near a mosque has no valid prayer except in the mosque. Muslim reported from Abū Hurayrah that a blind Companion asked the Prophet ﷺ for permission to pray at home, as he had no one to lead him to the mosque and back. The Prophet ﷺ gave permission, but as the man was leaving asked whether he could hear the call to prayer from his house. He said he could. The Prophet ﷺ told him that in that case he must come. In Abū Dāwūd's version of the same hadith the Prophet ﷺ said, "Then I see no room for a concession in your case." Al-Qurṭubī cites a hadith from Ibn ʿAbbās in which the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever hears the call and does not go to the congregation has not prayed at all, unless he has a valid excuse. On the strength of such reports, Companions like ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd and Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī ruled that a man within earshot of the mosque's call who still misses the congregation without excuse has no accepted prayer at home. Hearing the call here means the call of a man with an ordinary voice, not one with an extraordinarily loud voice or one amplified by a loudspeaker.

The majority of the Companions, their successors and the later jurists hold instead that congregational prayer is an emphasised sunnah (sunnah muʾakkadah), the most emphasised of its kind, like the sunnah prayer before Fajr, so that it comes very close to being obligatory. On the basis of other verses and hadiths they read the imperative "bow down with those who bow down" as emphasis. They understand the hadiths that seem to deny the validity of praying at home as meaning that this is not the complete way to pray.

The most comprehensive statement on the matter comes from ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd, as reported by Muslim. He said that whoever wishes to meet Allah tomorrow as a true Muslim should keep the five prayers steadfastly in the place where the call is regularly given, for Allah laid down for your Prophet ﷺ the ways of good guidance (sunan al-hudā), and praying the five prayers in congregation is one of them. If you pray at home as that man does, he said, pointing to someone, keeping away from the congregation, you will have abandoned your Prophet's ﷺ Sunnah, and abandoning his Sunnah leads to going astray. Whoever performs wuḍūʾ properly and walks to a mosque has a sin forgiven, a good deed recorded and a rank raised for every step. And he recalled that in their time no one stayed away from the congregation except a known hypocrite, so that even a sick man who could not walk would be brought to the mosque supported between two men and stood in the row.

This statement brings out the great importance of the congregation while fixing its precise status among the "ways of good guidance," which the jurists call as-sunan al-muʾakkadah. Hence if a man prays at home without a proper excuse such as illness, his prayer is valid. But he has earned Allah's displeasure by neglecting an emphasised sunnah. If neglecting the congregation becomes his habit, he commits a grave sin. And if all the people around a mosque desert it and pray at home, they become liable to punishment under the Sharīʿah. Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ said that if persuasion fails to correct such people, they are to be confronted with force.

### A reading for the inner life

Keep the good manners of the Presence, for preserving good manners is the most perfect form of service. The alms spoken of here allude also to the alms of aspirations: just as you pay the alms of wealth, pay the alms of your aspirations. As the poet said on their behalf, everything has an alms to be paid, and the alms of beauty is a mercy like Mine. Through the abundant increase of aspirations and the subtleties of vision that He creates in those who pursue and seek Him, they are raised high, until their states turn into states of bewilderment. And "bow down with those who bow down" means follow the practice of the pious forefathers, the salaf, in what they used to do, and avoid isolation, for being in a dense congregation is sounder than being separated from people.

### In short

- Having called the Israelites to faith, the verse calls them to the chief deeds: steadfast, properly performed prayer (iqāmah), zakāh as the purifying portion of wealth, and bowing with those who bow.
- Since the Israelites' prayer had prostration but no rukūʿ, "those who bow down" are the Muslims, and the verse invites the Jews to pray behind the Prophet ﷺ with his community.
- Patience and prayer are prescribed as the cure for love of money and love of power, and prayer becomes light only through khushūʿ, the restfulness of the heart.
- The verse is a chief proof for congregational prayer: some hold it obligatory, the majority an emphasised sunnah so stressed that habitual neglect is a grave sin, as Ibn Masʿūd's statement in Muslim shows.

<<<VERSE 2:44>>>
The address to the Children of Israel now exposes a contradiction in the conduct of their religious scholars. "Do you preach righteousness and fail to practice it yourselves, although you read the Scripture? Do you not understand?" They told others to do what they themselves refused to do, and they did so while reading a Book that condemned exactly that. The question is asked in a tone of disavowal and astonishment, and although it was addressed to the Jewish scholars of Madīnah, it reaches everyone who preaches goodness to others and neglects it in his own life.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do you preach righteousness"** uses the word al-birr, which embraces every kind of righteousness and every act of obedience to Allah. Here it includes the Oneness of Allah and following Muḥammad ﷺ, which the Jewish leaders would urge upon people of humble standing. Ibn ʿAbbās, in the narration of al-Kalbī from Abū Ṣāliḥ, said the verse came down concerning the Jews of Madīnah, one of whom would say to his brother-in-law, his relatives, or Muslims related to him by nursing: hold fast to your religion and to what this man, meaning Muḥammad ﷺ, commands you, for what he has brought is the truth. They urged people to follow Islam while holding back from it themselves. Qatādah, as reported by ʿAbdur-Razzāq from Maʿmar, said that the Israelites would order people to obey Allah, to fear Him and to do good, and then contradict their own orders, and as-Suddī said the same. Ibn Jurayj said the verse concerns the People of the Book and the hypocrites, who commanded people to pray and fast but did not practise what they commanded. Whoever commands others to do good, he said, should be among the first to do it.

**"and fail to practice it yourselves"** means, as Ibn ʿAbbās explained through Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq, that you forget to practise it yourselves. You forbid people to reject the prophethood and the covenant recorded in your Tawrāh (Torah), while you yourselves have forgotten the covenant that you would accept My Messenger. You have broken that covenant and rejected what you know is in My Book. Their advice to their relatives shows that they knew Islam to be the true faith, but being enslaved to their desires they were not prepared to accept it themselves.

**"although you read the Scripture"** means that you recite the Tawrāh (Torah), which contains the threat of punishment for those whose deeds contradict their words, and which denounces in the strongest terms the scholar who does not act on his knowledge. They could not plead ignorance.

**"Do you not understand?"** asks whether they have no sense of what they are doing to themselves, so that they might wake from their slumber and recover from their blindness, see the evil of their actions and repent. Have you not even the ordinary common sense of human beings? The question is one of rebuke, and the clause about forgetting themselves is its real target.

### Preaching without practice

Allah does not blame the People of the Book for commanding righteousness. Enjoining good is itself part of righteousness and an obligation for scholars. What He condemns is that the one who calls others to goodness should himself ignore the call. The scholar is required to heed what he invites others to. The Prophet Shuʿayb put it this way: "I do not want to do what I am forbidding you from. I only intend reform to the best of my ability" (11:88). Commanding good and doing good are both required, and according to the soundest view of the scholars of the early generations and those who came after, neither duty is cancelled by neglecting the other.

The hadith describes the punishment of those who fail in this. Imām Aḥmad reported that Abū Wāʾil said that he was riding behind Usāmah when Usāmah was asked why he did not advise ʿUthmān. He answered that he did advise him, in private, and would not open a door he would hate to be the first to open, nor flatter a man as the best of men even if he were his leader, because of something he had heard from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He said he heard him describe a man who, on the Day of Resurrection, will be brought and thrown into the Fire, his intestines will spill out, and he will go round and round dragging them like a donkey circling a millstone. The people of the Fire will gather round him and ask what happened to him, since he used to command them to do good and forbid them evil. He will say: yes, I commanded good and did not do it, and I forbade evil and did it myself. Al-Bukhārī and Muslim also recorded this hadith. Anas reported that on the night of the Ascension the Prophet ﷺ passed by people whose lips and tongues were being cut with scissors of fire, and when he asked who they were, Jibrīl (Gabriel) told him that they were preachers of his Ummah who urged people to good deeds and neglected themselves. According to a hadith reported by Ibn ʿAsākir, some people in Paradise will see acquaintances of theirs in Hell and ask how they came to be there, when they themselves reached Paradise through the very good deeds they had learned from them, and those in Hell will answer that they used to say it all with their tongues and never acted on it. Ibrāhīm an-Nakhaʿī said that three verses made him hesitate to preach to people: this verse, and "O believers! Why do you say what you do not do? How despicable it is in the sight of Allah that you say what you do not do" (61:2-3), and the words of Shuʿayb quoted above.

### Must a sinner stay silent?

None of this means that a person who is himself slack in good deeds, or a transgressor in some respect, may not give good counsel to others, or that someone who has fallen into a particular sin may not try to dissuade others from it. Doing a good deed is one virtue, and urging others to do it is a distinct virtue. Abandoning one does not oblige a person to abandon the other. A man who does not pray is not thereby required to give up fasting, and a man who does not pray is not thereby forbidden to tell others to pray. Likewise, doing something the Sharīʿah forbids is one sin, and failing to dissuade those one can influence from it is another, and committing the first does not entail committing the second.

Imām Mālik cited Saʿīd ibn Jubayr as saying that if everyone decided to stop enjoining good and forbidding evil on the grounds that he was himself a sinner with no right to preach until he was free of all sin, no one would be left to counsel anyone, for who is entirely free of sin? Al-Ḥasan of Baṣrah said that this is exactly what Satan wants: that people, obsessed with a false notion of purity, should neglect their duty to teach and advise others. Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānavī used to say that when he noticed a bad habit in himself, he would deliberately denounce that very tendency in his sermons, so that the sermon’s barakah, its blessing, might help him rid himself of it.

In short, the verse does not say that a person indifferent to good deeds in his own life may not preach. It says that the preacher must not neglect good deeds in his own life. Why, then, does it single out the preacher, when neglect of good deeds is impermissible for preacher and non-preacher alike? Because the preacher's offence is graver and more blameworthy. He sins knowing that it is a sin and cannot plead ignorance, whereas the non-preacher, especially if he is unlettered, may be guilty of failing to seek knowledge but can to some degree plead ignorance of the law. Moreover, when a scholar or a preacher sins, he is in effect mocking the Sharīʿah. Anas reported from the Prophet ﷺ that on the Day of Judgment Allah will forgive the unlettered and the ignorant far more readily than He will forgive the scholars.

### A reading for the inner life

Will you urge others to be foremost while you are content to lag behind? Will you call people to Us while you yourselves hang back from Us? Will you give drink to the arriving caravans while you keep away from the water? Will you argue with others over fine points while content with your own bankruptcy in everything but externals? Do you weigh a speck or a grain in other people while tolerating heaps of sand and mountains in yourselves? As the poet said, do you look at the mote in my eye and not see the beam in your own? Do you hand round the choicest drink and never drink yourselves? "Although you read the Scripture," you persist in secret pretensions, denying the shameful thoughts and the plain urges toward blameworthy things that your hearts conceal. Do you not understand how blameworthy such qualities are and how ugly such deeds?

### In short

- The Jewish scholars of Madīnah urged their relatives to hold to Islam as the truth while refusing it themselves, and the verse rebukes this contradiction in people who read the Tawrāh (Torah) and knew its warnings.
- Enjoining good is not what is condemned. Enjoining good while neglecting it is, and the hadith describes the punishment of such preachers: dragged intestines in the Fire, lips cut with scissors of fire.
- A sinner may and should still counsel others, for otherwise no one would preach. Doing good and urging good are separate duties, and neglecting one does not excuse neglecting the other.
- The preacher is singled out because he sins knowingly and mocks the law he teaches, and the unlettered will be forgiven more readily than the learned.
