<<<VERSE 6:66>>>
Still your people ˹O Prophet˺ have rejected this ˹Quran˺, although it is the truth. Say, “I am not a keeper over you.”

The passage turns from what the people asked for to what the Prophet is not. He has not been made their keeper.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Still your people have rejected this"** The people named are Quraysh, and what they rejected is the Qur'an, brought to them with guidance and clear explanation. The charge is stated flatly, without the rhetorical question that preceded it.

**"although it is the truth"** There is no truth beyond this one. The verse does not argue the point, and it does not need to. It says that the denial came from a people who had been shown what is true, and that this is the whole of the case against them.

**"Say, 'I am not a keeper over you'"** I have not been appointed a guardian or a watcher over you, and I do not guarantee that I will bring you to Allah as believers. The gloss adds what the office is, in case the refusal leaves a vacuum: I am only a warner, and your affair is left to Allah. This was revealed before the command to fight the idolaters, when the Prophet's business was the message and nothing else.

The division of labour that follows from this disclaimer is spelled out in the commentary: my duty is to convey the message, and yours is to hear it and obey. Whoever follows him acquires happiness in this life and the Hereafter, and whoever defies him becomes miserable in both. The line is put in the same terms as the verse in the cave: "Say, the truth is from your Lord. Whoever wills, let him believe, and whoever wills, let him disbelieve" (18:29).

### Why the verse closes the door on pride

The human condition, taken seriously, leaves nobody room to be proud. A man is buffeted by winds that could turn into a cyclone at any moment and wreck his life. He stands on land that could be torn apart by an earthquake whenever it is willed. He lives among a people so full of hidden enmity that a single spark is enough to push the whole society into bloodshed. The ummah addressed here is not being told that the rejecters will be left alone. It is being told that the reckoning is not in anyone's hands but Allah's, and that a man who is not a keeper over others should not behave as though he were their judge.

### In short

- The rejection is attributed to Quraysh of Makkah, and the object of it is identified as the truth itself.
- The Prophet is told to disclaim the office of keeper, because the guidance of a person is not his to supply.
- The verse was revealed before the command to fight, and it sets the terms of the invitation in the period when argument, not the sword, was the method.
- Whoever hears the message and turns away has settled his own case, since every tiding has its appointed outcome.

<<<VERSE 6:67>>>
Every ˹destined˺ matter has a ˹set˺ time to transpire. And you will soon come to know

A short sentence that answers a question about timing, and then refuses to answer it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Every destined matter has a set time to transpire"** For every tiding there is a reality, that is, a conclusion and a fixed time in which it takes place and is settled. Ibn Abbas and others took the sense to be that this news will come about, perhaps after a while. The phrase covers every announcement a person receives from Allah: commands, prohibitions, promises and threats, glad tidings of victory and warnings of punishment. Some of them fall in this world and some in the Hereafter, and the tidings about their punishment are included.

**"And you will soon come to know"** This is stated as a threat. You will come to know all of it, in this world or in the next. Another way of taking the clause is that every action and every speech of yours reflects a real meaning in your hearts, and that you will see what is done with you.

### The question behind the answer

Those who asked when the promised punishment would arrive were told that he had not been appointed to tell them. The point of time for everything is settled in the divine knowledge, and nothing can be pushed forward. It arrives when it arrives. They will see for themselves what that hour looks like, and nobody will be told in advance. Other verses say the same thing in other words: for each and every matter there is a decree (13:38), and "you will come to know the truth of it after a while" (38:88). A warning and a promise of this kind are certain to be met.

### In short

- The verse takes the question of timing away from the questioners and hands it to Allah.
- Every announcement has an outcome at a fixed time, and the punishment they mocked at is one of those announcements.
- "You will come to know" is a threat rather than a consolation, and it is left open as to when the knowing falls.
- The other verses cited here say the same thing, that a decree stands behind every matter.

<<<VERSE 6:68>>>
And when you come across those who ridicule Our revelations, do not sit with them unless they engage in a different topic. Should Satan make you forget, then once you remember, do not ˹continue to˺ sit with the wrongdoing people

This is a rule about sitting down, and it is stated as a rule about belief. A gathering is a place where a person's faith is either guarded or spent.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And when you come across those who ridicule Our revelations"** The wording in Arabic is built on a verb that means to enter into water and wade through it, and then comes to mean entering into activities that are vain, absurd or futile. The Qur'an usually uses it in that second sense. Two examples are enough: "we used to indulge in vain discourse with those who indulged" (74:45), and the talk of people who play with what they are busy with (6:91). The verb covers meddling with the verses of Allah for fun and ridicule, and fault-finding and disputing about them, and two scholars of the subcontinent, Shaykh al-Hind Mahmud al-Hasan and Ashraf Ali Thanavi, translated it in that latter sense. So the sense is: when you see these people meddling in the verses of Allah just for fun, or trying to find faults in them, turn away.

**"do not sit with them unless they engage in a different topic"** Stay away from them until they change the subject, until they talk about something other than the denial they were engaged in. Turning away can take several forms. A person can leave the gathering, or he can stay and busy himself with something else so that he pays no attention. The end of the verse makes clear which form is desirable. One should not keep sitting there. One should get up and leave.

**"Should Satan make you forget"** This covers a person who walked into their gathering unmindfully, whether he had forgotten the prohibition or had not recalled that these people speak against Allah and his Messenger in their meetings. In either case, once he remembers, he leaves at once. To keep sitting there after the reminder is a sin. The same subject appears in the women: "if you hear the verses of Allah being denied and mocked at, then do not sit with them until they engage in a talk other than that, for in that case you would be like them" (4:140). If you stay and sit with them, agreeing with what they say, you are one of them.

**"then once you remember, do not continue to sit with the wrongdoing people"** The remembrance is the turning point: the moment it arrives, the sitting has to end. The command includes every member of this ummah, and the address to the Prophet is there so that others hear it. Allah gave him this order in Makkah, and the companions found it hard to carry out, until the relief in the next verse let them sit with the idolaters in order to admonish and warn them. Nobody is to sit with people who deny the verses, distort them, and explain them wrongly. It is said that he never took part in such gatherings even in the days before his prophethood, so he needed no prohibition for himself.

### Who is exempt, and who is not

Imam al-Razi, in his great commentary, takes the real intent of the verse to be abstention from sinful gatherings and from their participants. The best course is to rise and depart. But if leaving would put a person's life, property or honour in danger, then for ordinary people it is permissible to turn away in another manner, by busying themselves with something else and paying no attention. The case of the people who are followed in religious matters is different. For them the only appropriate course is to get up and leave, because their sitting is read as approval.

Two further rulings are drawn from the wording. Imam al-Jassas, in a book on the legal rulings of the Qur'an, takes the verse to mean that Muslims should keep away from any gathering where things are being said against Allah, against his Messenger and against the law of Islam, in a case where it is not in their power to stop it or even to say what is right. Sitting in such a company with the intention of reforming it and carrying the truth to it does not fall under the prohibition. And al-Jassas goes on to say that sitting with people of this kind, unjust, irreligious and foul-mouthed, is a sin in itself, whether or not they happen to be saying anything impermissible at that moment. The reason is that the prohibition in the verse is unconditional. It does not make their behaving unjustly at that same hour a condition, because people of that character may break into their ridiculous rantings at any sudden moment. The same subject is put more explicitly in another verse: "do not incline towards the wrongdoers, lest the Fire should catch you" (11:113).

### Forgetting, and what it costs a prophet

The clause "should Satan make you forget" raises a question of its own. If the address is to Muslims at large, then forgetting is simply human, and there is no difficulty. If it is to the Prophet, someone may ask how the teaching of a man who can forget is to be trusted. The answer given is that a prophet may forget a thing under particular circumstances where divine wisdom decides that it should happen, in order to achieve a purpose. But Allah alerts him at once by a way that keeps the lapse from lasting, so that his teaching ends up free of any doubt left by forgetfulness.

The same clause carries a comfort for everyone else. If a person falls into an error inadvertently, that stands forgiven. There is a hadith to that effect, to the sense that error, forgetfulness and what a person is compelled to do have been removed from the account of this ummah. In other words, what is not chosen is not charged.

### A sin watched is a sin shared

The principle behind the rule is stated plainly: a sin is a sin whether you do it yourself or sit and watch others doing it. Muslims are being instructed, as a matter of principle, to keep out of the gatherings frequented by people who prefer to follow the false. The verse then goes on to details, and the details are the ones set out above, down to the last one: a man who cannot get out may cover his ears with his attention, but a man whose attention is looked up to has no such cover.

### In short

- The prohibition is about staying, not about being in the same city, and the remedy is to change the sitting or leave it.
- Wading into vain talk is the image the Arabic word carries, and the mockers and the fault-finders are covered by it alike.
- Sitting with people after you remember what they are doing is what the verse forbids, and the other cited verse says that whoever stays becomes like them.
- Danger to life, property or honour lets an ordinary person turn away in some other way, but not a scholar whose sitting is taken for endorsement.
- Forgetting is forgiven, in the general case and in the prophetic case, and the reminder obliges a person only from the moment it arrives.

<<<VERSE 6:69>>>
"Those mindful ˹of Allah˺ will not be accountable for those ˹who ridicule it˺ whatsoever—their duty is to advise, so perhaps the ridiculers will abstain"

The verse exists because the previous one was too much to bear.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those mindful of Allah will not be accountable for those who ridicule it"** When this was revealed, the companions put a difficulty to the Prophet. If the absolute prohibition on going into those gatherings stayed in force, they would be shut out of the prayer and out of the circumambulation of the Sacred Mosque, since those people were always sitting there, before the hijrah and before Makkah was opened to the Muslims, busy with nothing but fault-finding and ill-speaking. The verse that answered them is this one. When people who observe restraint come to al-Masjid al-Haram for their own rightful purpose, they are not responsible for the evil deeds of the wicked people sitting there. One gloss notes that the Arabic particle which might suggest a share of responsibility is extra, so that the sense is simply that the mindful are not accountable for them in anything, if they sit with them.

**"their duty is to advise"** They remain under the obligation of telling such people what is right and true. That is the duty that survives the relief, and it is stated as a reminder: remind them, so that perhaps they will become mindful. The believers are clear of the mockers' sins, and they are not clear of the obligation to warn them.

**"so perhaps the ridiculers will abstain"** The purpose of the mild treatment is their restraint, not the believers' comfort. It was said: we told you to leave them, so that they notice what they are indulging in, give it up, and never go back to it.

### The shape of the exemption

Those who ward off disbelief, idolatry, indecency and mockery are not accountable for the sins, the unbelief and the ridicule of others. The relief is real, and it is bounded. It covers sitting in a place a Muslim has a right to be in, for a purpose he is entitled to pursue. It does not cover staying to enjoy what they are saying, and it does not cancel the duty of speaking the truth in front of them. A reminder given through the Qur'an is what the mindful owe, and the hope attached to it is small and honest: perhaps they will be wary of talking like that.

### In short

- The verse lifts a hardship the previous one had created, and does so without giving permission to sit and listen.
- Being free of another person's accountability is not the same as being free of concern for him.
- The duty left over is remembrance and advice, delivered by the Qur'an and aimed at their restraint.
- The companions' difficulty was about prayer and tawaf in the Sacred Mosque, and the answer let them keep both.

<<<VERSE 6:70>>>
And leave those who take this faith ˹of Islam˺ as mere play and amusement and are deluded by ˹their˹ worldly life. Yet remind them by this ˹Quran˺, so no one should be ruined for their misdeeds. They will have no protector or intercessor other than Allah. Even if they were to offer every ˹possible˺ ransom, none will be accepted from them. Those are the ones who will be ruined for their misdeeds. They will have a boiling drink and painful punishment for their disbelief

Two commands in one verse: leave them, and remind them. The verse then explains why the second one cannot be dropped.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And leave those who take this faith as mere play and amusement"** The word for leaving is built on a root meaning to be displeased with a thing and to abandon it, so the sentence can be read two ways, and the outcome is nearly the same. Either it means leaving people who have turned the true religion, sent for their own sake, into a plaything and a laughing stock, or it means leaving people who have abandoned their real faith and made fun and play their own religion and purpose in life. Those named in the reports are the Jews, the Christians, and the idolatrous Arabs who took the religion of their fathers as their own. The men specifically in mind are ʿUyaynah, al-Nadr ibn al-Harith and their folk. To take a faith as a joke is described as treating it as merriment and fiction. This too was revealed before the command to fight them, so the order to leave them alone stands.

**"and are deluded by their worldly life"** The comforts and bounties of this world deceived them. This is a true diagnosis of the problem. They are wicked and disobedient because they are bewitched by the glamour of their mortal life, and they have forgotten that another state of life is ahead. If they had believed in the Hereafter and in Qiyamah, they would never have acted so recklessly.

**"Yet remind them by this Quran, so no one should be ruined for their misdeeds"** Remind the people with the Qur'an, and warn them against Allah's revenge and his painful torment on the Day of Rising. The word translated as ruined is explained through several early readings. Ad-Dahhak, reporting from Ibn Abbas, and Mujahid, Ikrimah, al-Hasan and as-Suddi took it to mean being made submissive. Al-Walibi, also from Ibn Abbas, took it as being exposed to what one has earned. Qatadah said it means being prevented, and Ibn Zayd said it means being recompensed, while al-Kalbi said it means being reckoned. All of these are close. They come together in being exposed to destruction, being kept away from good, and being held back from getting what one wants. Every soul is held back by what it has earned, except the people of the right hand (74:38-39).

**"They will have no protector or intercessor other than Allah"** A person in trouble relies on three sources, and all three fail here. When it suits him he uses the power and influence of his party or his group to stay safe from the consequences of what he did. When he is helpless he seeks recommendations from influential people. When that fails too, he tries to buy his freedom with money. None of the three is left. No friend or relative saves Allah's criminal from punishment, no intercession succeeds without Allah's permission, and no wealth is accepted. If a person owned the wealth of the whole world and offered all of it as a ransom to be saved from punishment, that ransom would not be taken from him, as the verse in the family of Imran says about those who disbelieved and died in unbelief, from whom even the earth full of gold would not be accepted (3:91).

**"They will have a boiling drink and painful punishment for their disbelief"** They have been detained in the punishment of their own deeds, and this is stated twice in the verse, once as a warning and once as a verdict. Their drink in Jahannam will be boiling water, about which another verse says that it will shatter their entrails (47:15), and there will be painful punishments besides it, in return for their disbelief and denial. The Qur'an describes their hearts by one word for rust and stain: what they have earned has covered their hearts with a layer that stops good getting in (83:14). A report from the Prophet puts the process in order: when a man falls into a sin the first time, a dot shows up on his heart. It is like a black spot on a white garment, which everybody dislikes and is disturbed by, and he too feels the distaste. But when he goes on to a second sin and a third, without repenting of the first, black dots keep appearing one after another, until the bright tablet of the heart is black through and through. The outcome is that he loses the very ability to tell good from bad.

### The company is the danger

The verse also carries a warning that the company of people who are heedless of the Hereafter and content with this life is fatal for whoever sits with them. Its end is that whoever stays in their company becomes exposed to the same punishment they have been caught in. Many clear statements of the Qur'an and of the reports, and repeated observation, point to the same root of evils and crimes: the society and the environment a person grows up in. Once he is snared in it he slides into things against his own nature and his own conscience. Then he gets used to it, and the sense of evil dies away, until he takes evil as good and good as evil. Wrong environment and bad company hurt everybody and push people into an abyss, and the practical conclusion is drawn for the people who are responsible for raising children: keep them out of it.

### In short

- Leaving these people and reminding them are ordered in the same breath, so neither command cancels the other.
- "Taking the faith as play" is described as a religion of amusement, and the same phrase also fits people who left faith and adopted amusement.
- Being ruined is explained by early glosses that cluster around exposure, debasement and being held back from good.
- Party, connections and money are the three refuges a sinner reaches for, and the verse removes all three.
- The black dots on the heart are the mechanism by which a taken habit of sin ends in the loss of the ability to tell good from bad.

<<<VERSE 6:71>>>
Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, “Should we invoke, other than Allah, those ˹idols˺ which cannot benefit or harm us, and turn back to disbelief after Allah has guided us? ˹If we do so, we will be˺ like those disoriented by devils in the wilderness, while their companions call them to guidance, ˹saying˺, ‘Come to us!’ Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Allah’s guidance is the ˹only˺ true guidance. And we are commanded to submit to the Lord of all worlds”

The answer to the offer of a bargain is a question, and the question comes with a picture attached.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Should we invoke, other than Allah, those which cannot benefit or harm us"** The interrogative is a disavowal, not a request for information. These three verses exist to refute shirk and to affirm Allah's oneness and the Hereafter, and the question is the refutation in the act. Worshiping an idol would bring no benefit in this life, in provision or livelihood, and no benefit in the Hereafter, and neglecting it brings no harm in either. The same answer is put to ʿUyaynah and his folk, and to the demand that preceded it.

**"and turn back to disbelief after Allah has guided us"** After he had honoured us with his religion. To go back after that is the thing the parable is about. As-Suddi reported that certain idolators pressed some Muslims to leave the religion of Muhammad and follow them instead, and that this verse came down about that exchange. Ibn Jarir recorded the report.

**"like those disoriented by devils in the wilderness"** The example, if a person reverts after believing, is that of a man who set out with some people on a road, lost his way, and was made to wander in confusion over the land by devils. He is bewildered, not knowing where to go. They call him by his name, and by his father's name and his grandfather's, and he follows the call, thinking it is a path of guidance. By morning he finds himself destroyed, and they may eat him, and the jinn leave him wandering in a wasteland where he dies of thirst. The word for the creatures who lead him is read as referring to ghouls. Meanwhile his companions on the road are calling him: come back to us, we are on the path. And he refuses.

**"while their companions call them to guidance"** The companions are the people on the road who want him to come back. The point of the parable is that the call is coming, and that the man who is destroying himself is being called out of it by people who are not lost. One account takes the verse to be about Abu Bakr and his son ʿAbd al-Rahman. Before he believed, ʿAbd al-Rahman used to call his parents to his own religion, while they called him to Islam and to repentance. On that reading the address tells the Prophet to put the question to his son in these terms: are you asking us to worship what neither benefits us in this world nor hurts us if we leave it, and to crawl back to what we were on before Allah led us into Muhammad's religion? If we did what you ask, we would be a man gone astray, lured off by devils.

**"Say, Allah's guidance is the only true guidance"** His guidance is Islam, and everything else is error. On one reading the sentence continues with a second identification: our qiblah is the Kaʿbah. The two are the same claim in different clothes, that the direction of the worship and the worship itself come from one source. Whoever Allah guides has nobody who can misguide him (39:37), and if you are eager for the guidance of those who refuse it, Allah does not guide those whom he lets go astray, and they will have no helpers (16:37).

**"And we are commanded to submit to the Lord of all worlds"** We are commanded to worship him with sincerity, without partners, and to make our worship and our confession of his oneness pure for him.

### What going astray looks like from the outside

When communities go astray, their religious acts change as well. People stop attending to the objective of an act and load everything onto its externals. Those who have reached the point of making religion a source of worldly fun, and who are oblivious to what it was for, show by their deeds that they are in earnest about nothing in it. Such people cannot have the point explained to them, because the explanation would go against their temperament. And what they own gives them the illusion that they own the truth as well. They see their needs met. They are the life and soul of every gathering, and nothing is missing from their lives. So they conclude that they will come out on top in the Hereafter as well. These are misapprehensions, and they will be undone by their own actions. Their arrogance will rebound on them, and there will be no easing of what they come to.

### In short

- The reply to the bargain offered is a question that puts the benefit and the harm of idols on the table, and finds neither.
- Reverting after guidance is illustrated as a man lured into the wasteland by voices that use his own name, while the people who could save him are calling.
- The call has not stopped. The companions on the road are calling, which is what makes the refusal indefensible.
- Allah's guidance is the true guidance, and the whole exchange is a claim about direction: the qiblah, the path, and the one who calls.
- Submission is stated as the command, with sincerity and oneness as its content.

<<<VERSE 6:72>>>
establish prayer, and be mindful of Him. To Him you will all be gathered together

Two obligations and one destination, in a fragment that depends on the verse before it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"establish prayer"** To submit to the Lord of the worlds takes shape in prayer, and the prayer meant here is the five daily prayers, established in a complete way rather than performed loosely. The same submission that refuses to worship idols is what stands up and prays.

**"and be mindful of Him"** Mindfulness of Allah, by fulfilling his instructions and staying away from what he has prohibited, is the second half of the command. The pairing matters. Prayer without mindfulness is the externals of an act, which is what the previous verse showed what happens to when a community goes astray.

**"To him you will all be gathered together"** He is the one to whom you will be gathered after death, and he will reward each person for what they did. The gathering is for the reckoning, on the Day of Rising. Whoever has been told that the call is still open, and that a path can be rejoined, is also being told that the gathering is not avoidable.

### In short

- Submission is given a body: prayer performed properly, and mindfulness in ordinary conduct.
- The two are addressed to the same people who were offered a bargain about idols, so the act of worship is itself the answer.
- The gathering to Allah is stated as the end of the road, and it is what makes seriousness about religion possible at all.

<<<VERSE 6:73>>>
"He is the One Who created the heavens and the earth in truth. On the Day ˹of Judgment˺ He will say, ‘Be!’ And there will be! His command is truth. All authority is His ˹alone˺ on the Day the Trumpet will be blown. He is the Knower of all—seen or unseen. And He is the All-Wise, All-Aware."

The passage about ridicule, company and return ends on the one who created all of it, and on the day when everything is handed to him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He is the One Who created the heavens and the earth in truth"** In justice, as the one who made them and who owns them and governs their affairs and the affairs of everyone in them. Another sense is offered: he made them in order to make the truth manifest and falsehood exposed. And it is also said that the point is to show that they are ephemeral and evanescent. The three readings agree that creation has a purpose and is not a stage set.

**"On the Day of Judgment He will say, 'Be!' And there will be!"** This refers to the Day of Resurrection, when Allah says to the creatures: rise up, and they do. It will come faster than the blink of an eye. One reading takes the address in the Arabic as being made to a wall, so that the heavens become a wall that is blown into, the way a horn is blown, and the heaven is then changed into a different heaven. On either reading, the word that produces the event is the same word that produced the heavens the first time.

**"His command is truth"** His word about the resurrection is the truth that will doubtless come to pass.

**"All authority is His alone on the Day the Trumpet will be blown"** "The sovereignty on that day will be the true sovereignty, belonging to the Most Merciful, and it will be a hard day for the disbelievers" (25:26). Then a question is asked and answered in another verse: "Whose is the kingdom this day? It is Allah's, the One, the Irresistible" (40:16). There will be no kingdom for anybody else. The word for the Trumpet here is Sur, and it is the trumpet into which the angel Israfil will blow, on the second blast. A hadith recorded by Muslim states that Israfil has held the Sur in his mouth with his forehead lowered, waiting for the command to blow. In another report, recorded by Ahmad from Abdullah bin Amr, a bedouin man asked the Messenger of Allah what the Sur is, and the answer was short: a trumpet which will be blown.

**"He is the Knower of all, seen or unseen"** The unseen is what will happen in the future, and the seen is what has already happened. It is also said that the unseen means what is hidden from people while the seen means what Allah's servants see, and that his knowledge covers what is inwardly hidden and outwardly manifest, so that nothing is hidden from him. This is where the passage began, with the keys of the unseen and with a leaf that no one watches falling.

**"And He is the All-Wise, All-Aware"** Wise in his command and decree, and aware of his creatures and of what they do. The two names close the claim: creation was not done at random, and it will not be settled by guesswork.

### The two travellers

The last stretch of the passage is worth reading as a story about two men in the desert. One puts his entire trust in Allah. When he goes astray he begs Allah's assistance, and soon his feet are back on the path. The other has faith in entities other than Allah. When he loses his way he is hardly aware that he is wandering at all, and he ignores the calls of the faithful, who would gladly redirect him. The difference between them is the state of their minds. The first keeps his open, so he has no difficulty seeing which way is right, and if he strays he is willing to accept guidance in order to return. The second is lost before he sets out, since Satan has him under his influence, and his mind works in a perverse way. He may listen without hearing. He may look without seeing.

### In short

- Creation is stated as an act done in truth, and the day of judgement is stated as the same word at work a second time.
- "Be, and it is" is read as the moment the creatures are told to rise, and one early reading takes the heavens as a wall blown into and remade.
- The trumpet is named, and the reports about Israfil waiting with it in his mouth and about the bedouin's question give the verse its scenery.
- Sovereignty on that day belongs to Allah alone, which is the answer to every partner who was invoked in this life.
- The passage that began with the keys of the unseen ends by naming its owner as the Wise and the Aware, and leaves the reader to choose which of the two travellers he is.
