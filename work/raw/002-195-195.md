<<<VERSE 2:195>>>
Verse 195 brings the tenth injunction under the theme of righteousness begun in verse 177. "Spend in the cause of Allah and do not let your own hands throw you into destruction ˹by withholding˺. And do good, for Allah certainly loves the good-doers". Muslims are obliged to spend money and materials according to the needs of the jihād commanded in Allah's way. From this the jurists deduce that besides the fixed duty of zakāh Muslims have other financial obligations, neither permanent nor tied to a threshold or quantity, but binding on all as and when the need arises, and lapsing when there is no need. Spending on jihād falls under this provision.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Spend in the cause of Allah"**. Spend your wealth in obedience to Allah, in striving for His sake and in the many areas of obedience and closeness to Him, above all in fighting the enemy and in whatever strengthens the Muslims against him. One account says the verses from "Fight in the cause of Allah" to the end of this verse were revealed about the Companions who were in iḥrām with the Prophet ﷺ on the way to the ʿumrah after the year of Ḥudaybiyyah, so the spending was for that journey. Al-Bukhārī records that Ḥudhayfah said the verse was revealed about spending, and Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports the same from him and adds that Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ʿAṭāʾ, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah, al-Suddī and Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān said similarly.

**"and do not let your own hands throw you into destruction ˹by withholding˺."**. The plain sense is that Muslims may not throw themselves into ruin by their own choice, and the explanations of what this means differ. Aslam Abū ʿImrān said that at the siege of Constantinople, when ʿUqbah ibn ʿĀmir led the Egyptian forces and Faḍālah ibn ʿUbayd the Syrians, a huge Byzantine column came out of the city and the Muslims lined up against it. A man of the Anṣār charged the Roman lines alone, broke through and came back, and people cried out that he was throwing himself to destruction. Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī, who was present, said that they were explaining the verse wrongly, for it was revealed about the Anṣār themselves, the Companions who fought beside the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and supported him. When Islam became strong and its following grew, they said to one another that Allah had honoured them with the Prophet's ﷺ companionship and victory, that they had neglected their families, estates and children, and that now that warfare had ceased they might go home and attend to them. Then this verse came down, and the destruction it speaks of is staying with one's family and estate and abandoning jihād. Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, al-Nasāʾī, ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Ibn Jarīr, Ibn Marduwayh, al-Ḥāfiẓ Abū Yaʿlā in his Musnad, Ibn Ḥibbān and al-Ḥākim record this, al-Tirmidhī grading it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ gharīb and al-Ḥākim saying it meets the criteria of al-Bukhārī and Muslim though they did not record it. This proves that banishing jihād from Muslim life is the cause of their degradation and destruction, and Abū Ayyūb accordingly spent his whole life in jihād away from his home in Madīnah, dying at Constantinople, where he was buried. The same understanding is reported from the earliest authorities, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ḥudhayfah, Qatādah, Mujāhid and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk. Abū Bakr ibn ʿAyyāsh reports from Abū Isḥāq al-Subayʿī that a man asked al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib whether he would be throwing himself to destruction if he charged the enemy alone and was killed, and al-Barāʾ said no, since Allah had told His Messenger ﷺ "So fight in the cause of Allah ˹O Prophet˺. You are accountable for none but yourself" (4:84), and this verse concerned withholding spending. Ibn Marduwayh and al-Ḥākim record this, al-Ḥākim again noting that it meets the criteria of the Two Shaykhs. Al-Thawrī and Qays ibn al-Rabīʿ relate it from al-Barāʾ with the addition that destruction is a man sinning and refusing to repent. Al-Barāʾ also said that to lose hope in Allah's mercy and forgiveness is to go voluntarily to one's destruction, so despair of forgiveness is forbidden. Ibn ʿAbbās said the verse is not about fighting but about refraining from spending for Allah's sake, by which a person throws himself into ruin. Others say that spending in Allah's way so excessively that the rights of one's family suffer is the destruction meant, so that such excess is impermissible, and others that the verse forbids throwing oneself into an attack when it is already plain that one can achieve nothing against the enemy but death at their hands. Al-Jaṣṣāṣ reconciles these views by saying that all these rules can be drawn from the verse. Withholding the funds needed for the struggle, or abandoning it altogether, gives the enemy the advantage. More than anything else the struggle for Allah's cause demands the spending of money, and it is the sacrifice of wealth that people find hardest. The Qur'an therefore tells us to treat Allah's cause as our own and to spend generously on it. Failing this invites destruction in this world and the next, for if a person will not surrender what he has to Allah, why should Allah give him what He has? People imagine that wealth is best spent on themselves and their families, but the Qur'an calls that the way to destruction. Wealth is properly used in generous spending for the religion, which earns divine help in this world and Paradise in the Hereafter.

**"And do good, for Allah certainly loves the good-doers"**. Allah commands excellence (iḥsān), the highest of the acts of obedience, as an incentive to do everything well, in worship, dealings and conduct. Where iḥsān concerns worship, the Prophet ﷺ explained it in the well-known hadith of Jibrīl: worship Allah as if you see Him, and if you cannot reach that perception, know that He sees you. Where it concerns social dealings, he explained it, as the Musnad of Aḥmad reports from Muʿādh, as liking for others what you like for yourself and disliking for them what you dislike for yourself. One reading takes the good here as spending well in Allah's way, or as having a good opinion of Him. Allah loves those who do good, rewarding them generously and leading them to true guidance.

### Spending wallets, hearts and spirits

In the devotional reading, the rich remove money from their wallets, the poor remove the rich from their hearts, and the voicers of tawḥīd remove all creatures from their secret cores, to which He alludes in "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ "Allah ˹revealed it˺!" Then leave them" (6:91). The rich let wealth go for the reward of the next world, the poor let the rich go for the sake of the Lord's religion, and the recognisers let creatures go for the sake of seeing the Glorified. The rich spend their possessions in zakāh and charity to escape Hell, the worshippers spend their souls in the duties of worship to reach Paradise, and the recognisers spend their spirits and hearts in the realities of witnessing to reach union with the Real. Muṣṭafā ﷺ said that beautiful doing is to worship Allah as if you see Him, for if you do not see Him, He sees you: to worship in wakefulness and awareness as if gazing on Him. This hadith alludes to the heart's encounter with the Real, the secret core's meeting with the Unseen and the spirit's contemplation of its Patron, and it urges purity in deeds, the curtailing of wishes and loyalty to what was accepted on the First Day, when the spirits heard "Am I not your Lord?" and answered "Yes, You are!" (7:172). Loyalty to that acceptance is serving the Protector, wishes are curtailed in "as if you see Him", and deeds are purified in "He sees you". When an eye has seen Him, how can it busy itself glancing at others? When a spirit has found His companionship, how will it make do with water and dust? The call "Return to your Lord" (89:28) is addressed to the pure spirit, which cannot make its home in a frame of clay. Whoever is used to that Presence will not long endure the abasement of the veil, nor will the ruler of his own city spend his life in exile. The attribute of the spirit is subsistence while water and dust undergo annihilation, and he who lives in the Real is not like him who lives in this world. The realiser knows the secret that the Real can be seen, and "as if you see Him" bears witness to it.

### In short

- Spending for jihād is obligatory as the need arises, over and above zakāh.
- "Destruction" means above all abandoning jihād to sit with one's family and estate, as Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī explained at Constantinople, and also withholding spending (Ibn ʿAbbās, Ḥudhayfah), despairing of mercy or refusing to repent (al-Barāʾ), reckless excess in giving, or a hopeless attack. Al-Jaṣṣāṣ accepts all of these.
- A lone charge against the enemy is not self-destruction (al-Barāʾ, with 4:84).
- Iḥsān in worship is to worship as if seeing Allah, and in dealings to wish for others what one wishes for oneself (the hadith of Jibrīl, Muʿādh).
- Inwardly, the rich spend wealth, the poor spend their attachment to the rich, and the recognisers spend their very spirits to reach the Real (6:91, 7:172, 89:28).
