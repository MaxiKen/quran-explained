Verse 195 brings the tenth injunction under the theme of righteousness begun in verse 177. "Spend in the cause of Allah and do not let your own hands throw you into destruction ˹by withholding˺. And do good, for Allah certainly loves the good-doers". Muslims are obliged to spend money and materials according to the needs of the jihād commanded in Allah's way. The jurists infer financial duties beyond fixed zakāh. These are neither permanent nor tied to a threshold or quantity. They bind all Muslims when need arises and lapse when that need is absent. Spending on jihād falls under this provision.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Spend in the cause of Allah"**. Devote wealth to Allah's obedience, striving for His sake and seeking nearness to Him. The command especially concerns fighting the enemy and strengthening Muslims against him. One account says the verses from "Fight in the cause of Allah" to the end of this verse were revealed about the Companions who were in iḥrām with the Prophet ﷺ on the way to the ʿumrah after the year of Ḥudaybiyyah, so the spending was for that journey. Al-Bukhārī records Ḥudhayfah's explanation that the verse concerned spending. Ibn Abī Ḥātim conveys the same from him. He names Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Mujāhid and ʿAṭāʾ, and ʿIkrimah among those giving it too. It also comes from al-Suddī and al-Ḥasan, from aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk and Qatādah, and from Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān.

**"and do not let your own hands throw you into destruction ˹by withholding˺."**. The plain sense is that Muslims may not throw themselves into ruin by their own choice, and the explanations of what this means differ. Aslam Abū ʿImrān describes the siege of Constantinople. ʿUqbah ibn ʿĀmir led the Egyptians, and Faḍālah ibn ʿUbayd led the Syrians. A large Byzantine column emerged from the city. The Muslims formed lines against it. A man of the Anṣār charged the Roman lines alone, broke through and came back, and people cried out that he was throwing himself to destruction. Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī, who was present, said that they were explaining the verse wrongly, for it was revealed about the Anṣār themselves, the Companions who fought beside the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and supported him. With Islam strong and its following increased, they recalled Allah's honour of companionship with the Prophet ﷺ and victory. They had neglected families, estates and children. Since warfare had ceased, they proposed going home to attend to them. Then this verse came down, and the destruction it speaks of is staying with one's family and estate and abandoning jihād. Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, al-Nasāʾī, ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Ibn Jarīr, Ibn Marduwayh, al-Ḥāfiẓ Abū Yaʿlā in his Musnad, Ibn Ḥibbān and al-Ḥākim record this, al-Tirmidhī grading it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ gharīb and al-Ḥākim saying it meets the criteria of al-Bukhārī and Muslim though they did not record it. This proves that banishing jihād from Muslim life is the cause of their degradation and destruction, and Abū Ayyūb accordingly spent his whole life in jihād away from his home in Madīnah, dying at Constantinople, where he was buried. The same understanding is reported from the earliest authorities, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ḥudhayfah, Qatādah, Mujāhid and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk. Abū Bakr ibn ʿAyyāsh conveys Abū Isḥāq al-Subayʿī's account of a question to al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib. Would charging alone and being killed count as self-destruction? Al-Barāʾ answered no, citing the command to the Messenger ﷺ: "So fight in the cause of Allah ˹O Prophet˺. You are accountable for none but yourself" (4:84). This verse, he explained, concerns withholding expenditure. Ibn Marduwayh and al-Ḥākim record this, al-Ḥākim again noting that it meets the criteria of the Two Shaykhs. Al-Thawrī and Qays ibn al-Rabīʿ relate it from al-Barāʾ with the addition that destruction is a man sinning and refusing to repent. Al-Barāʾ also said that to lose hope in Allah's mercy and forgiveness is to go voluntarily to one's destruction, so despair of forgiveness is forbidden. Ibn ʿAbbās said the verse is not about fighting but about refraining from spending for Allah's sake, by which a person throws himself into ruin. Others say that spending in Allah's way so excessively that the rights of one's family suffer is the destruction meant, so that such excess is impermissible, and others that the verse forbids throwing oneself into an attack when it is already plain that one can achieve nothing against the enemy but death at their hands. Al-Jaṣṣāṣ reconciles these views by saying that all these rules can be drawn from the verse. Withholding the funds needed for the struggle, or abandoning it altogether, gives the enemy the advantage. More than anything else the struggle for Allah's cause demands the spending of money, and it is the sacrifice of wealth that people find hardest. The Qur'an therefore tells us to treat Allah's cause as our own and to spend generously on it. Failing this invites destruction in this world and the next, for if a person will not surrender what he has to Allah, why should Allah give him what He has? People imagine that wealth is best spent on themselves and their families, but the Qur'an calls that the way to destruction. Wealth is properly used in generous spending for the religion, which earns divine help in this world and Paradise in the Hereafter.

**"And do good, for Allah certainly loves the good-doers"**. Allah commands excellence (iḥsān), the highest of the acts of obedience, as an incentive to do everything well, in worship, dealings and conduct. Where iḥsān concerns worship, the Prophet ﷺ explained it in the well-known hadith of Jibrīl: worship Allah as if you see Him, and if you cannot reach that perception, know that He sees you. Where it concerns social dealings, he explained it, as the Musnad of Aḥmad reports from Muʿādh, as liking for others what you like for yourself and disliking for them what you dislike for yourself. One reading takes the good here as spending well in Allah's way, or as having a good opinion of Him. Allah loves those who do good, rewarding them generously and leading them to true guidance.

### Spending wallets, hearts and spirits

The devotional reading distinguishes three kinds of spending. The rich empty their wallets of money. The poor empty their hearts of attachment to the rich. The voicers of tawḥīd remove created things from their secret cores. The image recalls "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ "Allah ˹revealed it˺!" Then leave them" (6:91).

Wealth is relinquished for the next world's reward. Attachment to the rich is relinquished for the Lord's religion. The recognisers relinquish creatures in pursuit of seeing the Glorified. The rich offer possessions through zakāh and charity to escape Hell. Worshippers offer their souls through worship's duties to attain Paradise. Recognisers offer spirit and heart through witnessing's realities, seeking union with the Real.

Muṣṭafā ﷺ describes beautiful doing as worship in the awareness of seeing Allah, or of being seen by Him. It requires wakefulness and awareness, as though gazing on Him. The hadith's allusion joins the heart's encounter with the Real, the secret core's meeting with the Unseen, and the spirit's contemplation of its Patron.

This urges purified deeds, shortened wishes and loyalty to the First Day's acceptance. The spirits heard "Am I not your Lord?" and answered "Yes, You are!" (7:172). Serving the Protector fulfils loyalty to that acceptance. "As if you see Him" curtails wishes, and "He sees you" purifies deeds. An eye that has seen Him cannot busy itself with others. A spirit that has found His company cannot settle for water and dust.

The pure spirit hears "Return to your Lord" (89:28) and cannot make its home in clay. Familiarity with that Presence makes the veil's abasement unbearable. A ruler of his own city cannot settle for lifelong exile. Spirit has the attribute of subsistence, while water and dust pass away. Life in the Real differs from life confined to this world. The realiser knows that the Real can be seen, and "as if you see Him" testifies to that secret.

### In short

- Spending for jihād is obligatory as the need arises, over and above zakāh.
- "Destruction" means above all abandoning jihād to sit with one's family and estate, as Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī explained at Constantinople, and also withholding spending (Ibn ʿAbbās, Ḥudhayfah), despairing of mercy or refusing to repent (al-Barāʾ), reckless excess in giving, or a hopeless attack. Al-Jaṣṣāṣ accepts all of these.
- A lone charge against the enemy is not self-destruction (al-Barāʾ, with 4:84).
- Iḥsān in worship is to worship as if seeing Allah, and in dealings to wish for others what one wishes for oneself (the hadith of Jibrīl, Muʿādh).
- Inwardly, the rich spend wealth, the poor spend their attachment to the rich, and the recognisers spend their very spirits to reach the Real (6:91, 7:172, 89:28).
