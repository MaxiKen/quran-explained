<<<VERSE 4:47>>>
The call across the divide, with its warning attached: "O you who were given the Book! Believe in what We have revealed—confirming your own Scriptures—before We wipe out ˹your˺ faces, turning them backwards, or We condemn the defiant as We did to the Sabbath-breakers. The People of the Book are commanded to believe in what was sent down to Muḥammad ﷺ, the Glorious Book conforming to the good news they already hold, and warned of a disfigurement should they refuse.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Believe in what We have revealed—confirming your own Scriptures"** addresses the Jews and Christians with knowledge of the Torah and the Gospel, asking them to recognize the Book that confirms what they possess of Allah's divine Oneness and the traits and description of Muḥammad ﷺ.

**"before We wipe out ˹your˺ faces, turning them backwards"** carries the threat. Al-ʿAwfī reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that the effacing refers to blindness, and the turning means their faces are put on their backs, so that they walk backwards with their eyes behind them, as Qatādah and ʿĀṭiyah al-ʿAwfī said. The expression admits two probabilities: the features eliminated and the whole face turned around, or the face flattened like the nape of the neck, devoid of all features. It is a parable Allah set for ignoring the truth and turning from the plain path onto the paths of misguidance. Its likeness is the iron collars reaching to the chins, and the barrier before them and behind them (36:8-9).

**"or We condemn the defiant as We did to the Sabbath-breakers"** refers to those who breached the sanctity of the Sabbath by deceit in order to do more work, whom Allah changed into apes and swine, as the explanation of Sūrat al-Aʿrāf sets out. Whether the disfigurement and the transformation would come before the Day of Doom was argued by commentators, some holding it never came to pass because some of the Jews entered the Faith. On that view the verse suggests probability rather than certainty. Given their crime they deserve the punishment, and should He spare them, that is His mercy.

**"And Allah's command is always executed"** means that when He commands something, no one can dispute or resist His command.

### Kaʿb al-Aḥbār's Islam

The story reached the next generation through ʿĪsā ibn al-Mughīrah, in Ibn Jarīr's record, from a session with Ibrāhīm where Kaʿb's conversion came up. Kaʿb al-Aḥbār turned Muslim in ʿUmar's reign. Journeying to Jerusalem, he passed through al-Madīnah, where ʿUmar urged him to embrace Islam. His answer was to point to his Book: does it not say "The likeness of those who were entrusted with the Tawrāh..." (62:5), and he was among the entrusted? ʿUmar left him alone. On to Ḥimṣ in Syria he went, and there heard a resident reciting this very verse in sadness, and the fear of the threat struck him: I believe, O Lord, I have embraced Islam, O Lord. From there he returned to his family in Yemen and brought them back with him, all Muslims. After the verse was revealed, ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his companions embraced Islam, and it was said the threat had been conditional, lifting when some of them believed. The passage then turns to the one sin that blocks every door of pardon, shirk, as the next verse states, and to the intercession reserved for the major sinners of the ummah, as hadith al-Bazzār relates in the Rūḥ al-Maʿānī's gathering of the reports.

### In short

- The People of the Book are called to confirm the revelation that matches their own, before a disfigurement they cannot answer.
- The effaced faces and the backward walk are a parable of choosing misguidance, and the Sabbath-breakers' transformation stands behind the warning.
- The threat reads as probability, and Kaʿb al-Aḥbār's Islam in Syria, and Ibn Salām's with his companions, show the mercy side of it.
- Allah's command, when it goes out, is executed without dispute.

<<<VERSE 4:48>>>
The one unforgivable, and everything else negotiable: "Indeed, Allah does not forgive associating others with Him ˹in worship˺, but forgives anything else of whoever He wills. And whoever associates others with Allah has indeed committed a grave sin". The verse before called the People of the Book to faith. This one explains why some will not be forgiven: their kufr and their joining of partners.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah does not forgive associating others with Him"** means He does not forgive a servant who meets Him while associating partners with Him, if he dies in that state.

**"but forgives anything else of whoever He wills"** covers all other sins, to whom He wills of His servants, forgiving whom He wills by admitting him to Paradise without punishment, and punishing whom He wills of the believers for their sins and then admitting them.

**"has indeed committed a grave sin"** is the invention of a tremendous wrong, as Luqmān said to his son: joining others in worship with Allah is a great wrong indeed (31:13).

### The abundance behind the exception

Abū Dharr is the narrator of the two reports that open the verse's abundance. The first, whose chain Only Aḥmad carries, gives the divine word:

> O My servant, as long as you worship and beg Me, I will forgive you, no matter your shortcomings. O My servant, if you meet Me with the earth's fill of sin, yet you do not associate any partners with Me, I will meet you with its fill of forgiveness.

The second is the exchange Aḥmad recorded, on the servant who dies affirming that none deserves worship but Allah. Such a one enters Paradise, even if he committed adultery and theft. The allowance was pressed three times, and the fourth answer added the word about Abū Dharr's own nose being put in the dust. Abū Dharr departed pulling his izār and repeating it, and ever after told the hadith with that comment, and the two Ṣaḥīḥs carry it. The hadith of al-Bazzār then brings Ibn ʿUmar's report. The Companions used to hold back from begging forgiveness for the major sinners until they heard the Prophet ﷺ recite this verse. He added that his intercession on the Day of Resurrection was reserved for those among his ummah who commit major sins. And in the two Ṣaḥīḥs, Ibn Masʿūd asked which sin is greatest, and the answer came: to make a rival with Allah, while He alone created you.

Behind the verse stands the whole scene of its passage. Kaʿb al-Aḥbār won to Islam in Syria, in ʿUmar's reign at al-Madīnah, on the Jerusalem road, as ʿĪsā ibn al-Mughīrah told it in Ibrāhīm's circle. Ibn Salām and his companions believed after the revelation. With them stand al-ʿAwfī's report from Ibn ʿAbbās on the effaced faces, the Rūḥ al-Maʿānī's gathering, and the warning of Sūrat al-Aʿrāf's transformed Sabbath-breakers.

### What counts as shirk

The verse's articles are the sine qua non of religion: beliefs relating to the Being and Attributes of Allah, and holding the like of them for whatever He created is shirk. It has forms. Shirk in knowledge: believing that some pious person or spiritual master knows everything in the universe all the time, or asking an astrologer or fortune-teller about what is to come, for these belong to the unseen world, Allah's exclusive domain. It also covers taking omens from a pious man's writings, calling someone from a distance in the belief that the call has been received, and keeping a fast dedicated to other than Allah. Shirk in power: taking anyone besides Allah to have the authority to give gain or inflict loss. It includes asking anyone for sustenance or children. Shirk in worship: going down in prostration for anyone, releasing an animal dedicated in someone's name, making a vow or making tawāf of someone's grave or house. It covers bowing to anyone in the manner of the prayer's rukūʿ, slaughtering in someone's name, taking the world's affairs as ruled by the stars, and regarding a month as ill-omened.

### The inner reading

The associationism of the common people is the greater associationism: to say that the tremendous Enactor has an associate, or make Him similar to something of creation, which is to call upon an idol and be held back from the religion of guidance. The right belief holds the Creator pure of spouse, child and partner, free of new arrival and change. His attributes are pure, His artisanry fitting, His speech sweet and His love complete, a God outside imaginations whom no one knows how He is. The lesser associationism is of two sorts: for the faithful it is eye-service and the letting go of self-purification in deeds, which lessens faith, fissures certainty and closes the door of clarity, and for the recognizers it is the paying of regard to deeds and the seeking of deliverance thereby. Muṣṭafā ﷺ said: "I am very frightened of the ancient associationism for my community", and explained it as the mixing of eye-service into the doing of good deeds. Shaddād ibn Aws said he saw God's Messenger weeping over it.

### In short

- Shirk alone is closed to pardon for the one who dies in it, and every other sin is within Allah's will to forgive.
- The earth's fill of sin meets the earth's fill of forgiveness where no partner is joined, and the intercession is reserved for the major sinners of the ummah.
- Shirk wears many dressings, in knowledge, in power and in worship, from fortune-telling to the tawāf of graves.
- The lesser associationism, eye-service in deeds, is what the Prophet ﷺ feared for his community, and what made Shaddād see him weep.

<<<VERSE 4:49>>>
Self-certified holiness: "Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who ˹falsely˺ elevate themselves? It is Allah who elevates whoever He wills. And none will be wronged ˹even by the width of˺ the thread of a date stone". A strange lot, attributing sanctity to themselves and then broadcasting it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who ˹falsely˺ elevate themselves?"** was revealed, in the reading of al-Ḥasan and Qatādah, about the Jews and Christians who said: "We are Allah's children and His loved ones", the claim echoed in their appeal to their father's faith (5:18). Ibn Zayd connected it with their claim that Paradise admitted none but a Jew or a Christian (2:111). One report names Buhayr ibn ʿAmr and Marḥab ibn Zayd.

**"It is Allah who elevates whoever He wills"** means the decision belongs to Allah alone, whose knowledge covers the true reality and the secrets of all things, and who purifies whom He will through faith.

**"none will be wronged even by the width of the thread of a date stone"** means no one's reward is reduced by the least injustice, for the fatīl is the scalish thread in the long slit of the date-stone, as Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid with ʿIkrimah, ʿAṭāʾ, al-Ḥasan and Qatādah, among others of the Salaf, said.

### Why self-praise is forbidden

Claiming sanctity for oneself or others is forbidden on three counts. First, the cause of self-praise is mostly pride and arrogance, so what is really forbidden is pride. Second, only Allah knows how a person's end will come, whether in taqwā and purity, so the claim contradicts being God-fearing. Zaynab, daughter of Abi Salamah, is the narrator: the Prophet ﷺ asked her name, she said Barrah, pure from sins, and he said: "Do not claim sanctity for yourselves, for it is Allah alone who knows best who among you is righteous." He then named her Zaynab. Third, the claim plants the false idea that the claimant is nearer to Allah for being faultless, a lie, since no mortal is free of shortcoming. Where these impediments do not exist, a person may speak of a trait as gratitude for Allah's blessing.

### The inner reading

The instruction runs: no self-praise, no welcoming of others' praise, no eye on one's own good deeds, no weight given to one's own travelling, and the considering of oneself lower than everyone low. The group that has stolen reality's ball from the world's creatures never shows itself to itself for the sake of showing, never walks the edge of making claims, and their worthlessness and abasement makes them look like the riffraff in people's eyes. At the threshold of a generous man, the more abased a person makes himself, the more exalted he becomes, and the abasement is not despair of the Friend but testimony of truthfulness. The Pir of the Ṭarīqah prayed:

> O God, I lament at my abasement, for I have seen no one with misery like mine. I wept so many tears in longing that I sowed the seeds of pain with my eyes' water. If my eyes fall upon You once, I will not have seen myself with those eyes. When God's deeming pure reaches someone, its mark is release from the company of the scatter-hearted, as the next verses will show with the prostration before idols, the judgment of Ibn Abī Ḥātim's reports, the trench dug around al-Madīnah, and the alliance struck at Makkah.

### In short

- Sanctity is not self-declared: the Jews and Christians who called themselves Allah's children and loved ones are the verse's first exhibit.
- No soul's reward is trimmed by even the thread of a date stone.
- Self-praise is barred for pride, for the unknowability of one's end, and for the lie it plants in others, and the Prophet ﷺ renamed Barrah to make the point.
- On the inward road, self-regard is the greatest veil, and abasement at the threshold is exaltation.

<<<VERSE 4:51>>>
The scene of the great betrayal: "Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures yet believe in idols and false gods and reassure the disbelievers that they are better guided than the believers". Scripture-holders prostrating to idols, and grading the idolaters above the believers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who were given a portion of the Scriptures"** names the Jews with knowledge of the Torah, one report naming Mālik ibn al-Sayf and some seventy friends.

**"yet believe in idols and false gods"** names al-Jibt and al-Ṭāghūt, two idols of the Quraysh. Jibt was originally the name of an idol, later used for every false god and for sorcery, and in Ethiopian, a magician. ʿUmar said Jibt is magic and Ṭāghūt is Satan. Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Jubayr and Abū al-ʿĀliyah said Jibt is a magician and Ṭāghūt a soothsayer. Mujāhid said Ṭāghūt is a devil in the shape of a man to whom they refer for judgment, and Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded from Jābir that they are the soothsayers on whom the devils descend. Imam Mālik's reading is the most inclusive: Ṭāghūt is every object worshipped instead of Allah. Al-Qurṭubī preferred it, supported by the Qur'an's command to worship Allah and avoid Ṭāghūt (16:36), and there is no real contradiction among the readings.

**"and reassure the disbelievers that they are better guided than the believers"** exposes their preference of the disbelievers over the Muslims, out of ignorance and disbelief in the Book before them.

### The bargain at Makkah

Ibn ʿAbbās narrates the occasion. After the battle of Uḥud, Huyayy ibn Akhtab and Kaʿb ibn Ashraf, chiefs of the Jews, came with a group to the Quraysh at Makkah, Kaʿb going to stir them to avenge their dead of Badr. The people of Makkah said: you are a people who cheat with false promises, so if you mean it, prostrate to these two idols. And he did, to reassure them. Then Kaʿb proposed that thirty of each side come forward and declare before the Lord of the Kaʿbah their resolve to fight Muḥammad ﷺ together. The Quraysh liked it, and that was the united front against the Muslims. Then Abū Sufyān asked the learned guests to judge between them and Muḥammad, and listed the ancestral credits: slaughtering camels for the ḥajj, offering milk to pilgrims, entertaining guests, keeping kinship ties, making tawāf of the House and performing ʿumrah. The other side was a man who had abandoned the ancestral religion, cut off his own people, and challenged the fathers with a new faith of his own. Kaʿb ibn Ashraf answered: "You are on the right path, and Muḥammad has gone astray." Thereupon Allah revealed these verses, exposing their lying and deceit, as the Rūḥ al-Maʿānī gathers the reports.

Kaʿb himself was a distinguished scholar among the Jews who worshipped none but Allah, yet the ghost of overweening desire overpowered him, and he swallowed the bitter pill of the prostration to achieve his end, showing no courage to part ways on a crucial issue. The Qur'an tells the like of Balʿam ibn Bāʿūr, a scholar with spiritual powers who launched his sinister plans against Mūsā (Moses), achieved nothing against him, and ended accursed and astray (7:175). Book-knowledge alone does not guarantee benefit without action, and action is impossible without abstaining from material greed. The alliance thus struck grew into the battle of al-Aḥzāb, where the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions dug a defensive trench around al-Madīnah. From it Allah saved the Muslims, driving the disbelievers back in their rage with no advantage gained, for He sufficed the believers in the fighting (33:25).

### The inner reading

Each person's Ṭāghūt is the lower self and its whim, the idol hunted among what is other than God. Heed paid to a person, a glance to a reason, a turn to a cause, obedience to a whim: that thing is his jibt and his Ṭāghūt. And whoever is at ease with the caprice of his own soul, remaining bound to what it desires, is a man of idols and a servant of demons, whatever his creed on paper.

### In short

- Jibt and Ṭāghūt are the idols of the Quraysh, and the names stretch to cover sorcery, soothsayers, Satan and every object of worship besides Allah.
- Kaʿb ibn Ashraf and Huyayy ibn Akhtab traded the Torah's judgment for a prostration and a verdict favouring Abū Sufyān's paganism.
- The bargain matured into the confederates' siege and the trench, where Allah sufficed the believers.
- Read inwardly, every soul's Ṭāghūt is its own whim, and whoever obeys it serves idols already.

<<<VERSE 4:52>>>
The verdict that ends the file: "It is they who have been condemned by Allah. And whoever is condemned by Allah will have no helper". The curse is pronounced, and the question the verse raises is who else ever stands under it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"It is they who have been condemned by Allah"** pronounces the curse, which means removal far from the mercy of Allah, with extreme debasement and disgrace, in this life and the next. For the Holy Qur'an says of the accursed that wherever they are found, they are to be seized and slain (33:61), and the disgrace of the Akhirah is far too grim to contemplate. In the reading of the passage, the immediate sense includes the humiliation put upon them, the capitation tax among its instruments.

**"And whoever is condemned by Allah will have no helper"** means no protector from His chastisement in this life or the Hereafter, for they sought the help of the idolaters and lured them with their verdict, and gathered the forces of al-Aḥzāb, and Allah drove them back.

### Who incurs the curse

The Prophet's ﷺ warnings reach a long list. He cursed the people of interest, the giver, the receiver, the consumer, the writer and the witness, all equally involved, in the report of Muslim. He said: "Cursed is he who does what the people of Lūṭ used to do", the reference being to the male act. He said Allah sends His curse on the thief who does not stop even at eggs and ropes, for which the hand is cut. He said: "Allah curses the eater of interest and its feeder, the woman who tattoos another and the woman who tattoos herself, and the picture-maker." He said Allah sends His curse on liquor, its consumer and server, its seller and purchaser, its squeezers, handlers, transporters and orderers, all of them. Abu Dāwūd and Ibn Mājah carry it. He said there are six types he had cursed and Allah had cursed them. They are the one who commits excess in the matter of Allah's Book, the one who rises to power by force and then honours whom Allah has disgraced and disgraces whom Allah has honoured, the denier of destiny, the one who takes as lawful what Allah made unlawful, the one of his progeny who rules as lawful what was forbidden, and the one who gives up his way, the sunnah. Al-Baihaqī records it in al-Mudkhal. He said Allah curses the ogler and the ogled at. The look at what is non-maḥram falls under the curse, and the looked-at likewise, where the look was deliberately invited. Abū Hurairah narrates his curse on men who take women's dress and women who take men's dress. ʿĀʾishah reported his curse on the woman who takes to masculine ways, in the report of Abu Dāwūd, and Ibn ʿAbbās his curse on the effeminate among men and the masculinized among women, with the command to expel them from the homes. From al-Bukhārī comes the report Ibn Masʿūd carries, and Abū ad-Dardāʾ the report of the curse's own journey:

The curse itself has rules, and the report of Abū ad-Dardāʾ, carried by Abu Dāwūd, shows its flight: when a servant curses something, the curse rises to the sky and its doors are shut against it. It comes down to the earth and the earth's doors are shut. It takes a right and a left, finds no access anywhere, and returns to the one cursed. If he deserves it, it falls on him, and otherwise it returns to its pronouncer. The wind showed the same lesson, in the report of Tirmidhī: it swooped away a man's cloak, he cursed it, and the Prophet ﷺ told him not to, for it was under orders from Allah, and whoever curses what does not deserve it gets the curse back on himself. Unless it is known that a person died in a state of kufr, invoking the curse on him is not permissible, even if he was sinful. Ibn ʿĀbidin advised against cursing Yazid on that principle. A confirmed kāfir like Abū Jahl or Abū Lahab, however, may be cursed by name. Cursing without naming anyone is permissible, as in God's curse upon liars and tyrants. And in the terminology of the sharīʿah, the curse on disbelievers means their far removal from mercy, while on Muslims it means their falling from the level of the righteous.

### The inner reading

The companions of al-Jibt and al-Ṭāghūt deserve the curse that expels from the carpet of service, ʿubūdiyya, and veils from witnessing lordship. Read inwardly, then, the curse is the loss of service itself, and no helper can restore the servant who has walked out from under the Real's care to serve his own whim, for the Ṭāghūt he chose has nothing to give.

### In short

- The curse is removal from mercy, disgracing its bearer in both worlds, and no helper stands beside the one Allah has cursed.
- The Prophet's ﷺ curse reached interest in all its parties, the act of the people of Lūṭ, theft, tattoos, pictures, liquor in every role, and the six types al-Baihaqī's al-Mudkhal carries.
- The curse is not a loose word: the undeserving curse returns to its pronouncer, the unknown end bars it, and Abū Jahl and Abū Lahab stand among the confirmed names.
- Inwardly, the curse is expulsion from the carpet of ʿubūdiyya, and the whim that replaced Allah cannot help its server.
