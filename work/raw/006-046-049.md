<<<VERSE 6:46>>>
"Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, 'Imagine if Allah were to take away your hearing or sight, or seal your hearts—who else other than Allah could restore it?'" The argument is put as an imagination exercise, and it starts from the one thing nobody disputes: these senses are there, and nobody put them there.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺"** means: say this to the people of Makkah, to these idolaters who deny what you have brought. **"Imagine if Allah were to take away your hearing or sight"** is a question about something He has already given. He would leave you unable to hear any admonition or guidance, and unable to see the truth. **"or seal your hearts"** means that nothing would reach you from either. The hearts would no longer take in the truth or understand anything. **"who else other than Allah could restore it?"** asks who besides Allah could hand back what He has taken. The gods meant here are their idols, the very beings they count as partners. **"See ˹O Prophet˺ how We vary the signs, yet they still turn away"** is the comment on it. Look at how the proofs are set out in more than one way and made plain and clear. After all that they still turn aside from the truth, and keep others from following it.

### The senses are evidence, not property

The two senses named here are also named elsewhere as gifts: it is He who created you and endowed you with hearing and sight (67:23). And: "Say: it is He who produces hearing and sight" (10:31). Another verse adds the third piece and the power over it: know that Allah comes in between a person and his heart, and that He withholds the use of a sense from whom He wills (8:24). A further reading takes the verse as a description of what is already happening rather than as a threat of physical loss. On it, the disbelievers are not allowed to benefit from their hearing and sight in religious matters at all. On that reading the sealing has begun, and the question only names what they have already brought on themselves. The signs, explained in this variety, testify to the oneness of Allah in lordship, and to the falseness of everything worshipped besides Him.

### The worse deprivation

Ears, eyes and a heart are given so that a person will hear the signs, see them and accept them through rational argument. What a man is meant to do with these capacities is decided by the One who gave them. If he does not use them for the purpose they were made for, he runs the risk of being judged incapable, with the capacities themselves snatched away. A person left deaf, blind and unable to reason is helpless enough in this world. That is the picture the verse puts in front of them. But a greater helplessness than that exists, and this is the point of the exercise. It is to have ears and be deaf to the truth. It is to have eyes and be blind to it, and to carry a heart in one's chest that cannot understand it. That deprivation is worse than the first, because it leaves a man debased and worthless where it matters. No shortcoming is more heinous than that.

### In short

- The verse asks the people of Makkah to name anyone but Allah who could restore hearing, sight and understanding once He has taken them.
- Their idols are the gods meant in the question, and they cannot give back what they never had.
- 67:23, 10:31 and 8:24 are quoted to show that these senses are produced and withheld by Allah alone.
- On one reading the sealing is not threatened but already in force, since no religious benefit reaches them through these senses.
- The real loss is not blindness but having the equipment and refusing to use it.

<<<VERSE 6:47>>>
"Ask, 'Imagine if Allah's punishment were to overwhelm you with or without warning—who would be destroyed other than the wrongdoers?'" The people of Makkah are now asked to say where the punishment would land if it came at all. The answer takes the fear out of one side of it and puts it back, heavier, on the other.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Imagine if Allah's punishment were to overwhelm you"** means it strikes while you are unaware and have no news of it, or comes in the middle of the night. **"with or without warning"** is the second half of the pair: openly, in the daylight, before your eyes, in public. **"who would be destroyed other than the wrongdoers?"** is answered by the verse itself: no one but the evildoing and unbelieving folk would perish. The torment falls on people who have done wrong against themselves by shirk. The people who worship Allah alone, without partners, are saved from it, and they have no fear and no sorrow. Another reading takes the wrongdoers here to be the idolaters specifically. The other half of that safety is spelled out later in this surah, in the words about those who believe and do not confuse their belief with wrongdoing, that is, with shirk (6:82).

### The dare that misreads the situation

A man who is warned about the result of denying the truth and still answers insolently is a man who has been well placed in the world. He has concluded that he need not fear the scourge of Allah, and he imagines himself exempt from divine retribution. The more daring among them put the challenge into words. They say to the messenger of God that if he is truthful, Allah should let His wrath fall on them, and they will watch it arrive. They do not understand that when Allah vents His anger, it lands on them and on nobody else. The verse before asked whether anyone could give their senses back. This one asks whether anyone can stand in the way of a punishment, and both questions have the same answer.

### In short

- The verse asks them to say whom the punishment would reach, with warning or without it.
- Its answer is that none perish except the wrongdoing people, and the idols cannot change that.
- Those who worship Allah alone are saved, and so have nothing to fear and nothing to grieve over.
- The insolent demand to be shown the punishment at once only proves that they think themselves exempt.
- Another verse in this surah names the other half of the same quality: belief that has not been confused with wrongdoing or shirk (6:82).

<<<VERSE 6:48>>>
"We have sent messengers only as deliverers of good news and as warners." The verse states the job description, and with it the limits of what a messenger is for. He is not sent to compel, and the two halves of his work belong together.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have sent messengers only as deliverers of good news"** means they bring glad tidings to Allah's servants, good news of Paradise for whoever believes, and they command whatever is good and righteous. **"and as warners"** is the other half: they warn whoever disbelieves of Allah's anger and of every kind of torment, with news of the Fire. **"Whoever believes and does good"** is the one who believes in his heart with what the messengers were sent with, trusts in them and in the Scriptures, and puts his deeds right by following them, setting straight what is between himself and his Lord. **"there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve"** says that nothing will befall them on the Day of Judgment when fear falls on the people of Hell, and that they will not grieve over the pleasures of this world they missed when the people of Hell grieve.

### Why the pair cannot be split

The preacher of Allah's word appears in the world as a bearer of good news and as a warner at once, and the test runs on exactly that. A person is asked to recognize the truth when it arrives in the language of admonition and reform himself accordingly. If he refuses to recognize it and makes magic feats and charms a condition for accepting it, he proves nothing except his own moral blindness. For people with that blindness nothing is left but ruin and destruction in this world of Allah. The good news and the warning are one message, delight that never ends on one side and severe punishment on the other.

### What they leave behind

The promise has a second edge, about the past rather than the future. Whoever believes and does right has no fear of what is ahead, and no grief over what he left behind in this world, because Allah is the Wali, the Protector, over whatever they leave. The protection covers what was given up, which is how the verse answers the complaint that a believer loses out. He does not, and the One who guards what he left is no distant party.

### In short

- The verse limits the messenger's work to two things: good news for the obedient and warning for the disobedient.
- Belief here is belief in the heart, matched by deeds put right through following the messengers.
- No fear reaches these people about what is ahead of them, and no grief about what they missed.
- Allah is their Wali and Protector over what they leave behind, which is what closes the complaint of loss.
- A demand for feats of magic as a condition of belief turns the warner's work into proof of the demander's blindness.

<<<VERSE 6:49>>>
"But those who deny Our signs will be afflicted with punishment for their rebelliousness." The other half of the pair is put in one line, and the line is about why the punishment falls.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But those who deny Our signs"** are the people who deny the Qur'an and deny the messenger it came through. **"will be afflicted with punishment"** is the torment falling on them, and **"for their rebelliousness"** names the reason: they were wicked and went against obedience. The punishment strikes because they disbelieved the message the messengers brought, defied Allah's commands, did what He prohibited and crossed the limits He had set. Had they not gone against Him, had they carried out His instructions and stayed away from what He prohibited, the punishment would not have touched them at all.

### In short

- Denying the signs is described as rebellion, which is a refusal that knows what it is refusing.
- The torment is caused by their own conduct, not by the warning that announced it.
- The counterfactual is stated plainly: obedience and keeping away from what is prohibited would have kept the punishment off them.
- The verse is the second half of the job description in 6:48, and it belongs to it.
