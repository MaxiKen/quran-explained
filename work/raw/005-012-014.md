<<<VERSE 5:12>>>
"Allah made a covenant with the Children of Israel and appointed twelve leaders from among them and ˹then˺ said, “I am truly with you. If you establish prayer, pay alms-tax, believe in My messengers, support them, and lend to Allah a good loan, I will certainly forgive your sins and admit you into Gardens under which rivers flow. And whoever among you disbelieves afterwards has truly strayed from the Right Way.”"

This verse recalls a covenant that Allah made with the Children of Israel. He appointed twelve leaders from among them, one for each tribe, and He gave them a set of duties and a promise. The duties are prayer, alms-tax, belief in His messengers, support for them, and a good loan to Allah. The promise is forgiveness of sins and admission to Gardens with rivers. The verse closes with a warning that whoever disbelieves after this has strayed from the right way. The material places the verse within the chapter's wider reminder of the covenants that the believers and earlier communities made with Allah. It follows directly from 5:11, where the believers were reminded of Allah's protection, and it is linked to the earlier call to keep the covenant of hearing and obeying in 5:7.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah made a covenant with the Children of Israel and appointed twelve leaders from among them."** The material explains that the twelve were representatives of their tribes, each of whom took responsibility for his people keeping the covenant. The material reports that Muhammad bin Ishaq and Ibn 'Abbas said this happened when Musa was about to fight a powerful enemy in the land, and Allah commanded him to choose one leader from each tribe. The leaders bound their tribes as well as themselves, so the covenant was a shared responsibility. Some readings say the twelve were chieftains and others say they were messengers, and the material does not settle the point, so the entry records only what is agreed.

The material draws a parallel with the Muslims. When the Ansar of Madinah came to pledge at the 'Aqabah, twelve of their leaders gave the pledge on behalf of their people. Three were from the tribe of Aws, among them Usayd bin al-Hudayr, and nine were from Khazraj, among them Abu Umamah As'ad bin Zurarah, Sa'd bin ar-Rabi', 'Abdullah bin Rawahah, al-Bara' bin Ma'rur, 'Ubadah bin as-Samit, and al-Mundhir bin 'Amr. Ka'b bin Malik named them in a poem that Ibn Ishaq recorded. The material says this shows that a representative from each group was a sound method of binding a people to a pledge, because a man of the tribe knows its feelings better than an outsider.

The material also notes Islam's principle on lineage. It says that in Islam a person is honoured by his faith, not by his family, and it cites the Prophet's address at his last Hajj, which said that no one has priority over another on the basis of colour, language or lineage. The material does not say that family and tribe have no value in administration, only that they must not outweigh faith.

**"I am truly with you."** The material explains that Allah's words "I am with you" mean two things. The first is that Allah gives support, protection and help to those who keep the covenant. The second is that Allah knows everything they do, and no one can escape His knowledge by breaking the pledge. The phrase is therefore both an encouragement and a warning.

**"If you establish prayer, pay alms-tax."** The material says that the duties of prayer and zakat were enjoined on the Children of Israel before Islam, and that these are not peculiar to any one community. It states that other verses and reports show every prophet and every law enjoined them. The material notes that zakat is an obligation on each individual, and it observes that many people pay it grudgingly and in less than the full amount.

**"Believe in My messengers, support them."** The material explains that belief in the messengers comes before the duties of prayer and zakat in the order of faith, but in this covenant the duties are mentioned first. The reason it gives is that the messengers who were to come later were not yet present when the covenant was made. The material says that supporting the messengers means helping them to carry their message.

**"And lend to Allah a good loan."** The material explains that a good loan means spending with sincerity, without any worldly motive, and from what a person holds dear, not from what is left over or worthless. Spending for Allah is called a loan because the return of a loan is owed in law, custom and morality. The material explains that because zakat has been mentioned separately, the good loan refers to voluntary charity. It also gives an example of such charity: building a mosque, or supporting religious education when the state does not. The material notes that zakat is an individual obligation, while such communal duties are a collective obligation that is discharged when enough people take them up.

**"I will certainly forgive your sins and admit you into Gardens under which rivers flow."** The material says that the promised forgiveness covers sins and errors, and that the Gardens are the protection from what is feared and the attainment of what is sought.

**"And whoever among you disbelieves afterwards has truly strayed from the Right Way."** The material explains that the warning is for those who break the covenant after accepting it. They have not simply erred, but have left the straight path to their own harm. The material notes that a person who denies a covenant he has made is more blameworthy than one who never made it.

### The twelve springs and the twelve rulers

The number twelve recurs in the story. The material cites Allah's favour in Surat al-A'raf, 7:160, where He divided the people into twelve tribes, and where twelve springs gushed from the rock when Musa struck it with his staff. The material says that the number is unusual and that it has given rise to popular interpretations, and it treats the springs as a sign of the care Allah took to give each tribe its own share.

The material also reports the saying recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim, through Jabir bin Samurah, that the affairs of the people will remain in order while twelve leaders govern them. It records Abd al-Razzaq's view that these need not rule one after another without a break. On that reading, the four rightful caliphs, Abu Bakr, 'Umar al-Faruq, 'Uthman al-Ghani and 'Ali al-Murtada, are counted among them, and 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz is accepted as the fifth.

### Those named as leaders at 'Aqabah

The material names the men who gave the pledge at 'Aqabah with more care. Rafi' bin Malik bin al-'Ajlan is named in the list from Khazraj, and 'Abdullah bin 'Amr bin Haram is also named there. Abu al-Haytham bin at-Tayhan is named from Aws, as one of the three. The material says the naming of these men was recorded in a poem by Ka'b bin Malik, and that the men acted by the Prophet's command, pledging obedience on behalf of their people.

### The covenant and the will to keep it

The material links the verse to the call in 5:8 to stand for justice and to be mindful of Allah, and to the call in 5:11 to recall the favour of Allah. Qatadah is reported to have said that a later verse, the one commanding the believers to fight those who do not believe in Allah, is linked to the pardon of this verse. The material links the verse to the call in 5:7 to be mindful of Allah. It says that the only thing that holds a person to a pledge is taqwa, the fear of Allah, and that where taqwa is absent, pledges are easily broken. It also says that the victory of the believers comes from taqwa and trust in Allah, not from weapons and wealth alone. The verse therefore closes by inviting the reader to reflect on the difference between the one who keeps the covenant and the one who breaks it.

### In short

- Allah made a covenant with the Children of Israel through twelve representatives, and the covenant bound each tribe.
- The duties were prayer, zakat, belief and support for the messengers, and a good loan to Allah, with forgiveness and Paradise as the promise.
- Breaking the covenant after accepting it is straying from the right way, and only taqwa keeps a pledge in force.

<<<VERSE 5:13>>>
"But for breaking their covenant We condemned them and hardened their hearts. They distorted the words of the Scripture and neglected a portion of what they had been commanded to uphold. You ˹O Prophet˺ will always find deceit on their part, except for a few. But pardon them and bear with them. Indeed, Allah loves the good-doers"

This verse describes what followed the breaking of the covenant. Because they broke it, Allah removed them from His mercy and hardened their hearts. They altered the words of the Scripture from their places and forgot a portion of what they had been reminded of. The Prophet ﷺ is told that he will always find deceit among them, except for a few, and he is told to pardon them and bear with them, because Allah loves those who do good. The material explains the verse as a description of a community that has been punished for its wrongdoing, and as an instruction to the Prophet ﷺ to forgive them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But for breaking their covenant We condemned them and hardened their hearts."** The material explains that the curse is removal from Allah's mercy. The hardening of the hearts means that the people cannot accept advice or the truth. The material cites a reading that the hearts were made rusty and unable to accept good, which it links to the saying of the Prophet ﷺ that each sin leaves a dark mark on the heart. If a person repents, the mark is erased, but if he persists, the marks accumulate until his heart becomes like an upturned pot, which takes in nothing. The material says this is the cash punishment for sin in this world: one sin draws the heart to the next, and one good deed draws the heart to the next good deed.

**"They distorted the words of the Scripture."** The material says this includes changing the words of the Scripture, altering their meaning and changing their recitation. It says that these changes are described in the Qur'an and in the books of hadith. The material gives examples of their deceit against the believers, and it says that a plot was made to kill the Prophet ﷺ, which Allah revealed to him.

**"And neglected a portion of what they had been commanded to uphold."** The material explains that they set aside part of their law, and that the portion they neglected concerned the description of the Prophet ﷺ and the duty to follow him.

**"You will always find deceit on their part, except for a few."** The material says that the exception refers to those who believed and held to the covenant. It gives the example of 'Abdullah ibn Salam and his followers. It also notes that the verse was read as a description of the Banu Qurayzah in one account.

**"But pardon them and bear with them."** The material explains that the Prophet ﷺ is told not to treat them with contempt, because after knowing their state, good advice is unlikely to reach them. Yet gentleness and forbearance can sometimes bring a person to his senses. The material cites the view of some early scholars that a gentle approach is better than revenge. It also says that a person's own conduct must remain correct, because the effort to set others right begins with oneself. This verse was, in the material's account, read as abrogated by the verse of the sword in Surat at-Tawbah, though other readings hold that the pardon remains in force.

**"Indeed, Allah loves the good-doers."** Mujahid is reported to have said the deceit refers to the plot against the life of the Messenger ﷺ, and the material says the surah al-Mutaffifin describes the stain that sin leaves on the heart. The material says that Allah loves those who act with good grace, and that this is the path that brings the believers nearer to Him. The material also says that the Torah contains the description of the Prophet ﷺ, which the people were bound to follow.

### The warning and the example

The material says that the verse serves as a warning against the same breaches that were committed by the earlier community. The believers are asked to keep their own covenant and to be patient with those who err. It notes that the believers must not repeat the behaviour that cost the earlier community the mercy of Allah.

### In short

- The Children of Israel were cursed and hardened for breaking their covenant, and they altered and neglected parts of the Scripture.
- The Prophet ﷺ is told to pardon them, because forbearance can sometimes reach a hard heart, and because Allah loves those who do good.
- The verse warns the believers to keep their own covenant and to avoid the path of the earlier people.

<<<VERSE 5:14>>>
"And from those who say, “We are Christians,” We took their covenant, but they neglected a portion of what they had been commanded to uphold. So We let hostility and enmity arise between them until the Day of Judgment, and soon Allah will inform them of all they have done"

This verse turns to the Christians. It says that Allah took a covenant from those who called themselves Christians, but that they neglected part of what they were commanded. As a result, Allah stirred up enmity and hatred among them until the Day of Judgment. The verse ends by saying that Allah will tell them of all that they did. The material reads the verse as the parallel of the earlier case of the Children of Israel, with the division among the Christians as the punishment.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And from those who say, “We are Christians,” We took their covenant."** The material explains that the covenant was in the Gospel, that they would follow the Prophet ﷺ, help him, honour him and believe in every prophet that Allah sends. The material says the Christians imitated the Jews in breaking their covenant.

**"But they neglected a portion of what they had been commanded to uphold."** The material says that they set aside part of what they had been reminded of in the Gospel concerning faith and other matters.

**"So We let hostility and enmity arise between them until the Day of Judgment."** Al-Baydawi, in a marginal note on the verse, reports three early Christian groups: the Nestorians, who took 'Isa as the son of God, the Jacobites, who held that 'Isa was one with God, and the Melkites, who held that 'Isa was one of the Trinity. The material notes that such differences of belief made hostility inevitable. The verse 5:11 before this one recalls the same protection of the believers, so the verse 5:14 reads as the counterpart of the favour in 5:11. The material explains that the enmity is the division among Christian groups, who accused one another of heresy and disbelief. It names the groups that the material describes, and it says that these sects still disagree and refuse to pray in one another's places of worship. The material notes that the differences over the nature of 'Isa were the basis of the division. It also says that where the groups have no religious disagreement, the conflict does not arise, because the enmity was based on religion. One reading says the verse describes genuine Christians who practise the faith, and not those who have left it.

**"And soon Allah will inform them of all they have done."** The material says this is a warning and a threat to the Christians for their lies against Allah and His messenger, and for attributing a companion and a son to Allah. The material affirms that Allah is one and that He neither begets nor is begotten.

### The two communities

The material reads 5:13 and 5:14 together. The Jews and the Christians both received a covenant, both broke parts of it, and both were punished. In the Jews' case the punishment was a hardening of the heart and a loss of guidance, and in the Christians' case it was division and enmity among themselves. The material says that the verse 5:11 gives the believers the favour of protection, which the earlier people lost through breaking the covenant. The material says the verses show that breaking a covenant leads to the loss of unity and of guidance, whichever community is involved.

### In short

- Allah took a covenant from those who call themselves Christians, and they neglected part of it.
- Allah stirred up enmity among them until the Day of Judgment, and they will be told of their deeds.
- The verse shows that breaking a covenant brings division, as it did for the Jews, and it is a warning to the believers.
