<<<VERSE 7:86>>>
The Prophet Shuʿayb goes on to forbid his people the roadblocks they set up: "And do not lie in ambush on every road—threatening and hindering those who believe in Allah from His Path and striving to make it ˹appear˺ crooked. Remember when you were few, then He increased you in number. And consider the fate of the corruptors". When the people of Midian went to extremes in their wrongdoing, Shuʿayb urged them to be righteous and honest in their dealings. He made the need for honesty plain with clear arguments. But they would not accept his warning, and they would not accept his mission either. They twisted his teaching and spread misunderstandings about him among the people. They even turned to violence to stop people from supporting him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And do not lie in ambush on every road"**: do not sit in wait on every route that people use.

**"threatening"**: threatening travellers with death unless they handed over their money, for they were highway robbers, according to as-Suddī. Others say they beat and frightened strangers and stripped them of their clothes, or seized their garments, or charged them excise taxes. Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and several others said it means threatening the believers who came to Shuʿayb to follow him. The first meaning is better, because Shuʿayb first speaks of "every road" and only then mentions the second matter.

**"and hindering those who believe in Allah from His Path"**: barring from the path of Allah, His religion and obedience to Him, those who believed in Shuʿayb (Jethro) or wanted guidance, threatening to kill them.

**"and striving to make it ˹appear˺ crooked."** You want the path of Allah to look twisted and turned aside, other than it is, so that people will not follow it.

**"Remember when you were few, then He increased you in number."** You were weak because you were few, and later you became mighty through your great numbers. So remember Allah's favour and be thankful.

**"And consider the fate of the corruptors"**: look at the earlier nations and generations who spread corruption on the earth. See the torment and punishment they suffered for disobeying Allah and denying His messengers, and how their story ended in destruction and ruin.

### Was it one crime or two?

Some commentators say the two phrases, "threatening" and "hindering", carry the same sense. On this view the people stopped and threatened those who came to Shuʿayb, and that is what they were told not to do.

Others say these were two separate crimes. They sat on the highways looting and snatching people's goods, and they also stopped people from believing in Shuʿayb. So the first phrase describes the first crime and the second phrase the other. Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ and other works rely on this second view.

### Does the ban reach unlawful road tolls?

This command against robbery and extortion also covers checkpoints set up on roads to collect taxes that the Sharīʿah does not permit. ʿAllāmah al-Qurṭubī said that those who sit on highways and make people pay undue taxes against the Sharīʿah are criminals like Shuʿayb's people. Indeed, they are more oppressive and tyrannical than those people were.

### What does "seeking crookedness" mean?

These people were always looking for something on the path of Allah that they could twist, turn and misrepresent. From it they drew up their lists of doubts and objections, hoping to lure people away from the true religion.

### Why remind them of their numbers and of others' fate?

Shuʿayb used both encouragement and warning. The encouragement was the reminder of Allah's blessing. He had turned their small numbers into a large nation, or their economic weakness into wealth that needed no one. The warning was the call to look at the sad end of the peoples who had spread disorder on the earth. The peoples of Nūḥ (Noah), ʿĀd, Thamūd and Lūṭ (Lot) were examples of so many different punishments. Their fate should help Shuʿayb's people understand their own position and correct it.

### In short

- Shuʿayb forbade his people to lie in wait on the roads, robbing travellers and threatening those who came to believe in him.
- The command also covers unlawful tolls on roads. Al-Qurṭubī counted those who levy them among the worst oppressors.
- They sought to twist Allah's path and raise doubts so that people would turn from it.
- He reminded them how Allah had multiplied them, and warned them by the fate of Nūḥ's people, ʿĀd, Thamūd and Lūṭ's people.

<<<VERSE 7:87>>>
Shuʿayb answers a doubt that troubled his people: "If some of you do believe in what I have been sent with while others do not, then be patient until Allah judges between us. He is the Best of Judges.”" His people had split into two groups. Some believed in his call, and others went on rejecting him. Yet outwardly there was no difference between them, and both groups lived in comfort. If disbelief and denial were a crime, they reasoned, the criminals would surely have been punished.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If some of you do believe in what I have been sent with"**: some of you have indeed accepted the message my Lord gave me.

**"while others do not"**: and another group who do not believe in it.

**"then be patient"**: wait and see, O deniers, and do not be hasty.

**"until Allah judges between us."** Until Allah judges between us and you, by rescuing those who affirm this truth and destroying those who deny it. Then it will be clear who was on the path of truth and who were the liars. Those on the path of truth will be saved, and the liars will be destroyed.

**"He is the Best of Judges.”"** He is the fairest of judges. The happy outcome belongs to the God-fearing and obedient, and destruction to the disbelievers.

### Why does punishment not come at once?

The answer to their doubt was: do not be in a hurry. Allah is Forbearing and Merciful. He gives wrongdoers respite, and His decree comes into force only when they become utterly wicked and high-handed. Their present state was like that. If they held on to their denial, the time was not far off when the decisive punishment would overtake the deniers.

### In short

- Shuʿayb's people asked why believers and deniers lived equally well if denial was a crime.
- He told them to wait patiently, because Allah would judge between the two groups.
- Allah in His forbearance gives wrongdoers time. His decisive judgement comes when their wickedness is complete.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:88>>>
Shuʿayb's people had argued that if he were truthful, his believers would be prospering and those who rejected him would be suffering, yet both sides lived comfortably, so how could he be true? He told them not to be hasty, for Allah would soon decide between the two groups. Unconvinced, the arrogant chiefs said what oppressive and arrogant people always say: "The arrogant chiefs of his people threatened, “O Shu’aib! We will certainly expel you and your fellow believers from our land, unless you return to our faith.” He replied, “Even if we hate it". They threatened him and those who believed in him with exile from their town, or a forced return to the religion of the disbelievers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The arrogant chiefs of his people threatened"**: the leaders and important people who were too proud to believe.

**"“O Shu’aib! We will certainly expel you and your fellow believers from our land"**: we will drive you out of our town, you and those who believe in you. The chiefs addressed these words to the Messenger Shuʿayb, but meant those who followed his religion too.

**"unless you return to our faith.”"** Unless you all come back to our religion and our old way of life and worship.

**"He replied, “Even if we hate it"**: will you compel us, though we loathe what you invite us to and know the falsehood beneath it? He said this in astonishment. The question is a refusal. In effect: are you telling us to return to your faith even though we dislike it and know it to be false? It is a way of saying that this can never happen.

### Why "return" to a faith Shuʿayb never held?

It is easy to see why they told the believers to "return" to their old faith. All of them had once been part of the disbelievers' religion and way of life, and had accepted Islam only later. But Shuʿayb's case was different. He had never followed their false faith for a single day. No prophet of Allah could ever follow a faith opposed to pure monotheism. So why would they tell him to return to it?

Perhaps before he was given prophethood, Shuʿayb kept silent about their false words and deeds and lived among them as one of them. So his disbelieving people took him for one of their own, a fellow-traveller who shared their faith. Only when he called them to true īmān did they discover that his religion was different from theirs. That led them to conclude that he had left their old faith.

There is also a point of grammar. In addressing Shuʿayb, the plural, which covers his followers, outweighs the singular, because Shuʿayb himself had never belonged to their religious community. And Shuʿayb answered in the plural in the same way.

### In short

- The arrogant chiefs threatened to exile Shuʿayb and the believers unless they returned to the old religion.
- The threat was aimed at his followers too, who had once shared that religion.
- Shuʿayb had never followed their faith, but before his prophethood they had taken him for one of them.
- His reply, "Even if we hate it?", is a firm refusal to go back to what they knew was false.

<<<VERSE 7:89>>>
Shuʿayb completes his answer: "We would surely be fabricating a lie against Allah if we were to return to your faith after Allah has saved us from it. It does not befit us to return to it unless it is the Will of Allah, our Lord. Our Lord has encompassed everything in ˹His˺ knowledge. In Allah we trust. Our Lord! Judge between us and our people with truth. You are the best of those who judge.”" Allah had saved them from the false faith. Going back to it now would be a grave and false accusation against Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We would surely be fabricating a lie against Allah if we were to return to your faith"**: if we followed your disbelief and polytheism, joining partners with Allah as rivals to Him, we would be uttering a tremendous lie against Him.

**"after Allah has saved us from it."** He rescued us from your religion by His grace.

**"It does not befit us to return to it"**: it is not right or permissible for us to adopt your false religion of associating others with Allah.

**"unless it is the Will of Allah, our Lord."** Unless Allah wills it and abandons us to it, or, as one explanation puts it, unless He takes away the knowledge from our hearts. We will do whatever He wills. These words refer every matter to Allah's will.

**"Our Lord has encompassed everything in ˹His˺ knowledge."** His knowledge embraces all things, including my situation and yours. He has perfect knowledge of every matter, and His watch covers everything. So He knows what is right for His servants, and nothing is hidden from Him.

**"In Allah we trust."** We rely on Allah alone in all our affairs, in what we do and what we leave. He will keep us firm on the right path and guard us from the ways that lead to Hell.

**"Our Lord! Judge between us and our people with truth."** Decide between us and our people with justice. Grant us victory over them, and support the people of truth and the oppressed against those who oppress. Ibn ʿAbbās said that the word fatḥ here means "decide". That is how fātiḥ comes to mean a judge (qāḍī).

**"You are the best of those who judge.”"** You are the Most Just, who never wrongs anyone in His judgement.

### Why would returning be a lie against Allah?

First, to give kufr and shirk the status of a faith implies that Allah commanded them, and that is inventing a lie against Him. Second, to return to disbelief after believing in the revealed truth (īmān), and after gaining knowledge and insight into it, would mean saying that the first way was false and the later one true. That is plainly a double lie and slander: taking the true (ḥaqq) for false (bāṭil), and the false for true.

### Why add "unless Allah wills"?

Shuʿayb's words "we can never return to your religion" carried a hint of self-assertion. Outwardly at least, such a claim does not fit the selfless submission (ʿabdiyyah) of a true servant of Allah. It does not suit those who are near to Allah and know Him. So he added, "unless it is the Will of Allah, our Lord. Our Lord has encompassed everything in ˹His˺ knowledge. In Allah we trust."

This sentence mirrors his humility (ʿajz), his trust in Allah (tawakkul) and his confident handing over of every matter to Him (tafwīḍ). These are the master virtues of the prophets. In effect he says: who are we to say that we will do something or keep away from something? The ability to do good and to keep away from evil is nothing but Allah's grace. The Prophet ﷺ said the same: had it not been for Allah's grace, they would not have been guided, nor given in charity, nor prayed.

### Why did Shuʿayb turn to prayer?

Because he trusted Allah alone, Shuʿayb realised after speaking with the arrogant chiefs that nothing would move them. So he stopped addressing them and made his duʿāʾ to Allah: "Our Lord! Judge between us and our people with truth." Through these words Shuʿayb was in fact praying for the destruction of the disbelievers among his people. Allah answered his prayer, and they were destroyed by an earthquake.

### Why was a believing people destroyed for dishonesty?

When Allah's punishment came, the people of Midian were destroyed. In Allah's eyes, respecting the rights of fellow human beings and honesty in dealings matter so much that a community was destroyed despite claiming to be believers. Allah is the best Judge, and His judgement is never biased.

### In short

- Returning to the old religion would be a double lie against Allah, calling shirk a faith and calling the truth false.
- Shuʿayb tempered his refusal with "unless Allah wills", showing the humility, trust and surrender that mark the prophets.
- Doing good and avoiding evil come only by Allah's grace, as the Prophet ﷺ taught.
- When words had no effect, Shuʿayb asked Allah to judge between them. His prayer was answered with the earthquake that destroyed the deniers.
