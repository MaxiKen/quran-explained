<<<VERSE 7:86>>>
The call of Shu'ayb turns to the roads, where his people had made a trade of the travellers. "And do not lie in ambush on every road—threatening and hindering those who believe in Allah from His Path and striving to make it ˹appear˺ crooked. Remember when you were few, then He increased you in number. And consider the fate of the corruptors". The verse names the practice, forbids it, and then puts two motives in front of them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And do not lie in ambush on every road"** takes up the sittings themselves. The people used to set themselves on the roads at the entry points leading to the town, and the verse forbids it.

**"threatening"** gives the first thing done there. As-Suddi said that what they threatened people with was death if they would not hand over their money, since they were bandits. The first of the two readings of the passage is that it is about robbing travellers, and that reading is the better one, since Shu'ayb first names the sitting on every road, and then adds the second matter separately.

**"and hindering those who believe in Allah from His Path"** gives the second thing done there. Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and several others read the words the other way round, and said that the people sat on the road threatening the believers who came to Shu'ayb to follow him. So on that reading the road was blocked precisely against the people who wanted to hear him.

**"and striving to make it ˹appear˺ crooked"** is the further charge. They wanted the path of Allah to be twisted and turned aside, and they hunted for anything in it to misrepresent, so that people could be drawn away from the true religion with doubts and objections of their own making. The words about sitting at the roadside and charging a toll are also taken by some as an excise tax, which is the other face of the same crime.

**"Remember when you were few, then He increased you in number"** is the first motive put to them, and it belongs to persuasion. They had been weak because they were few, and afterwards they became mighty because of their numbers. They were asked to remember the favour of Allah, whether it lay in numbers that grew from small beginnings, or in an economic weakness that turned into freedom from need.

**"And consider the fate of the corruptors"** is the second motive, and it works by warning. The sense is that they should look at the sad end of peoples who had made disorder on the earth before them, and that the peoples of Nuh, of Ad, of Thamud and of Lut stood in front of them as examples of the different forms of punishment. What happened to those peoples should have been enough to show them their own situation, and to bring them to correct themselves. A further reading of the same words turns them to the idolaters before them and asks what the end of those people was.

### What the community's story leaves behind

When the people of Madyan went to the furthest end of their wrong ways, God sent Shu'ayb to them with His message. He urged them towards the way of righteousness and towards honesty in their dealings, and with clear arguments he made them aware of why honesty was needed. They were not willing to take the admonition. More than that, they would not accept his mission at all. They twisted what he preached, they made trouble between him and the people, and they turned to violent means to keep people from supporting him. In the end the retribution of God came down on them and they were destroyed.

What their case shows is the weight God gives to the rights of human beings and to honesty in dealings between people. A community was destroyed in spite of laying claim to belief, and the judgement of God cannot be swayed, since He is the best of judges.

### In short

- The people of Shu'ayb used to sit on the roads and rob and frighten the travellers who passed.
- On the other reading, they were blocking the road against those who came to believe.
- They also sought to make the path of Allah look crooked, so as to turn people away from it with invented doubts.
- They were reminded of the favour of Allah, who raised them from a small and weak people to numbers and strength.
- They were sent to look at the end of those who had made corruption before them, since the peoples of Nuh, Ad, Thamud and Lut each carried a lesson.
- The ruin of a people who claimed belief shows how heavily God weighs honesty in dealings and the rights of other human beings, and with that the words at the end of verse 86 have said all they have to say.

<<<VERSE 7:87>>>
The call comes to the division that had opened in the town. "If some of you do believe in what I have been sent with while others do not, then be patient until Allah judges between us. He is the Best of Judges.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"If some of you do believe in what I have been sent with while others do not"** describes the state the community had been brought to. Some of them had taken the call and others went on refusing it, and no outward difference had opened between the two.

**"then be patient until Allah judges between us"** is the answer to what they made of that. The people had raised a difficulty out of the division in their ranks, since both sides were living comfortably, and the argument they put was that if disbelief were a crime, the criminal would have been punished already. The answer given here addresses the same doubt. They were told not to make haste, and to wait, and the waiting was to last until Allah judged between them.

**"He is the Best of Judges"** gives the ground of the patience. Allah is forbearing and merciful, and He grants respite to those who do wrong. His decree begins to move against them only when they have gone past every limit in their wickedness and their high-handedness. So the state the two sides were living in was not evidence that the denial was of no account. If they held to it, the time was not far off when the decisive punishment would overtake the deniers.

The two groups are also put in the plainest terms, that when the judgement comes it will be made clear which side stood on the truth and which were the liars, and that those who stood on the truth will be saved while the liars will be destroyed. What Allah will give is the best end to those who are mindful of Him and obey Him, and He will destroy those who deny Him.

### In short

- The division in the town is named plainly, with some believing and some refusing.
- The people treated the comfortable state of both sides as an argument, and the answer is that they should not hurry.
- Allah grants respite to wrongdoers, and His decree moves when the wrong has gone past every limit.
- The judgement will show which side held the truth, and the outcome will be deliverance for one and destruction for the other.
- The end given to those who fear Him and obey Him is the better end, and the deniers are the ones who will be destroyed.

<<<VERSE 7:88>>>
The chiefs lose patience and turn to the threat they had been holding in reserve. "The arrogant chiefs of his people threatened, “O Shu’aib! We will certainly expel you and your fellow believers from our land, unless you return to our faith.” He replied, “Even if we hate it"". The verse records the threat and the first words of the answer.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The arrogant chiefs of his people threatened"** names the speakers. They are the council of the people, the ones who looked down on belief, and they are described by their pride, as the chiefs of the earlier community were.

**"O Shu’aib! We will certainly expel you and your fellow believers from our land"** is the first arm of the threat. Not convinced by the answer they had been given, they set out the choice they had always intended to force, and there was nothing else they could say. Either Shu'ayb and the rest of the believers returned to the fold of the old faith, or they would be thrown out of the town.

**"unless you return to our faith"** is the second arm, and it is worth a look. In the case of the other believers, a return was possible to imagine, since they had all been part of the faith and the way of the disbelievers and had come to Islam later. The case of Shu'ayb was different, since he had never followed their false faith even for a single day, and a prophet of Allah cannot follow a faith that runs against the pure oneness of God. What explains the demand is the way matters looked to them. Before prophethood was given to him, Shu'ayb kept quiet about their sayings and their doings and went on living among the people as one of them, and so his people took him for one more of their own, a fellow traveller and a follower of their faith. It was only when he gave the call to true faith that they discovered that his religion was not theirs, and from that they concluded that he had turned away from the old way. So the words are addressed to him in the plural, and the plural in their address takes in the rest of the believers as well, since Shu'ayb had never been part of their religious community.

**"Even if we hate it"** is what he said first. It is put as a question in the original, and the question is meant as a refusal. The sense is whether they were trying to make the believers go back to a faith they disliked and knew to be false, and the manner of asking says plainly that such a thing can never be.

### In short

- The chiefs of the people, who were proud, demanded that Shu'ayb and the believers return to the old faith or leave the town.
- The demand could be imagined for the others, who had once followed that faith, but not for Shu'ayb, who had never followed it.
- His people had taken him for one of themselves because he had lived quietly among them before his prophethood.
- The plural in their address takes in everyone who believed with him.
- His answer begins as a question, which is the strongest form of refusal, and asks how they could return to something they hate.

<<<VERSE 7:89>>>
The answer continues and becomes a prayer. "We would surely be fabricating a lie against Allah if we were to return to your faith after Allah has saved us from it. It does not befit us to return to it unless it is the Will of Allah, our Lord. Our Lord has encompassed everything in ˹His˺ knowledge. In Allah we trust. Our Lord! Judge between us and our people with truth. You are the best of those who judge.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"We would surely be fabricating a lie against Allah if we were to return to your faith after Allah has saved us from it"** gives the first reason. Allah had rescued them from that faith, and to go back to it would be to invent a falsehood against Him. The lie would be twofold. To treat disbelief, and the shirk that joins partners to God, as though they were faith would mean saying that Allah had commanded it, which is forging a lie against Him. Beyond that, after a person has believed in the revealed truth and has come to knowledge and insight about it, a return to disbelief amounts to saying that the first way was false and mistaken and the later one true and correct. That is a compounded lie, since it takes the true for the false and the false for the true.

**"It does not befit us to return to it unless it is the Will of Allah, our Lord"** qualifies the claim. There was a flavour of assertion in saying that they could never go back, and making such a claim sits awkwardly with the spirit of selfless submission that belongs to a servant of Allah. It does not suit those who are near to the presence of their Lord and have come to know Him. So he added the condition of the divine will, and the sense of it is that nothing is theirs to guarantee. Another reading of the same words takes them as a way of saying that the matter is not theirs at all, since the knowledge of it belongs to Allah, and if He were to take that knowledge from their hearts they would have nothing to hold to.

**"Our Lord has encompassed everything in ˹His˺ knowledge"** states the ground of that submission. His knowledge takes in all things, including the situation of Shu'ayb and the situation of his people, and there is nothing hidden from Him. He knows what is right for His servants.

**"In Allah we trust"** takes the answer one step further. The words are a mirror of humility, an affirmation of tawakkul, the trust in Allah, and a confident handing over of the affair to Him, and those three are among the master virtues of the prophets. What they come to is this, that a person is nothing and has no claim of his own to do a thing or to hold away from it, and that being able to do good and being able to keep away from evil are nothing but the grace of Allah. There is no strength in which a servant may say that he will stand on his own. The Prophet ﷺ gave the same sense in the words that had there not been the grace of Allah, people would not have been guided, nor have been able to give in charity, nor to perform the prayer. The trust Shu'ayb put in Allah was over all his affairs, over what he did and over what he left alone, and its point is that his Lord would keep him firm on the right path and guard him from the ways that lead down.

**"Our Lord! Judge between us and our people with truth. You are the best of those who judge"** is the supplication, and it comes at the moment when he saw that nothing would move those people. He stopped addressing the arrogant chiefs and turned to his Lord. Ibn Abbas said that the word used here means to decide, which is how the word for a judge takes its sense in the language.

What the prayer asked for in reality was the destruction of those among his people who disbelieved, and the destruction came when they were overtaken by the earthquake. So the supplication of a prophet against a people who had been given every chance was answered, and the answer is the subject of the verses that follow.

### In short

- The believers could not go back, since that would be inventing a lie against Allah after He had delivered them from that faith.
- The lie would be twofold, since it treats what God never commanded as His command, and takes the true for the false and the false for the true.
- The claim that they would never return is made subject to the will of Allah, which is the manner of a servant who does not bind his own Lord.
- The Lord's knowledge takes in all things, and nothing of their situation is hidden from Him.
- Trust in Allah is stated as the foundation, and it is coupled with humility, since guidance and the strength to obey are the grace of Allah alone.
- The supplication asked Allah to judge between them and their people with truth, and what it asked for in reality was the undoing of the deniers, which came in the form of the earthquake.
