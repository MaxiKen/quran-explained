<<<VERSE 2:238>>>
Prayer must be guarded as carefully as something precious: "Observe the ˹five obligatory˺ prayers—especially the middle prayer—and stand in true devotion to Allah". The command covers their appointed times, proper performance and inward humility. It singles out the middle prayer for particular care. The strongest emphasis in the material identifies it as 'Asr, the afternoon prayer, while other interpretations are also mentioned.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Observe the ˹five obligatory˺ prayers"** The verb has the sense of guarding and preserving. It includes ablution, standing, bowing and prostration, and all that is prescribed in prayer. It also includes care for the times and avoiding conduct that damages the spirit of worship. Prayer is not something left to whatever attention remains after worldly business.

**"—especially the middle prayer—"** Al-salat al-wusta means the middle prayer. A large majority identify it as 'Asr. Two prayers, Fajr and Zuhr, precede it, and Maghrib and 'Isha' follow it. It also falls when many people are absorbed in their work. Alternative identifications include the morning prayer, midday prayer and other opinions. Its distinct mention marks its merit whichever identification is being considered.

**"and stand in true devotion to Allah"** Qunut is explained as obedience, submission, humility and silence within prayer. The bodily standing must be accompanied by the heart's reverence. Ordinary conversation is inconsistent with that devotion.

### Why is prayer at its proper time so important?

Ibn Mas'ud, also called 'Abdullah in the report, asked the Prophet ﷺ which deed was dearest to Allah. The reply was prayer at its appointed times. The account then gives jihad in Allah's cause and dutifulness to parents in response to further questions. Ibn Mas'ud said the Prophet ﷺ would have told him more had he continued asking. The report is recorded in the Two Sahihs. The order is retained as this material gives it rather than supplied differently from memory.

The interruption of family-law instructions by these prayer verses also has a purpose. Obedience to Allah is the centre of social duties as well as ritual acts. Marriage, divorce and prayer are not separate worlds in which His guidance applies with different seriousness. The danger-related adaptation is treated under (2:239), with related passages in (4:102) and (4:103).

### What supports identifying the middle prayer as 'Asr?

At-Tirmidhi and Al-Baghawi describe this as the majority view among the Companions. Al-Qadi Al-Mawardi adds the majority of the following generation. Abu 'Umar bin 'Abdul-Barr gives the same account among authorities on transmitted reports, and Abu Muhammad bin 'Atiyah also identifies it as the majority interpretation.

The Companions named with it include 'Umar and 'Ali, Ibn Mas'ud and Abu Ayyub, 'Abdullah bin 'Amr, Samurah bin Jundub, Abu Hurayrah and Abu Sa'id. Hafsah, Umm Habibah, Umm Salamah, Ibn 'Abbas and 'A'ishah are also listed. Among later authorities are 'Ubaydah, Razin, Zirr bin Hubaysh, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ibn Sirin, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Al-Kalbi, Muqatil and 'Ubayd bin Abu Maryam.

During the battle of Al-Ahzab, the Confederates, the Prophet ﷺ said that the enemy had kept them from the middle prayer, explicitly naming 'Asr. He prayed it between Maghrib and 'Isha'. Ahmad records 'Ali's account. Muslim and An-Nasa'i also transmit it, and versions appear through several chains in the Two Sahihs, Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi and other Sunan collections. Muslim gives similar wording from Ibn Mas'ud and Al-Bara' bin 'Azib.

Samurah bin Jundub reports the direct statement that the middle prayer is 'Asr. Ahmad records it, while another version describes the Prophet ﷺ identifying it when asked, as Ibn Ja'far mentions. At-Tirmidhi grades the report Hasan Sahih, good and authentic. Abu Hatim bin Hibban also gives an explicit identification from 'Abdullah in his Sahih. At-Tirmidhi records Ibn Mas'ud's identification with the same grading.

Warnings about missing 'Asr reinforce its importance. Ibn 'Umar relates an authentic report likening its loss to losing one's family and wealth. Buraydah bin Al-Husayb reports in the Sahih an instruction to attend to 'Asr early on a cloudy day, joined to the warning that missing it can annul a person's good deeds. These are severe cautions against treating that prayer as easily expendable.

### Why is ordinary speech excluded?

Prayer was once accompanied by conversation. Zayd bin Arqam says people addressed their friends about everyday matters during it. After the command to stand devoutly was revealed, they were told to stop speaking. Ahmad records the account. The group of hadith collections transmits it except Ibn Majah, and Al-Bukhari and Muslim are explicitly named in another presentation.

When Ibn Mas'ud greeted the Prophet ﷺ during prayer, he did not answer. Afterwards he explained that prayer was sufficient occupation in itself. Body, tongue and heart already had their work there.

Muslim also records the instruction given to Mu'awiyah bin Hakam As-Sulami after he spoke in prayer. Ordinary human conversation was not fitting within it. Its speech includes tasbih, glorifying Allah, takbir, declaring His greatness, and remembrance. Ahmad and others transmit a saying that qunut in the Qur'an denotes obedience. The explanation of silence thus accompanies, rather than displaces, submission to Allah.

### What does guarding prayer mean inwardly?

The inward reading describes entering with awe, behaving with courtesy while present and leaving with reverence. The body performs its outward service, while heart and spirit attend to intimate conversation with the Lord.

Abu Bakr Shibli says that if given a choice between entering prayer and entering Paradise, he would not prefer the Garden's pleasures to whispering with the Giver of blessings. He contrasts bodily delight with the heart's contemplation, connecting the image of repose with (56:89). The poetry urges a visit to the Beloved's garden to witness the generosity of nearness.

The Prophet ﷺ, called Mustafa in the reflection, described the delight of his eyes as placed in prayer. Its attraction is not merely a duty completed but being occupied with Allah. Another poetic appeal asks that the heart be examined for any goal besides Him.

Abu 'Ali Siyah described his delight as seeing someone on Allah's path or speaking about Him. A verse pictures speaking to the heart about the Beloved through the night and seeking His scent in the dawn breeze. 'Abdallah Ansari, described as a knower of the tariqah, the spiritual path, asks for one undivided breath of nearness without a later veil. These images enlarge the meaning of devotion without replacing the required acts and times.

### In short

- Guarding prayer includes its times, outward requirements and inward humility.
- The majority identification of the middle prayer as 'Asr is supported by explicit reports, while other interpretations are also mentioned.
- Ordinary conversation was forbidden in prayer by the command to stand devoutly.
- Prayer is intimate attention to Allah, not an act whose spirit can be left out while its form remains.

<<<VERSE 2:239>>>
Danger changes how prayer can be performed, not the importance of remembering Allah: "If you are in danger, pray on foot or while riding." The verse permits an adapted form when normal performance is impossible. When safety returns, the full manner of prayer returns too. The ability to worship is itself something Allah has taught and granted.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you are in danger, pray on foot or while riding."** Fear may come from an enemy, a torrent, a predatory animal or another danger. Prayer is performed as circumstances allow, on foot or mounted, facing the qiblah, the direction of prayer, or another direction where necessary. Some legal explanations permit walking prayer in severe fear, while another requires remaining stationary or delaying during actual fighting.

**"But when you are safe, ˹take time to˺ remember Allah for teaching you what you did not know"** Normal prayer resumes with standing, bowing, prostration, sitting, supplication and attentive remembrance. The worshipper thanks Allah for the guidance and forms of worship that were previously unknown. The concessions of danger are not the permanent form after danger has gone.

### What forms of fear prayer are described?

Malik relates through Nafi' that Ibn 'Umar described prayer under intense fear as possible on foot or mounted, facing the usual direction or otherwise. Nafi' thought that Ibn 'Umar was relating it from the Prophet ﷺ. Al-Bukhari and Muslim transmit the account with that qualification.

Muslim, Abu Dawud, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah and Ibn Jarir record Ibn 'Abbas saying that Allah prescribed through the Prophet ﷺ four units while resident, two while travelling and one in fear. Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Qatadah and Ad-Dahhak are named with that understanding.

Al-Awza'i discusses fighting near a fortress when victory appears close. If normal prayer is impossible, individuals may pray through gestures. If even those cannot be made, prayer is delayed until fighting ends. When safe, they perform two units, or one with two prostrations if two are impossible. Simply saying takbir is not regarded there as sufficient where those forms cannot be performed. Makhul gives the same view. Al-Bukhari includes a chapter on praying while confronting forts and enemies.

Anas bin Malik recalls the attack on Tastar. Fighting intensified around dawn so that the Muslims could not pray until daylight had spread. They then prayed the dawn prayer with Abu Musa and gained victory. Anas said he would not exchange that prayer for the world and all it contained. Al-Bukhari records this wording. The delay is described in an exceptional battle setting, not as casual disregard of the appointed time.

### Why must the legal differences remain visible?

One explanation of the verse allows prayer while walking, riding camels or horses, or in whatever manner the danger permits. Bowing and prostration may be indicated by gestures. This preserves prayer under circumstances that prevent its complete form.

Another explanation permits standing prayer if the person can remain in one place. The prostration gesture is made lower than the bowing gesture. It does not allow prayer while walking. Where actual fighting prevents this form, it permits delay and later performance as qada', making up the prayer. These are distinct rulings. The permission to adapt must not be presented as though every legal account gives exactly the same adaptation.

The resident and traveller distinctions are also recalled in the explanation of what Allah has taught. The related account of fear prayer in (4:102) and the instruction to restore normal prayer when safe in (4:103) keep the concession connected to its circumstances.

### What does remembering the teaching involve?

The verse calls for gratitude as well as resumed performance. Allah has taught duties, proper forms and guidance that benefit this life and the next. Normal prayer should therefore be performed fully when its obstacles end, rather than retaining a reduced form for convenience.

An inward reading hears a call not to abandon intimate conversation at its appointed times when a possible form remains. The enemies that cause fear are not beyond Allah's power. Turning to Him within the heart is described as refuge and a promise of victory. Once fear passes, the servant returns to established devotion and gives full attention to worship, privately and publicly. That allusion does not remove the legal distinctions about what physical performance is possible.

### In short

- Severe danger permits forms adapted to what is possible, with differing legal explanations of walking, gestures and delay.
- The ordinary complete form returns when safety returns.
- Remembering Allah includes gratitude for the worship and guidance He has taught.

<<<VERSE 2:240>>>
The verse describes a provision protecting widows from immediate displacement: "Those of you who die leaving widows should bequeath for them a year’s maintenance without forcing them out." The major interpretation treats this earlier arrangement as replaced by the later waiting-period and inheritance rules. Other explanations distinguish an optional year of residence from the compulsory waiting period. The differences concern both the development of the rules and the widow's remaining housing rights.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those of you who die leaving widows should bequeath for them a year’s maintenance without forcing them out."** The arrangement includes food, clothing and residence from the deceased husband's property. The heirs must not expel the widow while she is entitled to remain. The statement directs a testament or bequest for her support, not a requirement that she be made to stay against her will for the whole year.

**"But if they choose to leave, you are not accountable for what they reasonably decide for themselves."** The widow may relinquish the optional benefit. The explanation that combines this with the later rule requires her compulsory waiting period to finish before lawful remarriage. If she then leaves or marries, the heirs need not continue the earlier arrangement of housing and support. Reasonable conduct means conduct permitted by the law, not a private decision to discard an uncompleted obligation.

**"And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise"** Allah's commands cannot be overpowered by the heirs' preferences. His wisdom governs the provision and the later changes to it. The closing words warn against abandoning a duty while also affirming the wisdom of the rules that replace an earlier arrangement.

### How is the majority view of replacement explained?

The majority say the year-long rule was abrogated, meaning replaced as a governing command. The four-month-and-ten-day waiting period in (2:234) superseded the longer period. The inheritance shares in (4:12) replaced the provision made by a required will.

Ibn 'Abbas describes the earlier widow remaining in her husband's house for a year while receiving maintenance. Later she received one-quarter or one-eighth of what her husband left under the inheritance rules. A pregnant widow follows the rule ending at childbirth. The fixed inheritance share removes the earlier need for the year-long bequest.

Mujahid, 'Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Ar-Rabi' and Muqatil bin Hayyan are named with replacement of the earlier period by (2:234). Another explanation connects the earlier arrangement with the general bequest discussion in (2:180), before inheritance shares had been specified. The widow's rights then rested on the will. later she had an allotted share in the estate itself.

The sequence of revelation and the order in the Qur'an are not treated as identical. This verse appears after the verse that, under the majority understanding, replaced its rule. That placement does not itself prove that the earlier legal arrangement remains unchanged.

### Why does the verse remain in its place?

Al-Bukhari records Ibn Az-Zubayr asking 'Uthman bin 'Affan why the verse was retained if another had abrogated it. 'Uthman replied that he would not change any part of the Qur'an from its place. Its arrangement belonged to revelation, not a decision to remove passages according to whether their earlier legal operation continued.

The question raised a real concern: someone might assume that a verse appearing later in the text necessarily gives the final rule. 'Uthman's answer preserves the revealed order rather than rearranging it to express that assumption. Understanding the law requires understanding the relation between passages, not editing the text to remove the need for explanation.

### How does Mujahid distinguish residence from waiting?

Another account from Mujahid, recorded by Al-Bukhari, keeps the obligatory waiting period at four months and ten days. It understands the rest of the year, seven months and twenty days, as an optional testamentary benefit. The widow may remain and receive it, or leave after the obligatory period ends.

Under that explanation, the year does not compel a year of mourning or postpone every remarriage for twelve months. It adds a choice of support and residence beyond the required wait. The words about not expelling and about voluntarily leaving fit the difference between an enforceable option and a compulsory period.

'Ata' quotes Ibn 'Abbas in another explanation: the verse removed the requirement of spending the waiting period specifically with the deceased husband's family. She could spend it where she wished. 'Ata' then says the inheritance rule in (4:12) replaced the residence entitlement.

The discussion cautions that removing support beyond four months and ten days is one issue, while denying the residence claim within that period is disputed. The two questions must not be treated as though the same argument settles both.

### What does Fari'ah's report say about residence?

Malik records through Zaynab bint Ka'b bin 'Ujrah the account of Fari'ah bint Malik bin Sinan, sister of Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri. Her husband pursued servants who had run away. At Al-Qadum they killed him. He had left her neither a house he owned nor maintenance, so she asked the Prophet ﷺ whether she could return to her family in Banu Khudrah.

He initially permitted it. Before she had fully left, he called her back and asked her to repeat her account. He then directed her to remain in her home until the prescribed period ended. She stayed there for four months and ten days. Later 'Uthman asked her about the matter and judged accordingly.

Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah also collect the report. At-Tirmidhi grades it Hasan Sahih, good and authentic. It is used to support residence in the husband's home during the compulsory period. Another legal statement gives al-Shafi'i's view that the habitation remains the widow's right. These positions must be kept alongside, not erased by, the reported view allowing residence elsewhere.

### Why was the earlier provision important?

The widow faced loss of companionship and possible loss of a home. The earlier arrangement prevented heirs from immediately turning her out. Because the additional benefit belonged to her, she could give it up. its existence did not authorise others to force her to do so.

Once inheritance shares were established, she could use her allotted estate interest after the required period. The discussion describes her ability to live in her share of the house and spend from her share of the property. The legal change is therefore presented as a change in the structure of her claim, not an instruction to leave her destitute.

Leaving or marrying unlawfully during an uncompleted waiting period is not covered by the permission for reasonable conduct. The material warns both the woman and those able to stop an actual violation who fail to do so. Equally, lawful departure after the period must not be treated as a violation merely because heirs prefer control.

### What does the inward reflection say about mourning?

It recalls the earlier continuous year of mourning and a poetic saying that weeping through a whole year discharged the expected duty. The later rule shortens that period because formal mourning has an end.

Another poem challenges a claim that the survivor cannot live after the other's death. Living people ordinarily continue living despite the loss of someone beloved. The point is not to ridicule grief, but to deny that devotion requires an endless mourning condition. A widow's later life and lawful companionship are not excluded by respect for the deceased.

The return to household rules after the prayer instructions also matters. Constant orientation towards Allah belongs in social relations as well as worship. The same Lord who commands guarded prayer directs care for the woman whose household has been disrupted by death.

### In short

- The earlier arrangement protected a widow with a year of maintenance and residence that she could voluntarily relinquish.
- The majority regard it as replaced by the four-month-and-ten-day waiting rule and fixed inheritance shares.
- Other explanations distinguish optional residence beyond the compulsory period, and housing during that period remains a subject of disagreement.
- Fari'ah's report supports remaining in the marital home through the prescribed wait.

<<<VERSE 2:241>>>
Separation must not be doubled by deprivation: "Reasonable provisions must be made for divorced women—a duty on those mindful ˹of Allah˺." The verse speaks broadly enough to include divorced women in different financial and marital circumstances. The explanations differ over the extent of an additional gift, while all preserve the obligation to meet the claims that apply.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Reasonable provisions must be made for divorced women"** Provision may be understood as a compensatory gift, the marriage payment or maintenance during the required waiting period. It is given honourably and according to the husband's means. Clothing or money can offer some comfort for the emotional loss of divorce.

**"—a duty on those mindful ˹of Allah˺"** Al-muttaqin are those who fulfil Allah's instructions and avoid what He forbids. The duty is not dismissed because a husband decides he does not wish to be especially generous. Mindfulness makes a person sensitive to what is due to another, including needs beyond a narrowly defended legal minimum.

### Is a gift required in every divorce?

'Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam relates a man's response to (2:236). That verse called the gift a duty of good-doers. The man said he could choose whether to be one of them. This verse answers that claim with a stronger statement of duty.

Sa'id bin Jubayr and Ibn Jarir require a reasonable gift for every divorced woman. Other early authorities share that view. It applies whether a payment was fixed or not, and whether intercourse occurred or not. On that reading, (2:236) gives particular details within a wider rule.

Another legal view treats the cases differently. The gift is required in the earlier case, but desirable in others. If provision here means mahr, the marriage payment, its obligation follows the corresponding categories. A fixed payment is paid in full after consummation. If none was fixed, full mahr al-mithl, the payment comparable to that of women in her family, is due. Before the marriage is consummated, the earlier cases call for either a suitable gift or half the fixed sum.

If provision means maintenance, it is obligatory during the applicable 'iddah, waiting period, after either a revocable or irrevocable divorce. These readings cover all cases. They do not make the sum due the same in each case.

### What is the moral purpose?

The inward lesson is not to combine the sorrow of separation with the further difficulty of financial deprivation. A lawful ending should not become a method of leaving the woman unsupported.

Islamic law sets rights that can be enforced. A mindful person also sees claims to kindness beyond that legal boundary. Greater fear of Allah should produce greater care in discharging duties, not greater ingenuity in refusing them.

### In short

- A divorced woman's applicable financial claims must be met honourably.
- The explanations differ over whether an additional gift is obligatory in every case or recommended beyond certain cases.
- Mindfulness of Allah includes sensitivity to the loss and needs of the person being released.

<<<VERSE 2:242>>>
The passage closes by explaining the purpose of its legal detail: "This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so perhaps you will understand." The rules about marriage, waiting, support and release are guidance to be comprehended and lived. They are not disconnected formulas whose purpose can be ignored.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you"** Allah explains what He allows, forbids and requires. His commands, prohibitions and boundaries are made plain. Where the practical matter needs detail, the explanation does not leave people only with an undefined generality.

**"so perhaps you will understand"** Understanding includes reflection on the meaning and purpose, followed by practice. The instruction seeks the good of this life and the next. The person who grasps a rule should become better able to fulfil another person's claims, not simply better able to argue about wording.

### What should reflection produce?

The surrounding passage shows the value of distinguishing different circumstances. The widow's period and estate claim are considered under (2:234) and (4:12), while (2:236) explains a particular case of provision after divorce. These distinctions belong to the clarification praised here, not to a reason for repeating every ruling in the closing verse.

The inward reading calls the revelations guideposts. A person learns refined behaviour by attending both to the commands and to the indications within them. The earlier rules then become instruction in courtesy, restraint and responsibility.

This also keeps legal understanding joined to mindfulness. Some rights are explicitly fixed. others are recognised more fully as conscience becomes more alert to another's need. The aim of clear guidance is a person who understands and acts, not a person who uses clarity to excuse indifference.

### In short

- Allah clarifies permitted conduct, duties and limits so that people can understand them.
- Reflection should lead to practice and better treatment of others.
- The rules are guideposts towards responsible conduct in this world and the next.
