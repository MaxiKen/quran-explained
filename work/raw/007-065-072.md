<<<VERSE 7:65>>>
As Allah sent Nūḥ (Noah) to his people, so He sent one of their own to the people of ʿĀd: "And to the people of ’Âd We sent their brother Hûd. He said, “O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him. Will you not then fear Him?”" This is the second story in the series. It tells of the Prophet Hūd and a mighty people whose blessings turned into a curse through their own crooked thinking.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And to the people of ’Âd We sent their brother Hûd."** Hūd was their prophet. He is called their brother because Hūd and the people of ʿĀd share a family tree. Both are in the fifth generation after Nūḥ, descended from his son Sām, and their lines meet in Sām. So Hūd was their brother by lineage. One explanation calls them "the earlier ʿĀd".

**"He said, “O my people! Worship Allah"**: worship Allah alone and affirm His oneness.

**"you have no other god except Him."** You have no true god but the One I am calling you to.

**"Will you not then fear Him?”"** Will you not have taqwā, fearing Him and so believing? Will you not guard yourselves against worshipping others besides Him, by following His instructions and avoiding what He has forbidden, so that you are saved from His punishment? The terrible punishment of Nūḥ's people was still fresh in people's minds in Hūd's time. So he had no need to describe how severe Allah's punishment of the disobedient could be. It was enough to ask, "Will you not then fear Him?"

### Who were ʿĀd?

ʿĀd was the name of a man in the fifth generation after Nūḥ, from the line of his son Sām. His descendants and his people came to be known by his name. Muhammad ibn Isḥāq said the tribe of ʿĀd descended from ʿĀd, son of Iram, son of ʿAws, son of Sām, son of Nūḥ. These are the ancient ʿĀd whom Allah mentions, the children of ʿĀd son of Iram, who lived in the desert among lofty pillars. Allah says:

> "Did you not see how your Lord dealt with ’Ȃd— ˹the people˺ of Iram—with ˹their˺ great stature, unmatched in any other land" (89:6-8).

That was because of their might and strength. Another verse says:

> "As for ’Ȃd, they acted arrogantly throughout the land with no right, boasting, “Who is superior to us in might?” Did they not see that Allah ˹Himself˺, Who created them, was far superior to them in might? Still they persisted in denying Our signs" (41:15).

The Qur'an also speaks of "ʿĀd the First" and of "Iram" (89:7). This shows that the people of ʿĀd are also known by the name Iram, and that besides the first ʿĀd there was a second. Commentators and historians differ on the details. The better-known view is that Iram was ʿĀd's grandfather. This ʿĀd, descended from ʿAws, son of Iram, is called the first ʿĀd. Iram's second son, Jathw, had a son named Thamūd, who is called the second ʿĀd. So ʿĀd and Thamūd are two branches of Iram. Some commentators say that when the punishment struck ʿĀd, a delegation of theirs was visiting Makkah and escaped it, and they are known as the other ʿĀd.

Another account says that Iram, Nūḥ's grandson, was among the believers saved in the Ark, and was the forefather of the tribe of ʿĀd. They settled in Yemen and at first followed the religion of Nūḥ. Later they strayed from the right path, and Allah appointed Hūd as their prophet.

### Where did they live, and what were they like?

The people of ʿĀd lived in Yemen, in the region of al-Aḥqāf, which means sand dunes. Muhammad ibn Isḥāq reported from Abū aṭ-Ṭufayl ʿĀmir ibn Wāthilah that he heard ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib speak to a man from Ḥaḍramawt. ʿAlī asked whether he had seen a red sand dune, with a lot of arāk and lote trees around it, in a certain part of Ḥaḍramawt. The man said yes, and that ʿAlī described it as if he had seen it himself. ʿAlī said he had not seen it, but it had been described to him. When the man asked what was special about it, ʿAlī said that the grave of the Prophet Hūd lay close by. Ibn Jarīr recorded this. It shows that ʿĀd lived in Yemen, since their prophet was buried there.

ʿĀd had thirteen clans, and their settlements stretched from Oman to Ḥaḍramawt and Yemen. Their lands were fertile and full of gardens, and they built mansions and palaces to live in. They were tall and powerfully built, which is what the later phrase about increased stature (7:69) refers to. Allah had opened the doors of His blessings to them. But their crooked thinking turned those very blessings into a curse. They grew so drunk on their power and grandeur that they boasted, "Who is superior to us in might?" (41:15). How odd that they ignored their Lord, the Lord of the worlds, while bathed in His blessings, and sank into worshipping idols carved from rock.

Hūd was one of the noble men and chiefs of ʿĀd, for Allah chooses His messengers from the best and most honourable families and tribes. His people were mighty and strong, but their hearts were just as hard. They were among the nations that most denied the truth. His message to them was the worship of Allah alone with no partner, together with obedience to Him and fear of Him.

### What became of them?

Hūd urged ʿĀd to leave idol worship for pure monotheism, to give up injustice and oppression, and to practise moderation and fairness. Drunk with power and wealth, they would not listen. So the first punishment came: no rain fell for three years in a row. Their lands turned to desert and their gardens to dead wood. Still they clung to kufr, shirk and idol worship. Then came the second punishment, the windstorm described under verse 72, and ʿĀd were wiped out to the last man.

When the punishment came, Hūd and his companions took shelter in an open space fenced with stakes. Remarkably, the wind that brought palaces crashing down on their pillars slowed when it entered this enclosure and blew in gently. Hūd and his companions sat there in peace, untroubled, even while the punishment was falling. After ʿĀd were destroyed, they moved to Makkah and lived there until they died. Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ records this.

The Qur'an states plainly and decisively that ʿĀd's punishment came as a windstorm. In Sūrat al-Muʾminūn, after the story of Nūḥ (Noah), Allah says, "Then We raised another generation after them" (23:31). This other generation clearly seems to be ʿĀd. After describing their words and deeds, it says, "Then the ˹mighty˺ blast overtook them with justice" (23:41). On the basis of this verse, some commentators say ʿĀd's punishment was aṣ-Ṣaiḥah, a harsh, terrifying blast of sound. There is no contradiction. Both may have happened.

### Where did the Arabic language begin?

The well-known Arab genealogist Abū al-Barakāt al-Jaunī wrote that Hūd's son was Yaʿrub ibn Qaḥṭān, who went to live in Yemen. The people of Yemen descend from him. He said the Arabic language began with him, and that the language was called Arabic, and its speakers Arabs, after the name Yaʿrub. Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ records this.

The truth is that Arabic existed from the time of Nūḥ. He had a companion in the Ark named Jurhum, who spoke Arabic, and the settling of Makkah began with this same Jurhum. Still, it is possible that Arabic took shape in Yemen through Yaʿrub ibn Qaḥṭān, and that this is what Abū al-Barakāt meant.

### In short

- Allah sent the Prophet Hūd to ʿĀd, his brother by descent, since both traced their line to Sām son of Nūḥ.
- ʿĀd were a tall, powerful people of Yemen with fertile gardens and grand palaces, who boasted that no one was mightier.
- Hūd called them to worship Allah alone and to fear Him, a short reminder since the Flood was still remembered.
- Drought came first and then the wind, while Hūd and his companions sat safely in an enclosure and later moved to Makkah.
- Hūd's grave is reported to lie in Ḥaḍramawt. Arabic existed from Nūḥ's time, though it may have taken shape in Yemen.

<<<VERSE 7:66>>>
The leaders of ʿĀd answered the Prophet Hūd much as Nūḥ's (Noah's) people had answered Nūḥ, with only the words changed: "The disbelieving chiefs of his people responded, “We surely see you as a fool, and we certainly think you are a liar.”" To them he did not seem to have the greatness they expected in a prophet, so they concluded that he was either a fool or making a false claim.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The disbelieving chiefs of his people responded"**: the leaders and notables of his people who disbelieved in Allah and rejected His messenger. They spoke for the general public, the chiefs, masters and commanders among them.

**"“We surely see you as a fool"**: we think you foolish, ignorant and backward, misguided for calling us to leave the idols and worship Allah alone.

**"and we certainly think you are a liar.”"** In fact, they say, we are sure you are lying in your message when you claim that Allah has sent you.

### Why did the call to one God seem foolish to them?

The leaders of ʿĀd thought the call to give up their many idols for One God was folly. In the same way, the chiefs of Quraysh later marvelled at the call to worship One God:

> "Has he reduced ˹all˺ the gods to One God? Indeed, this is something totally astonishing.”" (38:5).

In their eyes Hūd did not have the stature they thought a prophet should have. So they decided he must be either a fool or a liar.

### In short

- The disbelieving chiefs of ʿĀd called Hūd a fool and a liar for calling them to worship Allah alone.
- Their stance repeated that of Nūḥ's people, and Quraysh would later show the same astonishment at the call to One God.
- They expected a prophet to have outward greatness, and when they did not see it, they dismissed him.

<<<VERSE 7:67>>>
The Prophet Hūd answered calmly, much as Nūḥ (Noah) had answered his people (7:61-63): "Hûd replied, “O my people! I am no fool! But I am a messenger from the Lord of all worlds". He was not what they claimed. He had brought them the truth from Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Hûd replied, “O my people! I am no fool!"** There is no folly or ignorance in me, nor am I backward, as you claim.

**"But I am a messenger from the Lord of all worlds"**: I bring you the truth from Allah, who created everything and is the Lord and King of all things. I am His messenger to you.

### What does his calm reply show?

They had insulted him as a fool and a liar, yet he did not answer insult with insult. He simply denied the charge and stated the plain truth of his mission. This is how the prophets answer, and it is the model for everyone who calls others to Allah.

### In short

- Hūd denied any folly and declared himself a messenger of the Lord of all worlds.
- His calm reply mirrors Nūḥ's, and shows the prophetic way of answering abuse with truth rather than anger.

<<<VERSE 7:68>>>
The Prophet Hūd describes his task: "conveying to you my Lord’s messages. And I am your sincere advisor". These are the qualities of the prophets: delivering the message, sincerity and trustworthiness.

### Phrase by phrase

**"conveying to you my Lord’s messages."** I deliver only what Allah has told me to deliver to you, explaining His commands and prohibitions. That is, that you should believe in Allah's oneness and His sacred law.

**"And I am your sincere advisor"**: I am a trustworthy adviser to you all. I warn you of Allah's punishment and call you to repentance and faith. I am trustworthy in conveying my Lord's message, delivering what I was told, no less and no more. One explanation adds a point. You used to regard me as trustworthy, so how can you now call me a liar?

### What should a caller to the truth feel toward his hearers?

Literally Hūd says, "I am your well-wisher." This shows that a caller to the truth is not his hearers' rival or competitor. Between them there is goodwill and trust. The caller should hold nothing in his heart for them but good wishes. However rude and unpleasant they are, he should remain their well-wisher to the last moment.

When he delivers his message, he should not feel that he is handing over something of his own. Rather, the message is theirs. He is only its trustee, and in handing it over he frees himself of a heavy responsibility.

### In short

- Hūd's task was to deliver his Lord's messages exactly, as a sincere and trustworthy adviser.
- A caller to the truth is his hearers' well-wisher, never their rival, however they treat him.
- He delivers the message as a trust that belongs to them, not as a favour of his own.

<<<VERSE 7:69>>>
The people of ʿĀd raised the same objection as Nūḥ's (Noah's) people: how could they accept a human being like themselves as their superior? Had he been an angel, perhaps they would. The Prophet Hūd answers as Nūḥ did, and then reminds them of Allah's favours: "Do you find it astonishing that a reminder should come to you from your Lord through one of your own so he may warn you? Remember that He made you successors after the people of Noah and increased you greatly in stature. So remember Allah’s favours, so you may be successful.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do you find it astonishing that a reminder should come to you from your Lord"**: indeed you think it a marvel. The reminder is prophethood.

**"through one of your own"**: through the tongue of a man from among you, a human being like you, not through an angel or a jinn.

**"so he may warn you?"** So that he may warn you of Allah's punishment, His "Days" of torment, and of the meeting with Him. Do not be surprised that Allah has sent you a messenger from among yourselves to warn you. Rather than wonder at it, thank Allah for this blessing. There is nothing odd in a human being coming as Allah's messenger to put His fear into people's hearts. It is a human being who can convince other human beings.

**"Remember that He made you successors after the people of Noah"**: He established you on the earth as successors after the people of Nūḥ, who were destroyed for their disbelief. Remember His favour in making you descendants of Nūḥ. It was at Nūḥ's supplication that the people of the earth were destroyed, once they had defied and opposed him. So thank Allah and praise Him.

**"and increased you greatly in stature."** He made you taller than other people and gave you greater height, bulk and strength. One explanation says the tallest of them measured 100 feet and the shortest 60. Allah says something similar of Ṭālūt (Saul): "blessed him with knowledge and stature" (2:247).

**"So remember Allah’s favours, so you may be successful.”"** Remember His blessings and graces, and believe in Him. Then you may prosper, gain what you hope for, and escape what you fear, Allah's wrath and punishment.

### Why do the prophets speak of blessings?

Remembering these blessings would have been for ʿĀd's own good. But they were wicked and drunk on power and wealth, so they paid no attention.

The prophets' call has always rested on two things. They remind people of the blessings given to them, and they warn that if they are not grateful to Allah they will suffer His punishment. Prophets never make communal or material issues the subject of their preaching. They do their best to keep their exchanges with their hearers on the truths that really matter. They want to be known to their people only as callers to the One God and to the Hereafter. That is all they say.

"Remember Allah's favours, so you may be successful" shows that the blessings of the Hereafter are earned only by someone who has acknowledged Allah's grace in this world. Paradise is the greatest expression of Allah's grace and generosity. So the Paradise of the Hereafter will be reached by the one who has found and recognised Allah's grace and generosity here. That recognition is the real price of Paradise.

### In short

- ʿĀd, like Nūḥ's people, wondered that a human being should bring Allah's message. Hūd told them it was a blessing to be thankful for.
- He reminded them that Allah made them successors to Nūḥ's people and gave them great stature and strength.
- The prophets' call reminds people of Allah's blessings and warns against ingratitude, without turning to worldly issues.
- Recognising Allah's grace in this world is the real price of Paradise.

<<<VERSE 7:70>>>
The answer of the people of ʿĀd shows their rebellion, defiance and stubbornness: "They said, “Have you come to us so that we would worship Allah alone and abandon what our forefathers used to worship? Then bring us what you threaten us with, if what you say is true!”" Their reply was no different from the one people who have gone astray usually give.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They said, “Have you come to us so that we would worship Allah alone"**: have you come to tell us to worship Allah alone?

**"and abandon what our forefathers used to worship?"** Must we give up the many gods our parents and ancestors worshipped? That, they meant, they would never do. If Hūd hoped to pull them away from their ancestors' faith, so that they would drop their idols and believe in One God, he would not succeed.

**"Then bring us what you threaten us with"**: then let the punishment you keep promising fall on us.

**"if what you say is true!”"** If your claims are true.

### Do people still dare Allah to punish them?

Long after ʿĀd, the disbelievers of Quraysh made the same kind of challenge:

> "And ˹remember˺ when they prayed, “O Allah! If this is indeed the truth from You, then rain down stones upon us from the sky or overcome us with a painful punishment.”" (8:32).

Daring a messenger to bring on the punishment is the mark of people who have stopped thinking about the consequences of their denial.

### In short

- ʿĀd refused to leave the idols of their forefathers for the worship of Allah alone.
- They mockingly challenged Hūd to bring the punishment he warned of, as Quraysh later did with the Prophet ﷺ.

<<<VERSE 7:71>>>
Their provoking answer called for a fitting reply from the Prophet Hūd, including the news that the punishment they asked for would soon come: "He said, “You will certainly be subjected to your Lord’s torment and wrath. Do you dispute with me regarding the so-called gods which you and your forefathers have made up—a practice Allah has never authorized? Then wait! I too am waiting with you.”" Yet he was a prophet. His fatherly love and wish for their good made him express regret that they worshipped lifeless, senseless things without any proof from reason or revelation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He said, “You will certainly be subjected to your Lord’s torment and wrath."** The word for torment is rijs. Ibn ʿAbbās said rijs means scorn and anger. You deserve it from your Lord because of what you have said. It has already been decreed for you and is on its way. Given your defiance and lack of sense, Allah's wrath and punishment are not far off, and may come sooner than you expect.

**"Do you dispute with me regarding the so-called gods"**: literally "names". Do you argue with me about the idols that you have made gods? Muhammad ibn Isḥāq said Hūd's people worshipped several idols, such as Ṣudāʾ, Ṣamūd and al-Habāʾ.

**"which you and your forefathers have made up"**: you and your fathers called them gods, though they bring neither harm nor benefit and have no basis.

**"a practice Allah has never authorized?"** Did Allah give you any authority, any scripture or decisive proof, allowing you to worship them? He has sent down no proof of their divinity. They had grown so attached to falsehood that they quarrelled with Allah's prophet over it.

**"Then wait! I too am waiting with you.”"** Wait for the punishment you ask for, as you wait for my ruin, and I will wait for yours. It is surely coming. With these words the Messenger threatened and warned his people. Then, because they denied him, a blighting wind was sent against them.

### How do mere names become objects of devotion?

People picture virtues and powers through names. When a good word is attached to someone, he seems good, and when a bad word is attached, he seems bad. Beings other than Allah become the focus of people's devotion through names like these. Some individuals are given titles such as mushkil kusha (solver of difficulties) or ghaus-e-pak (holy hearer of pleas). Others are called gharib nawaz, the benefactor of the poor, or ganj bakhsh, the granter of treasure. Over time the names cling so closely to these figures that people come to believe it. The one called ghaus can really hear their pleas, they think, and the one called mushkil kusha can really solve their problems. Yet all such names are human inventions. No real being bears them, and no religious or rational argument supports them.

That is the form this tradition takes among the ignorant masses. It has a refined form too, popular among the educated. There as well, extraordinary words are attached to certain figures. One is hailed as najat dehenda-e-millat, "saviour of the community", or sutun-e-Islam, "pillar of Islam". Another is called mahbub-e-khuda, "beloved of God", or qudsi sifat, "angelic in nature". Gradually these words become part of the persons' names, and people come to think them as extraordinary as their titles suggest.

Whatever has gained the holiness of the past through long custom looks grand and lofty to people. Beside it, the call of today's preacher seems trivial, so they ignore him as unimportant. They feel sure nothing can harm them, since they think of themselves as heirs to their forefathers' glory. Recklessness toward Allah slowly makes a person numb. He can no longer be reformed by warning or reminder. Only when he hears Allah's voice pronouncing the verdict of punishment will he wake up to his own failings.

### In short

- Hūd told ʿĀd that Allah's wrath and punishment were already decreed for them and close at hand.
- He rebuked them for arguing over idols that were mere names, which Allah had never authorised.
- Grand titles given to created beings can come to be treated as real powers, though they are human inventions.
- Reverence for the past makes people dismiss the present caller, until Allah's verdict wakes them too late.

<<<VERSE 7:72>>>
The long struggle of the Prophet Hūd against ʿĀd's stubbornness ended this way: "So We saved him and those with him by Our mercy and uprooted those who denied Our signs. They were not believers". Hūd and his believing followers were shielded from the punishment, while the people of ʿĀd who denied him were destroyed root and all.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So We saved him and those with him by Our mercy"**: Allah saved Hūd and the believers with him, through a mercy from Him.

**"and uprooted those who denied Our signs."** He destroyed and wiped out those who denied His Scripture and His messenger Hūd. Some commentators say this means that everyone alive at the time was destroyed. Others say it means Allah also cut off ʿĀd's line for the future.

**"They were not believers"**: all who were destroyed were disbelievers who deserved their punishment for their denial. Grammatically, "they were not believers" is joined to "those who denied".

### How were ʿĀd destroyed?

Muhammad ibn Isḥāq said that ʿĀd lived in Yemen between Oman and Ḥaḍramawt. They spread through the land and overpowered other peoples with the strength Allah had given them. They worshipped idols instead of Allah, and Allah sent them Hūd, who was of the heart of their lineage and the best of them in standing. Hūd told them to worship Allah alone, to join no partner with Him and to stop wronging people. They rejected him, ignored his call and said, "Who is stronger than us?" A few followed him, but they had to hide their faith. ʿĀd defied Allah's command, denied His prophet and spread corruption in the land. They grew arrogant and built lofty palaces on every height, with no real benefit to themselves. Hūd said to them:

> "˹Why˺ do you build a landmark on every high place in vanity and construct castles, as if you are going to live forever and act so viciously when you attack ˹others˺? So fear Allah, and obey me" (26:128-131).

His closing words were a call to taqwā, the fear of Allah, and to obedience.

They answered him, and he answered back:

> "They argued, “O Hûd! You have not given us any clear proof, and we will never abandon our gods upon your word, nor will we believe in you. All we can say is that some of our gods have possessed you with evil.” He said, “I call Allah to witness, and you too bear witness, that I ˹totally˺ reject whatever you associate with Him ˹in worship˺. So let all of you plot against me without delay. I have put my trust in Allah—my Lord and your Lord. There is no living creature that is not completely under His control. Surely my Lord’s Way is perfect justice" (11:53-56).

The Qur'an mentions several times that Allah destroyed ʿĀd with a barren wind that ruined everything it passed over:

> "And as for ’Ȃd, they were destroyed by a furious, bitter wind which Allah unleashed on them non-stop for seven nights and eight days, so that you would have seen its people lying dead like trunks of uprooted palm trees. Do you see any of them left alive" (69:6-8).

The wind levelled whatever was left of their gardens, mansions and palaces. It lifted them one by one into the air and brought each down on his head, smashing it and severing it from the body. That is why Allah says they lay "like trunks of uprooted palm trees" (69:7). So ʿĀd were wiped out to the last man.

### Who was the emissary of ʿĀd?

Imam Aḥmad recorded that al-Ḥārith al-Bakrī set out to complain to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about al-ʿAlāʾ ibn al-Ḥaḍramī. At ar-Rabdhah he met an old woman of Banū Tamīm, alone, who asked him to take her to the Prophet ﷺ about a need of hers. He brought her to Madinah. There he found the mosque full of people, a black flag raised, and Bilāl standing with a sword before the Prophet ﷺ. The people told him the Prophet ﷺ was about to send ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ on an expedition. Later al-Ḥārith was given leave to enter and greeted the Prophet ﷺ, who asked whether there had been a dispute between his tribe and Banū Tamīm. Al-Ḥārith said there had, and that his side had won. He mentioned the old woman, who was waiting at the door, and the Prophet ﷺ let her in.

Al-Ḥārith then asked the Prophet ﷺ to set a barrier between them and Banū Tamīm, such as the desert of ad-Dahnāʾ. The old woman grew angry and objected. Al-Ḥārith said he was like the sheep that carried its own death. He had brought this woman along without knowing she would turn against him. He sought refuge with Allah and His Messenger from being like the emissary of ʿĀd.

The Prophet ﷺ asked him about the emissary of ʿĀd. He knew the story better, but liked to hear it told. Al-Ḥārith said that ʿĀd once suffered a famine and sent an emissary named Qayl to seek relief. Qayl stayed a month with Muʿāwiyah ibn Bakr, who gave him wine while two singing girls sang for him. When the month was over, Qayl went to the mountains of Muhrah. He told Allah that He knew he had not come to cure a sick man or ransom a captive, and asked Him to give ʿĀd water as He used to. Black clouds appeared, and a voice told him to choose one. He pointed to one of the black clouds. Then he heard a voice from it say, "Take it as ashes, leaving no one of ʿĀd." Al-Ḥārith added that he had heard that the wind sent against them was no more than would pass through his ring, yet it destroyed them.

Abū Wāʾil said this was true. When a man or woman sent an emissary, they would tell him, "Do not be like the emissary of ʿĀd," meaning one who brings ruin instead of relief. Imam Aḥmad collected this story in his Musnad, and at-Tirmidhī, an-Nasāʾī and Ibn Mājah recorded it in similar wording.

### What lessons does Hūd's story leave?

Here Allah shows how He saved His messenger and the believers and destroyed their stubborn enemies. The story is full of counsel. It reminds heedless people to keep remembering Allah and to live a life of obedience to Him. It shows those who reject sincere advice and refuse to learn what end awaits them. And for preachers and reformers, it is a mirror of the prophetic way of conveying the message (tablīgh) and setting things right (iṣlāḥ).

### In short

- Allah saved Hūd and the believers by His mercy and wiped out ʿĀd, who persisted in denial.
- They built lofty palaces in vanity, oppressed others, and dismissed Hūd's call to taqwā as madness sent by their gods.
- A barren wind blew for seven nights and eight days and hurled them down like uprooted palm trunks.
- "Do not be like the emissary of ʿĀd" became a byword for an envoy who brings ruin instead of relief.
- The story teaches remembrance of Allah, warns those who reject advice, and models the prophetic method for those who call to Allah.
