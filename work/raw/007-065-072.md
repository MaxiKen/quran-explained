For the people of Ad there came an end in two stages. That the punishment of the people of Ad came as a wind storm is stated plainly in the Book. After their words and deeds have been set out there, it is said that a harsh and horrifying sound took them (23:41). On the strength of that statement some have held that the punishment that came on Ad was that sound, which is called As-Saihah. The point of the mention here is that this was a brief account of Ad and of Hud, of the same shape as the account of Nuh before it. What it shows is the way of the Lord with the communities that are sent messengers and refuse them.

### In short

- Hud and those with him were saved by the mercy of Allah and not by anything of their own.
- Those who called the signs of God false were rooted out, and one reading of the words is that their line was cut off for the future as well.
- The reason is given plainly, that they were not believers.
- The story leaves three things behind, a reminder to the heedless, a warning to those who reject advice, and a model of the prophetic way of carrying the message.

<<<NOTES>>>
- The lineage of Ad and of Hud, the descriptions of their country, and the two stages of their punishment are placed in 7:65, where the mission opens.
- The charge of the chiefs and the identical charge in the earlier story belong to 7:66, and the denial of it to 7:67.
- The description of the well-wisher's task, and the sense in which the messenger holds the message in trust, are given once, in 7:68.
- The favours of succession and of stature, and the place of the favours of God in the call of the prophets, are given in 7:69.
- The report of the emissary of Ad, with the narrative of Al-Harith Al-Bakri, is placed in 7:70, where the people challenge the messenger to bring the punishment.
- The argument about invented names, and the account of how names acquire weight over time, belong to 7:71.
- The closing counsel drawn from the story is placed in 7:72, where the story ends.
