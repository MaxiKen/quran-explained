<<<VERSE 5:112>>>
The chapter takes its name from this request: “O Jesus, son of Mary! Would your Lord be willing to send down to us a table spread with food from heaven?” The disciples asked for a table from the sky, and 'Isa answered with a command rather than a promise.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Remember˺ when the disciples asked"** introduces the story of the ma'idah, the table with food on it, from which this chapter takes its name. The hawariyyun are the disciples of 'Isa, described in one gloss as the chosen among them, and Simon the Pure is named as the one who spoke.

**"“O Jesus, son of Mary! Would your Lord be willing to send down to us a table spread with food from heaven?”"** is the request. One reading of the wording asks whether his Lord is able, and a variant reading asks whether he is able to request it of his Lord. Another gloss explains it as asking whether he would pray to his Lord to send it down.

Some scholars say the disciples asked because they were poor and deprived, and wanted a table of food sent down that they could eat from every day and so be better able to perform acts of worship.

**"Jesus answered, “Fear Allah if you are ˹truly˺ believers.”"** is his reply. He told them to have taqwa and not to ask for this, because it might become a trial for them, and to trust Allah for their provision if they were truly believers.

One gloss adds the reason for the caution. It may happen that they would not give gratitude for it and would be punished as a result, and 'Isa told Simon to convey that to them. Another explains it as fearing Allah when requesting signs, if they are believers.

### Why is asking for a sign discouraged?

A servant who bears faith should not test Allah by making such requests. Rather than demanding something supernatural, he should seek things like sustenance through the means naturally appointed for him.

People are constantly provided with what they need, and the whole earth has become a table laid with provision for them. The request of the believers in 'Isa nevertheless drew a severe warning, because provision received under normal conditions comes through a veil of cause and effect, while their demand involved removing that veil.

That is Allah's way, since if the veil is removed, how can the conditions of human trial continue?

### In short

- The disciples asked for a table from heaven, and the chapter takes its name from that request.
- Some explain the request by their poverty and their wish to be freer for worship.
- 'Isa answered with a command to fear Allah and trust Him for provision, warning that the request could become a trial.
- Provision normally comes through a veil of causes, and removing it would end the conditions of trial.

<<<VERSE 5:113>>>
The disciples explain themselves: “We ˹only˺ wish to eat from it to reassure our hearts, to verify you are indeed truthful to us, and to become its witnesses.” Their answer states three purposes, and each is about certainty rather than about food.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They said, “We ˹only˺ wish to eat from it"** states the immediate need. They need to eat from it, and one gloss explains the request as wanting to eat so that their hearts are satisfied about Allah's perfect power and about his being His Messenger.

**"to reassure our hearts"** is explained as becoming stronger in faith when they witness the table descending from heaven as sustenance for them, and as satisfying their hearts concerning the marvels he shows them, reaching certainty. Another describes the reassurance as increased certainty.

**"to verify you are indeed truthful to us"** concerns his message. Their faith in him and their knowledge would increase, and one gloss describes it as knowing and being certain that he spoke truth to them about everything he said.

**"and to become its witnesses.”"** means bearing witness that it is a sign sent by Allah, serving as proof of his prophethood and as confirmation of what he carried to them. Another gloss describes them as witnesses when they return to their people, and a third as witnesses to it for those not present.

### What kind of request is this?

The three purposes are connected. Eating is not the goal but the occasion, and the goal is a settled heart, verified truth and testimony to others. That is why 'Isa's caution and their explanation can stand together, since their aim was certainty rather than curiosity.

### In short

- The disciples name three purposes: eating, reassurance of heart and verification of his truthfulness, and bearing witness.
- The reassurance is described as stronger faith and increased certainty on seeing the table descend.
- Their witnessing is for those absent and for their own people when they return.
- Eating is the occasion rather than the goal, which is settled certainty and testimony.

<<<VERSE 5:114>>>
'Isa then makes the request himself, in words of worship: “Send us from heaven a table spread with food". The prayer asks for a festival for the first and the last of them, for a sign from Allah and for provision, and it ends by naming Him the best of providers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Jesus, son of Mary, prayed, “O Allah, our Lord!"** He takes the request upon himself after cautioning them. One gloss notes that he gave way to their request and prayed.

**"Send us from heaven a table spread with food"** asks for the table. Another reading takes it as asking for the grace of food, since they already had some food with them.

**"as a feast for us—the first and last of us—"** asks that the day of its descent be a festival. As-Suddi explains that they would treat the day of the table's descent as a day of festivity, one that they and their successors would hold sacred. Sufyan Ath-Thawri explains it as a day of prayer.

One gloss describes the first as the people of their own time and the last as those who come after, so that they would worship Allah on that day, which happened to be a Sunday. Another describes the day as one they would consecrate and honour. A further account says they would hold a festival on the day it came down in order to honour Allah out of gratitude.

**"and as a sign from You."** asks for proof that Allah is able to do all things and to accept his supplication, so that people would accept what he conveys from Him. One gloss describes it as a sign for whoever believes and an argument against whoever disbelieves, and another as a sign of Allah's power and of his prophethood. A third describes it as a sign and proof of Allah's oneness and of the truth of what he was sent with.

**"Provide for us! You are indeed the Best Provider.”"** asks for provision. It is explained as delicious food from Allah requiring no effort or hardship, and as giving them what they asked for, since He is the best of those who feed others. Another describes it as provision that would help them worship Allah.

### What does the prayer show?

'Isa began by warning that the request could become a trial, and he ends by praying for it. The difference lies in who asks and how. A demand addressed to Allah as a test is one thing, and a supplication that asks for a festival, a sign and provision while naming Allah the best of providers is another.

The prayer also frames the sign correctly. Everything asked for is tied to worship, gratitude and the acceptance of the message, not to satisfying curiosity.

### In short

- 'Isa prays for the table after cautioning the disciples, taking the request upon himself.
- The day of descent is to be a festival, explained as a day of celebration, of prayer and of consecration by the first and the last of them.
- The sign is asked for as proof of Allah's power, of His accepting supplication and of 'Isa's prophethood and oneness of Allah.
- Provision is requested as food needing no effort, and Allah is named the best of providers.
- The prayer ties every request to worship and gratitude rather than to curiosity.

<<<VERSE 5:115>>>
The answer comes with a condition: "Allah answered, “I am sending it down to you." The table will be sent, and whoever denies afterward will meet a torment unlike any other. Nothing else has been punished in that way.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah answered, “I am sending it down to you."** grants the supplication. Allah tells 'Isa to inform them that He is sending down what they asked for, and He fulfilled His promise and sent the table to them.

**"But whoever among you denies afterwards"** concerns whoever disbelieves after it has been sent down and eaten from, by denying the sign and defying what it implies. One gloss describes the denial as coming after they had seen an overwhelming proof, so that any denial is the result of stubbornness, and says they should blame only themselves.

**"will be subjected to a torment I have never inflicted on anyone of My creation.”"** describes a punishment unlike any other. One gloss limits it to the people of his time.

Similar verses are cited. On the Day the Hour is established, the angels will be told to make the people of Fir'awn enter the harshest torment (40:46). The hypocrites will occupy the lowest layer of the Fire (4:145).

Ibn Jarir reports a saying of 'Abdullah bin 'Amr about three groups. They will receive the severest torment on the Day of Resurrection. The first is the hypocrites. The second is those among the people of the table who disbelieved in it. The third is the people of Fir'awn.

### Was the table sent down?

Scholars differ over whether it came down, and the majority hold that it did. The apparent wording of the verse also states it, since Allah says He will send it down.

Such statements bear witness that the table came down for the Children of Israel in the time of 'Isa, because Allah accepted the prayer he made to Him. It is also among the favours Allah granted His servant and messenger 'Isa, accepting his request and sending it as clear proof and unequivocal evidence.

Ibn Abi Hatim records a statement from Ibn 'Abbas. They asked 'Isa to pray for a table of food from the sky. The angels then carried it down with seven fish and seven loaves on it and set it before them. The last of them ate as the first had eaten.

Ibn Jarir records Ishaq bin 'Abdullah on the same event. The table came down to 'Isa carrying seven loaves and seven fish. They ate from it as much as they wished. Some of them then stole food from it, saying it might not come down the next day, and the table rose again.

Another account relates that the angels descended with it from heaven, that on it were seven loaves and seven large fish, and that they ate until they were full. A hadith describes the table sent from heaven as bread and meat. They were commanded not to act treacherously and not to keep anything back for the next day. Yet they broke that trust and kept part of it back, and so they were changed into apes and swine.

At-Tirmidhi carries a report from 'Ammar ibn Yasir. The table came down from heaven with bread and meat on it. Some of those people then committed a breach of trust and kept it over to the next day. They were transformed into monkeys and swines as a result. They had eaten from it as they wished, but keeping it for later use was forbidden.

One gloss describes the punishment as turning them into swine, and adds that after eating they said this was clear magic. 'Isa is then reported as answering. O Allah, if You punish them for what they say, they are Your servants. If You forgive them, You are mighty in retribution toward whoever does not repent.

### Why is the punishment so severe?

Where Allah's blessing is extraordinary and unique, the emphasis on gratitude for it must be far above the ordinary, and the punishment for ingratitude will likewise be extraordinary and unique. That is what the verse teaches.

The reason lies in the removal of the veil. Crops flourishing in the fields are a miracle. So is a tree coming up stout and green from under the ground. Neither is less miraculous than a platter of food descending from the clouds. We do not perceive how miraculous they are, since they reach us from behind a covering.

Here lies the test of a human being. He must be able to perceive reality by ripping that covering away. He must look at the basic stuff of food coming up out of the ground and see it as though it were coming down out of the sky.

Whoever insists on accepting divine truth only after seeing it makes a different claim in effect. He is asking to receive Allah's grace without undergoing His trial. By Allah's way that is not possible.

Disbelief after a sign of this kind is therefore not one error among others. It is a refusal made with the veil removed, which is why the punishment attached to it has no parallel.

### In short

- Allah grants the request and warns that disbelief afterward will bring a punishment inflicted on no other creature.
- The three groups named for the severest torment are the hypocrites, the disbelievers among the people of the table and the people of Fir'awn.
- The majority hold that the table was sent down, with reports describing seven loaves and seven fish, eating until satisfied, and ascent after some stole or stored from it.
- A hadith describes the treacherous being transformed into apes and swine, and storing for the next day as prohibited.
- Ordinary provision is miraculous behind a veil, and demanding the veil be lifted removes the condition of trial, which explains the severity of the warning.
