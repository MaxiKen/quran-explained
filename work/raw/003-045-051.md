<<<VERSE 3:45>>>
The angels bring Maryam the news of a son: “Allah gives you good news of a Word from Him” (3:45). His name will be al-Masīḥ, ʿĪsā (Jesus), son of Maryam. He will be honored in this world and the Hereafter and brought near to Allah. The message begins the account of his birth and mission.

### Phrase by phrase

**“˹Remember˺ when the angels proclaimed”** introduces the good news from Allah to Maryam. One explanation identifies the angel as Gabriel. The announcement tells her that the child will be a sign of Allah’s power and a messenger, not an ordinary birth.

**“O Mary!”** is an address of honor. An inward reading reflects on the difference between a servant calling upon Allah and Allah addressing a servant. Abū Yazīd Basṭāmī is said to have described standing before Allah in a thousand encounters and refusing worldly dominion. When asked what he desired, he answered that he desired not to desire anything except what Allah desired. In that account, Allah then called him a true servant. The story highlights the honor of being accepted for service to Allah.

**“Allah gives you good news of a Word from Him”** refers to ʿĪsā, who will come into existence by Allah’s command, “Be.” This is like the earlier news that Yaḥyā would confirm a word from Allah (3:39). Calling ʿĪsā a word points to the manner of his creation. It does not make him a part of Allah. Allah creates without needing a father or any other cause.

**“his name will be the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary”** gives his titles and lineage. He is called al-Masīḥ. One explanation connects the title to his traveling from place to place. Another connects it with his touching those afflicted by illness and their healing by Allah’s permission. He is called “son of Mary” because he has no father. Muhammad bin Ishaq relates that the name would distinguish him among believers.

**“honoured in this world and the Hereafter”** means that Allah will grant him rank, prophethood, scripture, and other blessings in this life. In the Hereafter he will be honored and, by Allah’s permission, intercede for some people, like the other mighty messengers.

**“and he will be one of those nearest ˹to Allah˺”** describes his closeness to Allah in rank. It is a promise of nearness and honor, not divinity. His position is that of a righteous servant and prophet.

A report from Abū Hurayrah says that no infant spoke in the cradle except ʿĪsā and the young companion of Jurayj. Another report from Abū Hurayrah mentions three infants who spoke, including ʿĪsā and the child from the time of Jurayj. Muhammad bin Ishaq records one report, and Ibn Abī Ḥātim records another. The different wordings both present speech in infancy as a miracle.

One account places this announcement in the wider history of guidance. It says that the Children of Israel were entrusted with carrying divine guidance, but that the trust later passed to another branch of Abraham’s family, the descendants of Ismāʿīl. The extraordinary birth and signs of ʿĪsā were meant to establish that he was sent by Allah.

### What does “a Word from Him” mean?

The phrase points to Allah’s command by which ʿĪsā was created without a father. The verse calls him by his human name and his mother’s lineage, while also honoring him as a prophet brought near to Allah. His miracles and rank are gifts from Allah, not independent divine powers.

### In short

- Allah announces that Maryam will have ʿĪsā, created by His command without a father.
- ʿĪsā will be the Messiah, son of Maryam, honored in both worlds and near to Allah.
- His speech as an infant is described in hadith reports as a miracle.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:46>>>
The angels continue the good news: “And he will speak to people in ˹his˺ infancy and adulthood and will be one of the righteous” (3:46). ʿĪsā will speak while still a baby and again in maturity. His words and actions will be righteous, and his speech will serve Allah’s message.

### Phrase by phrase

**“And he will speak to people in ˹his˺ infancy”** means that ʿĪsā will speak before children ordinarily can. One account explains that, soon after his birth, when people accused Maryam, the infant spoke and declared himself a servant of Allah. This was a miracle and a defense of his mother’s innocence. His speech was meaningful and clear, not ordinary baby talk.

Abū Hurayrah transmits reports about the rare infants who spoke before the usual age of speech. One version names ʿĪsā and the companion of Jurayj, while another includes a third child. These reports identify ʿĪsā’s speech as an exceptional sign. It also shows that he is a prophet, rather than a divine being. The infant’s own testimony is that he belongs to Allah.

**“and adulthood”** translates kahl, the mature age of a man. Speaking as an adult would be ordinary in itself, so the explanations ask why it is mentioned alongside the miraculous speech of infancy. One answer is that his speech in adulthood would be wise and eloquent, like that of a mature man, rather than childlike. Another says that ʿĪsā was raised to heaven around the age of thirty to thirty-five, before reaching the period described as full maturity. His speaking as a mature man is therefore understood as a sign connected with his return to earth. An allusive reading says that Allah strengthened Maryam by telling her that her son would speak at each stage and that the deception of his enemies would not overcome him.

**“and will be one of the righteous”** describes ʿĪsā in his words and deeds. He will have sound knowledge and do righteous works. He is among Allah’s righteous messengers.

The promise of speech in infancy is part of the good news delivered to Maryam, not an incidental detail. The ordinary course of human development does not explain it. The message says that Allah can give a child speech, knowledge, and a prophetic mission by His command. These words point the Children of Isrāʾīl to the path of prophethood and to Allah’s power.

### Why mention both infancy and adulthood?

Infant speech is plainly miraculous. Mentioning adulthood shows that the speech will be meaningful and mature, and one explanation connects it with ʿĪsā’s return after being raised. The two stages mark Allah’s care for Maryam and the continuity of her son’s mission.

### In short

- ʿĪsā will speak as an infant, miraculously defending his mother and declaring his servitude to Allah.
- He will also speak in mature adulthood with wisdom and clarity.
- His words and actions will be those of a righteous prophet.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:47>>>
Maryam is astonished and asks, “How can I have a child when no man has ever touched me?” (3:47). The angel answers that Allah creates whatever He wills. When He decrees a matter, it comes into being by His command, “Be.”

### Phrase by phrase

**“Mary wondered”** describes her surprise at the announcement. She had not married, intended to marry, or been touched by a man. Her question reflects how the birth could happen, not doubt in Allah’s power.

**“My Lord! How can I have a child when no man has ever touched me?”** means she asks whether the child will be born through a husband or by a miracle. One explanation says the question also reflects her innocence: no lawful or unlawful contact had occurred. She was not an indecent woman. Muhammad bin Ishaq relates that Maryam was surprised by the news, and one explanation says she wondered whether Allah would perform a miracle or ask her to marry. The answer rules out the latter possibility.

**“An angel replied”** identifies the messenger who answers her. One explanation names Gabriel. The reply explains that the child will be created without a father, just as Allah has already willed.

**“So will it be. Allah creates what He wills”** means that Allah does not need a human cause or ordinary means. He can create as He chooses. The account of Zakariyyā says that Allah does what He wills (3:40). Here the wording specifically says that Allah creates, making clear that ʿĪsā is a created human being, not divine.

**“When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, ‘Be!’ And it is”** describes the ease and immediacy of Allah’s command. The Qur’an compares the speed of His command to the twinkling of an eye (54:50). Nothing needs to be prepared or assisted for Allah to create it.

An allusive explanation compares the birth to the unusual provision Maryam had seen in the sanctuary. Just as Allah gave her food beyond ordinary expectations, He will create a son without a man having touched her. When ʿĪsā later speaks as an infant, that speech will be another sign that what Allah decrees cannot be prevented.

### Does Maryam’s question express doubt?

No. She asks how the promised birth will occur given that she has had no contact with a man. The angel answers that Allah creates by His will, without needing a cause. Her question protects the truth of her chastity, and the reply affirms Allah’s power.

### In short

- Maryam is surprised because she has not been touched by a man.
- The angel explains that Allah creates without needing a father or ordinary cause.
- When Allah decrees a matter, it comes into being by His command.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:48>>>
The verse continues with what Allah will give ʿĪsā: “And Allah will teach him writing and wisdom, the Torah and the Gospel” (3:48). He will receive knowledge of scripture and judgment, including the Torah given to Mūsā (Moses) and the Injīl (Gospel) revealed to him.

### Phrase by phrase

**“And Allah will teach him writing”** means that Allah will teach ʿĪsā the skill of writing and the revealed books. One explanation says “the Book” can refer to writing, while another takes it to mean the scriptures given to earlier prophets. ʿĪsā will learn and retain the Torah and the Injīl. The verse includes Al-Hikmah, wisdom, alongside the Book. He will carry this learning to the Children of Isrāʾīl.

**“and wisdom”** means sound judgment and knowledge of what is right and wrong. One explanation describes it as knowing the lawful and the prohibited. Another connects it with the wisdom of the prophets who came before him. The word carries both knowledge and the ability to act rightly.

**“the Torah”** is the scripture Allah revealed to Mūsā, son of ʿImrān. One explanation says that ʿĪsā was taught it while still in Maryam’s womb. He knew and affirmed the revelation that came before him.

**“and the Gospel”** is the Injīl revealed to ʿĪsā. One explanation says he received it after leaving his mother’s womb. These scriptures are among the signs and gifts Allah gives him before sending him as a messenger to the Children of Israel.

The following verses explain that ʿĪsā’s teaching and signs do not make him divine. An allusive reading says that his speech in infancy rejected exaggeration about him by declaring that he was Allah’s servant (19:30). It also defended Maryam against those who accused her (19:28). The same reading relates the pain of childbirth (19:23) to the special burden she bore in bringing ʿĪsā into the world. It compares this with Muṣṭafā’s birth, saying that Allah did not place the same burden upon his mother because she would have no share of him in this world or the next. The comparison is an inward reflection on different forms of divine decree, not a denial of the honor of either mother.

The account also recalls that Allah told Nūḥ (Noah) not to grieve over what his people did (11:36), after which he prayed concerning the disbelievers (71:26). The reading places these stories alongside Maryam’s, as examples of how divine decree brings both trials and relief. The signs of ʿĪsā would also clarify matters over which people differed (43:63).

### What is the difference between the Book and wisdom?

The Book refers to revealed scripture and may also include the ability to write. Wisdom is sound understanding and judgment, including how to distinguish what is lawful and prohibited. Allah grants ʿĪsā both knowledge and the ability to teach and act upon it.

### In short

- Allah will teach ʿĪsā writing, scripture, and wisdom.
- He will know the Torah and the Injīl.
- His signs and teaching establish his prophethood and clarify disputes, while he remains Allah’s servant.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:49>>>
Allah will make ʿĪsā “a messenger to the Children of Israel” (3:49). He will bring signs from his Lord: shaping a bird from clay, healing the blind and the leper, raising the dead, and telling people what they eat and store at home. Each sign occurs by Allah’s permission.

### Phrase by phrase

**“and ˹make him˺ a messenger to the Children of Israel”** describes ʿĪsā’s mission. One account says he was sent after reaching the age of thirty. Gabriel had breathed into the opening of Maryam’s garment, and she conceived by Allah’s command. The opening of Sūrah Maryam continues the story of the pregnancy. ʿĪsā calls the Israelites, the Children of Isrāʾīl, to recognize that he has been sent by Allah. His knowledge of the Torah, the Injīl, and Al-Hikmah informs his message. Their response to his signs will reveal whether they are willing to believe.

The Children of Israel had been chosen to carry divine guidance, but a later generation became unworthy of that trust. One account says that the responsibility passed to another branch of Abraham’s descendants, the Banū Ismāʿīl. ʿĪsā’s extraordinary birth and miracles establish his prophethood and prepare for the transfer of the trust. The earlier choice of Israel is also mentioned in al-Baqarah (2:47).

**“I have come to you with a sign from your Lord”** introduces the miracles as proof of his mission. They are not displays of independent power. Their source is Allah, and the repeated qualification “by Allah’s permission” prevents people from attributing divinity to ʿĪsā.

**“I will make for you a bird from clay, breathe into it, and it will become a ˹real˺ bird—by Allah’s Will”** describes ʿĪsā shaping clay like a bird and breathing into it. It becomes alive and flies only by Allah’s command. One report says the creature was a bat. It lived for a time and then died, making clear that creation belongs to Allah and that the miracle was a sign, not a power independent of Him.

**“I will heal the blind and the leper”** refers to curing someone born blind and someone suffering from leprosy. These conditions were beyond ordinary treatment in the setting described. One explanation says that ʿĪsā cured people by supplication and that, on one day, he healed fifty thousand on the condition that each would believe. The account singles out these illnesses because those afflicted were especially helpless.

**“and raise the dead to life—by Allah’s Will”** is a further sign. One report mentions ʿĀzar, the son of an old woman, a tax collector’s daughter, and Sām, son of Nūḥ. They came back to life, and some lived on and had descendants, while Sām died again soon after. The verse emphasizes that ʿĪsā does this only by Allah’s permission.

**“And I will prophesize what you eat and store in your houses”** tells the Israelites that ʿĪsā will reveal what they have eaten and what they have kept at home. The knowledge is a sign from Allah, not something he learned by ordinary observation. The miracles show both outward power and knowledge of hidden matters.

**“Surely in this is a sign for you if you ˹truly˺ believe”** means that the miracles provide clear evidence for those prepared to accept the truth. They are signs of the messenger’s authority and of Allah’s power. Another verse says that ʿĪsā came to clarify matters over which people differed (43:63).

The explanations compare the miracles given to different prophets with the abilities valued by their communities. Mūsā (Moses) came when magic was prominent, so his sign overwhelmed the magicians. ʿĪsā came when medicine was advanced, and he healed conditions no physician could cure and brought the dead to life. Muhammad ﷺ came among eloquent speakers and poets with the Qur’an, which people could not imitate. The Qur’an is Allah’s speech, not the speech of created beings.

The opening of Sūrah Maryam continues the account of Maryam’s pregnancy and ʿĪsā’s birth. Here the emphasis is on the public signs through which the Children of Israel would recognize his mission.

### Why are the miracles repeatedly qualified by Allah’s permission?

The qualification makes their source clear. ʿĪsā performs signs as Allah’s messenger, but the power belongs to Allah alone. The miracles confirm the message without turning the messenger into the object of worship.

### In short

- ʿĪsā is sent to the Children of Israel with signs from Allah.
- He shapes a bird, heals illness, raises the dead, and reveals hidden information only by Allah’s permission.
- The miracles prove his prophethood for those willing to believe.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:50>>>
ʿĪsā says, “And I will confirm the Torah revealed before me” (3:50). He affirms the scripture sent to Mūsā while explaining that some restrictions in its law will be lifted. He calls the Israelites to be mindful of Allah and obey him as His messenger.

### Phrase by phrase

**“And I will confirm the Torah revealed before me”** means that ʿĪsā affirms the revelation given to Mūsā. He does not come to deny the Torah. His message continues the guidance that came before him and confirms its truth.

**“and legalize some of what had been forbidden to you”** means that Allah gives ʿĪsā authority to make lawful some things that had been prohibited under the earlier law. One explanation gives camel meat and the fat of cattle and sheep as examples, and also mentions restrictions connected with the Sabbath. Another explanation says that the earlier prohibitions were broadly removed, understanding “some” to refer to all of them. These are presented as differences in explanation.

This change is a new rule within the law given to ʿĪsā. It does not mean that a messenger may change Allah’s law by personal preference. Allah grants the dispensation, and ʿĪsā’s prophethood is the proof of his authority. His message clarifies disagreements among the Children of Israel (43:63). The Torah, the Injīl, and Al-Hikmah are all part of the guidance surrounding his mission.

**“I have come to you with a sign from your Lord”** restates that the miracles in the previous verse are proof from Allah. The signs do not originate from ʿĪsā independently. They confirm that his message and his legal teaching come from his Lord.

**“so be mindful of Allah”** translates taqwā. It means to fear disobeying Allah, fulfill His commands, and avoid what He has prohibited. The Israelites are asked to respond to the signs with obedience, not merely to witness them.

**“and obey me”** means to follow ʿĪsā in the religion and commands Allah has given him. Obedience to the messenger is part of obeying Allah because the messenger conveys Allah’s guidance. The instruction is not a call to worship ʿĪsā.

The verse also distinguishes the law of ʿĪsā from the law later given to the followers of Muḥammad ﷺ. One ruling says that making images of birds was permitted in the Sharīʿah of ʿĪsā as a miracle, but that this permission was abrogated in the Sharīʿah of Muḥammad. The point is that legal permissions can differ between revealed communities while all remain subject to Allah. The law still rests on tawḥīd, the oneness of Allah, and obedience to Him.

### What does it mean to confirm the Torah and change some rulings?

ʿĪsā affirms the revelation sent before him, while Allah permits him to lift certain restrictions for the Children of Israel. The change is part of a revealed law, not a contradiction or an independent choice. The signs establish his authority, and the people are told to practice taqwā and obey him.

### In short

- ʿĪsā confirms the Torah and the earlier revelation.
- Allah permits him to lift some restrictions under the law given to him.
- The signs establish his mission, and the Israelites are told to be mindful of Allah and obey.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:51>>>
ʿĪsā sums up his call: “Surely Allah is my Lord and your Lord. So worship Him ˹alone˺” (3:51). He and the Children of Israel are servants of the same Lord. Worship belongs to Allah alone, and this is the straight path.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Surely Allah is my Lord and your Lord”** establishes that ʿĪsā is a servant of Allah, just as the Israelites are. He does not claim lordship for himself. The same Allah is Lord of the messenger and those to whom he is sent.

**“So worship Him ˹alone˺”** is the practical consequence of that belief. Since Allah alone is Lord, worship and obedience belong to Him alone. The message is one of tawḥīd, affirming Allah’s oneness, and rejecting the worship of any created being.

**“This is the Straight Path”** means that worshipping Allah alone and following His messenger is the clear way to salvation. It joins belief with action: recognize Allah as Lord, obey His commands, and follow the guidance brought by ʿĪsā. The message is the same central call to worship Allah that all prophets delivered.

The surrounding passage has described the scripture, miracles, and legal guidance granted to ʿĪsā. He brings the Book and Al-Hikmah to the Children of Isrāʾīl, the people whom Allah had favored (2:47). These teachings all lead back to this foundation. The miracles are signs from Allah, the laws are revealed by Him, and the messenger calls people to worship his Lord rather than himself. The Children of Israel are therefore asked to believe in the messenger and remain mindful of Allah. His message also clarifies disagreements among them (43:63).

### What makes this the straight path?

It gives Allah alone the right to be worshipped and treats the messenger as the guide who conveys His commands. It avoids both rejecting the messenger and exaggerating his status beyond servanthood. The path is straight because its belief and practice point to one Lord.

### In short

- Allah is Lord of ʿĪsā and of the Children of Israel.
- The people are commanded to worship Allah alone.
- This monotheistic faith and obedience to the messenger is the straight path.
