<<<VERSE 3:45>>>
The announcement interrupted by verse 44 now resumes: "˹Remember˺ when the angels proclaimed, “O Mary! Allah gives you good news of a Word from Him, his name will be the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary; honoured in this world and the Hereafter, and he will be one of those nearest ˹to Allah˺"". Maryam (Mary) receives a son who exists by a word, carries three names at once, and holds rank in both worlds. The account prepares the ground for the birth, mission and miracles of ʿĪsā.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Remember˺ when the angels proclaimed, “O Mary! Allah gives you good news of a Word from Him"**: Gabriel announces a son brought into being through Allah's word "Be". Most scholars connect him with Yaḥyā's promised belief in "the Word from Allah" (3:39). Maryam hears of no worldly portion or future fortune for herself, only a tremendous sign, a prophet and confirmation of a miracle.

**"his name will be the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary"**: Al-Masīḥ, ʿĪsā, son of Maryam, known by this name in this life especially among the believers. He was called Al-Masīḥ because when he touched, mash, those afflicted with illness, they were healed by Allah's leave. The Messiah is also said to mean one who travels from country to country, or a king. He is attributed to his mother because he had no father, against the custom of attributing a child to its father, so that his fatherless birth is announced in his very name.

**"honoured in this world and the Hereafter, and he will be one of those nearest ˹to Allah˺"**: he will be a leader honoured by Allah in this life through the law revealed to him, the Scripture sent down to him and other bounties, illustrious among people with standing and position. In the Hereafter he has standing and position with Allah, high stations, and will intercede, by His leave, on behalf of some people, as his brethren the mighty messengers do. He is one of those brought near to Allah in the Garden of Eden.

### The caress inside the announcement

The universe's God, who enacts the world's folk, assigns provisions and bestows bounty, lovingly caresses His friends. Here He separates Maryam from other women and honours her. His first gift is address: "O Mary." How exalted that vocative and call are. Prophets and friends by the thousands have passed away either resting in its discovery or lingering in remorseful hope.

The asymmetry of address is then turned into a teaching. O spirit of the world, if you call on Him a thousand times saying "My Lord, my Lord", that is not like His calling on you once: "My servant, My servant." Your accepting Him in lordhood gains you nothing, because His lordhood is inseparable from you in any case. What does the work is His accepting you for servanthood, once. Abū Yazīd BasṬāmī said that God stood him before Him in a thousand standing places, presenting the empire to him, and he kept saying, "I do not desire it." In the last standing place He asked, "O Abū Yazīd, what do you desire?" He said, "I desire not to desire", meaning I desire what You desire. Then He said, "You are My servant in truth."

Those unable to address God with Abū Yazīd's boldness can still bring needs, burning and hope. "O Lord, a name and mark is enough for me! You have come from Your threshold to place a name on me. Whatever name You want, let it be." At a bazaar, a would-be slave buyer selected a man and asked his name. The answer was, "First buy me so that I may belong to you. Then call me by whatever name you want." 

Her heart was strengthened by hearing her son's future in step-by-step detail. Divine power's overwhelming force, qudra, brings a person before His ruling, ḥukm, and miracles beyond anyone's experience follow. Maryam's reputation for chastity was excellent, but circumstances made her appear blameworthy to confused observers. Foolish eyes missed jaryān al-taqdīr, the decree flowing beneath appearances.

### In short

- The son promised exists by Allah's word "Be", is named Al-Masīḥ, ʿĪsā, son of Maryam, and holds honour and nearness in both worlds.
- "Son of Maryam" itself proclaims the miracle: he is attributed to his mother because he had no father.
- The announcement is read as divine caress: being called "My servant" once outweighs a thousand calls of "My Lord", as Abū Yazīd's thousand standings show.

<<<VERSE 3:46>>>
The promise adds speech at both ends of life: "And he will speak to people in ˹his˺ infancy and adulthood and will be one of the righteous.”" In the cradle he will call people to worship Allah alone, as a miracle. In manhood he will speak by Allah's revelation. Between those two speeches stands the charge against his mother, which the first of them was sent to answer.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And he will speak to people in ˹his˺ infancy"**: he will speak while still in the cradle, in the lap of his mother, saying, "I am the servant of Allah and His Messiah." Speaking in infancy was certainly a sign and a miracle. When people chided Maryam after the birth on a false accusation, the newborn ʿĪsā spoke up: "I am a servant of Allah."

**"and adulthood"**: and in his manhood, after he reaches thirty years old, saying that he is a prophet. Adult speech sounds ordinary, so its inclusion demands explanation, taken up below.

**"and will be one of the righteous.”"**: of the righteous in his statements and actions, one of the messengers, possessing pure knowledge and righteous works, telling people what is best for them in their religious and worldly affairs.

### The infants who spoke in the cradle

Two hadith reports narrow the cradle-speakers. Muḥammad bin Ishaq recorded that Abū Hurayrah said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ stated, "No infant spoke in the cradle except 'Isa and the companion of Jurayj." Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded that Abū Hurayrah said the Prophet ﷺ declared, "No infant spoke in the cradle except three, 'Isa, the boy during the time of Jurayj, and another boy."

### Why mention speech in manhood at all?

Speaking to people during middle age is something everyone does, Muslim or non-Muslim, learned or illiterate, so why list it as a distinction? One answer is that its purpose is to sharpen the infant speech. The reference to middle age tells us the childhood speech would not be baby-talk. It would be rational, learned and eloquent, the speech of a man in advanced years.

History supplies another answer. Reports on his ascension put ʿĪsā at roughly thirty to thirty-five, still in youth's prime. Kahl, middle age, therefore never occurred during his earthly life before ascension. Middle-aged speech must await a renewed presence here, making it miraculous like infancy speech. Inwardly, "his manhood" is likewise interpreted as after his descent from heaven.

Fortification was a nearer purpose. Details about her son, including enemies' ineffective deception, were disclosed sequentially. Maryam would remain silent concerning her innocence after childbirth, while God made Jesus speak from childhood. His speech would prove sincerity and exalted honour for her.

### In short

- ʿĪsā will speak in the cradle as a miracle, declaring himself Allah's servant, and in manhood by revelation, righteous in word and action.
- Only Jesus, the companion of Jurayj and one more boy are said to have spoken as infants.
- Speech in middle age is named because he left this world at about thirty to thirty-five, so such speech awaits his descent, and because his infant words would already be learned and eloquent.

<<<VERSE 3:47>>>
The mother answers with a question about means: "Mary wondered, “My Lord! How can I have a child when no man has ever touched me?” An angel replied, “So will it be. Allah creates what He wills. When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, ‘Be!’ And it is"". She had not married, did not intend to marry, and was not an indecent woman. The answer removes the need for any of those routes.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Mary wondered, “My Lord! How can I have a child when no man has ever touched me?”"**: speaking to Gabriel as "O master!", Maryam asks how a child can exist when no mortal approached her either lawfully or unlawfully, conjugally or otherwise. Is God performing a miracle, or will marriage be requested?

**"An angel replied, “So will it be. Allah creates what He wills"**: Gabriel conveyed Allah's answer: so it will be, as I have told you. God will create from you a child without a father. The word "create" is used here rather than "does", as in the account of Zakariyya (3:40), precisely to eradicate any evil thought concerning ʿĪsā.

**"When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, ‘Be!’ And it is"**: when He has decreed a thing, willing its creation, He says to it only "Be", and it is. What Allah wills comes into existence instantly and without delay. "And Our commandment is but one as the twinkling of an eye" (54:50), meaning the command is issued once and existence follows as fast as, and faster than, a blink.

His will alone can originate anything, without medium or cause. Originating and producing things impose no difficulty. When He desires a ruling, ḥukm, it only needs "Be".

### The same break with ordinary reality she had already seen

Inwardly, her sanctuary had prepared the pattern. Provisions there already broke ordinary reality's rules, and a son created without a man's touch would do the same. Blame after birth triggered a further break: one-day-old Jesus spoke, saying, "I have come to you with a sign from your Lord" (3:49).

### In short

- Maryam asks about means, not possibility: she neither married nor intended marriage, and the answer leaves no human route in place.
- Allah "creates" rather than "does" here, cutting off evil suspicion, and His decree is a single "Be", faster than the twinkling of an eye (54:50).
- The fatherless birth repeats a pattern she had already witnessed in provisions that broke ordinary reality.

<<<VERSE 3:48>>>
The education of the promised son is announced with the birth: "And Allah will teach him writing and wisdom, the Torah and the Gospel". He will be taught the Book, al-Ḥikmah, the Tawrah given to Mūsā (Moses), and the Injīl given to himself, and he memorised both books. The teaching is part of the good news, because the miracles that follow stand on it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And Allah will teach him writing"**: the Book here appears to refer to writing, or to the Scriptures of past prophets. A variant reading changes the voice: nuʿallimuhu, "We will teach him", or yuʿallimuhu, "He will teach him". He will be taught to say and do things correctly.

**"and wisdom"**: al-Ḥikmah is the lawful and the prohibited, or the wisdom of the prophets who preceded him.

**"the Torah and the Gospel"**: Mūsā, son of ʿImrān, received the Tawrah from Allah. ʿĪsā, son of Maryam, received the Injīl, and memorised the two books. An odd division places Torah learning in his mother's womb and Gospel learning after emergence. Direct learning from Allah is the august virtue at work.

### Cradle speech against two future slanders

Foreknowledge shaped the cradle speech. Christians would later exaggerate Jesus, so his infancy words answer them in advance: "Surely I am the servant of God" (19:30). He means that God created him and remains his Lord. Critics of his mother receive a parallel rebuke in "O sister of Aaron! Your father was..." (19:28). Divine innocence was declared for her, and infant speech brightened her eyes with vindication.

Pain and joy are paired deliberately. Jesus would brighten Mary's eyes and gladden her heart in both worlds, so birth burden belonged at the start: "And the birth pangs took her to..." (19:23). Suffering made a due incumbent for her, and blessings with ease arrived as its counterpart.

MuṣṬafā's mother presents the inverse. God knew she would possess no portion of him here or hereafter, so birth imposed no comparable burden and created no due. The pattern reaches Noah's and Muṣṭafā's communities: Nūḥ was told, "Do not let yourself suffer for the..." (11:36), then prayed, "My Lord, leave no disbeliever dwelling on..." (71:26), and God acted.

MuṣṬafā's state with his mother was the opposite. In God's knowledge his mother would have no portion of him in this world or the next, so He did not place the burden of Muṣṭafā upon her, and no suffering reached her at his birth, lest a rightful due become incumbent for her. The same pattern is set out in the stories of Noah and Muṣṭafā and their communities. It was said to Nūḥ (Noah), "Do not let yourself suffer for the..." (11:36), and in keeping with that address he supplicated, "My Lord, leave no disbeliever dwelling on..." (71:26), and God did that.

A transfer of trust lies behind the education. Jews had borne divine guidance, following God's path and communicating it, until later corruption made them unworthy. God removed that trust to Abraham's other branch, Banū Ismāʿīl. Before enforcement, their untrustworthiness had to be demonstrated. Jesus's extraordinary birth and miracles were sent to convince Jews that God had commissioned him and that his speech came from God.

### In short

- ʿĪsā will be taught writing, wisdom, the Torah of Mūsā and his own Gospel, and he memorised both.
- His infant speech was given in advance against Christian exaggeration and against his mother's critics (19:30, 19:28).
- Mary's birth pangs created a due that blessings answered, the opposite of the burden-free birth of Muṣṭafā.

<<<VERSE 3:49>>>
The mission begins with a speech of signs: "I have come to you with a sign from your Lord". A clay bird will live, the blind and leper will be healed, the dead will rise, and hidden stores will be disclosed, each by Allah's Will. Four miracles are enumerated, and each returns its power to Allah's leave, so that the performer cannot be turned into a god.

### Phrase by phrase

**"and ˹make him˺ a messenger to the Children of Israel ˹to proclaim,˺ ‘I have come to you with a sign from your Lord"**: Allah sends ʿĪsā to Israel in tender years or after puberty with evidence of truthfulness. Gabriel had breathed into the opening of her garment, she became pregnant, and Surah Maryam tells later events. His opening self-announcement is, "I am God's Messenger to you." 

**"I will make for you a bird from clay, breathe into it, and it will become a ˹real˺ bird—by Allah’s Will"**: clay is shaped into a bird's likeness, breathed into as a sleeper breathes, and rises flying by Allah's command. The miracle testifies that He sent him.

**"I will heal the blind and the leper and raise the dead to life—by Allah’s Will"**: I heal the akmah, the one born blind, which perfects the miracle and makes the challenge more daring. The leper has a known disease. Both afflictions leave a person completely helpless, so they are singled out. Raising the dead is repeated "by Allah's leave" to preclude false attributions of divinity to him.

**"And I will prophesize what you eat and store in your houses"**: unseen meals and stores become reportable. He tells one person what was eaten recently and what is kept for tomorrow, across morning and night and from lunch to dinner to next day's lunch, without ever seeing it.

**"Surely in this is a sign for you if you ˹truly˺ believe"**: in all this is a sign testifying to the truth of what he was sent with, for those who believe, who wish to have faith and accept the proof.

### What happened when the signs were shown

His clay creation was a bat, the bird described as most perfectly formed. Spectators watched flight until distance hid it, at which point death followed. That boundary distinguishes Jesus's creaturely making from divine creation and points perfection solely to God. Life in clay still prompted cries of sorcery and demands for more signs.

Through supplication he healed fifty thousand in one day, conditioned on each becoming a believer. Raised dead include his friend ʿĀzar, an old woman's son and a tithe-collector's daughter, all living onward and producing offspring. Shem, Nūḥ's son, rose only to die immediately. Reports of eaten and future food completed what audiences had not seen.

### Miracles fitted to their age

Many commentators match prophetic miracles to historical circumstances. Magic dominated Mūsā's age and elevated magicians. Moses received a sign that captured eyes and bewildered professionals. Recognising the Almighty source, magicians believed and became pious. Jesus arrived amid advances in medicine and physics and performed what no physician can: animating clay, curing blindness and leprosy, and recalling the entombed. Muhammad ﷺ confronted eloquent poets with God's Book. Humans and jinn, even collectively, cannot imitate ten chapters or one, because Qur'anic speech is Allah's Word rather than creaturely language.

The signs were outward and dazzling, overpowering proofs: life to the dead, healing of the blind and the leper, disclosure of what people did secretly, and other miracles. But the proof only works on a willing reader. Extraordinary signs and astonishing speech cannot force a person whose prestige blocks the truth. The unbelievers of his time had become insensitive and insolent, and although the proofs were very clear they refused to believe in him. The sign makes sense to one who wants to believe, can come out of himself and ponder its veracity without letting personal prestige obstruct acceptance.

Astonishing speech reinforced signs. Effective, persuasive and logical expression had no equal in his era. As Luke records, "all that heard him were astonished at his understanding and answers" (Luke 2:47). Fatherless birth might have invited abuse, but his miraculous personality and speech forestalled it. His teaching stood in the same line: he was taught the Torah and the Gospel and confirmed earlier law in the Qur'an's account, while abrogating part and establishing part.

### A ruling drawn from the clay bird

Making the shape of a bird was the making of a picture, something permitted in that sharīʿah. In our sharīʿah, its permissibility was abrogated.

### In short

- As messenger to the Children of Israel, ʿĪsā makes a clay bird live, heals the born-blind and the leper, raises the dead and reports hidden food, always by Allah's leave.
- A bat that falls dead out of sight separates creaturely work from the Creator's, and fifty thousand cures in a day are tied to belief.
- Each prophet's miracle fits his age: magic for Mūsā's, medicine for ʿĪsā's, and inimitable speech for Muhammad's ﷺ.

<<<VERSE 3:50>>>
The new messenger places himself inside the older law before he changes any of it: "And I will confirm the Torah revealed before me and legalize some of what had been forbidden to you. I have come to you with a sign from your Lord, so be mindful of Allah and obey me". Confirmation and abrogation stand together. What he confirms gives him the standing from which some prohibitions can be lifted.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And I will confirm the Torah revealed before me"**: I have come confirming and upholding the Tawrah. In the fuller phrasing he confirms Allah's divine Oneness in the religion, and the Torah and all other Scriptures that came before him.

**"and legalize some of what had been forbidden to you"**: part of earlier prohibition becomes lawful. Camel meat, bovine and sheep fat and the Sabbath illustrate release. Another account permits fish and birds lacking spikes. A further view makes everything lawful and reads baʿd, "some", as kull, "all".

**"I have come to you with a sign from your Lord, so be mindful of Allah and obey me"**: he repeats that he has come with a proof from their Lord, containing affirmation and evidence of the truth of what he conveys. So have taqwa of Allah, fear Him in what He commanded and repent to Him, and obey me in what I command you about affirming God's Oneness and being obedient to Him. He makes things easy for them, and they are to follow what he calls them to.

### What abrogation by a new prophet means

The unlawfulness of some things in the earlier code stands abrogated by the new one brought by ʿĪsā, and his station of prophethood is itself the conclusive argument for the claim. The proof of his truth was the signs from their Lord. He also came to make clear some of the points in which people differed: "And in order to make clear to you some of the (points) in which you differ" (43:63). At the same time, the statement affirms the Tawrah and upholds it. Confirmation is not flattery of the old law, and abrogation is not contempt for it. One prophet verifies the book before him and then, on the authority of the same Lord, releases part of its burden.

### In short

- ʿĪsā confirms the Torah and, on the same authority, legalises part of what it forbade, such as camel meat, bovine and sheep fat and the Sabbath.
- Some reports expand the release to everything, reading "some" as "all", and the abrogation rests on his proven prophethood.
- The sign is repeated so that taqwa and obedience follow from evidence, not from novelty.

<<<VERSE 3:51>>>
The speech ends where it must: "Surely Allah is my Lord and your Lord. So worship Him ˹alone˺. This is the Straight Path.’”" After miracles, law and confirmation, the messenger names the one doctrine that organises them. He and his audience stand in the same servitude, and the straight path is that shared submission.

### Phrase by phrase

Verse 51 states the destination of everything before it. Once his prophethood is established, ʿĪsā asks them to beware of any contravention of divine commandments, to fear Allah, and to follow his teachings in matters of religion. In a nutshell they are that Allah is my Lord and your Lord, which is the ultimate in belief, and worship Him, which is the ultimate in deeds. The combination of right belief and right action is the straight path that leads to salvation and communion with Allah.

### In short

- ʿĪsā places himself and Israel under one Lord, so worship belongs to Allah alone.
- The straight path joins the ultimate belief, one Lord for messenger and audience, to the ultimate deed, worshipping Him alone.
- They rejected him anyway, which is why this closing sentence also becomes the charge against them.
