<<<VERSE 7:28>>>
The verse takes up an argument the idolaters were making for themselves. "Whenever they commit a shameful deed, they say, "We found our forefathers doing it and Allah has commanded us to do it." Say, "No! Allah never commands what is shameful. How can you attribute to Allah what you do not know?"" The verse does not argue about which practice is being defended. It goes straight to the two defences that were being offered for it, and it takes them apart one at a time.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whenever they commit a shameful deed"** names the act. The word Fāḥishah is the word for any evil act whose evil has reached the furthest limit and is loud and clear to ordinary sense and sound taste. According to most commentators the deed intended here is the walking round the House while naked, and the word also covers idolatry.

**"they say, "We found our forefathers doing it""** is the first defence, and it is an appeal to custom. It is a defence that a person of average sense can see through. A practice cannot be justified by the bare fact that one's ancestors followed it. If that were a sufficient reason, then the ancestors of every people on earth followed different and contradictory practices, and every wrong practice in the world would stand justified by it. The argument does not deserve an answer, and the Qur'an does not spend one on it here. Some narrations do answer it, and what they say is that an ignorant act committed by one's forefathers is worth nothing at all as a model.

**"and Allah has commanded us to do it"** is the second defence, and this one is a lie. They were laying on Allah a command He had never given. The answer to it is short: Allah never bids anything shameful. Commanding such a thing would run against His wisdom and against His being the purest of the pure.

**"Say, "No! Allah never commands what is shameful.""** is that answer, spoken to the Prophet ﷺ so that he can deliver it. What the practice amounted to was a despicable sin, and the claim that Allah had ordered it was the opposite of the truth.

**"How can you attribute to Allah what you do not know?"** is the rebuke, and the question is meant as a repudiation. What it charges them with is saying something about Allah on no evidence. That is rank effrontery in any case. When it is done in the case of Allah, it is a crime beyond the reach of imagination.

### What the practice was

Before Islam, one of the shameful customs Shaytan had planted among the people of the age of ignorance was this: nobody except the Quraysh was allowed to walk round the Kaʿbah in his own clothes. A man either borrowed a garment from a Qurayshi, or he made the circuit naked.

The Quraysh, who were known as al-Ḥums, wore their ordinary clothes for the ṭawāf. Anyone from the other Arab tribes who managed to borrow a garment from them wore that borrowed garment for it. Anyone who wore a new garment of his own discarded it when the circuit was finished, and after that nobody would wear it. Those who had no new garment, and who were not given one by al-Ḥums, had no way left but to go round with nothing on.

Mujāhid described the scene. The idolaters used to walk round the House while naked, saying that they did the ṭawāf the way their mothers had given birth to them. The women went round naked as well, and a woman would cover her sexual organ with something and say, as she did it, that some of it or all of it would appear today, but that whatever appeared of it was not permitted to anyone. In practice the women took care to make their circuit at night. The Quraysh could not possibly have clothed the whole of Arabia, so the outcome of the custom was that most people performed the ṭawāf naked, men and women alike.

The whole business was an invention of the idolaters, and they followed nobody in it but their own forefathers. Then they dressed the invention up as the law of Allah. That is the exact claim the verse refutes.

### The four kinds of cattle

Another form of the same argument appears in the matter of the cattle. The idolaters had taken four names out of the herds and declared them forbidden. These were al-Bahīrah, as-Sāʾibah, al-Waṣīlah and al-Ḥām. When they were challenged about it, they said again that they had found their fathers keeping to the practice and that Allah had commanded it. The answer given is that Allah does not command indecency, and that the forbidding of these animals was not a command of His at all.

What the two cases have in common is the way a human decision is moved backward in time until it looks like a tradition, and then moved upward until it looks like a revelation. The verse closes both doors at once when it says that Allah never commands what is shameful, and that nothing may be said about Him except what is known.

### A note on this verse and legal reasoning

One thing needs to be said clearly, because the verse could be misread. The recognised jurists, when they work out and state rulings by ijtihād from the verses of the Qur'an, are not doing what this verse condemns. Their reasoning works inside the words and the meanings that the Qur'an has given, and it rests on them. What the verse condemns is the opposite: a claim laid on Allah with no evidence behind it at all.

### How the enemy works on a person

The way Satan leads a man astray is by giving him wrong information. He makes the forbidden tree look like the fountainhead of every advantage. And he comes at a person by paths so innocuous that the man never suspects he is being misled. He knows the weak points and the delicate places in a person's moral armour, and he attacks exactly those places.

There is a further question in the verse about who he succeeds with. He succeeds with the people who cannot give an account of their faith when they are tested. He succeeds with those who do not think carefully about the signs of God, with those who turn down sound argument, and with those who cannot live up to what truth demands of them when it goes against their own inclinations. People of that kind do not recognise the truth as truth if it does not promote their interests.

### In short

- The verse answers the two defences the idolaters gave for a shameful practice, one from custom and one from a supposed command of Allah.
- The custom defended was the naked ṭawāf, imposed on every tribe except the Quraysh, and the four forbidden kinds of cattle were defended the same way.
- The appeal to forefathers proves nothing, since the ancestors of different peoples did contradictory things and every wrong practice in the world could be justified by it.
- The appeal to a command of Allah was a plain lie, and the rebuke is that they were saying things about Allah that they did not know.
- Satan misleads by misinformation delivered on innocent-looking paths, and he succeeds with those who cannot answer for their faith when tested.

<<<VERSE 7:29>>>
The command turns from what Allah never ordered to what He has ordered. "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “My Lord has commanded uprightness and dedication ˹to Him alone˺ in worship, calling upon Him with sincere devotion. Just as He first brought you into being, you will be brought to life again.”" Two verses earlier the wrong thing was being attributed to Allah. Here the right thing is set out in three parts, and the last part of it is a fact about the reader.

### Phrase by phrase

**"My Lord has commanded uprightness"** is the first part. The word used is al-qist, and it means justice, balance and moderation. In this place it refers to a conduct that has neither excess nor deficiency, one that neither falls short of the appointed limits nor goes past them. That is how the injunctions of the Sharīʿah work as a whole. So under this one word come all the acts of worship and obedience and every general injunction of the law. One of the early readings of the word takes it as the confession of Allah's oneness, that there is no god but Allah.

**"and dedication ˹to Him alone˺ in worship"** translates the instruction to set the face upright, and to do it at every place of worship. Most commentators take the place of worship here in the sense of prostration, so that the words mean a person should be upright and straight at the time of every prayer and every other act of worship. It can also mean that a person should take care to keep his orientation straight towards the qiblah. And it can reach further still. Keeping the orientation straight can mean putting everything one says and does under what Allah has commanded, holding to one stance that never varies and never drifts off elsewhere. Read that way, the instruction is not confined to the prayer. It takes in every act of worship, every dealing and every transaction.

**"calling upon Him with sincere devotion"** is the second of the two specific injunctions, and it belongs to the heart rather than to the body. It means worshipping Allah purely and alone, without making anything else a partner in the act in any capacity. The purity demanded reaches as far as what is called concealed shirk, the association of something else with the divinity of Allah. Under that heading come hypocrisy and showing off.

**"Just as He first brought you into being, you will be brought to life again."** is the third part. It was He who made you the first time, and it will be He who raises you again on the day of Qiyamah. For His perfect power this is not difficult at all. Perhaps it is to point to that ease that the verse says "you will return" and does not say "We shall make you rise". Bringing a person back requires no effort. Several commentators gave readings along these lines. Mujāhid read it as a promise of life after death. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī read it as: the One who began you here will raise you on the Day of Resurrection. Qatādah said that He started their creation when they were nothing, that they perished afterwards, and that He will bring them back again. ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said that as He created you at the start, He will bring you back at the end. This last reading was preferred by Ibn Jarīr, who supported it with what he reported from Ibn ʿAbbās.

### The outward and the inward together

The two specific injunctions are placed side by side on purpose, and one of them concerns what a person does outwardly while the other concerns his heart. The indication is that both dimensions of a person have to be put right and held together in line with the Sharīʿah. Obvious obedience is not enough on its own without sincerity, and sincerity of the heart does not become sufficient on its own without following the Sharīʿah in what people can see.

This is where a common error is exposed. Some people treat the Sharīʿah, the outward adherence to the faith, and the ṭarīqah, the inward adherence to it, as two different roads to religion. Some of them even suppose that it is enough to put the inward dimension right, even if they do what contradicts the Sharīʿah. That is a glaring mistake.

### What justice means in practice

This is one of those short passages in which a great deal has been gathered together. The foundations of service are in it, and so are the signposts of practice and the realities of recognition. It teaches the faithful which character traits please their Lord, and what beautiful worship of Him looks like, and what beautiful living with His creatures looks like. What is commanded is justice, and that justice runs in three directions at once.

Towards the Real, it means putting the commands and the prohibitions to work, and being content with His decree in every state. Towards the creatures, it means living with good character, giving them what is fair in the various dealings between people, and not demanding fairness for oneself. Towards the soul, it means setting oneself against it, drawing it into discipline and struggle, and closing the door on appetite and comfort.

The same thought appears in another verse, where Allah commands justice and beautiful doing (16:90). And the mark of a passage like this one is that a great deal of knowledge has been gathered into very few words. A short verse elsewhere carries the same weight, where Allah says that He is with those who are mindful of Him and those who do good (16:128). Everything the Real gives to a servant is contained in the first half of that promise. Everything the servant does for Allah comes under the second. And everything that people owe to one another comes under the third.

### Why the mention of the life to come stands here

There is a benefit in the sentence about being brought back again standing where it does. It is what makes the injunctions of the Sharīʿah bearable. What makes a hard thing easy and a painful thing endurable is the settled conviction that another life is coming, with a Last Day in it and reward and punishment for every deed. Experience confirms it. A person is a hard case, and unless he is held by that apprehension no piece of advice will move him to do what is right, and no restriction of any law will keep him out of crime.

### What was said about the return

The Prophet ﷺ stood up and gave the people a speech, and in it he said:

> O people, you will be gathered to Allah barefoot, naked and uncircumcised. As We began the first creation, We shall repeat it. It is a promise binding upon Us. Truly, We shall do it.

The verse quoted is 21:104, and the report was collected in the two Ṣaḥīḥs. Ibn ʿAbbās commented on the return in another way. Allah began the creation of the sons of Adam as some believers and some disbelievers, just as He said: "He it is Who created you, then some of you are disbelievers and some of you are believers" (64:2). He will bring them back on the Day of Resurrection as He started them, some believing and some not. The hadith that supports this is the one al-Bukhārī recorded from Ibn Masʿūd, in which the Prophet ﷺ said:

> By He other than Whom there is no god, one of you might do the deeds of the people of Paradise until only the length of an arm or a forearm is left between him and it. Then what was written in the Book takes precedence, and he does the work of the people of the Fire and enters it. And one of you might do the deeds of the people of the Fire until only the length of an arm or a forearm is left between him and the Fire. Then what was written in the Book takes precedence, and he does the work of the people of Paradise and enters Paradise.

Held together with this reading, the Qur'an also says: "So set your face towards the religion, turning away from all else. That is the natural disposition Allah instilled in people" (30:30). And the two Ṣaḥīḥs record from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Every child is born upon the fiṭrah, and it is only his parents who turn him into a Jew, a Christian or a Zoroastrian."

### In short

- What Allah commands is justice, which here means a conduct with neither excess nor deficiency, and it takes in every injunction of the law.
- The instruction to be upright at every place of prostration covers the outward act and reaches as far as holding everything one does under the command of Allah.
- Sincere devotion covers the heart, and it rules out concealed shirk along with hypocrisy and showing off. The two together mean the outward and the inward must both be put right.
- The return to life is certain, and it is mentioned here because the certainty of it is what makes the injunctions bearable in practice.
- Justice runs in three directions: towards Allah in obeying and accepting His decree, towards people in dealing fairly and asking nothing for oneself, and towards one's own soul in opposing it.

<<<VERSE 7:30>>>
The third verse names the two outcomes. "He has guided some, while others are destined to stray. They have taken devils as their masters instead of Allah—thinking they are ˹rightly˺ guided". The sentence is short and it separates two groups by name and by judgement. And the second group is not a group that knew it was lost. It is a group that believed it was on the right road.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He has guided some"** names the first group. What they were given is the knowledge and the felicity that Allah honoured them with, and they are the people of the right hand, the people of the right.

**"while others are destined to stray"** names the second. What has come down on them is denial and damnation, and they are the people of the left. The wording is exact: error was not merely something that happened to them. It was prescribed over them.

**"They have taken devils as their masters instead of Allah"** gives the reason. Instead of Allah they made the satans their comrades and their supporters, and they did it in ignorance, binding themselves to them.

**"thinking they are ˹rightly˺ guided"** is the last stroke. They believe that they are on a straight path and that they have been guided to it. The presumption is the conclusion of the verse and not a side remark in it.

### What the verse says about good faith

Guidance was open to everyone. They turned away from it and began following the satans instead. Then, on top of that, they were brought to think that their sickness was health and their error was guidance.

The important point that follows is that not knowing the injunctions of the Sharīʿah is no valid excuse. If a person takes up a way of error while being quite sincere in believing that it is correct, he is not excused by that sincerity in the sight of Allah. The reason is that Allah has not left people without means. He gave everyone reason and ordinary sense to use for telling the true from the false. And He did not leave them to reason alone. He sent prophets and books, and by those, right and wrong were made plain.

A person might object that someone acting in good faith but in error should carry no blame, since he does not know that he is in error. The answer is that he was given reason, and on top of reason the teaching of the noble prophets. Through those two he was in a position to compare his way of doing things with the way they showed. There had to be some doubt in his mind, some pinch, some question left over. His fault is that he paid no attention to what they taught and kept to the way of error he had chosen.

### The one who searched and did not find

There is a limit to this, and it is worth stating. A person who has done his utmost in the search for truth and still did not find the right road and the true teaching may be excused in the sight of Allah. That is what Imam al-Ghazālī said in his book on the distinction between Islam and unbelief, Al-Tafrīqah Bayn al-Islām wa az-Zandaqah.

### The argument the verse settles

Ibn Jarīr drew a conclusion from this verse and said it was one of the clearest proofs against those who claim that Allah does not punish anyone for a disobedient act or a deviation he sincerely believes in, until knowledge of what is correct reaches him and he deliberately avoids it all the same. Take the claim as true and the two groups collapse into one, since the guided group and the group that only believes itself guided would come out the same. But Allah has set them apart in this very verse, and He has done it both by what He calls them and by how He judges them.

### What is written and what is chosen

There is a wider setting for all of this, and the verse does not stand alone. Allah's decree will certainly come to pass among His creation, and He is the One "Who has measured ˹everything˺ and then guided" (87:3), and the One "Who gave each thing its form and nature, then guided it aright" (20:50).

The two Ṣaḥīḥs record the saying that those among you who belong to the people of happiness will have the deeds of the people of happiness made easy for them, and those who belong to the wretched will have the deeds of the wretched made easy for them. And a sentence reported from the Prophet ﷺ explains the whole of it. Every person goes out in the morning and puts himself up for sale, and the sale either sets him free or ruins him.

Another report carries the same matter from the beginning. Muslim records that ʿIyād bin Ḥimār said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that Allah said:

> I created My servants as people who turn to one God, but the devils came to them and turned them away from their religion.

The meaning of all of it taken together is that Allah created His creatures so that some of them would later turn out believers and some disbelievers. Every one of His servants was made able to recognise Him and to worship Him alone, and to know that nothing else deserves to be worshipped. He took their covenant to carry out what that knowledge implies, and He placed it in their consciousness and in their souls. And He has decided that some of them will be wretched and some happy.

### In short

- The verse divides people into two groups by name and by judgement, one group guided and one group already destined to stray.
- The reason given for the second group is that they took the satans as their masters instead of Allah, and the last stroke is that they thought they were guided.
- Sincerity is not an excuse for error, because reason and the teaching of the prophets were both given, and a person was in a position to compare his way with theirs.
- The one who searched for the truth with all his effort and still did not find it may be excused, as Imam al-Ghazālī stated in his book on the distinction between Islam and unbelief.
- The verse is one of the clearest proofs that a person is not excused for a deviation simply because he believes in it, and the divine decree and human choice both stand behind the two outcomes.
