<<<VERSE 7:28>>>
This verse was revealed to root out a shameful custom of the pagan Arabs: "Whenever they commit a shameful deed, they say, “We found our forefathers doing it and Allah has commanded us to do it.” Say, “No! Allah never commands what is shameful. How can you attribute to Allah what you do not know?”" When told to stop, the idolaters defended their deeds with two arguments: their forefathers had always done it, and Allah had commanded it. The verse answers the second, and the first hardly needs an answer.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whenever they commit a shameful deed"**: the word is fāḥishah. Most explanations say it refers here to circling the Kaʿbah naked. More broadly, fuḥsh, faḥshāʾ and fāḥishah mean any evil act whose wickedness reaches the furthest limit and is loud and clear to common sense and sound taste. That the good and bad of such acts can be known by reason is established everywhere. Other examples given are idolatry, and forbidding the dedicated animals called al-Baḥīrah, as-Sāʾibah, al-Waṣīlah and al-Ḥām.

**"they say, “We found our forefathers doing it"**: we found them practising it, so we followed their example. To give up the practice now, they felt, would be a disgrace.

**"and Allah has commanded us to do it.”"** This was a flagrant lie. They claimed that what their forefathers did was really obedience to Allah's order and law.

**"Say, “No! Allah never commands what is shameful."** Say to them, O Muhammad: the practice you indulge in is a vile sin, and Allah does not command such a thing. On the contrary, He forbids it. Commanding anything like it would go against His wisdom and against His being the Purest of the pure.

**"How can you attribute to Allah what you do not know?”"** The question is a rebuke. Do you put words into Allah's mouth that you cannot be sure He said? Or are you simply inventing things about Him?

### What was the naked ṭawāf?

Mujāhid said the idolaters used to circle the House naked, saying, "We make ṭawāf as our mothers bore us." A woman would cover her private part with something and say, "Today some or all of it may show, but whatever shows, I do not permit it." Then Allah sent down this verse.

Apart from the Quraysh, the Arabs used to make ṭawāf naked. They said they would not circle the House in clothes in which they had disobeyed Allah, so they banned wearing clothes for it. The Quraysh, known as al-Ḥums, circled in their ordinary clothes. Being servants of the sacred Ḥaram, they were exempt from this law of nakedness. An Arab who borrowed a garment from one of the Ḥums wore it for his ṭawāf. Someone who came in a new garment threw it off after the ṭawāf, and no one would wear it after him. Those with no new garment, and no loan from the Ḥums, went round naked. The Quraysh could not clothe the whole of Arabia, so most people made ṭawāf naked, men and women alike. The women usually went round in the dark of night.

The idolaters invented this practice themselves, simply following their forefathers. So lacking in sense were they that it never struck them how much worse nakedness was. It offended the House far more than their clothes could, and it offended human dignity itself.

### Is "our forefathers did it" an argument?

Their first argument was that ancestral customs must be kept, as if keeping them were good in itself. The answer is plain: blindly following ancestral custom is not reasonable. Anyone of ordinary sense can see that a practice is not justified just because one's forefathers did it. If it were, the forefathers of different peoples acted in different and even opposite ways, and every wrong practice in the world would become right and lawful. The argument did not deserve attention, and so the Qur'an did not answer it here. Some hadith narrations do answer it: a foolish act committed by one's forefathers is not worth following by any stretch of the imagination.

### Why is speaking about Allah without knowledge so serious?

Their second argument, that Allah had ordered it, was a bare lie. They were ascribing to Allah something He had never commanded, without any evidence. To attribute something to anyone without proper inquiry and authority is gross effrontery and plain injustice. Done about Allah, it is a crime and an injustice whose size cannot be imagined.

This verse does not cover the work of the qualified imams of ijtihād, independent legal reasoning. When they draw out and set down rulings from the verses of the Qur'an, they work within the warrant of the Qur'an's own words and meanings.

### How does Shayṭān make the shameful look right?

Shayṭān leads people astray by misinforming them. He made the forbidden tree look like the spring from which every advantage flowed. He comes by such harmless-looking ways that a person has no idea he is being misled. He knows all the weak points and tender places in human moral armour, and he aims at exactly those spots.

Whom does he succeed in misleading? He succeeds with:

- Those who cannot show their faith in times of trial.
- Those who do not think carefully about Allah's signs.
- Those who reject sound arguments.
- Those who will not put the demands of truth before their own inclinations.

Such people do not recognise the truth as truth if it does not serve their interests.

### In short

- The idolaters defended shameful acts, above all circling the Kaʿbah naked, by appeal to their forefathers and to a command from Allah.
- Following forefathers blindly proves nothing, since ancestors of different peoples did opposite things.
- Allah never commands what is shameful. Ascribing to Him what one does not know is an injustice beyond measure.
- Rulings that qualified scholars derive from the Qur'an's words are not speaking about Allah without knowledge.

<<<VERSE 7:29>>>
Against their claim, the Prophet ﷺ is told to state what Allah really commands: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “My Lord has commanded uprightness and dedication ˹to Him alone˺ in worship, calling upon Him with sincere devotion. Just as He first brought you into being, you will be brought to life again.”" Those who said Allah had ordered their naked ṭawāf are told that He always commands justice, not indecency or sin.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “My Lord has commanded uprightness"**: the word is al-qisṭ, which means justice, balance and moderation. Here it means conduct free of excess and of shortfall. It neither falls short of the set limits nor goes beyond them, as with every injunction of the Sharīʿah. So qisṭ takes in every act of worship and obedience, and all the general injunctions of the Sharīʿah. Others explain it as fairness, or as declaring Allah's Oneness, that there is no god but Allah.

**"and dedication ˹to Him alone˺ in worship"**: literally, "set your faces aright at every masjid". Grammatically, wa-aqīmū ("and set") is joined to the sense of "justice", as if He had said, "Be just, and set your faces." It may also be read with an implied "so turn". Most explanations take masjid here to mean prostration (sajdah, sujūd), that is, worship. One should be upright at every ṣalāh and every act of worship, prostrating purely for Allah. It could also mean taking care to face the qiblah correctly. Or it could mean making everything one says and does follow what Allah has commanded, with a direction that never wavers or drifts. On that reading the command is not limited to ṣalāh. It covers all worship, dealings and transactions. Allah commands loyalty to Him in worship in general, and in places of worship in particular.

To be straight in worship means following the messengers, who were supported by miracles, and obeying what they brought from Allah and the law they conveyed. Allah does not accept a good deed until it meets two conditions. It must be correct, in line with His law, and it must be free of shirk.

**"calling upon Him with sincere devotion."** Worship Him and call on Him alone, making your religion purely His, free of any idolatry. Nothing and no one should be joined with Him in that worship in any capacity. It should even be free of al-shirk al-khafiyy, the hidden shirk of hypocrisy and showing off (riyāʾ).

**"Just as He first brought you into being, you will be brought to life again.”"** Several explanations are given.

- Mujāhid said it means life restored after death.
- Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said that the One who started you off in this world will revive you on the Day of Resurrection.
- Qatādah said that Allah first created them out of nothing, then they died, and He will return them.
- ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said that He who made you at the start will restore you at the finish.
- Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as: He began the children of Ādam as believers and disbelievers, and He will bring them back the same way. Another explanation says He brought them into being on the Day of the Covenant as happy and wretched, knowing and denying, believing and disbelieving, and they will return in that state. This view is taken up under the next verse.

Abū Jaʿfar Ibn Jarīr preferred the explanation of ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd. He supported it with a report from Ibn ʿAbbās. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ stood and addressed the people. He told them they would be gathered to Allah barefoot, naked and uncircumcised. Then he recited, "Just as We produced the first creation, ˹so˺ shall We reproduce it. That is a promise binding on Us" (21:104). This hadith is in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs.

The One who could create you the first time, from nothing, can surely bring you back. For His perfect power this is no effort at all. Perhaps that is why the Arabic says taʿūdūn, "you will return", and not "We shall raise you". Raising people again needs very little action.

### Outward and inward together

After the command of justice and balance, two injunctions that bear directly on the idolaters' conduct are named. "Set your faces" concerns what a person does with the body, outwardly. "Call upon Him with sincere devotion" concerns the heart. Naming both together suggests that both sides of a person must be put right and brought into line with the Sharīʿah, the outward and the inward, the physical and the spiritual.

Outward obedience (iṭāʿah) is not enough without sincerity (ikhlāṣ). Nor is sincerity of heart enough without visibly following the Sharīʿah. Everyone must bring his outward conduct into line with the Sharīʿah and also keep his inner life reserved for Allah alone. This exposes the error of those who treat Sharīʿah, outward observance, and Ṭarīqah, the inward path, as two separate roads to religion. Some even suppose it is enough to put the inner self right through the Ṭarīqah while acting against the Sharīʿah. That is a gaping error.

### Why mention the Resurrection here?

Mentioning the Resurrection at this point has a further benefit. It helps people keep the injunctions of the Sharīʿah. Knowing that there is a life to come, a Last Day, and reward or punishment for deeds is what makes the hard easy and the painful bearable. Experience shows that man is a tough customer. Without that gripping awareness, no advice will make him do right, and no legal restriction will stop him from crime.

### What does justice demand?

Justice, qisṭ, is behaviour that meets every moral standard, being exactly what it should be. Worship is a natural urge in every person. He wants to accept some Being as supreme and to bow down before Him. Justice is done here only if he worships the one true God, his Creator, Lord and Sustainer. A person also wants some Being at the centre of his convictions, and justice means making the All-Powerful Allah the ground of his trust. In the same way, accepting life after death is justice in the fullest sense. Everyone came into being out of non-being when he was born. Believing in resurrection is believing in the very reality every person has already lived through at birth.

### The inner reading: one verse holding the whole religion

This verse, from the Lord of heaven and earth, brings three things together: the realities of recognition, the signposts of practice, and the foundations of service. Through it He shows the faithful which traits of character please Him. He teaches them to worship Him beautifully and to live beautifully with His creatures, and He honours them by showing them what earns His approval. This verse is among the "all-comprehensive words" of which al-Muṣṭafā said, "I was sent with the all-comprehensive words, and knowledge was made very concise for me."

The Qur'an has many such verses. Take one: "Surely Allah is with those who shun evil and who do good ˹deeds˺" (16:128). See how much meaning so short a verse holds. "Surely Allah is with" holds all of Allah's tenderness, generosity and bounty toward His servant. "Those who shun evil" covers every kind of worship and practice the servant offers Allah. "Who do good" covers all that people owe one another in their dealings.

In the same way, all the pillars of religion, the positions of the Sharīʿah and the gates of the Ḥaqīqah are contained in this verse. It is as if the Prophet ﷺ were saying, "Allah has commanded me to be just in my dealings with the Real, with His creatures and with my own soul."

- With the Real, by acting on what He commands and forbids, and being content with whatever He decrees.
- With people, by good character and fair dealing in every transaction, while never insisting on fairness for oneself.
- With one's own soul, by resisting it, forcing it to strive and train, and closing the door on appetite and comfort.

The counterpart of this verse in the Qur'an is "Indeed, Allah commands justice, grace" (16:90).

### In short

- Allah commands qisṭ: justice and balance in worship, in dealings and in all of life, never indecency.
- Worship must be outwardly correct according to His law and inwardly sincere, free even of hidden shirk and showing off.
- Sharīʿah and Ṭarīqah are not two roads. Inner purity never excuses outward disobedience.
- The One who created people from nothing will bring them back, and remembering this makes obedience easier.

<<<VERSE 7:30>>>
Humanity divides into two groups: "He has guided some, while others are destined to stray. They have taken devils as their masters instead of Allah—thinking they are ˹rightly˺ guided". Allah's guidance was open to all. Some accepted it, while others turned away, followed the devils, and then came to think that their sickness was health and their error was guidance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He has guided some"**: a group of you He has guided. He made the way to guidance easy for them and removed the barriers in their path. He honoured them with knowledge and happiness, and they are the people of the right.

**"while others are destined to stray."** Another group has earned going astray, and error is decreed for them. They have shown proof of their own wrongdoing. Allah humbled them with denial and wretchedness, and they are the people of the left.

**"They have taken devils as their masters instead of Allah"**: they made the devils their friends, helpers, patrons and even gods, bypassing Allah. In their ignorance they bound themselves to them, and so they strayed from the path of truth.

**"thinking they are ˹rightly˺ guided"**: the misguided believe they are following Allah's religion and walking a straight path.

### Were people created believers or disbelievers?

The explanation of the previous verse from Ibn ʿAbbās leads into this one. Allah began the creation of the children of Ādam with some believing and some disbelieving, as He says: "He is the One Who created you, yet some of you are disbelievers while some are believers" (64:2). He will bring them back on the Day of Resurrection as He began them.

A hadith from Ibn Masʿūd, which al-Bukhārī recorded, supports this meaning. The Prophet ﷺ swore by the One besides whom there is no god. One of you, he said, may do the deeds of the people of Paradise until only an arm's length lies between him and it. Then what was written in the Book overtakes him, and he does the deeds of the people of the Fire and enters it. Another may do the deeds of the people of the Fire until only an arm's length separates him from it. Then what was written overtakes him, and he does the deeds of the people of Paradise and enters Paradise.

If this is the correct meaning of the verse, it must be read together with other texts.

- Allah speaks of "the natural Way of Allah which He has instilled in ˹all˺ people" (30:30).
- Abū Hurayrah reported in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs that the Prophet ﷺ said every child is born upon the fiṭrah, the natural state, and it is his parents who make him a Jew, a Christian or a Zoroastrian.
- Muslim recorded from ʿIyāḍ ibn Ḥimār that the Prophet ﷺ reported Allah as saying that He created His servants ḥunafāʾ, pure in faith, but the devils came and led them away from their religion.

Taken together, the meaning is that Allah created His creatures so that some would later become believers and some disbelievers. In the beginning He made all His servants able to recognise Him and worship Him alone. They could know that none but He deserves worship. He took their covenant to act on that knowledge, which He placed in their minds and souls. He also decreed that some would be wretched and some happy. A hadith says that every person sets out at dawn to sell himself. Some free themselves by the bargain, and others ruin themselves.

Allah's decree will surely be carried out in His creation. He is the One "Who ordained precisely and inspired accordingly" (87:3). He is "the One Who has given everything its ˹distinctive˺ form, then guided ˹it˺" (20:50). The Two Ṣaḥīḥs report that those destined for happiness will find the deeds of the happy made easy for them. Those destined for misery will find the deeds of the miserable made easy for them. That is why Allah says here that He guided some while others were destined to stray, because they took the devils as patrons instead of Him.

### Does sincere error excuse a person?

This verse shows that ignorance of the Sharīʿah's injunctions is no valid excuse. If someone takes a wrong path in full sincerity, thinking it right, he is still not excused in Allah's sight. Allah has given everyone reason and common sense to tell true from false. Nor did He leave people to reason alone. He sent prophets and books that made right and wrong, truth and falsehood, perfectly clear.

Ibn Jarīr said this is one of the clearest proofs against those who claim Allah punishes no one for sins committed or errors believed until knowledge of the truth reaches him and he stubbornly turns from it. If that were so, he argued, there would be no difference between the misguided, who believe themselves guided, and those who really are guided. Yet Allah has distinguished the two in this verse, in both name and judgement.

Someone may object that a person acting in good faith, though mistaken, should bear no blame, because he does not know he is wrong. The answer is that Allah has blessed people with reason and sense, and with the teachings of the noble prophets as well. Through these, a person can at least measure his own way against the way they taught. There is bound to be some doubt, some twinge or question in his mind. His fault is that he ignored their teaching and kept to the wrong road he had taken.

Still, a person who searched for the truth as hard as he could, and never reached the right path or the true teaching, may be excused by Allah. Imam al-Ghazālī said so in his book Al-Tafriqah Bayn al-Islām wa al-Zandaqah.

### Following revered ancestors against the truth

People often lean on the great figures of the past in order to reject the one who calls them to the truth. The greatness of those forebears is established by history, and everyone takes it for granted that they were right. The one calling to the truth is new, with no history yet to vouch for him. A person looks at the ancients, revered as saints for their illustrious past, and compares them with this newcomer who has no tradition behind him. He then rejects the caller outright, sure that he himself is on the right path. This kind of misunderstanding is no excuse before Allah. In rejecting the caller he is not following Allah at all. He is only following Shayṭān in Allah's name.

### In short

- Allah's guidance was open to all. Some accepted it, and others earned error by taking the devils as patrons instead of Him.
- People are born on the fiṭrah, able to know Allah, yet His decree of happiness and misery is carried out, and each finds his chosen deeds made easy.
- Sincerely believing oneself guided is no excuse when reason and the prophets' teaching were available. Allah distinguishes the guided from the misguided in name and judgement.
- One who truly strove for the truth and still missed it may be excused, but clinging to revered ancestors against a caller to truth is following Shayṭān in Allah's name.
