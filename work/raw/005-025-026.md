<<<VERSE 5:25>>>
"Moses pleaded, “My Lord! I have no control over anyone except myself and my brother. So set us apart from the rebellious people.”"

This verse gives Musa's prayer. After his people had refused to enter the Holy Land and had said that they would sit where they were while he and his Lord fought, Musa turns to Allah. He says that he has no control over anyone except himself and his brother, and he asks Allah to set them apart from the rebellious people. The material reads the prayer as the cry of a prophet who has been worn down by the repeated breaches of his people, and who asks only for what he can rely on.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Moses pleaded, ‘My Lord!’"** The material says that Musa was very angry with the Children of Israel when they refused to fight, and that he turned to his Lord in supplication against them. The word "pleaded" in the translation captures the urgency of the prayer.

**"I have no control over anyone except myself and my brother."** The material explains that Musa speaks of himself and his brother Harun, because they alone among the people would obey and carry out Allah's command. The material notes that Harun was a prophet, and was protected from error, so Musa could be sure of his steadfastness. It also says that the two righteous men, Yusha' and Kalib, were not spoken of, since they were not protected from error in the same way. The material says that Musa's words reflect his pain at the treachery of his people, and that the same disobedience was the cause of what he said.

**"So set us apart from the rebellious people."** The material reports several readings of this phrase. Ibn 'Abbas, through al-'Awfi and 'Ali bin Abi Talhah, said it means judge between us and them. Ad-Dahhak said it means judge and decide. Others said it means separate between us and them. The material notes that the request is for the people to receive what they deserve, and for Musa and Harun to receive what they deserve, so that the two groups are not mixed.

### The prophet's pain and the reply

The material says that the prayer shows a prophet who has done everything he could for his people. He had freed them from slavery, shown them the signs of Allah, and led them out of Egypt. Yet they still refused the command. The material says that the prophets feel the disobedience of their people deeply, and that this is part of their nature. It also notes that Musa's trust in Allah is complete, and that he turns to Allah alone once human means have failed.

The material also contrasts the people's reply with the readiness of the Companions at Badr, who pledged to defend the Prophet ﷺ from every side. The contrast is the same one that the earlier verses drew, and this verse closes the scene of the refusal.

### In short

- Musa prayed to Allah after his people refused to enter the Holy Land.
- He asked for judgment and separation between himself and the rebellious people, because he had no power over them beyond himself and his brother.
- The prayer shows a prophet who has done all he could and who turns in trust to Allah.

<<<VERSE 5:26>>>
"Allah replied, “Then this land is forbidden to them for forty years, during which they will wander through the land. So do not grieve for the rebellious people.”"

This verse gives Allah's reply to Musa. The land is forbidden to the people for forty years, during which they will wander aimlessly across the land. Allah tells Musa not to grieve for the rebellious people. The material explains that the forbidden land was Syria, and that the people were kept in a small open area, moving in circles, until a new generation grew up and entered the land under Yusha'.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah replied, ‘Then this land is forbidden to them for forty years.’"** The material explains that the land of Syria was forbidden to them for forty years. They could not go there, and they could not return to Egypt either. They were kept in the wilderness of al-Tih. The material explains that the punishment fitted the offence. Because they had said "we will sit here," they were kept sitting in the wilderness for forty years.

**"During which they will wander through the land."** The material says that the wandering was a confinement without walls. The people walked all day, expecting to reach Egypt or Syria, and by evening they were back where they had started. The material notes that the area was small, and that the people, numbering several hundred thousand by one account, were held within it until the older generation died. The material says Allah does not need prisons. The whole creation is under His command, and when He wills to hold someone, the air, the earth and the open land become the jailer.

**"So do not grieve for the rebellious people."** The material explains that Allah is comforting Musa, and that the prophets are naturally moved by the suffering of their people even when it is deserved. Musa is told not to grieve over the punishment, because the people had earned it through their own rebellion.

### What the forty years held

The material says that the forty years were not only a punishment. During this time Allah gave the people clouds for shade, manna and quails for food, water from a rock struck with Musa's staff, and light by night. The material notes that these blessings came because of the presence of Musa and Harun, and perhaps because some of the people repented. It says that the Torah was revealed in this period, and that the Covenant was established for the Children of Israel.

The material says that Harun died during the forty years, and that Musa died soon after, and that Allah then raised Yusha' bin Nun as a prophet. When the forty years were over, Yusha' led the remaining people toward Jerusalem, and the land was conquered. The material notes that the conquest was in fulfilment of the promise that Allah had made, and that the generation who refused the command did not enter it. The material also relates that the sun was held back for Yusha' on the Friday when the conquest was completed, so that he could finish before the Sabbath began.

### The lesson of the wilderness

The material draws the lesson that the people were more afraid of men than of Allah, and that this fear cost them the land. It says that the promise was fulfilled through a new generation that had grown up in the hardship of the desert. It also says that the verse 5:21 and 5:22 show the fear of the people, and this verse shows its consequence. The material notes that the chapter speaks to the believers so that they will not repeat the same fear.

### In short

- The land was forbidden to the people for forty years, and they wandered in a small area until the older generation had died.
- The forty years were a punishment, but they also held blessings, through the presence of Musa and Harun.
- The promise was fulfilled through a new generation under Yusha', and the verse teaches that fear of people can cost a community what Allah has given it.
