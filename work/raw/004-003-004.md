<<<VERSE 4:3>>>
"If you fear you might fail to give orphan women their due rights if you were to marry them, then marry other women of your choice—two, three, or four. But if you are afraid you will fail to maintain justice, then content yourselves with one or those bondwomen in your possession. This way you are less likely to commit injustice." This verse continues the protection of orphans by addressing a specific abuse: a guardian marrying an orphan girl under his care in order to keep her property, fixing a low dowry and treating her as he pleases. It commands that if he fears he cannot do justice to such a girl, he should marry other women instead, up to four, and if he fears he cannot maintain justice even then, only one, or a bondwoman. The verse thus restricts polygamy and makes justice the condition for it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you fear you might fail to give orphan women their due rights"** refers to the guardian who holds an orphan girl and fears he cannot treat her justly in marriage, especially when her wealth and beauty tempt him to marry her without an adequate dowry.

**"Then marry other women of your choice—two, three, or four"** permits marrying other women instead, up to four, as many as he likes, provided they are lawful for him. The expression "ma taba" means women lawful for him, and also those he desires. There is no contradiction, since it means women he desires who are also lawful in the shariah.

**"But if you are afraid you will fail to maintain justice"** addresses the fear of not being able to do justice between wives.

**"Then content yourselves with one"** commands marrying only one wife when justice between several cannot be maintained.

**"Or those bondwomen in your possession"** permits satisfying oneself with a bondwoman, for treating bondwomen equally is not obligatory but recommended.

**"This way you are less likely to commit injustice"** means this is nearer to preventing deviation from justice (ta'ulu), which scholars explained as deviating from justice.

### The abuse addressed

During the time of ignorance, guardians of orphan girls would pick those with good looks or valuable property, marry them or arrange their marriage to their sons, fix the lowest possible dowry, and maintain them as they pleased, since their fathers were not there to protect their rights. A narration in Sahih al-Bukhari from Aishah reports that a man had an orphan girl under his guardianship and a fruit farm in which she held a share. He married her and, instead of giving her a dowry from his own pocket, took her share in the farm. Thereupon this verse was revealed: if you fear you cannot do justice to the orphans, marry the women you like. The verse therefore lays down total vigilance so that the marital rights of orphan girls are not violated. The responsibility is placed on people themselves to fear Allah and do what is right, and also on the enforcing agencies of government to oversee that no such violation occurs.

### Marriage of minors

The word yatama here means orphan girls. In the terminology of the shariah, a yatim is a boy or girl who has not yet attained puberty, so the verse proves that a guardian may give an orphan girl in marriage while she is a minor, provided the decision is based on wisdom and her future welfare. Orphan girls who have attained puberty are entitled to choose their husbands freely on the basis of their majority, but in practice girls tend to be modest and shy and may accept what their guardians do in good faith, so guardians must stay clear of violating their rights.

### Polygamy before Islam

Having more than one wife was considered permissible in all religions before Islam. The custom prevailed in Arabia, India, Iran, Egypt, Babylon and elsewhere. The Europeans later made multiplicity of marriage impermissible, which resulted in multiplicity of concubines and girl-friends out of wedlock. Vedic teachings permit unlimited wives, and Shri Krishna had hundreds of wives. Davenport, a Christian writer, deduced from the Bible that plurality of wives is favourable and a source of blessing. Father Nixon, John Milton and Isac Taylor supported this view. The truth is that a religion or law aiming to establish an infrastructure against adultery serves as a remedial approach to the problem of a comparatively larger female population. Nations that do not permit plurality of wives must live with rampant adultery. During the early period of Islam the custom continued without limit, and people took too many wives to satisfy greed, then could not do justice, so their wives lived like prisoners. The Qur'an stopped this injustice by restricting plurality to four and warning that equality in fulfilling the rights of wives taken at the same time must be maintained faithfully. The expression "ma taba" was explained by Hasan al-Basri, Ibn Jubair and Ibn Malik as "ma halla," women lawful for you. Others took the literal meaning "whom you like". There is no contradiction, since it means women you like who are also lawful in the shariah.

### The restriction to four

The Qur'an restricted multiplicity of wives by declaring that keeping more than four women in marriage is forbidden (haram), and gave stern warning against contravening this. The Prophet made this clearer: Ghailan ibn Aslamah al-Thaqafi embraced Islam with ten wives, and the Prophet told him to select and retain four and divorce the rest. Ghailan obeyed. Later, during the caliphate of Umar, Ghailan divorced the four and distributed his assets among his sons. Umar summoned him and said he had done this to deprive the women of inheritance, which was gross injustice, and ordered him to revoke the divorce and take back his assets, or else he would take possession of all and order his grave stoned like the grave of Abu Righal of Thamud, who was saved while in the Sacred Area but tormented when he left it. Ash-Shafii, At-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, Ad-Daraqutni and Al-Bayhaqi collected the hadith up to the Prophet's statement "Choose any four of them". Only Ahmad collected the full version. Had it been allowed to keep more than four, the Prophet would have allowed Ghilan to keep more, since they all embraced Islam with him. His command to keep only four shows no one may keep more than four under any circumstances.

### Only one wife where injustice is likely

After allowing up to four, the verse says if you fear you will not maintain equity, keep to one woman, or a bondwoman you own. Having more than one wife is permissible only on condition that equality is maintained as required by the shariah and the rights of all are fulfilled. If one lacks this capability, the rule is to keep to one wife. The Qur'anic expression for bondwoman has special conditions that generally do not exist today, so starting to live with someone out of wedlock by calling her a bondwoman is non sequitur and patently haram. Marriages within the limit of four are valid, but any shortcoming in treating wives equally is a grave sin, challengeable in an Islamic court. The Prophet laid great emphasis on maintaining full equality and justice between wives and demonstrated it in his own conduct. A hadith says anyone who has two wives and cannot fulfil their rights equally will be raised on the Day of Doom with one shoulder drooping. Equality is necessary in things within man's control, such as personal expenses and overnight stays. As for the natural inclination of the heart, there is no accountability, provided it does not affect matters within control. The Prophet treated his wives with full equality in everything within his control, yet prayed, "O Allah, this is my equalization in what I control, so do not hold me accountable in matters You control and I do not." Another verse says, "You shall be unable to maintain perfect equality between the women" (4:129), referring to love and the tilt of the heart, which are beyond man's power. Yet total indifference to the other wife is not permissible. The justice mentioned in "if you fear you will not maintain equity, keep to one woman" refers to justice in matters of choice and volition, any discrepancy in which is a great sin.

### In short

- The verse forbids a guardian marrying an orphan girl to keep her property, and commands him to marry other women instead, up to four.
- If he fears he cannot maintain justice, he should marry only one, or a bondwoman.
- Polygamy is restricted to four and made conditional on justice.
- The discussion adds the history of polygamy before Islam and the ruling of Ghilan ibn Aslamah.
- The verse ends by saying this is nearer to preventing injustice.

<<<VERSE 4:4>>>
"Give women you wed their due dowries graciously. But if they waive some of it willingly, then you may enjoy it freely with a clear conscience." This verse commands that the dowry (mahr) be given to the woman as a gift with a good heart, and permits the husband to take and enjoy any part she remits of her own free will. It follows the marriage rulings of the previous verses and completes the set of protections for women in marriage.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Give women you wed their due dowries"** commands paying the dowry that is her right. Ali bin Abi Talhah reported Ibn Abbas saying nihlah refers to the dowry. Muhammad bin Ishaq narrated from Az-Zuhri that Urwah said Aishah said nihlah means obligatory. Muqatil, Qatadah and Ibn Jurayj said it means obligatory, and Ibn Jurayj added "specified". Ibn Zayd said in Arabic nihlah refers to what is necessary. So a man may not marry unless he gives his wife her right, and no one after the Prophet may marry a woman except with the required dowry, nor by false promises about it.

**"Graciously"** means with a good heart, as one gives a gift with a good heart.

**"But if they waive some of it willingly"** permits the wife, of her own good pleasure, to remit part or all of the dowry.

**"Then you may enjoy it freely with a clear conscience"** permits the husband to take it and enjoy it without fear of any harm.
