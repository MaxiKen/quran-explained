<<<VERSE 7:34>>>
The previous verses exposed two crimes of the disbelievers: they treated the ḥalāl as ḥarām, and made the ḥarām ḥalāl for themselves on their own authority. Now comes a reminder of where such conduct ends: "For each community there is an appointed term. When their time arrives, they can neither delay it for a moment, nor could they advance it". Wrongdoers who are still enjoying Allah's blessings, and seem nowhere near punishment, should not forget how Allah deals with people.

### Phrase by phrase

**"For each community there is an appointed term."** Each ummah, meaning each generation and nation, has a fixed and limited span. One explanation says every people with a religion has a time appointed for its destruction.

**"When their time arrives"**: the term they were destined for, the time of their destruction.

**"they can neither delay it for a moment"**: they are not granted a fraction of a second beyond it.

**"nor could they advance it"**: nor are they destroyed before their time, even by a fraction of a second.

### Why do wrongdoers seem to escape?

Allah's customary way, out of His boundless mercy, is to give sinners a long rope, in the hope that they will somehow stop what they are doing. But the length of that rope and the time of that respite are fixed in Allah's perfect knowledge. When the time comes, it simply comes, neither late nor early, and the guilty are seized by punishment. Sometimes the punishment falls in this world. If it does not, it waits for them soon after death.

### Why mention "advance" at all?

Saying the term is neither late nor early is a turn of speech. It is like asking a shopkeeper, "What is your last price, more or less?" Plainly the question is about the lower price, and "more" is added only as a tag. In the same way, the real point here is that there will be no delay once the appointed time comes. Mentioning "before" alongside "late" simply brings the statement closer to ordinary ways of speaking.

### How is a nation's term decided?

In Allah's plan of creation, each individual has the chance to do well or badly in this world until his period of trial is over. For a community or nation, Allah's decision is not held back to such a limit. It is made on the basis of how that community responds once the truth has been put before it. When a community's time is up, it is sometimes destroyed by an extraordinary punishment. At other times it is punished by being removed from its position of honour and supremacy.

### In short

- Every nation has a term fixed in Allah's knowledge, and it cannot be put off or brought forward by a moment.
- Allah gives wrongdoers a long rope out of mercy, but when their time comes punishment falls, in this world or after death.
- A nation's fate turns on how it responds once the truth has been presented to it.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:35>>>
Allah again addresses all humanity, with good news and a warning: "O children of Adam! When messengers from among yourselves come to you reciting My revelations—whoever shuns evil and mends their ways, there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve". He sent them messengers who conveyed His signs, and the response to those messengers decides each person's end.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O children of Adam! When messengers from among yourselves come to you"**: messengers of your own kind, human beings like you, sent to you from Allah. In the Arabic, the word for "when" is immā, in which the nūn of the conditional particle in has merged with an extra mā.

**"reciting My revelations"**: reciting what Allah revealed to them of His scripture, and explaining His commands and prohibitions. Then follow them and what they bring.

**"whoever shuns evil"**: whoever has taqwā. That means leaving the forbidden and doing acts of obedience, following Allah's instructions and keeping away from what He has not allowed. Others explain it as believing in the scripture and the messenger, or as fearing to associate anything with Allah.

**"and mends their ways"**: sets right their deeds, and what lies between them and their Lord.

**"there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve"**: on the Day of Judgement they will have no fear of punishment. They will not grieve over missing Paradise, or over the pleasures of this world that passed them by.

### What decides a person's final home?

Whether a person's final home is Heaven or Hell is decided by how he responds once the truth has appeared before him. The truth must come with arguments whose soundness his own reason confirms. That is what it means for the message to be conveyed in the full sense. Allah sends messengers from among people themselves, so that this can happen.

### In short

- Allah sent humanity messengers of their own kind, reciting His revelations and explaining His commands and prohibitions.
- Those who respond with taqwā, leaving the forbidden and setting their deeds right, will have no fear or grief in the Hereafter.
- A person's final home depends on his response once the truth has reached him with clear arguments.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:36>>>
The other side of the choice follows: "But those who receive Our revelations with denial and arrogance will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever". Their hearts rejected Allah's signs, and they were too proud to live by them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But those who receive Our revelations with denial"**: those who deny Allah's signs, His scripture and His messenger, and reject them in their hearts.

**"and arrogance"**: too proud to believe in them, or to act on what their messengers brought.

**"will be the residents of the Fire."** They are its rightful companions.

**"They will be there forever"**: with no end to their stay, never dying there and never being taken out.

### Why does arrogance lead to the Fire?

If a person still refuses the truth after it has been fully conveyed to him, he does so out of egotism. His wish to keep his own superiority stops him from humbling himself before the truth. For such a person, before Allah, there is no fate but Hell.

### In short

- Those who deny Allah's revelations and are too proud to follow them will live in the Fire forever.
- Refusing the truth after it is clearly conveyed comes from ego, the wish to stay above it rather than bow to it.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:37>>>
The surah now turns to the worst of wrongdoers and their end: "Who does more wrong than those who fabricate lies against Allah or deny His revelations? They will receive what is destined for them, until Our messenger-angels arrive to take their souls, asking them, “Where are those ˹false gods˺ you used to invoke besides Allah?” They will cry, “They have failed us,” and they will confess against themselves that they were indeed disbelievers". No one is more unjust than such people, yet they still receive their share of this life until death comes for them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Who does more wrong than those who fabricate lies against Allah"**: none is more unjust, more insolent or bolder toward Allah, than one who invents a lie about Him. He may ascribe to Allah a partner or a child, or call Him deficient in some way. Or he may claim Allah said something He never said.

**"or deny His revelations?"** Or he rejects the signs Allah revealed, meaning the Qur'an and Muhammad ﷺ, the clear signs that guide to the right way of life.

**"They will receive what is destined for them"**: literally "their portion from the Book". Muhammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī said it means each person's deeds, provision and lifespan. Ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said much the same. Another explanation says it is their lot inscribed in the Preserved Tablet (al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ): provision, length of life and other matters. Others say it is their destined share of worldly pleasure. One explanation takes it as what they were promised in the Book, such as their faces turned black and their eyes blue.

Two other verses make the same point:

> "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Indeed, those who fabricate lies against Allah will never succeed.” ˹It is only˺ a brief enjoyment in this world, then to Us is their return, then We will make them taste the severe punishment for their disbelief" (10:69-70).

> "But whoever disbelieves, do not let their disbelief grieve you ˹O Prophet˺. To Us is their return, and We will inform them of all they did. Surely Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart. We allow them enjoyment for a little while, then ˹in time˺ We will force them into a harsh torment" (31:23-24).

**"until Our messenger-angels arrive to take their souls"**: the angel of death and his helpers. In effect: give them respite, O Muhammad, until then.

**"asking them, “Where are those ˹false gods˺ you used to invoke besides Allah?”"** When death comes to the idolaters, the angels take their souls toward the Fire and terrify them with a reproach. Where are the so-called partners you called on and worshipped in the world instead of Allah? Call them now, if they can help you, and let them save you from what you are suffering.

**"They will cry, “They have failed us,”"**: literally "they have gone astray from us". They have vanished and left us, and we no longer see them, so we hope for nothing from them. One explanation says the false gods are too busy with themselves to pay any attention to their worshippers. Others render it: the gods we worshipped are gone, and we do not know where.

**"and they will confess against themselves that they were indeed disbelievers"**: at death they admit and declare against themselves that they disbelieved in Allah and the messengers in the life of this world. But a confession made then will only stand as proof against them, and it will not help them.

### What does a denier lean on?

Whenever a person rejects the truth, he imagines he has something to back him. Some lean on money and influence, others on standing and popularity. He may feel sure that his affairs are so well arranged that turning down the truth cannot harm him. He may pride himself on a clever mind that can find fine words to present his own ideas as if they came straight from Allah.

All of these are grave mistakes. He has taken as props for his salvation the very things given to him as his test. On the Day of Judgement, when all these false props abandon him, he will easily see the truth. He rejected the truth out of plain ignorance, though he kept quoting "principles" to justify himself.

### In short

- No one does greater wrong than one who lies about Allah, by ascribing partners, faults or words to Him, or who rejects His revelations.
- Such people still receive their written share of provision and life, but only for a brief enjoyment.
- At death the angels mock them by asking where their false gods are, and they admit they were disbelievers, too late to help them.
- Wealth, status and clever words are a test, not a support, and they will desert the denier when he needs them.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:38>>>
The scene moves to the Hereafter, where those who joined partners with Allah, lied about Him and rejected His signs are sent into the Fire: "Allah will say, “Enter the Fire along with the ˹evil˺ groups of jinn and humans that preceded you.” Whenever a group enters Hell, it will curse the preceding one until they are all gathered inside, the followers will say about their leaders, “Our Lord! They have misled us, so multiply their torment in the Fire.” He will answer, “It has already been multiplied for all, but you do not know.”" Those who once stood together now turn on one another.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah will say, “Enter the Fire along with the ˹evil˺ groups of jinn and humans that preceded you.”"** On the Day of Resurrection, Allah will tell them to enter among the nations who were like them in conduct. These are the disbelieving nations of jinn and humans who went before them in disbelief and error. One explanation has the angels saying it to them. In the Arabic, "into the Fire" is tied to the verb "enter".

**"Whenever a group enters Hell, it will curse the preceding one"**: each nation, the followers of each religion, will curse its "sister" nation that went in before it, because it went astray on that nation's account. One explanation takes the group (ummah) here as the leaders who mislead, and its "sister" (ukht) as the misled public. Allah's Friend (al-Khalīl), Ibrāhīm (Abraham), warned his people of this: "But on the Day of Judgment you will disown and curse one another. Your home will be the Fire, and you will have no helper!" (29:25). Allah also says:

> "˹Consider the Day˺ when those who misled others will disown their followers—when they face the torment—and the bonds that united them will be cut off. The ˹misled˺ followers will cry, “If only we could have a second chance, we would disown them as they disowned us.” And so Allah will make them remorseful of their misdeeds. And they will never ˹be able to˺ leave the Fire" (2:166-167).

**"until they are all gathered inside"**: until every group, from the first to the last, has caught up with the others and they are gathered together in the Fire.

**"the followers will say about their leaders"**: literally "the last of them will say about the first of them". The followers, the lowly ones who entered last, will speak about the earlier nations, their leaders and masters whom they revered and followed. The earlier nations were greater criminals than those who followed them, which is why they entered the Fire first. So the followers will complain about them to Allah, since it was they who misled them from the right path.

**"“Our Lord! They have misled us, so multiply their torment in the Fire.”"** These led us astray from Your religion and from obeying You, and made the path of error look beautiful to us. So give them double what You inflict on us. Another verse records the same plea:

> "On the Day their faces are ˹constantly˺ flipped in the Fire, they will cry, “Oh! If only we had obeyed Allah and obeyed the Messenger!” And they will say, “Our Lord! We obeyed our leaders and elite, but they led us astray from the ˹Right˺ Way. Our Lord! Give them double ˹our˺ punishment, and condemn them tremendously.”" (33:66-68).

**"He will answer, “It has already been multiplied for all"**: each group, of you and of them, will have a double share. In effect: We have done what you asked, and repaid each according to their deeds. Allah says elsewhere, "For those who disbelieve and hinder ˹others˺ from the Way of Allah, We will add more punishment to their punishment" (16:88). He also says, "Yet they will certainly ˹be made to˺ carry their own burdens, as well as other burdens along with their own" (29:13). And: "as well as some of the burdens of those they mislead without knowledge" (16:25).

**"but you do not know.”"** You cannot perceive it, because of the severity of the torment. The verb may also be read yaʿlamūn, "they do not know", meaning that no party knows what the other is suffering.

### Why will each feel their own pain is the worst?

This verse shows a scene full of lessons. When misguiding leaders and their misled public are thrown into Hell together, every false veil will fall from their eyes, and everyone will be seen as he truly is. The followers will ask Allah to give their leaders double their own punishment. The answer will be that the leaders are already receiving double, but the followers do not know it. In Hell, whatever punishment anyone receives will feel so severe that he will think no one else suffers such agony. Each will feel himself the most afflicted of all.

### In short

- On the Day of Resurrection the deniers will enter the Fire with the earlier nations of jinn and humans who were like them.
- Each nation will curse the one before it, and followers will disown the leaders they once revered.
- The followers will ask double punishment for their leaders, but every group's torment is already multiplied.
- In Hell each person will feel his own suffering to be the worst, unaware of the others' pain.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:39>>>
The leaders answer their followers: "Then the leaders will say to their followers, “You were no better than us! So taste the torment for what you used to commit.”" Those who once flattered one another now try to push one another toward the worst fate.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then the leaders will say to their followers"**: literally "the first of them will say to the last of them", the nations who were followed speaking to those who followed them.

**"“You were no better than us!"** You have no advantage over us that would make our punishment double yours. As-Suddī explained it as: you were led astray just as we were led astray. You disbelieved as we disbelieved, and worshipped others besides Allah as we did. You did not disbelieve because of us, so you and we are equal in this plight. You have no claim on us that would earn you a lighter punishment. What you suffer is the result of your own actions, and you have no excuse for following falsehood.

**"So taste the torment for what you used to commit.”"** One explanation puts these words in Allah's mouth, and another in the leaders'. Taste the punishment, as we taste it, for the idolatry, disbelief and disobedience that you said and did in the life of this world.

### How do the leaders defend themselves?

In this world, such leaders and their followers posed as each other's well-wishers, ready to make sacrifices for one another. The leaders humoured every wish of the public, and the public made heroes of their leaders. In the Fire, the followers will turn on them: "Curse you! What a dreadful leadership you gave us, showing us false sights and then hurling us into this terrible torment!"

The leaders will reply: "You wanted a religion to your own taste. When you found one with us, you rushed to us. In that same age there were servants of Allah calling you to the path of real success. You heard their call and paid it no attention." They will add: "You are no better than us. We set up our leadership to serve our desires, and you followed us to serve yours. So in truth we stand in the same place, and here you must take the same punishment our deeds have earned."

The idolaters will argue in the same way at the gathering of the Resurrection, as another passage describes:

> "The arrogant will respond to the lowly, “Did we ever hinder you from guidance after it came to you? In fact, you were wicked ˹on your own˺.” The lowly will say to the arrogant, “No! It was your plotting by day and night—when you ordered us to disbelieve in Allah and to set up equals with Him.” They will ˹all˺ hide ˹their˺ remorse when they see the torment. And We will put shackles around the necks of the disbelievers. Will they be rewarded except for what they used to do" (34:32-33).

### What becomes of bonds built on self-interest?

In this world, self-interested leaders and self-interested followers are tied by strong bonds of friendship. Each side speaks well of the other and finds kind words for it. In the Hereafter each group will hate the other, and each will try to push the other toward the worst of fates.

### In short

- The misleading leaders will tell their followers that they were no better, for both chose disbelief.
- The followers cannot shift their guilt onto their leaders, since each followed falsehood by choice.
- Friendships built on shared desires and self-interest turn into hatred and blame in the Hereafter.
<<<END>>>
