<<<VERSE 7:34>>>
The two verses before this one described people who took what was ḥalāl as ḥarām and made what was ḥarām lawful for themselves. This verse states where that ends. "For each community there is an appointed term. When their time arrives, they can neither delay it for a moment, nor could they advance it". A term has been fixed for every people, and the fixed thing cannot be moved in either direction.

### Phrase by phrase

**"For each community"** means every generation and every nation, the whole ummah (community), and every people that has a religion.

**"there is an appointed term"** means a time already set, the time destined for their destruction as a people.

**"When their time arrives, they can neither delay it for a moment"** means that not one fraction of a second of reprieve is given after the appointed hour has come.

**"nor could they advance it"** means the opposite, that the destruction does not come before the appointed hour by so much as a fraction of a second either.

### What the fixity of the term is saying

Those who are committing these excesses are living in the sunshine of the blessings of Allah, and no punishment seems anywhere near them. This verse tells them not to be fooled by that. The practice of Allah is that He grants a long leash to sinners in His mercy, so that they might somehow stop what they are doing. The length of that leash, and the hour at which the respite ends, are fixed in His ultimate knowledge. When the time comes it comes, neither too late nor too soon, and the criminals are taken by the punishment. Sometimes the punishment comes down on them in this world, and if it does not visit them here it waits for them soon after their death.

The wording is worth a note. Naming both the delay and the advance is a way of speaking that makes the point land. It is rather like asking a shopkeeper whether his last price is more or less, where the real question is the lower price and the mention of the higher one is only a subordinate part of the sentence. The real point here is that there will be no delay after the appointed time. Saying "nor advance it" as well brings the matter closer to ordinary understanding.

### What the term is fixed against

For an individual, the plan of creation gives him room to do well or badly in this world until the period of his trial is over. For a community or a nation, the matter works differently. There is no such limit on the enforcement of God's decision, because the decision about a community is taken on the basis of how that community responded once the truth had been brought to it.

The end of a nation's period comes in one of two ways. One of them is a punishment sent down from outside the ordinary course of things, which destroys the people outright. The other is being thrown down from the rank of honour and supremacy they had held. The decision about Heaven or Hell as a person's final destination is taken on the basis of his response once the truth has appeared before him, supported by arguments whose reasonableness his own intellect can confirm. That means the message reaching him in the full sense. And then, if a man still refuses to accept the truth, he does so out of egotism, because the wish to keep his own superiority stops him from lowering himself in front of the truth.

### In short

- Every community has a term already fixed, and when the hour comes it cannot be moved forward or back.
- The respite granted to sinners is long, and the length of it is fixed in the knowledge of Allah, so no one should mistake patience for forgetfulness.
- The punishment may come in this world, and if it does not, it waits on the other side of death.
- A nation is judged by how it answered the truth once the truth had reached it, and the man who refuses after that refuses out of egotism.

<<<VERSE 7:35>>>
The address returns to all humankind. "O children of Adam! When messengers from among yourselves come to you reciting My revelations—whoever shuns evil and mends their ways, there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve". The verse names what a messenger does, and then names what the answer to him looks like.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O children of Adam!"** is the address to the whole of the human race.

**"When messengers from among yourselves come to you"** carries a point in the words "from among you". The messengers are human beings like the people they are sent to, and not a separate order of being.

**"reciting My revelations"** describes what the messenger does with what he brings. It means setting out the commands and the prohibitions, explaining them as he recites them.

**"whoever shuns evil"** means whoever believes in the Scripture and the messenger. The fear of Allah, and staying away from what has not been allowed, is what the word covers.

**"and mends their ways"** means that he puts right what lies between him and his Lord. It also means making right what he does.

**"there will be no fear for them"** takes away the fear of the torment.

**"nor will they grieve"** takes away the second grief, which is grief over the good things of Paradise they might miss. One gloss words it as grieving about the pleasures that passed them by in this world.

### The two halves of the answer

The verse puts two things together in the answer, and both are needed. One is taqwā, the fear of Allah and the keeping away from what He has not allowed. The other is setting things right. A person who keeps away from forbidden things but leaves his conduct unrepaired has done half of it, and so has the person who repairs his conduct without keeping away.

And what follows is stated positively. The believer is not merely let off. He is told that the fear is gone from him and the sadness too, so that the two things that weigh heaviest on a person are taken away at once.

### In short

- The verse addresses all humankind and says that the messengers come from among the people themselves.
- The answer that saves a person has two halves, keeping away from what is prohibited and putting right what lies between him and his Lord.
- What is promised is the removal of both fears at once, that of the torment and that of missing the good things that were prepared.

<<<VERSE 7:36>>>
The other side of the same matter. "But those who receive Our revelations with denial and arrogance will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever".

### Phrase by phrase

**"But those who receive Our revelations with denial"** means those who deny the Scripture and the messenger, and who deny the signs themselves.

**"and arrogance"** names the second fault, and it is a distinct one. Their hearts denied the signs, and then they were too proud to live by them. The word covers scorning them and refusing to believe in them.

**"will be the residents of the Fire"** is the sentence. They are its rightful owners, and the phrase carries the sense that the Fire is what belongs to them as their due.

**"They will be there forever"** means the dwelling has no end to it. There is no dying in it and no being removed from it.

### The two faults together

The verse is worth reading beside the one before it. There, the ones who were saved were saved by two things, keeping away from the forbidden and repairing their conduct. Here, the ones who are lost are lost by two things, denial in the heart and arrogance in the bearing. And the order matters. The denial comes first, and the arrogance is what keeps it in place, because a person who has decided in his heart that a thing is not true still needs something to hold him there.

### In short

- The verse names two faults together, denial of the signs and the arrogance that scorns them.
- The Fire is described as what belongs to such people by right, and the dwelling has no end.
- The two faults stand opposite the two parts of the answer in the verse before it.

<<<VERSE 7:37>>>
The scene begins to move. "Who does more wrong than those who fabricate lies against Allah or deny His revelations? They will receive what is destined for them, until Our messenger-angels arrive to take their souls, asking them, “Where are those ˹false gods˺ you used to invoke besides Allah?” They will cry, “They have failed us,” and they will confess against themselves that they were indeed disbelievers". The verse asks a question whose answer is meant to be nobody, and then it follows the wrongdoer from the provisions of this life to the moment his support is tested.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Who does more wrong"** asks who is more insolent and bolder in the face of Allah.

**"than those who fabricate lies against Allah"** names the first kind of wrongdoer. What he does is invent something about Allah. He gives Him a partner, or finds a defect in Him, or puts into His mouth a statement He never made.

**"or deny His revelations"** names the second kind. What is denied is Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, and the signs that guide to the right way of life. None of the two is more unjust than the other, because both of them come to the same thing, which is settling the truth by one's own decision.

**"They will receive what is destined for them"** names the share that reaches them from the Preserved Tablet, al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ, and from the Book of decrees. The share is their appointed lot. What is meant is each person's deeds, his allotted provision and his age. Another reading spells it out as what they were promised in the Book, such as their faces being turned black and their eyes blue. Muhammad bin Kaʿb al-Quraẓī said that the phrase refers to the appointed portion for each person. Ar-Rabīʿ bin Anas and ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said the same.

**"until Our messenger-angels arrive to take their souls"** marks the end of the portion. The messengers are the angel of death and his helpers, and they come to gather them and take their souls.

**"asking them, “Where are those ˹false gods˺ you used to invoke besides Allah?”"** is the question the angels put to them. They horrify them with it: where are the partners you used to call on in the world, worshipping them instead of Allah? Call them now, so that they may save you from what you are in.

**"They will cry, “They have failed us”"** is the answer. It means they are lost to us, and there is no benefit or help to be hoped for from them. Another reading has it that they are gone from us and not present before us, so that we cannot see them.

**"and they will confess against themselves that they were indeed disbelievers"** is the last line. They admit it and proclaim it against themselves, and the admission is made at the point where it can no longer help them.

### Two passages about the same people

What the Qur'an says of such people elsewhere is that they will never succeed. Their enjoyment is brief, and then their return is to Allah, and then He will make them taste the severest torment for the disbelief they held to (10:69-70). And again: if a person disbelieves, his disbelief should not grieve you, since the return of all of it is to Allah, and He will tell them what they did, and He knows what is in the breasts of people, and He let them enjoy themselves for a little while (31:23-24).

### The props that people trust

Whenever a person rejects the truth, he does it imagining that he has something behind him. He may be relying on his wealth and his power, or on the respect and the popularity he enjoys. He may feel sure that his personal arrangements are in such good order that refusing the truth will do him no harm at all, and that the whole matter is his own to settle and he has some say in it. He may take pride in a clever tongue, and use it to dress up his own ideas until they sound as though they came straight from God.

All of these are serious misreadings. What he has taken for supports on which he can depend for salvation are the very things that were given to him as his test. Once the Day of Resurrection comes and all these false props walk off and leave him, the lesson will be easy to read. He had been refusing the truth out of plain ignorance, and he had kept quoting principles the whole time to make the refusal look considered.

### In short

- No one is more unjust than the person who invents a lie about Allah or denies what He has revealed.
- Such people receive their appointed share of this world from the Book of decrees, which covers their deeds, their provision and their term.
- The angels come for their souls and put the question that tests the props they leaned on, and the props fail.
- Their confession is made when it can no longer help them, and the Qur'an states elsewhere that their enjoyment is brief and their end is the severest torment.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:38>>>
The answer comes from above. "Allah will say, “Enter the Fire along with the ˹evil˺ groups of jinn and humans that preceded you.” Whenever a group enters Hell, it will curse the preceding one until they are all gathered inside, the followers will say about their leaders, “Our Lord! They have misled us, so multiply their torment in the Fire.” He will answer, “It has already been multiplied for all, but you do not know.”" The verse sets out what the people of the Fire do to one another once they are inside it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Enter the Fire along with the ˹evil˺ groups of jinn and humans that preceded you."** is the command given to those who associated others with Allah, invented lies about Him and denied His signs. The groups they enter among are their likes and their similars in conduct, the disbelieving nations that passed away before them, from among jinn and human beings. One reading places the phrase about the Fire directly on the command to enter.

**"Whenever a group enters Hell, it will curse the preceding one"** describes what happens as they arrive. Each community curses its sister community, the one that came in before it, because it was through that one that it went astray.

**"until they are all gathered inside"** means they follow one another in until the first of them and the last of them are together in the Fire.

**"the followers will say about their leaders"** identifies the two parties. The last of them to arrive are the followers, and the first of them are the ones they revered and followed.

**"“Our Lord! They have misled us, so multiply their torment in the Fire.”"** is the request of the followers, and it is a complaint laid before Allah rather than an argument among themselves. Multiply their share of the torment, they say.

**"He will answer, “It has already been multiplied for all, but you do not know.”"** is the answer. The words carry the sense that what they asked for has been done, and that each has been recompensed according to his deeds. The reason they do not know it is the severity of the torment itself.

### What the leaders and the followers were to each other

In the world these two groups behaved towards one another as friends anxious for each other's good, ready to give up something of their own for the other. No wish of the ordinary people went unhonoured by the leaders, and the ordinary people in turn made heroes out of the leaders. When they are trapped in the fire, the artificial veils drop from their eyes and each is seen as he truly is.

Then the conversation runs as follows. The followers turn on the leaders and call down curses on them. What a leadership it was, they say, that showed them one spectacle and then pushed them into this. Their words are the words of people who have just seen what they were shown. The leaders answer that the followers wanted a religion to their own liking, and that finding such a religion with them, they rushed towards them. They add that during that very period there were servants of God calling them towards the path of real success, that their call was heard, and that no attention was paid to it.

The leaders then say to the followers that they are in no way better than they are. Leadership was taken up in pursuit of desire, and the following was done for the sake of desire as well. So both parties stand in the same position, and both are liable for the same punishment that has been laid down for their deeds. On this reading, the word for community means the leaders who misguide, and the word for the sister community means the misguided general public.

The followers then put their request to God, that the leaders misled them and should therefore be given double the punishment. The reply is that the leaders are already receiving a doubled punishment, and that the followers do not know it. The plain fact behind the exchange is that in the fire whatever punishment a person is given feels so severe that he thinks nobody else can be in such agony. Everyone there feels himself the worst afflicted.

So the bond that held the two groups together in the world is reversed. In the world, self-interested leaders and self-interested followers were linked in strong friendship, each group speaking well of the other and each finding words for the other's good. In the Hereafter the one group hates the other, and each tries to push the other towards the worst of fates.

### What the Qur'an says of the two parties elsewhere

The scene is set out in several other places. On the Day when their faces are turned over in the Fire, they will wish they had obeyed Allah and obeyed the Messenger. They will say that they obeyed their chiefs and their great ones, and that those misled them from the right way, and they will ask for a double torment for them (33:66-68). And there is the scene of the followers who see the torment and hear the ones they followed declaring that they have nothing to do with them. At that moment every tie that joined the two parties is severed. Those who followed will wish they had another chance to return, so that they could declare themselves innocent of them as they had declared themselves innocent of them, and Allah will show them their deeds as regrets, and they will never come out of the Fire (2:166-167).

A further passage gives the exchange between the arrogant and those they counted weak. The arrogant will ask whether they held the weak back from guidance after it came to them, and will say that it was the weak who were the criminals. Those who were counted weak will answer that it was the plotting of the arrogant by night and day, when they ordered them to disbelieve in Allah and set up rivals to Him. Then each party will conceal its own regret when it sees the torment, and iron collars will be put round the necks of those who disbelieved, who are recompensed for nothing except what they used to do (34:32-33).

The burdens are described as well. For those who disbelieved and held others back from the path of Allah, the torment is increased (16:88). Their own loads are carried by them, and loads beyond their own as well (29:13), and something also of the weight of those they led astray in ignorance (16:25).

And the example of Ibrāhīm (Abraham), al-Khalīl, is on record for the same truth. He said to his people that on the Day of Resurrection they would deny each other (29:25).

### In short

- The evil groups of jinn and human beings enter the Fire together, and each arriving group curses the group that came before it.
- The followers blame the leaders before Allah and ask for their torment to be multiplied, and the answer is that it has already been multiplied for all.
- The leaders answer that the followers wanted a religion to their own liking, and that both parties stand in the same position.
- In the world the two groups praised each other, and in the Fire each tries to push the other towards the worst of fates.
- Every one of them feels his own punishment to be the worst, and the Qur'an says elsewhere that the ties between them are cut and their regret is concealed.

<<<VERSE 7:39>>>
The leaders have the last word in the exchange. "Then the leaders will say to their followers, “You were no better than us! So taste the torment for what you used to commit.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then the leaders will say to their followers"** puts the first party in the place of the speaker. The followed now answer the followers.

**"You were no better than us!"** is the substance of the answer. The followers had asked for a double punishment on the leaders on the ground that they had been led astray, and the answer removes the ground. You did not disbelieve because of us, they say, since you and we stand together in this situation. Another reading of the words is that the followers have no advantage over the leaders that would earn them any lightening of the punishment. And another says simply that they disbelieved just as the leaders disbelieved, and worshipped others beside Allah just as the leaders did.

**"So taste the torment"** is the invitation to share what the leaders are tasting.

**"for what you used to commit"** gives the reason. The punishment is for what they used to earn, for the words and the deeds of idolatry they kept up through their life in this world.

### Why the answer closes the argument

As-Suddī put the sense of the answer in one line: you were led astray as we were led astray. That is the whole of it. The followers had presented themselves as people who were taken off the road by somebody else. The leaders point out that nobody took them anywhere. They went where they wanted to go, and they found leaders who told them what they wanted to hear.

There is no excuse at the end for having followed what was false, because the call of the truth had been there the whole time alongside the call of the leaders, and both were heard. So the followers taste the punishment exactly as the leaders taste it, because each of them earned it.

### In short

- The leaders answer the followers by telling them that the following was their own doing and that they stand in the same situation.
- The words are explained in more than one way, all of them arriving at the same sense, that the followers were led astray exactly as the leaders were.
- There is no excuse at the end for having followed what was false, since the call of the truth had been there to hear.
- Both parties taste the punishment, and each tastes it for what he earned.
