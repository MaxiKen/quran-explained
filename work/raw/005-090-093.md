<<<VERSE 5:90>>>
Four things are named together and given one description: "Intoxicants, gambling, idols, and drawing lots for decisions are all evil of Satan’s handiwork." The verse then commands avoidance and attaches a promise to it. Success depends on staying away.

The grouping matters. Intoxicants and gambling are placed beside idolatry and divination, which tells the reader how serious the first two are considered.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Intoxicants"** concerns khamr, what intoxicates and overcomes the mind. 'Umar bin Al-Khattab described it in a speech from the minbar of the Messenger ﷺ as extracted from five things: grapes, dates, honey, wheat and barley, and he defined it as whatever intoxicates the mind.

Al-Bukhari records Ibn 'Umar saying the prohibition came down when there were five kinds of intoxicant in Al-Madinah besides what was made from grapes. A report in Muslim gives the general rule that every intoxicant is khamr and every intoxicant is unlawful.

**"gambling"** is maysir. Ibn 'Umar says it means gambling, and Ad-Dahhak reports the same from Ibn 'Abbas, who added that people gambled during the period of ignorance until Islam came and Allah forbade that evil behaviour.

'Ali bin Abi Talib is reported as saying that chess is a kind of gambling. 'Ata', Mujahid and Tawus, or two of them, are reported as saying that every form of gambling counts, including children playing with a certain kind of nut.

**"idols"** concerns al-ansab. Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, 'Ata', Sa'id bin Jubayr and Al-Hasan describe them as altar stones near which sacrifices were offered in the period of ignorance. Another explanation gives the singular as nusub and describes it as anything set up to be worshipped, whether an idol, a stone or a tree.

**"and drawing lots for decisions"** concerns al-azlam, the arrows used for divination and lotteries. The practice is described below.

**"are all evil of Satan’s handiwork."** Rijs is explained as abomination or filth by 'Ali bin Abi Talhah from Ibn 'Abbas, as sin by Sa'id bin Jubayr, and as evil handiwork of Satan by Zayd bin Aslam. One gloss says they are unlawful because they are instigated by the devil's order and whispering, and another that Satan adorns them.

The word is explained in Arabic usage as something foul and disgusting to human taste. All four are repulsive to anyone with common sense and good taste.

**"So shun them so you may be successful"** commands avoidance of all these abominations and is a statement of encouragement. Success in this world and the next is made to depend on abstaining, and one gloss describes it as escaping Allah's anger and punishment and being safe in the Hereafter.

### How was the prohibition revealed in stages?

Ahmad records Abu Hurayrah describing three stages. When the Messenger ﷺ migrated to Al-Madinah, people were drinking and gambling and asked him about both. Allah revealed that in them is great sin and some benefit for people (2:219).

The people said that they had not been forbidden, since Allah had only mentioned sin along with benefit, and they went on drinking. Then one of the emigrants led his companions in the Maghrib prayer and confused the verses in his recitation.

A tougher statement followed, forbidding believers to approach prayer in a drunken state until they know what they are saying (4:43). People then drank before the time of prayer so that they would attend it sober.

The firmer verse came later, in this chapter, describing these things as an abomination of Satan's handiwork and commanding avoidance (5:90-91). The people said that they had abstained, O Lord.

### What is reported about 'Umar?

Ahmad records 'Umar bin Al-Khattab praying that Allah would make the ruling on khamr clear to them. The verse in Al-Baqarah was revealed and recited to him, and he repeated his request.

The verse in An-Nisa' then came, and the Prophet ﷺ had someone announce at prayer time that those in a drunken state must not approach prayer. 'Umar was again summoned and again asked for clarity.

When the verse in Al-Ma'idah was revealed and recited to him, and he reached the words asking whether they would then abstain (5:91), he said that they had abstained, twice. Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i record the hadith, and 'Ali bin Al-Madini and At-Tirmidhi graded it sahih, authentic.

### What happened on the day of prohibition?

Ahmad records Anas saying that he was pouring an alcoholic drink in Abu Talhah's house for Suhayl bin Bayda', 'Ubayy bin Ka'b, Abu 'Ubaydah bin Al-Jarrah and several of their friends. When they were nearly intoxicated, some Muslims came and asked whether they did not know that khamr had been prohibited.

They said they would wait and ask, then told Anas to spill what remained in his container. By Allah, they never drank it again, and their drink at that time was made from unripe and ordinary dates. This is also in the Two Sahihs.

Another narration from Anas describes him as the cupbearer in Abu Talhah's house when the prohibition came. A caller announced it, Abu Talhah sent him to see what it was about, and he found a person proclaiming that alcoholic drinks were forbidden. Abu Talhah ordered him to go out and spill the wine, and it flowed in the streets of Al-Madinah.

Ibn Jarir records Anas bin Malik pouring for Suhayl bin Bayda', Mu'adh bin Jabal, Abu Dujanah, Abu 'Ubaydah bin Al-Jarrah and Abu Talhah until they were intoxicated by a drink of mixed unripe and ordinary dates. He then heard someone announce that khamr had been made unlawful.

Nobody went in or out until they had spilled the drink and broken its barrels. Some performed ablution and others washed, and they put on perfume. They then went out to the mosque while the Messenger ﷺ was reciting this verse and the one after it.

Another account records Anas describing wine made from unripe dates, split unripe dates and dates, and the crier's announcement, after which wine ran in the streets of Madinah. Muslim narrates it through Abu'l-Rabi and Al-Bukhari through Abu Nu'man, both from Hammad.

### What does the curse attached to khamr include?

Ahmad records Ibn 'Umar reporting the Messenger ﷺ as cursing ten matters connected with khamr: the drink itself, its drinker, its server, its seller, its buyer, its brewer, the one who asks for it to be brewed, its carrier, the one it is carried to and the one who consumes its price. Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah record the hadith.

Ahmad also records Ibn 'Umar describing the Messenger ﷺ going out with him. He walked on the Prophet's ﷺ right until Abu Bakr came and he gave way, and 'Umar then walked on the left. The Prophet ﷺ found a hanging leather skin containing alcohol, asked for a knife and ordered it cut open.

He then repeated the curse on khamr and on those who drink, serve, sell, buy, carry, have it carried, brew, have it brewed and consume its price. The prohibition therefore surrounds the trade and not merely the act of drinking.

Ash-Shafi'i narrates from Malik from Nafi' that Ibn 'Umar reported the Messenger ﷺ as saying that whoever drinks khamr in this life and does not repent will be deprived of it in the Hereafter. Al-Bukhari and Muslim record it. Muslim also records that whoever drinks and dies addicted to it without repenting will not drink it in the Hereafter.

### What incident prompted the revelation according to other reports?

Al-Bayhaqi records Sa'd describing four verses revealed about khamr. A man from the Ansar prepared food and invited them. They drank before the prohibition and became intoxicated, then began boasting of their status.

The Ansar claimed to be better, and Quraysh among the emigrants claimed the same. One of the Ansar picked up a bone, hit Sa'd on the nose and opened a flesh wound there. The scar stayed with him from that day. The verses on intoxicants and gambling up to the question about abstaining were then revealed. Muslim records the hadith.

### What did 'Uthman say about drink?

'Abdur-Rahman bin Al-Harith bin Hisham reports hearing 'Uthman bin 'Affan tell people to avoid khamr because it is the mother of all sins. He described a worshipper of earlier times who lived secluded from people.

An evil woman loved him and sent her servant to say they wanted him as a witness. He went, and each door was locked behind him until he reached a beautiful woman with a young boy and some alcohol. She told him she had not invited him to witness anything but to have intercourse with her, kill the boy or drink the alcohol.

She gave him drink and he kept asking for more until, intoxicated, he had intercourse with her and killed the boy. 'Uthman concluded that khamr should be avoided because it is never combined with faith, and one of the two must expel the other.

Al-Bayhaqi records the statement and its chain is authentic. Abu Bakr bin Abi Ad-Dunya also recorded it in his book on the prohibition of intoxicants but related it from the Prophet ﷺ. Relating it from 'Uthman is described as more authentic.

### Is this prohibition found in earlier revelation?

'Abdullah bin 'Amr is reported as saying that this verse was also in the Torah. The wording given describes Allah sending truth to erase falsehood, joyful play, wind instruments, dances, cabarets using the lute and bagpipe, tambourine, guitar, harp and amatory poetry, and describes khamr as bitter to whoever tastes it.

Allah is then reported as vowing by His grace and power that whoever drinks it after the prohibition will be made thirsty on the Day of Resurrection, and whoever leaves it after the prohibition will taste it in the residence of grace. The chain is described as sahih.

### Why are idols named with drink and gambling?

The main purpose of the verse, evident from its background and the verse that follows, is that drinking and gambling are agencies of corruption and therefore unlawful. Altar stones and idols are added so that listeners understand drinking and gambling to be as serious a crime as idol worship.

Ibn Majah records the Prophet ﷺ saying that a drinker is like an idol worshipper, with some narrations naming the idols Al-Laat and Al-'Uzza. The comparison explains the grouping rather than equating the legal categories.

### How did the divining arrows work?

Azlam is the plural of zalam and names the arrows used for drawing lots in gambling, an Arab practice of the time. Ten people would join as partners and slaughter a camel.

Instead of dividing the meat into ten equal shares, they turned the division into a gamble. Of ten arrows they marked seven with signs indicating one, two or three shares and left three blank. All were put in a quiver and shaken, and each participant drew one.

Whoever drew a marked arrow received that number of shares, and whoever drew a blank went without. The practice resembles the many lottery style games and ventures familiar today. Taking lots this way is qimar, gambling, and the Qur'an makes it haram.

### Is any drawing of lots permitted?

One form is permitted and established from the Prophet ﷺ. Where everyone's rights are equal and the shares have already been divided equally, lots may decide who receives which share.

The example given is a house to be divided among four sharers in four portions of equal value. If mutual agreement cannot settle who takes which, lots may decide it. Another example is a thousand people with equal claims to only a hundred items.

The pagan custom of distributing meat by divining arrows was already prohibited earlier in this chapter, in the wording about determining shares through the arrows (5:3). Maysir and azlam are therefore the same in outcome, while ansab belongs with idolatry.

### What is the inward warning about drink?

A prophetic saying describes wine as the gatherer of sin and the mother of loathsome things. It is described as the root of what is loathsome, the key that opens great sins, the ground misdeeds grow from, the seed of crooked acts and the wellspring of strife.

It dries the spring from which obedience flows, dams the water of remembrance and swings the door open to negligence. The soul grows drunk from wine and is kept from prayer, and the heart grows drunk from heedlessness and is kept from intimate whispering.

The Pir of the Tariqah is quoted admonishing such people. Drunkards full of appetite and sleepers in negligence are told to feel shame before the Lord acquainted with the eyes' betrayal and with everything the chest hides (40:19).

The admonition asks where there is a whip like 'Umar's or a sword like 'Ali's to give these discourteous drunkards the punishment of the Shariah in the world of justice and to rouse the sleeping and heedless. It asks whether the wretch who drinks does not know that when he raises the cup the Throne and the Footstool tremble, and that a call comes from the exalted presence swearing by Allah's majesty to make them taste His chastisement in boiling water and the tree of Zaqqum.

### What does the inward reading make of gambling?

Arrow shuffling is gambling. The passage observes that in a gambling house someone who stakes everything and loses all is considered great and recognised as one who goes ahead.

The passage turns this into an allusion to how the chevaliers travel. They cast themselves onto the road of what is already decreed, content to be abased before any scrap of straw, to slip out of bondage to every colour and to reckon themselves as nothing at all.

As long as a person remains bound to colour, nature, the spheres and the stars, he is not in a position to speak like a Qalandar. The comparison contrasts staking everything on chance with surrendering everything to Allah.

### What damage do these practices do to a society?

Intoxicants, gambling, frequenting places where beings other than Allah are worshipped or offered sacrifice, and drawing lots that seek help from other than Allah are all base and satanic acts. They lead a person to mental and physical downfall.

Intoxicants destroy the refined feelings in a person. Arrows and lots rest on superficial thought or superstitious belief. Islam wants a person to be a rememberer and worshipper of Allah, wholly obedient to Him and His Prophet ﷺ, and seriousness is absolutely necessary for that. These misdeeds are the greatest destroyers of seriousness.

Islam aims to form a person who recognises realities and rises above materialism. Intoxicants make a person forgetful of realities, arrows and lots plunge him into the dark depths of superstition, and gambling drives him into materialism to a criminal degree.

Intoxicants produce an excessive loss of awareness of what is proper, and gambling produces excessive selfishness. These are described as the two root causes of disturbance and riot. Whoever loses moral awareness also loses respect for the dignity and property of others, becoming brazen in tyranny and injustice and in harassing people.

Gambling is the worst form of exploitation and selfishness, since it enables a person to strip many people and enrich himself. A drunkard has no awareness of other people's trouble or grief, and for a gambler other people are only objects of exploitation. A society containing such people is spoiled by clashes and enmity, and its members live in mutual mistrust and recrimination.

### How do these verses fit the surrounding passage?

The preceding verses forbade treating lawful things as unlawful. These forbid particular unlawful things. Together they set the rule for using a created world that serves human beings.

Everything in the universe is described as appointed to serve people, with one restriction: do not transgress the limits Allah has set while benefiting from what He created. Refraining from good and lawful things made halal is bad manners and ingratitude, while contravening what is prohibited is disobedience and rebellion.

A servant of Allah must use what was created for him according to the instruction of its Creator. That is 'abdiyyah, the state of being Allah's servant, which requires total submission and is the highest achievement a human being can hope for.

The spiritual ills are mentioned first. Indulgence in these activities is disgusting and is a trap of Satan. Whoever walks in does not walk out but becomes the victim of many further vices. The command to refrain follows from their nature, and success in both worlds depends on it.

### In short

- Intoxicants, gambling, altar stones and divining arrows are named together as filth and Satan's handiwork, and avoidance is tied to success.
- The prohibition came in stages, from the verse on sin and benefit through the restriction at prayer time to this decisive command.
- The reports of 'Umar, Anas, Sa'd and the spilled wine in the streets of Madinah show how the community responded at once.
- The curse covers the whole trade in drink, not only the drinker, and reports describe its appearance in earlier revelation.
- Divining arrows are gambling, while lots used to divide equal shares are permitted, and the inward and social damage of both is described at length.

<<<VERSE 5:91>>>
The verse explains what Satan is after: "Satan’s plan is to stir up hostility and hatred between you with intoxicants and gambling and to prevent you from remembering Allah and praying." Two harms are named, one between people and one between a person and Allah.

It closes with a question that functions as a command: "Will you not then abstain".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Satan’s plan is to stir up hostility and hatred between you"** describes his single aim. One gloss explains it as precipitating enmity and hatred because of the evil and discord that follow drinking and gambling. Another ties it to becoming drunk and to losing money.

**"with intoxicants and gambling"** names the two instruments. The description concerns the effects that follow their use rather than the substances alone.

**"and to prevent you from remembering Allah and praying."** adds the spiritual harm. Intoxicants are described as driving a person from obedience to Allah and as preventing the five daily prayers. Prayer is mentioned separately in order to magnify it, since it is itself part of the remembrance of Allah.

**"Will you not then abstain"** is a threat and a warning in the form of a question. One gloss renders its force as a direct command to desist, and another as asking whether the believers will leave these evil acts, adding that leaving them is plainly the right thing.

### What background produced this statement?

The verses were revealed against events in which actions taken under the influence of strong drink caused mutual anger and aggression. Such things do not happen by chance. A drunk person loses reason and acts compulsively in that manner.

The incident of Sa'd's nose being struck with a bone during a gathering of Ansar and emigrants, described with the preceding verse, is an example of the enmity named here. Boasting about status while intoxicated turned into violence.

Gambling works the same way. The loser takes his loss at the counter, but malice and anger toward his adversary continue to shape his behaviour afterward. Qatadah says it was the habit of some Arabs to wager all their possessions and even their families, lose them, and then live a wretched life.

### Why is the spiritual harm mentioned last?

The worldly bad effects are described first, and then the harm of being barred from remembrance and prayer. That order may hint that the real harm is spiritual, because the real life is the eternal life of the Hereafter.

A wise person therefore takes the betterment of that life as real betterment and its suffering as real suffering. This worldly life has a very limited span, and within those limits neither good nor bad stands out as something to be proud of or to pine over, since both conditions pass quickly.

### How is neglect of remembrance and prayer harmful in this world too?

Neglect of the remembrance of Allah and of prayer harms both worlds and both body and soul. Its harm to the Hereafter and the soul is obvious, since whoever skips prayer while forgetting Allah ruins his future life and deadens his soul.

A person heedless of Allah makes his own life a curse for himself. Once he turns from Allah and sets his heart on gaining wealth and standing as the goal of his life, those pursuits drag in so many entanglements that sorrow and worry follow. Once inside them he is deprived of the very peace and comfort he sought through possessions.

People of the material are therefore never at peace, whether they have what they work for or not. The contrast is the person whose heart is lit by thought of his Creator and by the bliss of his prayer. Wealth, power and office come to him with whatever peace they carry, and if they leave, nothing happens to his heart.

Gains are no happiness and losses no sorrow to him, since both are visiting guests of those who do right. Reflection on neglect of remembrance and prayer therefore shows its effects to be equally bad for this world and the next.

### How do the three descriptions divide the harm?

One reading distributes them. Calling these things filth and Satan's work may state their otherworldly and spiritual harm alone. Saying that Satan wants to plant enmity and malice may point to worldly and physical evils alone.

Saying that he bars people from the remembrance of Allah and from prayer may then describe the total ruin of life in both worlds. The three statements are not redundant on this reading, and each marks a different reach of the damage.

### Why is prayer named separately?

Prayer is part of the remembrance of Allah, so naming it separately needs an explanation. The answer given is that prayer holds its own importance and is the highest form of remembrance.

Singling it out also identifies the first casualty of intoxication. The stages of prohibition show the same concern, since the earlier verse forbade approaching prayer while drunk. What drink removes is not only knowledge but the capacity to stand before Allah.

### Why is the command put as a question?

After describing the physical and spiritual drawbacks of drinking and gambling, the way people are asked to stay away is disarming and wins the heart. The wording is would you then abstain.

The first two verses state the unlawfulness and strict prohibition as an article of divine law. To make that law easy to act upon, the verse asks rather than demands, leaving the answer with the hearer. The next verse then supplies the response required, which is obedience.

### In short

- Satan's aim is enmity between people and separation from remembrance and prayer, and both are named as effects of drink and gambling.
- The background includes violent incidents caused by intoxication and the ruin of those who wager everything.
- The spiritual harm is the real harm, yet neglect of remembrance and prayer damages this world as well.
- Prayer is singled out as the highest form of remembrance and as the first casualty of intoxication.
- The closing question is a command in gentle form, preparing the call to obedience that follows.

<<<VERSE 5:92>>>
The answer expected by the question is now stated: "Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and beware!" Obedience is commanded twice and caution is added. The verse then removes any idea that refusal damages the Messenger ﷺ, whose duty is only clear delivery.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Obey Allah and obey the Messenger"** repeats the command in respect of the prohibition just stated. One gloss specifies obeying them concerning intoxicating drink, and another describes it as fulfilling the instructions of the sacred law and avoiding its prohibitions.

**"and beware!"** adds caution. It is explained as warning against disobedient acts, against declaring these things lawful or against drinking them, and against breaking the law.

**"But if you turn away, then know that Our Messenger’s duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺ clearly"** answers the possibility of refusal. The Messenger's ﷺ duty is plain conveyance on behalf of Allah, in a language people understand, and your requital falls upon Allah.

### Why does refusal not diminish the Messenger ﷺ?

The command to obey Allah and His Messenger ﷺ is in a person's own interest and benefit. If someone does not listen to good counsel, his action brings no loss to Allah or to His Messenger ﷺ.

That Allah is far above gain and loss was obvious. Someone might nevertheless have thought that refusing to listen could affect or lessen the Messenger's ﷺ reward or station. The verse removes that doubt.

Even if nobody listened or obeyed, nothing would change in his integrity or standing, because he completed the mission entrusted to him. His mission was to convey Allah's commands openly and clearly, and he accomplished it. Whoever then chooses not to obey brings the loss on himself.

One account puts the same point in two directions. Coming to the right path is for a person's own good, and doing wrong is to his own detriment.

### What test of obedience follows immediately?

The command to obey and beware is followed by a practical test. During Hajj or 'Umrah, the pilgrimage to the Ka'bah, people enter ihram at appointed places called miqat before reaching the Ka'bah, and from then on hunting is forbidden to them.

On that journey pilgrims meet birds and animals close enough to be taken easily with arrows or spears, yet hunting is declared unlawful, whether done personally or through others. Tradition places this revelation in the journey of Hudaybiyyah, when the Muslims were in ihram intending 'Umrah and wanted to hunt as they normally would. They stopped at once on receiving the order.

That material belongs to the verses which state the ruling and its compensation, and it is not developed here. What matters for this verse is the shape of the test. Obedience is measured at the moment when a permitted thing is close at hand and a command forbids it, and the same discipline is required of an ordinary person in daily life.

### In short

- Obedience to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ is commanded twice, with caution added against disobedience and against declaring the forbidden lawful.
- Refusal harms the one who refuses, not the Messenger ﷺ, whose duty is only clear delivery.
- Reckoning belongs to Allah, and the right path benefits whoever takes it.
- The verses that follow present an immediate test of that obedience in the state of ihram, where game is close and hunting is forbidden.

<<<VERSE 5:93>>>
A question raised by the prohibition is answered: "There is no blame on those who believe and do good for what they had consumed before ˹the prohibition˺". People asked about companions who had died while drinking and gambling. The verse removes the blame, and it does so with a condition repeated three times.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There is no blame on those who believe and do good"** concerns those who believe in Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an and do good between themselves and their Lord. The verse was revealed about a group from the emigrants and the helpers who asked what would happen to those who died drinking before it was made unlawful.

**"for what they had consumed before ˹the prohibition˺"** covers what they drank in the past, whether those people are now dead or still living. One account adds gambling to what was consumed and indulged in before the prohibition.

**"as long as they fear Allah, have faith, and do what is good"** states the first condition. Fear is explained as warding off disbelief, idolatry and indecencies, and good deeds as what is done between a person and his Lord.

**"then they believe and act virtuously"** adds a second stage. It is explained as warding off any attempt to make intoxicants lawful after they were made unlawful, and as believing in their unlawfulness.

**"then become fully mindful ˹of Allah˺ and do righteous deeds."** adds a third. It is explained as warding off drinking and as leaving it altogether.

**"For Allah loves the good-doers"** gives the reward. Allah loves those who avoid drinking intoxicants, and the love is explained as meaning that He will reward them. One account describes the final stage as increasing in awareness of Allah until a person worships Him as though he sees Him, and Allah loves such people because they know He is always watching.

### What question produced this verse?

Ahmad records Abu Hurayrah describing the three stages of prohibition and then the question that followed. Some people said that others had died in Allah's cause and others in their beds, yet they used to drink alcohol and gamble, which Allah had now made a filth of Satan's work.

Allah then revealed this verse about those who believe and do righteous deeds having no sin for what they ate. The Prophet ﷺ said that had these things been made impermissible for them, they would have abandoned them as the questioners had abandoned them.

A report from Abu Hurairah in the Musnad of Ahmad, quoted in Al-Lubab, gives the same question in another form. When the verse forbidding drink and gambling was revealed, people asked about many who had drunk wine and lived on gambling earnings and then died before the prohibition, and what would happen to them now that these were known to be unlawful.

Al-Bara' ibn Azib reports that a group of the Prophet's ﷺ Companions died while still drinking wine. When intoxicants were made unlawful, some asked what would happen to their companions, and this verse was revealed.

Anas's account connects it with the day of prohibition itself. After the wine was spilled in the streets of Madinah, someone said that such and such people had been killed with wine in their bellies, and Allah revealed this verse in answer.

Ahmad bin Hanbal also records Ibn 'Abbas on the prohibition, and 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud reports the Prophet ﷺ saying, when this verse was revealed, that he had been told the addressees were among those it describes. Muslim, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i collect that report.

### Why is the condition repeated three times?

Scholars describe several degrees of taqwa, guarding oneself from what may harm the purity of faith, and the degrees of iman, faith, likewise differ in personal strength and weakness.

Both experience and the authority of the Shariah show that the further a person advances in dhikr, remembrance, in fikr, concern for this life and the next, in good deeds and in jihad in Allah's way, the more his heart fills with the fear of Allah and awe of His greatness and majesty, and the firmer and stronger his faith becomes.

There are therefore degrees of ascent along the paths to Allah, which spiritual masters describe as journeying toward Him. The deliberate repetition of taqwa and faith in this verse points in that direction.

What is identified at the end is the seeker's final station, ihsan, righteousness at its best, together with its reward, which is the love of Allah. The three stages are not three separate conditions but one condition deepening.

### Does the verse excuse the past without requiring anything now?

No. The removal of blame is attached to the continued state described. Whoever fears Allah, believes and does good is not blamed for what was consumed before the prohibition, and the verse goes on to require fear, belief and good action again and again.

That is why the explanations attach the ruling to those still living who drank before the prohibition. Their past is not counted against them, and their present is held to the standard.

### How does this verse fit between the two around it?

The verse before the prohibition of drink forbade making good things unlawful. Here the past use of things later forbidden is excused. The verse that follows states that Allah will test people with what is within reach of their hands and spears, and that it is Allah who has absolute power to declare particular things unlawful in particular circumstances.

Together they mark the difference between a person declaring the lawful unlawful and Allah doing so. The believer's task is not to invent restrictions but to obey the restrictions revealed, and to trust that what was done before a ruling arrived is not held against him.

### In short

- Believers who drank or gambled before the prohibition are not blamed, whether they have died or are still living.
- The question came from Companions concerned about those who had died, and several reports describe it.
- The condition of fear, faith and good action is repeated three times, marking degrees of ascent rather than three separate requirements.
- The final station is ihsan, and its reward is the love of Allah.
- The verse distinguishes Allah's power to forbid from any human power to declare the lawful unlawful.
