<<<VERSE 5:90>>>
"O believers! Intoxicants, gambling, idols, and drawing lots for decisions are all evil of Satan’s handiwork. So shun them so you may be successful"

This verse forbids intoxicants, gambling, the idols and the drawing of lots by arrows, and calls them all an evil of Satan's handiwork. It ends by urging the believers to shun them so that they may be successful.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Intoxicants, gambling, idols, and drawing lots for decisions."** The material explains the four things named in the verse. The material says that the word for the altar stones, ansab, refers to the stones and idols set up for worship. The word azlam refers to the arrows used for lots, and Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ata' and Sa'id bin Jubayr are reported to have said that the arrows were for drawing lots. The material says that the word for gambling, maysir, covers the games of chance, and Ali ibn Abi Talib and Ibn Umar are reported to have said that even chess belongs to it.

**"Are all evil of Satan's handiwork."** The material says that the word rijs means filth, something foul to the human taste. Sa'id bin Jubayr said it means sin, and Zayd bin Aslam said it means an evil work of Satan. The verse places these four things in one category, and the material notes that the same words appear in 2:219.

**"So shun them so you may be successful."** The material says that the call to avoid these things is a promise of success in this world and the next. Ibn Majah reports the saying of the Prophet ﷺ that the one who drinks is like the one who worships idols.

### The stages of the prohibition

The material gives the history of the prohibition in several stages. When the Prophet ﷺ migrated to Madinah, the people asked about wine and gambling, and the answer in 2:219 said that there is great sin and some benefit in them. Some drank, and then one of the emigrants led the Maghrib prayer while drunk and mixed up the recitation. The verse in 4:43 then forbade approaching prayer while drunk. Later people drank before the prayer times, and then came this verse. Abu Hurayrah and Ahmad ibn Hanbal recorded these stages. Umar bin al-Khattab asked for a clear ruling several times, and when this verse was recited to him he said, "We have abstained, we have abstained." Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i recorded this, and At-Tirmidhi graded it sahih.

### The narrations on wine

The material gives a number of reports that were recorded by Muslim and Al-Bukhari, and by Ahmad, Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah. Umar stood on the minbar of the Prophet ﷺ in Madinah and said that the prohibition was revealed, and that wine is made from five things: grapes, dates, honey, wheat and barley. Anas reported that when the call was heard, Abu Talhah told him to spill the wine, and it flowed through the streets of Madinah. Ahmad reported that the Prophet ﷺ cursed wine itself, and everyone involved in it, from the one who drinks it to the one who takes its price. Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas reported that a quarrel at a meal led to a wound on his nose.

The material also reports a saying from Abdullah bin Amr, who said that the wine prohibition was in the Tawrah. Uthman bin Affan said that wine is the mother of all sins and cannot be found together with faith, since one of them drives out the other from the heart. The report is recorded by Al-Bayhaqi.

### The inner harm of wine

The material says that wine is the root of many evils, and that it dries the spring of obedience and turns the heart away from remembrance. It says that gambling leads a man to lose everything he owns, including his family, and that it fosters malice and anger. The material says that these things are more dangerous to the soul than to the body, since the soul that is drunk with them is held back from the prayer.

### In short

- Intoxicants, gambling, the altar stones and the arrows of lots are an evil of Satan's handiwork.
- The prohibition came in stages, with the believers asking for a clear ruling until this verse came.
- The verse calls on the believers to shun these things, so that they may be successful.

<<<VERSE 5:91>>>
"Satan's plan is to stir up hostility and hatred between you with intoxicants and gambling and to prevent you from remembering Allah and praying. Will you not then abstain"

This verse says that Satan wants to create hostility and hatred between the believers through intoxicants and gambling, and to turn them from the remembrance of Allah and from prayer. It ends by asking whether they will abstain.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Satan's plan is to stir up hostility and hatred between you with intoxicants and gambling."** The material says the harm of these things is visible in this life. Drink makes a man lose his reason, and anger and aggression follow. Gambling leaves the loser with malice towards his opponent. Qatadah is reported to have said that some Arabs would wager all they had, even their families, and then live in misery.

**"And to prevent you from remembering Allah and praying."** The material says that the spiritual harm is the greater harm, since the life of the Hereafter is the real life. A wise person takes the good and bad of that life as the true good and bad. Drinking and gambling turn the heart from remembrance and from the prayer.

**"Will you not then abstain?"** The material says that this question is a threat and a warning. It asks the believers to put an end to these things. The verse is a call to abstain, with the outcome of success.

### In short

- Satan uses wine and gambling to create hostility among the believers.
- The spiritual harm is that they turn the heart from remembrance and prayer.
- The verse ends with a question that calls for abstinence.

<<<VERSE 5:92>>>
"Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and beware! But if you turn away, then know that Our Messenger's duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺ clearly"

This verse calls the believers to obey Allah and the Messenger, and to take care. If they turn away, the Messenger's duty is only to deliver the message clearly.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and beware!"** The material says that obedience to the Messenger is part of obedience to Allah, and that the two are tied together. The warning to beware is a call to fear the consequences of disobedience.

**"But if you turn away, then know that Our Messenger's duty is only to deliver the message clearly."** The material says that the Messenger's duty is only to convey, and that the responsibility for turning away rests with those who turn away. The material cites 5:3 for a related prohibition on the arrows, and it also says that the message is given in a clear form.

### The Messenger's duty

The material says that the verse is a comfort to the Prophet ﷺ, since his role is to deliver the message, and the response belongs to the listeners. It also says that the obedience to the Prophet ﷺ is a sign of the true faith, and that the believers should not turn away from his commands.

### In short

- Obeying Allah includes obeying the Messenger, and the believers are told to beware.
- The Messenger's duty is to deliver the message clearly.
- Turning away is the choice of the listeners, and the responsibility is theirs.

<<<VERSE 5:93>>>
"There is no blame on those who believe and do good for what they had consumed before ˹the prohibition˺, as long as they fear Allah, have faith, and do what is good; then they believe and act virtuously, then become fully mindful ˹of Allah˺ and do righteous deeds. For Allah loves the good-doers"

This verse says that there is no blame on those who believed and did good for what they had drunk before the prohibition, as long as they feared Allah, believed, and did good. It repeats these three stages and ends by saying that Allah loves the good-doers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There is no blame on those who believe and do good for what they had consumed before the prohibition."** The material explains that some Companions had died while drinking wine before the prohibition, and others asked what would happen to them. The verse answers that there is no blame for what they consumed before the prohibition. The material reports the account of Anas, who said that wine was spilled in the streets of Madinah, and that the question was raised about those who had died, and the verse came. It also reports from al-Bara ibn Azib that some Companions died while drinking, and the verse answered the question about them.

**"As long as they fear Allah, have faith, and do what is good."** The material says the condition is taqwa, faith and good deeds. These are the conditions for being excused for the past.

**"Then they believe and act virtuously, then become fully mindful and do righteous deeds."** The material says that the repetition of the three stages shows that the believer must keep on with taqwa, faith and good deeds. It is a call to steadfastness.

**"For Allah loves the good-doers."** The material says that the good-doers are those who keep to taqwa and do good, and that Allah loves them.

### The lesson of the verse

The material explains that the verse removes the fear of those who had consumed wine before the prohibition, and gives them a clear path. It also makes plain that the prohibition does not depend on the past of the believer, since the past is forgiven for those who keep their faith and good deeds in the time that follows. The material cites 5:95 in the context of the hunting prohibition that follows, and it is a reminder that the legal rules of the Qur'an are set in stages.

### In short

- There is no blame for what the believers drank before the prohibition, as long as they fear Allah, believe, and do good.
- The verse answers those who asked about their companions who died before the prohibition.
- Allah loves the good-doers, and the believer must keep to taqwa and good deeds.
