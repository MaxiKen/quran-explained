The verse of the two purities: "O believers! Do not approach prayer while intoxicated until you are aware of what you say, nor in a state of ˹full˺ impurity—unless you merely pass through ˹the mosque˺—until you have bathed. But if you are ill, on a journey, or have relieved yourselves, or been intimate with your wives and cannot find water, then purify yourselves with clean earth, wiping your faces and hands. And Allah is Ever-Pardoning, All-Forgiving". Before wine was forbidden outright, the prayer was fenced off from it, and the fence is where the verse begins.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not approach prayer while intoxicated until you are aware of what you say"** forbids the drunk from approaching the ṣalāh, for in that state one does not know the meaning of what one says. The drunk makes obvious mistakes in the recitation and cannot be humble in the prayer. It is the best description of intoxication itself: not knowing what one is saying.

**"nor in a state of ˹full˺ impurity—unless you merely pass through ˹the mosque˺—until you have bathed"** forbids the junūb, the one in major impurity from intercourse or ejaculation, from entering the masjid except as a passer-by, until the full bath, the ghusl, is taken. Ibn ʿAbbās, in the record of Ibn Abī Ḥātim, read it as: do not enter the masjid when junub unless just passing by, in which case pass through without sitting down. The same is reported from a wide company: ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd, Anas, Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib, Mujāhid, Ibrāhīm an-Nakhaʿī, Ibn Shihāb, Qatādah and many others.

**"But if you are ill, on a journey, or have relieved yourselves, or been intimate with your wives and cannot find water"** opens the concession. The illness is one feared to be aggravated by water. The journey is known regardless of length. Relieving oneself is the minor impurity, which breaks the wudu, and the touching, "Lāmastum" women, was recited Lamastum and Lāmastum both, referring to intercourse in the reading of Ibn ʿAbbās, with ʿAlī, Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, Mujāhid, Ṭāwūs, al-Ḥasan, ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ash-Shaʿbī, Qatādah and Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān saying similarly, as the Qur'an uses the word where it halves the dower of the untouched divorcee (2:237) and cancels her ʿiddah entirely (33:49). Another reading, that of Ibn ʿUmar and Imam al-Shāfī, takes the touching as the touch of the hand, extending to other parts of the skin.

**"then purify yourselves with clean earth, wiping your faces and hands"** is the tayammum, a substitute for the ablution rather than a cleaning of the same parts, so the wiping of face and hands with one strike on the earth is sufficient, as the consensus concurs. Linguistically tayammum is to intend, as the Arabs say, may Allah direct His care at you, and clean earth means dust.

**"And Allah is Ever-Pardoning, All-Forgiving"** makes the concession itself an act of pardon, allowing tayammum and the prayer after it when there is no water, to make things easy.

### The slow closing of the wine

This verse was revealed before alcohol was completely prohibited. Drinking was an old addiction in Arabia, a national habit and pastime involving everyone but a few naturally right-minded souls who felt a God-given distaste for it all along. One such was the Prophet ﷺ himself, who never touched liquor even before prophethood. Habit, any habit, is hard to leave, and none grips harder than drinking, to which farewell means a welcome to death. Had it been declared unlawful all at once, obedience would have been extremely hard. So the shift came gradually: first warnings of its evil effects, then this verse, which only kept the drunk away from the ṣalāh at its times, teaching that ṣalāh is fard and must rise to be offered as due. Many blessed souls made a spot decision to abandon the habit for good, and others began thinking seriously about its evils. Finally the verse of Sūrat al-Māʾidah came with the absolute command: "Intoxicants, gambling, al-Anṣāb and al-Azlam are an abomination of Shayṭān's handiwork, so avoid that in order that you may be successful" (5:90), to "So, will you not then abstain?" (5:91), and ʿUmar said: "We abstain, we abstain."

The stages show in ʿUmar himself. When the verse of the question in Sūrat al-Baqarah came, "They ask you about alcoholic drink and gambling" (2:219), the Messenger of Allah ﷺ recited it to ʿUmar, who said: "O Allah, explain the ruling about al-Khamr for us in a plain manner." When this verse came down, the Prophet ﷺ recited it to him, and he still said the same. After that they would not drink close to the time of prayer. In another narration, when this verse was revealed, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would have someone proclaim at prayer time: "Let not any drunk approach the prayer", in the wording collected by Abu Dāwūd.

### The two occasions

Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded the reports of the incident. Saʿd said: "Four verses were revealed concerning me. A man from the Anṣār once made some food and invited Muhājir and Anṣār men to it, and we ate and drank until we became intoxicated. We then boasted about our status." A man then took a camel's bone and injured Saʿd's nose, scarred ever since, for this was before al-Khamr was prohibited. Muslim recorded this hadith, and the collectors of the Sunan with the exception of Ibn Mājah.

The second occasion belongs to ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib. ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf made some food and served alcohol with it, and when the company grew intoxicated and the time for prayer came, someone was asked to lead. The leader recited Sūrat al-Kāfirūn (109) wrongly, saying "I do not worship that which you worship, but we worship that which you worship", the error of a tongue the drink had loosened. Then the verse came down. At-Tirmidhī collected this narration and called it ḥasan, ṣaḥīḥ. The same lesson reaches even the sleeper: if one feels sleepy while praying, let him sleep a while so that he knows the meaning of what he says, for he might want to ask forgiveness and instead curse himself, in the report of Aḥmad, al-Bukhārī and an-Nasāʾī from Anas, and of Qurṭubī's ruling that the prayer is not correct when sleepiness takes away control of speech.

### The mosque and the passer-by

The prohibition of the masjid for the junūb carried an exception with a history. Ibn Jarīr recorded that Yazīd ibn Abī Ḥabīb commented that some men of the Anṣār, whose doors literally opened into the masjid, would be junub without water, and their only way to reach water was to pass through the masjid, so Allah sent down the exception. Supporting this stands al-Bukhārī's report of the Messenger's ﷺ command: "Close all the small doors in this masjid, except that of Abū Bakr." It was given at the end of his life, knowing that Abū Bakr would be the khalīfah after him and would need to enter the masjid often to manage the Muslims' affairs. Some compilers of the Sunan recorded that only ʿAlī's door should remain open, but that is an error, and what is in the Ṣaḥīḥ is what is correct. In his Ṣaḥīḥ, Muslim recorded that ʿĀʾishah said: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to me, 'Bring me the garment from the masjid.' I said, 'I am having my period.' He said, 'Your period is not in your hand.'" Muslim. He also collected a similar narration from Abū Hurayrah, indicating that a woman may pass through the masjid during menses or post-natal bleeding, and Allah knows best.

The exception itself divides the jurists. According to most commentators, including the Ḥanafī jurists, "traversing a way" refers to travel when water is unavailable, so the junūb cannot approach ṣalāh without the bath, unless he is on a journey without water, in which case he prays after tayammum. Imam al-Shāfī interprets it in a totally different manner: going near the ṣalāh means entering the mosque, prohibited for the junūb except as a passer-by heading elsewhere. The Ḥanafī jurists do not accept this, holding that the verse has no relevance to entering the mosque, as the background of its revelation shows, and that the prohibition of entering the mosque in impurity rests on other grounds.

### The dry ablution

In the two Ṣaḥīḥs it is recorded that ʿImrān ibn Ḥuṣayn said that Allah's Messenger saw a person sitting away from the people, not praying with them, and asked him what prevented him. He replied that he was junub and there was no water. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Perform tayammum with clean earth, and that will be sufficient for you." Muslim recorded from Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman the three preferences: the lines arranged in rows like the rows of the angels, the whole earth made a masjid, and its dust made clean when there is no water. Had any other substance replaced dust for tayammum, the Prophet ﷺ would have mentioned it. Aḥmad and the collectors of the Sunan, except Ibn Mājah, recorded from Abū Dharr: "Clean earth is pure for the Muslim, even if he does not find water for ten years. When he finds water, let him use it for his skin, for this is better", which at-Tirmidhī called ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ.

The wiping was shown by the Prophet's ﷺ own hand. Aḥmad recorded that ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn Abzā said that a man came to ʿUmar saying he was junub without water, and ʿUmar told him not to pray. ʿAmmār said: "Do you not remember, O Leader of the Faithful, when you and I were on a military expedition and became junub and found no water? You did not pray, but I rolled myself in the sand and prayed. When we told the Prophet ﷺ, he said to me, 'This would have been sufficient for you', and he stroked his hand on the earth once, blew into it and wiped his face and hands." The Muslim ummah, above all other nations, was favoured with this allowance, and the shariah of Islam took the dry ablution as its symbol of purification where water fails. In the verses before this, the stress lay on acting with taqwā in mutual dealings, and the rules of worship here instil the fear of Allah that keeps those dealings right. In the two Ṣaḥīḥs, Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh narrated the five things given to no prophet before: victory by fright covering a month's distance, the earth as a place of prayer and an object of purification, the spoils made lawful, the right of intercession, and the sending to all mankind rather than one nation.

The occasion of the concession itself is ʿĀʾishah's. On one of the Messenger's ﷺ journeys, at al-Bayḍāʾ or Dhāt al-Jaysh, ʿĀʾishah's necklace was broken and lost. He stayed to search for it with all the people, in a place with no water. The people complained to Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq, who found his daughter detaining the Messenger and the people where there was no water, and admonished her and struck her flank with his hand while the Prophet ﷺ slept with his head on her thigh, which alone kept her from moving. At dawn the Prophet ﷺ rose with no water, and Allah revealed the verses of tayammum, and they all performed it. Usayd ibn Ḥuḍayr said: "O the family of Abū Bakr, this is not the first blessing of yours." Then her camel was moved and the necklace was found beneath it.

Prayer, the whole verse insists, is too sacred to be performed in a deficient manner. It does not mean the mere repetition of words and motions with accuracy, for the mind must submit along with the body, and consciousness must inhere in the prayer. The purpose of ablution is to create the spirit of purity, and tayammum maintains that spirit when the usual means fail. Islamic law has granted exceptional relaxations under exceptional conditions, the three named being sickness, travel and the want of water.

### The inner reading

Drunkenness comes in many kinds, and its drunkards differ. The grape has its wine, and heedlessness has its own. This world's love intoxicates one sort, and the soul's frivolity and self-love another. Hardest of all is the last. Self-love grounds idol-worship, seeds estrangement, curtains ill fortune and roots every darkness. A person martyred a hundred times a day in God's path is still an idol-worshipper if he sees himself in the midst. The wine-drunk who fears and shakes in dread of punishment may end in the fire, if He does not forgive him, and He may indeed forgive him, for He says: "Surely God forgives the sins altogether" (39:53). But the one drunk with the soul's arrogance, pride and self-worship, is in peril: his basis is loss, his deeds damage him.

As for the greater ablution, if the religion were based on reasoning, it would be incumbent after urination rather than after ejaculation, for urine is impure and semen pure. That the rule runs the other way teaches a lesson: religion stands on transmission, not on rational derivation, on the Book, not on reasoning, and on servanthood, not on self-exertion. The origin of the major ablution runs back to Adam. When he came from Paradise into this world and fell into companionship with Eve, Gabriel came and said: "O Adam, make a full ablution, for God commands you to do so." Adam obeyed and asked the reward, and Gabriel answered that for every hair on his body a year's reward would be written, and for every drop of water passing over his body God would create an angel to obey and worship until the Day of Resurrection, giving its reward to him, and that this was for him, the faithful, and his children until the resurrection. Thus the ablution for defilement was made mandatory in all the shariahs of the prophets, from Adam's era to the Master of the World's. One of the imams even said that the Trust Adam lifted up, concerning which God said "And man carried it" (33:72), was the trust of this ablution.

The closing names are the tenderest part of the verse. God passes over sins, curtains the defects of those who apologize, and conceals the offenses of the sighers. Before the commands and prohibitions came, He had taken away whatever was done up to that day. No one's sin can overcome His solicitude, and He says: if you straighten your aim, I am there for you in the road. Wherever there is someone with ruined life and destitute days, I buy him. Wherever there is a poor man wounded by offenses, helpless before an adversary, I am his Patron. Wherever there is someone weeping in shame, burnt from having lost his heart, I am the happiness of his spirit. Be wherever you will in the land, and you will be near My remembrance.

### In short

- Wine was closed slowly: first the prayer was fenced from it, then the total prohibition of Sūrat al-Māʾidah answered ʿUmar's repeated plea.
- The two occasions were Saʿd's wounded nose and ʿAlī's misrecited Sūrat al-Kāfirūn, both at tables where drink was still lawful.
- The junūb keeps out of the masjid, passing through only, and the doors of the Anṣār that opened into it explain the exception.
- Sickness, travel and the want of water open the tayammum, one strike of clean earth for face and hands, a favour given to this ummah alone, and revealed over ʿĀʾishah's lost necklace.
- Prayer demands the mind's presence, and even sleep sends the worshipper back to bed.
- Read inwardly, the worst drunkenness is self-love, the ablution's logic is transmitted not reasoned, and the verse ends with the solicitude of the Ever-Pardoning, All-Forgiving.
