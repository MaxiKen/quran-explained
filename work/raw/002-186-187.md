Between the injunctions of fasting in the three previous verses and the long verse on fasting and iʿtikāf that follows, this brief verse is placed to persuade Allah's servants to obey His commands by recognising how, in His special grace, He hears and answers their prayers. "When My servants ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about Me: I am truly near. I respond to one's prayer when they call upon Me. So let them respond ˹with obedience˺ to Me and believe in Me, perhaps they will be guided ˹to the Right Way˺". Fasting is undoubtedly a difficult obligation despite its many concessions, and to make the trial bearable Allah mentions His special grace: I am near My servants, and when they pray I answer and take care of their needs. Under such conditions it is fitting that servants bear the hardships that come with carrying out His injunctions.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When My servants ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about Me: I am truly near."**. A group of the Prophet's ﷺ followers asked him whether their Lord was near, so that they should speak to Him quietly, or far, so that they should call out to Him, and this verse came down. A villager is also reported to have put the question in these words. One reading has the question coming from the People of the Book. The answer is that Allah is near to them in His knowledge, aware of everything about them, and near in answering. From "I am near" comes the ruling that prayer should be made slowly and quietly, and that raising the voice in supplication is not desirable. Imam Aḥmad records from Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī that during a battle the Companions shouted "Allāhu akbar" whenever they climbed a hill or went down into a valley, and the Prophet ﷺ came to them and told them to be gentle with themselves, since they were not calling upon one who is deaf or absent but upon the All-Hearing, All-Seeing, who is closer to each of them than the neck of his mount. He then asked ʿAbdullāh ibn Qays, which was Abū Mūsā's name, whether he should teach him a phrase that is a treasure of Paradise, "lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh". The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record this, and Abū Dāwūd, al-Nasāʾī, al-Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah have similar wordings. Imam Aḥmad also records from Anas that the Prophet ﷺ related Allah's words: I am as My servant thinks of Me, and I am with him whenever he calls on Me.

**"I respond to one's prayer when they call upon Me."**. Allah answers the one who calls on Him sincerely, without mediator or intercessor and without raised voices, granting his request. Imam Aḥmad records from Abū Saʿīd that the Prophet ﷺ said that no Muslim makes a duʿāʾ free of sin and of severing kinship without Allah granting him one of three things: a swift answer, a reward stored up for the Hereafter, or an equal measure of evil turned away from him, and when the Companions said they would then pray more, he said that Allah has more. ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit relates similarly, as recorded by ʿAbdullāh the son of Imam Aḥmad and by al-Tirmidhī, that no Muslim on earth supplicates without Allah granting it or averting an equal harm, so long as he asks for no sin or breaking of kinship. Imam Mālik records from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said a person's supplication is answered so long as he does not grow impatient and say that he prayed and was not answered, which the Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from Mālik in al-Bukhārī's wording. Muslim's version adds the conditions of asking for no sin or severing of kinship, and explains impatience as saying that one has prayed and prayed without seeing an answer, and so losing interest and giving up supplication. In the Musnad of Aḥmad and the Sunan of al-Tirmidhī, al-Nasāʾī and Ibn Mājah, Abū Hurayrah relates that three are never refused: the just ruler, the fasting person until he breaks his fast, and the oppressed, whose prayer Allah raises above the clouds, opening the gates of heaven for it and swearing by His might that He will help him, even if after a while. Placing this verse amid the rulings of fasting also hints that the prayer made at the completion of a fast is accepted, so one should be particular about praying then, for the Prophet ﷺ said that the fasting person's prayer at the time of ifṭār is accepted, and ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar used to gather his family around him at ifṭār and pray.

**"So let them respond ˹with obedience˺ to Me and believe in Me"**. Let them answer My call to obedience, obeying My Messenger and devoting themselves to Me and My sacred law, and let them persevere in faith in Me and in My Messenger before they ask, for that is the surest route to being answered.

**"perhaps they will be guided ˹to the Right Way˺"**. So that they may be guided in worldly and sacred matters and have their prayers answered.

### Open a door

In the devotional reading, the servants who ask about Him are those who hold the ring of veneration for Him, who have fled to His street, put aside everything less than Him, undertaken His service, believed in Him and cut themselves off from secondary causes, wrapping the turban of trial round their heads and buying His love with spirit and heart. They came into existence with passion and leave with passion. To such servants He says that He is near: near uncalled and unsought, near unhoped for and unperceived, near by His firstness and His attributes, self-standing, near not by the servant's worthiness but by His own description. This is what He said to Moses on that pitch-black night at the edge of the Mount when he "was called from the bush in the sacred ground to the right side of the valley" (28:30), for after Ādam no one heard the speech of the Real with the ears of his head except Moses. Hearing the call, Moses was unsettled and his patience fled, since love always snatches away the hand of patience, and in his burning he asked where he should seek the One who had made him hear. The answer came: seek as you wish, for I am with you, nearer to you than your spirit is to your body, your life-vein to you and your speech to your mouth. In allusion it is as if the Exalted Lord said: I am near to you through knowledge but far from your imagination. The lovers have Me as their share, while the wage-earners receive their wages from Me. To remember Me is delight and to love Me is celebration. Whoever recognises Me holds the kingdom, whoever finds Me has joy, whoever keeps My company gives his spirit rest, and whoever is near Me has light. For the friends I stand in place of the spirit, and for the recognisers I am a resurrection that needs no Trumpet.

He says further: I am near to My servants and love the near ones. Those who call Me are answered, those who seek Me are given access, and those who reach proximity to Me have My good pleasure. My servant, draw near so that I may draw near, for whoever approaches Me by a span finds Me approaching by a cubit. If you call Me I will respond, and I also call you, to the aid of My religion and the acceptance of My messenger's message, so respond to Me. Open a door so that I may open a door. Open the door of supplication and I open the door of response: "Call upon Me, I will respond to you" (40:60). Open the door of turning back and I open the door of good news, as those "turning to Allah ˹alone˺, will have good news" (39:17). Open the door of spending and I open the door of replacement: "whatever you spend in charity, He will compensate ˹you˺ for it" (34:39). Open the door of struggle and I open the door of guidance: "those who struggle in Our cause, We will surely guide them along Our Way" (29:69). Open the door of trust and I open the door of sufficiency: "whoever puts their trust in Allah, then He ˹alone˺ is sufficient for them" (65:3). Open the door of asking forgiveness and I open the door of forgiveness, for whoever "seeks Allah's forgiveness will certainly find Allah All-Forgiving, Most Merciful" (4:110).

### In short

- Asked whether their Lord was near or far, the believers were told He is near in knowledge and in answering, so supplication, especially at ifṭār in Ramaḍān, should be quiet (the hadith of Abū Mūsā).
- Every sinless prayer is answered in one of three ways (Abū Saʿīd, ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit), provided one does not grow impatient (Abū Hurayrah), and the just ruler, the faster at ifṭār and the oppressed are never refused.
- The condition is to answer Allah's own call with obedience and firm faith, which leads to guidance.
- Inwardly, Allah is nearer to the lover than his life-vein, as He told Moses (28:30), and every door the servant opens He answers with another (40:60, 39:17, 34:39, 29:69, 65:3, 4:110).

This long verse explains the remaining injunctions of fasting, including iʿtikāf. "It has been made permissible for you to be intimate with your wives during the nights preceding the fast. Your spouses are a garment for you as you are for them. Allah knows that you were deceiving yourselves. So He has accepted your repentance and pardoned you." It continues: "So now you may be intimate with them and seek what Allah has prescribed for you. ˹You may˺ eat and drink until you see the light of dawn breaking the darkness of night, then complete the fast until nightfall." And it closes: "Do not be intimate with your spouses while you are meditating in the mosques. These are the limits set by Allah, so do not exceed them. This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to people, so they may become mindful ˹of Him˺". The verse brings relief from Allah by ending a practice of the early years, when Muslims could eat, drink and approach their wives only until the ʿishāʾ prayer, or until they slept. Whoever slept or prayed ʿishāʾ was barred from all three until the next night, and the Muslims found this hard.

### Phrase by phrase

**"It has been made permissible for you to be intimate with your wives during the nights preceding the fast."**. The opening words, uḥilla lakum, show that what is now made lawful was unlawful before. The word rafath literally covers everything a husband says or does in seeking his wife's consent, but the Ummah agrees that here it means intercourse, as Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAṭāʾ and Mujāhid explained, with the same from Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Ṭāwūs and Sālim ibn ʿAbdullāh, from ʿAmr ibn Dīnār, al-Ḥasan and Qatādah, and from al-Zuhrī, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī, al-Suddī, ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī and Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān. Al-Bukhārī records through Abū Isḥāq from al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib that when Ramaḍān was first ordained the permission to eat, drink and approach one's wife was conditional on not sleeping after ifṭār, so that a nap after breaking the fast made all three unlawful until the next evening. Qays ibn Ṣirmah al-Anṣārī had worked all day on his land and came home at ifṭār to find nothing to eat. His wife went out to find him something, and being exhausted he fell asleep before she returned, so that when she came back she cried out that he had slept. He fasted the next day in that state and fainted in the middle of the day, and when the matter was mentioned to the Prophet ﷺ this verse was revealed, down to the words about dawn. In another wording from al-Barāʾ, when the fast of Ramaḍān was ordained the men abstained from their wives all month, but some deceived themselves, and the verse came down. Al-Walibī reports from Ibn ʿAbbās that after ʿishāʾ in Ramaḍān the Muslims were forbidden food and intimacy until the following night, and that some, among them ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, approached their wives or ate after ʿishāʾ, complained to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and Allah sent down the verse. ʿUmar had gone in to his wife after finding her asleep, and told the Prophet ﷺ what he had done. The Muslims were very happy at the revelation. The rule abrogated here, the unlawfulness of eating and intimacy after sleeping, appears nowhere in the Qur'an. The Companions observed it as set by the Prophet ﷺ, as Aḥmad narrates in his Musnad, and the verse first confirms that rule as a divine command and then abrogates it for ease. This shows that rules established by the Sunnah can be abrogated through the Qur'an, and that in Islamic law the Messenger's ﷺ ruling carries the same authority as a Qur'anic injunction. The permission was then extended further when suḥūr, eaten towards the end of the night after rising from sleep, was declared a sunnah.

**"Your spouses are a garment for you as you are for them."**. Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah, al-Suddī and Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān explained that your wives are a resort for you and you for them, and al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said they are your cover and you theirs. The image is a metaphor for embrace and for the need of each for the other: husband and wife are intimate with one another, which is why intimacy was permitted on the nights of Ramaḍān to make things easier. Each protects the other and keeps the other pure.

**"Allah knows that you were deceiving yourselves. So He has accepted your repentance and pardoned you."**. You were betraying yourselves by approaching your wives on the nights of the fast, as happened with ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb and others, who apologised to the Prophet ﷺ. Allah relented towards you before you even turned in repentance, forgave you, relieved you of your self-deception and did not punish you.

**"So now you may be intimate with them and seek what Allah has prescribed for you."**. Now that it is lawful, lie with them and seek what Allah has decreed for you, meaning offspring, as Abū Hurayrah, Ibn ʿAbbās and Anas said, together with Shurayḥ the judge, Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, and also ʿAṭāʾ, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, al-Suddī and Zayd ibn Aslam, and Ḥakam ibn ʿUtbah, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, Qatādah and others. Qatādah also explained it as seeking the permission Allah has granted you. A righteous child is the gift sought.

**"˹You may˺ eat and drink until you see the light of dawn breaking the darkness of night"**. Eating, drinking and intimacy are allowed during any part of the night until the light of dawn is distinct from the darkness. The Arabic likens the darkness of night to a black thread and the light of dawn to a white one, as the two stretch along the horizon, and then makes it plain by adding "of dawn". Al-Bukhārī records from Sahl ibn Saʿd that when the words about the white and black threads were revealed without "of dawn", some who meant to fast tied black and white threads to their legs and went on eating until they could tell them apart, until the words "of dawn" came down and they understood that night and day were meant. Al-Bukhārī also records from al-Shaʿbī that ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim put a black string and a white one under his pillow and watched them all night without being able to distinguish them, and when he told the Prophet ﷺ in the morning he said that his pillow must be very wide, or in another wording that the back of his neck was wide. Some took this to mean that ʿAdī was slow-witted, a weak view, for al-Bukhārī's fuller narration has ʿAdī asking whether the two threads were real threads and being told that his neck would be wide indeed if he could see them, since they are the blackness of night and the whiteness of day. The qualifier "distinct" guards against extremes: one should not, like the chronically doubtful, treat eating as unlawful before dawn has broken, nor be so heedless as to go on eating once certain that it has. Certainty of daybreak is the dividing line. Before it, eating is not ḥarām, and after it even a minute's indulgence is ḥarām and spoils the fast. Reports of Companions who continued eating well past the usual time are explained by their not yet being certain of dawn and refusing to stop merely on others' caution.

**"then complete the fast until nightfall."**. Fast from daybreak until the night arrives with the setting of the sun, and break the fast then. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that when night comes from the east and day departs from the west, the fasting person breaks his fast. Sahl ibn Saʿd al-Sāʿidī relates that people will retain goodness so long as they hasten to break the fast, and Imam Aḥmad records from Abū Hurayrah, in a hadith al-Tirmidhī calls ḥasan gharīb, that Allah said the dearest of His servants to Him are those who hasten most to break the fast. One reading says the verse about completing the fast was revealed concerning Ṣirmah ibn Mālik ibn ʿAdiyy.

**"Do not be intimate with your spouses while you are meditating in the mosques."**. Literally iʿtikāf is to stay in a place in seclusion, and in the terminology of the Qur'an and Sunnah it is staying in a mosque under particular conditions. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reports Ibn ʿAbbās as saying that the verse concerns the man in iʿtikāf in the mosque in Ramaḍān or any other month, who is forbidden to approach women by night or day until he finishes. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk said that formerly a man in iʿtikāf would leave the mosque and, if he wished, be with his wife, and Allah forbade touching one's wife during iʿtikāf whether inside the mosque or outside it. Mujāhid, Qatādah and others say the same, and Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this explanation from Ibn Masʿūd, Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb, Mujāhid, ʿAṭāʾ and al-Ḥasan, as well as from Qatādah, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Suddī, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and Muqātil. One account says the verse was revealed about a group of Companions including ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib and ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir who, while in retreat in the mosque, would go to their wives when they needed, bathe and return, and were then forbidden. This is the agreed practice: whoever is in iʿtikāf may not have intercourse, and if he must leave the mosque to relieve himself or to eat he may not kiss or embrace his wife or busy himself with anything other than his retreat, not even visiting the sick, though he may ask after them in passing. Eating and drinking at night remain lawful for the one in retreat as for everyone else, but intimacy is forbidden even at night, which is why the verse states the rule separately. The generality of "in the mosques" shows that iʿtikāf may be performed in any mosque. The juristic condition that it be a mosque where congregational prayer is regularly held, and not a deserted one, derives from the very meaning of a mosque, whose main purpose is congregational prayer, since individual prayer can be offered in a house or shop. Other rules, such as fasting during iʿtikāf and not leaving the mosque without a need recognised by the law, derive partly from the word itself and partly from the Prophet's ﷺ words and deeds, and the jurists explain them after the rules of fasting, just as the Qur'an mentions the two together. By mentioning iʿtikāf after fasting Allah draws attention to practising it in the month of fasting, especially its last part. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ kept iʿtikāf in the last ten nights of Ramaḍān until he died, and his wives did so after him, as the Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from ʿĀʾishah. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs also record that Ṣafiyyah bint Ḥuyayy visited him in the mosque during his retreat, and when she rose to go home at night he walked her back to her lodging in the house of Usāmah ibn Zayd on the edge of al-Madīnah. Two Anṣārī men passed them hurriedly, shy of disturbing him, and he called them back, told them she was Ṣafiyyah bint Ḥuyayy, and when they protested their innocence of any suspicion he said that Shayṭān runs in a person wherever the blood runs and that he feared he might cast an evil thought into their minds. Imam al-Shāfiʿī commented that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was teaching his Ummah to remove any evil thought at once so as not to fall into what is forbidden, for those two men feared Allah too much to think ill of him. The verse forbids intercourse and whatever leads to it, such as kissing and embracing, during iʿtikāf, but a wife may attend to her husband: ʿĀʾishah said in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would lean his head into her room so that she could comb his hair while she was menstruating, and that he entered the house only for a human need.

**"These are the limits set by Allah, so do not exceed them."**. This is what Allah has explained, ordained, specified, allowed and forbidden concerning the fast, its objectives, what is permitted in it and what it requires, limits He has legislated and made clear so that you neither approach nor overstep them. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said that the limits are the four stated in this verse, and that his father and others would recite the verse and say the same. "Do not approach them" is stronger than "do not overstep them" used elsewhere, for whoever comes near the limits is close to falling into what is prohibited. The prohibitions of eating, drinking and intimacy while fasting are Allah's limits, and one should not even go near them for fear of crossing them. For this reason excessive gargling while fasting is makrūh, since water may slip down the throat, as is placing medicine in the mouth and kissing or embracing one's wife. It is also better to stop eating a couple of minutes before the end of suḥūr and to delay ifṭār a couple of minutes as a precaution, since carelessness in these matters goes against this command.

**"This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to people, so they may become mindful ˹of Him˺"**. Just as He explains the fast and its rulings, He explains His other rulings, His commands and prohibitions, through the words of His servant and Messenger Muḥammad ﷺ, so that people know how to attain true guidance and how to worship Him, doing what He commands and avoiding what He forbids. Similarly He says, "He is the One Who sends down clear revelations to His servant to bring you out of darkness and into light" (57:9).

### Suḥūr and the true dawn

Allowing food and drink until dawn is itself proof that suḥūr is encouraged, for it is a concession (rukhṣah), and Allah loves His concessions to be accepted. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from Anas that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to eat suḥūr because there is blessing in it. Muslim records from ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ that the difference between our fast and that of the People of the Book is the meal of suḥūr. Imam Aḥmad records from Abū Saʿīd that suḥūr is a blessed meal not to be abandoned even if one only sips water, for Allah and His angels send blessings on those who eat it, and several hadiths call it the blessed meal. It is preferable to delay it until near dawn. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record that Zayd ibn Thābit ate suḥūr with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and then went to pray, and when Anas asked how long there had been between the adhān and the meal, he said the time it takes to recite fifty verses. Imam Aḥmad records from Abū Dharr that the Ummah will keep its goodness as long as it hastens ifṭār and delays suḥūr. Many of the Salaf allowed suḥūr to continue until close to fajr: this is reported from Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, from ʿAlī and Ibn Masʿūd, from Ḥudhayfah and Abū Hurayrah, and from Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās and Zayd ibn Thābit, and among the Tābiʿīn from Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī ibn Ḥusayn and Abū Mijlaz, from Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī and Abū al-Ḍuḥā, and from Abū Wāʾil and other companions of Ibn Masʿūd. It is also the view of ʿAṭāʾ, al-Ḥasan and Ḥakam ibn ʿUyaynah, of Mujāhid and ʿUrwah ibn al-Zubayr, and of Abū al-Shaʿthāʾ Jābir ibn Zayd, al-Aʿmash and Maʿmar ibn Rāshid. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from al-Qāsim from ʿĀʾishah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that Bilāl's adhān should not stop anyone from suḥūr, because he calls it at night, so people should eat and drink until they hear the adhān of Ibn Umm Maktūm, who does not call until dawn, in al-Bukhārī's wording. Imam Aḥmad records from Qays ibn Ṭalq from his father that dawn is not the ascending white glow but the red light spreading along the horizon, and Abū Dāwūd and al-Tirmidhī have the wording that people should eat and drink without being hurried by the rising whiteness, until the redness appears. Ibn Jarīr and Muslim record from Samurah ibn Jundub that people should not be stopped by Bilāl's adhān or by the whiteness until it spreads. Because this hadith is sometimes reported only in part, some contemporaries have misunderstood it, suggesting that one may go on eating for a while after the fajr adhān and that someone who wakes late during the adhān may hurry and eat something. The hadith in fact clearly requires stopping at the adhān of Ibn Umm Maktūm, which was called precisely at daybreak, and the Qur'an itself fixes the deadline at the certainty of dawn, so that permitting even one minute beyond it contravenes the text. The reports from the Companions and early elders about latitude in suḥūr and ifṭār are to be understood, in the light of the text, as discouraging excessive precaution well before one is certain of dawn, as has also been explained of them, for no ordinary Muslim, let alone a Companion, would openly contravene the Qur'anic command, especially when the verse ends by stressing precaution with "do not approach them".

Those who can see the horizon with their own eyes, with a clear sky and the ability to recognise the first light, must act by looking at it directly. Where the horizon is hidden or unclear, or a person cannot recognise daybreak, the time is fixed by other signs or by calculation, and there will be a period when certainty is lacking. Al-Jaṣṣāṣ answers in his Aḥkām al-Qur'ān that in such doubt it is preferable not to eat or drink deliberately, but one who eats in a state of doubt well before certainty is not a sinner, though if it later proves that dawn had broken, a make-up fast becomes due. Likewise if the new moon is not sighted on the eve of Ramaḍān and people do not fast, and witnesses later establish that it was sighted on the twenty-ninth, those who missed the day thinking it the thirtieth of Shaʿbān are not sinners but owe qaḍāʾ by unanimous agreement, and someone who breaks his fast near sunset on a cloudy day and then finds the sun still up is not a sinner but must make the fast up. It follows that one who wakes late while the usual adhāns are being called, which makes dawn certain, and knowingly eats is both a sinner and liable to qaḍāʾ, while one who eats in doubt commits the sin with the qaḍāʾ due and a degree of blame.

### Starting the fast junub, and wiṣāl

One benefit of allowing intimacy, food and drink until dawn is that it is permissible to begin the fast in a state of major impurity and to bathe any time after waking, which is the view of the four imams and the majority. Al-Bukhārī and Muslim record from ʿĀʾishah and Umm Salamah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would rise junub from intercourse, not from a dream, bathe and fast, and Umm Salamah added that he neither broke that fast nor made it up. Muslim records from ʿĀʾishah that a man asked whether he should fast when dawn found him junub, and the Prophet ﷺ replied that the same happened to him and he fasted. When the man said that the Prophet ﷺ was not like them since his past and future sins were forgiven, he swore by Allah that he hoped to be the most fearful of Allah among them and the most knowledgeable of what taqwā is. Several authentic hadiths forbid wiṣāl, continuing the fast through the night into the next day without eating. Imam Aḥmad and the Ṣaḥīḥayn record from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade it, and when the people said that he practised it himself he replied that he was not like them, since his Lord gave him food and drink in his sleep. When they refused to stop, he fasted with them two days and two nights until the crescent of Shawwāl appeared, and then said that had it not appeared he would have made them fast longer, as a punishment. Wiṣāl was one of the Prophet's ﷺ special qualities, since he was assisted in it, and the food and drink he received were plainly spiritual rather than material, otherwise it would not have been wiṣāl at all. It is permitted, however, to continue from sunset until suḥūr, for Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī relates in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs that the Prophet ﷺ forbade wiṣāl but allowed whoever wished to continue until suḥūr, again explaining that he had One who fed him and gave him drink during the night.

### The essence of fasting

Fasting is by nature an act of patience, and patience means putting up with every kind of difficulty to carry out Allah's commandments, which brings a person to the state of mind that draws him near to Allah. Only those find Allah who surrender themselves wholly to Him, and only the words of those reach Him whose wavelength matches His. The command to fast is followed by the command not to consume one another's property unjustly, which shows the essence of fasting: to create such a sense of subservience that when Allah commands abstention from anything one abstains at once. If Allah wishes a person to stay away even from lawful things, he must not hesitate, and one who abstains from the lawful at His command will have no difficulty abstaining from the unlawful.

### The right of the Real and the portion of the servant

In the devotional reading, the verse shows that the customs that characterise human beings are not attributed to Allah. Whether one is engaged in worship (ʿibādah), which is the right of the Real, or in the customs of companionship with one's spouse, which is the aim of the lower self and a worldly concern, both are the same for one's state so long as there is refined conduct (adab). The verse was revealed concerning the lapse of al-Fārūq, and Allah made that lapse the occasion of a dispensation for all Muslims until the Resurrection. Such are the rulings of divine solicitude. It is said that Allah knew there was no escaping worldly concerns for the servant, so He divided the night and day of this month between His right and the servant's portion: for His right, complete the fast until night, and for your portion, eat and drink until the white thread is distinct from the black. The prohibition during retreat states that the place of nearness is sanctified from the pursuit of worldly concerns: when you are occupied with yourselves you are veiled through yourselves, but when your business is with Us, do not turn back from Us to yourselves. The jealousy of the Real comes at the times when serious business is mixed with play. ʿĀʾishah once told the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that she loved him and loved being near him, and he answered: let me be, daughter of Abū Bakr, I am worshipping my Lord, for I have a time in which none but my Lord is permitted to me.

### In short

- The early rule barring food and intimacy after sleep or ʿishāʾ, hard on men like Qays ibn Ṣirmah and ʿUmar, was confirmed as divine and then lifted: the nights of the fast are open until dawn, and spouses are each other's garment.
- Dawn is the moment the white thread of day is distinct from the black of night (Sahl ibn Saʿd, ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim). Certainty of dawn is the dividing line, so suḥūr is a blessed sunnah delayed until near dawn (Anas, Zayd ibn Thābit, the adhān of Ibn Umm Maktūm), but not a minute beyond it.
- Eating in genuine doubt is not a sin but requires qaḍāʾ if dawn had broken (al-Jaṣṣāṣ). The fast ends at sunset and should be broken promptly (ʿUmar, Sahl, Abū Hurayrah).
- One may start the fast junub (ʿĀʾishah, Umm Salamah). Wiṣāl is forbidden except until suḥūr, being a quality peculiar to the Prophet ﷺ. In iʿtikāf, in any mosque of congregation, intimacy and its preliminaries are forbidden day and night, though a wife may serve her husband, and the Prophet ﷺ kept the last ten nights until he died.
- "Do not approach" Allah's limits is stricter than "do not overstep", so even gargling excessively or kissing while fasting is makrūh, and Allah clarifies His rulings so people may fear Him (57:9). Inwardly, the month is divided between Allah's right and the servant's portion, and the place of nearness is kept clear of worldly concerns.
