<<<VERSE 2:23>>>
Having argued for the oneness of Allah, the surah now argues for the prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ, and it does so by issuing a challenge. "And if you are in doubt about what We have revealed to Our servant, then produce a sûrah like it and call your helpers other than Allah, if what you say is true". The guidance of the Qur'an rests on two principles, tawḥīd and risālah, the oneness of Allah and prophethood. Verses 21 and 22 proved the first by pointing to acts that only Allah can do. Verses 23 and 24 prove the second by pointing to speech that only Allah can produce. The method is the same in both places. Since no one but Allah can create the sky and the earth or send down rain, no one else deserves worship. Since no one can produce the like of this speech, it can be the word of no one but Allah, and the man who brought it is His messenger.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And if you are in doubt"** uses the word rayb for doubt. According to Imām ar-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī, rayb is a hesitation, indecision or suspicion with no basis, which a little reflection can clear away. That is why the Qur'an treats this kind of doubt as incompatible with knowledge, even in a non-Muslim: it mentions the number of the angels of Hell "so that the People of the Book will be certain, and the believers will increase in faith" and neither group falls into doubt (74:31). The surah opened by calling itself the Book in which there is no rayb, and now it uses the same word for the doubters, implying that its truths are so plain that no hesitation can arise except in people without knowledge. The verse is addressed to the Arab idolaters, and also to the Jews and Christians, to anyone uncertain whether this Qur'an is from Allah.

**"about what We have revealed to Our servant"** means the Qur'an, sent down through Jibrīl (Gabriel) to Muhammad ﷺ, the servant whom they accused of making it up.

**"then produce a sûrah like it"** means: if you claim that what he brought did not come from Allah, bring something similar yourselves. "Like it" means like the Qur'an in its eloquence, its fine arrangement and its disclosure of the unseen, and the Arabic min mithlihi is explicative, a surah of the same kind. This is the explanation of Mujāhid and Qatādah, of Ibn Jarīr aṭ-Ṭabarī, az-Zamakhsharī and ar-Rāzī, and ar-Rāzī reports it as the position of most scholars, going back to Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, ʿUmar and al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī. One gloss takes it as a surah like Sūrat al-Baqarah itself. The word sūrah means a limited, definite piece. Technically it is a passage of the Qur'an set apart from the others by revelation, with a beginning and an end and at least three verses, and there are 114 of them, some long and some very short. The verse says "a sūrah", without the definite article, so the shortest surahs are included in the challenge.

**"and call your helpers other than Allah"** answers an obvious objection: one man's failure, or one group's, does not prove that no one else could succeed. So the doubters are allowed every helper they can find. The word shuhadāʾ is the plural of shahīd, "one who is present", which is why a court witness is called a shahīd. Here it means either people in general, so that the doubters may summon anyone from anywhere in the world, or specifically the idols of Makkah, which their worshippers imagined would stand up as witnesses for them on the Day of Judgment. Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as "helpers". Abū Mālik, as reported by as-Suddī, said it means partners, other people to assist you, so go and ask your gods to support you. Mujāhid said it means people, wise and eloquent men who will give the testimony you want. It is also said to mean their leaders. The point is: you too are fluent speakers of Arabic like him, so do it.

**"if what you say is true"** means if you are truthful in claiming that Muhammad ﷺ speaks it from himself.

### The same challenge throughout the Qur'an

Allah challenged the disbelievers in many places. In Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ: "Bring then a scripture from Allah which is a better guide than these two so I may follow it, if your claim is true" (28:49). In Sūrat al-Isrāʾ: "If ˹all˺ humans and jinn were to come together to produce the equivalent of this Quran, they could not produce its equal, no matter how they supported each other" (17:88). In Sūrat Hūd: "Produce ten fabricated sûrahs like it and seek help from whoever you can—other than Allah—if what you say is true!" (11:13). In Sūrat Yūnus: "It is not ˹possible˺ for this Quran to have been produced by anyone other than Allah ... Or do they claim, “He made it up!”? Tell them ˹O Prophet˺, “Produce one sûrah like it then, and seek help from whoever you can—other than Allah—if what you say is true!”" (10:37-38). All of those were revealed in Makkah. The present verse repeats the challenge in Madinah. Ar-Rāzī preferred the reading "a surah like the Qur'an" and noted that Allah challenged the disbelievers as individuals and as groups, literate and illiterate alike, which makes the challenge complete, and bolder than a challenge aimed only at people who might lack learning. It is a general challenge to the Arab disbelievers, the most eloquent of all nations, issued several times in Makkah and Madinah to people who hated the Prophet ﷺ and his religion intensely. They never met it.

The challenge is addressed to all people everywhere, present and future: if you suppose this to be the work of a man, then produce even a short passage like it, for what one man has done other men should be able to do. If individuals fail, they may call in every helper they can. They could convene an international workshop for the purpose. The next verse warns in advance that the attempt will never succeed, and threatens the Fire, because once a person has admitted that he cannot produce the like of the Qur'an, which is clear evidence that it comes not from a man but from a Being above all created things, and still persists in disbelief, he is choosing Hell for himself.

### The greatest miracle

These verses present the miraculous Qur'an as the evidence of the prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ and of his truthfulness. His miracles are countless, each more wonderful than the last, but by mentioning only this one, a miracle in the sphere of knowledge, Allah has shown that it is the greatest. Among the miracles of all the prophets it has a special distinction. Allah has always displayed His power through miracles shown at the hands of each prophet, but each of those appeared with its prophet and ended with him. The Qur'an is a miracle that will last until the end of time. The two Ṣaḥīḥs record from Abū Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ said that every prophet was given a sign of the kind that brings people to faith, and that what he was given was a revelation that Allah sent down to him, and so he hoped to have the largest following on the Day of Resurrection. This is Muslim's wording. He meant that he was specially entrusted with the inimitable Qur'an, which challenged mankind to produce its like. The other revealed Books, according to many scholars, were not miraculous in this way. Allah knows best. He was also supported by innumerable other signs testifying to his prophethood and to what he brought, and all praise belongs to Allah.

The pagans of Makkah, as history records, were ready to give their lives to wipe out Islam. The challenge offered them an easy way to do it, and it wounded their tribal pride by predicting that they would never manage it. Yet not one contender stepped forward. That silence was an admission of helplessness and an acknowledgment that the Qur'an is the word of Allah, and it establishes the Qur'an as the Prophet's ﷺ manifest miracle. Because the challenge still stands, the miracle still lives, and it will live until the end of the world.

### What the Arabs themselves admitted

The most discerning Arabs of the time admitted, at least in private, that the Qur'an could not be imitated. Some were honest enough to say so in public, and some embraced Islam. Others, despite the admission, could not give up the ways of their forefathers or get over their tribal rivalries, especially their hostility to Banū ʿAbd Manāf, the Prophet's ﷺ clan. Jalāl ad-Dīn as-Suyūṭī collected several such incidents in al-Khaṣāʾiṣ al-Kubrā. When the Prophet ﷺ and the Qur'an began to attract attention beyond Makkah, the enemies of Islam worried about the crowds that would gather for the annual pilgrimage and fall under his spell. The chiefs took the problem to al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah, the eldest and shrewdest among them. They first proposed telling the pilgrims that the Qur'an was the raving of a madman, but al-Walīd foresaw that anyone who heard the Prophet ﷺ speak with such clarity and eloquence would know the charge was false. They thought of dismissing him as a poet, but al-Walīd warned that most Arabs had an inborn understanding of poetry and would see at once that he was no poet. They considered calling him a soothsayer, but al-Walīd feared that this too would be exposed and would turn the pilgrims against the accusers. Summing up his own impression he said: by God, none of you knows more about Arabic poetry than I do, and by God I find in this speech a sweetness and a grace I have never found in the speech of any poet or orator. After long thought he advised them to call the Prophet ﷺ a sorcerer who used his art to split sons from fathers and wives from husbands.

Many others felt the same: an-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith, a tribal chief, Unays, the brother of the Companion Abū Dharr, Asʿad ibn Zurārah, another chief, and Qays ibn Nasībah of Banū Sulaym. Even the Prophet's ﷺ bitterest enemies, al-Akhnas ibn Sharīq, Abū Sufyān and Abū Jahl himself, are reported to have crept to his house under cover of night to hear him recite, and to have been so gripped by what they heard that they stayed rooted there until dawn. Still they persisted in denial. Abū Jahl confessed the reason in plain words: his people had kept pace with Banū ʿAbd Manāf in every virtue, but now that their rivals had produced a prophet they had nothing to match the claim.

### Why the Qur'an is a miracle

The subject has been treated thoroughly by the greatest scholars of every age and in many languages. Al-Jāḥiẓ wrote Naẓm al-Qurʾān in the third century after the Hijrah. Early in the fourth came Abū ʿAbdullāh al-Wāsiṭī's Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān. Ibn ʿĪsā ar-Rabbānī wrote a shorter work with that title later in the same century. Qāḍī Abū Bakr al-Bāqillānī's long, comprehensive Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān followed early in the fifth. As-Suyūṭī discusses the subject at length in al-Itqān and al-Khaṣāʾiṣ al-Kubrā. Other treatments include Imām ar-Rāzī's at-Tafsīr al-Kabīr and Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ's ash-Shifāʾ. More recent examples are Muṣṭafā Ṣādiq ar-Rāfiʿī's Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān and Sayyid Rashīd Riḍā's al-Waḥy al-Muḥammadī. Shabbīr Aḥmad ʿUthmānī also wrote an Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān. It is itself a peculiarity of the Qur'an that, besides vast commentaries, scores of books have been written on its separate aspects and on the countless questions that arise from it. No summary of that literature is possible here, but the main reasons the Qur'an is held to be the Prophet's ﷺ miracle can be indicated.

1. It is incomparable in its comprehensiveness even among the world's sacred books. It brings the ultimate knowledge of the metaphysical order and at the same time guides every sphere of human life, spiritual and physical, individual and collective. Anyone who suspects a human author should remember that it appeared at a time and in a place that offered no means of acquiring the education such a book would need. The Arabs of the day were known as the ummiyyūn, the illiterate, and it came through a Prophet ﷺ who could neither read nor write and had never tried to learn even the poetry and rhetoric the Arabs prided themselves on. That alone is nothing short of a miracle.

2. The challenge was not confined to richness of meaning and quality of wisdom. It covered the mode of expression too, and the first people addressed were the Arabs of the age of ignorance, who made no claim to wisdom or learning but fancied themselves supremely for eloquence, so much so that to them all foreigners were al-ʿajam, "the dumb". Some of them hated the Prophet ﷺ so madly that they would have cut their own throats if it hurt him. Yet none came forward. The helplessness of a people so gifted with spontaneous eloquence, in a contest that should have been easy for them, argues that this is not the word of man but the word of Allah. And no one has succeeded since.

3. The Qur'an made many predictions that came true exactly. The Makkan pagans refused to believe its prophecy that the Rūm, the Byzantines, would defeat the Persians after their initial defeat. They made it a point of honour and wagered on it, and were humiliated when the prophecy was fulfilled before the stipulated ten years were out.

4. It gives a clear account of earlier prophets, their laws and their peoples, and of many events since the beginning of the world, information that even the best Jewish and Christian scholars did not possess so exactly. A Prophet ﷺ who never attended a school or sat with a learned man could not have supplied these details without knowledge from Allah.

5. Several verses disclosed what certain people had hidden in their hearts, and they had to confess that this was exactly what they had been thinking: "when two groups among you ˹believers˺ were about to cower, then Allah reassured them" (3:122), and the verse that reports what the hypocrites say within themselves, asking why Allah does not punish them for what they say.

6. The Qur'an predicted that certain people would not do certain things, and then, though able, they did not do them. The Jews called themselves God's chosen and His friends. A friend longs to meet his friend, so the Qur'an asked them to prove the claim by wishing for death and the return to Allah, and declared, "But they will never wish for that because of what their hands have done" (62:7). Wishing for death costs nothing, and for the Jews it would have been an easy way to refute the Qur'an, but for all their hatred of the Prophet ﷺ they knew in their hearts that it was Allah's Book and feared that if they lied they would really die. They kept silent.

7. When the Qur'an is recited in Arabic it affects even a casual listener, Muslim or not, in an odd and indefinable way. History records many people who accepted Islam simply because they happened to pass by while the Prophet ﷺ was reciting, among them the Companion Jubayr ibn Muṭʿim.

8. The best book in the world loses its charm for its keenest admirer after four or five readings. The Qur'an alone works the other way: each reading or recitation sharpens the appetite for the next. In this it is unique even among the sacred books.

9. The sacred books of many religions have been lost or survive only in corrupted form. Allah promised in the Qur'an that He Himself would guard this Book against the slightest change until the end of time. Over fourteen centuries millions of copies, handwritten and printed, have spread across the globe as no other scripture has. But the greatest miracle in this respect is that in every age and every land where Muslims have lived there have been millions who carried the whole Book in memory, not one consonant or vowel altered. Allah preserved His last Book not only in written words but above all in human hearts. He is Ever-Living, and His word will live forever beyond the reach of created beings.

10. No other book holds every form of knowledge and wisdom in so short a space, meeting every spiritual need of man and guiding every sphere of his inner and outer, individual and social life.

11. The guidance is not merely theoretical. What other book, sacred or otherwise, has had so vast and deep an impact on human history in so short a time, or changed the individual and collective lives of millions within a few years? When the Prophet ﷺ left this world, Islam had already established a new order of life across the whole Arabian peninsula, against every opposition and without modern means of communication, and a few decades later its message had reached as far as India in one direction and Spain in the other. Can such reach be anything but a miracle?

### The two halves of the testimony

The previous verse affirmed tawḥīd and argued against the Arab idolaters. This verse affirms prophecy and argues against the People of the Book and the protected communities. The formula of the shahādah has two sides, the affirmation of oneness and the affirmation of prophecy, and until a servant acknowledges both, believes both and acts as both demand, he has not entered the circle of submission. Affirming prophecy means knowing that the Chosen One ﷺ is the Real's elect and the best of creatures, accepting his prophethood with spirit and heart, taking his words, deeds, customs and conduct as one's leader and guide, and knowing in truth that his words are the Real's revelation, his explanation the Real's road, his decree the Real's religion, and his utterance and delivery of the message, in life and in death, the Real's argument. While Ādam was still hidden in water and clay, the secret of Muhammad's ﷺ created nature had already girded itself at the Exalted Threshold, and the gentle gaze of the Real had already reached his spirit, as he alluded in his words, "I was a prophet when Ādam was between water and clay." "Then produce a sûrah like it" unrolls, from the folds of its holiness, the carpet of the Qur'an's exaltedness, so that those not admitted to it lay the hand of rejection on their breasts, while for those burnt by passion He may lift the mask from its beauty. Then you will see the unveiled beauty of the Qur'an's face when it shows itself, and will speak with the tongue of remembrance.

### In short

- The challenge proves prophethood the way verses 21 and 22 proved tawḥīd: what no creature can do or say must come from Allah.
- Rayb is baseless doubt that reflection removes, "a sûrah" includes the shortest surah, and "your helpers" means anyone and anything the doubters can enlist, so the test is as easy as a test can be.
- The Arabs, the most eloquent of nations and the most hostile of enemies, never answered it, and their own experts privately admitted why.
- The Qur'an is the Prophet's ﷺ greatest and only permanent miracle: unmatched in comprehensiveness, prophecy, preservation, effect and reach, and the challenge is still open.

The challenge is followed by its result and by the consequence of ignoring it. "But if you are unable to do so—and you will never be able to do so—then fear the Fire fuelled with people and stones, which is prepared for the disbelievers". The verse does three things. It foretells that no one will ever meet the challenge, a prophecy that has held for fourteen centuries. It tells the doubters what follows from that failure: belief that this is not the speech of a human being. And it warns those who still refuse of a fire unlike any fire in this world.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But if you are unable to do so"** means if you do not do what was asked, because you cannot. Try as you may, you will not succeed.

**"and you will never be able to do so"** is a parenthesis: you will never be able to, because the Qur'an cannot be imitated. This is a second miracle inside the first. Allah stated flatly, without hedging, that nothing like the Qur'an would ever be brought against it, for all time. The statement has held true to the present day and will never change. How could anyone produce the like of the Qur'an, when it is the word of the One who created everything? How could the words of the created resemble the words of the Creator? The doubters are told in advance that they will fail even with all their individual and collective might, and if they persist in denial after that they are threatened with the Fire already prepared for stubborn disbelievers.

**"then fear the Fire"** means: if you are bent on not believing, then be afraid of Hell. The fear is to be acted on by believing in Allah and believing that this is not the speech of a man.

**"fuelled with people and stones"** means that its fuel, what is used to kindle and feed a fire, like firewood, is the disbelievers themselves and stones, as Allah said, "And as for the deviant, they will be fuel for Hell" (72:15), and "Certainly you ˹disbelievers˺ and whatever you worship instead of Allah will be the fuel of Hell. You are ˹all˺ bound to enter it" (21:98). The stones are the huge, foul, black sulphur stones, which burn hottest when heated, may Allah save us from that end. It is also reported that the stones are the idols and rivals worshipped instead of Allah, as the verse just quoted implies, so that the Fire punishes the idol-worshippers and what they worshipped together. That the Fire burns with stones shows how extreme its heat is, unlike the fires of this world, which burn wood and the like. If stones with all their hardness cannot withstand this fire, how will human beings with all their weakness bear it?

**"which is prepared for the disbelievers"** means created, made ready and preordained for them, so that they are punished in it. The phrase may describe the Fire fuelled with people and stones, or the stones themselves, and there is no contradiction, since the two go together. "Prepared" means kept in readiness, and it will certainly reach those who disbelieve in Allah and His Messenger ﷺ. Ibn Isḥāq reported through Muḥammad, from ʿIkrimah or Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, that Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as "for those who embrace the disbelief that you have embraced". Grammatically the clause is either a new sentence or a continuing description of the Fire's state.

### Hell exists now

Many of the Imāms of the Sunnah used this verse to prove that the Fire already exists, because Allah said "prepared", that is, made ready and kept. There are many hadiths to the same effect. The Prophet ﷺ told how Paradise and the Fire argued with each other. He told how the Fire asked permission of her Lord, complaining that parts of her were consuming other parts, and Allah allowed her two breaths, one in winter and one in summer. In a hadith from Ibn Masʿūd, the Companions heard the sound of something falling and asked about it, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that it was a stone thrown from the top of Jahannam seventy years ago that had only now reached its bottom. That hadith is in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. The reports on this subject are mutawātir, transmitted through many independent chains, including the hadiths of the eclipse prayer and of the night of the Isrāʾ.

### Short surahs are included

"Produce a sûrah like it" (2:23) and "Produce one sûrah like it then" (10:38) cover the short surahs as well as the long ones, so the challenge to creation stands for both, and there is no known disagreement about this among scholars old or new. ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ, while still a pagan, once met Musaylimah the Liar, who asked him what had lately been revealed to his companion in Makkah. ʿAmr said, "A short but eloquent surah," and recited, "By the ˹passage of˺ time! Surely humanity is in ˹grave˺ loss" (103:1-2). Musaylimah thought for a while and said that something similar had been revealed to him too: O wabr, O wabr (a small wild animal), you are only two ears and a chest, and the rest of you is thin and worthless. ʿAmr said, "By Allah, you know that I know you are lying."

### What the miracle looks like on the page

Whoever reads through the Qur'an finds degrees of excellence in both its plain and its deeper meanings. "˹This is˺ a Book whose verses are well perfected and then fully explained. ˹It is˺ from the One ˹Who is˺ All-Wise, All-Aware" (11:1): its wording is perfected and its meanings are set out, and every word and every meaning is eloquent beyond surpassing. It tells the stories of past peoples exactly as they happened. It commands every kind of good and forbids every kind of evil: "The Word of your Lord has been perfected in truth and justice" (6:115), true in what it narrates and just in what it legislates. It contains no exaggeration, no lies and no falsehood, unlike poetry, Arabic or otherwise, which runs on lies, as the well-known saying has it that the most eloquent speech is the one with the most lies in it. A long poem will mainly describe women, horses or wine, or praise some person, horse, camel, war, incident, danger or lion, and such descriptions bring no benefit beyond showing off the poet's skill. In many long poems only a sentence or two touch the poem's real theme, and the rest is padding and repetition. The Qur'an is eloquent through and through, in the most perfect way, as those who know the Arabic arts of speech agree. Its stories are fruitful whether long or short, repeated or told once, and the more they are repeated the more fruitful and beautiful they grow. It does not grow stale with repeated recitation, and the scholars never tire of it. When it warns, it states truths that would make solid mountains shake, so what of hearts that understand? When it promises, it opens hearts and ears and makes them long for the abode of peace and the neighbourhood of the Throne of the Most Merciful. In promise and encouragement: "No soul can imagine what delights are kept in store for them as a reward for what they used to do" (32:17), and "There will be whatever the souls desire and the eyes delight in. And you will be there forever" (43:71). In warning: "Do you feel secure that He will not cause the land to swallow you up, or unleash upon you a storm of stones?" (17:68), and "Do you feel secure that the One Who is in heaven will not cause the earth to swallow you up as it quakes violently" (67:16-17). In threat: "So We seized each ˹people˺ for their sin" (29:40). In gentle counsel: "Imagine ˹O Prophet˺ if We allowed them enjoyment for years, then there came to them what they had been threatened with, would that enjoyment be of any benefit to them ˹at all˺" (26:205-207). The examples of its eloquence, beauty and benefit are many.

When it turns to law, command and prohibition, it enjoins every righteous, good, pleasing and beneficial act and forbids every evil, hateful and immoral one. Ibn Masʿūd and others of the Salaf said that when you hear Allah say "O you who believe!" you should listen closely, because what follows is either a good He is commanding or an evil He is forbidding. So Allah describes the Prophet ﷺ: "He commands them to do good and forbids them from evil, permits for them what is lawful and forbids to them what is impure" (7:157). When the verses speak of the Resurrection and its terrors, of Paradise and the Fire and the joy and safety Allah has prepared for His friends and the torment He has prepared for His enemies, they carry glad tidings and warnings, call to good deeds and away from evil, make this world small and the Hereafter great, set out the right methods, guide to Allah's straight path and just law, and clear the hearts of the evil of the accursed devil.

### Answers to some doubts

It has been suggested that someone, at some time, must have taken up the challenge and produced something comparable, and that the attempt simply was not preserved. The objection is fanciful. In every age the enemies of Islam have outnumbered the Muslims and have had far greater means of publicity. Had any attempt looked even slightly successful it would have been not only preserved but trumpeted. The pagans of Makkah brought every wild charge they could think of against the Prophet ﷺ. They said he had learnt everything from the monk Buḥayrā, whom he had met once in Syria. They attributed the Qur'an to a Roman slave, a foreigner who could not have mastered Arabic, let alone the Arabs' own kind of eloquence, a slander the Qur'an itself records. Yet even they, with all their venom, never claimed to have produced anything like it. Whatever feeble or ridiculous attempts were made are on record. Musaylimah of Yamāmah, the Great Liar, answered the word of Allah with a string of obscenities that his own people dismissed for what they were. Later the celebrated man of letters ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Muqaffaʿ thought of pitting his wits against the Qur'an and soon gave up in despair. A latter-day adventurer, the Irish novelist James Joyce, congratulated himself on having faced the Qur'an's challenge in Finnegans Wake, a book soon recognised as at least very funny and already dissolving into a smoke of jokes. The point is that if anyone had really produced three or four verses comparable to the Qur'an, the enemies of Islam at least would have handed them down to us.

A different objection is raised nowadays: that the impossibility of imitating a book does not by itself make it the word of Allah or a miracle, since no one has successfully imitated Shakespeare or Ḥāfiẓ either. But a miracle, by definition, is something that occurs without the ordinary means being used. Every poet or writer, even the greatest, is known to have gone through education and training in his art and to have used methods that are humanly available. The Prophet ﷺ did not even know how to read or write and never cared to learn the arts of eloquence. Nor is it only a matter of literary style. In judging the Qur'an a miracle one must above all weigh its spiritual efficacy and transforming power, shown continuously for fourteen hundred years. A scholar of comparative religion, Frithjof Schuon, made the same point in 1970: the superhuman value of a revealed Book cannot be fully apparent from its earthly form or its conceptual content alone, because its divine and miraculous quality is of a different order from the most perfect dialectic or the most brilliant poetry. It shows first in a richness of meaning that cannot be imitated, and then in a kind of underlying divine "magic" that shines through the outward form and is proved by what it does in souls and in the world, across space and across time. Only that divine substance explains the spiritual power of the Qur'an's verses, the miraculously rapid spread of early Islam in the conditions in which it happened, the stability of Muslim institutions and the extraordinary fruitfulness of Islamic doctrine.

### A warning for the enemies and good news for the friends

Allah veiled the eyes of the outsiders, so His beloved ﷺ went unseen by them, and having lost the light of His care they wandered through the valleys of conjecture. Signs brought by the Messenger ﷺ and miracles shown to them only made their doubts and suspicions deeper. Such is the way of people who have turned from the Real: the clearer the proofs, the blinder they become to the truth, as Allah said, "neither signs nor warners are of any benefit to those who refuse to believe" (10:101). To force them to concede the proof, He told them that they were unable to compete with the miracle He had given them in the Qur'an, a miracle triumphant over mankind from first to last. He made them acknowledge that even if they joined forces, strengthened themselves with others like them, and spent every effort and stratagem, they could not bring a surah like any surah of the Qur'an. He said "if you are unable", then declared that they could not and would not, "and you will never be able". It is as if He said: look to yourselves, and beware the idolatry that leads to the punishment of the Fire fuelled by men and stones. When the believers' hearts were gripped with dread at the mention of the Fire, He steadied them by adding "prepared for the disbelievers", and in that is good news for the believers. This is the Real's way: when He frightens His enemies He gladdens His friends. The disbelievers' deceit vanishes before the prophets' miracles, and in the same way the pretensions of the confused melt away once the lights of the truthful shine out. The mark of the false claimant is that the driving cries return to his heart. The mark of the person of truthful intent is that the divine subjugation takes hold of his heart. Rare is the person who can tell these two apart.

Those whose hearts are attached to things other than Allah find the call of true religion repellent. They are so attached to their supposed deities that they cannot believe them unreal, and they cannot believe that the truth is what a mortal like themselves proclaims.

### In short

- "You will never be able to" is a prophecy made in the face of the Arabs' pride and hatred, and it has never been falsified. Its fulfilment is itself a miracle.
- The inimitability lies not only in style but in the Qur'an's truth, justice, freshness under repetition, and power to move and to transform.
- The Fire's fuel is the disbelievers and stones, sulphur or their own idols, and "prepared" shows that Hell exists already, as the hadiths of the falling stone and the Fire's two breaths confirm.
- The challenge covers the shortest surah, as Musaylimah's attempt showed, and every supposed answer to it, ancient or modern, has been a failure that the enemies of Islam themselves would have preserved had it been otherwise.
- The verse ends with a mercy: the Fire is "prepared for the disbelievers", which is a reassurance to those who believe.
