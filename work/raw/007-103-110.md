<<<VERSE 7:103>>>
After Nūḥ (Noah), Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lūṭ (Lot) and Shuʿayb comes the longest story in the surah: "Then after them We sent Moses with Our signs to Pharaoh and his chiefs, but they wrongfully rejected them. See what was the end of the corruptors". It is told in detail because the miracles of Mūsā (Moses) were more numerous and more striking than those of other prophets, and because his opponents were so stubborn.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then after them We sent Moses"**: after the messengers already mentioned, Allah sent Mūsā.

**"with Our signs"**: with proofs and clear evidence, nine signs in all. The signs may be the verses of the Torah or the miracles of Mūsā.

**"to Pharaoh and his chiefs"**: Pharaoh (Firʿawn) ruled Egypt in the time of Mūsā. The word was the title of the Egyptian kings, and the Pharaoh of Mūsā's time is said to have been Mernephtah. His chiefs were his people, his council and his court.

**"but they wrongfully rejected them."** Literally, "they did wrong by them". Injustice and stubbornness drove them to deny the signs and refuse belief. Instead of thanking Allah and believing, they treated His signs with indifference and rejected them. The Arabic word for wrong (ẓulm) means using a thing for the opposite of what it was made for. Another verse says, "And, although their hearts were convinced the signs were true, they still denied them wrongfully and arrogantly. See then what was the end of the corruptors" (27:14).

**"See what was the end of the corruptors"**: see how destruction was the end of those who spread corruption through disbelief. The āyah means: look how Allah punished those who blocked others from the path of Allah and denied His messengers. He drowned every one of them while Mūsā and his people watched. The public drowning added disgrace to the punishment of Pharaoh and his people, and comfort to the hearts of Allah's party, Mūsā and those who believed in him. Allah condemned them in this world and in the next. Once again people are invited to learn from these events and think about their own end.

### Why does a prophet speak first to the leaders?

A prophet's message is first addressed to the leaders of his day, those who lead the thinking of their society. With their sharper minds, such people are better placed to grasp the message of truth in all its depth. Yet history shows that they have nearly always opposed the prophet's call. They use their mental gifts to give the message of truth a false meaning.

### In short

- After the five earlier prophets, Allah sent Mūsā with nine signs to Pharaoh, the ruler of Egypt, and his chiefs.
- They rejected the signs wrongfully, against their own inner conviction, putting them to the opposite of their purpose.
- Their end was a public drowning that disgraced them and comforted the believers.
- The leaders a prophet first addresses are best able to understand him, yet they have usually been his fiercest opponents.

<<<VERSE 7:104>>>
The scene opens on a debate between Mūsā (Moses) and Pharaoh, in which Mūsā answers Pharaoh with decisive proof and clear miracles before Pharaoh and his people, the Copts of Egypt: "And Moses said, “O Pharaoh! I am truly a messenger from the Lord of all worlds". It is a plain declaration, made to Pharaoh's face, that he is the messenger of Allah, the Lord of all the worlds.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And Moses said, “O Pharaoh!"**: Mūsā spoke when Allah sent him to Pharaoh (Firʿawn).

**"I am truly a messenger from the Lord of all worlds"**: my Sender is the Maker of all things, their Lord and King, who owns and sustains everything. Pharaoh denied him and said, "You are lying!"

### Why should Pharaoh have believed him?

Mūsā wanted them to believe him because his truthfulness was beyond question. He had never spoken a single lie. Besides, his miracles were clear proof of his claim to prophethood.

### In short

- Mūsā declared before Pharaoh and his court that he was a messenger of the Lord of all the worlds, Creator and King of all things.
- Pharaoh called him a liar, though Mūsā's honesty was beyond doubt and his miracles were plain proof.

<<<VERSE 7:105>>>
Mūsā (Moses) explains what his messengership requires of him, and makes his demand: "obliged to say nothing about Allah except the truth. Indeed, I have come to you with clear proof from your Lord, so let the children of Israel go with me.”" His rank as a prophet does not allow him to attribute anything but the truth to Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"obliged to say nothing about Allah except the truth."** It is my duty, and only fitting for me, to convey nothing from Him but the truth, because of what I know of His might and power. Since I have been sent by Allah, I can only speak the truth about Him. In one reading the Arabic says ʿalā, "obliged", and in another ʿalayya, "it is binding on me". In that second reading "it is right" (ḥaqīqun) is the subject, and its predicate is "that I say nothing but the truth about Allah".

**"Indeed, I have come to you with clear proof from your Lord"**: I have brought decisive evidence that Allah gave me, to show that I am telling you the truth and that I am sent to you by Allah.

**"so let the children of Israel go with me.”"** Release them from your slavery and oppression, and from the misery you have put them through. Let them leave with me, with their belongings, great and small, so that they may worship your Lord and theirs. Some say he meant: send them with me to Syria. Pharaoh had enslaved them. They are the descendants of a noble prophet, Isrāʾīl, who is Yaʿqūb (Jacob), son of Isḥāq (Isaac), son of Ibrāhīm (Abraham), the Friend (Khalīl) of Allah. In the light of such clear signs, Pharaoh should have believed him and freed the children of Israel from his unlawful control.

### Why must a messenger speak only the truth about Allah?

The message Allah gives His prophets is a sacred trust, and tampering with it is a great sin. All of Allah's prophets are free of sin and could never do such a thing. Allah is the Truth, so it is not fitting for anyone who comes forward in Allah's name to speak of anything but truth and justice. If he did otherwise, he would lose the right to speak for Allah and would earn punishment instead of reward.

### Why did Mūsā ask for the children of Israel?

Mūsā was sent both to the children of Israel and to Pharaoh and his Coptic people. At that time the children of Israel had many weaknesses, but they basically supported Mūsā. Pharaoh and his people, with a few exceptions, rejected him. In the end, after forty years of calling them to Allah's command, Mūsā was ordered to leave Egypt with the children of Israel. He asked Pharaoh to let them leave the country, so that they could worship the one God in the open air of the desert.

### In short

- As a prophet, Mūsā was bound to say nothing about Allah but the truth, for the message is a sacred trust.
- He came with clear proof from Allah and asked Pharaoh to free the enslaved children of Israel, descendants of Yaʿqūb, son of Isḥāq, son of Ibrāhīm.
- After forty years of preaching, he was told to lead them out of Egypt to worship Allah freely.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:106>>>
Pharaoh, in his stubbornness, would listen to nothing, and answered with a challenge: "Pharaoh said, “If you have come with a sign, then bring it if what you say is true.”" In effect he told Mūsā (Moses): I will not believe what you have said, nor grant your request.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Pharaoh said, “If you have come with a sign"**: if you have brought a sign, as you claim.

**"then bring it"**: show it to me, so that we can see it.

**"if what you say is true.”"** If you are telling the truth in this, and if you really are a messenger, produce your proof so that we can know whether your claim is true.

### What lay behind the demand?

Pharaoh did not ask for a sign in order to believe. He had already said he would neither believe nor grant the request. His demand was meant to put Mūsā on the spot, in front of the court, in the hope that he would fail. It gave Mūsā the opening to show the signs Allah had given him.

### In short

- Pharaoh refused to believe Mūsā or free the children of Israel, and demanded a sign if Mūsā was truthful.
- The challenge, meant to embarrass Mūsā, became the occasion for his miracles before the whole court.

<<<VERSE 7:107>>>
Mūsā (Moses) answered Pharaoh's demand at once: "So Moses threw down his staff and—behold!—it became a real snake". This was the first of the signs.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So Moses threw down his staff"**: he cast his staff down on the ground.

**"and—behold!—it became a real snake"**: the word thuʿbān means a huge serpent. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported that Ibn ʿAbbās said it was a male snake, and as-Suddī and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk said the same. One explanation describes it as a huge, yellow, male serpent. The word mubīn, "real" or "manifest", shows that the miracle happened openly before the eyes of Pharaoh's people. It was not done secretly in a hidden or private place, as magicians usually work. It was an enormous snake, there for all to see.

### What happened when the staff became a serpent?

A report from Ibn ʿAbbās says the staff became a huge snake that opened its mouth and rushed at Pharaoh. When Pharaoh saw it coming, he leapt from his throne and cried out to Mūsā to take it away, and Mūsā did. Another historical report from Ibn ʿAbbās says that as the serpent moved toward Pharaoh, he jumped from his throne and sought shelter near Mūsā, and many of his courtiers died of fright.

As-Suddī said the snake opened its mouth and went for Pharaoh to swallow him. Its lower jaw rested on the ground and its upper jaw reached the top of the palace wall. Pharaoh was so terrified that he jumped up and wet himself, which had never happened to him before. He cried out, "Mūsā, take it away, and I will believe in you and let the children of Israel go with you." So Mūsā picked it up, and it became a staff again.

### Is such a transformation impossible?

A staff turning into a real serpent is not as impossible as it may seem. It is astonishing only because it is unusual. A miracle must be something unusual, beyond the power of ordinary people. Allah shows miracles through His prophets so that people may understand that the prophets have powers from Allah and are His true prophets.

### In short

- Mūsā threw down his staff, and it became a huge serpent in full view of Pharaoh's court.
- Terrified, Pharaoh leapt from his throne and begged Mūsā to take it away, promising belief and freedom for the Israelites.
- A miracle is not impossible, only unusual. It is a sign from Allah that confirms His prophet.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:108>>>
The second sign followed: "Then he drew his hand ˹out of his collar˺ and it was ˹shining˺ white for all to see". Mūsā (Moses) put his hand inside his garment and drew it out shining, not from leprosy or any sickness.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then he drew his hand"**: the verb nazaʿa means to pull something out of something else with force. It suggests that Mūsā drew his hand out with some effort. This verse does not say where he drew it from, but other verses do. One says, "Now put your hand through ˹the opening of˺ your collar" (27:12), and another, "And put your hand under your armpit" (20:22). So he drew his hand out either from under his arm or from inside his shirt, near his chest.

**"and it was ˹shining˺ white"**: the word bayḍāʾ means white. A hand can also turn white from disease. That is perhaps why other verses add "unblemished", literally "without harm" (28:32, 27:12), to remove any suspicion of illness. Ibn ʿAbbās said "without harm" means not from leprosy. When Mūsā put his hand back into his sleeve, it returned to its normal colour. Mujāhid and several others said the same. A tradition reported from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās says that the whiteness was no ordinary whiteness. It gave off a light that lit up everything around it. It was radiant, not the usual colour of his skin.

**"for all to see"**: the word nāẓirīn, "onlookers", suggests that the light so astonished people that they gathered to look at it.

### Why two miracles?

On this occasion, at Pharaoh's demand, Mūsā showed two miracles: the staff turning into a serpent, and the hand shining with light. The first served as a warning to the disbelievers, and the second as an invitation to the truth. The second also showed that Mūsā's message was a light, and that following it would lead people to lasting success.

### In short

- Mūsā drew his hand from his collar or from under his arm, and it shone white with a light that lit up its surroundings.
- The whiteness was not from leprosy or any illness, and the hand returned to normal when he put it back.
- The serpent was a warning and the shining hand an invitation, showing that his message was light.

<<<VERSE 7:109>>>
Pharaoh's court quickly found a label for what they had seen: "The chiefs of Pharaoh’s people said, “He is indeed a skilled magician". When his fright had passed and he was back on his throne, Pharaoh told his chiefs that Mūsā (Moses) was a skilled magician, and they agreed. In Sūrat al-Shuʿarāʾ the same words are spoken by Pharaoh himself, so it is as if they said them in consultation with him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The chiefs of Pharaoh’s people said"**: the word malaʾ means the influential chiefs, the notables and leaders. They spoke once they had seen the staff transformed and the hand turned white.

**"“He is indeed a skilled magician"**: a cunning sorcerer of great knowledge, outstanding and expert in magic. Knowing nothing of Allah's divine power, they could say nothing else. They believed Pharaoh was their god, and they had seen nothing in their lives but the tricks of sorcerers. Still, they added the word ʿalīm, "knowing", "skilled". This shows they felt Mūsā's miracles were of a kind no ordinary magician could perform. So they called him a sorcerer of great knowledge.

### How does a miracle differ from sorcery?

Miracles and sorcery differ so plainly in nature and effect that anyone with common sense can tell them apart.

- Sorcerers usually live in impurity, and the dirtier and more impure they are, the better their sorcery works. The prophets, by contrast, are by nature the cleanest and purest of people.
- A sorcerer never succeeds when he claims to be a prophet.
- The effects of sorcery have physical causes, like everything else, except that their causes are hidden from ordinary people, who therefore think they happen without any cause. Miracles are a direct display of Allah's power and have nothing to do with physical causes.

That is why the Qur'an attributes miracles directly to Allah and not to the prophets. The Qur'an says "but Allah threw" of the pebbles that the Prophet ﷺ threw at the Battle of Badr. In short, miracles and sorcery are entirely different. People of knowledge are never confused about this, and Allah has given clear signs of the difference so that ordinary people need not be confused either.

Even Pharaoh's people sensed that Mūsā's miracles were somehow unlike the usual work of sorcerers. So even while accusing him of sorcery, they admitted that he was "skilled", meaning his acts could not be compared with those of ordinary sorcerers.

### In short

- Pharaoh and his chiefs agreed in calling Mūsā a skilled magician, unable to imagine any power beyond sorcery.
- Calling him "skilled" admitted that his signs were beyond any ordinary magician.
- Sorcery works through hidden physical causes and impure practitioners. Miracles come directly from Allah through the purest of people.

### How do leaders discredit a call to truth?

The leaders of a prophet's day use their talents to give the message of truth a false meaning. They declare that a sign which came by Allah's intervention was produced by magic. Or, to belittle the prophetic movement, they give it a political colour and say that such preachers are only seeking power.
