<<<VERSE 7:103>>>
The five stories close and the longest account in the surah opens. "Then after them We sent Moses with Our signs to Pharaoh and his chiefs, but they wrongfully rejected them. See what was the end of the corruptors"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then after them We sent Moses with Our signs"** names the messenger who follows the five already told. The Book sent, after Nuh and Hud and Salih and Lut and Shu'ayb, Musa, with proofs and clear evidences. The signs may refer to the verses of the Torah that were given to him, or to the miracles that were placed in his hand, and in one reading they are counted as nine tokens.

**"to Pharaoh and his chiefs"** names the people he was sent to. The word Pharaoh was the title of the kings of Egypt, and the Pharaoh of the time of Musa is said to have been Mernephtah. His chiefs were the leading men of his people, the Copts of Egypt.

**"but they wrongfully rejected them"** states the answer they gave. The word used here for the wrong they did carries the sense of putting a thing to a use opposite to the one it was made for. Instead of being grateful to Allah for His verses and having faith in them, they turned cold towards them and refused them. Another verse describes the same response, that they belied those signs wrongfully and arrogantly, though they were themselves convinced of them (27:14).

**"See what was the end of the corruptors"** is the closing call to look. It was these very people who turned others away from the path of Allah and gave the lie to His messengers. What came to them was drowning, all of them together, while Musa and his people stood watching. That public drowning added disgrace to the punishment of Pharaoh and his people, and it added comfort to the hearts of the party of Allah.

The Ayah that carries this, verse 103 of this surah, asks the reader to look at how they were punished. The whole account that follows is set out as the answer to that call.

### In short

- Musa was sent after the five messengers already named, and he was sent with proofs and clear evidences.
- The signs given to him are taken either as the verses of the Torah or as his miracles, counted in one reading as nine tokens.
- Pharaoh was the title of the kings of Egypt, and the one of his time is named as Mernephtah by the historians.
- His people rejected the signs wrongfully and arrogantly, though they were convinced of them in their hearts.
- Their end was drowning in public, while Musa and the believers watched, and the verse calls the reader to look at it.

<<<VERSE 7:104>>>
The words of the messenger to the king are given now. "And Moses said, “O Pharaoh! I am truly a messenger from the Lord of all worlds"

### Phrase by phrase

**"And Moses said, “O Pharaoh! I am truly a messenger from the Lord of all worlds"** is his opening declaration. The One who sent him is the Creator, the Lord and the King of all things, and there is nothing outside His dominion. The account describes a debate that took place between Musa and Pharaoh, and the plainest of the matters raised in it was this claim of a message from the Lord of everything.

**"from the Lord of all worlds"** is the whole of the argument in a few words. A messenger does not come with a private word or with a claim of his own. He comes from the one who owns everything he is speaking about, and that is why the demand that follows carries weight.

Pharaoh did not accept the claim. In the report of his answer given in the Book, he told Musa plainly that he was lying, and the debate then turned on the proof rather than on the words. The matter was raised in the presence of Pharaoh and his people, and that is why the account insists on the open and public nature of everything that came next.

### In short

- Musa opened with a plain statement of who had sent him, the Creator and Lord of all things.
- The claim to a message from the Lord of all worlds is the foundation of everything he demands afterwards.
- Pharaoh met the claim with a flat denial and called him a liar.
- The exchange happened in front of Pharaoh's people, so the proofs that followed were seen by many.

<<<VERSE 7:105>>>
The demand that goes with the message is stated. "obliged to say nothing about Allah except the truth. Indeed, I have come to you with clear proof from your Lord, so let the children of Israel go with me.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"obliged to say nothing about Allah except the truth"** carries the sense of a duty laid on him by his position. Since the message given to the prophets is a sacred trust, it is a great sin to tamper with it, and every one of the prophets is free of sin and cannot do such a thing. What he says of Allah is therefore only what he was given to say, and no part of it can be adjusted to please a listener.

**"Indeed, I have come to you with clear proof from your Lord"** moves from the claim to the evidence. The proof he brought was an unequivocal sign that Allah had given him, and it stood as evidence that what he was conveying was the truth. Musa argued from two directions at once. His truthfulness was beyond question, since he had never spoken a lie in his life, and the miracles in his hand were a clear proof of the claim to prophethood.

To Pharaoh in the land of Egypt, **"so let the children of Israel go with me"** is the thing he asked for, and it is the demand that runs through the whole account. The demand was that they be released from slavery and from the treatment they had been under, and that they be allowed to leave with him. In one reading the words include their property, both the greater part of it and the smaller, and in another the destination is named as Syria. They were the offspring of an honourable prophet, Isra'il, who is Ya'qub the son of Ishaq the son of Ibrahim, the intimate friend of Allah, and the command was simply that they be allowed to worship their Lord and his Lord.

The children of Israel had spent a long time under the hand of the Egyptians. In one account the call of Musa in the land lasted forty years before the command came for the migration, and the request for their release was the first step of it.

### In short

- Musa states the duty that binds him, that a prophet says nothing of Allah except what he was given.
- The message is a sacred trust, and tampering with it is a sin the prophets are kept free of.
- He brings a clear proof, and he argues both from his own truthfulness and from the sign in his hand.
- He asks for the release of the Children of Israel from slavery so that they may worship their Lord.
- They were the descendants of Ya'qub, called Isra'il, the son of Ishaq and the son of Ibrahim.

<<<VERSE 7:106>>>
The answer of the king is obstinacy dressed as an offer to be convinced. "Pharaoh said, “If you have come with a sign, then bring it if what you say is true.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Pharaoh said"** introduces the reply of the king of Egypt to everything that had been said. He would not believe what he had heard, and he would not entertain the request that had been put to him.

**"If you have come with a sign, then bring it if what you say is true"** turns the matter into a test he sets. The words are the speech of a man who wants the proof produced so that he can judge the claim for himself, and the demand came out of his stubbornness rather than out of any readiness to accept the answer. There was no interest in the message itself, and no thought of the release that had been asked for. All that remained was the challenge, and the signs that followed were given in answer to it.

### In short

- Pharaoh refused both the message and the request that went with it.
- He demanded the sign be produced, so that he could judge the claim himself.
- The demand came out of obstinacy, not out of any readiness to believe.
- The proof that followed was given in direct answer to his challenge.

<<<VERSE 7:107>>>
The first sign is given in the sight of everyone present. "So Moses threw down his staff and—behold!—it became a real snake"

### Phrase by phrase

**"So Moses threw down his staff"** records the response to the challenge. He did what was asked of him and cast the staff on the ground.

**"and—behold!—it became a real snake"** describes what happened to it. The word used for the creature here means a huge serpent, and it is used of a male snake. Ibn Abbas explained it that way on the authority of Ali bin Abi Talhah, and As-Suddi and Ad-Dahhak said the same.

The description given of it was reported at length. On one account the staff became a serpent of great size. It opened its mouth and rushed towards Pharaoh. When he saw the snake coming at him, he jumped from his throne and cried out to Musa for help, asking him to take the creature out of his way, and Musa did so. As-Suddi gave the scene in more detail. The snake opened its mouth and headed for Pharaoh to swallow him, and it put its lower jaw on the ground while its upper jaw reached the top of the wall of the palace. At the sight of it Pharaoh was so frightened that he jumped up and lost control of himself, which had never happened to him before that moment. He cried out that Musa should take it away, and that he would believe in him and release the Children of Israel to him. Musa took the snake, and it turned back into a staff.

In the historical traditions reported on the authority of Ibn Abbas, the movement of the serpent towards Pharaoh was so violent that many of the courtiers around him died of extreme fear.

**Why the miracle was described as clear.** The word used as the description of the snake means clear or prominent, and the choice of it is telling. The event took place openly before the eyes of the people of Pharaoh, and it was not performed in a hidden place in the manner of the magicians. The transformation of a staff into a real serpent is not in itself impossible, and what makes it astonishing is that it is unusual. A miracle has to be an act beyond the power of an ordinary person, and Allah shows such acts through His prophets so that people may understand that they carry a divine support and are true prophets of Allah.

### In short

- Musa cast down the staff as the answer to the challenge, and it became a huge male serpent.
- Ibn Abbas, As-Suddi and Ad-Dahhak all described the creature as a male snake, and the reports are agreed on its size.
- On the reported account the serpent rushed at Pharaoh, whose terror made him jump from his throne and call on Musa to remove it.
- In As-Suddi's report its lower jaw rested on the ground while its upper jaw reached the palace wall, and Pharaoh promised to believe and to release the Children of Israel.
- The word describing it means clear, since the sign was worked openly before the eyes of the people and not in the manner of the magicians.

<<<VERSE 7:108>>>
The second sign follows at once. "Then he drew his hand ˹out of his collar˺ and it was ˹shining˺ white for all to see"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then he drew his hand"** uses a word that means pulling something out of another thing with force. Musa applied some strength as he brought his hand out. The verse does not name the place he drew it from, and other verses supply it. In one place the command is to put the hand under the robe (27:12), and in another to put it under the arm (20:22), so the hand came out from under his arm or from under his shirt.

**"and it was ˹shining˺ white for all to see"** describes the state of the hand. The word means white, and whiteness of the hand can also come from a disease. That is why the Book says in two other places that the hand comes out white without hurt, which removes any suspicion of leprosy (27:12 and 28:32). Ibn Abbas said that the words without hurt mean that the change was not caused by leprosy, and Mujahid and several others said the same. Musa put his hand back into his sleeve and it returned to its normal colour.

A tradition reported from Ibn Abbas adds what kind of whiteness this was. It was not the whiteness of an ordinary change of colour. It carried a light that lit up everything around it, and the word used for those who saw it means the onlookers, which shows how striking the sight was, since people gathered to look at it.

**The two signs together.** Musa performed two miracles at that meeting in answer to the demand of Pharaoh. The first was the change of the staff into a serpent, and the second was the drawing out of his hand with light coming from it. The first worked as a warning for those who would not believe, and the second was set out as an invitation to the truth, since it showed that the message of Musa was a light, and that following it would lead people to lasting success.

### In short

- The word for drawing out the hand carries the sense of pulling it out with force.
- Other verses place the hand under the robe or under the arm, and the Book leaves the exact place unstated here.
- The whiteness was not of an ordinary kind, since the reports from Ibn Abbas say it carried a light that lit up the surroundings.
- The Book adds the words without hurt in other places to remove the thought of leprosy, and the hand returned to its normal colour afterwards.
- The serpent served as a warning and the shining hand as an invitation, and the two together showed that the message was light.

<<<VERSE 7:109>>>
The court takes up the challenge and puts a name to what it has seen. "The chiefs of Pharaoh’s people said, “He is indeed a skilled magician"

### Phrase by phrase

**"The chiefs of Pharaoh’s people said"** names the speakers. The word used for them means the influential chiefs and the leading men, and the noblemen of his people agreed with the judgement Pharaoh had passed.

**"He is indeed a skilled magician"** is the verdict they reached. They were ignorant of the divine power of Allah and had seen nothing in their lives but the charms of the sorcerers, so there was nothing else they could say. Their addition of the word meaning knowing shows the impression the signs had made on them, since they were admitting that what Musa had done could not be performed by an ordinary magician. They were saying that he was a sorcerer of great knowledge.

**How a miracle differs from sorcery.** The two are distinct in their nature and their effect, and the difference does not need much explaining to anyone who applies common sense. The sorcerers usually live in impurity, and the more unclean and impure they are, the more successful they are held to be in their sorcery, while the prophets are by nature the cleanest and purest of people. Another clear distinction is that a sorcerer never succeeds when he makes a claim to prophethood. Then there is the working of the acts themselves. What is done under sorcery does have physical causes like everything else, and the only difference is that those causes are hidden from ordinary people, who then take the effect to be happening without any cause at all. Miracles are a direct manifestation of the power of Allah and have nothing to do with physical causes. That is why the Qur'an ascribes them directly to Allah and not to the prophets, and why it says of the pebbles thrown at Badr that it was Allah who threw them.

So the people of Pharaoh themselves felt that the signs of Musa were not like the ordinary acts of sorcerers. They went on accusing him of sorcery, and in the same breath they admitted that he was of great knowledge. That admission means they knew his act was not on the level of what the sorcerers did.

### What the chiefs did next

After Pharaoh had recovered himself and returned to his throne, he said to the leading men of his people that Musa was a well-versed sorcerer, and they agreed with him. They then took counsel among themselves about what should be done. They conspired to put out the light he had brought and to bring his word down. They plotted to present him as a liar and an impostor, and what they feared was that he would draw people to his side by what they called his magic, and so overcome them and drive them out of their land. What they feared came to pass, exactly as another verse says, that Allah let Pharaoh and Haman and their hosts receive from them the very thing they were afraid of (28:6).

### In short

- The leading men of Pharaoh agreed with his judgement and described Musa as a sorcerer of great knowledge.
- Their word knowing shows they admitted the act was beyond what an ordinary magician could do.
- Sorcery works through hidden physical causes and sorcerers live in impurity, while miracles are the direct power of Allah with no cause behind them but His will.
- No sorcerer has ever succeeded in making a claim to prophethood, and the Book ascribes miracles to Allah alone, as with the pebbles at Badr.
- The chiefs made common cause with Pharaoh on the matter, and what they feared most was that Musa would take the people and the land from them.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:110>>>
The verdict is given its political shape, and the king turns to his advisers. "who seeks to drive you from your land.” ˹So Pharaoh asked,˺ “What do you propose?”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"who seeks to drive you from your land"** is the charge they built on the name of sorcery. The land is Egypt, and the claim was that Musa intended by his magic to drive them all out of it. In one reading the thought was put more plainly still, that he wanted to remove them from the land and take it for himself and his people.

**"What do you propose?"** is Pharaoh's question to the chiefs, and in another reading the words are what do you command, which is the speech of a king who wants his advisers to name the measure rather than decide it themselves. The consultation that followed was the planning of a plot, and the verses that come after this one report what they settled on.

### What the passage shows about the leaders of a people

A prophet's discourse is addressed first to the leaders of the day, to those who hold the leadership of thought in their surroundings. Such people are often better placed than anyone else to understand the message in its depth, since they have the minds for it. What history shows instead is that they are almost always set against the call.

The way they work is worth setting out. They use their talents to put a wrong meaning into the message. A sign that came by the intervention of God is declared to have come by the force of magic, and to turn people further away, the whole movement is given a political colour, with the claim that these preachers are simply seeking power. The general public cannot examine such talk on its own, and the result is that they grow suspicious of the truth they were being offered.

To raise such points against the messenger of the truth is a great crime. The leading men of a people may hold on to their leadership by it, but the price they pay is the Hereafter, since God is the Truth, and a man who comes forward in the name of God has no room to speak of anything else. Such a man is bound to truth and to justice. Whoever does otherwise has lost the right to represent God and deserves punishment rather than reward.

Musa had been sent to the Children of Israel and also to Pharaoh and his community of Copts. The Children of Israel had many weaknesses at that time, but they stood with Musa in the main. Pharaoh and his people, apart from a few of them, refused him. At the end of the whole struggle, after the years of calling them, Musa was commanded to leave Egypt with the Children of Israel, and he asked Pharaoh to let them go so that they could pray to the one and only God in the open air. Musa was the bearer of the truth, and Pharaoh counted him a mere worker of magic and set out to overcome him through his own magicians.

### In short

- The charge against Musa was that he wanted to drive the Egyptians out of their own land.
- Pharaoh put the question of a measure to his advisers, and the consultation was the beginning of a plot.
- A prophet speaks first to the leading men of his people, since they are the ones best placed to weigh what he says.
- Those leaders usually meet the call by twisting it, calling the sign magic, and giving the movement a political colour.
- The general public, unable to examine the charge, is left suspicious, while the leaders keep their standing at the cost of the Hereafter.
- Musa was sent to the Children of Israel and to Pharaoh's people together, and Pharaoh set out to meet him with his own magicians.
