<<<VERSE 6:104>>>
Indeed, there have come to you insights from your Lord. So whoever chooses to see, it is for their own good. But whoever chooses to be blind, it is to their own loss. And I am not a keeper over you

The passage that began with rain and fruit now turns to the eyes of the person watching, and to what seeing costs.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There have come to you insights from your Lord"** The word is the plural of a noun for reason, intelligence and insight, and it names the power by which a person acquires knowledge of things that do not fall inside the range of the senses. On this verse it means the evidences and the avenues through which truth and reality can be known. Proof and evidence arrive here in the Qur'an and in the message of Allah's Messenger. The sense is that the means of seeing the truth have reached you from Allah. The Qur'an has come. The Prophet has come. His miracles have stood open, and his morals, his dealings and his teaching have been watched in the daylight. Say to them, as another reading of the verse begins it: clear proofs have come to you from your Lord.

**"Whoever chooses to see, it is for their own good"** Whoever perceives these things and believes, the reward that comes of his seeing is his own. Whoever understands them and is mindful, the benefit of it returns to him. It is the same sentence that occurs elsewhere: "whoever receives guidance does it for his own good, and whoever goes astray does so at his own loss" (10:108).

**"Whoever chooses to be blind, it is to their own loss"** He blinds himself, and so he only harms himself, since the evil consequence of going astray falls on the one who goes astray. That is the plain meaning of the verse that says: "it is not the eyes that grow blind, but it is the hearts which are in the breasts that grow blind" (22:46).

**"And I am not a keeper over you"** The word means one responsible for you, or a watcher over you. I am not set to guard you or to protect you, and I am not a keeper of your deeds. I am only a warner, and my duty is to convey. Allah guides whom he wills and leaves whom he wills to go astray. It is not the duty of the messenger to force people away from what is improper, the way someone appointed to oversee them would do it, or the way self-appointed vigilantes do in our own day. The work of a messenger is to convey the commands and to explain them. After that, whether the message is followed or not is a matter for the people it was sent to, and the responsibility is theirs. He is the one who watches over you.

### Vision, insight, and what separates them

The verse before this one used two words that decide the shape of these five verses of Surah Al-An'am. The plural rendered as vision means sight and the ability to see, and the verb rendered as encompass means to reach, grasp, perceive or take in. Ibn Abbas explained the second of the two at that place as comprehension, as Al-Bahr Al-Muhit records, so that the earlier sentence means this. The whole of the created ability to see, put together, the sight of jinn and human beings and angels and every other living thing, could never see Allah in a way that takes in his being. As for him, he watches the vision of every creature completely, and his seeing reaches all of them. That is why a report preserved from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri puts it in this form: if every human being, jinn, angel and shaytan from the first to the last stood in a single row, their combined sights still could not take in his being. It is entered in Mazhari with a reference to Ibn Abi Hatim. So unique an attribute cannot belong to anyone but the most exalted being. No vision in the whole universe reaches him, not even the joined eyesight of everyone who has ever lived.

Two attributes of Allah are named in those two clauses, and the passage on them is a long one. First, he is imperceivable, and second, he is all-perceiving, since nothing anywhere, not the smallest particle, is hidden from him. Knowledge with that reach is his alone, and no created being has ever arrived at it or ever will. The scholars of Ahl al-Sunnah wa al-Jamaʿah, the majority of Muslims who follow the sunnah of the Prophet and the way of his companions, are agreed that his being cannot be seen in the life of this world. That is why Musa asked to be shown his Lord, and the answer he was given was that he would never see him, and the answer was given to no lesser prophet (7:143). After Allah mentioned the proofs, he said that whoever blinds himself does so against himself, and that he is not a Hafiz over them.

Yet the vision is promised. Faces on that day will be glowing, looking toward their Lord (75:22-23), and the disbelievers and the deniers will not have that honour, and that refusal is itself their punishment: surely on that day they are veiled from their Lord (83:15). The honour of seeing him comes in the Hereafter at more than one place, on the Day of Gathering and afterwards when the people of Jannah have arrived. When they have entered Jannah, Allah will ask them whether they want any blessing beyond what they have already been given, and they will answer that he saved them from Jahannam and admitted them into Jannah, so what else is there to ask for. At that moment the veil will be lifted, they will be given the honour of seeing him, and of all the blessings of Jannah that will be the greatest, as the report in the Sahih of Muslim says, coming from Suhayb. A hadith in the Sahih of al-Bukhari has the Prophet sitting in the moonlight with his noble companions around him, looking at the moon and saying that they will see their Lord with their own eyes in the Hereafter as they are seeing that moon. In the collection of Tirmidhi and in the Musnad of Ahmad, from Ibn ʿUmar, people given special ranks in Jannah will be granted the sight of him every morning and every evening. So in this world of mortal life no one sees him, and in the next life everyone in Jannah does. The honour is granted at more than one point in the Hereafter, on the Day of al-Mahshar as well as after the arrival in Jannah. The Prophet's own visit on the night of the Miʿraj was in reality the visit that belongs to the Akhirah, since, as Shaykh Muhiyuddin ibn al-ʿArabi put it, the world is what the skies enclose, and beyond it is the domain of the Hereafter, where a sight of him cannot be called a sight in this world.

So how does the verse that says no vision can encompass him stand with all these texts? Because it does not say that seeing him is impossible for human beings. It says that human vision cannot comprehend or take in his being, since his being is unlimited and their vision is limited. Even the sight given in the Akhirah will be a seeing that never fully encompasses, while in this world neither a person nor his eyes have the strength to bear even that much of it. What the two verses put together, then, is that no eye reaches him and nothing escapes him, and the two names that close them carry the same pair. He is the Most Subtle and the All-Aware. The first of the names has two meanings in the language, kind, and fine or subtle as against thick and coarse, that is, what cannot be perceived by the senses at all. The second means all-aware. He is subtle, so the senses cannot take him in, and he is aware, so nothing in the universe lies outside his knowledge. The first of these names, al-Latif, has two meanings in the language, kind, and fine or subtle as against what is thick and coarse, that is, what cannot be perceived by the senses at all. The second, al-Khabir, means all-aware. And if al-Latif is taken in its other meaning, the sentence becomes something else again. He is aware of everything said and done, and even of what a person intends, thinks and feels, which would be enough to seize him for every sin. But he is kind as well, and so he does not call anyone to account for every single sin.

### In short

- Insights are read as the means of seeing, the Qur'an, the Prophet, his miracles and the open sight of his character.
- Whoever sees or stays blind is answering for himself, since the profit and the loss both land on the person doing it.
- The Prophet is not a keeper over them, which is stated as the limit of what a messenger is given to do.
- The verse about vision not encompassing him is resolved by the distinction between seeing and taking in, and the resolution is reported with its hadiths and its named collectors.
- The two closing names, Most Subtle and All-Aware, are read both as a statement about perception and as a statement about mercy.

<<<VERSE 6:105>>>

The same signs are set out in more than one way, and the verse says plainly what some people will make of it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And so We vary our signs"** The revelations have been explained in this surah, in its making plain of tawhid and of the fact that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah. In the same way the signs are explained and made clear in every kind of situation. The variety in the way an argument is presented is there so that the ignorant are given enough to end their ignorance. Promises, threats and advice are all turned over in this manner, and the various proofs of power are set out one after another.

**"to the extent that they will say: You have studied"** So that the idolaters and the disbelievers who deny you will say: O Muhammad, you have studied with those who came before you from among the People of the Book, and you have learned with them. The word they use is a verb meaning recited, argued and debated, as Ibn Abbas explained it in a report At-Tabarani brings through ʿAmr bin Kaysan. Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Saʿid bin Jubayr and Ad-Dahhak give the same account. Other readings take the clause the other way. On them the varying of the signs is done precisely so that they will not say you got these traits from another person. One such reading has it mean that no one will say you learned it from Abu Fukayhah, a client of the Quraysh, or from Jabr and Yasar, two other clients of the Quraysh. It is also taken to mean that these are old stories. Their claim, in one report, is made as a denial of revelation altogether, since it is not revelation but something you learnt from the People of the Scripture before you.

The accusation is old and it is stated at length elsewhere: "those who disbelieve say: this is nothing but a lie that he has invented, and others have helped him at it. Tales of the ancients, which he has written down, and they are dictated to him morning and afternoon" (25:4-5). The same word was used about the Prophet by the chief liar of the disbelievers, al-Walid bin al-Mughirah al-Makhzumi. Of him it was said: "he thought and plotted. Then he turned back and was proud" (74:18-25). And: "this is nothing but magic from that of old, this is nothing but the word of a human being".

**"And We make this clear for people who know"** So that the matter is explained to a people who know truth and follow it, and know falsehood and avoid it, and who believe that it is from Allah. The purpose of the variety is that they take heed. Allah's wisdom is perfect here. He lets the disbelievers go astray, and he guides the people who have knowledge, and the same pair appears again and again in the Qur'an. "By it he misleads many, and many he guides" (2:26). "He makes what Shaytan throws in a trial for those in whose hearts is a disease and whose hearts are hardened" (22:53). "Allah is the Guide of those who believe, to the straight path" (22:54). The number of the guardians of the Fire was fixed for the same reason. It was "a trial for the disbelievers, so that those given the Book may arrive at a certainty and the believers may increase in faith" (74:31). "We send down in the Qur'an that which is a healing and a mercy to the believers, and it increases the wrongdoers in nothing but loss" (17:82). "It is for those who believe a guide and a healing, and as for those who disbelieve, there is heaviness in their ears, and it is blindness for them. They are those who are called from a place far away" (41:44).

### Why the two classes of hearers matter

Aid to guidance of every sort was placed in front of everyone, the miracles, the signs, the proofs and the matchless Qur'an itself. Before that stood the sight of an unlettered man expressing, in speech nobody could imitate, volumes of knowledge and of reality, speech that has left the wise of the world wondering. The Word of Allah could not be matched even to the size of one small surah, and the challenge to jinn and human beings stands valid until the end of time. Anyone hearing that treasure of truth, even the most hardened denier, should have fallen at his feet. But people who were crooked as a habit said instead that he had been tutored.

The last clause of the verse sorts the audience into two. The statement proved beneficial for those who were understanding, sensible and wise, while the crooked took no advantage of what was offered them, and the wise took it up heartily and became the leaders of their world. Two kinds of people are described in the same way from another angle. Some have the urge to learn and are in search of the truth. Others have acquired wealth and power and count themselves so successful that nothing is lacking in them which needs to be supplied. When the call of truth comes, it is mostly the first kind who accept it, since the second kind attach no significance to it, never give it serious thought, and so its importance never becomes clear to them. For the first kind, the aim is to help them accept the truth. For the others, the aim is to make them concede that the message reached them in full.

### In short

- The variation in the signs is explained as a mercy with a second edge, since the same words heal one people and harden another.
- The charge of having been tutored is answered by naming the people it was made by, and by the older accusations it repeats.
- One reading takes the clause in reverse, so that the varying of the signs is what keeps the accusation from arising.
- The verse's two ends, the accusation and the clearing up, are both described as purposes, and the entry reports them side by side.
- Knowledge of truth is defined by what a person does with it, following it and avoiding what is false.

<<<VERSE 6:107>>>
Had Allah willed, they would not have been polytheists. We have not appointed you as their keeper, nor are you their maintainer

The verse that takes the weight of their refusal off the Prophet's shoulders, and says why.

### Phrase by phrase

No one would have taken anything besides him in worship. His will and his wisdom are perfect in every decree and every decision, and he is never questioned about what he does, while they will all be questioned. There is a wisdom behind the idolaters being left to go astray. Had he willed, in a way that answered to the way he makes things, the whole human race becoming Muslim, then shirk would have been impossible for them. Those who reject a message invent excuses for doing it, and a preacher watching that may feel he ought to change the way he puts his case so that it becomes acceptable to the people in front of him. That would not be right. He keeps to the style he was taught directly, because the work he is doing is to link a person with Allah, not to win him over by one means or another. This was revealed before the command to fight them, which is why the address here stays with patience and with the limits of the messenger's job.

- The clause on God's will is reported as an answer to grief over refusal, not as a licence to stop preaching.
- Keeper and maintainer are glossed line by line, watcher, guardian, disposer of affairs, and the negation covers all of them.
- Force is excluded expressly, since the Prophet is not given power to compel anyone into faith.
- The verse is placed in the time before the command to fight, as the material dates it.

<<<VERSE 6:108>>>
˹O believers!˺ Do not insult what they invoke besides Allah or they will insult Allah spitefully out of ignorance. This is how We have made each people’s deeds appealing to them. Then to their Lord is their return, and He will inform them of what they used to do

A prohibition that comes out of an incident, and then a rule that is drawn out of the prohibition.

### What happened before the verse came down

The verse was revealed about a particular event, and it gives guidance in a matter of principle at the same time. The account is recorded by Ibn Jarir. When Abu Talib, the uncle of the Holy Prophet, lay on his deathbed, the disbelieving chiefs of Quraysh grew uneasy. They had been hostile to the Prophet and had plotted against his life, and they were afraid of what his uncle's death would mean. They reasoned that their own honour would suffer if they killed Muhammad after the man who protected him had died. People would say that they had not dared to touch him while Abu Talib lived, and had fallen on his helpless nephew the moment he was gone. So they agreed to go to Abu Talib in a body and put the matter to him decisively.

Abu Talib had not embraced Islam, and yet he loved his nephew dearly, respected him in his heart as the bearer of a divine message, and defended him against his enemies. The chiefs chose a man called Muttalib to ask for the appointment, and he went to Abu Talib and brought the deputation in to him. Abu Sufyan, Abu Jahl, ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAs and others were among them. They said to him: you are our elder and one of our chiefs, and you know that your nephew has caused great pain to the idols we worship. Speak to him and prevail on him to say nothing offensive against them. On those terms we will make peace: he does as he wishes in his faith, he takes whoever he chooses as the object of his worship, and we raise no objection.

Abu Talib called the Prophet and told him that the chiefs of his clan were there, and asked what they wanted. They said: leave us and our idols alone, say nothing ill of them, and we will leave you and your object of worship alone, and the hostility between us will end. He asked them, in turn, whether they would accept one sentence, a sentence by which they would become masters of the whole of Arabia and be obeyed even beyond it. Abu Jahl answered that they were being offered a single sentence, and they would say ten like it, so tell us what it is. He said: there is no god worthy of worship except Allah. That infuriated the whole gathering. Abu Talib said to his nephew: say some other sentence than this one, because it has thrown your clansmen into confusion. He answered that he could not say anything else, and that even if they brought him the sun from the sky and put it in his hands he would not say anything else but that. He meant, it is explained, to close off their hope entirely. Still angry, they said: either you stop speaking ill of our idols, or we will start using insulting words against you, and against the being you claim to be sent by. Then the verse came down forbidding it. Ibn Abbas gave the same account of the occasion, in the report brought through ʿAli bin Abi Talhah: the disbelievers said to him, stop insulting our gods, or we will insult your Lord. Qatadah reported it the same way, in the account Ma'mar took from him. Muslims had been reviling the idols of the disbelievers, and the disbelievers reviled Allah in return, wrongly and out of ignorance, until the verse came down.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not insult what they invoke besides Allah"** The verb is drawn from a word for abusing someone or using insulting language against him, and not in the sense of mistreating or corrupting. The prohibition runs from the Prophet to the believers. It covers the false things they worship, even though those things deserve nothing else, because the idolaters would answer by insulting Allah. They would do it out of aggression and wrongdoing, through their ignorance of him. The Prophet's own speech had never been touched by abuse, and he was already living by this rule before it was revealed. Even in childhood no abusive word for any human being, or even for an animal, had come from him. Some harsh word may have escaped the lips of a Companion here or there. The disbelievers of Makkah exaggerated it into an invective, and the chiefs of Quraysh made it a pretext when they brought the case to a head.

**"This is how We have made each people's deeds appealing to them"** Just as he made the idolaters find it fair to love their idols and defend them, so he made every earlier nation find it fair to go on in the misguidance they were sunk in. In another reading, as he adorned for these people the deeds they practise, he adorns for every community its good deeds and its bad, and they go on doing them. Allah's proof is the most perfect and his wisdom the most complete in everything he wills and chooses.

**"Then to their Lord is their return, and He will inform them of what they used to do"** Their gathering place and their final destination are with him on the Day of Rising. He will tell them what they did and pay them back for it, good for good and evil for evil.

### Why the address changed

The verse before these two had been spoken to the Prophet himself, in sentences such as "follow what has been revealed to you from your Lord" and "we have not made you to stand guard over them". In this verse the form of address changes. The prohibition is turned away from him and given to the Muslims in general. The hint drawn from that, in Al-Bahr Al-Muhit, is that a man who had never used abusive speech against anyone could not be addressed directly with a prohibition of it, since that would have discouraged him. So the sentence was made general, and the companions too began to take care in this matter.

An objection comes at once, since the Qur'an speaks sharply about idols in several places, and those verses are not abrogated, since they are still recited. One answer is that such words are there as a way of arguing a case so that a reality is made clear. No aggression is intended at those places, and no sensible person would take them as an attempt to malign idols, to mock the disbelievers or to provoke them. Every language has these nuances, and people who know them understand the difference. A person's shortcoming is questioned in order to bring out the point at issue. It is common practice in courts, and nobody treats what is said before a court as abuse of someone. The same kind of defect is described to physicians, and what would count as an insult if said outside would not count as one there. So when the Qur'an describes the helplessness of idols, their lack of sense, consciousness, knowledge and power, it uses the manner of speech that lets understanding reach reality and exposes the error of those who refuse to see. "Weak are the seekers and weak the sought" (22:73). "You and what you worship besides Allah are fuel for Hell" (21:98). The purpose is not to speak ill of someone but to state where straying ends.

The jurists have carried this further. If a man recites such a verse with the intention of using it as a weapon to irritate disbelievers, then that recitation, at that time and for that purpose, falls under the abuse that is prohibited, and it is not permissible for him. It is like the recitation of the Qur'an at the times and in the places that are makruh, which is known to everyone, as Ruh al-Ma'ani records. No abusive word was ever spoken by the Holy Prophet, and none appears in the Qur'an, nor was there any fear that either could happen. What did exist was the probability that Muslims in general might act on impulse, and the verse stopped them from that.

### Becoming the cause of a sin

From this verse a general rule is drawn. When a thing that is in itself permissible, and sometimes even commendable, leads to corruption or lets people into sin, then doing it becomes prohibited. Identifying the idols as false objects of worship is at least permissible, and when said out of concern for religious feeling it might even be worthy of reward. But since it raised the fear that Allah himself would be insulted in return, and since the abusers of the idols would then be the cause of that evil, the act that had been allowed was closed.

The same rule is reported in a hadith. No one, the Prophet said, should abuse his own parents, and the companions asked how a man could abuse his parents. He answered: he abuses another man's father, and that man abuses his, and he abuses his mother, and that man abuses his. So a person can bring about the very thing that is cursed, without ever doing it himself. A third case happened in the Prophet's own lifetime, and it concerns the Ka'bah. He said to A'ishah that the Baytullah had fallen on its foundations in an accident during the time before Islam. Quraysh of Makkah rebuilt it before his prophethood, and in the rebuilding some details went against the original foundation laid by Ibrahim. One of them is the part now known as Hatim, which is part of the Baytullah and was left outside only because the money ran short. The building had had two doors, an eastern and a western, one for entering and one for leaving. The people of that time closed the western one and raised the door that was left above the ground, so that entry depended on their permission and nobody could go in freely. He said that he wished he could take the present structure down and rebuild it on Ibrahim's foundation. But he gave up the idea because his people, the ordinary Arabs who had only just entered Islam, might be shaken in their faith by the sight of the Ka'bah being pulled down. Rebuilding on the foundation of Ibrahim Khalilullah would have been an act of obedience and a deed of merit, and yet it was abandoned for the danger that lay behind it.

A strong doubt about the rule is reported from Abu Mansur in Ruh al-Ma'ani. Allah has made jihad binding on Muslims, and killing is its inevitable instrument, and when a Muslim kills some of the enemy, the enemy will kill Muslims, and killing a Muslim is haram. On this principle jihad would have to be prohibited. The same would follow for the propagation of Islam and the recitation of the Qur'an, and for the adhan and the salah, all of which are mocked and ridiculed by many disbelievers. Shall the right to worship and to proclaim the religion be surrendered?

Abu Mansur answered his own doubt by pointing out a condition that had been left out. A permissible act is prohibited for the evil it may lead to only where that act is not one of the basic aims of Islam, and not one of its duties. Speaking ill of false objects of worship serves no aim of the religion, and no aim of the religion depends on rebuilding the Ka'bah on Ibrahim's foundations. But where an act is itself an aim of Islam, or an aim of Islam depends on it, then the act is never surrendered, however much the wrongdoing of others seems to be producing harm around it. Instead, the permissible act is kept where it belongs, and steps are taken to shut the door against the evil and its causes as far as that can be done.

This is why two men of the early community acted as they did. Hasan al-Basri and Muhammad Ibn Sirin went out to attend the salah for a funeral, and when they saw a crowd of men and women mixed together, Ibn Sirin turned back. Hasan al-Basri said: why should we abandon what is necessary because of other people's corruption? The prayer over the dead is fard, and it cannot be given up because a corrupting thing is present. What we have to do is try, as far as we can, to root that evil out. The sum of the rule is this: a permissible act, even an act of obedience and merit, which is not a binding aim of the religion, is to be given up when evil will certainly follow from it, while the aims of the sacred law are never given up because evil is feared. The jurists of the community have settled thousands of questions with it. Two worked examples belong to it. If a man has a disobedient son and knows that any direct order from him will be refused and even deliberately defied, so that the boy will incur a fresh sin, the father should not give him orders, and should say instead, in the tone of good advice, that it would be nice if such and such a job were done, so that the son carries no additional sin if he refuses. The same applies to advice of any kind. Where the signs show that a man is likelier to take a worse course than to accept counsel, and will sink deeper in sin for the sake of arguing, then it is better to leave the advice ungiven, and al-Bukhari has a chapter in his Sahih on exactly this subject.

There are moments when what is permissible, even what is praiseworthy, is set aside so that ordinary people do not fall into misunderstanding, on the condition that the act is not one of the core aims of Islam. Where the acts are core, whether fard, or wajib, or a Sunnah al-Mu'akkadah, an emphasized sunnah, or one of the outward marks of the religion, they are not abandoned because people of little understanding take them wrongly. Misunderstanding and misbehaviour are met by other means instead. The early history proves the point. The disbelievers of Makkah were provoked by the salah, by the recitation of the Qur'an and by the preaching of Islam, and none of the standing marks of the religion was ever dropped because of it. The very episode behind this verse is the sharpest evidence. The chiefs of Quraysh offered a guarantee of peace if the Prophet would stop calling people to tawhid, to saying that Allah is one, and his answer was that he could not do it even if they brought him the sun and the moon and put them in his hands.

### In short

- The prohibition is traced to a specific negotiation at Abu Talib's deathbed, and to an ultimatum about the idols.
- The rule under it is that becoming the cause of a sin is itself a sin, and the entry gives the three cases the material uses to teach it.
- The condition that answers the doubt about jihad is reported in the same words it came in, since the objection and its reply belong together.
- Insult and argument are kept apart by the difference between a court, a clinic and a quarrel.
- The verse ends by handing the whole matter to Allah, since every community finds its own conduct fair, and the return is to him.
