<<<VERSE 7:130>>>
The next verses tell of the calamities that struck Pharaoh and his people one after another, until they finally led to their death. The first punishment from heaven, after Pharaoh rejected Mūsā (Moses), was a famine: "Indeed, We afflicted Pharaoh’s people with famine and shortage of crops so they might come back ˹to their senses˺". According to historical reports, this famine lasted seven years.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We afflicted Pharaoh’s people"**: Allah tested and tried them, and seized them.

**"with famine"**: literally "with years", meaning years of drought and hunger, one year after another, with little produce.

**"and shortage of crops"**: the failure and disappearance of fruit and crops. Mujāhid said this was the lighter of the two. Rajāʾ ibn Ḥaywah said that a date palm would bear only a single date. Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatādah said the drought struck the people of the countryside, while the loss of fruit struck those living in cities and towns.

**"so they might come back ˹to their senses˺"**: so that they might take heed and become believers. They might reflect, realise that this was a punishment from Allah for their disbelief, and repent to Him.

### Why does hardship come first?

The famine was a warning, not yet the final blow. As with earlier peoples, Allah first sent hardship so that they might humble themselves and return to Him. It gave Pharaoh's people the chance to recognise that their land, their harvest and their very survival lay in Allah's hands, not Pharaoh's. Whatever Pharaoh might say about his power, the failed harvests answered him.

### In short

- The first of the punishments on Pharaoh's people was years of drought and failed crops, lasting seven years by some reports.
- Drought struck the countryside and loss of fruit struck the towns, until a palm bore only one date.
- The aim was that they might take heed, recognise Allah's punishment and repent.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:131>>>
When a people are marked by Allah's anger, they lose the ability to tell right from wrong. So it was with Pharaoh's people. They learnt nothing from the warning, and instead blamed their calamities on Mūsā (Moses): "In times of prosperity, they said, “This is what we deserve,” but in adversity, they blamed it on Moses and those with him. Surely all is destined by Allah. Yet most of them did not know".

### Phrase by phrase

**"In times of prosperity"**: whenever good came to them, a fertile season, plenty and comfort, wealth, good harvests and a thriving economy.

**"they said, “This is what we deserve,”"** This is ours by right, because we have earned it. And they did not give thanks for it.

**"but in adversity"**: whenever evil struck them, such as drought, famine, failed fruit, disease or other hardship.

**"they blamed it on Moses and those with him."** They took it as a bad omen from Mūsā and the believers with him, the children of Israel, and would say the hardship came because of them and what they had done. The Arabic word for omen, ṭāʾir, literally means a bird. The Arabs had a superstition that a bird flying to their right or left meant good or bad fortune.

**"Surely all is destined by Allah."** Literally, "their omen is with Allah". ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported that Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as: their afflictions are with Allah and from Him. Their hardship and their plenty alike came from Allah, who brought it on them. The misfortunes happened by Allah's decree and permission. Neither they nor Mūsā had any hand in them, except for Mūsā's prayers against them.

**"Yet most of them did not know"**: most of them, or as one explanation puts it, all of them, did not know or believe that whatever befell them came from Him. They put down what happened to other causes rather than to Allah's decree.

### Is there any such thing as a bad omen?

The verse teaches that good and bad fortune come from Allah. Everything in this world happens by His will. No one can bring good or bad luck to anyone. It is sheer ignorance to believe in omens and to build one's actions on such baseless notions.

### How do words twist every warning?

Words have to be used to call a thing right or wrong, or a person innocent or guilty. But words are used by human beings, who in this world of testing are free to use them however they please. The most dangerous part of that freedom is the ability to justify calling truth falsehood, and falsehood truth. A person can label a prophet's plain miracle as magic and then ignore it.

If Allah gives him a blessing, he can describe it as though it came only from his own ability and effort. If Allah sends a warning to punish him for ignoring the truth, he is free to blame it on the bad luck brought by the very worshippers of Allah whom he mistreated, and on whose account the warning came. Everything comes from Allah so that people may learn from it. But with a few words, a person turns every warning upside down and so misses its lesson.

### In short

- Pharaoh's people took prosperity as their due and blamed every hardship on Mūsā and the believers.
- Their "omen" was with Allah: good and bad alike came by His decree, not from Mūsā.
- Belief in omens is ignorance, for nothing brings luck but Allah's will.
- By clever labels, people can explain away every blessing and every warning and so learn nothing.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:132>>>
Pharaoh's people showed the depth of their rebellion, tyranny and stubbornness with an open vow: "They said, “No matter what sign you may bring to deceive us, we will never believe in you.”" Whatever miracle, proof or evidence Mūsā (Moses) brought, they would neither accept it nor believe in him or his message.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They said, “No matter what sign you may bring"**: whenever and whatever sign, evidence or proof you bring us, O Mūsā, even proof of the falsehood of our own beliefs.

**"to deceive us"**: literally "to bewitch us with", to dazzle our eyes or cast a spell on us.

**"we will never believe in you.”"** We will not believe in your message, they say. They said this out of stubbornness against the truth. So Mūsā prayed to Allah against them.

### Why can't some people see the truth?

Pharaoh's words show that even when the truth appears in the clearest and most complete form, only a person who is open to it receives it. The truth shows itself only to someone who is serious about it, willing to receive and accept it wherever and in whatever form it comes. Someone who is not serious, who is content with things as they are, cannot see the truth as truth, and so cannot accept it.

Being pleased with one's own condition makes a person blind to what lies outside himself. He is ignorant despite his knowledge, and deaf though he has ears. A person will surely find the truth if his mind is free of negative conditioning. But most people have been conditioned in this way, and that is why the truth escapes them.

### In short

- Pharaoh's people swore they would never believe, whatever sign Mūsā brought, and called his signs sorcery.
- Their vow drew Mūsā's prayer against them.
- The truth reveals itself only to those who are open and serious. Contentment with the status quo blinds people to it.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:133>>>
Their vow brought a succession of punishments: "So We plagued them with floods, locusts, lice, frogs, and blood—all as clear signs, but they persisted in arrogance and were a wicked people". Mūsā (Moses) was given nine miracles during his time in Egypt, as warnings to Pharaoh's people. Two were shown before Pharaoh and his court, and the third was the famine. This verse names five more.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So We plagued them with floods"**: the word is ṭūfān. Ibn ʿAbbās said it was heavy rain that ruined the crops and fruit. He is also reported to have said it means mass death. Mujāhid said it was water that carried plague everywhere. One explanation says it was continuous, unbroken rain, day and night, from one Saturday to the next. Another says it was a flood of water that entered their houses and stood up to people's necks as they sat, for seven days. Most of the great commentators say it was a flood.

**"locusts"**: the well-known insect, which they sent to eat everything the earth produced, plants and fruit, for seven days. Ibn Abī Najīḥ reported that Mujāhid said they ate the nails in their doors and left the wood. Locusts are lawful to eat. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record that Abū Yaʿfūr asked ʿAbdullāh ibn Abī Awfā about locusts. He said that they took part in seven campaigns with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and that locusts were part of their food. Ibn Mājah, Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal and ash-Shāfiʿī recorded, through ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam from his father, that Ibn ʿUmar reported the Prophet ﷺ as saying that two kinds of dead creature and two kinds of blood had been made lawful. The creatures are fish and locusts, and the two bloods are named in the report as the kidney and the spleen.

**"lice"**: the word is qummal. Ibn ʿAbbās said it is the grain weevil, or by another view small wingless locusts, and Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah and Qatādah said the same. Al-Ḥasan and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said qummal are small black insects. Others compare it to woodworm or ticks, crawling creatures that ate whatever the locusts had left. The word is used both for lice and for an insect that eats grain. Possibly both were sent: small pests ate their grain while swarms of lice ate even their hair and eyelashes.

**"frogs"**: they swarmed into their houses and food stores and plagued them.

**"and blood"**: blood flowing in their water, filling their wells and rivers.

**"—all as clear signs"**: distinct signs, one after another. Ibn ʿAbbās said each punishment lasted a fixed time, followed by a period of relief and then the next punishment. One explanation says they came a month apart.

**"but they persisted in arrogance and were a wicked people"**: they were too proud to believe in Allah or in what Mūsā brought, and they were a sinful, criminal people, idolaters who would not give up falsehood or be guided to the truth.

### How did the plagues unfold?

Abū Jaʿfar ibn Jarīr recorded Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's account. When Mūsā came to Pharaoh and demanded the release of the children of Israel, Pharaoh refused. So Allah sent the flood, a rain that went on until they feared it was a punishment. They asked Mūsā to pray to his Lord to stop it, promising to believe and let the children of Israel go. He prayed, and the affliction was lifted, but they neither believed nor let them go.

That year Allah made the earth bring forth crops, fruit and grass as never before. "This is what we hoped for," they said. So Allah sent the locusts, which began to eat the grass. When they saw what the locusts did to it, they knew no crop would survive. They made the same promise, Mūsā prayed, and the locusts were removed. Still they did not believe or release the children of Israel.

They gathered grain and stored it in their houses, saying they had saved their crops. Then Allah sent the qummal, the grain weevils. A man would take ten sacks of grain to the mill and get back three small bags. They made the same promise, Mūsā prayed, and the weevils were removed. Still they did not let the children of Israel go.

Once, sitting with Pharaoh, Mūsā heard a frog croak. He asked Pharaoh what he and his people would suffer from these creatures. Pharaoh scoffed at the idea that frogs could do anything. Yet by nightfall a man would sit in a mass of frogs up to his chin, unable to open his mouth without a frog jumping in. They made the same promise, Mūsā prayed, but they did not believe.

Then Allah sent blood, which filled their rivers, wells and water jars. They complained to Pharaoh that they had nothing to drink. He told them Mūsā had bewitched them. They asked how that could be, when every time they looked in their jars the water had turned to blood. They went to Mūsā with the same promise. He prayed and the blood stopped, but again they neither believed nor let the children of Israel go. A similar account is reported from Ibn ʿAbbās, as-Suddī, Qatādah and several others among the Salaf.

### In short

- After the famine, Allah sent five distinct signs: flood, locusts, weevils or lice, frogs and blood.
- Each struck for a time, was lifted at Mūsā's prayer, and was followed by another when they broke their word.
- Locusts are lawful food, as the Companions ate them on campaign.
- Despite all these signs they stayed arrogant, a criminal people who would not believe.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:134>>>
Each time a punishment fell, Pharaoh's people turned to Mūsā (Moses) with the same plea: "When tormented, they pleaded, “O Moses! Pray to your Lord on our behalf, by virtue of the covenant He made with you." Then came their oath: "If you help remove this torment from us, we will certainly believe in you and let the Children of Israel go with you.”" Whenever the flood, the locusts, the weevils, the frogs or the blood came on them, they made this promise.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When tormented"**: whenever the punishment fell on them.

**"they pleaded, “O Moses! Pray to your Lord on our behalf"**: call on your Lord for us.

**"by virtue of the covenant He made with you."** Because of the prophethood He has given you, and His promise to you that He will lift the punishment if we turn to Him. One explanation says: because of what He has commanded you.

**"If you help remove this torment from us"**: the Arabic la-in, "if", carries the lām of an oath. They swore to it.

**"we will certainly believe in you and let the Children of Israel go with you.”"** We will release them from captivity and send them with you, with all their belongings.

### How did each plague begin and end?

Ibn-al-Mundhir cited Ibn ʿAbbās as saying that each punishment lasted seven days, beginning on a Saturday, followed by three weeks of relief. Imam al-Baghawī cited Ibn ʿAbbās as saying that when they were first relieved of the famine through Mūsā's prayer, they did not keep their promise to believe. So Mūsā asked Allah to send a painful punishment that would be a lesson to his people and their descendants.

Allah first sent the flood. Water filled and surrounded all the lands and houses of Pharaoh's people, leaving them nowhere to farm or live. Remarkably, the lands and houses of the Israelites were untouched. In great distress they begged Mūsā to pray for relief, promising faith and freedom for the Israelites. His prayer was answered and the flood subsided. Their fields are said to have become more fertile than before. Again they broke their promise. The flood was no punishment from Allah, they said. It had done them good, the bigger harvest came from it, and Mūsā had nothing to do with it.

They were given time to reflect. After a month the locusts came and ate all their crops and fruit, and some reports say even the wooden doors and roofs of their houses. Again they saw that the Israelites' houses and fields close by were unharmed. Once more they begged for help with firm promises, and Mūsā's prayer brought relief. Then, seeing they had enough grain for a year, they went back on their word as arrogantly as before.

After another spell of peace came the qummal, then the frogs, which filled their houses up to their necks, their beds, pans and pots. Again they came with all their old promises and were relieved. Yet those who earn Allah's anger lose the power to think straight. Now at peace, they said they were sure Mūsā was a sorcerer, not Allah's messenger, and their troubles were the work of his sorcery.

After a month's relief came the blood. Everything they used turned to blood, their drink, their food and their water, before they could use it. Water drawn from wells or tanks turned to blood. Reports say that when Egyptians and Israelites sat down to eat together, the Egyptian's mouthful turned to blood while the Israelite's did not. This punishment too lasted seven days, and was lifted by Mūsā's prayer after their usual false promises.

### In short

- With every plague, Pharaoh's people begged Mūsā to pray, swearing to believe and free the Israelites.
- Each plague is said to have lasted seven days, with weeks of relief between, while the Israelites nearby were spared.
- After each relief they explained the punishment away, as luck, as a benefit, or as sorcery.

<<<VERSE 7:135>>>
Their promises never lasted: "But as soon as We removed the torment from them—until they met their inevitable fate—they broke their promise". Pharaoh's people were struck by these punishments one after another, yet they persisted in their arrogance and showed no sign of learning from the warnings.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But as soon as We removed the torment from them"**: when Allah lifted the punishment from them, through Mūsā's (Moses') supplication.

**"—until they met their inevitable fate—"**: literally "for a term they were to reach". The term was their drowning. The relief was only for a set time, before they were destroyed by drowning.

**"they broke their promise"**: at once they went back on their word and broke their pledge to Mūsā. They did not believe him or send the Israelites with him, but held firm to their disbelief.

### Did they keep any of their promises?

Muhammad ibn Isḥāq ibn Yasār gave this account. After the sorcerers believed, the enemy of Allah, Pharaoh, went back defeated and humiliated, but insisted on his disbelief and kept on in his wickedness. Allah sent the signs on him one after another. First came famine, then the flood, the locusts, the qummal, the frogs and the blood.

The flood covered the face of the earth with water. When it went down, they could not use the land to farm or for anything else, and they went hungry. Then they pleaded with Mūsā and he prayed for them, yet their word was broken again. Next came locusts, which devoured the trees and even the nails in their doors, until the doors fell off their houses. Again they pleaded, he prayed and the affliction was lifted.

Still they broke their promise, and Allah sent the qummal. Mūsā was told to strike a mound with his staff. He struck a great mound, and the qummal poured out of it in vast numbers. They overran houses and food stores until no one could sleep or rest. Again they pleaded, he prayed and it was lifted.

They broke their promise, and Allah sent the frogs. These filled their houses, food and pots, so that no one could lift a garment or uncover food without finding frogs in it. When it became unbearable, they made the same promises, Mūsā prayed, and Allah removed it. They kept none of their promises, and Allah sent the blood. The water of Pharaoh's people turned to blood. Any water they drew from a well, a river or a jar turned to blood.

### How did their view of Mūsā's demand change?

Mūsā carried out his prophetic mission in Egypt for about forty years, though one report says he stayed 20 years. His mission had two parts. The first was to bring Pharaoh the message of Allah's oneness. The second was to lead the children of Israel, the descendants of Yūsuf (Joseph), out of Egypt, settle them in the Sinai desert and train them in their religion there, away from corruption. At that time the children of Israel were in the grip of the Coptic ruler, Pharaoh. The Copts used them as labourers in their farming and building, so the Coptic rulers did not want the Israelites to leave.

When Mūsā first asked Pharaoh to let the children of Israel leave with him, Pharaoh and his courtiers gave the request a political colour. They accused Mūsā of wanting to drive the Copts out of Egypt (7:110). That was absurd, since Mūsā's plan was to take himself out of Egypt. Pharaoh and his companions then had an inflated view of their power, so they saw even a straightforward request as crooked.

Later Allah struck Pharaoh and his people with every kind of calamity. For years they faced famine, along with violent storms of hail, lightning and thunder. Swarms of locusts ate their crops and gardens and destroyed all greenery. Lice multiplied until clothes and beds were full of them. Frogs hopped everywhere, in houses and along the paths, and the water of the rivers and lakes turned to blood. When Pharaoh and his people began to suffer these afflictions, they asked Mūsā to pray to his Lord to end them, promising to let the children of Israel leave with him. The demand that had once looked to the Copts like a political plot to evict them now looked like what it was, a migration of the children of Israel themselves.

### In short

- Each time the punishment was lifted, Pharaoh's people broke their word, until their appointed end by drowning.
- Ibn Isḥāq tells how the plagues came one after another and how every promise was broken.
- Mūsā's aim was to lead the Israelites out to worship Allah freely, not to overthrow the Copts.
- Under the plagues, the "political plot" they had imagined turned out to be a simple migration.
