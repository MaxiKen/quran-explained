<<<VERSE 7:130>>>
The punishments begin, and the first of them comes from the sky. "Indeed, We afflicted Pharaoh’s people with famine and shortage of crops so they might come back ˹to their senses˺"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We afflicted Pharaoh’s people"** introduces the sequence of events that carried that people to their end. The words mean that they were tested and tried, and what follows is the first of the heavenly punishments sent to them.

**"with famine and shortage of crops"** names the two things taken from them. The first is the years of drought and want, the famine that follows a poor yield, and the second is the loss of the fruits, which is the lighter of the two. Historical reports put the famine at seven years.

The two are distributed in one account. Ibn Abbas and the commentator Qatadah said that the drought fell on the people of the countryside, while those living in the cities and the towns were punished by the loss of the fruits. The scarcity was remembered long afterwards. It was reported from Raja' bin Haywah that in those years the date palm produced a single date.

**"so they might come back ˹to their senses˺"** gives the purpose. The famine was sent so that they would remember, take heed of the warning, and turn back to their Lord in repentance. That is the shape of the whole series. Each punishment came as a warning before the final one, and the door of return was left open for as long as they refused to walk through it.

### Where this falls in the account

The historical reports say that Musa spent twenty years in the land of Egypt. He called the people through that time and delivered to them the message of his Lord. He was given nine miracles during that stay, and those signs stood as warnings to the people of Pharaoh. The Book refers to them in the words about the nine miracles given to him.

Two of the nine were performed in front of Pharaoh and his people at the outset, which were the staff and the hand. The third was the famine reported in this verse. The verses that follow report the remaining six, and the account moves through them one after another.

### In short

- The people of Pharaoh were tested with years of drought and with the loss of their fruits.
- One account puts the famine at seven years, and the date palm was reported to have produced a single date in that time.
- The drought is said to have fallen on the countryside and the loss of fruit on the towns and cities.
- The purpose was to bring them back to their senses and to return them to their Lord in repentance.
- The famine was the third of the nine signs given to Musa, after the staff and the hand, and it came during the twenty years he spent calling them in Egypt.

<<<VERSE 7:131>>>
The way the people read their own fortunes is laid out for the record. "In times of prosperity, they said, “This is what we deserve,” but in adversity, they blamed it on Moses and those with him, the Children of Israel among them,. Surely all is destined by Allah. Yet most of them did not know"

### Phrase by phrase

**"In times of prosperity, they said, “This is what we deserve,”"** gives their reading of the good things. When a fertile season came, and provision, and land that gave its yield, they said that the thing was theirs by right. They took the good as a payment owed to them for what they were, and no thanks went to the One who had sent it.

**"but in adversity, they blamed it on Moses and those with him"** gives their reading of the bad. When drought came, and want, and hardship, they laid the whole of it on the messenger and on the believers who were with him, and said that the trouble had come because of those people and what they had done.

The word used for the bad omen carries the sense of a bird. The Arabs before Islam held a superstition that a bird coming down on the right or on the left foretold a good or a bad fate, and the verse takes their practice up and turns it on its head.

**"Surely all is destined by Allah"** is the correction. Good and bad come from Allah, and everything in this world happens under His will. There is no creature that brings good or bad fortune to anyone, and it is plain ignorance to hold such a belief and to build one's affairs on assumptions of that kind. Ibn Abbas said of the words that the afflictions of those people were with Allah and from Him.

**"Yet most of them did not know"** states the reason for the mistake. When a people come under the anger of Allah, they lose the faculty that tells right from wrong. The people of Pharaoh had lost it. They did not take the lesson from the warning, and they read the most plain of events backwards.

### In short

- Good seasons were taken by them as a right they had earned, with no thanks given for them.
- Bad seasons were blamed on Musa and the believers who were with him.
- The word for a bad omen carries the sense of a bird, which recalls the old superstition about birds and fate.
- The correction is that everything is from Allah, and no creature brings good or ill to another.
- A people who fall under the wrath of Allah lose the faculty that separates right from wrong, and most of them did not know.

<<<VERSE 7:132>>>
The refusal is put into words that shut the door on every sign in advance. "They said, “No matter what sign you may bring to deceive us, we will never believe in you.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"They said, “No matter what sign you may bring"** answers the signs before they see them. What the words mean is that whatever miracle, proof or evidence he brought them, they would not take it from him.

**"to deceive us"** is how they named the signs. Their claim was that the wonders were brought to work a magic on them and to dazzle their eyes, and the whole of the proof was thus dismissed as a deception in advance.

**"we will never believe in you"** is the conclusion they held to. The refusal covers both the man and what he had come with, and the decision had been taken before the evidence was in. It was after this that Musa prayed against them, and the punishments of the following verse came as the answer to that supplication.

### What their words show about a person and the truth

Words have to be used to call a thing right or wrong, and to name a man innocent or guilty. The one who uses them is a human being, and in this world of trial he has been given the power to use them in whatever way he pleases. The most critical part of that freedom is the ability to call the truth a falsehood and the falsehood a truth. A person can name as magic what is plainly a miracle worked by a prophet, and then set it aside. If God gives him a blessing, he can put it in words that make it appear to be the fruit of his own ability and effort. If God sends him a warning for the truth he has ignored, he is free to say that it came because of the unlucky presence of the very worshippers of God whom he has ill-treated.

Everything flows from God, and all of it is sent so that a person may learn from it. What he does with words is to give the opposite meaning to every admonition, and so he stays cut off from the lesson that was inside it. The statement of Pharaoh here is the plain example of it. The truth was clear in a perfect shape in front of him, and he refused it.

What follows from that is a rule about people and the truth. The reality of the truth reveals itself to the person who is serious about it, the one who has the inclination and the willingness to receive it wherever it comes from and in whatever form. The person who is not serious about it, and whose mind is content with the state he is already in, cannot see it as the truth and so cannot accept it. To be happy with the condition a man is in makes him unaware of everything outside himself, and he becomes ignorant in spite of his knowledge and deaf in spite of his ears. A man will certainly find the truth when his mind is free of the thinking that has been conditioned into it, and most people are held by such conditioning, which is why the truth eludes them.

### In short

- Their refusal was made in advance, covering every sign Musa might bring.
- They named the signs as a deception worked on their eyes, and dismissed the proof before it arrived.
- Musa prayed against them after that, and the punishments that follow came as the answer.
- A person is free to use words to call the truth a lie and a lie the truth, and that is the most dangerous part of the freedom he has been given.
- The truth reveals itself to the one who is serious about it and open to receive it, while the one who is content with his own condition stays deaf to it.

<<<VERSE 7:133>>>
The five remaining signs are named in one verse. "So We plagued them with floods, locusts, lice, frogs, and blood—all as clear signs, but they persisted in arrogance and were a wicked people"

### Phrase by phrase

**"So We plagued them with floods"** names the first. The word used for it means a continuous rain, falling day and night, and in one account from Saturday to Saturday. According to the greater commentators it was a flood that filled the lands and the houses of the people of Pharaoh, leaving them no place to farm and no room to live in their own homes.

**"locusts"** is the second, the well-known insect, and it ate everything the earth produced, both the vegetation and the fruit. In the account of what happened, the locusts consumed the trees and even the nails on their doors, until the doors fell away from the houses. In one description they ate the wood and left the nails.

The locust is also a food, and the reports about it are worth setting down. In the Two Sahihs there is a report that Abu Ya'fur put a question to Abdullah bin Abi Awfa about the locust. The answer was that the companions had fought seven battles beside the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and that locusts were among the things they ate. Ash-Shafi'i, Ahmad bin Hanbal and Ibn Majah all carried a report from Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam, from his father, from Ibn Umar, that the Prophet ﷺ named two kinds of dead animal and two kinds of blood as lawful. They are the fish, the locust, the kidney and the spleen.

**"lice"** is the third. The word used here was explained in more than one way. Ibn Abbas said it is the grain bug, and in another report from him the small locusts that have no wings. Mujahid, Ikrimah and Qatadah were reported with the same view. Al-Hasan, together with Sa'id bin Jubayr, held that they are small insects of a black colour. One account has Musa commanded to strike a mound with his staff. He did so, and the pests poured out of it in such numbers that they covered the houses and the stores of food, and robbed the people of sleep and of rest.

**"frogs"** is the fourth, and the reports describe how far they went. Musa once heard the croaking of a frog while he was with Pharaoh and asked him what he and his people would suffer from it, and Pharaoh asked what a frog could do. By that night a man would be sitting among frogs that came up to his chin. He could not open his mouth to speak, since a frog would jump into it. They filled the houses, the food and the cooking pots, and one of them could not lift a piece of clothing or uncover food without finding frogs in it.

**"and blood"** is the fifth. The waters of the people of Pharaoh turned to blood. The rivers and the wells and the containers they kept in their houses all gave blood, and any water they collected from a well or a river or a vessel turned to blood. One report describes the strangeness of it, that whenever an Egyptian and an Israelite sat down to eat together, the morsel taken by the Egyptian turned to blood while the one taken by the Israelite did not change.

Abu Ja'far bin Jarir recorded from Sa'id bin Jubayr the whole sequence of it. The rain came and they feared it as a torment and asked Musa to pray, and they promised faith and the release of the Children of Israel. He prayed and the rain was lifted, and they broke the word. Then the locusts came and ate the grass, and they made the same promise, and Musa prayed, and they broke it again. The grain pests followed, and then the frogs, and then the blood, and each time the words of the promise say the same thing and the result was the same. What the reports say of that first rain is that the crops of that year grew as they had never grown before, and one of them would take ten bags of grain to the mill and come away with three small ones. A report cited by al-Baghawi from Ibn Abbas adds that the first relief was granted after they asked Musa to pray for them, and that they did not keep the promise they had made.

**"all as clear signs"** describes the whole series, and it is verse 133 of this surah. Each punishment was a plain sign in itself, distinct from the others, and they came one after another with a fixed interval between them. Muhammad bin Ishaq bin Yasar gave the whole series in order, with the locusts eating the trees and the nails of the doors. A report cited by al-Mundhir from Ibn Abbas gives each punishment a length of seven days, beginning with Saturday, and gives the people three weeks of relief between one and the next. The like of that account was attributed to Ibn Abbas, to As-Suddi, to Qatadah, and to several others among the salaf. What those reports say is that the signs came in order, and that the promises were made and broken with each one.

**"but they persisted in arrogance and were a wicked people"** gives the answer of the people to all five. They were too proud to have faith in Allah and too set in their ways to accept what Musa had brought. They were a people who committed wrongs. They would not give up the falsehood they were on, and they were not guided to the truth.

### How the divine protection showed in the middle of it

One thread runs through the reports of the whole series, and it is worth following on its own. The punishments fell on the Egyptians and stopped at the door of the Israelites. When the flood filled the land and the houses of the people of Pharaoh, the lands and the houses of the Children of Israel were not touched by the water. When the locusts ate up the crops and the fruit, they did no harm to the houses or the fields of the Israelites, and those fields were close by. The bread of the Egyptian turned to blood at the table while the bread of the Israelite beside him stayed as it was.

So the series of punishments was also a series of signs for the people of the messenger, and it taught them where their safety lay while the whole land around them was being struck. The two peoples were living in the same country, working the same soil, and eating at the same tables, and the difference between them in those years was the difference between belief and denial.

### The mission of Musa and where the signs belong in it

The prophetic work of Musa in the land of Egypt had two parts. The first was to carry the message of the oneness of God to Pharaoh. The second was to take the Children of Israel out of Egypt, settle them in the desert of Sinai, and give them their religious training in that clean air. One account gives the length of the stay as twenty years, and another as about forty. The Children of Israel, who were the progeny of Joseph, were at that time in the grip of the Copt ruler, and the Copts used them as labourers in their fields and in their building works. That is why the rulers of the Copts did not want them to leave the land.

At the beginning, when Musa asked Pharaoh to let the Children of Israel go with him, the king and his courtiers gave the request a political colour and accused him of intending to drive the Copts out of Egypt (7:110). The charge made no sense, since the plan of Musa was to take himself and his people out of the land rather than to remove anyone else. At that stage the people of Pharaoh had a swollen opinion of their own power, and that is why they saw even a straightforward proposal as something crooked.

In the later stage the matter stood differently. Allah set the king and his community under every kind of affliction. For many years there were famines one after another. There were heavy storms with thunder and lightning. The locusts invaded the country and ate the crops and the gardens and left the greenery bare. The lice multiplied until clothes and beds were infested with them. In the houses and on the roads the frogs were everywhere. The water of the rivers and the lakes turned to blood. When the people of Pharaoh began to suffer under these things, they asked Musa to pray to his Lord to relieve them, and they promised that they would then let the Children of Israel leave with him. So the demand that had once looked to the Copts like a political plot to drive them out now appeared to them as what it was, a migration of the Children of Israel themselves.

### In short

- The five signs were the flood, the locusts, the lice, the frogs and the blood, and each came as a clear sign on its own.
- The flood filled the land and the houses, the locusts ate the crops and even the wood and nails of the doors, the lice consumed what was left, the frogs filled the houses and the food, and the water turned to blood.
- The locust is lawful as food, and the reports in the Two Sahihs and from Ibn Majah carry that ruling.
- Ibn Abbas said each punishment lasted seven days from Saturday, with three weeks of relief before the next, and the same was reported from As-Suddi, Qatadah and others among the salaf.
- The Israelites were protected through the whole series, since the water, the locusts and the blood passed over them while they lived and ate beside the Egyptians.
- The people persisted in arrogance through all of it and remained a people of wrong, and the signs came during the forty years of the mission, whose second part was the migration of the Children of Israel out of Egypt.

<<<VERSE 7:134>>>
Under the weight of it the people make promises they have no intention of keeping. "When tormented, they pleaded, “O Moses! Pray to your Lord on our behalf, by virtue of the covenant He made with you." Then comes the bargain. "If you help remove this torment from us, we will certainly believe in you and let the Children of Israel go with you.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"When tormented, they pleaded"** sets the condition under which the promises were made. The words describe each occasion when one of the punishments fell on them, whether that was the flood, the locusts, the lice, the frogs or the blood. What moved them was the pain, and not the proof.

**"O Moses! Pray to your Lord on our behalf"** is the request itself. The same men who had refused to believe him whatever sign he brought now asked him to stand between them and their Lord, which is the plainest admission of where the power over the punishment lay.

**"by virtue of the covenant He made with you"** is the argument they used. In one reading the sense is that they were appealing to what Allah had commanded him to do, and in another it is the favour of prophethood that had been given to him, with the promise that came with it, that the torment would be lifted when they turned back. They had no claim of their own, so they asked him to plead the standing he had been given.

**"If you help remove this torment from us, we will certainly believe in you and let the Children of Israel go with you"** is the bargain. The promise had two halves, and the second was the thing he had come to ask for from the very beginning, that he be allowed to take his people out of the land, releasing them from the captivity they were in. The first half is the harder of the two to understand, since they had already seen the signs and refused them, and belief had been made a thing of the tongue only.

### The whole thing, five times over

The account of the series shows the pattern repeating. The rain came and they feared, and they said that they would believe and send the Children of Israel with him. Musa prayed, and the affliction was lifted, and they did not believe and did not send them. The locusts came and they made the same promise, and the same thing happened. Then the grain pests, then the frogs, then the blood, each with the same words and the same result. By the time the promise was made for the fifth time, the whole of the court knew what the promise was worth.

### In short

- The promises were made under the pressure of the punishment, not out of any acceptance of the proof.
- They asked Musa to pray for them, which was in itself an admission of where the power over their affliction lay.
- They appealed to the covenant and the standing he had been given, having no claim of their own.
- The bargain promised belief and the release of the Children of Israel, which was the thing he had asked for from the start.
- The same promise was made and broken five times over, and the record of the series is a record of one broken word after another.

<<<VERSE 7:135>>>
The last word on those promises closes the passage. "But as soon as We removed the torment from them—until they met their inevitable fate—they broke their promise"

### Phrase by phrase

**"But as soon as We removed the torment from them"** records the other side of the bargain. The relief came through the supplication of the messenger, who prayed for them each time the punishment had done its work of warning.

**"until they met their inevitable fate"** sets the limit of the respite. The relief they were given was only for a fixed term, and there was a day appointed that they were bound to reach. The term named in the account is the drowning, when the whole matter came to its end in the sea.

**"they broke their promise"** states what they did with the bargain. The word carries the sense of reneging, of going back on what had been given. Immediately after the relief they were already breaking it. In one reading the sense is that they went on with their disbelief and refused to send the Israelites with him, holding to what they were on and giving the messenger nothing of what they had promised.

### Where the whole matter came to rest

So the passage ends as it began. The people had the plainest of signs in front of them, and what the words say is that they read every one of them as something to be escaped rather than as something to be answered. The proof had come to them in nine forms, the door of return had been held open through the whole of it, and the end of the story was the sea.

### In short

- The relief came each time through the supplication of Musa, and each time it was thrown back at him.
- The respite had a limit fixed for it, and the day they were bound to reach was the day of the drowning.
- Their breaking of the word is put in the past continuous sense in the text, since they were already reneging as the relief arrived.
- They held to their disbelief and refused to let the Children of Israel go with him.
- The passage closes with a people who turned the signs into things to be escaped, and with the end of the account waiting in the sea.
