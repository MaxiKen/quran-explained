<<<VERSE 2:213>>>
The preceding verses traced opposition to the truth to love of this world. This verse points out that throughout history Allah has shown clear signs to establish the true faith, while seekers of worldly benefit have worked against it to protect their material interests. "Humanity had once been one community ˹of believers before they lost faith˺. Then Allah raised prophets as deliverers of good news and as warners, and revealed to them the Scriptures in truth to judge among people regarding their disputes." It continues: "And no one disputed the Scriptures except the very people who received them after clear proofs had come to them—out of jealousy. Then Allah, by His grace, has guided the believers to the truth regarding those disputes. And Allah guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Humanity had once been one community ˹of believers before they lost faith˺."**. There was a time when all human beings belonged to a single community with a common religion and belief, a faith true and natural. Then differences of temperament and taste, opinion and thought produced contradictory beliefs, until the true could no longer be told from the false, and it was to make truth distinct from error and show people the straight path that Allah sent prophets with Books and revelations. Al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī says in al-Mufradāt that ummah denotes a set of people gathered together by faith, time or place, so the unity may be one of beliefs and ideas, of presence in a region at a given time, or of descent, language, colour and the like. Two questions arise: what sort of unity is meant, and when did it exist. The first is settled by the end of the verse, which mentions the later differences and says the prophets were sent to determine the true way among many. Prophets and Scriptures were plainly not sent to resolve differences of descent, language, colour, country or period, but differences of ideas and beliefs, so the unity meant is unity of belief. Was that shared belief the Oneness of God, or a shared state of disbelief and error? Ibn ʿAbbās, as Ibn Jarīr records, said that there were ten generations between Adam and Nūḥ, all on the religion of truth, that they later disputed, and that Allah then sent the prophets as warners and bearers of good news, adding that ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd read the verse as "the people were one community, and then they disputed". Al-Ḥākim records this in his Mustadrak with a ṣaḥīḥ chain not recorded by al-Bukhārī and Muslim. Abū Jaʿfar al-Rāzī relates from Abū al-ʿĀliyah that Ubayy ibn Kaʿb read it the same way, and Qatādah said they all had guidance, then disputed, and Allah sent the prophets, the first of them Nūḥ. One early gloss takes it to mean that in the times of Nūḥ and Ibrāhīm people were one community of disbelievers, though it also records that in the time of Ibrāhīm there were followers of the true religion, and the weightier view of the majority is that all were united on the Oneness of God. Other verses point the same way: "Humanity was once nothing but a single community ˹of believers˺, but then they differed. Had it not been for a prior decree from your Lord, their differences would have been settled ˹at once˺" (10:19), the decree being that truth and falsehood, good and bad, should exist side by side in this world, and the addresses to the prophets, "Indeed, this religion of yours is ˹only˺ one, and I am your Lord, so worship Me ˹alone˺" (21:92), and "Surely this religion of yours is ˹only˺ one, and I am your Lord, so fear Me ˹alone˺" (23:52). As to the period, Ubayy ibn Kaʿb and Ibn Zayd placed it in pre-eternity, when the spirits of all human beings were created and asked whether He was not their Lord, and every soul without exception answered yes, so that all were on the one true belief whose name is Islam. Ibn ʿAbbās placed it in the time when Adam came into the world with his wife and their children multiplied, all believing in Adam's religion and following his teaching of God's Oneness, all obedient to the religious law except Qābīl. In the Musnad of al-Bazzār an addition to his statement says this unity of belief lasted from Adam to Idrīs, ten qarns, and if qarn means a century that is a thousand years, during which all followed Islam and tawḥīd. Others date it to the flood of Nūḥ, which swept away all but those in the ark, so that the survivors were all Muslims and monotheists. The three statements do not contradict one another, for in each of these periods people were one community on the true faith. People were agreed and guided on the way of their father Adam until Satan misled them and they split into believers and disbelievers.

**"Then Allah raised prophets as deliverers of good news and as warners,"**. The first sentence says that mankind was one community, and the second that Allah sent prophets and Books to resolve disputes, and the two do not seem to fit, because the reason for sending prophets is people's disputing, which did not exist at the time. The answer is plain: in the early period people held to one true faith, later differences crept in and led to disputes, and then came the need for prophets and Books. Why, then, is the appearance of differences not mentioned? Those with insight into the Qur'an's style know that in relating past events it never tells the whole story but omits what can be understood from context. In the story of Yūsuf, the released prisoner proposes to the king that he be sent to Yūsuf for the interpretation of the royal dream, and the next thing related is his addressing Yūsuf, without any statement that the king approved, that he went to the prison and arrived there. Likewise the onset of differences after the single community did not need stating, since the world has always known disputes. What needed to be said was that before all these disputes there was a time when all human beings followed one true religion, and, very pointedly, that Allah provided for the removal of the disputes through guidance. The prophets, raised from the children of Nūḥ and Ibrāhīm, brought good news of Paradise and eternal peace to those who follow the true faith and warning of the Fire to those who turn away. Religion as revealed by Allah is one religion, yet men put a variety of interpretations on it to suit their own mental make-up, so that differing viewpoints arise, all claiming allegiance to one Scripture. When this happens Allah chooses one of His servants to proclaim the truth on earth. He speaks in human language and looks no different from anyone else, but those bent on finding truth recognise the divine quality in his words, forget their differences and flock to his standard, while others cling stubbornly to their self-made interpretations, asking why they should accept teaching from another, and their claim to a monopoly on truth makes them so proud and prejudiced that they deny the very truth they claimed to own.

**"and revealed to them the Scriptures in truth to judge among people regarding their disputes."**. Allah supported the prophets with revelations and Books sent down with Jibrīl, expounding what is true and what is false in matters of belief and idea, so that each prophet might judge by his Scripture between people in their religious differences. "In truth" is connected grammatically to "revealed". Some say the sense is that the Scripture serves as the final authority, or that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ rules the people by it. The purpose of sending prophets and Books was nothing other than to gather people who had left the single ummah and branched into sects back into one community. Whenever people strayed, Allah sent a prophet and a Book, and when they strayed again another prophet and another Book, to put them back on the straight path. It is like health, which is one, compared with diseases, which are many: each disease called for its own medicine and regimen. Finally came the most comprehensive prescription of all, effective against every disease for as long as Allah keeps this world going, the most complete of all earlier prescriptions and one that pre-empts all future treatments, namely Islam, with the last of the prophets and the Qur'an. The recurring problem of interpolated Scriptures and lost prophetic teachings, which had required new prophets and Books in the past, was removed when Allah Himself undertook to protect the Qur'an from alteration, and promised to raise and sustain within the community of Muhammad ﷺ a group that would always hold to the true faith and circulate the correct teachings of the Qur'an and Sunnah among Muslims everywhere, unflinching before any hostility. After that the doors of prophethood and revelation had to be closed for ever, and so came the proclamation of the end of prophethood. The coming of prophets and Books was never a source of division between people. All of them aimed at gathering people around the one true faith to which they had originally adhered.

**"And no one disputed the Scriptures except the very people who received them after clear proofs had come to them—out of jealousy."**. Despite prophets and Books bearing open truths, the world split into two groups. Some did not accept the clear guidance, and strangely the first to deny it were the very people to whom the prophets and verses were sent, the Jews and the Christians, so that some believed and others disbelieved. More astonishing still, there was no room for doubt in Scriptures that were neither beyond their understanding nor likely to confuse them. They knew and understood, yet their lust for taking the opposite stand made them deny. Those given the Torah differed about Muhammad ﷺ and his message, despite its clarity and the clear proofs in their own Scripture, out of injustice, resentful envy and insolence towards one another, and for this they disbelieved. Grammatically, "after clear proofs had come to them" belongs with "they differed" and is to be understood before the exception. The second group were enabled by Allah to follow the straight path and said yes to the prophets, messengers and divine Books sincerely and wholeheartedly. The two groups are the ones named in Sūrat al-Taghābun: "He is the One Who created you, yet some of you are disbelievers while some are believers" (64:2). So the gist is that all human beings were at first followers of the true faith. Then temperamental diversity gave way to self-interest, disputes surfaced, beliefs themselves were disputed, and the line between true and false was blurred. Allah sent prophets and Books to bring people back to the faith they had held, and despite all guidance and clear signs some followed dutifully while others, out of obstinacy and hostility, took the road of denial.

**"Then Allah, by His grace, has guided the believers to the truth regarding those disputes."**. ʿAbd al-Razzāq relates that Abū Hurayrah explained this with the Prophet's ﷺ words that the Muslims are the last nation but the first on the Day of Resurrection and the first to enter Paradise, although the Jews and Christians were given the Book before them, that Allah guided them to the truth wherever the others disputed, and that Friday is the day they disputed over and Allah guided this ummah to it, so the others follow, the Jews with the next day and the Christians with the day after. Ibn Wahb relates from ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam that his father listed the disputes. They differed over the day of congregation, the Jews taking Saturday and the Christians Sunday, and Allah guided the ummah of Muhammad ﷺ to Friday. They differed over the qiblah, the Christians facing east and the Jews Bayt al-Maqdis, and Allah guided this ummah to the Kaʿbah in Makkah. They differed over prayer, some bowing without prostrating and others prostrating without bowing, some praying while talking and some while walking, and over fasting, some fasting part of the day and others abstaining from certain foods, and Allah guided this ummah to the truth in both. They differed over Ibrāhīm, whom the Jews called a Jew and the Christians a Christian, when Allah made him a ḥanīf and a Muslim, and over ʿĪsā, whom the Jews rejected while accusing his mother of a grave sin and the Christians made a god and the son of God, when Allah made him by His word and a spirit from Him, and in each case guided this ummah to the truth. "By His leave" means, according to Ibn Jarīr, by His knowledge of them and by what He directed and guided them to, that is, by His will. Allah guided the believers through the prophets to the truth of what others disputed in religion, or protected them through the prophets from those disputes and turned them from falsehood to the truth, enabling them to tell guidance from misguidance.

**"And Allah guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path"**. He guides whom He wills of His creation, whoever deserves it, or holds firm by His grace whomever He wills, to the path of truth, an established religion with which He is pleased, the path of faith, and He commands the decision and the clear proof. Al-Bukhārī and Muslim record from ʿĀʾishah that when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ rose to pray at night he would address Allah as the Lord of Jibrīl, Mīkāʾīl and Isrāfīl, Creator of the heavens and the earth, Knower of the unseen and the seen, who judges between His servants in what they dispute, and ask to be guided by His leave to the truth in what is disputed, since He guides whom He wills to a straight path. Another supplication asks Allah to show us the truth as truth and grant us to follow it, to show us falsehood as falsehood and keep us from it, not to let it become confused for us so that we go astray, and to make us leaders of the believers.

### Lessons of the verse

Three lessons follow. First, the verse supports the identification of nationhood on the basis of religion, the two-nation view of Muslim and non-Muslim, and shows that its real foundation is the united nationhood that flourished at the beginning, based not on country of origin but on believing the truth and following the straight path. There was real unity at the start of the world when people followed the true faith, disputes came later, and the prophets called people back to that unity. Those who rejected the call cut themselves off from it and became a separate nation. Second, evil people have always chosen to oppose every prophet and every Book and have been eager to muster their maximum forces against them. Since Allah has allowed this to be the customary state of affairs, people with strength of faith should not be dismayed by the chronic intrigues and hostility of the wicked. Just as the disbelievers follow their elders in denial and hostility to the prophets, true Muslims should follow their own elders, the prophets, in bearing injuries patiently while continuing to call to the faith with wisdom, sound advice and good grace, which is perhaps why the next verse counsels forbearance and patience in distress. Third, in the devotional reading the verse carries another intimation. When Allah first created creatures He created them in the veiling wrap of createdness, the darknesses of created attributes surrounding them, all one group in the curtain of obscurity, gathered in the darkness of absence, captive to their own make-up. Then a courier came from the infinite world to their tininess, of which the Prophet ﷺ reported that Allah created the creatures in darkness and then cast upon them something of His light, and whoever the light struck was guided and whoever it missed went astray. When that messenger turned its face towards them they all became aware, captive to desire and subject to will, their ears fixed on their own lot, asking what would be decreed for them. Then the hand of predetermination divided them into two groups, the fortunate, of whom He said that they belong to the Garden and He does not care, and the unfortunate, of whom He said that they belong to the Fire and He does not care, meaning that He fears no blame and has no regrets, that He gains nothing from loyalty and loses nothing from disloyalty, and that whoever gains faith is himself the one who profits while He remains as He was, without equal or need.

### In short

- Mankind was first one community of believers in God's Oneness, in pre-eternity (Ubayy, Ibn Zayd), from Adam to Idrīs (Ibn ʿAbbās, ten generations, al-Bazzār), or after the flood, and only later split, as 10:19, 21:92 and 23:52 confirm.
- Prophets with good news and warning, and Books to judge disputes, were sent to gather people back to that one faith, like remedies for successive diseases, until the final, protected prescription of the Qur'an closed prophethood.
- The very recipients of Scripture disputed it out of envy after clear proofs, splitting mankind into believers and disbelievers (64:2).
- Allah guided this ummah to the truth in what they disputed, Friday, the qiblah, prayer, fasting, Ibrāhīm and ʿĪsā (Abū Hurayrah, Zayd ibn Aslam), and the Prophet ﷺ prayed nightly for that guidance (ʿĀʾishah).
- Lessons: nationhood by faith, the constancy of hostility to prophets and the patience it calls for, and the inward truth that guidance is the light Allah casts.
