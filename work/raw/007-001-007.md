<<<VERSE 7:1>>>
The surah opens with four letters of the Arabic alphabet, "Alif-Lãm-Mĩm-Ṣãd", each pronounced on its own by its name. They are not a word and they do not make a sentence. Letters of this kind stand at the head of a number of surahs. They are called the disconnected letters, and their general explanation was already given at the start of Sūrat al-Baqarah. Here only what is said about this particular set needs adding.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Alif-Lãm-Mĩm-Ṣãd"** is four separate letters: Alif, Lām, Mīm and Ṣād. Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to have read them as Allah saying, "I am Allah, who knows best and is best." It is also said that they are an oath, words by which Allah swore. Others gave no reading of their own and said only that Allah knows best what He means by these letters.

### In short

- The surah begins with four disconnected letters, read one by one, as several other surahs do.
- Their general explanation was given at the opening of Al-Baqarah.
- Ibn ʿAbbās read them as "I am Allah, who knows best and is best". Some called them an oath, and some said only that Allah knows what He meant by them.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:2>>>
After the opening letters the surah names its subject, the Qur'an itself: "˹This is˺ a Book sent down to you ˹O Prophet˺—do not let anxiety into your heart regarding it—so with it you may warn ˹the disbelievers˺, and as a reminder to the believers". The verse is spoken to the Prophet ﷺ. It tells him three things. This Book comes to him from his Lord. Nothing about it should weigh on his heart. And it has two tasks: to warn those who deny and to remind those who believe.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹This is˺ a Book sent down to you ˹O Prophet˺"** refers to the Qur'an, a scripture that Allah revealed to Muhammad ﷺ from his Lord. Jibrīl (Gabriel) is the one who brought it down to him.

**"do not let anxiety into your heart regarding it"** is built on the word ḥaraj, a tightness or constriction in the chest. It was explained in several ways.

- Mujāhid, Qatādah and as-Suddī said it means doubt. Have no doubt about it, since there can be no doubt that the Qur'an is from Allah.
- It was also said to mean hesitation. Do not hold back from conveying the Qur'an and warning people with it. Read this way, the verse is like the command "So endure patiently, as did the Messengers of Firm Resolve" (46:35).
- Abū al-ʿĀliyah is reported to have explained ḥaraj as anguish or apprehension about conveying the Qur'an and its commands, in case people called it false and hurt him. A similar explanation says the fear was of being called a liar.
- Some held that the constriction came from his loving concern for people. It pained him when they heard the Qur'an and its commands and still did not believe.

Each reading has its own reassurance. The One who sent the Book down to him has also arranged for his protection, and that removes any cause for anguish. His duty, too, is only to call people and convey the message. Once he has done that, who becomes a Muslim and who does not is not his responsibility, so he has no reason to be anxious.

**"so with it you may warn ˹the disbelievers˺"** gives the first purpose: the Qur'an was sent down so that he might warn the disbelievers with it. One explanation names the people of Makkah, warned so that they might believe. The grammar points the same way. The words "so you may warn" (li-tundhira) are tied to "sent down" (unzila), so the sense is that the Book was revealed for the purpose of warning. It was also revealed to stand as a proof.

**"and as a reminder to the believers"** gives the second purpose. For those who believe in it, the same Book is a reminder. They are the ones named because they are the ones who benefit from being reminded.

### Guidance for some, a warning for others

In its essence the Book of Allah is a book of guidance. Yet as guidance it helps only the few who have kept their conscience alive. For everyone else it is only a warning, a warning of the painful end they are heading toward because of their arrogance.

This is what lay behind the anxiety the verse addresses. The one who calls to the truth watches the majority reject, as falsehood, what he sees as absolute truth. What matters most in his eyes, people treat with indifference, as if it had no substance at all and were entirely unreal.

### In short

- The Qur'an is a Book sent down by Allah to the Prophet ﷺ, brought to him by Jibrīl.
- "Anxiety" (ḥaraj) was explained as doubt about it, as hesitation in conveying it for fear of denial or harm, and as grief at people's refusal to believe.
- Allah answers each: the Book is certainly from Him, the Prophet ﷺ is protected, and his only duty is to convey.
- The Book was sent to warn the disbelievers and to remind the believers, who are the ones who benefit.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:3>>>
Having spoken to the Prophet ﷺ, the surah turns to all who hear the Qur'an: "Follow what has been sent down to you from your Lord, and do not take others as guardians besides Him. How seldom are you mindful". The command is plain. Take your guidance from what your Lord revealed, and do not exchange it for anyone else's word. The verse closes with a sad remark on how rarely people take this to heart.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Follow what has been sent down to you from your Lord"** is addressed to people in general, and one explanation adds "Say to them" before it, as words the Prophet ﷺ is to pass on. What was sent down is the Qur'an. Following it means treating as lawful what it makes lawful and as unlawful what it makes unlawful. It also means following the unlettered Prophet ﷺ who brought it, and his traditions. He brought them a Book revealed for their sake by the Lord and Master of everything.

**"and do not take others as guardians besides Him"** uses the word awliyāʾ: protectors, patrons, allies. Do not leave aside what the Messenger ﷺ brought and follow something else. Whoever does that has turned from Allah's judgement to someone else's decision. The "guardians" meant are of several kinds:

- idols, taken as lords and worshipped
- anyone obeyed in disobedience to Allah
- devils, or scholars who lead people astray, whom people imagine to be their friends and protectors. People turn to them and follow their desires, and leave what was revealed to them.

**"How seldom are you mindful"** means that they take heed hardly at all, neither much nor little. Had they truly taken heed, they would never have preferred falsehood to the truth. They would have followed what their Messenger ﷺ brought, obeyed it and left everything else. The verb is read in more than one way. One reading is tadhakkarūn, "you take heed", and another is yadhakkarūn, "they take heed". The full form is tatadhakkarūn, and its first tāʾ has merged into the dhāl. A further reading is tadhkurūn, "you remember". The little word mā in qalīlan mā, "how seldom", adds no new meaning. It only stresses how little it is.

### Why do so few take heed?

The remark that people seldom remember matches what other verses say about most of humanity: "And most people will not believe" (12:103), "If you were to obey most of those on earth, they would lead you away from Allah's Way" (6:116), and "most of them do not believe in Allah without associating others with Him ˹in worship˺" (12:106).

### A world of trial

This world is a place of testing. Everyone here is free to accept the truth or reject it as he likes, and he can even find clever words to defend his position. But that freedom is temporary. When the time of testing ends, everyone will see that the caller's words rested on eternal truths. They will see that sheer bias and egotism kept his opponents from the truth at the core of his arguments. And the arguments they raised against him will turn out to be a distortion of the facts, not reasoning in any real sense.

### In short

- Follow the Qur'an that your Lord revealed, and the Prophet ﷺ who brought it: take as lawful what it permits and avoid what it forbids.
- Taking other guardians, whether idols, devils, misleading scholars or anyone obeyed against Allah, means leaving His judgement for someone else's.
- "How seldom are you mindful" laments that most people do not take heed, as other verses also say.
- The freedom to reject the truth belongs to a time of trial. When it ends, every false argument will be exposed.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:4>>>
To show where turning away leads, the surah points to the ruins of earlier nations: "˹Imagine˺ how many societies We have destroyed! Our torment took them by surprise ˹while sleeping˺ at night or midday". These communities were destroyed because they defied the messengers sent to them and called them liars. The punishment came without warning, at the very hours when people feel safest.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Imagine˺ how many societies We have destroyed!"** The word for "how many", kam, states a large number here. It is not a question, and in the grammar of the sentence it is the object of the verb "We destroyed". The "societies" are towns and cities, which here means the people living in them. Allah destroyed them with His punishment because they persisted in their disbelief and error. "We have destroyed" can also be taken as "We willed their destruction", with the account of how it came following in the next words.

**"Our torment took them by surprise"**: Allah's command, His torment and His vengeance came upon them while they were unaware. They could do nothing to escape it, and the false gods they relied on could do nothing to shield them.

**"˹while sleeping˺ at night or midday"**: it struck some at night, like a raid, and some at midday as they rested. The word used, qāʾilūn, comes from qaylūlah, a rest taken in the middle of the day even when it involves no sleep. So the torment came to some cities by night and to others by day. Both are times of rest and leisure, or of heedlessness and amusement, when nobody expects disaster.

### Why were they destroyed?

They defied Allah's messengers and rejected them. That conduct brought disgrace on them in this life, and their disgrace in this life led on to disgrace in the Hereafter. Other verses say the same:

> "˹Other˺ messengers had already been ridiculed before you ˹O Prophet˺, but those who mocked them were overtaken by what they used to ridicule" (6:10).

> "Many are the societies We have destroyed for persisting in wrongdoing, leaving them in total ruin. ˹Many are˺ also the abandoned wells and lofty palaces" (22:45).

> "˹Imagine˺ how many societies We have destroyed that had been spoiled by their ˹comfortable˺ living! Those are their residences, never inhabited after them except passingly" (28:58).

### Why at night or at midday?

The timing is part of the warning. The punishment came when people were most at ease and least alert. The surah comes back to this later: "Did the people of those societies feel secure that Our punishment would not come upon them by night while they were asleep? Or did they feel secure that Our punishment would not come upon them by day while they were at play" (7:97-98). Another passage asks whether those who plot evil feel safe from four things. The earth might swallow them. Torment might reach them from where they cannot perceive. They might be seized as they go about their day, with no way out. Or they might be destroyed little by little. It ends: "But your Lord is truly Ever Gracious, Most Merciful" (16:45-47).

### In short

- Many communities were destroyed for defying and rejecting their messengers, which earned them disgrace in this world and in the next.
- "How many" (kam) states a great number, and the "societies" are the people who lived in them.
- The torment came at night or during the midday rest (qaylūlah), times of ease and heedlessness, and neither they nor their false gods could hold it off.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:5>>>
This verse records what the destroyed nations said when the end came: "Their only cry—when overwhelmed by Our torment—was, “We have indeed been wrongdoers.”" They had no defence and no other words. All they could do was admit the truth they had denied, at the moment when admitting it could no longer help them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Their only cry"**: the word means their plea, the only words they had. Nothing else came from them.

**"when overwhelmed by Our torment"**: when the punishment that destroyed them came down on them.

**"“We have indeed been wrongdoers.”"** They confessed their sins and admitted that they deserved to be punished. The wrong they owned up to was their disbelief in Allah. One explanation takes "wrongdoers" here to mean idolaters.

### A confession that came too late

A passage in another surah shows the same scene. It begins, "˹Imagine˺ how many societies of wrongdoers We have destroyed" (21:11). It tells how they ran from their homes, and how they cried, "Woe to us! We have surely been wrongdoers", and kept repeating their cry until they were left "lifeless" (21:15).

### In short

- When the punishment struck, the only words left to the destroyed nations were a confession of their own wrongdoing.
- They admitted their disbelief and that they deserved what came, but the admission came too late to save them.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:6>>>
The surah now looks ahead to the Day of Judgement, when every nation will answer for how it treated the messengers: "We will surely question those who received messengers and We will question the messengers ˹themselves˺". Both sides will be asked. The communities will be asked how they responded, and the messengers will be asked whether they delivered what they were given.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We will surely question those who received messengers"**: on the Day of Resurrection Allah will question the nations to whom He sent messengers. He will ask how they received the messengers and the Books sent with them, and how far they acted on what reached them. Another verse describes this question: "And ˹watch for˺ the Day He will call to them, asking, “What response did you give to the messengers?”" (28:65).

**"and We will question the messengers ˹themselves˺"**: the messengers will be asked whether they delivered the messages they were sent with, and how their communities answered. Ibn ʿAbbās, in the report passed on by ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah, put it briefly: they will be asked "about what they conveyed." Another verse gives the scene and the messengers' reply: "˹Consider˺ the Day Allah will gather the messengers and say, “What response did you receive?” They will reply, “We have no knowledge ˹compared to You˺! You ˹alone˺ are indeed the Knower of all unseen.”" (5:109).

Al-Bayhaqī narrated from Ibn ʿAbbās the same two questions. On the Day of Qiyāmah (Resurrection) people will be asked how they received the messengers and Books Allah sent them. The messengers will be asked whether they conveyed to their communities the message and the commands Allah gave them.

### "O Allah, be witness"

The Prophet ﷺ prepared his community for this question. Jābir reported, in a hadith in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, that in his address at his last Ḥajj the Prophet ﷺ put a question to the people. On the Day of Qiyāmah, he said, you will be asked about me, whether or not I conveyed Allah's message to you. What will you say? All the Companions present answered that they would say he had conveyed Allah's message to them. They would say he had fulfilled Allah's trust in the best way, and had wished for and done what was best for the community. Hearing this, the Prophet ﷺ said, "O Allah, be witness."

In a narration in the Musnad of Aḥmad, he said that on the Day of Qiyāmah Allah will ask him whether he conveyed His message to His servants, and he will answer that he did. "So let those of you who are present convey my message to those who are absent."

### Who are "those who are absent"?

They are the people alive at that time who were not at that gathering, and also the generations still to come. Conveying the Prophet's ﷺ message to them means that the people of every age must keep the chain unbroken and hand the message on to the next. In that way every child of Ādam born until the Day of Qiyāmah will have received it.

### In short

- On the Day of Resurrection Allah will ask every nation how it answered its messengers, and ask the messengers whether they conveyed the message.
- Ibn ʿAbbās said the messengers will be asked about what they conveyed.
- At his last Ḥajj the Prophet ﷺ had his Companions testify that he had conveyed the message, and he called on Allah to witness it (Muslim).
- He told those present to pass his message to those absent, which makes every generation responsible for handing it on.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:7>>>
The questioning is not because anything is unknown: "Then We will give them a full account with sure knowledge—for We were never absent". Allah will tell every person and every nation exactly what they did. He was present through all of it, and nothing they did, great or small, escaped Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then We will give them a full account"**: Allah will narrate their deeds to them, informing everyone of what they did in the world, all they said and did, whether great or small. Ibn ʿAbbās said: "The Book will be brought forth on the Day of Resurrection and it will speak, disclosing what they used to do."

**"with sure knowledge"**: the account comes from Allah's own knowledge, the knowledge He already had of what they did. It was also explained as "with clear explanation", an account that leaves nothing in doubt.

**"for We were never absent"**: Allah was not absent when the messengers delivered the message, and not absent when the people gave their response. He was not absent in the days of the communities that have passed away, or from anything they did. He was always aware of whatever they did, and nothing is hidden from Him.

### Nothing escapes Him

Allah is witness to everything. Nothing escapes His sight and He is never unaware of anything. He knows fully the deceptions of the eyes and the secrets the hearts conceal. As another verse says: "Not even a leaf falls without His knowledge, nor a grain in the darkness of the earth or anything, green or dry, but is ˹written˺ in a perfect Record" (6:59).

### In short

- On the Day of Resurrection Allah will tell everyone what they did, from His own complete knowledge.
- Ibn ʿAbbās said the Book of deeds will be brought out and will speak, revealing what people used to do.
- Allah was present when the messengers conveyed the message and when people answered, so nothing great or small is hidden from Him.
<<<END>>>
