<<<VERSE 2:47>>>
Verse 47 opens a new movement in the address to the Children of Israel. The passage that began at verse 40 has so far rebuked them. Now Allah turns to remind them of what He did for them, telling them to remember "the favours I granted you and how I honoured you above the others". The verses that follow, from 49 onward, list those favours one by one, so this verse is the heading over the whole story that follows.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O Children of Israel!"** means the descendants of Yaʿqūb (Jacob), whose name Israel was. The people actually being spoken to are the Jews living in Madīnah in the time of the Prophet ﷺ, while the blessings being recalled were received by their distant ancestors. Scholars explain the link in two ways. When a man receives a special favour, his children and grandchildren usually go on enjoying its fruits, so the later Jews could fairly be said to have received the blessing too. And the address to the descendants about what was done for their forefathers is meant to remind them of Allah's grace so that they would believe.

**"Remember the favours I granted you"** means recall My blessings. Early commentators spell them out as the Scripture, the Messengers and the religion of submission itself, together with sacred and worldly gifts of every kind. The command to remember is a command to be thankful, and thankfulness is shown through obedience. The idea is that a person who honestly recognises what he has been given will be moved to gratitude, and gratitude will move him to obey the Giver. Allah showed this people a preference over all the earlier nations by raising Messengers from among them and sending down Books to them. The Qur'an says elsewhere that He chose the Israelites knowingly above the others (44:32), and Mūsā (Moses) reminded his people that Allah had raised prophets among them, made them sovereign and given them what He had given no one else in the world (5:20).

**"and how I honoured you above the others"** is literally "preferred you over the worlds", al-ʿālamīn. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, in a report transmitted by ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, explained it as the kingship, the Messengers and the Books that were given to them rather than to the other kingdoms of their day, adding that every age has its nation. Mujāhid, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, Qatādah and Ismāʿīl ibn Abī Khālid are reported to have said the same. The preference, then, was in certain matters, and it was over the people of their own time, that is, over a large part of mankind rather than over every nation that would ever exist.

### Why the preference was limited to their own time

The qualification is necessary because the Ummah of Muḥammad ﷺ is better than theirs, and the verse cannot be read in a way that contradicts that. Allah told this community, "You are the best community ever raised for humanity" (3:110). A hadith recorded in the Musnad and the Sunan collections from the Companion Muʿāwiyah ibn Ḥaydah has the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say that the Muslims complete seventy nations and are the best and most honoured of them in Allah's sight. Many hadiths make the same point, and they are gathered under the verse of Sūrat Āl ʿImrān just cited.

### A chosen people, but not a saved people

The Israelites were indeed chosen by God, and chosen for a definite task: to receive His scriptures and carry His commandments to the rest of mankind. Attached to that office came many other blessings, among them victory over enemies, forgiveness of sins, special help in times of danger and provision for their bodily needs. Later generations of Jews drew a false conclusion from all this. They came to believe that they were God's chosen people in a way that guaranteed them salvation in the next world. But salvation is not a hereditary right of any group. Each generation is judged on its own merits, and on the Day of Judgement people are judged one by one according to what they did. That is exactly where the next verse takes the argument.

### Witnessing the favour, or witnessing the Giver

The devotional reading notices a difference of wording between this verse and the way the Qur'an speaks to the Muslims. Here Allah calls the Children of Israel to bear witness to the favour (faḍl) granted to them: "I have preferred you", faḍḍaltukum. To the community of Muḥammad ﷺ He says instead, "In Allah's grace and mercy let them rejoice" (10:58), calling them to bear witness to the bounty of God Himself. What a difference between those who contemplate the favour they have received and those who contemplate the bounty of their Lord. When a servant acknowledges a favour done to him, that acknowledgement carries gratitude, but it also carries the danger of self-conceit (iʿjāb). When he acknowledges the bounty of the Real, whose majesty lies in His description and whose beauty lies in the worthiness of His attribute, his acknowledgement is praise, and praise leads to His approval (ījāb).

### In short

- The Jews of the Prophet's ﷺ time are told to recall the favours done to their forefathers, because descendants share in such favours and remembering them should produce gratitude and obedience.
- The favours were prophethood, Scripture, kingship and the faith itself, and the preference "over the others" was over the nations of their own time, in particular respects.
- The Ummah of Muḥammad ﷺ is the best community (3:110), as the hadith of the seventy nations also says.
- Being chosen for a mission is not a guarantee of salvation. Each generation and each person is judged individually.

<<<VERSE 2:48>>>
Having reminded the Children of Israel of His favours, Allah now warns them about the Day on which none of those favours will count unless faith stands behind them. "Guard yourselves against the Day on which no soul will be of help to another. No intercession will be accepted, no ransom taken, and no help will be given", the verse says. Four doors through which people in this world escape trouble are named, and each is shown to be shut on that Day.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Guard yourselves against the Day"** means fear the Day of Resurrection. One early commentary spells out the condition: if you do not believe and do not turn away from your Judaism, then fear the torment of that Day. Guarding oneself against it is done by following Allah's instructions and keeping away from what He has prohibited, which is what taqwā means in practice.

**"on which no soul will be of help to another"**. The phrase should be understood in the sense of one person settling another's dues. Imagine a man who falls short in obligatory worship such as Ṣalāh and Ṣawm (fasting), and a friend who offers to transfer his own prayers and fasts to the first man's account to make up the shortfall. No such transaction will be possible on that Day. The Qur'an says that no burdened soul will carry another's burden, even for a close relative (35:18), that on that Day everyone will have enough concern of his own (80:37), and that no parent will benefit his child nor any child his parent (31:33). That last verse is a frightening warning: if even father and son cannot help each other, no one can.

**"No intercession will be accepted"**. The Arabic verb has been read as tuqbal and as yuqbal, and the point, as one commentary puts it, is not that a soul will try to intercede and be refused, but that it will have no power of intercession at all that could be accepted or rejected. The people of the Fire will say, "Now we have none to intercede for us, nor a close friend" (26:100-101), and Allah says that the pleas of intercessors will be of no benefit to them (74:48).

**"no ransom taken"**. The Arabic word ʿadl here means a ransom (fidāʾ), the payment made in this world to buy a criminal's release. Allah will not accept the disbelievers' attempts to ransom themselves. If any of them offered enough gold to fill the whole world it would not be accepted (3:91). Even if they owned everything in the world twice over and offered it all, it would be refused (5:36). Whatever ransom such a person offers will not be taken from him (6:70), and on that Day no ransom will be accepted from the hypocrites or the disbelievers (57:15).

**"and no help will be given"** means no one will be angry or anxious on their behalf, no one will stand up for them, and no one will try to save them from Allah's punishment. Neither relatives nor people of authority will feel pity for them, and no ransom will be taken for them, so they will receive no help from others and will be helpless in themselves. Allah protects all while none can protect against Him (23:88). On that Day He will punish as no other punishes and bind as no other binds (89:25-26). The wrongdoers will be asked what is the matter with them that they can no longer help each other (37:25), which Ibn ʿAbbās, in the report of aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, explained as meaning: today you have no refuge from Us. And the false gods they took to draw near to Allah will not come to their aid (46:28). Ibn Jarīr summed up the clause: on that Day no helper will help them and no one will intercede for them, no reversal of judgement and no ransom will be accepted, every courtesy toward them will have ended along with any useful intercession, and no kind of help or cooperation will be left. Judgement that Day belongs to the Most Great and the Most Just, against whom no intercessor or helper can prevail. He repays an evil deed with its like and multiplies the good deeds. The scene is the one described when the wrongdoers are detained for questioning, asked why they do not help one another, and found to be completely submissive (37:24-26).

### Does the verse deny all intercession?

No. The phrase about intercession is not a total denial of intercession on the Day of Judgement. It means that no intercession will be accepted for a person who does not have īmān (faith). The Qur'an states clearly in other places that Allah will permit intercession on behalf of some people: the intercession of the angels benefits once Allah gives permission to whom He wills and approves (53:26), no intercession benefits with Him except by those He permits (34:23), and the Throne Verse later in this sūrah says that no one intercedes with Him except by His permission. For those who have no faith the Qur'an rules intercession out: on that Day it will benefit no one except those the Most Compassionate permits and whose word pleases Him (20:109), and the angels intercede only for those He approves (21:28). Since there will be no intercession for the faithless in the first place, the question of its being accepted does not arise.

The context of the passage draws the same line. Those who refuse to believe in Allah's Messenger ﷺ and to follow what he brought will find, when they meet their Lord on the Day of Resurrection after dying on the path of disbelief, that their lineage and the intercession of their masters are no use to them, and no ransom will be taken even if it were the earth's fill of gold. Allah told the believers to spend before a Day comes when there will be no bargaining, friendship or intercession (2:254), and to establish prayer and give before a Day in which there is no ransom or friendly connection (14:31). Those verses too are addressed to believers as warnings, but what they shut out is the kind of rescue that disbelievers imagine.

This matters for a doctrinal dispute. The Muʿtazilah, and some groups of more recent origin, used this verse to deny the possibility of any intercession in favour of Muslims. The answer is that the negation here applies only to disbelievers.

The devotional commentary agrees and sharpens the point. On the Day of Resurrection no intercession will be heard except from the one whom the Real has commanded to intercede, and whom He has permitted. In reality He Himself is the greatest intercessor, although the title "intercessor" is not applied to Him, because there is no textual sanction (tawqīf) for it. A line of verse captures the believer's position: the Beloved has become my intercessor to my intercessor before Him. For those struck by the calamity of fate, however, the intercession of intercessors will not benefit, there will be no helpers for them, and no ransom will be accepted from them even if they owned the fill of the heavens and the earths.

### "Receiving support" in this world and the next

In ordinary language, receiving support means getting out of a tight spot with the help of a strong friend or patron. The verse lists exactly the means people rely on here: someone to pay your debt for you, someone to put in a word for you, money to buy your way out, and a powerful protector. Not one of the ways of getting help that work in this world will work in the other world unless a person has faith. Without appeal, ransom or helper, where will they turn? Earlier the Israelites were told that they had been honoured above other peoples. This verse closes the door on the thought that such honour is itself a passport. People will be judged individually according to their deeds, and to think that anything other than good deeds will help is to underestimate the severity of that Day.

### Three levels of fear

The devotional reading also observes how differently Allah instils fear in different people. Ordinary people He makes afraid through His acts: "fear the Day", and "fear the Fire" (2:24, 3:131). The elect He makes afraid through His attributes: "Do as you will. Your deeds will be observed by Allah and His Messenger" (9:105), and "there is no deed you may be doing except that We are a Witness over you" (10:61). And the elect of the elect He makes afraid through Himself: "Allah warns you about Himself" (3:28, 3:30). The deeper a person's knowledge, the less he needs the threat of punishment to keep him careful. The thought of being seen, and then the thought of Allah Himself, does the work.

### In short

- The Day of Resurrection shuts the four doors of worldly rescue: no one can pay another's dues, intercede for him, ransom him or come to his aid.
- Prayers and fasts cannot be transferred from one person's account to another's, and no ransom, not even the earth's fill of gold, will be taken.
- The denial of intercession applies to those without īmān. For believers the Qur'an affirms intercession by Allah's permission (53:26, 34:23, 20:109, 21:28), against the view of the Muʿtazilah and later groups.
- Being an honoured people is no substitute for personal faith and deeds, since judgement on that Day is individual.

<<<VERSE 2:49>>>
With verse 49 the account of the favours promised in verse 47 begins. The first and greatest of them is the rescue from Pharaoh. The verse recalls "how We delivered you from the people of Pharaoh, who afflicted you with dreadful torment, slaughtering your sons and keeping your women. That was a severe test from your Lord". From here to the end of the passage, each "remember when" introduces another episode in the history of the Israelites with Mūsā (Moses).

### Phrase by phrase

**"how We delivered you from the people of Pharaoh"**. The "you" means your forefathers. From this verse onward the address is directed to the Jews living in the Prophet's ﷺ time, and it tells them how God blessed their ancestors so that they might be moved to believe. Allah is saying: I saved you from them and took you out of their hands in the company of Mūsā, after they had subjected you to a dreadful torture.

The word "Pharaoh" (Firʿawn) is worth a note. It is not a personal name but a title, given to every disbelieving king who ruled Egypt, whether from the Amalekites or from elsewhere, just as Caesar (Qayṣar) was the title of the disbelieving kings who ruled Rome and Damascus, Khosrau (Kisrā) the title of the kings of Persia, Tubbaʿ the title of the kings of Yemen, and Negus (an-Najāshī) the title of the kings of Abyssinia.

**"who afflicted you with dreadful torment"**. The verb yasūmūnakum was explained by Abū ʿUbaydah as "they humiliated you", and al-Qurṭubī reported another view, that it means "they went to extremes in tormenting you". Besides killing the children, Pharaoh had the Israelites put to hard labour and assigned the most degrading tasks. The torment named here is above all the killing of the male infants, as the parallel verse in Sūrat Ibrāhīm makes explicit (14:6). The clause is a circumstantial description attached to "We delivered you": you were rescued while in that condition.

**"slaughtering your sons and keeping your women"** explains what the torment was. The boys were killed in infancy, and the women were kept alive, of age and put to use. The cause, as the early reports tell it, was a prediction. Some of Pharaoh's priests told him that a boy would be born among the Israelites who would bring down his rule. A fuller form of the report runs: the accursed Pharaoh saw in a dream a fire coming out from Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem) and entering the houses of the Copts in Egypt, sparing only the houses of the Israelites, and this was read to mean that his kingdom would fall at the hands of a man from among them. It was also said that some of his courtiers told him the Israelites were expecting a man to arise among them and set up a state for them. The hadith on the subject is kept for Sūrat Ṭā Hā, and the opening of Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ gives the story its fullest treatment. So Pharaoh ordered that every newborn Israelite boy be killed and the girls be left. There was nothing to fear from the girls, and when they grew up they could serve as maidservants, so even this show of leniency was driven by self-interest. Scholars observe that this phrase, by spelling out the torment, also spells out the size of the favour mentioned in verse 47, since Allah recalled the saving of their children in order to remind them of how much He had done for them. In Sūrat Ibrāhīm, after Mūsā is told to remind his people of the days of Allah, meaning His favours (14:5), the very next verse recalls this same deliverance (14:6).

**"That was a severe test from your Lord"**. The word balāʾ, test, can be read in two directions, and scholars keep both. The pronoun "that" may refer to the slaughter of the sons, which was a calamity, and a calamity tests a person's patience. Or it may refer to the deliverance from Pharaoh's people, which was a blessing, and a blessing tests a person's gratitude. Ibn Jarīr took the second sense: Our saving your fathers from the torment they suffered at Pharaoh's hands was a tremendous blessing from your Lord. Early commentators give both readings side by side, a tremendous calamity or a tremendous blessing. There is a test inside every blessing just as there is inside every hardship, for Allah says that He tests people with good and with evil as a trial (21:35), and that He tested the Israelites with prosperity and adversity (7:168).

### What patience under persecution earns

The devotional reading draws a rule from the story. Whoever bears with patience, for God's sake, the harm done by His enemies, God compensates him with the companionship of His friends and grants him His beautiful gift. The Children of Israel bore the injury of Pharaoh and his people with patience, so He raised prophets from among them, made them kings and gave them what He had given no one else in the worlds. On "a severe test", it notes that scholars have called the event both a great blessing (niʿmatun ʿaẓīmah) and a severe trial (miḥnatun shadīdah), and reconciles the two: what came from God was outwardly a trial (miḥnah), but for the one who recognised it for what it was, it was in reality a blessing (niʿmah) and a kindness (minnah).

### In short

- The first favour recalled is the rescue from Pharaoh, whose title was borne by every unbelieving king of Egypt, as Caesar, Khosrau, Tubbaʿ and Negus were titles elsewhere.
- Pharaoh killed the Israelite boys and spared the girls because of a prediction that an Israelite child would end his rule, and the sparing of the girls was itself self-interested.
- "That" in "a severe test" may be the slaughter, which tested patience, or the deliverance, which tested gratitude. Both blessing and hardship are tests (21:35, 7:168).
- Patience under the persecution of God's enemies was repaid with prophethood and kingship.

<<<VERSE 2:50>>>
The second favour is the crossing of the sea. "And remember when We parted the sea, rescued you, and drowned Pharaoh’s people before your very eyes", the verse says, compressing into one line an event the Qur'an tells at length elsewhere. Scholars call this one of the briefest references to the story in the whole Book.

### Phrase by phrase

**"when We parted the sea"**. After the rescue described in the previous verse, the Israelites left Egypt with Mūsā (Moses) and Pharaoh set out after them. The fuller background is this. Mūsā, as Allah's messenger, spent a long time trying to bring Pharaoh and his people to the truth. When they persisted in denial, Allah commanded him to take the Israelites and leave Egypt quietly. On the way they came to the sea with Pharaoh and his army close behind in hot pursuit. Allah commanded the sea to split and open a way for Mūsā and his people, and it did. The verb means that Allah divided it in two "for you", that is, on your account, so that you could cross and escape your enemy, and it became a dry path for you to walk through.

**"rescued you"** means saved you from drowning. The Israelites went over smoothly.

**"and drowned Pharaoh's people before your very eyes"**. When Pharaoh and his army followed them into the sea, the water closed over them and they drowned then and there. "Before your very eyes" means that you watched the sea crash down on top of them. Allah thus saved the Israelites from their enemies, drowning the enemy while they looked on, which brought relief to their hearts and humiliation to those who had oppressed them. One early commentary adds the detail that they looked upon them three days afterwards.

### Miracles are real

Scholars draw a doctrinal lesson from the verse. The splitting of the sea is a plain proof that miracles do occur at the hands of prophets. Some Westernised Muslims have tried to deny this, and the verse stands against them.

### The day of ʿĀshūrāʾ

It is reported that the day on which the Israelites were saved from Pharaoh was the day of ʿĀshūrāʾ, the tenth of Muḥarram. Imām Aḥmad recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās that when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ came to al-Madīnah he found the Jews fasting that day and asked them what it was. They told him it was a good day, the day on which Allah saved the Children of Israel from their enemy, and that Mūsā used to fast it. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ replied that he had more right to Mūsā than they had. So he kept the fast himself and told the Muslims to keep it. This hadith was also collected by al-Bukhārī, Muslim, an-Nasāʾī and Ibn Mājah.

### Seeing openly and seeing inwardly

The devotional commentary uses the scene to contrast two kinds of perception. The inner sight (baṣāʾir) of the Children of Israel had grown dim, so He showed them His miracles openly (ʿayānan). The community of Muḥammad ﷺ had sharp inner sight, and to them He unveiled His signs secretly (sirran). This is His way (sunnah): the keener a person's perception, the more subtle the matter becomes for him and the richer its allusions. The Prophet ﷺ said that he was given the all-comprehensive words (jawāmiʿ al-kalim) and that speech was made concise for him. The Israelites watched the sea split and Pharaoh's people drown, and still doubt seized them, so that some said the Egyptians had not really drowned even though they were cast into the sea, when they had seen them drowning with their own eyes. In this community, by contrast, because of the strength of their belief in the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and the power of their perception, a young man could say that it was as if he were with the people of the Garden visiting one another, as if he were with the people of the Fire crying out to one another, and as if he saw the Throne of his Lord in plain sight. What a difference between someone who sees with his own eyes and still doubts, and someone who only hears a report and yet sees it as an eyewitness through the power of his faith.

### In short

- Pursued by Pharaoh, the Israelites were given a dry path through the sea, and the water closed over Pharaoh's army while they watched.
- The verse is one of the Qur'an's shortest tellings of the story and a plain proof that prophets are given miracles.
- The day of the deliverance was ʿĀshūrāʾ, which the Prophet ﷺ fasted and ordered to be fasted, saying he had more right to Mūsā than the Jews had.
- The Israelites saw the miracle openly and still doubted, while believers of this community see with the certainty of faith what they have only heard.

<<<VERSE 2:51>>>
The third episode is the one the Israelites would least like to remember. The verse recalls "when We appointed forty nights for Moses, then you worshipped the calf in his absence, acting wrongfully". The favour here is not the calf, of course, but what came with the forty nights, the giving of the Torah, and what came after the calf, the forgiveness recorded in the next verse.

### Phrase by phrase

**"when We appointed forty nights for Moses"**. Once Pharaoh had drowned, the Israelites either returned to Egypt, as one report says, or, as another has it, made their home somewhere else. Having found peace at last, they wished to receive from Allah a Sharīʿah, a religious code of laws to live by. Allah answered Mūsā's (Moses') prayer and promised him a Divine Book on condition that he come to Mount Ṭūr (Sinai) and give himself to worship there for a month. He gladly obeyed and was granted the Torah, but he was then ordered to continue for ten more days. The reason given is that he had broken his fast at the end of the month and so lost the particular odour that rises from the mouth of a fasting person, which is very pleasing to Allah, so he was commanded to fast ten further days and regain it. In this way he completed forty days of total fasting and devotion. Sūrat al-Aʿrāf gives the arithmetic: thirty nights were appointed and then ten were added to complete the term of forty (7:142). It was said that these were the month of Dhū al-Qaʿdah followed by the first ten days of Dhū al-Ḥijjah, after the Israelites had been delivered from Pharaoh and had crossed the sea in safety. The verb has been recited as wāʿadnā and as waʿadnā, and the sense is: We appointed a term for Mūsā at the end of which We would give him the Torah for you to live by. The forty nights were themselves a blessing, because in them the sending down of the Torah, with its light and guidance, was completed.

**"then you worshipped the calf in his absence"**. While Mūsā was on the mountain something shameful happened in the camp. A man among them called as-Sāmirī shaped the figure of a calf out of gold or silver and threw into it some dust he had picked up from under the hoof of the horse of Jibrāʾīl (Gabriel) at the time when the angel brought about the drowning of Pharaoh and his army. The golden calf at once acquired life, and the ignorant among the Israelites were so impressed that they began to worship it. They said, "This is your god and the god of Moses, but Moses forgot" (20:88). "In his absence" means after he had gone off to the mountain for the appointment Allah had made with him.

**"acting wrongfully"** translates ẓālimīn, wrongdoers. Injustice (ẓulm) means putting a thing in a place that is not its own, and idolatry is exactly that, since it directs worship to the wrong object. They wronged themselves above all, and others as well.

### Two communities compared

The devotional reading sets the Israelites' failure beside the steadfastness of the community of Muḥammad ﷺ. When Mūsā withdrew from his people for forty days they took a calf for their object of worship and were pleased with it. The community of Muḥammad ﷺ, by contrast, spent many years with their Prophet, and had they heard anyone describe the One they worship in any material form they would have given their last breath rather than accept it. Mūsā, before leaving, entrusted his people to his brother with the words, "Take my place among my people" (7:142), and when he came back he found that they had fallen into temptation (fitnah). Our Prophet ﷺ, when his life drew to its close, put his trust in God and named no one to lead the community, and his last words were a request for the highest companionship. Yet see how the Real took the care of his community's belief into His own hands. By my life, says the commentator, they may lose their borders, but they will never give up their declaration of God's oneness (tawḥīd).

### In short

- The forty nights were thirty plus ten (7:142), a period of fasting and devotion at the end of which Mūsā received the Torah, the code of law the Israelites had asked for.
- In his absence as-Sāmirī made a calf that lowed, and the ignorant among the people worshipped it (20:88).
- They are called wrongdoers because idolatry puts worship in the wrong place, and the wrong fell first on themselves.
- The Israelites lapsed after forty days without their prophet, while the community of Muḥammad ﷺ has kept its tawḥīd through every loss.

<<<VERSE 2:52>>>
After the calf came pardon. "Even then We still forgave you so perhaps you would be grateful", the verse says, and the words "forgave you so perhaps you would be grateful" carry the lesson of the whole episode: a pardon is given in the expectation of thanks.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Even then"** means after that, after you had worshipped the calf. The forgiveness did not come for nothing. The Israelites were forgiven only once they had offered the repentance (tawbah) described in verse 54, and when they asked for forgiveness Allah did not call them to account for what they had done.

**"We still forgave you"** means that Allah erased their sins and let them be, rather than wiping them out as the sin deserved. The favour being recalled is, in effect, Allah saying: remember My favour on you when I forgave you the calf.

**"so perhaps you would be grateful"**. The Arabic word laʿalla expresses expectation. That does not imply any doubt or misgiving on Allah's part about the outcome, which is impossible for Him. It simply conveys that when a person is pardoned, those who look on expect him to feel grateful. Gratitude here means thanking Allah for the pardon by worshipping Him in the best way and doing what He has instructed.

### Why so quick a pardon?

The devotional commentary notices how swiftly so grave a sin was pardoned, and draws a sobering conclusion: the speed of the pardon points to the lesser standing of those who were pardoned. Allah's own words bear this out. To the wives of the Prophet ﷺ He said that if any of them committed a blatant misconduct her punishment would be doubled (33:30), because higher rank brings stricter account. The Children of Israel worshipped the calf, and Allah said, "Even then We still forgave you". To the community of Muḥammad ﷺ He said, "whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it" (99:8). A light reckoning is not always a compliment.

### In short

- The pardon came after the repentance of verse 54, and it meant that the sin was erased and the people spared.
- "Perhaps" expresses what onlookers expect of a pardoned person, not any uncertainty on Allah's part.
- Gratitude for forgiveness is shown by obedience.
- The ease of the Israelites' pardon is read as a sign of their lesser station, since those of higher rank are judged more strictly (33:30, 99:8).

<<<VERSE 2:53>>>
The next favour is the Book itself. "And remember when We gave Moses the Scripture", says the verse, calling it "the standard" that distinguishes right from wrong, and giving its purpose: "that perhaps you would be rightly guided".

### Phrase by phrase

**"when We gave Moses the Scripture"** means the Torah, the Book given to Mūsā (Moses). This is the Book he received at the end of the forty days described in Sūrat al-Aʿrāf (7:142), after the Israelites had come safely through the sea. Another verse puts the gift in its historical place: Allah gave Mūsā the Scripture after destroying earlier nations, as an insight for people, a guide and a mercy (28:43).

**"the standard"** translates al-furqān (also written al-Furqan). In the language of the Qur'an a furqān is something that separates truth from falsehood or tells the one apart from the other, from the root faraqa, to divide. Grammatically the word is an explanatory supplement to "the Scripture". The Torah is thus being described as the discriminator between truth and falsehood, guidance and error, the lawful and the unlawful, commands and prohibitions, and more besides. Scholars offer three accounts of what exactly the furqān is here. It may be the injunctions of the Sharīʿah found in the Torah. The Sharīʿah settles the disagreements that arise about doctrine and about good conduct. It may be the miracles that decide in a visible way between a true claim and a false one. Or it may be the Torah itself, which is at once a Book of Allah and a tool for telling truth from falsehood. One early commentary adds a further reading: the furqān is the victory over Pharaoh, which separated the party of truth from the party of falsehood by force of events.

**"that perhaps you would be rightly guided"** means so that you might be guided by it and kept away from error.

### The way of life the Book points to

The verses that follow show what the Israelites did with their guidance, so it is worth stating what true piety looks like according to the Book. It is to worship God alone, to believe in Him although one has not seen Him, to live in fear of the Day of Judgement, to live on the pure things of the earth, and to hold back from sin whoever one can. Those who adopt this way of life are the ones who succeed in the next world.

### The inner standard

The devotional reading finds in this verse a contrast between what Mūsā's people were given and what the people of Muḥammad ﷺ were given. He gave Mūsā's people the discernment outwardly, in a Book. In the people of Muḥammad ﷺ He placed the discernment inwardly as well as outwardly. This inner discernment is a light in the hearts of His friends by which they separate truth from falsehood, and the Qur'an alludes to it when it tells the believers that if they are mindful of Allah He will grant them a furqān (8:29). This is why al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ told the Companion Wābiṣah (Wabisa) to seek a ruling from his own heart, and why he said to beware the perceptiveness of the man of faith, because he looks with the light of God. When this discernment appears in a person's inner life, his drinking and his aspiration are cleansed of the dust of others. The walkway of his desire is kept clear of the rubble of habit. The carpet of his days is shaken free of the clouding of mortal nature. The eyes of his moment are kept from reaching for newly created things. Then what is a mere report for other people becomes face-to-face vision for him. What had been the knowledge of certainty becomes the eye of certainty. Nothing new comes into existence in the kingdom without his heart being aware of it. Al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ was asked whether this state has a sign, and he said that when light enters the heart the breast is opened. That opening is the mark. When the breast is opened the aspiration climbs high and every sorrow is comforted. Scatteredness becomes gatheredness. The carpet of subsistence is spread and the rug of annihilation rolled up. The corner of heartache is closed and the door of the garden of union opened. The tongue of such a person's state speaks in verse. In the quarter of hope he has a delightful home. In the story of passion he has a delightful problem. If the spirit of the world asks after the details of his heart, let it know in short that his heart is delighted.

### In short

- The Scripture is the Torah, and al-furqān, "the standard", describes it as the thing that separates truth from falsehood.
- Scholars identify the furqān with the Torah's laws, with the miracles that proved Mūsā's claim, with the Torah itself, or with the victory over Pharaoh.
- The Book was given so that the Israelites might be guided (28:43), and true piety under it means worshipping God alone and fearing the Day of Judgement.
- The believers of this community are promised an inner furqān as well (8:29), the light by which a believer's heart tells truth from falsehood.

<<<VERSE 2:54>>>
Verse 54 goes back to the calf and tells how the sin was put right. Mūsā (Moses) said to his people, "O my people! Surely you have wronged yourselves by worshipping the calf, so turn in repentance to your Creator", and the repentance prescribed was that they should execute the calf-worshippers among themselves. The verse ends: "Then He accepted your repentance. Surely He is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful". This was the repentance required of the Children of Israel for worshipping the calf, and it belongs among the favours because without it there would have been no pardon.

### Phrase by phrase

**"when Moses said to his people"**. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī placed the moment precisely. When the Israelites' hearts awoke to what they had done in worshipping the calf, they were filled with remorse, realised they had gone astray, and cried out that if their Lord did not have mercy on them and forgive them they would certainly be losers (7:149). That was when Mūsā spoke these words to them. The address is to those who had worshipped the calf.

**"you have wronged yourselves by worshipping the calf"** means you have harmed yourselves by taking the golden calf as a god. The harm of idolatry falls first on the idolater.

**"so turn in repentance to your Creator"**. The word for Creator here is Bāriʾ, and Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas all explained it as your Creator. The choice of this name is itself a rebuke. It confronts the Israelites with the enormity of what they did: repent to the One who created you, after you set up partners beside Him in worship. One early commentary reconstructs the exchange. When the people asked Mūsā what they should do, he told them to turn in penitence to their Creator. When they asked how they were to do that, he gave them the command that follows.

**"and execute yourselves"** means that those who had not worshipped the calf were to kill those who had. An-Nasāʾī, Ibn Jarīr and Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded Ibn ʿAbbās's account: Allah informed the Children of Israel that their repentance was to put to the sword every person they met, whether father or son, without regard to whom they killed. Those who were guilty, and whose guilt Mūsā and Hārūn (Aaron) had not known, confessed their sin and did as they were ordered, and Allah forgave both the killer and the killed. This forms part of the long hadith about the trials of Mūsā that is kept for Sūrat Ṭā Hā. Ibn Jarīr also narrated from Ibn ʿAbbās the way it was carried out. Allah ordered Mūsā to have his people kill one another. Those who had worshipped the calf were told to sit down, and those who had not were told to stand holding knives. When the killing began, a thick darkness fell over them, and when it lifted they had killed seventy thousand. Those who were killed were forgiven, and those who were left alive were forgiven too. Another account describes the darkness as a dark cloud Allah sent down so that no one could see another and be moved to spare him, until nearly seventy thousand had died.

**"That is best for you in the sight of your Creator"**. The repentance and the killing were better for them than persisting in disbelief, which would have led to eternity in the Fire. Allah also made the task easier for them, as the darkness shows, since it kept kinsman from recognising kinsman.

**"Then He accepted your repentance. Surely He is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful"**. He relented toward them and forgave them. One commentary reads the phrase as describing Allah turning toward them in mercy even before their own turning in repentance, since no one repents unless Allah first turns to him. He is at-Tawwāb, the One who accepts the repentance of the penitent, and ar-Raḥīm, merciful toward whoever dies repentant.

### A severe form of repentance

Scholars note that this verse describes a special mode of repentance prescribed for the Israelites in this particular situation, in which the innocent were to execute the guilty. The principle is not foreign to Islamic law. In the Sharīʿah too, certain major sins carry the death penalty even when the sinner has repented: a life for a life in deliberate murder, and stoning for adultery established by proper evidence. When the Israelites carried out the divine command, they became worthy of Allah's mercy and favour in the next world. Allah mentions the whole affair among His favours because He allowed them to seek forgiveness at all, and because when they did what Mūsā told them, with Allah's approval, He forgave them, for He is forgiving and merciful toward His servants.

### What the sin did and did not harm

The devotional reading puts a further speech in Mūsā's mouth. Take care not to imagine that your worship of the calf has damaged the majesty of the Self-Sufficient, or that it is a flaw in His kingship and lordship. The harm and the misfortune are yours alone. If something bad happened, it happened to you, in that you were held back from a Lord like Him, for He has servants in plenty. Sahl ibn ʿAbdallāh at-Tustarī told a story to the same effect. When God spoke to Mūsā at Mount Sinai, the exaltedness of His speech turned the mountain to carnelian, and Mūsā glanced at himself and thought: who is like me, with the God of the world speaking to me without intermediary and the ground I stand on turned to carnelian? God did not let the thought pass. He told Mūsā to look to his right and his left, and Mūsā saw a thousand mountains of carnelian like Sinai, on each one a man in the form of Mūsā, wearing a blanket like his, a hat on his head, a staff in his hand, speaking with the Lord of the universe. The tongue of Mūsā's state then said: I imagined that You were one body with me. How could I have known that You are everyone's intimate? The commentary adds the whisper of a poor man heard praying: O God, accept me as Your lover, and if not as a lover then as Your servant, and if not as a servant then as Your dog, the dog that lies at Your threshold. O Friend, if You do not give me the front row of honour, at least keep me outside the door with the dogs.

Finally, the command "execute yourselves" is read inwardly as an address to the chevaliers of the Ṭarīqah, the Sufi path: take the sword of struggle to the heads of your lower souls, and so arrive at Me.

### In short

- When the Israelites woke to their sin (7:149), Mūsā told them that they had wronged only themselves and that their repentance was to be the execution of the calf-worshippers by those who had not worshipped it.
- Ibn ʿAbbās reports that a darkness fell over them as they carried it out, that seventy thousand were killed, and that killer and killed alike were forgiven.
- The name Bāriʾ, Creator, underlines the enormity of worshipping a made thing instead of the Maker.
- The Sharīʿah too retains capital penalties for certain sins even after repentance, and the Israelites' obedience earned them Allah's mercy.
- The sin harmed the sinners, not their Lord, who has servants without number.
