<<<VERSE 2:282>>>
Trust and practical safeguards belong together: "O believers! When you contract a loan for a fixed period of time, commit it to writing." The verse gives detailed rules for recording obligations, taking witnesses and protecting those who assist. Clear evidence serves justice before a dispute arises and helps preserve relationships when memory fails.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! When you contract a loan for a fixed period of time, commit it to writing."** The instruction includes loans and deferred transactions such as payment now for later delivery. The amount, description and due date need clarity. Recording them protects rights beyond the moment when both parties remember the agreement.

**"Let the scribe maintain justice between the parties."** The writer must record what was agreed without adding, omitting or favouring one side. Skill must not become a means of exploiting a party unable to check the record.

**"The scribe should not refuse to write as Allah has taught them to write."** Knowledge is a divine gift to be used in helping others. The repeated command to write emphasises that duty. Mujahid and 'Ata say a requested scribe is required to record the terms.

**"They will write what the debtor dictates, bearing Allah in mind and not defrauding the debt."** The person against whom the claim stands acknowledges it through dictation. They must not hide any part, diminish the value or alter its description. Taqwa guards the record from dishonesty even where manipulation might pass unnoticed.

**"If the debtor is incompetent, weak, or unable to dictate, let their guardian dictate for them with justice."** The cases include reckless handling of wealth, minority, incapacity, old age, illness, inability to speak or a language barrier. A wali, guardian or authorised representative, supplies the dictation without abusing that position.

**"Call upon two of your men to witness."** The explanation requires mature, sane, upright Muslim men. Some glosses also expressly mention free status. The witnesses preserve the agreement in addition to its written form.

**"If two men cannot be found, then one man and two women of your choice will witness"** The specified alternative concerns financial contracts, directly or indirectly involving property. Trustworthiness and approved character remain necessary. One man alone or only two women are not the combination described for ordinary dealings here.

**"so if one of the women forgets the other may remind her."** The explanation concerns error or forgetfulness corrected through the other's reminder. Readings of the verb for reminding and of the conditional clause give grammatical variations while retaining that stated purpose.

**"The witnesses must not refuse when they are summoned."** The interpretations distinguish being asked initially to witness from being called later to testify. All emphasise preserving the truth rather than suppressing known evidence.

**"You must not be against writing ˹contracts˺ for a fixed period—whether the sum is small or great."** Do not become weary of documenting a modest debt. A small amount can also produce disagreement, and the term matters alongside the sum.

**"This is more just ˹for you˺ in the sight of Allah, and more convenient to establish evidence and remove doubts."** The document supports witnesses' recollection and ends disagreement over what was promised. Their handwriting or signatures can help them remember the transaction.

**"However, if you conduct an immediate transaction among yourselves, then there is no need for you to record it,"** An exchange completed on the spot does not carry the same deferred obligation. Its omission from writing is not sinful merely because a record was not made.

**"but call upon witnesses when a deal is finalized."** Witnesses provide an additional safeguard even in immediate trade. The discussion gives different explanations of whether this is obligatory or recommended, particularly in light of the following verse's permission to rely on trust.

**"Let no harm come to the scribe or witnesses."** Their assistance must not expose them to wrongful burden. Another reading also directs them not to cause harm through falsified records or distorted testimony. Both sides of the transaction deserve protection.

**"If you do, then you have gravely exceeded ˹your limits˺."** Causing the prohibited harm is sin, not merely a breach of etiquette. Protecting one's own claim does not authorise violating another person's rights.

**"Be mindful of Allah, for Allah ˹is the One Who˺ teaches you."** He teaches conduct beneficial here and in the Hereafter. Awareness of Him should govern both receiving the instruction and acting upon it.

**"And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things"** Amounts, descriptions, consequences and intentions are known to Him. A technical pretext cannot make a deception invisible to the Lord.

This is the Qur'an's longest verse. Abu Ja'far bin Jarir records Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib's report that he had been told the debt verse was the last revealed from above the Throne. That report differs from the earlier identification of the verse about the final return as the last revelation. It is retained as a reported view, not merged into one chronology.

### Why specify the term rather than leave it vague?

The legal explanation requires a determined due date without ambiguity. Merely saying at harvest is not sufficiently precise, because weather may change when harvesting takes place. A date and month remove that uncertainty.

The Two Sahihs record from Ibn 'Abbas that on arriving in Al-Madinah the Prophet ﷺ found people paying ahead for fruit to be delivered after one or two years. He instructed that such payment be for a specified measure, weight and delivery term.

The allowance therefore does not permit an undefined obligation. Clear quantities and timing protect the parties from later disagreement. Written terms preserve that clarity when the transaction remains open for a long period.

### Is writing a strict requirement in every case?

One account treats it as initially necessary, then relaxed by the following permission to entrust one another. That view is transmitted from Ibn Zayd, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas, Al-Hasan, Ash-Sha'bi, Abu Sa'id and Ibn Jurayj.

Another explanation treats taking witnesses as recommended rather than universally obligatory. Both discussions preserve the practical value of documentation. Relaxation where genuine trust exists is not a claim that evidence is useless.

Ibn Jurayj distinguishes writing terms when borrowing from taking witnesses when buying. The common aim is securing rights and reducing disputes, not encouraging suspicion for its own sake.

### Why is writing itself a form of service?

The scribe received a capacity others may lack. Using it to assist an unlettered person can be charity, just as a hadith describes helping a worker or doing what a weak person cannot do.

Another hadith warns of a fiery bridle for someone who withholds knowledge. The account uses these sayings to discourage selfish refusal, without adding collectors or grades not supplied for them.

The writer must also refuse dishonesty. Helping one party obtain a passing advantage by falsifying terms causes lasting harm to the writer's own moral account. Gratitude for skill therefore means service joined to fairness, not compliance with every request regardless of truth.

### Who may dictate when the debtor cannot?

The guardian can be a parent, executor, custodian or interpreter, depending on the incapacity. A representative can assist someone whose language the scribe does not understand. The word wali is broad enough in the discussion to cover guardianship and authorised representation.

One gloss identifies the guardian of the debtor's financial interests with the creditor. Others emphasise the person in charge of the debtor's affairs. Whichever role is described, justice is explicit: no extra amount may be added because the person cannot dictate.

The debtor's own dictation is otherwise preferred because the record then expresses the acknowledgment from the liable party. Guardianship repairs a genuine inability rather than removing the person's responsibility without cause.

### Why require the stated witness combination?

The explanation connects two women's testimony in the described financial cases with the provision for one to remind the other. Muslim records from Abu Hurayrah the Prophet's address urging women towards charity and forgiveness, with a warning about frequent cursing and ingratitude towards husbands.

When a woman asked about his reference to shortcomings in mind and religion, he explained the first through the stated witness rule and the second through nights without prayer and the interruption of fasting during Ramadan. The report also remarks on women's influence over men of otherwise sound minds.

Those are the explanation and examples supplied in the hadith. The passage should not be expanded into an unsupported claim about every woman's intelligence, honesty or capacity in every subject. Here its legal application is specified financial evidence, alongside the requirement that approved witnesses be trustworthy.

The reminder provision matters because preserving a right includes correcting forgetfulness, not merely counting names. The account notes readings fa-tudhakkira and fa-tudhkira, and a conditional reading giving the reminder as the response if error occurs.

### When must a witness come forward?

Qatadah and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas apply the command to accepting the role when called. Ibn 'Abbas and Al-Hasan Al-Basri are reported as including both initial witnessing and subsequent testimony.

Mujahid and Abu Mijlaz distinguish them: one may choose whether to become a witness, but someone who has witnessed must come forward when testimony is needed. The majority explanation in the discussion particularly understands the verse through testifying to what is already known.

Some describe the initial duty as fard kifayah, a communal obligation fulfilled by enough suitable people in the ummah, the Muslim community. Later testimony can likewise become communal if the needed evidence has already been properly supplied. Otherwise the knowledgeable witness must not leave a right unsupported.

### What happened in the horse transaction?

Ahmad records through the family of Khuzaymah bin Thabit Al-Ansari an account of the Prophet ﷺ agreeing to buy a horse from a Bedouin. He went ahead to obtain its price while the seller followed.

Other men, unaware of the agreement, offered more. The seller then told the Prophet ﷺ to buy or he would sell elsewhere. When the Prophet ﷺ affirmed that he had already bought it, the seller denied the sale and demanded a witness.

Khuzaymah Al-Ansari testified that the horse had been sold to him. Asked his basis, he said his trust in the messenger. The Prophet ﷺ made Khuzaymah's testimony equivalent to two men's. Abu Dawud and An-Nasa also record the account.

The discussion uses the transaction to support the view that witnesses are not always a condition required before a sale can occur. The exceptional status granted to Khuzaymah is part of that report, not a permission for everyone to claim observation of events they never saw.

### Why protect scribes and witnesses from burdens?

If service routinely means unpaid cost, humiliation or repeated disruption, truthful people become reluctant to assist. The shariah discussion therefore permits a scribe's requested wage and a witness's legitimate return travel expense. Refusing such dues can be part of the prohibited harm.

The social criticism describes witnesses kept waiting for hours by police, treated as criminals or repeatedly summoned as hearings are postponed. Work, family needs and livelihoods suffer, while threats of arrest make avoidance harder.

It argues that such treatment deters honest witnesses and leaves professional witnesses willing to say either truth or falsehood. The remedy joins the duty to testify with safeguards against unnecessary harm. Justice should not be preserved for the disputants by destroying it for those helping them.

Al-Hasan and Qatadah also apply the prohibition to the helpers' own conduct. The scribe must not record something other than dictated, and the witness must not substitute false evidence or suppress what was heard.

### How does taqwa strengthen this system?

The verse begins and ends legal detail with awareness of Allah. Some jurists draw twenty important rulings from it, but the rules still depend on people willing to apply them honestly.

The link between taqwa and being taught is illustrated through (8:29), the gift of a criterion distinguishing right from wrong, and (57:28), mercy and light by which to walk. Allah's guidance includes insight as well as an external document.

The inward reading calls this caution an expression of mercy. Allah teaches particulars of transactions, strengthens rights and calls for witnesses both at the outset and through execution so that people do not become adversaries. Sidq, sincerity and truthfulness, is its underlying character.

### In short

- Record deferred obligations with clear amounts, descriptions and due dates.
- Fair dictation, competent witnesses and accurate writing protect both parties.
- Interpretations distinguish obligatory safeguards from permissions where genuine trust exists.
- Those providing records or testimony must neither cause harm nor be wrongfully burdened.
- Taqwa makes the evidence system an expression of truth and mercy rather than a tool of manipulation.

<<<VERSE 2:283>>>
When the usual written safeguard is unavailable, another can secure the debt: "If you are on a journey and a scribe cannot be found, then a security can be taken." But even a pledge is not required where the parties rely on genuine trust. The responsibility to repay and to speak truth remains, with or without documents or collateral.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you are on a journey and a scribe cannot be found,"** The situation particularly concerns a deferred debt during travel. Ibn 'Abbas includes lack of paper, ink or pen even when a person who can write is present. The need is for a usable means of recording, not merely a literate companion.

**"then a security can be taken."** Rahn is a pledge held to secure the claim. The readings ruhun and rihan are its plural forms. Being taken into possession is explained as a condition of validity and assurance for the creditor or representative.

**"If you trust one another, then ˹there is no need for a security, but˺ the debtor should honour this trust ˹by repaying the debt˺"** Trust allows omission of the pledge. Ash-Sha'bi also permits omission of writing and witnesses in that case. The absence of a safeguard does not remove the obligation itself.

**"and let them fear Allah, their Lord."** The trusted debtor must not deny the debt or exploit the creditor's confidence. Taqwa guards a right that may lack easily produced external proof.

**"And do not conceal the testimony,"** A person knowing the truth must not hide it when called. If trust is betrayed by denial, the knowledgeable witness must come forward rather than help the wrong through silence.

**"for whoever conceals it, their hearts are indeed sinful."** The heart is where the decision to conceal begins. The wrong is not merely an idle tongue. As-Suddi explains it as being a sinner inwardly too.

**"And Allah ˹fully˺ knows what you do"** He knows whether the trust was discharged and whether testimony was given or hidden. A lack of human evidence does not create a gap in His knowledge.

### Is a pledge allowed only during travel?

The sunnah also permits pledges in towns where writers are available. Travel is singled out because obtaining a record can be harder and security more urgent there. The circumstance explains the example without restricting every permissible use to it.

The Two Sahihs record from Anas that the Prophet ﷺ died while his shield was pledged to a Jewish person for barley bought on credit to provide for his household. Another wording places the creditor among the Jews of Al-Madinah.

The supplied account describes thirty Wasq and gives an approximate weight of 180 kilograms. Those are its stated unit and estimate, retained without silently substituting a different unit from outside knowledge.

The example shows that securing a genuine debt did not contradict trust in Allah. It also gives a concrete settled-town case, beside the journey mentioned in the verse.

### May the creditor benefit from pledged property?

The legal explanation limits the creditor to holding it until the debt is paid. Benefits arising from it remain the original owner's right. Taking it into possession is security, not a transfer of ownership or an unrestricted licence to profit from it.

The distinction protects the debtor even while protecting the creditor's claim. A debt safeguard should not become another extraction beyond what was lent.

### How does trust affect the earlier commands?

Ibn Abi Hatim records with a sound chain Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri's view that this verse abrogated the prior requirement to record and have witnesses. Other explanations describe trust as a permission not to use those means.

In either account, the relaxation concerns the external safeguards, not honesty or repayment. The debtor remains responsible for what was taken. Ahmad and the Sunan record from Samurah, through Al-Hasan and Qatadah, the saying that the hand bears what it took until it returns it.

No interpretation here says that an unwritten debt can be denied lawfully. The creditor's choice to trust is precisely why faithfulness is required of the debtor.

### Why is concealed testimony a heart offence?

Someone might think only the tongue failed to perform a duty. But an inward choice usually directed that silence. Prestige, fear of losing advantage or attachment to someone can make the person withhold the truth.

The discussion places false evidence and hiding true evidence among the gravest sins. It recalls (5:106), a pledge not to hide testimony, and (4:135), the duty to stand for justice even against oneself, parents or relatives, and regardless of wealth or poverty.

That reference also rejects following desire through distortion or refusal. Justice cannot depend on whether the witness prefers the person who would benefit from the evidence.

The social reflection calls concealment a retreat from the believer's role in making just decisions. Conscience asks for truth to be acknowledged and falsehood exposed. A person hiding evidence for worldly standing becomes implicated through the silence chosen.

### What does the shared reflection add about human freedom?

The world from particles to stars follows Allah's command. Human beings appear able to choose different paths, but their freedom is a test rather than independence from Him.

The application here is voluntary fidelity. Other creation follows its appointed course, while the person must choose restraint and obedience. Feeling unobserved is not proof of being outside the Lord's watch.

That shared principle continues into the next verses concerning inward knowledge and belief. Here it guards the entrusted debt and the testimony that might otherwise remain hidden.

### In short

- A pledge can secure a debt when recording is unavailable, and is also permitted beyond travel.
- Trust allows fewer external safeguards but never cancels repayment.
- The creditor's possession of security does not authorise taking its benefits.
- Concealing evidence is an inward decision as well as a failure of speech.
- Allah's knowledge protects the truth even where human records are incomplete.
