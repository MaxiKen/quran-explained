<<<VERSE 7:150>>>
Mūsā (Moses) came down from his meeting with Allah to find his people worshipping the calf: "Upon Moses’ return to his people, ˹totally˺ furious and sorrowful, he said, “What an evil thing you committed in my absence! Did you want to hasten your Lord’s torment?”" In his anger he set down the Tablets and seized his brother: "Then he threw down the Tablets and grabbed his brother by the hair, dragging him closer." Hārūn (Aaron) answered him, "O son of my mother! The people overpowered me and were about to kill me." He went on, "So do not ˹humiliate me and˺ make my enemies rejoice, nor count me among the wrongdoing people.”" Allah had already told Mūsā on Mount Sinai what his people had done. Seeing it with his own eyes filled him with indignation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Upon Moses’ return to his people, ˹totally˺ furious and sorrowful"**: when he came back after speaking with his Lord and heard the noise of their revolt. He was angry with them and bitterly grieved. Abū ad-Dardāʾ said that the word used here for sorrow, asif, means the most intense kind of anger.

**"he said, “What an evil thing you committed in my absence!"** Evil is what you did after I left you for the mountain, worshipping the calf. Literally: evil is the way you have taken my place after me. It is an evil state that leads only to ruin and misery.

**"Did you want to hasten your Lord’s torment?”"** Literally, "Did you hasten your Lord's matter?" Several meanings are given. Mūsā was saying: you wanted me to rush back to you, though my staying there was Allah's decision. Or: were you so tired of waiting out the forty nights your Lord had appointed for me that you turned to the calf? Or: did you act in haste against your Lord's command, when you should have waited for Allah's Book to reach you? Or: did you rush to bring on your Lord's promised judgement by worshipping the calf? Some say it means they hastily decided that Mūsā had died.

**"Then he threw down the Tablets"**: the Tablets of the Torah. He did this out of anger at his people, anger for the sake of his Lord, according to most scholars of earlier and later times. Some reports say the Tablets broke, two of them, or into pieces.

**"and grabbed his brother by the hair, dragging him closer."** He seized Hārūn by the hair of his head with his right hand and by the beard with his left, and pulled him close in anger. Hārūn had been with them and had not managed to stop them, and Mūsā feared he had not tried hard enough to forbid them. Another passage records the exchange: "O Aaron! What prevented you, when you saw them going astray from following after me? How could you disobey my orders?”" (20:92-93). Hārūn replied, "O son of my mother! Do not seize me by my beard or ˹the hair of˺ my head. I really feared that you would say, ‘You have caused division among the Children of Israel, and did not observe my word.’" (20:94).

**"Aaron pleaded, “O son of my mother!"** The two were full brothers, sons of the same father too. Hārūn spoke of their mother so that Mūsā would feel more tender and gentle toward him. Naming the mother is the more affectionate appeal to the heart. The words can be read ibna ummi or ibna umma, both meaning "son of my mother".

**"The people overpowered me and were about to kill me."** They judged me weak, humiliated me and showed me no respect. They nearly killed me because I opposed them.

**"So do not ˹humiliate me and˺ make my enemies rejoice"**: do not punish or humiliate me in a way that lets my enemies, the calf-worshippers, gloat over me and spite me.

**"nor count me among the wrongdoing people.”"** In your anger and your reproach of them, do not treat me as one of those who did wrong by worshipping something other than Allah. Do not put me on their level, as if I were one of them.

### Why did Mūsā react only on seeing it?

This verse shows the meaning of the hadith, "Hearing about something is not the same as seeing it." Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: may Allah have mercy on Mūsā. One who sees a thing is not like one who is only told of it. His Lord told him that his people had been tested after he left, and he did not throw down the Tablets. When he saw them with his own eyes, then he threw them down.

### Did Mūsā show disrespect to the Torah?

The Arabic word used here, ilqāʾ, means dropping or throwing. That raises a doubt: did Mūsā show disrespect to the Tablets of the Torah by throwing them down? Throwing down the Tablets would plainly be a grave sin. It is equally plain that all the prophets are innocent and free of sin. The meaning, therefore, is that Mūsā put the Tablets aside very quickly to free his hands so he could take hold of his brother, so quickly that it looked as if he had dropped them. The Qur'an uses this word as a sign of warning.

### Why did he blame Hārūn?

Mūsā had left Hārūn among the people as his deputy. When he saw the Israelites worshipping the golden calf, he held Hārūn responsible, thinking he had neglected his duty as a reformer. He turned to his people first, then to his brother, and took hold of him in anger. Hārūn then explained his position. He had tried everything he could to set them on the right path, but they would not listen.

### In short

- Mūsā returned furious and grieved, and rebuked his people for the evil they had done in his absence.
- Being told of the calf did not move him to throw the Tablets, but seeing it did, for hearing is not like seeing.
- He set the Tablets down so fast that it seemed he had thrown them, and seized Hārūn, fearing he had not done enough.
- Hārūn appealed to him as "son of my mother", explaining that the people had overpowered him and nearly killed him.

<<<VERSE 7:151>>>
Once Mūsā (Moses) was satisfied that his brother was innocent, he turned to Allah in prayer: "Moses prayed, “My Lord! Forgive me and my brother! And admit us into Your mercy. You are the Most Merciful of the merciful.”" Hārūn (Aaron) had given him the true account. It was not his fault. He had tried to stop the people from this wicked act, but they would not listen, and they were so stubborn they almost killed him. Hārūn asked Mūsā not to count him among the ignorant or let his enemies laugh at him, and Mūsā's anger cooled.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Moses prayed, “My Lord! Forgive me"**: forgive me for what I did to my brother.

**"and my brother!"** Forgive Hārūn any shortcoming in his duties, including that he did not fight them. Mūsā included him in the supplication to make peace with him and to deny the enemies any chance to gloat over his misfortune.

**"And admit us into Your mercy."** Enclose us in Your mercy and bring us into Your Garden.

**"You are the Most Merciful of the merciful.”"** You show us more mercy than anyone who shows mercy.

### What showed Hārūn was innocent?

Hārūn had warned the people before Mūsā returned: "O my people! You are only being tested by this, for indeed your ˹one true˺ Lord is the Most Compassionate. So follow me and obey my orders." (20:90). When Mūsā saw that his brother had not failed in his duty, he checked himself and began praying for him.

Misunderstandings often arise between believers. Once things are made clear, they return to normal, as if no misunderstanding had ever happened.

### Why did Mūsā ask forgiveness for himself?

He had done nothing like what the people did, yet he named himself first. One reason may be the haste with which he set down the Tablets of the Torah, which the Qur'an called "throwing" as a sign of warning. Another may be to teach people that when they seek forgiveness for others, they should include themselves, so that they do not grow complacent.

### The inner reading: asking forgiveness for sins not committed

In this prayer lies the lesson that the servant must ask forgiveness in every state. It is Allah's right to punish even the innocent, since all creation belongs to Him and the Owner's decree runs through all He owns. The children of Israel sinned, yet Mūsā and his brother were the ones who apologised and asked forgiveness. This is the path of the chivalrous and the road of the Sufis. They take the sin upon themselves and apologise for sins they did not commit. As the verse goes: "When we are ill, we come to You and visit You. They sin, and we come to You and apologise."

### In short

- After Hārūn showed he had warned the people, Mūsā's anger cooled and he prayed for them both.
- He sought forgiveness for his own haste and for any shortcoming of his brother, and asked to be enclosed in Allah's mercy.
- Including oneself when asking forgiveness for others guards against complacency.
- The servant asks forgiveness in every state, even for sins committed by others.
