Musa comes down from the mountain and finds his people around the calf. "Upon Moses’ return to his people, ˹totally˺ furious and sorrowful, he said," and the first words are for the people. "What an evil thing you committed in my absence! Did you want to hasten your Lord’s torment?”" Then he turns. "Then he threw down the Tablets and grabbed his brother by the hair, dragging him closer." The answer of Harun follows. "Aaron pleaded, “O son of my mother! The people overpowered me and were about to kill me." And he asks for two things. "So do not ˹humiliate me and˺ make my enemies rejoice, nor count me among the wrongdoing people.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Upon Moses’ return to his people, ˹totally˺ furious and sorrowful, he said,"** describes the state he came back in. Verse 150 opens with what happened once the meeting on the mount was over. He was angry, and he was filled with regret. One of the companions, Abu Ad-Darda, said of this that the grief which comes with anger is the severest kind of anger there is.

**"What an evil thing you committed in my absence!"** is said to the people first. What they had done after he went up the mount was to worship the calf, and the words name it as an evil thing in the plainest terms. He told them that the condition they had fallen into led only to ruin and misery.

**"Did you want to hasten your Lord’s torment?”"** is the second part of the question. One sense of it is that they wanted him to come back to them sooner than the appointment Allah had set, and another is that they were in a hurry for the judgement of their Lord and brought it on themselves by worshipping the calf. Some of those who explained the Book said the words mean that they had quickly decided that Musa was dead, and that the decision was made before the book of Allah had come to them.

**"Then he threw down the Tablets and grabbed his brother by the hair, dragging him closer."** is what he did next. He had left Harun among them as his deputy, and seeing the people as they were, he turned on his brother. In the report of the words it is said that he took hold of him by the head, seizing the hair itself with his right hand, and taking the beard with his left, and then drew him toward himself in his anger.

### The Tablets and the question of respect

What Musa did with the Tablets has been taken up carefully. The word used in the verse is one that carries the sense of putting a thing down or letting it fall, and it has raised the question whether a prophet showed disrespect to the book of the Torah by his action. The answer given is that every prophet is free of sin, so the act must be understood in its setting. He was putting the Tablets aside quickly in order to free his hands to take hold of his brother, and the effect of haste made it look as though they had been thrown down. The Book uses that word as a gesture of warning, and not as a record of disrespect. In one report it is added that two of the Tablets were broken in the process.

### Why Harun was taken to task

Musa took hold of his brother because he feared that Harun had not tried hard enough to forbid the people from the evil they had fallen into. That fear was answered in the words that came back to him.

**"Aaron pleaded, “O son of my mother!"** is where Harun begins. He called on the mother rather than on the father, although the two of them were full brothers, so that mercy and gentleness would come into the heart of Musa. The form of address was chosen for its effect on his brother's feelings, and it did its work.

**"The people overpowered me and were about to kill me."** is his account of what he had been through. He had stood against them and had forbidden the calf, and the people had counted him weak and had not respected him. They were so set on what they were doing that they came close to killing him. In another place in the Book the fuller exchange is recorded, where Musa asked him what had kept him from following after the people when he saw them going astray, and Harun answered that he should not take him by his beard or by his head, since he had feared that Musa would say that he had caused a division among the Children of Israel and had not respected his word (20:92-94).

**"So do not ˹humiliate me and˺ make my enemies rejoice, nor count me among the wrongdoing people.”"** is the plea he ends with. He asked his brother not to punish him in a way that the enemy, that is the people of the calf, would gloat over. He asked not to be placed on the same level as the wrongdoers, which would be a reproach laid on him by his brother. What he had said to the people beforehand was already on record, that they were being tried in this matter and that their Lord was the Most Gracious, so they should follow him and obey his order (20:90). When Musa had been satisfied that his brother was innocent, he turned to his Lord.

### Information is not the same as observation

The passage carries a lesson about the difference between hearing of a thing and seeing it. Allah had told Musa on the mountain that his people had been put to trial after he left them, and As-Samiri had led them astray. The news of it did not move him to throw the Tablets. It was when he came down and saw the calf and the people around it with his own eyes that he threw them down. So the verse demonstrates the meaning of a hadith of the Prophet ﷺ, that information is not the same as observation.

Ibn 'Abbas reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said of this, asking that Allah grant His mercy to Musa, and noting that one who observes a thing is nothing like one who is told about it. His Lord had told him that his people had been tried after him, and he did not throw the Tablets, and when he saw it with his eyes he threw them.

### The two brothers and the way of the believer

What happened between the two of them is also read as a lesson for every community. Musa held Harun responsible for what had happened, under the impression that his brother had failed in the duty of a reformer. He took hold of him in anger, and Harun answered by setting out the facts of the matter, saying that he had done everything in his power to show the people the right path. Then Musa checked himself and began to pray for his brother. Misunderstandings arise between believers, and once the matter is clarified they return to where they were, as though nothing had come between them.

There is an allusion in the passage that was drawn out by the people of the path. In this verse the servant of God is shown asking forgiveness in every state, and it is worth noticing that the Children of Israel were the ones who sinned, while Musa and his brother were the ones who asked forgiveness. From this it is taken that all of creation belongs to its Lord, and that the owner's disposal runs through everything that He owns. This is the path of the chevaliers and the road of those who travel the inner way, that they lay the sin upon themselves and ask pardon for wrongs they did not commit.

### In short

- Musa came back to his people angry and grieved, and Abu Ad-Darda said that the grief which comes with anger is the most severe form of anger.
- He charged the people with the evil they had done in his absence and asked whether they had wanted to hurry the judgement of their Lord, and one explanation is that they had quickly decided he was dead.
- He put the Tablets aside in haste to free his hands, and the word used in verse 150 is read as a gesture of warning rather than as disrespect, with two of the Tablets broken in the report.
- He seized Harun because he feared his brother had not forbidden the evil, and Harun answered that the people had counted him weak and had come close to killing him, and asked not to be humiliated in front of the enemy.
- The scene is read as proof that information is not the same as observation, and as a lesson in the way believers clear a misunderstanding and return to where they were.

<<<VERSE 7:151>>>
The anger is put down and the two brothers ask together. "Moses prayed," and the supplication is short and complete. "My Lord! Forgive me and my brother! And admit us into Your mercy. You are the Most Merciful of the merciful.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Moses prayed,"** marks the turn. He had held his brother responsible, heard the account of what Harun had been through, and then turned from the dispute to his Lord. What the words say is that the one who had been sent to lead the people put the matter back into the hands that it belonged to.

**"My Lord! Forgive me and my brother!"** brings the two of them into one request. He asks forgiveness for his brother for whatever shortcoming there may have been in the duties laid on him. Harun had been left among the people to keep them on the way of the Torah while his brother was away, and it was against that charge that Musa measured him. Then he asks forgiveness for himself. The reasons given for his own part differ and they sit together. One is that he put the Tablets aside in haste, which the Book described in the way it did in order to give a warning. Another is that the request was meant to teach people to include themselves whenever they ask forgiveness for someone else, so that they do not fall into a sense of being above the fault.

The mention of the brother inside the same supplication carries a purpose of its own. By bringing Harun into his prayer in that public setting, he reconciled his brother to himself and shut the door on any gloating over what Harun had suffered. The two of them had been through an argument in front of the people, and the prayer ended it in the sight of everyone.

**"And admit us into Your mercy."** asks for more than forgiveness. In one reading it is a request to be brought into the Garden of their Lord, and it takes in the two of them together as the forgiveness did. The mercy here is wide enough to cover the man who had been carried away by anger and the man who had been carried away by nothing at all and had still been blamed for it.

**"You are the Most Merciful of the merciful.”"** is the close of the supplication, and it names the ground on which the request is made. It is the attribute of Allah that the two of them are relying on, and the words leave the outcome entirely with Him.

### What the supplication teaches

The state of the two brothers at this point is worth noticing. One of them had come down the mountain to find the people in the middle of a great sin, and the other had stood in front of those people and had nearly been killed for it. Neither of them had done the evil, and both of them asked for pardon. The lesson drawn from that is that a person should ask forgiveness in every condition he may be in, and not only when he is aware of a fault. It is the way of those who take the inner road, that they put the fault upon themselves and ask pardon for things they did not commit, and the account of this verse is given as the pattern for it.

The supplication also shows how a disagreement between two believers closes. It closes with the two of them standing in the same request, asking for the same mercy, and naming the same Lord over them both.

### In short

- Musa turned from the dispute with his brother to his Lord, and asked forgiveness for himself and for Harun together.
- The forgiveness he sought for himself is read either as covering the hasty setting down of the Tablets or as a lesson teaching people to include themselves when they ask pardon for others.
- Bringing his brother into the supplication reconciled the two of them in front of the people and ended any gloating over what Harun had been through.
- The request for mercy asks to be admitted into it together, and the close names the attribute of Allah on which the whole prayer rests.
- The prayer marks the way believers leave a misunderstanding behind, and the way a servant asks forgiveness in every state he is in.
