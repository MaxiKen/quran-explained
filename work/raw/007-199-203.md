<<<VERSE 7:199>>>
After describing the stubborn ignorance and bad conduct of Islam's opponents, the Qur'an gives the Prophet ﷺ a contrasting code of conduct: "Be gracious, enjoin what is right, and turn away from those who act ignorantly". This verse is a complete code and model of moral excellence. Through it the Holy Prophet ﷺ was trained, and he was given the distinction, unique among all humankind, of embodying every moral excellence.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Be gracious,"** Literally, "take what is pardoned", khudh al-ʿafw. The word ʿafw has many meanings, and any of them may be meant here, which is why interpreters of the Qur'an have explained it in different ways. The most widely agreed meaning is something done easily, without difficulty. So the phrase means: accept from people what comes easily to them. Accept what issues naturally from people's manners and conduct, and do not scrutinise them. Do not ask them to do what is too hard for them, since that will drive them away. Another meaning of ʿafw is pardon and forgiveness, and some interpreters take it that way. On their view, the Prophet ﷺ is told to pardon people's mistakes and shortcomings. One explanation took it to mean the surplus wealth of one's family and dependents, a ruling later abrogated.

**"enjoin what is right,"** The word is al-ʿurf, which al-Bukhārī explained as righteousness. It means "the recognised": everything done in a good and praiseworthy way, kindness, decency and benevolence. He should tell people to speak beautifully and do good. The sense is that he should not avenge the oppression of his enemies but forgive them, while still urging them to do what is good and beneficial. So he would not merely repay evil with good and injustice with justice. He would repay it all with a higher degree of grace, that of iḥsān. Calling people to the oneness of Allah, the Hereafter, a virtuous life and justice is calling them to ʿurf, to deeds whose goodness reason itself can confirm.

**"and turn away from those who act ignorantly"** He should give up revenge, deal with his enemies in goodwill and concern, and explain the truth to them gently. But some ignorant diehards will be unmoved even by this gentle response, and will show yet more ignorance and harshness. He is told not to let their wounding remarks and crude challenges affect him, and not to answer them in their own terms. He should keep away from them and ignore them. Whoever harms him, he should not harm. Turning away also means not repaying evil with evil. It does not mean he should stop advising them, since that would not befit the mission of a prophet and messenger of Allah. One explanation names the ignorant as Abū Jahl and his people, who mocked the Prophet ﷺ, and says this turning away was later abrogated.

### "Accept what comes easily"

The prayer, ṣalāh, illustrates the first meaning. Its true spirit is to set aside every worldly thought and act and stand before one's Lord in perfect reverence. The worshipper offers his praise as though speaking to his Lord directly, making his requests to Him with nothing and no one in between. This state shows itself in signs of submission and humility, khushūʿ and khuḍūʿ, which only a few fortunate worshippers reach. Such reverence and love cannot be expected of every worshipper. So the Holy Prophet ﷺ was told not to demand the highest standard from people, but to accept whatever degree of excellence they could easily reach.

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī records this meaning. In it, ʿAbdullāh ibn az-Zubayr is reported, through his brother ʿUrwah and ʿUrwah's son Hishām, as saying the verse concerned nothing but people's character. Similar reports come through Hishām from Ibn ʿUmar and from ʿĀʾishah, and Allah knows best. Hishām ibn ʿUrwah reported his father saying that Allah commanded His Messenger ﷺ to pardon people their behaviour. Mujāhid, in more than one narration, said it means pardoning people's conduct and deeds. Another report says that when this verse was revealed, the Holy Prophet ﷺ said he had been commanded to accept ordinary obedience from people in their worship and conduct, and would do so as long as he was among them. Many interpreters of the Qur'an, including ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar, ʿAbdullāh ibn az-Zubayr, ʿĀʾishah and Mujāhid, confirmed this meaning.

ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said Allah commanded the Prophet ﷺ to forgive the idolaters and turn away from them for ten years, after which He ordered him to be firm with them.

### "Forgive whoever wrongs you"

Ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded, through Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah, that Umay said that when Allah revealed this verse, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked Jibrīl (Gabriel) what it meant. Jibrīl first confirmed the meaning with Allah. Then he said that Allah commanded him to forgive whoever wronged him, give to whoever withheld from him, and keep ties with whoever cut them off.

Ibn Marduwaih reported from Saʿd ibn ʿUbādah that the verse came down after the battle of Uḥud. Ḥamzah had been martyred there and his body savagely mutilated. Seeing him in that state, the Holy Prophet ﷺ said he would treat seventy of their men as they had treated Ḥamzah. This verse was then revealed, telling him that such revenge did not suit his noble station, and that he should pardon and forgive.

Imām Aḥmad supports this with a hadith from ʿUqbah ibn ʿĀmir. The Holy Prophet ﷺ taught him the same: to pardon whoever wrongs him, keep ties with whoever cuts him off, and give to whoever deprives him. Al-Bayhaqī also reported from ʿAlī that the Holy Prophet ﷺ said he would teach him manners better than those of all earlier and later peoples. He should give to whoever deprives him, pardon whoever wrongs him, and keep ties with whoever breaks with him.

The two meanings of ʿafw differ, but their essence is one. He should not demand the highest standard of worship and conduct from people. He should pardon their mistakes, take no revenge for their wrongs, and be generous to them without expecting anything in return.

### How did the Prophet ﷺ live this verse?

The authentic records of his life show that the Holy Prophet ﷺ gave the truest picture of this Qur'anic model of conduct. It reached full flower at the conquest of Makkah, when the enemies who had sworn to destroy him lay at his mercy. He set them all free. Far from seeking revenge for their injustices, he said, he would not even blame them for anything that had passed between them.

### ʿUmar and the verse

Al-Bukhārī recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās that during ʿUmar's caliphate, ʿUyaynah ibn Ḥiṣn ibn Ḥudhayfah came to Madinah and stayed with his nephew, al-Ḥurr ibn Qays. Al-Ḥurr was one of the learned men whom ʿUmar kept near him. ʿUmar liked to have those who knew the Qur'an by heart close to him and to consult them, whether they were old or young. ʿUyaynah told his nephew that he was close to the Commander of the Faithful, Amīr al-Muʾminīn, and asked him to get him a meeting. Al-Ḥurr asked ʿUmar, and ʿUmar gave permission.

Once in the presence of ʿUmar al-Fārūq, ʿUyaynah spoke rudely and untruthfully. "O Ibn al-Khaṭṭāb," he said, "you neither give us enough nor judge fairly among us." ʿUmar grew so angry that he almost punished him. Then al-Ḥurr said: "O Commander of the Faithful, Allah said to His Prophet ﷺ, 'Be gracious, enjoin what is right, and turn away from those who act ignorantly.' This man is one of the ignorant." By Allah, once ʿUmar heard the verse recited he did nothing more. He was one who stopped at the limits of Allah's Book. It was well known of him that he readily yielded to the commands of the Book of Allah, the Mighty, the Exalted.

### Two kinds of people

This verse gathers the noble traits of character. Some scholars sum it up this way. People are of two kinds. One kind does good. Accept their good deeds as they are, do not pry or ask more than they can bear, and do not demand the very best from them. The other kind falls short or does wrong. Teach them what is good and show them the way of righteousness. If they still refuse, cling to their error and speak arrogantly and aggressively, the right course is to keep away and not answer their ignorant talk. Ignoring them may avert their harm, and it is hoped that this may one day bring them round, when they realise their error.

### What should a caller to the truth do?

People find an upright life very hard. The desire for quick gain pulls them toward worldly profit, and they often exploit religion for worldly ends. When the call to pure truth reaches them, they fear for their interests and oppose it with all their might. In such a situation the only right response for the caller, the dāʿī, is avoidance. He should go on spreading the message without stirring up clashes or confrontation. Otherwise daʿwah turns into debate, and his time and energy are wasted arguing with those he addresses. To keep the call pure, the dāʿī must stay patient in every unpleasant situation and carry on his work in a positive way.

### The inner reading: the generous Lord's command

The command came from the generous, lovingly kind Lord, Lord of all lords and masters, generous and gentle in name and sign. It came to Muhammad, the Seal of the Prophets, whom the world's people follow. O master, pass over the sins of the sinners, cover their faults, and draw the pen of pardon across the record of wrongdoers. O master, take from Allah a pleasing character, praiseworthy conduct, truthful speech and peace with people. Be the friend of the good in company and attend to them in solitude.

Allah is a forbearing Lord who loves the forbearing. He hears the unworthy words of His enemies and sees their insolence in private, yet keeps the curtain drawn over them. He does not hurry to punish. He offers them repentance and pardon and calls them back to His threshold, as He says: "Tell the disbelievers that if they desist, their past will be forgiven." (8:38).

A tradition has Allah saying: "You called on Me, and I said, 'Here I am!' You asked of Me, and I gave to you. You rose against Me, and I gave you respite." He goes on: "You left Me, and I took care of you. You disobeyed Me, and I covered you. So if you return to Me, I will accept you, and if you turn away, I will wait for you." And He says: "I am the most munificent of the munificent and the most generous of the generous." Another report says that when an old man repents, Allah says: "Now? When your strength is gone and your appetite cut off? Yet I am the Most Merciful of the merciful, the Most Merciful of the merciful."

### In short

- The verse gives the Prophet ﷺ a complete moral code: accept what comes easily from people and pardon them, enjoin what is right, and turn away from the ignorant.
- Jibrīl explained it as forgiving whoever wrongs you, giving to whoever withholds from you and keeping ties with whoever cuts you off.
- The Prophet ﷺ lived it most fully at the conquest of Makkah, and ʿUmar's anger subsided the moment the verse was recited to him.
- Callers to the truth should keep calling patiently and avoid confrontation with those who answer with ignorance.

<<<VERSE 7:200>>>
The previous verse asked for something hard: forgiving the wrongs of the unjust and the ignorant and not answering evil with evil. This verse completes it: "If you are tempted by Satan, then seek refuge with Allah. Surely He is All-Hearing, All-Knowing". Such forbearance is very hard on human nature. On just such occasions Satan is at hand to provoke even a calm person to anger and get him ready to fight. So when anger flares while one's patience is being tested, one should recognise at once that the prompting comes from Satan. Its standard treatment is to seek refuge with Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you are tempted by Satan,"** If Satan stirs you with a whisper or a doubt, tries to bring you an evil suggestion, or turns you away from what you were commanded to do. In the Arabic, "if" (in) is merged with an added "any" (mā) to form immā. Ibn Jarīr explained: if the devil lures you to anger, turning you away from forgiving the ignorant and toward punishing him.

**"then seek refuge with Allah."** Ask Allah for protection from his whispering and take refuge with Him, and He will ward it off from you. The fuller sense is left implied. Allah commands seeking refuge with Him from the devil's whispers. The hadiths on istiʿādhah, seeking refuge, are not repeated here.

**"Surely He is All-Hearing, All-Knowing"** He hears your seeking refuge with Him and whatever you say, and He knows the devil's whispering and what is done. Ibn Jarīr said Allah hears the ignorance that fools inflict on you, your seeking refuge with Him, and all the speech of His creation, none of which escapes Him. He knows what drives the devil's lures away from you, and everything else His creatures do.

### Words that remove anger

A hadith tells of two men quarrelling in front of the Holy Prophet ﷺ. One of them was losing control in his anger. The Prophet ﷺ looked at him and said he knew words that would take his rage away if he said them: "I seek refuge with Allah from Satan, the accursed." When the man heard the Holy Prophet ﷺ say this, he repeated it at once, and his anger vanished.

### Three verses that end in seeking refuge

There is a remarkable pattern here. Three verses in the whole Qur'an embody high moral teaching, and all three end with the need to seek refuge from Satan. These three passages, in Sūrat al-Aʿrāf, Sūrat al-Muʾminūn and Sūrat as-Sajdah, are unique in the Qur'an. One is this verse. The second is in Sūrat al-Muʾminūn: "Respond to evil with what is best. We know well what they claim. And say, “My Lord! I seek refuge in You from the temptations of the devils. And I seek refuge in You, my Lord, that they ˹even˺ come near me.”" (23:96-98).

The third is in Sūrah Ḥā Mīm as-Sajdah, also called Fuṣṣilat, verses 34 to 36: "Good and evil cannot be equal. Respond ˹to evil˺ with what is best, then the one you are in a feud with will be like a close friend. But this cannot be attained except by those who are patient and who are truly fortunate. And if you are tempted by Satan, then seek refuge with Allah. Indeed, He ˹alone˺ is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing" (41:34-36). Verse 35 adds that only the patient and the truly fortunate reach this. The last of these verses (41:36) is worded almost exactly like the verse of al-Aʿrāf.

### Why is Satan mentioned after forgiveness?

All three verses teach people to overlook and forgive those who provoke their anger, to return good for evil, and at the same time to seek refuge from Satan. Allah encourages gentle treatment of wrongdoers, since it may deter them from persisting in their wrong, Allah willing. But the devil is not deterred by gentleness, because he wants a person's total ruin. He is humankind's open enemy, as he was the enemy of their father Adam before them. So the remedy against him is refuge with Allah.

This shows that Satan takes a special interest in human quarrels. Whenever a quarrel breaks out, the devils converge on it as their favourite hunting ground. However calm and forbearing a person may be, they will still try to inflame him and push him beyond the limits. The treatment is this. Once someone notices his temper slipping beyond control, he should understand that Satan is gaining the upper hand over his better self, turn to Allah and seek refuge with Him. This lets noble traits of character flourish at their best. That is why the next two verses lay further stress on seeking Allah's protection against Satan.

### In short

- Forgiving wrongs is hard, and Satan uses such moments to inflame anger, so the cure is to seek refuge with Allah.
- The Prophet ﷺ taught an angry man to say, "I seek refuge with Allah from Satan, the accursed", and his anger left him.
- Three Qur'anic passages on returning good for evil all end with seeking refuge from Satan, who loves to exploit quarrels.

<<<VERSE 7:201>>>
Those who fear Allah are quickly alerted when Satan approaches: "Indeed, when Satan whispers to those mindful ˹of Allah˺, they remember ˹their Lord˺ then they start to see ˹things˺ clearly". Allah describes His servants who have taqwā, obeying His commands and avoiding what He forbids.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, when Satan whispers to those mindful ˹of Allah˺,"** When an evil thought, anger, or Satan's whispering crosses their minds, or a doubt or insinuation reaches them, or they intend to do wrong or commit an error. The word is ṭayf, "a visitation", with a variant reading ṭāʾif. These are those who guard against the devil's whispers.

**"they remember ˹their Lord˺"** They remember Allah's might, His punishment of wrongdoers and His immense reward for those who do good. They remember His promises and threats, and they come to know Allah's guidance. Then they repent and turn back to Him, seeking refuge with Him and asking His forgiveness before death.

**"then they start to see ˹things˺ clearly"** They become upright and see the error of their ways. They tell truth from falsehood, hold back from transgression and turn their faces back to Allah.

### Sensitivity, the believer's shield

No one in this world is safe from the attacks of the self and of Satan. What saves a person on all such occasions is the fear of Allah. Fear of Allah makes one extremely sensitive, and this sensitivity is the greatest shield in this world of trial. Whenever an evil thought enters his mind, or a negative mood takes hold of him, his sensitivity warns him at once. His eyes open immediately. He asks Allah's forgiveness and resolves never to commit such wrong again.

### In short

- When Satan whispers to those who fear Allah, they remember Allah's punishment and reward, His promises and threats.
- They then see clearly, repent and turn back to Him.
- Fear of Allah makes a person sensitive, and that sensitivity is his shield against the self and Satan.

<<<VERSE 7:202>>>
The opposite is the state of Satan's companions: "But the devils persistently plunge their ˹human˺ associates deeper into wickedness, sparing no effort". Those who have no fear of Allah have no such shield.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But the devils persistently plunge their ˹human˺ associates"** Their brothers are the devils' brothers among humankind, the disbelievers who commit immorality. They follow the devils, listen to them and obey their orders. The verse quoted under 7:31 (17:27) calls the wasteful brothers of the devils. One explanation reverses it: the brothers of the idolaters, meaning the devils, drag the idolaters along with their insinuations.

**"deeper into wickedness,"** The devils help them commit sins, making that path easy and attractive to them. They draw them into ever greater error, into disbelief, error and sin, one sin after another.

**"sparing no effort"** The devils never stop urging people to do wrong. Ibn ʿAbbās, as ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported, said that neither do humans stop short of the evil they are doing, nor do the devils stop luring them. So "sparing no effort" means the devils never tire or cease their whispering. Neither the devils in their misleading nor the immoral who follow them ever stop. Unlike those who fear Allah, these people never come to see clearly. Allah says in another verse: "Do you ˹O Prophet˺ not see that We have sent the devils against the disbelievers, constantly inciting them" (19:83). Ibn ʿAbbās and others explained this as the devils constantly luring the disbelievers into evil.

### Why do some never wake up?

A heart emptied of the fear of Allah keeps no watch against Satan. Step by step he draws such people toward the pit of ruin, and they never notice. Sensitivity is a person's greatest shield, while insensitivity leaves him defenceless against Satan's attacks.

### In short

- The devils keep drawing their human brothers, the immoral and the disbelievers, deeper into error.
- Neither the devils nor their followers ever stop, and those followers never come to see clearly.
- A heart without the fear of Allah is defenceless and is led to destruction unawares.

<<<VERSE 7:203>>>
The idolaters met every sign with a demand for another: "If you ˹O Prophet˺ do not bring them a sign ˹which they demanded˺, they ask, “Why do you not make it yourself?”" The Prophet ﷺ is told how to answer: "Say, “I only follow what is revealed to me from my Lord. This ˹Quran˺ is an insight from your Lord—a guide and a mercy for those who believe.”" The verse offers proof that the Holy Prophet ﷺ is the true Messenger of Allah and answers the doubts of his opponents. When he brought a sign they rejected it, and when he did not, they asked why he had not produced one.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you ˹O Prophet˺ do not bring them a sign ˹which they demanded˺,"** When you do not bring the people of Makkah one of the miracles they ask for. "Sign" here means a miracle. Allah says elsewhere: "If We willed, We could send down upon them a ˹compelling˺ sign from the heavens, leaving their necks bent in ˹utter˺ submission to it" (26:4).

**"they ask, “Why do you not make it yourself?”"** Ibn ʿAbbās, as ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported, explained it as: why have you not been given a miracle? Or: why have you not brought one about or made it yourself? Ibn Jarīr reported Mujāhid's reading, that they were telling him to come up with a miracle himself. Qatādah, as-Suddī, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam and Ibn Jarīr held the same view. One explanation gives: why do you not take it on yourself to bring it from Allah? Another gives: why do you not invent it yourself? The pagans were asking why he did not strive to bring them a miracle from Allah that they could witness and believe in.

**"Say, “I only follow what is revealed to me from my Lord."** I do and say only what my Lord reveals to me, and it is not for me to bring anything of my own accord. The Prophet ﷺ was to say that he does not ask his Lord for such things. He only follows what Allah reveals and commands him. If Allah sends a miracle, he will accept it. Otherwise he will not ask for one unless Allah permits him. Surely Allah is All-Wise, All-Knowing.

**"This ˹Quran˺ is an insight from your Lord"** Allah then points His servants to the Qur'an itself as the mightiest miracle, the plainest evidence, and the truest proof and explanation. It is a collection of proofs and miracles from your Lord, your Creator, who arranges your lives. It sets out commands and prohibitions. Whoever reflects on it, even briefly, cannot leave it without believing that it is the Word of Allah alone, and that nothing created had any part in it.

**"—a guide and a mercy for those who believe.”"** It guides people out of error and is a mercy that shields them from punishment. The Qur'an is a proof of the truth for the whole world. But it leads to the purpose of life, and to Allah's mercy, only for those who believe in it. Those who do not believe are lost and wretched.

### Why were their demands not met?

To prove that they are messengers of Allah, all the prophets were given miracles. In keeping with this, the Holy Prophet ﷺ, the foremost of the prophets, was blessed with miracles that outnumber those of earlier prophets and are just as clear. His miracles, established by the Qur'an and authentic hadith reports, are many. Muslim scholars have written whole books on them. One well-known work on the subject is as-Suyūṭī's two-volume al-Khaṣāʾiṣ al-Kubrā. Yet despite so many miracles, his opponents kept making unreasonable demands for new miracles of their own choosing. Such demands appear earlier in this surah too.

The verse answers their demand on principle. A prophet's miracle is testimony and proof of his mission. Suppose a plaintiff's claim is proved by trustworthy evidence, and the other side has not challenged it. No court in the world would let that side insist that it will accept the claim only if evidence comes from certain people it names, while leaving the existing evidence unchallenged. In the same way, after seeing so many clear miracles, his opponents had no right to say they would accept him as Allah's Messenger only if he produced miracles made to their order. That was simply a hostile demand that no court of justice would accept.

So when he did not show them the miracle they specified, they used it as an excuse to deny that he was Allah's Messenger. Had he been one, they said, he would have shown them the miracle they chose. The Holy Prophet ﷺ was told to answer that his mission was not to produce miracles on his own. His basic task was to follow the commands his Lord sent him through revelation, waḥy, and to preach them, and he was busy doing what he had been assigned. The miracles they had already seen were more than enough to confirm his status. Having seen them, their demand for a particular miracle was mere hostility, not worth attention.

Among all his miracles, the Qur'an is itself a great miracle. It challenged the whole world to produce even a short surah like it, and the world failed. That is open proof that the Qur'an is not human speech but the inimitable Word of Allah, the Most Exalted.

### In short

- When the Prophet ﷺ did not produce the miracles they demanded, the idolaters asked why he did not make one himself.
- He was told to say that he follows only what is revealed, and asks for no sign without Allah's permission.
- Having seen many miracles, they had no right to demand others to order, just as no court would allow such a demand.
- The Qur'an itself is the greatest miracle, insight from Allah and a guide and mercy for believers.
