<<<VERSE 7:199>>>
Verse 199 gives the Messenger ﷺ a code of conduct for dealing with the people he calls. "Be gracious, enjoin what is right, and turn away from those who act ignorantly"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Be gracious"** has several shades of meaning in the reports, and each of them fits the place. The reading that most of the teachers settle on is that the word asks for what people can do with ease. On this reading the Messenger ﷺ is told to accept from people the obedience they are able to give, and not to demand the highest standard of worship and conduct from them.

One of the spiritual reports pictures the command as coming to Muhammad, the seal of the prophets. It asks him to pass over the sins of the sinners, to cover their defects, and to strike a line of pardon through the record of the wrongdoers. The report of Ibn Jarir al-Tabari follows the same line. He relates that when this verse came down, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked Jibril about its meaning. Jibril confirmed the sense from Allah. He answered that the Messenger ﷺ was commanded to pardon the one who does him wrong, to give to the one who gives him nothing, and to keep his ties with the one who cuts himself off from him.

The word is also read as an instruction to forgive. Those who take it so say that the verse asks the Messenger ﷺ to overlook the errors and the shortcomings of the people. He was to pardon what they bring of rough behaviour and to wipe the register of their faults. The reports note that the Messenger ﷺ was commanded to bear with the idolaters in this manner for ten years, and that he was ordered afterwards to deal firmly with them.

The report of Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam states this plainly. He said that Allah commanded the Messenger ﷺ to show forgiveness and to turn away from the idolaters for ten years, and that afterwards Allah ordered him to be harsh with them. Reports from Mujahid say much the same, that the words concern the conduct and the manners of the people, and that the Messenger ﷺ was told to pardon them.

Hashim bin 'Urwah reported from his father that Allah ordered His Messenger ﷺ to pardon the people for their behaviour. Another report from the same family of narrators passes the same meaning down. Sahih Al-Bukhari carries the report of Hisham, from his father 'Urwah, from his brother 'Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr, and he said that the verse was sent down about the character of the people. One chain reaches Ibn 'Umar by way of Mughirah and Hisham and his father, and another chain reaches 'A'ishah from the same Hisham from his father. Both of them said the same as the others.

### The example of the prayer

The sense of accepting what people can easily do is shown in the report about the prayer. The prayer in its true sense means that a person cuts himself off from the concerns of the world and stands before his Lord in full veneration. He speaks his praise and his petitions to his Lord face to face, with nothing standing between the servant and his Lord. That state of the heart shows itself in the worshipper through humility and submission, and it is reached only by a few worshippers. Such a degree of veneration and love cannot be expected from every worshipper. The verse therefore tells the Messenger ﷺ not to demand the highest standard of performance from the people, and to accept the degree of perfection that they reach with ease.

This reading is reported in Sahih Al-Bukhari on the authority of 'Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr. Another report has the Messenger ﷺ say at the time this verse came down that he had been commanded to accept the ordinary obedience of the people in their worship and in their conduct, and that he had decided to do so as long as he was in their company. A large number of the teachers of the Book, among them 'Abdullah Ibn 'Umar, 'Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr, 'A'ishah and Mujahid, confirmed this meaning for the phrase.

### The words of the Prophet ﷺ on the three duties

The reports gather a set of sayings that carry the same three duties. Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim recorded it through Yunus, who had it from Sufyan bin 'Uyaynah, who had it from Umay. He said that when this verse was revealed to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, the Messenger ﷺ asked what it meant, and Jibril answered that Allah commands him to forgive those who wrong him, to give to those who deprive him, and to keep relations with those who cut their ties with him.

Another report carries the same instruction from the Messenger ﷺ to 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir, and it names the same three acts. A report of the same kind comes from 'Ali. In it the Messenger ﷺ told him that he was teaching him a code of conduct finer than that of the first and the last of the people. The three duties are the same, to give to the one who deprives him, to pardon the one who does him wrong, and to keep up his relation with the one who breaks off with him.

The teachers drew the essence of all of this together. They said that the Messenger ﷺ was not to demand the highest standard of worship and deeds from the people, was to pardon them for their errors, was not to take revenge for the wrongs done to him, and was to be generous to them without looking for anything in return.

### The manners of the Messenger ﷺ in practice

The records of the life of the Messenger ﷺ show the model of this verse in full. He forgave at the greatest of the moments of his triumph, when Makkah was taken and the enemies who had fought him for years stood at his mercy. He set them all free. He said that he had no thought of taking revenge for their injustices against him, and that he would not even blame them for what had passed between them.

### The second phrase and the third

**"enjoin what is right"** takes the instruction further. The word used for the right is Al-'Urf, which covers everything that is done in a good and praiseworthy way. The sense is that the Messenger ﷺ was not to seek revenge for the harsh treatment given to him by his enemies. He was to forgive them, and beyond that he was to keep asking them to do what is good and beneficial for them. In this way he would not only return good for evil, but would repay them with a higher degree of favour and grace, which is the level of ihsan.

**"and turn away from those who act ignorantly"** gives the third instruction. One report names the ignorant here as Abu Jahl and the people with him who mocked the Messenger ﷺ. The Messenger ﷺ was to forgo revenge for the injustice, to deal with his enemies with goodwill and concern, and to explain the truth to them gently. Beyond that there would be some diehards who were not touched by this gentle answer and who showed more of their ignorance and harshness. He was told not to be moved by their bitter comments and their rough challenges, not to answer them in their own terms, and to stay away from them instead. The teaching is not that he should stop giving good advice, since that would not suit the mission of a prophet. It means that he was not to repay evil with evil.

The reports of the teachers of the Book add a further word here, and their own account of the divine speech carries the promise that the past will be forgiven to those who stop (8:38). They said that people come in two kinds. The first kind is the good-doer, and such a person is to be accepted as he is. The teachers said that no one should ask more of him than he can bear and no one should put him to hardship. The second kind is the one who falls short, and he is to be told what is right. If he insists on his evil and goes on in his ignorance, the right course is to turn away from him, so that ignoring him may put his evil aside. The same counsel appears in another passage of the Book, that the person should repel evil with what is better, and that Allah knows best what they say (23:96-98). Another passage states that the good deed and the evil deed are not equal, that the evil deed should be answered with what is better, and that a person between whom and another there was enmity will become as a close friend. It then adds that this quality is given only to those who are patient and only to the owner of a great share (41:34-35). The next verse of that passage says that if a whisper from Satan turns a person aside, he should seek refuge with Allah, who hears and knows (41:36). The same instruction appears in a verse of this surah that follows (7:200).

### The report of 'Uyaynah in the presence of 'Umar

The report in Sahih Al-Bukhari from 'Abdullah ibn 'Abbas shows how this verse worked among the Companions. The event belongs to the time of the leadership of 'Umar, who was given the title al-Faruq, and it took place in Madinah.

'Uyaynah bin Hisn bin Hudhayfah came and stayed as the guest of his nephew Al-Hur bin Qays. Al-Hur was one of the people whom 'Umar kept near him. 'Umar used to like to have the reciters of the Qur'an close to him, and he used to listen to their opinion whether they were old or young. 'Uyaynah said to his nephew that he was close to the leader, and he asked him to arrange a meeting. Al-Hur asked the Amir al-Mu'minin for permission, and it was given.

When 'Uyaynah came into the presence of 'Umar, he said to him, "O Ibn Al-Khattab, you neither give us enough nor rule with justice between us." The name of the man is written 'Uyaiynah bin Hisn in some of the reports. 'Umar became so angry that he was close to punishing him. Al-Hur bin Qays said, "O Amir al-Mu'minin, Allah said to His Prophet ﷺ, be gracious, enjoin what is right, and turn away from those who act ignorantly, and this man is one of the fools." By Allah, 'Umar did nothing after he heard that verse recited to him. He was a man who stood by the Book of Allah. It was said of him that he readily softened before the commandments of the Book of the Mighty.

### In short

- Verse 199 gives the Messenger ﷺ three duties, to be gracious in taking what people can do, to enjoin the good that reason itself approves, and to turn away from those who act ignorantly.
- The word behind "be gracious" is read in more than one way. The most accepted sense is that he should accept from people the obedience they manage with ease and should not demand the highest standard of worship from them, and the reports support this reading with Sahih Al-Bukhari on the authority of 'Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr and with the testimony of Ibn 'Umar, 'A'ishah and Mujahid.
- The other reading is forgiveness. Ibn Jarir al-Tabari relates that Jibril answered the Messenger's question about the verse with three duties, to pardon the one who does him wrong, to give to the one who deprives him, and to keep ties with the one who cuts him off, and reports from 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir and from 'Ali carry the same instruction.
- The report of Yunus from Sufyan bin 'Uyaynah from Umay, and the reports of Hashim bin 'Urwah and Hisham from his father 'Urwah from 'Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr, all agree that the verse was revealed about the character of the people. The Messenger ﷺ was told to pardon them.
- The report of Ibn Marduwaih from Sa'd ibn 'Ubadah ties the verse to the battle of Uhud, when Hamzah was killed and his body was cut about. The Messenger ﷺ said that he would treat seventy of their people as they had treated Hamzah, and the verse came down to tell him that such a course did not suit his rank.
- The report of 'Uyaynah bin Hisn with his nephew Al-Hur bin Qays and with 'Umar al-Faruq in Madinah shows the verse at work, since the anger of 'Umar cooled when the verse was recited and he did nothing to the man who had insulted him.

<<<VERSE 7:200>>>
Verse 200 gives the remedy for the moment when patience fails. "If you are tempted by Satan, then seek refuge with Allah. Surely He is All-Hearing, All-Knowing"

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you are tempted by Satan"** The phrase covers a strike or a touch of any kind from the devil. One account explains it with the setting in which it was revealed. The order to pardon the ignorant and not to answer their evil with evil is a heavy duty, and it is hard on human temper. In such a moment Satan is present to coax an ordinary man into anger, and he sets him up for a fight. The verse therefore tells the person who feels his anger rising to recognise that the push is coming from Satan and to seek refuge with Allah at once.

**"then seek refuge with Allah"** names the remedy. The step is known among the teachers as the istiadhah, the seeking of refuge with Allah against the devil. One account of the verse reads the words as asking for protection from the whisper of the devil. The same account confirms that the remedy is answered, since Allah hears the call of the one who turns to Him, and He knows the whisper of the one who drove him to it.

**"Surely He is All-Hearing, All-Knowing"** gives the ground of the remedy. Ibn Jarir said on this phrase that Allah hears the ignorance that the foolish bring to a person. He hears the person's seeking refuge with Him from the whisperings of the devil, and He hears the rest of the speech of His creation, and none of it escapes His knowledge. He also said that Allah knows the thing that drives the lures of the devil away from the person, and knows the rest of what His creatures do.

### The method that works

The reports show the remedy working. Two men were quarrelling in the presence of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and one of them lost control of himself in his rage. The Messenger ﷺ looked at him and said that he knew some words which, if the man were to say them, his anger would go away. Then he said the words, that one should seek refuge with Allah from Satan, the accursed. When the man heard the Messenger ﷺ reciting it, he said it after him at once. His anger was gone.

The same treatment is described in another report. The teachers say that the person should say the words with his heart and his tongue. The anger that had lifted him out of his temper then settles, and the restraint that the preceding verse asks for becomes possible.

### The three passages that carry this instruction

The reports point out a coincidence in the Book. There are three passages that gather a high teaching of morals, and all three of them close with the command to seek refuge from Satan. One of them is this verse of the surah. The second is in the surah called Al-Mu'minun. There the words ask the person to repel evil with what is good. They then put the prayer in the mouth of the servant, that he seeks refuge with his Lord from the urgings of the devils, and that he asks that they not come near him (23:96-98). The third stands in the surah called As-Sajdah, which is also called Ha Mim. It states that the good deed and the evil deed are not equal, and that a person should repel the evil deed with what is better. It then adds that the one between whom and another there was enmity will be as a fast friend (41:34). The same passage states that this quality is granted only to those who observe patience, and only to the one endowed with a great fortune (41:35). It closes with the command that if a person is struck by a strike from Satan, he should seek refuge with Allah, who hears and knows (41:36).

The three passages together teach that a person is to forgive those who stir him to anger, to answer evil with good, and to seek refuge from Satan while he does it. They also show how closely Satan watches human quarrels. Wherever a quarrel is under way, the devils gather there as on their favourite ground. No man is so steady that they will not try to stir him to anger and push him past the limit.

### In short

- Verse 200 tells the person who is struck by a temptation or a push from Satan to seek refuge with Allah, and it names Allah as the One who hears the call and knows the whisper.
- The step is called the istiadhah, and the reports describe it as the remedy that works, since two men were quarrelling before the Messenger ﷺ and one of them repeated the words after hearing them and his anger left him.
- Ibn Jarir explained the verse as the answer to a moment when the devil drives a man to anger, so that he leaves the pardon of the ignorant and turns to punishing them.
- The same command closes three passages of the Book that gather the highest teaching of morals, the verse here, the words of Al-Mu'minun (23:96-98) and the words of As-Sajdah (41:34, 41:35, 41:36).
- The teaching of those passages is that a person forgives the one who stirs him, answers evil with good, and seeks refuge from Satan while he does it, since Satan takes a special interest in the quarrels of men.

<<<VERSE 7:201>>>
Verse 201 describes the people who guard themselves and what happens when the whisper reaches them. "Indeed, when Satan whispers to those mindful ˹of Allah˺, they remember ˹their Lord˺ then they start to see ˹things˺ clearly"

### Phrase by phrase

**"when Satan whispers to those mindful of Allah"** names the people who have taqwa. They are the servants who follow the commands of Allah and keep away from what He has forbidden. The whisper that reaches them may be an evil thought, or a surge of anger, or a doubt that crosses the mind, or an intention to do wrong, or a slip already made.

**"they remember their Lord"** gives their answer. They recall the punishment that Allah has prepared for the wrongdoer and the great reward He has kept for those who do right. They remember His promises and His threats. They turn back to Him in repentance, they seek refuge with Him, and they ask for forgiveness before death comes.

**"then they start to see things clearly"** names the result. They see through the error of the way they had turned toward, and they return to Allah. One reading describes the same step, that the person wakes to the guidance of Allah and becomes one who sees, and holds himself back from the wrong.

### What the whisper and the guard look like

One of the reflections on the passage sets out the working of this guard. Fear of God makes a person extremely sensitive, and that sensitivity is his greatest shield in a world of trial. When an evil thought comes to his mind, or when a bad temper settles on him, the sensitivity wakes at once and puts him on his guard. His eyes are opened in that moment, he seeks the forgiveness of God, and he resolves not to commit such an act again.

The opposite case is described in the same reflection. A person whose heart is free of the fear of God is never on his guard. Satan leads him on from one step to the next, down to the pit of destruction, and he never becomes conscious of what is happening. One of the teachers adds that the person who turns aside in this way may then start to see things clearly, and the man in the other case never does.

### In short

- Verse 201 describes those who guard themselves with taqwa, and it states that when a whisper from Satan reaches them they remember their Lord and then see things clearly.
- The whisper may come as an evil thought, a blow of anger, a doubt, an intention to do wrong, or a slip already made.
- Their answer is to remember the punishment of Allah and His reward, to repent, to turn back to Him, to seek refuge with Him and to ask forgiveness before death.
- The fear of God gives a person a sensitivity that works as his greatest shield, since it alerts him the moment an evil thought arrives and opens his eyes to it.
- The one whose heart is empty of that fear keeps no guard, and Satan leads him on to the pit of destruction while he remains unaware that he is being led.

<<<VERSE 7:202>>>
Verse 202 draws the opposite case in a sentence. "But the devils persistently plunge their ˹human˺ associates deeper into wickedness, sparing no effort"

### Phrase by phrase

**"their human associates"** means the men who take the devils as their brothers and follow them. The word is used in the Book of those who are brothers of the devils because they listen to them and carry out their orders, and the passage about the wasteful is one of the places where that word is used (17:27).

**"plunge them deeper into wickedness"** describes the work of the devils on their brothers among mankind. They help them to commit sins. They make the road of sin easy and attractive for them, and they add one sin to the next, and they never turn aside from the task.

**"sparing no effort"** states that the devils never stop. They carry on inciting mankind to error without a pause. One of the teachers reports from Ibn 'Abbas, through 'Ali bin Abi Talhah, that neither mankind stop short of the evil they do nor the devils stop short of luring them. The Book elsewhere states that devils are sent against the disbelievers to push them on to evil (19:83), and the pushing goes on without end.

### What the wording carries

The same picture is drawn in the short reports of the passage. The devils drag their brothers further into disbelief, error and sin by means of the suggestions they put into them, and they do not hold back from the work. The people who are drawn along do not stop either, and so the two parties are joined in the same motion, the ones who whisper and the ones who follow. It is against that background that the verse before this one shows the other kind of person, the one who remembers his Lord and sees clearly, so that the two ways stand side by side.

### In short

- Verse 202 states that the devils plunge their human associates deeper into error and never cease from the work.
- The human associates of the devils are the men who follow them, listen to them and obey them, and the Book calls the wasteful brothers of the devils in the same sense (17:27).
- The devils make sin easy and attractive, add sin to sin, and never tire of inciting those who follow them.
- Ibn 'Abbas reported, through 'Ali bin Abi Talhah, that neither the people stop short of the evil they do nor the devils stop short of drawing them to it.
- The Book states in another passage that devils are sent against the disbelievers to push them to evil (19:83), and the pushing continues without end.

<<<VERSE 7:203>>>
Verse 203 answers the demand of the idolaters for a sign of their own choosing. "If you ˹O Prophet˺ do not bring them a sign ˹which they demanded˺, they ask, “Why do you not make it yourself?” Say, “I only follow what is revealed to me from my Lord. This ˹Quran˺ is an insight from your Lord—a guide and a mercy for those who believe.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you do not bring them a sign, they ask, why do you not make it yourself?"** describes the manner of the demand. The people of Makkah asked the Messenger ﷺ to produce a sign from among those they named. When he did not bring the one they wanted, they used its absence as a reason to deny that he was a messenger of Allah. One reading of the phrase reported from Ibn 'Abbas says that they asked why he had not received a miracle, or why he had not made one himself. Another report, carried by Ibn Jarir, gives the words of Mujahid on the same phrase, that they said he should produce a miracle of his own. That reading was taken up by Qatadah and by As-Suddi, and 'Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam agreed with it, as did Ibn Jarir.

**"Say, I only follow what is revealed to me from my Lord"** is the answer given to the Messenger ﷺ. He is to tell them that he does not order such things from his Lord. He follows what is revealed to him and what he is commanded to do. If Allah sends a sign, he accepts it. If He does not, he does not ask for one of his own choice. The matters of Allah are ordered by His wisdom, and He knows what is best.

**"This Quran is an insight from your Lord, a guide and a mercy for those who believe"** turns the attention of the people to the greatest of the signs already standing before them. The Qur'an is a collection of proofs, and it is the clearest evidence and the truest proof of the message. Whoever looks into it, even briefly, cannot leave it without holding that it is the Word of Allah and that no created thing has any part in it. The Book itself has challenged the whole world to produce a single surah like it, and the world has not met the challenge. The verse then names two more qualities of the Book, that it is a guidance and a mercy. It comes as a proof of the truth for the whole world, while the guidance and the mercy reach those who believe in it and follow it. As for those who refuse faith, they are lost and wretched.

### The principle behind the answer

The teachers set out the principle that stands behind this answer. The miracles given to a prophet are the testimony and the proof of his mission, and every prophet was given them. The Messenger ﷺ was given more of them than the prophets before him, since his miracles are many and the reports of them are clear, and the scholars wrote whole books to gather them. One well-known work on the subject is the two-volume al-Khasais al-Kubra of as-Suyuti.

Even so, his opponents went on demanding fresh miracles of their own choosing. The point of the answer is the same as a matter in a court of law. A claimant whose case is proved by trustworthy evidence does not have to produce the evidence from the particular people his opponent names. Once the signs had been shown, the opponents had no right to say that they would accept the Messenger ﷺ only if he showed the sign they had ordered for themselves. That is a demand made out of hostility, and no court of justice accepts such a demand as valid.

So the Messenger ﷺ was told that his mission did not consist of producing signs at the bidding of his opponents. His mission was to follow the revelation sent to him by his Lord, and that mission included the duty of conveying the message to them. He was busy with what he had been assigned to do. As for the proof of his status, the signs already shown among them were more than enough. The demand for one particular sign, after all of that, was an exercise in hostility that was not worth noticing.

Another passage of the Book speaks along the same line. It states that if Allah had willed, He could have sent down from the heaven a sign to which the people would have bowed their heads in humility (26:4). The signs are in the hand of Allah, and they come when He wills and in the form He chooses, and the mercy of the Book stands open to those who believe.

### In short

- Verse 203 states that when the Messenger ﷺ did not bring the sign the idolaters demanded, they asked why he had not produced one himself, and the answer given to him is that he only follows what is revealed to him from his Lord.
- The reports read the demand in several ways. Ibn 'Abbas said that they asked why he had not received a miracle or made one himself, and Mujahid said that they asked him to produce a miracle of his own, a reading that Qatadah, As-Suddi, 'Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam and Ibn Jarir all took.
- The Qur'an is named in the verse as the insight from the Lord, and as a guidance and a mercy for those who believe, which makes it the greatest of the signs already before them.
- The Book has challenged the world to produce a single surah like it, and no one has met the challenge, so the demand for a chosen sign after the signs already shown is a demand made out of hostility.
- The teachers compare the demand to a case in court, where a claim proved by trustworthy evidence does not have to be proved again in the way the opposing party names, and another passage states that if Allah had willed He could have sent down from the heaven a sign before which the people would bow (26:4).
- The miracles of the Messenger ﷺ are many and the scholars wrote books to gather them, among them the two-volume al-Khasais al-Kubra of as-Suyuti, and the reports of them stand as the testimony of his mission while the Qur'an remains the clearest of all.
