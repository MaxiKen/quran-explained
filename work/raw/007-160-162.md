The verse turns back to the Israelites in the wilderness and names the provisions given to them there. "We divided them into twelve tribes—each as a community." The first provision was water. "And We revealed to Moses, when his people asked for water, “Strike the rock with your staff.” Then twelve springs gushed out. Each tribe knew its drinking place." The second was shade and food. "We shaded them with clouds and sent down to them manna and quails, ˹saying˺, “Eat from the good things We have provided for you.”" The verse closes with a judgement on their conduct. "They ˹certainly˺ did not wrong Us, but wronged themselves"

### Phrase by phrase

**"We divided them into twelve tribes—each as a community."** describes the order given to the Children of Israel after they came out of Egypt. They were separated into twelve groups. The word used for the tribes in the verse is asbatan, and it stands in the place of the word for the groups themselves. Each of the twelve had its own leader, and all of them were under the charge of Moses. They were settled in the desert of Sinai, away from the polytheistic air of Egypt, and there they were organised into those groups.

The verse answers a question that follows from the division. **"And We revealed to Moses, when his people asked for water, “Strike the rock with your staff.” Then twelve springs gushed out. Each tribe knew its drinking place."** The people asked Moses to call on Allah for water, and he was commanded to strike the rock with the staff that was in his hand. He struck it, and twelve springs broke out of it, one for each tribe, and no tribe shared another's spring. Each group came to know the place that belonged to it.

**"We shaded them with clouds and sent down to them manna and quails, ˹saying˺, “Eat from the good things We have provided for you.”"** names the second provision. The white cloud overshadowed them in the wilderness, and it served them in two ways. In the day it kept the sun off them, and in the night it gave them light like a lamp. The manna was a substance like gum with a sweet taste, and the quails were the small birds sent down with it. Both were ready for them in front of their tents, and the command that came with them was to eat of what had been provided.

**"They ˹certainly˺ did not wrong Us, but wronged themselves"** states the result. The wrongdoing of the people did not harm Allah in anything. What their disobedience did was to bring ruin on their own souls, since they did not value the blessings given to them as they should have, and they went against the commands of their Lord.

### The differences between the two accounts

This passage has its counterpart in Al-Baqarah, where the same favours are named with more detail. The two passages do not sit in the same setting. The passage of Al-Baqarah belongs to what was revealed in Al-Madinah, while these verses belong to what was revealed in Makkah, and the two narrations differ in the way they are told. That difference is why the two are not simply repeats of one another. Each brings out the lesson that suits the situation it was revealed in.

The favours listed here are favours of need, given to a people who had left a settled land for a desert. The tribes were given their order, then their water, their shade and their food, and with the food came the command to be content with what had been handed to them. The verse then records that, with all of that in front of them, they turned to what had been forbidden to them.

### The springs and the inward reading of them

One of the reports carries a reading of this verse that turns on the meaning of the water. It was said by Ja'far ibn Muhammad that Allah made twelve streams to flow from the wellspring of recognition, and He gave each of them to be the drink of one group, so that the water of the good fortune of the religion was drawn for each group at its own watering place.

The same idea is put briefly in another place in the Book, in the words that if they go straight on the Tariqah, that is the path, then the watering promised for them will be appointed (72:16). The twelve rivers are named in that reading, and the first of them is familiarity and the last is friendship. Between the two are ten others, and they are named as the truthfulness of belief, self purification in deeds, approval of the decree, the eye of certainty, the joy of ecstasy, the lightning of unveiling, the bewilderment of witnessing, the dissolving of the marks that give witness, the observation of togetherness and the reality of solitariness.

The point of the list is what happens to the servant who tastes them. When his spirit tastes these drinks and finds how sweet they are, and when the divine attraction joins with that, he becomes a wellspring of life himself. Whoever takes a drink from his hand will be prosperous for ever. The reading is that of inward states and it stands beside the plain meaning of the verse, which is that water was brought out of a rock for a people in a desert.



One account of the division has it that nine of the tribes and a half settled towards the east, beyond China, on the river al-Raml, which is also called the Jordan, while two tribes and a half were spread over the world. That reading comes from the men of the earlier books, and it does not change what the verse says, that every one of the twelve found its own drinking place when the rock was struck.

### In short

- The Children of Israel were brought out of Egypt and divided into twelve tribes, each with its own leader, and all of them under the charge of Moses.
- The water came when the people asked for it, and Moses was told to strike the rock with his staff, and twelve springs broke out of it, one for each tribe.
- The cloud shaded them by day and gave them light by night, and the manna and the quails came down for them in the wilderness, with the command to eat of the good things provided.
- The inward reading takes the twelve springs as twelve streams from the wellspring of recognition, of which the first is familiarity and the last friendship, and the promise attached to them is the watering given to those who stay on the path.
- The wrongdoing of the people did not harm Allah in anything, and what it did was to bring ruin on their own souls.

The next verse carries the command given to the same people when they came to the town. "And ˹remember˺ when it was said to them, “Enter this city ˹of Jerusalem˺ and eat from wherever you please. Say, ‘Absolve us,’ and enter the gate with humility. We will forgive your sins, ˹and˺ We will multiply the reward for the good-doers.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Enter this city ˹of Jerusalem˺ and eat from wherever you please."** is the first part of the command given to the Israelites. The town named here is Jerusalem, and one of the accounts names it as the town of Jericho. The people were told to enter it and to eat from whatever they wished, and one reading adds that the permission covered whatever they wished and whenever they wished.

**"Say, ‘Absolve us,’"** is the second part. The word they were told to say carries the sense of a request that the wrong be taken away from them. It is understood as the declaration that there is no deity except Allah, and as the asking of forgiveness for their sins.

**"and enter the gate with humility."** is the third part. The word used for the entering is that of bowing down. The meaning given to it is that they were to enter their place of worship with humility and modesty. They were to bow their heads, remember the majesty and supremacy of their Lord, and admit their shortcomings before Him. That was the path asked of them at the gate.

**"We will forgive your sins, ˹and˺ We will multiply the reward for the good-doers."** is the promise that came with the command. Their transgression would be wiped out. Those among them who were virtuous in their obedience would be given more than the ordinary measure in reward, in this world and in the world to come.

The command given at the gate carries the whole of the religion in three words. The town is the place of settlement, and the food is the provision. The word to be said is the admission of the wrong, and the way of entering is the manner of the heart when it comes before its Lord. The people were asked to hold all four together, and the promise was attached to the doing of them.

### What the command was meant to teach

The command was meant to bring the people to contentment and gratitude. They had been given a settled town after the wilderness, and the instruction with it was that they should leave aside greed for the pleasures of the world. The manner of the entry speaks to the same thing. Coming into the place of worship with a bowed head is the outward form of a heart that has stopped claiming anything for itself.

### In short

- The people were told to enter the city of Jerusalem, named in one account as the town of Jericho, and to eat from wherever they wished.
- They were told to ask that their wrongs be taken away from them. The word is read both as the declaration that there is no deity except Allah and as the asking of forgiveness.
- They were told to enter the gate with humility and with the head bowed. The meaning is that they were to come into the place of worship with modesty and remember the majesty of their Lord.
- The promise attached to it was that their sins would be forgiven and the reward of the good among them increased.
- The instruction as a whole was meant to bring the people to contentment and gratitude, away from greed for the pleasures of the world. The passage stands with the verses of Al-Baqarah that tell the same story in the setting of Al-Madinah.

<<<VERSE 7:162>>>
The third verse tells what the people did with the command. "But the wrongdoers among them changed the words they were commanded to say." The result follows in the same verse. "So We sent down a punishment from the heavens upon them for their wrongdoing"

### Phrase by phrase

**"But the wrongdoers among them changed the words they were commanded to say."** states the offence. The word they had been given to say was the word of the seeking of forgiveness, and the command had been given to them by their Lord. The evildoers among them replaced it with another saying. The word they put in its place was the word for a grain, or a grain inside a hair, or a grain in a rite. Those are the forms in which the substitution is reported.

The same people changed the manner of their entering as well. They had been told to enter the gate bowing down. One account says that they went in dragging themselves on their rears, so that the humility asked of them was turned into mockery.

**"So We sent down a punishment from the heavens upon them for their wrongdoing."** names the answer to the offence. What came down on them was a chastisement from the heaven, and one account names it as the plague. The punishment was sent for the exchange they had made. They had taken a word of their own devising in place of the word that had been told to them.

### What the change shows

The change touched the heart and not only the tongue. The word set aside carried the admission of the wrong, and once it was dropped nothing was left of the state the command had asked for. Speech of insolence took the place of speech of gratitude, and the path of the people's own devising was given the name of the religion. When they reached that point the grace that had been with them was withdrawn, and blessings were replaced by punishments until they were surrounded on every side. The Book tells the story of the same people in Al-Baqarah as well. The passage here was revealed in Makkah, while the passage there belongs to Al-Madinah, and the two tellings serve the same lesson for the people who hear them.

### In short

- The wrongdoers among the Israelites replaced the word of the asking of forgiveness with a saying of their own. They also changed the way of entering the gate from humility to mockery.
- One account names the substituted words as a grain, a grain inside a hair or a grain in a rite. Several readings add the way they dragged themselves in at the gate.
- A punishment was sent down from the heaven on them for the exchange they had made, and one account names it as the plague.
- The offence touched the heart and not only the tongue, since the word set aside carried the admission of the wrong. The people were left with their own way dressed in the name of the religion.
- The verses were revealed in Makkah while the telling of the same story in Al-Baqarah belongs to Al-Madinah. The two match each other in the order of the favour, the command, the refusal and the punishment.
