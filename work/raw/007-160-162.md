<<<VERSE 7:160>>>
Allah lists the favours He showed the Israelites in the wilderness: "We divided them into twelve tribes—each as a community. And We revealed to Moses, when his people asked for water, “Strike the rock with your staff.”" The rock answered at once: "Then twelve springs gushed out. Each tribe knew its drinking place." He protected and fed them: "We shaded them with clouds and sent down to them manna and quails, ˹saying˺, “Eat from the good things We have provided for you.” They ˹certainly˺ did not wrong Us, but wronged themselves". This verse and the two after it recount Allah's favours to the Israelites, then their turning from His commands, which brought punishment from heaven.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We divided them into twelve tribes"**: Allah separated the children of Israel into twelve tribes, tribe by tribe. In the Arabic, "twelve" describes their state, asbāṭan, "tribes", stands in for it, and umaman, "communities", stands in for "tribes".

**"each as a community."** Each tribe formed its own distinct nation. One explanation says nine and a half of the tribes live toward the east, beyond China, on the river al-Raml, while two and a half are spread across the world.

**"And We revealed to Moses, when his people asked for water,"** Allah commanded Mūsā (Moses), when his people in the wilderness asked him to pray to Allah for water.

**"“Strike the rock with your staff.”"** Strike it with the staff you carry.

**"Then twelve springs gushed out."** He struck it, and twelve springs, or streams, burst out of the rock, one for each tribe.

**"Each tribe knew its drinking place."** Every tribe knew its own spring, which no other tribe shared.

**"We shaded them with clouds"**: a white cloud that went with them wherever they moved and stopped when they stopped. By day it shielded them from the heat of the sun, and by night it gave them light like a lamp.

**"and sent down to them manna and quails,"** The manna was something like gum with a sweet taste, said to be a kind of turunjabīn. The quails were quails, or a bird like them.

**"˹saying˺, “Eat from the good things We have provided for you.”"** Eat the manna and quails We have given you.

**"They ˹certainly˺ did not wrong Us, but wronged themselves"**: their disobedience and their ingratitude for these blessings did Allah no harm. They harmed only themselves, bringing ruin on themselves by going against Allah's commands and failing to value His gifts as they should have.

### How were the Israelites provided for?

Allah took the Israelites out of the idol-worshipping atmosphere of Egypt and settled them in the desert of Sinai. There they were organised into twelve groups, each with its own leader, all under the charge of Mūsā. The children of Israel were given everything they needed to live. Water came from mountain springs. For food, manna and quails were sent down and lay ready for them right in front of their tents.

Allah then reminded them that He had arranged for all their needs. So they should stop being greedy for worldly and bodily pleasures and not rush toward unholy things. Instead they should take the way of contentment and gratitude to Allah.

### Where else is this story told?

These verses were discussed in Sūrat al-Baqarah, which was revealed in Madinah, while these verses were revealed in Makkah. The details, and the differences between the two tellings, were set out there and need not be repeated here.

### The inner reading: twelve rivers from the wellspring of recognition

On the realities behind this verse, Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad said that Allah made twelve streams flow from the wellspring of recognition and made each the drink of one group. At that watering place He let each group draw the water of the religion's good fortune. Allah said the same briefly elsewhere, by way of allusion: "Had the deniers followed the Right Way, We would have certainly granted them abundant rain to drink—" (72:16). The Right Way there is the Ṭarīqah, and the meaning is: We would grant them an unfailing watering place.

There are twelve rivers. The first is familiarity and the last is friendship. The ten between them are:

1. sincere belief
2. purity in deeds
3. contentment with the decree
4. the eye of certainty
5. the joy of ecstasy
6. the lightning of unveiling
7. bewilderment in witnessing
8. the dissolving of the signs that bear witness
9. the vision of togetherness
10. the reality of being alone with Him

Once the servant's spirit has drunk from these rivers and known their sweetness, and the divine pull is joined to that, he himself becomes the Wellspring of Life. Whoever sips from his hand will prosper forever.

The Pir of the Ṭarīqah said: "O Allah, I know the drinking place, but I cannot drink. Thirsty at heart, I weep in hope of a drop." No fountain could quench him, he said, for he sought the ocean, and he had passed a thousand springs and streams in search of it. He likened himself to one drowning in the fire of passion, to one dying of thirst in the middle of a lake, and to one lost in the desert. "Help me! I am at my wits' end. I have lost my heart."

### In short

- Allah organised the Israelites into twelve tribes and, when they asked for water, made twelve springs gush from a rock, one for each tribe.
- He shaded them with a cloud, fed them with manna and quails, and told them to eat of His good provision.
- Their ingratitude and disobedience harmed only themselves, never Allah.
- In the inner reading, Allah lets each group drink from its own river of recognition, from familiarity to friendship.

<<<VERSE 7:161>>>
Allah reminds the Israelites of the city He opened to them and the simple conditions He set: "And ˹remember˺ when it was said to them, “Enter this city ˹of Jerusalem˺ and eat from wherever you please." They were to enter humbly, asking forgiveness: "Say, ‘Absolve us,’ and enter the gate with humility. We will forgive your sins, ˹and˺ We will multiply the reward for the good-doers.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember˺ when it was said to them,"** Remember, O Messenger, when Allah told the Israelites.

**"“Enter this city ˹of Jerusalem˺"**: dwell in the Holy House of Jerusalem. Another explanation names the town of Jericho. For their settlement, a whole city, Jericho in the Jordan valley, was given to them.

**"and eat from wherever you please."** Eat of the town's fruits wherever and whenever you wish.

**"Say, ‘Absolve us,’"** The word is ḥiṭṭah. It means: our plea is for release from our sins. In other words, ask Allah to lift your disobedience from you, saying, "Forgive our sins." One explanation takes it as the declaration that there is no deity except Allah.

**"and enter the gate with humility."** Literally, "enter the gate prostrating", meaning bowing or kneeling. This was the gate of the city. The prostration here is really a bow. They were to enter their place of worship with humility and modesty and worship their Lord. They were to remember Allah's majesty and supremacy, and confess their shortcomings before Him.

**"We will forgive your sins,"** We will forgive you your transgressions. The verb can be read "We will forgive" or "your sins will be forgiven".

**"˹and˺ We will multiply the reward for the good-doers.”"** Those who do good through obedience will be given more reward for their good works, in the good things of this world and of the Hereafter.

### What was asked of them?

Very little was asked of them in return for a city and its produce. They were to walk in humbly, with their heads bowed, and ask forgiveness. Entering the gate this way was meant to show their humility before Allah, and the plea "Absolve us" to admit their sins. In return Allah promised to forgive them and to add to the reward of those who did good. Their test lay in whether they would keep to this path of humility.

### In short

- The Israelites were told to enter the city, Jerusalem or by another view Jericho, and eat freely of it.
- They were to enter its gate bowing in humility and say, "Absolve us", asking Allah to forgive their sins.
- Allah promised forgiveness, and more reward in this world and the next for those who did good.

<<<VERSE 7:162>>>
The Israelites answered even this easy command with insolence: "But the wrongdoers among them changed the words they were commanded to say. So We sent down a punishment from the heavens upon them for their wrongdoing". They changed both the word and the deed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But the wrongdoers among them"**: those among them who sinned.

**"changed the words they were commanded to say."** They were told to say ḥiṭṭah, "Absolve us", or as another explanation has it, that there is no deity except Allah. Instead they said ḥinṭah, "wheat". Other explanations report their words as "a grain inside a hair". They also changed what they were told to do. Instead of entering humbly with lowered heads, they came in dragging themselves on their backsides.

**"So We sent down a punishment from the heavens upon them"**: a chastisement and terror from the sky, which one explanation identifies as a plague.

**"for their wrongdoing"**: for their disobedience, and for changing what they had been commanded to say.

### Why did they change the words?

The Jews forgot Allah's admonition. Instead of taking the way Allah had shown them, they followed a path they invented themselves in Allah's name. They chose arrogance over the path of humility. Their words were not words of gratitude but of insolence. When the Jews went this far in their wrongdoing, Allah withdrew His grace from them. Instead of His blessings, all kinds of punishment closed in on them.

### In short

- The wrongdoers among the Israelites changed "Absolve us" into an insolent word, and entered the gate dragging themselves instead of bowing.
- They replaced the way Allah showed them with arrogance and insolence.
- So Allah withdrew His grace and sent a punishment from the sky for their wrongdoing.
