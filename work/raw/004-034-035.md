<<<VERSE 4:34>>>
The most closely argued verse of the surah's family law: "Men are the caretakers of women, as men have been provisioned by Allah over women and tasked with supporting them financially. And righteous women are devoutly obedient and, when alone, protective of what Allah has entrusted them with. And if you sense ill-conduct from your women, advise them ˹first˺, ˹if they persist,˺ do not share their beds, ˹but if they still persist,˺ then discipline them ˹gently˺. But if they change their ways, do not be unjust to them. Surely Allah is Most High, All-Great". It opens with a principle, draws a portrait, and then builds a ladder of correction that never leaves the house.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Men are the caretakers of women"** is the working rule. The word qawwām, from qayyām, names the person charged with a duty. It is the one who must manage a job or run a system, taking care of what has to be done about something, with the related factors under control. The man is responsible for the woman, her maintainer, caretaker and leader, who disciplines her if she deviates. Other renderings used are guardians, custodians, overseers and protectors. Read as a functional head, and not a ruler or dictator, the word rests on common experience. Any group-living, big or small, and any organized system rationally and customarily needs some head to arbitrate differences and run affairs. Countries and states accept this, and the older tribal organizations accepted it in their chiefs. A family, a micro-sample of the larger organizations, needs it no less. Allah in His wisdom elected men for the responsibility, because their natural capabilities are more pronounced. This is an open fact of human life. No sane person, man or woman, can deny it, and denial does not change reality.

**"as men have been provisioned by Allah over women"** gives the first reason: a God-given precedence in some qualities, like the sacred environs of Makkah where a particular House was declared Allah's House and Qiblah, and like the special honour given to the Bayt al-Maqdis in Jerusalem. Men did nothing to earn the precedence, and nothing is wrong with women for lacking it. It is a grace of creation, and prophethood and other great positions of leadership were made exclusive to men with it. The Prophet ﷺ said: "People who appoint a woman to be their leader will never achieve success", recorded by al-Bukhārī, and the same holds for appointing women as judges. Yet the verse does not say that Allah made men excel women. It says He made some of them excel the others, and the phrasing is the whole wisdom. Men and women are parts of each other, so the precedence of man over woman is like the precedence of the head over the hand, or the heart over the stomach. The head's precedence does not diminish the hand, and man's precedence lowers nothing in woman. If man is the head, woman is the body. The precedence is in terms of genus, and as for individuals, a woman may excel a man in qualities and accomplishments, even in the qualities of a caretaker.

**"and tasked with supporting them financially"** gives the second reason, and this one is earned: the dower, the expenditures and the various expenses laid on men for women by Allah's Book and by the sunnah His Messenger brought. This is why the degree spoken of elsewhere belongs to him: "But men have a degree of responsibility over them" (2:228). A comment of Ibn Ḥibbān, noted in al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ, is worth attention: these two reasons prove that the right of authority is not established by force but deserved on competence and capability. The second reason also answers the inheritance arithmetic. All financial responsibilities rest with men. Before marriage the woman is her father's charge, after it her husband's. The twofold share is therefore not a gain that stays with men, for it zooms back to women after all. And the distribution is functional, not a value judgment. In her creation and nature, woman is not meant to go out and sweat for a livelihood. She has been made the means of human procreation. Motherhood and the holding of the house and family rest on her shoulders, and these are burdens man cannot carry. Authority here is hemmed with balancing factors: it is bound by the supreme law of the sharīʿah, must act on consultation and good counsel, and follows the command to treat women well. Consultation appears in the verse that bids husbands to act by mutual agreement with their wives in family affairs (2:233). Authority is thus hemmed with balancing factors throughout.

**"And righteous women are devoutly obedient"** describes the first of the two classes the principle produces. Qānitāt, obedient, as Ibn ʿAbbās and others stated: they accept the arrangement, devoted to their Lord and their husbands.

**"and, when alone, protective of what Allah has entrusted them with"** means she protects her honour and her husband's property when he is absent, as as-Suddī and others said. Abū Hurayrah is the narrator, in Ibn Jarīr's record, of the Messenger's ﷺ words: "The best woman is she who, when you look at her, pleases you, when you command her she obeys you, and when you are absent she protects her honour and your property", after which he recited this verse to its end. From ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf, in the Musnad of Aḥmad, comes the word of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ: "If the woman prayed her five daily prayers, fasted her month, protected her chastity and obeyed her husband, she will be told, 'Enter Paradise from any of its doors you wish.'" The unseen is where the guarding happens. The duty is not restricted to his absence, for it applies during his presence too. Absence is named because that is when negligence threatens. What must be avoided is a show of alertness while men are in the house, and neglect once they are out of sight. The closing words credit the guarding to Allah's protection, for the temptations of self and satan surround every man and woman all the time. Women are weaker than men in some capabilities, yet in carrying these duties they tend to be much stronger. That is the taufīq of Allah, His help, and the reason women as a general rule are less involved in sinful immodesties than men.

**"And if you sense ill-conduct from your women, advise them ˹first˺"** turns to the second class. Ill-conduct is what appears from a woman towards her husband: acting as if she is above him, disobeying, ignoring, disliking him. The first step is to talk it over nicely and softly, reminding her of Allah's torment if she disobeys. Allah ordered the wife to obey her husband because of the enormity of his rights and all he does for her. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "If I were to command anyone to prostrate before anyone, I would have commanded the wife to prostrate before her husband." And in al-Bukhārī from Abū Hurayrah: "If the man asks his wife to come to his bed and she declines, the angels will keep cursing her until the morning." Muslim's wording reads: "If the wife goes to sleep while ignoring her husband's bed, the angels will keep cursing her until the morning."

**"˹if they persist,˺ do not share their beds"** is the second step. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that the abandonment means no intercourse with her, and lying on her bed with his back to her. As-Suddī, Ad-Daḥḥāk, ʿIkrimah and Ibn ʿAbbās in another narration added not speaking with her. The Qur'an says "in beds", and from here the jurists deduced that the separation is limited to beds and not the house itself. The woman is not to be left alone in the house, which would hurt her far more and strain relations further. The compilers of the Sunan and the Musnad recorded from Muʿāwiyah ibn Ḥaydah the question, "O Allah's Messenger, what right does the wife of one of us have on him?", and the answer: "To feed her when you eat, clothe her when you buy clothes for yourself, refrain from striking her face or cursing her, and not to abandon her except in the house."

**"˹but if they still persist,˺ then discipline them ˹gently˺"** is the third step, and the restraint in it is deliberate. Ibn ʿAbbās and several others said the verse means a beating that is not violent, and al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said a beating that is not severe. It must not affect the body or reach the limits of hurt to skin or bones. As for slapping or hitting the face, it is absolutely forbidden. The first two steps, convincing and leaving apart in beds, are an exercise in nobility against arrogant intransigence. The prophets preached them and practised them. The third was permitted as a forced option, and alongside the permission stands the hadith that good men among you will never beat women. No such action is reported from the blessed prophets of Allah. Muslim recorded from Jābir that in the Farewell Ḥajj the Prophet ﷺ said:

> Fear Allah regarding women, for they are your assistants. You have the right on them that they do not allow any person whom you dislike to step on your mat. However, if they do that, you are allowed to discipline them lightly. They have a right on you that you provide them with their provision and clothes, in a reasonable manner.

The daughter of Abū Bakr is the narrator, in the records of Ibn Saʿd and al-Baihaqī, of a report that in the first days of Islam beating women was forbidden outright, then they grew far too oppressive, and the permission was reinstated.

**"But if they change their ways, do not be unjust to them"** closes the ladder. When the wife obeys in all that Allah has allowed, no means of annoyance are allowed against her. The husband has no right to beat her or shun her bed, and no right to go hair-splitting for faults on flimsy issues. The ending is a reminder aimed at him: if he transgresses without justification, Allah, the Ever Most High, Most Great, is her Protector, and He will exert revenge on those who deal with their wives unjustly.

### The occasion of the permission

The verse has a background. Zayd ibn Zuhayr had married his daughter Ḥabībah to Saʿd ibn al-Rabīʿ. During a dispute the husband slapped her. She complained to her father, who took her to the Prophet ﷺ. He declared that Ḥabībah had the right to hit Saʿd as hard as he had hit her, and they started back home to take their revenge. Then this verse was revealed, permitting the beating of women only as the very last option and allowing no retaliation against men. The Prophet ﷺ called both of them back, asked them to abide by the injunction from Allah, and set aside his first directive. One report of the occasion, given at the revelation of this pair of verses, names the woman as the daughter of Muḥammad ibn Salamah and says her husband, spelled Ibn al-Rabi there, slapped her because she refused to sleep with him, so that she came to the Prophet ﷺ seeking retaliation and Allah prohibited it.

### The inner reading

More was given to the men because the whole burden rides on them, theirs being the perfection of strength and the eminence of aspiration, and burdens come measured to strength or to aspiration. The folk of resoluteness are given resolutions in their measure. And in the matter of obedience itself a limit stands: her obedience in the body belongs to the husband, but love and inclination of the heart toward him belong to God's giving. He should not burden her with what God has not provided for her to give, for hearts are in the power of God, who causes love in them toward whom He will and hate toward whom He will. And when it is said "if they then obey you, do not seek a way against them", the warning reaches the past as well: do not forget her loyalty of the past because of a single incident of antipathy in the present, for perhaps the situation will become amicable again. Allah has not burdened man with what he cannot bear, so let him not burden women with what they cannot bear of affection.

### In short

- Men are caretakers by function, not by worth: headship in a family is as necessary as headship in any system, and it is bound by the sharīʿah, consultation and kindness.
- The precedence rests on a grace of creation no one earned and on maintenance men alone carry, and the twofold inheritance share circles back to women.
- The good wife guards herself and the house in the unseen, carried by Allah's help, and the birds of the air, the fish of the sea, the angels and the beasts pray for her.
- Correction climbs a ladder of three: counsel, the bed apart, a discipline that leaves no mark, and the face is never touched.
- When she returns, all means of annoyance are forbidden, for the Most High, All-Great is her protector.
- Love of the heart is God's to give, not the husband's to demand, and past loyalty is not erased by one present fault.

When the ladder of the house runs out: "If you anticipate a split between them, appoint a mediator from his family and another from hers. If they desire reconciliation, Allah will restore harmony between them. Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Aware". The previous verse kept the correction inside the couple. This one keeps the fallout inside the family.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you anticipate a split between them"** is said to the community around the couple. The genitive construction shiqāq baynihimā, a breach between the two, covers the long-drawn feud, whether the woman is temperamentally obstinate or the man unjustly oppressive. One thing is certain: it will not stay within four walls. Supporters of each party go about maligning the others, tempers rise, and the disagreement of two individuals becomes a confrontation between two families.

**"appoint a mediator from his family and another from hers"** names two arbiters, a ḥakam from each side. The pair of verses was revealed, one report relates, over the daughter of Muḥammad ibn Salamah, whose husband, spelled Ibn al-Rabi in that report, had slapped her when she refused to sleep with him, and who came to the Prophet ﷺ seeking retaliation until Allah prohibited it. The word itself fixes the qualification: the capability to decide the dispute, found in a person both knowledgeable and trustworthy. In the ordinary course the judge, when estrangement occurs, refers the couple to a trusted person who examines the case to stop the wrongs between them. If the matter continues or worsens, he sends a trustworthy person from her family and one from his to determine whether parting or staying is best.

**"If they desire reconciliation, Allah will restore harmony between them"** carries the whole theology of the verse. If both arbitrators genuinely wish for peace, there will be unseen help from Allah, and through their efforts He creates love and harmony in the couple's hearts. Where rapport is not restored, it may be that one of the arbitrators lacks perfect sincerity in pursuing peace. Allah gives preference to staying together.

**"Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Aware"** warns the arbitrators as much as the spouses. No injustice or crookedness from them will go unnoticed, for they appear before the One who knows all and is aware of the unseen in every heart.

### What the arbitrators may do

The Qur'an does not determine their rights, and the fuqahā', the scholars of fiqh, worked them out. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported from Ibn ʿAbbās, in a report collected by Ibn Abī Ḥātim and Ibn Jarīr, that Allah commands a righteous man from the husband's side and one from the wife's side, and their task is to discover which of the spouses carries the wrong. A husband at fault is kept back from his wife and pays some restitution. A wife at fault stays with her husband, who pays nothing. Whatever the arbitrators decide, to keep the marriage intact or to dissolve it, is upheld. Should they decide it stays and one spouse dissents while the other consents, then at death the consenting one inherits, and the dissenting one does not.

An incident from before ʿAlī, narrated by ʿUbādah as-Salmānī in the Sunan of al-Baihaqī, sets the boundary. A man and a woman came to ʿAlī with groups of people on both sides, and he asked them to appoint an arbitrator from each family. To the two he said: do you know your responsibility? If you both agree to keep the husband and wife together and make peace, do it, and if you conclude matters cannot be set right and both concur that separation is the expedient course, do it. The woman accepted, leaving the decision to the arbitrators by Divine law whether it met her wishes or went against them. The husband said he would never accept separation, but authorized the arbitrators to impose whatever financial penalty they wished so his wife would return to him in peace. ʿAlī refused: he must authorize them exactly as she had done. From this, some mujtahid Imams deduced that the arbitrators have an inherent authority to divorce, while Imam Abū Ḥanīfah and Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, as the Rūḥ al-Maʿānī relates, took the opposite position: had the power been inherent, ʿAlī would have had no need to obtain the parties' authorization, and the very seeking of their agreement proves the arbitrators hold no such power. They become authorized only when the husband and wife delegate the authority, and then if both agree on divorce, divorce is effected, and if they decide release under khulʿ, the khulʿ takes effect and binds the parties.

Shaykh Abū ʿUmar ibn ʿAbdul-Barr summarized the agreed position: when the two arbitrators disagree, the opinion dissolving the marriage is not adopted, and their decision to keep the couple together is binding even if the spouses did not appoint them as agents. On separation the scholars differ, and the majority holds that it is binding all the same.

### Beyond the married couple

Muslim jurists apply the two arbitrators beyond marriage, profitably and especially where the parties are related, for a court decision is a short-term solution that leaves the germs of hatred in hearts, reappearing later in unpleasant forms. ʿUmar promulgated an order for his judges: "Send disputes between relatives back to them so that they make peace with the help of each other, as a court decision breeds heart burnings and hostility." The reason covers non-relatives too, and the jurists hold it appropriate for officials and judges, before hearing cases formally, to seek a conciliation between the disputing parties, as al-Tarablusī's Muʿīn al-ḥukkām and Ibn al-Shahnah's Lisān al-ḥukkām bear out. The two verses together present a comprehensive system of family life. Disputes are resolved in the house, method after method. When that fails, officials or kinsfolk settle them through two arbitrators, so the dispute leaves the house but not the family. Only then does the court investigate and decide justly, and a world that practised this would be spared the local dissensions that swell into tribal, racial, national and even international feuds.

### In short

- Two arbiters, one from each family, knowledgeable and trustworthy, take the feud before the families are pulled into it.
- Their ordinary task is peace, and their authority to part the couple exists only by the spouses' delegation, as ʿAlī's insistence on full authorization from both proved.
- Sincere desire for reconciliation draws unseen help from Allah, and its absence usually means an arbitrator's sincerity failed.
- The same arbitration serves relatives and strangers, for court victories breed hatreds, and ʿUmar ordered his judges to try peace first.
- The All-Knowing, All-Aware watches the arbiters as closely as the spouses.
