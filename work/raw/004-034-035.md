<<<VERSE 4:34>>>
The earlier verses on women forbade withholding or wasting their rights. This verse (4:34) now sets out the other side, a man's place in the family, and the way to handle trouble inside a marriage: "Men are the caretakers of women, as men have been provisioned by Allah over women and tasked with supporting them financially. And righteous women are devoutly obedient and, when alone, protective of what Allah has entrusted them with. And if you sense ill-conduct from your women, advise them ˹first˺, ˹if they persist,˺ do not share their beds, ˹but if they still persist,˺ then discipline them ˹gently˺. But if they change their ways, do not be unjust to them. Surely Allah is Most High, All-Great."

The verse begins with a principle and then describes two kinds of wives, the good and those who fall short, and a graded way to deal with the second kind that is meant to keep the matter inside the home.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Men are the caretakers of women"**. The Arabic word is qawwām. It describes a person who holds the responsibility, the duty and the charge of managing a job or running a system, and who looks after everything that goes with it. The word has been translated in several ways, most often as one who rules or decides, and also as guardian, custodian, overseer and protector. Understood as a carer and a functional head, and not as a ruler in the political sense or a dictator, it fits what ordinary experience shows. The man is responsible for the woman. He is her maintainer, her caretaker and the leader who corrects her if she goes astray. Short explanations say that he oversees her proper conduct, and that he has authority over her, to discipline her and keep her within bounds.

**"as men have been provisioned by Allah over women"**. The Arabic says that Allah has made some of them excel others. Men excel women and are better for certain tasks. This is why prophethood belonged to men, as did other important positions of leadership. The Prophet ﷺ said, as al-Bukhārī recorded, that a people who put a woman in charge of their affairs will never succeed. The same applies to appointing women as judges or to other positions of leadership. Other explanations name the ways in which men were favoured, such as reason, the division of booty and estates, knowledge and authority. The inner reading adds that Allah gave men more because the burden falls entirely on them. They have the full strength and the eminence of aspiration, and they carry burdens in proportion to their strength or their aspiration.

**"and tasked with supporting them financially"**. This means the dowry, the spending and the many expenses that Allah set down in His Book and in the Sunnah of His Messenger for men to meet for women. It is these that make it fitting for a man to be her maintainer, as Allah says elsewhere: "But men have a degree (of responsibility) over them" (2:228). Allah has made this responsibility the men's. The women are not required to pay the dowry or spend on others in that way.

**"And righteous women are devoutly obedient"**. The Arabic word is qānitāt. Ibn ʿAbbās and others said that it means obedient to their husbands. Another explanation says they are kind to their husbands and obedient to Allah regarding them. A short explanation says they are devoted to their Lord and to their husbands.

**"and, when alone, protective of what Allah has entrusted them with"**. As-Suddī and others said that she guards her honour and her husband's property when he is away. Another explanation says that she guards in the unseen, that is, she guards her private parts and everything else while her husband is absent, because of what Allah has guarded for them when He told their husbands to look after them well. According to another, it means that the husband is truly protected only when Allah protects him. It is Allah's own help, His tawfīq, that lets women meet these heavy responsibilities. The words mention the husband's absence because that is when a woman is more likely to be careless. The point is to avoid showing a lot of care and alertness while the husband is in the house and neglecting these rules when he is out of sight.

**"And if you sense ill-conduct from your women, advise them ˹first˺"**. This is a woman who shows bad conduct toward her husband. She may act as if she is above him, disobey him, ignore him, or dislike him. When such signs appear, the husband should advise her and remind her of Allah's punishment for disobedience. Allah ordered a wife to obey her husband and forbade her to disobey him, because of the greatness of his rights and all that he does for her. Other explanations specify that the rebellion is disobedience to him in bed, and that the advice is given with sacred knowledge and with the Qur'an, so as to make her fear Allah.

**"do not share their beds"**. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported that Ibn ʿAbbās said that the abandonment means not having intercourse with her and lying on the bed with one's back to her. Several others said the same. As-Suddī, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, ʿIkrimah and Ibn ʿAbbās, in another report, added that the husband should not speak with her. The words say "in the beds". Jurists conclude that the separation should be limited to the bed and not extended to the house. A woman left alone in the house would be hurt far more, and relations would be strained more. Muʿāwiyah ibn Ḥaydah asked, "O Messenger of Allah, what is the right that the wife of one of us has over him?" The Prophet ﷺ said, as the Sunan and Musnad compilers recorded:

> "To feed her when you eat, to clothe her when you clothe yourself, not to strike her face or curse her, and not to leave her apart except within the house."

**"then discipline them ˹gently˺"**. If advice and ignoring her in bed do not work, the husband is allowed to discipline his wife, without severe beating. Muslim recorded that Jābir said that in the Farewell Ḥajj the Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Fear Allah regarding women, for they are your helpers. You have a right over them that they do not allow into your bed anyone you dislike. If they do this, you may discipline them lightly. They have a right over you to be provided with their food and clothing in a reasonable manner."

Ibn ʿAbbās and several others said that the beating is not a violent one. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said it is not a severe one. Other explanations speak of a mild beating that is not exaggerated, one that does not hurt the body or injure the skin or bones. Slapping or hitting the face is absolutely forbidden.

This is a last resort. The first two steps, gentle explanation and leaving the bed, are a way of showing nobility against stubbornness, and the prophets and their righteous followers spoke in favour of them. The third was allowed as a forced choice in a limited form. A saying alongside it is that the good men among you do not beat women, and no such act is reported of the prophets. According to a report from the daughter of Abū Bakr, given by Ibn Saʿd and al-Baihaqī, beating women was forbidden outright in the early days. But women became far too oppressive as a result, and the permission was restored. The Prophet ﷺ did not like this method, and said that gentlemen would not use it.

**"But if they change their ways, do not be unjust to them"**. When a wife obeys her husband in all that Allah has allowed, he may not look for any means of annoyance against her. He then has no right to beat her or to shun her bed. Other explanations say that "seeking a way" means looking for a reason to strike her unjustly, or seeking a way against her with regard to love. A man should forgive and let go of what happened, and not look for faults on flimsy grounds. The inner reading adds: do not forget her loyalty in the past because of one moment of dislike in the present, since things may well become pleasant again.

**"Surely Allah is Most High, All-Great"**. This reminds men that if they wrong their wives without cause, the Most High and Most Great is the Protector of those women, and He will take revenge on those who wrong them and deal with them unjustly. The advantage Allah gave men over women is not absolute. His own position over men weighs heavily. If a man goes beyond the limits set for him, he is the one who will be punished. Another explanation says: Allah has not loaded you with what you cannot bear, so do not load women with what they cannot bear, such as affection.

### Authority with limits

For any group that lives together, big or small, and for any organised system, it is both reasonable and customary to have a head, a chief or an authority who can arbitrate in a disagreement and make decisions so that matters run smoothly. This is accepted everywhere in the running of countries and governments, and it was also felt in older tribal societies, where the chief was the authority for the tribe. A family is a small sample of such larger bodies, so it too needs someone to head it, support it and run it. Allah in His wisdom chose men for this duty, because their natural abilities are stronger than those of women and children. This is so plain a fact of human life that no sane person, man or woman, can deny it, and denying it does not change reality.

The sum of the matter is that the rights of women are as binding on men as the rights of men are on women, and the two sets of rights are alike, with one exception. Men have a certain precedence in functional authority, and even that is hedged by balancing factors. As other verses explain, this is not the authority of a dictator or a tyrant. A man who exercises it is bound by the supreme law of Islam, the Sharīʿah. He must act by consultation and follow good advice. He cannot act on a whim or on wild instinct. He is commanded to treat women well, in the recognised way. The principle of consulting appears in another verse (2:233), where husbands are told to act in consultation with their wives in family matters. After this explanation, a man's authority should not be a cause of heartburn for women.

### Two reasons for men's responsibility

Because women might take this arrangement unhappily, Allah did not just proclaim the man's authority. He gave two reasons, one that belongs to the wisdom of creation, which no human being controls, and one that comes through a person's own effort.

The first is in the words "Allah has made some of them excel others". It is the wisdom and choice of Allah alone. It is like the sacred precincts of Makkah, where Allah named one house of worship as His House and the qiblah, and like Jerusalem, where the Bayt al-Maqdis was given special honour. The precedence of men is similar. It is a grace from Allah. Men did nothing to earn it, and nothing is wrong with women because they do not have it. It rests on the wisdom of creation and is the Creator's own prerogative.

The second reason is what comes through effort. Men spend their money, pay the dower and bear the burden of meeting all of women's needs. This is why they have the precedence. A comment in al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ is worth noting. These two reasons also show that the right to authority is not established by force alone. It can be earned by a person's competence and ability.

The wording is also noteworthy. The Qur'an does not say that Allah made men excel women. It says that He made some of them excel others. The expression hints that men and women are parts of each other. Men's precedence, even where it is proved, is no more than the precedence of a person's head over his hand, or of his heart over his stomach. Just as the head's precedence does not lessen the role or importance of the hand, a man's precedence does not lower a woman's status, since each is part of the other's body. One may say that the man is the head and the woman the body. Some commentators see another hint. The precedence is in terms of the kind, and as individuals a woman may exceed a man in her qualities and achievements, even in those of a caretaker, over some individual men.

The second reason also removes a doubt that might arise from the share of men being double that of women in the verses on inheritance (4:11). All the financial duties lie with men. Before a woman marries, her expenses are her father's, and after marriage they pass to her husband. In that light, the double share is not too much, because it returns to women in the end.

It also points to a principle about functions. By her creation and nature, a woman is not made to go out, work and earn her own living. The qualities of her being do not suit running around offices and markets, doing jobs and heavy labour for a living. To keep her from having to rough it out like men, Allah placed her whole responsibility on men. Before marriage her father looks after her, and after marriage her husband. She has been made the means of human procreation, and the duties of being a mother and keeping the house and family together are on her shoulders. A man cannot handle those burdens. So it cannot be concluded that her status is lowered by making her dependent on a man for her expenses. No value judgment is being made. It is only a functional division of duties, with a precedence that works both ways. In short, the two reasons show that men's precedence neither lowers women's status nor holds any special gain for men, and whatever gain there is goes back to women.

Two things ought to be present when deciding which child of one father and mother is formally authorised to take care of the other. The one with authority should have the mental and physical ability to carry out its demands, and the one under it should agree and be pleased. The first reason in the verse points to the ability of the man. The second points to the agreement of the party who will be under that authority. When a woman agrees to enter the marriage bond, in return for her dower and full maintenance, she is agreeing to the husband's authority and accepting it with pleasure.

### The wife the Qur'an praises

Ibn Jarīr recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> "The best of women is the one who pleases you when you look at her, obeys you when you command her, and guards herself and your property when you are away."

Then he recited the verse "Men are the caretakers of women" to its end. Aḥmad recorded from ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> "If a woman prays her five prayers, fasts her month, guards her chastity and obeys her husband, she will be told, 'Enter Paradise by whichever door you like.'"

The Prophet ﷺ also said, as al-Bukhārī recorded from Abū Hurayrah, that if a man calls his wife to his bed and she refuses, the angels curse her until morning. Muslim has it in the words that if a wife spends the night ignoring her husband's bed, the angels curse her until morning. In another saying, the Prophet ﷺ said that if he were to command anyone to prostrate before another, he would command the wife to prostrate before her husband, because of the greatness of his right over her. A further hadith says that for a woman who is dutiful to her husband, the birds in the air, the fish in the sea, the angels in the sky and the beasts in the forest ask forgiveness from the Lord for her.

Protecting her own honour and modesty and everything in her care at home is the base of happiness in a well-run household. Women are weaker than men in some abilities, and yet they tend to be much stronger than men in carrying out these duties. This too comes from Allah's help. That is the reason that women, in general, are less involved than men in sinful immodesty.

### The three steps for a wife's ill-conduct

The Qur'an gives three methods, to be used in the order in which they are named. A good woman accepts the role of the man as leader according to Allah's plan. If she does not, the man must not turn vengeful or slander her with accusations. If he has found her guilty of improper conduct, he tries to reform her. First he counsels her and talks it over with her gently. If that works, the matter ends there. She is saved from a lasting sin, and he is saved from an aching heart.

If she remains stubborn, the next step is to sleep on a separate bed, as a sign of his displeasure and as a warning that may change her mind. This is a mild form of admonition. If she takes the hint, the dispute ends. If she ignores even this and keeps up her crooked ways, the third step permits a token beating, the outer limit of which is that it must leave no mark on the body. The Prophet ﷺ did not like this and said that gentlemen would not do it. But if this last, symbolic reproof restores normal relations, the aim is achieved.

The three options given to men are followed by the words "But if they change their ways, do not be unjust to them". If, after all three steps, the women begin to listen, men should not split hairs or start making more accusations. It is better to let it go, remembering that the authority given to them over women is not absolute, since Allah's authority over them is heavy.

So the verse offers a compact system for settling a quarrel inside the four walls of the home, without a third party having to step in.

### Why the verse came down

Zayd ibn Zuhayr had given his daughter Ḥabībah in marriage to Saʿd ibn ar-Rabīʿ. In a dispute over something, the husband slapped her. Ḥabībah complained to her father, and he took her to the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ ruled that Ḥabībah had the right to hit Saʿd as hard as he had hit her. As they set off home to take their revenge, this verse was revealed. It allowed a beating as the very last option and did not allow retaliation or revenge against husbands. The Prophet ﷺ soon called the two back and told them to follow what Allah had decreed, and he withdrew his first ruling.

Another account ties the passage from "Men are the caretakers of women" to "If they desire reconciliation, Allah will restore harmony between them" to the daughter of Muḥammad ibn Salamah. Asʿad ibn ar-Rabīʿ, her husband, had struck her face with his hand after she refused to share his bed. She came to the Prophet ﷺ asking for retaliation, and Allah forbade it.

### The inner reading

Men "stand over" women through what Allah has preferred some over others with and through what they spend. He gave men more because the burden is wholly theirs, since they have the perfection of strength and the height of aspiration. They carry burdens in measure of their strength or their aspiration. It is said that resolutions are made according to the measure of the people of resolve.

### In short

- Men are caretakers of women, not as tyrants, but as heads of the family. They must consult and follow the Sharīʿah. The two reasons given are Allah's choice and the fact that men spend on women.
- Righteous wives are devout and protect themselves and their husband's property in his absence, with Allah's help.
- For a wife's ill-conduct there are three steps in order: advice, leaving the bed, and a light, non-violent discipline as a last resort. Striking the face is absolutely forbidden. When the wife changes her ways, the husband must not seek any way against her.
- The verse was revealed after Saʿd ibn ar-Rabīʿ slapped his wife Ḥabībah and her father wanted retaliation. It allowed no retaliation and set the order of steps.
- Allah is Most High and Most Great, and He takes the part of wives who are wronged.

<<<VERSE 4:35>>>
The verse that follows provides for the case in which the quarrel is not settled at home: "If you anticipate a split between them, appoint a mediator from his family and another from hers. If they desire reconciliation, Allah will restore harmony between them. Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Aware." The previous verse dealt with a wife's rebellion. This one deals with estrangement and alienation between husband and wife.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you anticipate a split between them"**. A family feud can drag on, either because the woman is stubborn, or because the husband has been unjust and oppressive. In either case it will not stay inside the walls of the home. Supporters of one side will start to run down the other with accusations, tempers will rise, and what began between two people will turn into a clash between two families. The Arabic phrase is shiqāq baynihimā, a breach between the two. Another explanation says that the verse speaks of a dispute where you cannot tell which of them is at fault. A short explanation says it is a disagreement that is turning into aggression and a clash of natures.

**"appoint a mediator from his family and another from hers"**. The scholars of fiqh say that when estrangement arises, the judge first refers the couple to a trusted person, who looks into their case to stop any wrongs between them. If the matter goes on or gets worse, the judge sends a trustworthy man from the wife's family and another from the husband's, to meet them and examine whether it is better for them to part or stay together.

The Qur'an addresses the officials of the government, the guardians of the two parties, their supporters and the Muslims in general. It suggests a decent method that cools tempers, closes the way to accusations and makes a settlement possible. The aim is that the quarrel, though it cannot stay in the couple's house, is at least settled within their families and does not go to court and become public. The concerned officials, the guardians or a group of Muslims who have integrity, influence and authority should appoint two arbitrators, one from the man's family and one from the woman's. The word used for both is ḥakam, which points to the qualifications they need. They must be able to decide the dispute, and that means a person who is both knowledgeable and trustworthy. One explanation says the arbitrators should be sent with the couple's consent. Another adds that the husband gives his arbitrator the matter of divorce or of accepting compensation in its place, while the wife gives hers the matter of separation.

**"If they desire reconciliation, Allah will restore harmony between them"**. The Qur'an does not lay down what the two arbitrators are to do or what their rights are. It adds only this remark, and two things come out of it. First, if both arbitrators have good intentions and truly want peace, they will receive unseen help from Allah, and He will create love and harmony in the couple's hearts. If harmony is not restored, it may be that one of the arbitrators lacks full sincerity. Second, the purpose of appointing them is to bring about peace between husband and wife, and nothing beyond that.

Allah gives preference to the couple staying together. Short explanations say the same: staying together is preferable and better, and the arbitrators should use the best means to bring them back together.

Ibn ʿAbbās explained that Allah commands that a righteous man be appointed from the husband's family and another from the wife's, to find out which of the two is in the wrong. If the man is at fault, they keep him from his wife, and he pays some compensation. If the wife is at fault, she stays with her husband and he pays no compensation. If the arbitrators decide that the marriage should stay or be dissolved, their decision stands. If they decide that it should stay, but one spouse agrees and the other does not, and one of them then dies, the one who agreed inherits from the other, and the one who did not agree does not inherit. Ibn Abī Ḥātim and Ibn Jarīr collected this.

Shaykh Abū ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-Barr said that the scholars agree that when the two arbitrators disagree, the view in favour of dissolving the marriage is not adopted. They also agree that the arbitrators' decision is binding, even if the spouses did not appoint them as agents, when the decision is that the couple stay together. They differ on whether it is binding when the arbitrators decide for separation. He added that the majority hold that the decision is still binding even if the spouses did not appoint them to decide anything.

One account says that this whole passage was revealed about the daughter of Muḥammad ibn Salamah, whose husband Asʿad ibn ar-Rabīʿ had slapped her. Another explanation says that, when the arbitrators and the couple wish to put things right, Allah will make them all of one mind. He will grant the couple success and determine for them what counts as obedience, which may be reconciliation or separation.

**"Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Aware"**. This is a warning to the two arbitrators as well. Neither injustice nor crookedness from them will go unnoticed, since they will stand before the One who knows everything and is aware of everything. Allah knows whether the arbitrators agree or disagree, and He is aware of what the woman and the man are doing, and of what is hidden and what is open.

### Do the two arbiters have the power to divorce?

If the couple has made the arbitrators their representatives and agents in everything, they have admitted that any joint decision of the two is binding on both. The arbitrators then have full authority to decide. If both agree on a divorce, they can divorce. If both decide that the wife should be released with khulʿ, a divorce she buys, it takes effect, and the parties are bound by it. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and Imām Abū Ḥanīfah held this view, as Rūḥ al-Maʿānī and others record.

There is a report from the time of ʿAlī that supports the view that the arbitrators have no inherent right except to make peace, unless the parties give them full authority. ʿUbaydah as-Salmānī narrated it, and it is in the Sunan of al-Baihaqī. A man and a woman came to ʿAlī, each with groups of people. He asked them to appoint an arbitrator from each family. When the two had been appointed, he said to them, "Do you know your responsibility? Do you know what you must do? Listen to me. If you both agree that the couple should stay together and be reconciled, do it. If you find that things cannot be mended, or that they will not stay on good terms later, and you both agree that separating them is the sensible course, do that."

The woman said, "I accept this. Let these two act according to Allah's law, and I will accept any decision they give, whether it matches my wishes or not." The husband said, "I will not accept separation or divorce under any condition. But I authorise the arbitrators to tell me to pay whatever financial penalty they impose, and let my wife come back to me in peace." ʿAlī said, "No. You too must authorise the arbitrators in the same way the woman has done."

Some mujtahid Imams drew from this the rule that the arbitrators have an inherent power to divorce, since ʿAlī asked the parties to give that authority. Imām Abū Ḥanīfah and al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī held that if the power were inherent, ʿAlī would not have needed to seek the authorisation of the parties. The very effort to obtain their agreement proves that the arbitrators do not have such a power by themselves. They do become authorised if the husband and wife hand it to them.

### Why a family settlement is better than a court

When two people disagree, their minds become biased. They do not think about each other fairly. In such a case the best way to settle it is to bring in an arbiter who is not personally involved, who can think without prejudice and decide from the facts of the case.

This teaching of the Qur'an opens a new way of resolving disputes. It saves people from going to courts and officials, and it lets them settle a lot of their disputes and claims through arbitration inside the family. Muslim jurists say that appointing two arbitrators to make peace is not limited to quarrels between husband and wife. It can be used, and should be used, for other kinds of discord, especially when the parties are relatives. A court decision is a short-term answer to the underlying problem. Such a decision leaves seeds of hatred and hostility in people's hearts, and they come up again after a time in far more unpleasant forms.

ʿUmar issued an order to his judges, "Send disputes between relatives back to them, so that they make peace with the help of one another, since a court's decision breeds heartburning and hostility." The reason he gave, that court decisions tend to create hatred and hostility in hearts, applies to non-relatives too. Wisdom lies in saving all Muslims from mutual hatred. So jurists hold that it is right for officials and judges, before hearing cases formally, to try to find a way for the disputing parties to meet and agree to a settlement. They mention al-Ṭarāblusī's Muʿīn al-Ḥukkām and Ibn ash-Shahnah's Lisān al-Ḥukkām.

Though short, the two verses give a complete system of family life. If it were put into practice, it could remove many quarrels and hatreds from the world, and men and women would live in peace among their families, safe from the local feuds that grow into tribal, racial, national and even international ones. In summary:

1. Settle family disputes inside the house, using one method after another.
2. When that is not possible, government officials or relatives make peace between the parties through two arbitrators, so that the quarrel does not leave the wider family circle, even if it leaves the house.
3. When that fails too and the matter reaches a court, the judge must look into the background of both sides and give a just ruling.

A good woman, then, accepts the man's role as leader by Allah's plan. A good man does his part and does not forget that Allah is always watching him. Allah's court of judgment does not distinguish between a man and a woman. The difference between the sexes serves only the good management of worldly life, and has no bearing on repayment and reward in the Hereafter.

### The inner reading

It is said that a husband has a right to his wife's obedience in the body. As for love and inclination of the heart toward him, that is up to Allah. So do not burden her with what Allah has not provided for you from her. Hearts are in Allah's power. He puts love in them toward whomever He wills and makes them hate whomever He wills.

### In short

- When the split between a couple cannot be settled at home, two arbitrators are appointed, one from each family, to look into the case, so that the quarrel stays within the families and out of the public eye.
- They should be knowledgeable, trustworthy and sincere, since Allah promises to restore harmony if both truly want it, and staying together is preferred.
- Scholars differ on whether the arbitrators may divorce on their own, with al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and Abū Ḥanīfah requiring the spouses to delegate that power, as ʿAlī required in the case brought to him.
- This kind of family arbitration can be used for other disputes among relatives, as ʿUmar told his judges.
- Inwardly, love lies in Allah's hands, and a husband should not demand from his wife what Allah has not given him.
