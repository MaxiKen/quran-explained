<<<VERSE 6:40>>>
"Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, 'Imagine if you were overwhelmed by Allah's torment or the Hour—would you call upon any other than Allah ˹for help˺?'" The verse puts a hypothetical question, and the answer to it is already known. It is not a question about what these people say they believe in calm weather. It is about what they will do when there is no calm left.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺"** is addressed to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and the words "imagine if you were overwhelmed" mean: tell them to look at themselves and consider what they are saying. **"if you were overwhelmed by Allah's torment"** is about this world, and one reading points to what actually happened, the way things went on the Day of Badr, on Uḥud and at the battle of the Confederates. **"or the Hour"** is the torment of the Day of Judgment, and it also covers the Hour that comes suddenly, with no warning they can prepare for. **"would you call upon any other than Allah ˹for help˺?"** asks whether they would then cry out to anyone besides Him to lift the punishment off them. **"˹Answer me˺ if your claims are true"** is the challenge: say that these idols are His partners and that they can bring benefit or push harm away, and then call on them if it is so. The point Allah is making about His creatures is that He does with them whatever He wills, and no one can resist what He has decided. He has no partner, and He accepts the supplication of whomever He wills.

### Why the question cannot be dodged

The answer was given long before it was asked. It is a fact about them rather than a claim about the message. In that kind of hour they will not call on anyone but Allah, because they know that none except Him can remove an affliction. Another verse states the same reaction at sea: when harm touches you on the water, those you used to call upon besides Him vanish from you, and He alone is left (17:67). A person in necessity forgets his idols and his false deities, and calls on the One he had put aside. History testifies to it, since a man who relied on other beings, or who did not even believe that God exists, starts invoking His name at the crucial moment. When the outward veils are lifted and a person dismisses what he had been entertaining, nothing is left in his mouth but the name of Allah. That is nature's own witness that Allah exists and that the authority is His alone.

One reading presses the same question from inside it: if harm touches you, whom do you want to remove it, and if an affair turns against you, from whom do you hope for gentleness? The child of Adam is pitiable, for he does not know the worth of that gentleness and does not recognize the danger of that exaltedness. The verse is at once a manifestation of Allah's majesty, exaltedness and independence of His servants, and a preparation for His gentleness, bounty and mercy. Hear it as though from Him: if in My lordship I were to assault these creatures out of justice, who would hold the assault back and come to their aid? If I were to raise the banner of the resurrection suddenly out of the ambush of the unseen, where would these servants flee, whose hand would they take hold of, whom would they call? Then generosity answers on their behalf, and the verse goes on to tell them that He is the one they will call on.

### The test is the uncovered moment

What the Qur'an asks for is not this kind of admission. Recognition at the point of helplessness is compulsory, and a compulsory confession is not what the attestation of Allah and His Messenger was meant to be. Every animal except man leads a realistic life by instinct, without being argued into it. In the human world the thing that does the work instinct does for animals is fear of Allah, taqwa, held on while everything is still going well. The verse is there to show them the shape of their own behavior under pressure, and then to ask why they will not give that behavior now, when the test is still open.

### In short

- The verse asks the people of Makkah to imagine the torment or the Hour arriving and answer honestly whom they would call on.
- One reading connects it to Badr, Uḥud and the battle of the Confederates, another to the Day of Judgment.
- Their own known reaction is that they would call on no one but Allah, since only He can remove an affliction, as 17:67 says about the sea.
- One reading takes the question as a display of majesty that opens the door to mercy.
- Compelled recognition at the point of helplessness is not faith, because faith is meant to be given before the compulsion arrives.

<<<VERSE 6:41>>>
"No! He is the only One you would call." The answer to the question is spoken for them, because it has been spoken many times already by their own behavior in distress. The verse then adds what follows from it, and what they will do with the partners they had appointed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"No! He is the only One you would call"** means that in a time of tribulation they call on Him and on no one else, and they will not summon anyone besides Allah to lift the torment. **"And if He willed, He could remove the affliction that made you invoke Him"** puts the relief entirely in His hands. He removes what you ask Him to remove, such as suffering, if He wills to remove it. He is the One worthy of that and able to do it. **"Only then will you forget whatever you associate with Him ˹in worship˺"** means that you leave the idols, abandon them and stop calling on them. You neglect them, in other words, and do not address them at all, because you know in that hour that they can bring no benefit and cause no harm.

### What the forgetting proves

The verse turns their own instinct against the arrangement they had built. The partners were appointed as problem-solvers and granters of wishes, and the devotion to them was supposed to start working exactly in the hour of trial. That is precisely the hour in which they are dropped. So when will the devotion work, if the moment it was needed is the moment it is forgotten? The affliction itself is not what is being denied here, since Allah could take it away if He willed, and the removal is not produced by the intensity of the crying out but by His will.

A Sufi commentary gives the answer a voice of its own, in which Allah says: you will call on Me, you will know Me, and you will ask Me to take the trial off you. I am the powerful one, the bounteous one, the friend whose doing and whose wanting are alike beautiful. Then the reports of David are brought in. In them the Lord says: ask the dwellers on earth why they will not be My friends, when I am worthy of friendship. I am generous without stinginess, knowing without ignorance, patient without weakness, and there is no change in My attributes and no turning back from bounty. In the beginningless I wrote mercy toward them against Myself, burned the incense of love, and lit up their hearts with the light of recognition. Those who turn away, He says, would die of yearning and cut every tie for love, if they knew how long He waits for them and how He longs to set their disobedience aside. He gives blessings and they thank someone else for them, He wards off the trial and they credit someone else with it, their shelter is His presence and they take shelter elsewhere. Let them go, let them flee, and in the end they will come back. You too have a brightness from Me, and you will wander and then arrive. He is the friend of whoever befriends Him, and the companion of whoever takes Him as companion.

The state of the servants answers that with a line of thanksgiving. Love for Your essence is the belief of Your friends, and remembering Your description, Lord, takes the grief of the grieving away. The cash a servant holds in hand is the treasure-house of His bounty, so the hopeful go on stitching their purses of hope.

### In short

- The verse answers its own question: in distress they call on Allah alone.
- The removal of the affliction depends on His will, not on the strength of the call.
- At that moment the partners are forgotten, because a dying heart knows they can do nothing.
- The question the verse leaves open is when a devotion that fails in the hour of need was ever going to succeed.
- One Sufi reading hears in it an address about friendship, waiting and returning, drawn out of the reports of David.

<<<VERSE 6:42>>>
"Indeed, We have sent messengers before you ˹O Prophet˺ to other people who We put through suffering and adversity ˹for their denial˺, so perhaps they would be humbled." This verse widens the argument from one city to the whole history of prophethood, and it explains what suffering is for. Allah did not send punishment on any people all at once. What came first was admonition, and admonition is a call to come back. The question put in 6:41 had already been answered for them, and this verse shows that the same method has run through every nation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have sent messengers before you to other people"** is sent to this people exactly as it was sent to those peoples, and their answer was to deny the messengers and turn away from what they brought. **"who We put through suffering"** means being seized with extreme poverty, the loss of wealth and diminished provision, and with loss of health, illness, disease and pain. **"and adversity"** is tribulation, fear of one another, hardship and hunger. **"so perhaps they would be humbled"** states the aim: that they would believe, submit, grow humble, and call on Allah with humbleness, and that they would pray for the punishment to be lifted.

### The second test

A community that fails the first test is not simply abandoned to it. They were tried with hardship and suffering to see whether it would turn them to Allah, and when they failed and became more absorbed in disobedience instead, they were given a different kind of trial. The doors of worldly comfort were opened for them, and they were given everything they wanted in the material world, so that through these blessings they might come to know their Benefactor. Instead they were so lost in the labyrinth of luxury that they forgot the message and the teachings of Allah and His Messenger. Having traded their souls for the glitter of passing comforts, they failed at both tests, and the case against them stood proved. Then the punishment came suddenly and consumed them.

That is also the answer to an excuse that appears in every age. There are always people who disobey Allah and His Prophet ﷺ openly and keep prospering in wealth, property, influence, recognition and power. They say that the prophet's word is a delusion. Weighing the whole world against their own short experience and taking the two sides of the argument at once, they are pushed to that conclusion by their own excuse-seeking, and by Satan. So the fate of the nations before them is quoted back at them, a punishment that came from the sky and from under the earth, and by means that reduced whole peoples to ashes. The people of Nūḥ were drowned in a flood that did not stop at the mountain tops. The people of ʿĀd were destroyed by a wind that lasted eight days and left no survivor. The people of Thamud were killed by a terrifying sound. The whole habitation of the people of Lūṭ was turned upside down. What is left of it lies in the west of Jordan as a low, lifeless body of water, which is why it is called the Dead Sea and the Sea of Lūṭ.

### Punishment, sample and showroom

Allah makes hardship do two jobs at once. Outwardly it looks like punishment, and the word for being seized here is the word for punishment. But its point is to shake heedlessness off, and in that sense it is mercy, since everyone turns to Allah naturally when he is in distress. The same is said of this world's terrors elsewhere: We will make them taste the nearer punishment instead of the greater one, so that they may come back (32:21).

What is settled here is a question about this world, which is no abode of reward. Where, then, is the point of hardship and ease in a place where good and bad seem weighed on one scale and the evil often fare better than the good? The answer is that the hereafter is the place of recompense, the Day of Judgment, Yawm al-Jazāʾ, and that some hardships here are a sample of punishment while some comforts are a sample of reward. Nobody is won over to a thing, or warned off an approaching danger, except by a model he can see. Some people of insight went further: every comfort in this world points to the comforts of Paradise, so that a person learns to long for them, and every sorrow points to the punishment of the hereafter, so that he prepares to keep clear of it. On that reading the whole world is a showroom of the hereafter. Prosperity and poverty, happiness and sorrow are then not reward and punishment in the real sense. They are a way of bringing creation back to the Creator it had grown alienated from.

### The smaller doom

There is also a reading of the word "Hour" that brings it closer. Besides the commonly acknowledged Qiyāmah, the Hour here can mean the smaller doom, al-Qiyāmatuṣ-Ṣughrā. It stands established for every single person the moment he dies, because for whoever dies his doom begins right then. A preliminary sample of the final reckoning meets the deceased in his grave and in the state of barzakh, the interval between death and resurrection. The taste of what is coming begins to show itself from this side. That is why the warnings in these verses work on two clocks at once: a nation can be destroyed in one stroke, and a person can be overtaken the moment his life ends. Kufr and shirk are refuted in this passage by the same questions that affirm tawḥīd, and the disbelievers of Makkah are asked who would deliver them when the calamity arrives. At such a time even the greatest mushrik forgets all about his idols and the objects of worship he had appointed, and calls on none but Allah, the One, the All-Powerful.

### The closing of all roads

In a reading of this verse that follows the inward sense, Ibn ʿAṭāʾ said of the seizing with misery and hardship: He cut off from them every road so that they would come back to Us, and He shut all their ways so that they turned away from the realm of being altogether and busied themselves only with His companionship, putting love for Him in their hearts. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ took as his illustration the story of Majnūn. The man was seen going around the Kaʿbah out of touch with himself, the sea of passion throwing waves in his chest, his hands lifted: "O Allah, increase me in love for Laylā." May Allah increase the passion for Laylā in his heart and multiply her trial a thousand times. His father, the prince of that time, said to him: many people have risen against you, so leave for a few days, and perhaps they will forget you and this madness for Laylā will lessen. Majnūn went away, came back on the third day and said: excuse me, passion for Laylā has closed every road to me, and the only road left is the road to her street. Everyone has a niche he prays toward, and each one's hope leans on it, but the niche of Sanāʾī is your street.

### In short

- The verse generalizes the Prophet's experience: messengers before him were sent to peoples who were then tried.
- The trial has two forms, poverty and sickness first, and then every kind of comfort, and a people can fail both.
- Life's events are messages sent to wake a person up, and hardship aimed at humility is mercy in method.
- The destructions of Nūḥ's, ʿĀd's, Thamud's and Lūṭ's peoples are listed as evidence that the pattern is old.
- Ibn ʿAṭāʾ reads the closing of roads as a mercy that leaves the heart one road only, and illustrates it with Majnūn and Laylā.

<<<VERSE 6:43>>>
"Why did they not humble themselves when We made them suffer?" The verse is an asking-that-marks-a-fact, and the fact it marks is the strangest part of the story. The proof that a person can be shown what he needs and still not use it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Why did they not humble themselves when We made them suffer?"** means: why did they not believe and abase themselves before Us when We tested them with disaster? The thing that required humility was standing right in front of them. **"Instead, their hearts were hardened"** says that they did not become soft or humble, and their hearts dried up and would not yield to faith. **"and Satan made their misdeeds appealing to them"** is the other half of the explanation. Shayṭān made fair-seeming what they used to do: the shirk, the defiance and the rebellion. They went on doing them because they had been made to look good, and they kept saying that nothing was wrong. Their disbelief was dressed up as something creditable, so they persisted and took no lesson from what had happened to them.

### What a hardened heart is

The condition named here is not a shortage of information. A person faced with the truth who refuses it is not left alone with his refusal. Allah gives him jolts, a monetary loss or a physical trouble, so that he reviews his way of life and his thinking turns around. Events of this kind are messages rather than happenings, and their whole purpose is to wake a person out of the sleep of forgetfulness. What he does instead is to console himself: these are ordinary ups and downs, and everyone has them. That is how Satan all too often pulls a mind away from the possibility of divine displeasure. He supplies plausible justifications, and says the kind of thing that leaves a man negligent about his duties. When this is repeated, the heart loses its sensitivity to what is true and what is false, to right and wrong, and the conscience ends blunted. The hardening in this verse is that end, and it came to these people after the test had already been made as clear as a test can be made.

A further layer sits in another reading: what the devil made to seem fair to them was the ordinary shape of life itself, the way hardship alternates with ease and comfort, so that nothing in it looks like a message and nothing looks like a warning. If that is their case, then the verse's question carries both reproach and surprise. The one thing they had been given, a heart that reacts, had been spent.

### The mercy that was still available

The counterfactual is drawn out plainly. Had they humbled themselves and begged Allah to lift the calamity off them, mercy would have followed. That is what "so perhaps they would be humbled" in 6:42 was pointing at. The door was not shut when the suffering arrived. What they did not do was to ask, and asking is what humility looks like when it is real.

### In short

- The question is reproach, not inquiry: nothing stopped them from humbling themselves except themselves.
- Hardened means dry, unyielding and past softness, and it followed the test rather than replacing it.
- Satan's work here is described as making a person's own deeds look fair, so he keeps on doing them.
- Conscience is dulled by repetition, and a heart that keeps explaining events away ends unable to read them.
- Had they asked when the calamity came, mercy was the answer waiting.

<<<VERSE 6:44>>>
"When they became oblivious to warnings, We showered them with everything they desired." This is the verse people fear, because it says that relief can be the beginning of the end. The gates of good things are opened, and the opening is not approval.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When they became oblivious to warnings"** means they forgot what they had been reminded of and neglected it. They ignored the admonition given through hardship, and turned their backs on what they had been commanded with in the Scripture. **"We showered them with everything they desired"** is read as: We opened the gates of all things to them. Provision, vegetation, the fertility of the land and comfort came in from whichever side they wanted, and the stated purpose is to draw them on by degrees. **"But just as they became prideful of what they were given"** catches them in the middle of their enjoyment of wealth, children and provision, rejoicing wantonly in what they had been handed. **"We seized them by surprise, then they instantly fell into despair"** is the sudden taking, and the despair is being confounded, cut off from any hope of good. We seek refuge with Allah from such an end.

### What the affluence is not

This verse warns everyone not to be deceived by affluence. Do not see wealth and comfort pouring out on a person or a group and conclude too fast that these are the people on the correct path, or that their life is a model of success. Among them there may be someone picked out to be seized with a sudden and terrible punishment, and a hadith names that state as being lured on to destruction. When you see blessings and wealth raining on a man while he is deep in sin and disobedience, know that he is being drawn on, and that his affluence is a sign he will be taken in punishment. Aḥmad records it through ʿUqbah ibn ʿĀmir.

Ibn Jarīr reports another wording of the same rule through ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit. When Allah wills that a nation survive and increase, He creates in them two qualities: moderation in everything they do, and modesty and chastity, which means abstinence from what is not right. And when He wills a nation to come to ruin, the doors of breach of faith are opened for them, so they seem successful in the world in spite of their treachery and their wrongdoing.

### Al-Ḥasan al-Basrī on the same verse

Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said that whoever is given provision and thinks Allah is not testing him with it has no wisdom. Whoever has little and thinks Allah will not provide for him has no wisdom either. He then recited this verse. He also swore by the Lord of the Kaʿbah that Allah had deceived these people: He gave them what they wanted, and then He punished them for it. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded that statement of his. On the same reading, the prosperity after poverty and the health after sickness are not a change of mind about them, and they are not a reward for their pride in it either. They are the completion of the second test, and once it is failed the case is closed.

### In short

- The verse describes abundance as a form of trial rather than as evidence of favor.
- Forgetting the warning is what precedes the opening of the gates of everything good.
- The rejoicing is cut short from the middle, which is what makes the seizure sudden.
- Affluence over an unrepentant sinner is named in a hadith as luring on to destruction, and the doors of breach of faith are its sign.
- Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī took both ends of provision as tests, and swore by the Lord of the Kaʿbah that giving people what they want can be how they are deceived.

<<<VERSE 6:45>>>
"So the wrongdoers were utterly uprooted." The sentence closes the account of the nations that were tried twice and failed twice. Nothing of them is left standing, and the verse finishes with praise.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So the wrongdoers were utterly uprooted"** means that the roots of the people who did wrong were cut off, the last remnant of them exterminated through punishment when the help of Allah came to His messengers. Those who ascribed partners to Allah were wiped out, and the phrase for uprooting leaves no survivor behind to remember them. **"And all praise is for Allah—Lord of all worlds"** is said in gratitude for that. Say: praise and thanks be to Allah, for giving victory to the messengers and destroying the disbelievers, for annihilating the enemies and helping the friends. It is praise because He is the Lord of all that exists.

### Why praise closes the passage

One note on this verse brings out the turn. When mass punishment came, the wrongdoers were uprooted to the last man, and immediately after that comes: praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds. The hint is that whenever the wrongdoers and the unjust are visited by punishment or calamity, that is a blessing for the whole world, and a blessing the people should be grateful for. The passage that began by comforting a grieving prophet ends by telling him that the outcome is settled and that the right to have one's greatness praised in this world belongs to one Being alone. A person who ignores divine truth is disrespecting the one whose world this is, and setting up his own greatness under a sky already filled with the majesty of Allah.

### In short

- Uprooting means the last remnant of a wrongdoing people being cut off, and it came as help to the messengers.
- The praise at the end is gratitude for victory given and for enemies removed.
- Allah is named Lord of all worlds, which is why the outcome belongs to Him and not to the strongest party.
- The destruction of the unjust is presented as a mercy to the rest of the world, and so as a thing to be thankful for.
