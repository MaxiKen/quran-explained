<<<VERSE 6:7>>>
This verse takes the demand for a sign and pushes it to the length it would have to go to. "Had We sent down to you ˹O Prophet˺ a revelation in writing and they were to touch it with their own hands, the disbelievers would still have said, “This is nothing but pure magic!”" A book from the sky is not enough. It has to be written down, they have to hold it, and even then it will be called sorcery. The verse is not describing a lack of evidence. It is describing malice, and it says that the people making the demand would not stop at anything they were given, because they were never going to follow it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Had We sent down to you a revelation in writing"** means a Scripture inscribed on parchment, brought down in one piece. On the plainest sense, this is the Qur'an itself coming down as a finished written document, which is what ʿAbdullāh ibn Umayyah al-Makhzūmī and his friends had asked for, with Gabriel bringing it down in a single bulk.

**"and they were to touch it with their own hands"** is stronger than merely seeing it, because touching removes doubt more effectively than looking. They would hold it and read it, and be eye-witnesses of its coming down, with no room to claim sleight of hand, a trick of the eye, or enchantment.

**"the disbelievers would still have said"** identifies them here as ʿAbdullāh ibn Umayyah al-Makhzūmī and the people of Makkah. **"This is nothing but pure magic"** is what they would say, and the comment on it is blunt: a clear lie. Their answer to the evidence would be stubbornness and denial. They would say: what you have brought is obvious sorcery, and we will never believe in it.

### The man who made the demand

The verse came down against a particular scene. ʿAbdullāh ibn Abī Umaiyyah put a hostile condition in front of the Prophet ﷺ. He said he could not bring himself to believe until he saw the man climb all the way into the heavens and come back down with a book brought from there, in front of their eyes, with his own name in it, ordering him to testify to his prophethood. And after asking all that he added that even this would not impress him, since he was not a likely convert in any case. Fate has strange turns: that same man later accepted Islam, and accepted it with such seriousness and quality that he became a victorious fighter in its cause, and he was given his wish in another way, receiving martyrdom, shahādah, at the battle of Ṭāʾif.

Al-Kalbī gives the demand another shape. The idolaters of Makkah said: by Allah, we will not believe, O Muhammad, until you bring us a book from Allah, with four angels alongside it, their testimony confirming that it comes from Him and that you are His Messenger. Verses of this kind were revealed in answer.

The same stubbornness appears elsewhere in the Qur'an, where it is described in the same impossible terms. If a gate from the heaven were opened for them and they kept climbing up through it, they would say their eyes had been dazzled and that they were a people bewitched (15:14-15). And if they saw a piece of the heaven fall down, they would say it was only clouds piled up together (52:44).

### What the consolation is for

These demands and the mocking, insulting talk that came with them were aimed at a man who was gentler toward his own people than their own parents ever were to them. What such coarse manners do to a heart like that is beyond measuring. It can only be sensed by someone consumed with the good of his people the way this Prophet ﷺ was. So Allah comforts him, by showing that the demands were not put forward to be answered. The people making them were not the kind who follow a sign when it arrives. The saying comes from malice and hostility, not from an open question, and that is why no amount of evidence changes it.

There is also a general rule in this verse about how a person goes astray. In this world he has complete freedom to reject the Truth, and he even gets the chance to build fine justifications for wrong thoughts, words and deeds. If the one who brings God's word looks like an ordinary man, he is explained away as a born leader whose grip on a crowd has nothing to do with righteousness or with truth. If a complete book came down from the sky, the reason given is that it is mere magic. People devoted to externals cannot recognise the Messenger of God from the weight of his message. He appears to them as a man who suddenly emerged and started claiming very high standing, they assess him on appearances, find him small there, and deny him.

### In short

- A written book from heaven, handled and read, would not have moved them, which is the verse's proof that the problem is disposition and not evidence.
- Touching was set above seeing because contact is the stronger remover of doubt.
- The demand is tied to ʿAbdullāh ibn Umaiyyah al-Makhzūmī, who later became a fervent Muslim and was martyred at Ṭāʾif.
- Al-Kalbī reports the demand in a form that asks for a book escorted by four angels testifying to it.
- The verse consoles the Prophet ﷺ: such demands are not asked in order to be met.

<<<VERSE 6:8>>>
""They say, “Why has no ˹visible˺ angel come with him?” Had We sent down an angel, the matter would have certainly been settled ˹at once˺, and they would have never been given more time ˹to repent˺"." The demand now is for a second speaker, an angel to back the Prophet ﷺ up. The answer is that an angel does not come down to argue. An angel comes down to end the argument, and ending it that way is the destruction of the people who asked.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They say"** is again ʿAbdullāh ibn Abī Umaiyyah al-Makhzūmī, and this verse also came down out of an episode involving him. He came to the Prophet ﷺ in the company of an-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith and Nawfal ibn Khālid, and their condition was belief only on condition that he go and bring a book from the heavens, with four angels as its escort, its witnesses attesting that Allah alone sent it and that Muhammad is His Messenger.

**"Why has no angel come with him?"** means: send someone down to confirm his truthfulness. ʿAbdullāh ibn Umayyah is the one named as asking for this, and what he wants is an angel who will testify for what the Prophet ﷺ is saying, speaking to them and bear witness that he is a messenger, conveying the message with a warning alongside.

**"Had We sent down an angel, the matter would have certainly been settled"** means the judgement would be executed. The angel comes down to take their souls and to punish them, and it is also said that if an angel came down he would destroy the whole of them. **"and they would have never been given more time"** means no delay, no further respite for repentance, and no chance to offer an excuse.

### The law behind the answer

The divine rule runs like this: when a people ask their prophet to produce a miracle of their own choosing, and the miracle is then worked by Allah's will and power, and they still hold back from faith, they are destroyed by a general punishment that takes all of them. These people of Makkah were not asking with good intentions, which would at least have left some hope that they might accept. So the fact that the demanded miracle is not produced is real good fortune for them. Without the delay they would be nowhere.

This is Allah's way with those who came before: a nation was destroyed when it disbelieved after its request had been granted. The Qur'an states the rule in other places, in the sense given here. "We send not the angels down except with the truth, and in that case they would have no respite" (15:8). And on the day they see the angels, there will be no good news at all that day for the criminals (25:22).

### In short

- The demand for an angel is a demand for a witness who testifies rather than argues.
- An angel sent down to settle a claim ends the case at once, and ending it that way means punishment.
- Not producing the requested sign is mercy: it keeps the respite open, and the respite is the only room in which faith is possible.
- The answer appeals to a known pattern in earlier nations, and to verses that state it, 15:8 and 25:22.

<<<VERSE 6:9>>>
"And if We had sent an angel, We would have certainly made it ˹assume the form of˺ a man—leaving them more confused than they already are." The verse closes the loophole. Their complaint was that a human messenger carries no weight. If the messenger were an angel, he would have to appear as a man to be dealt with at all, and the same objection would be back on the table, with the added confusion of a being that is not a man and looks like one.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And if We had sent an angel"** covers two cases: an angel sent along with the human Messenger, and an angel sent as a messenger to mankind in his place.

**"We would have certainly made it a man"** means He would have sent him in the shape of a human being, so that people would be able to look at him, speak with him and take teaching from him. Ibn ʿAbbās, as ad-Daḥḥāk reported from him, said that if an angel were sent to them he would come in the form of a man, because they would not be able to bear the sight of an angel because of the light. No human being is capable of seeing an angel as he is.

**"leaving them more confused than they already are"** means confusing them over the confusion they already have. Al-Walībī transmitted from Ibn ʿAbbās the shorter form of it: We would throw doubts back on them. They are already confusing the matter for themselves when they say of a man of flesh and blood that he is nothing but a mortal like the rest of you. Making the sender a man in outward form would obscure his real nature for them, exactly as they now obscure the qualities and the description of Muhammad.

### Why a human messenger, then

An angel can come down in one of two ways. He comes as he actually is, in his own form and state of being, and then no human being can stand before that real awesomeness. The likelier result is death at a sight never imagined. Or he comes in human shape and form, which is how Jibrīl al-Amīn, Gabriel the Trusty, came to the Holy Prophet ﷺ more than once in the shape of a man. In that second case the objector has the very same objection available against the angel as he has against you, because he will take him to be nothing but human. Asking for an angel is therefore a demand that cancels itself.

Sending every kind of creation a messenger from among their own kind is counted here as a mercy from Allah to His creation. A messenger of their own sort can call his people to Allah, and they can talk to him, ask him questions and benefit from him. That favour is stated elsewhere in the Qur'an: "Allah conferred a great favour on the believers when He sent among them a Messenger from among themselves, reciting His verses and purifying them" (3:164). And the rule behind it is stated as an answer to the demand: if there were angels on the earth, walking about in peace and security, We would have sent down to them from the heaven an angel as a Messenger (17:95).

### In short

- An angel sent to people would have to be a man in form, or they could neither look at him nor learn from him.
- Their objection would then repeat itself, and their confusion would be doubled rather than solved.
- The sight of an angel as he really is is beyond human capacity, and the likelier answer to it is death rather than belief.
- The demand is thus self-defeating, and a human messenger from among their own kind is the mercy that makes teaching possible.

<<<VERSE 6:10>>>
"˹Other˺ messengers had already been ridiculed before you ˹O Prophet˺, but those who mocked them were overtaken by what they used to ridicule." After three verses of argument about signs, this verse turns to the Prophet ﷺ himself. It is a comfort, and it comes with a warning attached for the people mocking him. He is not the first to be laughed at, and the laughers have a record: what they mocked arrived and closed around them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Messengers had already been ridiculed before you"** means their own people ridiculed them, as this people is ridiculing you now. The denial of him by his people is set in a line of denials that went before.

**"but those who mocked them were overtaken"** means the punishment they denied and laughed at when it was announced to them surrounded them and befell them. It is said of those of them who mocked, meaning the disbelievers, that the consequence of their mockery is what reached them.

### Why the task is hard

Conveying the Word of God in this world is a complicated business, and the reason given is that Allah has decreed that the Truth be wrapped in doubt, so that a person finds reasons for rejecting it next to arguments for accepting it. The real test is whether he can tear that veil of doubt down and lift himself to certainty: abandoning doubt in every disguise, and holding firmly to the truth that comes from above. To believe without seeing is what the test is. Once reality is put in front of the eyes, agreeing with it is worth nothing.

The same page explains why mockery keeps happening. Man denies an obvious truth, and when he gets power he puts others down, and one person makes another the victim of his tyranny. It is fair to ask whether that means he has absolute power here, and whether there is nobody to stop his hand. Is there a contradiction in Allah's rule, since He seems to have filled the human world with oppression and injustice and the rest of creation with mercy and meaning? The answer is no. The Lord of heaven and earth is also the Lord of the creature that works by day and rests by night. As His grace is given to the universe without stint, so it is given to human beings. The difference is one of timing. For everything else that grace is visible in full from the first day, and for people it will be shown in full on the Day of Judgement.

### The other half of the comfort

The verse does more than say that others suffered the same. It promises the Messenger ﷺ and the believers with him Allah's victory and a good ending, in this life and in the Hereafter. Its point is that his duty is to convey Allah's commands. Once he is satisfied that he has conveyed them, his heart can rest. Whether the message moves anyone or not is not part of his job as Allah's Messenger, and that worry should not be allowed into his heart. The earlier prophets faced the same heart-breaking treatment from their people and did not give up hope.

### In short

- Ridicule is not evidence against the message. It is the standard treatment of messengers, and the verse names it as such.
- What the mockers denied came and encompassed them, which is the warning inside the comfort.
- The Truth is deliberately veiled in doubt, so that certainty has to be earned by believing without seeing.
- The Prophet's ﷺ duty ends with delivery, and the outcome is not his to carry.

<<<VERSE 6:11>>>
"Say, “Travel throughout the land and see the fate of the deniers.”" The passage that began with a demand for a miracle ends by sending the demanders on a journey. No sign needs to be produced for them. They are told to look at what is already there, in the landscape, and to notice where denial ended.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say"** addresses the Prophet ﷺ, and what he is told to say is an instruction to the people of Makkah.

**"Travel throughout the land"** means travel on earth, in the trading routes and the places where earlier nations lived. **"and see the fate of the deniers"** means: reflect on what the end was for those who denied Allah and His messengers, how they were destroyed by chastisement. They were in a position of strength and power when Allah's punishment fell on them.

### Looking instead of asking

They had demanded a written book and an escort of angels. They are sent instead to think about their own case, and to consider the afflictions Allah struck the earlier nations with for defying and denying His messengers. Torment, affliction and punishment came to those nations in this life, and the painful torment of the Hereafter is with them, while Allah saved His Messengers and the servants who believed. The hope in the command is small but real: perhaps they will take heed. Travel is here an instrument of thought, and the verse treats the ruins and the reports of the past as evidence already in circulation, which needs only to be read honestly.

### In short

- The command to travel converts the demand for a new sign into a duty to inspect an old one.
- The fate in view is not only destruction in this world. It includes the torment of the next, and the salvation of the messengers and the believers.
- Strength and power were no protection for the nations destroyed, which is the exact contrast the people of Makkah needed.
- Reflection is offered as the last courtesy before the judgement the previous verses described.
