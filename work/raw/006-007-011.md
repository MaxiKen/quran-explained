<<<VERSE 6:7>>>
The verse answers a stubborn demand. "Had We sent down to you, O Prophet, a revelation in writing and they were to touch it with their own hands, the disbelievers would still have said, 'This is nothing but pure magic!'" The point is that the disbelievers' rejection is not a matter of evidence. Even if they saw the revelation descend and touched it with their own hands, they would still call it magic. Their words come from malice and hostility, not from a lack of proof.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Had We sent down to you a revelation in writing"** refers to a book sent down from heaven, as 'Abdullah ibn Umayyah al-Makhzumi and his friends demanded. Al-Kalbi said that the idolaters of Makkah said something. O Muhammad, by Allah we will not believe until you bring us a book from Allah. It must come along with four angels with it. Al-Walibi reported Ibn 'Abbas saying: We brought doubts around them. Strange are the ways of fate, for this very gentleman embraced Islam later on and received his shahadah (martyrdom) in the Battle of Ta'if. Gabriel (Jibril) al-Amin came to the Prophet ﷺ in the shape of a human being. They asked for a book from Allah along with four angels bearing witness that it was from Allah and that Muhammad ﷺ was His messenger. Even if such a book were sent, they would not accept it.

**"and they were to touch it with their own hands"** means they could see it for themselves and even physically touch it, so that they would know they were not day-dreaming and that it was a reality. Touch is more effective than sight in removing doubt. Yet even this would not move them.

**"the disbelievers would still have said, 'This is nothing but pure magic!'"** is the charge. They would still say it is nothing but obvious magic, a clear lie. This is because what they say comes from malice and hostility. They are a kind of people who would not stop at what they demanded. Even if they were presented with far clearer proofs of their Prophet's veracity, they would still not believe.

### Why the disbelievers reject even clear proof

The reason a man goes astray in this world is that here he has complete freedom. He can reject the truth. He even has the opportunity to give fine justifications. These are for erroneous thoughts, words, and deeds. Man enjoys full freedom in this world of trial. He can accept the truth. He can reject the truth. He does as he pleases. Should the preacher of God's word appear in the shape of an ordinary man, the reason given for ignoring him could be this. So be it. His effectiveness hinges on a talent for leadership. It has nothing to do with truth. It has nothing to do with righteousness. Similarly, if a book complete in every detail comes down from the sky, he will say, "This is mere magic." He says this to justify his rejection of it. The people of Makkah used to ask for such proofs. But they were not making their demands out of any good intention. They were not likely to accept the truth even if their demands were met.

This is similar to Allah's description of the disbelievers' defiance of facts and truth: "And even if We opened to them a gate from the heaven and they were to continue ascending thereto. They would surely say: Our eyes have been as if dazzled. Nay, we are a people bewitched" (15:14-15), and "And if they were to see a piece of the heaven falling down, they would say, Clouds gathered in heaps!" (52:44).

### Comfort for the Prophet

The verse comforts the Prophet ﷺ concerning the denial of him by his people. The demands of his antagonists were not put forward for any useful purpose, nor did they want to follow the truth. They were a kind of people who would not stop at what they demanded. So let this concern bring no sorrow to his heart. His task is to convey the commandments of Allah. When he feels he has done that, let his heart be at rest. Whether someone is affected by the message or not is not for him to worry about. It is not a part of his duty as a Messenger of Allah.

### In short

- Even a written revelation from heaven, touched by their own hands, would not move the disbelievers.
- Their rejection comes from malice and hostility, not from a lack of evidence.
- A man in this world of trial has freedom to reject the truth and to justify his rejection.
- The verse comforts the Prophet ﷺ: his duty is to convey, not to force acceptance.

<<<VERSE 6:8>>>
The verse answers another demand. "They say, 'Why has no visible angel come with him?' Had We sent down an angel, the matter would have certainly been settled at once, and they would have never been given more time to repent." The disbelievers asked for an angel to testify that the book was from Allah and that Muhammad ﷺ was His messenger. The answer is that if such an angel were sent, the matter would be judged at once, and no respite would be granted to them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They say, 'Why has no visible angel come with him?'"** refers to the demand of 'Abdullah ibn Abi Umayyah, accompanied by Nadr ibn al-Harith and Nawfal ibn Khalid. Ibn Umayyah al-Makhzumi was the one who demanded a book from heaven. Abi Umaiyyah came with Nadr ibn al-Harith. Al-Walibi reported Ibn 'Abbas saying: We brought doubts around them. They said they would believe only if Allah sent down an angel with Muhammad ﷺ who would speak to them and bear witness that he was a messenger.

**"Had We sent down an angel, the matter would have certainly been settled at once"** means that if We sent the angel they demanded, then these people, who are not the kind who would accept truth, would still act contrary and hostile even after having seen the miracle. Then the Divine command to have them destroyed would stand activated. The angel would come down to punish them and take away their souls.

**"and they would have never been given more time to repent"** means they would be given no respite after that happens. They would not be allowed any delay. The fact of not showing the miracle demanded by them spells out real good for them, without which they would be nowhere. This is because the Divine law is this. Should a people ask their prophet to show a miracle of their choice, and as a result of their demand such a miracle is shown by the power and will of Allah, then, should they remain reluctant even then to believe and accept faith any longer, they are destroyed. They are destroyed by a mass general punishment.

### The mercy in withholding the miracle

These particular people of Makkah were really not making their demands as based on any good intentions, which may have at least given some hope of their accepting the truth. Therefore, the fact that the miracle was withheld is a mercy for them. We send not the angels down except with the truth, and in that case they would have no respite (15:8). On the Day they will see the angels, no glad tidings will there be for the criminals that day (25:22).

### In short

- The disbelievers demanded an angel to testify for the Prophet ﷺ.
- If an angel were sent, the matter would be judged at once and they would be destroyed.
- Withholding the miracle is a mercy, since they would not accept the truth anyway.
- The Divine law destroys those who demand a miracle and then still refuse to believe.

<<<VERSE 6:9>>>
The verse gives a second answer. "And if We had sent an angel, We would have certainly made it assume the form of a man, leaving them more confused than they already are." People asking such questions are strange. There are only two ways an angel can come down. If an angel comes as he is, in his form and state of being, no human being can stand before his real awe. The sight is too much. In fact, he is more likely to die. He would die at the never imagined sight. The second form in which the angel could come down is different. is that the angel comes in human shape and form, as Jibril (Gabriel) al-Amin came to the Prophet ﷺ several times in the shape of a human being. In this situation too, the questioner will have the same objection against this angel as he has against the Prophet ﷺ, because he would take him to be nothing but human.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And if We had sent an angel"** means if We appointed an angel as Our messenger. Al-Walibi reported Ibn 'Abbas saying: We brought doubts around them. Abi Umaiyyah demanded a book from heaven.

**"We would have certainly made it assume the form of a man"** means We would have made him in the form of a man. So that they could listen and learn from him. They would not be able to learn from an angel in the original form that Allah created him. They would be able to look at him. They would benefit from his teachings.

**"leaving them more confused than they already are"** means We would have confused them over their confusion. They would be confused about him, just as they are confused about the Prophet ﷺ. They would say, "This is but a mere mortal like the rest of you." Say: "If there were on the earth, angels walking about in peace and security, We should certainly have sent down for them from the heaven an angel as a Messenger" (17:95).

### The mercy of human messengers

It is a mercy from Allah to His creation. He sends every type of creation. Messengers come from among their kind. So that they are able to call their people to Allah. And their people are able to talk to them. They can ask them. They can benefit from them. Indeed Allah conferred a great favor on the believers. He sent among them a Messenger from among themselves. He recited unto them His verses. He purified them (3:164). Ad-Dahhak said that Ibn 'Abbas said about this verse: "If an angel was sent to them, he would come in the shape of a man. This is because they will not be able to look at the angel due to light."

### In short

- An angel sent down would have to appear as a man, or the people could not bear it.
- Either way, the disbelievers would still object and be confused.
- Sending human messengers is a mercy, so people can talk to them and benefit.
- The verse removes the disbelievers' excuse for rejecting the Prophet ﷺ.

<<<VERSE 6:10>>>
The verse consoles the Prophet ﷺ. "Other messengers had already been ridiculed before you, O Prophet, but those who mocked them were overtaken by what they used to ridicule." This attitude of mockery and injury directed against him by his people is not something particular to him. It happened to others too. Other prophets and messengers before him had to face similar heart-breaking experiences. Yet they did not lose hope. Finally, what happened was that those who used to mock were overtaken. They were overtaken by the punishment which they mocked at.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Other messengers had already been ridiculed before you"** means that communities in the past also poked fun at their messengers. Al-Walibi reported Ibn 'Abbas saying: We brought doubts around them. Abi Umaiyyah demanded a book from heaven. Messengers of Allah have been derided before you, as your people are deriding you now. This is meant as a consolation for the Prophet ﷺ.

**"but those who mocked them were overtaken by what they used to ridicule"** means that the punishment, which they used to deny and mock when they were warned about it, surrounded them. Those who scoffed at them were encompassed by that which they mocked. Those who mock you will be encompassed likewise.

### In short

- Mockery of messengers is not new. It happened to prophets before.
- Those who mocked were overtaken by the very punishment they ridiculed.
- The verse comforts the Prophet ﷺ and promises a good end for the believers.

<<<VERSE 6:11>>>
The verse closes the passage with a command. "Say, 'Travel throughout the land and see the fate of the deniers.'" The Prophet ﷺ is told to say to these people who deny and mock him: travel on earth. Then think about what happened. Think about those who disbelieved in Allah's messengers. Allah's punishment fell upon them after they were in a position of strength and power. Contemplate about yourselves. Think about the afflictions Allah struck the previous nations with. Those nations defied His Messengers. They denied them. Allah sent torment, afflictions, and punishment on them in this life. He sent the painful torment in the Hereafter. He saved His Messengers and believing servants.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say"** is the command to the Prophet ﷺ to address the disbelievers. Al-Walibi reported Ibn 'Abbas saying: We brought doubts around them. Abi Umaiyyah demanded a book from heaven. The people of Makkah were the ones addressed.

**"Travel throughout the land"** means go and see, travel in the land and reflect.

**"and see the fate of the deniers"** means see the nature of the consequence for the deniers of the messengers, how they were destroyed through chastisement. Perhaps they will take heed.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to tell the disbelievers to travel and see.
- The fate of past deniers is a warning for the present.
- Allah saved His messengers and believers while punishing the deniers.
