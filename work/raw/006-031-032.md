<<<VERSE 6:31>>>
"Losers indeed are those who deny the meeting with Allah until the Hour takes them by surprise." The verse names the loss before it describes the scene, and the scene is the moment the loss is discovered. These are people who denied the encounter with Allah through resurrection, who thought it farfetched, and who went on living as if nothing were owed. Then the Hour arrives without notice, and the death that begins it is part of what they refused to expect.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Losers indeed are those who deny the meeting with Allah"** speaks of a loss that is total, not a bad bargain. The denial is not of a story but of an encounter, their own meeting with Allah after death. **"until the Hour takes them by surprise"** marks the end of the delay they counted on, and the wording is there to expose how far their claim reached: nothing announces the Hour to them first. **"then they will cry, 'Woe to us for having ignored this'"** is regret in its purest form. The cry is an appeal to grief itself, as if to say: now is the time for you, grief, come out. What they mourn is not a sin they committed but an attention they never gave, faith and repentance left undone in the world. **"They will bear ˹the burden of˺ their sins on their backs"** is their load, and **"Evil indeed is their burden"** is the verdict on it.

### What the load looks like

The phrase in verse 31 about carrying their burdens is explained by narrations about the Day of Qiyamah, in which the deeds of good people become the mount they ride and the evil deeds of evil people are laid on them like a weight. A saying preserved from As-Suddi tells what happens at the threshold of the grave. Every unjust person is met, on entering it, by a man with an ugly face, dark skin, a foul smell and dirty clothes, who goes in with him. The unjust man asks how ugly that face is, and the answer comes: your own work was as ugly as this. He asks about the stench, and is told his work stunk. He asks about the filthy clothes, and is told his work was filthy. Then he asks who the stranger is, and the reply is: I am your deeds. That companion stays with him in the grave. When he is raised on the Day of Resurrection, the companion says: in the world I carried you, because you followed desire and lust, and today you carry me. So he climbs onto the unjust man's back and rides him until they both enter the Fire. This is how the burdens are borne, and this is why they are called evil. On the Day itself they come to them in the vilest forms and with the most putrid smells, and they are made to ride them.

### Why belief has to be about the unseen

These people will try anything on that Day to save themselves. They are nervous and confused. Sometimes they take refuge in a false oath, sometimes they beg to be returned to the world they had. What none of them ever says is: now we have believed, and now we will do nothing but good. The reason is that the hereafter is not the place of deeds. Once a person has seen, he is only agreeing with what is in front of him. That is not what a declaration of belief in Allah and His Messenger was ever meant to be. The hereafter is not the Dar al-Amal, the place where deeds are done. Sound iman depends on being about the ghayb, the unseen, with no need to see. And the other side of that is the terrible usefulness of this present life. Working for the pleasure of Allah, earning the promised everlasting bliss, hoping for Paradise and for a quiet good life right now: all of it can happen only in this world, neither before it nor after it. It cannot be done in the universe of spirits beforehand, and it cannot be done once the world of our experience is gone. That is why this life is a great blessing and an asset nothing else matches, and why harming oneself is forbidden, and praying for death, and even wishing for it, since all of that is ingratitude for a blessing.

### In short

- The verse states the loss first, then the surprise: people who denied the meeting with Allah are overtaken by the Hour.
- Their cry is regret for neglect, an appeal to grief itself, and a wish for the faith and repentance they left undone.
- The burden on their backs is their own deeds, described as a companion at the grave who turns the riding around on the Day of Rising.
- Acceptance after seeing does not count, because faith is meant to be about the unseen.
- This world is the only place where any of it can be earned, which is why it is a blessing and not to be thrown away.

<<<VERSE 6:32>>>
"This worldly life is no more than play and amusement, but far better is the ˹eternal˺ Home of the Hereafter for those mindful ˹of Allah˺." The two halves are set side by side on purpose. On one side is what is passing, on the other what is better. The choice between them is made with the mind.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This worldly life is no more than play and amusement"** is about the part of a life spent being heedless of Allah. The sense is that most of it is that, a pastime and a worthless activity. **"but far better is the ˹eternal˺ Home of the Hereafter"** means Paradise. It is better for the people who keep their duty, who ward off disbelief, association and indecency. **"for those mindful ˹of Allah˺"** ties the better home to taqwa. Taqwa is a life built on the assumption that the world belongs to Allah. **"Will you not then understand"** is the question that closes the verse. One reading takes it as addressed to the idolaters: do you not understand this, so that you believe and do good actions.

### What is actually censured

The condemnation of worldly life here, in other verses and in many hadiths, is not a condemnation of wealth or of the world itself. It is aimed at the time of life spent heedless of Allah. Set against that, nothing in this world is worth what one hour of dhikr is worth, of remembering Him and doing what He commanded. A hadith puts the same line very sharply: the world and everything in it lies under a ban, except the remembrance of Allah, a knowledgeable person and a student. The teacher of religion and the student of religion belong under that exception. The knowledge in question is knowledge that earns the pleasure of Allah. Imam al-Jazri drew the circle wider still. Whatever is done in this world in obedience to Allah, inside the limits of the Shari'ah, counts as remembrance of Him. On that reading, everything that has to be done to hold a life together is included, and so is every permissible way of earning a living. The rights of family, relatives, neighbours, friends and guests have been named as sadaqah, and as worship.

So the dearest thing a person owns is his time. That time is limited, and nobody knows how long it is. It may be seventy years. It may be seventy hours. It may be no more than the span of one breath. Everything in this world that is worth Allah's favour comes down to obedience and remembrance. A hadith of the Prophet ﷺ gives the arithmetic. The wise man is the one who takes hold of himself, is content with little, and sends his deeds ahead to what comes after death.

### A question with two readings

The closing question begins with a-fa-lā, and the verb qilūna at its end is read two ways, and both readings are kept. With a ta it is put to the listeners, with a ya it asks about them. A second point of the same kind sits in the middle of the verse, where the Home of the Hereafter can be read as one phrase or split into two. Either way the question does the same work. Do you not understand that this world is passing and the hereafter lasting, and will you not believe?

### In short

- The verse weighs a passing life of heedlessness against a lasting home, and calls the second one better.
- Play and amusement means the time spent careless of Allah, not the world as such.
- Whatever is done in obedience to Allah inside the limits of the Shari'ah falls under remembrance. The rights of family and neighbours fall under worship.
- Life is short and its length is unknown, which is why the hadith names self-control, contentment and deeds sent ahead as wisdom.
- The closing question is left open on purpose, and its two readings make the address either about them or to you.
