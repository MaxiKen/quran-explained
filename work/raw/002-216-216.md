<<<VERSE 2:216>>>
After spending comes the giving of life itself. This verse establishes the obligation of jihād even though some temperaments find it burdensome, on the principle that Allah knows the reality of everything while man does not. "Fighting has been made obligatory upon you ˹believers˺, though you dislike it. Perhaps you dislike something which is good for you and like something which is bad for you. Allah knows and you do not know".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Fighting has been made obligatory upon you ˹believers˺,"**. Allah made it obligatory for the Muslims to fight in jihād against the evil of enemies who transgress against Islam, fighting the disbelievers. One early report says the verse was revealed about Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ, al-Miqdād ibn al-Aswad and their associates, and refers to the general call to arms with the Prophet ﷺ, and that the following verse, 217, came down about ʿAbdullāh ibn Jaḥsh and his companions who killed ʿAmr ibn al-Ḥaḍramī on what they reckoned the last afternoon of Jumādā al-Ukhirah, the thirtieth, before the moon of Rajab was seen, when the disbelievers of Quraysh censured them for fighting in a sacred month. Al-Zuhrī said that jihād is required of every person, whether he joins the fighting or stays behind: whoever stays behind must give support if support is warranted, provide aid if aid is needed, and march forth if commanded, and if he is not needed he remains behind. The Ṣaḥīḥ records that whoever dies without having fought or sincerely intended to fight dies a death of the Jāhiliyyah, and on the day of al-Fatḥ, the conquest of Makkah, the Prophet ﷺ said that there is no hijrah from Makkah to al-Madīnah after the conquest, only jihād and intention, and that when called upon to march forth one should march forth. The words seem at first to make jihād obligatory on every Muslim in every condition, but other verses and the sayings of the Prophet ﷺ make clear that the obligation is not absolute. It is not farḍ ʿayn, binding on each individual, but farḍ ʿalā al-kifāyah, so that if a group of Muslims comes forward to discharge it the rest are absolved, though if at any time or in any country no group at all is ready to discharge it, all the Muslims fall into the sin of abandoning an obligation. The hadith that there must remain until the Day of Judgment a group of Muslims who keep discharging jihād points the same way. Allah says, "Allah has elevated in rank those who strive with their wealth and their lives above those who stay behind ˹with valid excuses˺. Allah has promised each a fine reward" (4:95), extending the promise of good to those unable to take part because of a compelling excuse or because of engagement in another religious service, a promise that would never have been made to those absent from jihād if it were an absolute duty on every individual. Likewise, "Only a party from each group should march forth, leaving the rest to gain religious knowledge" (9:122) proposes a division of labour in which some fight and others serve religious education, which is possible only if jihād is farḍ kifāyah. Al-Bukhārī and Muslim record that a man sought the Prophet's ﷺ permission to join the jihād, and when asked whether his parents were alive and answering yes, was told to go and serve them and earn the reward of jihād in that way, which also shows that it is farḍ kifāyah. While a group is staunchly discharging the duty, the remaining Muslims may engage in other services. But when the Imām, the leader of the Muslims, issues a general call under the pressure of need and summons everyone, jihād becomes an absolute obligation on all, as in the verse of Sūrat al-Tawbah, "What is the matter with you that when you are asked to march forth in the cause of Allah, you cling firmly to ˹your˺ land?" (9:38). Similarly, if disbelievers invade an Islamic country and the group engaged in its defence is too weak or too few, the obligation spreads from them to all the Muslims nearby, and if these too are incapable, to those beyond them, until it may become binding on every individual Muslim in the world. On the strength of these verses the majority of jurists and scholars of hadith have laid down that under normal conditions jihād is farḍ ʿalā al-kifāyah. From this follow two rulings: so long as jihād remains farḍ kifāyah, children may not go without their parents' permission, and a debtor may not take part until he has cleared his debt. But when a general call or an aggressive encirclement by disbelievers makes jihād farḍ ʿayn, no such condition remains operative, neither the permission of parents nor of a husband nor of a creditor.

**"though you dislike it."**. Fighting is difficult and heavy on the heart, hateful by nature because of the hardship involved, for it brings death and wounds, struggle against enemies and the rigours of travel, and risks life and wealth. The closing part of the verse persuades by acknowledging that jihād may seem hard while reminding us that human intelligence and effort so often fail when it comes to outcomes. In the devotional reading, engagement in fighting is hard for the lower selves, whose comforts are deferred because they follow the rule of discipline, whereas the comforts of the hearts are immediate because they follow the rule of drawing near. Happiness lies in opposing the lower selves, since whoever conforms to them abandons the exemplary path, and in conforming to the hearts, since whoever opposes them strays from the highest way, and it is better to accept the good news of the Real's guarantee of ease than the anxious thoughts of the lower self warning of difficulty and harm.

**"Perhaps you dislike something which is good for you"**. Fighting in the way of Allah is followed by victory, dominance over the enemy and possession of their lands, wealth and offspring, by booty, martyrdom and reward, by the defeat of the enemy and the raising of Allah's word. The most intelligent person may take the beneficial for harmful and the harmful for beneficial. Anyone looking back over his life will find things he pursued as beneficial that proved very harmful, and things he avoided as harmful that proved very beneficial. Since human reasoning and planning fail time and again, fighting in Allah's way may appear a loss of life and property, yet the time will come when realities are unveiled and the loss is seen to be no loss but the ultimate gain and a source of eternal peace.

**"and like something which is bad for you."**. The statement is general: one may covet what is in truth neither good nor beneficial, such as staying away from jihād, which may lead to the enemy seizing the land and the government, to subjugation, impoverishment and the loss of martyrdom, spoils and reward. The soul inclines to desires that bring about its destruction and rejects the religious duties that would bring it happiness. The same holds for all of life. People like to live free and unrestricted, but it is better for them to keep to Allah's laws. They befriend those who praise them, but would do better to cherish the critics kind enough to point out their faults. To save face before men they defy the truth, though they would do better to seek honour with Allah, who honours only those who uphold the truth. They shrink from sacrifice and struggle and prefer a religion that promises Paradise without such total involvement, but engaging in the sacrifice and struggle it entails is what benefits them in the long run. The trouble is that they are deeply enmeshed in the present life, when they would be wiser to attend to what lies beyond death.

**"Allah knows and you do not know"**. He knows better than you how things will turn out and what benefits you in this life and the Hereafter, that fighting in His way is better for you and abstaining from it worse, so obey Him and hold to His commands, striving in what He orders, in order to attain true guidance. One should not judge things good or bad by personal desire but say yes to the command of Allah and follow it consistently as the most expedient course.

### In short

- Fighting the transgressing enemy is obligatory (al-Zuhrī: everyone must support, aid or march as needed), and whoever never fights nor intends to dies a Jāhilī death, though after the conquest there is only jihād and intention, not hijrah.
- It is farḍ kifāyah, not farḍ ʿayn, as 4:95, 9:122 and the hadith of the man sent to serve his parents show, becoming binding on all only at a general call (9:38) or an invasion the defenders cannot repel.
- Under farḍ kifāyah, parental permission and payment of debts are required, but under farḍ ʿayn no such conditions apply.
- Fighting is naturally hated, yet it brings victory, booty, martyrdom and reward, while shirking it brings defeat and subjugation, for human judgment of benefit and harm fails repeatedly.
- Allah knows the outcomes we cannot see, so His command, not personal preference, is the measure, and inwardly happiness lies in opposing the lower self.
