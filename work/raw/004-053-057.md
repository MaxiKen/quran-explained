<<<VERSE 4:53>>>
The question that strips the claim: "Do they have control over shares of the kingdom? If so, they would not have given anyone so much as the speck on a date stone". The Jews projected their community as sacred, destined for deliverance by race alone, and the verse asks what, in fact, they hold.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do they have control over shares of the kingdom?"** is a statement of rebuke in the form of a question, for they have no share in the dominion whatever. What is the basis of the envy, the passage asks? If it is the belief that they are the real inheritors of the kingdom and that what the Prophet ﷺ was blessed with is theirs, the belief is wrong twice over.

**"they would not have given anyone so much as the speck on a date stone"** exposes the first error. Even if they came to hold a share of the sovereignty, they are not the kind to give anything to anybody, especially not to Muḥammad ﷺ. Not even the naqīr would leave their hands, the speck on a date-stone's back, as Ibn ʿAbbās and the majority of the scholars read the word. The like stands elsewhere in the Qur'an: "Say: if you possessed the treasure of the mercy of my Lord, you would surely withhold it out of fear of spending it" (17:100), for fear that what they have might end, though there is no such possibility. Man is ever stingy, and this only demonstrates their greedy nature.

### Envy of the chosen

The second reason comes with the next verse: the kingdom did not pass from them onto him, and the question becomes why it had to go to him at all, seeing that the House of Abraham had already held the Book and wisdom. The envy of the Jews for the Prophet ﷺ was over the great prophethood entrusted to him, and their envy made them reject him and turn others from the path, his Arabness and his non-Israelite birth being the whole of their grievance. At-Ṭabarānī recorded that Ibn ʿAbbās read "do they envy men" as their claim to be the worthy ones above all other people. Mujāhid's reading of what follows, some who believed and some who rejected, takes the object as the Prophet ﷺ himself, and the rejection of him by their disbelief is the more severe, the further from the truth they stand. The envy is unreasonable either way, and its rule is old: no one may have honour on the basis of race or community, for these depend on the divine law of justice, and one who fails by his actions will never be honoured, whatever his community.

### In short

- They hold no share of the dominion, and even if they did, their niggardliness would keep the speck of a date-stone back from everyone.
- The envy behind their rejection is of an Arab prophethood, against their claim of being the worthy people.
- Community-based salvation is an invention, and honour follows the divine law of justice, not the bloodline.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:54>>>
The ancestry they forgot: "Or do they envy the people for Allah's bounties? Indeed, We have given the descendants of Abraham the Book and wisdom, along with great authority". The kingdom they begrudge has been in the family of prophets all along.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Or do they envy the people for Allah's bounties?"** names their envy of Muḥammad ﷺ and his Companions, for the prophethood, the faith and the establishment in the land, and, in one reading, for the abundance of women given him, they wishing him deprived of such things, saying: were he truly a prophet, he would not be concerned with women.

**"We have given the descendants of Abraham the Book and wisdom"** means prophethood went to the tribes of the Children of Israel, the offspring of Ibrāhīm, with the Books sent down to them, the prophets ruling by the prophetic tradition and Al-Ḥikmah, the wisdom, given with the Scripture. The house of Abraham held the likes of Mūsā (Moses), Dāwūd (David) and Sulaymān (Solomon), and it was honoured with prophethood and al-Islām's way and sovereignty over the Children of Israel. David had 100 legitimate wives by one count, 99 by another, and Solomon 300 wives plus 700 concubines, or a thousand free women and slavegirls by the other count.

**"along with great authority"** is the mighty kingdom, and the envy of it forgets that the kingdom has not come to a stranger. He too comes from the family of prophets who ruled kingdoms before him.

### The rules of envy

ʿAllāmah an-Nawawī, the commentator on Muslim, defines envy, ḥasad, as the desire to see the other person's blessing disappear, and this is forbidden. The Prophet's ﷺ hadith on it runs:

> Do not hate and do not envy and do not turn your backs on each other. Instead, become servants of Allah, as brothers. And it is not lawful for a Muslim that he abandons his brother for more than three days.

Another hadith says: "Beware of envy, for envy eats up good deeds as the fire eats up wood." Zubayr narrates: "Stealthily creeping into you is the disease of earlier peoples, envy, and hatred, which is a shaver. I do not say that it shaves hair, but it does shave the religion off." Envy over worldly excellence or spiritual excellence is forbidden on both counts, for verse 53 pointed at the worldly and this verse's Book and wisdom point at the spiritual.

### The inner reading

The mighty kingdom is read four ways: true knowledge of the King, dominion over the lower self, standing above the secrets of the realm so that nothing is hidden, and the viewing of the secrets of creation from on high. Abū ʿUthmān al-Maghribī said that whoever responds to the Real finds the empire responding to him, so that whatever appears in it is brought to him as news. Ibn al-Barqī, one of the great shaykhs of Egypt, was once ill and refused his medicine, saying that some disaster had happened in the empire, and until he found out what, he would not drink it. Thirteen days passed before word came: the Qarmatians had struck the Sanctuary, slain many and worked great destruction. Shaykh al-Islām Anṣārī set the limit: servanthood cannot bear more than knowing some and not knowing some. God does not show His unseen to anyone except a messenger He approves (72:26-27), and He will not inform you of the unseen (3:179). God knows all, and no one else. The Pir of the Ṭarīqah said: "O God, who is like me in becoming worthy for this work? Enough for me is that I have the worth of Your companionship. Tell them to call me nothing but lord, that's a name suited for Your serving boy."

### In short

- The bounty envied is old family property: prophethood, Book and wisdom ran through the house of Abraham before it reached an Arab prophet.
- David's and Solomon's households answer the sneer at the Prophet's marriages, with the counts of the wives given as the reports carry them.
- Envy, defined by an-Nawawī as wishing the blessing away, eats good deeds as fire eats wood and shaves the religion off.
- Read inwardly, the mighty kingdom is knowledge of the King, and its news reaches those who respond to the Real, within the limits God set on the unseen.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:55>>>
The split down the family line: "Yet some believed in him while others turned away from him. Hell is sufficient as a torment". The bounty given to the house of Abraham divided its heirs as soon as it arrived.

### Phrase by phrase

**"some believed in him"** refers, in Mujāhid's reading, to Muḥammad ﷺ, and in another to the bounty itself, the prophets, the Books and the kingship, which some of the Children of Israel believed in.

**"while others turned away from him"** means disbelieving it, ignoring it, and barring the people from its path, though the bounty was from them and for them. They disputed with their own prophets, so what of Muḥammad ﷺ, who is not even of the Children of Israel? Their rejection of him is the more severe, and they are further from the truth he brought. One reading names the object of belief as the Scripture of David and Solomon, and the burned ones of the close as Kaʿb and his followers.

**"Hell is sufficient as a torment"** means the Fire is a just punishment for their disbelief, rebellion and defiance of Allah's Books and Messengers. It needs no addition.

### The old pattern

The verse is the family portrait of every prophet's people: some who believed in what was revealed to Abraham and the prophets among his descendants, and some who denied it and turned others away from it, and now do the same with what was revealed to Muḥammad ﷺ. At-Ṭabarānī's record from Ibn ʿAbbās and the whole scene of verses 53 and 54 stand behind the split, the envy an-Nawawī defined, the speck of a date stone withheld, and the Book and wisdom given to the house of Ibrāhīm, with David and Solomon its royal heirs. The believers inherited the path, and the deniers inherited the blaze, the old pattern the hadith records of every prophet's people.

### In short

- The bounty divided its heirs: some believed in the prophets and the Book, and some rejected and barred the path.
- Their own prophets were disputed with before Muḥammad ﷺ, so his rejection is the older habit renewed.
- Hell needs no helper as a torment, being a just punishment for the defiance of Books and Messengers.

<<<VERSE 4:56>>>
The anatomy of the punishment: "Surely those who reject Our signs, We will cast them into the Fire. Whenever their skin is burnt completely, We will replace it so they will ˹constantly˺ taste the punishment. Indeed, Allah is Almighty, All-Wise". After the particular believers and disbelievers, the universal rule of the two ends.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who reject Our signs"** are those who disbelieved in the verses and hindered from the path of the Messengers.

**"We will cast them into the Fire"** names the Fire of Jahannam, which will encompass every part of their bodies, and the torment in it is everlasting.

**"Whenever their skin is burnt completely, We will replace it"** means fresh skins replace the burnt ones, returning the burned to their first state, that the punishment may keep its taste and its severity. Al-Aʿmash gives Ibn ʿUmar's word: at each burning a replacement skin arrives, and it comes white as paper, in a report Ibn Abī Ḥātim collected. Ibn Abī Ḥātim also recorded from al-Ḥasan the daily count: seventy thousand roastings through. Ḥusayn, with Fudayl adding from Hishām, recorded al-Ḥasan's other reading of verse 56: once the Fire has roasted them through and eaten their flesh, the command comes to go back as they were, and they do. Muʿādh explained verse 56's replacement as coming with such speed that the skin is changed a hundred times in one moment.

**"Indeed, Allah is Almighty, All-Wise"** means nothing is beyond His power, and He is Wise in His creation and His ruling that burnt skins be exchanged for fresh ones.

### The lightest of them

The scale of the torment is set by its least. The lightest in punishment among the people of the Fire will be the man with two smouldering embers in the soles of his feet. Their heat keeps his brain boiling like a cauldron to the brim, in the report al-Mundhirī carries in his at-Targhīb. Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said the fire will eat them every day seventy thousand times, and when it has eaten them up, they will be commanded: return, and they shall return to be as they were, as al-Baihaqī records, quoted by al-Mazharī.

Read inwardly, the verse alludes to those who deny the signs of the friends of God. He keeps them in a state of insignificance and leaves them to the estrangement of denial. Whenever something of this story appears to their hearts, rejection makes them abandon faith in it and dismiss its people with contempt, and so the punishment repeats.

### In short

- The Fire encompasses every part, and the skins are renewed at each burning so the taste never dulls.
- The replacement is instantaneous by Muʿādh's account, seventy thousand times a day by al-Ḥasan's, and the new skin comes white as paper.
- The lightest punishment boils a brain with two embers in the feet, and the rest rise from there.
- Inwardly, the renewed skin is the repeated denial that punishes the denier of the friends each time the truth appears.

<<<VERSE 4:57>>>
The other end of the rule: "As for those who believe and do good, We will admit them into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there for ever and ever. There they will have pure spouses, and We will place them under a vast shade". After the burning skins, the gardens.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who believe and do good"** are the believers in Muḥammad ﷺ, in the Qur'an and in all the Scriptures and messengers, sincerely performing what lies between them and their Lord.

**"We will admit them into Gardens under which rivers flow"** describes the destination of the happy ones, the gardens of Eden beneath which rivers flow in all their areas and corners, wherever they desire: rivers of wine, milk, honey and water.

**"to stay there for ever and ever"** means they reside for eternity, not transferred or removed, nor wanting to move.

**"they will have pure spouses"** describes the purified mates: no menstruation, no postnatal bleeding, no filth, no bad manners, no shortcomings. Ibn ʿAbbās said they are purified of filth and foul things, with as-Suddī, ʿĀṭiyah, Abū Ṣāliḥ, an-Nakhaʿī, Ad-Daḥḥāk, al-Ḥasan and ʿAṭāʾ saying similarly. Mujāhid's list adds urine, spit, mucous and pregnancies to the freedoms. According to al-Ḥākim's record from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, the women of Jannah will be pure from menstruation, defecation, urination and nasal mucus, and Mujāhid added freedom from procreation and seminal impurity, the answer to verse 56's renewing burn.

**"We will place them under a vast shade"** means wide, extensive, pure and elegant shade, everlasting, never replaced by any sun. Ibn Jarīr recorded that Abū Hurayrah said that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There is a tree in Paradise, that if a rider travels under its shade for a hundred years, he will not cross it. It is the Tree of Everlasting Life." Abū Hurairah narrates the like with the invitation: read, if you wish, the verse of the shade, ever-extended, ever-stretched. Rabīʿ ibn Anās read it as the Throne's shade, al-ʿarsh, that never declines.

### The shades of this world and the next

Read inwardly, the believers are in shade already: today in the shade of God's care, riʿāya, tomorrow in the shade of His protection, ḥimāya, and His sufficiency, kifāya. They are in the shade of His solicitude, ʿināya, in this world and the next, and the people of this group are of different types: those in the shade of His mercy, those in the shade of His care, those in the shade of His special generosity, those in the shade of His solicitude, and those in the shade of His nearness.

### In short

- The gardens hold rivers of wine, milk, honey and water in every corner, and their stay has no end.
- The purified spouses are free of every impurity the material lists, from menstruation to mucous, and Mujāhid adds freedom from childbearing.
- The vast shade is the Tree of Everlasting Life, a hundred years' ride across, or the shade of the Throne that never declines.
- Inwardly the shade begins now, as care, protection, sufficiency and solicitude, ending in the shade of nearness.
