<<<VERSE 3:69>>>
The verse warns believers: “Some of the People of the Book wish to mislead you” (3:69). Their effort will not take the believers away from the truth. Instead, the harm of their attempt returns upon themselves, though they do not understand what they are doing.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Some of the People of the Book”** refers to a group among the Jews and Christians, not every individual. The verse describes people who oppose the new Muslim community and want its members to abandon their faith.

**“wish to mislead you ˹believers˺”** means they hope to turn Muslims away from Islam. One report connects the verse to Kaʿb al-Ashraf after the Battle of Uḥud. He invited three companions, Muʿādh, Ḥudhayfah al-Yamān, and ʿAmmār bin Yāsir, to leave Islam and adopt Judaism. They did not accept the invitation.

The invitation tries to use the authority of an older religious community to unsettle new believers. The People of the Book had received the Torah and Injīl, but that did not give them permission to hide the truth or demand that Muslims follow their religion. A person should judge a claim by its truth, not by the age or reputation of the group presenting it.

**“They mislead none but themselves”** means their attempt does not overturn the believers’ faith. The plot only adds to the plotters’ own error and accountability. They may hope to make others follow them, but their actions deepen their own misguidance.

**“yet they fail to perceive it”** means they do not recognize the outcome of their conduct. They think they are weakening the believers, but they are exposing their own rejection of truth. The verse gives the believers confidence that deceptive efforts cannot defeat Allah’s guidance.

### Why does their attempt mislead only themselves?

Their strategy is rooted in envy and in the wish to keep others from accepting Muhammad ﷺ. The following verses 70 and 71 explain that they reject signs they recognize and conceal truth they know. Their effort can expose their own wrongdoing, but it cannot cancel the guidance Allah has given the believers.

### In short

- Some among the People of the Book try to turn believers away from Islam.
- Their effort does not mislead the believers who hold to the truth.
- Their own rejection and deception increase their misguidance.

<<<VERSE 3:70>>>
The verse asks, “Why do you reject the signs of Allah while you bear witness” (3:70). The People of the Book recognize signs of the Prophet’s truth in their scriptures, yet still reject them. Their knowledge makes the rejection more serious.

### Phrase by phrase

**“O People of the Book!”** addresses Jews and Christians who have received earlier revelation. The statement concerns those who know what their own scriptures say about Allah’s messengers.

**“Why do you reject the signs of Allah”** means that they deny the verses and evidence Allah has given. The signs include descriptions of Muhammad ﷺ in the Torah and Injīl. One explanation says they know that these descriptions are true, yet refuse to accept the prophethood they indicate.

**“while you bear witness ˹to their truth˺”** means they acknowledge the signs or know the truth from their scriptures. They cannot reasonably claim that they have no evidence. Their testimony stands against their rejection.

One explanation stresses that disbelief, kufr, is intrinsically wrong and prohibited in every condition. The reference to knowledge and witness intensifies the rebuke. It does not mean disbelief would become permissible if a person lacked knowledge or did not openly testify.

The message also warns against treating revelation as a possession of one community. Recognition of a prophet’s signs creates a responsibility to follow the truth. It is not enough to preserve a scripture while refusing the messenger whose coming it describes.

### In short

- The People of the Book are challenged for rejecting Allah’s signs.
- They know that their scriptures contain evidence for Muhammad’s prophethood.
- Knowledge makes their deliberate rejection more blameworthy.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:71>>>
The verse asks, “Why do you mix the truth with falsehood and hide the truth knowingly” (3:71). Some among the People of the Book distort what their scriptures say and conceal the descriptions of Muhammad ﷺ, despite knowing what they are hiding.

### Phrase by phrase

**“O People of the Book!”** again addresses those who have received revelation and should recognize the signs of a true messenger.

**“Why do you mix the truth with falsehood”** means they confuse genuine statements about the Prophet with invented claims or misleading interpretations. One explanation says they distort the descriptions of Muhammad ﷺ so that people will not recognize him. The mixture makes falsehood appear to have religious authority.

**“and hide the truth knowingly”** means they conceal what they know about his prophethood. The concealment is deliberate, not an innocent misunderstanding. Another explanation says some people publicly agree with Muslims to avoid trouble while privately remaining loyal to their own false claims.

The verse condemns both distortion and silence. A person may mislead by openly changing a statement, or by withholding the truth while allowing others to be deceived. In either case, knowledge brings responsibility.

### In short

- Some People of the Book mix true revelation with false interpretation.
- They conceal signs of Muhammad’s prophethood even though they know them.
- Deliberate concealment is not the same as honest uncertainty.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:72>>>
The verse exposes a strategy of deception: “A group among the People of the Book said ˹to one another˺, ‘Believe in what has been revealed to the believers in the morning and reject it in the evening’” (3:72). They hope that a public change of position will make Muslims doubt their faith.

### Phrase by phrase

**“A group among the People of the Book said ˹to one another˺”** describes a plan discussed privately by some Jewish leaders. The verse does not accuse every person among them. It identifies a particular group that wants to manipulate how Muslims judge the new revelation.

**“Believe in what has been revealed to the believers in the morning”** means they would pretend to accept the Qur’an at the beginning of the day. One interpretation says twelve rabbis from Khaybar and Uraynah were to make the display. They would claim to have examined their scriptures and found Muhammad ﷺ genuine, so that Muslims would consider their apparent conversion powerful evidence.

Another interpretation connects the plan with the change of qiblah. The group would pray toward the Kaʿbah at the opening of the day, then reject it and return to Jerusalem at the end of the day. Their change would be presented as proof that they had discovered a flaw in the new religion. The Kaʿbah is connected with Abraham, and the change from Jerusalem was meant to provoke doubt. Kaʿb al-Ashraf is named among the leaders behind this plot. Mujāhid, Muqātil, and al-Kalbī connect the scheme to the qiblah change. Al-Suddī and al-Ḥasan mention the plan of the rabbis to feign belief and then reject it. They say the display should fool ordinary Muslims into thinking the learned had found a defect.

**“and reject it in the evening”** means that the group would reverse its public position. In the first interpretation, they pretended to believe and then announced disbelief. In the second, they followed one qiblah in the morning and another later. Either way, their aim was to make the believers think that learned people had found a reason to leave Islam.

**“so they may abandon their faith”** describes the intended result. The group hopes that Muslims will reason that people who know scripture better must have seen a defect in Islam. The plan depends on believers being impressed by an apparent religious expert rather than examining the claim itself.

This strategy fails because Allah reveals it to the Prophet and the believers. Its exposure prevents it from producing the confusion its planners expect. It also shows that a display of belief is not proof of sincerity if it is followed by a deliberate return to disbelief for the purpose of deception.

### Why use belief in the morning and rejection in the evening?

The quick reversal is meant to create doubt. The group thinks its religious learning will make Muslims trust the performance and assume the Qur’an has a defect. The verse explains the deception so that believers will not mistake a staged change of heart for evidence against their faith.

### In short

- A group plans to pretend to accept the revelation and then publicly reject it.
- Another interpretation connects the plan to switching the qiblah from the Kaʿbah back to Jerusalem.
- The aim is to make Muslims doubt, but Allah exposes the scheme.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:73>>>
The plotters also say that only those who follow their religion should be trusted. The Prophet is told to answer: “Surely, ˹the only˺ true guidance is Allah’s guidance” (3:73). Guidance and all bounty belong to Allah, who gives them to whomever He wills.

### Phrase by phrase

**“And only believe those who follow your religion”** reports their instruction to keep their knowledge within their own religious group. They do not want Muslims to learn what they know from earlier scripture or use it as evidence. They fear that if others receive similar revelation, they will be able to argue against them before Allah.

The statement reflects envy and communal pride. They assume that guidance belongs only to their group and that Allah will not give it to anyone else. Another explanation says they try to keep Muslims from learning the descriptions of Muhammad ﷺ because those descriptions could be used to establish his prophethood.

**“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ ‘Surely, ˹the only˺ true guidance is Allah’s guidance’”** commands the Prophet to state that guidance is not controlled by a community. Allah gives it as He wills. The Jews’ refusal to acknowledge a messenger does not make their religion the standard of truth. The earlier qiblah had been toward Jerusalem, but Allah directed the Muslim community toward the Kaʿbah associated with Ibrāhīm. This change was part of His guidance, not a reason to return to an earlier direction.

The verse also answers those who fear that others might be given the same scripture, wisdom, or virtue. Allah’s guidance is not a limited possession that one group can guard against others. The Muslim ummah is favored with the Qur’an and the message of Muhammad ﷺ, while earlier communities received their own revelations. The law and religious paths of earlier communities were superseded by the final Sharīʿah. The Tariqah and deeper spiritual realities are also gifts from Allah, not privileges a group can demand.

The Qur’an describes the completion of the religion as a blessing (5:3). An inward reading says the Muslim community was given a final scripture, a final prophet, Ramadan, and Friday, and that earlier laws were superseded by the final law. It calls believers to gratitude and warns them not to lean toward wrongdoing (11:113).

**“Do not believe that someone will receive ˹revealed˺ knowledge similar to yours”** conveys their fear that Allah might favor another people with revelation and knowledge. They assume that the gifts they received must remain exclusive to them. The verse rejects that assumption.

**“or argue against you before your Lord”** means they fear that knowledge will become evidence against them on the Day of Judgment. If Muslims receive clear guidance, they can challenge claims that contradict it. The correct response to this possibility is not to hide revelation, but to accept the truth.

**“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ ‘Indeed, all bounty is in the Hands of Allah’”** means every gift, including prophethood, revelation, knowledge, and faith, comes from Allah. No community owns His favor.

**“He grants it to whoever He wills”** means Allah chooses those who are fit to receive His gifts. He grants knowledge and sound understanding to some and may leave others in misguidance when they turn away. His choice is based on wisdom, not on the claims people make about their ancestry.

**“And Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing”** means Allah’s gifts are vast, and He knows who deserves them. He is not limited in what He can give and does not need permission from any community to grant revelation.

### Why does guidance not belong to one community?

The claim that only one group can receive revelation treats Allah’s bounty as if it were private property. The verse corrects that assumption. Allah’s guidance is given by His wisdom, and a community should respond to revelation with gratitude rather than envy or concealment.

### In short

- Guidance belongs to Allah, not to a single religious community.
- The People of the Book fear that others may receive knowledge and use it as evidence.
- Allah gives bounty, faith, and revelation to whomever He wills, with full knowledge.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:74>>>
The verse concludes, “He chooses whoever He wills to receive His mercy” (3:74). Allah selects whom He wills for guidance and other blessings. His bounty is vast, and no one can claim that Allah’s mercy belongs only to their group.

### Phrase by phrase

**“He chooses whoever He wills”** means Allah selects whom He wills for His gifts. He may grant some people provision, others excellent character, worship, or spiritual insight. He chooses those who receive revelation and guidance according to His wisdom.

**“to receive His mercy”** includes guidance, prophethood, faith, and the ability to recognize the truth. Allah’s mercy is not confined to a nation or lineage. A person’s efforts do not force Allah to grant a particular spiritual station. Even when people strive in worship, their final salvation depends on His mercy.

The Qur’an reminds people that if they tried to count Allah’s blessings, they could not number them (14:34). Another verse describes people who hope for Allah’s mercy and fear His punishment (17:57). Hope and fear keep a believer from despair and from becoming arrogant about their deeds.

A hadith says that no one enters Paradise by deeds alone. When the Prophet ﷺ was asked whether this applied even to him, he said that he too would need Allah’s mercy to encompass him. The report teaches that righteous actions matter, but they do not make a person independent of Allah’s grace.

**“And Allah is the Lord of infinite bounty”** means His generosity has no limit. He can grant guidance, knowledge, faith, and nearness to whom He wills. The verse answers envy by showing that Allah’s gifts are not diminished when He grants them to another person.

One inward reading describes different forms of mercy: outward provision, good character, worship, spiritual desire, and insight into the heart. Allah does not specify one group as the only recipient, so people may hope for His favor while remaining humble and fearful of falling short.

### In short

- Allah chooses whom He wills for guidance and mercy.
- His gifts include provision, character, worship, faith, and spiritual insight.
- Believers hope for His mercy and do not rely on deeds alone.
