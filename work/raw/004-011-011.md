<<<VERSE 4:11>>>
This is the verse that sets the main shares of inheritance for children and parents: "Allah commands you regarding your children: the share of the male will be twice that of the female. If you leave only two ˹or more˺ females, their share is two-thirds of the estate. But if there is only one female, her share will be one-half. Each parent is entitled to one-sixth if you leave offspring. But if you are childless and your parents are the only heirs, then your mother will receive one-third. But if you leave siblings, then your mother will receive one-sixth—after the fulfilment of bequests and debts. ˹Be fair to˺ your parents and children, as you do not ˹fully˺ know who is more beneficial to you. ˹This is˺ an obligation from Allah. Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise."

The earlier verse gave the principle that men and women both inherit from their parents and nearest kin. This verse gives the detail for the closest relatives of all, the children and the parents. They are the nearest and most direct of all human relationships, so they inherit in every case, and the Qur'an takes up their shares first.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah commands you regarding your children"**. The Arabic word is yūṣīkum, from waṣiyyah, which here means a command. Allah is telling parents to be just toward their own children. In the days of ignorance before Islam, the males received a share and the females did not. Ibn ʿAbbās described the old custom in a report recorded by al-Bukhārī. The dead man's property went to his children, while his parents received something only if he named them in his will. Allah cancelled whatever He wished of that custom. He ordained that a male gets twice what a female gets, that each parent gets one-sixth of the whole estate, that a wife gets an eighth or a quarter and that a husband gets a half or a quarter. Another explanation puts it simply: Allah is setting out for you what your children inherit after you die.

**"the share of the male will be twice that of the female"**. This is a general rule. It makes boys and girls both heirs, and it fixes their shares. When a person leaves both sons and daughters, a son's lot is twice a daughter's. If someone leaves one boy and two girls, the estate is divided into four parts. The boy takes two of them and each girl takes one. In the same spirit, another explanation says that with two females beside him a son takes half the estate and they share the other half, with one sister he takes two-thirds and she takes a third, and if he is alone he takes everything.

**"If you leave only two ˹or more˺ females, their share is two-thirds of the estate"**. Some people said this wording means only two daughters, and that "or more" is idle. That is wrong. Nothing in the Qur'an is useless. If the verse were about two women alone, it would have said that the share of both of them is two-thirds. The Arabic speaks plainly of more than two daughters. For exactly two, the same two-thirds applies, and the proof is in the sunnah, as the next section shows. It is also found in the last verse of this surah (4:176), where two sisters receive two-thirds. A female with a brother already gets a third, so two females on their own are surely entitled to the same two-thirds.

There is another thought on the word "more". One explanation says it guards against a false impression. Someone might think that the more females there are, the bigger their portion, because he wrongly takes the two-thirds for two females to come from the third a female gets beside a male. The remaining third goes to the other rightful heirs, such as the parents and the husband or wife of the dead person. All the daughters share the two-thirds equally.

**"But if there is only one female, her share will be one-half"**. If the dead person leaves one daughter and no other children, she receives half of what her father or mother left. The rest goes to the other heirs. If there is a single daughter, the parents take one-sixth each and a further sixth goes to the father.

**"Each parent is entitled to one-sixth if you leave offspring"**. The Arabic words "to each one of the two" repeat the parents, to show that the sixth is not shared between them. Each one receives a sixth of his own. The word for "child" also covers a grandchild, and the word for "parent" also covers a grandparent. This holds whether the child is a boy or a girl, and whether there is one child or more. After the children, the wife or husband and the parents have taken their shares, some of the remainder can return to the father on top of his sixth. In the science of inheritance, that is called taʿṣīb, inheritance as a male relative on the father's side.

**"But if you are childless and your parents are the only heirs, then your mother will receive one-third"**. The father takes the rest, which is twice the mother's share. Allah has given the mother half of what the father gets. If the dead person also left a surviving spouse, the spouse takes first, a half if it is a husband and a quarter if it is a wife. The mother then receives a third of what remains, and the father the other two-thirds, because the remainder is treated as the whole estate for the parents' purposes.

**"But if you leave siblings, then your mother will receive one-sixth"**. When the dead person leaves brothers or sisters, whether full siblings or half-siblings, their presence does not reduce the father's share. It reduces the mother's from a third to a sixth, and if there are no other heirs the father takes the rest. The siblings themselves inherit nothing, because the father is nearer than they are. The Arabic term for this is ḥajb an-nuqṣān, a block that reduces a share and does not remove it. It applies to full siblings, to siblings from the same father only, and to siblings from the same mother only. It applies when there are two or more. Qatādah said that their presence reduces the mother's share and they do not inherit. With one brother only, the mother's share stays at a third. The early scholars explained the reduction by saying that the father is the one who helps these brothers and sisters get married and pays for it out of his own money, while the mother does not. This is a sound opinion.

**"after the fulfilment of bequests and debts"**. All these shares apply only after the dead person's will and debts have been dealt with. The next sections explain the order.

**"˹Be fair to˺ your parents and children, as you do not ˹fully˺ know who is more beneficial to you"**. Benefit may come to a person from his parents in this life or in the Hereafter, in a way he could never get from his children, and the opposite may be true too. Since benefit might come from either, Allah appointed a fixed share for each. This is why He broke with the practice of the days of ignorance and the early period of Islam, when the estate went to the children and the parents received something only if the will named them. According to one explanation, the benefit meant is rank in the Hereafter. According to another, it is usefulness in this world, with regard to inheritance. A short explanation gives an example. A man may think well of someone and leave him everything, or think badly of another and leave him nothing, and he may be wrong both times.

**"˹This is˺ an obligation from Allah. Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise"**. These appointed shares, which give some heirs more than others, are a command that Allah has decided and ordained. He is the All-Knowing and the All-Wise, who puts everything in its rightful place and gives each person his rightful share. He knows the division of inheritance, and He is wise in setting the male and female shares at different amounts.

### The science of inheritance

This verse, the next one and the last verse of the surah (4:176) contain the knowledge of al-farāʾiḍ, the fixed shares of inheritance. This knowledge is drawn from these three verses and from the hadiths that explain them. Learning it is encouraged, especially the specific things the verses mention. Ibn ʿUyaynah said that the knowledge of al-farāʾiḍ was called half of knowledge, because it touches everyone.

The verse about those entitled to inherit was given briefly in the previous section, with some of the details. More details come later, near the end of the surah, and the remaining shares were fixed by hadiths. Muslim jurists gathered all the rules from the Qur'an and the hadith and made them a permanent science, ʿilm al-farāʾiḍ, the science of the laws of inheritance. This verse covers the shares of children and parents, along with some related points.

### Why the verse came down

Al-Bukhārī recorded that Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and Abū Bakr came on foot to visit him at Banū Salamah's dwellings when he was ill. They found him unconscious. The Prophet ﷺ asked for water, did wuḍūʾ (the ablution) with it and sprinkled some on Jābir, and he came to. Jābir asked, "What do you command me to do with my money, O Messenger of Allah?" Then this verse was revealed. Muslim and an-Nasāʾī also recorded it, and the other compilers of the Six collected it as well. It came through Ibn Jurayj, who reported it from Ibn al-Munkadir.

Another report from Jābir was recorded by Aḥmad. The wife of Saʿd ibn ar-Rabīʿ came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and said, "These are the two daughters of Saʿd ibn ar-Rabīʿ, who was killed as a martyr at Uḥud. Their uncle took their money and left them nothing. They will not be married unless they have money." The Messenger ﷺ said, "Allah will decide on this matter." The verse was then revealed, and the Messenger ﷺ sent word to the uncle:

> "Give two-thirds of Saʿd's money to his two daughters and one-eighth to their mother, and whatever is left is yours."

Abū Dāwūd, at-Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah collected this hadith. In another version, an Anṣārī woman near Aswāf said that the two girls were the daughters of Thābit ibn Qays, who had fallen at Uḥud, and that their uncle had taken everything. Yet another report has her naming Thābit ibn Qays, or perhaps Saʿd ibn ar-Rabīʿ, and says the Prophet ﷺ summoned the woman and her opponent and ordered the same division: two-thirds to the girls, one-eighth to their mother, and the remainder for the uncle.

The first hadith of Jābir is more likely about the last verse of the surah (4:176), because at the time Jābir had sisters, and no daughters, parents or children to inherit from him. The hadith is mentioned here only because al-Bukhārī placed it here.

### A mercy greater than a parent's

The verse tells parents to be just and fair with their children, and that shows Allah is more merciful toward children than their own parents are. An authentic hadith tells of a woman among the captives who was searching for her child. She found him at last, picked him up and nursed him at her breast. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ then asked his Companions:

> "Do you think this woman would throw her child into the fire willingly?"

They said, "No, O Messenger of Allah." He said:

> "By Allah, Allah is more merciful to His servants than this woman is to her child."

### Why a male gets twice as much

There is a reason for the difference. Men have to find money for their dependants, for business dealings, for work and for meeting their obligations. That is why they receive twice what women receive.

The way the verse is phrased also shows how much it cares that girls are given their share. It does not say that two females receive the equal of one male. It says that a male receives the equal of two females, so the girl's share is the measuring unit. A modern view notes that ancient tribal societies made the boy all-important, since he was a source of strength to the tribe. They gave him the sole right to inherit and left the girl out completely. People resent this today, and so the boy and the girl are now treated as equal. But if the old rule was unjust, the new rule is unrealistic. A law free from every kind of inequity can be expected only from Allah, whose knowledge and wisdom are without limit. His laws on this subject are a real source of social justice and are also tied, in the deepest way, to the life to come.

### Do not take your sisters' share by "forgiveness"

Some people do not give their sisters a share on the pretext that the sisters have given up their right. This is a mistake. Sisters seldom give up their rights willingly. They do it reluctantly, knowing they will not get anything anyway and asking themselves why they should cause bad blood between brothers and sisters. Such forgiveness is not valid in Islamic law. Their claim against their brothers remains, and those who seize inheritance are terrible sinners. If minor girls hold shares in such an estate, not giving them their shares is a sin committed twice, once by taking an heir's share and once by devouring an orphan's property.

### What the parents receive

The parents' shares fall into three cases. First, the dead person leaves living parents and children, even one boy or one girl. The father and the mother get a sixth each. What remains goes to the children and to the wife or husband. Second, he leaves no children and no brothers or sisters, only parents. The mother gets a third and the father the rest, which is twice her share. If a husband or wife survives, that share is taken out first, and a third of the remainder goes to the mother and two-thirds to the father. Third, he leaves no children but two or more brothers or sisters. The mother then gets a sixth. If there are no other heirs, the father gets the remaining five-sixths. The siblings receive nothing.

### The order: burial, debts, will, then shares

The shares are paid in a fixed order. The cost of shrouding and burying the dead Muslim comes out of the estate first, in a way the Sharīʿah (the revealed law) approves, avoiding both extravagance and stinginess. Then his debts are paid. If the debts equal or exceed the estate, there is nothing to divide and no will can take effect. If something remains, or if there are no debts, his will is carried out, as long as it is not a will to commit a sin. A will is carried out up to a third of what remains. If someone wills his whole estate, the will is valid only for a third. Willing more than a third is not appropriate, and when it is done to shut out the heirs, it is a sin. Whatever remains is divided among the legal heirs. If no will was made, the whole remainder after the debts is divided as inheritance.

The scholars of the Salaf, the early generations, and the Khalaf, the later ones, agree that paying debts comes before carrying out the will. This is clear to anyone who reads the verse carefully.

### Why "nearness" and not "benefit"

Allah fixed these shares in His infinite wisdom, and there are great considerations behind them. If the division had been left to people's opinion, they would have taken usefulness as the measure. But who will actually give or receive the most benefit is hard to know with any certainty. So nearness in kinship was chosen as the test instead of usefulness.

This verse makes it clear that the shares set by Allah are settled commands. Nobody has the right to impose an opinion, or to increase or decrease them. They should be accepted wholeheartedly. This command comes from the Creator and Master of all, and it rests on what is wise and good for human beings. No kind of benefit lies outside His knowledge, and no command of His is without wisdom. A person cannot by himself recognise his real gain and loss. If the division of inheritance were left to personal opinion, people would surely have decided wrongly, because of the limits of their understanding, and fairness and moderation would have been lost. So Allah took this duty on Himself, so that justice and equity prevail in dividing what the dead leave, and so that wealth circulates among capable heirs in a just and balanced way.

### The inner reading

The word for "command" here, waṣiyyah, means an order. Allah set two principles for what the heirs are owed from the estate. One is the obligatory portions, farḍ. The other is inheritance by the group of male relatives, taʿṣīb, whose members are called the ʿaṣaba. Inheritance by the ʿaṣaba is stronger than the fixed portions, because it can take in the whole of the estate, while most of the fixed portions cannot go beyond two-thirds. The division begins with those who hold the fixed portions, who have the weaker claim. Then it moves on to the ʿaṣaba, who have the stronger right. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "What is left over of the shares goes to the nearest male relative."

That is Allah's way, His sunnah, forever. The same pattern appears in His words: "Then We granted the Book to those We have chosen from Our servants. Some of them wrong themselves, some follow a middle course, and some are foremost in good deeds by Allah’s Will" (35:32). He gave them the Book as an inheritance, and then He began with the wrongdoer, ahead of those who take the lead. The wrongdoer has the weakest claim to put forward for generosity, because he is broken-hearted and cannot bear to wait long.

"To the male the equivalent of the portion of two females". If the matter were decided by analogy, qiyās, with those earlier cases where the weak are preferred, it would be more fitting to favour the female, because she is weak and cannot move about freely. But His ruling is one that does not need a reason. "You do not know which of them is nearer in benefit to you". Children benefit you with their service, and parents benefit you with their compassion. Parents give it when you are weak as a newborn, and children give it when you are weak in old age.

### In short

- Allah fixes the shares of the closest heirs. A son receives twice a daughter's share. Two or more daughters share two-thirds, one daughter gets a half, each parent gets a sixth if there are children, and a mother gets a third, or a sixth if there are two or more siblings.
- A husband or wife takes first, and the mother's third is then a third of the remainder. Siblings reduce the mother's share but receive nothing themselves.
- Everything comes after burial costs, debts and a will of up to a third. Debts come before the will.
- The verse is said to have come down for Jābir, who was ill, and for the daughters of Saʿd ibn ar-Rabīʿ, whose uncle took their inheritance. The Prophet ﷺ gave the girls two-thirds and their mother one-eighth.
- Allah chose nearness in kinship over supposed benefit because no one can know who will benefit them most, and no heir may add to or reduce what He fixed.
- Inwardly, the shares follow the pattern of Allah's gifts, which come before deeds and need no justification.
