<<<VERSE 7:111>>>
The advisers give their answer, and it is a delay rather than a judgement. "They replied, “Let him and his brother wait and send mobilizers to all cities"

### Phrase by phrase

**"They replied"** records the answer of the chiefs and the noblemen to the question Pharaoh had put to them. They did not speak of the message, and they did not speak of the signs, and they did not speak of the Children of Israel. The whole of their answer was a proposal about how to handle the two men.

**"Let him and his brother wait"** is the first part of it. The sense given by Ibn Abbas is that the words mean delay him for a time, and in one reading the instruction is not to kill them. The brother named here is Aaron, and in the Arabic the two are named together as Musa and his brother, since Aaron was his brother in blood and his partner in the mission.

**"and send mobilizers to all cities"** is the second part. The cities meant are the areas and the provinces of the kingdom, and the men to be sent into them are summoners or soldiers, whose work was to gather the magicians from the different lands and bring them in.

**What they were counting on.** The land of Egypt at that time was a place where magic was the trade of the day. What the words of their counsel say is that the whole trade could be gathered in one place, widespread and followed with honour, and the sorcerers held a high standing among the people. The men around Pharaoh had the idea that what Musa had brought was a kind of magic like the magic their own sorcerers practised. That assumption was wrong from the beginning, and because of it they gathered the whole trade together in order to defeat what he had shown them. In their thinking the difficulty was one of skill, and a greater skill would settle it.

The verses of this passage tell the remaining part of the account of Musa, which reaches its end forty years after he first came to the land. What had happened should have been enough to convince Pharaoh and to bring him to believe, since a staff had turned into a serpent and a hand had come out shining white. Instead the wrongdoers turned to false readings of what they had seen, so that they could cover the truth up. Pharaoh and his people gave the lie to Musa and said to the people that he was a great sorcerer who wanted to drive them out of their homes and take the rule of the country for himself.

The Book records their proposal in the words of those who made it, and it records where the plan led, since the appointment for the contest was set as the whole of this passage goes on to describe.

### In short

- The advisers answered with a plan and left the message and the signs untouched.
- Their first counsel was to delay the matter, and in one reading not to put the two men to death.
- The brother named with Musa is Aaron, who shared the mission with him.
- They asked for summoners to be sent through the provinces of the kingdom to gather the magicians of every land.
- Their hope rested on the strength of the magic of their time, and on the belief that what Musa had brought was magic of the same kind.

<<<VERSE 7:112>>>
The purpose of the gathering is stated in the closing words of the passage. "to bring you every clever magician.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"to bring you every clever magician"** gives the errand of the men sent out. The word for the magician here carries the sense of one who is cunning and practised in his craft, and a variant reading makes it stronger still, so that the sense is a sorcerer who outdoes the rest. The whole of the kingdom's skill was to be brought to one place.

**What Musa was given, and why.** The signs that Musa was sent with belong to a pattern. The practice of Allah with His prophets follows a rule. Each is given miracles that answer the demand of his own time. In the time of the prophet Isa, the Greek thought and the sciences of medicine stood at their highest, so his signs were of that kind. He opened the eyes of those born blind, and a leper was cured the moment his hand touched him. When the Holy Prophet ﷺ came, the Arabs took their pride in eloquence and in what they could do with the language. The Qur'an was therefore given as the greatest of all his miracles. The time of Musa in Egypt was ruled by sorcery and by the repute of the sorcerers, and what he was given was greater than all of the spells they could gather, so that the people might see for themselves that the master of the craft could not stand against the messenger of Allah.

Their own account of the appointment is given in the Book. They said that they could produce magic the like of what he had shown, and they asked for a meeting between them, to be kept by both sides, in an open place where neither would have an advantage. Musa named the day of the festival, with the people assembled when the sun had risen, and Pharaoh withdrew, devised his plot and came back (20:58-60).

**What the two verses leave in place.** The scene is set for the contest between the messenger and the gathered magicians, and the gathering itself had been ordered by the very men who claimed his proof was only magic. The two verses close by putting every skilled sorcerer in the land under one roof, and the next verses show what came of it.

### In short

- The men sent out were to bring every cunning and practised sorcerer from the lands of the kingdom.
- A variant reading carries the sense of a sorcerer who surpasses the others, which is what the contest required.
- Prophets are sent with miracles that answer the demand of their own time, as Isa was given the cure of the blind and the leper when medicine stood at its height.
- The Arabs took pride in eloquence, so the Qur'an was given as the greatest of the miracles of the Prophet ﷺ.
- The Book reports their own claim that they could match his magic, and the meeting was set for the festival day at sunrise (20:58-60).
