<<<VERSE 7:111>>>
Having seen the staff turn into a serpent and the hand shine with light, Pharaoh should have been convinced and believed in Mūsā (Moses), as logic and sense required. But wrongdoers always look for false interpretations of the truth in order to hide it. Pharaoh and his people denied him, and his chiefs advised: "They replied, “Let him and his brother wait and send mobilizers to all cities". They had told the people that Mūsā was a great sorcerer who wanted to drive them from their homes and take over the country.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They replied, “Let him and his brother wait"**: Ibn ʿAbbās said "wait" here means "delay him for a time". Put off the case of Mūsā and his brother Hārūn (Aaron), and postpone any decision about them. One explanation adds: do not kill them.

**"and send mobilizers to all cities"**: send summoners, gatherers or soldiers to the cities, regions and provinces of your kingdom, O Pharaoh (Firʿawn). Send people to all the cities of Egypt to call every magician and gather them from many lands.

### Why did the chiefs suggest delay?

Pharaoh's people said there were many expert sorcerers in their cities who could defeat Mūsā. Some soldiers should be sent to gather them and bring them for the purpose. Rather than act against Mūsā at once, they wanted a public contest, sure that their magicians would show his signs to be nothing more than magic.

### In short

- Instead of believing after the two signs, Pharaoh's chiefs advised him to put off Mūsā and Hārūn for a while.
- They urged him to send men to every city of Egypt to gather the magicians, confident they could beat Mūsā.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:112>>>
The chiefs complete their advice: "to bring you every clever magician.”" The gatherers would bring every skilled and powerful sorcerer to outdo Mūsā (Moses) in magic, and so they summoned them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"to bring you every clever magician.”"** They will bring you every expert and accomplished magician, cunning and well versed in sorcery. In the Arabic, "magician" is read sāḥir, and in another reading saḥḥār, a master of magic.

### Why did they think magicians could defeat Mūsā?

At that time magic was the trade of the day, widespread and popular, and sorcerers enjoyed high status among the people. Pharaoh and his court assumed that what Mūsā brought was the same kind of magic as the sorcerers of their age practised. On that mistaken assumption they gathered all the magicians to defeat the miracles he had shown them. Allah tells how Pharaoh set the meeting:

> "We can surely meet you with similar magic. So set for us an appointment that neither of us will fail to keep, in a central place.” Moses said, “Your appointment is on the Day of the Festival, and let the people be gathered mid-morning.” Pharaoh then withdrew, orchestrated his scheme, then returned" (20:58-60).

Mūsā had spent 40 years calling his people to Allah's command. Pharaoh still saw in him only a practitioner of magic, and decided to overcome him with his own magicians.

### Why do miracles match their age?

Allah sent Mūsā with the miracles of the staff and the shining hand so that people could plainly see the humiliation of the sorcerers once they took him on. It is Allah's usual way to send His prophets with miracles suited to what their age prizes.

- In the time of the Prophet ʿĪsā (Jesus), Greek philosophy and medicine were at their height. So his miracles were to open the eyes of people blind from birth, and to heal lepers at once by touching them.
- In the time of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, the Arabs prided themselves on their eloquence and command of language. So the Qur'an was sent as the greatest of all his miracles.

### In short

- The chiefs proposed gathering every skilled magician in the land to outdo Mūsā, and Pharaoh set a public meeting on the day of the festival.
- In an age that prized magic, they took his signs for magic of the same kind.
- Allah gives each prophet miracles that surpass what his age excels in: magic for Mūsā, medicine for ʿĪsā, and eloquence for Muhammad ﷺ.
