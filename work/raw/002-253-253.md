<<<VERSE 2:253>>>
The messengers share a divine mission, but not an identical rank or set of gifts: "We have chosen some of those messengers above others." The verse names direct speech, elevated rank and the signs granted to 'Isa (Jesus). It then explains that clear guidance did not remove disagreement among later people. Some believed and others rejected. Their conflict never placed events beyond Allah's will.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have chosen some of those messengers above others."** Allah favoured them in revelation, followers, special qualities and rank. Their shared message does not require every individual honour to be the same. The related statement in (17:55) mentions the preference of some prophets and the Zabur, Psalms, given to Dawud (David).

**"Allah spoke directly to some, and raised some high in rank."** Musa (Moses) is the principal example of direct speech. Muhammad ﷺ is also included, and a report in Ibn Hibban's Sahih from Abu Dharr includes Adam. Other illustrations of elevation are Ibrāhīm (Abraham), whom Allah took as a friend, and Enoch, who was raised to an elevated place. Muhammad's ﷺ mission to all humanity and his finality among prophets are also given as reasons for his rank.

**"To Jesus, son of Mary, We gave clear proofs and supported him with the holy spirit."** 'Isa's proofs include his commands, prohibitions and miracles, such as bringing the dead to life and curing blindness and leprosy. They establish him as Allah's servant and messenger to the Children of Israel. Ruh al-Qudus, the holy spirit, is explained as Jibril (Gabriel), who strengthened him and accompanied him in carrying out the mission.

**"If Allah had willed, succeeding generations would not have fought ˹among themselves˺ after receiving the clear proofs."** The people after the messengers could have been universally guided had Allah so willed. The proofs did not leave their disagreement outside His knowledge or power. One explanation includes the descriptions of Muhammad ﷺ in the earlier scriptures among those clear indications.

**"But they differed—some believed while others disbelieved."** Some accepted the books and messengers, while others rejected. The distinction is not a denial that the message was clear. It describes different responses to it. Christians' departure after 'Isa al-Masih, Jesus the Messiah, is given as an example in one explanation.

**"Yet if Allah had willed, they would not have fought one another. But Allah does what He wills"** The repetition emphasises His sovereignty. Guidance comes through His grace and mercy, while withholding it is connected with justice and wisdom. No human party controls the final order of events by claiming religious ancestry.

### How can different ranks be affirmed without insulting prophets?

The Two Sahihs record from Abu Hurayrah an argument between a Muslim and a Jew. The Jew swore by the One who had favoured Musa above humanity. The Muslim struck him, objecting to including Muhammad ﷺ within that comparison. The Jew complained to the Prophet ﷺ.

The Prophet ﷺ forbade making claims of superiority in that setting. He described people becoming unconscious at the Resurrection and finding Musa holding a pillar of the Throne when he arose. He did not know whether Musa had arisen earlier or whether Musa's earlier experience of unconsciousness sufficed for him. Another wording forbids preferring some prophets above others.

The explanation distinguishes Allah's disclosed preference from a partisan contest. Human beings do not appoint the ranks themselves. They accept what Allah has revealed rather than strike someone in defence of their own conjecture. Ranking as an occasion of dispute and contempt is not the same as believing the verse.

Other sayings similarly warn against giving the Prophet ﷺ precedence over Musa and against claiming superiority over Yunus ibn Matta. One answer says comparison must rest on proof from the Qur'an and Sunnah, the Prophet's ﷺ teaching, not guesswork. Another relates those particular sayings to an earlier time before the Prophet ﷺ was told of his higher rank, which he later disclosed. These are the answers supplied to the apparent difficulty and should not be reduced to just one.

The Isra' journey also gives an illustration of rank: the Prophet ﷺ saw prophets in different heavens according to their standing before Allah. The point is an honour known through revelation, not a private scale invented by later admirers.

### Does direct speech mean there was no veil?

Musa's conversation is explained as occurring without an angel as intermediary, but not without hijab, a veil preventing direct sight. This keeps it consistent with (42:51), in surah al-Shura, which describes the ways Allah speaks to a human being.

The discussion distinguishes this worldly setting from the possibility of speech without a veil after death. No conflict is created by making directness mean the removal of every other distinction. The absence of an angelic messenger and the absence of a veil are separate questions.

### Why can gifts to earlier prophets become an excuse to reject guidance?

Special favours helped each messenger deliver the truth in the circumstances of his time. Later followers could attach themselves to the particular miracle and turn its honour into a claim that no later messenger deserved attention.

Followers of Musa could reject 'Isa while invoking Musa's direct speech. Followers of 'Isa could reject Muhammad ﷺ while relying on 'Isa's birth from a virgin mother. The passage's discussion extends the criticism to later Muslim reformers: people attached to the reputation of earlier saints may dismiss someone calling them back to obedience merely because the living caller has not acquired the same inherited prestige.

Such attachment can become a defence for worldly interests. People wish to preserve a claim to salvation while neglecting ethical conduct. They imagine association with a revered personality will guarantee their end regardless of their own deeds. That false security can make them resist the very admonition that would correct them.

Recognising a messenger's special honour should therefore deepen fidelity to truth, not supply a reason to refuse every subsequent call. The light of guidance must not be hidden behind the social prestige of a name.

### What comfort does the verse offer the Prophet ﷺ?

Denial hurt the Prophet ﷺ despite the evidence for his mission. The verse recalls that no earlier prophet's community displayed universal belief either. Some supported and others opposed. Their differing ranks and signs did not eliminate that pattern.

The existence of rejection therefore does not disprove the message or make the messenger responsible for every person's refusal. The discussion affirms wisdom in Allah's arrangement even where its full reasons are not visible. The previous affirmation of messengership is not withdrawn by the fact that people continue to fight.

Muhammad ﷺ is described here as the final prophet sent to all peoples, with many miracles and special qualities, and a community favoured over others. These honours still do not mean that every individual automatically acts rightly or that the community may abandon accountability.

### How are human response and divine will presented together?

One explanation describes freedom of action as part of the test. Allah could have made refusal impossible, but instead people must recognise His word spoken through a human messenger while the Lord remains unseen. They are asked to look through outward forms to the truth rather than respond only to appearance.

An inward reading stresses the eternal will around which events turn. It describes people as deprived of an independent choice capable of escaping that will. Servanthood means diligent service while recognising prior allotment. That emphasis is on dependence upon Allah, whereas the other explanation focuses on the responsibility of the tested person.

Both emphases belong to the material. The verse itself names belief and unbelief and places their history within Allah's sovereignty. The commentary does not replace either emphasis with a separate theory from outside the passage.

The inward reading also describes messengers as united in the message but distinct in details and the lights granted them. Their virtues are gifts of a good decree, not personal entitlements they can demand. Recognising difference should therefore lead to reverence for the Giver rather than competition among followers.

### In short

- Allah grants different ranks and signs while the messengers share a divine mission.
- Revealed preference differs from conjectural ranking used for quarrels or contempt.
- Special honours of past prophets must not become excuses for rejecting truth or neglecting conduct.
- Clear evidence did not remove every human disagreement, and none of that disagreement escapes Allah's will.
