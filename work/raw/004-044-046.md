<<<VERSE 4:44>>>
The turn from the family law to the old adversary: "Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures yet trade it for misguidance and wish to see you deviate from the ˹Right˺ Path". Those entrusted with revelation are shown buying the opposite of it, and wishing the same trade for the believers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who were given a portion of the Scriptures"** are the Jews, who were given knowledge of the Torah. Receiving a portion of the Book, they could read its words, but could not mould their actions to them, defeating the Scripture's real purpose. They remained its bearers as far as words go, while in practice they adopted the ways of worldly communities, and then had the audacity to justify those ways by misquoting the Book itself.

**"yet trade it for misguidance"** means they purchased the wrong path instead of guidance, ignoring what Allah sent down to His Messenger ﷺ and the knowledge inherited from earlier prophets about his description, all for a small amount of the delights of this life. One report names the buyers as al-Yasaʿ and Rāfiʿ, the sons of Harmalah, two rabbis who pressed ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy and his friends to take up their religion and choose Judaism over Islam. Ibn Zayd's reading holds the like.

**"and wish to see you deviate from the ˹Right˺ Path"** makes the wish explicit: they would like the believers to disbelieve in what was sent down to them and abandon the guidance and beneficial knowledge they hold, straying from the path of truth and becoming like them.

### The exchange rate of error

The commands of God, in a community of declining bearers, become the subject of futile hair-splitting. In the name of theology, philosophies are concocted, and the divine book is altered to give wrongdoing a religious licence. God's words are detached from their context and read through the readers' own assumptions. That is the purchase this verse prices: the words of the divine Book recited to solicit blessings without any attempt to understand their meaning or apply them to life. The full story of their twisted courtesy toward the Prophet ﷺ, the Rā’inā affair, waits at the verse of the displaced words. There the warning already given to the believers, "Say not Rā’inā but say Unẓurnā" (2:104), is recalled against them. There too their little faith is weighed in the scale of "so little is that which they believe" (2:88).

### In short

- The people of the portion bought error with guidance, trading eternal gain for the small delights of this life.
- Their wish is company in ruin: that the believers leave the right path and become like them.
- Reading without practising, and justifying worldliness by misquoting the Book, is the essence of the trade.

<<<VERSE 4:45>>>
The answer to the wish: "Allah knows best who your enemies are! And Allah is sufficient as a Guardian, and He is sufficient as a Helper". The adversaries wish deviation on the believers, and the believers are told who actually holds them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah knows best who your enemies are"** means His knowledge of your enemies outruns yours, and He informs you of them so that you avoid them, from among the hypocrites and the Jews alike.

**"And Allah is sufficient as a Guardian"** names Him the wali, the Protector and Preserver of those who seek refuge with Him, keeping them safe from the enemies' harm.

**"and He is sufficient as a Helper"** completes the pair: a Sufficient Supporter for those who seek His help, defending them against the plotting.

### The two sufficiencies

The verse is placed between the enemy's wish and the enemy's methods, and it settles both. The wish to see the believers deviate from the path comes to nothing against a Guardian who cannot be circumvented, and the word-twisting that follows in the next verse, the mockery and the curse hidden in courtesy, cannot injure a community whose Helper is Allah. The path the enemies want abandoned is the very path the Guardian preserves. What the believers face here is the same adversary already warned of in the instruction not to say Rā’inā (2:104), and the same little faith measured by "so little is that which they believe" (2:88). Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Zayd read the enemies named here as the Jews and the hypocrites together, and the reading of the passage holds them both.

### In short

- Allah knows the enemies better than they can be known, and names them so they can be avoided.
- Guardianship and help are both sufficient in Him, and the enemies' wish runs against His protection.
- The verse stands as a wall between the adversary's wish and the community it was wished against.

<<<VERSE 4:46>>>
The methods itemized: "Some Jews take words out of context and say, 'We listen and we disobey,' 'Hear! May you never hear,' and 'Râ'ina!' [Herd us!]—playing with words and discrediting the faith. Had they said ˹courteously˺, 'We hear and obey,' 'Listen to us,' and 'Unẓurna,' [Tend to us!] it would have been better for them and more proper. Allah has condemned them for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few". Three phrases said with a twist, and three phrases they might have said instead.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Some Jews take words out of context"** means the meanings of Allah's words are deliberately and falsely altered, explained in a manner other than what Allah meant. One report names Mālik ibn al-Sayf and his friends, who changed the traits and description of Muḥammad ﷺ after they had been expounded in the Torah, wrenching the words from the contexts in which they were placed.

**"'We listen and we disobey'"** was said to the Prophet's face and behind his back, meaning: your words are heard, O Muḥammad, but your command is not obeyed, as Mujāhid and Ibn Zayd explained. The phrase exhibits their disbelief, stubbornness and disregard for Allah's Book after understanding it, sin and punishment fully in view.

**"'Hear! May you never hear'"** was their mocking jest: hear our words, and may you never hear anything, as Ad-Daḥḥāk reported from Ibn ʿAbbās.

**"'Râ'ina!' [Herd us!]"** is the sharpest of the twists of the tongue. They pretend to say "Hear us", which is what rā’inā means in Arabic, while in their own language it is an insult, and their true aim is to curse the Prophet ﷺ. The believers had already been told: "Say not Rā’inā but say Unẓurnā" (2:104), and the word is forbidden as an address to him for that reason. With tongues twisted and the religion slandered, they say one thing to his face and mean another.

**"Had they said ˹courteously˺, 'We hear and obey,' 'Listen to us,' and 'Unẓurna'"** sets the three courteous forms opposite the three twisted ones. They would have been better for them and more proper, worthier and more correct behaviour in the Prophet's presence.

**"so they do not believe except for a few"** closes with the curse. Their hearts are cast away from the path of righteousness, and no beneficial part of faith enters them, as with "so little is that which they believe" (2:88). The few who believed are ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his companions, who accepted Islam.

### The blessing they barred

They practised a deception and did not realize the way they were deceived in turn: given the book, they were deprived of the blessing of understanding, so that they distorted the words and persisted in unbelief. They acted shamelessly toward the Messenger ﷺ and refused him respect, and were punished with doubt concerning his affair. Their shamelessness raised a barrier between them and the blessing of his companionship and the bounties of serving him. Had they hastened instead to reject the envy they felt, and honoured him with veneration for his state, blessing would have met them on his road, and happiness in both abodes. But what had already been determined distanced them. They were kept back by fate from the carpet of service, and there is no manoeuvring that can help those held back by the divine decrees.

### In short

- Words were wrenched from their contexts, and three phrases of obedience were said as twists of mockery.
- Rā’inā wore the mask of courtesy while carrying a curse in another tongue, and the believers were already warned off it.
- The courteous forms stood ready, and would have carried blessing in both abodes.
- The curse removed them from mercy, and only a few, like ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām, believed.
- Envy of the Messenger barred them from the blessing of his service, and no manoeuvring beats the decree that holds a person back.
