<<<VERSE 4:44>>>
"Have you, O Prophet, not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures yet trade it for misguidance and wish to see you deviate from the Right Path." This verse chastises those given a portion of the Book who purchase the wrong path instead of guidance and wish that believers go astray from the right path.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Have you not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures"** refers to those given a portion of the Book.

**"Yet trade it for misguidance"** means purchasing the wrong path instead of guidance.

**"And wish to see you deviate from the Right Path"** means wishing believers to go astray.

### Choosing misguidance

The Jews ignored what Allah sent down to His Messenger Muhammad and ignored the knowledge they inherited from earlier Prophets about the description of Muhammad. They did this so that they may have a small amount of the delights of this life. They would like that believers disbelieve in what was sent down and abandon the guidance and beneficial knowledge. The verse cites 2:88 and 2:104. It was commented on by Ibn Abbas, Ibn Zayd, and others. The verse involves say, the path, torah, wali, and related terms. The scholars and terms mentioned include Ibn Abbas, Ibn Ubayy, Ibn Zayd, say, the path, torah, wali, al-Yasa.

### In short

- Those given a portion of the Scriptures trade it for misguidance.
- They wish believers to deviate from the Right Path.
- The verse chastises them.
- The discussion adds the views of Ibn Abbas and others.
- The verse follows the previous passage.

<<<VERSE 4:46>>>
"Some Jews take words out of context and say, 'We listen and we disobey,' 'Hear! May you never hear,' and 'Râ'ina!'—playing with words and discrediting the faith. Had they said courteously, 'We hear and obey,' 'Listen to us,' and 'Unẓurna,' it would have been better for them and more proper. Allah has condemned them for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few." This verse describes some Jews displacing words from their right places, saying "We hear your word and disobey," "Hear and let you hear nothing," and "Ra'ina" with a twist of their tongues and as a mockery of the religion. It states that if they had said "We hear and obey" and "Do make us understand," it would have been better and more proper. Allah has cursed them for their disbelief, so they believe not except a few.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Some Jews take words out of context"** means they intentionally and falsely alter the meanings of the Words of Allah.

"And say, 'We listen and we disobey'" means "We hear what you say, O Muhammad, but we do not obey you in it."

"'Hear! May you never hear'" is another mocking phrase.

"And 'Râ'ina!'" is said with a twist of their tongues and as a mockery of the religion.

"Playing with words and discrediting the faith" describes their mockery.

"Had they said courteously, 'We hear and obey,' 'Listen to us,' and 'Unẓurna'" gives the proper alternative.

**"It would have been better for them and more proper"** states the benefit.

**"Allah has condemned them for their disbelief"** means Allah has cursed them for their disbelief.

**"So they do not believe except for a few"** means they believe not except a few.

### Altering words and mocking

Mujahid and Ibn Zayd explained that "We hear your word and disobey" means "We hear what you say, O Muhammad, but we do not obey you in it." This demonstrates the Jews' disbelief, stubbornness and disregard for Allah's Book after they understood it. They were aware of the sin and punishment that this behavior would earn for them. The verse cites 2:88 and 2:104. It was commented on by Ibn Abbas, Ibn Zayd, and others. The verse involves say, the path, torah, wali, and related terms. The scholars and terms mentioned include Abd Allāh, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Salam, Ibn al-Sayf, Ibn Zayd, Rā’inā, Salām, say, the path, torah, wali.

### In short

- Some Jews displace words from their right places.
- They mock the religion with twisted phrases.
- Proper speech would have been better.
- Allah has cursed them for their disbelief.
- They believe not except a few.
- The discussion adds the views of Ibn Abbas and others.
