<<<VERSE 2:99>>>
Verse 99 answers the complaint that the Prophet ﷺ had brought no proof. "Indeed, We have sent down to you ˹O Prophet˺ clear revelations. ˹But˺ none will deny them except the rebellious". The signs were there for anyone willing to see them, so denial could only come from defiance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We have sent down to you ˹O Prophet˺ clear revelations"**. Ibn ʿAbbās said this verse was revealed in reply to Ibn Ṣūryā (also written Ibn Suwriyā), who said to Allah's Messenger ﷺ: "Muḥammad, you have brought nothing that we recognise, and no clear sign has been revealed to you on account of which we should follow you." The Jews used to say, in denying the Prophet ﷺ, that no evidence of his prophethood had come to him of a kind they could recognise and accept. The Holy Qur'an answers that Allah has revealed many very clear signs of his prophethood, signs which they themselves know and understand very well. Imām Abū Jaʿfar ibn Jarīr explained the phrase as "We have sent you clear signs that testify to your prophethood", signs contained in the Book of Allah itself. The Qur'an narrates the secret knowledge the Jews possessed and concealed, the stories of their earlier generations, and the texts of their own Books known only to the rabbis and scholars, including the places where they altered and distorted the rulings of the Torah. That Allah mentioned all this in the Book revealed to His Prophet ﷺ should be evidence enough for anyone honest with himself and anxious not to destroy himself through envy and transgression. Human nature too testifies to the truth he was sent with and to the clear signs he brought, which he neither learned nor acquired from any human being. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported Ibn ʿAbbās putting it this way: day and night you recite this Book to them and convey it, you who are unlettered and never read a book, and yet you tell them what their own Books contain. Allah made this a lesson, a clear sign and a proof against them, if only they knew. One early gloss adds that the signs came through Jibrīl (Gabriel) and expound commands and prohibitions. The word for "clear" describes the state in which the revelations came down.

**"˹But˺ none will deny them except the rebellious"**. The wicked, the unbelievers and the Jews who reject the signs, those who have abandoned Allah's path. Their denial does not stem from any lack of recognition or knowledge but from their habitual disobedience, for as a rule no one rejects such evidence except the wilful and the stubborn.

### Why clear signs are still denied

The devotional reading observes that a person disbelieves in Allah's clear signs only when his eyes are blocked from perceiving and his lot from God has already been determined as unfortunate. There is no reasoning with someone who denies that day is day, and likewise there is no union for anyone who has not been aided by lights and insights from the Real.

A modern reflection traces the perversion of the People of the Book to a single cause. They come to believe that uttering words and performing lifeless rituals will earn them salvation, when salvation can only be earned by righteous action. In this stage of decline superstition is valued above good deeds, and people expect mysterious benefits from senseless rites. What begins as a theory of salvation ends by dominating the whole policy of the community, and a realistic outlook gives way to a superstitious one. Those who make only an outward show of religion are inevitably averse to responding in earnest when pure, unadulterated religion is proclaimed, because they see the call as a threat to the worldly honour and status their worldly version of religion secures for them. Out of this negativity they put forward objections that look correct on the surface but are meaningless in reality. The coming of the angels and the messengers was wholly part of the divine plan. Every argument shows that the Arab Prophet ﷺ received the same message that was sent to Ibrāhīm (Abraham), Mūsā (Moses) and ʿĪsā (Jesus), and that his revelation tallied exactly with the predictions of the earlier scriptures, which proves beyond doubt that it came from God Himself. People make many protestations of faith, but when these are hollow they have nothing to do with true faith and piety.

### In short

- To Ibn Ṣūryā's claim that the Prophet ﷺ had brought no recognisable sign, Allah answers that the Qur'an itself is a clear sign, revealing to the Jews the secrets of their own Books through an unlettered man.
- Only the rebellious deny such evidence, so the Jews' denial was wilful disobedience, not ignorance.
- Eyes blocked from the Real cannot see, and a religion reduced to ritual resists the true call because its worldly interests are at stake.

<<<VERSE 2:100>>>
Verse 100 turns to the Jews' record with promises. "Why is it that every time they make a covenant, a group of them casts it aside? In fact, most of them do not believe".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Why is it that every time they make a covenant"**. After the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had been sent, Allah reminded the Jews of their covenant with Him, and in particular of the part concerning Muḥammad ﷺ. Mālik ibn aṣ-Ṣayf said: "By Allah, Allah never made a covenant with us about Muḥammad, nor did He ever take a pledge from us." Allah then revealed this verse. The Torah itself reports that Allah had made the Jews pledge to believe in the Prophet ﷺ when he appeared, and when reminded of it they flatly denied ever having given such a pledge. The covenants in question include the pledge to believe in the Prophet ﷺ when he came and the pledge not to help the idolaters against him. One early gloss applies the verse to the covenants that the Jewish leaders made with Muḥammad ﷺ.

**"a group of them casts it aside?"**. They reject it, repudiate it and break it. There is no promise they make that they do not break and abandon, making a promise today and breaking it tomorrow. The question is a rebuke. The Holy Qur'an points out that this was no isolated case, for the Jews had never kept even the pledges they acknowledged, and one group or another among them was always breaking the compacts they had entered into, especially in religious matters. Their corrupt state meant that some group would always break any commitment, including the duty to believe in what the Torah had revealed about Muḥammad's prophethood.

**"In fact, most of them do not believe"**. The particle marks a transition: the truth is that most of them are disbelievers, or, as one gloss says, all of them in fact. It can even be said that most of those guilty of such gross violations did not believe at all in the compacts they made with Allah. Breaking a pledge is a sin and a transgression, but having no faith in the pledge one gives to Allah is outright unbelief. Faith leads to keeping and honouring commitments and promises, so a people who keep none have little of it.

### Why "a group", not all

The Qur'an deliberately speaks of one group or another breaking the pledge, not of all the Jews, because some among them did keep their pledges, and in the end many Jews recognised Muḥammad ﷺ as a true prophet and took their place among the Muslims. The devotional reading adds that their predetermined lot confounded them and their subsequent contrivances broke their covenant, and God prevails over His affair.

### In short

- Reminded of the covenant about the Prophet ﷺ, Mālik ibn aṣ-Ṣayf denied it ever existed, and the verse came down.
- A group among the Jews broke every covenant they made, and not believing in a pledge given to Allah is worse than merely breaking it.
- The verse blames "a group", not all, since some kept faith and many eventually embraced Islam.

<<<VERSE 2:101>>>
Verse 101 gives the latest and most relevant instance of the habit just described. "Now, when a messenger from Allah has come to them—confirming their own Scriptures—some of the People of the Book cast the Book of Allah behind their backs as if they did not know".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Now, when a messenger from Allah has come to them"**. Namely Muḥammad ﷺ, a prophet from Allah.

**"confirming their own Scriptures"**. Confirming what they possess of the Book, conforming in traits and description to what the Torah said of him. The Torah had given the Jews the good tidings of his coming, and he came with every sign it had indicated, so that he fitted its description and confirmed their sacred Book. As-Suddī explained what happened next. When Muḥammad ﷺ came, the Jews wanted to contradict him and dispute with him using the Torah, but the Torah and the Qur'an confirmed each other. So the Jews gave up on the Torah and turned instead to the Book of Āṣaf and to the sorcery of Hārūt and Mārūt, neither of which agreed with the Qur'an.

**"some of the People of the Book cast the Book of Allah behind their backs"**. A party of those given the Scripture flung the Torah behind them. They did not act on what it said about belief in the Messenger and other matters, and they disbelieved in what it contained of Muḥammad's traits and description and did not expound them. Great numbers of them refused him recognition as a prophet, and in refusing it they were in fact denying the Torah itself. They turned away from the scripture that pointed to him, unmindful of it, drawing no benefit from its truth and guidance.

**"as if they did not know"**. As though they were ignorant people who knew nothing of what it contained about his being a true Prophet, or about its being the Book of Allah. Qatādah said that they knew the truth but abandoned it, hid it and denied that they even possessed it. They behaved as if they knew nothing of the prophecy, or of the Torah being a Book of Allah, and so were guilty of unbelief (kufr) even by the Torah's own terms. They acted, as one commentary puts it, like fools.

### The habit and its latest instance

The previous verse showed how breaking pledges and disobedience had become a settled habit with the Jews. This verse presents the freshest example and the one most relevant to the context. Everything that follows in the passage, the turn to sorcery and the tale of what the devils recited in the reign of Sulaymān (Solomon), flows from this moment of throwing the Book away. A people who abandoned the Scripture that confirmed the Messenger would look for something else to set against him, and they found it in books that had nothing to do with revelation.

### Denying the messengers within and without

The devotional reading sees a double denial. The messengers of the Real that reached their hearts as intuitions (khawāṭir) they denied, and the messengers who came to them outwardly they called liars. What ignorance, without a sliver of deeper knowledge (ʿirfān)! What deprivation, with disappointment (khidhlān) for its companion!

### In short

- When the Messenger ﷺ came matching the Torah's own description, a party of the People of the Book threw the Torah behind their backs rather than follow where it led.
- As-Suddī relates that, unable to use the Torah against the Qur'an, they fell back on Āṣaf's book and on the sorcery attributed to Hārūt and Mārūt.
- Qatādah: they knew the truth, abandoned it, hid it and denied having it, which is unbelief even by the Torah's standard.
- Rejecting the inner promptings of the Real goes hand in hand with rejecting His outward messengers.
