<<<VERSE 112:1>>>
This is the verse that answers the question "What is your Lord like?" The Prophet ﷺ is told: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ 'He is Allah—One ˹and Indivisible˺'". It is the shortest possible statement of the faith, and the three verses that follow only unfold what is packed into it. The surah is about tawḥīd, the oneness of Allah, presented in its purest form and cleared of everything people in every age have mixed into the idea of God. There are not many gods. There is one.

The command "Say" is addressed directly to Muhammad ﷺ. That small word carries two things: that he is Allah's Prophet and Messenger, and that he is under orders to pass this message on to everyone. The answer he is to give is brief, but it covers everything that long books of theology discuss about who Allah is.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺"** translates qul. The order is given to the Prophet ﷺ, which marks him as the one Allah speaks through, and it tells him to convey the words rather than keep them. The answer was given to the people who had asked him to describe his Lord.

**"He is Allah"** names the one being asked about. "Allah" is the personal name of the Being who must exist, whose non-existence cannot even be imagined. He has every attribute of perfection and is free of every flaw. In the grammar of the sentence, "He" is the subject and "Allah" is what is said about Him, and "One" either stands in for it or is a second statement about Him. The sense is: He is Allah, the only one who is God, and there is no god but Him.

**"One ˹and Indivisible˺"** translates aḥad. Allah is also called wāḥid, and both words are usually rendered "One", but aḥad says more. It means He is beyond being put together from parts, beyond being more than one, and beyond resembling anything. He is not made of any material, He has no partner, and nothing is like Him. He is the One, the Single, with no peer, no helper, no rival, no equal and nothing comparable to Him. The word aḥad, used as a plain statement, fits no one but Allah, because only He is perfect in all His attributes and acts. Ibn ʿAbbās summed up what the Prophet ﷺ was to tell his questioners: He is Allah, the One, and He has no son and no partner.

### Who asked, and what they asked

Ubayy ibn Kaʿb reported that the idolaters said to the Prophet ﷺ, "O Muhammad, tell us the lineage of your Lord," and Allah revealed this surah in answer. Aḥmad recorded it, and so did at-Tirmidhī, Ibn Jarīr and Ibn Abī Ḥātim. At-Tirmidhī also mentioned a version with a gap in its chain and said that version is the most correct. In another report the questioners were the people of Quraysh, and the question was, "Describe your Lord for us. Is He made of gold or silver?" Another version adds pearls. The whole surah was sent down to describe His attributes, and this first verse is the direct reply: He is not made of anything.

ʿIkrimah gave a wider setting. The Jews said, "We worship ʿUzayr (Ezra), the son of Allah," the Christians said, "We worship the Messiah, the son of Allah," the Zoroastrians said, "We worship the sun and the moon," and the idolaters said, "We worship idols." Against all of them Allah revealed, "Say: He is Allah, One."

Some reports place the question in Makkah with the pagans, and some place it in Madinah with the Jews, so scholars differed over whether the surah is Makkan or Madinan. ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, ʿAṭāʾ, ʿIkrimah and Jābir held that it is Makkan. Qatādah, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk and others held that it is Madinan. Two different reports come from Ibn ʿAbbās, one for each view.

### Why this short verse says so much

Everything that follows in the surah grows out of "One". Because He is one, everyone depends on Him and He depends on no one. Because He is one, He has no son and no father. Because He is one, nothing is equal to Him in any way. Human beings have fathers and children and equals. He has none of these. He has power over everything in the heavens and the earth, and He is eternal, without beginning and without end.

### The inner reading

The verse has also been read as Allah speaking to His Prophet ﷺ: "The strangers have asked you about My lineage. Say: Allah, One." He is one in His essence and His attributes, one in exaltedness and power, one in divinity and lordship, one in having no beginning and no end. He alone is worthy of being God and alone knows how to do what God does. He is generous and lovingly kind, gentle and ever merciful. He knows every secret and every whisper. He created the Throne and the earth. He is near to everyone who draws near to Him, deserving of every praise, hidden from the eyes but seen in the hearts of His friends and shown by His handiwork. As the lover's line puts it: You are far from the eyes, yet You and my heart are in one place.

### In short

- "Say" is an order to the Prophet ﷺ to convey this answer, and it marks him as Allah's Messenger.
- "Allah" is the personal name of the Being who must exist, perfect in every way and free of every flaw.
- "One" (aḥad) means more than a count: He has no parts, no partner, no material, and nothing like Him.
- The verse answered people who asked about Allah's lineage, or whether He was made of gold or silver, and it answers every belief that gives Him a son or a rival.

<<<VERSE 112:2>>>
Having said that Allah is One, the surah now says what kind of One He is: "Allah—the Sustainer ˹needed by all˺". The Arabic is two words, Allāhu ṣ-Ṣamad, and the second is a name of Allah that no single English word carries. It describes the master whom everyone turns to and who turns to no one. Every creature depends on Him for what it needs, and He depends on nothing. This is the second answer to the people who had asked what the Prophet's ﷺ Lord was like, and it rules out not only other gods but every other "helper" that people imagine standing between them and their needs.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah"** is repeated rather than replaced by "He". In the grammar of the verse "Allah" is the subject and aṣ-Ṣamad is what is said about Him, so the verse is a complete sentence of its own: Allah is aṣ-Ṣamad.

**"the Sustainer ˹needed by all˺"** translates aṣ-Ṣamad. The word has several senses in Arabic, and the early commentators explained it in different ways. Its root meaning is the chief who has no one above him and to whom people turn to have their needs and wishes met. Everyone depends on such a chief, but he depends on no one. Applied to Allah, it means the One who is always sought in times of need, the Master to whom all sovereignty belongs and in whom every perfect and beautiful quality is found, the One that all creation turns to.

### How the early scholars explained aṣ-Ṣamad

Ibn ʿAbbās, in a report through ʿIkrimah, said that aṣ-Ṣamad is the One whom all of creation depends on for their needs and their requests. In another report, through ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah, he unfolded the name into a list of perfections: the Master whose mastery is complete, the Noble whose nobility is complete, the Magnificent whose magnificence is complete, the Forbearing whose forbearance is complete, the Knowing whose knowledge is complete, the Wise whose wisdom is complete. He is perfect in every kind of honour and authority, and these attributes fit no one but Him. He has no equal and nothing is like Him. Ibn ʿAbbās ended with words of praise: Glory be to Allah, the One, the Irresistible. Abū Wāʾil, in the report of al-Aʿmash from Shaqīq, put it in a phrase: aṣ-Ṣamad is the Master whose control is complete.

Other explanations were handed down as well. Aṣ-Ṣamad is the Master whose glory is at its height and whom every created being needs. It has also been said to mean the One who does not eat or drink, the One who has no interior, the Everlasting, the Sufficient, and the One who has no entrance and no exit. Aṭ-Ṭabarānī, a leading authority on hadith, gathered all of these interpretations in his book on the Sunnah and concluded that all of them are sound, and that together they take in every attribute that belongs to our Lord. The original sense remains the chief who has no superior and to whom everyone turns for their needs.

The report about the surah's revelation, in the version that at-Tirmidhī and Ibn Jarīr recorded from Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, adds an explanation that ties this name to the next verse. Aṣ-Ṣamad is the One who does not give birth and was not born, because everything born will die, everything that dies leaves an inheritance behind, and Allah does not die and leaves no inheritance.

### What this verse rules out

Verse one answered everyone who denies that there is one God. This verse addresses a subtler error. There are people who worship Allah alone and yet believe that other beings can also fulfil their needs, hopes and ambitions. The name aṣ-Ṣamad rejects that outright. If He alone is the One whom all depend on, then there is no second source of help. All are dependent upon Him and He is not dependent on anyone. He Himself has power over everything, and He reigns over everything in the heavens and the earth.

### The inner reading

Aṣ-Ṣamad is the One from whom sufficiency is sought in need and in whom refuge is sought when fortune turns. He is the One the servants need and cannot do without. The hope of the disobedient and the destitute rests in Him, the remedy for trials comes from His generosity, and the happiness of the poor lies in His majesty and beauty. Blessed is the one whose intimate companion is His name, exalted the one whose share is His remembrance, happy the heart that is bound to Him, pure the tongue that mentions Him, and full of delight the life whose days pass in love for Him. One person is joyful in Paradise and another is joyful in the Friend, and the Friend is the share of the one whose whole aspiration is Him. The lover's lines say it: an eye entirely filled with the form of the Friend is an eye to be happy with, and separating the eye from the Friend is not good, for either He takes the place of the eye or the eye itself is He.

Read this way, aṣ-Ṣamad is also the One who is far too holy to be grasped by the knowledge of created things, by the sight of their eyes or by their recognition. Intellects are bewildered in His majesty, minds are distracted by His beauty, understandings fail to reach His innermost secret, thoughts are overturned by His command, livers bleed from His severity, and hearts melt from recognising Him.

### In short

- Aṣ-Ṣamad is the chief above whom there is no one, whom everyone turns to for their needs, and who needs no one.
- Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as the One all creation depends on, and as the Master perfect in every attribute of mastery, nobility, magnificence, forbearance, knowledge and wisdom.
- Other explanations (the Everlasting, the Sufficient, the One who does not eat or drink, the One with no interior) were all judged sound and together describe our Lord.
- The verse rules out any helper besides Allah, not only any god besides Him.

<<<VERSE 112:3>>>
The people who asked about the Prophet's ﷺ Lord had asked for His lineage, and this is the verse that answers them: "He has never had offspring, nor was He born". Lineage is a chain of parents and children. Allah stands outside any such chain. He has no child and no parent, and the surah states both halves because people have gone wrong on both.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He has never had offspring"** translates lam yalid, He did not beget. He has no son and no daughter, no offspring of any kind, may He be glorified. No likeness of Him can exist, and a child is a likeness of its parent. The phrase is the strongest possible rejection of every creed that gives Allah a child.

**"nor was He born"** translates wa lam yūlad. He has no parent, because being created is ruled out in His case, and whatever is born has been brought into being by another. The two phrases together deny Him any child, any parent and any spouse.

### Why the Creator cannot be part of a family

There is no analogy between Allah, the Creator, and what He has created. Created things come into existence through procreation, one generation from another. Allah is not like that. He is not the child of anyone and He has no children. Unlike human beings, He has no son and no father. He is eternal, without beginning and without end, and so He is a unique Being with no equal.

The report on the surah's revelation gives the reason in a sentence: everything that is born will die, everything that dies leaves an inheritance, and Allah does not die and leaves nothing to be inherited. It has also been said that the verse means He did not inherit and will not be inherited, and that He has no son who will inherit His dominion and no parent from whom He inherited it.

### Those who gave Him a child

Among those who deny Allah's oneness there is a group who ascribe children to Him, and "He has never had offspring" repudiates them in the strongest terms. The Qur'an deals with them at length elsewhere. "And they say: Ar-Rahman has begotten a son. Indeed you have brought forth a terrible evil thing. Whereby the heavens are almost torn, and the earth is split asunder, and the mountains fall in ruins, that they ascribe a son to Ar-Rahman. But it is not suitable for Ar-Rahman that He should beget a son. There is none in the heavens and the earth but comes unto Ar-Rahman as a slave. Verily, He knows each one of them, and has counted them a full counting. And all of them will come to Him alone on the Day of Resurrection" (19:88-95). Those they called His children are in fact His servants: "And they say: Ar-Rahman has begotten a son. Glory to Him! They are but honored servants. They speak not until He has spoken, and they act on His command" (21:26-27). The claim of kinship between Him and the jinn is answered in the same way: "And they have invented a kinship between Him and the Jinn, but the Jinn know well that they have indeed to appear before Him. Glorified is Allah from what they attribute unto Him!" (37:158-159).

Two hadiths from al-Bukhārī show how grave the claim is and how patiently Allah bears it. The Prophet ﷺ said that no one is more patient with something hurtful that he hears than Allah: people attribute a son to Him while He is the One who feeds them and cures them. And in a hadith qudsi that Abū Hurayrah reported, Allah says that the son of Adam denies Him and has no right to, and insults Him and has no right to. The denial is his saying that Allah will never re-create him as He created him the first time, when re-creating him is easier than creating him in the first place. The insult is his saying that Allah has taken a son, when "I am the One, the Self-Sufficient Master. I do not give birth, nor was I born, and there is none comparable to Me."

### In short

- Allah has no child and no parent, and so He has no lineage to ask about.
- A child is a likeness of its parent and a parent is a maker, and neither likeness nor being made is possible for Him.
- Whatever is born dies and leaves an inheritance, and Allah does not die and leaves nothing to inherit.
- Those who ascribe a son to Him are answered in 19:88-95, 21:26-27 and 37:158-159, and in the hadith qudsi where Allah calls the claim an insult that He bears with patience.

<<<VERSE 112:4>>>
The surah closes by shutting the last door through which anything could be set beside Allah: "And there is none comparable to Him." Verse one said He is One, verse two that everyone depends on Him, verse three that He has no family. This verse says that nothing, anywhere, in any respect, is His match. There is none like Him, none equal to Him, and nothing that resembles Him at all.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And there is none"** translates wa lam yakun ... aḥad, "and there has not been anyone". The negation is total. It covers everything that exists, everything that has ever existed and everything that could ever exist.

**"comparable to Him"** translates kufuwan lahu. Kufuw means a match, a like, something of the same kind, or someone equal in rank and standing. So no one is similar to Allah, equal to Him in rank, or like Him in His attributes, His works or His powers in the slightest degree. He has no opposite, no peer and no equal, and no one resembles Him or even begins to. It has also been explained as: He has no rival who could compete with Him for dominion and sovereignty. And He has no equal from among His creation.

A note on the word order. "To Him" (lahu) belongs with "comparable" (kufuwan), yet it comes before it, because having an equal "to Him" is the very thing the verse sets out to deny. "Anyone" (aḥad) is the subject of "there is", yet it is placed last, after "comparable", so that the verse ends on the same sound as the verses before it and the rhyme of the surah holds.

### No spouse, no partner, no relative

Mujāhid explained "none comparable to Him" as: He has no spouse. That reading joins this verse to the one before it, because a child needs a mother. The Qur'an makes the link itself: "He is the Originator of the heavens and the earth. How can He have children when He has no wife? He created all things" (6:101). He owns everything and He created everything. How then could one of His creatures be His peer and equal, or His relative and resemble Him? Glorified, exalted and far removed is He from any such thing.

### The whole surah against every kind of shirk

Taken together, the four verses answer every group that denies Allah's oneness or gives Him partners and rivals. Some deny that God exists at all. Some accept that He exists but deny that His existence is necessary, that He must exist and cannot fail to exist. Some accept that He necessarily exists but deny His attributes of perfection. Some accept all of that but deny His oneness, and worship many gods and goddesses. The first verse, "He is Allah, One", refutes all four. The second verse answers those who worship Allah alone yet believe other helpers can meet their needs, and the name aṣ-Ṣamad denies any such thing. The third verse answers those who ascribe children to Him. And this last verse leaves nothing standing: He is so unique a Being that no one is like Him or equal to Him in any way whatever. The surah is a complete statement of tawḥīd and a complete denial of shirk, which is why it has been called the essence of the Qur'an.

### In short

- Nothing that exists, has existed or could exist is Allah's match in essence, rank, attributes, works or power.
- The word order puts "to Him" early and "anyone" last so that the denial is sharp and the rhyme of the surah holds.
- Mujāhid read the verse as denying Him a spouse, which fits with 6:101: He has no children because He has no wife, and He created everything.
- The four verses together answer the atheist, the one who denies His necessary existence, the one who denies His perfection, the polytheist, the one who looks to other helpers, and the one who gives Him a son.
