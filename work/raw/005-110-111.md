<<<VERSE 5:110>>>
On the Day of Gathering a particular address is given: “O Jesus, son of Mary! Remember My favour upon you and your mother”. A long list of favours follows, and each miracle is tied to Allah's permission. The verse ends with the response of the disbelievers among the Children of Israel, who called clear proofs plain magic.

The listing is not merely honour for 'Isa. It answers two opposite errors, one that abused him and one that exalted him beyond his place.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹on Judgment Day˺ Allah will say"** places the address on the Day of Resurrection. The past tense used throughout indicates a matter already settled that will certainly occur, and the verse contains some of the secrets of the unseen that Allah revealed to His Messenger Muhammad ﷺ.

**"“O Jesus, son of Mary! Remember My favour upon you"** recalls the blessing given to him when Allah created him from his mother without male intervention and made him a sign and clear proof of His perfect power over all things. One gloss describes the favour as the bestowal of prophethood, and another as creating him without a father.

**"and your mother:"** recalls the favour to Maryam. Allah made him testify to her chastity, and so he cleared her of the charge brought by unjust and ignorant liars. Another gloss describes the favour to her as being graced with complete surrender to Allah and with worship, and another as being chosen above the women of her time.

**"how I supported you with the holy spirit"** names the angel Jibra'il, the pure Gabriel. He taught and helped 'Isa to speak to people, and Allah made him a prophet calling to Allah in the cradle and in manhood.

**"so you spoke to people in ˹your˺ infancy and adulthood."** means that he called people to Allah in childhood and in maturity. The word used is explained as meaning invited, since his speaking as a child is not remarkable in itself as mere speech. In his mother's lap and in his cot he said that he is Allah's servant and His anointed one, and Gabriel helped him after his thirtieth year when he said that he is Allah's Messenger.

**"How I taught you writing, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel."** The Book and wisdom are explained as the power of writing and understanding. Another gloss describes the Scripture as the scriptures of the earlier prophets, or writing with the pen, and wisdom as the wisdom of earlier sages, or as the lawful and the unlawful. One account says the Torah, revealed to Musa son of 'Imran who spoke with Allah directly, was taught to him in his mother's womb and the Gospel after his birth. Another describes being taught the secrets of the sacred law, its benefits and its wisdom.

**"How you moulded a bird from clay—by My Will—and breathed into it and it became a ˹real˺ bird—by My Will."** describes shaping the figure of a bird from clay by Allah's permission, breathing into it, and its becoming a bird with a soul, flying in the air, by Allah's permission, command and will. One gloss names the bird as a bat, and describes the breathing as like the breath of a sleeping person.

**"How you healed the blind and the lepers—by My Will."** concerns healing the person born blind and the leper by Allah's command, will and power. The subject was explained earlier in Surah Āl-'Imrān and is not repeated here. One account describes curing the person born blind and treating the leper and healing his skin.

**"How you brought the dead to life—by My Will."** means that he called them and they rose from their graves by Allah's leave, power, intent and will. Another describes raising the dead by Allah's will and through Him giving life to them, and a third describes bringing them to life by praying to Allah to give them life.

**"How I prevented the Children of Israel from harming you when you came to them with clear proofs"** recalls Allah stopping them from harming him when he brought clear proofs and evidence testifying to his prophethood and message. They refused him, accused him of magic and tried to kill him by crucifixion, but Allah saved him, raised him to Himself, purified him from their vulgarity and protected him from their harm.

One gloss specifies that they were restrained when they had decided to kill him, and that the clear proofs were commands, prohibitions and the wondrous things he showed them.

**"and the disbelievers among them said, “This is nothing but pure magic.”"** gives their response. What he was showing them was, they said, nothing but evident sorcery. A variant reading gives the word as sorcerer, so that the sense is that he is nothing but a manifest sorcerer.

### Why are the favours listed at this point?

The verse before spoke of prophets in general, with a question asked and answered. From here to the end of the chapter the text speaks of 'Isa, the last prophet of Bani Isra'il, and mentions Allah's blessings on him, together with a particular question put to him on the Day of Gathering and its answer in the verses that follow.

The purpose is to display a disturbing sight to Bani Isra'il and to all creation. When the one called the Spirit of Allah and the Word of Allah is asked on the plain of gathering why his people made him a partner with Allah, he will be distressed, despite being a great prophet, and will hasten to plead that he is free of what his followers did.

He does so in more than one way. First he declares that Allah is pure and that it does not befit him to say what is not right for him. Then he pleads his innocence from another angle, calling Allah to witness that had he said such a thing Allah would have known it, since Allah knows what is in his heart and he does not know what is in Allah's, and Allah alone has full knowledge of the unseen.

Only after that does he answer the main question. He taught his people precisely what he was commanded to teach, namely to worship Allah, his Lord and their Lord. As long as he lived among them he was a witness to what they said and did, and none of them said such things about him at that time. After his Lord raised him to Himself they remained under Allah's watch, and it is He who knows their words and deeds fully and truly.

### How does the listing admonish two groups at once?

By placing the bestowal of blessings alongside the scene of answerability, both groups among Bani Isra'il are admonished. One group insulted, accused and harassed him, and the other took him for God or a son of God.

The description of the blessings admonishes the first group, and the mention of the questions and answers warns the second. The juxtaposition is deliberate, and neither half of the passage can be read alone.

### What is significant about speaking in infancy and in middle age?

One sentence in the long description deserves particular attention, the statement that he spoke to people in the cradle and while middle aged. A special miracle given to 'Isa was that he talked to people as a child and also talks to them in middle age.

The first is obviously a miracle and a particular blessing, since children cannot speak meaningfully in the early period after birth. A child who begins to talk in the cradle or on his mother's lap has been given a distinction.

Speaking while middle aged does not appear worth mentioning, because everyone speaks at that age. Consider the circumstances of 'Isa, however, and this too appears as a miracle. He was raised from the earth before reaching middle age, so his speaking to people on earth after reaching it becomes possible only if he returns to this world.

That is the collective belief of Muslims, established on clear statements of the Qur'an and Sunnah. Just as his speech as a child was a miracle, his speech in middle age because of his return to this world is nothing other than a miracle. One gloss draws the same inference, that this implies he will descend before the Hour, since he was raised up to Allah before middle age, as already mentioned in Surah Āl-'Imrān.

### Where does 'Isa stand among the prophets?

Among the prophets he is an intermediate link between the earlier prophets and the last of the prophets. He was given very special miracles to perform. Those who embraced faith through him were very few in number, and his opponents among the Jews held worldly power.

Despite that, they could not inflict harm on him and did not succeed in destroying his companions. The result of those miracles should have been that people accepted the religion he brought. What happened instead was that his opponents dismissed him, saying that his miracles were merely feats of magic.

Those who embraced his faith later gave him the status of a god. On the Day of Judgment it will be made clear to those claiming to follow him that every feat he performed was done at Allah's behest, and that it was Allah who saved him from the dangers his opponents presented.

When this was the true state of affairs, and 'Isa himself testifies to it while standing before them, his people will have to explain who gave them the religion they wrongly attributed to him.

### In short

- The favours recalled cover his creation without a father, his mother's vindication, support through Gabriel, speech in infancy and maturity, and being taught writing, wisdom, the Torah and the Gospel.
- Every miracle is tied to Allah's permission, command and will, from the clay bird to healing and raising the dead.
- Allah restrained the Children of Israel from killing him after they called his clear proofs evident sorcery.
- The listing admonishes both those who abused him and those who deified him, and the questions that follow answer the second group.
- His speech in middle age is read as pointing to his return before the Hour, since he was raised before reaching it.

<<<VERSE 5:111>>>
Another favour is recalled: "And how I inspired the disciples, “Believe in Me and My messenger!”" The inspiration came from Allah, and the response was immediate. They believed and asked to be witnesses that they had submitted.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And how I inspired the disciples"** is also a reminder of Allah's favour to 'Isa, in making disciples and companions for him. The word used is explained as inspired, and Allah inspired them just as He inspired the mother of Musa to suckle him (28:7), and just as the Lord inspired the bee to take habitations in the mountains, the trees and what people erect, then to eat of all fruits and follow the ways of its Lord made easy for it (16:68-69).

Al-Hasan Al-Basri says of the hawariyyun that Allah inspired them, and As-Suddi says that He put it in their hearts. One gloss describes the inspiration as a command given to them through the tongue of 'Isa.

The disciples are described as twelve in number.

**"“Believe in Me and My messenger!”"** calls them to faith in Allah and in His messenger 'Isa. The command joins belief in the sender with belief in the one sent, and does not separate them.

**"They declared, “We believe"** is their answer. They believed in Allah and in His messenger 'Isa. One account renders the reply in the singular, as a believer saying that he believes and asking his Lord to witness that he submits in devotion.

**"and bear witness that we fully submit ˹to Allah˺.”"** asks for testimony to their submission. 'Isa is addressed as a witness, and they also bore witness for one another, being sincere in worship and in declaring Allah's oneness.

### What does the inspiration of the disciples show?

The one who calls people to truth performs the duty of invitation, but a positive response to that call cannot be given unless it is inspired by Allah. Even after being convinced that a call is true, a person does not necessarily go on to accept it, because many obstacles remain.

The caller appears as an ordinary man. Acceptance seems likely to crumble the structure of a life already built. And if this is the truth, the person wonders, were certain great men strangers to it?

That is a critical point at which a person cannot decide, even having reached the stage of decision. It is the point at which Allah helps him. He takes by the hand someone in whom He sees some good, leads him across the borders of doubt and brings him into the region of certainty.

The disciples' answer is therefore presented as a favour to 'Isa as much as to them. A messenger's success is not produced by his own eloquence, and the response of twelve men is itself a gift.

### In short

- Allah inspired the disciples, twelve in number, to believe in Him and in His messenger 'Isa.
- The inspiration is compared with Allah's inspiration of the mother of Musa and of the bee.
- They answered with belief and asked to be witnesses that they had submitted, being sincere in worship and in Allah's oneness.
- Acceptance of a call is itself a gift, since a convinced person may still be held back by obstacles that only divine help removes.
