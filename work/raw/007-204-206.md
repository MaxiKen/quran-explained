<<<VERSE 7:204>>>
After Allah described the Qur'an as clear evidence, guidance and mercy, He commanded that it be listened to whenever it is recited, out of respect and honour for it: "When the Quran is recited, listen to it attentively and be silent, so you may be shown mercy". The Holy Qur'an has come as a mercy for the believers. But to benefit from that mercy there are conditions and rules of conduct, and this verse states them as a general command.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When the Quran is recited,"** One explanation says this refers to recitation in the prescribed prayers. Another says it was revealed about keeping silent during the sermon in the mosque, called "the Qur'an" here because the sermon contains it. Others say it concerns silence and attention whenever the Qur'an is recited.

**"listen to it attentively"** Lend your ears to its recitation and pay heed. Do not talk, and do not busy yourself with anything else. Listening here includes not only hearing it but obeying it and striving to act on its commands.

**"and be silent,"**: refraining from speech while it is being recited.

**"so you may be shown mercy"** Do this so that Allah may have mercy on you and you are not punished. These closing words make it clear that the Qur'an's mercy depends on observing this etiquette. By the same token, whoever flouts these rules and shows disrespect to the Qur'an deserves Allah's wrath, not His mercy.

### Why was this command given?

This command runs against the practice of the pagans of Quraysh, who said: "Do not listen to this Quran but drown it out so that you may prevail." (41:26). Ibn Jarīr reported that Ibn Masʿūd said they used to greet one another with salām during the prayer, and then this verse was revealed.

There are different reports about the background of these verses. They differ over whether the command concerns recitation in the prayer, in the sermon (khuṭbah), or recitation of any kind, in prayer, sermon or anywhere else. According to most scholars, the correct view is that the verse's words are general, so its rule too applies generally, except on some particular occasions.

### Does the follower recite behind the imam?

The followers of the Ḥanafī school take this verse as proof that those praying behind an imam should not recite. Even the jurists who tell followers to recite al-Fātiḥah behind the imam have kept this verse in view. They say the follower should recite only when the imam pauses. This is not the place to pursue that debate, which is treated in the standard works of the scholars.

The main point of the verse is that those for whom the Qur'an is declared a mercy must recognise the etiquette and respect it is owed, and observe it in practice. The chief etiquette is that listeners give it their ears when it is recited and stay silent.

### Silence in prayer and in the sermon

Muslims generally know that they must listen and keep silent when the Qur'an is recited in the prayer, though they fall short in practice. Some do not even notice which surah the imam is reciting. Such people must realise the greatness of the Qur'an and listen to it attentively.

The same rule applies to the Friday sermon, the khuṭbah of Jumuʿah. Besides this verse, the Holy Prophet ﷺ said of the sermon that once the imam comes out, there is no prayer and no conversation. A hadith also says that no one should speak to anyone during the sermon, not even to tell someone else to be quiet. If that must be done, a sign with the hand is enough. The point is that during the sermon no conversation of any kind is allowed, nor tasbīḥ, nor invoking blessings on the Prophet ﷺ, nor prayer, nor anything similar. Jurists say the rule for the Friday sermon also applies to the sermons of the two ʿĪds and the sermon of nikāḥ, marriage. At those times listening and keeping silent is wājib, obligatory.

### Must everyone listen when someone recites aloud?

When someone recites on his own, outside prayer and sermon, must others keep silent and listen? Is it wājib for them? The jurists differ here. Some hold that listening and keeping silent is wājib in this case too, and that acting otherwise is a sin. So they rule that no one may recite the Qur'an aloud where people are busy at work or resting, and that whoever does so is a sinner. This appears in Khulāṣat al-Fatāwā and other works of fiqh.

Other jurists distinguish between situations. They say attentive listening is wājib only where the Qur'an is recited expressly to be listened to, as in the prayer and the sermon. If someone is reciting on his own, or several people are each reciting in one place, listening and silence are not wājib. Sound hadiths prove that the Holy Prophet ﷺ used to recite aloud in his night prayers while his blessed wives were asleep, and at times his voice could be heard outside his room.

A hadith in al-Bukhārī and Muslim says that the Holy Prophet ﷺ halted on a journey. In the morning he said he had recognised his Ashʿarī companions by the voices of their recitation in the dark of night. He could locate their tents by them, though by day he had not known where they were staying. He never asked these companions why they recited aloud, nor told those asleep to get up and listen.

On the basis of reports like these, jurists allow some latitude for recitation outside prayer. But they all consider it better to listen and keep silent whenever the sound of the Qur'an's recitation reaches one, even outside prayer. For the same reason, it is not right to recite loudly where people are resting or working.

This shows the error of turning radios up to full volume when a Qur'an recitation is broadcast, especially in places and gatherings where people will not listen attentively. Likewise, it is wrong for mosques to broadcast recitation over loudspeakers late at night in a way that disturbs people's sleep or work.

ʿAllāmah ibn al-Humām wrote that when the imam in prayer, or the preacher in the sermon, recites something about Jannah or Jahannam, it is not permissible even to pray for Paradise or seek refuge from Hell at that moment. According to this verse, Allah's promise of mercy is for the one who stays silent while the Qur'an is recited, and it does not apply to one who does not. In voluntary prayers, though, supplicating silently after such verses is established by the Sunnah and brings reward.

### Why not miracles, but the Word?

The Makkans used to ask the Prophet ﷺ why, if he was Allah's Prophet, he did not bring miracles from Allah. It would indeed have been easy for Allah to send His messenger with miracles. But then the real purpose of his coming would have been defeated. Suppose he had been given a modern car fitted with loudspeakers to help spread his message. Fifteen hundred years ago, such a car would have been a breathtaking miracle. People's attention would have been drawn away from the message.

The real purpose of the Prophet's coming was that Allah's word should make people think deeply. They were to use their minds to reflect on the words of Allah, purify their souls and come into contact with Him. With miracles, their attention would have been diverted, and they would have lost themselves in the miracles instead of in Allah. True religion consists in giving attention to the word of Allah, so as to build a deep relationship with Him.

### The inner reading: two kinds of listening

Listening, samāʿ, is the reality of giving ear to the Qur'an. It gives more life to a person's days than the spirit gives to the body. It is a spring that wells up from the heart, fed by the well of truthfulness. Truthfulness is to listening what the sun's disc is to its rays. As long as the darkness of mortal nature has not lifted from the heart, the sun of true listening will not show itself in the desert of a person's breast.

Listening is of two kinds, that of ordinary people and that of the elect. Ordinary people's share of listening is the sound and its melodies. The elect's share is a subtlety within the sound, its meanings and allusions. Ordinary people listen with the ear of the head, the faculty of discernment and the stirring of their natures, to be freed of sorrow and eased of their cares. The elect listen with a dead self, a thirsty heart and a burning breath. Their harvest is the breeze of intimacy, remembrance without end and happiness that lasts forever.

Some say that listening, in its reality, means recalling the eternal call that came on the Day of the Covenant, from the court of Allah's compelling might and the side of His oneness: "Am I not your Lord?" (7:172). That call reached the servants' hearing, and its taste reached their spirits. It is stored in that world, and it settles, to use the Qur'an's word for a dwelling (11:6), in the spirit. The mark is what testifies to it. What gives it expression is its title. Reports give only supposition, but finding gives face-to-face vision. The servant's seven members all listen to the Friend, for the Friend's call does not belong to the present moment. It is everlasting.

### In short

- The Qur'an's mercy is for those who listen to it attentively and keep silent when it is recited, unlike the pagans who tried to drown it out.
- Silence is obligatory during recitation in prayer and during the Friday, ʿĪd and marriage sermons.
- Outside prayer, jurists allow some latitude, but it is better to listen, and wrong to recite loudly where it disturbs people at rest or work.
- The Prophet ﷺ was given the Word, not spectacles, so that people would reflect on it and come close to Allah.

<<<VERSE 7:205>>>
The previous verse dealt with listening to the Qur'an and the etiquette due to it. Most scholars hold that this verse and the next give the command to remember Allah in general, with its etiquette, which includes reciting the Qur'an: "Remember your Lord inwardly with humility and reverence and in a moderate tone of voice, both morning and evening. And do not be one of the heedless". In short, the verse commands people to remember Allah and tells them when and how to do it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Remember your Lord inwardly"** Literally, "remember your Lord in yourself", that is, in secret. According to ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās, "remembrance" (dhikr) here means the Qur'an, and the etiquette stated concerns its recitation. This makes little difference, since all agree that the other forms of remembrance (adhkār) follow the same command and etiquette. One explanation addresses the Prophet ﷺ: recite on your own, even when you are leading the prayer.

**"with humility and reverence"** Humbly, with lowliness before Him, and with fear and awe of Him, eager for His mercy and afraid of His punishment.

**"and in a moderate tone of voice,"** Literally, "without loudness in words": louder than a whisper and quieter than speaking out, a middle way between the two. Call on Him moderately, not too loudly. So it is recommended that remembrance of Allah not be done in a loud voice.

**"both morning and evening."** At the day's start and its close, since these hours have special qualities. One explanation applies it to the morning prayer and the evening prayers, Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ.

**"And do not be one of the heedless"**: do not be among those who neglect the remembrance of Allah. One explanation applies it to neglecting recitation in prayer, whether praying alone or leading others.

### When was this command given?

Allah commands that He be remembered especially in the mornings and afternoons, just as He commanded worship at these two times: "And glorify the praises of your Lord before sunrise and before sunset" (50:39). This verse was revealed in Makkah before the night of the Isrāʾ, when the five daily prayers were made obligatory, and it ordered that Allah be worshipped at these times.

The words "morning and evening" may mean that one should remember Allah no less than twice daily, once at each end of the day. They may also stand for every hour of the day and night, as "East and West" can stand for the whole world. Then the verse would mean staying devoted to remembrance and recitation always, in every state. ʿĀʾishah said that the Holy Prophet ﷺ remembered Allah at all times and in every condition.

### Why should remembrance be quiet?

The Companions once asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ whether their Lord was near, so that they should call on Him quietly, or far, so that they should raise their voices. Allah then sent down: "When My servants ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about Me: I am truly near. I respond to one’s prayer when they call upon Me." (2:186). The hadith of Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī in the two Ṣaḥīḥs, given under 7:55, makes the same point: those who raised their voices in duʿāʾ were told that they were not calling on someone deaf or absent. These texts urge people to remember Allah often, especially morning and evening, so that they are not among those who neglect Him.

### Remembrance of the heart and of the tongue

The verse gives two options for dhikr. It may be done secretly and silently, or openly and aloud. Silent remembrance, "in yourself", takes two forms. In the first, one does not move the tongue at all but simply thinks of Allah and His attributes in the heart. This is called al-dhikr al-khafī, hidden remembrance, or tafakkur, reflection. In the second, the tongue follows what is happening deep in the heart, and the divine names are spoken in a lowered voice.

The best way is first to understand the meaning of what one is saying. Then one should do two things together: keep its meaning present in the heart with full attention, and also speak it aloud. In this form the tongue joins the heart in remembrance. Someone who stays with the thought in his heart alone, without saying a word, also earns reward. The lowest degree is to say the words of dhikr while the heart stays empty and heedless of them. Of such remembrance the great sage Rūmī said: "On the tongue, 'Glory be to Allah', and in the heart, cow and donkey! What remembrance is that? How much effect will it have?"

Rūmī meant that remembrance from a heedless heart brings none of the traces and blessings of true dhikr. He did not deny the reward and benefit of even a dry verbal dhikr. Sometimes that very verbal dhikr becomes the channel and cause of remembrance in the heart, since constant repetition by the tongue begins to affect the heart too. Even if that does not happen, at least one part of the body is busy with the remembrance of Allah, and that too has benefit and reward. So people who feel no peace, attention or presence in their dhikr and tasbīḥ should not give it up as useless. They should keep at it and keep striving for the attention it needs.

### How loud may one be?

The second option comes in the words "without loudness": a lowered voice rather than a raised one. A person doing dhikr may do it aloud, but its etiquette forbids raising the voice to shouting and screaming. It is better done in an even voice that respects the reverence the act deserves. Very loud dhikr or recitation shows a lack of respect in the heart for the One addressed. No one naturally raises his voice before someone he truly reveres. So when dhikr or recitation is done aloud, one should take care not to raise the voice more than needed.

In sum, the verse yields three ways of remembering Allah and reciting the Qur'an:

1. Remembrance of the heart alone. One reflects on the meanings of the Qur'an and of the dhikr while keeping the tongue entirely still.
2. Moving the tongue along with the heart's remembrance, without raising the voice so that others hear. Both of these come under "remember your Lord in yourself".
3. Moving the tongue with the voice, with the heart attentive, but no louder than needed, at a moderate level. This is meant by "without loudness". Another verse explains it further, telling the Prophet ﷺ not to recite too loudly or too softly but to keep to a middle course: "Do not recite your prayers too loudly or silently, but seek a way between" (17:110).

### Abū Bakr's quiet voice and ʿUmar's loud one

The Holy Prophet ﷺ gave this same instruction to Abū Bakr and ʿUmar about recitation in prayer. According to an authentic hadith, he went out late one night. At Abū Bakr's house he found him praying and reciting in a low voice. At ʿUmar's house he found him reciting aloud. In the morning he told Abū Bakr that he had heard him reciting softly. Abū Bakr replied that the One he was reciting to had heard him, and that was enough. He told ʿUmar that he had heard him reciting loudly. ʿUmar said he raised his voice to drive away both sleep and Satan. The Prophet ﷺ then told Abū Bakr to raise his voice a little and ʿUmar to lower his somewhat. Abū Dāwūd recorded this.

At-Tirmidhī reports that some Companions asked ʿĀʾishah whether the Holy Prophet ﷺ recited aloud or softly. She said that sometimes he recited aloud and sometimes softly. He recited both ways.

Some early elders preferred reciting aloud in voluntary (nafl) prayers at night and in recitation outside the prayer, while others preferred reciting softly. So Imām Abū Ḥanīfah said the reciter may choose either. All agree, though, that reciting aloud has conditions. First, there must be no fear of showing off. Second, the reciter's voice must not trouble or hurt others. It must not interfere with their prayer or recitation, their work or their rest. In such cases, all agree that reciting quietly is better.

What applies to reciting the Qur'an applies also to other forms of dhikr and tasbīḥ. They may be done aloud or quietly, provided the voice is not so loud as to go against humility of heart and the etiquette of devotion. And the voice of the one remembering and glorifying Allah must not disturb people who are working or resting. Which is better, loud or quiet, depends on the person and the circumstances, as was also said under 7:55. For some, aloud is better, and for others quietly. At some times aloud is better, and at others a low voice or silence is more beneficial.

### Humility and fear

The verse's second rule of etiquette is that recitation and dhikr be done humbly. Humility comes from realising that Allah is great and most exalted, and from keeping in view the meaning of what one is saying.

The third rule comes from the word khīfah, fear, in this same verse. One should recite and remember Allah in a state of awe and fear. It is the fear of failing to do justice to the great task of worshipping Allah and recognising His greatness, of falling short or doing something unworthy of the reverence He is owed. One should also be keenly aware of one's sins and fear His punishment, and fear one's own end, since no one knows how it will be. So dhikr and recitation should be done like a person overwhelmed by awe and fear.

These rules also appear earlier in this surah, where they concern prayer: "Call upon your Lord humbly and secretly" (7:55). There the word is khufyah, "secretly", rather than khīfah, "fear", meaning softly, in a low voice. It suggests that dhikr and recitation too should be done softly. But the present verse makes clear that voiced dhikr is not forbidden. One simply must not raise the voice more than needed, and certainly not so high that fear and humility disappear.

### Who forgets Allah, and who cannot?

To give up remembering Allah and fall in with the heedless is a grievous loss. True religion softens the devotee's heart, and he becomes someone who always remembers Allah. His heart and mind are filled with Allah's majesty, making him truly humble and fearful of Him. Once a person frees himself from arrogance, he is so absorbed in Allah's wonders in creation that he cannot help remembering Allah always, from the depths of his heart.

The rememberers are of three kinds. The first has a tongue that remembers and a heart that is unaware. Another remembers with tongue and heart together, but his work is still at risk, for it has been said that those who purify themselves are in grave danger. A third is silent of tongue, but his heart is drowned in Allah.

The Pir of the Ṭarīqah said: "O Allah, how can I remember You, when I myself am all remembrance? I have scattered the harvest of my traces to the wind." He asked how he could remember One he had never forgotten. "O remembrance of the spirits, remembered by the hearts and named by the tongues! Remember me with Your bounty and make me happy with gentle remembrance!"

### In short

- Allah commands that He be remembered inwardly, humbly and fearfully, in a moderate voice, morning and evening, and that no one be heedless.
- Remembrance may be in the heart alone, in a low voice, or aloud at a moderate level, and the best joins the heart's attention to the tongue.
- The Prophet ﷺ told Abū Bakr to raise his voice a little and ʿUmar to lower his, and reciting aloud must never show off or disturb others.
- Even remembrance with a heedless heart should be kept up, since it can lead the heart to remembrance.

<<<VERSE 7:206>>>
The surah closes by holding up a model to follow: "Surely those ˹angels˺ nearest to your Lord are not too proud to worship Him. They glorify Him. And to Him they prostrate". This last verse describes the state and manner of those close to Allah, so that others may learn from them. Allah praises the angels, who glorify Him night and day without tiring. He reminds His servants of this so that they will imitate the angels in tireless worship and obedience.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Surely those ˹angels˺ nearest to your Lord"** Those who are "with" Allah are those He approves of and accepts. They include every angel, every blessed prophet, and the most righteous among the Muslim community, men and women alike.

**"are not too proud to worship Him."** They do not disdain to worship and obey Him, or to acknowledge that they are His servants. They put on no airs and do not fall short in worship from a false sense of being great or special. Instead, they see themselves as weak and needy before Allah. They give themselves to worship with dedication and never tire.

**"They glorify Him."** They declare Him free of everything unworthy of Him, praising Him day and night.

**"And to Him they prostrate"** They devote their submission and worship to Him alone, showing their utmost humility by prostrating before their Lord. So be like them!

### Why prostrate at this verse?

This is the first place in the Qur'an where, by agreement of the scholars, prostration is prescribed for those who recite the Qur'an and those who listen to its recitation. Prostrating here, at the mention of the angels' prostration, is legitimate. Sūrat al-Aʿrāf ends here, and its last verse is a verse of prostration, sajdah, requiring both reciter and listener to prostrate.

A hadith asks why people do not line up for prayer as the angels line up before their Lord. The angels complete the first rows, then the next, and stand close together in their rows.

According to Abū Hurairah, Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim reports that the Holy Prophet ﷺ described Satan's reaction to such prostration. When a son of Adam recites a verse of prostration and then prostrates, Satan runs off weeping. "Alas!" he says. "Man was told to prostrate, and he obeyed, so Paradise is his home. I was told to prostrate and disobeyed, so Hell is my home."

### The worth of prostration

Prostration is singled out here among the parts of the prayer because it holds a special place among them. Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim reports that a man asked Thawbān to tell him a deed that would take him to Paradise. Thawbān stayed silent. The man asked again, and again he stayed silent. When he asked a third time, Thawbān said he had put the same question to the Holy Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ told him to prostrate often, because for every prostration Allah raises a person one rank and forgives one sin. The man later met Abū al-Dardāʾ, asked him the same question, and received the same answer.

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim also reports from Abū Hurairah that the Holy Prophet ﷺ said a servant is closest to his Lord when prostrating, so in prostration one should supplicate earnestly, since it is very likely to be answered. Supplication within the prostration of the prayer belongs to voluntary (nafl) prayers, using the Arabic supplications found in the Qur'an or the Sunnah. In a prostration made purely for supplication, one may pray in any language.

Prostration on its own is not a recognised act of worship. So, according to Imām Abū Ḥanīfah, "prostrating often" means praying many voluntary prayers, since more voluntary prayers naturally bring more prostrations. Still, there is no harm if someone prostrates on its own and supplicates in it. The instruction to supplicate in prostration applies to voluntary prayers and is not obligatory.

### What does nearness to Allah look like?

This verse also teaches that those given the ability to worship and remember Allah constantly carry a sign that they are "with" Allah at all times, blessed with His company. People are told to develop an angelic character so that they may enjoy the company of angels in the next life. Whoever reaches that state attains the level of the angels. The highest thing a person can achieve in this world is to develop, while still human, an angelic character.

In the inner reading, "those nearest to your Lord" points to the centre of togetherness (jamʿ), and "are not too proud to worship Him" speaks of the attribute of dispersion (farq). Their being "with" Him affirms their nobility, and it keeps them within the rules of servanthood. So the servant moves between togetherness and dispersion. Togetherness reveals the Ḥaqīqah, the Reality, and dispersion sets out the Sharīʿah. The verse "To each of you We have ordained a code of law and a way of life" (5:48) alludes to this.

### In short

- The angels and those closest to Allah never grow too proud to worship Him. They glorify Him and prostrate to Him, and people are told to be like them.
- This is the first verse of prostration in the Qur'an, and Satan weeps when a believer prostrates at it.
- A servant is closest to Allah in prostration, and each prostration raises a rank and removes a sin.
- Constant worship and remembrance are signs of being near Allah, and an angelic character is the highest human achievement.
