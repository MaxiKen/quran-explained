<<<VERSE 7:204>>>
Verse 204 lays down the first rule of conduct owed to the Book. "When the Quran is recited, listen to it attentively and be silent, so you may be shown mercy"

### Phrase by phrase

After Allah called the Qur'an a clear evidence, a guidance and a mercy for mankind, He commanded that it be heard with respect and honour when it is recited. The words set the conduct of the believers against the conduct of the pagans of Quraysh, who had said to one another, "Listen not to this Qur'an, and make noise in the midst of its recitation" (41:26).

The report of Ibn Mas'ud gives the setting of the command. He said that the Companions used to greet one another with the greeting of peace while they were praying, and that this verse came down, that they should listen when the Qur'an is recited. The greeting during the prayer was set aside, and attention to the recitation took its place.

**"listen to it attentively"** carries more than the work of the ear. The teachers say that lending the ear to the Book includes obeying what it says and making the effort to act on its orders. **"and be silent"** means that a person should not speak while it is being recited, and should not be busy with anything else. One short account of the verse adds the point of the prayer, that the command concerns the recitation during the prescribed prayers, and that the one who gives ear and pays heed will obtain mercy and not be punished. The closing words of the verse, **"so you may be shown mercy"**, show that the mercy of the Book is tied to the conduct asked for. It is a clear hint that the blessing depends on keeping the rule of respect that the verse names. A person who turns the rule aside and shows disrespect to the Book moves away from mercy and earns the anger of Allah instead.

### Where the rule applies

The reports differ on the setting of the verse, whether it concerns the recitation in the salah, or the recitation in the khutbah, or the recitation of the Qur'an in general, in the prayer, in the sermon or in any other place. Most of the teachers hold that the words are general, and that the rule they lay down is general too, with a few particular occasions set aside from it.

The followers of the Hanafi school take the verse as proof that a person praying behind an imam should not recite for himself. Even those jurists who direct the praying person to recite the opening surah behind the imam hold this verse in view. They suggest that his recitation be limited to an interval when the imam is silent. The reports leave the wider debate on the point to the books of the jurists, and the main subject of the verse stands clear without it. The people for whom the Book was declared to be a mercy should recognise the respect that belongs to it and keep to that respect in practice.

The same rule applies to the sermon of the Friday prayer, which is the Jumu'ah khutbah. Muslims generally know that they are to listen to the recitation in the prayer and keep silent, though they fall short in practice, and some do not even know which surah the imam is reciting. The Prophet ﷺ said of the sermon that when the imam comes out for the khutbah, then there is no surah and no conversation. A hadith of the Prophet ﷺ goes further, that no one should say anything to anyone during the khutbah, and that even a word of advice spoken to another person to keep silence is not allowed, with a sign of the hand being enough for that purpose. The aim of the rule is that during the sermon no conversation of any kind should take place, and no tasbih, no durud and no prayer, nor any act of similar nature. The jurists add that the rule which holds for the Friday sermon holds also for the sermon of the two Eid prayers and for the sermon of the nikah, since listening to those and keeping silence during them are wajib.

### Recitation outside the prayer

Where a person recites on his own in an ordinary setting, the question arises whether others must keep silent and listen. The jurists differ on the point. Some hold that listening and silence are wajib in this case too, and that turning away from it is a sin. On that view it is not permitted for anyone to recite the Qur'an in a loud voice in a place where people are busy with their work or taking their rest. A person who recites loudly in such a place is counted a sinner. Some of the juristic works state this position plainly.

Others draw a line between the occasions. They say that attentive listening is wajib where the recitation is made for the purpose of being heard, as in the prayer and the sermon. Where a person recites on his own, or where several people recite in one place, silence and listening are not wajib. They prove this from sound reports of the conduct of the Prophet ﷺ, who used to recite the Qur'an in his night prayer in a raised voice while his wives were asleep, and whose voice could be heard from outside his room. A hadith in al-Bukhari and Muslim tells of a journey of the Prophet ﷺ. He halted for the night, and when morning came he said that he had recognised his companions of the tribe of Al-Ash'ari by the sound of their recitation in the darkness of the night, and had been able to tell the direction and the place of their tents, though in the daytime he had no idea where they were staying. The Prophet ﷺ did not ask those men why they recited so loudly. He did not tell those who were sleeping that they had to get up and listen to the recitation.

On the strength of reports like these, the jurists give some room in the case of recitation outside the prayer. They all count it better and more fitting to listen and keep silence when the sound of recitation reaches a person, even outside the prayer. For that reason it is not proper to recite the Qur'an in a loud voice in a place where people are resting or working.

This shows the error in a practice of the present day, when a person turns his radio to full volume at the time a recitation programme is broadcast. This is especially so in a place or a gathering where the people will not listen with attention. The same holds for relaying the recitation through the public address system of a mosque late at night in a way that disturbs the sleep of those sleeping or the work of those working.

### The word of Ibn al-Humam on the promise

The scholar Ibn al-Humam wrote on this subject. He said that when the imam in the prayer, or the speaker in the sermon, is reciting something about Jannah or about Jahannam, then at that moment it is not permitted for the listener even to pray for Jannah or to seek refuge from Jahannam. The reason is that the promise of mercy in the verse is given to the one who keeps silent while the Qur'an is recited. The promise does not reach the one who does not keep silent. If a person makes a silent supplication after such verses during his nafl prayers, that is an act proved from the sunnah and it is worthy of reward.

### The listening of the heart

One of the spiritual commentaries opens the verse out into its inner sense. It says that listening is the reality of giving ear to the Qur'an, and that through it a man's days gain more life than his body gains from the spirit. Listening is a spring that bubbles up from the middle of the heart, and the well of truthfulness feeds it. As long as the darkness of human nature has not lifted from the heart, the sun of listening will not be allowed to show itself in the field of a man's breast.

The same commentary divides listening into two kinds. The listening of ordinary people is one thing, and the listening of the elect is another. The share of ordinary people in listening is the sound and its melodies. The share of the elect is a subtlety inside the sound, together with its meanings and its allusions. Ordinary people listen with the ear of the head, which is the organ of discernment, and with the movement of their natures. The aim is that they may be free of sorrow and at ease from their concerns. The elect bring to it a soul that has died to itself, a heart that is thirsty and a breath that has been burnt. They gather the fruit of the breeze of intimacy, of endless reminder and of everlasting happiness.

The reality of listening, in this account, is the remembrance of the eternal call that came out on the Day of the Compact from the court of the All-Compelling and from the side of unity, the call "Am I not your Lord?" (7:172). That call became joined to the hearing of the servants, and its taste reached their spirits. Its storehouse is in that world, and its lodging place is in the spirit (11:6). The servant's seven members listen to the Friend, and the call of the Friend does not belong to the present moment, since it is everlasting.

### In short

- Verse 204 commands the listener to give ear to the Qur'an when it is recited and to keep silence, so that the mercy of the Book may reach him.
- The command answers the practice of the pagans of Quraysh, who had told one another to make noise during the recitation and not to listen to it (41:26).
- Ibn Mas'ud reported that the Companions used to greet one another during the prayer, and that this verse came down to replace that practice with listening to the recitation.
- The reports differ on the setting of the rule, and most of the teachers hold that its words are general and cover the recitation in the salah, in the Jumu'ah khutbah and elsewhere, with the sermon of Nikah and the Eid prayers following the rule of the Friday sermon, since listening and silence there are wajib.
- The jurists differ over recitation outside the prayer, and all of them prefer listening and silence when the sound of the Book reaches a person, while agreeing that a voice raised for display or one that troubles the work and the rest of others is not proper.
- Ibn al-Humam held that the promise of mercy belongs to the silent listener, so that even a prayer for Jannah or a seeking of refuge from Jahannam has no place while the imam or the speaker recites those verses. The spiritual commentary describes listening as a spring in the heart that keeps the memory of the first call alive.

<<<VERSE 7:205>>>
Verse 205 turns from listening to the Book to the remembrance of the Lord and the manner of it. "Remember your Lord inwardly with humility and reverence and in a moderate tone of voice, both morning and evening. And do not be one of the heedless"

### Phrase by phrase

Most of the teachers hold that this verse commands the remembrance of Allah in a general sense, together with the conduct that belongs to it, and that the recitation of the Qur'an falls under the command as one of the forms of remembrance. Ibn 'Abbas read the word for remembrance in this verse as the Qur'an itself, so that the conduct named in the verse belongs to its recitation. The difference between the two readings is small, since all agree that every other form of dhikr is governed by the same rule and the same conduct as the Book. In short, the verse tells the human being to remember Allah, and it tells him when to do it and how.

**"Remember your Lord inwardly"** gives the first rule, which concerns the voice. The reports explain the phrase as a command to keep the remembrance in the heart, or to recite it to oneself without raising the voice. One reading of the verse says that the Messenger ﷺ was to recite alone by himself, in a low voice below the breath, avoiding a voice that is raised too high or lowered too far. The teachers describe two forms of this hidden remembrance. The first is that a person does not move his tongue at all, and simply thinks of Allah and of His attributes in his heart, which is the remembrance of the heart, or pondering. The second is that he brings his tongue into line with what is in the depths of his heart, lowers his voice, and gives voice to the letters that carry the names of Allah.

The most preferred form joins the two. A person should first understand the sense of what he is saying, and then, while keeping his attention fixed on it, speak it with his tongue. The aim is that the tongue takes part in the remembrance along with the heart. If someone stays with the thought in his heart alone and never utters a word with his tongue, that too is an act worthy of reward in its own place. The lowest degree is the opposite case, where the words are uttered and the heart is bare of them, and even heedless of them.

**"with humility and reverence"** gives the second rule. Recitation and remembrance should be made humbly, out of the realisation that Allah is great and exalted, and with the sense of what is being said kept in view.

**"in a moderate tone of voice"** gives the third rule, which concerns the level of the voice. The words mean that the remembrance should be in a lowered voice as against a raised one. A person who remembers Allah aloud has the option of doing so. Yet the conduct of the act asks that he not raise his voice to the outer limit of shouting and screaming. An average voice, with the reverence that belongs to the act, is better. A voice raised very high in the remembrance of Allah or in the recitation of the Qur'an is a sign of a want of respect in the heart toward the One who is addressed. A person does not naturally speak in a loud voice in the presence of someone he reveres with all his heart. The Book states the same measure in another passage, where the Messenger ﷺ is told not to recite in a voice too high nor in one too low, but to keep to a middle path between the two (17:110).

Three methods of remembrance and recitation come out of the verse on this account. The first is the remembrance of the heart alone, where a person keeps his tongue still and thinks about the meanings of the Book and the words of remembrance. The second joins the tongue to the heart, without raising the voice to the level where others hear it. Both of those fall under the words "remember your Lord inwardly". The third is the remembrance of the tongue with the heart's attention, with a voice that is not raised beyond what is needed, and that is what the words about a moderate tone cover.

### The closeness of the One who is called

The Companions asked the Messenger ﷺ whether their Lord was near, so that they should call upon Him in secret, or far, so that they should raise their voices. In answer the verse came down that states that when the servants of Allah ask about Him, He is near, that He answers the invocation of the supplicant when he calls on Him (2:186).

The reports carry an event on a journey. Abu Musa al-Ash'ari said that the people raised their voices with dua as they travelled. The Prophet ﷺ said to them that they should take it easy on themselves, since the One they were calling was not deaf and was not absent. He said that the One they were calling was the All-Hearing, and that He was close, closer to one of them than the neck of the animal he was riding. In the two Sahihs this report is recorded, and it stands as one of the texts that encourage the servants to keep up the remembrance of Allah and to call on Him often. This is especially so in the mornings and the evenings, so that they are not counted among those who neglect Him.

The earlier part of the surah gives the same conduct for supplication, when it commands the servants to call upon their Lord humbly and secretly (7:55). The word used there is that of a secret and hidden call, which shows that softness of voice belongs to the conduct of remembrance and recitation. The verse here makes it clear at the same time that a voiced remembrance is not forbidden. It is allowed, with the condition that the voice is not raised further than the need, and certainly not so far that the state of fear and humility disappears from the heart.

### The night visit to the two houses

A report describes how the Messenger ﷺ guided two of his companions in this matter. He came out of his house late one night and passed the house of Abu Bakr, where he heard him praying in a lowered voice. He went on and passed the house of 'Umar, and heard him reciting in a raised voice. In the morning the two companions came to him, and he told Abu Bakr that he had heard him reciting in a lowered voice. Abu Bakr answered that the One he was reciting for had heard him, and that this was enough for him. He told 'Umar that he had heard him reciting in a raised voice, and 'Umar answered that by raising his voice he had intended to drive away sleep and Satan together. The Messenger ﷺ then instructed Abu Bakr to raise his voice a little, and asked 'Umar to bring his voice down somewhat. The report is carried in the collection of Abu Dawud.

Another report tells that some of the Companions asked 'A'ishah about the recitation of the Messenger ﷺ, whether it was in a raised or a lowered voice. She said that he did it both ways at different times, reciting aloud on some occasions and softly on others.

### Loud and soft in the practice of the community

The early elders of the community differed over loud recitation during the nafl prayer at night and over recitation outside the prayer, with some preferring the raised voice and others the lowered one. For this reason Imam Abu Hanifah said that a person who recites has the choice either way. All agree that a raised voice carries conditions. There should be no fear of showing off in it. The voice should not bring inconvenience or pain to others, whether by disturbing the salah or the recitation of another person, or by troubling their work or their rest. Where such harm is feared, the low voice is the better course by the agreement of all.

The rule that holds for the recitation of the Book holds also for other adhkar and tasbihat. They may be voiced or kept low, on the condition that the voice is not raised to a point that goes against the humility of the heart and the conduct of devotion, and that it does not disturb those who are working or resting. Which of the two is better depends on the person and on the state he is in. For some people the raised voice is better, and for others the low one. There are times when the raised voice is better, and times when keeping it low or silent is of more benefit. The works on the subject, among them the Ruh al-Bayan, set out these distinctions.

### The times of remembrance

This verse came down in Makkah, before the night of Isra' and before the five daily prayers were ordained. It ordered the worship of Allah at those two times of the day. The verse names the mornings and the evenings as the times of remembrance. One reading takes the words to mean that a person should keep up the remembrance of his Lord at least twice a day, in the morning and in the evening. Another reading takes the two times as standing for the whole day and the whole night, in the way that the words East and West are used to mean the whole world. On that reading the verse asks a person to stay with the remembrance of Allah and the recitation of the Book at all times and in every state. 'A'ishah said of the Messenger ﷺ that he was devoted to the remembrance of Allah at all times and in all conditions.

### The heedless and the heedless remembrance

The verse closes with the command not to be one of the heedless, which the teachers read as a warning that to leave the remembrance of Allah and to join the heedless is a great loss. One of the sages of the community, Rumi, drew a picture of the remembrance that is spoken without the heart. He said that such a person has the words "glory to God" on his tongue while a cow and a donkey stand in his heart. He asked what kind of remembrance that is and what effect it can have.

The remark is not a denial of the reward that a bare verbal remembrance may carry. The teachers explain that the tongue's constant utterance of the words can open the way for the heart to remember at last, and that where this does not happen, at least one part of the body is busy with the remembrance of Allah. So those who find no peace, no attention and no sense of presence in their dhikr and their tasbih should not abandon the practice as something useless. They should continue with it and keep trying to reach the degree of attention that the act asks for.

One of the teachers of the spiritual path, called the Pir of the Tariqah, spoke of the same goal in a prayer, asking how he could remember when he himself was all remembrance, and asking his Lord to remember him with a gentle remembrance. The same commentary divides those who remember into three. One remembers with his tongue while his heart pays no attention. Another remembers with his tongue and his heart together, though his work remains in danger, since the one who purifies himself stands in danger. The third is silent with his tongue while his heart is drowned in his Lord.

### In short

- Verse 205 commands the remembrance of Allah in the heart, with humility and reverence, in a moderate voice, in the mornings and the evenings, and it warns against being among the heedless.
- The teachers hold that the command covers every kind of dhikr and that the recitation of the Book is included in it, and Ibn 'Abbas read the word for remembrance here as the Book itself.
- Three methods of remembrance come out of the verse, the remembrance of the heart alone, the tongue joined to the heart without a voice that others hear. The tongue with the heart's attention in a voice that is not raised beyond the need.
- The Companions asked whether their Lord was near or far. The answer came in the verse that He is near and answers the supplicant (2:186), while the report of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari in the two Sahihs shows the Prophet ﷺ telling his companions that the One they called was not deaf or absent but closer to them than the neck of their mounts.
- The night visit to the houses of Abu Bakr and 'Umar led the Messenger ﷺ to ask Abu Bakr to raise his voice a little and 'Umar to bring his down, and 'A'ishah said that he recited both ways, while Imam Abu Hanifah held that a reciter may choose either course so long as there is no display and no disturbance to the prayer, the work or the rest of others.
- The earlier verse of the surah asks for supplication to be made humbly and secretly (7:55), the middle course is named in another passage (17:110). The warning against a remembrance spoken while the heart is empty is carried in the words of Rumi about the cow and the donkey in the heart.

<<<VERSE 7:206>>>
Verse 206 closes the surah with the picture of those who stand near their Lord. "Surely those ˹angels˺ nearest to your Lord are not too proud to worship Him. They glorify Him. And to Him they prostrate"

### Phrase by phrase

**"those nearest to your Lord"** names the angels, and the reports open the word wider. Being with Allah in this place means being among those whom He has approved and accepted. The group takes in every angel, every blessed prophet, and the most upright of the men and the women of the Muslim community.

**"are not too proud to worship Him"** describes their standing. Arrogance in this setting means that a person gives himself airs and falls short in the acts of worship on the false assumption that he is great and special. These servants do the opposite. They count themselves weak and needy before Allah, and they keep to His remembrance, His worship and His glorification without a pause.

**"They glorify Him. And to Him they prostrate"** names what they do. One short account of the verse says of the angels that they are not too proud to obey Him and to acknowledge their servitude to Him, that they praise Him, obey Him and pray to Him. Another says that they give themselves to worship with full dedication and never tire, glorifying Him day and night and prostrating before Him. The prostration at the end of the verse is the act that shows the height of their humility.

The reports note that the prostration here is a prostration of recitation, and that this is the first place in the Book where the scholars agree that a person reciting the verse and a person listening to it should prostrate. Prostration in the prayer itself was mentioned in the same context, since the angels prostrate before their Lord. A hadith of the Prophet ﷺ asks the worshippers why they do not stand in line as the angels stand in line before their Lord, who complete the first row, then the next, and stand close together. Another passage commands the servants to glorify their Lord before the sun comes up and before it goes down (50:39), which is the practice of those who never tire.

### The worth of prostration

The reports gather a set of sayings on the worth of prostration. In the collection of Muslim it is related that a man asked Thawban to tell him of an act that would take him to Paradise. Thawban stayed silent the first time and the second time the man put the question, and when he asked a third time Thawban said that he had put the same question to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and that he had ordered him to make sajdah often, since for every sajdah that a person makes, Allah raises him a rank and forgives him a sin. The man said that he later met Abu al-Darda' and asked him the same thing, and the answer he received was the same.

Also in the collection of Muslim, on the authority of Abu Hurairah, the Messenger ﷺ said that a servant of Allah is closest to his Lord when he is in sajdah. He told the people therefore to pray with ardour while they are in sajdah, since it is strongly hoped that the prayer made there will be answered. The promise of the answered invocation is stated in the Book, where Allah says that He answers the supplicant when he calls on Him (2:186).

The teachers set out the rules that belong to this. Prostration is not by itself a recognised act of worship. On the reading of Imam Abu Hanifah, the instruction to prostrate often means that a person should offer the nafl prayer often. The reason is that a greater number of voluntary prayers brings a greater number of prostrations. If a person goes into sajdah on its own and makes his supplication there, that is not a matter of any harm. The instruction to supplicate in sajdah belongs to the nafl prayers and it is not a duty. In the nafl prayer the supplication is made with the words that come from the Book or from the sunnah. Where a person makes a sajdah for supplication alone, he may pray in whatever language he wishes.

### The word of the spiritual commentary

One of the spiritual commentaries reads the verse in its inner sense. It says that the words about those who are with the Lord point to the centre of togetherness. In contrast, the words about their refusal to be proud report the quality of dispersion. Their being with Him affirms their nobility, and at the same time it keeps them within the ruling properties of servanthood. In this way the servant moves between togetherness and dispersion. Togetherness shows the reality, and dispersion sets out the shariah. The verse that says that for each community Allah has appointed an avenue and a method (5:48) alludes to this.

### What the community learns from the verse

The last verse of the surah shows the state and the manner of those who are close to Allah so that others may take their lesson from it. The servants are reminded of the angels who praise Allah night and day without tiring, so that they imitate them in their worship and their obedience. The lesson drawn by the teachers is that a person who is given the ability to keep up worship and remembrance without slackness carries a sign that he is with his Lord and enjoys His company. The surah closes on that picture, and it closes with a prostration. The aim is that the one who recites its last verse takes his place with those who bow before their Lord.

The final word of the reports on this verse belongs to the prostration. In the collection of Muslim it is related from Abu Hurairah that the Messenger ﷺ said that when a son of Adam comes upon a verse of sajdah and prostrates, Satan draws away weeping. He says, in his grief, that the son of Adam was commanded to prostrate and obeyed, and that Paradise became his home. He himself was commanded to prostrate and disobeyed, and Hell became his home.

### In short

- Verse 206 states that those nearest to the Lord, the angels, are not too proud to worship Him, that they glorify Him, and that they prostrate before Him.
- The teachers read being with Allah as belonging to those He has approved and accepted. They include in that rank the angels, the prophets, and the upright among the men and the women of the Muslim community.
- Their worship is free of the arrogance that gives a person airs and makes him fall short. It stands as the opposite of that pride, since they count themselves weak and needy before their Lord.
- The verse is a verse of prostration, the first place in the Book where the scholars agree that the reciter and the listener should prostrate. The reports tie it to the glorifying of the Lord before sunrise and sunset (50:39) and to the rows of the angels who complete the first line and then the next.
- The collections of Muslim carry the word of Thawban that the Messenger ﷺ ordered him to make sajdah often, since a rank is raised and a sin forgiven with every prostration. The word of Abu Hurairah that a servant is nearest to his Lord in sajdah and should pray there with ardour, with the answer of the invocation promised by Allah (2:186).
- Prostration by itself is not a recognised act of worship, so Imam Abu Hanifah read the instruction as a call to offer nafl prayer often, while supplication in a sajdah made for that purpose may be made in any language. The surah closes with the report that Satan weeps when the son of Adam prostrates and is forgiven.
