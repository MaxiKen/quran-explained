<<<VERSE 2:185>>>
Verse 185 extends the brief verse before it and asserts the great merit of Ramaḍān. "Ramaḍân is the month in which the Quran was revealed as a guide for humanity with clear proofs of guidance and the standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺. So whoever is present this month, let them fast." It goes on: "But whoever is ill or on a journey, then ˹let them fast˺ an equal number of days ˹after Ramaḍân˺. Allah intends ease for you, not hardship, so that you may complete the prescribed period and proclaim the greatness of Allah for guiding you, and perhaps you will be grateful". The "prescribed number of days" of verse 184 was somewhat vague. This verse explains that the days meant are those of the month of Ramaḍān.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ramaḍân is the month in which the Quran was revealed"**. Allah honoured Ramaḍān above the other months by choosing it for sending down the glorious Qur'an, as He did for all the Books He revealed to the prophets. Imam Aḥmad records from Wāthilah ibn al-Asqaʿ that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that the scrolls of Ibrāhīm came down on the first night of Ramaḍān, the Torah on the sixth, the Injīl on the thirteenth and the Qur'an on the twenty-fourth, while a narration from Jābir places the Psalms on the twelfth and the Injīl on the eighteenth. The earlier Books came down whole on those dates. The Qur'an's peculiarity is that it was sent in its entirety from the Preserved Tablet to the heaven of this world on one night of Ramaḍān, and then revealed to the Prophet ﷺ gradually over twenty-three years. One account says that Gabriel brought the whole Qur'an to the first heaven, dictated it to the angelic scribes, and then brought it down to Muḥammad ﷺ day after day, sometimes one, two or three verses and sometimes a whole sūrah. That night was Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Power, as the Qur'an itself says that it was revealed in the Night of Power. The hadith puts it on the twenty-fourth, and since al-Ḥasan held that Laylat al-Qadr falls on the night of the twenty-fourth the two agree, but if that alignment is not accepted the Qur'an's statement prevails. Whichever night is the Night of Power is the one intended.

**"as a guide for humanity with clear proofs of guidance and the standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺."**. Allah praises the Qur'an for guiding the hearts of believers who observe its commands, leading people away from error. Its clear proofs are plain, unambiguous signs for those who understand, testifying to its truth and guidance as opposed to misguidance, to the straight path as opposed to the crooked, and to the distinction between truth and falsehood and between the lawful and the forbidden, including legal rulings, punishments and the avoidance of doubtful matters.

**"So whoever is present this month, let them fast."**. The word shahida comes from shuhūd, presence, and al-shahr here means Ramaḍān. Therefore, whoever is present in the month must fast throughout it. This requires the healthy person who witnesses the start of the month while resident in his land to fast. It abrogated the general choice of paying ransom instead, leaving fasting as the only option. Being present means finding Ramaḍān with the ability to fast: being Muslim, sane, of age, resident and free of impurities including menstruation and post-natal bleeding. Those who lack the basic ability, the disbeliever, the minor and the insane, are not addressed by the obligation, while those who have the ability but are prevented at some point by a valid excuse, a menstruating or post-natal woman, a sick person or a traveller, have found the month in a state of ability. Therefore, the command applies to them, relief is granted for the time of the excuse and qaḍāʾ is required later.

**"But whoever is ill or on a journey, then ˹let them fast˺ an equal number of days ˹after Ramaḍân˺."**. Having ordered the fast, Allah repeats the permission for the sick person who cannot fast or fears harm, and the traveller, to break the fast and make up the days. The concession had already appeared in the previous verse, but since the option of ransom had now been cancelled, a doubt might arise that the concession for the sick and travelling had been abrogated as well, or swallowed by the general words "whoever is present". Therefore, it was positively repeated. The authentic Sunnah records that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ travelled in Ramaḍān for the conquest of Makkah, marched as far as Kadīd, then broke his fast and ordered those with him to do the same, as the Two Ṣaḥīḥs record. That order did not make breaking the fast compulsory. This is because the Companions used to travel with him in Ramaḍān with some fasting and some not, neither group criticising the other. He would have criticised those who fasted had it been required. He himself sometimes fasted on journeys: Abū al-Dardāʾ relates in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs that on one Ramaḍān journey the heat was so intense that men put their hands on their heads, and only the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and ʿAbdullāh ibn Rawāḥah were fasting. Still, using the permission is better. This is because he said that those who did not fast had done well and there was no harm on those who fasted, and in another hadith he told people to hold to the permission Allah had granted. Some scholars hold the two equal, since ʿĀʾishah relates in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs that Ḥamzah ibn ʿAmr al-Aslamī, who fasted a great deal, asked whether to fast when travelling and was told to fast or not as he wished. When fasting becomes hard, breaking it is better: Jābir relates that the Prophet ﷺ saw a man being shaded by others, was told he was fasting. Said that it is not piety to fast while travelling, as al-Bukhārī and Muslim record. Those who ignore the Sunnah and believe breaking the fast on a journey to be disliked must break it and may not fast. Making up the days need not be consecutive, for consecutive fasting is required only in Ramaḍān itself and afterwards what matters is simply the number, which is why Allah said "an equal number of days".

**"Allah intends ease for you, not hardship"**. Out of mercy He allowed the sick and the travelling to break the fast while keeping it binding on the healthy resident, this ease being the very reason He commanded the fast as He did. One reading takes it as His wish that the traveller take the dispensation and not bear the hardship of fasting on the road. Imam Aḥmad records from Anas ibn Mālik, and the Two Ṣaḥīḥs confirm, that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to treat people with ease and not hardness, giving glad tidings and not driving people away, and the Ṣaḥīḥayn record that he said the same to Muʿādh and Abū Mūsā when sending them to Yemen. The Sunan and Musnad compilers record his words that he was sent with the easy Ḥanīfiyyah.

**"so that you may complete the prescribed period"**. You were allowed to break the fast when ill or travelling because Allah wanted ease for you, and He commanded the make-up only so that you complete the days of a full month once you are home and well.

**"and proclaim the greatness of Allah for guiding you"**. Remember Allah upon completing the act of worship, as in "When you have fulfilled your sacred rites, praise Allah" (2:200), "Once the prayer is over, disperse throughout the land and seek the bounty of Allah. And remember Allah often" (62:10) and "glorify Him during part of the night and after the prayers" (50:39-40). Hence the Sunnah encourages tasbīḥ, taḥmīd and takbīr after the obligatory prayers. Ibn ʿAbbās said that they used to know the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had finished the prayer by the takbīr. Several scholars hold that the takbīr of ʿĪd al-Fiṭr is derived from this verse, magnifying Allah on the day of ʿĪd for guiding you to His religion and the principal rites of His religion.

**"and perhaps you will be grateful"**. If you keep to what Allah commanded, performing the obligations, leaving the prohibitions and observing His limits, you may be among the grateful, thankful for the dispensation and for being guided to the religion He approved for you.

### Rulings

Fasting Ramaḍān becomes obligatory on condition that one finds the month in a state of ability. Whoever finds the whole month fasts the whole month and whoever finds less fasts the days he finds. Therefore, a disbeliever who embraces Islam or a minor who comes of age in mid-Ramaḍān fasts from then on without qaḍāʾ for the earlier days. The insane person, being a Muslim adult, has the basic ability. Therefore, if he regains sanity during the month he must make up the earlier days, and the same applies to a woman purified in mid-Ramaḍān, a sick person who recovers or a traveller who becomes resident. The month is established in Islamic law in one of three ways: sighting the new moon oneself, proof of the sighting through a trustworthy witness, or, failing both, completing thirty days of Shaʿbān. If on the eve of the twenty-ninth of Shaʿbān clouds or bad weather hide the horizon and no admissible witness appears, the next day is the day of doubt (yawm al-shakk). This is because the moon may or may not have been there. Fasting that day is not obligatory but makrūh, forbidden in the hadith so that farḍ and nafl are not confused, as al-Jaṣṣāṣ notes. In lands where day or night lasts for months, finding Ramaḍān plainly does not apply and such people would not fast. As for ṣalāh, the Ḥanafī jurists al-Ḥuluwānī and al-Qabālī ruled that they pray according to their own day and night, so that where dawn follows immediately on maghrib ʿishāʾ is not obligatory. Where a day lasts six months there would be only five prayers in that period and no Ramaḍān, hence no obligatory fast, the position also taken in Imdād al-Fatāwā.

### Fasting as training in gratitude

Fasting trains the believer in two things at once, thanksgiving and the fear of Allah. Food and water are great blessings whose worth people fail to appreciate. The one who fasts goes hungry and thirsty all day and then eats and drinks his fill at sunset in a state of extreme need, and through his own experience realises how great these blessings are, which produces boundless gratitude towards his Lord. At the same time total abstention from food and drink from dawn to sunset is an exercise in making Allah one's guardian and a training for a God-fearing life that abstains from everything He has forbidden. The whole life of the believer is a life of fasting: in Ramaḍān temporary abstention prepares him to renounce, throughout the rest of life, all that Allah disapproves of. The Qur'an is a great blessing, and through fasting a person becomes truly thankful and able to live by its teachings.

### A month that washes and burns

In the devotional reading, Ramaḍān has turned towards the friends. It is a month that both washes and burns: repentance’s water cleanses sinners’ hearts, while hunger’s fire burns servants’ bodies. The name Ramaḍān is derived either from ramḍāʾ, the hot stone that burns whatever is laid on it, or from ramaḍī, the rain that washes whatever it meets. Asked what Ramaḍān is, Muṣṭafā ﷺ replied that in it Allah burns the sins of the faithful and forgives them. Anas ibn Mālik heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say that when Ramaḍān comes the Garden’s gates open, the Fire’s gates close. Devils are put in fetters, and that if someone reaches Ramaḍān and is not forgiven, then when will he be. He also said that if Allah gave the heavens and the earth leave to speak they would announce the Garden’s good news to Ramaḍān’s fasters. The poor man remains unaware of this blessing’s worth: eminence has been set down beside you and you are unaware. Islam, the highest of creeds, is your religion. The Qur'an, the most exalted of books, is your Book. Muṣṭafā ﷺ, master of the children of Ādam, the eye and lamp of the kingdom and the Resurrection’s leader of the world’s people, is your messenger. The Kaʿbah, the most eminent of spots, is your qiblah. And Ramaḍān, the most excellent of months, is your month and the season of your practice, a month in which acts of disobedience are forgiven, the devils subdued, Paradise adorned with its doors open and the doors of Hell shut, in which corruption’s market collapses, while obedient deeds gain self-purification and past sins and recorded stains are burned away. The Commander of the Faithful ʿAlī said that had Allah wished to chastise Muḥammad's community He would have withheld Ramaḍān and the sūrah "Say: He is Allah, One" (112).

### In short

- Ramaḍān was chosen for the revelation of the Qur'an, sent down whole to the lowest heaven on Laylat al-Qadr and then revealed gradually over twenty-three years, as the earlier Books also came down in this month (Wāthilah ibn al-Asqaʿ, together with Jābir).
- Whoever finds the month with the ability to fast must fast it. This ended the option of ransom for the able.
- The concession for the sick and travelling is repeated so no one thinks it abrogated. Breaking the fast on a journey is permitted and often better (Jābir’s account of the march to Makkah), though the Prophet ﷺ himself sometimes fasted (Abū al-Dardāʾ). Missed days are made up in any order.
- Allah intends ease, as the Prophet's ﷺ instructions to Muʿādh and Abū Mūsā show. The fast ends with takbīr, as worship generally ends in remembrance (2:200, 62:10, 50:39-40), the root of the ʿĪd takbīr.
- The month is fixed by sighting, testimony or completing Shaʿbān. Fasting the day of doubt is makrūh. Converts and new adults fast from when they qualify, while the recovered, the purified and the returned traveller make up earlier days.
- Fasting teaches gratitude for food and drink and trains a God-fearing life, and inwardly Ramaḍān washes hearts and burns sins (ʿAlī, Anas).
