<<<VERSE 2:284>>>
The truth hidden from a court or neighbour is not hidden from its Lord: "To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth." His ownership and knowledge embrace outward action and the heart. The warning therefore completes the instruction against concealing evidence and also reaches the sincerity underlying every duty.

### Phrase by phrase

**"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth."** He created, owns and governs everything. His ownership comes from bringing things into existence, not acquiring them from someone else. No part of creation falls outside His authority.

**"Whether you reveal what is in your hearts or conceal it, Allah will call you to account for it."** Concealment from people is not concealment from Allah. One interpretation particularly concerns knowingly suppressing evidence. The broader explanation includes chosen beliefs, intentions and inward conduct as well as outward deeds.

**"He forgives whoever He wills, and punishes whoever He wills."** Forgiveness comes through His grace and mercy, punishment through His wisdom and justice. A gloss distinguishes someone repenting from someone refusing repentance. Reckoning does not itself mean every item will end in punishment.

**"And Allah is Most Capable of everything"** His power includes disclosure, accounting, forgiveness and recompense. The readings of the preceding verbs differ over whether they are joined grammatically to the conditional clause or begin a new statement. Both affirm His authority.

### Why were the Companions worried?

Ahmad records from Abu Hurayrah that the Companions came to the Prophet ﷺ and knelt in distress when they heard the verse. They could bear prayer, fasting, jihad and charity, but felt unable to control everything occurring in the heart.

The Prophet ﷺ asked whether they wished to repeat the response of the people of the two Scriptures who said they heard and disobeyed. The account recalls (2:93), with one narration's reservation about the exact quotation. He taught them instead to acknowledge hearing and obedience, seek forgiveness and affirm return to Allah.

They accepted that instruction. The following verses then praised their belief and gave the assurance that no soul is charged beyond capacity. Muslim also records the sequence, describing the later verse as abrogating the earlier burden they had feared.

Another narration momentarily describes their silence when instructed, while the fuller account says they accepted and recited the response. The shared outcome is the divine clarification after their distress, not a portrait of settled refusal.

### What did Ibn 'Abbas tell Mujahid?

Ahmad records that Mujahid told Ibn 'Abbas, addressing him as Abu Abbas, that Ibn 'Umar had wept while reading the words about disclosing or hiding the heart's contents. Ibn 'Abbas explained the Companions' similar fear at revelation.

They knew themselves answerable for speech and action, but did not control every thought appearing inwardly. The Prophet ﷺ told them to say that they heard and obeyed. The later reassurance pardoned what occurred beyond their control and held them responsible for their chosen conduct.

The report therefore helps distinguish intrusive experience from voluntary action. It does not make deliberate disbelief or consciously cultivated pride morally neutral because they happen inwardly.

### Is the later verse an abrogation or a clarification?

Some transmitted accounts use the language of abrogation: the feared burden was lifted by the assurance of capacity. Another explanation says the earlier verse never intended punishment for involuntary thoughts. The general wording had led the Companions to fear that meaning, and the later wording clarified it.

These are distinct ways of describing the relationship. Both reject imposing guilt for uncontrollable intrusions merely because they entered the mind. The disagreement should not be erased by presenting every account as using exactly the same technical explanation.

Muslim records the reassurance from Ibn 'Abbas as well as the account from Abu Hurayrah. The divine responses accepting the petitions are treated with the final verse, where the prayer itself appears.

### What do the hadiths say about inner speech?

The Group records from Abu Hurayrah the Prophet ﷺ saying that Allah pardoned the ummah for what people say within themselves unless they speak it or act upon it. The Two Sahihs also record the difference between an intended good and an intended evil in the angels' record.

An evil intention not carried out is not entered as the evil deed. If carried out, it is written as one. An intended good not completed is written as a good deed, and performing it receives ten good deeds. The account displays generosity rather than a mechanical equality between every mental occurrence.

The explanation must be read with the distinction between passing thoughts and settled inward conduct. It should not be made into permission to deliberately cultivate every prohibited belief or resolve.

### What are the different explanations of accountability?

Al-Qurtubi offers one distinction between worldly legal effects and the Hereafter. A mere inward decision does not complete divorce, freeing a slave, sale or a gift. Those require the relevant spoken or practical act. On that reading, the hadith concerns such worldly effects while the verse concerns final accountability.

Other scholars instead distinguish involuntary thoughts from freely chosen intentions. Thoughts can arise despite attempts to resist them. Allah pardons those intrusions. A person who firmly nourishes a plan and tries to act upon it is not in the same condition, even if an accidental obstacle prevents completion.

The latter explanation emphasises choice. The former emphasises the sphere of legal effect. Both are supplied answers to the apparent tension and should remain separately stated.

### Can reckoning end in mercy?

Al-Bukhari and Muslim record from Ibn 'Umar that the believer is brought near and asked to acknowledge sins one by one. After confession, Allah says He concealed them in worldly life and now forgives them. The believer receives the record of good deeds.

The account contrasts that private mercy with the public announcement of the wrongs of disbelievers and hypocrites. Being questioned is therefore not identical with being condemned for every admitted offence.

Another report, cited by al-Qurtubi without a new collector here, pictures qiyamah as the disclosure of secrets. Angels recorded outward actions, while Allah also knows what was not disclosed to them. The scene again ends in His authority to forgive or punish.

### Which inward acts are actually chosen?

The discussion attributed to al-Mazhari distinguishes physical duties and prohibitions from those of the heart. Salah, sawm, hajj and dealings have outward forms. Iman, belief, and its opposites, kufr and shirk, belong inwardly.

Humility, patience, contentment and generosity also have an inward reality. So do arrogance, envy, resentment, greed and attachment to worldly gain. Their being hidden does not place consciously adopted qualities outside responsibility.

The passage therefore rejects two extremes: treating every fleeting intrusion as a culpable decision and treating the entire heart as beyond moral concern. Sincerity is indispensable to fulfilling the outward law.

### Why is this warning placed near the chapter's end?

Al-Baqarah has discussed a wide field of duties: prayer, zakah, fasting, qisas, pilgrimage, struggle, purity, talaq, iddah, marital separation, suckling, intoxicants, riba and financial dealings. Qisas concerns retaliation, talaq divorce and iddah the waiting period.

The explanation connects the chapter's importance with its description as the Qur'an's high peak. That surah-level designation is already part of the introduction's subject. Here the important point is that all these laws require ikhlas, sincerity.

People may be evaded through excuses, but the Raqib and Hafiz, the One watching and guarding, knows the inward and outward truth. Fear of Allah and the Hereafter is pictured as a sentry upon the heart, reminding the person of the law even in darkness and privacy.

The shared reflection about human freedom similarly treats it as a trial rather than independence. Allah's watch extends to small and great things within and outside the person. The choice is whether to submit voluntarily to the Lord whom the rest of creation obeys.

### How is divine ownership different from ours?

The inward reading distinguishes ownership through trade, gift, inheritance or acquisition from ownership through creation. Human titles arise under rules that also impose rightful dues. Allah's dominion comes from bringing nonbeing into being, not receiving property from another owner.

It recalls (3:40), His doing what He wills, and (5:1), His decreeing what He desires. His action upon His creation is described as just by virtue of His lordship, not the intrusion of someone taking what belongs to another.

The lesson is not to attach the heart merely to the heavens and earth because they are His. It is to recognise their Artisan. The discussion recalls (41:37), turning worship away from sun and moon towards their Creator.

Related assurances in (3:29) and (20:7) concern His knowledge of the concealed and the still more hidden. Looking at the workmanship should therefore lead towards the One who knows the observer's heart as fully as He knows the world observed.

### In short

- Allah's ownership and knowledge embrace outward conduct and inward choice.
- The Companions' fear was answered through obedience and the later assurance of capacity.
- Explanations distinguish involuntary thoughts from chosen resolve and reckoning from punishment.
- Hidden moral qualities remain part of responsibility, while intrusive thoughts are not automatically sins.
- The chapter's laws reach their purpose through sincerity before the Lord who sees all.

<<<VERSE 2:285>>>
The chapter's many commands are answered with a united affirmation: "The Messenger ˹firmly˺ believes in what has been revealed to him from his Lord, and so do the believers." They accept the Lord, His angels, Books and messengers, then join hearing with obedience. Their confession ends in asking forgiveness, not claiming they have no further need of mercy.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Messenger ˹firmly˺ believes in what has been revealed to him from his Lord, and so do the believers."** Muhammad ﷺ acknowledges the truth of the Qur'an and all it contains. The believers join him in that assent. Naming him by his office honours him, while the distinct description of his faith recognises its exceptional degree.

**"They ˹all˺ believe in Allah, His angels, His Books, and His messengers."** Allah is One, with perfect attributes, sole Lord and sole rightful object of worship. His angels exist, the revealed Scriptures are true, and His prophets are truthful servants sent to guide.

**"˹They proclaim,˺ “We make no distinction between any of His messengers.”"** They do not accept some while rejecting others. They believe in Musa (Moses), 'Isa (Jesus) and Muhammad ﷺ, along with all the other messengers. The statement concerns acknowledgment, not a denial of every difference in station or law.

**"And they say, “We hear and obey."** They receive the instruction, understand it and undertake its implications. Hearing is not complete obedience when the command is deliberately set aside.

**"˹We seek˺ Your forgiveness, our Lord!"** Acceptance remains joined to supplication for forgiveness, mercy and kindness. In one gloss the plea specifically includes inward discourse that had worried the believers.

**"And to You ˹alone˺ is the final return.”"** All matters end before Allah at resurrection. The confession therefore recognises the Lord both as giver of the instruction and as final judge.

### What relation does this have to the previous verse?

The Companions feared the apparent breadth of the accountability in verse 284. The Prophet ﷺ did not answer by authorising refusal. He taught the response of hearing, obedience, forgiveness and return.

The narrative recorded by Muslim through Abu Hurayrah contrasts that with the response recalled in (2:93), hearing followed by rebellion. This verse praises the believers' acceptance, and the next gives the reassurance they needed about capacity.

Another explanation says the Prophet ﷺ knew the intended meaning but waited for revelation rather than issuing a personal interpretation merely to relieve anxiety. Their surrender came while the question was still troubling, not only after all difficulty disappeared.

The main distress narrative belongs to the preceding entry. Here its result is the affirmation and praise of the believing community.

### How is the messenger's faith distinguished?

The Prophet ﷺ and his followers share iman, belief, but not an identical degree of knowledge. His knowledge is described through seeing and hearing the revelation, while theirs is iman bi'l-ghayb, belief without seeing, based on his testimony.

The wording gives the main statement to his faith and then joins the believers. That honours the certainty of the messenger without denying the reality of the followers' faith.

The inward reading also notes the third-person title rather than a direct address. It regards this as a manner of declaring greatness, like the opening praise of Allah in Al-Fatihah being expressed through His name rather than as a first-person statement.

### Why accept every messenger while following the final law?

The prophets were righteous and guided, even where Allah authorised one law to supersede another. The explanation says Muhammad's final law superseded the earlier laws and remains until the Last Hour, with a group of the ummah continuing on the path of truth.

Acceptance of all messengers therefore does not require practising every earlier legal system simultaneously. Nor does following the final messenger justify denying the truthfulness of earlier ones.

The discussion contrasts this comprehensive acknowledgment with earlier communities that accepted their own recognised prophet but rejected another. Its praise is directed towards refusing that selective belief, not towards dismissing the honoured place of Musa or 'Isa.

### What virtue is given to the last two verses?

Al-Bukhari records from Abu Mas'ud the Prophet ﷺ saying that reciting the last two verses at night will suffice the person. The rest of the six collections also record related wording. The Two Sahihs give it through several transmissions, and Ahmad records it too.

The discussion commends recitation rather than treating these lines as merely the conclusion of a long reading. Their combination of belief, obedience and supplication gives the practice its content.

A report from Ibn 'Abbas says the two verses came from Paradise's treasures and were written by the Most Merciful two thousand years before the creation. It adds that recitation after 'isha stands for tahajjud, the night prayer, in the account's description.

Al-Hakim's Mustadrak and Bayhaqi also contain the statement that they were given from a special treasure beneath the 'arsh, the divine Throne. It urges learning them and teaching women and children. 'Umar and 'Ali are described as thinking that a sensible person would not sleep without reciting them.

No absent grade is supplied for these reports. Their stated merits are retained as part of the material rather than silently reduced to the first saying alone.

### What was given on the Isra journey?

Muslim records from 'Abdullah that during the Isra the Prophet ﷺ reached Sidrat Al-Muntaha in the sixth heaven. It is described as the limit for what ascends from earth and what descends from above.

The account recalls (53:16), what covered the Lote Tree, and explains it through a covering or mat of gold. It then names three gifts: the five prayers, the final verses of Al-Baqarah and forgiveness for those of his community who do not associate partners with Allah.

The details are retained as that account gives them, without altering the heaven's number or adding another description from memory. Another gloss connects the verse's praise with the Prophet's prostration during his ascent.

### What are the two lights?

Ibn 'Abbas gives an account of the Prophet ﷺ with Jibril when a sound came from above. Jibril said a previously unopened heavenly door had opened. An angel descended announcing two lights not given to any earlier prophet.

They were Al-Fatihah and the last verses of Al-Baqarah. The report promises their benefit with recitation. Muslim and An-Nasa'i record it, with the wording supplied here identified as An-Nasa'i's.

The image joins the Book's opening to the chapter's ending. The benefit is a gift from Allah, not a claim that the reader needs no faith, obedience or sincere prayer beyond pronouncing sounds.

### What does the final affirmation gather together?

The inward reading reviews what the chapter has set out: belief, resurrection, Garden and Fire, worship, family law, financial conduct, vows, stories and signs of divine power. The messenger and believers assent to all of it.

The Prophet ﷺ is praised for explaining the rulings and delivering the message. The faithful are praised for recognising, accepting and holding firm to its limits and truth. The ending is therefore not detached from the detailed commands that preceded it.

Its first movement is not the servant's boast about performance but acknowledgment of revelation. Its next is obedience, and then forgiveness. That order keeps acceptance joined to humility.

### In short

- The messenger and believers affirm Allah, angels, Books and every messenger.
- Accepting all prophets is compatible with following the final revealed law.
- Hearing is joined to obedience and a plea for forgiveness.
- Reports commend reciting the last two verses at night and learning them as special gifts.
- The affirmation gathers the chapter's laws, narratives and signs into a single acceptance.

<<<VERSE 2:286>>>
Mercy answers the fear of an impossible burden: "Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford." Responsibility belongs to what a person can choose and bear, not every intrusive thought beyond control. The believers then ask for forgiveness, ease, mercy and help, recognising Allah as their guardian in every part of the task.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford."** His commands do not exceed capacity. This expresses kindness, compassion and generosity. The reassurance lifts the feared burden of punishment for uncontrolled inward intrusions, while preserving responsibility for chosen belief and conduct.

**"All good will be for its own benefit, and all evil will be to its own loss."** The soul receives the return of its earned good and bears responsibility for its earned evil. It is not punished for someone else's sin or for a temptation it did not adopt. The verbs distinguish what was actually earned from what merely appeared in the mind.

**"˹The believers pray,˺ “Our Lord! Do not punish us if we forget or make a mistake."** The request covers forgotten duties, unintended slips and errors made without knowing the ruling. Forgetfulness and inadvertence are not treated as deliberate refusal. The prayer also acknowledges Allah's favour in lifting that burden.

**"Our Lord! Do not place a burden on us like the one you placed on those before us."** Isr is a heavy burden. The explanation includes severe restrictions placed on earlier communities and asks not to be charged with such difficulty. Another interpretation concerns punishments following their transgressions.

**"Our Lord! Do not burden us with what we cannot bear."** The request extends to obligations, hardships and afflictions. It seeks protection from trials beyond strength as well as relief from an overly difficult task.

**"Pardon us, forgive us, and have mercy on us."** The pleas are related but distinct. One explanation takes pardon as overlooking shortcomings before Allah, forgiveness as concealing faults from His servants, and mercy as protection from falling into further error. Another describes effacing sins, forgiving and then adding mercy.

**"You are our ˹only˺ Guardian."** Mawla means master, protector, supporter and helper. Every kind of help is sought from Him, with trust and reliance. Power and strength are not independent possessions of the servant.

**"So grant us victory over the disbelieving people.”"** The explanation concerns those rejecting the religion and message and associating others with Allah. It asks for definitive proof and victory in the described conflict, and for prevailing in this life and the Hereafter. It is a petition to the Guardian, not a personal licence to wrong others.

### What is excused and what remains accountable?

Outward actions can be voluntary or involuntary. Deliberately speaking or striking someone differs from a slip of the tongue or movement of a palsied hand. The discussion relates direct moral reward and punishment to what is chosen.

The same distinction applies inwardly. Deliberately adopting kufr or shirk, cultivating pride or firmly resolving to drink is not identical with an unwelcome thought entering without permission. The involuntary thought is excused, while chosen inward conduct remains a deed of the heart.

Disliking evil thoughts is itself described as part of faith. Their arrival therefore does not prove the person's wish to act on them. The reassurance answers the Companions' worry without making the heart's conscious commitments morally irrelevant.

Some accounts call this abrogation of the burden feared from the earlier verse. Another explanation calls it clarification of the intended limitation already present. Their shared lesson is mercy concerning what cannot be controlled.

### Does this exclude effects transmitted through other people?

A good act can inspire imitation, bringing continuing reward to its originator. An evil practice can be followed too, extending responsibility to the person who introduced it. The discussion also recognises transmitting the reward of a deed to someone else through isal al-thawab.

Those cases do not contradict responsibility for one's own conduct. The originating choice helped establish the good or evil example, even if every later effect was not separately planned. In the explanation of transmitted reward, another person's voluntary kindness can likewise arise from an earlier favour or relationship.

The distinction is between direct responsibility for an unchosen intrusion and benefits or harms mediated through an actual chosen deed. The verse's assurance should not be used to deny every continuing consequence of one's own conduct.

### How were the petitions answered?

Muslim records from Abu Hurayrah the divine response accepting the prayer at its successive requests. The wording is that Allah will grant them. Ibn 'Abbas gives another narration in which the response is that He has granted them.

The acceptance concerns forgiveness for forgetting or error, relief from the earlier heavy burdens, protection from what exceeds strength, pardon, forgiveness, mercy and help. One account describes the Prophet ﷺ hearing granted after each petition while reciting.

The differing forms of the answer reinforce the promised mercy rather than introduce contradictory prayers. The servant still asks, acknowledging that the ease is Allah's favour, not a right independent of Him.

Ibn Jarir records from Abu Ishaq that Mu'adh would say Amin on finishing the chapter's final petition. The response expresses assent and hope in what has just been asked.

### Which earlier burdens are mentioned?

The explanations refer to Bani Israil. Examples include cutting away or burning an impure piece of cloth, repentance involving killing, and an alms burden described as a quarter of property.

Another gloss concerns breaking a covenant and losing permission to eat previously wholesome things. It mentions camel meat, bovine fat and small cattle among the described restrictions. Those particulars are reported explanations of the heavy burden, not new dietary commands for this community.

The ease brought through Muhammad ﷺ is contrasted with those burdens. A hadith describes his mission through the easy Hanifiyyah, the way of pure monotheism. No collector or grade beyond what is supplied for that saying is added.

The alternative interpretation asks to be protected from the punishment of earlier wrongdoing rather than only from demanding legal duties. Both concern mercy, but one relates to requirements and the other to consequences.

### What further allusions are attached to pardon and mercy?

A reported reading takes pardon as protection from transformation into animals, recalling some of the people of 'Isa (Jesus). It takes forgiveness as protection from being swallowed by the ground, recalling Qarun (Korah), and mercy as protection from being tossed by waves, recalling the people of Lot in that account.

The details are preserved as the explanation supplies them, without correcting or enlarging the stories from outside knowledge. They are applications of the prayer's terms rather than words naming those incidents in the verse itself.

The account says the supplication lifted inward discourse, forgetfulness, error and coercion, and gave protection from those punishments. It extends that favour to those following the believers' path. Its emphasis is the accepted petition, not a guarantee that every difficulty in ordinary life disappears.

### Why distinguish pardon, forgiveness and future mercy?

An error leaves several needs. The servant needs Allah to overlook the failure, conceal it rather than expose it to people, and prevent further descent. Asking only for the past to be erased would leave the future unprotected.

The three petitions therefore gather the servant's whole vulnerability. They also counter pride: even a person affirming hearing and obedience remains dependent on pardon and continuing help.

The preceding promise of capacity is not a reason to stop praying. It is a reason to trust that the prayer is addressed to a merciful Lord who knows exactly what His servants can bear.

### What kind of faith answers these gifts?

The concluding reflection distinguishes conscious surrender from practical surrender. Faith recognises Allah as Creator and Sustainer, while obedience puts that recognition into conduct.

The universe is not pictured as a soulless machine. Angels are Allah's devoted workers, and chosen messengers communicate His guidance. Revealed Books must become part of the believer's actual thinking, not merely objects of formal acknowledgment.

When belief in Allah, angels, Books and messengers is rooted in the heart, a person follows the shown path wholeheartedly. It is not a ceremonial display separated from the soul. Remembrance becomes continuous and life depends upon Allah.

That completes the shared reflection begun with the entrusted debt. Human freedom is a trial. The believer chooses the restraint that other creation follows under divine command, aware of the Lord's watch inside and outside.

### How do the last two verses complete the chapter?

The chapter ends neither in despair over detailed responsibility nor in dismissal of it. Acceptance of revelation is followed by assurance of capacity and a comprehensive prayer.

The believers remain responsible for their chosen good and evil, yet receive a promise that involuntary intrusions are not impossible duties. They ask for mercy where memory fails, where error occurs, where tasks are heavy and where trials exceed strength.

Finally they affirm the Guardian who can help them fulfil the whole purpose. The special merits of night-time recitation, the two lights and the Isra gifts are explained with the preceding verse, where belief and the account of those gifts belong. Here their living result is surrender, prayer and hope.

### In short

- Allah's commands respect capacity, and uncontrolled intrusions are not deliberate sins.
- Chosen inward and outward deeds remain accountable, including their continuing effects.
- The prayer seeks pardon for error, relief from heavy burdens and mercy for the future.
- Reports promise acceptance of the petitions and commend answering the final request with Amin.
- The chapter closes with responsible obedience joined to complete dependence on Allah's help.
