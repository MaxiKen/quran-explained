<<<VERSE 4:5>>>
"Do not entrust the incapable among your dependants with your wealth which Allah has made a means of support for you—but feed and clothe them from it, and speak to them kindly." This verse commands that property not be handed over to those who cannot manage it, whether children, women, or the feeble-minded, since Allah has made wealth a means of support for people. Instead, the guardian should feed and clothe them from it and speak to them kindly. It protects both the property and the dependants.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not entrust the incapable among your dependants with your wealth"** forbids giving property to those who do not know how to manage it well, including children, women, and the feeble-minded, as Ad-Dahhak reported Ibn Abbas saying, and as Ibn Masud, Al-Hakam bin Uyaynah, Al-Hasan and Ad-Dahhak also said: "Women and boys." Sa'id bin Jubayr said "the unwise" refers to orphans. Mujahid, Ikrimah and Qatadah said they are women. The same tafsir was reported from Abu Musa al-Ashari, and al-Tabari adopted it. The generality of the words includes all children, orphans and non-orphans.

**"Which Allah has made a means of support for you"** means wealth is a means of livelihood and support, not for luxury or pride.

**"But feed and clothe them from it"** commands the guardian to spend on their food and clothing from the property.

**"And speak to them kindly"** commands kind words, keeping good relations, and making a firm commitment to give them their property when they gain sound judgement.

### Holding property in escrow

The verse affirms the role of property in man's economic effort and corrects the general weakness in protecting it. People sometimes hand over property to inexperienced minor children and ill-informed women out of natural love, which results in waste and quick poverty. Ibn Abbas explained that one should not hand over all one's property to feeble-minded children and women and become dependent on them. Rather, hold the property in safe custody, spend on them as necessary, and if they demand possession, explain honestly and reasonably, saying that it is there for them when they grow up. The property of orphans remains under the guardian's safe custody as if it were his own, until they become mature and discerning, as verse 2 commands giving orphans their property. Ali bin Abi Talhah reported Ibn Abbas saying one should not give one's wealth, which Allah has made a means of sustenance, to wife or children, but hold on to it, take care of it, and be the one who spends on them. Mujahid said "speak to them words of kindness and justice" refers to kindness and keeping good relations. The verse commands kind treatment in deed with family and those under one's care.

### The value of property

Protecting property is necessary and wasting it is a sin. A person killed while defending his property is a shahid (martyr), similar to being killed in defence of one's life. The Prophet said, "Whosoever is killed while protecting his property is counted among Muslim martyrs." He also said, "For a good man, his good and clean property is the best asset of his life," and "Being rich is not harmful for one who fears Allah." The anti-wealth teachings of many saints apply only to those who spend lustily-earned wealth for sinful purposes. Poverty can take one to the point of being a disbeliever. Sufyan al-Thawri said that previously owning wealth was not considered good, but today wealth is a shield of the true Muslim, and whoever has wealth should make it serve him well, since these are times when one may first spend his faith to take care of a need. The verse also connects to 4:10, the stern warning to those who eat up an orphan's property. One reading takes "the foolish" as those who keep you from the Real and distract you from the Lord, those dependents and children who prefer their worldly portions over the rights of God. It is better to be generous than withholding for the liberation of the heart, and when your lower self calls you to spend in a useless way you are the most foolish of the foolish, so do not give in.

### In short

- The verse forbids entrusting property to those who cannot manage it.
- The guardian must feed, clothe and speak kindly to them from it.
- Wealth is a means of support, and protecting it is a duty.
- The discussion adds the views of Ibn Abbas, Abu Musa, al-Tabari and others, and the hadith literature records these points.
- One reading adds a spiritual reading of "the foolish."

<<<VERSE 4:6>>>
"Test the competence of the orphans until they reach a marriageable age. Then if you feel they are capable of sound judgment, return their wealth to them. And do not consume it wastefully and hastily before they grow up to demand it. If the guardian is well-off, they should not take compensation. But if the guardian is poor, let them take a reasonable provision. When you give orphans back their property, call in witnesses. And sufficient is Allah as a vigilant Reckoner." This verse commands testing orphans before they reach maturity, handing over their property once they show sound judgement, and not consuming it wastefully or hastily. It permits a poor guardian to take a reasonable provision and requires witnesses when returning the property. It ends with the reminder that Allah is sufficient as Reckoner.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Test the competence of the orphans"** means test their intelligence through small assignments of buying and selling before they become pubert and marriageable, to determine their ability to conduct transactions on their own. Imam Abu Hanifah deduced from this that transactions entered into by minor children with the permission of their guardian are sound, valid and operative.

**"Until they reach a marriageable age"** means until they become pubert and mature, which is the time of special assessment.

**"Then if you feel they are capable of sound judgment"** means once it is clear they understand profit and loss and handle their affairs satisfactorily.

**"Return their wealth to them"** commands handing over the property in full.

**"And do not consume it wastefully and hastily before they grow up to demand it"** forbids using up the wealth overstepping the limits Allah approved, or rushing to consume it for fear they will take it when they reach maturity.

**"If the guardian is well-off, they should not take compensation"** means a rich guardian should abstain from the orphan's property.

**"But if the guardian is poor, let them take a reasonable provision"** permits a poor guardian to take in proportion to his work, or as a loan to be paid back, in measure such that he is not in need of the orphan's wealth.

**"When you give orphans back their property, call in witnesses"** requires witnessing the transaction to protect rights and prevent disputes.

**"And sufficient is Allah as a vigilant Reckoner"** reminds that Allah suffices as witness and takes account of all.

### Testing and maturity

The verse divides children into three stages: minority before puberty, after puberty, and after self-reliance and discretion in conducting personal affairs (rushd as opposed to safahah). During the first stage, guardians should educate and train them through hands-on experience. Once they become mature, pubert and marriageable, the guardian should check their state of growth in terms of experience, intelligence and dealings, and once dependable, hand over the property. Real maturity is not tied to a particular count of years but depends on particular indicators and signs experienced by adults entering the threshold of adulthood. The verse was revealed about Thabit ibn Rifa'ah al-Ansari. The age of marrying according to al-Shafi'i is the completion of fifteen years. Test them when near maturity by giving them part of their wealth to see how they manage it. Perceiving maturity means decency and integrity, generosity and preservation of honour, keeping company with elders and striving to look to the good and perform acts of devotion. The one who is mature (rashid) is the one who is guided to his Lord and does not rely on his own power or strength when a need arises.

### The poor guardian's provision

A poor guardian may take a reasonable provision from the orphan's property in proportion to his work, or as a loan to be paid back. The condition is "in proportion to need." Many fix an insignificant monthly emolument on paper to satisfy legal requirements but spend recklessly beyond it. The last words "Allah is sufficient for reckoning" invite everyone to realize that the man who stays untouched by unlawful acquisitions is the one who fears the final accounting by Allah, and the ability to do so comes from Allah. Property is a means of livelihood, not for luxury or pride. Misappropriation of anyone's belongings is akin to creating a breach in God's arrangement of bestowing livelihood upon His servants. In the case of an orphan, the weakest member of society, it is even more imperative that his property be well-protected and he be dealt with in fairness and justice. A justified settlement with orphans should be recorded in writing and witnessed so that the executor's responsibility is honourably discharged with no question of future disputes. Whenever one takes someone's affairs in hand, he must proceed with the realization that any injustice is in the knowledge of God, who will catch hold of the arbiter on the Day of Reckoning and punish him severely. This is inevitable and none can escape it.

### In short

The hadith supports this. 

- The verse commands testing orphans before maturity and returning their property once they show sound judgement.
- It forbids consuming their wealth wastefully or hastily.
- A poor guardian may take a reasonable provision. A rich one should abstain.
- Returning the property must be witnessed.
