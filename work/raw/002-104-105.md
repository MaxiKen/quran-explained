<<<VERSE 2:104>>>
Verse 104 turns from the Jews to the believers, and teaches them to watch their words. "O believers! Do not say, “Râ’ina.” [Herd us!] But say, “Unẓurna,” [Tend to us!] and listen ˹attentively˺. And the disbelievers will suffer a painful punishment". Behind a single word lies a story of mockery, and a lasting rule about not giving enemies an opening.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers!"**. Those who believe in Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'an are addressed directly, because the fault being corrected had crept into their own speech.

**"Do not say, “Râ’ina.” [Herd us!]"**. Rāʿinā is an Arabic imperative from murāʿāh, meaning "be mindful of us", "attend to us" or "look after us", and the Arabs used it freely when asking a speaker to give them his attention. In the language of the Jews, however, a word of the same sound was a term of vile abuse, connected with ruʿūnah, thoughtlessness, and carrying the sense of "fool". Ibn ʿAbbās, in the narration of ʿAṭāʾ, told what happened. When the Jews heard the Arabs addressing the Prophet ﷺ with this word they were delighted, since in their own tongue it was a curse. They said to one another: until now we have abused Muḥammad in secret, now we can abuse him openly, because the word is part of their speech. So they would come to the Prophet ﷺ, say "Muḥammad, rāʿinā", and laugh. Saʿd ibn ʿUbādah of the Anṣār, who knew the Jews' language well, saw what they were doing and said: "Enemies of Allah, may Allah's curse be on you. By Him in whose hand is Muḥammad's soul, I will cut off the head of any one of you who says this word." They protested: "Do you not say the same thing to him yourselves?" Then Allah revealed the verse. Some Muslims had indeed begun to use it with the Prophet ﷺ in its innocent Arabic sense, to the great glee of the Jews, who had found a way to insult him openly and had even tricked the Muslims into joining in.

Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that the Jews used to say to the Prophet ﷺ "arʿinā samʿak", a form of the phrase they intended as an insult, and the same was related from Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Abū Mālik, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, ʿAṭiyyah al-ʿAwfī and Qatādah. A modern writer explains the trick of pronunciation: said properly, rāʿinā means much the same as unẓurnā, but drawing out the second vowel turns it into "our shepherd", and drawing out the first turns it into "idiot". The Jews would twist the word when they themselves addressed the Prophet ﷺ, corrupting it to mean "foolish" or "evil". Mujāhid gave two further explanations, that "do not say rāʿinā" means "do not dispute", and that it means "do not say, we hear from you and you hear from us". ʿAṭāʾ said it was simply an expression in the dialect of the Anṣār which Allah forbade. As-Suddī said that Rifāʿah ibn Zayd, a Jew of the tribe of Qaynuqāʿ, used to come to the Prophet ﷺ and say "Hear, may you hear nothing", and that some Muslims, imagining that prophets were greeted and honoured with this sort of speech, began to say the same. The Qur'an records the Jews' habit elsewhere: "Some Jews take words out of context and say, “We listen and we disobey,” “Hear! May you never hear,” and “Râ’ina!” [Herd us!]—playing with words and discrediting the faith" (4:46). After that Allah forbade the believers to use the word at all. ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said the same.

**"But say, “Unẓurna,” [Tend to us!]"**. The two words mean the same in Arabic, "look at us", "watch over us", "regard us and hear us", but unẓurnā carried no second meaning and gave no one anything to laugh at. By commanding the believers to use it Allah frustrated the Jews' design. Allah forbade His believing servants to imitate the speech and conduct of the disbelievers, for the Jews used devious words that concealed what they really meant. The ḥadīths record the same habit in their greetings: they would say "as-sāmu ʿalaykum", "death be upon you", which is why Muslims were commanded to answer "wa ʿalaykum", "and upon you", so that the supplication of the Muslims against them would be answered rather than theirs against the Muslims. Imām Aḥmad narrated from Ibn ʿUmar that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "I have been sent with the sword before the Hour, so that Allah alone is worshipped without partner. My provision has been placed beneath the shade of my spear. Humiliation and lowliness have been decreed for whoever opposes my command, and whoever imitates a people is one of them." Abū Dāwūd narrated the Prophet's ﷺ words, "Whoever imitates a people is one of them." With their threats and warnings these ḥadīths show that Muslims may not imitate the disbelievers in their sayings, deeds, dress, festivals or acts of worship, in anything of theirs that was not legislated for us.

**"and listen ˹attentively˺"**. Give ear to what you are commanded, be ready to accept it, and obey. Muslims were told to avoid ambiguous words and to speak plainly, shunning above all words with a mocking shade of meaning. They were also to pay close attention to what the Prophet ﷺ said and to try to understand it for themselves without excessive questioning, meeting his instructions with receptiveness rather than obstinacy, for this was the way to gain faith, and the strengthening of faith was the thing to be sought.

**"And the disbelievers will suffer a painful punishment"**. For the Jews who insulted the Prophet ﷺ and tried to be clever with him there is the Fire, a torment that will reach into their hearts. The verse calls these insolent Jews disbelievers (kāfirīn), which shows that deliberate disrespect towards a prophet, even by indirect means, is itself unbelief.

### Closing the door to harm

The verse establishes a principle of law. If a perfectly legitimate act on one's part gives others room to commit illegitimate acts, that act ceases to be lawful for one. A scholar's permissible deed, for instance, becomes forbidden for him if it is likely to lead the ignorant into error and induce them to do forbidden things, provided the deed is not something the Sharīʿah requires or counts among its objectives. The Qur'an and the ḥadīth supply many examples. Before Islam the Quraysh, rebuilding the Kaʿbah, had departed from the design laid down by Ibrāhīm (Abraham). A ḥadīth reports that the Prophet ﷺ wished to pull down the existing structure and rebuild it on the Abrahamic plan, but refrained, because such a step could have led ignorant people into misunderstanding and error. In the vocabulary of legal theory rulings of this kind are called sadd adh-dharāʾiʿ, blocking the means to wrongdoing. All the jurists accept the principle, and the Ḥanbalī school applies it with particular care, as al-Qurṭubī notes.

The devotional reading draws the same line in the language of friendship and enmity. The aims of enemies in all their states (fī jamīʿi aḥwālihim), in their deeds and words alike (min aʿmālihim wa aqwālihim), are malicious, since they stay bound to their own ways in what they do and leave undone. The way of the friends of God is to avoid resembling them and to walk a path that is not their path.

### In short

- The Jews turned the innocent Arabic request rāʿinā into a hidden curse and laughed when Muslims used it to the Prophet ﷺ, so Allah told the believers to say unẓurnā instead and to listen attentively.
- Saʿd ibn ʿUbādah exposed the trick, and the verse, with 4:46, records the Jews' habit of playing with words, including the greeting "death be upon you".
- Deliberate disrespect to a prophet, even indirect, is unbelief, and a painful punishment awaits it.
- Believers must not imitate disbelievers in word or deed ("whoever imitates a people is one of them"), and a lawful act becomes unlawful when it opens the door to wrong, the principle of sadd adh-dharāʾiʿ, as the Prophet's ﷺ decision not to rebuild the Kaʿbah shows.

<<<VERSE 2:105>>>
Verse 105 moves from how the Jews treated the Prophet ﷺ to how they felt about the Muslims. "The disbelievers from the People of the Book and the polytheists would not want you to receive any blessing from your Lord, but Allah selects whoever He wills for His mercy. And Allah is the Lord of infinite bounty".

### Phrase by phrase

**"The disbelievers from the People of the Book and the polytheists"**. Among the People of the Scripture this means Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and his companions, and among the idolaters of Arabia Abū Jahl and his companions. The "from" is explanatory, and the polytheists are joined to the People of the Book for emphasis, to make the point that the Jews can no more be well-wishers of the Muslims than the idolaters can, the two being alike in their hatred of them.

**"would not want you to receive any blessing from your Lord"**. Whenever the Muslims urged their Jewish allies to believe in Muḥammad ﷺ, the Jews would answer: "What you are inviting us to is no better than what we have. We only wish it were better." Allah revealed this verse to give them the lie. Some Jews used to assure the Muslims of their sincerity, pretending they would dearly have liked the Muslims to receive from Allah teachings and laws superior to their own, so that they too could accept them, but that Islam, sadly, did not appear to be the better religion. The Qur'an rejects their claim to be well-wishers and declares that the unbelievers, Jews and idolaters alike, are so jealous of the Muslims that they cannot bear the thought of their receiving any blessing from Allah whatsoever, big or small. The blessing in question is revelation itself, that Allah should send Jibrīl (Gabriel) down to their Prophet with the good of prophethood, Islam and the Book. Their motive is envy.

These Jews made two claims, that Judaism was a better religion than Islam and that they wished the Muslims well. The first they could never support with any valid argument, and it remained an empty assertion. In any case the difference between Islam and Judaism does not turn on which is better. When something new comes to abrogate something older, the older is given up as a matter of course, and Allah sent Islam to abrogate all earlier religions. Because this is so obvious, the Qur'an says nothing in refutation of the first claim and deals only with the second. In describing the deep enmity of the polytheists and the People of the Scripture, whom the believers had just been warned against imitating, Allah teaches the Muslims to keep no intimate friendship with them. At the same time He reminds them of what He has given them, the perfect Law He legislated for His Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ.

**"but Allah selects whoever He wills for His mercy"**. He chooses for His religion, His prophethood, Islam and the Book whoever deserves to be chosen, and He singled out Muḥammad ﷺ for the office of His Prophet. Through His mercy He favours whom He wills of His servants with prophethood, revelation and faith. Their jealousy can do the Muslims no harm, for Allah is Beneficent and All-Powerful and showers His special blessings on whomever He chooses.

**"And Allah is the Lord of infinite bounty"**. His favour in granting Muḥammad ﷺ prophethood and Islam is great indeed. Allah's bounty is vast, no one in creation receives any good except from Him, and it is of His bounty that He sent His Prophet and revealed His Book to us.

### Envy that cannot prevail

The believers were themselves told not to be jealous of another person's precious possession, for it was God who bestowed that blessing on His servant. God decreed that the Prophet ﷺ should receive divine revelation, and anyone earnest about learning from him could share in the blessing he received. The devotional reading observes that the hostility of enemies towards the steady decency of the friends of God never ceases, but the envier does not prevail (al-ḥasūd lā yasūd) and never obtains what he seeks. The special qualities of mercy are enough for the friends, even while the enemies are abased and the very borders of the lands where they once rejoiced are laid waste.

### In short

- Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf's party and Abū Jahl's party alike hated to see any good, above all revelation, come down to the Muslims.
- The Jews' pose as well-wishers who merely found Islam no better than Judaism is exposed as envy, and the claim of Judaism's superiority is left unanswered as an empty assertion, since the later revelation abrogates the earlier.
- Allah chooses whom He wills for His mercy and chose Muḥammad ﷺ for prophethood, and His bounty is boundless.
- The envier does not prevail, and whoever learns sincerely from the Prophet ﷺ shares in the blessing given to him.
