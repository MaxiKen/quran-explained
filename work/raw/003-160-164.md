<<<VERSE 3:160>>>
> “If Allah helps you, none can defeat you. But if He denies you help, then who else can help you? So in Allah let the believers put their trust” (3:160)

This verse completes the instruction to consult, decide, and then rely on Allah. Human planning matters, but no plan or alliance can guarantee victory if Allah does not grant it. Believers should neither despair when a setback comes nor become arrogant when they succeed.

### Phrase by phrase

**“If Allah helps you”** means that Allah’s support is the decisive factor in the believers’ success. The Muslim victory at Badr came from Him, as the earlier verses explain (3:126). At Uhud, the believers initially gained the advantage, then lost it after the archers left their position. The contrast teaches that victory is a favor, not proof that people can disregard instructions.

Allah’s help does not mean that believers should wait passively. They are to prepare, consult, act justly, and follow sound leadership. The Prophet ﷺ used practical means at Badr and Uhud. His companions used weapons, knowledge of the terrain, and discipline. Trust in Allah gives these efforts their proper place without treating them as independent powers.

**“none can defeat you”** means no enemy can overcome a community if Allah grants it support. This is not a promise that believers will never lose a battle or suffer injury. The Qur’an has already shown that the believers were tested at Uhud. The verse promises that Allah’s help is greater than any opponent, while reminding people that His wisdom may include allowing a temporary setback.

**“But if He denies you help, then who else can help you?”** asks believers to consider whether any ally can replace Allah. Human beings may offer useful assistance, but none can guarantee the final outcome. This is not a prohibition on seeking support from people. It is a warning against putting ultimate trust in alliances, leaders, or resources instead of Allah.

**“So in Allah let the believers put their trust”** means rely on Him alone in the deepest sense, while taking reasonable action. The instruction follows consultation and a decision in the preceding verse. It does not contradict planning. It prevents planning from turning into self-reliance or despair when events change.

### Trust through Badr and Uhud

The believers saw both sides of the lesson. At Badr they were few but Allah gave them victory. At Uhud they initially drove back Quraysh, then experienced a reversal after some archers left their post. Neither event proves that numbers alone control outcomes. The believers had responsibilities in both battles, and Allah remained the ultimate helper.

The verse also relates to the Prophet’s gentleness and consultation. A leader may act with mercy, seek advice, and make a careful decision, but the result is not theirs to command. Reports on consultation include narrations from Abu Hurayrah and Abdullah bin Amr. Abu Dawud, An-Nasa'i, At-Tirmidhi, Ibn Jarir, and Ibn Majah preserve reports on the subject, and At-Tirmidhi graded a related report hasan. Al-Hasan al-Basri explained that the Prophet’s gentle conduct was itself a mercy from Allah.

The Prophet’s practice at Badr, Uhud, al-Ahzab, and Hudaybiyyah shows that tawakkul can include consultation and careful planning. He asked companions for their views and sometimes accepted advice that differed from his initial inclination. Then he entrusted the outcome to Allah. The believer can follow this pattern in family, community, and public affairs.

### In short

- Allah’s help, not numbers or alliances, determines the ultimate outcome.
- Trust in Allah follows preparation, consultation, and action.
- A temporary setback does not mean Allah has abandoned the believers.

<<<VERSE 3:161>>>
> “It is not appropriate for a prophet to illegally withhold spoils of war. And whoever does so, it will be held against them on the Day of Judgment. Then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged” (3:161)

The verse declares that a prophet cannot betray the trust of the community by withholding part of the spoils. It also warns everyone against misappropriating shared property: what a person takes unjustly will be brought forward for judgment, and every person will receive the full consequence of their actions.

### Phrase by phrase

**“It is not appropriate for a prophet to illegally withhold spoils of war”** means a prophet does not secretly take what belongs to the community or distribute it dishonestly. The term ghulul refers to misappropriating property entrusted for common distribution. The verse clears the Prophet ﷺ of an accusation that arose when a piece of red cloth was missing from the spoils at Badr.

Ibn Abbas explains that some people wondered whether the Prophet had taken the missing cloth. The rumor was answered by revelation. Ibn Jarir records this explanation, and Abu Dawud and At-Tirmidhi transmit reports about the incident. The accusation was grave because a prophet is entrusted to convey Allah’s message and administer the community’s affairs. The verse rejects the idea that the Messenger would betray the people who trusted him.

The word can also refer broadly to betrayal of a trust, not only theft from battle spoils. The common property belonged to the whole group. Taking from it secretly harms many people, and the individual wronged may be difficult to identify. This makes public or shared wealth especially important to protect.

**“And whoever does so, it will be held against them on the Day of Judgment”** means that the person will appear with what they misappropriated and be accountable before Allah. A hadith from Abu Hurayrah warns against anyone appearing with unlawfully taken property, whether it is an animal, clothing, or wealth, asking the Prophet ﷺ to intercede. The Prophet’s responsibility was to convey the warning, not to erase a person’s deliberate injustice after the fact.

The warning applies to public funds and charitable property as well as spoils. A person entrusted with zakah, a school fund, a mosque account, a government treasury, or a shared family asset must not treat it as private income. The size of the amount does not make betrayal acceptable. Nor does a claim that the property was “only a little” remove the right of those to whom it belongs.

**“Then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done”** means both good and evil deeds receive their due. The verse does not say that a person who has misappropriated property cannot repent. They should stop, return what they took where possible, and seek forgiveness. But they should not assume that private remorse alone repairs another person’s material right.

**“and none will be wronged”** means Allah’s judgment will be perfectly just. No one’s good deeds will be lost, and no one will be charged with what they did not do. A person entrusted with office or property may escape human detection, but cannot escape Allah’s knowledge.

### A public trust, not a private gift

Abu Humayd al-Saʿidi reports that the Prophet ﷺ appointed Ibn al-Lutbiyyah to collect zakah. When the official returned, he separated what he said was for the public treasury from what he called a personal gift. The Prophet stood on the minbar and asked why the man had not stayed at home to see whether the gift would still have been given to him. The question exposed the conflict of interest: a gift received because of public office may in fact belong to the public.

Hisham ibn ʿUrwah transmits Abu Humayd’s account. It is recorded in the Two Sahihs. Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah, and al-Tabarani also preserve reports relevant to the prohibition of ghulul and the accountability of public officials. Ahmad records a warning against taking another person’s land, and another report from Abu Hurayrah describes the disgrace of carrying stolen property on the Day of Resurrection. These narrations make clear that public service is an amanah, a trust.

A report about Muʿadh bin Jabal says that when the Prophet sent him to Yemen, he warned him not to take anything without permission. Muʿadh’s assignment included responsibilities concerning zakah and the community. The instruction shows that a public representative remains accountable even when far from the leader’s direct oversight.

The same duty of honesty applied during the campaigns at Badr and Uhud, and in the community’s affairs in Madinah. Abu Sufyan led the Quraysh at Badr, while the Prophet later consulted the companions about the treaty at al-Ahzab and the journey for Umrah at Hudaybiyyah. On each occasion, the lawful purpose of the campaign did not make private misappropriation acceptable. The companions had rejected the attitude of the Children of Israel who told Musa to fight while they stayed behind. Their service on the path of Allah was a trust, not a license to take what belonged to the Ummah.

Abu Malik al-Ashja'i transmits a warning against taking even a small portion of another person’s land. Ahmad records the report. Abu Hurairah also reports the warning that a person who steals from the spoils will be brought forward carrying what they took. Al-Bukhari and Muslim preserve versions of this teaching. The point is that even something small, whether land, clothing, or al-mal, property, is not harmless if it belongs to another person or the public.

Umar ibn al-Khattab is also mentioned in a report about a man who died at Khaybar and was called a martyr by the companions. The Prophet ﷺ corrected them after learning that the man had taken a garment from the spoils. The point is not to declare a particular person’s eternal fate from limited knowledge. It is to show that apparently small dishonesty can carry serious consequences, and that worldly courage does not cancel the duty of honesty.

### In short

- Prophets are protected from betrayal, and the verse rejects an accusation against Muhammad ﷺ.
- Misappropriating shared or public property is a serious breach of trust.
- Repentance should include restoring what was taken, and Allah’s judgment wrongs no one.

<<<VERSE 3:162>>>
> “Are those who seek Allah’s pleasure like those who deserve Allah’s wrath? Hell is their home. What an evil destination” (3:162)

The verse asks whether a person who seeks Allah’s pleasure through obedience can be equal to one who earns His anger through wrongdoing. Their choices lead to different outcomes, and the verse warns that Hell is a terrible destination.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Are those who seek Allah’s pleasure”** refers to people who try to obey Allah and pursue what He loves. Their aim is not to gain praise or seize what belongs to others. They seek Allah’s approval through sincere conduct, including honesty with shared property and obedience to the Messenger.

**“like those who deserve Allah’s wrath?”** means the two paths are not morally equivalent. The verse does not invite believers to declare that a named person is destined for Hell. It describes the consequence of persistent wrongdoing and betrayal, while final judgment remains with Allah.

The verse follows the warning against ghulul. A person who seeks Allah’s pleasure will not conceal public property or treat an entrusted position as an opportunity for private gain. The one who betrays a trust chooses a path that brings Allah’s anger unless they repent and repair the harm.

**“Hell is their home”** names the consequence of dying in persistent rejection and wrongdoing. **“What an evil destination”** urges the listener to turn away from that path. The warning is serious, but it does not close the door of repentance during life.

The Qur’an elsewhere contrasts the person who knows the truth with the one who is blind to it (13:19), and the promised Garden with temporary worldly pleasure (28:61). A believer should consider not only what an action gains today, but where it leads in the Hereafter.

### How intention and action differ

The contrast is not simply between people who make mistakes and people who never do. It is between a direction of life that seeks Allah’s approval and a direction that persists in defiance. The person who has done wrong can still repent, return what was taken, and seek forgiveness. The warning is for anyone who chooses the path of betrayal and refuses correction.

The Qur’an also teaches that people will be judged according to what they know and do. The one who recognizes the truth is not like someone who turns away from it (13:19). This should make the believer examine their own conduct before condemning another person.

### In short

- Seeking Allah’s pleasure and betraying trust are not equal paths.
- The warning concerns the consequence of persistent wrongdoing, not a license to judge individuals.
- Repentance and restitution remain open before death.

<<<VERSE 3:163>>>
> “They ˹each˺ have varying degrees in the sight of Allah. And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do” (3:163)

People have different ranks with Allah, according to their deeds and the choices they make. Allah sees them fully and will judge each person with perfect knowledge and justice.

### Phrase by phrase

**“They each have varying degrees in the sight of Allah”** means people are not all equal in their standing. Those who seek Allah’s pleasure and those who disobey Him have different outcomes. Even among the righteous, people differ in sincerity, service, knowledge, and perseverance. The verse does not make social status or worldly wealth the measure of rank.

The Qur’an says that all people have degrees according to what they do (6:132). A person entrusted with wealth or leadership has an added responsibility, not an automatic higher rank. Someone with less public visibility may be closer to Allah through sincerity and faithful conduct.

The preceding example of misappropriation makes the distinction practical. A person who manages zakah or spoils honestly is not like one who secretly takes them. A small-looking act can affect a person’s standing because it reveals whether they treat the trust as Allah’s property or as an opportunity for themselves.

**“And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do”** means nothing is hidden from Him. He sees public actions and private intentions, and He knows whether a person has repented or persisted. People may be deceived by outward appearances, but Allah will compensate or judge each person according to the full truth.

The rank of any individual is not for people to declare with certainty. Believers should strive for what pleases Allah and avoid arrogance about their own deeds. Allah’s knowledge encompasses every person, from the most visible leader to one whose good deeds are known only to Him.

### How Allah’s ranks are known

The rank of a person is not measured by their wealth, lineage, or public position. A wealthy official may be low in Allah’s sight if they betray a trust, while a person with little status may be elevated by sincerity and service. The Qur’an says that all people are assigned degrees according to their deeds (6:132).

Al-Hasan al-Basri and Muhammad ibn Ishaq explain that the righteous and wrongdoers occupy different ranks. Abu ʿUbaydah and Al-Kisa'i also discuss the degrees and stations of reward and punishment. Their reflections direct the reader to the reality that Allah sees each action, not to ranking people by appearances.

### In short

- People’s ranks depend on their deeds and sincerity, not wealth or office.
- Allah sees outward actions and hidden intentions.
- Believers should strive without claiming certain knowledge of another person’s final rank.

<<<VERSE 3:164>>>
> “Indeed, Allah has done the believers a ˹great˺ favour by raising a messenger from among them—reciting to them His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom. For indeed they had previously been clearly astray” (3:164)

The verse names the Prophet’s mission as one of Allah’s greatest favors to the believers. He is from among them, recites revelation, purifies the community, and teaches the Book and wisdom. Before his guidance, people were in clear error.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Allah has done the believers a great favour”** means the coming of the Messenger is a gift from Allah, not an achievement the community produced for itself. His teaching guides people toward worship, justice, and a life that leads to the Hereafter. The believers should respond with gratitude, learning, and obedience.

The Prophet’s favor is not limited to worldly power. Allah is the Most High and free of need, yet He sent guidance for the benefit of people. The Qur’an describes the Prophet as deeply compassionate toward the believers (9:128). The benefit of his message is available to all humanity, although believers are those who receive and follow it most fully.

**“by raising a messenger from among them”** means Muhammad ﷺ was human and belonged to a community with a shared language, history, and ordinary life. People could speak with him, ask questions, and understand how revelation applied in practice. The Qur’an says messengers before him were human beings from the towns to whom revelation was given (12:109), and that the messengers ate food and walked in markets (25:20). It also says that Allah created spouses from among people themselves (30:21), illustrating the wisdom of familiarity and shared human life.

The Qur’an directs the Prophet to say that he is a human being like others, with revelation that Allah is One (18:110). This protects tawhid, belief in Allah’s oneness, while affirming the Prophet’s honored role. He is neither an angel nor divine. He is the Messenger Allah chose to convey and embody His guidance.

The mission also recalls the prayer of Ibrahim, who asked Allah to send a messenger among his descendants to recite the revelations and teach the Book and wisdom (2:129). The Prophet came from the lineage of Abraham and from Quraysh in Makkah, but his message was not limited to one tribe. Allah sent messengers from among different peoples so they could understand and respond to the call (6:130).

**“reciting to them His revelations”** means the Prophet conveyed the Qur’an faithfully. Recitation gives the community access to Allah’s words and reminds people that guidance is revelation, not personal invention. The Prophet’s recitation called the listeners to faith, challenged injustice, and gave them words for worship and remembrance.

**“purifying them”** means helping the people leave disbelief, false worship, and harmful character. Purification includes turning from shirk and developing honesty, mercy, patience, and self-restraint. It does not mean that the companions became incapable of error. The preceding verses describe their mistakes and Allah’s pardon. Spiritual growth involves learning, repentance, and correction.

The Prophet’s closeness to the people mattered. He listened, answered questions, and consulted them in affairs. His gentleness helped them accept correction rather than withdraw. Revelation and moral formation worked together: the Qur’an taught what was true, while the Prophet’s example demonstrated how to live it.

**“and teaching them the Book”** means explaining the Qur’an and showing its guidance in practice. The companions were Arabic speakers, yet understanding revelation required more than knowing vocabulary. They learned context, commands, and how general principles applied to new situations.

**“and wisdom”** refers to the Prophet’s teaching and guidance alongside recitation of the Book. The Qur’an elsewhere connects the Messenger’s role with teaching wisdom. The Sunnah preserves his practical example in worship, public conduct, and personal dealings. The distinction does not place the Sunnah above the Qur’an. It explains how the Messenger taught and clarified Allah’s message.

The Prophet’s teaching gave the community ways to practice salah, pay zakah, perform hajj and ʿumrah, and govern shared affairs. His companions learned through speech and observation, then passed the instruction to the Ummah. Reports of hadith preserve this teaching, while scholars assess their reliability and apply them carefully within the Shariah.

**“For indeed they had previously been clearly astray”** means that before revelation, people lacked the guidance that would orient their worship and moral life. The phrase describes the serious need for a Messenger. It does not deny that individuals might have had virtues, but their community needed the clear revelation that would distinguish truth from error.

The Qur’an mentions the Children of Israel and other peoples who received prophets and revelation. It also tells believers that a messenger from among themselves is a favor from Allah. This special address is not a reason to boast over other people. It calls the believers to gratitude, humility, and sharing the guidance with wisdom.

### The four connected parts of the prophetic mission

The verse describes a sequence. The Messenger recites the revelation so people hear Allah’s words. He purifies them by calling them away from sin and toward repentance. He teaches the Book so they understand its guidance. He teaches wisdom so they can embody that guidance in real situations. These functions belong together.

If people recite without learning, they may miss how revelation should shape their conduct. If they seek moral improvement without revelation, they may mistake personal preference for divine guidance. If they study rules without wisdom, they may apply them without mercy or understanding. The Messenger’s example unites revelation, knowledge, worship, and character.

This favor remains through the Qur’an and the Sunnah. A person today cannot sit with the Prophet as the first companions did, but can study his words, actions, and character through reliable reports. They should learn from qualified teachers, distinguish sound hadith from weak attribution, and apply the guidance with humility.

Allah’s favor is described as being **“among the believers”** because they received the message and benefited from it. Yet the Prophet’s mission reaches beyond them. The call to worship Allah alone and live with justice is for all people. The believers honor the favor by embodying the qualities the Messenger taught: truthfulness, mercy, fairness, and trust in Allah.

### In short

- The Messenger’s coming is a favor from Allah and a source of guidance.
- His mission joins recitation, purification, teaching the Book, and teaching wisdom.
- Gratitude for the Prophet means learning from his guidance and living it with humility.
