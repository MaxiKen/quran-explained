<<<VERSE 7:142>>>
The account moves from the sea to the mountain, and the law is given over forty nights. "We appointed for Moses thirty nights then added another ten—completing his Lord’s term of forty nights. Moses commanded his brother Aaron, “Take my place among my people, do what is right, and do not follow the way of the corruptors.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"We appointed for Moses thirty nights"** records the promise made to him. The word used here in the Book is read in two ways, wa'ada and wa-'adnā, and it carries the sense of making an offer of something good, so that what was promised was that Allah would speak to him directly and give him the guidance for his people. The promise was that he should go to the mount and spend thirty nights there in devotion.

**"then added another ten—completing his Lord’s term of forty nights"** gives the rest of it. The thirty were followed by ten more, and the whole of the term appointed by his Lord came to forty nights.

**"Moses commanded his brother Aaron, “Take my place among my people”"** is what he said before he left. He set his brother over the people in his absence and gave him charge of their affair until he came back to them. The account notes in this place that Allah was reminding the Children of Israel of the guidance He had sent to them, by speaking to Musa directly and giving the Torah to him. That book contained their law and the details of their legislation, and the promise of those forty nights was the beginning of it.

**"do what is right"** is the first instruction. Its object is not named in the words, and that may be deliberate, so that it stands as a general command covering the whole conduct of the people and of the deputy himself.

**"and do not follow the way of the corruptors"** is the second. Since Harun was himself a prophet of Allah, the command cannot be taken as a warning against committing corruption in his own person. What it means is that he was not to do anything that would help or encourage the people of corruption. That command was tested soon enough. When the people were taken up by the calf that Samiri made for them, Harun stood against the deed and warned Samiri, and the account of it later shows Musa calling his brother to account over what had happened, since he took it for a failure of diligence. The verse leaves a lesson in that for a community that confuses negligence with piety.

### Why thirty nights and then ten

The term was fixed at forty nights in the will of Allah, and the question of why the first part was thirty and the rest was added afterwards has been taken up by those who explained the Book. No one has access to the whole of the wisdom in the acts of Allah, and what they have offered are explanations by way of likely reasoning.

One of them is that the wisdom behind the arrangement was the gradual enjoining of the law, so that it would be easier for the people to put into practice. Another is that those who are in authority are being taught to give their subordinates time when the work is not finished by the day it was set for. That is what happened here. The spiritual readiness that was being sought was not reached within the thirty nights, so ten more were added, and the time needed for that perfection was given to him. The Book recalls the same appointment in another place, where it says that Allah delivered them from their enemy and made a covenant with them on the right side of the Mount (20:80).

The reports of what he did in those days are also given in detail. Musa fasted through the thirty days and nights without breaking the fast. When the thirty came to an end he took his meal, cleaned his teeth with a twig, and presented himself at the appointed place on the mount. Then Allah told him of the odour that the fasting mouth carries, and that it is dear to Him. Since the cleaning had taken the odour away, he was told to fast for ten more days, so that the odour would be there again.

That report about the odour is not a proof that cleaning the teeth during a fast is forbidden or disliked. The report is quoted without a chain of narrators, and even where it is sound it may be a command that belonged to Musa alone, or to the people of the Torah. The permission to clean the teeth while fasting is established by the practice of the Prophet ﷺ. Al-Baihaqi carried the tradition from Aishah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that the best act of one who is fasting is the cleaning of the teeth with the miswak, and Al-Jami' Al-Saghir records the status of that report as hasan, which is a grade of authentic tradition.

### The two journeys of Musa

A question was raised about a difference in the accounts. Musa spent thirty days fasting through the night without breaking his fast at all, while on the journey he made to visit Khizr, of which the story is told in another surah, he could not hold out even half a day. He said there, give us our food, since this journey of ours has left us tired (18:62). Ruh al-Bayan explained the difference through the nature of the two journeys. The journey after knowledge was a journey of one created being to another created being, and it was a journey of teaching courtesy and of hardship. In it he was carrying himself along, he was aware of his own suffering, and he saw the marks of hunger because he was still with himself. The thirty nights on the mount were different in kind. That was a journey for the Creator, made by a devoted servant who had cut himself off from the creation in the search for his Lord. In a journey of that kind, the vigour of the body's demands for food and drink is weakened, and a person becomes able to fast through the days and the nights together.

The inner reading of the whole passage is put in a way that suits the subject. It was said of these verses that the promises of lovers, even when they are put off, show intimacy. In the matter of ordinary dealings, adding days to a promised meeting is no part of good faith, and the rule does not hold in the way it does between friends. What the Lord of the worlds did with Musa is taken as the illustration. He promised him thirty days, and when they passed He added ten more, and the addition was made because Musa was pleased with it. He counted the thirty as the capital and the ten as the profit, since the speech of his Lord was heard a second time in the extra nights. In that journey he waited thirty days without recalling food or drink and without being aware of hunger, carried along as he was by the journey of generosity, while on the other journey he was still in the road of creatures.

### The nights and the months

Another point is drawn from the wording. In the laws given to the prophets, the date turned over at night, and that is why the verse says thirty nights and not thirty days. The lunar month begins with the sighting of the moon, and the moon can only be sighted at night, so the dates in that calendar turn over at sunset. Al-Qurtubi carried the saying of Ibn al-Arabi on this, that the solar calendar serves the benefits of worldly affairs while the lunar calendar serves the acts of religion.

The particular months are named in the reports. Ibn Abbas said that the thirty nights were the nights of Dhul-Qa'dah, the eleventh month of the lunar calendar, and that the ten nights added to them were the first ten of Dhū’l-Hijja. On that reckoning the Torah was given to Musa on the day of Eid al-Adha, and the report is carried by Al-Qurtubi.

### The number forty and the practice of doing things by degrees

The number forty is taken as having an effect of its own in the correction of the heart. It is reported from the Prophet ﷺ that the one who worships Allah for forty days with sincerity of heart is given wisdom as a spring in his heart, and the report is given in Ruh al-Bayan.

The verse also teaches the people to fix a period for the thing they are working for, and to come at their aims by stages. That is the way Allah does things. Haste and hurry in affairs are not approved by Him, and the creation of the universe in six days, when He had the power to create it in a single moment, is the plainest instruction in the principle. The Torah was not given to Musa in one moment either, and the period fixed for it was a way of stressing the same practice.

It was by ignoring that principle that the Children of Israel lost their footing. When Musa left for the mount he told them that he would be away for thirty days, and when he did not come back in that time, since he had been kept ten days longer, they grew impatient and said that he had been lost somewhere and that they should choose another leader. So they fell in with Samiri and began to worship the calf. Had they been a people of patience, and had they understood that things come by degrees, they would not have fallen into that error.

### The deputy and the weight of the trust

The appointment of Harun carries two lessons of religious importance.

The first is that a person who holds a responsible office should appoint somebody to take care of the work while he is away. Musa made a point of this before he went up the mount. The Prophet ﷺ used to appoint a deputy whenever he left Madinah, and Ali and Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum were given that trust on different occasions. The report is carried by Al-Qurtubi.

The second is that the person leaving should set out instructions for the one who is to take his place. Musa gave two. The general one was that his brother should put things right, and the specific one was that he was not to follow the path of those who work corruption. Both of them belong to the handing over of a trust, and both of them were in place before he left.

### What the community needed to be told

There is an order in the commands of the religion, and this verse marks a stage in it. This passage speaks of the time that followed the destruction of the Pharaoh and his people. What Musa carried in Egypt was the call, while the laws were given to him after he reached the desert of Sinai. In ordinary conditions, what is asked of believers is that they put their own lives right and live as true servants of God, and that they invite others to the oneness of God and to a life directed towards the Hereafter. Once believers have become a community with a standing of their own and have the freedom to order themselves, as the Israelites did in the desert, it becomes binding on them to build their social life on the basis of the sacred law.

When Musa set Aaron over the Israelites in his absence, he said to him to take his place among the people, to act rightly, and not to follow the way of those who spread corruption. That is the basic principle of carrying responsibility at the head of a community, and the verse gives it in the words of one prophet to another.

### In short

- Allah promised Musa thirty nights on the mount and completed the term with ten more, so that the appointed time came to forty nights.
- The promise was the beginning of the revelation of the Torah, which carried the law of the Children of Israel and the details of their legislation.
- Musa fasted the term, and the reports explain the addition of ten through the odour that Allah loves in the mouth of one who fasts, while the permission to clean the teeth while fasting stays established by the practice of the Prophet ﷺ.
- The nights were the month of Dhul-Qa'dah with the first ten of Dhul-Hijjah, which places the giving of the Torah on the day of Eid al-Adha, and the counting of dates at night is why the verse speaks of nights rather than days.
- Musa set his brother Aaron over the people with two instructions, to put things right and not to follow the people of corruption, and the verse teaches both the appointment of a deputy and the giving of instructions to him.
