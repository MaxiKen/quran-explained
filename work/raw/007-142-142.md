<<<VERSE 7:142>>>
With Pharaoh and his people destroyed, the Israelites enjoyed a time of peace. They asked Mūsā (Moses) for a religious code of law that they could live by, and Mūsā prayed to Allah for a code of guidance for them. Allah now reminds the children of Israel of the guidance He sent them by speaking directly to Mūsā and revealing to him the Torah (Tawrāh), which held their law and the details of its rulings: "We appointed for Moses thirty nights then added another ten—completing his Lord’s term of forty nights." Before leaving, Mūsā gave his brother charge of the people: "Moses commanded his brother Aaron, “Take my place among my people, do what is right, and do not follow the way of the corruptors.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"We appointed for Moses thirty nights"**: the verb can be read wa-wāʿadnā or wa-waʿadnā. It comes from waʿdah, a spoken promise of something good. Allah promised to send His word to Mūsā. The condition was that Mūsā should go to Mount Sinai and spend thirty nights there in devotion to Allah, after which Allah would speak to him, once he had fasted through that period. Those thirty nights were the month of Dhū’l-Qaʿda.

**"then added another ten"**: ten more nights, from the month of Dhū’l-Ḥijja. The scholars of tafsīr said that Mūsā fasted through the thirty, and when they ended he cleaned his teeth with a twig. Then Allah told him to complete the term by adding ten more, making forty in all. One explanation says he disliked the stale odour of his mouth at the end of the fast and so cleaned his teeth. Allah then told him to fast ten more nights, so that He would speak to him with that odour on his breath.

**"completing his Lord’s term of forty nights."** The time appointed by his Lord, the time Allah had promised to speak to him, was completed as forty nights. In the Arabic, "forty" describes a state and "nights" makes the count specific. When the term was over, Mūsā was about to go to the Mount, as Allah says: "O Children of Israel! We saved you from your enemy, and made an appointment with you on the right side of Mount Ṭûr" (20:80).

**"Moses commanded his brother Aaron, “Take my place among my people"**: when he was setting out for the Mount, to be alone with his Lord, Mūsā told his brother Hārūn (Aaron) to be his deputy and lead his people in his place.

**"do what is right"**: enjoin them to do right, act rightly among them in their affairs, govern them wisely and treat them kindly. The word aṣliḥ, "set right", is given here without an object. Perhaps it was a general command meant for the Israelites and for Hārūn alike.

**"and do not follow the way of the corruptors.”"** Do not follow the path of those who spread corruption, whether by committing transgressions yourself, by going along with them in disobedience, or by helping the wrongdoers. This was only a reminder, for Hārūn was an honourable and noble prophet with a high standing before Allah.

### Why thirty nights, and then ten more?

In Allah's will the number of nights Mūsā was to spend was forty from the start. Why then was he first told thirty, and then given ten more? No one can reach all the wisdom in Allah's acts, but scholars have offered some explanations. The well-known commentary Rūḥ al-Bayān says one wisdom was to lay down commands gradually, so that people find them easier to carry out. Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī says it was to teach those in authority to grant their subordinates extra time when they fail to finish a task in the set period. On this view, the spiritual perfection required of Mūsā was not reached in thirty nights, so ten more were added to give him time to attain it.

It is reported that Mūsā kept his fast unbroken through all thirty days and nights. After completing the thirty days he ate, and then presented himself at the appointed place on Mount Sinai. Allah told him that He loves the particular odour that fasting leaves in the mouth. Mūsā had lost it by cleaning his teeth, so he was told to fast ten more days to bring it back.

### Does this mean cleaning the teeth while fasting is disliked?

The report about the lost odour cannot be taken to mean that cleaning the teeth after fasting is forbidden or disliked. First, it is cited without any chain of narrators. Second, it may have been a command meant only for Mūsā, or only for the followers of the Torah, and not for others. The permissibility of using the tooth-stick while fasting is established by the Prophet's ﷺ practice. Al-Baihaqī reported, on the authority of ʿĀʾishah, that the Prophet ﷺ said the best act of a fasting person is cleaning the teeth with the miswāk. Al-Jāmiʿ al-Ṣaghīr grades this hadith ḥasan.

### How could Mūsā go thirty days without food?

One may wonder how Mūsā could fast for thirty days without even eating at night. On his journey to meet Khiẓr, he could not wait even half a day, and said: "Bring us our meal! We have certainly been exhausted by today’s journey" (18:62).

Rūḥ al-Bayān explains that the difference lay in the nature of the two journeys. The journey to Khiẓr was one created being travelling to another created being. The journey on Mount Sinai was a devoted servant travelling to his Creator, having cut himself off from creation in search of his Lord. That spiritual journey weakened the pull of hunger and thirst, and made him able to fast for thirty days and nights on end.

The inner reading says the same. On this journey Mūsā waited thirty days without a thought of food or drink, unaware of hunger, because the Real was carrying him on a journey of generosity, waiting to speak with Him in intimate prayer. The other time, when he was sent to Khiẓr on his first journey in search of knowledge, he could not bear half a day of hunger. That was a journey of learning courtesy and of hardship. At the start of that journey he was the one carrying, not the one being carried. Being with himself, he felt his own pain, and walking the road of creatures, he noticed the signs of hunger.

### Why "nights" and not "days"?

The verse also shows that in the laws of the prophets the date changed at night. That is why it speaks of thirty nights rather than thirty days. The prophets' laws reckoned time by the moon. A lunar month begins with the sighting of the new moon, which can only happen at night, so in the lunar calendar the date changes at sunset. Al-Qurṭubī reports Ibn al-ʿArabī as saying that the solar calendar serves worldly interests, while the lunar calendar serves religious observance.

According to ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās, the thirty nights were the nights of Dhū’l-Qaʿda, the eleventh lunar month, and the ten added nights were the first ten of Dhū’l-Ḥijja. This suggests that the Torah was given to Mūsā on the day of ʿĪd al-Aḍḥā.

### Why forty?

The verse also suggests that the number forty has a special effect in setting the heart right. A hadith says that whoever worships Allah sincerely for forty days will have his heart made a spring of wisdom, as Rūḥ al-Bayān reports.

### Why move toward a goal by stages?

The verse also teaches people to set a period for reaching their goal and to approach it step by step, for it is Allah's way to do things gradually. Allah does not approve of haste. He created the universe in six days, though He could have created it in an instant without needing a single moment. In this He gave people a wise principle: approach your goals in stages, over time, so that you can give them proper attention. In the same way, the Torah was not given to Mūsā in a moment. A period was fixed for it to stress the same principle.

By ignoring this principle the Israelites lost their faith. Mūsā had told them, as he left for Mount Sinai, to expect him back after thirty days. He was kept ten days longer, and the impatient Israelites said Mūsā was lost somewhere and they should choose another leader. So they fell prey to the sorcerer Sāmirī and began worshipping the golden calf. Had they been patient and willing to go step by step, they would not have committed that fatal act of disbelief.

### Should a leader appoint a deputy?

Mūsā made a point of appointing Hārūn as his deputy when he set out for Mount Sinai, telling him to take responsibility for his people in his absence. This makes it necessary for anyone holding a position of responsibility to appoint someone to look after the work while he is away. The Prophet ﷺ always appointed a deputy whenever he left Madinah. On different occasions ʿAlī and ʿAbdullāh ibn Umm Maktūm were appointed to this role.

Mūsā also gave Hārūn instructions before leaving, which shows that giving guidelines to one's deputy is also a religious requirement. Hārūn, being a prophet of Allah, could never be imagined to take part in corruption. So the instruction not to follow the corrupters meant that he should do nothing that could help or encourage them. That is exactly what Hārūn did when he saw his people following the magician Sāmirī, until they began worshipping the golden calf. He tried to stop them and warned Sāmirī against his mischief. Later Mūsā called him to account for what had happened, thinking it was the result of some weakness on Hārūn's part. This is also a lesson for those who neglect sound management of affairs and take that neglect as a sign of piety.

### What order did Allah's commands follow?

Mūsā was given the commands of calling people to Allah (daʿwah) in Egypt, while the laws came only once he was in the Sinai desert. This shows the order of Allah's commands. In ordinary circumstances, believers are required to set their personal lives right and live as true servants of Allah. Alongside this, they must invite others to believe in Allah's oneness and to live for the Hereafter. But when believers become an independent community, free to organise themselves as the Israelites were in the Sinai desert, it becomes their duty to build their social life on Islamic law. Mūsā's charge to Hārūn, to take his place, act rightly and not follow the way of the corrupters, sets out the basic principle for anyone who bears responsibility as the head of a community.

### The inner reading: promises in friendship

How exalted it is to make promises in friendship! How great to sit at the meeting place friendship has promised! How sweet it is when a promise is put off within the religion of friendship! Of the hints in this verse, the master of the spiritual path (ṭarīqah) said, "The promises of lovers, even when put off, are a sign of intimacy." And he said, "You put me off, You delay, You promise, and You do not come."

Pushing back a promised time and adding days before the promised moment is not approved, except in the religion of friendship. There even disloyalty counts as loyalty. See what the Lord of the worlds did with Mūsā in this exchange. He promised him thirty days, and when they had passed, He added ten more. He added them because Mūsā was glad of it. Mūsā counted the thirty days as his capital and the ten days as profit. It was as if he said, "When He added those days, I heard the ready coin of the Real's speech a second time."

As the poet says: "Come, by your life! Do not stay away! Give us hope of meeting you, then put us off. Promise and delay as you please, for we love you even when you keep the longing waiting. If you keep your promise we will rejoice, but we will live on your promise, content for a while."

### In short

- Allah appointed thirty nights for Mūsā on Mount Sinai and added ten, making forty, before speaking to him and giving him the Torah.
- The report that the ten days restored the fasting odour does not make using the miswāk while fasting disliked, as a hadith praises it.
- Counting by nights reflects the lunar calendar of the prophets' laws, and the forty may have ended on the day of ʿĪd al-Aḍḥā.
- The verse teaches gradual progress, appointing and instructing a deputy, and building community life on Allah's law once believers are free to do so.
- Mūsā could fast thirty days because he was being carried toward his Lord, unlike his tiring journey to Khiẓr.
