<<<VERSE 4:163>>>
The equal sending: "Indeed, We have sent revelation to you ˹O Prophet˺ as We sent revelation to Noah and the prophets after him. We also sent revelation to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants, ˹as well as˺ Jesus, Job, Jonah, Aaron, and Solomon. And to David We gave the Psalms" (4:163). Verse 163 answers the demand for a written book from heaven by putting the Prophet ﷺ in the company of every name the demanders themselves accept.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We have sent revelation to you ˹O Prophet˺ as We sent revelation to Noah and the prophets after him"** was revealed as a rebuttal. Sukayn and ʿAdī bin Zayd said to the Prophet ﷺ that they did not know that Allah had sent down anything to any human after Mūsā. The answer came down: revelation reached Muḥammad ﷺ exactly as it reached Nūḥ and the prophets after him. Wahy is a command of Allah, unique and inimitable, His message sent to prophets, and whoever believed in the revelation given to the past prophets must believe in this one too. Whoever rejects it becomes, so to say, a rejecter of all those revelations.

**"We also sent revelation to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants"** continues the line: Ibrāhīm and his two sons Ismāʿīl and Isḥāq, then Yaʿqūb the son of Isḥāq, and the al-Asbāṭ, the offspring of the twelve sons of Yaʿqūb, the prophets of the twelve tribes of the Israelites. To each of these, the tradition notes, Gabriel was sent as he was sent to Ibrāhīm.

**"˹as well as˺ Jesus, Job, Jonah, Aaron, and Solomon"** continues the register, and **"And to David We gave the Psalms"** closes it. The Zabūr is the name of the Book revealed to Dāwūd, known in earlier scripture as the Psalms. One reading of the Arabic word would make it the name of the revealed Book, another a verbal noun meaning inscribed.

### The demand answered

The question the People of the Book had asked, that the Prophet ﷺ bring them a book all written up from the heavens, was first answered in the mode of reproach. Here it is proved false differently. The great prophets named in the verse are prophets these demanders themselves accept, and they accept them on the basis of their miracles, without any book lowered from the sky before their eyes. Let them keep to that line of argument, then, for Sayyidnā Muḥammad al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ has his miracles as well, and there is no reason they cannot step forward and believe in him too. The truth of the matter is that the demand is no quest for truth. It is rooted in prejudice and hostility.

Why the likeness to Nūḥ in particular? In the days of Ādam the wahy stood at its beginnings, attaining perfection only with the prophethood of Nūḥ. The first state was one of educational initiation, and by Nūḥ's time it had reached the stage of testing: reward held out to draw the obedient, punishment held over the disobedient. From Nūḥ the chain of great prophets began, and from his time the first punishment descended on those who transgressed against divine revelation. Before that, people were considered excusable and allowed to learn through good counsel. Once religious education was widespread and no hidden reason for avoidance remained, punishment did descend, the Great Flood in the days of Nūḥ, then different punishments in the days of Ṣāliḥ, Shuʿayb and many others, peace on them all. The likeness thus serves full warning on the People of the Book and the disbelievers of Makkah: whoever will not believe in the revelation of the Last of the Prophets ﷺ will deserve the greatest punishment.

The personal presence of Nūḥ was itself a miracle. He was blessed with nine hundred and fifty years, lost none of his teeth, had no streak of white in his hair, and faced no deficiency in his strength, yet he spent his whole lifetime braving the pain his people inflicted on him with matching patience and fortitude.

### The inward reading

Prophethood's exaltedness is the foundation on which both worlds rest, and the fruit of prophethood is the Sharīʿah's beauty. Right the path of the Sharīʿah is, and the prophets are its marks. Until the leader sees the road, he does not lead: "I invite to God upon insight, I and whosoever follows me" (12:108). The Exalted Lord sent the prophets to make apparent the road of obedience, so that servants might reach generous bestowal and reward, and to make apparent the road of disobedience and warn against it, so that servants might avoid it and not be deemed worthy of punishment. That is infinite bounty and endless generosity. Had He left the servants in place, sent no Messenger and held no lamp of guidance in their road, they would have stayed wrapped in createdness, darkened by their own opinions, eating everything that is their poison and doing everything that is their destruction. The prophets are mercy and leaders for the world's folk, the best of creatures, the callers at the top of the street of friendship, the cupbearers at the lip of the water of life. The Sharīʿah's title-page they are, and the Ḥaqīqah's proof. To him who was given the Qur'ān it was said: "I was given the Qur'ān and the like of it along with it," and he said elsewhere: "I have a moment with God embraced by no proximate angel, nor any sent prophet."

### In short

- Revelation to Muḥammad ﷺ is of one kind with revelation to Nūḥ, Ibrāhīm, the tribes, and the rest, and rejecting it rejects them all.
- The written-book demand collapses on the demanders' own acceptance of miracle-bearing prophets.
- From Nūḥ onward the excuse ran out: education completed, punishment began, and the warning now stands for Makkah and the People of the Book alike.

<<<VERSE 4:164>>>
The unnamed and the spoken-to: "There are messengers whose stories We have told you already and others We have not. And to Moses Allah spoke directly" (4:164). Verse 164 opens the register that no register can close.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There are messengers whose stories We have told you already"** points to the names Allah named in the Qur'ān. Set down before the revelation of this verse, they are: Ādam, Idrīs, Nūḥ, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Ibrāhīm, Lūṭ, Ismāʿīl, Isḥāq, Yaʿqūb, Yūsuf, Ayyūb, Shuʿayb, Mūsā, Hārūn, Yūnus, Dāwūd, Sulaymān, Ilyās, al-Yasāʿ, Zakariyyā, Yaḥyā and ʿĪsā, and their leader, Muḥammad ﷺ. Several scholars also listed Dhūl-Kifl among the prophets.

**"and others We have not"** means there are other prophets whom Allah did not mention in the Qur'ān, messengers not told of by name. As reported by Sayyidnā Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī, the Holy Prophet ﷺ said that Almighty Allah had sent one hundred and twenty-four thousand prophets, of whom three hundred and thirteen were Sharīʿah-bearing messengers. Another count relates eight thousand prophets in all, four thousand of them from the Children of Israel and four thousand from other peoples, as stated by Shaykh Jalāl al-Dīn al-Mahallī in his commentary on sūrat Ghāfir. The modes of wahy differ: sometimes an angel with a message, sometimes a written Book, sometimes Almighty Allah speaking directly to His Messenger. When wahy comes, in whatever mode, acting in accordance with it becomes obligatory. The Jewish saying that they would believe only if a written Book like the Torah came to them is, to say the least, short on sense and tall on disbelief.

**"And to Moses Allah spoke directly"** confers Mūsā's honor, whence his name Kalīm, the one Allah addressed directly, without intermediary, in a manner befitting Him. Al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Marduwyah recorded that ʿAbdul-Jabbār bin ʿAbdullāh said that a man came to Abū Bakr bin ʿAyyash and said he had heard someone recite the verse as "and to Allah, Mūsā spoke directly." Abū Bakr answered that only a disbeliever would recite it so. Al-Aʿmash recited it with Yaḥyā bin Wathāb, then to Abū ʿAbdur-Raḥmān as-Sulamī, then to ʿAlī bin Abī Ṭālib, and so to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ: "and to Mūsā Allah spoke directly." Abū Bakr bin Abī ʿAyyash was angry with the man because he altered the words and their meanings, for he was of the Muʿtazilah, who denied that Allah spoke to Mūsā or speaks to any of His creation. One teacher of that school who recited it so was told: what then would he do with Allah's word, "And when Mūsā came at the time and place appointed by Us, and his Lord spoke to him" (7:143)? The later verse cannot be altered or changed.

### The inward reading

God's way with His friends is to veil some and make others known, and so it is with the prophets: He proclaims the names of some and refers to others generally. All the prophets, taken whole or one by one, are owed belief, just as all the friends, whole or single, are owed respect. With the servants too He veils some states and makes others apparent, asking purity and sincerity for what He shows, and jealously protecting their hearts from treating what He veils as an entitlement. That Allah spoke directly to Mūsā tells of His selecting him to hear His speech without intermediary.

### In short

- The told and the untold both stand under one obligation of belief, by name or in general.
- The counts differ, 124,000 with 313 law-bearing, or 8,000 split between the Children of Israel and others, and the register stays open.
- The direct speech to Mūsā is an honor guarded by the reciters, for whoever reverses it recites disbelief.

<<<VERSE 4:165>>>
The purpose of them all: "˹All were˺ messengers delivering good news and warnings so humanity should have no excuse before Allah after ˹the coming of˺ the messengers. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise" (4:165). The verse states the reason for the whole institution.

### Phrase by phrase

**"messengers delivering good news and warnings"** means good news of Paradise for those who believe and obey, and practice the good things that please Allah, and warning of His punishment and torment for those who defy His commandments and disbelieve.

**"so humanity should have no excuse before Allah"** states the arrangement: books sent down, messengers sent with good news and warnings, and what He likes and is pleased with, and what earns His displeasure, all explained. With that, no excuse survives for anyone before Allah on the Day of Judgement. He had already said that if He had destroyed them with a torment before this, they would have said: "Our Lord! If only You had sent us a Messenger, we should certainly have followed Your signs, before we were humiliated and disgraced." The messengers were sent to pre-empt that plea, the plea of the person who never heard, who might say: our Lord, why no messenger to us, that we might follow Your signs and join the believers?

**"after ˹the coming of˺ the messengers"** marks the cutoff: now that messengers have come with miracles and pointed out the straight path of truth, no one may be excused from accepting the true faith on any pretext. Such is the nature of divine revelation: an absolute argument, final, conclusive and disarming, before which all arguments lie surrendered. This is the wisdom of Allah and His way of working.

**"And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise"** reads as Mighty in His kingdom and in vengeance upon those who disbelieve in His messenger, Wise in His actions and in His decree that they believe in the messengers.

The Two Ṣaḥīḥs carry the report of Ibn Masʿūd from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ:

> No one is more jealous than Allah. That is why He prohibited all types of sin, committed in public or in secret. No one likes praise more than Allah, and that is why He has praised Himself. No one likes to give excuse more than Allah, and that is why He sent the prophets as bearers of good news and as warners.

In another narration the Prophet ﷺ added that this is why He sent His messengers and revealed His Books.

### The plan the messengers serve

God created man, and then He created Paradise and Hell, and man was settled on the earth with freedom to do as he wishes. The freedom is temporary, meant for his trial, so that good and bad may be distinguished. Both kinds of people have been mixed in this world, with equal opportunity to gain from the blessings of God, and after the appointed time of trial the groups will be separated, one lodged in the Gardens of Paradise forever, the other consigned to Hell for all eternity. The plan set man in an extraordinarily delicate spot: the outcome of his brief stay would arrive as one of two extremes, comfort without end or chastisement without end. Hence, alongside the natural arrangements for divine guidance, God sent His messengers and His scriptures, that none stay unaware of the reality of life, and none excuse his misconduct by pleading ignorance of the divine plan. When all human beings without exception are weighed in the divine scale, their test papers cannot differ from one another, and so every prophet brought the same message: everyone stands at a perilous watershed, Paradise on one side and Hell on the other.

The inward reading adds the measure of worth. God informed created beings of the messengers' worth, that reward is gathered from them alone and punishment avoided by what they warn against, for no other road leads created beings to the comfort they seek or away from the hurt they dread, now or at the end. How could one who has need of God have an argument against God? Yet God addressed them in accordance with their understandings.

### In short

- Good news and warning together exhaust the messenger's task, and after them the excuse is dead.
- The Two Ṣaḥīḥs carry the reason in the Prophet's ﷺ own words: none likes to give excuse more than Allah.
- One scale, one message, one watershed: the messengers made the trial fair.

<<<VERSE 4:166>>>
The testimony: "Yet ˹if you are denied, O Prophet,˺ Allah bears witness to what He has sent down to you—He has sent it with His knowledge. The angels too bear witness. And Allah ˹alone˺ is sufficient as a Witness" (4:166). The verse turns from the deniers to the Witness whose word closes the file.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah bears witness to what He has sent down to you"** holds whatever they do. Denial, defiance, disbelief in you, O Muḥammad, none of it changes the testimony: you are His Messenger, the Glorious Qur'ān, which Gabriel brought you, the Book in which falsehood cannot come to it from before it or behind it, sent down by the All-Wise, Worthy of all praise. Ibn ʿAbbās relates the scene: a deputation of Jews reached the Holy Prophet ﷺ, who said to them: surely you know that I am a true messenger of Allah? They refused, and the verse came down. Al-Kalbī's report names the leaders of Makkah instead: they went to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and said they had asked the Jews about him and the Jews claimed not to know him, so let him produce a witness that Allah had sent him, and thereupon the verse came down. The Book was revealed with full realization that he deserved it.

**"He has sent it with His knowledge"** means such of His knowledge as He willed His servants to reach: the clear signs of guidance and truth, what draws Allah's pleasure and what draws His displeasure, and the Unseen, past and future together. It includes His honorable attributes, unknowable even to a sent messenger or an illustrious angel save by Allah's leave: "And they will never compass anything of His knowledge except that which He wills" (2:255).

**"The angels too bear witness"** to the truth of what he came with and what was revealed to him, along with Allah's own testimony.

**"And Allah ˹alone˺ is sufficient as a Witness"** closes the matter: with no other testimony, His suffices. By naming His knowledge of the Prophet's sincerity, God comforted him against the lies told by created beings.

### Whose validation counts

Different prophets received different kinds of succour from God, according to the requirements of the age, and so it is still, that those who come forward to spread His message receive special divine succour fitted to their circumstances. In the Prophet's time, the Jews enjoyed the status of representatives of the divine religion. They supposed themselves the chosen people, the sole custodians, occupying all the religious positions of honor, unwilling to acknowledge any other's greatness, and so they refused to believe that the Arab prophet had been sent by God. They thought religion was their monopoly, that without their testimony a person claimed to be God's representative would lack all credibility. They had forgotten that this is God's universe, its system tended by God's obedient angels. The real validation comes from God, supported by the entire system of the universe. It is God, with His entire universe, who supports His messenger, not anyone's self-styled suppositions. Such, too, is the sordid conduct of those who conceal his character traits and the circumstantial indicators of his appearance present in the Torah, and keep others from the true faith by producing things that are not there or jumbling together what distorts the truth.

### In short

- Allah's own testimony, with the angels', stands behind the revelation, and it needs no commission from the Jews of Madīnah or the leaders of Makkah.
- The Book came down carrying knowledge of guidance, of the Unseen, and of attributes no angel can compass.
- Validation belongs to God and the universe He runs, not to self-appointed custodians of religion.

<<<VERSE 4:168>>>
The sealed verdict: "Those who disbelieve and wrong themselves—surely Allah will neither forgive them nor guide them to any path" (4:168). The verse carries the judgment against the wrongers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who disbelieve"** in Allah's signs, Book and Messenger.

**"and wrong themselves"** by their disbelief and by hindering others from His path, committing sins and violating His prohibitions. One reading identifies the wrongers as those who ascribe partners to Allah, another as those who wronged the Prophet ﷺ by concealing his descriptions.

The inward reading sets the weight. God set their blocking of believers from the truth on a level with disbelief in Him, weighing the rights of His friends no lighter than His own right. He made their wrongdoing, ẓulm, the same as their kufr, and attached to both the justly deserved abiding punishment. Wrongdoing may not equal disbelief in earning endless stay, yet its misfortune runs deeper: God may let the wrongdoer go until disbelief swallows him. Such perverse behavior leads them, day by day, further from the truth, until they fall into the pit of eternal damnation.

### In short

- Disbelief plus hindrance plus sin: the self-wronging is complete, and forgiveness waits on change that never comes.
- The right of God's friends weighs like His own right, and barring from truth equals unbelief.
- Abandonment is the quiet road from wrongdoing to disbelief.

<<<VERSE 4:170>>>
The call to everyone: "O humanity! The Messenger has certainly come to you with the truth from your Lord, so believe for your own good. But if you disbelieve, then ˹know that˺ to Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise" (4:170). With the objections answered, the address swings open to all human beings, for they are the direct addressees here.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O humanity!"** addresses the people of Makkah first, and beyond them every people, for once the prophethood of Sayyidnā Muḥammad al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ stands confirmed, the Qur'ān carries Allah's message out to all human beings wherever they are.

**"The Messenger has certainly come to you with the truth from your Lord"** lists what he brought: guidance, the religion of truth and clear proof from Allah, with the declaration of Allah's divine Oneness and the Qur'ān.

**"so believe for your own good"** urges belief in what he has brought and follow him, for it is better for you in this world and the Afterlife than what you are presently seeking. The essence of the message is that salvation lies nowhere but in belief in the prophethood of Muḥammad ﷺ, so believe and prosper.

**"But if you disbelieve, then ˹know that˺ to Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth"** counts all of it His possessions, creatures and servants. Allah is too rich to need your faith, and your disbelief could never reach Him with harm. He said it through Mūsā: "If you disbelieve, you and all on earth together, then verily, Allah is Rich, Owner of all praise." The disbelievers lose everything while Allah loses nothing, and with His limitless domain and power, who could conceive of bringing loss or harm to Him? Let them not be put on the wrong foot by the delay or decrease of punishment in this world, for He does what His wisdom allows to prevail.

**"And Allah is All-Knowing"** covers those who deserve to be guided and makes it easy for them, and He knows those who do not deserve it and makes them blind to it, leading them to deviation.

**"All-Wise"** spans His statements, His actions, His legislation and all that He decrees.

### The threshold of Lordhood

The verse alludes to this: Lordhood's threshold and Unity's majesty need no obedience from the obedient, and the worship of creatures in heaven and earth leaves Him untouched. Were all creation to fold back into the concealment of nonexistence, spheres, heavens, existing things and things coming to nothing alike, His purity and lordhood would lose nothing. His Unity's beauty is His eternity, and His eternity's majesty is His solitariness. From Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī comes a sound hadith, from God's Messenger, from God:

> O My servants, I have forbidden Myself to do wrong, and all of it is forbidden to you. So do not wrong each other! Surely you are the ones who err by night and day, and I am the one who forgives sins and does not care. So ask forgiveness of Me, and I will forgive you. O My servants, if the first of you and the last of you and the men of you and the jinn of you had the heart of the most godwary man among you, that would add nothing to My kingdom. O My servants, if the first of you and the last of you and the men of you and the jinn of you had the heart of the most depraved man among you, that would diminish nothing from My kingdom. O My servants, if the first of you and the last of you and the men of you and the jinn of you were to ask from Me and I were to give every man among them what he asked for, that would diminish nothing from Me, except as the ocean is diminished when a needle is dipped into it once.

And the verse says more: even if all creatures put aside the activity of servants and loosened the belt of obedience, they could not go outside of servanthood, nor lift from themselves the bonds of slavehood in respect of creation. The denial of the call of God is in truth the denial of oneself, so clearly is the call made that no one need fail to understand it. With the windows of the heart kept open, the divine call arrives as an exact answer to one's own quest, the truth veiled in human interpretations brought to him pure and original by God's own arrangement, an expression of God's own wisdom and knowledge rather than the personal ambition of a human being.

### In short

- The Messenger ﷺ came with truth, and believing in him profits the believer, not the Lord.
- To Allah belongs everything in the heavens and the earth: disbelief diminishes nothing, not even a needle's dip in the ocean.
- He knows who deserves guidance and who deserves deviation, and His wisdom runs through statements, actions and decree.
