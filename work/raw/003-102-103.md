<<<VERSE 3:102>>>
The remedy for discord starts with the individual: "O believers! Be mindful of Allah in the way He deserves, and do not die except in ˹a state of full˺ submission ˹to Him˺". Taqwa and unity are the two principles announced here and in the next verse. A life managed under responsibility before Allah, avoiding what is forbidden and undesirable as He deserves, is the only soil in which unity can hold.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Be mindful of Allah in the way He deserves"**: Have Taqwa of Allah as is His due. Taqwa in Arabic denotes avoidance and abstinence, translated as "fear" because what must be abstained from carries the danger of Divine retribution.

**"and do not die except in ˹a state of full˺ submission ˹to Him˺"**: die not except as true Muslims, professing His Oneness. Keep Islam intact while you are well and safe. Refuge is sought from dying on anything other than Islam.

### What "as is His due" demands

Ibn Abi Hatim recorded ʿAbdullah bin Mas'ud's comment:

> "(Have Taqwa of Allah as is His due,) 'That He is obeyed and not defied, remembered and not forgotten and appreciated and not unappreciated.'"

An authentic chain reaches ʿAbdullah bin Mas'ud. Al-Hakim collected the report in his Mustadrak from Ibn Mas'ud, relating it to the Prophet ﷺ, and said: "It is authentic according to the criteria of the Two Shaykhs [Al-Bukhari and Muslim], and they did not record it." Even so, it appears to be Ibn Mas'ud's own statement, and Allah knows best. Rabi', Qatadah and Hasan al-Basri give the same triad in Al-Bahr Al-Muhit: obey without disobeying, remember without forgetting, and be grateful without ingratitude. The report is also carried with a chain ascending to the Prophet ﷺ himself.

Different senses are added. Due fear can mean obeying Allah while dismissing all derogatory criticism, standing firm on justice even when justice hurts oneself, one's children or one's parents. Anas said the servant will not attain it until he keeps his tongue idle, others make protection of the tongue the gate to it. When people asked who was strong enough for such a task, the qualification came: "So fear God as much as you can" (64:16). Ibn 'Abbas and Tawus treat that as the explanation of "as is His due": do your best with all attention and energy to guard against evil, and the obligation stands fulfilled. Falling into something impermissible despite that best effort is not a failure of due taqwa.

### Three degrees of a single word

Taqwa is not one flat state. At its lowest degree, it is abstaining from Kufr and Shirk: disbelief in Allah and His Message, and assigning partners to His divinity. Every Muslim counts as Muttaqi in this sense, even if involved in sins, and the Qur'an often uses Muttaqin and Taqwa at this level.

The desired degree is higher: avoiding whatever Allah and His Messenger dislike. The merits and blessings promised for Taqwa in Qur'an and Hadith attach to this degree of abstinence.

The third degree is a station for prophets, their devoted deputies and men of Allah. It means standing guard over the heart against every onslaught unrelated to Allah, keeping it filled with His remembrance and the desire for His pleasure. That is not within everybody's grasp.

Because total obedience to Allah and His Messenger and total avoidance of disobedience to both are exactly what Taqwa means, Taqwa is in reality the whole of Islam. This is why the same verse can command both taqwa as His due and dying only as Muslims.

### Living determines dying

Death is not in human control, so the command needs explanation. The rule supplied is: "As you live, so shall you die; and as you die, so shall you be raised." The Most Generous Allah has decided that a person's settled state in life is the state of his death and resurrection. A determined person who lives by Islam's tenets to the best of his determination and ability will, God willing, die in Islam. Reports of people whose lifetime of good deeds is wasted by something awful later apply, in this reading, to those who lacked sincerity and steadfastness from the very beginning. And Allah knows best.

Two deathbed protections follow. Imam Ahmad recorded that Mujahid described people circling the Sacred House while Ibn 'Abbas sat with a bent-handled walking stick. Ibn 'Abbas said the Messenger of Allah recited this verse and then said:

> "(Verily, if a drop of Zaqqum (a tree in Hell) falls, it will spoil life for the people of earth. What about those whose food is only from Zaqqum)"

This was recorded by At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah, Ibn Hibban in his Sahih and Al-Hakim in his Mustadrak. At-Tirmidhi said, "Hasan Sahih", Al-Hakim said it meets the conditions of the Two Sahihs, which did not record it.

Imam Ahmad recorded that Jabir heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say three nights before his death: "None of you should die except while having sincere trust in Allah, the Exalted and Most Honorable." Muslim also recorded it. The Two Sahihs record Abu Hurayrah's report of the Prophet's words: "Allah said, 'I am as My servant thinks of Me.'"

The verse's own occasion is placed in an Aws and Khazraj dispute where Tha'labah Ibn Ghanam and As'ad Ibn Zurarah boasted about their killings and raids on rivals in pre-Islamic days. The command tells believers to surrender sincerely, acknowledging that they worship Him and declare His divine Oneness.

### Godwariness addressed to two kinds of people

The forms of address sort the audience. "O people" pairs with "Be wary of your Lord". Common people hear that address. Their godwariness is based on seeing blessings, and they nurture the body for serving the Real. "O you who have faith" pairs with "Be wary of God". That address reaches the folk of caresses and generosity. Their godwariness is watchfulness of the Beneficent, and their aim is the spirit's repose in witnessing the Real.

So the wage-earners are told, "Be wary of your Lord", and the recognizers are told, "Be wary of God". Wage-earners seek joy and blessings, things other than Him. Recognizers seek the mystery of the Beneficent, God Himself. AḤmad ibn Khiẓrūya saw the Real in a dream and heard: "O AḤmad! Everyone is seeking something from Me, except Abū Yazīd. He is seeking Me." Other people wish for repose and comfort, but the recognizer wishes only to encounter Him empty, and on reaching union would disdain even the state of Paradise's dwellers.

Godwariness is also sorted into three kinds. Wariness of punishment, through patience against disobedience, belongs to the text "Be wary of the Fire that has..." (3:131). Godwariness of gratitude for blessings belongs to "Be wary of your Lord" (4:1). Godwariness at the vision of Unity, taking no account of reward or punishment, belongs to this verse. The first is the godwariness of the wrongdoers, the second of the moderate, and the third of the preceders.

### In short

- Taqwa as His due is obedience without defiance, remembrance without forgetting, and gratitude without ingratitude, measured at the edge of one's best effort (64:16).
- Its degrees climb from avoiding kufr and shirk, to avoiding everything Allah dislikes, to guarding the heart for His pleasure alone.
- Since states settle at death, sincerity from the beginning, protection of the tongue, and sincere trust in Allah are its safeguards.

<<<VERSE 3:103>>>
Discord is answered with a single grip: "And hold firmly to the rope of Allah and do not be divided. Remember Allah’s favour upon you when you were enemies, then He united your hearts, so you—by His grace—became brothers. And you were at the brink of a fiery pit and He saved you from it. This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so that you may be ˹rightly˺ guided". The binding factor is named before division is forbidden, and then a people pulled back from a pit is reminded what division nearly cost.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And hold firmly to the rope of Allah"**: Hold fast, all of you together, to the Rope of Allah, to the Religion of Allah and His Book.

**"and do not be divided"**: be not divided among yourselves. Stick to the community of the believers and do not separate in religion. Avoid scattering once Islam has been submitted to.

**"Remember Allah’s favour upon you when you were enemies, then He united your hearts, so you—by His grace—became brothers"**: For Aws and Khazraj, favour takes concrete form: former enemies were joined through Islam, becoming religious brothers and companions.

**"And you were at the brink of a fiery pit and He saved you from it"**: Ye were upon the brink, the edge of an abyss of fire because of disbelief, He saved you by means of faith. Dying disbelieving would have completed the fall.

**"This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so that you may be ˹rightly˺ guided"**: Thus Allah maketh clear His revelations, His commands, prohibitions and favours (unto you, that haply ye may be guided) from error. Just as He has made clear what has been mentioned, God makes clear to you His signs that you might be guided.

### Habl: a cord that is also a covenant

Abdullah ibn Masud narrated the Prophet's saying:

> The Book of Allah is the cord of Allah, extended from the heavens to the earth.

In Zayd ibn Arqam's narration, the words are: "The cord of Allah is the Qur'an." Habl in Arabic also means covenant. In an absolute sense, it covers everything that can serve as a connecting link. The metaphor suggests a link that establishes communion between believers and their Lord on one side, and brings all believers close together into one group on the other. Grip that strong rope while climbing, and the community remains protected against a fall, stable and impregnable in collective strength like a climber's body.

Rope can also mean Allah's covenant itself, as in the verse of humiliation: "(Indignity is put over them wherever they may be, except when under a covenant (of protection) from Allah, and from men;)" [3:112], said in reference to pledges and peace treaties.

Holding fast to the Book with total strength, all in unison, prevents Satan from dividing believers. Scattered forces are united, and a dead nation gets new life. Breaking away from it ruins national and collective life, and individual life fares no better. More clearly still: "Surely, those who believe and do good deeds, among them the All-Merciful Allah shall create (mutual) affection" (19:96).

### Three things loved, and the verdict on sects

Several hadiths require adhering to the Jama'ah, the congregation of believers, and prohibit division. Muslim recorded that Abi Hurairah said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "(It pleases Allah for you to acquire three qualities and displeases Him that you acquire three characteristics. It pleases Him that you worship Him Alone and not associate anything or anyone with Him in worship, that you hold on to the Rope of Allah altogether and do not divide, and that you advise whoever Allah appoints as your Leader. The three that displease Him are that you say, 'It was said,' and, 'So-and-so said,' asking many unnecessary questions and wasting money.)"

The Prophet ﷺ has also said that three things are liked for you by Allah, and three disliked. Liked are: worship of Allah alone without attributing partners to His divinity, holding on to the Book of Allah firmly and avoiding disunity, and goodwill for those in authority from among you. Displeasing Him are unnecessary argumentation, needless asking, and wastage of resources.

The positive comes first, then the negative is identified and forbidden: "Surely, this is the straight path, so, follow it. And do not follow (other) ways which will cause you to become separated from His way" (6:153). And the sectarian verdict: "Those who have made divisions in their religion and become sects, you have nothing to do with them" (6:159).

### Brotherhood after a pre-Islamic war

During Jahiliyyah, Aws and Khazraj lived at war, with great hatred, enmity and ill feelings, and long conflicts and battles. Islam changed those who embraced it into brothers who loved each other by Allah's grace, having good ties for Allah's sake and helping each other in righteousness and piety: "(He it is Who has supported you with His Help and with the believers. And He has united their hearts. If you had spent all that is in the earth, you could not have united their hearts, but Allah has united them)" (8:62-63).

Before Islam, disbelief left them at a pit's edge. Faith delivered them. The Messenger later reminded the Ansar of the bounty while dividing Hunayn's spoils: some disliked receiving less than others, although Allah had directed the distribution. He asked whether he had not found them misguided before Allah guided them through him, divided before Allah united them around him, and poor before Allah enriched them through him. Each question received the answer: "Indeed, Allah and His Messenger have granted us bounty." 

Both tribes were Arabs inhabiting Madinah. Continuous war, attacks and counter-attacks considerably weakened them until Islam ended hostilities and made them like brothers. Allegiance explains the change. A truth-denier is faithful only to himself. A believer gives allegiance solely to God. Many self- or group-allegiances naturally multiply strife and dissension, a society bowed only to one God is directed towards a single focal point. Bound by one cord, its members cease mutual clashes.

Centuries-deep enmities were erased when Allah made them brothers through Islam's benediction and the noble Prophet ﷺ. Life became worth living materially and spiritually, with exemplary friendship even enemies found awesome. No combined treasures of the world could purchase that brotherly unity. The verse also eliminates the mischief of those who tried to disunite Aws and Khazraj by reviving past feuds. Once in Islam, division is unthinkable.

### Why no human program can be the centre

A centre of attraction is required for unity. Peoples have tried race and tribe, as with Quraysh and Banu Tamim in Arabia, colour, black as one nation and white as another, geography or language, Indian against Arab, and ancestral custom, as with the Arya Samajists in India.

Human unity appeals normally ask everyone else to accept a self-made program. Other parties have programs of their own, so calls fragment instead of uniting. A few people can fuse temporarily around shared objectives, but once interests are served or frustrated, bickering and hostility replace unity. Sects within sects and parties within parties can make even two-person unity a myth.

Imposing one group's conception on humanity and expecting unanimous acceptance is against commonsense and justice, and is cheeky self-deception. A system from the Creator-Sustainer of all worlds, Rabb al-ʿAlamin, is different in principle. Rational human beings cannot deny it on principle, although identification is contested: Jews claim the Torah's system, Christians claim the Evangel's system, and even polytheist groups attribute their rites to god.

Qur'an makes the Book of Allah the centre instead. Muslims become a nation attached to Ḥablillah, disbelievers another nation, unattached to that strong cord. "(It is He who created you. So, some of you are infidels and some of you are believers)" (64:16). Geography, race, tribe and language are unchosen and uncontrollable. Black cannot become white, Qurayshi cannot become Tamimi, Indian cannot become Arab. Limited unities follow, never a platform for all humanity. A divinely revealed way of life can be elected freely from East or West, by black or white, in Arabic, English or any language, by any family, tribe or human group. Humanity can then gather concentrically around God-given guidance and tune material and spiritual deeds to one accepted way.

Muslims can be proud of that principle and confident when inviting others to join. Conspiracies hatched by Europeans for centuries to crush Muslim unity have divided claimants to Islam, race, language and nationalities have been accepted as dividing forces, severing Arabs from Non-Arabs and Indians from non-Indians. Qur'an instead proclaims those distinctions ill-founded and divisive, and unity based on them irrational and false. Muslims have no choice but to hold on to the cord of Allah, all of them, as a way of life. That produced honour before and would produce any future success.

First, Muslims live by Allah's prescribed system. Second, they hold fast to His cord all together. Past ascendance followed those instructions, and no reason prevents a future rise. Firm grip on the Qur'an also acts like a climber's strong rope, uniting scattered forces, reviving a dead nation and keeping individual and collective life from ruin when it is not broken away from.

### Differences that are mercy, and differences that divide

Not all differences are blameworthy. Blame begins where people stay away from the Qur'an and think by whims and wishes. A different path stays united around the Qur'an while accepting the Prophet's explanation and detail. From that centre, God-given natural ability and intellectual quality may generate subsidiary opinions. Companions, Successors and leading jurists differed in exactly this natural, unforbidden way, and such difference was called a "mercy" for the community.

Status changes the verdict. If subsidiary debates are invested with mainstream religion's status, and controversy, confrontation, insult and vilification follow, blame returns.

### Unity as obedience rewarded

Allah Almighty is the Master of hearts. Activating mutual love and consideration in the hearts of a people is purely a Divine blessing. One becomes deserving of Allah's blessings only through obedience to Him. With disobedience and sin, one cannot hope for this reward. It follows that for Muslims, if they desire a stable organization among them and unity, the only alternative open is to make obedience to Allah their lifestyle. This point is hinted at towards the end of the verse: "This is how Allah makes His signs clear to you, so that you may take the right path" (103). Obedience marks the Muttaqin, sustains the ummah, and keeps remembrance alive from the opening command onward.

### Holding fast without scattering

Clinging to the traces of the intermediary, āthār al-wāsiṭa (the noble one ʿazīz ﷺ), is what holding fast to His bond means. Confirming what is true and right, taḥaqquq, and remaining devoted, taʿalluq, to the Book and Sunna create the grip. "Hold fast to God's bond" addresses the elite, khawāṣṣ, "hold fast to God" (4:146 and 22:78) addresses the elect of the elect, khāṣṣ al-khāṣṣ.

Turning away from His auspicious occurrences, sawāniḥ, toward one's own choosing and devising, iḥtiyāl, thinking and reasoning, istidlāl, and ways of knowing and behaving, ashkāl, is dangerous. Seeking refuge in the shade of one's planning, tadbīr, and illumination from one's intellect and speculation, tafkīr, denies the shade of divine solicitude and leaves an unfortunate state.

"And do not scatter." Scattering or separation, tafriqa, is the most intense punishment, companion to shirk. Enmity arose when people remained with worldly concerns, turned toward narrow human nature and competed in lower-self stinginess. Deliverance from captivity to created things brought hearts together and drove afflictions from innermost selves. Their sought object became one, so even a thousand people in one pursuit would truly be one. Brotherhood came by grace as safeguarding, ʿiṣma. Aim and aspiration united, and worldly concerns of the lower self, with furtive stinginess and avarice, were annihilated. Before that, objects of desire, munākum, held them captive, bound by the shackles of worldly concerns and whims, hawākum.

### In short

- Hold fast together to Hablillah, the cord of Allah, the Qur'an and His covenant, and do not divide, since division and sectarianism are forbidden (3:112, 6:153, 6:159, 64:16).
- Aws and Khazraj were saved from the brink of the Fire into brotherhood no earthly spending could have purchased (8:62-63, 19:96).
- The only centre that can unite all humanity is the revealed way of life itself, held by the elite as devotion to Book and Sunna (4:146, 22:78), and differences inside it are mercy only while they stay subsidiary and civil.
