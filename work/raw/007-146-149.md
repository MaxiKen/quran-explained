<<<VERSE 7:146>>>
Allah describes what arrogance does to a person's understanding: "I will turn away from My signs those who act unjustly with arrogance in the land. And even if they were to see every sign, they still would not believe in them." It also bends their choices: "If they see the Right Path, they will not take it. But if they see a crooked path, they will follow it. This is because they denied Our signs and were heedless of them". Those too proud to obey Allah, and arrogant toward people without any right, are kept from understanding the signs and proofs of His might, His law and His commands. Just as they were proud without cause, Allah has disgraced them with ignorance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"I will turn away from My signs"**: I will turn them from believing in My revelations and from reflecting on My signs, the proofs of My power in creation, on the horizons and within themselves. Other verses say the same: Allah turns hearts and eyes away from the truth because people refused it the first time (6:110). And: "So when they ˹persistently˺ deviated, Allah caused their hearts to deviate" (61:5).

Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah explained it as: I will take away from them the understanding of the Qur'an and turn them away from My signs. Ibn Jarīr took Sufyān's words to mean that this part of the verse is addressed to this Ummah. That is not necessarily so. Ibn ʿUyaynah meant that this happens in every Ummah, with no difference between one Ummah and another. Allah knows best.

**"those who act unjustly with arrogance in the land."** Those who are proud on earth without any right, proud toward Allah's servants and toward the truth. Allah humbles them, so that they cannot magnify themselves. One explanation says the arrogant meant here are Pharaoh and his people. Another says Abū Jahl and his people. The same explanation links the "home of the rebellious" promised just before to the site of Badr, or to Makkah.

**"And even if they were to see every sign, they still would not believe in them."** A similar verse says, "Indeed, those against whom Allah’s decree ˹of torment˺ is justified will not believe— even if every sign were to come to them—until they see the painful punishment" (10:96-97).

**"If they see the Right Path, they will not take it."** Even if the way of guidance and safety appears before them, the way of Islam and goodness that leads to Allah's pleasure, they will not take it or like it.

**"But if they see a crooked path, they will follow it."** If the road of error, disbelief and idolatry that leads to ruin and Allah's displeasure appears, they take it.

**"This is because they denied Our signs and were heedless of them"**: they rejected Allah's scripture and messengers in their hearts, opposed Allah and His Messenger, and paid the signs no attention, learning nothing from them. A similar statement came earlier in the surah.

### Is all pride condemned?

The words "unjustly", literally "without right", show that answering the arrogant with a show of pride is justified. It is pride in appearance only, not in reality. As a saying recorded in Masāʾil as-Sulūk puts it: showing pride to the proud is an act of humility.

### What does arrogance do to a person?

"I will turn away from My signs those who act with arrogance" means that people with an offensive sense of superiority are deprived of knowledge and understanding. They cannot benefit from Allah's signs. These signs may be the revealed verses of the Torah, the Gospel and the Qur'an, and also the signs placed in everything in the heavens and the earth. A false sense of superiority is the worst of habits, because it keeps a person from reflecting on Allah's signs and coming to know the truth.

Rūḥ al-Bayān says that arrogance is so wicked a trait that it raises a wall between a person and knowledge from Allah. Divine knowledge comes only through Allah's mercy, and His mercy visits the humble. The spiritual guide Rūmī said: "Water flows down toward the slope, and the remedy goes where the hardship is."

There are two ways to live in this world. One is to keep one's eyes and ears open. Then a person sees things as they are, and when the truth appears he recognises it, for Allah's signs are everywhere in the universe and lessons are sure to be found in them. The other way is to live with a superiority complex. Such a person lives as if he owned the world. He cares for nothing but his own interests. He thinks every good thing he receives comes from his own ability and effort, so he gives no thought to others' needs. This attitude blocks him from recognising and accepting the truth.

The first kind of person is open to the world around him. With an open mind he grasps the meaning of Allah's signs and quickly shapes himself by them. The second, because of pride and self-righteousness, is closed. He meets the truth, thinks it unimportant and ignores it. Nature sings its melodies in a silent language, but he does not listen. Any truth that lies beyond his own beliefs holds no appeal for him. People of his kind have no place in the world to come. As he ignored Allah's message in this world of testing, so Allah will ignore him in the eternal world, while the first kind will be richly rewarded.

The path of error comes from following the desires of one's self. The path of guidance is reached by rising above the pull of the self and one's surroundings, and choosing it purely for Allah's sake. The self-centred person, who values nothing but his own desires, rushes headlong down the path of transgression. The right path seems odd and foreign to his temperament, so he does not follow it. Someone who feels superior readily accepts any idea that does not lessen his sense of superiority. Where he senses he will not be given due importance, he loses interest.

### The inner reading: two kinds of pride

Pride is of two kinds, one with the Real and one without the Real. The first kind, pride with the Real, is what the poor feel toward the rich. Their aims are lofty, and their hearts are made rich by the Real. Their aspiration has turned away from the Throne and all beneath it. They have cut their hearts off from creatures and occupied themselves with loving the Real. What they aspire to is greater than this world and better than the next, and they long for the vision of their Master. Wāsiṭī said that the pride which is with the Real is directed at the rich and the sinful, toward disbelievers and the people of innovation. The traditions say, "Meet the wicked with stern faces." Pride without the Real is the pride of the rich and the rulers of the world toward the poor, and that is what this verse means.

Ibn ʿAṭāʾ said of this verse: "I will hold back their hearts, their inmost selves and their spirits from roaming the realm of holiness." In other words: I will tie their hearts and inmost selves so they cannot travel. I will make their own being their veil and turn them from My road, so that their inmost selves cannot roam the world of holiness and the unseen kingdom. They will be kept from seeing its wonders and will grow familiar only with their own selves and with this world's creatures. They will never taste the joy of finding, and will know nothing of the gifts given to the chosen. They will never see a day of good fortune, and the rose of union will never bloom for them. Poor is the wretch who has never caught the scent of these words! How can he have any share of the ocean, when he has not even a stream?

### In short

- Allah turns the arrogant away from understanding His signs, so that they disbelieve whatever they see.
- They reject the path of guidance and choose the path of error, because they denied the signs and ignored them.
- Answering the proud with pride is humility in disguise, but unjust pride raises a wall between a person and knowledge from Allah.
- A closed, superior mind meets the truth and ignores it, and so is shut out of the Hereafter's reward.

<<<VERSE 7:147>>>
The verse states the end of those who deny: "The deeds of those who deny Our signs and the meeting ˹with Allah˺ in the Hereafter will be in vain. Will they be rewarded except for what they have done?”" Whoever lives this way and stays on that path until death will find all his deeds wasted.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The deeds of those who deny Our signs"**: those who reject Allah's scripture and His messengers, and the signs that prove the messengers true.

**"and the meeting ˹with Allah˺ in the Hereafter"**: who deny the resurrection after death and their meeting with Allah on the Day of Judgement.

**"will be in vain."** Their good deeds will fail and earn no reward.

**"Will they be rewarded except for what they have done?”"** The question means they will not. They are repaid only for what they did and said of evil in this world, their denial and their disobedience. Allah repays people according to their deeds, good for good and evil for evil. As you sow, so you reap.

### Why are their good deeds lost?

Even the good they did in this world, such as keeping family ties or giving voluntary charity, is void and will not be rewarded. Those deeds were not built on faith, and their idolatry cancelled them. The loss is complete for whoever keeps to this denial until death. What remains is the repayment for their disbelief and for joining partners with Allah, which is to live forever in the Fire.

### In short

- Those who deny Allah's signs and the meeting with Him will find even their good deeds void, since these were not built on faith.
- Allah repays each according to his deeds, and their denial earns them the Fire.

<<<VERSE 7:148>>>
While Mūsā (Moses) was away on the Mount, his people fell into the worship of a calf: "In the absence of Moses, his people made from their ˹golden˺ jewellery an idol of a calf that made a lowing sound." The verse rebukes their folly: "Did they not see that it could neither speak to them nor guide them to the ˹Right˺ Path? Still they took it as a god and were wrongdoers". The calf was the work of as-Sāmirī, who shaped it from ornaments the people had borrowed from the Copts, and the verse describes how far astray its worshippers went.

### Phrase by phrase

**"In the absence of Moses"**: after Mūsā had left them for his retreat on the mountain, to speak with Allah. Allah told him what had happened while he was on Mount Ṭūr: "We have indeed tested your people in your absence, and the Sâmiri has led them astray" (20:85).

**"his people made from their ˹golden˺ jewellery"**: from their ornaments of gold, of a saffron colour, which they had borrowed from Pharaoh's people on the pretext of a wedding and still had with them.

**"an idol of a calf"**: literally "a calf, a body". The word "body" (jasadan) stands in for "calf" (ʿijlan), meaning a body of flesh and blood, or, by another view, a statue with no soul. In the Arabic, the second object of "they made", "as a god", is left unsaid.

**"that made a lowing sound."** It made audible sounds like a cow, a sound the Sāmirī produced for them.

**"Did they not see that it could neither speak to them nor guide them to the ˹Right˺ Path?"** Did Mūsā's people not know that the calf had no speech for them, and no guidance toward any good, worldly or spiritual, and could bring them no benefit or remove any harm? Another verse says, "Did they not see that it did not respond to them, nor could it protect or benefit them" (20:89). So how could it be taken as a god?

**"Still they took it as a god and were wrongdoers"**: they worshipped the calf out of ignorance, and wronged and harmed themselves by doing so.

### How was the calf made?

When Mūsā did not come back from Mount Sinai after thirty days, because he was to spend ten more nights there, the hasty and impatient Israelites began to make a fuss. Among them was a man named Sāmirī, a prominent figure who also held superstitious beliefs. He told the people that the Egyptians' jewels and ornaments in their possession were unlawful for them. Spoils taken from a defeated enemy were also unlawful for the Israelites. They gathered all their jewels and handed them to him. He melted them down and cast a golden calf from them.

It is reported that he had once collected some dust from where the hooves of the horse of Jibrīl (Gabriel) had trodden. Allah had given this dust a kind of life-giving effect. As-Sāmirī mixed it into the molten metal, or threw a handful of it into the calf's mouth, and the calf began to low like a cow. The scholars of tafsīr hold two views here. One is that the calf actually came alive and lowed. The other is that it remained gold, and air passing through it made it seem to low. Allah knows best. Another account says the sculpting art of the ancient Egyptians was such that air passing through such a figure made a bellowing sound come from its mouth.

Having made this satanic invention, Sāmirī called the people to worship the calf, declaring it a god. Mūsā, he said, had gone off to the mountain to speak with his Lord, but their god had come to them here, in the shape of this calf. It is reported that when the statue lowed, they began dancing around it and fell into error through their love of it. "This is your god and the god of Mūsā," they said, "but Mūsā has forgotten it." Sāmirī already enjoyed respect among them, and this unusual display increased their trust in him, so they began to worship the calf and took it as their god.

Allah condemned them for this error, for worshipping a calf and ignoring the One who created the heavens and the earth, Lord and Sovereign over everything. They worshipped besides Him a statue shaped like a calf that seemed to low, yet it neither spoke to them nor did them any good. Their very reason was blinded by ignorance and error.

### Why did the people follow Sāmirī?

Sāmirī was a clever man. When Mūsā left for the mountain, leaving his people in Hārūn's (Aaron's) care, Sāmirī set about misleading them. People are generally awed by unusual things, so this "miracle" of the calf made them think there was something sacred about it, and they began to worship it. Sāmirī's persuasive speech drew people to him. His influence grew until he had a large following, with only Hārūn and a few companions holding out. No one dared protest openly. Against this mass following, even Hārūn's protests were not strong enough to sway the people, and no one else had the courage to speak.

This has always been the public's way, from ancient times to the present. A clever person can draw a huge crowd by sheer eloquence, even over a non-issue. People drawn to trivialities, unconnected to the facts, cannot think deeply enough to sort truth from falsehood. So they rally to such a person and add to his popularity and importance. Anyone who tries to reveal the truth is simply ignored, as Hārūn was.

### The inner reading: every person's calf

Sahl ibn ʿAbdallāh said, "Whatever in this world turns a servant away from the Real and keeps him from obeying Him is his calf, and he is its worshipper." The calf-worshippers among the children of Israel were saved when, by Allah's command, they put themselves to death: "turn in repentance to your Creator and execute ˹the calf-worshippers among˺ yourselves" (2:54). In the same way, a servant is saved on the road of the Ḥaqīqah when he is cleansed of his own shares and of secondary causes. Better still, when he renounces all that is not the Real. As someone said: "Disown everything in the realm of being, and be that Heart-taker's companion of the cave."

"Did they not see that it could neither speak to them" points to what is rightly due to the Real. He is a speaker who addresses His creatures and speaks to His servant. The kings of the earth, in their grandeur, disdain to speak to their servants with their own tongues. But the Real has His own custom with His faithful servants. To His enemies He says, "Be despised in there! Do not ˹ever˺ plead with Me ˹again˺" (23:108). Of the faithful the Prophet ﷺ said that every one of them will be spoken to by his Lord, with no interpreter between them. On this theme it has been sung: "His majesty does not keep them from ease when they speak to Us and We speak a little to them."

### In short

- In Mūsā's absence, as-Sāmirī cast a calf from the borrowed gold jewellery, and it gave out a lowing sound.
- Whether it truly came alive through Jibrīl's dust or merely made noise as air passed through it, it could neither speak nor guide nor help.
- The people, awed by a novelty and swayed by Sāmirī's eloquence, worshipped it, while Hārūn's warnings went unheeded.
- Whatever turns a servant from Allah is his calf, and a god who cannot speak to his worshippers is no god at all.

<<<VERSE 7:149>>>
When Mūsā (Moses) returned from the Mount, the calf-worshippers came to their senses: "Later, when they were filled with remorse and realized they had gone astray, they cried, “If our Lord does not have mercy on us and forgive us, we will certainly be losers.”" They regretted what they had done, and did not know how to make up for their wrong.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Later, when they were filled with remorse"**: when they were at a loss, regretting that they had worshipped the calf and fearing its consequences. They felt sorrow for what they had done.

**"and realized they had gone astray"**: they saw, knew for certain, that they had strayed from the truth, from guidance and the straight path, by worshipping the calf besides Allah. This came after Mūsā's return from his meeting with his Lord.

**"they cried, “If our Lord does not have mercy on us and forgive us"**: the mercy they hoped for was help to carry out His commands, and the forgiveness was pardon for worshipping the calf instead of punishment. The two verbs can be read in the third person, "if our Lord does not have mercy", or in the second, "if You, our Lord, do not have mercy".

**"we will certainly be losers.”"** We will be among the lost and the destroyed, losers in this world and in the Hereafter, overtaken by the punishment.

### What made their words a turning point?

This verse records their remorse over that guilt, and their repentance. Their words admitted the sin and sought salvation from Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. Not knowing how to undo what they had done, they placed themselves at the mercy of their Lord, and saw that without His forgiveness they had lost everything.

### In short

- Once Mūsā returned, the calf-worshippers realised their error and were filled with remorse.
- They admitted that without Allah's mercy and forgiveness they would be lost in this world and the next.
- Their plea admitted the sin and asked Allah for salvation, the start of their repentance.
