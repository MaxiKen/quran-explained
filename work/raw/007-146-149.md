<<<VERSE 7:146>>>
The law has been written on the Tablets, and the warning that goes with it is set out in full. "I will turn away from My signs those who act unjustly with arrogance in the land." The next sentences explain the state such people are left in. "And even if they were to see every sign, they still would not believe in them." Then the two paths are put side by side. "If they see the Right Path, they will not take it. But if they see a crooked path, they will follow it." The reason is given at the end. "This is because they denied Our signs and were heedless of them"

### Phrase by phrase

**"I will turn away from My signs those who act unjustly with arrogance in the land."** is a statement of what is done to the proud, not only of what they do. The words say that the hearts of those who are too proud to obey Allah, and who behave as tyrants over people with no right on their side, will be kept from the understanding of the signs and the proofs that stand as witness to His might and His law and His commands. Because they lifted themselves up with no justification, they were brought low with ignorance. The Book says in another place that the hearts and the eyes of such people are turned away from guidance, as they refused to believe in it the first time (6:110). And it says that when they turned away, Allah turned their hearts away from the right path (61:5). The turning away of the servant is answered with a turning away from his Lord, and the two things are of the same kind.

What the signs of Allah are has been taken in a wide sense. They may be the revealed verses of the Torah, of the Gospel and of the Qur'an, and they may also be the signs placed in the heavens and the earth and in the human being himself. Whichever sense is taken, the proud person is turned away from them all.

**"without any right"** is a phrase worth stopping over. It names the fault that turns a strong feeling into a sin, and it leaves open the case where the feeling is deserved. Arrogance in the face of the arrogant is a justified act, since what appears as arrogance there is not arrogance in reality but a putting of things in their place. The dictum is carried in one of the treatises of the path, Masail as-Suluk, that showing arrogance to the arrogant is itself an act of humbleness.

**"And even if they were to see every sign, they still would not believe in them."** describes a condition that has already gone past the reach of evidence. The Book says of such people in another place that those against whom the word of the Lord has been justified will not believe, even if every sign were to come to them, until they see the painful punishment (10:96-97). So the refusal to believe is not a shortage of proof. It is the settled state of the heart that the proof cannot move.

**"If they see the Right Path, they will not take it. But if they see a crooked path, they will follow it."** is where that state becomes visible in action. The right path is the way of guidance and of safety, and if it is laid out plainly in front of them they will not take it as their way. The crooked path is the way that ends in destruction and in misguidance, and they take it at once. One explanation names the right path as the way of Islam and of goodness, and the crooked path as the way of disbelief and of the worship of idols.

**"This is because they denied Our signs and were heedless of them."** gives the reason plainly. They rejected the Book and the Messenger, and they were heedless of the signs, so the signs gave them no lesson. Denial in the heart and heedlessness in the conduct between them explain the whole picture that the verse has drawn.

### What one of the early teachers said

Sufyan bin 'Uyaynah read the opening of verse 146 in a way that shows what this turning away comes to. He said that the sense of it is that the understanding of the Book is snatched away from such people, and that they are turned from the signs. Ibn Jarir drew a conclusion from that statement, holding that this part of the verse is addressed to the community of the Prophet ﷺ. The conclusion does not have to be pressed that far, since what was meant is that the pattern runs in every ummah, and that no ummah is treated differently from another in this matter. And Allah knows best.

### What arrogance does to a person

There are two ways of living in this world, and the verse divides them.

The first way is to keep one's eyes and ears open. A person who lives that way sees things as they really are, and when the truth comes into view he takes it, since the signs of God lie open in every part of the universe and the lesson is there for whoever will read it. The second way is to live with a sense of being above others. Such a person lives as though the world belonged to him. He cares for nothing except his own interests, and he takes the good things that come to him as the fruit of his own ability and his own effort, so the needs of other people carry no weight with him. That attitude stands between him and the truth. The first man is open to what is around him, and because his mind is open he understands what the signs of God point to and sets himself straight without delay. The second man is closed, because pride and self-righteousness have shut him in. The truth stands right in front of him and he pays it no attention, since it has no importance in his own scale. The world of the Hereafter is not for the people of that second kind. As they ignored the message of God in the world of trial, so will they be left out in the world that never ends, while the first kind are given richly by God.

What lies at the root of it is desire. Treading the path of misguidance is the work of a man who follows the wishes of his own self. The path of guidance opens when a person rises above the pull of the self and the pull of his surroundings, and takes a road for the sake of God alone. The self-centred man who values nothing but the fulfilling of his own wants rushes along the road of transgression. Right guidance looks strange to his temperament, and so he cannot follow it. One who feels himself superior finds it easy to accept any proposal that leaves his superiority intact, and where he sees that he will not be given his due importance he turns away with no interest in the matter.

The explanation known as Ruh al-Bayan puts the same point in another figure. Arrogance is a habit so destructive that it raises a wall between a person and the knowledge that comes from Allah. Knowledge of divine things is given only out of the mercy of Allah, and the mercy of Allah visits the humble. Jalal al-Din Rumi put the same law into a saying, that water flows down towards the slope and the remedy appears where the difficulty is.

### Where the warning is placed

One reading takes the words "if they see" as pointing at the people of Pharaoh, and another takes them as pointing at Abu Jahl and his companions. On that second reading the promise that the abode of the evil-doers would be shown was fulfilled at Badr, and there is also a report that names Makkah as the abode meant. Read either way, the verse is telling the community of the Prophet ﷺ that the pattern it describes is not the property of any one nation.

### The two kinds of pride

Pride has been divided into two sorts, and the division settles what the verse is blaming.

The first is pride with the Real. It is the pride of the poor man toward the rich, of the one who is high in his aspiration and rich in his heart through his Lord. Such a person has turned his aspiration away from the throne and from everything beneath it, cut his heart off from creatures, and given himself to the love of his Lord. His aspiration is greater than this world and better than the world to come, and his longing is for the sight of his Patron. Al-Wasiti put it that when the pride is the pride that belongs with the Real, it is the poor man's bearing toward the wealthy and the sinful, and toward the unbelievers and the people of new-fangled ways. He recalled that the reports carry the instruction to meet the ungodly with a dark face.

The second is pride without the Real. It is the pride of the wealthy and of the rulers of the world toward the poor, and it is this second sort that the words name when they speak of those who lift themselves in the land with no right from Him.

Ibn Ata'i said of the verse that their hearts and their secret cores and their spirits would be held back from roaming in the dominion of holiness. Their own being would become the veil over them. They would be turned away from the road of their Lord, so that their secret cores could not travel in the world of holiness and the dominion. They would be kept from seeing the wonders of that dominion, and left to find their company in their own souls and in the creatures of this world. They would not taste the flavour of finding, and would stay unaware of the gifts given to the people who are chosen. No day of good fortune would come to them, and the rose of union would not open for them. And the poor wretch who has never caught the scent of these words at all, how could he have anything of the ocean when he has no stream running into him?

### In short

- The proud are turned away from the signs of Allah, and the turning of their hearts is the answer to their own turning away, as the Book says in the sixth surah and the sixty-first.
- The phrase "without any right" leaves room for the case where standing up to the arrogant is itself humbleness, as the treatise Masail as-Suluk records.
- The verse describes a state that has gone past proof, and it names its cause, that they denied the signs and were heedless of them.
- Two ways of living are set against each other, the open way that reads the signs and the closed way that lives on its own sense of superiority, and the root of the second is the following of the self.
- Pride is of two kinds, the pride of the poor man toward the rich which is pride with his Lord, and the pride of the wealthy and the rulers toward the poor, which is the pride the verse condemns.

<<<VERSE 7:147>>>
The warning closes on the fruit of denial. "The deeds of those who deny Our signs and the meeting ˹with Allah˺ in the Hereafter will be in vain." Then the question is put. "Will they be rewarded except for what they have done?”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"The deeds of those who deny Our signs and the meeting ˹with Allah˺ in the Hereafter will be in vain."** states what comes of work done without faith. Those who reject the signs that prove the truth of the messengers, and who deny that they will meet Allah on the Day of Judgement, find that their good deeds come to nothing. The reason is that those deeds were never built on faith, and so there is no foundation under them to hold them up. What is recorded against them instead is their denial of Allah and their joining of partners with Him, and the repayment for that is a life that goes on in the fire.

The words say more than that good deeds are lost. One explanation holds that the works in question are the acts a person would count as his best, the keeping of the ties of kinship and the giving of voluntary alms, and that all of it fails and brings no reward, since such acts are not binding in this case. The reward of a deed follows the faith of the one who does it, and when the faith is gone the deed has nothing to stand on.

**"Will they be rewarded except for what they have done?”"** is put as a question, and the answer is folded into it. The recompense of a people is drawn from the deeds they have sent ahead, good repaid with good and evil repaid with evil. It is said of this that a person reaps only the harvest of what he has brought forth, so that the question leaves no room for complaint on the day when the account is settled.

### Where these words are placed

The verses around this one are speaking of the people of the calf, and the sentence is set here to name the end of what they did. The Israelites had been left with Harun while Musa was away at the meeting with his Lord, and the sin that came out of that interval was the worship of the calf. The verse tells them that the deed done in those days was not a small lapse that would be forgotten. Deeds that are done while denying the signs and the meeting with Allah do not survive, and the people who did them would be repaid for exactly what they had done.

There is a lesson in the arrangement of the passage for every community that comes after. The warning about the proud in the verse before this one, and the account of the calf that follows, are placed side by side with a statement that no community carries a privilege that exempts it from this rule. What a people sends ahead is what a people meets.

### In short

- The deeds of those who deny the signs and the meeting with Allah in the Hereafter come to nothing, since they were never built on faith.
- One explanation names the deeds as the acts a person takes most pride in, the ties of kinship and the voluntary alms, and says that none of it brings reward in that case.
- The closing question establishes that the recompense of a people is drawn from what they have done, with no injustice in it.
- The words are placed among the verses of the calf, so that the sin of that interval is answered by the statement that such deeds do not survive.

<<<VERSE 7:148>>>
The story turns back to the people left behind on the plain. "In the absence of Moses, his people made from their ˹golden˺ jewellery an idol of a calf that made a lowing sound." Two questions follow it. "Did they not see that it could neither speak to them nor guide them to the ˹Right˺ Path?" And then the verdict. "Still they took it as a god and were wrongdoers"

### Phrase by phrase

**"In the absence of Moses, his people made from their ˹golden˺ jewellery an idol of a calf that made a lowing sound."** brings in the man behind it. Among the Israelites there was a person named As-Samiri, a man of standing among them, and one who held to superstitions. He told the people that the jewels and the ornaments of the Egyptians which they had with them were not lawful for them, and the plunder taken from an enemy after a defeat was not lawful for the Israelites either. So they gathered all their jewellery and handed it over to him. He melted the ornaments down and forged from them the shape of a calf.

What he did next is reported in more than one way. He had collected some dust from under the hooves of the horse of the angel Gabriel, and Allah had placed in that dust the effect of life. He threw it into the molten metal as the calf was made, and the figure gave out a sound like the lowing of a cow. The ornaments themselves had been borrowed from the people of Pharaoh on the pretext of a wedding celebration, and it was from those borrowed things that the calf was made.

The reports differ on what the calf actually was. One view holds that it became a living body of flesh and blood, which is how it lowed. What the Book says of the same days in another place is that Allah told Musa that his people had been put to trial after he left them, and that As-Samiri had led them astray (20:85). Verse 148 gives a short account of that event, while the fuller account is carried elsewhere. Another holds that it stayed a figure of gold, and that the air entering it as it passed through made the sound that seemed to come from its mouth. Knowledge of the exact matter rests with Allah.

**"Did they not see that it could neither speak to them nor guide them to the ˹Right˺ Path?"** is the question put to the makers of it. The calf could not answer them with a single word. It could not return a reply to anyone who spoke to it, and it had no power to bring them harm or to bring them benefit. It could not lead them to any good way, whether in the affairs of this world or in the affairs of the spirit. The Book says of the same calf in another place that it could not give them back a word in reply, and that no power lay in its hands either for their hurt or for their benefit (20:89).

**"Still they took it as a god and were wrongdoers"** is what happened after all of that. They took it for worship, and by that act they wronged themselves. Their own sense of reason was blinded by ignorance and misguidance, and they turned to a statue in the shape of a calf while leaving the Creator of the heavens and the earth out of the account.

### How the people were drawn in

What brought the whole people in was not argument but spectacle, and the account explains the way it worked.

When the statue lowed, the people began to dance around it, and they fell into misguidance because they had come to adore it. They said that this was their god and the god of Musa, and that Musa had forgotten. As-Samiri had been a man of respect among them before that day, and the strange thing he had made raised their trust in him still higher, so they began to gather around him. He told them that Musa had gone up the mountain to speak with God, and that God had now come to them in the shape of the calf itself. He invited them to worship it, and they took it as their god.

By that time, the account says, the people were gathered around him in great numbers, and only Harun and a few of his companions were standing apart. Nobody dared to raise an objection openly. In the face of that mass following, even the protests of Harun were not strong enough to turn them, and no other person in the community had the courage to speak up. This has been the way of crowds from ancient times to the present day. A clever man, by the sheer flow of his words, can draw a great crowd after him even on a matter that carries no weight at all. The public, drawn to the small and the striking and the things that have nothing to do with the facts, has not the ability to think the matter through and separate what is true from what is not. And if anyone does stand up to show the truth, he is simply passed by, in the way that Harun was passed by.

There was also the art of it. The sculpting of the ancient Egyptians was of the kind that allows air to pass through a figure and come out as sound, and it was by that means that the sound came out of the calf's mouth. A people can be brought to worship a thing because a noise comes out of it, and the verse asks them afterwards whether that thing could speak to them or guide them anywhere.

### The calf in every age

The story was read by the people of the path for the meaning it carries into every life. Sahl ibn 'Abdullah said of it that whatever in this world turns a servant from the Real, and keeps him from obedience to Him, is his calf, and he is the one who worships it. The idol changes shape with the age, and the worship stays where it was.

The account adds where deliverance is found. The worshippers of the calf among the Children of Israel were freed when they were commanded to kill themselves, and the Book records that command in the words "So kill your own souls!" (2:54). In the same way, the servant finds deliverance on the road of truth when he is made clean of his own portions and of the secondary causes, or rather when he disowns everything other than his Lord.

The verse also carries a contrast in it. The calf could not speak, and this is set against the One whose description is that of a speaker who addresses the creatures. The kings of the earth count it beneath them to address their servants with their own tongue, because of their high rank, and their way of dealing with the lowly is to keep silent. The Lord of the worlds acted in the opposite way with His faithful servants. To the enemies in the fire He will say, "Slink into it, and do not talk to Me" (23:108). To the faithful the Prophet ﷺ gave the glad news that there is not one of them who will not be spoken to by his Lord with no interpreter between them.

### In short

- As-Samiri gathered the jewellery of the people, which he had told them was not lawful for them, and forged from it the shape of a calf that gave out a lowing sound.
- The dust from under the hooves of the horse of the angel Gabriel was placed in the metal, and the reports differ on whether the calf became a living body or stayed gold with air passing through it.
- The people were drawn in by spectacle and by the standing of the man, and only Harun and a few companions stood apart, with nobody daring to object openly.
- The verse asks whether the calf could speak to them or guide them to the right path, and records that they took it as a god and wronged themselves.
- Sahl ibn 'Abdullah said that whatever turns a servant away from his Lord and holds him back from obedience is his calf, and he is its worshipper.

<<<VERSE 7:149>>>
The story closes on what the people did once the truth came home to them. "Later, when they were filled with remorse and realized they had gone astray, they cried," The plea that follows is all they had left. "If our Lord does not have mercy on us and forgive us, we will certainly be losers.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Later, when they were filled with remorse and realized they had gone astray, they cried,"** records a change of state. They regretted what they had done. In the words of verse 149 they came to know for certain that they had wandered from the truth and from guidance by taking the calf as a god besides Allah. Their regret came when they saw the consequences of the deed and understood that they did not know how to make up for the wrong they had done.

**"If our Lord does not have mercy on us and forgive us, we will certainly be losers.”"** is the plea itself, and it carries an admission. They ask for mercy, which would enable them to do what they were instructed to do, and they ask forgiveness for having worshipped the calf. Then they name the outcome of being left without either. If mercy and forgiveness are not given, they are of the losers both in this life and in the world to come.

It is worth noting what the sentence is not. It is not a bargain, and it is not an argument. The people who had just made a god out of their own ornaments have nothing to offer and nothing to plead. All they can do is put the whole matter into the hands of their Lord and name their own condition truthfully. Repentance in the Book at this point is made of exactly these two parts, the recognition of the sin and the seeking of salvation from Allah.

**Where the words sit in the story.** The remorse came after Musa returned from the meeting with his Lord, since the account of the calf belongs to the time of his absence. So the people who had been left with a prophet among them to guide them came back to their senses and, finding nothing in their hands, turned to the door they should have used from the beginning.

### In short

- The people came to regret the worship of the calf and knew that they had gone astray from the truth and the guidance.
- Their plea asked for mercy that would let them do what they had been commanded and for forgiveness for the worship of the calf.
- They named the outcome of being left without mercy, that they would be of the losers in this life and in the world to come.
- The words carry the two parts of repentance, the admission of the sin and the asking of salvation from Allah, and they came after Musa had returned from the meeting with his Lord.
