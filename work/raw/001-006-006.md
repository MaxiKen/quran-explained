With "Guide us along the Straight Path" the surah becomes a request. The first three verses praised Allah, the fourth pledged worship and reliance to Him alone, and now the servant asks for the one thing on which everything else depends. The last three verses of the surah are a prayer, and the prayer was not left for man to compose: Allah Himself, in His mercy, taught him what to ask for. This is the wellspring of worship and the marrow of obedience, the supplication, the asking, the pleading and the imploring of the believers. What they are asking for is straightness and a firm footing in the religion. In the plainest terms the verse means: show us the right way, the way of submission to You in which there is no crookedness, make it easy for us, make us travel it, and keep us firm on it. Or, as the believer puts it in his own words, Lord, show us the path that is, in Your sight, the true path.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Guide us"** is ihdinā, from hidāyah, guidance. The best definition of the word is the one given by ar-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī in his Mufradāt: hidāyah is to lead someone towards his destination gently and kindly, and guidance in the full sense of the word comes from Allah alone. In this verse it means guide us, direct us, lead us and grant us right guidance. The Qur'an uses the verb in all these shades. "And We showed him the two ways" (90:10) means that Allah explained to man the paths of good and evil. "He chose him and guided him to a straight path" (16:121) is said of Ibrāhīm (Abraham). "And lead them to the way of the Blaze" (37:23) uses the same verb for directing the wrongdoers to the Fire. "You are surely guiding to a straight path" (42:52) is said to the Prophet ﷺ, and "All praise is for Allah, who guided us to this" (7:43), spoken by the people of Paradise, means that He guided them, directed them and made them fit for that end. In the simplest reading the request is "show us the way to it". Other readings take it as "make us firm in holding to it", or take the Straight Path to be the Book of Allah, so that being guided to it means being guided to what it declares lawful and unlawful and to the explanation of what it contains.

**"along the Straight Path"** is aṣ-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm. Abū Jaʿfar aṭ-Ṭabarī said that the whole community agrees that in the language of the Arabs the straight path is the clear road with no branches off it. He quoted the poet Jarīr ibn ʿAṭiyyah al-Khaṭafī, who said of the caliph that he walks a path that stays straight while the other paths are crooked, and he noted that the evidence for this usage is plentiful. The Arabs apply the word ṣirāṭ to any deed or statement, good or bad, and so they describe the honest man as straight and the wicked man as crooked. The Straight Path the Qur'an means is Islam, the established religion that Allah is pleased with.

### The road with two walls

An-Nawwās ibn Samʿān related that the Prophet ﷺ described the Straight Path in a parable, recorded by Aḥmad in his Musnad. Allah has set forth the likeness of a straight road with a wall on either side of it. In the walls are open doors, and over the doors hang curtains. At the entrance to the road stands a caller who cries, "People, keep to the road and do not swerve from it." Another caller stands above the road, and whenever anyone goes to open one of the doors he warns him, "Woe to you, do not open it, for if you open it you will go through." The Prophet ﷺ then explained it: the road is Islam, the two walls are the limits Allah has set, the open doors are the things Allah has forbidden, the caller at the entrance is the Book of Allah, and the caller above the road is the admonisher Allah has placed in the heart of every Muslim.

### Why ask for guidance if you already have it?

The prayer for the Straight Path is taught to everyone alike: to ordinary Muslims, and equally to the saints and the prophets who have already been guided and who guide others. The question comes naturally. Why does the believer ask for guidance in every prayer and outside it, when he is already guided? Has he not already got what he is asking for?

The first answer is that if the believer did not truly need to go on asking for guidance night and day, Allah would not have directed him to ask. The servant needs Allah every hour of his life to keep him firm on the path and to make him firmer and more constant on it. He has no power to benefit or harm himself except by Allah's leave, and so Allah directed him to ask constantly for His help, His steadying and His success. The happy person is the one whom Allah guides to ask of Him, and all the more when he urgently needs help. The Qur'an itself speaks this way. It says, "O you who believe, believe in Allah and His Messenger and the Book He sent down to His Messenger and the Scripture He sent down before," and the command to the believers to believe is not redundant, because what is being asked of them is firmness and continuity in the deeds that keep a person on the path of faith. In the same spirit Allah taught the believers to say, "Our Lord, do not let our hearts deviate after You have guided us, and grant us mercy from Yourself. You are the Bestower" (3:8). So "Guide us along the Straight Path" means: keep us steady on the path of guidance and never allow us to drift from it.

The fuller answer depends on knowing everything that guidance covers. Once that is understood, the question disappears, and so do the difficulties of readers who, not knowing what guidance means, begin to suspect that some verses of the Qur'an contradict others.

### The three degrees of guidance

Guidance comes in degrees. The first degree is general and reaches everything in the universe, minerals, plants and animals included. Guidance for minerals sounds odd, but the Qur'an is explicit that every existing thing, down to a particle of dust, has life and sensitivity, and even a consciousness and an understanding of its own, each in its own degree and within its own sphere. Some things have more of this than others, and those with very little are counted as lifeless and unconscious. The Sharīʿah recognises the gradation: such creatures bear no obligation to keep Allah's commands. Creatures with clear signs of life but none of consciousness and reason are called living but not rational, and those that show consciousness and reason along with life are called rational beings. Because of this difference, human beings and the jinn alone, of all that exists, have been made subject to the law and answerable for what they do. That does not mean that everything else is empty of life and awareness. "There is nothing that does not glorify Him with praise, but you do not understand their glorification" (17:44). "Have you not seen that everything in the heavens and the earth glorifies Allah, and the birds with wings outspread? Each one knows its prayer and its glorification, and Allah knows what they do" (24:41). No one can praise Allah without knowing Him, and knowing Him is the highest knowledge there is, impossible without consciousness and understanding. So these verses show that everything in existence has life, sensitivity, understanding and consciousness, however hidden from the ordinary observer, a truth that all the great religions, some ancient philosophers and lately even experimental science have confirmed. The Qur'an describes this first, general guidance in the words "He gave everything its form, then guided it" (20:50), and "Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High, who created and proportioned, who determined and guided" (87:1-3). Allah gave each creature its particular nature and function and guided it to act in keeping with its place in the scheme of things. That is why everything in the universe performs its allotted task with such marvellous efficiency: it is the ear that hears a sound, not the eye or the nose, the nose smells but cannot see, and the eye sees but cannot smell. "There is nothing in the heavens and the earth but comes to the Most Compassionate as a servant" (19:93).

The second degree is not general but particular. It is confined to the rational creatures, human beings and jinn, and it comes to every person through the prophets and the revealed books. Some accept it and become believers, others reject it and become disbelievers.

The third degree is more particular still, reserved for true believers and the God-fearing. Like the first degree it descends directly from Allah to the individual, and its name is tawfīq: Allah's grace arranges a person's inner and outer means and circumstances so that accepting the guidance of the Qur'an and acting on it becomes easy and even pleasant for him, and ignoring or opposing it becomes hard. This third degree has no upper limit and its levels are without number. Here is the field in which a person not only can but must make progress in the true sense of the word, and the engine of that progress is good deeds: every increase in good deeds brings an increase in divine guidance. The Qur'an promises it: "As for those who are guided, He increases them in guidance" (47:17), "Whoever believes in Allah, He guides his heart" (64:11), and "Those who strive for Us, We will surely guide them to Our paths" (29:69). It is in this field that even the greatest prophets and saints are seen striving, and it is an increase of guidance and help that they go on seeking to their last breath.

Put the three degrees together and the picture is clear. Everyone already has guidance in some form, and yet no one, not even the greatest, can do without wanting more of its higher stages. That is why, of all the prayers a person can make, the prayer for guidance is the most important, and why it is taught in the very first surah of the Qur'an, as necessary for the greatest prophet as for an ordinary Muslim. Sūrat al-Fatḥ, listing the worldly and spiritual fruits of the conquest of Makkah in the last years of the Prophet's ﷺ life, includes among them "and that He may guide you to a straight path" (48:20). When that verse came down, the Prophet ﷺ had long since been guided and was himself the guide of others, so the good news of guidance can only mean that he was raised at that time to some very high station of it.

### Three cautions for readers of the Qur'an

Knowing the degrees of guidance clears up three things that confuse readers.

1. The Qur'an sometimes speaks of guidance as general, shared by believers and disbelievers and indeed by all creatures, and sometimes as special to the God-fearing. The unwary reader senses a contradiction. There is none: one degree is common to all, another is limited to particular people.
2. The Qur'an says again and again that Allah does not guide the unjust and the wicked, and it also declares repeatedly that He guides everyone. The same distinction resolves it. The general guidance is given to all without exception, and the third, very special degree is withheld from the unjust and the wicked.
3. The first and third degrees are a direct act of divine grace in which no prophet has any part. The work of the prophets belongs to the second degree alone. Whenever the Qur'an calls the prophets guides, it means this second degree and nothing else, and when it says to the Prophet ﷺ "You cannot guide whom you love" (28:56), it means the third degree: it is neither a prophet's function nor within his power to give anyone tawfīq, that is, to make accepting guidance easy for him.

In sum, "Guide us along the Straight Path" is one of the most comprehensive prayers ever taught to man and one of the most important. No member of the human family can claim to be beyond needing it. No success and no prosperity in this world or the next can really come to anyone who is not on the Straight Path, and for a person lost in the worries of this passing life this prayer is an elixir, though few people realise it.

### Showing, travelling, being drawn

Read for its inner meaning, the verse asks for three things at once: lead us to this path, make us travel it, and make us firm on it. The believers are saying, "O Allah, show us Your road, then make us set out on it, then take us from travelling to being drawn." These are the three great roots: showing, then travelling, then being drawn. Showing is what Allah means by "It is He who shows you His signs" (40:13). Travelling is what He means by "You will surely ride stage after stage" (84:19). Being drawn is what He means of Mūsā (Moses) by "We brought him near as a confidant" (19:52). The Prophet ﷺ asked for all three. For showing he prayed, "O Allah, show us things as they are." Of travelling he said, "Travel! The solitary ones will be the foremost." Of being drawn he said, "One pull from the Real is equal to all the deeds of jinn and men." The believers ask for all three because not everyone who sees the road travels it, and not everyone who travels it arrives. Many hear but do not see, many see but do not recognise, many recognise but do not find. Many a shaykh at his prayers has fallen from his horse, and many a tavern-goer has saddled a lion. Of the words "Guide us" it has also been said that they mean: cut our innermost hearts off from looking at anything other than You, make the dawning lights rise in our hearts, free our intentions from the stain of created things, carry us past the stations of seeking and inference to the courtyards of nearness and union, keep us from resting content with images and forms by treating us with the gentleness of finding You, and unveil to us by that the witnessing of Your majesty and beauty.

### In short

- After praise and pledge comes the request, and the request is for guidance: to be shown the straight road of Islam, the clear road without branches, to be led along it and kept firm on it.
- The Prophet ﷺ pictured it as a road between two walls, Allah's limits, with curtained doors, the things He forbade, the Qur'an calling at the gate and a warner in every Muslim's heart.
- The guided still ask for guidance because they need Allah every hour to keep them on the path, and because guidance has degrees without end: the general guidance of all creatures, the guidance of prophets and books, and the tawfīq that grows with every good deed.
- Prophets guide only in the second sense. "You cannot guide whom you love" refers to the third, which is Allah's alone.
- The verse asks for three things in one: to be shown the road, to travel it, and to be drawn along it to the end.
