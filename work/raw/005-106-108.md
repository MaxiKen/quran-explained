<<<VERSE 5:106>>>
A precise legal procedure is given for a traveller facing death: "When death approaches any of you, call upon two just Muslim men to witness as you make a bequest". The verse allows two others from outside the community when no Muslim is available, and it sets a solemn oath after prayer if their word is doubted.

The ruling is narrow in its conditions and careful in its safeguards, and the reports about its occasion explain why each condition exists.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! When death approaches any of you"** concerns the time of making a will, when the signs of death appear. One gloss describes it as death drawing near with one of its causes.

**"call upon two just Muslim men to witness as you make a bequest"** requires two witnesses, described as just, from your own people, meaning Muslims. The wording shows a preference for Muslim witnesses.

**"otherwise, two non-Muslims if you are afflicted with death while on a journey."** permits two others from outside, understood by Ibn 'Abbas as the People of the Book, in a report given by Ibn Abi Hatim. Two conditions permit this: being on a journey and needing to write a will, as Sharih al-Qadi states.

Ibn Jarir records Sharih saying that the testimony of Jews and Christians is not accepted except while travelling, and even then only to witness the dictation of the will. One gloss describes the two others as people from another tribe or from outside your own community, and another as two free men.

**"If you doubt ˹their testimony˺, keep them after prayer"** addresses the case where the heirs suspect treachery or theft. The prayer meant is 'Asr, according to Al-'Awfi from Ibn 'Abbas, and Muhammad bin Sirin, 'Ikrimah, Qatadah, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i and Sa'id bin Jubayr are reported with the same explanation.

Az-Zuhri says they are detained after the Muslim prayer, that is, after prayer in congregation. The two witnesses are therefore held after a congregational prayer.

One explanation gives the reason for that time. The People of the Book held 'Asr in esteem, and lying at that hour was particularly forbidden among them. This shows that restricting a solemn oath to a special time or place is permissible.

**"and let them testify under oath ˹saying˺, “By Allah! We would never sell our testimony for any price"** is their oath. One gloss explains the wording as not exchanging the oath for a paltry portion of this soon-ending world, whether by swearing falsely or by testifying falsely for that price. Another explains it as not seeking worldly gain in what they vow.

**"even in favour of a close relative"** adds that even if the beneficiary, or the person for whose sake testimony is given, is a near kinsman, they will not compromise the truth. One gloss reads it as applying even if the deceased is one of their own kin.

**"nor withhold the testimony of Allah."** describes the testimony as Allah's, out of respect for it and recognition of its weight. It is the testimony Allah commanded them to give, and they will not conceal it if asked.

**"Otherwise, we would surely be sinful.”"** states the consequence of distorting, altering or wholly concealing it. One gloss renders the sinful as the wrongdoers.

### What happened to produce this ruling?

A Muslim named Budayl travelled to Syria on business with Tamim and 'Adiyy, who were then Christians. On reaching Syria he fell ill. He wrote a list of his belongings and put it in his baggage without telling his companions.

When his illness grew serious he called his Christian companions and made a will before them, instructing them to deliver everything to his heirs. On their return they handed over the belongings, but they removed a silver bowl inlaid or plated with gold.

When the heirs found the written list in the baggage, they asked whether the deceased had sold anything or had been so ill that unusual expenses were needed. The answer was no. The matter was finally brought for hearing before the Prophet ﷺ.

The heirs could produce no witnesses, so the two Christians were put to oath. They swore that they had committed no breach of trust concerning the deceased's property and had hidden nothing of it, and the verdict went in their favour on the strength of that oath.

Some time later it emerged that the two had sold the bowl to a goldsmith in Makkah. Asked about it, they said they had bought it from the deceased, and explained that they had no witnesses to the purchase, so they had not mentioned it earlier for fear of being contradicted.

The heirs appealed to the Prophet ﷺ. The position had now reversed, with the executors claiming a purchase and the heirs denying it. In the absence of evidence, the two closest to the deceased gave a sworn statement that the bowl had belonged to him and that the two Christians had lied in their oath. The price for which it had been sold, a thousand dirhams, was then handed over to the heirs.

Another account names the deceased as Budayl ibn Abi Maryam, a client of 'Amr ibn al-'As, who died on the road while travelling with 'Adiyy ibn Bida' and Tamim ibn Aws al-Dari. A third, from Ibn 'Abbas, describes Tamim al-Dari and 'Adiyy ibn Badda' travelling to Makkah with a man of Quraysh from Banu Sahm who died in a land where there was not one Muslim, so that he had to entrust his belongings to them for his family.

These differing accounts agree on the substance and differ in names, and they are preserved as reported.

### How does the procedure work in legal terms?

The person with whom the deceased leaves property in trust, to be handed to someone, is called a wasiyy, an executor or trustee, and there may be one or more. That a trustee should be Muslim and just is better but not required, whether the will is made on a journey or at home.

In a dispute the complainant is the plaintiff and the other party is the respondent. Witnesses are taken from the plaintiff first. If he produces witnesses recognised under the norms of Islamic law, the case is decided for him. If he cannot, the respondent is put to oath and the case is decided for him, unless he denies the claim, in which case the plaintiff prevails.

In the case of Budayl the heirs were the plaintiffs, since they claimed that a silver bowl had been given to the executors. Failing witnesses, the defendants were required to swear that they had committed no breach of trust. That is why the Qur'an advises the judge to detain them after prayer and have them swear.

Taking an oath at a particular time or place to make it more emphatic depends on the judge's opinion and is not compulsory. Its compulsory nature is not established by this verse, and the opposite is established by other verses and hadith reports. It is nevertheless advised here so that the sanctity of the time and place may further move people to speak truly.

### What is deduced about testimony between non-Muslims?

Abu Hanifah deduces from the permission of two others from outside that testimony given by unbelievers for one another is acceptable. Since their testimony has been made acceptable even in the case of Muslims here, its acceptance between themselves is more fitting.

Later, testimony by unbelievers for Muslims was abrogated on the authority of the verse requiring two witnesses from among your own men (2:282). The testimony of unbelievers for one another remains as it was. This is set out in Al-Ahkam, the legal work on the rulings of the Holy Qur'an, and the same discussion asks whether the testimony of a kafir, an unbeliever, may be accepted at all.

Support for Abu Hanifah's position is also drawn from a hadith about a Jew who had committed unlawful intercourse. His people blackened his face with soot and brought him to the Prophet ﷺ, who asked the reason and was told what had happened. After hearing the witnesses he ordered the prescribed stoning.

### What does the word about detention imply?

The wording about detaining the two shows that a person who genuinely owes something to another, who holds a right of return, may be detained by that other for the recovery of his right when needed.

### How does the verse read as a practical instruction?

A man on a journey who senses that his death is approaching should find two Muslims and hand over his possessions to them with clear instructions about passing them to his heirs. If he cannot find two Muslims in time, he should do the same with two non-Muslims.

Those two should bring the possessions and hand them to the heirs. If the heirs then doubt the witnesses' account, the witnesses should be detained in a mosque and made to swear before the general body of Muslims, who will attest that what they said on the deceased's behalf was correct.

If the heirs are still not satisfied with the sworn statement, two of the heirs should swear to that effect, and a decision should then be taken in accordance with their declaration. Giving this right to the heirs establishes a check, so that nobody will dare commit a breach of trust.

### In short

- Two just Muslim witnesses are preferred for a bequest, and two others from outside are permitted on a journey when no Muslim is present.
- If their word is doubted they are detained after a congregational prayer, 'Asr being named, and swear an oath whose wording covers price, kinship and concealment.
- The occasion was the case of Budayl and the silver bowl, preserved with differing names in the reports.
- The plaintiff brings witnesses, and failing them the respondent is sworn, with the oath's time and place left to the judge.
- Abu Hanifah deduces from the verse that testimony between unbelievers remains acceptable, while testimony for Muslims was later abrogated.

<<<VERSE 5:107>>>
The procedure continues into the case of dishonesty: "If they are found guilty ˹of false testimony˺, let the deceased’s two closest heirs affected by the bequest replace the witnesses". The heirs then swear, and their oath is preferred. The verse explains why that preference is just.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If they are found guilty ˹of false testimony˺"** describes the two witnesses being found to have sinned, whether through breach of trust or through perjury in their testimony. One gloss gives the example of the accused item being found in their possession, after which they claim to have bought it from the deceased or to have received it as a bequest.

Another explains it as their being found to have cheated or stolen from the money the will concerns. A third says that after their oath it was established that they had lied in their testimony or in their oath.

**"let the deceased’s two closest heirs affected by the bequest replace the witnesses"** puts two others in their place, nearest in kin among those who claim a lawful right. One gloss names them as those most concerned with the bequest, that is the inheritors, and describes them as standing in the place of the two Christians. Another account names 'Amr ibn al-'As and Muttalib ibn Abi Wada'ah as the two who took their place representing the deceased.

**"and testify under oath ˹saying˺, “By Allah! Our testimony is truer than theirs."** is the heirs' oath. It is explained as their testimony that the two cheated being more truthful than the testimony the two offered, and as an oath to the breach of faith of the two witnesses and to the truth of what the heirs suspected.

**"We have not transgressed."** means that they did not exceed the truth when they accused the two of treachery, and did not overstep the bounds in their claim.

**"Otherwise, we would surely be wrongdoers.”"** states the consequence if they had lied about them. One gloss describes the wrongdoers as liars who harm others, and another as those who overstep Allah's limits.

### Why is the heirs' oath preferred?

This is the oath of the heirs, and preference is given to what they say. The comparison offered is the oath of a murdered person's relative when someone tries to tarnish the victim's case, so that the family swears in defence of his honour. The subject is treated in the discussions of oaths in Al-Ahkam and the other books of legal rulings on the Holy Qur'an, where the testimony of a kafir is weighed against that of a believer.

In the case described with the preceding verse, the two Christians claimed to have purchased the bowl from the deceased. They should have substantiated that claim with witnesses and failed to do so, so the oath was given to the legal heirs that no such sale had taken place to the best of their knowledge.

### What does the oath accomplish?

One account describes the heirs swearing that the property the deceased carried was more than what the two returned, and that the testimony of the Muslims is truer than the testimony of the Christians. The reversal is exact: those who first swore are now contradicted by those whose right was affected.

A gloss adds that this stipulation stands for a pair of trustees, while for a pair of witnesses it has been abrogated, and that testimony from people outside the community has likewise been abrogated. The distinction between a trustee and a witness therefore matters for how far the ruling still applies.

### What lesson does the procedure teach beyond the case?

In Islamic law such instructions about everyday life carry lessons in a wider sphere. Once a person dies, dividing his estate is a domestic and financial affair. It has nevertheless been turned into training in two important respects.

The first is that people should adopt the attitude of giving no weight in their dealings to connections and relations, taking account only of rights and truth. They should look at what the truth is and not ask who is favoured by it and who is not.

The second is that every procedure should be treated as giving evidence before Allah. Whatever is in a person's custody is in Allah's trust, because he saw it given by Allah with his own eyes, preserved it in the memory Allah gave him, and now has to declare it with the tongue Allah gave him.

In those circumstances it is a breach of trust not to state the facts as one saw them or committed them to memory. The oath of the heirs exists because the first oath failed that standard.

### In short

- Where the two are found guilty of breach of trust or perjury, the two nearest heirs take their place and swear.
- Their oath states that their testimony is truer and that they have not transgressed, with the consequence of wrongdoing if they lied.
- Preference is given to the heirs' oath, as in the defence of a murdered person's honour.
- The stipulation is described as holding for trustees and abrogated for witnesses, and the testimony of outsiders as abrogated.
- The procedure trains people to weigh rights rather than relationships and to treat every statement as evidence before Allah.

<<<VERSE 5:108>>>
The verse explains the purpose of the arrangement: "In this way it is more likely that witnesses will give true testimony or else fear that their oaths could be refuted by those of the heirs." Two motives are built into the procedure, and both aim at the truth.

It closes with a command to mindfulness and obedience, and a warning about guidance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"In this way it is more likely that witnesses will give true testimony"** describes the ruling as bringing them nearer to giving testimony in its true form, the form in which they were charged to give it, without distortion or breach of faith.

The requirement that two witnesses from outside the community swear, when their truthfulness is doubted, may compel them to admit the testimony in its true shape. Requiring them to swear by Allah may encourage that admission, because they respect swearing by Allah and glorify and revere Him.

**"or else fear that their oaths could be refuted by those of the heirs."** adds the second motive. They also dread being exposed should the deceased's heirs be called on to swear in their place, for then those heirs would take the oath and recover the inheritance the two had failed to disclose.

One gloss describes them as fearing that after their oath the oath of others will be taken, so that they should not withhold their testimony. Another describes the fear as being disgraced if the heirs give conflicting testimony, since they would then incur penalties, and so they will not lie.

**"Be mindful of Allah and obey."** calls for taqwa in all their affairs, and for listening and obeying. One gloss describes fearing Allah concerning what is entrusted to them, and hearkening to what they are commanded with a listening that accepts.

**"For Allah does not guide the rebellious people"** warns those who do not obey Him or follow His law. One gloss describes them as lying, transgressing unbelievers, and adds that Allah does not guide to His religion or clear proof those who are not fit for His guidance. Another describes the wicked as those rebelling against obedience to Him, whom He does not guide to the way of goodness.

### Why are these oaths and refusals effective?

The several oaths taken by the witnesses after prayer, when their testimony is doubted, together with the possibility of their testimony being overturned, increase the likelihood that they will testify as the sacred law requires, without deceitfully distorting the facts.

The procedure works on two levels at once. Reverence for the oath itself moves a person to speak truly, and the prospect of a contradicting oath by the heirs deters him from lying. Neither motive is a substitute for honesty, but together they make dishonesty harder to sustain.

That is why the ruling gives the heirs a right rather than leaving them without remedy. The check established by that right is what makes a breach of trust a risky undertaking. The same legal discussion in Al-Ahkam and the other books of rulings on the Holy Qur'an asks whether the testimony of a kafir may be accepted at all, and answers that question from this verse.

### What does mindfulness add to the procedure?

The verse does not end with the mechanism. It commands mindfulness of Allah and obedience, and warns that guidance is withheld from the rebellious.

The legal device therefore rests on a religious disposition. A person who fears Allah concerning a trust will not betray it, and the oaths exist for those whose fear needs reinforcing at a moment of temptation.

The warning about guidance also explains the stakes. Obedience is not merely a procedural requirement in a court. It is connected with being guided at all, and rebellion cuts a person off from that guidance.

### In short

- The arrangement makes truthful testimony likelier by working on reverence for the oath and on fear of a contradicting oath by the heirs.
- Witnesses would otherwise face disgrace and penalties if the heirs swore against them.
- Mindfulness of Allah concerning trusts, and listening that accepts, are commanded alongside the procedure.
- Allah does not guide the rebellious, who are described as lying and transgressing, and the warning sets the stakes beyond the courtroom.
