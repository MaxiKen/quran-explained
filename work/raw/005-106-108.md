<<<VERSE 5:106>>>
"O believers! When death approaches any of you, call upon two just Muslim men to witness as you make a bequest; otherwise, two non-Muslims if you are afflicted with death while on a journey. If you doubt ˹their testimony˺, keep them after prayer and let them testify under oath ˹saying˺, “By Allah! We would never sell our testimony for any price, even in favour of a close relative, nor withhold the testimony of Allah. Otherwise, we would surely be sinful.”"

This verse sets out how a believer who is near death should make his bequest before witnesses. He should call two just Muslim men, or, while travelling, two others, and if there is doubt about their word, they are to swear by Allah that they will not sell their testimony or hide it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! When death approaches any of you, call upon two just Muslim men to witness as you make a bequest."** The material explains that the witnesses must be two just men, and that the preference is for Muslims from among the believer's own people. The material says the word for just means men of good standing, who can be trusted.

**"Otherwise, two non-Muslims if you are afflicted with death while on a journey."** The material says that the two conditions for admitting non-Muslim witnesses are the journey and the approach of death, when no Muslims are present. Ibn Abbas, as recorded by Ibn Abi Hatim, identified the others as the People of the Book. Sharih al-Qadi and Ibn Jarir reported that the witness of Jews and Christians is allowed only on a journey and only to witness the dictation of a will.

**"If you doubt their testimony, keep them after prayer and let them testify under oath."** The material explains that the time is after the prayer, and that according to Al-Awfi, reporting from Ibn Abbas, the prayer is the afternoon prayer. Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Qatadah, Ikrimah and Muhammad bin Sirin are reported to have said the same. Az-Zuhri is reported to have said that the time is after the congregational prayer. The material says that the Holy Qur'an gives this procedure to the believers, and the time was chosen because the People of the Book held it in esteem, and that the setting of a special time or place for an oath is permissible.

**"By Allah! We would never sell our testimony for any price, even in favour of a close relative, nor withhold the testimony of Allah."** The material says that the witnesses swear that they will take no worldly gain, even when the beneficiary is a close relative, and that they will not hide the testimony of Allah. Muqatil bin Hayyan is reported to have explained the vow in this way.

**"Otherwise, we would surely be sinful."** The material says that to distort, change or hide the testimony is a sin.

### The case of Budayl

The material gives the background of the verse. Budayl, a Muslim, travelled to Syria with two Christians, Tamim al-Dari and Adiyy ibn Bida. He fell ill, wrote a list of his property, and asked the two to hand over his goods to his heirs. When they returned, the heirs found the list, but a silver bowl was missing. The matter came before the Prophet ﷺ, and the two Christians swore that they had not betrayed the trust, and judgement was given in their favour. Later the bowl was found in Makkah, sold to a goldsmith, and the two said they had bought it from the deceased. The material reports that the heirs then brought the case again, and two close kinsmen of the deceased swore that the bowl had been his, and that the two Christians were liars. The price of one thousand dirhams was handed to the heirs.

### In short

- A believer making a bequest on a journey may call two witnesses, and if no Muslims are present, two from among others.
- The witnesses swear that they have not sold or hidden their testimony, even for a close relative.
- The verse rests on the trust that a witness owes to Allah.

<<<VERSE 5:107>>>
"If they are found guilty ˹of false testimony˺, let the deceased's two closest heirs affected by the bequest replace the witnesses and testify under oath ˹saying˺, “By Allah! Our testimony is truer than theirs. We have not transgressed. Otherwise, we would surely be wrongdoers.”"

This verse says that if the two witnesses are found to be guilty of false testimony, then two of the heirs who are most closely concerned should take their place, and swear that their testimony is truer, and that they have not transgressed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If they are found guilty of false testimony, let the deceased's two closest heirs affected by the bequest replace the witnesses."** The material explains that the two heirs nearest in kin, who have a lawful claim, stand in the place of the witnesses. The material reads the two as the custodians of the deceased, and it names Amr ibn al-As and Muttalib ibn Abi Wada'ah as the two who took the place of the witnesses. The material also reports from the Qurtubi that a variant reads the nearest of those concerned with the bequest.

**"By Allah! Our testimony is truer than theirs. We have not transgressed."** The material says the oath is that the heirs' testimony is more truthful than that of the two witnesses, and that the heirs did not accuse them falsely. If they had lied, they would be wrongdoers.

**"Otherwise, we would surely be wrongdoers."** The material says that the heirs' oath is preferred in such cases, as in the oath of the relatives of a murdered person, and that the rules of oaths are set out in the books of the rulings.

### In short

- If the two witnesses are found to have betrayed the trust, two of the nearest heirs replace them.
- The heirs swear that their testimony is truer and that they did not accuse falsely.
- The oath of the heirs is given weight in such a case.

<<<VERSE 5:108>>>
"In this way it is more likely that witnesses will give true testimony or else fear that their oaths could be refuted by those of the heirs. Be mindful of Allah and obey. For Allah does not guide the rebellious people"

This verse says that this procedure makes it more likely that witnesses will give true testimony, since they fear that the heirs' oaths will overturn theirs. It calls the believers to fear Allah and to obey, and says that Allah does not guide the rebellious people.

### Phrase by phrase

**"In this way it is more likely that witnesses will give true testimony."** The material explains that the procedure of the oath is designed to draw out the truth. The witnesses, who respect the oath by Allah, are more likely to speak the truth in its true form, since they fear exposure.

**"Or else they would fear that other oaths could be refuted by those of the heirs."** The material says that the witnesses fear that the heirs will swear and take the rightful share that they hid.

**"Be mindful of Allah and obey."** The material says that the taqwa is in all affairs, and that the obedience is to what Allah has commanded.

**"For Allah does not guide the rebellious people."** The material says that Allah does not guide those who disobey Him or do not follow His law.

### The lesson on trust

The material says that in Islamic law such rules are given for daily life, and that they teach a lesson in the wider sphere of life. The division of property is a family and economic matter, but it is made a school of two lessons. The first is that a person should not favour relations over the truth. The second is that every act is witnessed by Allah. The material says that the things in a man's keeping are a trust from Allah, and that a man who does not declare them as he has seen them has broken the trust.

### In short

- The oath procedure makes the witnesses more likely to tell the truth.
- Taqwa and obedience are required in all affairs.
- Allah does not guide the rebellious people.
