### Phrase by phrase

**"And relate to them ˹O Prophet˺ the story of the one to whom We gave Our signs"** is a command to the Prophet to tell the people about a man of the past. The people meant are the Jews who were with him. The story is told so that the fate of that man may serve them as a warning.

**"but he abandoned them"** describes what the man did with what he was given. The expression carries the sense of a creature coming out of its skin, the way a snake leaves its old skin behind and takes no further interest in it. The man put the knowledge and the wisdom he had been granted out of his hand and left it lying behind him.

**"so Satan took hold of him, and he became a deviant"** names the result. So long as the man held the verses of Allah, Satan could not get a hold of him. Once he had thrown that gift away, Satan overtook him and led him to the side of the perverse. He became a companion of Satan and one of those who went astray.

### The man of the story

The story is the story of Bal'am bin Ba'ura. The report of Ibn Mas'ud, carried by Abdur-Razzaq, says that the verse was revealed about him. He was, in those words, a man of the Children of Israel. The same statement came down from Mansur through Shu'bah and through several other narrators. The head of the chain in each case is Ibn Mas'ud.

The identification of the man is given in more than one form in the reports, and the scholars of the community recorded them side by side.

One chain runs from Sa'id bin Abi 'Arubah to Qatadah, and Qatadah reported from Ibn 'Abbas that the man was Sayfi, the son of Ar-Rahib. Ka'b, as Qatadah reported him, gave a different name for the place. The man came from Al-Balqla, a province of Jordan. He knew the Greatest Name of Allah, and his home was Bayt Al-Maqdis, where he lived among the tyrants.

Ibn 'Abbas, in the report that came through Al-'Awfi, named him as a man of Yemen. His name was Bal'am bin Ba'ura, and Allah had given him the knowledge of His verses, and he abandoned what he had been given. The description that Malik bin Dinar gave of him is that he ranked among the scholars of the Children of Israel and that his supplication was acceptable. When a difficulty came on the people, they looked to him to lead them in supplication.

One report has him sent by Musa to the king of Madyan, with the task of calling that king to Allah. The king flattered him and settled land and gifts on him. The man then left the religion of Musa and took up the religion of the king.

'Imran bin 'Uyaynah reported from Husayn, and Husayn from 'Imran bin Al-Harith, that Ibn 'Abbas gave the name as Bal'am son of Ba'ura. Mujahid and 'Ikrimah said the same. The identification of the man as one of the Children of Israel of ancient times is therefore well known. The say of the early teachers on it is recorded in full. It stands on Ibn Mas'ud, and on a number of the Salaf besides him.

The report of 'Ali bin Abi Talhah from Ibn 'Abbas places him in the city of the tyrants, meaning Jerusalem. His name was Bal'am, and the Greatest Name of Allah was known to him.

### The story as the reports tell it

The setting of the story comes out in the report that runs through Muhammad bin Ishaq bin Yasar, from Salim, from Abu An-Nadr. When Musa entered the land of Canaan in the area of Ash-Sham, which is greater Syria, the people of Bal'am came to him. They said that this was Musa, the son of 'Imran, with the Children of Israel. That he wanted to drive them out of their land, to kill them and to put the Children of Israel in their place. They said that they were his own people and that they had no other place to live. That he was a man whose supplication was acceptable. So he should go out and call on Allah against them.

He answered them with words of warning. He said that Allah's prophet was there with the angels and the believers. He asked how he could supplicate against him when he knew from Allah what he knew. They answered that they had no other place to live, and they went on luring him and begging him until he was tempted by the trial.

He set out on his donkey towards Mount Husban, which stood behind the military camp of the Israelites. When he had gone some way up the mountain, the donkey sat down and refused to move. He got down and struck it until it stood up, and he rode on. The donkey did the same thing a second time, and he struck it again until it stood up.

He went on, and when he tried to supplicate against Musa and his people, Allah made his tongue speak evil of his own people and good of the Children of Israel. His people called out to him and asked what he was doing, since he was praying for the Israelites and against them. He answered that it was against his will, and that the matter was one Allah had decided. His tongue was then made to hang out of his mouth, and he said that he had lost this life and the world to come. The verse was revealed about the story of Bal'am son of Ba'ura.

### The story as the other report tells it

The account that the majority of the scholars trusted most came down through Ibn Marduwaih, on the authority of the companion 'Abdullah ibn 'Abbas. The name of the man in that account is Bal'am bin Ba'ura. His home was in Syria, in Canaan near Jerusalem. One report makes him an Israelite by birth. Certain books revealed by Allah were known to him. It is that knowledge the phrase about the one to whom the verses were given refers to.

The event falls in the time that followed the death of Pharaoh and his people. The victory of the Israelites over Egypt had already come. After it Allah commanded the prophet Musa and his people to fight a people called the Jabbarin. The Jabbarin had seen what had happened to Pharaoh and his people. They were greatly frightened when they saw the army of Musa coming towards them.

They came to Bal'am and told him that Musa was a strong man who had come with a great army to drive them out of their land, and that he should pray to Allah to turn him back without a war. Bal'am knew the most gracious name of Allah. He called on Allah with it, and his prayers were generally granted.

Bal'am answered that Musa was the prophet of Allah, with the angels at his back. That no prayer of his could be turned against such a man. He said that he knew the rank Musa held with Allah. That by praying against him he would ruin himself in this world and in the world to come. The people insisted, and Bal'am promised that he would first seek the consent of Allah for such a prayer. That if Allah permitted it he would pray as they asked.

He performed an act to seek the consent of Allah, and in a dream he was forbidden from making that prayer. He came to the people and told them of the prohibition. The Jabbarin then presented him with a gift of considerable value, and he accepted it. After the gift their insistence increased. One report says that his wife advised him to accept the gift and to pray for them. Blinded by the love of wealth and of his wife, he began to pray against the prophet Musa and his people.

That moment was the occasion of an unusual thing. The words he wanted to utter against Musa came out against the people of the Jabbarin themselves, by the will of Allah. They cried out and told him that he was invoking against them. Bal'am said that he was unable to utter a single word against Musa, and that he had no control over what his tongue said.

The Jabbarin met the fate of disaster from the punishment of heaven. The punishment of Bal'am was that his tongue came out from under his mouth until it hung on his chest. He had ruined his life in this world and in the world to come, and he had been stripped of all the spiritual power he had.

The report goes on to the suggestion he made afterwards. He told the people of the Jabbarin that there was one way left by which they might overcome the Israelites. They should decorate their beautiful girls and send them to the Israelites, with instructions that the girls should put up no resistance to whatever the Israelites did. Being away from their homes, the Israelites would be likely to fall into adultery, which is the most detestable act before Allah. A people given to it can never overcome its enemy.

The suggestion was accepted and acted on. A prominent man of the Israelites fell into the trap. The prophet Musa made every effort to stop him, but the man went on in the wicked act. Plague then came on the people as a punishment, and the death toll in one day was seventy thousand people. The man who had committed the sin was killed along with the girl and was hung up by the Israelites in a public place. The people then turned to Allah in repentance and asked His forgiveness, and the disgraceful punishment was lifted from them.

Because of the blessings already given to him, he formed the idea that the blessing of prophethood should have come to him rather than to anyone else. The story also shows how the pledge taken from the people of the Book was broken. The Israelites were waiting for a prophet, and they used to describe his attributes to others. When the Prophet ﷺ appeared, they turned away from him for worldly considerations, just as the man of the story turned away from what he had been given. The verse with which the passage opens speaks to the Children of Israel in their own history and to the Jews of the Prophet's own time at once.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever Allah guides is truly guided."** states the first half of the rule. The person whom Allah leads aright is the guided one. Once Allah has led him aright no one can lead him into misguidance.

**"And whoever He leaves to stray, they are the ˹true˺ losers."** states the second half. The person whom Allah leaves to stray has taken on failure, loss and a sure misguidance. No one will be found to guide him.

The rule that covers both halves is that whatever Allah wills comes about. Whatever He does not will does not come about.

### The teaching on guidance and misguidance

Guidance and misguidance both come from Allah. He is the creator of good and of evil, of guidance and of misguidance. The people who are guided are the ones to whom Allah has given guidance. The losers are the ones whom He lets go astray.

The theme is set out in many places in the Book so that human beings may know that Allah created good and evil. That He gave clear instructions by which the two are told apart, together with a kind of free will by which a person chooses one of the two paths. The ones who choose the right path by their own will and do good deeds receive their reward from Allah. The ones who take the other path by their own will and do evil deeds are punished and sent to the Fire.

The wording of the verse carries a point of its own. The people of guidance are spoken of in the singular, while the people who are wrong and misguided are spoken of in the plural. The path of guidance is the one true faith. It was followed by all the prophets from Adam to the last of them ﷺ. The basic message of every prophet was the same in every age. A person of any age who believed in his prophet and followed the teaching that prophet brought is counted by Allah among the people of guidance, whoever he is and wherever he lived. The ways of misguidance, on the other hand, run into the thousands. That is why they are spoken of in the plural.

Another point in the verse is that the people who follow the path of misguidance are named together with their punishment of the Fire, while no reward is named for the people who follow the right path. Of them the verse says only that they are the guided. That is because guidance is itself the greatest of all blessings. It takes in every reward and bounty of this world and of the world to come, so no separate mention of rewards is required.

The likeness given for it is that of a great king who assures a man of his close friendship and of the certainty that he will be heard and trusted. Such a man holds the highest position among the officials. He has no need of a formal rank to go with it. In the same way, when Allah has given a person the title of the guided, that person has been given everything worth having. The say of the elders is that the remembrance of Allah and the worship of Him are themselves the reward of the worship, since the ability to do them is the great favour of Allah.

A person who is engaged in the worship of Allah is receiving the greatest of the rewards of Allah at the same time. The other blessings that wait for him in this world and in Paradise come second to it, since they are the fruit of that guidance.

The same teaching explains a verse in which the believers are promised their reward from their Lord. The reward is expressed there by two different words, one of them for a recompense and the other for a gift. The word that carries the sense of a recompense stands for what is offered in return for a service. The word for a gift stands for what is given without any service or performance at all. The two words together lead to the point that the things which people take to be the rewards of their own attainments are in fact gifts from their Lord, since the very performance that drew the reward was itself a gift from Allah, who enabled the person to do that service.

### The report attached to the rule

A hadith narrated from 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud states the same rule in the words of the Prophet ﷺ. The words are the praise of Allah, whom the people praise and from whom they seek help, guidance and forgiveness. The seekers take refuge with Allah from the evils within themselves and from the burden of their evil deeds. The report adds that the one whom Allah guides will never be misled, and that the one whom He leaves to stray will never find anyone to guide him. The witness of the report is that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah, without any partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and His Messenger. The complete hadith was collected by Imam Ahmad and by the collectors of the Sunan and by others.

### In short

- The rule is that the person whom Allah leads aright is the guided one. That once Allah has led him aright no one can lead him into misguidance.
- The person whom Allah leaves to stray has taken on failure and loss. No one will be found to guide him, since whatever Allah wills comes about and whatever He does not will does not come about.
- The wording of the verse speaks of the people of guidance in the singular and of the misguided in the plural, since the path of guidance is the one faith followed by every prophet from Adam to the last of them, while the ways of misguidance run into the thousands.
- The guided are named without a separate reward, because guidance is itself the greatest blessing and takes in every bounty of this world and the next, in the way a man given the friendship of a great king has no need of a formal rank beside it.
- The hadith narrated from 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud states the same rule, that the one whom Allah guides will never be misled and the one whom He leaves to stray will never find a guide. The complete report was collected by Imam Ahmad and by the collectors of the Sunan.
- The two words used for the reward of the believers, one for a recompense and one for a gift, show that what people take to be the fruit of their own attainments is a gift from their Lord, since the performance itself was given to them. Verse 178 closes the passage with that rule of guidance.
