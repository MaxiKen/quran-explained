<<<VERSE 7:175>>>
Allah tells His Prophet ﷺ to relate a warning drawn from history: "And relate to them ˹O Prophet˺ the story of the one to whom We gave Our signs, but he abandoned them, so Satan took hold of him, and he became a deviant". These verses concern an Israelite religious leader, widely followed for his learning and devotion, who suddenly strayed and ended among the rejected. The event holds many lessons. It also links with what came before. The preceding verses spoke of the covenant Allah made with the children of Adam in general, and with particular people in various circumstances. They also mentioned those who did not keep their pledge with Allah. An example is the Israelites, who awaited a prophet and described his qualities to others, yet rejected him for worldly reasons once the Holy Prophet ﷺ appeared.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And relate to them ˹O Prophet˺"** Recite, O Muhammad, to the Jews, the Israelites. The Prophet ﷺ was asked to tell his people this story. It shows how a devoted scholar and great spiritual leader lost all his knowledge, wisdom, popularity and fame simply by following worldly desires.

**"the story of the one to whom We gave Our signs,"** The man was one of the Israelites to whom Allah gave His signs, so that he knew and understood the truth they pointed to. He had knowledge of certain books Allah had revealed, and "Our signs" refers to that knowledge. One explanation says the signs were Allah's Greatest Name.

**"but he abandoned them,"** He did not act on them. He left them and moved away from them. The Qur'an pictures this as "wriggling out", like a snake leaving its old skin behind with no further concern for it. So he cast off the knowledge and wisdom Allah had given him and emerged in disbelief.

**"so Satan took hold of him,"** Satan pursued and caught up with him and became his close companion, deluding him. As long as he held the knowledge of Allah's signs, Satan could not take hold of him. As soon as he lost that great gift, Satan overtook him and led him astray.

**"and he became a deviant"**: he joined the erring disbelievers who go astray and are ruined, after having been rightly guided and on the path to salvation.

### Who was he?

The Holy Qur'an gives no name or identifying detail for this person. Many Traditions, however, from the Companions, their disciples and the interpreters of the Qur'an, give varied information about him. ʿAbdur-Razzāq recorded that ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd said the verse is about Balʿam ibn Bāʿūrāʾ, a man of the children of Israel. Another report from Ibn Masʿūd names him Balʿam ibn Abrah. In one report Ibn ʿAbbās said he was Ṣayfī, son of ar-Rāhib. Kaʿb said he was a man from al-Balqāʾ, a province of Jordan, who knew Allah's Greatest Name and lived in Jerusalem with the tyrants. Al-ʿAwfī reported Ibn ʿAbbās saying he was Balʿam ibn Bāʿūrāʾ, a man from Yemen to whom Allah had given knowledge of His signs, but who abandoned them. Mujāhid and ʿIkrimah said the same.

According to Mālik ibn Dīnār, he was an Israelite scholar whose supplication Allah used to answer. People would ask him to lead them in supplication in times of hardship. Mūsā (Moses) sent him to invite the king of Madyan to Allah. The king won him over with land and gifts, and he abandoned Mūsā's religion for the king's.

So, as Ibn Masʿūd and several others among the Salaf held, the verse is well known to concern an ancient man from the children of Israel. The Tradition that most scholars consider most authentic and reliable is the one Ibn Marduwaih reported from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās. It says his name was Balʿam ibn Bāʿūrāʾ, from Syria, living in Canaan near Jerusalem, and by one report an Israelite by birth.

Another view, held by ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ and Zayd ibn Aslam, is that the verse came down about Umayyah ibn Abī as-Ṣalt al-Thaqafī. A student of the Scriptures, he knew a messenger was due to be sent about then, and hoped the choice would fall on him. When Allah sent Muhammad ﷺ, he envied him bitterly and disbelieved in him. One explanation calls him Umayyah ibn al-Ṣalt and says Allah had honoured him with knowledge and eloquence, then took them away when he refused to believe.

### Balʿam and the city of the tyrants

ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah al-Wālibī reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that the man came from the city of the tyrants, the Jabbārīn, which is Jerusalem. His name was Balʿam, and he knew Allah's Greatest Name. When Mūsā and those with him came to that city, Balʿam's cousins and his people came to him. They said Mūsā was a strong man with a huge army, and if he defeated them they would be destroyed. So he should pray to Allah to keep Mūsā and his followers from overcoming them. Balʿam replied that if he prayed for that, he would lose both this world and the next. But they kept pressing him until he prayed against Mūsā and his people, and Allah stripped him of what He had given him. Hence Allah's words "but he abandoned them, so Satan took hold of him".

### The story as Ibn ʿAbbās told it

According to Ibn Marduwaih's report, these events came after the destruction of Pharaoh and his people and the Israelites' deliverance from Egypt. Allah commanded Mūsā and his people to fight a people called the Jabbārīn. The Jabbārīn had seen what became of Pharaoh's people, and they were terrified when they saw Mūsā's army approach. They came to Balʿam and told him that Mūsā was a powerful man who had come with a great army to drive them from their land. He should pray to Allah to turn them back without a fight. Balʿam was known to know the Greatest Name of Allah, and his prayers made through it were usually granted.

Balʿam told them that Mūsā, as Allah's prophet, had the support of Allah's angels, so he could not pray against him. He knew Mūsā's rank with Allah, and praying against him would ruin him in this world and the next. When they insisted, he promised to seek Allah's permission first, and to pray against Mūsā only if Allah allowed it. He did something to seek Allah's consent, and in a dream Allah forbade him to make such a prayer. He told the people of this prohibition.

The Jabbārīn then gave him a valuable gift, which he accepted, and after that they pressed him even harder. Some reports say his wife urged him to accept the gift and pray for them. Blinded by love of wealth and of his wife, he began to pray against Mūsā and his people. Then something unusual happened. By Allah's will, every curse he meant for Mūsā came out against the Jabbārīn themselves. They cried out and rebuked him for cursing them. He said he could not utter a word against Mūsā and had no control over what he said.

The Jabbārīn were then destroyed by a punishment from heaven. Balʿam's punishment was that his tongue hung out of his mouth until it dangled on his chest. Having ruined his life in this world and the next, and stripped of all his spiritual powers, he made a suggestion to the Jabbārīn. They could defeat the Israelites only one way. They should dress up their beautiful girls and send them among the Israelites with orders not to resist whatever was done to them. Far from home, the Israelites were likely to fall into adultery. Adultery is the act Allah hates most and must draw His wrath, and an adulterous people can never defeat its enemy.

The people accepted this satanic plan and carried it out. A prominent Israelite fell into the trap. Mūsā did all he could to stop him, but he went ahead with the shameful act. A plague then struck them as punishment, and seventy thousand people died in a single day. The Israelites killed the man who had sinned, along with the girl, and hung them up in a public place. After that they repented to Allah and sought His forgiveness, and He relieved them of the shameful punishment.

### The wasted prayers of al-Basūs

ʿIkrimah reported from Ibn ʿAbbās a different account. The verse concerns a man who was granted three prayers that would be answered. He had a wife named al-Basūs, who loved him and bore him children. One day she asked him to use one of his prayers for her. He agreed and asked what she wanted. She asked him to pray that Allah make her the most beautiful woman among the children of Israel. When she saw that no one could match her, she turned away from him and wanted another life. So he prayed that Allah would turn her into a barking dog, and two of his prayers were gone.

Then her children came to him. They could not bear it, they said, since their mother had become a barking dog and people insulted them because of her. They asked him to pray that Allah restore her. He prayed, and she became as she had been. So all three answered prayers were wasted. This is the al-Basūs whose ill luck became proverbial, so that people say of someone that he or she is unluckier than al-Basūs.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ was told to relate the story of a man given Allah's signs who cast them off as a snake sheds its skin.
- Most reports name him Balʿam ibn Bāʿūrāʾ, an Israelite scholar who knew Allah's Greatest Name. Another view names Umayyah ibn Abī as-Ṣalt.
- Pressed by his people, won over by a gift and urged by his wife, he prayed against Mūsā, but his curses turned on his own side.
- Once he lost the knowledge of Allah's signs, Satan overtook him, and he even devised a plan to lure the Israelites into sin.

<<<VERSE 7:176>>>
Allah describes what Balʿam could have become, and what he chose instead: "If We had willed, We would have elevated him with Our signs, but he clung to this life—following his evil desires." Then comes the image: "His example is that of a dog: if you chase it away, it pants, and if you leave it, it ˹still˺ pants." The verse widens the lesson: "This is the example of the people who deny Our signs. So narrate ˹to them˺ stories ˹of the past˺, so perhaps they will reflect".

### Phrase by phrase

**"If We had willed, We would have elevated him with Our signs,"** Through the signs He had taught him, Allah would have raised him above the filth of this worldly life. He would have let him benefit from them and act on them, raising him in this world and the next. One explanation says Allah would have raised him to heaven through the Greatest Name and made him a ruler over the people of the world. Another says He would have raised him to the ranks of the scholars by easing his way to good deeds.

**"but he clung to this life"** The verb akhlada means to incline toward something or cling to it. Literally, he clung to the earth. The word for earth, arḍ, covers the globe and everything on it. Furniture, machines, clothes and food are all gifts of the earth, so the one word stands for all worldly possessions. He was drawn to the adornment and delights of this life. He indulged in its pleasures and was deceived by it, as it deceives others like him who lack sound understanding.

**"—following his evil desires."** He followed his craving for leadership, or the lusts of his ego in doing evil, and called others to them. So Allah brought him low. He chose what led to his failure, preferring worldly desires to the Hereafter. The heart of the verse is that knowledge of Allah's signs is the real source of honour and advancement. For those who disregard them and prefer worldly desires and possessions, that very knowledge becomes a source of disgrace and disaster.

**"His example is that of a dog:"** His description, in his extreme greed for this world, is like a dog's. The likeness is Balʿam's, or by another explanation that of Umayyah ibn al-Ṣalt. The "therefore" that opens this clause in the Arabic ties it to what came before, his leaning toward this world and following his whims. So the likeness is in condition and in baseness.

**"if you chase it away, it pants, and if you leave it, it ˹still˺ pants."** The word lahath means to breathe hard with the tongue hanging out. Breathing in fresh air and breathing out stale air is vital to every living creature. Allah has made it automatic and effortless for all of them except the dog. The dog alone must labour to breathe, while other animals do so only when attacked or straining. No other animal is like it in this. Whether driven off or left alone, its tongue lolls out in disgrace. Balʿam is likened to a dog because his tongue hung out and he panted like one.

**"This is the example of the people who deny Our signs."** These are the people who deny Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, the Jews. Ibn ʿAbbās said this refers to the Israelites who had always longed for someone to be sent to them as a leader, to invite them to Allah and teach them the right path. When the long-awaited Prophet came with signs of truth so clear that they left no room for resistance, they too wriggled out of the Torah's commands, just as Balʿam had done.

**"So narrate ˹to them˺ stories ˹of the past˺, so perhaps they will reflect"** Recite the Qur'an to them, O Messenger, so that they may think about its parables, reject their denial of the truth and come to believe. Tell the story especially to the children of Israel, who knew what happened to Balʿam when Allah let him stray and expelled him from His mercy.

### What does the dog's panting stand for?

Scholars differ over the meaning of this parable. Some say it refers to Balʿam's tongue, which hung out of his mouth, as in the story narrated through Ibn Isḥāq, Sālim and Abū an-Naḍr. So he is like a dog whose tongue lolls whether it is chased away or not.

Others say it is a parable of this man, and those like him, in their misguidance. They persist on the wrong path and cannot benefit from faith or grasp what they are called to. Like a dog that pants whether chased or left alone, such a person gains nothing from advice or the call to faith. It is as if he had never been advised at all. Allah says in other verses: "As for those who persist in disbelief, it is the same whether you warn them or not—they will never believe" (2:6). And: "Even if you pray for their forgiveness seventy times, Allah will never forgive them." (9:80).

A third view holds that the wicked, the hypocrite and the disbeliever all have a weak heart, empty of guidance, which keeps faltering. A similar view was narrated from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī. One explanation applies the parable to both Balʿam and Umayyah: when they were admonished they paid no heed, and when they were left alone they understood nothing.

### The donkey that would not go on

Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq ibn Yasār narrated, through Sālim, from Abū an-Naḍr, a fuller account. When Mūsā (Moses) entered the land of the Canaanites in ash-Shām, Greater Syria, Balʿam's people came to him. They said that Mūsā son of ʿImrān had come with the children of Israel to drive them out of their land, kill them and settle the Israelites in their place. They had nowhere else to live. Since his supplication was answered, he should go out and pray against them.

Balʿam answered: woe to you! This is Allah's Prophet, and the angels and the believers are with him. How can I pray against them when I know from Allah what I know? But they kept saying they had nowhere else to go, coaxing and begging him until he gave in to the temptation. He rode his donkey toward Mount Ḥusbān, behind the Israelite camp. After a while the donkey sat down and refused to go on. He dismounted and beat it until it got up, and he rode on. A little later the donkey did the same, and again he beat it until it rose.

When he went on and tried to curse Mūsā and his people, Allah made his tongue speak ill of his own people and well of the children of Israel. His people protested that he was praying for the Israelites and against them. He said it was against his will, a matter Allah had decided. Then, with his tongue hanging out of his mouth, he told them that he had now lost this world and the Hereafter. It was about this story of Balʿam son of Bāʿūrāʾ that the verse came down.

### Why was the story told to the Israelites?

Allah had favoured Balʿam by teaching him His Greatest Name. When Allah is asked by it He gives, and when He is called by it He answers. But Balʿam used it in disobedience to Allah. He invoked Allah against His own party, the people of faith who followed Mūsā son of ʿImrān, His servant and Messenger in that age, to whom Allah spoke directly. The story was told so that the Israelites would reflect and avoid what Balʿam did.

Allah gave the Jews knowledge and raised them above the bedouins around them. He gave them a description of Muhammad ﷺ that let them recognise him as they recognise their own children. Of all people they had the most right to follow, help and support Muhammad ﷺ, in obedience to their own prophets, who told them of him and commanded them to follow him. So any of them who goes against what their Books teach, or keeps it hidden from Allah's servants, will meet disgrace from Allah in this life and humiliation in the Hereafter.

### The man who wanted to be the prophet

In the Prophet's time there was a man of great moral and intellectual distinction named Umayyah ibn Abī as-Ṣalt. When he learned that the holy books of the Christians and Jews foretold a coming prophet, he believed he himself was that prophet. Later, when he heard of the Prophet Muhammad's claim and the fine discourse revealed to him, he was bitterly disappointed and turned into an opponent.

The proper use of Umayyah's fine gifts would have been to recognise Allah's Prophet and become his companion. But because Allah had already blessed him so much, he thought the blessing of prophethood should have come to him and no one else. He saw worldly gain in rejecting the Prophet, though accepting him would have brought gain in the Hereafter. Had he chosen to accept, he could have had the angels as companions on his journey. Once he took the path of jealousy and pride, Satan was his only companion.

This example applies to everyone who ignores the truth, or refuses it out of jealousy and arrogance. Such a person falls from the level of humanity to that of a dog. A dog pants whether it is treated well or badly, and so does he. When Allah blessed him, he grew arrogant, and when He withheld a blessing, he stayed arrogant. He should have been grateful to Allah for His blessing. When a blessing was withheld, he should have accepted Allah's decision and turned back to Him.

### In short

- Allah's signs would have raised Balʿam in both worlds, but he clung to the earth and its possessions and followed his desires.
- He is likened to a dog that pants whether chased or left alone, a picture of greed, of a tongue hanging out, and of a heart no advice can reach.
- The story was told to the Israelites, who knew the Prophet's description, so that they would not repeat Balʿam's betrayal.
- Whoever rejects the truth out of jealousy or pride, like Umayyah ibn Abī as-Ṣalt, falls from humanity to the level of a dog.

<<<VERSE 7:177>>>
Allah passes judgement on those the parable describes: "What an evil example of those who denied Our signs! They ˹only˺ wronged their own souls". Could anything be worse than a people who reject Allah's signs and proofs, wronging themselves by doing so and gaining only their own ruin?

### Phrase by phrase

**"What an evil example of those who denied Our signs!"** Those who deny Allah's signs, Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, are likened to dogs, which care for nothing but finding food and satisfying their lusts. Anyone who steps outside knowledge and guidance to chase his cravings and whims is therefore no better than a dog. What an evil example that is. The Ṣaḥīḥ records that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that the evil example is not fitting for believers. One who takes back his gift is like a dog that returns to eat its own vomit.

**"They ˹only˺ wronged their own souls"** Allah did not wrong them. They wronged themselves by denying, rejecting guidance and disobeying their Lord. They were content with a life that will soon end, while chasing their desires and obeying their lusts. They harmed only themselves, bringing torment on themselves.

### What lessons does Balʿam's story teach?

This passage holds several useful lessons for those who take heed and use their understanding. They follow from the parable of the previous verse (7:176):

1. No one should boast of his knowledge or take pride in his piety. Things can change without warning, as they did for Balʿam ibn Bāʿūrāʾ. A person should thank his Lord for whatever knowledge and wisdom he has, and keep praying to Allah for firmness in faith.
2. One should avoid situations that threaten one's faith and knowledge. This is especially so where money, wife and children are involved, because love of these is the most powerful agent of misguidance.
3. One should avoid the company of the misguided and be careful about accepting their gifts or invitations. Balʿam met his evil end through accepting the gift of the Jabbārīn.
4. Shameless acts such as adultery and fornication bring ruin and disaster on the whole society. Those who want to save themselves from distress must stop their people from such crimes, or they will draw Allah's wrath and punishment on themselves.
5. Turning away from Allah's commands is itself a punishment. It opens the door to satanic influences, which work on people without their knowing and lead them astray. So anyone with knowledge must be aware of this precious treasure and seek Allah's help to protect it from evil influences. He must keep carefully correcting himself against error.

### In short

- Those who deny Allah's signs are like dogs that care only for food and lust, and the Prophet ﷺ said the believers must not fit such an evil example.
- Allah wronged them in nothing, for they harmed only themselves.
- Balʿam's fall warns against pride in knowledge, love of wealth and family, gifts from the misguided, and shamelessness, and calls for constant self-correction.

<<<VERSE 7:178>>>
The passage ends by tracing guidance and loss back to Allah: "Whoever Allah guides is truly guided. And whoever He leaves to stray, they are the ˹true˺ losers". The main theme of verse 178 is that guidance and misguidance both come from Allah. He is the Creator of good and evil, of guidance and misguidance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever Allah guides is truly guided."** Whoever Allah guides to His religion is truly guided, and no one can lead him astray. Such people are guided because they chose the path of their Lord.

**"And whoever He leaves to stray,"** Whoever Allah does not lead to His path, but turns away from it because of his deviation and bad deeds, has earned failure, loss and certain misguidance. Whatever Allah wills happens, and whatever He does not will does not happen.

**"they are the ˹true˺ losers"**: they will be punished, and they lose themselves and their families on the Day of Judgement. Is that not complete loss?

### Who chooses guidance?

The Qur'an returns to this theme in many verses, so that people may know that Allah created both good and evil. He gave clear instructions for telling one from the other, and a kind of free will to choose between the two paths. Those who freely choose the right path and do good are rewarded by Allah. Those who freely take the other path and do evil are punished and sent to Hell.

Allah does not appear openly to guide people. He shows them His way through signs. Guidance in this world comes to those able to see the truth in the form of signs and reasoning, and willing to yield to it. For those who give no weight to signs and arguments, nothing awaits but eternal ruin.

A hadith narrated from ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd, the opening words of a sermon, puts this into words. It begins by praising Allah and asking His help, guidance and forgiveness, and by seeking refuge with Him from the evils within oneself and the burden of one's evil deeds. It continues: whomever Allah guides, no one can mislead, and whomever He leaves astray, no one can guide. It ends with the testimony that there is no god but Allah, without partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger. Imām Aḥmad, the compilers of the Sunan and others collected the full hadith.

### Why is "guided" singular and "losers" plural?

The verse speaks of the guided in the singular and of the misguided losers in the plural. This shows that the path of guidance is one true faith, followed by all the prophets from Adam to the last of the prophets ﷺ. The basic message of all the prophets has been the same, and its fundamentals were the same in every age. So people of any age or community who believed in their prophets and followed their teachings belong, in Allah's sight, to one and the same category: the guided. The paths of misguidance, by contrast, number in the thousands, so they are mentioned in the plural.

### Why is no reward mentioned for the guided?

The verse names the losers together with their punishment, but mentions no reward for the guided. It simply calls them "the guided". This implies that guidance itself is the greatest blessing of all, embracing every reward and favour in this world and the Hereafter. The reward of guidance needs no separate mention of the other rewards it contains.

Suppose a great king assures someone that he is the king's close friend, whose word will be heard and trusted. That person certainly holds the highest place among the royal officials and needs no formal rank or post. In the same way, whoever Allah has called "the guided" has received every reward. So the elders say that remembrance and worship of Allah are themselves the reward for worship, because they are Allah's great favour. Anyone engaged in worshipping Allah is receiving His greatest reward in that very moment. The other blessings waiting for him in this world and in Paradise come second, as the fruit of this guidance.

This also explains another verse, which promises the believers "a recompense from your Lord, a gift". Two different words describe their reward. Jazāʾ, recompense, is given in return for a service, while ʿaṭāʾ, a gift, is given with no service performed. So what people take to be the reward of their own deeds and achievements is in fact a gift from their Lord. The very deed that earned the reward was itself His gift, since He enabled them to perform it.

### In short

- Guidance and misguidance come from Allah, who created good and evil and gave people the free will to choose.
- "Guided" is singular because there is one true faith shared by all the prophets, while the paths of error are many.
- No reward is named for the guided because guidance is itself the greatest reward, and even good deeds are Allah's gift.
