<<<VERSE 7:180>>>
The verse turns from the heedless to the remedy. "Allah has the Most Beautiful Names. So call upon Him by them, and keep away from those who abuse His Names. They will be punished for what they used to do"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah has the Most Beautiful Names."** states what belongs to Him alone. The names are the ones that carry the attributes of the highest degree of perfection. Perfection of that kind is not held by anyone besides Allah. A perfect man may always have someone above him in one quality or another, and the Book says that above every man of knowledge there is one more knowing (12:76). The names are His in a way that belongs to no other.

**"So call upon Him by them."** gives the command that follows. The call has two sides. It is praise and remembrance of Allah, and it is a request for help in a time of need. He alone is worthy of the praise and He alone is to be called for relief, so the verse names the one to be called and the words to be used for calling Him. We are not able to choose fitting words for His being, and He has given us the words that suit His glory. The best way to call Him is to call on Him by His beautiful names.

**"and keep away from those who abuse His Names."** orders the Prophet ﷺ to leave the company of the people who turn the names aside. The word used here for that turning aside means to decline from the centre. In the language of the Book it covers any deviation from the correct reading of the words, and any distortion of the message they carry.

The abuse took shape among the idolaters in the names they gave their gods. Al-Lat came from the name of Allah, and al-'Uzza came from al-Aziz, the All Mighty, and Manat came from al-Mannan, the Lord of favours. Ibn Jurayj narrated from Mujahid that the people took the name of the first idol from Allah, and the name of the second from the All Mighty. Al-'Awfi reported that Ibn 'Abbas said that to abuse the names includes saying that Al-Lat was derived from the name of Allah. Qatadah said that the abuse lies in joining others with Allah in His names, as when an idol is called al-'Uzza. The word also carries the sense of deviation, wickedness, injustice and going astray. The hollow dug in the side of a grave is called a lahd, because it is a hollow within a hollow, and it is turned towards the Qiblah, the direction of the prayer.

**"They will be punished for what they used to do."** closes the verse. Those who turn the names aside will be given their requital in the world to come for what they did and for what they said in this life. The warning was sent down before the command to fight them.

### The ninety-nine names

The reports on the number of the names are well known. Abu Hurayrah narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that Allah has ninety-nine names, a hundred less one. Whoever counts them and keeps them will enter Paradise. Allah is Witr, the One, and He loves what is odd, the witr. The Two Sahihs collected that hadith.

In the wording that comes through Abu Hurairah, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that there are ninety-nine names of Allah, and that whoever learns them by heart shall enter Paradise. Al-Bukhari and Muslim reported it. The teachers then added that the names of Allah are not limited to ninety-nine. They gave the supplication recorded by Imam Ahmad in his Musnad among the proofs. 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ taught a supplication for the man overcome by sadness or grief. The man says, O Allah, I am Your servant, the son of Your male servant and of Your female servant. My forelock is in Your hand. Your decision about me will certainly come to pass, and Your judgement of me is just. I call on You by every name that You have, the name You called Yourself by, the name sent down in Your Book, the name taught to any of Your creatures, and the name kept with You in the knowledge of the unseen. Then he asks that the Glorious Qur'an be made the spring of his heart, the light of his chest, the remover of his grief and the dispeller of his concern. Allah will take away his grief and his sadness and give him delight in their place.

The Prophet ﷺ was asked whether the people should learn those words. He said that they should and that it is an obligation on all who hear the supplication to learn it. Tirmidhi and Hakim set out the names in detail, one by one.

### The two instructions of the verse

The verse carries two valuable pieces of information. The first is that there is none besides Allah who is worthy of praise and worthy of being called for help. The second is that Allah has given us the words with which to call on Him, since we cannot choose words proper to His being, and we are required to call on Him by the attributes of perfection.

The reports attach a promise to those words. Invocations made with the names are generally granted, and the Book has said, Call on Me and I will answer you (40:60). The most certain way to seek help in a need and relief from distress is to call on Allah and ask Him, and that way does not fail.

The immediate benefit of the prayer is that it is itself a worship, and the reward of that worship is written on the sheet of a person's deeds. The Prophet ﷺ said in a Tradition that invoking Allah is the quintessence of worship. The answer comes in more than one way. Sometimes the very thing asked for is granted. At other times, when a person asks for what is untimely, or for what is not good for him in the knowledge of Allah, he is given something more fitting and more beneficial in its place.

Remembering Allah by glorifying and praising Him is food for a person's iman, the faith in Allah. It magnifies the love of Allah, the respect for Him and the awareness of Him, and it makes the hardship of this world look worthless in a man's eyes.

Al-Bukhari, Muslim, Tirmidhi and Nasa'i reported through sound chains that the Prophet ﷺ gave words for the man who falls into difficulty or is weighed down by grief. The man says that there is no god but Allah, the Greatest, the Most Forbearing. He says the word again with the great throne named. He says it a third time with the heavens, the earth and the glorious throne.

Hakim reported in his Mustadrak on the authority of the companion Anas that the Prophet ﷺ spoke to his daughter Fatimah al-Zahra. He asked what prevented her from hearing his advice and saying, morning and evening every day, the words that call on the Living, the All Sustaining, by His mercy, and ask Him to set right every situation and not to leave the speaker to his own self even for the wink of an eye. That invocation is an effective remedy for hardship and distress as well.

### The ways the names are turned aside

The deviation from the names of Allah takes more than one form. The first is to call on Allah by names that are not given in the Qur'an or in the Sunnah. The scholars of the ummah agree that it is not permitted to call on Allah by names or attributes of one's own choosing, or to praise Him by names that the Qur'an and the Sunnah have not given. A person may give Him the name Nur, which means light, but he may not use the word white for Him. He may be called al-Karim, the generous, but not by another word of the same meaning that the revelation did not use. He may be called al-Shafi, the one who grants cure, but not by the word for a doctor, even though the two words carry the same sense. The words are refused simply because the Qur'an and the Sunnah have not reported them.

The second form is to abandon one of the names because a person judges it unfit for some situation. That is a failure of respect towards the glorious names.

The third form is to give the names of Allah to people. Some of the names in the list have been used in the Qur'an and the Sunnah for human beings, and those may be used for others. Rahim, Rashid, 'Ali, Karim and 'Aziz are of that kind. Other names stand for what belongs to Allah alone, and their use for anyone else is a deviation. Rahman, Razzaq, Subhan, Khaliq, Quddus and Ghaffar are of that kind, and giving them to another is forbidden in the Shari'ah.

If a person calls another by those names out of a false belief that the other holds the attributes the names carry, the act is unbelief. If he uses them heedlessly, out of ignorance, it is not unbelief, though it comes close to it, and it is counted a major sin.

The naming of children among the Muslims has suffered in this matter. Some have left the practice of giving their children Islamic names, and the modern names they choose carry no Islamic mark, so that a person is not known as a Muslim by his name, all the more when his appearance and his manners carry no such mark either. The names of the early women, Khadijah, 'A'ishah and Fatimah, have been exchanged for other names with no such history. Worse still, the practice has grown of cutting short the names that begin with the word servant of. 'Abd al-Khaliq, 'Abd al-Rahman, 'Abd al-Razzaq and 'Abd al-Quddus are shortened to Khaliq, Rahman, Razzaq and Quddus when the person is called. The attributes of the Creator, the Merciful, the Sustainer and the Pure belong to Allah alone, and using them for another is a major sin. Every time a person calls someone by those names he commits the sin again and makes himself liable to a heavy punishment.

The practice has spread among Muslims with nothing gained by it in this world. It is done out of thoughtlessness about the weight of the error. The verse has warned against it in the words that those people will soon be given their requital for what they have been doing. Many evil deeds are done for some worldly gain, and the man who does them can claim that necessity pressed him. A great number of evil deeds bring no worldly benefit at all, and people fall into them out of carelessness about the commands of Allah and indifference to what He has permitted and what He has forbidden.

### What the names are

The names of Allah are His attributes. By those names He is renowned, praised and recognised. They are names full of blessing and sweet to the hearts. Pure words they are, and they come from a pure Lord. They come from the one Lord in a worthy arrangement. They are the ornament of the tongue and the lamp of the spirit, and the praise of them lasts.

One report carries the words spoken in the first person. He Himself says that His light is His guidance, that the word of His oneness is His word, and that He is He. The servant who gains access gains it with His light, and when he finds the way he finds it with the lamp. The lamp of the Sunnah is lit in the hearts, the lamp of recognition in the secret cores, and the lamp of love in the spirits. Happy is the servant who walks among these three lamps. There is no one more exalted than the man whose heart holds the greatest light and who sees the Friend face to face within it. He also says that the word of oneness is His word and His attribute, and that the name Allah is His name.

The teacher of the Tariqah has words that fit the place. He calls on the One worthy of His own praise, the One grateful for His own giving, the One who shows His own trial as sweet. He confesses that in himself he is incapable of praising Him, that in his intellect he is incapable of recognising His favour, and that in his ability he is incapable of what is worthy of Him. He is a captive to the pain for which Allah is the remedy and a slave of the praise of which Allah is worthy. He does not know what to say of Him. Allah knows. Allah is what He has said of Himself, and what He has said, He is.

The word of the chosen one, MuṣṬafā, agrees with that. He said that he does not count the praises of Allah, and that Allah is as He has praised Himself.

### Why the wrong picture forms

The wrong image of God in a man's mind is the reason he goes astray about Him. A man may accept that Allah exists and still frame an image that does not fit His glory. He imagines close ones of God after the pattern of human beings. He looks at kings and assumes that Allah has deputies and assistants, the way kings have regents and advisers. He takes the decisions of God to be the ones that suit his own desires, whether or not they accord with the justice of Allah. To attribute to Allah what does not fit His majesty and His glory is itself a distortion of His names.

Allah does not seize a man at once for his wrongdoing. He gives him room to take a lesson from the warnings and mend himself, or to grow more fearless until his guilt is well established. The verses that follow take up that delay.

### In short

- The beautiful names of Allah are the ones that carry the attributes of the highest perfection, and no one holds them but He, since even the most perfect man may have one above him (12:76).
- Calling on Allah has two sides, praise and remembrance on one hand and the request for help on the other, and the verse teaches that He alone is to be called and that He has given us the words, since the best way to ask Him is by His beautiful names, with the promise that He answers (40:60).
- Abu Hurayrah reported that Allah has ninety-nine names, a hundred less one, and that whoever counts them and keeps them will enter Paradise, in the hadith collected by the Two Sahihs, while the reports through Abu Hurairah add that whoever learns them by heart shall enter Paradise. The names are not limited to that number, since Imam Ahmad recorded from 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud the supplication of the man overcome by grief, which calls on Allah by every name He has, including the name kept with Him in the knowledge of the unseen.
- The abuse of the names began with the idolaters, who took Al-Lat from Allah, al-'Uzza from al-Aziz and Manat from al-Mannan, as Ibn Jurayj reported from Mujahid and as Ibn 'Abbas explained through al-'Awfi, and the word also covers deviation from the centre, the way the lahd is a hollow within a hollow turned towards the Qiblah.
- Deviation from the names has three forms. A person may call on Allah by names of his own choosing, which the scholars of the ummah forbid, since the Qur'an and the Sunnah have given the words. He may abandon a name because he judges it unfit. Or he may give to people the names that belong to Allah alone, such as Rahman, Razzaq, Khaliq, Quddus and Ghaffar, a major sin each time, while 'Abd al-Khaliq, 'Abd al-Rahman, 'Abd al-Razzaq and 'Abd al-Quddus are cut short among the Muslims and the exclusive attributes used for men.
- The names are the attributes by which Allah is renowned and recognised, sweet to the hearts and a lamp for the spirit. One report of them gives the words of the first person, that His light is His guidance and that the lamps of the Sunnah, of recognition and of love are lit in the hearts, the secret cores and the spirits, and the teacher of the Tariqah adds that he cannot praise Allah as He deserves. The chosen one, MuṣṬafā, said the same. The wrong picture a man frames of God, with close ones and deputies after the pattern of kings, is a distortion of the names, and Allah does not seize him at once, but gives him room to mend or to grow more fearless.

<<<VERSE 7:181>>>
The verse names the group that answers the truth when it is put before them. "And among those We created is a group that guides with the truth and establishes justice accordingly"

### Phrase by phrase

**"And among those We created is a group"** speaks of some nations among the creations, and the group named is the one that stands on the truth in its words and in its deeds.

**"that guides with the truth"** names their first work. They proclaim the truth and they call others to it.

**"and establishes justice accordingly"** names the second. They hold to the truth themselves and they judge by it.

The verses before this one gave a remedy to the people who made no proper use of their faculties. The remedy was to call on Allah by His beautiful names and to remember Him often by them. The present verse speaks of the people who took the right path and became guides for others. The group is a people who guide others to the straight path and decide their matters according to the truth, which is the divine law.

### The group named in the reports

A number of reports hold that the group named here is the ummah of Muhammad ﷺ. The Two Sahihs record the word of Mu'awiyah bin Abi Sufyan, who said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ gave the promise that there will always be a group of his ummah who are apparent on the truth. Such a group will not be harmed by those who fail them or oppose them, and matters will go on so until the Hour begins. In another narration the words are that this will hold until the command of Allah comes while they are still in that state. In a third narration the report adds that they will dwell in Ash-Sham, which is greater Syria.

Ibn Jarir reported from his own sources that the Prophet ﷺ recited this verse. He then said that the people named in it are his people. They will decide their disputes according to justice and truth, and they will observe justice in all their dealings.

'Abd ibn Humaid reported that the Prophet ﷺ addressed his companions. He said that this verse had been revealed in their favour, and that before them there had been a group of people bearing the same attributes. He then recited the verse about the community of Musa, that among the community of Musa there is a group who guide by the truth and act justly by it (7:159). A group among the Israelites carried the attributes named in this verse, guiding others to the truth and deciding their matters by the law of the Torah. The Muslim ummah carries them as its own mark.

### The way of the verse

The message of the passage has two parts. The first is to guide people to the right path in accordance with the demands of the Shari'ah. The second is to decide all disputes according to the law of Islam.

Those two features are the guaranteed way of success in this world and in the world to come. Any group or individual who follows the truth and does justice in every part of life belongs to the people of truth named in the verse. Such a person does not let personal prejudice in when he judges between people, even for enemies. That was the standing mark of the Muslim ummah in the time when its people held strictly to the demands of truth and justice. They put their own priorities aside when they judged between others. History has recorded unique examples of that practice in the lives of the Companions and of their disciples.

The decline set in from the day the Muslims began to show disregard for those two marks, and it showed itself in every part of their life. A large part of the ummah has fallen prey to personal desires, and its activities, in politics, in society and in education, are driven by worldly gain. Its guiding of others is aimed at some benefit in this world. Many of its family ceremonies carry no Islamic character and show behaviour opposed to the Shari'ah. Some family traditions have been set up in the name of religion, and a person who goes against them or shows dislike for them faces active opposition. People no longer organise themselves purely for the spread of truth and justice.

There is no one to make them see that this course will bring disaster on the Muslims at large. The dignity of the ummah can be restored, and the path of progress found again, only by the way of truth and justice set out in this verse. Effort has to be made to keep those values alive among the Muslims.

### In short

- Verse 181 names a group among the creations of Allah who stand on the truth in their words and their deeds. They proclaim it, call others to it, hold to it themselves and judge by it.
- The group is reported to be the ummah of Muhammad ﷺ. The Two Sahihs record from Mu'awiyah bin Abi Sufyan the promise that a group of the ummah will remain apparent on the truth until the Hour begins. One narration adds that they will dwell in Ash-Sham, which is greater Syria.
- Ibn Jarir reported that the Prophet ﷺ recited this verse and said that the people named in it are his people, who decide their disputes by justice and truth. 'Abd ibn Humaid reported that he told his companions the verse had been revealed in their favour, and that a group before them had carried the same marks, which he joined to the word about the community of Musa (7:159).
- The message has two parts, to guide people to the right path as the Shari'ah demands and to decide disputes by the law of Islam. Those two features are the way of success in this world and the next, and the Companions and their disciples gave the recorded examples of them.
- Disregard for the two marks brought the later decline, with worldly gain driving the affairs of a large part of the ummah and family ceremonies opposed to the Shari'ah. The way back is the truth and the justice of this verse. It is enough to say that the group is named in verse 181 as the answer to the heedless.

<<<VERSE 7:182>>>
The verse tells how the delay works on those who reject. "As for those who deny Our signs, We will gradually draw them to destruction in ways they cannot comprehend"

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who deny Our signs"** names the people meant. The signs are the revelation given to Muhammad ﷺ and the Book itself. The words came down among the people of Makkah first of all, and the denier named in the reports is Abu Jahl, with his followers after him, and every person who makes a mockery of any punishment sent on them.

**"We will gradually draw them to destruction"** states what the delay brings. The doors of provision are opened for them, and the means of livelihood are spread out, until they are deceived by it all and come to think that they are on the correct path. A passage of the Book gives the same picture. When a people forgot what they had been reminded of, the gates of every pleasant thing were opened for them, and in the middle of their enjoyment of what they had been given they were taken suddenly, and they were plunged into destruction with deep regrets and sorrow. The wrongdoers were cut off at the root, and praise belongs to Allah, the Lord of all that exists (6:44-45).

**"in ways they cannot comprehend"** states that the punishment comes to them without their knowledge of what is being done. One report adds that the punishment came on them in a single day, each of them destroyed in a different way.

### The question the verse answers

A question troubles many minds, and the verse answers it. If prosperity and progress come only by following the way of truth and justice, why are the nations that stand far from the truth seen to be more prosperous, more advanced and more powerful than others?

The answer is that Allah does not punish those who reject His verses at once for their sins. Out of His wisdom and His mercy, He lets them enjoy their worldly life for a while, and then He draws them to their shameful end so gradually that they do not know that they are being led to it. The present prosperity of such nations should not deceive anyone. Their prosperity is not a good sign for them. It is a respite, a period of relief before the punishment is carried out, and the Book has a name for it, istidraj, the drawing on by degrees.

Istidraj is a heavier punishment in disguise. Its most essential element is that a person is not punished for his evil deeds at once. The more he adds to his evil deeds, the more he is increased in prosperity and in worldly possessions, and the increase keeps him from any sense of guilt and from taking warning against his crimes. In the end he is kept from turning to Allah in repentance and from saving himself from the lasting punishment. The state of mind turns a man around, so that he takes illness for health and poison for a remedy. He is left to go on in that perverted life until death overtakes him and draws him to the lasting punishment.

The Book mentions istidraj in many places, and one of them is the passage already quoted about the gates of every pleasant thing (6:44).

The dealing is not limited to the disbelievers. The iniquitous among the Muslims are treated in the same way. It was for that reason that the elders used to fear Allah when they were favoured with prosperity and comfort. They feared that their prosperity might be a sign of istidraj.

### What the delay is for

God does not seize a person at once for his wrongdoing. In the delay a man is given the opportunity either to take a lesson from the warnings of God and reform himself, or to grow still more fearless, so that his guilt becomes well established. The next verse states the length of the rope that is given.

### In short

- The verse names the people who deny the signs of Allah, the revelation and the Book, among them Abu Jahl and his followers, and everyone who makes light of any punishment sent on them.
- Such people are not punished at once. The doors of provision are opened and the means of livelihood spread out, so that they think they are on the correct path, and then they are taken suddenly, the way the passage describes the people who forgot what they had been reminded of, whose gates of pleasant things were opened and who were then plunged into destruction with deep regret (6:44-45).
- The question answered by the verse is why nations far from the truth are seen to be more prosperous and more powerful, and the answer is that the delay is out of the wisdom and the mercy of Allah, and that their ease is a respite before the punishment rather than a sign of favour.
- The Book calls the drawing on by degrees istidraj, which is a heavier punishment in disguise, since a man punished at once may turn back while a man left in increasing prosperity loses the sense of guilt and the warning, takes illness for health and poison for remedy, and reaches death still in that state.
- The dealing is not limited to the disbelievers, since the iniquitous among the Muslims are treated in the same way, and for that reason the elders feared the granting of prosperity and comfort, lest it should be a sign of istidraj.
- The delay gives a man the room to take a lesson and reform or to grow more fearless until his guilt is established, and it is enough to say that verse 182 sets out that gradual leading on.

<<<VERSE 7:183>>>
The verse states the length of the delay and the strength behind it. "I ˹only˺ delay their end for a while, but My planning is flawless"

### Phrase by phrase

**"I ˹only˺ delay their end for a while"** means that the respite is given for a time. The disbelievers are left to enjoy their worldly life only for a while, and then the punishment takes them. The punishment is held back until they come to think that no punishment will reach them, so they keep adding to their rejection and their disbelief, and their punishment grows with it. One report describes the outward face of it, that Allah displays kindness to them while intending that they should be forsaken.

**"but My planning is flawless"** states what stands behind the waiting. The plan of Allah is strong and perfect, and His seizure is mighty. Nothing can withstand it.

The verse belongs with the one before it. Istidraj, the drawing on by degrees, is the working out of the plan in full. The respite is given, and the plan is carried through to the end, and no one can turn it aside.

### In short

- Verse 183 states that the disbelievers are left to their worldly life for a while, and that the punishment takes them after the respite, when they have come to think that no punishment will reach them and have added to their rejection.
- The plan of Allah behind the delay is strong and perfect, and His seizure is mighty, so that nothing can withstand it.
- The verse completes the one before it, with istidraj, the leading on by degrees, as the working out of that plan, and it is enough to say that the passage closes with those words.
- The passage as a whole, from verse 180 to this one, gives the beautiful names to be called upon, names the group that guides with the truth, and states the manner in which the deniers are drawn on and the strength of the plan that waits at the end of their rope.
