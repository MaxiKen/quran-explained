<<<VERSE 7:180>>>
After describing the heedless people of Hell, Allah points to the cure: "Allah has the Most Beautiful Names. So call upon Him by them, and keep away from those who abuse His Names. They will be punished for what they used to do". The people of Hell described above did not use their faculties to seek the lasting comforts of the Hereafter, and so made themselves liable to eternal punishment. This verse gives the remedy for their sickness. It is to seek Allah's help by calling on Him with His beautiful Names and remembering Him often through them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah has the Most Beautiful Names."** These are His loftiest attributes, such as knowledge, power, hearing and sight, which show His majesty and perfection. The Names are ninety-nine, as mentioned in a hadith. Al-ḥusnā, "most beautiful", is the feminine of al-aḥsan. The beautiful Names are those that denote attributes of the highest perfection, and perfection of the highest degree belongs to no one but Allah. Even a "perfect" human being may be surpassed by someone else in some quality, as the Qur'an says: "But above those ranking in knowledge is the One All-Knowing" (12:76).

**"So call upon Him by them,"** Invoke Him and recite them, and use them when asking Him for whatever you wish. The way the verse is worded shows that these beautiful Names belong to Allah alone. No one else has these attributes, so He alone deserves to be called on in need. "Calling" here has two aspects. One is praising, glorifying and remembering Allah. The other is calling on Him for help in need or difficulty. So Allah alone deserves praise and glorification, and He alone should be called on for help and relief. The verse also shows that the best way to call on Him is by His beautiful Names.

**"and keep away from those who abuse His Names."** The word for abusing is ilḥād, which means turning away from the centre. It also carries the senses of deviation, wickedness, injustice and straying. The niche in a grave is called laḥd for this reason: it is a hollow within a hollow, turned toward the qiblah. The verb can come from alḥada or from laḥada, and means those who turn away from the truth. In Qur'anic usage, ilḥād means departing from the correct meaning of the Qur'an's words or twisting its message. The Prophet ﷺ was told to keep away from those who distort Allah's prescribed Names or turn away from them.

Such people derived names for their gods from Allah's Names: al-Lāt from Allāh, al-ʿUzzā from al-ʿAzīz, "the Almighty", and Manāt from al-Mannān, "the Bestower of favours". Al-ʿAwfī reported that Ibn ʿAbbās said abusing Allah's Names includes claiming that al-Lāt was derived from Allah's Name. Ibn Jurayj narrated that Mujāhid said they derived al-Lāt from Allah and al-ʿUzzā from al-ʿAzīz. Qatādah said ilḥād means associating others with Allah in His Names, as in calling an idol al-ʿUzzā. Others say these are people who deny His Names and attributes, or distort their meanings, or give them to false deities. By another reading, they are people who refuse to acknowledge His Names. This was revealed before the command to fight them.

**"They will be punished for what they used to do"**: in the Hereafter Allah will repay them with a painful punishment for what they did and said in this world.

### How many Names does Allah have?

Abū Hurayrah narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that Allah has ninety-nine Names, one hundred less one, and that Paradise is for whoever enumerates and safeguards them. Allah is Witr, One, and He loves al-Witr, what is odd in number. The two Ṣaḥīḥs collected this hadith, and al-Bukhārī and Muslim's version speaks of learning them by heart. At-Tirmidhī and al-Ḥākim listed these Names in detail. Prayers made by reciting them are usually answered.

Yet Allah's Names are not limited to ninety-nine. In his Musnad, Imām Aḥmad recorded from ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ taught a supplication for anyone overwhelmed by sadness or grief. The person says that he is Allah's servant, the son of His servant, his forelock in His hand. Allah's decree concerning him will certainly come to pass, and His judgement on him is just. He then asks Allah by every Name He has, by which He named Himself, revealed in His Book, taught to any of His creatures, or kept with Himself in the knowledge of the Unseen. He asks Him to make the Glorious Qur'an the spring of his heart and the light of his chest, the remover of his sorrow and the dispeller of his worry. Allah will then take away his grief and sadness and replace them with joy. The Prophet ﷺ was asked whether they should learn these words. He said yes: everyone who hears this supplication should learn it.

### Why call on Allah alone?

The verse teaches two valuable things. First, no one but Allah deserves praise or deserves to be called on for help. Second, Allah has given people the very words with which to call on Him, words fit for His glorious Being, since people cannot choose suitable words themselves. So they are to call on Him by His attributes of perfection, without changing them.

Allah has promised to answer those who call on Him, in words quoted under 7:55 (40:60). The surest and most certain way to seek help in need and relief from distress is to call on Allah and pray for His help. It is the one way certain to succeed. Its immediate benefit is that prayer is itself an act of worship, whose reward is entered in the record of one's deeds. The Holy Prophet ﷺ said in a Tradition that calling on Allah is the essence of worship.

Allah answers supplication in many ways. Sometimes He grants the very thing asked for. At other times, when someone asks for something ill-timed or, in Allah's knowledge, unsuited to him, He gives something better or more beneficial instead. Remembering Allah by glorifying and praising Him is food for faith, īmān. It increases love, reverence and awareness of Allah, until the hardships of this world seem worthless.

### Supplications for times of distress

Al-Bukhārī, Muslim, at-Tirmidhī and an-Nasāʾī reported through authentic chains that the Holy Prophet ﷺ taught words for anyone in difficulty or grief. They begin: "There is no god but Allah, the Greatest, the Most Forbearing." They go on: "There is no god but Allah, Lord of the great Throne. There is no god but Allah, Lord of the heavens and the earth, Lord of the Glorious Throne."

Al-Ḥākim reported in his Mustadrak from Anas that the Holy Prophet ﷺ asked his daughter Fāṭimah al-Zahrāʾ what kept her from following his advice to say every morning and evening: "O Ever-Living, O Self-Sustaining, by Your mercy I seek help. Set right all my affairs, and do not leave me to myself even for the blink of an eye." This supplication too is an effective remedy for hardship and distress.

In short, the verse gives two instructions. One is to call on Allah alone, whether seeking help or seeking to please the Creator. The other is to call on Him by His attributes of perfection, without altering them.

### What counts as abusing Allah's Names?

There are many ways of deviating from Allah's prescribed Names:

1. Calling Allah by names not found in the Holy Qur'an or the Sunnah. The scholars of the Ummah agree that it is not permissible to call Allah by names or attributes of one's own choosing, or to praise Him with names the Qur'an and Sunnah have not given. So Allah may be called Nūr, "Light", but not "white". He may be called Karīm, "Generous", but not Sakhī, though it means the same. He may be called Shāfī, "the Healer", but not Ṭabīb, "the Doctor". The matching words have the same meanings, but they are not allowed because the Qur'an and the Sunnah do not report them.
2. Avoiding a certain Name because one thinks it unsuitable in some situation. This plainly shows a lack of respect for Allah's glorious Names.
3. Giving any of Allah's beautiful Names to people. There are exceptions here. Some Names in the list are used by the Qur'an and the Sunnah for human beings, such as Raḥīm, Rashīd, ʿAlī, Karīm and ʿAzīz, and these may be used for others. Other Names denote attributes that belong to Allah alone, such as Raḥmān, Razzāq, Subḥān, Khāliq, Quddūs and Ghaffār. Using these for anyone other than Allah is forbidden in the Sharīʿah.

If someone calls a person by one of these exclusive Names out of a false belief that he has the attribute it denotes, that is unbelief. If he does so carelessly or out of ignorance, it is not unbelief. Because it resembles unbelief, though, it is a major sin.

### Why should such names not be shortened?

Sadly, many Muslims have fallen into wrong practices in naming their children and addressing people. Some have given up Islamic names altogether. Their modern names have no Islamic character, so it is hard to tell from their names that they are Muslims, especially when their appearance and manners have already lost their Islamic character. Islamic names for girls, such as Khadījah, ʿĀʾishah and Fāṭimah, have given way to fashionable names with no such character.

A sadder practice still is the careless shortening of Islamic names such as ʿAbd al-Khāliq, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, ʿAbd al-Razzāq and ʿAbd al-Quddūs to Khāliq, Raḥmān, Razzāq and Quddūs when addressing their bearers. Khāliq, the Creator, Raḥmān, the Most Merciful, Razzāq, the Provider, and Quddūs, the Most Pure, are all attributes that belong to Allah alone. Applying them to anyone else is a major sin. Each time someone addresses a person by one of them, he commits that sin again and becomes liable to great punishment.

This sinful practice has become very common among Muslims, though it brings them no gain. They fall into it only because they do not realise how grave the error is. The verse warns against it: "They will be punished for what they used to do". Many sins are committed for some worldly benefit, and the sinner may plead that necessity drove him to it. But a great many sins bring no worldly benefit at all. It is sad to see people commit them simply out of carelessness about Allah's commands and indifference to what He has forbidden or allowed. May Allah protect people from such ignorance.

### Misreading Allah

People go astray about Allah because, though they accept that He exists, they often form a wrong picture of Him. They attribute to Him things unworthy of His glory. Thinking of Him as they think of human beings, they imagine that Allah has favourites. Looking at kings, they assume He has deputies and assistants, as kings have regents and advisers. In His decisions they imagine whatever suits their own desires, though it does not accord with His justice. To attribute to Allah anything that does not befit His majesty and glory is to distort His Names.

### The inner reading: the three lamps

Allah has Names, and those Names are His attributes. Through them He is renowned, praised and recognised. They are Names full of blessing, sweet to the heart. From the pure Lord they come as pure words in a pure arrangement, and from the One Lord as beautiful words in a worthy arrangement. They are the ornament of the tongue, the lamp of the spirit and everlasting praise.

He Himself says: "My light is My guidance, 'No god but Allah' is My word, and I am He." The servant who gains access gains it through His light. When he finds the way, he finds it by His lamp: the lamp of the Sunnah, the lamp of recognition and the lamp of love. In their hearts Allah kindles the Sunnah's lamp, in their innermost selves the lamp of recognition, and in their spirits love's lamp. How happy is the servant who walks between these three lamps! Who is more exalted than one in whose heart the greatest light shines, who sees the Friend face to face in his heart? "No god but Allah" is His word and His attribute, and "Allah" is His Name. His Name tells of the One who requites and is lovingly kind, the Deity of all, the Keeper of the world, who gives the people of the world their turns.

The Pir of the Ṭarīqah had words that fit this place: "O You who alone are worthy of Your praise, O You who thank Your own giving, O You who make Your own trial sweet!" He confessed that in himself he could not praise Allah, in his intellect he could not recognise His favour, and in his ability he could not do what He deserves. "O Generous One! I am captive to the pain for which You are the cure, and slave to the praise You are worthy of. What do I know of You? You know." Allah is as He has described Himself, and what He has said of Himself, He is. This echoes the words of al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ: "I cannot count Your praises. You are as You have praised Yourself."

### In short

- Allah alone has the Most Beautiful Names, attributes of perfect excellence, and their number is not limited to ninety-nine.
- He is to be praised and called on by these Names alone, and supplication through them is the surest remedy in distress.
- Abusing His Names includes deriving idols' names from them, inventing names for Him, avoiding some of His Names, and giving His exclusive Names to people, as when names like ʿAbd al-Raḥmān are shortened.
- In the inner reading, His Names light three lamps in the servant: the Sunnah, recognition and love.

<<<VERSE 7:181>>>
The previous verse offered the heedless a remedy: calling on Allah by His beautiful Names. Verse 181 now speaks of those who follow the right path: "And among those We created is a group that guides with the truth and establishes justice accordingly". They guide others to the straight path and settle all their affairs as the truth, the divine law, requires.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And among those We created"**: among some of the nations Allah created.

**"is a group"**: a community that stands by the truth in word and deed. This group is the community of Muhammad ﷺ, as stated in a hadith. They are leaders of guidance whom Allah has blessed with faith and good works.

**"that guides with the truth"**: they are guided by the truth, proclaim it and call others to it so that they too may be guided.

**"and establishes justice accordingly"**: they hold to the truth themselves and judge justly by it.

### Who is this group?

It is reported that this verse refers to the Ummah of Muhammad ﷺ. Ibn Jarīr reported that the Holy Prophet ﷺ recited this verse and went on to say that it describes his own people. They will settle their disputes with justice and truth and be fair in all their dealings. ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd reported that the Prophet ﷺ told his Companions that this verse had been revealed about them, and that before them there had been a group with the same qualities. Then he recited: "There are some among the people of Moses who guide with the truth and establish justice accordingly" (7:159). So among the Israelites there was a group with these qualities, guiding people to the truth and judging their affairs by the law of the Torah, given through Mūsā (Moses). In the same way, these qualities are the hallmark of the Muslim Ummah.

In the two Ṣaḥīḥs, Muʿāwiyah ibn Abī Sufyān reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that a group of his Ummah will always remain openly upon the truth. Those who let them down or oppose them will not harm them, until the Hour comes. In another narration, they remain so until Allah's command arrives. In yet another, they will live in ash-Shām, Greater Syria.

### Truth and justice, then and now

The heart of the message has two parts. The first is to guide people to the right path according to the Sharīʿah. The second is to settle all disputes by the law of Islam. Together, these two qualities are the sure way to success in this world and the Hereafter. Any group or person who follows truth and justice in every area of life, and keeps personal bias out of judgement even toward enemies, belongs to the people of truth this verse describes. This was the chief mark of the Muslim Ummah. Its people kept strictly to the demands of truth and justice in all their dealings and set aside their own interests when judging between others. History records unique examples of such truthfulness in the lives of the blessed Companions and their followers.

From the day Muslims began to neglect these two qualities, decline set in plainly in every area of their life. It must be admitted, with regret, that most of the Ummah have fallen prey to their own desires. Their political, social and educational institutions are driven by petty worldly gain, and even their guidance of others is aimed mostly at worldly benefit. Many of their family ceremonies lack Islamic character and even run against the Sharīʿah. Some family customs are dressed up as religious rites, and anyone who objects to these innovations meets fierce opposition. People no longer care to organise themselves purely to spread truth and justice.

No one makes them see that this way of thinking and acting must bring disaster on the Muslims as a whole. The only way to restore the Ummah's dignity and bring it back to the path of progress and prosperity is the way of truth and justice this verse lays down. Conscious efforts should be made to awaken Muslims to these values.

### In short

- Allah has created a community that guides by the truth and judges justly by it, identified in the hadith as the Ummah of Muhammad ﷺ.
- A similar group existed among the people of Mūsā, and a group of this Ummah will always stand openly on the truth until the Hour.
- Guidance by the Sharīʿah and justice in judgement are the sure path to success, and neglecting them brought the Ummah's decline.

<<<VERSE 7:182>>>
Verse 182 answers a question that troubles many Muslims: "As for those who deny Our signs, We will gradually draw them to destruction in ways they cannot comprehend". If progress and prosperity come only by the way of truth and justice, why do the nations far from the truth often seem more prosperous, advanced and powerful? The answer is that their prosperity is not a blessing but a respite.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who deny Our signs,"** Those who deny Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, rejecting Allah's signs without faith. These were the people of Makkah, Abū Jahl and his followers, and all who mock the punishment sent on them.

**"We will gradually draw them to destruction"** The doors of provision and livelihood will be opened for them in this life. Allah goes on providing for them, not to honour them, but to lead them on step by step until they stray even further. They are deceived by all this and say to themselves that they are on the right path.

**"in ways they cannot comprehend"**: from where they do not realise that punishment is coming. Then the punishment overtakes them when they least expect it. Allah destroyed those deniers of Makkah, each meeting a different kind of destruction.

### What is istidrāj?

Allah does not punish those who reject His signs at once for their sins. In His wisdom and mercy He lets them enjoy worldly life for a while. Then He draws them toward their disgraceful end so gradually that they do not know they are being led to it. So no one should be deceived by the present prosperity and progress of those who reject the truth. Their prosperity does them no good. It is a respite, a short period of relief before their punishment is carried out. The Qur'an calls this istidrāj, being drawn on by degrees.

Istidrāj is a severe punishment in disguise. Its essential feature is that a person is not punished for his evil deeds right away. The more evil he does, the more prosperity and possessions he is given, which keeps him from feeling guilty or heeding any warning. So he is kept from turning to Allah in repentance and saving himself from everlasting punishment. This twisted state of mind makes a person take sickness for health and poison for cure. He is left to go on with his corrupt life until death overtakes him and delivers him to eternal punishment.

### How has the Qur'an described it elsewhere?

The Holy Qur'an mentions istidrāj in many verses. One of them, quoted under 7:96 (6:44), tells how Allah poured every good thing on those who forgot His warnings, then seized them suddenly. Its sequel adds: "So the wrongdoers were utterly uprooted. And all praise is for Allah—Lord of all worlds" (6:45).

Istidrāj is not limited to disbelievers. Sinful Muslims are dealt with in the same way. That is why the righteous elders used to fear Allah when He favoured them with prosperity and comfort. They feared that their prosperity might be a sign of istidrāj.

### In short

- Allah leads those who deny His signs step by step to destruction, opening the doors of provision so that they think they are on the right path.
- Their prosperity is istidrāj, a respite before punishment that keeps them from guilt and repentance.
- Sinful Muslims can be dealt with in the same way, so the righteous feared that their comforts might be such a sign.

<<<VERSE 7:183>>>
Verse 183 continues the theme of istidrāj: "I ˹only˺ delay their end for a while, but My planning is flawless". The disbelievers are left to enjoy their worldly life only for a short time, and then punishment seizes them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"I ˹only˺ delay their end for a while,"** Allah gives them rein, that is to say, He prolongs the state they are in. He delays their punishment until they think it will never come. So they keep increasing in rejection and disbelief, and their punishment increases with it.

**"but My planning is flawless"**: Allah's plan is strong and perfect. His punishment and His seizing are mighty and cannot be withstood. Outwardly He shows them kindness, while intending them to be forsaken.

### Why does Allah not seize them at once?

Allah does not seize a person immediately for his wrongdoing. The delay gives him the chance either to learn from Allah's warnings and reform himself, or to grow still more fearless, so that his guilt is firmly established.

### In short

- Allah delays the punishment of the deniers for a while, giving them rein.
- The delay either lets a person reform or fixes his guilt, and Allah's plan cannot be resisted.
