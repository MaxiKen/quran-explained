<<<VERSE 5:15>>>
"O People of the Book! Now Our Messenger has come to you, revealing much of what you have hidden of the Scriptures and disregarding much. There certainly has come to you from Allah a light and a clear Book"

This verse addresses the People of the Book, the Jews and the Christians. It says that the Messenger has come to them, and that he makes plain much of what they had hidden from their Scriptures and passes over much of it. It then says that a light and a clear Book have come to them from Allah. The material explains that the Prophet ﷺ made clear the parts of their Scripture that they had altered or concealed, and left aside what would bring no benefit if it were explained. The verse presents the Qur'an as the clear light that replaces the darkness into which their own distortions had led them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O People of the Book! Now Our Messenger has come to you."** The material explains that the address is to the Jews, who had the Torah, and to the Christians, who had the Gospel. The word "now" marks the arrival of the Messenger after the earlier revelations.

**"Revealing much of what you have hidden of the Scriptures and disregarding much."** The material says that the Prophet ﷺ explained the places where they had changed, distorted and lied about Allah, and that he ignored much of what they had altered, since explaining it would bring no benefit. Al-Hakim recorded that Ibn 'Abbas said that whoever denies stoning as the punishment for adultery has denied the Qur'an, because stoning was among the things the People of the Book had hidden, and this verse revealed it. The material adds that Al-Hakim said the chain of this report was sound.

**"There certainly has come to you from Allah a light and a clear Book."** The light is the Prophet ﷺ, and the clear Book is the Qur'an. The material explains that the Qur'an is light and plain guidance, and it contrasts the Qur'an with the darkness of distortion.

### The truth made clear

The material says that the Messenger came with the guidance and the religion of truth for all people, Arabs and non-Arabs, those who could read and those who could not. He came with clear proofs and a clear distinction between truth and falsehood. The verse therefore ends the concealment that had hidden the truth from people, and it shows the purpose of the Messenger's mission as making the truth plain.

One reading in the material says that the People of the Book had made two mistakes. They omitted parts of their teaching, or distorted it, so that they did not need a new prophet. They also imposed restrictions that Allah had not commanded, through the subtle reasoning of their scholars. The Qur'an, the material says, restored their teaching to its original form and freed them from the burdens they had added. The material adds that the truth is shown by argument, and that reason appeals to those whose minds are open. The false deities that people set up cannot create or destroy anything, and that alone shows that there is no god but the one God.

### In short

- The Messenger came to the People of the Book and made clear the parts of their Scriptures that they had hidden or altered.
- The Qur'an is a light and a clear Book from Allah, which restores the truth they had lost.
- The verse shows that the purpose of the Messenger's mission is to make the truth plain to all.

<<<VERSE 5:16>>>
"through which Allah guides those who seek His pleasure to the ways of peace, brings them out of darkness and into light by His Will, and guides them to the Straight Path"

This verse says what the clear Book does. It guides those who seek the pleasure of Allah to the ways of peace, brings them out of darkness and into light by His will, and leads them to the straight path. The material explains the verse as a description of the blessing of the Qur'an, which saves the reader from destruction and gives him the clearest path.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Through which Allah guides those who seek His pleasure to the ways of peace."** The material explains that "the ways of peace" means the paths of safety and righteousness. It also explains that the Qur'an guides the one who seeks Allah's pleasure, so that the seeker is the one who receives the guidance. The material notes that peace here means the religion of Islam, and that the word peace is one of the names of Allah.

**"Brings them out of darkness and into light by His Will."** The darkness is disbelief and the light is faith. The material explains that the phrase "by His will" means by Allah's decree and by the success and grace He gives. The person who accepts the guidance is kept on it after he has accepted it.

**"And guides them to the Straight Path."** The material says the straight path is the religion of Islam, and that Allah keeps the believer on it. The guidance does not stop at the first step but continues to the end.

### The Qur'an as a blessing

The material says that the verse describes the Qur'an as a means of protecting people from what they fear, and of bringing them what they seek. It saves them from ruin and frees them from error. The verse therefore links the one who seeks Allah's pleasure with the one who receives the light, so that the approach to the Qur'an is itself a form of seeking Allah.

### In short

- The Qur'an guides those who seek Allah's pleasure to the ways of peace.
- It brings them out of darkness into light by Allah's will and keeps them on the straight path.

<<<VERSE 5:17>>>
"Indeed, those who say, “Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary,” have fallen into disbelief. Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Who has the power to prevent Allah if He chose to destroy the Messiah, son of Mary, his mother, and everyone in the world all together?” To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth and everything in between. He creates whatever He wills. And Allah is Most Capable of everything"

This verse refutes the belief that the Messiah, son of Mary, is Allah. It asks who could prevent Allah from destroying him, his mother, and everyone on earth, if Allah willed it. It then affirms that the kingdom of the heavens and the earth belongs to Allah alone, that He creates what He wills, and that He is capable of everything. The material explains that the argument is aimed at a belief held by one group of Christians, but that it defeats the false belief of every group against the oneness of Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, those who say, ‘Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary,’ have fallen into disbelief."** The material explains that the belief in question is the claim of one sect among the Christians, the Jacobites in one report. It says that the argument of the verse covers all of the false beliefs, whether that 'Isa is a son of God or that he is one of three gods.

**"Say, ‘Who has the power to prevent Allah if He chose to destroy the Messiah, son of Mary, his mother, and everyone in the world all together?’"** The material explains that the question refutes the claim of divinity. If 'Isa were God, he could prevent his own destruction, but no one has the power to stop Allah. The material says that the mention of his mother is significant, because 'Isa and his mother both showed humility before Allah, and because the belief of one group that Maryam was one of three gods is also refuted by this mention. The material notes that the verse speaks of the death of 'Isa and of his mother as a supposition, even though Maryam had already died when the verse was revealed. The material explains that this is either because the point concerns 'Isa, or because the power that took her life is the same power that could take his.

**"To Allah alone belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth and everything in between."** The material explains that everything in the heavens and the earth belongs to Allah, and that everything is under His power.

**"He creates whatever He wills."** The material explains that this refutes the reasoning that 'Isa must be divine because he was born without a father. Allah creates as He wills. The verse 3:59 says that the case of 'Isa in the sight of Allah is like the case of Adam, who was created without a father or a mother, and so the miraculous birth of 'Isa cannot make him God.

**"And Allah is Most Capable of everything."** The material says that Allah has the perfect and complete power to create as He wills, and that He is never asked about what He does.

### Power and creation

The material says that the verse answers a particular argument. The Christians reasoned that the birth of 'Isa, without a father, proved his divinity. The reply is that Allah created Adam without either father or mother, and that Allah's power is not limited by the ordinary course of nature. Creation shows the power of the Creator, and the creature is not divine because of how it came to be.

### In short

- Whoever says that the Messiah, son of Mary, is Allah has fallen into disbelief.
- No one can prevent Allah from destroying him, his mother, or anyone else, and this shows that they are all creatures.
- Allah creates what He wills, as He did with Adam, and this refutes the claim that a miraculous birth proves divinity.

<<<VERSE 5:18>>>
"The Jews and the Christians each say, “We are the children of Allah and His most beloved!” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Why then does He punish you for your sins? No! You are only humans like others of His Own making. He forgives whoever He wills and punishes whoever He wills. To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth and everything in between. And to Him is the final return.”"

This verse refutes a claim made by both the Jews and the Christians, that they are the children of Allah and His beloved. The Prophet ﷺ is told to ask them why Allah punishes them for their sins, if they are His beloved. The verse replies that they are only human beings, created like the rest, and that Allah forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills. The material explains that the verse ends with the kingdom of Allah and with the return to Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Jews and the Christians each say, ‘We are the children of Allah and His most beloved!’"** The material explains that both communities made this claim. The Jews relied on a statement in their Scripture that Allah called Isra'il his firstborn, and they read it as a claim of special status. The Christians said that 'Isa called God his father, and they read it as a claim of sonship. The material says that the phrase can be read as a claim of closeness and honour, not of literal descent, but the verse rejects the claim in either sense.

**"Say, ‘Why then does He punish you for your sins?’"** The material explains that the question is a refutation. If the claim of sonship were true, the Fire would not be prepared for their disbelief and their lies. A father does not punish a beloved son in this way.

**"No! You are only humans like others of His Own making."** The material says that they are created as all of the children of Adam are created, and that Allah is the Lord of all His creation.

**"He forgives whoever He wills and punishes whoever He wills."** The material explains that Allah does as He wills, that no one can escape His judgment, and that He is swift in reckoning.

**"To Allah alone belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth and everything in between. And to Him is the final return."** The material explains that everything is Allah's, and that the return of all people is to Him, when He will judge between His servants with justice.

### Claims of privilege

The material reads this verse as a warning against the belief that membership of a community brings special treatment. It says that earlier generations received blessings because of their deeds, but that later generations sometimes assume they are entitled to the same blessings simply because of their community. It says the verse removes this misunderstanding by showing that the consequences of conduct appear in this world, so that the people can see how Allah deals with them.

### In short

- The Jews and the Christians both claimed to be the children of Allah and His beloved.
- The verse refutes the claim by pointing to their sins and by stating that they are human beings, like others, whom Allah forgives or punishes as He wills.
- The verse also warns that no community is saved by its name alone, and that the return of all is to Allah.
