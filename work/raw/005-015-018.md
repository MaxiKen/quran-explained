<<<VERSE 5:15>>>
Allah addresses Jews and Christians with the arrival of guidance that exposes concealment without making exposure an end in itself: "O People of the Book! Now Our Messenger has come to you". Muhammad ﷺ makes clear much that had been hidden from earlier scripture, while leaving aside matters whose disclosure would bring no benefit. The verse presents his coming and the clear Book as light from Allah.

The address offers correction as a favour. It restores neglected guidance and removes burdens added under the name of religion. People who sincerely seek Allah's pleasure can follow it out of confusion, while those determined to follow desire may refuse its evidence.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O People of the Book!"** addresses the Jews, recipients of the Torah, and the Christians, recipients of the Gospel. Their connection to earlier revelation makes the call to receive and follow guidance especially direct.

**"Now Our Messenger has come to you"** identifies Muhammad ﷺ as Allah's Messenger. His mission brings clear evidence and a distinction between truth and falsehood. It is not confined to one ethnicity or educational class: Arabs and non-Arabs, the lettered and the unlettered, are all addressed by his message.

**"revealing much of what you have hidden of the Scriptures"** includes explaining alteration, distortion and false claims made about Allah. Specific examples are the description of the Prophet ﷺ and the legal ruling on stoning married people who commit unlawful intercourse. Concealing such instruction does not remove its place in the revelation.

**"and disregarding much."** He does not expose everything that could be exposed. Some disclosure would only humiliate people without advancing guidance. Passing it over is therefore not endorsement of the alteration, but restraint where recounting it would serve no useful purpose.

**"There certainly has come to you from Allah a light and a clear Book"** is explained in two closely related ways. One identifies the light as the Prophet ﷺ and the clear Book as the Qur'an. Another describes the Qur'an itself as both illuminating and plain. It makes lawful and unlawful matters clear and supplies guidance for religious and worldly life.

### What concealment is illustrated by the stoning ruling?

Al-Hakim records in his Mustadrak a statement from Ibn 'Abbas connecting the concealed instruction with stoning. Ibn 'Abbas warns that rejecting that ruling would, without the person realising it, amount to rejecting the Qur'an, because it was among the matters whose concealment this verse exposes.

Al-Hakim describes the chain as sahih, authentic, and notes that the two leading collectors did not record it. The attribution and its stated assessment belong with the report. The example shows how the general language about concealed scripture is understood through a particular legal matter.

### How can religion be damaged by both omission and addition?

One error is to remove a demand through distortion or deliberate misinterpretation. The discussion gives the example of treating inherited attachment to ancestral religion as sufficient for salvation, thereby avoiding the need to accept a later prophet.

The other error is to impose restrictions Allah did not appoint. Elaborate details of sacrificial practice are given as an example of burdens created through excessive interpretation rather than commanded by the prophets. A person may therefore lose genuine guidance while becoming increasingly strict about invented requirements.

The Qur'an's corrective role addresses both errors. It renews the obscured teachings and releases people from unnecessary restraints. This is why its exposure is described as light rather than merely accusation. It shows where people had mistaken a path toward divine anger for a path toward Paradise.

Clear reasoning still requires a receptive mind. Evidence can distinguish truth from falsehood, but a person intent on desire can refuse to listen. The saving effect of this light is developed in the following verse through the response of those seeking Allah's pleasure.

### In short

- The Messenger ﷺ brings clear guidance to the People of the Book and to humanity beyond them.
- He exposes concealed instruction where it serves guidance, while passing over disclosure that would only disgrace people.
- The light and clear Book restore neglected duties and remove invented burdens.
- Receiving that correction requires an open mind rather than loyalty to desire or inherited error.

<<<VERSE 5:16>>>
The Book's light is effective guidance for a willing response: "through which Allah guides those who seek His pleasure to the ways of peace". Allah brings those people out of darkness and directs them toward a straight way. The change is both a movement away from disbelief and disobedience and a movement into faith, safety and obedience.

### Phrase by phrase

**"through which Allah guides"** refers to the Book just mentioned. Another explanation includes the guidance brought through Muhammad ﷺ together with the Qur'an. The Messenger and revelation are the means, while Allah is the One who grants guidance.

**"those who seek His pleasure"** describes people who believe, act rightly and wish to please Him. A gloss connects this response particularly with accepting His oneness. Guidance is not merely possession of a text while resisting its demands.

**"to the ways of peace"** means ways of safety and righteousness, protecting from punishment and leading to Paradise. Another explanation connects peace, al-Salam, with Allah Himself and understands these as the ways of His religion, Islam.

**"brings them out of darkness and into light by His Will"** means release from disbelief and disobedience into faith and obedience. It happens by His command and permission. The phrase is also explained through the success and grace He grants, not through a claim that the person independently produces divine guidance.

**"and guides them to the Straight Path"** identifies the clear way of Islam. A gloss adds the meaning of keeping them firm upon that religion after they have accepted it. Guidance includes constancy, not only an initial change of direction.

### What do these images promise?

The images of safety, illumination and a straight road describe guidance from different sides. Allah saves people from ruin, removes confusion and shows the clearest course. He protects them from what they fear and brings them toward the good they desire.

That promise belongs to a response seeking His pleasure. Those who accept correction can leave the darkness in which they previously mistook error for guidance. The destination is not simply greater confidence, but a righteous condition and the way leading to Paradise.

### In short

- Allah guides through the revelation and the Messenger ﷺ those who sincerely seek His pleasure.
- Peace means safety and righteousness, while light means faith and obedience instead of their opposites.
- The straight way is Islam, and guidance includes remaining firm upon it by Allah's grace.

<<<VERSE 5:17>>>
Allah rejects the claim that a created messenger is himself divine: "Indeed, those who say, “Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary,” have fallen into disbelief." The answer asks who could prevent Allah from ending the life of 'Isa (Jesus), his mother Maryam (Mary), or everyone on earth. Their dependence upon Him distinguishes them from the One whose authority encompasses all creation.

The verse also answers the argument based on 'Isa's extraordinary birth. Allah creates as He wills. A birth outside the ordinary pattern demonstrates the Creator's power, not the child's divinity.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, those who say, “Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary,” have fallen into disbelief."** The specific claim is identified in the glosses with the Jacobites. 'Isa is Allah's servant, messenger and creature. Making him Allah contradicts divine oneness, however honoured his place among the messengers.

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Who has the power to prevent Allah if He chose to destroy the Messiah, son of Mary, his mother, and everyone in the world all together?”"** asks about the power to resist Allah's will. No one can provide that resistance. If the Messiah possessed divinity, he would not stand helpless before another's power over his own existence.

**"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth and everything in between."** All beings and all the resources and wonders of creation belong to Him. His authority is not one portion of a shared rule. Those people whom others worship are themselves within His dominion.

**"He creates whatever He wills."** Allah can create with or without the ordinary means. His creative power is not restricted by the familiar pattern of a father and mother.

**"And Allah is Most Capable of everything"** affirms the power behind the whole argument. It includes creating beings, rewarding His faithful servants and punishing His enemies. Nothing in creation lies beyond His ability.

### Why mention Maryam with her son?

One explanation draws attention to 'Isa's complete submission. He cannot resist Allah's will concerning himself, nor can he independently protect the mother whose care a good son values so highly. Their close relationship does not give either of them divine independence.

Another explanation sees the joint mention as answering the view of a sect that placed Maryam among three divine beings. The argument defeats that claim along with the claim about her son. Anyone subject to Allah's power over life and death is not a partner in His divinity.

Although the question is framed as a supposition, Maryam's death had already occurred by the time of the Qur'an's revelation. Two explanations are given. Her case may be included under the hypothetical wording primarily directed at 'Isa. Alternatively, the sense is that Allah who had already caused her to die could likewise cause the Messiah and all others to die.

### Does a birth without a father prove divinity?

No. The comparison with Adam directly addresses that reasoning (3:59). Allah created Adam without either father or mother. The explanation also mentions Eve without a mother and 'Isa without a father. The different modes of creation belong to one Creator's power.

If departure from the usual pattern proved that the created being was divine, the same reasoning would have to be applied to these other cases. Instead, the examples show that the ordinary means do not limit Allah. He is worthy of worship because He creates and rules, not because His creatures can restrict how He acts.

### How wide is the argument?

The verse directly names one assertion, that Allah is the Messiah. The explanation says its reasoning also answers other claims against oneness, including divine sonship and a division into three gods. Created beings cannot create or destroy independently of Allah.

The inability to control one's own existence is therefore central. Honouring a messenger does not require giving him the power of the One who sent him. Allah's complete ownership and justice remain undivided.

### In short

- The Messiah and his mother belong to Allah's creation and cannot prevent His will.
- Their dependence refutes making either of them divine or a partner in divine rule.
- Extraordinary birth shows Allah's freedom to create, as the comparison with Adam makes clear.
- All sovereignty and creative power belong to Allah alone.

<<<VERSE 5:18>>>
Allah challenges a claim to guaranteed favour: "The Jews and the Christians each say, “We are the children of Allah and His most beloved!”" The response asks why they are punished for their sins if their asserted relationship places them beyond judgment. They are human beings among His creation, subject to forgiveness and punishment rather than protected by an inherited title.

The verse ends where its argument begins: with Allah's authority. He owns all creation, and everyone will return to Him. No community can make its own claim of privilege the rule by which He must judge it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Jews and the Christians each say, “We are the children of Allah and His most beloved!”"** describes a claim of special closeness, care and rank. A gloss identifies the Jews of Madinah and Christians of Najran. The claim is also explained as identifying themselves with the prophets they follow and assuming the same favoured status.

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Why then does He punish you for your sins?"** exposes the contradiction in their claim to assured exemption. The explanations mention punishment in worldly life and the threatened Fire in the Hereafter. A specific gloss recalls calf-worship lasting forty days. Their own experience of accountability contradicts the absolute privilege they assert.

**"No! You are only humans like others of His Own making."** places them among the children of Adam. They are created servants, not beings whose relationship to Allah removes them from the common judgment of good and evil.

**"He forgives whoever He wills and punishes whoever He wills."** His pardon is grace and His punishment is justice. Neither can be compelled by communal self-description. One gloss applies pardon to those who repent from the rejected beliefs described and punishment to those who die persisting in them.

**"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth and everything in between."** all creation and its resources lie under His ownership and control. There is no separate domain in which a group can claim immunity from Him.

**"And to Him is the final return.”"** means the final journey of believers and disbelievers alike ends before Allah. He judges His servants with perfect justice and does not err in that judgment.

### Did they mean literal children in the same sense as their claim about 'Isa?

The explanation distinguishes the claims. Christians did not generally call themselves Allah's children in the same sense in which they made that assertion about 'Isa (Jesus). For themselves, the language concerned nearness, compassion and care, as though Allah's relationship to them were that of a father to favoured children.

A statement associated with their scripture calls Isra'il the firstborn. Some members of the People of the Book who later accepted Islam explained such wording as an expression of honour in the speech of that time, not literal divine parentage. Misreading the expression changes its meaning.

Similarly, words attributed to 'Isa about returning to his father and their father are explained as referring to his Lord and their Lord. Language of closeness cannot establish either literal sonship or guaranteed salvation regardless of conduct.

The Qur'anic answer addresses the privilege they draw from that language. If their special love meant that punishment could never reach them, the punishment for their sins would be inexplicable. The argument concerns their claim to exemption, not an independent theory about how every human parent behaves.

### How can the gifts to earlier believers be misunderstood by descendants?

A community receiving a prophet and scripture may gain help, victory, pardon and the promise of Paradise through sincere submission. In the earlier generation those gifts correspond to its actual response. Later generations may inherit the communal identity without preserving the obedience.

The error is then to treat the identity itself as a guarantee. What was received through faithful action becomes imagined as a racial or national entitlement. People assume the same blessings will continue whatever they do.

The discussion uses a community's fortunes in this world as a warning against that confidence. It argues that continued defeat and humiliation should force a scriptural community to question its claim to automatic divine favour, even when its numbers are large. It presents worldly help and ascendancy as signs within this covenant argument, not as a new measurement of every individual's worth through wealth or power.

The purpose is to remove complacency. A group that disregards the responsibilities of revelation cannot confidently claim that its name alone assures honour in the Hereafter. The final decision remains Allah's, not a status conferred by the group's own description of itself.

### In short

- Claims of divine sonship or special love do not remove accountability for sin.
- The language of honour and closeness must not be turned into literal parentage or inherited immunity.
- Gifts received by faithful ancestors do not guarantee the same outcome to descendants who abandon their conduct.
- All people are Allah's creation and return to His just judgment, receiving pardon through His grace rather than through self-awarded privilege.
