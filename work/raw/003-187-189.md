<<<VERSE 3:187>>>
> “˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when Allah took the covenant of those who were given the Scripture to make it known to people and not hide it, yet they cast it behind their backs and traded it for a fleeting gain. What a miserable profit” (3:187)

Allah reminds the People of Scripture of a covenant to make divine guidance clear and not conceal it. Some broke that trust, hiding what they knew for a small worldly gain. The verse warns anyone entrusted with religious knowledge against trading truth for status, money, or influence.

### Phrase by phrase

**“when Allah took the covenant of those who were given the Scripture”** refers to the responsibility placed on the learned among earlier religious communities. They had received revelation and were expected to preserve and explain it faithfully. Their knowledge was a trust, not a private possession to use only when it served their interests.

The covenant included making known the signs and description of the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ so that people could recognize the truth of his message. The verse addresses those who were expected to convey their scripture honestly. It does not claim that every Jew or Christian concealed knowledge, nor that every individual had equal access to the relevant texts.

**“to make it known to people and not hide it”** sets out the duty plainly. Religious knowledge should be taught clearly and not suppressed to mislead people. Concealment becomes especially serious when someone withholds a command or evidence for the sake of money, authority, or control over a community.

The warning also applies to later scholars and teachers. A person who knows a teaching has a responsibility to share it with care and accuracy. They should not hide it because it is inconvenient, unpopular, or threatens their position. The verse condemns intentional concealment for selfish ends.

This does not mean that every religious teaching must be broadcast in every setting without judgment. There may be circumstances where explaining a complex issue publicly would predictably create confusion or harm. Responsible teaching considers audience, timing, and the likelihood of misunderstanding. That is different from concealing truth to secure a personal advantage.

**“yet they cast it behind their backs”** describes their disregard for the promise. They treated the covenant as though it had no authority over them. The image conveys deliberate neglect: they did not merely forget an incidental detail, but pushed aside a responsibility they had accepted.

**“and traded it for a fleeting gain”** means they chose a temporary benefit instead of faithfulness to revelation. That gain could include money, influence, or a position of superiority through control of religious information. Whatever they received was small compared with the guidance and lasting reward they forfeited.

**“What a miserable profit”** evaluates the exchange. They may have gained something in the moment, but the purchase was ruinous. A short-term advantage cannot compensate for betraying a covenant with Allah.

### Knowledge as a trust

The verse does not condemn learning or the honour that comes from useful teaching. It condemns turning knowledge into a tool of manipulation. A teacher may earn a lawful living, but should not falsify or hide revelation to secure that income. A community may ask its scholars difficult questions, and scholars should answer honestly, acknowledge uncertainty, and avoid pretending to know what they do not.

A report from Ibn Abbas says that the Prophet ﷺ asked a group of Jews about something in the Torah. They concealed the truth and gave an answer contrary to what they knew, then left pleased with their deception. The report is transmitted by Al-Bukhari and Muslim, and also by At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa’i. It shows how the covenant was violated in a concrete exchange: knowledge was withheld in order to mislead the questioner.

The same principle warns Muslim scholars not to repeat such conduct. If a teacher knows a ruling or an ethical principle, deliberate concealment for a worldly benefit is a betrayal. If they do not know, they should say so. Honesty about the limits of one’s knowledge is better than inventing an answer that pleases an audience.

The verse also discourages people from demanding that religious teachers confirm whatever they already want to hear. A sincere student seeks truth even when it corrects them. The responsibility to disclose knowledge belongs to the teacher, while the responsibility to receive it honestly belongs to the listener.

### In short

- People entrusted with scripture were required to explain it faithfully.
- Concealing guidance for money, status, or influence is a betrayal of that trust.
- Responsible discretion is not the same as hiding truth for personal gain.

<<<VERSE 3:188>>>
> “Do not let those who rejoice in their misdeeds and love to take credit for what they have not done think they will escape torment. They will suffer a painful punishment” (3:188)

The verse warns people who take pleasure in wrongdoing and seek praise for good deeds they never performed. They should not imagine that public approval or a successful excuse will protect them from Allah’s judgment.

### Phrase by phrase

**“those who rejoice in their misdeeds”** describes people who are pleased with a wrong choice rather than remorseful or ready to correct it. Rejoicing here is not simple happiness after a good deed. It is satisfaction in deception, concealing truth, or avoiding responsibility while others bear the cost.

**“and love to take credit for what they have not done”** condemns a false claim to virtue. Some people avoid a difficult duty and then want others to praise their commitment. Others mislead people about what scripture says, conceal the truth, and still wish to be regarded as knowledgeable and faithful.

Reports explain the verse in two related settings. One connects it with people who hid knowledge and then congratulated themselves on the answer they had given. Another concerns hypocrites who stayed behind when the Prophet ﷺ went on an expedition, then offered excuses and wanted praise for supposed loyalty. Abu Saʿid al-Khudri explains that they were pleased when the Muslims suffered a setback because they had not joined the expedition, but wanted to share in the praise when the Muslims succeeded.

These accounts do not make every person who misses an expedition a hypocrite. Someone may have a genuine illness, disability, family duty, or other valid excuse. The warning concerns those who deliberately evade responsibility, lie about it, and then seek honour for conduct they did not show.

**“think they will escape torment”** rejects the assumption that a false story or public reputation can prevent accountability. People may deceive other people for a time, but Allah knows what happened and what was intended. The verse directs attention to the final judgment rather than the impression a person manages to create.

**“They will suffer a painful punishment”** makes the consequence explicit. This is a warning against deliberate dishonesty and self-congratulation in wrongdoing. It is not a command for believers to expose or punish people on the basis of suspicion. Allah’s judgment rests on complete knowledge, while human beings must use evidence and fairness.

### Deception, showing off, and a fair reputation

A person may hope that others recognize a good deed, or may feel pleased after accomplishing something beneficial. That ordinary desire is not the same as fabricating a deed or demanding praise for work not done. The verse targets deception: pretending to have obeyed, claiming knowledge one concealed, or presenting oneself as more faithful than one’s conduct shows.

The Prophet ﷺ warned against making a false claim to gain an advantage. A hadith in the Two Sahihs compares the person who claims what they have not done to someone wearing two garments of falsehood. The image suggests that the lie is not a single accidental mistake but a performance meant to create a false identity.

Ibn Abbas explained that the verse was revealed about the People of Scripture who hid the answer to a question and then rejoiced in the reply they had given. The same verse also addresses the hypocrites who excused themselves after staying behind from a military expedition. These settings share the same moral pattern: avoiding the truth, then seeking praise for the appearance of righteousness.

Marwan once asked Ibn Abbas whether the verse applied to everyone who enjoys a good deed and hopes to be praised. Ibn Abbas explained that its immediate setting concerned the People of Scripture who hid what they knew. Abu Saʿid al-Khudri similarly explained that the hypocrites who stayed behind were pleased when the Muslims were harmed but later wanted praise for loyalty they had not shown. These explanations prevent the verse from being stretched to condemn every natural happiness or every person who appreciates recognition.

The lesson for believers is to be truthful about what they have done, acknowledge the help they received, and accept correction when they fail. A person who missed a duty should explain honestly rather than invent an excuse. A teacher should not claim expertise they do not possess. A public figure should not take credit for the effort of others.

### In short

- The verse condemns delight in wrongdoing and false claims to good deeds.
- Its reported settings include concealing knowledge and hypocrites seeking praise after avoiding duty.
- Wanting fair recognition is not the same as lying about one’s actions.

<<<VERSE 3:189>>>
> “To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. And Allah is Most Capable of everything” (3:189)

This verse closes the passage by affirming that sovereignty over the heavens and earth belongs to Allah alone. He has power over all things, including the judgment of those who conceal truth or claim credit falsely.

### Phrase by phrase

**“To Allah alone belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth”** means that all creation and authority belong to Him. People may hold property, office, or influence for a time, but none of these makes them independent owners. Their authority is temporary and accountable.

The heavens and the earth include everything people can see and what lies beyond their perception. Allah owns the provisions and resources within creation. People are entrusted with their wealth and position, but they will return to Him and answer for how they used them.

**“And Allah is Most Capable of everything”** means no action or consequence is beyond His power. He can reward those who convey truth faithfully and punish those who conceal or distort it. He can bring the hidden record into view, even if a person’s deception succeeds in this world.

The verse also reminds the reader that Allah does not need the people whose conduct is criticized. The earlier language of a “loan” was an invitation to give, not evidence of need. Allah’s dominion is complete. People are the ones who need His guidance, mercy, and reward.

### In short

- Allah alone owns and governs the heavens and the earth.
- Human wealth, knowledge, and authority are temporary trusts.
- Nothing is beyond Allah’s power, and every hidden deed remains within His knowledge.
