<<<VERSE 2:234>>>
A widow is given a defined period of waiting, not a lifelong prohibition on remarriage: "let them observe a waiting period of four months and ten days." This rule applies to the nonpregnant widow whether or not the marriage was consummated. Pregnancy has its own rule. After the required period, she may prepare for marriage and marry lawfully. Her relatives are not blamed for allowing that freedom.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those of you who die and leave widows behind,"** The command concerns wives whose husbands die. Unlike the waiting rule after divorce, the general rule here includes an unconsummated marriage. The marriage bond itself carries rights that remain relevant at death.

**"let them observe a waiting period of four months and ten days."** This is the widow's 'iddah, the interval during which she does not remarry. Mourning also belongs to this period. The rule for a pregnant widow ends at childbirth, as explained in (65:4), rather than requiring her to wait the longer of two possible terms.

**"When they have reached the end of this period, then you are not accountable for what they decide for themselves in a reasonable manner."** Once the waiting period finishes, lawful preparation for marriage and remarriage are allowed. Az-Zuhri identifies the people addressed as the woman's wali, guardian. Another explanation includes the deceased husband's relatives. They must not treat permissible adornment or a lawful new marriage as an offence against the deceased.

**"And Allah is All-Aware of what you do"** Allah knows private and public conduct, good and evil. The end of the waiting period permits what was temporarily restricted, but does not remove the requirement that subsequent conduct remain lawful and honourable.

### What if the husband died before consummation?

Ibn Mas'ud was asked about a man who died before consummating his marriage and had not specified a mahr, marriage payment. After repeated questioning, he offered a judgment while acknowledging that any error was his own, not attributable to Allah or His Messenger ﷺ. He said the widow should receive a full payment comparable to that of women of her standing, without stinginess or extravagance. She also had a right to inheritance and was required to observe the waiting period.

Ma'qil bin Yasar Ashja'i then said he had heard the Prophet ﷺ give the same judgment concerning Barwa' bint Washiq. Another version describes several men from Ashja' testifying to that ruling. Ibn Mas'ud was delighted by the confirmation. Ahmad and the compilers of the Sunan record the account, and At-Tirmidhi grades it Sahih, authentic.

The case shows that an unconsummated marriage does not remove the death-related waiting period or erase the widow's claims. It must not be confused with the different rule for divorce before consummation.

### How does pregnancy change the period?

Subay'ah Al-Aslamiyah's husband, Sa'd bin Khawlah, died while she was pregnant. She gave birth a few nights later. After the postnatal bleeding, called nifas, had ended, she prepared herself for possible suitors. Abu Sanabil bin Ba'kak told her that she could not marry until four months and ten nights had passed.

She went to the Prophet ﷺ to ask. He explained that her waiting period had ended when she gave birth and allowed her to marry if she wished. The account appears through several chains in the Two Sahihs. It confirms the application of (65:4) to a pregnant widow, even where childbirth follows very soon after the death.

### Why is this length of time prescribed?

Sa'id bin Musayyib and Abu Al-'Aliyah connect the interval with making a possible pregnancy evident. The discussion brings in Ibn Mas'ud's report in the Two Sahihs describing development in the womb through three stages of forty days, followed by an angel's breathing life into the child. The additional ten days are connected with certainty when some months are shorter than thirty days.

This is the explanation given for the period, not permission to replace the prescribed rule with a private calculation about likely pregnancy. It also retains respect for the deceased, whose departure was not a chosen separation. An inward reading says that the earlier death-related period was a year before the rule became four months and ten days. Mourning honours the bond without requiring the survivor never to marry again.

A historical legal distinction is also mentioned. Ahmad records 'Amr bin Al-'As describing four months and ten nights for an enslaved mother when her owner dies. Another explanation gives an enslaved woman's period as half the full term under the Sunnah. These statements address differing legal descriptions and must not be silently reduced to one unqualified rule about every enslaved woman.

### What does mourning require?

The Two Sahihs contain reports from Umm Habibah and Zaynab bint Jahsh that a believing woman does not mourn another deceased person for more than three days, whereas she mourns her husband for four months and ten days. The restriction concerns the formal conduct of mourning, not a command that every feeling of sorrow disappear at a deadline.

Umm Salamah reports a woman asking whether kohl could be applied to her widowed daughter's painful eye. The Prophet ﷺ repeatedly refused in the account given, then contrasted four months and ten nights with the former practice of a whole year. The discussion lists restrictions on perfume, adornment, henna, unnecessary cosmetic treatment, hair oil and conspicuous dress intended to attract suitors.

A note on surma, the eye cosmetic, describes it as galena, a lead compound, and disputes translating it as antimony. Its further remark connects a decline in favour with confusion over antimony's use in explosives. This is the explanation attached to the cosmetic terminology in the material, not an added chemical assessment.

The account of pre-Islamic mourning is much harsher. Zaynab, Umm Salamah's daughter, describes a woman withdrawing for a year in her poorest clothes without perfume or adornment. At its end she threw dung and took part in an animal-related ritual described as often killing the animal. The prescribed period replaces such prolonged degradation with defined duties.

One explanation applies the mourning rule broadly to young and old widows, free and enslaved, Muslim and non-Muslim. The practical account also says that a widow should remain in her husband's home rather than spend nights elsewhere. It extends comparable restrictions to an irrevocably divorced woman and says she should not leave even by day except under severe necessity. Those are the particular legal instructions presented here, not a reason to invent further exceptions or conditions.

### How are the months counted?

If the husband dies at the beginning of a lunar month, the months are counted by the lunar calendar, whether they have twenty-nine or thirty days. If death occurs after that beginning, the calculation described here treats each month as thirty days. Four such months and ten days give 130 days. The period ends at the corresponding time of day at which the husband died.

This calculation belongs to the stated legal explanation. It should not be mixed with the pregnancy rule, whose end is childbirth.

### What freedom returns afterwards?

Ad-Dahhak and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas explain reaching the term as completing the waiting period. Ibn 'Abbas says that after divorce or a husband's death and the required wait, a woman may make herself presentable for marriage proposals. Muqatil bin Hayyan gives the same explanation. Mujahid understands the phrase about reasonable conduct as lawful, honourable marriage. Al-Hasan, As-Suddi and Az-Zuhri are also named with it.

The qualification matters: relatives are not blamed for lawful choices, but someone able to prevent an actual violation of Islamic law should not endorse it. Ma'ruf, recognised right conduct, includes observing the conditions of a valid marriage. It is not a blank permission for any arrangement after the period.

An account transmitted through Ishaq ibn Ibrahim al-Hanzali from Muqatil bin Hayyan describes a man from Ta'if dying in Madinah after arriving with parents, children and wife. The Prophet ﷺ made reasonable provision for the parents and children and directed that the wife be maintained from her husband's bequest for a year. The report concerns the earlier arrangement recalled in this discussion and is not substituted for the present four-month-and-ten-day rule.

The inward reflection acknowledges that grief and attachment change with time. Its poetic comparison sets fading sorrow beside faces decaying in the earth. The practical conclusion is that respect for a deceased spouse does not impose permanent exclusion from new companionship.

### In short

- A nonpregnant widow waits four months and ten days, including after an unconsummated marriage.
- A pregnant widow's waiting period ends at childbirth, as Subay'ah's report confirms.
- Mourning restricts adornment and remarriage for a defined period, not for the rest of life.
- After the period, guardians should allow lawful preparation for marriage and remarriage.

<<<VERSE 2:235>>>
The verse distinguishes restrained interest from a premature marriage commitment: "There is no blame on you for subtly showing interest in ˹divorced or widowed˺ women or for hiding ˹the intention˺ in your hearts." During the relevant waiting period, a man may give an appropriate hint or keep his wish private. He may not secure a secret pledge or contract the marriage before the period ends. Allah knows the heart, yet offers forgiveness to those who return from wrongdoing.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There is no blame on you for subtly showing interest in ˹divorced or widowed˺ women or for hiding ˹the intention˺ in your hearts."** The permission concerns indirect speech to a widow, and also to a woman whose divorce is final and irrevocable. It does not permit another man to propose, directly or indirectly, to a woman still within the waiting period of a revocable divorce. Her existing marriage remains capable of restoration.

**"Allah knows that you are considering them ˹for marriage˺."** The desire itself is known to Allah. The law recognises it rather than demanding that every thought of future marriage be erased. It allows a limited and honourable expression without removing the period's protection.

**"But do not make a secret commitment with them—you can only show interest in them appropriately."** A hint is not a concealed agreement that binds the woman to marry a particular man later. Taking a pledge that she will reject all others crosses the stated boundary. Another explanation also warns against promises concerning forbidden sexual relations.

**"Do not commit to the bond of marriage until the waiting period expires."** The marriage contract must not be concluded during the 'iddah, the waiting period. Marriage contracts made then are described as invalid by scholarly agreement. The discussion also explicitly forbids consummation before the period has ended.

**"Know that Allah is aware of what is in your hearts, so beware of Him."** Private purposes are not beyond judgment simply because public speech remains indirect. Intentions should be good, not plans to disrespect the deceased, evade the waiting rule or prepare unlawful conduct.

**"And know that Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing"** Allah forgives those who repent and does not rush to punish. The warning about His knowledge must not drive people to despair. His patience is an opportunity to return, not permission to continue disobedience.

### What kinds of hints are permitted?

Ibn 'Abbas describes general expressions of wishing to marry or hoping Allah will grant a suitable wife. Al-Bukhari records examples about desiring a wife or looking for a good one. Another version speaks of looking for someone with particular qualities. The speaker does not turn these comments into a direct proposal during the restricted period.

Other examples include saying that one would welcome a lawful union if Allah brought it about, or asking to be told when the waiting period has ended. Compliments that indicate admiration are also mentioned, such as wondering who could find someone like the woman. The permitted speech remains restrained and recognised as appropriate under the law.

Among the salaf, the early authorities, named with permission for an indirect hint to a widow are Mujahid, 'Ikrimah, Tawus, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Ash-Sha'bi, Al-Hasan and Qatadah. The discussion also names Yazid bin Qusayt, Az-Zuhri, Muqatil bin Hayyan and Al-Qasim bin Muhammad. Ath-Thawri, Shu'bah and Jarir are named in transmitting Ibn 'Abbas's explanation. The common point is a distinction between a hint and a direct or secretly binding arrangement.

### How does a final divorce differ from a revocable one?

A woman whose divorce cannot be revoked may receive the same sort of indirect hint. The account of Fatimah bint Qays illustrates this. She had received a third divorce from Abu 'Amr bin Hafs, her husband. The Prophet ﷺ directed her to observe her waiting period in Ibn Umm Maktum's house and asked her to inform him when it finished. Afterwards Usamah bin Zayd, the Prophet's ﷺ freed servant, sought to marry her, and the Prophet ﷺ arranged their marriage.

In a revocable divorce, no man other than the husband may make a direct or indirect proposal before the waiting period ends. The discussion states that there is no disagreement on this distinction. The broad wording about divorced women must therefore not be taken to cover every kind of divorce without qualification.

### What is a forbidden secret commitment?

Ibn 'Abbas gives examples of declaring love as part of a private undertaking or asking the woman to promise that she will marry no one else once the period ends. Sa'id bin Jubayr, 'Ikrimah, Ash-Sha'bi, Abu Ad-Duha, Ad-Dahhak, Az-Zuhri, Mujahid and Ath-Thawri likewise understand the prohibition as obtaining that exclusive pledge.

An honourable indication of interest leaves the legally restricted time intact. A hidden commitment tries to settle the marriage while avoiding its public form. The exception for appropriate speech cannot be used to permit precisely what the preceding words forbid.

Muhammad bin Sirin asked 'Ubaydah about the permitted expression. 'Ubaydah gave the example of a man asking the woman's wali, guardian, to inform him before arranging her marriage elsewhere. Ibn Abu Hatim transmits this explanation. It is presented as a recognised indication of interest, not a completed contract or a pledge extracted from the woman herself.

The inward application allows laying a lawful foundation for future companionship while forbidding sinful acts and preparation for them. Seeking affection does not make every means of seeking it acceptable.

### Why does the verse repeatedly mention the heart?

A man may keep his wish to himself without blame. Allah already knows it. The discussion connects this with (28:69), concerning what hearts conceal and people reveal, and (60:1), concerning Allah's knowledge of hidden and open conduct.

This knowledge also prevents a false distinction between an innocent-looking phrase and an unlawful purpose. The same words may be used with respect for the waiting period or as part of a plan to circumvent it. Allah knows the difference.

Marriage and divorce instructions repeatedly combine law with kindness, justice and fear of Allah. Carrying out their spirit requires more than compliance with the narrowest wording. On the Day of Judgment, no excuse can hide mistreatment. Goodwill towards other people must accompany restraint in one's own desire.

### Why must the bond wait?

The prescribed interval belongs to the earlier marriage and must be respected. In the widow's case, rushing into a new bond would disregard the deceased and the duties attached to his death. In the relevant divorce cases, the legal waiting period likewise cannot be removed by the couple's private willingness.

Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Ash-Sha'bi, Qatadah, Abu Malik, Zayd bin Aslam, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas, Muqatil bin Hayyan, Az-Zuhri, 'Ata' Al-Khurasani, As-Suddi, Ath-Thawri and Ad-Dahhak are named in explaining that the prescribed period must finish before consummation. The broader legal statement also forbids contracting the marriage before that end.

There is therefore space for thought and an appropriate hint, but not for a marriage that has already been privately secured. The closing assurance of forgiveness calls anyone who has crossed the boundary to repentance and warns against mistaking delayed punishment for approval.

### In short

- An appropriate hint is permitted to a widow or an irrevocably divorced woman during the waiting period.
- Another man may not propose to a revocably divorced wife during her waiting period.
- Secret pledges and premature marriage contracts are prohibited, even where future marriage is desired by both.
- Allah knows the hidden purpose, forgives repentance and grants time to return from wrongdoing.
