One group was known as the people of Ad, and the other group was known as the people of Thamud.

They made palaces in the open plains, and they were known for cutting living spaces and structures into the mountains and inside them. **"O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him"** is the call, and it is the call that every messenger from Adam to the last of them has given. What Salih asked of his people was that they should profess the divine oneness and turn from every other object of worship. The Book says it plainly, that among every ummah a messenger was raised to tell its people to worship Allah and to keep away from every false deity (16:36), and the same is put in another place (21:25). What Salih asked of his people is what the prophets before him asked of theirs, and the reason for it is that the community of human beings is one community in its basic articles of faith.

**"A clear proof has come to you from your Lord"** introduces the sign. It came about in a particular way, and the account of it belongs with the call, because the call was resisted and the demand was made.

**"this is Allah’s she-camel as a sign to you"** names the proof. The detail of this animal appears elsewhere in the Book, and its background is given in the reports. It was a great creature with a strikingly beautiful appearance, and it used to pass through their valleys by one pass and come out by another, since it needed to move easily, and it drank a lot of water. Salih began to preach when he was a young man, and he kept at his duty until the marks of age began to show on him, and still he did not give it up. His people grew impatient with his repeated efforts to bring them to belief in one God, and they decided to put a demand to him that he could not possibly meet, so that they would come out of the confrontation as the winners. What they asked for was that a she-camel should come out of Katibah, the hill of theirs, in its tenth month of pregnancy, strong and healthy.

The first thing Salih did was to take a pledge from them. He asked whether, if the demand were met, they would all believe in him and in his call, and they all entered into a compact with him on it. Then he prayed two cycles of salah and made his supplication, that nothing was difficult for Allah, and that their demand should be granted. Soon after the supplication there was a rumbling in the hill, a great rock burst open, and out of it came the she-camel as they had described her.

Some of the people believed at once when they saw this. As for the rest, they too made up their minds to enter the faith they had been refusing until then, but some of their chiefs, who were the real patrons of the worship of idols, talked them out of it.

In the text the animal is called the she-camel of Allah. That is because it stood as a proof of the perfect power of Allah and had come about in an extraordinary way as a sign of the prophet's truthfulness. The words about leaving it to graze on the earth of Allah carry the point that it was to be left free to eat and drink because what it would take was not the property of the people. The earth belonged to Allah, and He was the one who brought out what grew on it. So the animal was to be left to feed, unhindered, on the natural grazing of His earth, and was not to be harmed in any way, whether by hamstringing it or by striking it.

**"So leave her to graze ˹freely˺ on Allah’s land and do not harm her"** is the command that goes with the sign. When Salih saw that his people had broken the pledge, he was anxious for them, since the danger was that punishment would come on them for it. He advised them, out of care for them, not to touch the animal and to keep it safe, since in that case they might stay out of the reach of punishment, and otherwise they might be seized at once.

**"or else you will be overcome by a painful punishment"** is the warning that closes the verse, and the warning was not idle.

### The water and the turn

The well from which the people drew their water was the same one the she-camel came to. She was an extraordinary creature, and when she drank her share she took the whole of the water in it. So it was settled, as the divine will had it, that the animal would have the water on one day and the people of Thamud would have it on the next.

What happened on the day she drank was reported as part of the same arrangement. On that day the people were given milk from her instead of water, in such quantity that they filled their water containers with it.

The two turns are given in the Book in more than one place. In one passage the prophet is addressed and told that the water is to be divided between them, one day for her and one day for his people, and that the division will be watched, so that nobody goes against it (54:28). In another place the same camel is described as belonging to Allah, with one day of water fixed as her right and the other day fixed for the people (26:155).

### The journey that passed by their ruins

The report of the Tabuk journey is carried by Al-Bukhari, and by Ahmad in his collection. Abdullah bin Umar said that when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ travelled to the area of Al-Hijr in Tabuk with his people, he camped near the homes of Thamud, at Al-Hijr. The people brought water from the wells that Thamud had used before them, and they used that water to make their dough and set their pots on the fire to cook.

The Prophet ﷺ gave orders that the pots should be emptied and the dough given to the camels. Then he moved on with them from that place to another, near the well that the camel used to drink from, and he forbade the Companions from going into the area where the people had been tormented, and said that he feared what had befallen them might befall them too, so they should not go in upon them.

The same instruction is given in another report of the same journey. He told them that nobody should enter the land of the punished settlement, and that nobody should use the water of its wells.

### Where the story stands in the surah

The account of the people of Nuh and of Hud has gone before, and the same pattern runs on from here to the end of the surah. Prophets are sent to communities, they give the call of truth, the communities deny them, and a terrible end follows. The main subject of the remaining part of the surah is that sequence.

A general observation belongs to the story. Worldly wealth and high living tend to estrange people from the remembrance of their Creator and from the meeting with Him in the Hereafter, and to push them towards the ways of error, and that is what happened among the people of Thamud. The punishment of the flood that came on the people of Nuh had not been forgotten by the world of that time, and the destruction of their neighbours the people of Ad was current history for them. Wealth and power, however, pull people by a force of their own. When one wealthy structure comes down on its foundation, along come other people, or another group, who raise a higher structure on that same foundation, paying no attention at all to what happened there before. Their own families had come into the mansions and the lands of Ad once Ad was destroyed, and it was on the very spots where their kin had met destruction that they raised their own places of luxury. Not content with that, they went into the same deeds that the people of Ad had done. They too became careless of their Creator and unmindful of the Hereafter, and stooped to the worship of idols and to joining others to the divinity of Allah. So Allah, in the way He always deals with such people, sent Salih as His messenger to guide them back to the straight path.

A further observation comes from the shape of their story. After the destruction of the community of Ad, certain righteous people whom God had saved settled in the district of Hajr, in the north-west of Arabia. Their number grew, and they made great progress in farming and in building. They put up palaces on the plains and cut stone houses out of the hills. In time they fell into the evils that commonly follow on a community's material development and worldly comfort, which are debauchery, forgetfulness of the Hereafter, heedlessness about the limits God has laid down, self-conceit, and disregard for the greatness of God. At that point God raised up Salih to warn them of His chastisement, and they would not accept the warning and would not mend their ways.

In a universe where everything else lives in complete obedience to God, they chose to live in arrogance and to disobey Him. Where everything kept strictly to the limits set for it, man wanted to live by going past them. Their transgression created disorder in a world that had been set right, and so they were held unfit to live in it. Yet the trial given to them was not what they feared. God assigned them a she-camel and commanded them not to harm her, on pain of destruction. He could have assigned them a terrifying lion for the purpose, and He assigned a she-camel instead. The reason is that the test of a person's fear of God is taken at the level of the she-camel, and not at the level of the lion. In every society there are people who are like the camel of God. They are the weak, who have no material strength that could hold oppression in check. Through such people God tests the faith of a community, and the certificates of Paradise and of Hell are issued on the basis of how that test turns out.

- The tribe was descended from Thamud bin Athir, among the ancient Arabs, and their land lay between the Hijaz and Ash-Sham, with Hijr as their chief city and their ruins still standing when the Book was sent down.

Abdur-Razzaq narrated from Ma'mar, who had it from Isma'il bin Umayyah, that the Prophet ﷺ passed by the grave of Abu Righal and asked the Companions whether they knew whose grave it was. They said that Allah and His Messenger knew better. He told them that it was the grave of Abu Righal, a man of Thamud, who had been inside the sacred area of Allah and had been saved from His torment by that, and that when he left the sacred area what had befallen his people befell him as well, and that he had been buried here with a branch of gold. The people went at the grave with their swords, looking for the golden branch, and found it. Another report from Abdur-Razzaq, through Ma'mar from Az-Zuhri, says that Abu Righal is the ancestor of the tribe of Thaqif.

The reports of the Prophet ﷺ speaking in this way about a man long dead belong with the other report on the same subject, which is given in the entry for the next verse.

The account is also carried in a report of the journey to Tabuk, in the collection of Al-Bukhari, that the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions passed by Hijr, the place where the people of Thamud had met their punishment. There he gave the instruction that nobody should enter the land of the punished settlement, and that nobody should use the water of its wells. The reports of the same journey have been given already in this passage.

### What the place was left for

Allah in His wisdom allowed the settlements of the punished peoples to remain as signposts of warning for the generations that came after. The Book warns the people of Arabia again and again that the places which lay on their travel route to Syria should teach them the essential lesson of the rise and fall of peoples, and it says of those dwellings of the ungrateful that they were not lived in after their people, except a little (28:58).

### In short

- The earthquake seized them, and the Book describes them as left motionless on the spot where each of them stood.
- The earthquake and the cry came at the same hour, one from below and one from above.
- None of the people of Thamud remained except the prophet and those who had believed with him.
- Abu Righal was spared because he was inside the sacred area of Makkah, and was killed by a stone from the sky once he left it, and his grave and the golden branch buried with it are given in the reports.
- The Prophet ﷺ passed by Hijr on the way to Tabuk and forbade his Companions to enter the punished dwellings or to use their water.
- The settlements were left standing as signposts, so that later peoples would read the lesson of the rise and fall of nations on their own travel routes.

<<<VERSE 7:79>>>
The story closes with the prophet speaking over the people who are past hearing. "So he turned away from them, saying, “O my people! Surely I conveyed to you my Lord’s message and gave you ˹sincere˺ advice, but you do not like ˹sincere˺ advisors.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"So he turned away from them"** gives his leaving. After the punishment had come on his people, Salih and those who had believed with him left the place and went elsewhere. According to some narrations there were four thousand believers with him. He went with them to Hadramaut in Yaman, and it was there that he passed from this world. Others report that he went to Makkah, and that it was there he died.

**"O my people! Surely I conveyed to you my Lord’s message"** is the content of what he said as he left. He had delivered the message of his Lord to them, with its commands and its prohibitions explained.

**"and gave you ˹sincere˺ advice"** is the second part. He had warned them of the torment of Allah and called them to repentance and to faith, and he had wished them well.

**"but you do not like ˹sincere˺ advisors"** is the verdict he left with them. They did not listen to those who advised them, and they took no benefit from what he brought, because they did not love the truth and did not follow those who offered them sincere counsel.

These words are put as an admonition and a reproach addressed to a people who had already perished by the time they were spoken, and they heard him, which was a miracle granted to their prophet by Allah.

### The same manner of speech at Badr

What is recorded in the Two Sahihs gives the parallel. After the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had defeated the disbelievers at Badr, he stayed in that area for three days. Then he rode his camel, which had been made ready for him in the latter part of the night, and went on until he stood by the well of Badr, into which the bodies of the disbelievers had been thrown. He called out to Abu Jahl bin Hisham, and to Utbah bin Rabi'ah, and to Shaybah bin Rabi'ah, and asked whether they had found what their Lord had promised them of torment to be true, since he had found what his Lord had promised him of victory to be true.

Umar said to him, "O Messenger of Allah, why do you speak to people who have rotted?" He said, "By the One in whose hand my soul is, you do not hear what I am saying any better than they do, but they cannot reply."

So it was with Salih and his people. The words were spoken over them after they had perished, and the point of them is not that the dead should benefit but that the living should take the lesson.

### Why he addressed them when they were gone

A question is put against the outward arrangement of the text, since the people had been destroyed by the punishment and there is no use in addressing them afterwards. The answer is that one good may come out of it, which is that people in general should learn their lesson from it, and that this form of address resembles the address of the Prophet ﷺ when he said a few words over the dead disbelievers of Quraysh at Badr.

It is also possible that the saying of Salih belongs to the time before the punishment came and his people were destroyed, even though it stands later in the order of the text. From the outward arrangement, it appears that he said it as he was departing, telling them that he had delivered the message of his Lord and wished the best for them, but that they did not seem to like honest advisers.

### What the last words of the story show

The community that gives way to its desires is not moved by plain speech at all. It is not prepared to support anyone who calls it to serious and sincere action. On the contrary, great crowds gather around people who say pleasant things and trade in false promises. Such a community is not drawn to the true well-wishers, and it rushes instead towards those who will use it.

### In short

- Salih left his people after they had been destroyed, and he went with the believers to Hadramaut, or according to other reports to Makkah.
- What he said over them was that he had conveyed the message and given sincere counsel, and that they did not like sincere advisers.
- The same manner of speech was used by the Prophet ﷺ at the well of Badr, and Umar asked him why he spoke to those who had rotted.
- The address was not meant for the dead to benefit from it, but for the living to take the warning.
- The saying may also have come before the punishment, though it stands last in the order of the text.
- A community that gives itself to its desires turns away from serious counsel and gathers instead around those who flatter it.
