<<<VERSE 7:73>>>
After Nūḥ (Noah) and the people of ʿĀd comes the story of the Prophet Ṣāliḥ and his people, Thamūd: "And to the people of Thamûd We sent their brother Ṣâliḥ." He told them, "O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him. A clear proof has come to you from your Lord". The proof was a camel: "this is Allah’s she-camel as a sign to you. So leave her to graze ˹freely˺ on Allah’s land and do not harm her, or else you will be overcome by a painful punishment". Accounts like this one, of earlier prophets calling their peoples to the truth, the peoples' denial and the terrible results, remain the main subject up to the end of Sūrat al-Aʿrāf.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And to the people of Thamûd We sent their brother Ṣâliḥ."** In the Arabic, Thamūda is read without full declension, as the name of the tribe itself. Ṣāliḥ was their prophet. In lineage and homeland he was one of Thamūd, also descended from Sām, so the Qur'an calls him their brother. One explanation says he was their brother in lineage but not in religion.

**"He said, “O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him."** Declare Allah's oneness and worship Him alone. You have no god worthy of worship but the One I call you to believe in. Every messenger of Allah, sent to every ummah, called to the worship of Allah alone without partners, as Allah says in other āyāt:

> "We never sent a messenger before you ˹O Prophet˺ without revealing to him: “There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Me, so worship Me ˹alone˺.”" (21:25).

> "We surely sent a messenger to every community, saying, “Worship Allah and shun false gods.”" (16:36).

Ṣāliḥ's call to his people was no different from the call every messenger has made, from Ādam (Adam) to the last of them.

**"A clear proof has come to you from your Lord"**: a miracle from Allah testifying to the truth of what I have brought you. It is a plain sign of my truthfulness.

**"this is Allah’s she-camel as a sign to you."** In the Arabic, "a sign to you" describes the state of the camel, governed by the force of "this". She is called "Allah's she-camel" because she was a proof of Allah's perfect power. She appeared in an extraordinary way as a miracle of Ṣāliḥ, a sign of Allah's message. They had asked him to bring her out of a particular rock they had chosen.

**"So leave her to graze ˹freely˺ on Allah’s land"**: let her eat its grass unhindered. The words "Allah's land" show that she was to be left free to eat and drink. What she ate and drank did not belong to them. The earth was Allah's, and He created what grew in it.

**"and do not harm her"**: do not touch her with any harm, whether by hamstringing her or by beating her.

**"or else you will be overcome by a painful punishment"**: if you hurt her, a painful punishment will seize you.

### Who were Thamūd, and where did they live?

According to the scholars of tafsīr and genealogy, Thamūd's ancestor was a man of the same name whose line ran back through ʿĀthir, Iram and Sām to Nūḥ. He was the brother of Jadīs son of ʿĀthir, and the tribe of Ṭasm was related to them. They were among the ancient Arabs, al-ʿĀribah, who lived before the time of Ibrāhīm (Abraham), and Thamūd came after ʿĀd. As explained in the story of ʿĀd, ʿĀd and Thamūd were two men descended from the same grandfather. Their descendants took their names and became two separate peoples.

Thamūd lived in north-western Arabia, between the Ḥijāz and ash-Shām (Greater Syria). Their main city was called al-Ḥijr, known today as Madāʾin Ṣāliḥ. Like ʿĀd, they were wealthy, powerful, brave and artistically gifted, skilled in sculpture and architecture. They built palaces on open ground and carved dwellings and structures into the mountains. In his book ʿArḍ al-Qurʾān, Maulānā Sayyid Sulaimān Nadvī says their monuments still stand, carved with Iramic and Thamudic inscriptions.

After ʿĀd were destroyed, some righteous people whom Allah had saved settled in al-Ḥijr. Their numbers grew, and they made great progress in farming and building. But later they fell prey to the evils that usually follow material progress and comfort: debauchery, forgetfulness of the Hereafter, disregard for Allah's limits, self-conceit and contempt for Allah's greatness.

Wealth and luxury usually draw people away from the remembrance of their Creator and their final meeting with Him, and push them onto the paths of error. So it was with Thamūd. The world had not forgotten the Flood of Nūḥ, and for Thamūd the destruction of their kin, the people of ʿĀd, was within living memory. Yet riches and might exert their own pull. No sooner does one proud structure collapse than another person or group builds a higher one on the same foundation, ignoring what happened before. When ʿĀd were destroyed, Thamūd inherited their mansions and lands. They put up their places of luxury on the very spots where their kin had perished, and began doing the same deeds. They forgot their Creator and the Hereafter and sank into idol worship and shirk. So Allah, as is His constant way, sent Ṣāliḥ as His messenger to guide them to the straight path.

### What happened at al-Ḥijr?

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ passed by the land and ruins of Thamūd on his way to Tabūk, in northern Arabia, in the ninth year after the hijrah. Imam Aḥmad recorded from Ibn ʿUmar that when the Prophet ﷺ reached al-Ḥijr with the people, he camped near the homes of Thamūd. The people drew water from the wells Thamūd had used, made dough with it and set their cooking pots on the fire. The Prophet ﷺ ordered them to empty the pots and feed the dough to their camels. Then he led them to another spot, near the well from which the she-camel used to drink. He forbade his Companions to enter the place where people had been punished, saying he feared that what struck them might strike them too.

Aḥmad also recorded from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar that, in al-Ḥijr, the Prophet ﷺ told the people not to enter the dwellings of those who had been punished unless they were weeping. If they were not weeping, they should not go in, lest what struck those people strike them as well. The core of this hadith is in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs.

### How did the she-camel appear?

Ṣāliḥ began preaching as a young man and kept at it until signs of old age appeared on him, yet he did not give up. His people grew tired of his repeated calls to believe in One God. So they decided to make a demand they thought impossible to meet, so that they would win the contest. They chose a lone, solid rock in the region of al-Ḥijr called al-Katibah. If he really was Allah's messenger, they said, let him bring out of that rock a she-camel in the tenth month of pregnancy, strong and healthy.

First Ṣāliḥ took their pledge. If he met their demand, would they all believe in him and his call? When they had all given their word, he prayed two rakʿahs of ṣalāh. Then he called on Allah, saying that nothing was hard for Him and asking Him to grant their demand. Soon after the supplication the rock rumbled, moved and split apart. Out came a she-camel with a thick coat, pregnant, with her unborn young visibly moving inside her, exactly as they had asked.

At this astonishing miracle some believed at once, among them their chief, Jundaʿ ibn ʿAmr, and several who followed him. The other nobles of Thamūd wanted to believe too. Three men held them back: Rabbāb ibn Ṣumʿar ibn Jilhis, Dhuʾāb ibn ʿAmr ibn Labīd, and al-Ḥabbāb, the keeper of their idols. These chiefs were the real champions of idol worship. A cousin of Jundaʿ named Shihāb ibn Khalīfah ibn Mikhlāt ibn Labīd ibn Jawwās, one of Thamūd's leaders, also wanted to accept the message, but they talked him out of it and he gave in.

When Ṣāliḥ saw that his people had broken their pledge, he feared they would be punished for it. Out of concern for them he urged them not to harm the she-camel in any way and to protect her. Then they might stay safe. Otherwise punishment could seize them at once. That is what this verse says.

### How was the water shared?

The she-camel stayed among Thamūd, and so did the young she gave birth to before their eyes. She drank from the same well that Thamūd used, and because she was an extraordinary creature of Allah, she drank a lot of water, emptying the well. By Allah's will, Ṣāliḥ ruled that she would drink from the well one day and the people the next. On the days she drank, they milked her instead, and filled their water vessels with her milk. Allah refers to this division of water elsewhere:

> "And tell them that the ˹drinking˺ water must be divided between them ˹and her˺, each taking a turn to drink ˹every other day˺.”" (54:28).

The division was to be watched over by angels, so that no one would break it. Another verse says, "Here is a camel. She will have her turn to drink as you have yours, each on an appointed day" (26:155).

She grazed in some of their valleys, entering by one pass and leaving by another, so that she could move easily. She was a huge animal of striking beauty, and their cattle took fright whenever she passed.

### Why a she-camel and not a lion?

To test the people of Thamūd, Allah gave them a she-camel and commanded them not to harm her, or they would be destroyed. Allah could just as well have appointed a fearsome lion for the purpose. But He chose a she-camel. The reason is that a person's fear of Allah is always tested at the level of a "she-camel", never at the level of a "lion".

In every society there are people like Allah's she-camel. They are the weak, who have no worldly strength to deter anyone from wronging them. Through them Allah tests the true faith of His servants, and the certificates of Paradise and Hell are issued on the results of these tests. In a universe where everything lives in full obedience to Allah, Thamūd chose to live in arrogance and disobedience. Where everything keeps to the limits Allah has set, they wanted to live by overstepping them. Their transgression spread disorder in a world that had been set right, so they were judged unfit to live in it.

### In short

- Allah sent Ṣāliḥ, their brother by descent, to Thamūd of al-Ḥijr in north-western Arabia, with the same call as every messenger: worship Allah alone.
- At their demand, Allah brought a pregnant she-camel out of the rock al-Katibah as a clear sign. Yet their chiefs kept most of them from believing.
- The well was shared by turns, and they were warned that harming the camel would bring a painful punishment.
- At al-Ḥijr the Prophet ﷺ forbade using Thamūd's water and told the Companions to pass the ruins only in tears.
- Faith is tested by how people treat the weak, Allah's "she-camels", not the strong.

### Was their progress the problem?

Thamūd brought the art of building to perfection. They must have been well versed in related sciences such as mathematics, geometry and engineering, or such development would have been impossible. Yet they were not condemned for material progress. They were condemned for spreading corruption on earth. This shows that Allah does not forbid progress within lawful limits. But in the way he lives, a person should follow the balance that Allah has set throughout the universe.

### What rulings come from this verse?

Some basic and secondary rulings follow from this verse.

1. All the prophets agree on the fundamental articles of faith, and their codes of law are united in the same way. All of them call to tawḥīd, pure monotheism, as the basis of worshipping Allah. All of them warn that going against it brings punishment in this world and the next.
2. In earlier peoples too, the wealthy and the traditional holders of social standing refused the prophets' call. They were disgraced and destroyed in this world, and they deserve punishment in the Hereafter.
3. According to Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī, the verse shows that Allah's worldly blessings reach disbelievers too, as with ʿĀd and Thamūd, to whom He gave great wealth and power.
4. According to al-Qurṭubī again, the verse shows that palaces and mansions are blessings from Allah, and building them is permissible.

It is another matter that the noble prophets and the friends of Allah did not favour such buildings, because they make people heedless. The sayings of the Prophet ﷺ about tall buildings are of this kind.

### In short

- Ṣāliḥ reminded Thamūd that Allah made them successors to ʿĀd and gave them the skill to build palaces and carve homes into mountains.
- They were condemned not for their progress but for spreading corruption. Progress within Allah's limits is allowed.
- All prophets share one creed, and the wealthy and powerful have often been the first to reject them.
- Building palaces is permissible, though the prophets and the friends of Allah avoided them for the heedlessness they bring.

Thamūd split into two groups, and the verse records their exchange: "The arrogant chiefs of his people asked the lowly who believed among them, “Are you certain that Ṣâliḥ has been sent by his Lord?” They replied, “We certainly believe in what he has been sent with.”" One group had come to believe that Ṣāliḥ was a prophet, and the other denied and disbelieved.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The arrogant chiefs of his people"**: the leaders and important people who were too proud to believe in him.

**"asked the lowly who believed among them"**: literally "those who were made weak". They spoke to those they had overpowered and looked down on as weak, the believers among them. In the Arabic, "to such of them as believed" restates "to those who were made weak", repeating the preposition "to".

### Why "the arrogant" but "the weakened"?

Imam Rāzī says in Tafsīr Kabīr that the Qur'an names a quality of each group here, but in different forms. The disbelievers' quality is in the active voice: "who were arrogant". The believers' state is in the passive: "who were made weak". This shows that the disbelievers' arrogance was their own choice, blameworthy and the final cause of their punishment. The weakness and lowliness of the believers, on the other hand, was only how the disbelievers described them. It had nothing to do with the believers' real state, and nothing in it was blameworthy at all. Those to blame are the ones who called them weak and lowly, and treated them so, without any reason.

### What made the believers' answer so apt?

The well-known Tafsīr Kashshāf says it was a most eloquent answer. In effect they said: your whole debate, whether he is Allah's messenger or not, is not worth having. That is plain, settled and certain. Just as certain is that what he says is a message from Allah. If anything is worth discussing, it is who believes in him and who does not. As for us, praise be to Allah, we believe in every word of guidance he has brought.

A prophet usually appears in his own time as a controversial figure, not an established personality. No worldly glory surrounds him, and he holds none of the important positions of his day. So his contemporaries cannot see his prophethood and reject him. They find it hard to believe that the man they know as ordinary is the very one Allah has chosen to deliver His message.

The reply of Ṣāliḥ's companions shows the difference between the two groups. The deniers looked at Ṣāliḥ's person, while the believers looked at his message. The deniers saw no outward glory around him, so they ignored him. The believers saw the light of truth in his message, so they accepted his words at once and became his companions.

### In short

- The arrogant chiefs challenged the humble believers to say whether they were sure Ṣāliḥ was sent by Allah.
- The believers answered that his messengership was beyond debate. The only question was who believed, and they did.
- The Qur'an calls the disbelievers' arrogance their own choice, while the believers' "weakness" was only the label the arrogant gave them.
- The deniers judged the messenger's person and status, while the believers judged the truth of his message.

He asked the Companions whether they knew whose grave it was, and they said Allah and His Messenger knew best. He told them it was the grave of Abū Righāl, a man of Thamūd. Being in Allah's Sacred Precinct had saved him from the punishment, he said, but when he left it, what struck his people struck him. He had been buried with a branch made of gold. The people dug with their swords, looking for the golden branch, and found it. Some narrations call it a walking stick of gold. ʿAbd ar-Razzāq reported through Maʿmar that az-Zuhrī said Abū Righāl was the ancestor of the tribe of Thaqīf, the people of Ṭāʾif.

### How much of this story is certain?

The important parts of Thamūd's story are told in various surahs of the Qur'an itself, and some parts appear in hadith narrations. Other details were taken by commentators from Israelite narratives. But the truth of the event does not depend on those.

### In short

- After three days of yellow, red and black faces, a violent earthquake from below and a terrible cry from above destroyed Thamūd.
- They fell dead where they were, kneeling and motionless, and none escaped but Ṣāliḥ and the believers.
- Even as the signs came true, their hatred of Ṣāliḥ only grew, a mark of hearts turned upside down by Allah's wrath.
- Abū Righāl escaped while in the Ḥaram of Makkah, but was struck dead when he left it.

<<<VERSE 7:79>>>
After the punishment had fallen, Ṣāliḥ turned from his people: "So he turned away from them, saying, “O my people! Surely I conveyed to you my Lord’s message and gave you ˹sincere˺ advice, but you do not like ˹sincere˺ advisors.”" These were words of reproach and blame for a people Allah had destroyed for defying Him, rebelling against Him, refusing the truth, avoiding guidance and preferring error.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So he turned away from them"**: Ṣāliḥ turned his back on them, having given up all hope for them, and left them. One explanation says he left them before they were destroyed. After the punishment, Ṣāliḥ and those who believed in him left that place for somewhere else. Some narrations say four thousand believers went with him to Ḥaḍramawt in Yemen, where he died. Others say he went to Makkah and died there.

**"saying, “O my people! Surely I conveyed to you my Lord’s message"**: I delivered what Allah told me to deliver to you, explaining His commands and prohibitions.

**"and gave you ˹sincere˺ advice"**: I advised you well, encouraging and warning you. I warned you of Allah's punishment and called you to repent and believe.

**"but you do not like ˹sincere˺ advisors.”"** You did not benefit from my advice, because you do not love the truth and do not follow those who advise you sincerely, who want to lead you to good and away from evil. You do not listen to them.

### Why speak to people who were already dead?

The wording suggests that Ṣāliḥ spoke to his people as he was leaving, after they were destroyed. What use was it to address them then? One benefit is that people in general would learn the lesson. Some say his destroyed people heard him, as a miracle Allah granted to Ṣāliḥ.

The Prophet ﷺ did something similar. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs report that after defeating the disbelievers at Badr he stayed three days, then rode out late at night to the well where their corpses lay. He called out to the dead leaders of Quraysh by name, Abū Jahl ibn Hishām among them, asking whether they had found their Lord's promise true, as he had found his. When ʿUmar asked why he spoke to bodies that had rotted, he swore that they heard him as well as ʿUmar did, though they could not reply.

It is also possible that Ṣāliḥ said these words before the punishment came and his people were destroyed, even though the text places them afterwards.

### Why were the ruins left standing?

On the Tabūk journey, as al-Bukhārī records, the Prophet ﷺ told his Companions not to enter that stricken land and not to use water from its wells. In His infinite wisdom, Allah has let the dwellings of punished peoples stand as signposts of warning for later generations. The Qur'an repeatedly told the people of Arabia that these places, which lay on their trade route to Syria, should drive home the lesson of how nations rise and fall:

> "˹Imagine˺ how many societies We have destroyed that had been spoiled by their ˹comfortable˺ living! Those are their residences, never inhabited after them except passingly" (28:58).

### Why do people turn from sincere advisers?

A community that gives in to its desires is not moved by honest talk. It will not stand behind anyone who calls it to serious and sincere effort. Instead, huge crowds gather around those who speak pleasant words and deal in false promises. People are not drawn to their true well-wishers. They rush toward those who exploit them.

### In short

- Ṣāliḥ left his destroyed people, reminding them that he had delivered Allah's message and advised them sincerely.
- He and the believers moved on, to Ḥaḍramawt by most reports, or to Makkah.
- Like the Prophet ﷺ at Badr, he addressed the dead so that the living might learn.
- The ruins of punished peoples remain as warnings, and people who follow their desires prefer flatterers to sincere advisers.
