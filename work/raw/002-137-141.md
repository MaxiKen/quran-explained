<<<VERSE 2:137>>>
From the start of Sūrah al-Baqarah up to this point, verse after verse has explained the nature and essence of īmān (faith), sometimes briefly and sometimes at length. Verse 137 defines it in one short phrase. "So if they believe in what you believe, then they will indeed be ˹rightly˺ guided. But if they turn away, they are simply opposed ˹to the truth˺. But Allah will spare you their evil. For He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing". The phrase is simple, yet so comprehensive that every possible detail and explanation is already contained in it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So if they believe in what you believe"**. If the disbelievers among the People of the Book, and the other disbelievers, believe in all of Allah's Books and messengers without differentiating between any of them. The Jews and the Christians, that is, can find guidance only if they believe just as you believe, in all the prophets and their Scriptures. The word "like" in the Arabic ("the like of what you believe") is for emphasis.

**"then they will indeed be ˹rightly˺ guided."**. They will have grasped the truth and been directed to it, guided out of error by following the religion of Muḥammad ﷺ and Ibrāhīm (Abraham), the way of life that Allah is pleased with.

**"But if they turn away"**. From truth to falsehood after the proof has been set before them, refusing to believe in all the prophets or in some of them.

**"they are simply opposed ˹to the truth˺."**. They are in schism, in conflict with the true religion and in opposition to you.

**"But Allah will spare you their evil."**. Allah will protect you, O Prophet, from their harm and give the believers victory over them. He will lift their burden from you by their being killed and expelled. Allah did suffice him against them, by the killing of Qurayẓah, the expulsion of Naḍīr and the taking of the jizyah from them. The verse points out that the enemies of Islam may still remain unconvinced out of sheer obstinacy and malice, and asks the Prophet ﷺ not to be sad about them, since Allah Himself will deal with them and keep His Prophet safe. The promise is made more explicitly elsewhere: "Allah will ˹certainly˺ protect you from the people" (5:67), and later events showed it fulfilled.

**"For He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing"**. He hears what they say and knows their circumstances, their intentions, their actions and the punishment that awaits them.

### The faith of the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions as the standard

The immediate addressees of "you believe" are the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions. In matters of belief above all, their īmān is thereby set before us as the model. The verse is in effect a divine command laying down a fundamental principle: the only īmān acceptable to Allah is the one the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions held, and beliefs that deviate from it in the slightest are neither valid nor acceptable to Him. One must believe in Allah and His attributes, in the angels, in His Books, in His messengers and prophets and what they taught, exactly as the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions did. Nothing may be added or taken away on one's own account, and no private interpretation may distort the authentic meaning of these doctrines. Nor may one assign to the angels or the prophets a rank higher or lower than the one given them by the Prophet's ﷺ word or deed. One's faith must also be sincere and pure, since the opposite would be nifāq (hypocrisy).

This helps us see in true proportion the position of the heterodox sects among the Muslims, who make tall claims about the genuineness of their faith but lack faith in the full sense. The idolaters of Arabia, after all, proclaimed the authenticity of their faith, as the Jews and Christians still do, and as heretics have done in every age. Because their beliefs about Allah, the prophets, the angels and the Day of Judgement did not conform to the Prophet's ﷺ faith, Allah rejected them out of hand. Some of the Arab idolaters denied the very existence of angels, while others took them for daughters of God. Among the Jews, some groups would not obey the prophets and hated them so much that they murdered several of them, while other Jewish and Christian groups revered the prophets so extravagantly that they identified them with God, set them on His level or called them His sons. These two attitudes are the two extremes of deviation, and this verse shows both to be forms of misguidance.

Under the Sharīʿah every Muslim is bound to respect and love the Prophet ﷺ, and whoever falls short in this does not possess faith in the true sense. All the same, it must be clearly understood that making him Allah's equal in an attribute like knowledge or power is misguidance and shirk (associating partners with Allah). According to the Qur'an, shirk in its essence is to make someone besides Allah His equal in a divine attribute, as the idolaters confess in Hell: "when we made you equal to the Lord of all worlds" (26:98). Some Muslims consider the Prophet ﷺ all-knowing and present everywhere like Allah Himself, and congratulate themselves on showing the respect and love required of a Muslim, while in fact they disobey him and go against his teaching. They should learn from this verse that the respect and love Allah demands for His Prophet ﷺ is exactly the kind his Companions had, neither more nor less, for either would be a deviation and a sin.

At the other extreme are people who deny the plain and unambiguous declaration of the Qur'an that Muḥammad ﷺ is the last of all the prophets, and who try to make room for a new prophet. To serve this purpose they have manufactured exotic kinds of prophethood out of their own fantasy and given them fanciful names such as burūz (incarnation) and ẓill (manifestation). This verse exposes that fraud too, because the faith of the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions shows no trace of belief in prophets of this kind, and anyone who professes such a belief is an open heretic.

Then there are people whose minds are so fogged by modern materialism and so-called rationalism that they struggle to accept the idea of the other world and what belongs to it. They then subject these matters to crooked interpretations, which they suppose make Islam more acceptable to the modern mind and so count as a great service to it. Insofar as such interpretations break the rule indicated here, in that they do not conform to the faith of the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions, they are wholly false and must be rejected. A Muslim is obliged to believe without demur in whatever the Qur'an and hadith tell us about the other world. It is quite inadmissible, for instance, to hold that on the Day of Judgement people will be raised only "spiritually" and not bodily, that the reward and punishment of the next world will be "spiritual" rather than physical, or that the "weighing of deeds" is merely a metaphor. All such interpretations are doctrinally false and unacceptable to Allah, as this verse establishes.

### Belief in truth for its own sake

What was so special about the Companions' faith was that it amounted to accepting the truth at an abstract level, belief in truth for the sake of truth. That had become rare in a society which revered the words of the ancient prophets and treated as truth whatever centuries of tradition had sanctified. Continued belief in such "truth", embellished with myth and legend, had become a matter of national pride, and to view it in any other light would have meant denying a great legacy from the past. Unlike the ancient prophets, great figures celebrated in their nations' histories, the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ came fresh to the world, with no accumulated tradition behind him to give his words weight. In his time truth stood on its own, with neither history nor tradition to support it, so those who accepted it did so purely and simply because it was true. Belief of this kind is what Allah recognises and accepts, and only faith of this strength and purity is acceptable to Him.

In the devotional reading the verse is an address to the Prophet ﷺ, the master of east and west and the messenger to men and jinn: We have laid these tasks on your path and commanded the world's people to follow you. We have written the Compact of Love for those who serve you and brought them under Our gaze. We have thrown your opponents down into the lowland of abasement. Whoever opposes you stands with the enemies, and whoever serves you stands with the friends. If someone wants you, We want him and let him reach Us, and if someone turns away, We burn him and cast him down: "Whoever obeys the Messenger has truly obeyed Allah" (4:80). So do not let your heart grow tight at these estranged people's turning away and speaking ill. We will take care of their business and remove their torment from you.

### In short

- Guidance for the People of the Book lies in believing exactly as the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions believed, in all of Allah's Books and messengers without distinction. Anything else is schism.
- Their faith is the binding standard for belief in Allah, the angels, the Books, the prophets and the Hereafter, ruling out both those who belittle the prophets and those who raise the Prophet ﷺ to Allah's level (26:98), those who invent new kinds of prophethood, and those who explain away the realities of the next world.
- The Companions believed in truth for its own sake, without the support of tradition, which is why their faith is the model.
- Allah promised to protect His Prophet ﷺ from his opponents (5:67, 4:80), and He did.

<<<VERSE 2:138>>>
Verse 138 gives Islam a striking name. "This is the ˹natural˺ Way of Allah. And who is better than Allah in ordaining a way? And we worship ˹none but˺ Him". The Arabic word is ṣibghah, a dye or colouring: "the colouring of Allah". The verse presents Islam as the colouring of Allah and explains that colouring as unalloyed worship of Him and total submission to Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is the ˹natural˺ Way of Allah."**. Ibn ʿAbbās, as aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reports, explained "the ṣibghah of Allah" as the religion of Allah, and the same explanation is given by Mujāhid, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ʿIkrimah, Ibrāhīm, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, ʿAṭiyyah al-ʿAwfī, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, as-Suddī and others. Grammatically the word restates the earlier "we believe", as if to say, "Allah has dyed us". It denotes His religion, the one He made human beings naturally inclined to, which leaves its mark on a person just as a dye leaves its mark on a garment. The believers are told to hold tightly to this religion, their natural way of life, inside and out. The Qur'an calls it "the natural Way of Allah which He has instilled in ˹all˺ people" (30:30), and directs Muslims to hold to it.

**"And who is better than Allah in ordaining a way?"**. Who has a better colouring than Allah? No one. No way of life is better than Allah's path. It agrees with our nature, benefits us in every way and protects us from evil and corruption.

**"And we worship ˹none but˺ Him"**. We are monotheists. We acknowledge that we worship Him, declare that there is only one true God, and worship nothing apart from Him.

### Why "colouring"?

Verse 135 identified Islam with the religion of Ibrāhīm (Abraham). Put verses 135 and 138 together and it becomes clear that Islam, or any authentic religion for that matter, is essentially the religion of Allah, and that linking a religion to a prophet's name can only be symbolic.

The expression carries several levels of meaning. The immediate allusion is to a Christian ceremony. On the seventh day after birth they would bathe an infant in coloured water, probably yellow, as a substitute for circumcision, supposed to guarantee the child's outward and inward purification, a fast and indelible "colouring" of Christian faith, so to speak. The verse suggests that this colour washes away with the water, leaving no trace outside or inside, and that such a baptism neither serves the purpose of circumcision nor cleanses a person of physical impurity. The only colouring worth the name is that of a genuine, unabrogated religion, Islam, the one colouring that guarantees physical and spiritual purification and the only one that will last.

The word has a deeper meaning too. Just as a colour is plainly visible to anyone who looks, so the signs of genuine, pure faith should shine through a Muslim's face, movements, habits and behaviour. In this sense the verse commands Muslims to dye themselves, outwardly and inwardly, in Allah's colouring: to offer unalloyed worship to Him alone, to submit totally to His commandments and to accept His will gladly.

### The colour that is colourlessness

In the devotional reading the verse says: We will bring a people who have come out in the colour of tawḥīd (Allah's oneness), adorned with the attribute of love and clothed in Allah's colour, which is colourlessness. Whoever is pure of the colour of the colour-mixers is coloured with Allah's colour. He who has painted a thousand worlds with colour, why would He buy my colour or yours, O bankrupt man? When someone reaches Allah's colour and falls back on Him, He colours him in His own colour, just as the elixir turns copper and iron to its own colour and makes them precious. Should the estranged fall back on Him, they become His familiars, and should the disobedient fall back, they become obedient.

Many stories of the shaykhs are told on this point. Ibrāhīm al-Khawāṣṣ related that he once went into the desert and met a Christian with his sash round his waist, who asked to travel with him. They walked for seven days. Then the Christian said: O monk of the unswerving folk, bring out what you have of expansiveness, for we are hungry. Ibrāhīm prayed, "O Allah, do not disgrace me before this unbeliever," and saw a tray with bread, roast meat, dates and a pitcher of water. They ate and drank and walked another seven days. Then Ibrāhīm, growing hasty, said: O monk of the Christians, now bring out what you have, since my turn is over. The man leaned on his staff and supplicated, and there appeared two trays holding double what Ibrāhīm's tray had held. Bewildered and upset, Ibrāhīm refused to eat. The man insisted, and finally said: eat, for I have two pieces of good news for you. The first is that I bear witness that there is no god but Allah and that Muḥammad ﷺ is Allah's messenger, and he took off the sash. The second is that I said, "O Allah, if there is any danger here, let me begin with this," and so he began. They ate, walked on, performed the Hajj and lived in Makkah for a year. Then the man died and was buried in al-Baṭḥāʾ, may Allah have mercy on him.

### In short

- The "colouring of Allah" is His religion, Islam, the natural disposition He instilled in people (30:30), which marks a person as a dye marks cloth.
- Set against the Christian baptismal "colouring" that washes away, it is the only colouring that truly purifies and lasts, and Muslims are commanded to let it show in their whole conduct.
- "We worship none but Him" is the declaration of pure monotheism, and in the devotional reading Allah's colour is colourlessness, which can turn even a stranger into a familiar, as the story of the Christian traveller shows.

<<<VERSE 2:139>>>
Verses 139 to 141 close the section of the surah that has refuted certain claims of the Jews and the Christians. "Say, “Would you dispute with us about Allah, while He is our Lord and your Lord? We are accountable for our deeds and you for yours. And we are devoted to Him ˹alone˺". The claims in question were that Ibrāhīm (Abraham), Ismāʿīl (Ishmael), Isḥāq (Isaac), Yaʿqūb (Jacob) and the prophets of his line were Jews or Christians, and that the Jews and Christians were the chosen people who alone would go straight to Paradise, a privilege denied to the Muslims. Earlier verses showed that all these prophets followed Islam in the general sense of the word, that the earlier Sharīʿahs are now abrogated, and that the title "Islam" has been specially given to the religion of Muḥammad ﷺ. Allah directs His Prophet ﷺ here to pre-empt the arguments of his opponents.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, “Would you dispute with us about Allah"**. The occasion is recorded. The Jews told the Muslims: we are the people of the first Book, our qiblah is the more ancient one, and no prophet was ever sent from the Arabs, so had Muḥammad been a prophet he would have been one of us. The answer begins: do you argue with us about the religion of Allah, about His oneness, about obedience and submission to Him and keeping away from what He forbids, and about His choosing a prophet from among the Arabs? The Arabic question form expresses rejection. If the People of the Book argue that they have more right to Allah and His religion because their way of life is older and their Scriptures came first, it will do them no good.

**"while He is our Lord and your Lord?"**. He has full control over us and over you, and He alone deserves worship, without partners. It is for Him to choose whom He wills. Allah is the Lord of everyone, not of one group alone. Being the Lord of all, He cannot show special favour to one particular set of His creatures. On the Day of Judgement He will assess Jews, Christians and Muslims alike by what each believed and how each behaved, a principle the People of the Book themselves accepted.

**"We are accountable for our deeds and you for yours."**. We have our religion and you have yours. We will be requited for our deeds and you for yours, so it is hardly improbable that some of our deeds will earn us honour. You will not be asked about our actions nor we about yours, and everyone will be rewarded according to what he has done. The sense is also: we disown you and whatever you worship, as you disown us. Allah said elsewhere, "If they deny you, then say, “My deeds are mine and your deeds are yours. You are free of what I do and I am free of what you do!”" (10:41), and, "So if they argue with you ˹O Prophet˺, say, “I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers.”" (3:20). Of Ibrāhīm He said, "And his people argued with him. He responded, “Are you arguing with me about Allah, while He has guided me?" (6:80), and He tells of the king who argued with Ibrāhīm about his Lord because Allah had given him kingship, until "the disbeliever was dumbstruck" (2:258).

**"And we are devoted to Him ˹alone˺"**. Sincere to Him in worship and submission, in religion and in deed, unlike you, so we are more worthy to be chosen. We acknowledge that we worship Him and that there is only one true God, doing as He instructs and worshipping nothing besides Him. The Muslims are thus told to announce that they recognise no god but Allah and have purified their religion of every trace of shirk (associating partners with Allah). The Jews and Christians, by contrast, hold ʿUzayr (Ezra) and ʿĪsā (Jesus) respectively to be "the Son of God", and their religions have in any case been abrogated. Here at least Muslims stand above them.

### Ikhlāṣ, the mark of this Ummah

This verse brings out the essential and distinctive characteristic of the Islamic Ummah: it has purified itself of every possible admixture of shirk and devoted itself, outwardly and inwardly, to Allah. The Arabic word is mukhliṣūn, plural of mukhliṣ, "one who has purified himself", related to ikhlāṣ, purifying oneself. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr defined ikhlāṣ as worshipping Allah alone, associating no one with Him, and doing good deeds solely to obey Him rather than to win people's admiration. Certain spiritual masters have said that ikhlāṣ is a deed that neither men, nor angels, nor Satan can identify, a secret between Allah and His servant.

Ikhlāṣ is usually translated "sincerity", and mukhliṣ "sincere", but it is doubtful whether "sincerity" ever carried the full range of the Arabic word in any Western language. In current usage, moreover, the sense of "sincerity" has become not merely perverted but at times inverted. The word used to imply harmony between outward action and inner inclination, with the tacit assumption that the action was at least socially acceptable to some degree. As used today it suggests compliance with one's emotions, or even one's instincts, so that "sincerity" is invoked to justify fornication or even murder. Such an idea is the exact opposite of ikhlāṣ. One cannot reach even the lowest degree of ikhlāṣ without a clear intention to obey the injunctions of the Sharīʿah rather than be led by instinctual urges or emotional inclinations, whereas the fashionable "sincerity" requires one to ignore the Sharīʿah, and even worldly considerations, and obey the impulse of the moment, reducing a human being to an automaton at the mercy of his reflexes.

There is a further, related notion of "sincerity" which has troubled the peace of many pious people in the past and has become devastating in our own days. It demands fixity and unbroken continuity in an emotional state, which is simply not possible for human beings as they are made. Some pious people, once they begin seeking this kind of "sincerity" in their prescribed prayers, find they cannot keep up unbroken concentration, and are so frightened by the lapse that they sometimes give up praying altogether, believing such worship "insincere" and therefore invalid. Let it be clear once and for all that the Sharīʿah requires only that we have the correct intention and attitude when we begin our prayer or any other good deed. That alone is the prerequisite of ikhlāṣ, which in any case is not a matter of emotions and feelings. Islam asks us to perfect the quality of ikhlāṣ as the Sharīʿah defines it, not to chase "sincerity" in the Western sense, ancient or modern. For a fuller treatment see Tarbiyat al-Sālik by Maulānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānavī.

In the devotional reading: how can dispute with strangers be fitting? They are covered by absence and shaded by concealment, while unveiling and the bright noon of witnessing light up the friends. When is the state of one made bankrupt by his absence equal to that of one whose selection, sincere devotion and immersion in acts of drawing near have already been decreed? It is preposterous. There is no equality.

### In short

- The Jews argued that their Book, their qiblah and their prophets were older, so no Arab could be a prophet. The answer is that Allah is the Lord of everyone and chooses whom He wills, and that each party will answer only for its own deeds (10:41, 3:20), as Ibrāhīm himself answered those who argued with him (6:80, 2:258).
- The Muslims declare that they are devoted to Allah alone, free of the shirk of calling ʿUzayr or ʿĪsā the son of God.
- Ikhlāṣ, the Ummah's distinctive mark, means worshipping Allah alone and acting only to obey Him. It is a matter of correct intention under the Sharīʿah, not of emotional "sincerity" or unbroken concentration.

<<<VERSE 2:140>>>
Verse 140 turns the People of the Book's claim about the patriarchs into a question they cannot answer. "Do you claim that Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants were all Jews or Christians?” Say, “Who is more knowledgeable: you or Allah?” Who does more wrong than those who hide the testimony they received from Allah? And Allah is never unaware of what you do". Allah criticises them for claiming that Ibrāhīm (Abraham), the prophets who came after him and the Tribes followed their religion, whether Judaism or Christianity.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do you claim that Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants were all Jews or Christians?”"**. The Arabic "or" at the start carries the force of "nay". The verb is read both "do you say" and "do they say". The question is put to the Jews and the Christians: do you say that Ibrāhīm, Ismāʿīl (Ishmael), Isḥāq (Isaac), Yaʿqūb (Jacob) and the children of Yaʿqūb, the prophets of his descendants, were Jews or Christians, as you claim?

**"Say, “Who is more knowledgeable: you or Allah?”"**. Do you know their religion better, or does Allah? Allah knows best, and He has stated that they were neither Jews nor Christians: "Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian; he submitted in all uprightness and was not a polytheist" (3:67). By this He dissociated Ibrāhīm from both groups, and those named with him were his followers in belonging to neither. If the People of the Book claim they were Jews or Christians, the claim is false, because these prophets had been sent, and had died, before the Torah and the Gospel came down. So their claim is a lie against Allah and against His Prophet ﷺ, and it shows that they have hidden the truth of what was revealed to them. If, on the strength of their link with the earlier prophets, the People of the Book still insist on their own rectitude, the Muslims may put this basic question to them: who knows the truth better, Allah or they? In the Last Revelation Allah has announced the truth finally and definitively, and the People of the Book know themselves that the earlier prophets' religion was Islam.

**"Who does more wrong than those who hide the testimony they received from Allah?"**. No one is more unjust in his disbelief, or more insolent and daring towards Allah, than one who conceals from people a testimony he received from Allah. These are the Jews, who concealed Allah's testimony in the Torah about Ibrāhīm's pure faith and about the prophethood of this Prophet ﷺ. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said that they used to recite the Book Allah sent them, which stated that Islam is the true religion and Muḥammad ﷺ Allah's Messenger, and that Ibrāhīm, Ismāʿīl, Isḥāq, Yaʿqūb and the Tribes belonged neither to the Jews nor to the Christians. They bore witness to these facts and yet hid them from people. No one does greater wrong than someone who hides firm evidence he has from Allah, as some of the People of the Book did. They were trying to conceal the truth, and were being unjust in the gravest sense of the term.

**"And Allah is never unaware of what you do"**. He is not heedless of your concealing your testimony. This is a threat and a warning: His knowledge encompasses everyone's deeds, and He will reward each accordingly. He will judge them by their own deeds, not by the deeds of their ancestors.

### Measuring the prophets by oneself

In the devotional reading, whoever looks out from himself at creation forms an image of each thing from his own character and takes everything to be something like himself. When these people, being outsiders to the Real, judged the prophets by their own state, the Real threw their conjecture back on them and left them to their own opinion. Are those drawn away from their own testimony like those whom it veils? Is the one snatched away from all of it equal to the one thrown back on what resembles himself? That is the conjecture of those who disbelieve, and what misery is theirs!

### In short

- The People of the Book claimed the patriarchs as Jews or Christians. Allah, who knows better, has declared Ibrāhīm neither (3:67), and these prophets lived and died before the Torah and the Gospel existed.
- Their scholars knew from their own Book that the true religion is Islam, that Muḥammad ﷺ is Allah's Messenger and that the patriarchs belonged to neither sect, yet they hid this testimony, the gravest injustice.
- Allah is aware of the concealment and will judge each by his own deeds.

<<<VERSE 2:141>>>
The section ends by repeating, word for word, the warning given in verse 134. "That was a community that had already gone before. For them is what they earned and for you is what you have earned. And you will not be accountable for what they have done". At the close of this long passage against the claims of the Jews and the Christians, the verse warns them of the consequences of their vanity and pretension, and advises them to look after themselves rather than rely on ancestral glory.

### Phrase by phrase

**"That was a community that had already gone before."**. A group of people who existed before you and have passed away.

**"For them is what they earned and for you is what you have earned."**. They have what they earned of good, and you have what you earn of good. They bear their deeds and you bear yours. They earned for themselves by their own actions, and you earn by yours.

**"And you will not be accountable for what they have done"**. On the Day of Judgement you will not be asked about what they did in this world, and they will not be asked about what you do. Being their relatives will not be enough for you unless you imitate their good deeds. Do not be deceived by being their descendants unless you follow them in obeying Allah's commands and in following the messengers He sent as warners and bringers of good news. Whoever disbelieves in even one prophet has disbelieved in all the messengers, above all if he rejects the master and final Messenger sent by Allah, the Lord of the worlds, to all mankind and the jinn. No one is held accountable for another's disobedience, and no one benefits from another's deeds. Each person receives his reward according to what he himself did. Allah knows what the People of the Book have been doing, and He will judge them by their own deeds, not those of their ancestors.

### A repetition with a purpose

That the same verse appears twice in the space of a few lines is a sign of how central its principle is. Verse 134 closed the account of Ibrāhīm (Abraham), Yaʿqūb (Jacob) and his sons. Here it closes the argument with those who claimed these prophets as their own while refusing the religion the prophets actually taught. In both places the point is the same: a nation's record belongs to that nation, and no later generation can draw on it as capital.

In the devotional reading, fate's barriers came between you and them. They built their structures on separation and heedlessness, whereas you have pitched your tents on closeness and connection. The one freed by Our favour is not like the one driven out by Our force.

### In short

- Ibrāhīm and his family have gone with their own deeds, and the People of the Book, like everyone else, will be judged only by theirs.
- Descent from the prophets counts for nothing without following them in obedience, and rejecting even one messenger, let alone the final one, is to reject them all.
- The verse repeats 134 to seal the refutation of ancestral pride.
