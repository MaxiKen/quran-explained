<<<VERSE 6:80>>>
"And his people argued with him. He responded, “Are you arguing with me about Allah, while He has guided me? I am not afraid of whatever ˹idols˺ you associate with Him—˹none can harm me,˺ unless my Lord so wills. My Lord encompasses everything in ˹His˺ knowledge. Will you not be mindful”"

The debate that began with a star now turns into a stand-off, and the verse records what Ibrahim says when he is threatened.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And his people argued with him"** The argument is about their gods, and it comes with a threat attached, and the threat is meant to push him out of the religion of Allah. The shape of the threat is specific: they feared that as a result of this insolence he would be punished by the deities he belittled. He would go blind, or go mad, or meet destruction. A people who suppose that a thing has a high status in the scheme of the universe naturally assume that pleasing it brings good fortune and displeasing it brings the reverse, and Ibrahim's words had removed the protection his gods were supposed to provide.

**"Are you arguing with me about Allah, while He has guided me?"** Do you dispute with me about Allah? There is no god worthy of worship besides him, and he has guided me to it and shown me what it is. The dispute is about Allah's oneness, and about tawhid as against the partners they keep attaching to him, and he has been given the certainty of it. The reciters of the Qur'ānic text read the verb in two ways, with the two letters nūn intact or with one of them dropped. Grammarians call the dropped one the nūn of modal independence, and the reciters call it the nūn of preservation. The meaning is unaffected, and the sentence is a question that has no answer in it: how could I weigh your doubts against what I have been shown?

**"I am not afraid of whatever idols you associate with Him, none can harm me, unless my Lord so wills"** Part of his proof is that these gods he is being warned about produce no effect at all. He does not fear them and does not take them into account. So if they can cause harm, use them against him now, and give him no respite. Except that only Allah brings about benefit or harm. One gloss takes "unless my Lord wills" as a further refinement: he is not afraid of them unless his Lord wills to take the knowledge out of his heart, so that he would fear as they fear. The meaning is that a man can lose what he has been given, and everything rests on being held.

**"My Lord encompasses everything in His knowledge"** Allah's knowledge surrounds all things, and nothing escapes his complete observation. He knows that you are not following the truth.

**"Will you not be mindful"** Will you not then remember what I have explained to you, see your idols for the false gods they are, and stop worshipping them? The question is the last courtesy in the argument. It is also the pivot of the verses that follow, since verse 76 opened with a star and ended in a demand for proof.

### The same answer from another prophet

Ibrahim's reasoning here belongs to a pattern. Hud answered his people, ʿAd, in almost the same terms when they told him that some of his gods had seized him with evil. He said: "I call Allah to witness, and bear me witness, that I am free of what you ascribe as partners to him, so plot against me, all of you, and give me no respite. I put my trust in Allah, my Lord and your Lord. There is no moving creature but he has a grasp of its forelock, and my Lord is on the straight path" (11:53-56). Whoever is on the straight path does not need the protection of things that cannot speak.

### In short

- The threat against Ibrahim is not abstract. He is told that his safety depends on the gods he has insulted, and he answers by taking that claim literally.
- His fearlessness is not bravado but a definition: only Allah brings benefit or harm, so nothing else is a candidate for fear.
- The reciters' two readings of one verb in the verse change nothing of the sense, and are recorded for that reason.
- The knowledge of Allah is the closing weight in the sentence: whatever they do is already encompassed.

<<<VERSE 6:81>>>
"And how should I fear your associate-gods, while you have no fear in associating ˹others˺ with Allah—a practice He has never authorized? Which side has more right to security? ˹Tell me˺ if you really know!”

One question, put twice, and then a third that decides the case.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And how should I fear your associate-gods"** How should I fear what you have set up beside him, when it can neither profit nor harm? The idols he is being threatened with are themselves set up, and set up wrongly. He asks them to notice the asymmetry: a thing that needs its worshippers in order to exist at all cannot be what keeps a man safe.

**"while you have no fear in associating others with Allah, a practice He has never authorized"** And you do not fear Allah in associating with him things that he created, for which he has sent down no warrant to you. The word for warrant here means proof and authority, as Ibn Abbas and others among the salaf explained it. Two other verses state the same standard. "Do they have partners who institute for them a religion that Allah has not permitted?" (42:21). "They are but names which you and your fathers have named, for which Allah has sent down no authority" (53:21). Worship, in other words, is not a matter of taste or inheritance. It needs a permission, and no permission has come.

**"Which side has more right to security? Tell me if you really know"** Which of the two parties is on the truth? Is it the one that worships him in whose hand are harm and benefit, or the one that worships what cannot bring harm or benefit without any authority to justify it? Which of the two is more deserving of safety from Allah's punishment on the Day of Rising? The question is put to them as an examination, and the wording of the gloss is blunt: if you have knowledge of which party is more deserving, then follow that party. The answer, in the sense of the verse, is the believers who are monotheists, and they are the ones without doubt.

### Why the question is left open

One reading takes the sentence as addressed to the two factions together, asking which of the adherents of the two religions has more right to be safe from the God he worships. The question is not asked for information, and it is not answered in the verse either. It is left open so that the hearers have to say it themselves, and the next verse then gives the answer out loud. The silence after a question like this is what makes the answer in verse 82 sound like a verdict rather than a claim.

### In short

- The verse turns the threat back on the people making it, by asking which side has a warrant for what it does.
- A sent-down authority is the missing item in idolatry, and two other verses are quoted to show that the standard is general.
- Security is measured against the God one worships, not against the crowd one belongs to.
- The question goes unanswered by his people, which is why the answer in the next verse is stated as a rule.

<<<VERSE 6:82>>>
It is ˹only˺ those who are faithful and do not tarnish their faith with falsehood who are guaranteed security and are ˹rightly˺ guided

The answer to the open question, and the sentence that frightened the companions when it was revealed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"It is those who are faithful"** When they did not answer what Ibrahim asked, the answer came as a statement about a class of people rather than about the argument. The verse does not say: you are all ruined. It says who is safe. Those who believe in Allah and follow his laws.

**"and do not tarnish their faith with falsehood"** The verb here is built on a root that means to wear, to cover, and to mix, so the sense is that they do not mix up their faith with injustice and do not pollute it with idolatry. The word for injustice is left indefinite in Arabic, which by the rules of grammar makes it general, and it therefore takes in every kind of shirk, not only the open prostration to a stone. One gloss adds that they are not hypocrites either, since a hypocrite is exactly a person who mixes something else into what he professes.

**"who are guaranteed security"** For them is safety from Allah, and safety from his punishment, and they are the guided ones in this life and in the Hereafter. They are guided to what is right, and it is also said that they are the people who have safety and are guided to the proof of faith, and their Lord has shown them the path of guidance.

### What the companions took from it

When the verse came down, saying "and do not tarnish their faith with injustice", it was hard on the people, and they said: messenger of Allah, who among us has not been unjust to himself? If the only way to be safe is to have done no injustice at all while believing, how can anybody be saved? The answer came back: "It is not what you understood from it. Did you not hear what the righteous servant said?" And that servant said to his son: "O my son, do not join others in worship with Allah. Verily joining others in worship with Allah is a great injustice indeed" (31:13). So the injustice in the verse is shirk. The report is in the two Sahīhs, that of al-Bukhari and that of Muslim, and Ahmad recorded it through Abdullah as well. Whoever enters into faith and then does not associate anyone with Allah in his being or in his attributes remains safe from the punishment, and is counted among the well guided.

### A wider definition of the same crime

The verse also settles a question that people tend to answer too narrowly. Shirk is not restricted to becoming an idolater in the customary sense. A person may recite the words of Islam, and still take some angel, messenger or saint as a sharer in attributes that belong to Allah alone. The warning in this verse is stern for anyone who treats the men of Allah and their resting places as able to answer a prayer or grant a need, and who behaves practically as though the divine powers had been delegated to them. The verse also decides that shirk is not confined to the man who becomes a mushrik in the usual sense. Those who worship idols, rocks, trees, rivers and stars do so out of a bland simplicity, taking them to be holders and wielders of power. Ibrahim gave such people the key to their problem when he told them to fear the Allah who knows everything they do and has power over whatever reaches them, rather than to fear things that have neither knowledge nor power. That fear is unreasonable, and whoever believes that it is unreasonable is out of danger.

### In short

- The guarantee of safety is attached to a combination, not to a label: faith plus the absence of any mixture in it.
- The verse's first readers understood "injustice" as ordinary sin and had to be corrected to shirk, which is how the range of the word got fixed.
- Mixing covers more than statues, since it covers borrowing attributes for a creature, and the entry says so as the material does.
- Guidance is named in the same breath as security, because the verse's promise is not only safety but direction.

<<<VERSE 6:83>>>
This was the argument We gave Abraham against his people. We elevate in rank whoever We please. Surely your Lord is All-Wise, All-Knowing

The debate closes with a note about who won it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This was the argument We gave Abraham against his people"** That is Our proof, which We gave to Ibrahim. The word "that" is the subject of the sentence, and "Our argument" is what substitutes for it, so the sense is: this was the argument of ours with which Ibrahim inferred Allah's oneness, as in the case of the setting stars and what followed them. Mujahid and others took the argument specifically to be the words he had just said: how can I fear what you associate with Allah, while you fear to associate with him what he has sent down no warrant for, and which of the two parties has more right to security? Allah testified to the truth of what Ibrahim said, and affirmed security and guidance for the people of faith. We directed him to proclaim that proof against them, and We inspired it into him. The ranks that were given to him are degrees of merit, of power, of status, of argument, and of the knowledge of Allah's oneness. Or, on the other reading of the last words, degrees in knowledge and wisdom.

### The two readings of "We elevate in rank whoever We please"

The end of the sentence can be read in two ways. Taken as a genitive annexation, the degrees belong to whoever he wills, darajāti man nashā’. Taken as an accusative, the raising is directed at whoever he wills, darajātan man nashā’. Whoever is fit for it is raised. The point of the verse's ending is that elevation is distributed by will and not by descent, and the list of prophets in the verses after this one will make the same point in the form of a genealogy. "Surely your Lord is All-Wise, All-Knowing" means that he is wise in his statements and his actions, and knowing about those he guides and those he leaves astray, and knowing whether the proof has been established against people or not. That is why the verse about those against whom the word of your Lord has been justified belongs here: they will not believe, even if every sign came to them, until they see the painful torment (10:96-97).

### A warning against pride in one's own clarity

The triumph in the debate, and the silencing of his people, was a blessing from Allah alone. He gave Ibrahim a sound theory to propound and glowing arguments to employ. No one, therefore, should wax proud about his intelligence, his understanding, his art of discourse or his power of oration, as though these were self-sufficient. Nothing crosses the barrier of what is possible without the support and help of Allah. Bland human reason is not enough to grasp realities, and every age shows people what that looks like. Men of great standing in philosophy go astray, while many who cannot read hold on firmly to correct belief and right thinking. Rumi said it on the same beam: "Without the graces of the True One and those close to him, dark shall be the record of deeds, even if one be an angel." The station of reverence Ibrahim received in this world, and kept through generations until the Last Day, a homage paid by Jews, Christians, Muslims and Buddhists alike, was not a feat of personal acquisition. It was grace, and it was reward.

### In short

- The argument is credited to Allah before it is credited to Ibrahim, which is what the verse's opening words are for.
- Elevation in rank is by will and fitness, not by inheritance, and the next verses prove it with a family tree.
- Two readings of one word at the end of the sentence both keep the emphasis on degrees rather than on desert.
- The closing pair of names, All-Wise and All-Knowing, is used as the reason some people are shown the proof and do not believe.

<<<VERSE 6:84>>>
And We blessed him with Isaac and Jacob. We guided them all as We previously guided Noah and those among his descendants: David, Solomon, Job, Joseph, Moses, and Aaron. This is how We reward the good-doers

The list begins, and it begins with a child given when there was no reason to expect one.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And We blessed him with Isaac and Jacob"** Allah gave Ibrahim a son when he had grown old, and when he and his wife had lost hope of children. The angels came to him on their way to destroy the people of Lut, and they brought the good news of a child to him and to his wife. His wife was astonished at it, and said: "Alas! Shall I bear a child while I am an old woman, and my husband here is an old man? This is a strange thing indeed." They answered her: "Do you wonder at the decree of Allah? The mercy of Allah and his blessings be upon you, O family of Ibrahim, for he is praiseworthy, glorious" (11:72-73). The good news did not stop at the child. He was given news of Isaac as a prophet from among the righteous (37:112), which completes the favour, and of a grandson in his own lifetime, so that his eyes would be comforted by both of them (11:71). A man rejoices when he becomes a grandfather, because it means his offspring will continue, and an elderly couple would normally be expected to leave no descendants at all. That is why the news came in two generations at once. The name of the grandson, Yaqub, literally means multiplying and having offspring.

**"We guided them all"** Each one of them, Ibrahim, Isaac and Jacob, was guided by being given prophethood and surrender to Allah. Ibrahim had left his people and his country for the sake of worshipping Allah alone, and this was what came of it. Allah compensated him with better than his people and his tribe, by giving him righteous children who followed his religion. "So when he turned away from them and from those they worshipped besides Allah, We gave him Ishaq and Yaqub, and each of them We made a prophet" (19:49).

**"as We previously guided Noah"** Noah was guided before Ibrahim, honoured with prophethood and surrender, and given righteous offspring in the same way. "And those among his descendants" is read by Ibn Jarir as the offspring of Nuh, and it can also be taken as the offspring of Ibrahim, since the blessing was originally mentioned about him. When Allah drowned the people of the earth, and saved only those who believed in Nuh and were with him in the ark, he made the offspring of Nuh the inhabitants of the earth, and since then the people of the earth have been his descendants. About Ibrahim, no prophet was sent after him except from among his progeny. Allah ordained for his offspring prophethood and the Book (29:27), and he sent Nuh and Ibrahim and placed prophethood and the Book in their descendants (57:26).

### The reward that goes on

**"This is how We reward the good-doers"** Thus do We reward those who do good in their works and their speech. It is also said that the good-doers here are those who confess Allah's oneness, and that each of these men was guided to the path of what is straight. The reward given to the prophets is not reserved for prophets. Similarly Allah said in another verse that he bestowed his grace on them from among the prophets, "of the offspring of Adam, of those whom We carried in the ship with Nuh, and of the offspring of Ibrahim and of Israel", and that they were guided and chosen (19:58).

### In short

- The list is introduced as a compensation, which is why the first item in it is a son born against all expectation.
- Guidance is defined in the list's own terms as prophethood and surrender, repeated for every name.
- The descent in the verse is treated both as Nuh's line and as Ibrahim's, and the two readings are not in conflict.
- The reward for the good-doers is stated as a rule that continues past the prophets, so that the list is an example rather than an exception.

<<<VERSE 6:85>>>
Likewise, ˹We guided˺ Zachariah, John, Jesus, and Elias, who were all of the righteous

Four more names, and one of them raises a question about what a descendant is.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Zachariah, John, Jesus, and Elias"** Zakariyya, Yahya, ʿIsa the son of Maryam, and Ilyas. Each of them was guided by being given prophethood and surrender to Allah, and all of them were of the seed of Ibrahim. Ilyas is described as the paternal nephew of Harun, the brother of Musa. The list keeps Ibrahim's family wide rather than narrow: it takes in uncles, nephews and grandsons through daughters as well as sons.

**"who were all of the righteous"** All of them were among the righteous, and they were messengers chosen by Allah. The same claim that was made about the previous names is made about these, without any of them being set apart as a special case.

### The difficulty about Isa, and how the scholars answered it

If the list is Ibrahim's progeny, then ʿIsa is a problem, because he had no father, and is a descendant of Ibrahim only through the daughter's side. He is a grandson through Maryam rather than a grandson through any son. The great majority of the learned scholars and jurists answered that progeny includes both the grandsons of a man's sons and the grandsons of his daughters, and they use this same verse as their evidence for holding that al-Hasan and al-Husayn are counted among the progeny of the Prophet. A report about that argument runs as follows, transmitted by Abu Harb ibn Abi al-Aswad and recorded by Ibn Abi Hatim. Al-Hajjaj sent to Yahya bin Ya'mar, and said: "I have been told that you claim that al-Hasan and al-Husayn are among the offspring of the Prophet. Did you find it in the Book of Allah? I read the Qur'an from beginning to end and I did not find it." Yahya asked him: "Do you not read, in this surah, the words 'and among his progeny Dawud and Sulayman', running on until 'and Yahya and ʿIsa'?" He said yes. Yahya said: "Is not ʿIsa among the progeny of Ibrahim, although he had no father?" Al-Hajjaj said: "You have spoken the truth." The practical rule that grows out of it is drawn in the same way. When a man leaves a legacy, a trust or a gift to his offspring, the children of his daughters are included in it. When he gives it to his sons, then it belongs only to his male children and their male children.

### In short

- Four names, and the fourth is placed in the list by a family relation rather than by a father's line.
- The verse becomes the evidence for a legal definition of offspring, and the report about al-Hajjaj shows the definition being argued from this verse in a court.
- Being of the righteous and being a messenger are said about all of them together, which keeps the list from being read as a ranking.
- The widening of the word progeny is the reason a nephew and a maternal grandson can both be in the same sentence as sons.

<<<VERSE 6:86>>>
˹We also guided˺ Ishmael, Elisha, Jonah, and Lot, favouring each over other people ˹of their time˺

The list ends with four names, one of which had to be explained away as a grandson in the first place.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ishmael, Elisha, Jonah, and Lot"** Ismaʿil the son of Ibrahim, and Ilyasaʿ, in which the first letter is an addition to the root, and Yunus, and Lut the son of Haran. Lut is not a descendant of Ibrahim at all. He was his nephew, the son of his brother, and he is named in his progeny the way an uncle is named a father in ordinary Arab speech. The same device explains the other oddities in this list. In another verse the sons of Yaqub are told to worship the God of their fathers, Ibrahim, Ismaʿil and Ishaq, one God (2:133), although Ismaʿil was Yaqub's uncle. And when Allah commanded the angels to prostrate, Iblis was included in the command and was blamed for refusing, because he was with them in the order even though he was not one of them. He was created from fire while the angels were created from light (15:30-31).

**"favouring each over other people of their time"** Each of them was preferred above the creatures, through prophethood, over the people of their own age, believers and unbelievers alike. The favour is not a natural advantage and it is not inherited. Excellence is nobody's racial or national prerogative. It is given to those individuals who keep themselves righteous on Allah's guidance, who keep clear of every kind of shirk, and who throw themselves body and soul into admonition without payment. They are the people who make Allah's Book their real guide and identify with it so closely that its mysteries are uncovered to them, and the result of that work is what is called wisdom. Such people Allah picks out, and he sends some of them as prophets while prophethood lasts, and after that as those who carry the call. His reward to the prophet and to the one who carries the call rests on ihsan, on doing what is beautiful.

### What the whole list was for

Seventeen prophets are named in these six verses. Some of them are Ibrahim's forefathers, most of them his progeny, and some his brothers or nephews. They are described as guided, as righteous in conduct, as firm on the straight path, and as chosen and blessed for the service of Allah's religion. The point of putting them in a genealogy is not genealogy. Ibrahim gave up his father, his community and his country in the way of Allah, and before the reward of eternal bliss there came a compensation in this world: a community better than the one he left, a country better than the one he lost, and the distinction that every prophet and messenger sent into the world after him, until the end of time, came from his progeny. From the branch that came through Ishaq rose the prophets among the children of Israel. From the branch that came through Ismaʿil came the foremost and the last of them, Muhammad al-Mustafa. Honour and ruin do turn on a person's own deeds, and nothing in these verses says otherwise. But having a prophet or a man of Allah among one's forefathers, or having scholars of religion and people of upright conduct among one's children, is itself a blessing, and it brings its benefits. And the disbelievers of Makkah are being told, through all of this, that the men they themselves revere were saying one thing: Allah is the only object fit for worship, and to share anything in his exclusive attributes is unbelief and straying.

### In short

- The last four names include a nephew, and the verse lets the word progeny stretch to cover him, exactly as it stretched for ʿIsa.
- Preference is defined as prophethood, and it is preference over the people of that time, not over every person who ever lived.
- The list is addressed to the Qur'an's first audience as much as to Ibrahim, since their own patriarchs are the ones being quoted.
- Excellence is open to the unlettered and shut against the well born, and the closing claim of the passage is that reward follows conduct.
