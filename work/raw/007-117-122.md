<<<VERSE 7:117>>>
The moment that separated the two comes now. "Then We inspired Moses, “Throw down your staff,” and—behold!—it devoured the objects of their illusion"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then We inspired Moses, “Throw down your staff"** gives the instruction at the height of the contest. It was a tremendous moment, and it was the moment in which Allah set the truth apart from the falsehood. The revelation came to His servant and messenger with the order to throw the staff he was holding in his right hand.

**"and—behold!—it devoured the objects of their illusion"** is what happened when he threw it. The staff swallowed straight away everything they had put on show, which was the magic they had used to make their trick appear real when it had no reality in it at all. The account here begins with verse 117 of this surah.

**What the reports add to the scene.** Ibn Abbas said that the staff of Musa swallowed all the ropes and the sticks that the magicians had thrown. Muhammad bin Ishaq described the way it happened, that the staff followed the ropes and the sticks one after another, until nothing the sorcerers had thrown was left. Then Musa took it in his hand, and it turned back into a stick as it was at the first.

Al-Qasim bin Abi Bazzah gave the same event with more of its detail. He said that when Musa threw the staff it became a huge serpent, plain for all to see, and it opened its mouth and swallowed the ropes and sticks of the magicians. It was at that point that the magicians went down in prostration, and they did not raise their heads until they had been shown the Garden, the Fire, and the recompense that belongs to the people of each. Thousands of staffs and ropes had been turned into snakes leaping across the ground, and the crowd stood stunned and bewildered at the sight, and within no time at all the great snake had eaten every one of them.

### In short

- The order came by revelation at the height of the contest, and it was the moment that set truth apart from falsehood.
- The staff swallowed the whole of the illusion the magicians had put on show.
- Ibn Abbas said the staff took all the ropes and sticks, and Ibn Ishaq that it followed them one after another until nothing remained.
- The report of Al-Qasim bin Abi Bazzah adds that it became a huge serpent that opened its mouth and swallowed what they had thrown.
- The magicians went down in prostration at that sight, and the crowd stood stunned as the last of the snakes was eaten.

<<<VERSE 7:118>>>
The verdict of the contest is stated in one sentence. "So the truth prevailed and their illusions failed"

### Phrase by phrase

**"So the truth prevailed"** says that the case was settled in its favour. Another reading of the words gives the sense that the matter became clear, that Musa was in the right, and that what he had said was shown to be true by what the people had seen with their own eyes.

**"and their illusions failed"** gives the other side of it. Their sorcery came to nothing, and the work they had done with such confidence turned out to have no weight at all. The word used here carries the sense of a thing that fizzles away and leaves nothing behind.

**Why the two halves belong together.** The contest had been arranged so that the difference could be seen rather than argued. What the magicians had done was an illusion laid over the eyes of the crowd, and the thing itself stayed as it was. What Musa was given was a real change in the creature, and when the two met in the same field of sight, one of them ate the other. There was nothing left for argument afterwards. The truth was confirmed by the event itself, and the craft that had been the pride of the land stood empty in front of everybody.

### In short

- The words state that the truth came out clear and established, and that Musa was shown to be in the right.
- The sorcery of the magicians was made vain and came to nothing.
- What they had done was an illusion over the eyes, while what Musa was given was a real change in the creature.
- When the two were set side by side in the same field, the illusion was swallowed whole.
- The case was settled by what the people saw, and the craft of the land stood empty in front of them.

<<<VERSE 7:119>>>
The outcome for the court is named. "So Pharaoh and his people were defeated right there and put to shame"

### Phrase by phrase

**"So Pharaoh and his people were defeated right there"** fixes the place of the defeat. It happened in the assembly, in the open place where the meeting had been set, and the word has the sense of being overcome on the spot without any room to recover.

**"and put to shame"** gives the second half of the sentence. It is not enough to lose a contest. The loss here carried humiliation with it, since the whole weight of the kingdom and the whole of its pride in the craft had been put into the gathering, and it was the king's own men who went down.

The defeat was also the defeat of the magicians, who had come to the assembly sure of themselves and sure of the reward Musa would never give them. They were beaten and humbled in front of the nation they had been brought to impress, and the king who hired them was humbled with them, since the two had staked everything on the same plan. So the verse gathers both parties into one sentence, since the plan had been made in one place, and it was broken in one place.

### In short

- The defeat came on the spot, in the assembly where the contest had been arranged.
- The humiliation is named alongside the loss, since the whole of the kingdom's pride had been placed in that gathering.
- The magicians were beaten in front of the nation they had been brought to impress.
- The king who hired them was humbled with them, and both are gathered into the one sentence.

<<<VERSE 7:120>>>
The men who had been bought with a court place do the opposite of what was asked of them. "And the magicians fell down, prostrating"

### Phrase by phrase

**"And the magicians fell down, prostrating"** records what they did at the sight. One reading of the words is that they went down so quickly that it looked as though they had fallen rather than lowered themselves, and the sense of it is a surrender that could not wait.

**What brought them down.** When they saw the greatness of the power of Allah and the clear signs in front of them, they had nothing left to do but go down before Him. Their own position in the matter was the one that made the sight decisive. Theirs was the craft of Egypt, and they were the masters of it, so they knew better than anyone where its limits lay. Since the work of Musa was outside every one of those limits, they understood that what they were watching was not magic at all. It was prophethood, and the difference was plain to the men who had spent their lives in the trade.

The account also carries another sense, that the prostration was a favour placed in them at the very hour they were expected to fight.

### What the defeat of magic did to the whole picture

The men who had come for a reward had known of the message of Musa beforehand. His calling in Egypt went on for about forty years, and this contest came in the last stage of it, so the name was not new to them. What had not yet gone was the veil over their eyes, and when they saw his superiority in the very field they had made their own, that veil was torn away at once.

What they saw in that hour was how small human devices are, even at their most complete, and how great God is. From then on Pharaoh and all his authority counted for nothing with them. The men who had asked for a reward before the contest treated the threat of the worst punishment afterwards as a thing of no account.

### In short

- The magicians went down at once, and one reading has them falling rather than lowering themselves in the usual way.
- They knew the limits of their own craft, and the work of Musa stood outside every one of them, so they recognized prophethood and not magic.
- On one reading the prostration itself was a favour placed in them by Allah at that hour.
- The calling of Musa in Egypt had lasted about forty years before this contest, so the message was not new to them.
- What the sight did was to show them the smallness of human devices and the greatness of God, and after that Pharaoh and his authority counted for nothing with them.

<<<VERSE 7:121>>>
The confession of the gathered sorcerers is reported in their own words. "They declared, “We ˹now˺ believe in the Lord of all worlds—"

### Phrase by phrase

**"They declared, “We ˹now˺ believe in the Lord of all worlds"** is the declaration they made as they came up from the prostration. The words were spoken in front of the king and in front of the nation, and they were spoken by the men who had been hired to defeat the messenger.

The confession is also a statement about whom they were leaving. In one report Pharaoh heard the words and shot back a question at them, asking whether they were referring to him. He had presented himself to the people as their lord, and the craft that had been hired for his sake was now raising a claim about who owned the worlds, so the question came out of him before anything else did.

The reason the words carried that force is set out plainly in another place. Those men knew that what they had witnessed with the staff could not have been done by sorcery. Every kind of trick in their field had just been eaten in front of them, and the conclusion was not a matter of argument any more. What they had seen was a sign from the Lord of everything, and the first thing to do was to say so out loud.

### In short

- The declaration was made openly before the king and the nation, by the men who had been hired to defeat Musa.
- Their belief was in the Lord of all worlds, which set them against every claim of the king beside him.
- Pharaoh heard the words and asked whether they meant him, since he had set himself up as the lord of the people.
- The magicians knew that what they had seen with the staff was beyond the whole of their craft.
- What they witnessed left nothing to argue about, and their first act was to say plainly what they believed.

<<<VERSE 7:122>>>
The last words of their declaration name the two men in front of them. "the Lord of Moses and Aaron.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"the Lord of Moses and Aaron"** is added after the words about the Lord of all worlds, and the addition has a purpose. It makes it clear that the Lord worshipped by Musa and by Harun is in fact the Lord of the universe. The words also mark the difference between them and the king. Pharaoh had pretended to be the lord of his people, and the magicians named the two men whom he had set himself against as the servants of the true Lord.

**Aaron in the account.** His name comes here beside his brother's, as it does in the account of the mission and in the answer to the call. The two of them stood before the king together, and the confession of the magicians names them together, which is the plain way to make a statement about who sent them and whom they serve.

**What the verse settles.** The men who make this declaration had come for a reward and left with their prostrations. What they said in these two verses outlasted everything the court could do afterwards. Their words also mark the end of the ground the king had built on. He had claimed the lordship of the land, and his own hired men now named the Lord of all worlds, the Lord of Musa and of Aaron, in front of the whole nation. The rest of the account reports what he did about it, and how little it moved them.

### In short

- The name of Aaron is joined to that of Musa, so the Lord they worship is named as the Lord of both.
- The addition makes plain that the Lord of the two men is the Lord of the whole universe.
- The words mark the break with the king, who had pretended to be the lord of his people.
- The men who had come for a reward left the ground with their declaration of faith, and it outlasted everything the court did afterwards.
- The king's own hired men named the Lord of all worlds before the whole nation, and the account goes on to show how little his answer moved them.
