<<<VERSE 7:117>>>
At that tremendous moment, when Allah separated truth from falsehood, He sent a revelation to His servant and messenger Mūsā (Moses): "Then We inspired Moses, “Throw down your staff,” and—behold!—it devoured the objects of their illusion". Thousands of staffs and ropes seemed to be snakes leaping all over the ground when Allah told Mūsā to throw down the staff in his right hand.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then We inspired Moses, “Throw down your staff,”"** Allah revealed to Mūsā: cast your staff, O Mūsā. And he threw it.

**"and—behold!—it devoured the objects of their illusion"**: it swallowed up their lying show, their staffs and ropes. In the Arabic, "it devoured" is read talaqqafu, with one of the two original tāʾ letters of tatalaqqafu dropped. "The objects of their illusion" means what they were transforming by deception, the magic with which they made things look real though they were not real at all.

### How did the staff overcome the magic?

When Mūsā threw his staff, it became a huge serpent and at once began to eat up the magicians' snakes. Al-Qāsim ibn Abī Bazzah described it as a huge serpent in plain view, its jaws open, gulping down the ropes and sticks of the magicians. Ibn ʿAbbās said Mūsā's staff swallowed every rope and stick the magicians had thrown. Muhammad ibn Isḥāq said it went after the ropes and sticks one after another until nothing the sorcerers had thrown was left. Then Mūsā took it in his hand, and it became a staff again, just as before. The crowd stood bewildered and stunned. In no time the great serpent of Mūsā had eaten every one of their snakes, destroying the illusion that they were crawling snakes.

### In short

- Allah told Mūsā to throw his staff, and it became a great serpent that swallowed every rope and stick of the magicians.
- What the magicians had made to look real was exposed as illusion, and the staff returned to its form in Mūsā's hand.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:118>>>
The contest was decided: "So the truth prevailed and their illusions failed". It became clear that Mūsā (Moses) was right and that the magicians' work was false.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So the truth prevailed"**: the truth came to pass, confirmed and made plain. It proved that what Mūsā said was true.

**"and their illusions failed"**: literally "what they were doing became void". Their sorcery came to nothing, fizzled away and was shown to be false.

### Why did the magicians' tricks collapse?

When the magicians threw down their sticks and ropes, people saw moving snakes because of an optical illusion. But when Mūsā's staff turned into a snake and moved along the ground, their sticks and ropes went back to being sticks and ropes. The magicians knew the limits of magic. They could see that what Mūsā's staff did lay far beyond anything their art could achieve.

### In short

- The truth of Mūsā's message was confirmed before everyone, and the magic was shown to be empty.
- Once the real sign appeared, the illusion lost its hold and the ropes were plain ropes again.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:119>>>
The defeat was public and complete: "So Pharaoh and his people were defeated right there and put to shame". Mūsā (Moses) triumphed over them in the assembly, before the very crowd that had been gathered to watch his humiliation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So Pharaoh and his people were defeated right there"**: Pharaoh and his people, and the magicians with them, were beaten on the spot, at that very gathering, and Mūsā was victorious over them.

**"and put to shame"**: they were humiliated and left disgraced.

### Why does "right there" matter?

Pharaoh had chosen the time and place himself, a festival day with the people gathered, hoping to expose Mūsā before the whole nation. Instead the defeat fell on him in that same public setting. The stage he had built to shame Allah's messenger became the scene of his own disgrace.

### In short

- Pharaoh and his magicians were defeated at the very assembly they had called.
- The public contest meant to disgrace Mūsā ended in Pharaoh's own humiliation.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:120>>>
Then came the most astonishing turn of the day: "And the magicians fell down, prostrating". The magicians realised that what they had seen came from heaven and was in no way magic.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And the magicians fell down"**: literally "were thrown down". The magicians were so overwhelmed by Mūsā's (Moses') miracle that they could do nothing but fall. It may also mean that Allah favoured them and placed them in prostration. One explanation says they went down so quickly that it looked as if they had fallen.

**"prostrating"**: they prostrated to Allah, having seen the greatness of His power and His clear signs. Al-Qāsim ibn Abī Bazzah said they did not raise their heads until they had seen Paradise, the Fire and the reward of their inhabitants.

### How could magicians turn to faith in a moment?

Mūsā's mission in Egypt lasted about 40 years, and this contest took place in its final stage. So the magicians must already have known his message, though the veil over their eyes had not yet lifted. When they saw his superiority in the very field in which they were experts, every veil was torn away. They could see that this was not magic but divine prophethood. Of their own free will, without hesitation, they prostrated themselves before Allah.

### In short

- Seeing the staff swallow their work, the magicians knew it was no magic and fell down in prostration to Allah.
- As experts in magic, they recognised the sign at once. Their knowledge, which had served Pharaoh, now led them to faith.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:121>>>
The magicians declared their faith openly: "They declared, “We ˹now˺ believe in the Lord of all worlds—". Muhammad ibn Isḥāq reported that they added, "Had Mūsā been a magician, he would not have defeated us."

### Phrase by phrase

**"They declared, “We ˹now˺ believe in the Lord of all worlds—"**: they cried out that they believed in the Lord of all creation. Pharaoh, it is said, asked, "Are you speaking of me?"

### What did the magicians come to see?

In this event the magicians saw how small human devices are, even at their most perfect, and how great and all-powerful Allah is. After that, Pharaoh, with all his authority, looked worthless to them. The same men who, before witnessing Allah's greatness, had longed for Pharaoh's rewards now brushed aside his threat of the worst punishment as if it meant nothing.

### In short

- The magicians declared their faith in the Lord of all worlds, knowing no magician could have defeated them.
- Having seen Allah's power, they lost all regard for Pharaoh's rewards and threats.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:122>>>
The magicians completed their declaration: "the Lord of Moses and Aaron.”" They named the Lord of the worlds as the Lord whom Mūsā (Moses) and Hārūn (Aaron) worshipped.

### Phrase by phrase

**"the Lord of Moses and Aaron.”"** He is the Lord of Mūsā and Hārūn, who alone deserves the worship of all people, while the false gods deserve none. They said this because they realised that what they had seen the staff do could not be done by sorcery.

### Why add "the Lord of Moses and Aaron"?

After saying "the Lord of all worlds", the magicians added "the Lord of Mūsā and Hārūn". They did so to make it clear that the Lord whom Mūsā and Hārūn worshipped is the true Lord of the universe, and not Pharaoh, as he claimed to be. Since Pharaoh posed as their lord, this addition left no room for doubt about whom they now believed in.

### In short

- The magicians specified their faith in the Lord of Mūsā and Hārūn, the one God worthy of worship.
- The addition shut out Pharaoh's claim to lordship, making plain that he was not the lord they meant.
<<<END>>>
