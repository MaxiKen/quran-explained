<<<VERSE 6:33>>>
"We certainly know that what they say grieves you ˹O Prophet˺." The verse is a comfort before it is anything else. Allah tells His messenger that the grief is known to Him, that the sorrow he carries over his people has not escaped notice, and then it re-labels what the grief is really about.

### Phrase by phrase

**"what they say grieves you"** covers their denial of him, their slander and their demands for proofs. Other verses say the same thing in harder words: "So destroy not yourself in sorrow for them" (35:8), "It may be that you are going to kill yourself with grief, that they do not become believers" (26:3), and "Perhaps you would kill yourself in grief over their footsteps, because they believe not in this narration" (18:6). **"It is not your honesty they question"** means they do not accuse him of lying. They knew him too well for that. A variant reading takes the words as a statement rather than a denial, and renders it: they do not charge you with mendacity. **"it is Allah's signs that the wrongdoers deny"** turns the rejection back onto its real object. What they refuse is the truth itself and the signs of Allah, and the denial is carried out openly while it is refused inwardly. On another reading they do reject the Prophet outwardly, but the outcome of belying him is the belying of Allah and His signs, and a hadith puts the same equation the other way round: whoever causes pain to the Prophet causes pain to Allah.

### What three chiefs did at night

Muhammad ibn Isḥāq related from az-Zuhrī that three men came to listen to the Prophet ﷺ reciting the Qur'an at night, each of them unaware of the others: Abū Jahl ibn Hishām, Ṣakhr ibn Ḥarb who is Abū Sufyān, and al-Akhnas ibn Shurayq. They stayed until morning and left, met on the way back, were asked by one another what had brought them, told the truth, and swore not to repeat it, so that the young men of Quraysh would not hear of it and imitate them. On the second night every one of them came back, each assuming the others would keep away because of the oath. They met again in the morning, blamed one another and swore again, and on the third night they did the same thing for the third time.

Then al-Akhnas took his staff and went to Abū Sufyān at his house and said: "O Abū Ḥanzalah, what is your view of what you heard from Muḥammad?" He answered: "O Abu Tha'labah, by Allah, I heard things I recognized and knew the reach of, and things whose meaning was closed to me." Al-Akhnas said the same of himself, by Him whom the other had sworn by, and went on to Abū Jahl, whom he addressed as "O Abū al-Ḥakam". Abū Jahl said: we raced with Banū ʿAbd Manāf and matched them. We fed as they fed and gave as they gave, and when we were neck and neck with them, like two horses in a race, they said: there is a prophet among us, and revelation comes down to him from heaven. How could we catch them up on that? By Allah, believing in him and accepting what he says is something we will never do. In Arabia he had been called Abū al-Ḥakam, a man of wisdom, and was renamed for his hostility.

A second account, preserved through As-Suddi, lays the same scene out more briefly. Akhnas ibn Shurayq met Abū Jahl and said: you and I are alone, no third person is listening, so tell me what you really think of Muḥammad ibn ʿAbdullāh, is he telling the truth or not. Abū Jahl answered that Muḥammad is truthful, with no doubt about it, and that he has never told a lie in his life. But we cannot bear that one branch of Quraysh, Banū Quṣayy, should hold every virtue while the rest of the tribe has nothing. They have the banner. They hold the duty of giving water to the pilgrims at the Hajj. They are the keepers of the Kaʿbah and its keys are in their hands. If prophethood is theirs as well, what is left for the rest of Quraysh? A third version comes through Abū Maysarah: the Prophet ﷺ passed by Abū Jahl and his companions, and they said to him: by Allah, we do not call you a liar, we count you truthful, and what we deny is what you have brought. Yet another report names the man this verse was about as al-Ḥārith ibn ʿAmir, spelled al-Ḥarth in other copies, a man of the line of ʿAbd Manaf ibn Quṣayy who used to deny the Prophet in public and then said at home: Muḥammad is not one of those who lie.

### Why honesty was not enough to accept

The people of Makkah who had not believed still admitted that he was a righteous man. But calling a man truthful is a way of glorifying him, and that was the honour they would not hand over. As long as he was a man of their own level they had a kind of satisfaction. To accept that Allah's own discourse came from his lips was to raise him above themselves, and few things are harder for a proud man to say yes to. God does not appear in this world in His own shape. He appears before people in the shape of arguments, signs and symbols. So to refuse an argument for the truth, or to shut one's eyes at a sign pointing to it, is to refuse God and to look away from His face. But it was never part of His plan to appear with compelling miracles. Where the call arrived with no room to refuse it, the freedom that makes the test a test would be over. So whoever carries this message should not be crushed by having only the weight of argument to offer and no power to compel. The effort is a trial of his patience and of his hearers at once. Can they see a glimpse of Allah's representative in a man like themselves, and bow before an argument that has no army behind it? For whoever has life in him the whole universe is full of signs. Whoever has put out his own sensitivity will take a lesson from nothing except the terror of Doomsday.

### In short

- The verse begins by telling the Prophet ﷺ that his grief over them is known to Allah.
- Their quarrel was not with his honesty but with the signs of Allah, and three separate reports record them calling him truthful.
- The chiefs listened to the Qur'an at night for three nights and kept returning despite their oaths.
- Abū Jahl's own answer names the real obstacle: tribal rivalry with Banū ʿAbd Manāf and Banū Quṣayy.
- Belief asked for in a world of arguments and unforced signs is worth what it is, because compulsion would end the test.

<<<VERSE 6:34>>>
"Indeed, messengers before you were rejected but patiently endured rejection and persecution until Our help came to them." This verse sets the experience of one messenger inside the experience of many. What he is living through is not evidence that he has been abandoned. It is the road every one of them was sent along.

### Phrase by phrase

**"messengers before you were rejected"** means their own people denied them, as this people is denying him now. **"but patiently endured rejection and persecution"** tells how they carried it: patience under the denial of their people and under the harm they were given, and the patience was kept up while the calling went on, not after it stopped. **"until Our help came to them"** is the help that arrived through the destruction of their peoples, victory in this world, and the good end that was theirs after the denial and the hurt. **"And Allah's promise ˹to help˺ is never broken"** refers to His decision that victory in this life and the next belongs to His believing servants. **"And you have already received some of the narratives of these messengers"** means the news of them has reached him, how they were denied, how they were patient, and how they came out on top, and in that there is a good example and something for the heart to settle on.

### The promise that cannot be changed

The sentence about Allah's words being beyond alteration is a ruling and not only encouragement. Other verses carry the same content. "And verily, Our Word has gone forth of old for Our servants, the messengers, that they shall be made triumphant, and that Our hosts shall be the victors" (37:171-173). And: "Allah has decreed: it is I and My messengers who shall be victorious. Allah is All-Powerful, Almighty" (58:21). Read the two together and the shape of the verse here is clear. The help is written, so patience has something to wait for, and the delay is not a defeat.

### Why the consolation is put in the past tense

The verse speaks of messengers already gone, and that is how it does its work. Do not think that this kind of denial is something new, only aimed at what you have brought. No one can hold back the help Allah has written and promised to His messengers, and the destruction of their enemies was that help. So the instruction buried in the report is simply: be patient until the victorious help reaches you, the way it reached them.

### In short

- The verse comforts the Prophet ﷺ by putting his case inside the case of earlier messengers.
- Their patience covered both denial and physical harm, and it ran to the end.
- Help came to them in this world through the ruin of their people, and victory in the hereafter followed it.
- "Allah's promise is never broken" is about His settled decision that His messengers come out on top.
- The narratives that had reached him were there to steady his heart and to give an example.

<<<VERSE 6:35>>>
"If you find their denial unbearable, then build—if you can—a tunnel through the earth or stairs to the sky to bring them a ˹more compelling˺ sign." The verse meets a grief that has run out of options with a command nobody can carry out, and that is the whole method of it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you find their denial unbearable"** is addressed to the Messenger of Allah, whose concern for his people made their turning away hard to bear. **"then build—if you can—a tunnel through the earth"** means: dig an underground passage and go down into it. **"or stairs to the sky"** means a ladder, or any way that lets you climb up. The purpose is stated plainly. Bring them a sign, a proof of the kind they are asking for, or a better one than what has already been given them. **"to bring them a ˹more compelling˺ sign"** adds nothing to the burden except this, that it cannot be done. The meaning of the command is: you will not be able to do it, so be patient until Allah gives His judgment.

### What Ibn ʿAbbās drew from it

ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported this from Ibn ʿAbbās: if you can find a tunnel and bring them a sign through it, then do it. Or climb a ladder into the sky and bring them a better sign than the one I have already given them, and then do that too. Qatādah, As-Suddi and others gave it the same drift. Then comes the sentence that closes the matter: had Allah willed, He could have brought them all together on true guidance. Ibn ʿAbbās explained the background: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was eager for everyone to believe and to follow him. Allah told him that only those whose happiness has been written for them in the first Dhikr will believe. Had your Lord willed, the words say elsewhere, everyone on earth would have believed, all of them at once.

### The two things the verse forbids

It forbids the demand for a sign as the price of patience, and it forbids ignorance about how guidance actually happens. On the first, the tunnel and the stair are a way of saying that the messenger is not the one who produces proofs. On the second, the sentence "so do not be one of those ignorant of this fact" means: do not be among the foolish about My decree concerning their disbelief, and do not lose yourself in regret over it. Guidance, when it comes, is the declaration of Allah's oneness, and hearts are not turned by an increase in visible evidence. Had Allah willed to gather them all on guidance He would have done it. He did not, because of His wisdom, and the verse expects the Prophet ﷺ to rest in that.

### In short

- The answer to unbearable grief is a command to do the impossible, which cancels the grief's demand rather than the messenger's patience.
- Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah and As-Suddi all took the tunnel and the stair as a rebuke, not as a possibility.
- Allah could have gathered them all on guidance, and the reason He did not belongs to His wisdom.
- Only those whose happiness was written in the first Dhikr will believe.
- The verse ends by forbidding ignorance of this, because ignorance of it turns into sorrow that accomplishes nothing.

<<<VERSE 6:36>>>
"Only the attentive will respond ˹to your call˺." After the tunnel and the stair, this verse narrows the matter down to the one condition that makes a response possible, which is attention, and then it names the people who do not have it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Only the attentive will respond"** means those who hear in a way that lets them understand and take heed. Those who listen to the speech, take it in and act on it are the ones who answer the call. The ones who only let it pass over them answer nothing. **"As for the dead, Allah will raise them up"** speaks of the disbelievers, whose hearts are dead, and they are compared to corpses as a way of holding them cheap and showing how little a dead heart can respond to anything. The raising is after death, and it is real rather than figurative. **"then to Him they will ˹all˺ be returned"** places the return in the gathering, where each of them is repaid for what he did. A further reading takes "the dead" to mean the dead of Badr, of Uḥud and of the battle of the Confederates.

### Attention is the whole difference

This is why the message is addressed to the living. It is so that it may warn whoever is alive, and so that the charge may stand against the disbelievers (36:70). The verse just before had said that Allah could have gathered them all on guidance. This one says what guidance needs in order to land, and it is not a stronger sign but a listener who is attentive. A person who has extinguished that attention cannot be reached by argument, and he will not be reached by a miracle either. What is left for him is the raising, the return, and the repayment. The dead of heart are called worthless here, and the sharpest part of the picture is that they are not even asked to respond, because the demand to respond only makes sense to something alive.

### In short

- Response is limited to those who hear so as to understand and take heed.
- Deniers of the message are called dead, because a dead heart cannot answer a call.
- Allah will raise them after death and they will be returned to Him for repayment.
- One reading attaches "the dead" to the dead of Badr, Uḥud and the battle of the Confederates.
- The verse explains why the warning is aimed at the living, in the words of 36:70.

<<<VERSE 6:37>>>
"They ask, 'Why has no ˹other˺ sign been sent down to him from his Lord?'" The demand is for a sign of their own choosing, and the answer refuses the terms of it without denying the power behind it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They ask"** means the disbelievers of Makkah, and the material names them: al-Ḥarth ibn ʿAmir and his people, Abū Jahl ibn Hishām, al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, and Umayyah and Ubayy the two sons of Khalf. **"Why has no ˹other˺ sign been sent down to him from his Lord?"** is the question of people who want a miracle they have designed, something to prove his prophethood: a sign like the she-camel of Sālih, or the staff of Moses, or the Table sent down to Jesus. They also said: "We shall not believe in you, until you cause a spring to gush forth from the ground for us" (17:90). **"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ 'Allah certainly has the power to send down a sign'"** is the answer, and the verb can be read in two forms, either of them saying that He is able to send down the very sign they have asked for. **"though most of them do not know"** is the reason the request is not granted. They have no knowledge that a sign is sent down by Allah's wisdom and not on demand.

### Why the sign is withheld

Allah is able to send a sign, and He decided out of His wisdom to hold it back. If a sign of their liking were sent and they still refused to believe, their punishment would be brought forward, the way it came to the nations before them. Two other verses say exactly this. "And nothing stops Us from sending the signs except that the people of old denied them. And We sent the she-camel to Thamud as a clear sign, but they wronged her. And We send the signs only to warn, and to make people afraid of destruction" (17:59). And: "If We willed, We could send down to them from the heaven a sign, to which they would bend their necks in humility" (26:4). A sign sent at the demand of stubborn people, and then denied, is not a mercy. It turns belief into recognizing what is standing in front of you, and it ends the test at the same moment it ends the excuses. The delay of acceptance in these verses is a stalling tactic, and the demand for a sign is part of the stalling, not a step toward faith.

### In short

- The demand was for a sign chosen by the demanders, in the style of the she-camel, the staff or the Table.
- The answer concedes the power and refuses the schedule: Allah is able to send down a sign.
- Most of them do not know that signs come down by wisdom, so their asking cannot set the terms.
- A granted sign that is then denied would hasten destruction, as 17:59 explains about Thamud.
- 26:4 states the other half: a compulsion sign would leave them with necks bent, not with hearts convinced.

<<<VERSE 6:38>>>
"All living beings roaming the earth and winged birds soaring in the sky are communities like yourselves." The verse answers the demand for a sign by pointing at what is already moving around them, and it says that these creatures are not a background to the story but a people of their own.

### Phrase by phrase

**"All living beings roaming the earth"** means every animal that creeps or moves on the earth, and **"winged birds soaring in the sky"** means every creature that flies through the air between earth and heaven with its wings. **"are communities like yourselves"** was explained in more than one way. Mujāhid took it as various species that carry distinct names. Qatādah said: birds are an ummah, human beings are an ummah, and the jinn are an ummah, the word used for a community that is a distinct creation with its own name. As-Suddi took it as creations and species, and another reading adds that they are servants like you, resembling you in eating, in reproduction and in understanding one another, and that this resemblance is itself a sign. **"We have left nothing out of the Record"** means the Preserved Tablet, the Lawḥ Maḥfūẓ, in which nothing has been left unwritten, and it also means that everything needed has been spelled out in the Qur'an. **"Then to their Lord they will be gathered all together"** is the Day of Judgment, when the beasts and the birds are assembled along with everyone else, and each is recompensed for what it deserves.

### What the Record contains

Knowledge of all of them sits with Allah, and He forgets none of His creatures, nor their provision, nor their affairs, whether they live on land or in the sea. "And there is no creature moving on earth but its provision is due from Allah, and He knows its dwelling place and its deposit, all of it in a Clear Book" (11:6). And: "And so many a moving creature carries not its own provision. Allah provides for it and for you, and He is the All-Hearer, the All-Knower" (29:60). In these records there is written the name of every creature, its number, its movements and its stillness. O children of Adam, the likeness the verse draws between you and the animals is in how creation is ordained for them, together with sustenance and affairs, exactly as it is ordained for you.

### Gathered, settled and turned to dust

On "then to their Lord they will be gathered", Ibn ʿAbbās said that death gathers them. Others said the Day of Resurrection gathers them, and the verse that says it plainly is: "And when the wild beasts are gathered together" (81:5). ʿAbdur-Razzaq recorded from Abū Hurayrah that all creatures will be assembled on that Day, the beasts, the birds and the rest, and Allah's justice will be so exact that the sheep without horns takes retribution from the sheep with horns. Then He will command them: be dust. At that moment the disbeliever says what the Qur'an reports: "Woe to me, would that I were dust!" (78:40). The same account reaches the Prophet ﷺ in the hadith about the Trumpet. A second chain runs from Abū Hurairah, and Ibn Jarīr, Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Baihaqī transmit it that on the Day of Qiyāmah the rights of every holder of a right are vindicated, to the point that a hornless goat is vindicated against the horned one, which is also how Al-Baghāwī reports it. The point of all that is not that animals are accountable.

### Why the animals are a sign

The verse is also read as an answer to the people who wanted a miracle of their own choosing. Only human beings and jinn are given a law to keep, and reward and punishment do not run over creatures that were never addressed. The retaliation is Allah's regard for equity, and it tells you how serious the rights of His creation are, since even the non-obligated are not let off. What God requires of these living creatures is the same as what He requires of you. His writ runs over animals as instinct and over human beings as Islamic law, and the birds and the beasts carry it out to the letter while a man is not ready to accept it. Their world is also a lesson in another way: they are out for food and do not persecute one another for it, their needs are met without selfishness, and when one of them bothers another there is no settled hostility. Tasks are done among them without anyone claiming credit. The animals are the world of nature, and the question the verse leaves with you is what justification there is for a person to choose a way other than the one every creature of Allah is already walking on the path of.

### In short

- Every animal and every bird is a community of the same kind as us, with its creation, provision and affairs ordained.
- Nothing has been left out of the Preserved Tablet, and 11:6 and 29:60 spell out what that includes.
- Gathering to Allah is stated both as death and as the Day of Resurrection, with 81:5 as its proof.
- The hornless sheep takes its right from the horned one and then all of them are ordered to become dust, which is when the disbeliever wishes he were dust (78:40).
- Animals fulfill God's writ by instinct and human beings by Islamic law, which is why the demand for a new sign misses the point.

<<<VERSE 6:39>>>
"Those who deny Our signs are ˹wilfully˺ deaf and dumb—lost in darkness." The verse leaves the animals and returns to the people who asked for a sign, and it ends by describing what is wrong with the asking.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who deny Our signs"** are described by what they have become rather than by what they lack. **"deaf"** means deaf of heart, and it is also taken to mean that they act as if they cannot hear the truth. **"and dumb"** means they pretend to be mute where the truth and guidance are concerned. **"lost in darkness"** means they are holding fast to disbelief, and the sense of the three together is that they are in a darkness in which they cannot see, unable to take in what is said and unable to say what is right. Their case is like that of a deaf-mute who can neither hear nor speak and is blinded by darkness on top of it, and the question the verse implies is obvious: how can such a person be put on the path, or change the state he is in?

### The darkness spelled out

Elsewhere the same condition is drawn as a picture. "Their parable is that of a man who kindled a fire, and when it lit up everything around him, Allah took their light away and left them in darkness, so they cannot see. They are deaf, dumb and blind, and they do not turn back" (2:17-18). And: "Or like darkness in a vast deep sea, overwhelmed by a great wave, with a great wave above it, and dark clouds above that, darkness one above another. When a man stretches out his hand he can hardly see it. And for whoever Allah has not appointed light, there is no light" (24:40). Those two passages explain the ordering here. The loss is not a shortage of evidence. It is a condition in which evidence cannot arrive, and so it is described as a chain of coverings over a covering.

### Who is guided and who is left

"Allah leaves whoever He wills to stray and guides whoever He wills to the Straight Way" is read in more than one way, and none of them makes the sentence arbitrary. One reading takes it as: He lets whoever He wills die in disbelief, and whoever He wills He lets die on the straight path He is pleased with. Another takes it as: He abandons whoever He wills, and He gives guidance, success and firmness to whoever He wills. The straight way is a road without crookedness, and it is Islam. The point that runs through all of it is that Allah does with His creatures what He wills, and the guidance being asked about is not produced by a stronger sign but by light being appointed. Whoever has none is left with none, and whoever is given it is given a path he can walk.

### In short

- Deniers of the signs are called deaf, dumb and in darkness, which describes a state rather than a lack of proof.
- Their likeness is the deaf-mute who cannot speak and is blinded besides, so the path cannot be found from inside that condition.
- 2:17-18 and 24:40 give the same picture as a fire taken away and as darkness layered over a deep sea.
- Leaving someone to stray and guiding someone else is Allah's will at work, and neither reading makes it unfair.
- The straight path is a road with no crookedness, and guidance in this verse means light being appointed to a person.
