The map of prohibited marriages closes, and the verse turns to what remains: "Also ˹forbidden are˺ married women—except ˹female˺ captives in your possession. This is Allah's commandment to you. Lawful to you are all beyond these—as long as you seek them with your wealth in a legal marriage, not in fornication. Give those you have consummated marriage with their due dowries. It is permissible to be mutually gracious regarding the set dowry. Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise". One sentence forbids, one opens, one commands the dower, and one permits graciousness after it is fixed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Also ˹forbidden are˺ married women"** adds the married woman to the list. So long as a woman is bound to a husband, no other man may marry her. From this the verse settles, on its face, that a woman cannot live with more than one husband at a time.

**"except ˹female˺ captives in your possession"** makes the one exception, women acquired through war, as the sections below explain.

**"This is Allah's commandment to you"** means the prohibition was ordained by Allah, and the believer's part is to adhere to His Book, never to transgress His set limits, the ḥudūd, and to hold to His legislation and decrees.

**"Lawful to you are all beyond these"** opens everything not prohibited: the cousins on either side of the family, an uncle's wife once he has died or divorced her, the wife of an adopted son once he releases her, and, after one's wife has died, even her sister, provided no other bar exists.

**"as long as you seek them with your wealth in a legal marriage, not in fornication"** sets the method. A man may use his money to marry up to four wives, and for as many bondwomen as lawfully come to him, all through lawful means. The purpose named is chastity, the guarding of modesty, and not the spending of wealth on women for zinā. The fornicator also spends from his assets, but that spending is patently unlawful, and a woman procured by it never becomes lawful to enjoy.

**"Give those you have consummated marriage with their due dowries"** makes the dower an obligation once the marriage is enjoyed. **"It is permissible to be mutually gracious regarding the set dowry"** allows, after the duty is settled, an increase from the husband or a willing forfeiture from the wife.

**"Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise"** closes with the two names that govern everything before them.

### The occasion at Awtas

The exception was revealed over a real scruple. Imam Aḥmad recorded that Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī said:

> We captured some women from the area of Awtas who were already married, and we disliked having sexual relations with them because they already had husbands. So we asked the Prophet ﷺ about this matter, and this verse was revealed: "Also forbidden are married women, except those whom your right hands possess." Consequently, we had sexual relations with these women.

At-Tirmidhī, an-Nasāʾī, Ibn Jarīr and Muslim in his Ṣaḥīḥ collected it in this wording. The fuller reports tell of the day of Hunayn, when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ sent an army to Awtas, which met the enemy, defeated them and took many female prisoners. Some of the Companions were uncomfortable, for the women had husbands who were idolaters. In one version they said plainly: "O Prophet of Allah! How can we possibly have physical relationships with women whose lineage and husband we know very well?" And the verse came down.

The rule the verse carries is careful. Such women came into Muslim hands when jihād had to be carried out against the infidels of Dār al-Ḥarb, and they are lawful only after it is certain they are not pregnant, by waiting for the lapse of one menstruation, and if pregnancy appears, by waiting until the child is delivered.

### Why one husband and not several

Some ignorant and loudmouthed people in later times took the permission of plurality for wives to mean that women should equally enjoy several husbands. The verse answers them before they were born. A woman with more than one husband at once is not only a burden on herself and a disgrace to the men, it destroys lineage itself: when many men share one woman, no method remains of attributing the fatherhood of the child to any one of them, and when lineage goes unproved, who is charged with the mutual rights and duties of parents and children? Even on nature and reason alone, the case fails. The basic purpose of marriage is procreation, and several women can conceive from one man, while one woman conceives from one man only, so the procreative power of all the husbands but one is wasted. The woman is the more delicate, and there are long stretches and many conditions in which she cannot fulfil the rights of one husband, let alone several. And where a man's strength is above average, restriction to one wife drives him into impermissible ways that ruin society, a ruination hardly to be feared from the woman's side.

The law goes further than the moment. A divorced or widowed woman may not marry another until her ʿiddah, the waiting period, has expired. And where the wife embraces Islam while her husband remains a disbeliever in Dār al-Ḥarb, the non-Muslim land at war, she is released from the bond after three menstrual periods. Where she embraces Islam in Dār al-Islām, the legal authority induces the husband toward Islam, and if he refuses, the judge effects a separation that counts as a divorce, after which she completes her ʿiddah and may marry whom she chooses.

### What remains lawful, and the limit of four

The permission "all beyond these" is wide but not unmeasured. It is not permissible to have more than four wives at once, a ruling the surah has already given, and the absence of the number here should mislead no one into reading the generality as unrestricted. Beyond the women the Qur'an itself prohibits, the sunnah prohibits others, such as a woman together with her aunt, and the books of fiqh gather them all.

### The dower

Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ, in his Aḥkām al-Qur'ān, draws two rulings from the words "seek them with your wealth". First, marriage cannot stand without a dower. Even if the parties agree between themselves on a marriage without one, the dower remains necessary. Second, the mahr must be māl, wealth of some kind, property or assets. According to the Ḥanafiyyah it should not be less than ten dirhams, a dirham being equivalent to 17 grains Troy of silver.

The phrase "those you have consummated marriage with" was read by the entire ummah, from the revered early elders to their successors, as coition. The full dower falls due only when the husband has had that benefit of the marriage. If the contract was made but the wife never came to live with him and he divorces before consummation, half the mahr is due. Once the benefit has been received, any shortcoming in payment is against the letter and spirit of the law, and honour itself says the wife's due should not be delayed. The sharīʿah adds her weapon: where the dower is prompt, she may refuse to go to her husband until it is paid. The whole rule hangs on the Qur'an's own words: "It is not lawful for you to take back any of what you have given them" (2:229).

### The mutʿah question

Mujāhid stated that "those you have consummated marriage with" was revealed about the mutʿah, the temporary marriage that ends on a predetermined date, and a later sect built on that reading a claim that mutʿah is ḥalāl. The claim fails inside the verse itself. The Qur'an allows the seeking of women only "in a legal marriage, not in fornication", binding oneself in wedlock and not only for lust, and the relationship it approves is the one that aims at chastity through the permanent bond. Mutʿah is the opposite of that. It is a contract for a specified period in exchange for money, creating no permanent rights or obligations, no family, no aim of children, no inheritance for the woman, who does not even rank as a wife, and it drives its practitioners to hunt ever-new partners in temporary settings. It is not the guarantor of modesty and chastity. It is their enemy.

The prohibition is hadith upon hadith. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib reported that the Holy Prophet ﷺ, on the occasion of the battle of Khaybar, prohibited mutʿah with women and the eating of the meat of domesticated donkeys. The hadith appears in al-Bukhārī and in Muslim, the two Ṣaḥīḥs, word for word: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ prohibited mutʿah marriage and eating the meat of domesticated donkeys on the day of Khaybar." In his Ṣaḥīḥ, Muslim recorded that ar-Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabrah ibn Maʿbad al-Juhanī said that his father accompanied the Messenger of Allah ﷺ during the conquest of Makkah and heard him say:

> O people! I allowed you the mutʿah marriage with women before. Now, Allah has prohibited it until the Day of Resurrection. Therefore, anyone who has any women in mutʿah, let him let them go, and do not take anything from what you have given them.

At-Tirmidhī devoted a chapter to the question and reported the same two hadiths, the second from Ibn ʿAbbās, who said mutʿah was only in the early period of Islam, until this very verse came down and closed every private part but those of the legally wedded wife and the bondwoman one lawfully comes to own. It is true that Ibn ʿAbbās held mutʿah permissible up to a certain time. He revoked the position on the good counsel of ʿAlī, as recorded in the Ṣaḥīḥ of Muslim, and under the chastening impact of the verse itself. Strange, then, that the sect which claims to love and obey ʿAlī elects to oppose him on precisely this issue. The author of the Hidayah attributed permission to Imam Mālik, but scholars who explained that book clarified that the attribution was inadvertent. The author of Rūḥ al-Maʿānī reports from Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ the settled timeline: mutʿah was lawful before Khaybar, made unlawful during it, declared lawful on the day of the Conquest of Makkah, and three days later prohibited forever. And where two interpretations are equally possible, the settled principle of Islamic jurisprudence prefers the one supporting prohibition. The Qur'an's own words are explicit besides: those who guard their private parts, save from their wives or their bondwomen, are not to be blamed, and everything beyond that is unlawful. By extension, the time-bound marriage, al-nikāh al-muwaqqat, a marriage contracted with a fixed term even under the ordinary word nikāḥ, is equally unlawful.

### Graciousness after the settlement

The fixed dower is not frozen. Once the obligation is settled, the husband may add to it on his own accord, and the wife, willingly and happily, may forgo part of it or all of it. She may also agree to defer a dower that was originally prompt. Ibn Jarīr recorded from al-Ḥaḍramī a case of the mercy in it: men would designate a certain dowry and then fall into financial difficulties, and the verse teaches there is no harm in the mutual agreement that follows the requirement, for if she gives up part, the husband may accept.

### All-Knowing, All-Wise

The closing names carry the verse's authority. Allah knows. He is aware of everything, and if someone acts against His injunctions and no judge, ruler or other human being ever finds out, Allah in His most exalted state of being knows it all, so keep fearing Him in every circumstance. And the injunctions He revealed are all based on ḥikmah, wisdom, too deep to be understood by everyone. The commands concerning the lawful and the unlawful, whether or not one grasps their cause or justification, must be believed, accepted and obeyed, for the Creator and Master of the Command certainly knows them. There are educated but ignorant people, spread through contemporary societies, who go about searching for the causes of divine injunctions, and when they fail to find any, set the injunction aside as contrary to the modern age. The verse silences them forever: you are ignorant, and your Creator is All-Knowing. You lack understanding, and Allah is All-Wise. Do not make your reason the touchstone of the Truth.

The verse's whole frame is the law of desire. Natural desires make themselves felt in human beings, sexuality among them, and the Islamic law fixes a limit for every emotion. Only the alliance established through the standard, solemn social agreement of marriage is valid, and the family it builds must carry an environment of chastity.

### The inner reading

Read inwardly, the verse's permission has a condition of the heart. When you stay mindful of the ḥudūd, the limits, respect the ʿuhūd, the agreements, and obtain mutual consent with women according to the law, there is no enmity in it for people and no punishment to follow from the Real, for what is lawful carries no further reckoning.

### In short

- Married women are prohibited except those lawfully acquired in war, and only after pregnancy is ruled out, the scruple of the Companions at Awtas being the occasion of the ruling.
- All women beyond the prohibited degrees are lawful, sought with wealth in marriage and not in fornication, within the limit of four wives.
- No marriage stands without a dower, due in full upon consummation and halved by divorce before it, and gracious increase or willing forfeiture after the settlement is no sin.
- Mutʿah, the temporary marriage, is prohibited forever, by the verse's own words, by the two Ṣaḥīḥs from ʿAlī, and by the farewell prohibition at the Conquest of Makkah, and Ibn ʿAbbās's early leniency was retracted on ʿAlī's counsel.
- The verse ends where obedience begins: Allah knows what no court finds out, and His wisdom needs no one's permission.
