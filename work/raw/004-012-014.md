<<<VERSE 4:12>>>
The previous verse gave the shares of those related by birth. This verse turns to those related by marriage and then to a third case: "You will inherit half of what your wives leave if they are childless. But if they have children, then ˹your share is˺ one-fourth of the estate—after the fulfilment of bequests and debts. And your wives will inherit one-fourth of what you leave if you are childless. But if you have children, then your wives will receive one-eighth of your estate—after the fulfilment of bequests and debts. And if a man or a woman leaves neither parents nor children but only a brother or a sister ˹from their mother’s side˺, they will each inherit one-sixth, but if they are more than one, they ˹all˺ will share one-third of the estate—after the fulfilment of bequests and debts without harm ˹to the heirs˺. ˹This is˺ a commandment from Allah. And Allah is All-Knowing, Most Forbearing."

So the passage moves from lineage to marriage, and then to the case of a person who dies with no parents and no children, which the Arabic calls kalālah. Together with 4:11 and the last verse of the surah, this verse is one of the three that hold the knowledge of the fixed shares.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You will inherit half of what your wives leave if they are childless. But if they have children, then ˹your share is˺ one-fourth of the estate"**. Allah tells the husband that he receives half of what his wife leaves if she dies without a child. If she has a child, he receives one-fourth, after her bequests and debts. The word "child" is not limited to the husband's own children. It covers a child by another husband, and boys and girls alike. It also covers grandchildren and further descendants, as the scholars agree. After the husband takes his share, the rest goes to the other heirs, such as her parents, brothers and sisters, according to their own rules.

**"after the fulfilment of bequests and debts"**. These words come after each case and give the order in which the estate is used. In short, debts are paid first, then the will is carried out, and then the shares are divided. The scholars agree on this order.

**"And your wives will inherit one-fourth of what you leave if you are childless. But if you have children, then your wives will receive one-eighth of your estate"**. A wife receives one-fourth if her husband leaves no child and one-eighth if he does, whether the child is hers or another wife's. If the husband leaves more than one wife, each does not get a fourth or an eighth of her own. All the wives share that single fourth or eighth equally. What remains after the husband's or wife's share is divided among the other heirs.

**"And if a man or a woman leaves neither parents nor children but only a brother or a sister ˹from their mother’s side˺"**. This is the case of kalālah. The word comes from iklīl, the crown that goes round the head. It is said to mean that the person's heirs come from outside the first degree of relatives. The best-known definition is that a person who dies leaving no ancestors and no descendants is kalālah. Al-Qurṭubī reports a number of other definitions. Al-Ālūsī, the author of Rūḥ al-Maʿānī, explains that the word is a verbal noun from kalāl, to become worn out, and that it carries the idea of weakness. Any relationship other than that of father and son was called kalālah, because it is weak beside that bond. The word was also used for the deceased who left neither son nor father, for an heir who is neither the son nor the father of the dead person, and in the end for the property left by such a person. One explanation says it can mean any kin other than parents and children.

The verse says "a brother or a sister", and what is meant is a brother or sister from the mother's side. Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ and some others of the Salaf, the early generations, said so, and Qatādah reported that it was also the view of Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq. Saʿd's recitation of the verse has the words "from his mother". That recitation was not passed down by tawātur, the report of a vast number of people in every age. But the consensus of the whole Muslim ummah makes it worth following. There is another clear proof. Allah mentions the inheritance of kalālah again at the end of the surah. There it says that one sister receives a half, a brother inherits all of his sister's estate, two sisters receive two-thirds, and when there are several brothers and sisters, the male receives twice the female (4:176). That verse is about full siblings and siblings from the father's side only. If those were meant here as well, the two rulings would contradict each other.

**"they will each inherit one-sixth, but if they are more than one, they ˹all˺ will share one-third of the estate"**. A single brother or sister takes a sixth. If there are more, whether it is one brother with one sister or two of each, they all share one-third of the estate. A male does not take twice a female here, and the males and females have equal shares. Half-siblings from the mother's side differ from other heirs in four ways. First, they inherit because of their mother. Second, males and females among them receive the same. Third, they inherit only when the deceased is kalālah, since they have no share if he leaves a living father, grandfather, child or grandchild. Fourth, they never receive more than a third, however many they are.

**"after the fulfilment of bequests and debts without harm ˹to the heirs˺"**. The will should be fair and free of any kind of harm. It must not take from some rightful heirs all or part of their share, and it must not add to the portion Allah fixed for some heirs. Whoever does this is arguing with Allah about His decision and His division. An authentic hadith says:

> "Allah has given each person his due right. So there is no will for an heir."

Other explanations spell out the harm. One is leaving more than a third by will. The words "without harm" warn that even though carrying out the will and paying the debts come before the heirs' shares, this priority must never be misused to wrong the true heirs.

**"˹This is˺ a commandment from Allah. And Allah is All-Knowing, Most Forbearing"**. It is imperative to act on all that has been laid down about the shares, the payment of debts and the carrying out of wills. This is a command of great weight, and nobody should do anything that goes against it. The warning is added that Allah is All-Knowing and Forbearing. He knows everything, and He fixed these shares knowing the true state of every person. Whoever obeys will not find his good deed outside Allah's knowledge. Whoever disobeys will find his wrong conduct set out before Allah, and he will answer for it. A person who has wronged his heirs by a false debt or a harmful will is known to Allah as well. No one should feel safe from His punishment. Allah may not punish at once in this world, because He is Forbearing, but the one who disobeys should not fool himself into thinking he got away with it. Another explanation says that "Forbearing" points to the ignorance and deception that happen among people when they divide an estate. Allah does not hasten His punishment despite them.

### Why the husband is named first

The husband's share is mentioned first, perhaps to show how important it is. After a wife dies, her husband becomes part of another family. If she dies in her parents' house, and her property is there too, her people might avoid giving the husband what is due to him. By naming his right first, the Qur'an may well be condemning that practice.

### Pay the dower first

Before the estate is divided, it must be checked whether the wife's dower, her mahr, has been paid. If her husband did not pay it, it counts as a debt and is paid first from the whole estate, like any other debt. The woman then receives her fixed share as an heir on top of it. If the estate is worth no more than the dower, the whole estate goes to her against that debt. In that case no other heir receives anything, because nothing is left.

### Abū Bakr and the word kalālah

Ash-Shaʿbī reported that when Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq was asked what kalālah means, he answered, "I will give my own opinion. If it is right, then its rightness is from Allah." He went on, "If it is wrong, the error is mine and comes from Shayṭān's evil efforts, and Allah and His Messenger are free of it." Then he said, "Kalālah is a man who has neither descendants nor ascendants." When ʿUmar became khalīfah, he said, "I hesitate to go against an opinion of Abū Bakr." Ibn Jarīr and others recorded this.

Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded in his Tafsīr that Ibn ʿAbbās said, "I was one of the last people to see ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb. He said to me, 'What you said was the right opinion.' I asked, 'What did I say?' He said, 'That kalālah is a person with no child or parents.'" Al-Ḥakam, Jābir ibn Zayd, Qatādah, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, an-Nakhaʿī, ash-Shaʿbī, Zayd ibn Thābit, Ibn Masʿūd and ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib held the same view. It is the view of the people of al-Madīnah, Kūfah and Baṣrah, of the Seven Fuqahāʾ and the Four Imams, and of most scholars past and present, so some scholars declare a consensus on it.

### Wills that harm the heirs

A will in favour of an heir is not lawful. If someone makes a will for his son, his daughter, his husband or wife, or anyone else who will have a share of his estate, it cannot be enforced. The heirs receive what is theirs and are not entitled to more. The Prophet ﷺ said in his famous address at his last Ḥajj:

> "Allah has given every person his right. So there is no will for an heir."

This is in Mishkāt, from Abū Dāwūd. If the other heirs agree, however, a will in favour of one heir may be carried out first, and the rest of the estate is divided by the Sharīʿah's method, with that heir also taking his fixed share. The hadiths carry this exception in the words "unless the heirs wish", as al-Hidāyah notes.

If someone makes a will, or a false admission of debt, to deprive his heirs, he does something strictly forbidden and a major sin. There are many ways to do harm through debt and will. A man may lie that he is in debt, so that the money goes to a friend. He may present something he owns outright as something he holds in trust for another, so that it stays out of the estate. He may will more than a third. He may lie about a loan he made, claiming it was repaid so that it will not pass to his heirs. Or, in the illness that ends in his death, he may give away more than a third. Anyone who is about to leave this world should do his best to stay away from all of these.

The words "without harm" appear only with the rules for kalālah, yet the rule is general. It is understood at the two earlier places in these verses where will and debt come first. It applies not just to a person who dies as kalālah, but to everyone who makes a will.

### Why "will" comes before "debt" in the wording

The shares are given three times in this passage, and each time they are said to come after the will and the debt. As stated, one-third of what remains after the funeral costs and debts is used for the will. If the will goes beyond a third, it cannot be enforced. In law the debt comes before the will. If the debts use up the whole estate, there is no will to carry out and nothing to divide.

Yet in the verse's wording the will is named before the debt, and this gives the impression that the will comes first. A report removes the doubt: "You recite the verse with the will first, but the Prophet ﷺ settled the debt before the will."

Al-Ālūsī gives the reason for the order of the words. The will is named first to stress that it must be carried out. The person who benefits from a will gets it without paying anything for it and often has no tie of kinship with the dead person. The heirs might ignore it or put it off, because they do not like to see property that would have been theirs go to someone else. Not everyone has debts, and one who has them in his lifetime has not necessarily left them unpaid at his death. Even if a debt remains, the creditors' claim is one the heirs cannot refuse, so the chance of falling short is small. A will is different. The dead person earnestly wished that part of his wealth be put into something good that would serve as ongoing charity for him. No claimant stands behind it, so the heirs themselves might fall short. To offset that risk, the will is named first, as a special measure.

### The inner reading

Inheritance and the right to receive it rest on one of two grounds, a cause or a bloodline. Marriage is the cause, and kinship is the bloodline. Marriage brings love, as Allah says: "And He has placed between you compassion and mercy" (30:21). Lineage is help and strength, as in the saying, "A man is many through his brothers."

When a relative by blood or a person close through the cause of marriage dies, it wounds the heart and hurts the spirit. The Lord of the Worlds puts balm on that pain. After the suffering He commands comfort through the dead person's wealth. As the pain came from his passing, so the balm comes from his property. This is the Lord's way with His friends. If He lays a hardship on them by prescribing the Law, then after the hardship He makes a treasure appear, to ease it.

Shaykh al-Islām Anṣārī said:

> "How could I have known that suffering is the mother of happiness, and that under one disappointment lie a thousand treasures? That hope is the messenger of union, and that beneath the cloud of generosity despair is impossible? That the Lovingly Kind is so forbearing that His gentleness to the sinner is beyond counting? How servant-caressing the Possessor of Majesty is, and how much joy His friends find in Him? That what I seek is in the middle of the spirit, and that the glory of union with Him is my opening?"

A line of verse goes with this:

> One night in all my life, near dawn, the image of that comfort of the spirit came to me and asked, "How are you, wounded one?" I said, "In my passion for You, that is the first opening."

### In short

- A husband receives half of his wife's estate if she leaves no child and a quarter if she does. A wife receives a quarter if her husband leaves no child and an eighth if he does. Several wives share that quarter or eighth equally.
- Where a person leaves neither parents nor children (kalālah) and has a brother or sister from the mother's side, each receives a sixth. If there are more than one, they share a third, with males and females equal. The last verse of the surah covers full and paternal siblings.
- Everything comes after the dower, the debts and a will of no more than a third. A will for an heir is not lawful unless the other heirs allow it, and false debts and other tricks to deprive heirs are a major sin.
- Abū Bakr's definition of kalālah, no descendants and no ascendants, was accepted by ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās and most scholars.
- Allah knows everything and is Forbearing, but His patience is no reason to feel safe. Inwardly, the wealth of the dead is balm for the pain of losing them.

<<<VERSE 4:13>>>
After setting out the shares, the Qur'an gives a promise and a warning, as it usually does after stating beliefs and rulings: "These ˹entitlements˺ are the limits set by Allah. Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger will be admitted into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there forever. That is the ultimate triumph." The aim is to press Muslims to obey these rules.

### Phrase by phrase

**"These ˹entitlements˺ are the limits set by Allah"**. The fixed shares of inheritance are Allah's limits. They include what He has set aside for each heir according to the degree of relation to the dead person and the degree of dependence on him. They must not be crossed or broken. Other explanations call the limits the legal rulings and duties Allah has imposed. One says that the rulings about orphans and what came after them are His bounds, laws He has marked out for His servants so that they act on them and do not go beyond them.

On the inner side, His limits are His commands and prohibitions, and the means by which His servants show their devotion. The root of servanthood, ʿubūdiyyah, is keeping to the limits and keeping the promises, the ʿuhūd. Whoever keeps His limit will not be reached by anything bad or harmful. The root of every affliction is going beyond the limits.

**"Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger"**. The obedience meant here is about inheritance. It means not adding to these fixed shares or cutting them down by tricks and schemes. Instead the person gives each heir his appointed share, as Allah commanded, ordained and decided. Another explanation says it means obeying in the division of estates. A short one says it means following Allah and His Messenger in His instructions and staying away from what He has forbidden.

**"will be admitted into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there forever"**. One explanation says that the rivers flow beneath the dwellings and trees, and that they are rivers of wine, water, honey and milk. The people there will live in Paradise forever and will never die or leave it. In a variant reading, the verb for "will admit him" is in the first person plural: "We will admit him." Another short explanation says the gardens have rivers flowing beneath their palaces.

**"That is the ultimate triumph"**. This is the greatest salvation, achieved by entering Paradise. It is the great success, and no other success comes near it.

### Who may not inherit

The distribution of an estate is based on nearness of kinship, but there are exceptions. The Sunnah specifies that the people named may receive their share as long as they are not barred by having committed murder, by belonging to a different religion, or by being slaves.

First, the dead person and his heir must not be of different religions. A Muslim does not inherit from a kāfir, and a kāfir does not inherit from a Muslim, whatever family tie they have. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "The Muslim does not inherit from the kāfir, nor does the kāfir inherit from the Muslim."

This is recorded in Mishkāt. It concerns someone who is a Muslim or a kāfir from birth. If a person who was a Muslim leaves Islam and dies or is killed as an apostate, everything he earned while he was Muslim goes to his Muslim heirs. What he earned after his apostasy is paid into the Bayt al-Māl, the public treasury. If a woman becomes an apostate, all of her property, whether acquired during her days as a Muslim or after, goes to her Muslim heirs. An apostate, man or woman, does not inherit from any Muslim or from another apostate.

Second, a killer is shut out. If someone kills a person from whose estate he would have had a share, he is no longer an heir and receives nothing from the one he killed. The Prophet ﷺ said, "The killer does not inherit." This is recorded in Mishkāt too. Some kinds of accidental homicide, qatl al-khaṭāʾ, are an exception, and the details are in the books of fiqh.

### The unborn child and the woman waiting out her ʿiddah

If a man leaves children and his wife is pregnant, the unborn child counts among the heirs. It is hard or uncertain to know the child's sex or the number of children, so it is best to delay the distribution until the birth. If the estate must be divided at once, one should suppose two cases, a boy and a girl, and give the other heirs the smaller of the two portions. What is left is held for the child who will be born.

If a man divorces his wife by a divorce that can be revoked and dies before revoking it and before her waiting period, her ʿiddah, ends, she receives a share, because the marriage is still in force. If he divorces her during the illness that ends in his death, then even if the divorce is irrevocable or pronounced three times, she still receives a share if he dies before her waiting period ends. For this purpose, the longer of two waiting periods is used. The waiting period after a divorce is three menstrual periods, and after the death of a husband it is four lunar months and ten days. Whichever lasts longer is taken as her ʿiddah, so that she may inherit wherever possible.

If a man divorces his wife irrevocably or by three pronouncements before any illness that ends in his death, and he dies a few days later during her waiting period, she gets no share. If the divorce was revocable, she does inherit. If a wife obtains a separation at her own request, khulʿ, during his final illness, she does not inherit even if he dies during her ʿiddah.

### Fixed shares, residuary heirs and the rest

The Sharīʿah fixes shares for twelve kinds of heir. They are called the aṣḥāb al-furūḍ, those with obligatory shares in the Qur'an. If no such heir exists, or if something is left after they receive their shares, the remainder goes to the ʿaṣabāt, the residuary heirs on the male side. A person sometimes inherits in both capacities. In other cases, the dead person's children and father, or the father's offspring, such as a brother, also become residuary heirs. There are several kinds of ʿaṣabāt, and the books on the shares give the details.

An example shows how it works. Zayd died leaving a wife, a daughter, a mother and an uncle. His estate is split into twenty-four parts. Half of that, twelve parts, goes to the daughter. The wife has an eighth, which is three parts. The mother has a sixth, which is four parts. The residue of five parts goes to the uncle, as the nearest male relative on the father's side.

If there are no residuary heirs, the remainder returns to those with fixed shares in proportion to their shares. This is called radd, return. A husband or wife does not receive any of this return. If there is no one in either group, the estate goes to the dhawū al-arḥām, relatives on the mother's side. This is a large group that includes the children of a daughter or sister, paternal aunts, maternal uncles and maternal aunts. There is a lot more to say on this subject than can be covered here, but the remarks above should be enough.

### In short

- The fixed shares of inheritance are Allah's limits. Obeying them by giving each heir his appointed share, with no tricks, leads to Gardens and the great triumph.
- Those who differ in religion do not inherit from each other, and a killer does not inherit from his victim. The Sunnah also bars slaves.
- An unborn child counts as an heir, and the estate should wait for its birth. A wife in her waiting period may inherit, depending on the kind of divorce and whether the husband was in his final illness.
- After the fixed shares, the remainder goes to the residuary heirs, then back to those with fixed shares, then to maternal relatives.
- Inwardly, servanthood is keeping Allah's limits and promises, and every affliction begins with crossing them.

<<<VERSE 4:14>>>
This verse is the other side of the promise before it: "But whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger and exceeds their limits will be cast into Hell, to stay there forever. And they will suffer a humiliating punishment." The shares of inheritance are not only matters of family arrangement. They are among those on which a person's entry into Paradise or Hell can depend.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger"**. The disobedience meant here concerns the division of estates. Another explanation says it covers anyone who does not follow His laws or who doubts them. According to another, the person has changed what Allah ordained and has disputed with His judgment. This is the conduct of those who do not accept what Allah has decided and divided.

**"and exceeds their limits"**. This means going against His rulings and duties through deviation and disobedience. It means overstepping the limits He has made.

**"will be cast into Hell, to stay there forever"**. The Arabic can be read in two ways, as "He will admit him" or as "We will admit him". One explanation says the person remains in the Fire until whenever Allah wills. A grammar note on both this verse and the last explains that the singular pronouns and verbs agree with the word for "whoever", while the plural in "to stay there forever" agrees with the general plural meaning of that word.

**"And they will suffer a humiliating punishment"**. This is a punishment through which the person will be put to shame. It is also said to mean a severe torment. It is an eternal, painful torment with humiliation, and this is why Allah punishes those who dispute what He decided and divided.

### The unfair will

Aḥmad recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> "A man may do the deeds of righteous people for seventy years, but when it is time to write his will, he commits an injustice. His last deed is his worst, and he enters the Fire. And a man may do the deeds of evil people for seventy years, yet he is fair in his will. His last deed is his best, and he enters Paradise."

Abū Hurayrah said, "Read, if you wish: 'These are the limits set by Allah,' up to 'a disgraceful torment.'"

Abū Dāwūd has a chapter on injustice in wills in his Sunan. There Abū Hurayrah reports that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "A man or a woman may act in obedience to Allah for sixty years. Yet when they are near death, they leave an unfair will, and so they earn the Fire."

Abū Hurayrah then recited the verse about wills "without harm", and the words "that is the great success". At-Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah also recorded it, and at-Tirmidhī called it ḥasan gharīb.

### Two punishments, one now and one later

There are two punishments, one that comes at once and one that is postponed, and shame joins them. If created beings tried to degrade the disobedient as much as their own disobedience has already degraded them, they could not manage it. This is why a poet said that a person who spends a night in sin wakes up already wearing his disgrace, wa-ʿalayhi madhallatuhu. And of one who wakes up and spends the day in pious acts, people say that he has his dignity upon him, wa-ʿalayhi mahābatuhu.

### Following Allah's law, not family convenience

Paying orphans what they are due, keeping to wills and handing the inheritance to the heirs are among the matters on which a person's going to Hell or Paradise depends. Islamic law allows a will for a third of one's property. But anyone who makes a will with the aim of depriving someone of his rightful inheritance commits a sin that could condemn him to Hell. A person must follow Allah's law in this matter, and not his own desires or the convenience of his family.

### In short

- Whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger in the shares of inheritance and goes beyond His limits will be put in the Fire to stay there, with a humiliating punishment.
- This covers changing what Allah fixed, doubting His laws, or disputing His judgment.
- The Prophet ﷺ warned that a man may do righteous deeds for decades and then lose everything by an unfair will at the end of his life.
- Disobedience brings two punishments, one in this life, in disgrace, and one later. A person is to follow Allah's law, not his own wishes or his family's convenience.
