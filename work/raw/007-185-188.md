<<<VERSE 7:185>>>
The verse asks the deniers to look at what stands in front of them. "Have they ever reflected on the wonders of the heavens and the earth, and everything Allah has created, and that perhaps their end is near? So what message after this ˹Quran˺ would they believe in"

### Phrase by phrase

Verse 185 invites human beings to two things. The first is to observe the creation of Allah with thought, the heavens, the earth and what exists between them. The second is mindfulness of the period of life given to a person, which may end at any moment.

**"Have they ever reflected on the wonders of the heavens and the earth, and everything Allah has created"** puts the first invitation as a question to the people who denied the faith. Did they never think about the signs of Allah in the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. About what was created in them? The word used for the kingdom carries the sense of a superlative, the great kingdom. The dominion of the heavens covers the sun, the moon, the stars and the clouds. The dominion of the earth covers its trees, its mountains, its oceans and its beasts.

The point of the reflection is set out plainly. A person who thinks about all of this takes a lesson from it. The lesson is that the One who holds all of it has no equal and no rival. All of it was made by the One who alone deserves worship and sincere religion. The reflection is meant to bring a person to faith in Him and in His Messenger. To the oneness of their Creator, which is what the signs of the Book call the people of Makkah to see. It is meant to bring them to obedience to Allah, to the rejection of every rival to Him and to the rejection of the idols.

The reflection asked for is not the work of a genius. The signs of the power and the wisdom of Allah are in everything around us. A little serious thought is enough to see that every particle of this world praises its Maker and stands as a proof of His power and His wisdom. Anyone who applies his intellect in conscious observation of things cannot escape the natural demand of faith in Allah, the One.

**"and that perhaps their end is near"** gives the second invitation. The word rendered as perhaps is used of Allah for what is required to come about. The reflection is that no man knows how much of his life is left to him. Being mindful of that uncertainty makes a person more active in his aims. The crimes and the evil deeds of people are stirred up by disregard for death. Those who are conscious of the indefinite period of their life keep away from what they should not do. The Prophet ﷺ gave the instruction that a person should frequently remember the thing that cuts off the pleasures, which is death.

The verse also carries a warning inside that phrase. A person should fear that his life has already drawn to its end. That he will meet his death as a disbeliever, ending in the punishment and the severe torment of Allah.

**"So what message after this ˹Quran˺ would they believe in?"** closes the verse. The Book has brought them warnings and threats from Allah, and the question asks what further warning could compel them if these do not. The signs of the right path and of the true faith have been given to them, both within themselves and outside them. A people who are heedless of signs so clear have nothing left in the world to guide them.

One report puts the same words plainly. Do these people learn nothing from seeing the control of Allah over the heavens and the earth. Over all the animals and plants and other things He created in them. From their own life spans, whose end may be near? Let them turn back to Allah in repentance before the time is gone. If they do not have faith in the Qur'an and in the promises it carries, what other book will they believe in?

### In short

- The verse calls human beings to think about the creation of Allah in the heavens and the earth, where every particle praises its Maker and stands as a proof of His power and His wisdom. To see that the One who holds all of it has no equal and deserves worship alone.
- The second call is to be mindful of the life given to a person, which may end at any moment. The word for perhaps carries the sense of what is bound to come about when it is used of Allah.
- The warning inside the verse is that a person should fear that his life has reached its end while he still denies. That he will meet death as a disbeliever, ending in severe punishment.
- No genius is required for the reflection. A little serious thought is enough to make a person see that everything created is a proof of the power and wisdom of Allah. Anyone who uses his intellect in that observation cannot escape the demand of faith in Allah, the One.
- The verse closes by asking what further message could move the deniers if the warnings of this Book do not, since the signs of the right path have been given to them within themselves and outside them. One report adds the plain question of what other book they would believe in.

<<<VERSE 7:186>>>
Verse 186 states what follows when the guidance is refused. "Whoever Allah allows to stray, none can guide, leaving them to wander blindly in their defiance"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever Allah allows to stray, none can guide"** states the rule. Those who were destined to be misguided have no one to lead them to the guidance. No effort of their own will bring it to them. A passage of the Book puts the same truth in the words of Allah to His Messenger ﷺ, that the one whom Allah wishes to place in fitnah, in error or in trial, is beyond any help that the Prophet could give him against Allah (5:41). Another passage says that when the Prophet is told to say that all that is in the heavens and the earth belongs to Allah, the answer comes that neither the signs nor the warners benefit a people who do not believe (10:101).

**"leaving them to wander blindly in their defiance"** names the state they are left in. They wander on in their disbelief and their error, hesitating out of perplexity and not being directed to anything that will benefit them in the end.

### The consolation in the verse

The verses before this one spoke of the stubborn ignorance of the disbelievers, who rejected the true faith in spite of every clear sign given to them. A prophet who was the greatest well-wisher of human beings could take that refusal as a source of grief.

The verse gives him his consolation. The one whom Allah leaves wandering in his ignorance. Whom He lets go on with his evil deeds, cannot be guided by anyone. He is left to the mercy of his own low desires.

The point of the consolation is that the Prophet ﷺ had done his duty. He had conveyed the message of Allah as clearly as it could be conveyed. The rejection of the true faith was not the fault of any shortcoming in him.

Three subjects stand at the centre of this surah, and they are the mainstay of the whole religion. The first is the oneness of Allah, the second is prophethood. The third is the life of the world to come. The first two have been set out in the verses that came before. The verse that follows this one takes up the third.

### In short

- The rule is that the person whom Allah leaves to stray has no one to guide him. No effort of his own will bring the guidance to him.
- Two passages of the Book are quoted for it, one stating that the person whom Allah places in error is beyond any help against Allah (5:41). One stating that neither the signs nor the warners benefit a people who do not believe (10:101).
- Such people are left to wander in their disbelief and their error, hesitating in perplexity and never directed to what would benefit them. One report adds that they are left to the mercy of their own low desires.
- The verse consoles the Prophet ﷺ over the refusal of his people, since the one whom Allah leaves in his ignorance cannot be guided by anyone. It shows that he had carried out his duty by conveying the message as clearly as it can be conveyed.
- The surah turns on three subjects, the oneness of Allah, prophethood and the life of the world to come. The first two have been set out in the verses before this one, while verse 187 takes up the third.

<<<VERSE 7:187>>>
The verse answers the question that the deniers kept putting to the Prophet ﷺ. "They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ regarding the Hour, "When will it be?" Say, "That knowledge is only with my Lord. He alone will reveal it when the time comes. It is too tremendous for the heavens and the earth and will only take you by surprise." They ask you as if you had full knowledge of it. Say, "That knowledge is only with Allah, but most people do not know.""

### Phrase by phrase

Verse 187 speaks of the third of the great subjects of the surah, the life of the world to come. Its revelation is tied to an incident. The report of Qatadah, carried by Ibn Jarir and by 'Abd ibn Humaid, gives the setting. The Quraish of Makkah once asked the Prophet ﷺ, in mockery, when the Hour of doom would come, the Hour he warned them about so often. The verse came down in answer to their question.

**"They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ regarding the Hour, "When will it be?""** reports the question itself. The word used for the Hour means a short period of time, a unit of the day and the night. In the language of the Book it stands for the day when every created being will cease to exist. Also for the day when the creation will be raised and brought before the Lord of all the worlds. The question about its appointed term means the question about its beginning, and that is how Ibn 'Abbas read it. The people were asking when the end of this world would begin.

The question came from the Quraysh and not from the Jews, since the verse was revealed in Makkah. The Quraysh used to ask about the Hour because they denied it and made light of its coming. One passage gives their question, when they ask when this promise will come about, if the warning is true (10:48). Another passage draws the difference between the two tempers. Those who do not believe in the Hour try to hurry it on. Those who believe stand in fear of it and know that it is the truth. Those who dispute about the Hour are far away in their error (42:18). Another passage says of the Prophet that people ask him about the Hour (33:63).

**"Say, "That knowledge is only with my Lord. He alone will reveal it when the time comes.""** is the answer the Prophet ﷺ was commanded to give. When he was asked about the appointed term, he was to refer the knowledge of it to Allah. Only Allah knows when the Hour will certainly occur, and no one besides Him holds that knowledge.

**"It is too tremendous for the heavens and the earth"** describes the weight of the matter they were asking about. The words are read in two ways. Qatadah, in the report of 'Abdur-Razzaq from Ma'mar, said that the knowledge of it lies heavy on the residents of the heavens and the earth, since they have no knowledge of it. Al-Hasan said that when the Hour arrives, its weight will be felt by the residents of the heavens and the earth.

Ad-Dahhak reported from Ibn 'Abbas that every creature will suffer its heaviness on the Day of Resurrection. Ibn Jurayj said that when it begins the heavens will be torn, the stars will be scattered, the sun will be wound round and lose its light, the mountains will be made to pass away. Everything else that Allah has spoken of will take place. That is what the weight of it means. As-Suddi said that its knowledge is hidden in the heavens and the earth. That no one knows its appointed term, not even a close angel or a sent Messenger.

**"and will only take you by surprise"** states that the Hour will begin all of a sudden, while people are unaware of it. Qatadah reported the word of the Prophet ﷺ on it. The Hour will start for a people while a man is mending the trough for his animals. Or giving water to his cattle. Or putting his wares out in the market. Or weighing goods with a scale that he lowers and then raises. This is the business of buying and selling.

Al-Bukhari recorded from Abu Hurairah the report that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that the Hour will not begin until the sun rises from the west. When it rises and the people see it, every one of them will believe. That is the moment when faith will not benefit a soul that did not believe beforehand and did not earn good in its faith. The Hour will begin while a garment lies spread out between two men. They will have no time either to conclude the sale or to fold it up. It will begin after a man has milked his animal and before he can drink. It will begin while a man is digging a watering place for his animals and before he can use the pool. It will begin while a man has raised a bite of food to his mouth and before he eats it.

**"They ask you as if you had full knowledge of it."** describes the manner of their asking. Al-'Awfi reported that Ibn 'Abbas said about this phrase that the people asked as if the Prophet had good relations and friendship with them. When the pagans of the Quraysh put their question to him, the Prophet ﷺ was asked as though he were their close friend. Allah revealed to him that the knowledge is with Him alone. That He had not informed any close angel or Messenger of it. Mujahid, in the report of Ibn Abi Najih, read the phrase as meaning that the people asked as if the Prophet had himself asked about its time. So that the knowledge of it had come to him.

**"Say, "That knowledge is only with Allah, but most people do not know.""** is the closing answer. The words are for emphasis. Most of mankind do not know that the knowledge of the Hour lies with Allah alone. They do not know that the question is not for them to press.

### The coming of Jibril

One well-known report addresses the same matter from another side. Jibril came to the Prophet ﷺ in the shape of a bedouin man in order to teach the people the affairs of their religion. He sat beside the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and put his questions to him as a learner would.

Jibril asked him about Islam, then about iman, the faith, and then about ihsan, the excellence of worship. He then asked when the Hour would start. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ answered that the one who is questioned about it knows no more of it than the person who puts the question. By that answer the Prophet ﷺ was saying that he had no more knowledge of it than Jibril. That no one has more knowledge of it than anyone else.

The Prophet ﷺ then recited the passage which says that the knowledge of the Hour is with Allah alone (31:34). In another narration Jibril asked about the portents of the Hour. The Prophet ﷺ named them. In that narration he said that five matters have their knowledge with Allah only. Then he recited the same passage.

Something in his answers left the Companions wondering. After every answer the questioner said that the Prophet ﷺ had spoken the truth. So that the Companions were astonished at a man who asked questions and then confirmed every answer he was given. When Jibril had gone away, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told the Companions that the man was Jibril. That he had come to teach them the affairs of their religion. In yet another narration the Prophet ﷺ said that he had recognised Jibril in every shape he came to him in, except that one.

### The nearness of the Hour

The reports that have come down carry the same answer in different forms.

Muslim recorded that 'Aishah said that when the bedouins came to the Prophet ﷺ, their habit was to put the question of the Hour to him. The Prophet ﷺ would point at the youngest person among them and say that if this young man lived, he would not become old before the Hour of those people came. The Prophet ﷺ meant the end of their life, which introduces a person to the life of the barzakh, the stage between this life and the world to come.

Muslim also recorded that Anas said that a man asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about the Hour. The Messenger ﷺ answered that if this young boy lived, it might be that he would not become old before the Hour started. Only Muslim collected that hadith.

Jabir bin 'Abdullah said that he heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ saying, one month before he died, that the people kept asking him about the Hour while its knowledge was with Allah. He swore by Allah that no living soul on the face of the earth at that time would be alive a hundred years later. Muslim collected the hadith, and a similar hadith is recorded in the Two Sahihs from Ibn 'Umar, who explained that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ meant that his generation would have reached its appointed term by that time.

The Sahih also records, from Anas and from Sahl bin Sa'd, that the Prophet ﷺ said that his sending and the Hour are like two fingers held together. He joined his index finger and his middle finger. The same Prophet ﷺ was commanded to leave the knowledge of the Hour to Allah whenever he was asked about it.

### The night of Isra and the three messengers

Imam Ahmad recorded that Ibn Mas'ud said that the Prophet ﷺ said that during the night of Isra he met Ibrahim, Musa and 'Isa. That they mentioned the matter of the Hour among themselves. They asked Ibrahim about it and he said that he had no knowledge of it. They asked Musa about it and he said the same. They then asked 'Isa, and he said that as for the time of its occurrence, only Allah knows it.

'Isa went on to tell them what his Lord had conveyed to him. The false messiah would appear, and he would have two staffs with him. When the false messiah saw him he would dissolve the way lead dissolves, and Allah would destroy him at the sight of him. The tree and the stone would say, O Muslim, there is a disbeliever behind me, so come and kill him. Allah would destroy the false messiah and his army, and the people would return safely to their lands and their homes.

After that, Gog and Magog would appear, swarming from every mound and sweeping over the earth, destroying everything they passed and drinking every water source they came to. The people would come to 'Isa complaining about them and calling on Allah against them. Allah would bring death to all of them, until the earth stank with their odour. Then Allah would send down rain on them, and the rain would carry their bodies away until it threw them into the sea. 'Isa said that his Lord had conveyed to him that when all of this happens, the Hour will be like a pregnant woman whose term is full, whose family does not know when she will come upon them with the birth, whether by night or by day.

Ibn Majah collected a similar hadith. The point of the report is that the greatest of the Messengers were asked about the appointed term of the Hour and none of them had knowledge of it. They asked 'Isa, and he spoke about its signs instead. He will descend in the last generations of this ummah, and he will put the law of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ into practice. He will kill the false messiah and destroy Gog and Magog by the blessing of his supplication. In all of that he passed on only what Allah had given him knowledge of.

### The signs that precede the Hour

Imam Ahmad recorded that Hudhayfah said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was asked about the Hour. He answered that its knowledge is with his Lord, and that none can reveal its time except Him. He then said that he would tell them about its portents and the signs that would come before it. That before it began there would be fitnah, the trials, and harj.

They asked what the harj was, since they knew what the fitnah meant. He said that it is a word for killing in the language of the Ethiopians. He then said that isolation and loneliness would become common among people. So that almost no one would be able to recognise anyone else.

None of the collectors of the six Sunan books carried that hadith through that chain. The report of Tariq bin Shihab says that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ kept mentioning the Hour, because people kept asking him about it, until this verse came down. An-Nasa'i collected that report, and it has a strong chain.

### The report from the pulpit of Basrah

The same answer came down through another chain, in which the name of Yunus is found. The words in it are those of Abu Musa. He said that the words he was passing on were heard by him on a Friday, from the pulpit of the main mosque of Basrah. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ was asked about the timing of the last Hour while he was present. He said that none knows its timing except Allah. That He alone will make it manifest at its proper time.

The Prophet ﷺ then said that he would tell them about its signs and about what would happen ahead of it. That there would be a lot of trials and a lot of harj before the last Hour. They asked what the harj meant. He said that it is an Abyssinian word which means killing. He went on to speak of the dryness that would come over the hearts of people. Of the state in which a person is cast among strangers. So that people hardly know one another. The people of good minds would disappear, and only a group of weak people would remain, who would neither know good nor forbid evil.

### The names of the Prophet ﷺ in the same passage

The reports describe the Prophet ﷺ with a series of names in the same context. He is the unlettered Prophet, the chief of the Messengers and their seal. He is the Prophet of mercy and the Prophet of repentance. He is Al-Malhmah, the one at whose coming the disbelievers met their great demise. He is Al-'Aqib, the one who came after the many prophets. He is Al-Muqaffi, the last of a succession. He is Al-Hashir, the one below whom all people will be gathered on the Day of Gathering. It is this Prophet ﷺ who, as the Sahih records from Anas and from Sahl bin Sa'd, said that his sending and the Hour are like two fingers held side by side.

### The two misconceptions behind the question

The question of the Quraysh carried two mistakes inside it, and the verse answers both.

The first mistake was to think that the Prophet ﷺ had somehow obtained knowledge of the exact time of the Hour and was withholding it from them for some purpose of his own. The verse tells him to answer that the knowledge lies with Allah alone, and that most people do not know it. It is not necessary for a prophet to know the exact time of the Hour. Or the other matters that Allah keeps to Himself. A prophet's not knowing such matters cannot be turned into an argument against his prophethood, since the knowledge given to a prophet can never equal the all-encompassing knowledge of Allah.

The Prophet ﷺ was, however, given knowledge of certain signs that would show the nearness of the Hour. He informed the Muslim ummah of them in clear terms. He said, in one tradition, that his coming and the Day of the Hour stand as close as two fingers of a hand.

A second matter is tied to the same question, and the reports address it directly. Some books carry reports that the total age of this world is seven thousand years. Those reports were borrowed from Israelite literature. They carry no authenticity, and they have no basis in the Qur'an or in the sunnah. The physical data of modern knowledge, which gives the age of the world in millions of years, does not confront or contradict any verse of the Book or any authentic tradition of the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ did give the Muslim ummah a measure of the matter in a tradition. He said that beside the early peoples they were a single white hair on the body of a black ox.

The scholar Ibn Hazm of Spain concluded from all of this that the exact age of this world cannot be assessed by anyone. The reasoning given for the conclusion is that an exact assessment of the age of the universe would yield the exact time of its end. That is the very knowledge which the verse shuts away from every creature. Al-Bukhari and Muslim report from Abu Hurayrah the word of the Prophet ﷺ that the coming of the Day has been made a close secret. The wisdom behind the concealment is also set out in the reports. People live their lives in peace because the appointed time is hidden from them, since a known date of death would destroy that peace. And those who do not believe in the Hour would have made a mockery of it had they known the long period that remains. The divine wisdom left the time undefined so that people would live in fear of its dreadful events, which is itself a strong restraint against crime and corruption.

Once a person believes that the Day will certainly come. That every created being will be presented before the Lord of the creation and called to account for the smallest and the greatest of his deeds. Will be rewarded or punished according to what he did, the knowledge of the time becomes a matter of little consequence. It is not wise to spend the precious time of a life on arguments over the date of the Day. Reason and wisdom ask instead that every moment of an uncertain life be spent preparing for it. The question of the Quraysh was a product of their ignorance, and the answer of the verse closed it.

### In short

- Verse 187 answers the Quraish of Makkah, who asked the Prophet ﷺ in mockery when the Hour of doom would come, in the report of Qatadah carried by Ibn Jarir and by 'Abd ibn Humaid. Joining two fingers to show how close his sending stands to the Hour. On the night of Isra he met Ibrahim, Musa and 'Isa. None of them had knowledge of the appointed term. 'Isa spoke of the signs instead, of the false messiah who will dissolve at the sight of him, of Gog and Magog who will sweep the earth. Of the Hour that will come like a woman whose term is full.
- Hudhayfah reported that the Prophet ﷺ was asked about the Hour and answered with the fitnah and the harj, the killing in the language of the Ethiopians. Tariq bin Shihab reported that he kept mentioning the Hour until this verse came down, which An-Nasa'i collected. The same answer came through the report in which Yunus stands in the chain and the words belong to Abu Musa, who heard the Prophet ﷺ speak from the pulpit of the mosque of Basrah of a lot of trials and of the harj that would come before the last Hour.
- The verse closes two misconceptions. The first is that the Prophet ﷺ knew the time and kept it back, when the knowledge belongs to Allah alone and no prophet's knowledge can equal His. The second is the claim of a fixed age for the world, which the reports call a borrowing from Israelite literature with no basis in the Qur'an or the sunnah, while the traditions of the Prophet ﷺ and the reasoning of Ibn Hazm of Spain leave the exact age unknowable. The concealment of the time stands as a restraint on crime.

<<<VERSE 7:188>>>
The verse has the Prophet ﷺ declare the limits of what he holds. "Say, "I have no power to benefit or protect myself, except by the Will of Allah. If I had known the unknown, I would have benefited myself enormously, and no harm would have ever touched me. I am only a warner and deliverer of good news for those who believe.""

### Phrase by phrase

Verse 188 clears away a common misbelief about the prophets of Allah. People took it that a prophet must hold the knowledge of everything in the universe, including the unseen past and the unseen future. That he must hold the power to bring benefit or harm to anyone by his own will. The verse makes it clear that all-encompassing knowledge is an attribute of Allah alone, and that it cannot be ascribed to any created being.

The point turns on the oneness of Allah. To ascribe such attributes to anyone besides Allah is the greatest of transgressions, and it amounts to joining partners with Him. The very purpose of the revelation of the Book and of the coming of the Prophet ﷺ was to remove every trace of plurality and to establish the purest oneness of Allah.

**"Say, "I have no power to benefit or protect myself, except by the Will of Allah.""** is the declaration the Prophet ﷺ was commanded to make. He was to say that, not to speak of other people, he could not bring benefit to himself or drive harm away from himself except as Allah wills. The dominion over those things belongs to Allah alone.

**"If I had known the unknown, I would have benefited myself enormously, and no harm would have ever touched me."** is the proof attached to it. Had he held the knowledge of the unseen, he would have stored up all the good for himself and saved himself from every harm. Ibn 'Abbas, in the report of Ad-Dahhak, said that the abundance named in the verse refers to money. In another narration from him, the words mean that he would have known how much profit he would make with what he bought. He would have sold only what brought him profit. Poverty would never have touched him. Ibn Jarir reported another reading of the same words, that a man who knew the unseen would prepare in the prosperous years for the years of famine. Would make provision before the time of high prices. 'Abd al-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam said that it means he would have kept himself safe from every kind of harm before it came.

**"I am only a warner and deliverer of good news for those who believe."** states the office he was given. He warns against the torment and he brings the good news of Paradise to the believers. A passage of the Book says the same of the message itself, that it was made easy on the tongue of the Prophet ﷺ for two ends, the giving of glad tidings to the pious and the warning of the most quarrelsome of people (19:97). The passage about the knowledge of the unseen belongs here as well, since the Book says of Allah that He alone knows the unseen and reveals His unseen knowledge to none (72:26).

One report gives the reason the verse came down. Al-Kalbi said that the people of Makkah put a question to the Prophet ﷺ. They asked whether his Lord did not tell him about prices before they rose. So that he could buy cheaply and sell at a profit. Whether he was not told about the land that was about to suffer drought. So that he could travel to land that was more fertile. The verse came down in answer to that.

Another report sets out the reading of the words about wealth and harm in more detail. A person might read them as a word about the punishment of a people. Say that if the Prophet ﷺ had known when punishment would come down, he would have done good in abundance out of gratitude. No grief would have touched him on account of others. A person might read them as a word about his own death. Say that if he had known when he was to die he would have done good and performed many righteous works. Hardship would not have come to him. A person might read them as a word about the seasons. Say that if he had known the times of drought and of poor harvest and of rising prices he would have gathered good in abundance and no hardship would have reached him.

### What the prophets hold and what they do not

The declaration of the verse is measured. The prophets of Allah carry a measure of knowledge and power that no other human being has ever held. The Prophet ﷺ was favoured with a degree of knowledge that surpassed that of the other prophets. He informed the Muslims of many things that were to happen. His foretellings came about exactly as he had told them. He was given knowledge of a number of unseen things. But in the language of the Book the knowledge of the unseen is not the name for what he was given. That name belongs to Allah alone, and the Prophet ﷺ may not be called the knower of the unseen.

The hidden knowledge and the divine power were kept from him. The events of his own life show it plainly. He and his companions travelled to Makkah in order to perform the umrah, and the unbelievers of Makkah stopped them. With all their wish and all their effort, they had to turn back to Madinah without performing the umrah. At the battle of Uhud the Prophet ﷺ was wounded and the Muslims met with a temporary defeat. The events were allowed to happen so that the matter would be shown in practice.

The demonstration has a purpose. It shows that the prophets, for all their nearness to Allah and for all that they are the best of human beings, do not hold divine power and do not hold encompassing knowledge. They are human beings like other human beings. The Christians fell into the same error that the verse corrects. They ascribed the attributes of Allah to their prophet and went astray.

The last sentence of the verse gives the office that remains to the Prophet ﷺ. He was assigned the duty of warning the wrongdoers against the punishment of Allah and of giving the good news of the great reward to the righteous.

### In short

- The verse clears the misbelief that a prophet must hold the knowledge of everything, the unseen past and future among it. The power to bring benefit or harm by his own will. It shows that such knowledge and power belong to Allah alone.
- The Prophet ﷺ was told to declare that he could not bring benefit to himself or drive harm away except as Allah wills. The verse adds that if he had known the unseen he would have stored up the good for himself and no harm would have touched him, with Ibn 'Abbas reading the good as wealth and poverty kept away. Other reports reading it as provision against famine or safety from harm before it came.
- To ascribe the knowledge or the power of Allah to any created being is the greatest of transgressions and a joining of partners with Allah. The purpose of the revelation and of the coming of the Prophet ﷺ was to establish the purest oneness of Allah.
- The report of al-Kalbi gives the reason for the verse, that the people of Makkah asked whether his Lord did not tell him of rising and falling prices and of the coming drought so that he could profit from them. The verse came down in answer.
- The office left to the Prophet ﷺ is that of a warner against the torment and a bringer of good news of Paradise to the believers, which is the word of the Book about making the message easy on his tongue so that he may give glad tidings to the pious and warn the quarrelsome (19:97), while the passage about the unseen states that Allah alone knows it and reveals it to none (72:26).
- The prophets carry knowledge and power beyond anything other human beings have held. The Prophet ﷺ was given knowledge of many unseen things that came about as he had told them. Yet he was not given the knowledge of the unseen or the power of a god. The events of the treaty at Makkah and of the battle of Uhud showed that in practice, since he was stopped from the umrah and wounded at Uhud. The lesson of it is that the prophets were human beings like others, the very error into which the Christians fell.
