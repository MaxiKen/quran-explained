<<<VERSE 7:185>>>
Allah challenges the deniers to look around them, and to look ahead: "Have they ever reflected on the wonders of the heavens and the earth, and everything Allah has created, and that perhaps their end is near?" Then He asks: "So what message after this ˹Quran˺ would they believe in". Verse 185 invites people to two important things. The first is thoughtful observation of Allah's creation, the heavens, the earth and everything between them. The second is mindfulness of the span of life each person has been given.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Have they ever reflected on the wonders of the heavens and the earth,"** Have the people of Makkah never considered the dominion of the heavens, with its sun, moon, stars and clouds, and the dominion of the earth, with its trees, mountains, oceans and beasts? The word malakūt means kingdom in a superlative degree, the great Kingdom. It is the same as mulk, dominion. Allah's control of the heavens and the earth holds signs. Had the deniers contemplated them and learned from them, they would be certain that the Owner of all this has no equal or rival.

**"and everything Allah has created,"** All the animals, plants and other things Allah created in them. "Everything" spells out what "what Allah has created" refers to. From all this they could infer the power of their Creator and His oneness. All of it was made by the One who alone deserves worship and sincere devotion. So they should believe in Him and in His Messenger, turn to obedience to Allah and reject every rival and idol.

**"and that perhaps their end is near?"** Have they not thought about their own lifespans, which may be close to ending, and turned to Allah in repentance while there is still time? "Perhaps" (ʿasā), when said by Allah, expresses something certain. Their destruction draws near. They should be warned that their lives may have reached their end. Then they would die as disbelievers and meet Allah's torment and severe punishment. So they should hurry to believe before they die in disbelief and move toward the Fire.

**"So what message after this ˹Quran˺ would they believe in"** If they do not believe in this Scripture, the Qur'an, with the promises and warnings it contains, what other book will they believe? Muhammad ﷺ brought them warnings and threats from Allah in His Book. If those do not move them to believe, what further warnings could?

### Why reflect on creation?

No one needs to be a genius to see the signs of Allah's power and wisdom in everything around him. A little serious thought shows that everything, indeed every particle in the world, sings the praise of its Creator. Each is in itself strong proof of Allah's power and wisdom. Anyone who uses his intellect to observe things consciously cannot escape the natural demand for faith in Allah, the One.

The deniers have not used their ordinary intelligence to seek the truth, which is evident in everything Allah has created. Allah has given them clear signs of the right path and the true faith, both within themselves and outside. For those who ignore such clear signs, nothing in the world can guide them to the right path.

### Why remember that life is short?

Awareness that one's lifespan is uncertain makes a person more active in pursuing his aims, since he cannot be sure how much time he has. All crimes and evil deeds spring from forgetting death. Those who are aware that their lifespan is uncertain mostly keep away from harmful pursuits. The Holy Prophet ﷺ said: remember often the destroyer of pleasures, death.

The deniers are not mindful that life may end at any moment. When it does, it takes away every chance to accept the truth and repent to Allah of their evil deeds.

### In short

- Allah invites people to reflect on the great kingdom of the heavens and the earth, which proves His power and oneness.
- He also reminds them that their own end may be near, a thought that keeps a person from evil.
- If they will not believe in the Qur'an, no other message will bring them to faith.

<<<VERSE 7:186>>>
The deniers rejected the true faith despite every clear sign Allah gave them. That could have grieved the Holy Prophet ﷺ, who cared deeply for people and wished them nothing but good. So verse 186 consoles him: "Whoever Allah allows to stray, none can guide, leaving them to wander blindly in their defiance". He had done his duty by conveying Allah's message as clearly as possible. Their rejection of the true faith was no fault of his.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever Allah allows to stray,"** Whoever Allah does not guide to the truth, but sends away from the right path and from His religion. These are the people destined to be misguided.

**"none can guide,"** No one can lead them back to guidance or to His religion. Even if they try their hardest to gain guidance, it will not help them. Allah says elsewhere, of those He wills to put to fitnah, trial or error: "Whoever Allah allows to be deluded, you can never be of any help to them against Allah." (5:41). And: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Consider all that is in the heavens and the earth!” Yet neither signs nor warners are of any benefit to those who refuse to believe" (10:101).

**"leaving them to wander blindly in their defiance"** Allah leaves them in their insolence, disbelief and error, wandering blindly and hesitating in confusion. They are given up to their base desires and guided to nothing that will benefit them in the end. The verb can be read "He leaves them" or "We leave them". Read in one form, it begins a new sentence. Read in another, it joins the clause "so he has no guide".

### How do the surah's three themes fit together?

Among the themes of Sūrat al-Aʿrāf, three stand out: the oneness of Allah (tawḥīd), prophethood (risālah) and the life of the Hereafter. These three principles are the mainstay of the whole edifice of Islam. The verses so far have set out the first two, tawḥīd and risālah. The next verse turns to the third.

### In short

- Verse 186 consoles the Prophet ﷺ: no one can guide those Allah leaves to stray.
- Signs and warners do not benefit those who refuse to believe, and Allah leaves them wandering in their defiance.
- The surah's three great themes are tawḥīd, prophethood and the Hereafter.

<<<VERSE 7:187>>>
Verse 187 turns to the third principle, the life of the Hereafter. It answers a question the deniers kept asking: "They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ regarding the Hour, “When will it be?”" The Prophet ﷺ is told how to answer: "Say, “That knowledge is only with my Lord. He alone will reveal it when the time comes." He is to add: "It is too tremendous for the heavens and the earth and will only take you by surprise.”" Their manner of asking is exposed: "They ask you as if you had full knowledge of it." So he repeats: "Say, “That knowledge is only with Allah, but most people do not know.”" Allah says the same in another verse: "People ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about the Hour. Say, “That knowledge is only with Allah. You never know, perhaps the Hour is near.”" (33:63).

### Phrase by phrase

**"They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ regarding the Hour,"** Those who reject the truth in their pride, the people of Makkah, ask you, O Muhammad, about the timing of the Hour, the Resurrection. The word sāʿah means a short span of time with no fixed number of minutes or hours. In ordinary reckoning, an hour is one of the twenty-four units of a day and night. In the Qur'an, though, it names both the day when all creation will cease to exist and the day when all creation will be raised and brought before the Lord of all creation.

**"“When will it be?”"** Literally, "when is its anchoring?" Mursā means stopping or settling. Ibn ʿAbbās, as ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported, said they were asking when it would begin. Their question concerned the Hour's fixed term, the moment the world's end would begin.

**"Say, “That knowledge is only with my Lord."** Allah commanded His Messenger ﷺ, when asked about the time of the Hour, to refer its knowledge to Allah. Only Allah knows its appointed term and when it will certainly come. No one else has that knowledge. The Prophet ﷺ was to tell them that neither he nor anyone else knows it.

**"He alone will reveal it when the time comes."** The verb comes from a root meaning to disclose or uncover. No one but Allah will make its time and occurrence manifest. "When the time comes" means at its appointed time.

**"It is too tremendous for the heavens and the earth"** Literally, "it weighs heavily in the heavens and the earth". ʿAbdur-Razzāq narrated through Maʿmar that Qatādah said its knowledge weighs on the dwellers of the heavens and the earth, since none of them knows it. Maʿmar reported that al-Ḥasan said that when it comes it will weigh heavily on them. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported that Ibn ʿAbbās said every creature will suffer its heaviness on the Day of Resurrection.

Ibn Jurayj said that when it begins, the heavens will be torn apart and the stars will scatter. The sun will be rolled up and lose its light, the mountains will be moved away, and all that Allah has described will happen. That is its heaviness. As-Suddī said its knowledge is hidden in the heavens and the earth. Not even an angel drawn near or a messenger sent knows its time. Others say it weighs on them because of its awesomeness, and that its coming is hidden from all in the heavens and the earth.

**"and will only take you by surprise.”"** Baghtatan means suddenly. It will come upon them all at once, while they are unaware. Qatādah said that Allah has decreed it will come only suddenly. He said it had reached them that the Prophet ﷺ described the Hour overtaking people while a man is mending his water trough, watering his cattle, laying out his goods in the market, or lowering and raising his scales.

**"They ask you as if you had full knowledge of it."** The word used is ḥafiyy. Ibn ʿAbbās said it means a scholar or someone well informed. It is generally used for a person who spends his effort and all his resources to find something out. The sound explanation, narrated from Mujāhid through Ibn Abī Najīḥ, is: as if you had asked about its time, so that you now know it. Others render it: as if you were preoccupied with asking about it until you came to know it, or as if you were eager to know it. They did not realise that he did not ask about it because he knew his Lord. One explanation adds other meanings: as if you were ignorant of it, or as if you had forgotten it.

Al-ʿAwfī reported Ibn ʿAbbās explaining it as: as if you were on friendly terms with them! When the pagans of Quraysh asked the Prophet ﷺ about the Hour, they asked as though he were their close friend. Allah then revealed that He alone holds this knowledge and has shared it with no messenger and no angel near Him.

**"Say, “That knowledge is only with Allah, but most people do not know.”"** "Only" is for emphasis. Most people, the people of Makkah, do not know that its knowledge lies with Allah alone, and they do not believe in it.

### Why was the question asked?

It is said that the verse came down about the Quraysh or about the Jews. It seems to concern the Quraysh, since the verse was revealed in Makkah. The Quraysh used to ask about the Hour because they denied it and dismissed its coming. Allah says: "They ask ˹the believers˺, “When will this threat come to pass if what you say is true?”" (10:48). And: "Those who disbelieve in it ˹ask to˺ hasten it ˹mockingly˺. But the believers are fearful of it, knowing that it is the truth. Surely those who dispute about the Hour have gone far astray" (42:18).

Ibn Jarīr and ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd reported from Qatādah that the Quraysh of Makkah once asked the Holy Prophet ﷺ, mockingly, when the Hour he so often warned them of would come. The verse was revealed in answer. In another report from Qatādah, the Quraysh told him that there were ties of kinship between them, so he should tell them the Hour's timing. Ibn ʿAbbās said that two Jews, Jabal ibn Qushayr and Shamwāl ibn Zayd, told him to say when the Hour would be if he really was a prophet, since they claimed to know it. Ṭāriq ibn Shihāb said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ kept speaking of the Hour, because people kept asking about it, until this verse was revealed. An-Nasāʾī collected this report, which has a strong chain.

### Why is its time kept secret?

The heart of the verse is the answer the Quraysh of Makkah were to receive. The exact time of the Hour is not known and will never be made known to anyone. It is a divine secret that will reveal itself by happening suddenly, as the words "will only take you by surprise" decree. Al-Bukhārī and Muslim reported from Abū Hurayrah that the Holy Prophet ﷺ spoke of the sudden coming of the Qiyāmah, the Day of Resurrection, saying that it has been kept a close secret.

The wisdom of this is plain. It lets people live their lives in peace, which would be impossible if they knew the exact time of their death. Besides, those who deny the Last Day would mock it all the more if they knew it lay far in the future. Divine wisdom chose to leave it undefined, so that people stay in a general fear of its terrors. That fear is an effective check on crime and corruption.

Once a person believes that the Day of Resurrection must come, that all creation will stand before the Lord of all creation, and that every deed, small or great, will be rewarded or punished, the time of its coming no longer matters. It is unwise to waste precious hours on idle debates about when the Last Day will come. Reason and wisdom demand instead that every moment of an uncertain life be spent carefully in preparing for the great Day of Judgement. This answer showed the Quraysh of Makkah that their question about its time came from ignorance.

Questions like "On what day will the Last Day come?" come from minds that are not serious. The sincere believe in the Day of Judgement without wanting to know its date. Since this world is a place of testing, the Last Day can be spoken of here only as a caution and a warning.

### Did the Prophet ﷺ know, and hide it?

The words "They ask you as if you had full knowledge of it" expose another misconception of the disbelievers of Makkah. They thought the Holy Prophet ﷺ had somehow learned the exact time of the Last Day and was keeping it back for some reason, and they pressed him to reveal it. He was told to answer that its knowledge is with Allah alone, though most people do not know.

A prophet need not know the exact time of the Last Day, or certain other matters Allah keeps secret. A prophet's lack of knowledge in such matters is no argument against his prophethood. A prophet's knowledge can never equal Allah's all-embracing knowledge.

### The questioner who knew no less

Jibrīl (Gabriel) once came in the form of a bedouin to teach people their religion. He sat beside the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and questioned him as if to learn. He asked about Islām, then about īmān, faith, then about iḥsān, excellence in religion. Then he asked when the Hour would come. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ replied that the one being asked knew no more about it than the one asking. He meant that he knew no more about it than Jibrīl, and that no one knows more about it than anyone else. Then the Prophet ﷺ recited: "Indeed, Allah ˹alone˺ has the knowledge of the Hour." (31:34).

In another narration, Jibrīl asked about the signs of the Hour, and the Prophet ﷺ described them. He also said that five things are known only to Allah, and recited the same verse (31:34). After each answer, Jibrīl would say, "You have spoken the truth." The Companions wondered at a questioner who asked a question and then confirmed every answer. When Jibrīl left, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told the Companions that this was Jibrīl, who had come to teach them their religion. In yet another narration, the Prophet ﷺ said he had recognised Jibrīl in every form in which he came to him, except this one.

### "Your Hour" and the hundred years

Muslim recorded from ʿĀʾishah that when bedouins came to the Prophet ﷺ, they would ask him about the Hour. He would point to the youngest among them and say that if this one lived, he would not grow old before their Hour came. He meant the end of their own lives, which brings them into the barzakh, the realm between this life and the Hereafter. Muslim also recorded from Anas that a man asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about the Hour. He answered that if this young boy lived, perhaps he would not grow old before the Hour came. Only Muslim collected this hadith.

Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh said he heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say, a month before he died, that people kept asking him about the Hour though its knowledge is with Allah. He swore by Allah that not one soul then living on the earth would be alive a hundred years later. Muslim collected this hadith. A similar hadith is in the two Ṣaḥīḥs from Ibn ʿUmar, who explained that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ meant that his generation would have come to its end by then.

### What did the greatest prophets know?

Imām Aḥmad recorded from Ibn Masʿūd that the Prophet ﷺ said that on the night of the Isrāʾ he met Ibrāhīm (Abraham), Mūsā (Moses) and ʿĪsā (Jesus). They spoke of the Hour and asked Ibrāhīm about it, but he said he had no knowledge of it. They asked Mūsā, who also said he did not know. Then they asked ʿĪsā. He said that only Allah, the Exalted and Most Honoured, knows when it will come. But his Lord had told him that the Dajjāl, the False Messiah, would appear, and that ʿĪsā would have two staffs with him. When the Dajjāl saw him, he would melt as lead melts, and Allah would destroy him. Even tree and stone would call out to the Muslim that a disbeliever was hiding behind it, and tell him to come and kill him. Allah would destroy the Dajjāl and his army, and people would return safely to their lands.

After that, ʿĪsā said, Gog and Magog would come swarming from every height, overrunning the earth and destroying all they passed. They would drink dry every water source in their path. People would come to him complaining of them. He would call on Allah against them, and Allah would bring death to them all, until the earth reeked with their stench. Then Allah would send rain to wash their corpses into the sea. His Lord had told him that when this happens, the Hour will be like a woman whose pregnancy has reached full term. Her family cannot tell when she will surprise them with the birth, by night or by day. Ibn Mājah collected a similar hadith.

These were the greatest of the messengers, yet they did not know the Hour's appointed time. When they asked ʿĪsā, he spoke only of its signs, because he will descend in the last generations of this Ummah. He will rule by the Law of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, kill the Dajjāl and destroy Gog and Magog through the blessing of his supplication. ʿĪsā simply told them what knowledge Allah had given him on the matter.

### Trials and harj before the Hour

Imām Aḥmad recorded from Ḥudhayfah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was asked about the Hour. He said that its knowledge is with his Lord, the Exalted and Most Honoured, and no one but He will reveal its time. But he would tell them of its signs and what comes before it: there would be fitnah, trials, and harj. They said they knew what fitnah meant, but asked what harj was. He said it means killing, in the language of the Ethiopians. Then he added that people would become so isolated and estranged that hardly anyone would recognise anyone else. None of the six collections has this hadith through this chain.

Abū Mūsā said the same, speaking on a Friday from the pulpit of the main mosque of Baṣrah. He had been present when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was asked about the time of the Hour. He answered that no one but Allah knows it, and that He alone will reveal it at its time. But he would tell them of its signs and what would precede it: there would be a lot of trials and harj. Asked what harj meant, he said it is an Abyssinian word meaning killing. He also described hearts growing dry, and people cast among strangers so that they hardly know one another. Those of sound mind would pass away, and only a weak people would remain, who neither recognise good nor forbid evil.

### The Prophet ﷺ who came close to the Hour

The Holy Prophet ﷺ was, however, given knowledge of certain specific signs showing that the Last Day was near, and he told the Muslim Ummah of them plainly in his Traditions. He is the unlettered Prophet, the chief and seal of the messengers, Muhammad ﷺ. He is the Prophet of mercy and the Prophet of repentance. He is also the Prophet of al-Malḥamah, the great battle against the disbelievers, and al-ʿĀqib, who came after many prophets. He is al-Muqaffī, the last in their succession, and al-Ḥāshir, at whose feet all people will be gathered. As recorded in the Ṣaḥīḥ from Anas and Sahl ibn Saʿd, and also by at-Tirmidhī, he said that his sending and the Hour are as close as these two, joining his index and middle fingers. Yet even he was commanded, when asked about the Hour, to leave its knowledge to Allah.

### How old is the world?

Reports that give the world a total age of seven thousand years were borrowed from Israelite literature into certain Islamic books. They are not authentic and have no basis in the Qur'an or the Sunnah. Likewise, modern geophysical findings that put the world's age at millions of years conflict neither with the Qur'an nor with any sound Tradition of the Holy Prophet ﷺ.

On the contrary, the Holy Prophet ﷺ told the Muslim Ummah in a Tradition that, compared with the peoples before them, they were like a single white hair on the body of a black ox. This gives some idea of how vast the world's age is. Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥazm of Spain therefore concluded that no one can calculate the exact age of the world. It seems impossible in any case to work out the exact age of the universe. Doing so would reveal exactly when it will end, and the verse rules that out, since only Allah knows when the Last Day will come.

### In short

- The deniers of Makkah asked mockingly when the Hour would come, and the Prophet ﷺ was told to answer that only Allah knows.
- It weighs on the heavens and the earth, and it will come suddenly, amid people's everyday business.
- Not even Jibrīl or the greatest prophets knew its time, though the Prophet ﷺ was shown its signs, such as trials and killing.
- Its time is hidden so that people live in peace yet stay in awe of it, and the wise spend their lives preparing for it, not asking its date.

<<<VERSE 7:188>>>
Verse 188 clears away a common misbelief about Allah's prophets: "Say, “I have no power to benefit or protect myself, except by the Will of Allah." The Prophet ﷺ goes on: "If I had known the unknown, I would have benefited myself enormously, and no harm would have ever touched me." He states what he is: "I am only a warner and deliverer of good news for those who believe.”" People imagined that prophets must know everything in the universe, including the unseen future and the unknown past. They also thought prophets could bring benefit or harm to anyone by their own will. The verse makes clear that all-embracing knowledge is an attribute of Allah alone and cannot be ascribed to any creature.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, “I have no power to benefit or protect myself,"** Say, O Muhammad, to the people of Makkah: I cannot bring good to myself or ward off harm from myself. Allah commanded His Prophet ﷺ to entrust all matters to Him.

**"except by the Will of Allah."** Except whatever benefit or harm Allah wills for me. The control of these things belongs to Allah alone. The Prophet ﷺ was told to say that he does not know the unseen future, and knows of it only what Allah tells him. Allah says in another verse: "˹He is the˺ Knower of the unseen, disclosing none of it to anyone" (72:26).

**"If I had known the unknown,"** If I had knowledge of the ghayb, the unseen, of benefit and harm and of what is hidden from me.

**"I would have benefited myself enormously,"** Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported that Ibn ʿAbbās said this refers to wealth. In another narration Ibn ʿAbbās explained: I would know how much profit I would make on what I bought, and I would always sell at a profit. Ibn Jarīr said that others explained: if I knew the unseen, I would store up in the good years for the years of famine, and prepare in advance for times of high prices. One explanation says: if I knew when I would die, I would do good and righteous deeds in abundance. Another says: if I knew when punishment would fall on you, I would do much good out of gratitude, and grief for you would not touch me.

**"and no harm would have ever touched me."** Poverty would never have touched me, Ibn ʿAbbās said. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam explained: I would have avoided every kind of harm and protected myself from it before it came. Knowing things before they happened, I would have taken precautions against poverty and every other hardship.

**"I am only a warner and deliverer of good news for those who believe.”"** I am only a Messenger from Allah. I warn the disbelievers of the Fire and its painful punishment, and I bring the righteous good news of Paradise and Allah's generous reward. This is for people who believe in Paradise and Hell, who believe that he is Allah's Messenger, and who believe in what was revealed to him. As Allah says: "Indeed, We have made this ˹Quran˺ easy in your own language ˹O Prophet˺ so with it you may give good news to the righteous and warn those who are contentious" (19:97).

### Why was the verse revealed?

Al-Kalbī said that the people of Makkah asked the Prophet ﷺ whether his Lord did not tell him when prices were low before they rose, so that he could buy and make a profit. They also asked whether He did not tell him which land was about to suffer drought, so that he could move to more fertile land. Allah then revealed this verse.

### Did the Prophet ﷺ know the unseen?

Ascribing Allah's exclusive attributes to anyone else is the greatest transgression and amounts to setting up partners with Allah. The very purpose of the Qur'an's revelation and of the Holy Prophet's coming ﷺ was to remove every trace of plural gods and establish the purest tawḥīd, the oneness of Allah. So the Holy Prophet ﷺ was told to declare that, quite apart from others, he could not even harm or benefit himself, and that he did not have knowledge of the unseen. Had he had such knowledge, he would have gathered every good for himself and saved himself from every possible harm.

There are clear instances when the Holy Prophet ﷺ could not achieve his aims despite all his efforts, and many others when he could not protect himself from harm. The treaty of Ḥudaybiyah is one clear example. Intending ʿUmrah, the Holy Prophet ﷺ made the long journey to Makkah with his Companions, only to be stopped by its unbelievers. Despite all their longing and effort, they had to return to Madinah without performing the ʿUmrah. Likewise, the Holy Prophet ﷺ was wounded at the battle of Uḥud, and the Muslims suffered a temporary defeat.

Perhaps such events were allowed to happen to show in practice that the prophets, though closest to Allah and the best of all people, did not have divine power or all-embracing knowledge. They were human beings like others. The Christians fell into exactly this error, ascribing Allah's divine attributes to their Prophet, and went astray.

### What knowledge were the prophets given?

Allah does give the prophets more knowledge and power than any other human being has ever had. The Holy Prophet ﷺ was certainly granted knowledge beyond that of all the other prophets. He told the Muslims of many things that would happen in the future, and every one of his predictions came true exactly as he foretold.

So it is right to say that the Holy Prophet ﷺ was given knowledge of a number of unseen things. In the Qur'an's terms, however, this is not called "knowledge of the unseen" (ʿilm al-ghayb). For this reason it is not permissible to call the Holy Prophet ﷺ "the Knower of the unseen", since no one but Allah is the Knower of the unseen. His assigned duty is to warn evildoers of Allah's punishment and to give the righteous the good news of Allah's great reward.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ was told to declare that he had no power to benefit or protect himself except as Allah willed, and no knowledge of the unseen.
- Had he known the unseen, he would have gathered wealth and avoided all harm, yet he was turned back at Ḥudaybiyah and wounded at Uḥud.
- All-embracing knowledge belongs to Allah alone, and ascribing it to any creature is a form of setting up partners with Him.
- The Prophet ﷺ was given knowledge of many unseen things, but he is not called "the Knower of the unseen". His role is to warn and give good news.
