<<<VERSE 4:17>>>
After the earlier verses on offences, repentance comes into view. The previous verse mentioned it, and these two verses say when it is accepted and when it is not. The first says: "Allah only accepts the repentance of those who commit evil ignorantly ˹or recklessly˺ then repent soon after—Allah will pardon them. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise." The second, 4:18, gives the other side.

The verse promises that sincere repentance is accepted, and the scholars explain that the two conditions it names, "ignorantly" and "soon after", are much wider than they sound.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah only accepts the repentance of those who commit evil ignorantly ˹or recklessly˺"**. The word for "ignorantly" is jahālah, and it looks as though it limits repentance to those who sin by mistake or without knowing. The explanation of the Companions is that it does not mean the person fails to know that the act is a sin, or that he lacks the intention or will to do it. It means that heedlessness toward the evil end of sin and its punishment made him bold enough to commit it, although he knew it was a sin and approached it deliberately. In other words, jahālah here means carelessness or foolishness.

There is support in Sūrat Yūsuf. Yūsuf (Joseph) addressed his brothers in a way that calls them jāhilūn, ignorant ones, although what they did was neither an error nor a lapse of memory. They did it knowingly and with full intent. They were called ignorant because they were heedless of the bad end of what they were doing (verse 89).

Abū al-ʿĀliyah and Qatādah report that the Companions used to say that every sin a servant commits, he commits out of ignorance. ʿAbd ar-Razzāq narrated, from Maʿmar, from Qatādah, that the Companions agreed that every sin, whether done on purpose or not, is committed in ignorance. Mujāhid said that every person who disobeys Allah, even willfully, is ignorant while he does it. Ibn Jurayj related the same from Mujāhid, and said that ʿAṭāʾ ibn Abī Rabāḥ told him something similar. Abū Ṣāliḥ said that Ibn ʿAbbās commented that a person makes the error only because he is ignorant. Mujāhid, the master of exegesis, added that this is so even if the person looks like one of great learning on the outside.

Abū Ḥayyān explained further in al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ. It is like the hadith that a person who commits zinā does not do so as a true believer. At the moment he gives in to the temptation, he has been thrown far from what his faith demands. This is why ʿIkrimah said that everything a person does in this world outside obedience to Allah is ignorance. The reasoning is clear. A person who disobeys Allah prefers short-lived pleasures to lasting ones. Anyone who takes a punishment that never ends in exchange for a short series of pleasures cannot be called sensible, however well he knows the evil of the act and however firm his intention.

The result is that every sin a person commits, on purpose or by mistake, comes from nothing but jahālah. The whole Muslim ummah therefore agrees that the repentance of a person who sinned deliberately can also be accepted. The same wording is explained in short as: sins done in disobedience out of foolishness and impulse, or even if they do so on purpose without knowing the punishment.

**"then repent soon after"**. The words are min qarīb, "from near". The verse sets a condition that one must repent soon, without delay. How soon is "soon"? The Prophet ﷺ answered this himself. Aḥmad recorded that Ibn ʿUmar said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "Allah accepts the repentance of His servant as long as his soul has not reached the throat."

At-Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah also collected this hadith, and at-Tirmidhī called it ḥasan gharīb. Ibn Mājah said by mistake that it came through ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr. The correct narrator is ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb.

Early scholars said the same. Ibn ʿAbbās said it means until just before the person sees the angel of death. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk said that everything before death is "soon". Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said it is until just before the last breath leaves the throat. ʿIkrimah said that all of this life is "soon". Ibn Marduwayh narrated from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar that he heard the Prophet ﷺ say that a believing servant who repents a month before his death, or a day or a moment before, will have his repentance accepted by Allah, provided that it is genuine and sincere.

So by the Prophet's own explanation, nearly a person's whole lifetime counts as "near", and any repentance offered well before death is accepted. Repentance made in the throes of death is not.

Ashraf Thānawī explains in his work on the Qur'an that a person near death faces two states. The first is utter hopelessness, when every medicine and effort has failed and he realises that death is at hand. This is the state of conscious suffering. The second comes after that, when the pangs of the soul's departure begin and the rattle in the throat, the ghargharah, which announces death, draws near. This is total despair. The first state comes within "soon", and repentance in it is accepted. The second does not, because angels and things of the Hereafter may start to appear to the dying person, and these are not included in "soon". The Qur'an itself points to this in the next verse, where it says that repentance is not accepted once the sure signs of death are visible. The words "soon after" also warn that a person's life is limited, and that death, which he thinks is far away, may be quite near.

**"Allah will pardon them"**. Allah's relenting that He has taken upon Himself is a form of promise that is certain to be kept. Strictly, Allah does not owe anything to anyone. One short explanation says that He overlooks the sins of these people. Another says that the repentance He accepts is the kind He has bound Himself, in His generosity, to accept. A third says that Allah forgives these people by overlooking their disobedience.

**"And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise"**. He knows His creatures and what they do. One explanation says He is Knower of your repentance and Wise in accepting it before death, while He does not accept it when a person is in the pangs of death or after he has died. Another says He is Wise in His decree and His sacred laws.

### What repentance is

After the literal explanation, it is necessary to say what tawbah, repentance, really is. In his Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm ad-Dīn, Imām al-Ghazālī describes three situations with regard to sin. The first is total sinlessness, where no sin has ever been committed. That belongs to the angels and the prophets. The second is when a person takes the initiative, ventures into sin, repeats it and persists, never feeling shame or regret and never thinking of stopping. This is the way of the satans and devils. The third belongs to human beings, the children of Ādam (Adam), who regret a sin soon after committing it and firmly resolve not to go near it again. So failing to repent after a sin is the way of devils alone. This is why the whole ummah agrees that repentance is obligatory. The Qur'an says: "O believers! Turn to Allah in sincere repentance, so your Lord may absolve you of your sins and admit you into Gardens, under which rivers flow" (66:8).

Allah's mercy is generous. A person may spend his whole life disobeying Him. If he repents sincerely before he dies, he is not only forgiven. He is admitted to the circle of favoured servants and made an heir of Paradise. The Prophet ﷺ is reported to have said that one who repents from a sin is loved by Allah, and that one who has repented from a sin is like one who never committed it. This is recorded by Ibn Mājah. According to some reports, when a servant repents and his repentance is accepted, he is not only excused from any reckoning for it. The very record the angels wrote of it is erased from his book of deeds, so that he is not disgraced either.

The repentance must be genuine and sincere, and it stands on three pillars. First, a person must feel regret and shame for what he has done. A hadith says, "Repentance is remorse." Second, he must leave the sin at once and firmly resolve to stay away from it in the future. Third, he should think of making amends for what has gone before and do what he can to put right what was wrongly done.

For example, if someone has missed a prayer or a fast, he should make it up, which is called qaḍāʾ. If he cannot remember the number of missed prayers and fasts, he should estimate it and then make them up in earnest. If it is impossible to do it all at once, he may offer one extra qaḍāʾ prayer with each salah at its time, and that is called ʿumrī qaḍāʾ, a lifetime's make-up. In the same way he should make up obligatory fasts as time allows. If he did not pay the zakah due on his wealth, he should pay it for previous years, all at once or in instalments. If he has taken someone's right, he should return it. If he has hurt someone, he should seek that person's forgiveness. If a person does not regret what he did, or regrets it but does not give up the sin for the future, his repentance is not repentance, even if he says it a thousand times. A Persian poet put it well:

> Repentance on the lips, a rosary in the hand, and a heart full of the taste of sin. Sin laughs at my way of asking for forgiveness.

Once a person repents as described, he becomes a servant dear to Allah, whatever sins he has been in. If he slips again, out of human weakness, he should renew his repentance at once, hoping that Allah, the Most Forgiving, will relent toward him this time as every time before. Another Persian couplet says:

> This is the court of My presence, not the court of despair. Even if you have broken your repentance a hundred times, come again.

The same point is made in another way. Repentance is not just words such as "I repent" or "tawbah". It is not lip service. It is an intense realisation of wrongdoing. A sinner who truly repents feels an agony like self-punishment, and Allah will surely pardon one who repents out of deep fear of Him. The heart of contrition is that the wrongdoer turns to his Lord so that his Lord also turns to him. Tawbah is for the one who does something wrong under the pull of a moment's feeling or desire, but whose conscience soon makes him see his fault, so that he leaves evil ways and returns to righteousness, reforming his life by the divine law. That is real penitence. Such a person is like one who strayed away and came home.

### The inner reading

Repentance marks the road. It leads to the Court, opens the treasure, pleads for union, crowns every joy and underlies freedom. It has three steps. First comes regret in the heart. Second comes apology with the tongue. Third comes cutting oneself off from bad things and bad people. A report says that whoever repents but does not put aside bad friends is not a repenter. Whoever repents but does not put aside food and drink is not a repenter. Whoever repents but does not leave his bed and drive sleep from his eyes is not a repenter. Whoever repents but does not spend whatever wealth is left over after his food is not a repenter.

Repentance begins when you free your heart from everything that exists and turn it to the Real, and when you train every drop of blood and piece of flesh in your seven limbs. It goes ahead of the fire that rises from the bottom of Hell, so that today, with the water of your eyes, you do to yourself what the fire would do to you tomorrow. Repentance is also a message sent to you from the Presence:

> "O knight! How long will you wage war? How long will you break the covenant? Come back and make peace! O falcon taken up into the sky, come back, do not go! My fingers hold the end of your thread! O free man, how long have you slept? Wake up, it is morning, and you are thirsty for the wine of yearning. Look, it is time for the morning draught! How long will your heart and your covenant stay broken? It is time to take advice and to repent sincerely."

### In short

- Allah accepts the repentance of those who sin out of "ignorance", which the Companions explained as heedlessness of the end of sin, so even a deliberate sinner can repent, and the ummah agrees on this.
- "Soon after" means any time before death comes into view. The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah accepts repentance as long as the soul has not reached the throat.
- Repentance rests on regret, giving up the sin with a firm resolve, and making amends, such as making up missed prayers and fasts, paying unpaid zakah and returning what belongs to others.
- Repentance is a duty for every person. It is the way of human beings, and failing to repent is the way of devils, and a person who repents is loved by Allah.
- Inwardly, repentance is turning the heart from all else to the Real, and it is Allah's own call back to Himself.

<<<VERSE 4:18>>>
This verse shows the limit of repentance: "However, repentance is not accepted from those who knowingly persist in sin until they start dying, and then cry, “Now I repent!” nor those who die as disbelievers. For them We have prepared a painful punishment." The previous verse said when repentance is accepted. This one describes those whose repentance is not accepted, those who go on sinning fearlessly throughout their lives and start offering repentance only when death is standing over them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"However, repentance is not accepted from those who knowingly persist in sin until they start dying"**. These are people who kept running wild with their lives and wasted every chance to repent while there was still time. When death arrives, the pangs of the soul's removal begin and the angels become visible, and then they start to repent. When hope of staying alive disappears, the angel of death comes forward, the soul reaches the throat and then the chest, and it is gradually pulled out. At that point no repentance is accepted and there is no way out of that certain end. Short explanations say the same. Allah does not turn in mercy to those who persist in disobedience, overstepping the limits and insisting on it, and who do not stop until they are dying and then say they regret it. The Arabic conveys that forgiveness, the overlooking of sins, is not for them. That is the state that lies outside what the previous verse called "soon".

**"and then cry, “Now I repent!”"**. When such a person sees his predicament and says, "Now I repent," it would not help him, and it will not be accepted from him. Allah has likewise decided that no repentance will be taken from the people of the earth on the day the sun rises in the west. As He said: "On the Day your Lord’s signs arrive, belief will not benefit those who did not believe earlier or those who did no good through their faith" (6:158). It is similar to the people who, when they saw the punishment, said, "˹Now˺ we believe in Allah alone and reject what we had been associating with Him" (40:84).

This is very much like Firʿawn (Pharaoh) and his people. While they were drowning, they cried out that they now believed in the Lord of Mūsā (Moses) and Hārūn (Aaron). They were told that their declaration of faith meant nothing at that point, since the time for it was over.

**"nor those who die as disbelievers"**. When a disbeliever dies as a disbeliever and polytheist, his sorrow and his repentance will not benefit him. If he tried to ransom himself with the earth full of gold, it would not be accepted from him. Ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, Abū al-ʿĀliyah and Ibn ʿAbbās said that this part was revealed about the people of shirk, those who ascribe partners to Allah. What is a declaration of faith worth when it is made in the middle of a struggle with death, with the soul being pulled from the body? Such a confession is out of tune with the time and is worthless, because their punishment is already prepared. In the Hereafter too, if they repent when they see the punishment, it will not be accepted.

Aḥmad recorded, from Usāmah ibn Salmān, that Abū Dharr heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say:

> "Allah accepts His servant's repentance, or forgives His servant, as long as the veil does not drop."

They asked, "And what is the dropping of the veil?" He said:

> "When the soul is taken while the person is a polytheist."

**"For them We have prepared a painful punishment"**. It is a severe, eternal and enormous torment. One explanation says that these words came down about Ṭuʿmah and the men with him who had abandoned Islam.

### The inner reading

Repentance is accepted until death is present, and on the level of learning, one must repent before death, even if it is only for a moment. On the level of practice, one must repent before the soul has formed the habit of looking at itself and worshipping itself. When a person is satisfied with himself and gazes at himself out of habit, the door of repentance is closed against him and the water of rescue is held back. Stop being a companion of those who nurse the self and worship their own habits. Kiss the dust beneath the feet of those who have given up the self.

Not everyone who has repented on the road of the Sharīʿah, and so reached pardon and forgiveness, has reached truthfulness in love, on the level of the Ḥaqīqah, the inner reality. Dāwūd (David), the prophet, wept and pleaded for a long time. At last Allah said to him, "O Dāwūd! Why do you weep when I have forgiven you, made your opponents content and accepted your repentance?" He said, "Lord, I know. But give me back the sweet moments I had in Your company and the breaths I had with You in seclusion!" Allah said, "Dāwūd, take heed. That is a love that has passed." So let the eyes weep freely from now on, since the days of clear joy cannot come back. A line of verse says:

> Oh pain, oh regret! Of all that standing and sitting in prayer I have dust on my head and wind in my hand.

### In short

- Repentance is not accepted from those who keep sinning knowingly until death arrives and then say, "Now I repent", nor from those who die as disbelievers.
- Once the soul is being pulled out, or once the signs of the Hour appear, it is too late, as it was too late for Firʿawn when he was drowning.
- The last part is about the people of shirk, as ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, Abū al-ʿĀliyah and Ibn ʿAbbās said. The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah accepts repentance until the veil drops, which is when the soul is taken from a polytheist.
- Inwardly, repentance must come before the self becomes a habit of self-worship, and even the repentant long for the sweetness they once had, as Dāwūd did.
