<<<VERSE 4:17>>>
Repentance has just been mentioned at the end of the previous verse, and the two verses that follow now set its conditions: "Allah only accepts the repentance of those who commit evil ignorantly ˹or recklessly˺ then repent soon after—Allah will pardon them. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise". The verse names who may be forgiven, how quickly they must turn, and what the pardon rests on. Its companion verse, coming next, will name those whose turning comes too late.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who commit evil ignorantly ˹or recklessly˺"** does not mean sins committed by accident. Read plainly, the words could suggest that only the unintentional sin is forgiven and the deliberate one is not. The explanation given by the Companions closes that door. The ignorance here is not unawareness that the act is a sin, nor the absence of intention. It is the heedlessness toward the evil end of sin and its ultimate punishment that gives a person the audacity to venture into it, even while knowing it as sin and approaching it with full volition. The word means carelessness, stupidity.

**"then repent soon after"** sets the time. The limit was drawn by the Prophet ﷺ himself, as the hadith below shows, and the scholars read every stage of life inside it.

**"Allah will pardon them. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise"** closes the promise. He is Knowing of His creatures and Wise in what He does with them, accepting the repentance before death and refusing it in the pangs of death.

### Every sin is committed in ignorance

The Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to say that every sin a servant commits, he commits out of ignorance, and they agreed that every sin committed by intention or otherwise is committed in ignorance. Mujāhid, the master of exegesis, said that everyone who disobeys Allah is, for that matter, ignorant while doing it, even if on the outside he appears to be a person of great learning. Ibn ʿAbbās said it is because of one's ignorance that one commits the error. ʿAbdur-Razzāq and Maʿmar carried the same from Qatādah, and Ibn Jurayj reported it from ʿAṭāʾ ibn Abī Rabāḥ as well.

The Qur'an itself uses the word this way. The brothers of Yūsuf (Joseph) are addressed as the ignorant ones, in verse 89 of that surah, though what they did to him they did knowingly and with full deliberation, not by error or forgetfulness. They are called ignorant because of their heedlessness toward the evil end of their act.

Abū Ḥayyān, in his al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ, adds the hadith that a person who commits zinā does not do so while in a state of being a true Muslim. At the moment he succumbs to the temptation, he is flung far from the demand of his faith. ʿIkrimah said that everything done in this mortal world beyond the bounds of obedience to Allah is ignorance. The logic is plain. Whoever disobeys Allah is trading punishment that lasts forever for pleasures that vanish, and no one who makes that exchange can be called rational or smart. The conclusion is a consensus of the entire Muslim ummah: the repentance of a person who commits a sin deliberately can also be accepted.

### How late is "soon after"?

The interpretations all run in one direction. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that it means until just before the person looks at the angel of death. Ad-Daḥḥāk said everything before death is "soon after". Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said it is just before the last breath leaves the throat. ʿIkrimah said all of this life is "soon after". Imam Aḥmad recorded from Ibn ʿUmar that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> Allah accepts the repentance of the servant as long as the soul does not reach the throat.

At-Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah collected it, and at-Tirmidhī graded it ḥasan gharīb. Ibn Mājah's chain names ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr, which is a slip, for the correct narrator is ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb. Ibn Marduwayh narrated from Ibn ʿUmar that he heard the Prophet ﷺ say that a believing servant who repents a month before his death, or a day or a moment earlier, will have his repentance accepted, provided it is genuine and sincere.

So virtually the whole of a person's lifetime falls inside "soon after". The exception is spelled out by the next verse: repentance in the throes of death is not accepted. One scholar of the later generations divided the approach of death into two states. In the first, medicines and efforts have failed and the person knows death is near, but the soul has not yet begun its departure. This is still within "soon after", and repentance here is accepted. In the second, the rattling sound rises from the throat and the pangs of the soul's withdrawal begin, a state of total despair in which the angels and the things of the Hereafter may already be appearing to the dying person. That state is outside "soon after". The addition of "soon after" in this verse is itself a sermon: a person's span is short, and the death he thinks far away may be quite near.

The acceptance is a promise Allah has taken upon Himself, and His promise is certain of fulfilment. In truth He owes nothing to anyone, and the relenting is pure bounty.

### What repentance is

Repentance is not the uttering of words. It is the intense realization of wrongdoing, an agonizing condition on a par with self-punishment, and one who repents from fear of his Lord is surely pardoned. The root of it is that the wrongdoer turns to his Lord so that the Lord turns to him. The one who repents is like a man who, after straying far away, returns home.

Imam al-Ghazālī, in his Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm ad-Dīn, identified three stations regarding sin. The first is total sinlessness, the hallmark of the angels and the prophets. The second belongs to one who ventures into sin, repeats it and persists in it, never ashamed, never thinking of stopping: the degree of the satans and the devils. The third belongs to human beings, the children of Ādam (Adam), who immediately after sinning regret it and resolve firmly never to return. Failing to repent after sin is the style of devils exclusively, and it is the consensus of the ummah that tawbah, repentance, is obligatory: "O those who believe, repent before Allah" (66:8).

Genuine repentance stands on three pillars. First, regret and shame over what was done, for the hadith says tawbah is another name for remorse. Second, immediately leaving the sin and firmly resolving to stay away from it in the future. Third, making amends for what has gone: a missed prayer or fast is made up through qada, the compensatory worship, and one who has lost count should estimate it and offer the qada in all seriousness, even if that means offering, with each ṣalāh due at its time, one qada of every prayer missed in a lifetime. Unpaid zakah is paid for the previous years, all at once or gradually. A usurped right is returned, and a hurt person is asked for forgiveness. Without these, the word "repent" said a thousand times is no repentance. A Persian poet put it delightfully:

> Repentance on the lips, rosary in hand and a heart full of the taste of sin. Sin laughs at my style of seeking forgiveness.

And if a servant falls again out of human weakness, he renews the repentance at once, in the hope that this time, like every other time, the Most-Forgiving will relent toward him. Another couplet says:

> This is the Court of My Presence, not the Court of Despair. Even if you have broken your repentance a hundred times, come again!

How generous is that mercy. A person may spend a lifetime disobeying, and on turning sincerely before death, is not only forgiven but admitted among the favoured servants of Allah and made an inheritor of Paradise. A hadith says the one who repents from sin is loved by Allah, and the one who has repented is like one who never committed the sin. According to some narrations, when repentance finds acceptance, the written record of the sin is erased from the book of deeds, so that on the Day the servant is not disgraced either.

### The inner reading

Read inwardly, tawbah is the road's mark, the leader into the court, the key that opens the treasure, the intercessor that ushers in union, the first of every happiness and the foundation of freedom. Its order is regret first in the heart, then the tongue's apology, and after that the cutting away from evil things and evil company. The report is severe on half-measures: the one who repents but keeps his bad friends has not repented, nor the one who will not put aside excess food and drink, nor the one who will not leave his bed and empty his eyes of sleep, nor the one who will not spend the wealth left over after his needs. The precondition of repentance is detaching the heart from all existent things and turning it toward the Real, disciplining every limb of the body. It is the forerunner of the fire of hell: weep today with the water of the eyes against what the fire would do tomorrow. And it is a message sent from the Presence:

> O chevalier! How long will you make war? How long will you break the covenant? Come back, make peace! O falcon taken to the sky, come back, don't go! My fingers hold the end of your thread! O free man, how long have you been asleep? Wake up, it is morning, and you are thirsty for the wine of yearning.

### In short

- "In ignorance" covers every sin, deliberate or not, for the Companions agreed that all disobedience is ignorance, as the brothers of Yūsuf were called ignorant though they acted with deliberation.
- "Soon after" reaches to the soul's arrival at the throat, covering virtually the whole of life, except the death-rattle itself, where the next verse takes over.
- Repentance is remorse, quitting, and repair: qada for missed worship, arrears of zakah, rights returned, forgiveness sought.
- The one who repents is loved by Allah, is like one who never sinned, and may even have the record of the sin erased.
- Read inwardly, repentance is the beginning of every happiness, and the Court it leads to is not a court of despair.

<<<VERSE 4:18>>>
The companion verse draws the line that the first verse left open: "However, repentance is not accepted from those who knowingly persist in sin until they start dying, and then cry, “Now I repent!” nor those who die as disbelievers. For them We have prepared a painful punishment". One verse holds the door open, and this one shows at exactly which moment it closes.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who knowingly persist in sin until they start dying"** pictures a lifetime spent in fearless disobedience. They had all their years to turn, and wasted every one of them, and only when death stands over them and the withdrawal of the soul has begun do they remember their Lord.

**"and then cry, “Now I repent!”"** is the cry that no longer counts. When hope in continued living has gone, when the soul has reached the throat and is being pulled from the chest, there is no accepted repentance and no way out of that certain end.

**"nor those who die as disbelievers"** extends the refusal beyond the deathbed to the grave. The disbeliever and polytheist who dies as he lived finds that his sorrow and his repentance avail him nothing. Were he to ransom himself with the earth's fill of gold, it would never be taken from him. Ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, Ibn ʿAbbās and Abū al-ʿĀliyah said this part was revealed about the people of shirk. One report ties the verse to Tuʾmah and his companions, who had left Islam.

**"For them We have prepared a painful punishment"** is torment that is severe, eternal and enormous.

### Pharaoh at the water

The type of the too-late believer is Pharaoh and his people, who called out while drowning that they believed in the Lord of Mūsā (Moses) and Hārūn (Aaron), and were told that their declaration of faith was of no consequence, because the time set for it was over. The Qur'an records the pattern: "So when they saw Our punishment, they said: We believe in Allah Alone" (40:84). A declaration of faith in the middle of the death-pangs, with the soul being torn from the body, is out of tune with the time and worthless, and no different are the people who wait to confess their sins until the terrors of the Hereafter stand in front of them.

Repentance from the people of the earth will also be refused once the sun rises from the west, for Allah has so decreed: "The day that some of the signs of your Lord do come, no good will it do a person to believe then, if he believed not before, nor earned good through his faith" (6:158).

### The dropping of the veil

The report of Usāmah ibn Salmān from Abū Dharr is recorded by Imam Aḥmad, that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> Allah accepts the repentance of His servant, or forgives His servant, as long as the veil does not drop.

They asked what the dropping of the veil meant. He said:

> When the soul is removed while one is a polytheist.

That is the boundary named from Abū Dharr's report, and it matches the limit of the soul-throat hadith collected by at-Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah. What remains forgivable is decided before that veil drops, and for the children of Ādam (Adam) the door stands open the whole of their lives before it.

### The tongue of learning and the tongue of practice

Read inwardly, the verse distinguishes two tongues. In the tongue of learning, repentance must come before death, even if only by a moment. In the tongue of practice, it must come before the soul takes up the habit of gazing at itself and worshipping itself. Whoever becomes pleased with himself and habitually gazes upon his own glory has had the door of repentance shut in his face and the water of deliverance taken from him. The counsel follows hard: "Quit the companionship of the self-nurturing habit-worshippers, and kiss the dust beneath the feet of those who have disowned self." Not everyone who repents on the road of the sharīʿah and reaches pardon has reached truthfulness in love, and the story of Dāwūd (David) shows the difference. The prophet wept and pleaded for a long time, until it was said to him: "O David, why do you weep, when I have forgiven you, made your adversaries content, and accepted your repentance?" He answered: "Lord God, I know, but give me back those sweet moments I had in Your companionship, and those breaths I had with You in seclusion." The answer came: "David, take heed, that is a love that has passed. From now on, leave the way open for the eyes to weep, for the days of limpidness have no way to return."

### In short

- Repentance is refused at two points: in the pangs of death for the sinner who ignored a lifetime of chances, and at death itself for the disbeliever, whose faith at that moment, like Pharaoh's at the water, comes after the time set for it is over.
- The sun rising from the west will close the door of repentance for everyone on earth.
- The veil drops when the soul leaves the body in polytheism, and before that the servant's whole life is his chance.
- Read inwardly, there is a repentance before the death of the body and a repentance before the death of the heart, and the second door, once the self becomes its own god, is the harder one to reopen.
