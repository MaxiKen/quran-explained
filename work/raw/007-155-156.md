<<<VERSE 7:155>>>
After the calf, Mūsā (Moses) took the best of his people to meet their Lord: "Moses chose seventy men from among his people for Our appointment" and, at the meeting, disaster struck: "and, when they were seized by an earthquake, he cried, “My Lord! Had You willed, You could have destroyed them long ago, and me as well." He went on pleading, "Will You destroy us for what the foolish among us have done? This is only a test from You—by which You allow whoever you will to stray and guide whoever You will." And he ended, "You are our Guardian. So forgive us and have mercy on us. You are the best forgiver".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Moses chose seventy men from among his people"**: seventy of the best men, chosen by Allah's command from those who had not worshipped the calf.

**"for Our appointment"**: for the time and place Allah had set, when they were to come and apologise for the calf-worship of the foolish among them.

**"and, when they were seized by an earthquake,"** A violent tremor seized them, struck them down and killed them.

**"he cried, “My Lord! Had You willed, You could have destroyed them long ago, and me as well."** Had You wished, You could have destroyed them before this day, before I set out with them, so that the children of Israel would have seen it and not accused me falsely. You could have destroyed me with them back when I killed the Copt. One reading of his words is: they were foolish, so will You now destroy every one of the children of Israel who comes after me?

**"Will You destroy us for what the foolish among us have done?"** The question is a plea for compassion: do not punish us for the sins of others. Mūsā thought they had been destroyed because of the calf-worship.

**"This is only a test from You"**: the trial that the ignorant fell into, the worship of the calf, was only Your test and Your affliction upon us.

**"by which You allow whoever you will to stray and guide whoever You will."** You send astray whom You will, and You guide whom You will away from the turmoil.

**"You are our Guardian."** You are our Protector who looks after our affairs, and You have more right than anyone to show us mercy.

**"So forgive us and have mercy on us."** Forgiveness here means covering the mistake and not punishing the sin. Whenever mercy is asked alongside forgiveness, it includes the hope that Allah will keep one from falling into the same act again.

**"You are the best forgiver"**: the best of those who forgive and overlook, for no one but You can forgive sins.

### What brought the seventy to the mountain?

One account says that when Mūsā brought the Torah and told his people to follow it, they, being crooked and contrary, said they were not sure it was Allah's word. Mūsā might have written it himself. Mūsā prayed for them to be made certain. Allah told him to choose seventy men and bring them to Mount Sinai, where He would let them hear His speech. As-Suddī said that Allah told Mūsā to bring thirty men of the children of Israel to apologise for the calf-worship, and set them a time and place. Mūsā then chose seventy and went with them so they could apologise.

Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq related that Mūsā told the seventy to go to the meeting with Allah and repent of what they had done, and to ask forgiveness for those they had left behind. They were to fast, purify themselves and wash their clothes. He took them to Mount Ṭūr in Sinai, at the time and place his Lord had set, and only with Allah's leave and knowledge. On the way they asked to hear their Lord's words too, and he agreed.

As Mūsā drew near, the mountain was wrapped in columns of cloud, and he went into it, telling the people to come close. When Allah spoke to Mūsā, a dazzling light surrounded his cloak that no human could bear to look at, so a screen was placed below him. The people came near, entered the cloud and fell down in prostration. They heard Allah speaking to Mūsā, giving him commands and prohibitions. When the cloud lifted, they faced Mūsā and said they would not believe him until they saw Allah directly. The thunder shook them, their souls were taken, and every one of them died.

### Why were they seized?

There are several accounts. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that the seventy went with Mūsā to pray to their Lord. In their supplication they asked Allah to give them what He had never given anyone before them and would never give anyone after them. Allah disliked this request, and the earthquake seized them.

In as-Suddī's account they demanded to see Allah. They told Mūsā they would never believe him until they saw Allah plainly, since he had spoken to Him, so he should show Him to them. As two other verses put it: "We will never believe you until we see Allah with our own eyes" (2:55), and "Make Allah visible to us!” So a thunderbolt struck them for their wrongdoing" (4:153). They died, and Mūsā stood weeping and calling on Allah. What would he tell the children of Israel when he returned to them, now that Allah had destroyed their best men?

Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah, Mujāhid and Ibn Jarīr aṭ-Ṭabarī said that the tremor or the lightning seized them because they neither kept away from the calf-worshippers among their people nor forbade them. Mūsā's words, "for what the foolish among us have done", support this. Ibn ʿAbbās added that these were not the same people who asked to see Allah and were struck by the thunderbolt (2:55).

### Earthquake or thunderbolt?

In Sūrat al-Baqarah the punishment is called a thunderbolt, while here it is an earthquake. The two can be reconciled: they were struck by an earthquake from beneath and a thunderclap from above. Their demand came from ignorance and stubbornness, so it drew Allah's wrath. They fell to the ground lifeless. They either looked dead or were truly dead.

Mūsā was deeply grieved. They were the leading men of the Israelites, and he did not know how he would face his people. They would accuse him of killing these men, and might even kill him. So he pleaded: My Lord, I know it is not Your will to kill them. There were earlier occasions better suited to it. They could have drowned with Pharaoh, or died when they worshipped the calf, yet You spared them. You mean only to warn them. Nor would You destroy all of us for what a few fools did. I know this is only a trial from You. Through it some go astray, while those who see the wisdom and lesson in it are guided. You are the Wise, the All-Knowing, the Most Merciful and Most Forgiving, so forgive the insolence of these people. Through his prayer, all seventy were brought back to life.

### What does "test" mean here?

The word is fitnah. Ibn ʿAbbās, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and many of the Salaf and later scholars said it means affliction, test and trial. This is the only plausible meaning. Mūsā was saying: the decision and the judgement are Yours, and whatever You will comes to pass. No one can guide those You leave astray, or lead astray those You guide. No one can give what You withhold or hold back what You give. Sovereignty, judgement, creation and command all belong to You.

### The inner reading: chosen by Mūsā, chosen by Allah

The community of Mūsā differs from the community of Muhammad ﷺ. Mūsā's community was chosen by Mūsā, as the verse says, "Moses chose seventy men". Muhammad's community was chosen by Allah. Those Mūsā chose demanded to see Allah openly, and here the earthquake took them. Of those Allah Himself chose, He said that their faces will be bright on that Day, looking at their Lord (75:22-23). The wanting is the Real's wanting and the choosing is His choosing: "Your Lord creates and chooses whatever He wills—the choice is not theirs" (28:68).

Mūsā was bold on the carpet of nearness, in the station of intimate prayer. Anguish and bewilderment had left him broken and poor, and from that state of realisation he voiced his remorse: "This is only a test from You." Then he became aware of himself and returned to helplessness, entering by the door of awe and reverence. He handed the decree back to the Real entirely: You lead astray whom You will and guide whom You will. Not content with that, he let loose his tongue in praise joined to pleading and weeping: "You are our Guardian. So forgive us and have mercy on us." He laid his need and lowliness before Allah and begged for mercy and forgiveness.

The traditions say that on the day of the intimate prayer, Mūsā reached the edge of Mount Sinai praising Allah, calling on Him and showing his need with every step. The Pir of the Ṭarīqah said, "The needy are never turned away. There is no deceit behind the door of need. And there is no way of approach to the Friend like need."

### In short

- Mūsā took seventy of the best men to seek forgiveness for the calf, and an earthquake seized them at the meeting.
- They were seized for asking to see Allah, for asking an unprecedented gift, or for not shunning the calf-worshippers, depending on the account.
- Mūsā pleaded that Allah would not destroy them for the deeds of fools, and through his prayer they were brought back to life.
- The calf-worship was a test by which Allah guides and leaves astray whom He wills, and Mūsā submitted the decree wholly to Him.

<<<VERSE 7:156>>>
Mūsā (Moses) ended his prayer with a request for good in both worlds: "Ordain for us what is good in this life and the next. Indeed, we have turned to You ˹in repentance˺.”" Allah answered with a statement about His punishment and His mercy: "Allah replied, “I will inflict My torment on whoever I will. But My mercy encompasses everything." He then said for whom that mercy would be written: "I will ordain mercy for those who shun evil, pay alms-tax, and believe in Our revelations". The first part of Mūsā's prayer asked Allah to ward off what should be avoided. This part asks for what is sought.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ordain for us what is good in this life and the next."** Write down every good thing for us, in both lives, and give it to us. Good in this world means knowledge, worship and protection from sin. It also means being among those blessed with favour and well-being, and enabled to do good works. Good in the next world is Paradise and its bliss, among the righteous servants for whom it has been prepared. The meaning of "good" was explained earlier, in Sūrat al-Baqarah.

**"Indeed, we have turned to You ˹in repentance˺.”"** The word is hudnā. Qatādah, as-Suddī, Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Ibrāhīm at-Taymī, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and others said it means: we repent and come back to You. It can also mean: we are betaking ourselves to You, admitting our faults.

**"Allah replied, “I will inflict My torment on whoever I will."** This answers Mūsā's words, "This is only a test from You." I single out for My punishment whom I will, those who do the deeds that lead to misery. I do what I will and decide as I will, and there is wisdom and justice in all I do. All the disobedient deserve punishment. Yet out of His mercy, which reaches everything, even His wrath, He strikes only a few whom He wills.

**"But My mercy encompasses everything."** His mercy covers every created thing, righteous or sinful. Nothing has been left out of it, and all is immersed in His grace and kindness in this world. Even those who rebel against Allah and deny Him are shown some of His mercy, though only in this world. The angels who carry the Throne and those around it pray, "Our Lord! You encompass everything in ˹Your˺ mercy and knowledge." (40:7).

**"I will ordain mercy for those who shun evil"**: I will write My mercy for them as a favour and a kindness from Me. Another verse says, "Your Lord has taken upon Himself to be Merciful." (6:54). Those who shun evil are those who keep away from shirk, disbelief, indecency and the major sins, doing what Allah commands and avoiding what He forbids. They are the Ummah of Muhammad ﷺ.

**"pay alms-tax,"** The zakah here may mean purifying oneself, or the zakah paid on wealth, giving the poor their due. Both meanings may be meant, since this verse was revealed in Makkah, before zakah was fixed in set shares.

**"and believe in Our revelations"**: who believe in Allah's scripture and His Messenger, and hold firmly to His verses without excuses or false interpretations.

### The hundred mercies

Imām Aḥmad recorded from Jundub ibn ʿAbdullāh al-Bajalī that a bedouin came, made his camel kneel and tied it, then prayed behind the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. When the prayer ended, the man untied his camel, mounted it and called out, "O Allah, have mercy on me and on Muhammad, and give no one else a share in it." The Messenger ﷺ asked his Companions whether this man was more misguided or his camel, and whether they had heard what he said. They said they had. He told the man that he had narrowed a vast mercy. Allah created a hundred mercies and sent down one of them, through which all creation, people, jinn and animals, show mercy to one another. The other ninety-nine He kept with Himself. Aḥmad and Abū Dāwūd collected this hadith.

Imām Aḥmad also recorded from Salmān that the Prophet ﷺ said Allah has a hundred mercies. Through one of them His creatures show mercy to each other, and even wild animals are gentle with their young. Ninety-nine He has kept for the Day of Resurrection. Muslim recorded it.

### Was Mūsā's prayer answered?

Opinions differ on how to read this exchange. Here, unlike elsewhere, the verse gives no plain words of acceptance. On another occasion Allah said, "All that you requested has been granted, O Moses" (20:36). Of the prayer of Mūsā and Hārūn (Aaron) He said, "Your prayer is answered!" (10:89). No such words appear here. So some suggested that this prayer was answered not for the Israelites but for the Muslim Ummah. The author of Rūḥ al-Maʿānī called that a remote possibility.

The correct reading is that the prayer held two requests. The first was mercy and forgiveness for those who had been punished. The second was that good be written for Mūsā and his people in this world and the next. The first is granted in this verse, and the next verse answers the second. To the first request Allah replied that He does not punish everyone who sins, only a few whom He wills for their transgression, so these people would not be punished either. His mercy is so great that it covers all He has created. Even those who are punished are not wholly cut off from it, since they could have received something harsher.

The second request was made conditional. In this world Allah may extend His mercy to all, believers and disbelievers alike. The Hereafter is a separate world, where His mercy is kept for those who meet certain conditions. They fear Allah and live piously, carrying out every religious duty and avoiding what He forbids. They pay zakah regularly from their earnings. And they believe firmly in His verses without pretexts or false interpretations. If Mūsā's people met these conditions, the full favour would be written for them in both worlds. The next verse shows that those who would fit this description perfectly are people to come in the last age, following the last Prophet ﷺ.

Mūsā prayed for his whole community, but Allah's reply made clear that salvation and success do not depend on belonging to a particular community. They are decided for each individual according to his deeds. Allah is the Most Merciful, yet someone who does not do righteous deeds cannot escape His punishment, whatever group he belongs to.

### Does a mercy that encompasses everything include Iblīs?

Hearing this, Iblīs seized his chance and said, "I am one of the things." In Qatādah's report too, Satan claimed a share in the mercy, since it reaches everything and he is one of those things. But the conditions that follow, beginning with those who shun evil, and above all the condition of īmān (faith), excluded him. Then the People of the Book seized their chance. They said they were the people of fear of Allah and of scripture, who believed in Allah and paid zakah, and so the full mercy would be theirs in both worlds. The next verse excluded them by making faith in the final Prophet ﷺ and in the Qur'an's verses a condition, which the Jews and Christians who rejected him did not have.

Shaykh Anwar Kashmīrī said that the verse shows that Allah's mercy can embrace everything. It does not say that it will embrace everything. Another verse says it more plainly: "Your Lord is infinite in mercy, yet His punishment will not be averted from the wicked people." (6:147). The vastness of His mercy does not stop sinners from being punished.

### The inner reading: coming back to Allah entirely

"We have turned to You" means: we have turned toward Your religion and come home to You entirely, keeping nothing back for ourselves. O Lord, we have come back to You wholly. We have given up our own power and strength and are content with whatever You decree. Do not leave us to ourselves, and lift our self-regard out of our way. This is what al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ asked: "Do not leave me to myself for the blink of an eye, or less." He also asked to be shielded as a son is shielded.

Revelation came to Dāwūd (David): O Dāwūd, what have My friends to do with the sorrows of this world? Worldly sorrow takes the sweetness of intimate prayer from their hearts. I love My friends to be spiritual, to grieve over nothing, to cut their hearts off from this world, to leave all their affairs to Me and to be pleased with My decree. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that being pleased with the decree is Allah's greatest gate.

Among the children of Israel was a worshipper who had spent long days in devotion. In a dream he was shown that his close companion in Paradise would be a certain man. He went looking for him to see what his worship was. He found that the man neither prayed at night nor fasted by day, doing only what was obligatory. He asked what he did. The man said he did little worship beyond what had been seen. But he had one trait. In trial or illness he did not wish to be well, and in the sun he did not wish for shade. He was pleased with whatever Allah decreed and did not add his own wish to Allah's will. The worshipper said, "That is what brought you to this station."

### In short

- Mūsā asked for good in this world and the next, and declared that his people had turned back to Allah in repentance.
- Allah punishes whom He wills, but His mercy reaches everything, one part of it shared among all creatures and ninety-nine kept for the Day of Resurrection.
- Mercy in the Hereafter is written only for those who shun evil, pay zakah and believe in Allah's revelations, which shuts out Iblīs and those who reject the last Prophet ﷺ.
- Salvation depends on each person's faith and deeds, not on belonging to a favoured group.
