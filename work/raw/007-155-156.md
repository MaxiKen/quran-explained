<<<VERSE 7:155>>>
Seventy men are chosen, and the earth shakes under them. "Moses chose seventy men from among his people for Our appointment and, when they were seized by an earthquake, he cried," Then the plea begins. "My Lord! Had You willed, You could have destroyed them long ago, and me as well." The argument of the plea follows. "Will You destroy us for what the foolish among us have done?" Then the confession of the whole matter. "This is only a test from You—by which You allow whoever you will to stray and guide whoever You will." And it closes with the asking. "You are our Guardian. So forgive us and have mercy on us. You are the best forgiver"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Moses chose seventy men from among his people for Our appointment"** records the choosing and the reason for it. Ali bin Abi Talhah reported that Ibn 'Abbas said that Allah commanded Musa to choose seventy men. He chose them and went on with them so that they would supplicate to their Lord. The supplication they made asked Allah to give them what He had never given to anyone before them or after them. Allah disliked that supplication, and the violent earthquake came on them. As-Suddi said that the command given to Musa was to bring thirty men out of the Children of Israel with him, to offer apology for the worship of the calf. A time and a place were appointed for them, while the verse itself speaks of seventy.

Muhammad bin Ishaq gave the account in more detail. Musa chose seventy of the best of the Children of Israel and said to them that they should go to the meeting with Allah, repent for what they had done. They were also to beg forgiveness for their people whom they had left behind. He told them to fast, to purify themselves and to clean their clothes. Then he went with them to Mount Tur in Sinai, to the place and the time that had been appointed by his Lord. He went only with the leave and the knowledge of Allah. According to what was told to him, when the seventy had done what he ordered, they said that they wished to hear the words of their Lord themselves. He told them that he would ask for it.

What happened at the mountain is given in that account. When Musa came near the mountain it was covered over completely with columns of cloud, and he went in among them, and he said to the people to come forward. When Allah spoke to Musa, a brilliant light came round his cloak which no human being could bear to look at, so a barrier was placed below him, and the people came forward. They entered the cloud and fell down prostrate, and the words of their Lord reached them as He spoke with Musa. He laid commands and prohibitions on him and set out what was to be done and what was to be left. When He had finished the command and the cloud was lifted from Musa, he turned to face the people. They said to him that they would not believe in him unless they saw Allah directly.

**"and, when they were seized by an earthquake, he cried,"** is what came of that demand. The Book records the demand itself in the words that they would never believe in him, O Musa, until they saw Allah plainly, and they asked that He be shown to them (2:55). In the other place it is recorded that a bolt of lightning struck them (4:153) and they died. One account of the events says that the demand came out of ignorance and obstinacy. They were caught by the earthquake from beneath and by the thunderclap from above, and they fell to the ground at once as senseless as the dead. Whether they looked like dead men or were in fact dead is left in the reports, and what is certain is that they were taken away in front of Musa.

**"My Lord! Had You willed, You could have destroyed them long ago, and me as well."** is the first sentence of the plea, and it carries an argument in it. One reading of the words is that if He had willed He could have destroyed them before this day, and Musa with them. One report explains the sense as being that they could have been drowned with Pharaoh, or killed at the time of the calf, and that none of that happened. One detail in the explanation is that Musa counted himself with them in the words, remembering the day he killed the Egyptian.

The grief that drove the plea is described. Musa was deeply grieved by what had happened. The men who had died were the leading figures of the Children of Israel, and on top of that he did not know how he would face his people when he came back without them. They would lay the death of all those men at his door, and there was a real danger that they would turn on him.

**"Will You destroy us for what the foolish among us have done?"** is the request that the punishment should not fall on all for the act of some. The question is not put as a demand, and it carries its own submission inside it. What it asks for is compassion, and what it refuses to accept is that the many should be wiped out for the deed of the few.

**"This is only a test from You—by which You allow whoever you will to stray and guide whoever You will."** names the whole event as a trial. The word used in the verse is fitnah, and it carries the sense of an affliction and a testing. That reading is given by Ibn 'Abbas, and by Sa'id bin Jubayr, by Abu Al-'Aliyah, by Ar-Rabi' bin Anas, and by a number of the early generations. What Musa was saying is that the decision belongs to Allah and the judgement belongs to Him, and that whatever He wills comes to pass. He leads astray whom He wills and He guides whom He wills, and no one can guide the one He has led astray, or lead astray the one He has guided. No one can give what He withholds or hold back what He gives, and the sovereignty and the judgement and the creation are all His.

**"You are our Guardian. So forgive us and have mercy on us. You are the best forgiver"** closes the supplication. Forgiveness here is the covering of the mistake and the not punishing for the sin. Where mercy is named along with forgiveness, as it is in this supplication of Musa, it carries a further hope, that the one forgiven will not be allowed to fall into the same act again. The last words state the ground of the request, that there is none besides Him who can forgive the sin.

### Why the seventy were taken

Why the punishment fell on that particular group has been answered in more than one way, and the answers are not far apart.

Ibn 'Abbas, Qatadah, Mujahid and Ibn Jarir At-Tabari said that they were seized by the tremor, or by the lightning. Their fault was that they had neither shunned the worship of the calf nor forbidden their people from it. Ibn 'Abbas added that the earthquake came on them because they did not break away from their people on the day the people took to the worship of the calf. He added a further detail, that these were not the same men who had asked to see God and were struck by the thunderbolt. So two events are being kept apart in his report, the demand of the seventy at this meeting, and the demand of another group among the Children of Israel at another time.

The wider lesson stands out of both. The making of the calf had shown that the people did not have in their hearts the conviction they should have had about God. The answer to that weakness was to call them to the mount, where thunder and lightning and the shaking of the earth brought them back with everything they had. They cried out and wailed before God, and they repented as one body, and they made a solemn undertaking to carry out the commandments of the Torah.

### The two accounts of what struck them

The two places in the Book use two different words for what came on the men. The word in this verse points to the earthquake, and the word in Al-Baqarah points to the thunderbolt. The two accounts have been reconciled by taking it that both punishments came on them, the shaking from below and the crash from above.

In one telling of the events, the demand that was made at the mountain was not the first step. Musa had brought the Torah to his people and had told them to follow it. They answered as a crooked and pretentious people, saying that they were not sure it was the word of Allah, and that Musa might have written it himself. He prayed to Allah to make them certain. Allah told him to choose seventy men of his people and to bring them to Sinai, where He would let them hear His word, and that was done as promised. It was after hearing the voice that they said they would not be certain the voice was from God, and that they would believe only when they saw Allah vividly. So the demand came after the hearing, and the punishment followed the demand.

### What the choosing of the two communities shows

The difference between the community of Musa and the community of Muhammad has been drawn from this verse. Musa chose his own people, as the words say. Of those whom Musa chose it is recorded that they said, show us God openly, and the thunderbolt took them for their wrongdoing (4:153). The community of Muhammad was chosen by God and not by any man, and the Book says of them that they were chosen with knowledge above the worlds (44:32). Here He says of the men whom Musa chose that the earthquake took them. Of the ummah whom God chose for Himself the Book says that their faces on that day will be radiant, looking at their Lord (75:22-23).

The point of the comparison is that the want is the want of the Real and the choosing is the choosing of the Real. The Book states it in the words that your Lord creates what He wills and chooses, and they have no choice (28:68).

### What the supplication shows of the one praying

The state Musa was in when he made this supplication has been described in some detail. He was bold on the carpet of nearness, standing in the station of whispered prayer, and he spoke out of the realisation that had come to him in his brokenness and his need. Then he saw himself as he was, and came back to his own incapacity and need, entering by the door of awe and reverence. So he threw the decree back entirely to his Lord, saying that He leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills. He was not content to leave it there, so he set his tongue to praise. He joined the praise to his asking and his weeping, saying that his Lord is his Guardian and that He should forgive and have mercy. He laid his need and his lowliness before Him, and asked, and ended on the note that the best of all who forgive is the One he was addressing.

It is also reported that on the day of the whispered prayer Musa reached the edge of Mount Sinai. With every step he took he praised God, called upon Him and showed his need. And of the same meaning the Pir of the Tariqah said that there is no turning away of the needy. There is no deception behind the door of need, and there is no way of approach to the Friend like need itself.

### In short

- Musa chose seventy of the best of the Children of Israel for the appointed meeting, on the command of Allah, so that they should apologise for the calf and ask forgiveness for their people.
- The demands of verse 155 follow the hearing of the divine speech. The words of the people at that point were that they would not believe unless they saw Allah plainly, as Al-Baqarah and the fourth surah record.
- The two accounts of what struck them use the word for the earthquake and the word for the thunderbolt, and the two are reconciled by taking it that both came on them at once.
- They were seized because they had neither shunned the calf nor forbidden their people from worshipping it. One report from Ibn 'Abbas keeps the men of this meeting apart from those who were struck by the thunderbolt on another day.
- Musa pleaded that the many should not be destroyed for the deed of the few. He named the whole event as a trial from his Lord, and asked for forgiveness and mercy, since there is no forgiver besides Him.
- The difference between the community of Musa and the community of Muhammad is drawn from the choosing. Musa chose his people, while God chose the followers of the last Prophet ﷺ. The decree and the choice belong to Allah, as the Book says in the forty-fourth surah and the twenty-eighth.

<<<VERSE 7:156>>>
The plea widens out from forgiveness to the whole of life, and the answer comes back naming the limits of mercy. "Ordain for us what is good in this life and the next." The ground of the asking is stated next. "Indeed, we have turned to You ˹in repentance˺.”" Then the answer of the Lord. "Allah replied, “I will inflict My torment on whoever I will. But My mercy encompasses everything." And the condition is given. "I will ordain mercy for those who shun evil, pay alms-tax, and believe in Our revelations"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ordain for us what is good in this life and the next."** is the second half of the supplication, and it asks for something rather than turning something away. The first part of the plea had been aimed at holding off what should be avoided, and this part seeks what is wanted. In one reading the good asked for in this world is knowledge, worship, and protection from sin, and the good asked for in the life to come is Paradise and its bliss. The meaning of the sentence is that Allah should grant them everything good in both of the lives, and what that good is had already been set out in the explanation of Al-Baqarah.

**"Indeed, we have turned to You ˹in repentance˺.”"** is the ground on which the request is made. The word used here in the Book carries the sense of turning back and returning. That is the meaning given by Ibn 'Abbas, and by Sa'id bin Jubayr, and by Mujahid, and by Abu Al-'Aliyah, and by Ad-Dahhak, and by Ibrahim At-Taymi, and by As-Suddi, and by Qatadah. Several others besides them said the same.

The inner reading of it is put in a way that fills out the sense. The words mean that they have turned towards the religion of their Lord and come home to Him entirely, leaving nothing over for themselves. It is the speech of one who has come back to his Lord with nothing of his own. He is quit of his own power and his own strength, and content with whatever has been decreed for him. The prayer of Al-Mustafa in the same spirit was that he should not be left to himself for the blink of an eye, or for less than that. He also asked to be shielded as a son is shielded.

The same meaning was given to David in revelation, in the words that his friends have nothing to do with the sorrow of this world. The sorrow of this world, it says, takes the sweetness of whispered prayer out of their hearts. What the Lord loves in His friends is that they should be spiritual. They should not grieve over anything. They should detach their hearts from this world, throw all their work and business upon Him, and approve of what He decrees. The Messenger of God said on this last point that the approval of the decree is the greatest gate that leads to God.

A worshipper among the Children of Israel once spent long days in worship, and it was shown to him in a dream that his close friend in Paradise would be a certain man he knew. He set off to find him, to see what worship the man had. He found that the man kept no night prayer and no fasting beyond the obligatory acts. He asked him to tell him what he did, and the man said that he had never done much beyond what had been seen. He had one trait. When he was in a trial or an illness he did not wish to be well, and when he was standing in the sun he did not wish to be in the shade. Whatever God decreed, he approved of it, and he did not add his own want to the want of God. The worshipper said to him that this was what had carried him to that station.

He, God, says in verse 156 that He will strike with His torment whoever He wills, and the answer holds the door of punishment open while it narrows it. The sense is that though all the disobedient deserve the punishment of Allah, out of His mercy, which takes in everything, He strikes with His punishment only whom He wills. So the men who had been taken were not punished because a whole people had sinned. The Lord punishes whom He wills for their transgression, and no one can overturn His decision.

**"But My mercy encompasses everything."** states the breadth of the mercy. The angels who carry the Throne and those around it supplicate with the words that their Lord comprehends all things in mercy and knowledge (40:7). The mercy spoken of here reaches every created thing in this world. It is also remembered that even those who are punished are not cut off from mercy altogether, since the punishment they receive could have been harder than it was. In a similar place the Book says that your Lord has written mercy upon Himself (6:54).

The word of the Prophet ﷺ on this came with two reports. Ahmad recorded from Jundub bin 'Abdullah Al-Bajali that a bedouin came, made his camel kneel, tied it, and prayed behind the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. When the prayer was finished the man untied the camel and mounted it. Then he called out aloud in prayer that Allah should grant His mercy to him and to Muhammad, and give no share of it to anyone else. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked his companions whether they thought that man or his camel was the more misguided, and whether they had heard what he said. Then he told them that the man had put a limit on a mercy that has no limit. God's Messenger said that Allah had made the mercies a hundred in number, and that one of them was sent down. By that one the created beings show kindness among themselves, men and jinn and animals alike, while the ninety-nine that remain stay with Him. Ahmad and Abu Dawud collected that hadith. Ahmad also recorded from Salman that the Prophet ﷺ said that the mercies of Allah come to a hundred. One of them is at work among the creatures, so that even a beast is gentle with its young. The other ninety-nine are held back with Him for the Day of Resurrection. Muslim recorded that report.

**"I will ordain mercy for those who shun evil, pay alms-tax, and believe in Our revelations."** sets out where the mercy is placed. The first condition is that a person keep clear of shirk and of the major sins, which is what the fear of Allah means here in practice. The second is the giving of the alms-tax, which has been read either as the purifying of the self or as the alms-tax on wealth. It is possible that both senses are meant, since this passage was revealed in Makkah before the fixed shares of the alms-tax had been ordained. The third is belief in the signs of Allah, that is in His Book, in His Messenger and in the verses themselves.

What the answer amounts to is that the mercy is ordained for the ummah of the Prophet ﷺ, since those who carry the three qualities are the people of that faith. Moses had asked for the good of his own community. The answer given to him names the people who will receive it, and it does not make the matter depend on belonging to any one group. Salvation and success are decided for each person on the basis of his own deeds, and the mercy of God does not carry anyone past the demand for righteous action.

### The claim that the mercy covered everyone

A dispute arose over the reach of the sentence, and it is worth stating plainly.

The Satan claimed a share in the mercy on the basis of these words, arguing that he too is one of the things that everything covers. The answer given to him is that the verse does not say that His mercy shall take in everything. It says that it has taken in everything, and the condition that follows closes the door on his claim. In another place the Book says it in clearer words, that the Lord has an all-encompassing mercy, and that His punishment cannot be turned away from the people of evil (6:147). So the breadth of the mercy does not save the sinner from being punished.

The people of the Book made a claim of the same shape. They said that they believed in Allah and gave the alms-tax, and that the perfect mercy would therefore be written for them. The verse that follows this one names belief in the last Prophet and in the verses of the Book among the conditions, and by that the claim is shut out.

### Whether the supplication was granted

A question was raised about the answer that Musa received, and the answer to it turns on what exactly he had asked. There is no clear statement in this place that his prayer was accepted, unlike other places where the acceptance is stated. On one occasion he was told that he had been given what he asked for (20:36), and on another that the prayer of Musa and Harun had been accepted (10:89). Here no such word is given. On that basis some suggested that this particular prayer of Musa was not accepted on behalf of the Children of Israel, and that it was accepted instead on behalf of the Muslim community. Ruh al-Ma'ani said that this is a remote possibility.

The better reading is that the supplication had two requests in it. The first was for mercy and forgiveness for those who had been punished. The second was for the good of this world and the next to be written for him and for his people. The first request was granted at once, and the answer to the second is given in the verse that follows this one. As for the first, the answer was that Allah does not punish everyone who acts sinfully. He punishes whom He wills, and these people accordingly were shown mercy. His mercy is wide enough to cover the whole of creation. Even those who are punished are not shut out of the mercy of Allah entirely, since a harder punishment than theirs was possible and was not given. He has power over all things and does whatever He wills.

The two conditions belong to the two lives separately. In this world the mercy of Allah reaches every creature without regard to belief or disbelief. The world to come is a different place. There the mercy is written for those who carry the fear of Allah. They fulfil their obligations and keep away from what is forbidden. They give the alms-tax regularly out of what they earn, and they believe firmly in the verses of their Lord without turning them aside by false readings. For the people who carry those qualities the perfect favour is written in both worlds.

### In short

- The second half of the supplication asks for what is good in this world and the next. The good is read as knowledge and worship and protection from sin, and then Paradise and its bliss.
- The turning back to Allah named in the verse means coming to Him entirely with nothing of one's own left over. The reports from the companions and the early generations give it the sense of repentance and return.
- The answer keeps the punishment of Allah within His will and describes His mercy as having taken in everything. The angels around the Throne supplicate with the words that their Lord comprehends all things in mercy and knowledge.
- The report of the bedouin who asked for mercy for himself and the Prophet ﷺ alone, collected by Ahmad and Abu Dawud, shows how vast the mercy is. The report of Salman in Muslim says that a hundred mercies were created and one of them was sent down.
- The mercy is written for those who keep clear of shirk and major sins, give the alms-tax, and believe in the signs of their Lord. The passage was revealed in Makkah before the fixed shares of the alms-tax were ordained.
- The Satan and the people of the Book both claimed a share in the mercy on the strength of its breadth. Both claims are answered by the conditions attached to it and by the verse that follows.
