<<<VERSE 2:21>>>
After describing the three kinds of people, the surah speaks to all of them at once. "O humanity! Worship your Lord, Who created you and those before you, so that you may become mindful ˹of Him˺". This is the first command in the Qur'an's order of arrangement, and it carries the message for which the whole Book was sent down: give up the worship of created things and worship the Creator alone. The verse does not simply assert this. It argues it, in a sentence so short and so clear that an ordinary person using ordinary common sense cannot help being convinced.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O humanity!"** translates an-nās, mankind in general, so the address takes in all three groups just described: the believers, the disbelievers and the hypocrites. It is also understood as addressed to the people of Makkah, and some say to the Jews. Alqamah laid down a rule, reported through al-Aʿmash and Ibrāhīm, that whatever revelation contains "O mankind" is Makkan and whatever contains "O you who believe" is Madinan, meaning that the first is addressed to the people of Makkah and the second to the people of Madinah. On that basis this verse addresses the idolaters of Makkah, and the address to them runs on until "Give good news ˹O Prophet˺ to those who believe" (2:25), which was revealed about the believers, because Allah wished to mention the reward of the believers after mentioning the reward of the disbelievers in "the Fire fuelled with people and stones, which is prepared for the disbelievers" (2:24).

**"Worship your Lord"** means profess His oneness and worship no one but Him. ʿIbādah, worship, means spending every energy one has in total obedience to someone and shunning all disobedience out of awe and reverence for him. The choice of the name Rabb, the One who nurtures, from among Allah's beautiful names is deliberate, because it folds the argument into the command. The Rabb is the final and absolute cause of a human being's nurture, the One who takes him by gradual stages from a drop of fluid to a healthy, feeling, reasoning person and who provides the means of his sustenance and growth. This is so obvious that even a dull or ignorant man, on a little reflection, would see that only Allah, and no created being, can have such a power. What can a creature do for a man when its own existence is owed to the Creator? Can one needy being rescue another? If it seems to, the nurturing in reality belongs to the One Being on whom both depend in order to exist at all. So who but the Rabb deserves adoration and worship?

The command means something different to each group it addresses. To the disbelievers it says: stop worshipping created beings and turn to the Creator. To the hypocrites: be sincere and true in your faith. To sinning Muslims: change your ways and perfect your obedience. To the mindful Muslims: stay steadfast in worship and obedience and strive harder in Allah's way.

**"Who created you and those before you"** names a quality that no one could even imagine belonging to a created being: giving existence to what did not exist, making you when you were nothing, and bringing out of the darkness and uncleanness of the womb a creature as lovely and noble as a human being. He created you in your different lineages, beginning from a drop of sperm, just as He created those who came before you. Adding "and those before you" makes Him the sole Creator of the whole human race. It is also telling that the verse speaks of those before you and says nothing of those who will come after. The silence hints that no community will succeed the community of the Prophet ﷺ, because no prophet will be sent after the Last Prophet ﷺ and so no new ummah will arise.

**"so that you may become mindful ˹of Him˺"** can also be rendered "so that you may save yourselves from Hell" or "so that you may guard yourselves against evil": so that you may ward off His anger and punishment by obeying Him, following His instructions and keeping away from what He has forbidden. The point is that salvation and Paradise can be hoped for only by worshipping Allah alone and associating nothing with Him. An important doctrinal point lies in the particle laʿalla, "so that ... may", which expresses expectation or hope and is used where it is not definite that something will happen. A person who truly has faith and truly believes in Allah's oneness will certainly attain salvation and Paradise, as Allah Himself has promised. The certainty is expressed here as a hope to make a person realise that no human act, by itself, can earn salvation as a necessary reward. Salvation and Paradise come by the grace of Allah alone. The ability to do good, and faith itself, are signs of divine grace, not its cause. When Allah uses this hopeful word, it carries the force of an affirmation. In the inner reading, the word brings the command closer to people and makes it lighter, and keeps them between fear and hope.

### Where the verse stands

The second verse of the surah answered the prayer of al-Fātiḥah, "Guide us along the Straight Path": the guidance asked for is in this Book, which from beginning to end is a detailed account of the straight path. The surah then divided people into three groups by their acceptance or rejection of that guidance: the true, mindful Muslims who accept it and act on it, the disbelievers who oppose it openly, and the hypocrites who are hostile to it but hide their disbelief, for petty worldly interest or to harm the Muslims, and present themselves as Muslims. In sorting people this way the first twenty verses show that the right basis for dividing mankind is neither race nor colour, neither language nor geography, but religion. Those who believe in Allah and follow His guidance in the Qur'an form one nation and those who disbelieve form another. The Qur'an calls the first the party of Allah and the second the party of Shayṭān: "They are the party of Satan. Surely Satan's party is bound to lose" (58:19-22). Now verses 21 and 22, addressing all three groups together, present the message itself.

### The hadith on Allah's right

The two Ṣaḥīḥs record that Ibn Masʿūd asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ which sin is the gravest with Allah, and he answered: that you set up an equal to Allah when He alone created you. Muʿādh narrated the Prophet's ﷺ words: do you know what Allah's right over His servants is? That they worship Him alone and associate nothing with Him. Another hadith says that none of you should say, "What Allah wills and so-and-so wills," but rather, "What Allah wills, and then what so-and-so wills."

Imām Aḥmad recorded from al-Ḥārith al-Ashʿarī that the Prophet ﷺ said that Allah commanded Yaḥyā ibn Zakariyyā to carry out five commands and to order the Children of Israel to carry them out, and Yaḥyā was slow about it. ʿĪsā (Jesus) said to him: you were ordered five commands and to order the Children of Israel with them, so either you give the order or I will. Yaḥyā said, "My brother, I fear that if you do it before me I will be punished, or the earth will be made to shake under my feet." So Yaḥyā gathered the Children of Israel in Bayt al-Maqdis in Jerusalem until they filled the masjid. He sat on a raised place, thanked and praised Allah, and said that Allah had ordered him five commands and ordered him to order them with them. The first was to worship Allah alone and associate nothing with Him. The likeness of this, he said, is a man who buys a servant with his own money, in silver or gold, and the servant works but hands the earnings to someone else. Which of you would be pleased with such a servant? Allah created you and sustains you, so worship Him alone and associate nothing with Him. He also commanded them to pray, because Allah keeps His face turned toward the face of His servant for as long as the servant does not look away, so do not turn your heads this way and that in prayer. He commanded them to fast, whose likeness is a man in a company of men carrying musk wrapped in a cloth, so that everyone smells it, for the breath of the fasting person is better with Allah than the scent of musk. He commanded them to give charity, whose likeness is a man captured by the enemy, his hands tied to his neck, brought forward to be beheaded, who asks whether he may ransom himself and keeps paying small sums and large until he frees himself. And he commanded them to remember Allah always, whose likeness is a man pursued hard by the enemy who takes refuge in a strong fortress, for when the servant remembers Allah he has taken the best refuge from Shayṭān. Al-Ḥārith went on to relate the Prophet's ﷺ own addition: and I order you with five that Allah ordered me: keep to the community, listen and obey, and perform hijrah and jihād in the way of Allah. Whoever leaves the community by so much as a hand-span has removed the tie of Islam from his neck unless he returns, and whoever uses the slogans of the days of ignorance will be among those kneeling in Jahannam. They asked, "Even if he prays and fasts?" He said, "Even if he prays and fasts and claims to be a Muslim. So call the Muslims by the names Allah gave them: the Muslims, the believing servants of Allah." This is a ḥasan hadith, and its words "Allah created you and sustains you, so worship Him and associate nothing with Him" are exactly the argument of these two verses.

### Oneness as the ground of peace

Tawḥīd, the most fundamental doctrine of Islam, is not a mere theory. It is the only effective way of making a human being truly human, his first and last refuge and the cure for all his ills. Its essence is that every change the physical universe can undergo, down to its own birth and death, hangs on the will of the One Being and displays His wisdom. When this doctrine takes hold of a person's mind and heart and becomes his settled state, all inner conflict stops and the world turns into a paradise for him, because he knows that the enmity of an enemy and the love of a friend both proceed from Allah, who rules both their hearts. Such a man lives in perfect peace. He fears no one and expects nothing from anyone. Heap gold on him or clap him in irons and he is unmoved, because he knows where each comes from. This is the meaning of the basic declaration of the creed, that there is no god but Allah. Saying it is not enough. One must have complete certainty and keep the truth always close to the heart, because tawḥīd is seeing Allah as one, not just saying so. Today the number of people who repeat the formula runs to millions, more than ever before, yet for most of them it is only breath spent. The colour of tawḥīd does not show in their lives. Otherwise they would be like their forefathers, whom neither wealth nor power daunted and neither numbers nor pomp overawed into turning their backs on the truth, when a prophet could stand alone against the world and say, "Call upon your associate-gods and conspire against me without delay" (7:195). If the Companions and their successors came to dominate the world within a few years, the secret lay in tawḥīd rightly understood and practised. May Allah grant all Muslims this great gift.

### The inner reading

Worship is bringing oneself into harmony with the command and pouring all one's energy into seeking the realisation of the unseen. The declaration of oneness enters through the heart, detachment through the innermost self, single-mindedness through intention, and humility through the self. Surrender is to the decree. It is also said: worship Him by renouncing what is forbidden, persevering in acts of obedience, welcoming the obligatory acts with humility and lowliness, and avoiding detours through the way-stations of laziness and indifference. The real meaning of taqwā, mindfulness of Allah, is protecting oneself, by conforming to His command, from the threats of punishment.

### In short

- "Worship your Lord" is addressed to everyone, and says something different to each: turn, be sincere, repent, persevere.
- The name Rabb carries the proof inside the command. The One who brought you from nothing and nurtures you is the only One worthy of worship, as the gravest sin is to set up an equal to Him who created you.
- "Those before you" makes Him the Creator of all mankind, and the silence about "those after you" hints that no community follows this one.
- "So that you may" expresses salvation as a hope, because Paradise is Allah's grace, not a wage. And tawḥīd, truly held, is the deepest peace a human being can have.

<<<VERSE 2:22>>>
The argument moves from the human self to the world around it. "˹He is the One˺ Who has made the earth a place of settlement for you and the sky a canopy; and sends down rain from the sky, causing fruits to grow as a provision for you. So do not knowingly set up equals to Allah ˹in worship˺". Verse 21 spoke of Allah's blessings in your own being. This verse speaks of His blessings in your surroundings. The two dimensions are anfus, the inward self, and āfāq, the surrounding world. Together these verses gather Allah's blessings in both. Their conclusion is not to set up rivals to the One who gives them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹He is the One˺ Who has made the earth a place of settlement for you"** means He made it a bed to rest on, a place for dwelling and sleeping, like a carpet laid out, stable with the firm mountains. It is neither soft and fluid like water, on which nothing can settle, nor hard like stone or steel, which would be difficult to put to use, but a middle state between soft and hard, convenient for daily life. The word firāsh, bed, literally means something spread out. This does not deny that the earth is round. The great globe, round as it is, looks flat to anyone standing on it, and the Qur'an's way is to describe things as they appear to an ordinary person, literate or not, townsman or villager.

**"and the sky a canopy"** means a ceiling, a roof raised high above, an ornamented and beautiful one, masterfully built. Allah said elsewhere, "And We have made the sky a well-protected canopy, still they turn away from its signs" (21:32).

**"and sends down rain from the sky"** means through the clouds, when people need it. Water coming "from the sky" does not mean it falls without the medium of clouds. In everyday speech anything that comes down from above is said to come from the sky, and the Qur'an itself speaks several times of water sent down from the clouds: "Is it you who bring it down from the clouds, or is it We Who do so" (56:69), and "and sent down from rainclouds pouring water" (78:14).

**"causing fruits to grow as a provision for you"** means that by the rain He brings out every kind of vegetation and fruit as sustenance for people and their animals, a bounty the Qur'an mentions again and again. A similar verse reads, "It is Allah Who made the earth a place of settlement for you and the sky a canopy. He shaped you ˹in the womb˺, perfecting your form. And He has provided you with what is good and lawful. That is Allah—your Lord" (40:64). The repeated lesson is that Allah is the Creator, the Sustainer, the Owner and the Provider of this life and everything in it, so He alone deserves worship and nothing is to be associated with Him.

**"So do not knowingly set up equals to Allah ˹in worship˺"** means do not set up partners in worship, and do not say that Allah has peers, likenesses or equals, when you know that He fashioned all these things, that He is the Creator and you create nothing, and that only the One who creates can be God. It is also said that "knowingly" means: when you know from your own scripture that Allah has no son, no equal and no peer. The verse ends this way to rescue people from slavery to everything other than Allah.

### Four things no creature can do

The verse names four qualities of Allah that cannot possibly be found in a created being. The first three, the earth, the sky and the rain, belong to an order where human effort, human action and even human existence play no part whatever. Man did not yet exist when the earth and sky were already there and the clouds and rain were already doing their work, and not even a fool could imagine that these were the work of a man, an idol or any created being. The fourth, bringing out fruit and making it nourishment, is different. A simple person, looking at the surface, might credit it to human effort and ingenuity, because he sees people digging the earth, sowing seed and protecting the plants. But the Qur'an makes it plain that human effort has nothing to do with the actual growing of trees and the bringing out of fruit. All that human activity does is remove hindrances to a plant's birth and growth or protect it from being destroyed. Even the water that feeds the plant is not the farmer's creation. He only brings it to the plant at the right time and in the right amount. The birth and growth of the tree and the putting out of leaves, branches and fruit are the work of divine power and of no one else: "Have you considered what you sow? Is it you who cause it to grow, or is it We Who do so" (56:63-64). The only answer anyone can give is that Allah alone makes things grow.

Once these two verses have taught that Allah, and no one else, brings a human being out of nothing and provides for him by means of earth, sky, rain and fruit, anyone with a little common sense must admit that Allah and no one else deserves all worship and obedience. The ultimate wrong is to turn away from the One who gave you existence and the means of living and growing, and to prostrate yourself before others as helpless as you are. Allah placed man above all His other creatures so that the universe would serve him while he devoted himself wholly to worshipping, remembering and obeying Allah without distraction. Yet some people are so sunk in laziness and ignorance that they forget the One God, and in consequence end up serving a billion gods. Once one has understood that Allah alone is the Creator and the Provider, one has also understood that no one else can deserve worship or be set beside Allah as an equal or rival. These two verses call people to the essential purpose of every revealed Book and every prophet: tawḥīd, the affirmation and the worship of the one God.

### The signs of the Creator

Several scholars, among them ar-Rāzī, used these verses as a proof of the Creator's existence, and it is a most worthy line of argument. Whoever reflects on the things that exist, the higher creatures and the lower, their varied shapes, colours, habits, benefits and roles, comes to recognise the power, wisdom, knowledge, perfection and majesty of their Creator. A bedouin asked for evidence of Allah's existence replied: praise be to Allah, camel droppings point to a camel and footprints point to someone walking, so does a sky holding the giant stars, a land with its open roads and a sea with its waves not point to the existence of the Most Kind, the All-Knowing? Consider the vast sky and its planets, some appearing fixed. Seas surround the land, while mountains steady it for its varied inhabitants. The mountains bear white, red and raven-black streaks. People and cattle display a similar variety of colour. The description ends with awe before Allah in (35:27-28). Rivers carry benefit from land to land. Earth and water bring forth plants and animals of different tastes, scents, shapes and colours. The argument sees all these as testimony to the Creator's existence, perfect power and wisdom. They also disclose His mercy, kindness, generosity and compassion. There is no god but Allah and no Lord besides Him. On Him is our reliance and to Him is our return in repentance. The Qur'an has many verses on this theme.

### Seeing through the causes

Tawḥīd has an all-pervading and radically transforming effect on every sphere of life, inner and outer, individual and collective. The believer recognises one Creator, Lord and Master of the universe. All power belongs to Him, including the smallest atom's movement. Without His will no one can harm or help. Rich or poor, joyful or sorrowful, the person then turns towards Him. Apparent causes become a veil through which the believer recognises the same divine power. If the modern worshippers of "energy" understood tawḥīd at all, they would see at once that power lives neither in steam nor in electricity, and that every power comes from the One Being who created both. Seeing this takes insight. The greatest philosopher in the world, if he misses this truth, is no better than the rustic who saw a train move when a green flag was waved and stop when a red one was waved, concluded that the flags controlled the huge machine, and bowed to them. People laugh at him because he does not know that the flags are only signals and the train is run by the driver, or better, by the engine. A more perceptive observer credits the steam inside the engine. The believer in one God laughs at all these wise men, because he sees through the steam, the fire and even the water to the might of the One Being who created fire and water and whose will makes them do their appointed work.

### Why people worship what they can see

Allah alone created mankind and everything in the heavens and the earth. He invested the world with deep meaning and constantly tends its needs. The right course for a human being is to take Allah as his Creator, Master and Sustainer and to set no partner beside Him, that is, to give himself up wholly to Allah. But because Allah is unseen, it often happens that a person gives weight to some visible thing and worships it instead. He equates the created with the Creator, sometimes a part of creation and sometimes creation as a whole, sometimes calling it God and sometimes not. This is man's basic error. The prophets taught people to glorify Allah alone and abandon the other things they had raised to glory. People whose hearts are attached to things other than Allah find the call of true religion repellent. They have grown so attached to their supposed deities that they cannot believe they are unreal, and they cannot believe that the truth is what a mortal like themselves proclaims.

### The inner reading

Earth and sky are full of wonders of power and marvels of wisdom, and these are evidence of a Lord and Creator, witnesses to His oneness, His knowledge and His power. He set the seven green domes upon the wind, one above another, without pillar or joint, as a mark of His power, and set the seven dust-coloured hills upon the water as a display of His wisdom. Of the one He says, "Indeed, We have placed constellations in the sky, and adorned it for all to see" (15:16), and of the other, "As for the earth, We spread it out. How superbly did We smooth it out" (51:48). Consider the likeness of two doves, one black and one white, flying within the blue dome. On the wings of one is written "We made the sign of the night devoid of light" and on the wings of the other "We made the sign of the day ˹perfectly˺ bright" (17:12). The black is born from the white and the white appears from the black: "He wraps the night around the day, and wraps the day around the night" (39:5), "Allah causes the night to merge into the day, and the day into the night" (22:61). Pure and faultless is the God who brings the brightness of day out of the pitch-black night and makes the darkness of night appear out of the brightness of day. More wondrous still, He placed the brightness of knowing at the centre of the heart's dark blood and the brightness of seeing at the centre of the eye's black pupil. By this you know that He is the perfectly powerful, the generous and bountiful giver. The bright day is the sign of the covenant of good fortune, and the dark night the likeness of days of trial, as if to say: you who enjoy the ease of daylight, do not feel secure, for the darkness of trial's night follows on its tracks.

### In short

- Earth as a bed, sky as a roof, rain from above and fruit from the ground: four blessings that no creature could produce and that no one was present to witness being made.
- Even the fourth, the harvest, is Allah's work. The farmer clears the way. Allah brings the growth.
- These verses are a proof of the Creator from the order and variety of creation, and the believer learns from them to see Allah's power behind every apparent cause.
- The conclusion is the verse's own command: knowing all this, set up no equal to Allah, whether a visible idol, a force of nature, or anything else the heart clings to.
