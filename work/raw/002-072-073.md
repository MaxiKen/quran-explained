<<<VERSE 2:72>>>
Verse 72 goes back to the beginning of the story of the cow. "This is when a man was killed and you disputed who the killer was, but Allah revealed what you concealed", it says. The sacrifice described in the previous verses was the means Allah chose to answer the question this verse poses: who committed the murder?

### Phrase by phrase

**"when a man was killed"**. Allah reminds the Israelites of the time when they killed one of their own. The murder is attributed to the whole people because it happened among them and they then hid it, and one early commentary gives the victim's name as ʿĀmīl. Grammatically, this clause is where the story actually begins, "and when you killed a soul", and the previous verses about the cow are an inserted scene that comes first in the telling. The verb for "you disputed" is iddāraʾtum, whose form shows a letter assimilated into the one next to it.

**"and you disputed who the killer was"**. Al-Bukhārī explained the word as "disputed", and this is also the explanation of Mujāhid, while ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk said they quarrelled over the matter. Ibn Jurayj and ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam described the quarrel: some of them said, "you killed him", and the others said, "no, you killed him". Each party rejected the accusation and threw it at someone else, until they came to blows. The murderer had his own supporters who wanted to bury his crime, and so they went about accusing different people.

**"but Allah revealed what you concealed"**. Mujāhid explained the last word as "what you were hiding". Allah willed that the criminal be brought to account and so exposed what they had hidden about the killing of an innocent man, by the miraculous means the next verse describes. One commentary treats this clause as a parenthesis that gives the outcome in advance, before the story continues.

### Why the order is reversed

The murder and the mutual accusations came first in time, and the questions about the cow came afterwards, yet the Qur'an narrates the cow first and the murder second. Scholars find a purpose in the inversion. This long section of the sūrah is concerned with the transgressions of the Israelites, and the stories are told to bring those out rather than for their own sake. The present story exposes two misdeeds: committing a murder and then trying to conceal it, and raising uncalled-for objections to divine commands. Had the chronological order been kept, readers would have assumed that only the first was really intended and that the second was mentioned merely to round off the story. The arrangement as it stands makes clear that both misdeeds are being stressed equally.

### The secret that will not stay hidden

The devotional reading draws out the moral of "Allah revealed what you concealed". A disloyal person lives in fear, and out of fear that his secret will come to light he leans on concealment, deception, denial and evasion. But his fault (ʿawāruhu) is bound to be discovered, his secrets (asrāruhu) exposed and his coverings (astāruhu) pulled away, because of the shame of what he did. That is the force of Allah's words: He disclosed what they were hiding.

### In short

- A man was murdered among the Israelites, and the people fell to accusing one another while the killer's supporters tried to hide his guilt.
- Allah brought the hidden crime to light, and the sacrifice of the cow was the means.
- The story is told out of order so that both faults, concealing a murder and quibbling over a command, receive equal weight.
- No concealment can keep a guilty secret from Allah.

<<<VERSE 2:73>>>
Verse 73 completes the story and states its lesson. "So We instructed, 'Strike the dead body with a piece of the cow.' This is how easily Allah brings the dead to life, showing you His signs so that you may understand", it says. The cow, the murder and the questions all turn out to have been building toward a demonstration of resurrection.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So We instructed, 'Strike the dead body with a piece of the cow'"**. Allah commanded them to strike the murdered man with part of the cow they had been told to sacrifice, and told them that He would then bring him to life to say who had killed him. Any part of the cow would have produced the miracle, and the Qur'an does not say which part they used. Scholars observe that this detail would benefit us neither in religion nor in worldly life, or Allah would have made it clear, so since He left it unspecified we should leave it so. Some reports nonetheless name the part: an organ of the cow, and specifically its tongue or its tail. When the corpse was struck, the dead man came back to life, announced the name of his murderer, and died again. One account has him point out two of his cousins before dying, after which the two killers were denied the inheritance and were later put to death.

**"This is how easily Allah brings the dead to life"**. They struck him and he lived. The verse points to Allah's power to raise the dead, and Allah made the event a proof against the Jews that the Resurrection will take place, while at the same time ending their dispute and their stubbornness over the dead man. Just as He brought this man back to life, so He will bring the dead back to life on the Day of Judgement. Life after death is no less a reality than life before death, and just as this corpse was revived, every soul will be raised and given new life in the Hereafter.

**"showing you His signs so that you may understand"**. His signs are the proofs of His power, here the raising of the dead. The purpose is that people should reflect and realise that the One who can revive a single soul can revive a multitude of souls, and so believe in resurrection after death and have true faith in Him. A precedent of this kind should move people to use their reason and see that what happened once in the past can as easily happen in the future.

### Five resurrections in one sūrah

Scholars note that Allah mentions His raising of the dead in five places in Sūrat al-Baqarah. The first was the raising of the Israelites after the thunderbolt, "then We brought you back to life after your death". The second is this story of the cow. The third is the story of the people who fled death in their land though they numbered thousands. The fourth is the story of the prophet who passed by a ruined town. The fifth is the story of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and the four birds, together with the earth that is brought back to life after it was dead. Every one of these alerts us to the fact that bodies will be made whole again after they have decayed. The same proof of the Resurrection is given in Sūrat Yā Sīn, where the dead earth that Allah brings to life, with its grain, its gardens of palms and vines and its gushing springs, is called a sign for them (36:33-35).

### Why this way?

One may ask why Allah made the dead man's revival depend on his being touched with a piece of flesh, when He had the power to revive him without any such means. One may also ask why the man needed to be brought back to life at all, when the murderer's name could have been revealed in some other way. The answer is that Allah is omnipotent and acts under no compulsion, and that all His acts proceed from His all-embracing wisdom. He alone knows, and can know, the reasons for what He does. The Sharīʿah does not oblige us to discover the reason behind every divine act, and it is neither necessary nor possible that we should grasp it in every case. The right course is to accept what Allah and His Prophet ﷺ have said and keep silent about the rest.

### A legal note

In this case the statement of the murdered man was accepted as evidence to convict the murderer, because Allah had informed Mūsā (Moses) through revelation that the man would speak the truth when he returned to life. In the ordinary way no one can be convicted of murder without proper evidence, according to the rules the Sharīʿah lays down.

### The life of the heart

The devotional reading finds a second resurrection in the verse. God wished to bring the dead man to life so that his own testimony would expose his killer, and He made the slaughter of one of their animals the cause of his revival. The allusion is to anyone who longs for the life of his heart: he will attain it only by sacrificing his lower self, for the heart of whoever sacrifices his lower self through spiritual struggle (mujāhadāt) comes alive with the lights of witnessing (mushāhadāt). In the same way, when God wants a person's good name to live for ever, He lets his reputation in this world fade into obscurity first.

### In short

- Struck with a piece of the sacrificed cow, the murdered man revived, named his killer and died again. Which part of the cow was used is unspecified, and is best left so.
- The event is a proof of the Resurrection, one of five such proofs in this sūrah, and its stated purpose is to make people reflect and believe.
- Allah needed no means to revive him or to expose the killer. His acts flow from His wisdom, which we are not obliged to fathom.
- The dead man's testimony counted as evidence only because revelation vouched for it. Ordinary murder cases require the Sharīʿah's standards of proof.
- The heart lives only when the lower self is sacrificed.
