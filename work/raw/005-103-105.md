<<<VERSE 5:103>>>
"Allah has never ordained the ˹so-called˺ baḥîrah, sâ'ibah, waṣîlah, and ḥâm camels. But the disbelievers just fabricate lies about Allah, and most of them lack understanding"

This verse says that Allah has never ordained the practices of the baḥîrah, the sâ'ibah, the waṣîlah and the ḥâm among the camels and sheep. It states that the disbelievers invent lies against Allah, and that most of them lack understanding.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah has never ordained the so-called baḥîrah, sâ'ibah, waṣîlah, and ḥâm camels."** The material explains each of these practices as they were known in the time of ignorance. The baḥîrah was a female camel that had given birth five times, and whose ears were slit, and whose milk and benefit were given to idols and kept from women. The sâ'ibah was a camel let loose for the idols, which was not ridden or loaded. The waṣîlah was a sheep or camel that had given birth to a series of females, and whose males were kept for the men only. The ḥâm was a male camel that had finished a set number of mating, and which was let free for the idols. Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib is reported by Al-Bukhari to have explained these terms in this way, and Abu Hurayrah is reported to have said that the Prophet ﷺ saw 'Amr ibn Amir al-Khuza'i pulling his intestines in the Fire, since he was the first to start the practice of the sâ'ibah. Ibn Mas'ud is reported by Ahmad to have said the same. The material says that 'Amr ibn Luhayy was the chief of the Khuza'ah who changed the religion of Ibrahim in Makkah and brought idol worship into the Hijaz.

**"But the disbelievers just fabricate lies about Allah."** The material explains that the practices were not legislated by Allah, and that the idolaters made them into acts of worship that they hoped would bring them near to Allah. The material says that these innovations only harm those who follow them. The material cites 3:136, which says that the people assign to Allah a share of the tilth and cattle that He has created.

**"And most of them lack understanding."** The material says that most of them had no knowledge of the command of Allah. They had only the custom of their forefathers.

### The names and the material of the customs

The material says that Muslim and An-Nasa'i recorded the report of Abu Hurayrah on 'Amr ibn Amir, and Ahmad recorded the report of Ibn Mas'ud, which names Abu Khuza'ah, the same man, as the first to begin the sâ'ibah and the worship of idols. The material says that this 'Amr was from Khuza'ah, the caretakers of the House after Jurhum and before Quraysh, the tribe of the Prophet ﷺ. The material says that he changed the religion of Ibrahim, the Khalil, in Makkah, and brought idol worship into the Hijaz. Surah Al-An'am is cited for the claim that the people assign to Allah a share of the tilth and cattle. The material reports that Muhammad ibn Ishaq, Abu Rawq and Ibn Wahb recorded further explanations of these terms, and that Ibn Wahb heard Malik ibn Anas explain the ḥâm as the male camel given a set number of matings. The material reports from Abu al-Ahwas, from his father Malik ibn Nadlah al-Jushami, the saying of the Prophet ﷺ that the wealth Allah gives is lawful, and that the cutting of the ears of camels as a sacred thing is not to be done. Al-Musayyib is named in several of these reports.

### The hadith of the man in old clothes

The material gives a report that Malik ibn Nadlah came to the Prophet ﷺ in old clothes, and that the Prophet ﷺ asked him about his wealth and whether his camels bore full ears. The Prophet ﷺ then said that he should not cut the ears of the animals and call them sacred, since all the wealth Allah has given is lawful. Ibn Abi Hatim and Ahmad recorded the report. The material also reports from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, As-Suddi, Qatadah and Malik ibn Anas, and from Az-Zuhri, descriptions of the same customs.

### Shirk and the invented lies

The material explains that the idolaters' invented practices were the lies that they told against Allah, and that the shirk they committed was in their claim that Allah had made these things lawful or unlawful. The material says that most of them had no understanding of the command of Allah, and that their error was a form of shirk.

### In short

- Allah did not ordain the baḥîrah, the sâ'ibah, the waṣîlah or the ḥâm.
- These practices were invented by the idolaters, and they harm the people who follow them.
- Most of those who followed them had no knowledge.

<<<VERSE 5:104>>>
"When it is said to them, “Come to Allah's revelations and to the Messenger,” they reply, “What we found our forefathers practicing is good enough for us.” ˹Would they still do so,˺ even if their forefathers had absolutely no knowledge or guidance"

This verse describes how the idolaters, when called to the revelation of Allah and to the Messenger, reply that the way of their forefathers is enough for them. It asks whether they would still follow their forefathers even if those forefathers had no knowledge or guidance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When it is said to them, Come to Allah's revelations and to the Messenger."** The material explains that the call is to accept the commands of Allah and the teaching of the Messenger, including the permission of what they had forbidden to themselves. The material says that the call was given to the idolaters of Makkah.

**"What we found our forefathers practicing is good enough for us."** The material says this is the master argument of the one who follows custom without thought. The material explains that custom was a source of many evils, and that a person who accepted the truth was put to shame by his people, who said he had shamed his forefathers.

**"Would they still do so, even if their forefathers had absolutely no knowledge or guidance?"** The material says that this question is a sharp reply. Following a leader makes sense only if the leader has knowledge and guidance. The material says that a person who follows the forefathers who had neither knowledge nor guidance is following blindly.

### The criterion of a leader

The material explains that the verse gives a principle for choosing a leader. A person must first know the aim of his life, and then look for someone who knows the way to it and walks in it. The material says that following the mujtahid imams is of this kind, since they know the religion and follow it. The material says that following a leader who is himself astray, or who is headed the wrong way, is a waste of effort and a path to ruin. The material says that following the mass of people is not a criterion either, since the majority are not the wisest.

### The Quraysh and the call to the revelation

The material says that when the Prophet ﷺ called the people of Quraysh to the revelation and to the Messenger, they answered with the custom of their forefathers. The material explains that the Al-Durr Al-Manthur reports from Ibn Abi Hatim that a person who listened to the truth and embraced Islam was put to shame by his people, who said he had proved his forefathers to be no good. The verse answers this with the question about forefathers who knew nothing.

### In short

- The idolaters refused the call to the revelation by appealing to the custom of their forefathers.
- The question asks whether custom is enough when the forefathers had no knowledge or guidance.
- A leader must have knowledge and walk the right path, and the crowd is not the measure.

<<<VERSE 5:105>>>
"O believers! You are accountable only for yourselves. It will not harm you if someone chooses to deviate, as long as you are ˹rightly˺ guided. To Allah you will all return, and He will inform you of what you used to do"

This verse tells the believers that they are accountable only for themselves. It will not harm them if someone chooses to deviate, as long as they are rightly guided. All will return to Allah, who will inform them of what they did.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! You are accountable only for yourselves."** The material explains that the believers must first look after their own souls, keep them in their best interest, and do what is right. The material says that the verse comforts the reformers who have done their best to call people to the truth, and who are not responsible for those who stick to their error.

**"It will not harm you if someone chooses to deviate, as long as you are rightly guided."** The material says that the verse should not be read as a removal of the duty to call others. The material reports from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that the believers must keep doing what their religion obliges them to do, including jihad and enjoining good. The verse says that if they do this and others still go astray, that will not harm them. The material says that the converse of the condition shows that one who has given up enjoining good is not on the right path.

**"To Allah you will all return, and He will inform you of what you used to do."** The material says that the final accounting is before Allah, who informs each person of what he did.

### The reading of the hadith

The material reports the hadith of Abu Tha'labah al-Khushani, who asked the Prophet ﷺ about this verse, and was told to enjoin the good and forbid the evil, and that when greed is obeyed and desire is followed and the world is preferred, then each person must look to his own soul. The material says that this is the balance between the personal duty and the duty to others.

### The khutbah of Abu Bakr and the command to enjoin good

The material reports that Ibn Umar was asked about a dispute between two people who called each other polytheists, and he said that the believers were not commanded to fight them, but to speak to them gently, and that if they did not listen the believers should look after themselves. He recited this verse as a confirmation. The material reports that Abu Bakr as-Siddiq, in a khutbah, warned the people who used this verse out of context, and said that he had heard the Prophet ﷺ say that those who see a sin and do not try to stop it are likely to be seized by punishment along with the sinners. The material says that Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah recorded this, and that Abu Dawud recorded that the punishment falls on all when an oppressor is not stopped.

The material explains that enjoining the good, al-amr bil-ma'ruf, and forbidding the evil, nahy 'anil-munkar, are duties. It cites 16:83 for the meaning of recognising and denying the blessings of Allah. Al-Raghib al-Isfahani, in the Mufradat, is reported to define the ma'ruf as what reason and revelation know as good, and the munkar as what is strange and taken as bad. The material notes that in matters of ijtihad, the mujtahid imams may differ, and neither opinion is to be censured as munkar, and the Companions and the Successors did not call one another sinners for their differences. It also cites Sa'id ibn Jubayr, as recorded in the Al-Bahr Al-Muhit, that the believers must keep doing what their religion obliges them to do, and that the verse does not remove that duty.

### The soul and the duty to others

The material reports that Abu 'Uthman, when asked about this verse, said that one who occupies himself with correcting his own soul and covering its faults will have no time to look at the creatures. Al-Hallaj advised his disciple that if one does not keep the soul busy, it will keep one busy. Abu Yazid al-Bistami said that if the Lord asked him to make a wish in the next world, he would ask permission to enter Hell to punish his soul, for it had always made him suffer in this world. The material gives the saying of the Prophet ﷺ that the worst enemy is one's soul between one's two sides, and that a person who acts well towards his soul in this world will find it an enemy in the next, and one who acts badly towards it will find it grateful there.

The material also reports that Abu al-Layth, Makkah and the people of the Holy Qur'an read the verse with the sense that the Shari'ah requires the believers to occupy themselves with the reform of their own souls before they judge others. Al-Baqarah is cited as the place where this story is told before.

### In short

- The believers are accountable for their own souls, and the deviation of others does not harm them if they are rightly guided.
- The verse does not remove the duty to enjoin good and forbid evil.
- All return to Allah, who will inform them of their deeds.
