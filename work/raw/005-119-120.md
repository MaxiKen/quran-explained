<<<VERSE 5:119>>>
The chapter closes with Allah's own declaration: “This is the Day when ˹only˺ the faithful will benefit from their faithfulness.” The answer follows 'Isa's disowning of those who lied about him, and it states what profit remains when everything else has fallen away.

The verse then describes the reward and its crown, which is Allah's pleasure, and calls it the ultimate triumph.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah will declare, “This is the Day"** answers His servant and messenger 'Isa son of Maryam after he disowned the disbelieving Christians who lied about Allah and His Messenger, and after he referred their end to the will of his Lord.

One gloss identifies the Day as the Day of Resurrection, the day of requital. Another describes the first two sections of the chapter as recounting happenings of the Day of Gathering, such as the reckoning and the questions and answers, with this verse giving the outcome of that investigation.

**"when ˹only˺ the faithful will benefit from their faithfulness."** Ad-Dahhak reports Ibn 'Abbas explaining that on the Day named in verse 119 the tawhid of those who believed in it will be of benefit to them.

One gloss widens the profit: their truthfulness benefits the truthful, their faith the believers, their delivery the conveyors of messages and their loyalty the loyal. Another describes the truthful in this world, such as 'Isa, profiting by their truthfulness on that Day.

Allah will say this to 'Isa, and the truthfulness meant covers intentions, statements and actions.

**"Theirs are Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there for ever and ever."** describes gardens beneath whose trees and couches flow rivers of water, milk, wine and honey, where they dwell forever and never die or are removed. Another describes gardens with palaces and trees overlooking flowing streams, in which they live eternally and death never comes to them.

**"Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him."** His pleasure is because of their faith and works and their obedience to Him, and their pleasure is because of His reward and the honour bestowed on them, and because of the everlasting delight they have received.

A hadith reports that after blessing the true believers with Jannah, Allah will say that the real blessing is that He is pleased with them and will never be displeased with them again. Another account says Allah will be pleased with them and never become angry with them.

The greatest bliss is described elsewhere as the good pleasure of Allah Ta'ala (9:72), and the hadiths about that verse are mentioned later in the compilation from which this report comes.

**"That is the ultimate triumph.”"** means the great success beyond which there is no greater success. One gloss describes it as the ultimate safety, since they earned Paradise and were saved from the torment of the Fire. Another describes it as a supreme success that nothing else can equal.

When Allah, the Master and Creator, is pleased with a person, what else could there be? Two other verses point to the same aim, telling the workers to work for the like of this (37:61) and telling whoever wishes to strive to strive for this (83:26).

### What does truth mean here?

Generally, what accords with what happened is called truth, and what does not accord with it is called false or a lie. According to the Qur'an and Sunnah, however, truthfulness and lying are general terms covering both words and deeds.

A hadith calls a deed contrary to the fact a lie, saying that whoever adorns himself with what he has not been given, claiming a quality or deed that is not in him, has put on two garments of falsehood. It is recorded in Mishkat.

Another hadith calls a person who performs his salah with care and concern, in public and in private, a true servant of Allah. Whoever prays openly and does it well, and prays in private and does it well, is the one of whom Allah says that this is His servant in truth. That is also recorded in Mishkat.

Truthfulness is therefore a matter of agreement between the inside and the outside, in claim and in conduct. That is what profits on this Day.

### What will not profit on that Day?

This world was created to put people to the test, so everybody has freedom here. A person may prosper in this world even after attaching to Allah and His prophet a religion not connected with them. Here the right to Paradise can be claimed on the strength of imaginary hopes and false wishes.

Here a person can raise a great clamour about his own leadership and demonstrate that whatever he does is exactly in accordance with Allah's religion. None of that will be of use on the Day of Judgment.

What will be of use is that a person proves truthful in the eyes of Allah. The test of a community bearing divine scripture is not whether its members claim faith. Their real test is a demonstration of their ability to make that claim good.

The sincerity of those who were liars in this world will not avail them on that Day, just as belief will not avail disbelievers when they believe on seeing the punishment.

### In short

- The Day named is the day of requital, when truthfulness profits the truthful in intention, word and deed.
- Ibn 'Abbas describes the profit as that of tawhid for those who believed in it.
- Gardens with flowing rivers and eternal dwelling are given, and Allah's pleasure is described as the real blessing.
- The triumph is called the greatest success, with other verses urging workers and strivers toward the like of it.
- Claims, clamour and imaginary hopes are of no use then, and the test of a scripture-bearing community is proving its claim good.

<<<VERSE 5:120>>>
The chapter ends where it began, with sovereignty: "To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth and everything within." Nothing in the long account of covenants, rulings, disputes and questions stands outside that ownership.

The final words name His power over everything, which is the ground of both the reward and the punishment just described.

### Phrase by phrase

**"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth"** means that He is the Creator of all, the Owner of all, the Disposer of every affair, and able to do anything. One gloss describes the sovereignty as the stores of the heavens and the earth, including rain, lands, vegetation and fruits, and another as their storehouses of rain, vegetation, sustenance and everything else.

**"and everything within."** covers created beings and marvels. The wording used indicates the predominance of non-rational creation within that all, and the address here is specifically to rational beings, since none among them has power over all things.

**"And He is Most Capable of everything”** includes creating the heavens and the earth and bestowing reward and punishment. His power covers rewarding the truthful and punishing the liar.

### What does this closing statement settle?

All things and all people therefore stand inside His dominion, subject to His power and His will. Nothing resembles Him. He has no rival, no ancestor, no son and no wife, and there is no lord or god other than He.

That sentence answers the claims exposed earlier in the chapter, including the attribution of a rival, a wife and a son to Allah Ta'ala, and the taking of 'Isa and his mother as gods. Sovereignty and capability leave no room for any of it.

It also completes the movement begun in verse 119 and in the two ruku' before it. The declaration of the Day and its triumph is followed by the reason it holds: the One who rewards and punishes owns and controls all that exists, and nothing can overpower Him.

### What is reported about the end of this chapter?

Ibn Wahb reports hearing Huyay bin 'Abdullah say, from Abu 'Abdur-Rahman al-Habli, that 'Abdullah bin 'Amr said the last surah revealed was Surat Al-Ma'idah.

The report is recorded here as the closing note of the chapter. It is one of several statements about which surah came last, and it does not require every such statement to be identical.

### In short

- Allah owns the kingdom of the heavens and the earth and everything in them, and controls all their affairs.
- His capability covers creation, reward and punishment, including rewarding the truthful and punishing the liar.
- The statement answers the claims of rival, son, wife and associate exposed earlier in the chapter.
- 'Abdullah bin 'Amr is reported as calling this the last surah revealed, one statement among several on that question.
