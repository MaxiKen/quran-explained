<<<VERSE 5:1>>>
Allah begins by calling on believers to keep their commitments: "O believers! Honour your obligations. All grazing livestock has been made lawful to you". The command includes the duties accepted through faith and the lawful agreements people make with one another. It then becomes concrete. Livestock is permitted as food, but that permission has exceptions, and a pilgrim in the sacred state must not hunt land game.

These are connected instructions, not unrelated subjects. Accepting Allah as Lord means accepting His right to determine what is lawful and unlawful. His permission to eat is a gift, and His restrictions are part of the covenant too. The closing words place both under His authority and wisdom.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers!"** addresses those who believe in Allah, follow His Messenger ﷺ and undertake to live by His law. It calls them by their faith because the obligation that follows belongs to that faith. Ibn Abi Hatim records that a man asked 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud for advice. He told him to listen closely whenever Allah addresses believers, because a command to do good or to avoid evil follows. Khaythamah said that the corresponding address in the Torah calls people by their need. Believers are honoured by the address, but they remain dependent on the One addressing them.

**"Honour your obligations."** The word al-'uqud means binding commitments. Its singular, 'aqd, originally means tying a knot. An agreement similarly binds its parties to what they have accepted. 'Uhud, pledges or covenants, expresses the same broad sense here. Obligations toward Allah, vows and agreements with other people all fall within the command. The believer must carry them out rather than treat a promise as words without consequences.

**"All grazing livestock has been made lawful to you"** grants permission to eat the animals specified, after lawful slaughter. Al-Hasan and Qatadah explain them as camels, cattle and sheep. Goats are included in the fuller explanation of the flocks. Ibn Jarir notes that this agrees with ordinary Arabic usage. The permission is an act of mercy, not something people create for themselves. Some explanations also include the corresponding wild animals, such as gazelles, wild cattle and wild donkeys. Another gloss mentions wild oxen and wild camels among the game covered by the permission.

**"except what is hereby announced to you"** makes clear that the permission is not unrestricted. The prohibitions are recited later in the surah, especially in the list of forbidden food (5:3). Ibn 'Abbas identifies carrion, blood and pork among the exceptions. Qatadah includes an animal that died without slaughter and one slaughtered without Allah's name being pronounced. Even an animal of an otherwise permitted kind may become forbidden because of how it died or was sacrificed.

**"and hunting while on pilgrimage."** Ihram is the sacred condition entered for Hajj or 'Umrah, the pilgrimage and the lesser pilgrimage. The word hurum describes people in that state. Land game must not be hunted then. One explanation also brings the Sacred Precinct within the prohibition. Permission to eat ordinary livestock does not remove the limits that apply to game in a sacred condition or place.

**"Indeed, Allah commands what He wills"** affirms His authority to permit and forbid. His decisions accord with His wisdom. No one can compel Him, and no created person has authority to overrule Him. He may allow something in one condition and prohibit it in another. Both decisions belong to the same Lord, and both call for obedience.

### Which agreements must be honoured?

Ibn Jarir reports agreement among the early authorities that the command concerns covenants or treaties. Ibn 'Abbas and Mujahid explain it in that sense. It includes the binding alliances known among people, but its reach is wider than political alliances alone.

Al-Jassas explains the terms 'aqd, 'ahd and mu'ahadah as contract, pledge and pact. They describe an undertaking in which parties accept responsibility toward each other for doing something or refraining from it. Each agrees to be bound. Once a permissible undertaking has been made, fulfilling it is not left to convenience.

Different explanations name different kinds of covenant. Ibn 'Abbas includes the obligations established by Allah: His permissions, prohibitions, commands and limits. A person who accepts faith must not betray that undertaking. The warning against breaking Allah's covenant and severing what He commanded to be joined gives the same duty a stern consequence (13:25).

Ad-Dahhak likewise explains the command through what Allah has allowed and forbidden. Those who profess belief in the Prophet ﷺ and the Book have accepted responsibility for observing these limits. Another explanation looks back to the obligations accepted on the Day of the Covenant, together with those laid down in the Book.

Ibn Zayd and Zayd ibn Aslam give attention to agreements between people, including marriage and buying and selling. Qatadah, Rabi' and Mujahid mention the sworn alliances through which tribes in the time before Islam promised mutual assistance. These explanations do not have to compete. Each identifies something included within a comprehensive word.

Raghib al-Isfahani gathers binding commitments into three kinds. First is the covenant with the Creator, including faith, obedience and respect for His lawful and unlawful limits. Second is a commitment a person places upon himself. A nadhr, a vow, or an oath to perform an act belongs here. Third is an agreement with another person or party. That extends from agreements between individuals to those between groups and governments.

The command therefore includes marriage, sales, partnerships, leases and deeds of gift. It also reaches treaties between governments, agreements between parties, charters and alliances. The scale of an agreement does not remove its binding character. Neither a private person nor a public body may simply discard the permissible conditions it has accepted.

The qualification "permissible" is essential. A contract cannot make lawful what the Shari'ah, the revealed law, forbids. It is not permissible to enter or accept an agreement against that law. Honouring obligations means respecting valid undertakings under Allah's authority, not using an undertaking as a reason to disobey Him.

### Why does the verse turn from contracts to food?

One of the commitments contained in faith is to accept Allah's distinction between lawful and unlawful. The ruling on livestock is therefore an example of the opening command. It takes the broad duty to honour obligations and applies it to something people need every day.

A believer has the freedom to choose, but voluntarily accepts regulation by the Lord he acknowledges. That includes dealings with Allah and with other people. It also includes eating what Allah permits and giving up what He forbids. A restriction does not cease to bind because the same thing is permitted at another time. The rules of ihram make that especially clear.

Respect for religious signs belongs to this outlook too. A thing that represents a sacred reality is honoured because of what it represents. The motive should be fear of Allah, not merely outward custom. The further rules about sacred symbols belong to the next verse.

The permission to eat livestock also rejects two opposite mistakes. One is to declare slaughter itself absolutely forbidden, as the discussion describes among fire-worshippers and idolaters. That objects to the Creator's wisdom and refuses His blessing. The other is to eat every kind of animal without restraint. A believer follows neither unrestricted appetite nor self-invented prohibition.

The reason given is that Allah created the animals and knows their natures. He also knows their effects upon human bodies and character. The explanation connects His permission with what is wholesome and His prohibition with what is impure or harmful. His knowledge, rather than a person's uncontrolled preference, governs the distinction.

### What does the expression for livestock mean?

Bahimah is a word for an animal whose speech people do not understand. Its obscurity to human beings lies behind the term. Al-an'am narrows that general word to grazing livestock. It is the plural of na'am and refers to the eight kinds described in the surah devoted to livestock. The explanation gives camels, cows, buffaloes and goats as examples.

The word bahimah should not be taken as proof that an animal has no awareness at all. Al-Sha'rani rejects that assumption. Animals have awareness suited to their needs, though not the human degree of understanding. The same explanation includes trees and rocks in their own degrees. Differences in understanding matter, and animals do not bear the revealed legal responsibilities imposed on human beings.

The Qur'an says that everything glorifies Allah with praise, though people do not understand its glorification (17:44). The argument drawn from this is that glorification presupposes some recognition of the Creator. What remains obscure to people is not therefore empty of meaning. An animal's inability to speak intelligibly to a human being is not the same as absence of all awareness.

The explanations of the food permission differ somewhat in scope. One takes the expression directly as domestic livestock. Another includes wild animals corresponding to it. On the wider understanding, the following restriction on hunting explains why permitted game cannot be taken in ihram. On another reading, livestock is permitted generally while the person addressed remains bound by a separate restriction concerning game. Both readings preserve the difference between the ordinary permission and the sacred-state prohibition.

### What if a fetus is found in a slaughtered animal?

Ibn 'Umar and Ibn 'Abbas used the verse as evidence concerning a fetus found dead inside its mother after the mother has been slaughtered. On this understanding, its meat may be eaten. Its mother's lawful slaughter is sufficient for it.

Abu Sa'id reports that people asked the Messenger ﷺ about this situation when slaughtering livestock. Should the fetus be thrown away or eaten? He allowed them to eat it if they wished, explaining that the mother's slaughter counted for it. The report appears in the Sunan of Ibn Majah, Abu Dawud and At-Tirmidhi. At-Tirmidhi grades it hasan, meaning good.

Abu Dawud also records a report from Jabir bin 'Abdullah in which the Messenger ﷺ gives the same rule. This particular narration is recorded by Abu Dawud alone. The point concerns the fetus within the lawfully slaughtered mother, not a general permission to eat every animal found dead.

### How do the exceptions limit the permission?

The later list of prohibited food explains what the opening permission leaves out (5:3). Al-maytah is carrion, an animal that has died without the required slaughter. Blood and pork are also excluded. Other animals may be of a lawful species but still be prohibited because of the manner of death or sacrifice.

That distinction explains why the later verse mentions animals that can still be lawfully slaughtered before they die. It also excludes what is sacrificed on an-nusub, stone altars. Being a sheep or a cow does not by itself settle every question about the meat. The circumstances have to be considered as well.

The exception can be understood in two grammatical ways. It can interrupt the general permission by mentioning prohibited things outside its immediate category. It can also remove particular cases from the permitted category itself. The second understanding is especially clear where an ordinarily lawful animal has died in a prohibited way. The grammatical alternatives do not erase the practical exceptions.

There is a further qualification for necessity. The cited declaration of Allah's forgiveness and mercy allows otherwise prohibited food when someone is compelled by need, provided the person is not deliberately disobedient or exceeding the limits. Necessity is not a pretext for unrestricted consumption. The permission remains tied to its condition.

The prohibition of hunting is also grammatically attached to the people receiving the permission. The word ghayra, expressing "not", introduces a statement of their condition. In plain terms, livestock is made lawful to them while they must not treat game as lawful to hunt during ihram. The reference is to those entering the sacred condition, not to a new species of animal.

### Why can the Creator permit animals to be slaughtered?

The closing declaration answers a possible objection to the permission itself. The Creator who brings the forms of life into being also determines their relations. Allowing people to slaughter certain animals is not described as injustice, because the Owner of those lives has authorised it in His wisdom.

The explanation presents a sequence of nourishment. Trees draw sustenance from the earth, animals feed on plants, and human beings feed on animals. Lower forms support higher ones within this account of the created order. Humanity occupies the highest place in that worldly ordering, so human beings are not assigned as food for others. This is offered as an explanation of the permission, not as authority to disregard its restrictions.

The verse therefore combines gratitude and restraint. The believer does not reject a permitted gift as though the Creator lacked wisdom. Nor does he turn a gift into a claim to unrestricted use. The One who grants permission is also the One who sets its limits.

### What does the call to believers say to the heart?

Ja'far ibn Muhammad finds four forms of honour in the opening address. Its call gives dignity, its direct address suggests mercy, its identifying words point to love, and its naming of faith bears witness to recognition. The command thus begins with an honour given to the people who must obey it.

In this inward reading, Allah's care precedes their appearance in the world. He names and calls those who have not yet come into existence. The statement that He named them submitters beforehand expresses this prior favour (22:78). Their faults were not hidden from Him when He chose to honour them.

The contrast is between the pure beings of the higher world and flawed human beings in the lower world. Allah's choice is not explained as ignorance of human failure. The allusion emphasises mercy toward the repentant. The sinner's remorseful sobbing can be dearer than the low murmur of someone already engaged in glorification.

An image makes the point. A mother dresses her child in fresh clothes and warns him not to dirty them. He goes out to play, stains them, and returns ashamed. He hides and says he wants to sleep, hoping to avoid rebuke. His mother understands his fear. She calls him close and tells him that she had already prepared soap and water before sending him out. She knew what he might do.

The image is applied to the human being brought from nonexistence into the world. The holy spirits objected to the prospect of corruption, as in the question about placing one who would cause corruption on earth (2:30). Human disobedience and the worship of others alongside Allah are pictured as smoke and dust staining a fine garment. The garment is associated with the declaration that the religion has been perfected (5:3).

The supplied account stops at this image. Its emphasis is nevertheless clear: the opening honour rests on Allah's prior generosity, not on a claim that human beings never fail. The mother has not called dirt cleanliness. She has prepared a way to wash it away. The call to honour obligations retains its force within that assurance of mercy.

### In short

- Faith binds a person to Allah's commands and to lawful commitments made with others. Vows, private contracts and public treaties all belong within the duty to keep promises.
- Livestock is permitted through Allah's mercy, but the permission excludes forbidden foods and prohibited circumstances of death or sacrifice.
- Land game must not be hunted in ihram. A permission in ordinary circumstances does not cancel a restriction attached to a sacred condition.
- Reports allow the fetus found within its lawfully slaughtered mother to be eaten. Necessity also has its own limited permission, not an unrestricted exemption.
- Allah's authority governs both permission and prohibition. His honouring address to believers joins responsibility with mercy that precedes their failures.
