<<<VERSE 3:38>>>
After seeing Allah provide Maryam with food out of season, “Then and there Zachariah prayed to his Lord” (3:38). He asks for a righteous child despite his old age and his wife’s barrenness. The provision he witnessed makes him hopeful that Allah can grant what is normally beyond reach. He turns to Allah, who hears every supplication.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Then and there Zachariah prayed to his Lord”** connects Zakariyyā’s prayer to what he had just seen in Maryam’s sanctuary. She had received summer fruit in winter and winter fruit in summer. One explanation says that witnessing this unusual provision strengthened his hope that Allah could also give him a child in old age. Zakariyyā had no child at that time, and he and his wife were elderly. He nevertheless believed in Allah’s power and prayed.

**“My Lord! Grant me—by your grace—righteous offspring”** is a request for a good child, not for wealth or worldly status. An inward explanation says that Zakariyyā sought a child to help him obey Allah and continue the work of prophethood, upholding the ḥaqq, the right due to Allah. The request was directed to Allah’s cause rather than the desires of the nafs, the lower self. He knew that a child at his age would be outside the ordinary course of events, but he asked Allah for His favor.

The Qur’an mentions that messengers before Muhammad ﷺ had wives and children (13:38). Another prayer asks for spouses and children who will be a comfort to the eyes because they obey Allah (25:74). Together these passages show that asking for righteous children is a good prayer. The explanation also says that marriage and family life are part of the Sunnah of the Messenger, and that a person who is able to marry and fulfill a spouse’s rights should not turn away from marriage. One report gives the Prophet’s encouragement to marry loving, fertile women so that the community may grow.

A report about Umm Sulaym illustrates prayer for children and blessing. She asked the Prophet ﷺ to pray for her attendant Anas. He prayed that Allah increase Anas’s wealth and children and bless what he had been given. Anas later had about a hundred children and abundant wealth, according to the account.

**“You are certainly the Hearer of ˹all˺ prayers”** expresses Zakariyyā’s trust that Allah hears those who call upon Him. His prayer is made with humility, and he knows that Allah is able to answer it in a way no human could arrange.

### Why did Zakariyyā ask after seeing Maryam’s provision?

The fruit in the miḥrāb, the sanctuary where Maryam worshipped, was a sign that Allah provides beyond ordinary seasons. Zakariyyā saw that provision and became hopeful. His prayer does not imply that he doubted Allah’s power. Rather, the sign moved him to ask for a blessing that he had not thought possible under the usual conditions of age and barrenness.

### In short

- Zakariyyā prayed after seeing Allah provide Maryam with food out of season.
- He asked for righteous offspring to help in worship and the work of faith.
- Allah hears supplication and can answer beyond ordinary expectations.

<<<VERSE 3:39>>>
While Zakariyyā stood in prayer in the miḥrāb, “the angels called out to him” (3:39) with the news that Allah would grant him Yaḥyā (John). The child would confirm the word of Allah, become a noble and chaste leader, and be a prophet among the righteous. This is the answer to Zakariyyā’s supplication.

### Phrase by phrase

**“So the angels called out to him while he stood praying in the sanctuary”** places the good news during Zakariyyā’s worship. One explanation identifies the angel as Gabriel. The miḥrāb is the sanctuary or place of prayer. An inward reading says that a person who remains at the door of a king’s court continues to ask until the request is granted. It compares this to staying devoted in service to Allah rather than turning away from Him.

**“Allah gives you good news of ˹the birth of˺ John”** promises Zakariyyā a son named Yaḥyā. One explanation connects his name with life because Allah fills his heart with faith. Another says that the news revives his mother, who had been barren, and gives spiritual life to believers. Zakariyyā had asked for a child, and the angels now answer his supplication.

**“who will confirm the Word of Allah”** refers to ʿĪsā (Jesus), son of Maryam. He is called a word from Allah because he was created by Allah’s command without a father. Yaḥyā would affirm the truth of ʿĪsā’s message. The same description of Allah’s power appears in the account of Mūsā (Moses), whose mother was told that Allah would return him to her and make him a messenger (28:7).

**“and will be a great leader”** translates the quality sayyid. The explanations describe Yaḥyā as noble, wise, learned, pious, and honored by Allah. Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Thawrī, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk explain the word through nobility, wisdom, and piety. Saʿīd bin al-Musayyib describes a sayyid as a scholar and jurist. ʿAṭiyyah emphasizes noble conduct and piety. ʿIkrimah says he was not overcome by anger. Ibn Zayd calls him noble, while Mujāhid says he was honored by Allah. These explanations present leadership as character and service, not merely authority.

**“chaste”** describes Yaḥyā’s protection from unlawful sexual conduct and domination by desire. One explanation says this does not necessarily mean he never married. Zakariyyā had asked for offspring, so the description can mean that Yaḥyā was protected from unlawful relations while remaining able to have a family lawfully. Another explanation describes him as abstinent from women and other ordinary pleasures because he was wholly occupied with worship. A further inward reading says he was free from bondage to created things and had realized ʿubūdiyya, true servanthood to Allah. These are explanations of his exceptional devotion, not a general rejection of lawful family life.

**“and a prophet among the righteous”** promises more than the birth of a son. Yaḥyā would himself receive prophethood and join the righteous. The good news includes his mission, not only his arrival. Some explanations also say that his mother’s barrenness would be overcome, just as the mother of Mūsā received good news about her son’s future mission (28:7).

Among those named in the explanations of Yaḥyā’s qualities are al-Baṣrī, Qatādah, Abū Ash-Shaʿthāʾ, al-Suddī, al-Rabīʿ bin Anas, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Saʿīd bin Jubayr, Saʿīd bin al-Musayyib, ʿAṭiyyah, Ibn Zayd, and Mujāhid. Their comments explain the promise as a child of faith, noble leadership, chastity, and prophethood.

### What does “chaste” mean here?

The explanations distinguish protection from unlawful desire from a universal rule against marriage. Some understand Yaḥyā’s quality as complete abstinence, while another clarifies that it does not mean he could not marry lawfully. The descriptions agree that he was protected from wrongdoing and devoted to Allah.

### In short

- The angels announced Yaḥyā’s birth while Zakariyyā was praying.
- Yaḥyā would confirm ʿĪsā, the word created by Allah’s command.
- He would be a noble, chaste, and righteous prophet.

<<<VERSE 3:40>>>
Zakariyyā asks how he can have a son when he is very old and his wife is barren. The answer is that “Allah does what He wills” (3:40). His question seeks to understand how the promise will happen, not to deny Allah’s power.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Zachariah exclaimed, ‘My Lord! How can I have a son when I am very old and my wife is barren?’”** (3:40) expresses his amazement at the news. He and his wife were both advanced in age, and she had been unable to bear children. One account gives Zakariyyā’s age as 120 and his wife’s as 98. Their circumstances make the promised birth extraordinary.

The question is not a rejection of the angels’ message. Zakariyyā already believes that Allah can create and provide beyond ordinary causes. He asks about the manner of the birth: will their condition change, or will a child come while they remain as they are? One explanation says they would remain in their condition and Allah would grant them a child. Another says the question arose from eagerness to understand how the promise would be fulfilled. An allusive explanation offers several possibilities, including whether the child would be born through his wife or by another means, then affirms that the promise concerned a child from both of them. The answer came through Gabriel, who had brought the good news, and the Qur’an presents this birth as part of Allah’s path of prophethood. The announcement of Yahya’s qualities includes nobility and piety, which ʿAṭiyyah uses to explain the title sayyid.

The story recalls the miraculous birth of ʿĪsā (Jesus), son of Maryam, and the promise given to the mother of Mūsā (Moses). The creation of Yaḥyā is within Allah’s power even when it departs from ordinary expectation. The sign that will follow makes the pregnancy known to Zakariyyā (19:10), and the promise of a child through an unlikely circumstance recalls the good news given to Mūsā’s mother (28:7).

**“He replied, ‘So will it be. Allah does what He wills.’”** answers that Allah’s will is not limited by age or barrenness. Nothing escapes His power and nothing is beyond His ability. The reply does not say that the ordinary laws of nature have power over Allah. It says that Allah creates as He chooses and in His wisdom.

### Why did Zakariyyā ask how it could happen?

He had received good news that challenged ordinary experience. His question seeks clarity about the circumstances, not reassurance that Allah has power. He had already seen Allah provide Maryam with food outside its season. That sign had opened his heart to asking for a child, and now he learns that Allah will answer his prayer despite the age of both parents.

### In short

- Zakariyyā and his wife were elderly, and she was barren.
- His question expressed wonder about how the promise would occur, not doubt in Allah.
- Allah’s will is not restricted by ordinary causes.

<<<VERSE 3:41>>>
Zakariyyā asks Allah for a sign. He is told that he “will not ˹be able to˺ speak to people for three days except through gestures” (3:41). He can still remember Allah and glorify Him morning and evening. The sign directs him to gratitude and remembrance while the promise unfolds.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Zachariah said, ‘My Lord! Grant me a sign’”** means he asks for an indication that his wife is pregnant. One explanation says that he wants to rejoice and begin thanking Allah before the child is born. He is not asking because he doubts the promise. He wants a sign that will help him remain grateful.

**“Your sign is that you will not ˹be able to˺ speak to people for three days except through gestures”** describes a temporary inability to speak to people, not a lasting disability. Zakariyyā can communicate through signs. The period is also described as three nights (19:10). One explanation stresses that he was not mute because of bodily defect. Another says the sign was suited to his request because it turned him away from ordinary conversation and toward remembrance of Allah.

The verse also establishes that when a valid reason prevents speech, gestures can serve as communication. Al-Qurṭubī cites a report about the Prophet ﷺ asking a servant woman where Allah is. She pointed toward the sky, and he accepted her faith. The gesture conveyed what she intended to say. Zakariyyā’s supplication continues in another form through the remembrance and glorification he is commanded to perform.

**“Remember your Lord often”** commands Zakariyyā to keep Allah present in his heart and on his tongue. An inward explanation describes three levels of remembrance: praising and supplicating with the tongue, remembering inwardly in the heart, and recognizing Allah’s remembrance of the servant. It points to remembrance in battle (8:45), strong remembrance (2:200), and remembering Allah when one forgets (18:24).

**“and glorify ˹Him˺ morning and evening”** directs him to praise Allah at the beginning and end of the day. The sign is therefore not merely silence. It becomes an opportunity for prayer, thanks, and worship.

### Why was silence given as the sign?

Zakariyyā asked for a sign so that he could rejoice and give thanks in advance. The sign makes ordinary speech unavailable for a short time, while leaving him able to signal to people and remember Allah. One allusive explanation presents the silence as a special conversation with Allah: his tongue is restrained from people, while his attention turns more fully to the One who answered his prayer.

### In short

- Zakariyyā asks for a sign of his wife’s pregnancy.
- He cannot speak to people for three days but can communicate by gestures.
- He is commanded to remember and glorify Allah often, morning and evening.

<<<VERSE 3:42>>>
The angels tell Maryam, “Surely Allah has selected you, purified you, and chosen you over all women of the world” (3:42). Her selection reflects her devotion and purity, and her unique place among the women of her time. The verse honors her before describing the worship commanded in the next verse.

### Phrase by phrase

**“And ˹remember˺ when the angels said”** introduces the message given to Maryam by Allah’s command. One explanation says that Gabriel conveyed the message. Maryam is being prepared for a special test and for the birth of ʿĪsā (Jesus), without a father.

**“O Mary!”** addresses Maryam directly and recognizes her as the recipient of this honor. The words call her to understand her standing before Allah.

**“Surely Allah has selected you”** means Allah singled her out through worship, modesty, honor, and conviction. One report says that ʿAlī bin Abī Ṭālib heard the Prophet ﷺ describe Maryam, daughter of ʿImrān, as the best woman of her time, and Khadījah, daughter of Khuwaylid, as the best woman of the Prophet’s time. At-Tirmidhi recorded it, and it is also found in the Two Sahihs.

**“purified you”** means that Allah made her free of disbelief, indecency, and sin. One explanation adds that she was kept from harm. Another describes her as protected from physical contact with men. An inward reading says her purity included a beautiful protection from sin and a special immunity.

**“and chosen you over all women of the world”** is understood by one explanation to mean the women of her own time. Her distinction includes being chosen to bear ʿĪsā without a father, a sign of Allah’s power unlike that granted to any other woman. It does not conflict with reports that name other women as foremost in later times.

A report from Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī, recorded by Ibn Jarir, says that many men reached perfection, while among women Maryam and Āsiyah, the wife of Firʿawn, reached it. The Six collections, except Abū Dāwūd, recorded this report. Al-Bukhari’s wording also mentions the excellence of ʿĀʾishah, comparing it with the superiority of tharīd, a dish of meat and bread, over other meals. Another account notes that details of the report’s chains are discussed in Al-Bidayah. These reports describe excellence among women in different times and do not cancel Maryam’s distinction in her own time.

### What does “chosen” mean?

The verse uses the idea of choice twice. Allah first selected Maryam for devotion and purity, then honored her above the women of her time through the unique role she would have in the birth of ʿĪsā. Her rank is a favor from Allah, and the next verse shows that she is instructed to continue in worship.

### In short

- Allah selected and purified Maryam.
- Her distinction includes the unique honor of bearing ʿĪsā without a father.
- Reports about Maryam, Khadījah, Āsiyah, and ʿĀʾishah describe women of excellence in their respective contexts.

<<<VERSE 3:43>>>
Maryam is told to worship Allah: “Be devout to your Lord, prostrate yourself ˹in prayer˺ and bow along with those who bow down” (3:43). Her selection does not remove the need for worship. It calls her to gratitude, humility, and prayer with the worshippers.

### Phrase by phrase

**“O Mary!”** renews the direct address to Maryam and connects the command to the special favor described in the previous verse.

**“Be devout to your Lord”** translates qunūt, devotion and obedient submission. It means she should remain humble and steady in worship. One explanation says she should prolong her prayer as a way to thank Allah. Another says that her exceptional station calls her to continue serving Allah without becoming complacent.

**“prostrate yourself ˹in prayer˺”** commands her to lower herself before Allah. Prostration expresses submission and humility, not merely an outward movement. The instruction includes the worship due to Allah in response to the favor she has received.

**“and bow along with those who bow down”** directs Maryam to bow in prayer with the worshippers. The wording links her worship with the community of believers. One explanation notes that the command to bow is qualified by “with those who bow,” while the command to prostrate is not. It suggests that people may bow too quickly or incompletely, and that Maryam should follow the ar-rākiʿīn, those who bow fully and properly. Rukūʿ is the bowing position in prayer. The interpreters say that this shared worship keeps her on the path of humility and devotion.

The verse also reminds readers that submission belongs to all creation. Allah says that everything in the heavens and earth belongs to Him and submits to Him (2:116). Maryam’s prayer is a special instruction, but the principle of humility and obedience is universal.

### Why is Maryam told to worship after being chosen?

Her selection is not permission to stop worshipping. Rather, the honor increases her responsibility and gratitude. She is told to continue in devotion, prostration, and proper bowing with the worshippers. The command turns the favor described in 3:42 into an active life of service.

### In short

- Qunūt means devotion and obedient humility.
- Maryam is told to prostrate and to bow with the worshippers.
- Allah’s favor is met with gratitude and continued worship.

<<<VERSE 3:44>>>
The verse tells the Prophet that Maryam’s story is “news of the unseen that We reveal to you” (3:44). He was not present when the people argued over her care or cast lots to choose her guardian. The account is therefore presented as revelation, not as something he learned by witnessing the event.

### Phrase by phrase

**“This is news of the unseen that We reveal to you ˹O Prophet˺”** refers to the account of Maryam and Zakariyyā. These events happened long before Muhammad ﷺ. One explanation says there was no ordinary way for him to have witnessed or learned the details. The story is evidence that Allah revealed knowledge of the unseen to him.

**“You were not with them when they cast lots to decide who would be Mary’s guardian”** recalls the dispute among the caretakers of Bayt al-Maqdis, the sacred place in Jerusalem. Maryam’s mother brought her there as a dedicated child, and those responsible for the sanctuary argued about who should care for her. They cast lots with their pens, and Zakariyyā was selected. In one account, the pens were thrown into water and the one that floated against the current identified the guardian.

The people involved were caretakers among the descendants of Hārūn (Aaron), brother of Mūsā (Moses). They served at Bayt al-Maqdis, just as others cared for the Kaʿbah. Maryam’s father, ʿImrān, had led them in prayer and their sacrificial rites, so they felt a strong claim to care for his daughter. Zakariyyā argued that his wife was Maryam’s maternal aunt. His claim was not accepted immediately, and the group resolved the dispute by casting lots.

One explanation says that drawing lots is not a way to take away a known, fixed right under the Sharīʿah. It would be wrong to use a lottery to decide which of several people receives jointly owned property in full, or to determine a disputed child’s parentage. But when everyone has an equal claim and the lot only allocates equal portions or choices, it can be permissible. The lots in Maryam’s story settle a choice among people seeking the honor of her care.

**“nor were you there when they argued ˹about it˺”** confirms that the Prophet was not an eyewitness to the disagreement. The verse mentions both the dispute and the later drawing of lots. The details come to him through revelation, as if he had been present.

### What does the drawing of lots show?

The caretakers sought the responsibility of raising Maryam, not a material prize. When they could not agree, the lots settled the selection. The explanation distinguishes this kind of allocation among equal claims from gambling or overturning a right already fixed by law. The lots also form part of the unseen history revealed to Muhammad ﷺ.

### In short

- The Prophet was not present at the dispute or the drawing of lots.
- The people argued over Maryam’s guardianship, and Zakariyyā was selected.
- The account is presented as revelation, and lots are treated differently from gambling or taking away a fixed right.
