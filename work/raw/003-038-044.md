<<<VERSE 3:38>>>
Seeing Maryam's provisions moved her guardian to pray for the impossible: "Then and there Zachariah prayed to his Lord, saying, “My Lord! Grant me—by your grace—righteous offspring. You are certainly the Hearer of ˹all˺ prayers.”" Zakariyya was an old man with feeble bones and a head full of gray hair, and his wife was old and barren. Until he watched fruit arrive out of season, he had not had the courage to extend the hand of prayer for a child.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then and there Zachariah prayed to his Lord"**: at that point, upon entering the sanctuary to pray in the middle of the night, he called on his Lord in secret. Seeing provision outside its season taught him that natural timing does not bind the One who governs it. An old man could receive a child even after his family line had died out.

**"saying, “My Lord! Grant me—by your grace—righteous offspring"**: grant me from You, from Ladunka, a good offspring, meaning a righteous child.

**"You are certainly the Hearer of ˹all˺ prayers.”"**: You are the All-Hearer of invocation, the One who hears the prayer of the one who prays and knows his condition, and answers prayers.

### What gave him courage to ask

Zakariyya firmly believed Allah could change the usual course of things and bless him at an advanced age. What he lacked was a witnessed instance. When he saw unusual and out-of-season food provided for Maryam, he was persuaded: Allah who gives a servant fruit out of season can also give an old man offspring in late age. He saw the special gift, karāma, shown to her and grew increasingly certain and hopeful, so he asked for a son in spite of his age, and the granting of such a request is a rupture of ordinary reality, naqḍ al-ʿāda.

His request also had its aim. It is said that he asked for a son to help him in obedience and to be an heir from his progeny in prophethood, upholding God's right, ḥaqq Allāh. Requests belonging to the Real, ḥaqq al-ḥaqq, rather than the lower self's worldly share, ḥaẓẓ al-nafs, are not refused. Zakariyya wanted the old-age son to be a sign, āya, and a miracle, muʿjiza.

### Praying for children is the practice of prophets

This verse also teaches that asking Allah for children is a blessed practice of the prophets and the righteous. Allah said, "And We sent messengers before you and provided them with wives and children" (13:38), making children among the blessings bestowed on the Prophet ﷺ and on other prophets. Whoever tries to stop the birth of children rebels against nature and deprives himself of harmony with the common practice of prophets. The Prophet of Islam ﷺ gave marriage and children such importance that he allowed no capable person to avoid married life and remain counted among his followers:

1. "Marriage is my sunnah (way)."
2. "So, whoever turns away from my sunnah, he is not from me."
3. "Therefore, marry a woman who is loving and fertile, for I shall compete with other communities on the basis of your numbers."

Allah praises those who pray for family righteousness: "And those who say: 'O our Lord, bless us with such among our wives and our children as be the delight of our eyes'" (25:74). Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī explained "delight of the eyes", qurrata aʿyun, as seeing one's wife and children devoted in obedience to Allah. Umm Sulaym asked the Prophet ﷺ to pray for his attendant Anas, and he prayed, "O Allah, increase his wealth and his children and make him prosper in what You have bestowed on him." Anas ended with about a hundred children and ample wealth, as the outcome of that prayer.

### In short

- Maryam's out-of-season provision persuaded old, childless Zakariyya to ask for a righteous son, trusting the Hearer of prayers.
- Praying for children belongs to the prophets' practice, and marriage with a loving and fertile spouse is praised, not shunned (13:38, 25:74).
- His request sought an heir in obedience and prophethood, a matter of God's right rather than the lower self's share.

<<<VERSE 3:39>>>
The answer came while prayer was still in progress: "So the angels called out to him while he stood praying in the sanctuary, “Allah gives you good news of ˹the birth of˺ John who will confirm the Word of Allah and will be a great leader, chaste, and a prophet among the righteous.”" The angels spoke to him directly as he stood secluded at his place of worship. The child promised has a name, a mission and three qualities, and the promise of prophethood is better news than the promise of birth.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So the angels called out to him while he stood praying in the sanctuary"**: the angels, that is Gabriel, called to him as he stood praying in the miḥrāb, the sanctuary in the temple, in the mosque, at worship. A variant reading makes the call a direct statement, "God gives you good tidings", read yubashshiruka or yubshiruka.

**"Allah gives you good news of ˹the birth of˺ John"**: glad tidings of a child from your offspring, and his name is Yaḥyā. Qatādah and other scholars said he was called Yaḥyā, literally "he lives", because Allah filled his life with faith.

**"who will confirm the Word of Allah"**: he believes in the Word sent from Allah, namely ʿĪsā, son of Maryam. The attribution runs widely: ad-Daḥḥāk, ar-Rabīʿ bin Anas, as-Suddī, Abū ash-Shaʿthāʾ, Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah, Qatādah, al-Ḥasan and several others join Ibn ʿAbbās through al-ʿAwfī. ʿĪsā is called God's Word because creation came through kun, "Be", and God's Spirit denotes his fatherless origin.

**"and will be a great leader, chaste"**: Sayyid and Ḥaṣūr. The Sayyid is explained in many ways. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ar-Rabīʿ bin Anas, Qatādah and Saʿīd bin Jubayr said he is a wise man. Ibn ʿAbbās, ath-Thawrī and ad-Daḥḥāk said the noble, wise and pious man. Saʿīd bin Al-Musayyib said the Sayyid is the scholar and jurist, the faqih. ʿAtiyah said the man noble in behaviour and piety. ʿIkrimah said a person not overcome by anger. Ibn Zayd said the noble man. Mujāhid said one honoured by Allah. He will be a leader of his people through his knowledge and worship.

**"and a prophet among the righteous.”"**: this is further good news, sending Yaḥyā as a prophet after announcing his birth. Allah said the same thing in a harder case, to the mother of Mūsā (Moses): "Verily, We shall bring him back to you, and shall make him one of the Messengers" (28:7).

### What "chaste" means for Yaḥyā

Ḥaṣūr does not mean that Yaḥyā would refrain from lawful relations with women. It means he is immune from illegal sexual relations. It does not follow that he would not marry and have lawful relations, since Zakariyya prayed for a good offspring, a son who would himself have offspring, and Allah knows best. In another telling he is chaste as having no desire for women, forbidden from women, and it is said he never sinned and never intended to.

Abstinence is the wider gloss: he was particular about shielding himself from temptations and habitual enjoyments, even from permitted things such as good food, fine dress and marital life. Because this quality is praised here, abstinence appears to be the better way, yet sound reports prove the higher merit of marriage. The considered opinion is that both stand, for different people. One who is so absorbed in the life to come that he feels no need of a wife and has neither time nor frame of mind to fulfil the rights of wife and children does better to abstain, as Yaḥyā did. One who can marry and is capable of fulfilling his wife's due rights should marry, for that is better for him. Otherwise he is not advised to marry.

### The inward portrait of Yaḥyā

Petitioners should stay at the door. Anyone seeking something from kings remains there until an answer arrives, so Allah gives requests to people who embrace His service, khidma, while turning away from obedience sinks a person into estrangement's lowliness.

Three etymologies explain Yaḥyā. His heart's life, ḥayāt, through God may supply it. His mother's barrenness may have been revived, ḥayy, through him. Or believers' hearts quicken to life through him. Confirming God's word can mean confirming Allah's own word used in worship, or existing because God's word brought him into being.

A Sayyid, lord, is captive to no created thing. Freedom, taḥarrara, from whims and creatures defines him. He may embody actualised servanthood, ʿubūdiyya, outrank his contemporaries, as Yaḥyā did, or seek no rank and assign himself no worth. Humility before God then raises him over the group as its lord. Chastity frees him from passions and human carnal traits, guards the lower self through exaltation and nearness, and follows realities uprooting worldly preferences. Prophethood among the righteous confirms his worthiness for their rank.

### In short

- Yaḥyā's name, his confirmation of ʿĪsā the Word, his wise and noble leadership, his chastity and his prophethood are all announced before his birth.
- Chastity, ḥaṣūr, is immunity from unlawful relations and freedom from desire's grip, and it suits one absorbed in the next life, while marriage remains better for the capable.
- Inwardly Yaḥyā is a lord because he is no creature's captive, and a servant because his servanthood, ʿubūdiyya, is complete.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Zachariah exclaimed, “My Lord! How can I have a son when I am very old and my wife is barren?”"**: how can a boy arrive now that old age has overtaken me? The reply settles the manner. They would remain at the same age, and they would have offspring in that very state. So the question was not unbelief. It asked whether present extreme old age would stay unchanged or be altered first. ### In short

- "How can I have a son?" asks about the state of fulfilment, not about Allah's power, and the answer is that the elderly couple would remain as they were.
- His age is given as 120 and his wife's as 98, so the birth is creation outside the ordinary by a will that nothing can prevent.
- Possible means, adoption, procreation or another woman, are inwardly narrowed to one: the son is for both of them together.

A sign is requested for gratitude, not for proof: "Zachariah said, “My Lord! Grant me a sign.” He said, “Your sign is that you will not ˹be able to˺ speak to people for three days except through gestures. ### Phrase by phrase

**"Zachariah said, “My Lord! Grant me a sign.”"**: appoint for me a sign, an indication of my wife's pregnancy and a mark that alerts me that the child will come. His purpose was to rejoice at the prospect and show gratitude all along, even before the child's actual birth.

**"He said, “Your sign is that you will not ˹be able to˺ speak to people for three days except through gestures"**: for three days and nights he would refrain from speaking to men, but not from remembrance of God, save by tokens and gestures. He would not be able to speak except with signals, although he was not mute. The days are also given as nights: "For three nights, though having no bodily defect" (19:10). Gestures could use lips, eyebrows, eyes and hands, or writing on the ground.

**"Remember your Lord often and glorify ˹Him˺ morning and evening.”"**: remember Him with tongue and heart much, in all circumstances, and glorify Him by performing prayer at the end of the day and its beginning, in the early hours of night and morning, praying in the morning and at night as you used to do.

### Gesture as speech, and the gratitude the sign produces

The sign was remarkably suited to its purpose. His request came from a wish to show gratefulness. The sign leaves him incapable of anything but that, and a hundred other signs would not have done what this one did. The last phrase also establishes that where speech is impossible for a valid reason, gesture counts as its substitute. It appears in a hadith that the Prophet ﷺ asked a maid-servant, "Where is Allah?" She pointed toward the sky. He said, "This maid-servant is a Muslim." Al-Qurtubi transmitted that report.

### Three days of speech given to one Speaker

The sign redirects his audience. His tongue would be stopped from the people for three days, so secret whispering would belong to God and speech would speak of Him. By allusion, the promised child would be severed from world and creatures and have the face of his heart turned toward God. His qiblah would be the divine Presence and his ease would lie in conversing with Him. Name, image and passion become all that appear in speech, heart and eyes.

Zakariyya's command extends to the faithful: "And remember your Lord often" (8:45). Days are to be carried out in obedience and service, belonging totally to Him, calling on Him while knowing Him through all states and works. Ease should rest in His remembrance and message, joy in His name and mark. Do not pass God's house like a stranger without knocking on its door. If you talk, talk only of Him, and if you drink, drink only in remembrance of Him.

### The three degrees of remembrance

Remembrance of Allah has three degrees. The first is outward remembrance, with the tongue, in laudation and supplication: "And remember thy Lord often." The second is concealed remembrance in the heart: "Or with more intense remembrance" (2:200). The Prophet ﷺ said, "The best remembrance is the concealed and the best provision the sufficient." The third is true remembrance, which is witnessing God's remembrance of you: "And remember thy Lord when thou forgettest" (18:24).

### In short

- The sign of pregnancy is three days without speech to people, speech surviving only in gestures, writing and the remembrance of God (19:10).
- The sign fits its purpose perfectly, since a silenced tongue can do little besides show gratitude, and al-Qurtubi's hadith makes gesture a lawful substitute for speech.
- Remembrance itself climbs three degrees, from the tongue to the heart, to witnessing God's remembrance of you (8:45, 2:200, 18:24).

<<<VERSE 3:42>>>
The angels turn from the guardian to the ward: "And ˹remember˺ when the angels said, “O Mary! Surely Allah has selected you, purified you, and chosen you over all women of the world". Maryam is chosen twice in one sentence, and the hadiths that follow measure what that means: in her own time she had no equal among women, and in perfection she stands with one other woman in all history.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember˺ when the angels said, “O Mary! Surely Allah has selected you"**: the angels spoke to Maryam by His command. The speaker is also given as Gabriel. Allah chose her because of her service to Him, her modesty, honour, innocence and conviction, and with Islam and worship.

**"purified you"**: He made her pure of the touch of men, pure from disbelief, from associating partners with Allah and from blemishes. It is also said this means He saved her from being killed.

**"and chosen you over all women of the world"**: He preferred her above the women of the nations, the inhabitants of her time, by the birth of Jesus. She is chosen because of her praiseworthy qualities and purified from all defects, over all the other women of her time.

### What the hadiths say about her rank

At-Tirmidhī recorded that ʿAlī bin Abi Talib said, "I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say, 'The best woman (in her time) was Maryam, daughter of 'Imran, and the best woman (of the Prophet's time) is Khadijah (his wife), daughter of Khuwaylid.'" The Two Sahihs recorded this hadith.

Ibn Jarir recorded Abū Mūsā Al-Ash'ari's report that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Many men achieved perfection, but among women, only Maryam the daughter of 'Imran and Asiah, the wife of Fir'awn, achieved perfection." The Six, apart from Abu Dawud, recorded it. Al-Bukhari's wording says:

> "Many men reached the level of perfection, but no woman reached such a level except Asiah, the wife of Fir'awn, and Maryam, the daughter of 'Imran. The superiority of 'A'ishah (his wife) to other women, is like the superiority of Tharid (meat and bread dish) to other meals."

The chains and wordings of this hadith are treated in the story of ʿĪsā, son of Maryam.

The phrase "women of all the worlds" is referred to all women in that period of time. On that basis the statement in hadith that Fāṭimah is the foremost among the women of Paradise does not contradict it.

### Why the choice is stated twice

This may begin a message elevating Maryam's state, whether she saw the angels or heard only a voice. Preference singled her out from her peers and kind. Purity removed indecency and sin with beautiful immunity and kept her from men's physical contact. Preference placed her over the women of her time. Repetition divides the honour. First come special gifts, position and exaltation. Then comes fatherless bearing of Jesus, unmatched by any woman until Resurrection Day. Hence "above all women of the worlds".

### In short

- Allah selected Maryam for her service, modesty, honour and conviction, purified her from defect and the touch of men, and chose her over the women of her time.
- At-Tirmidhī and the Two Sahihs rank her the best woman of her time, and Ibn Jarir's report gives perfection to her and Asiah alone among women.
- The doubled choosing ends in the unmatched second gift: bearing Jesus without a father.

**"O Mary! Be devout to your Lord"**: submit yourself with obedience, Aqnuti, to your Lord. Qunut means to submit with humbleness. It is noticed that the command to bow carries a qualification that the command to prostrate does not: with those who bow. This seems to hint that people generally show less concern in their rukūʿ, bowing. They bend a little and then resume standing, as if in a hurry, and that kind of bowing is closer to standing, qiyām, than to bowing. By attaching "those who bow" to the command, Allah gives a model to follow, so that one's own bowing becomes like the bowing of those who bow fully and perfectly, as is due.

Inward instruction matches rank. Stay on worship's carpet and keep practising obedience, never falling short in service, khidma. Singled out by station, she was to surpass her contemporaries in worship. Growth in devotion also prepared the demonstration of divine power: a son formed within her with no male intervention.

- Bowing alone is qualified with a model, because hurried bowing slides back toward standing and must be learned from those who bow fully.
- Continuous service is both gratitude for election and preparation for the miracle decreed for her.

<<<VERSE 3:44>>>
The narrative closes by pointing at its own source: "This is news of the unseen that We reveal to you ˹O Prophet˺. You were not with them when they cast lots to decide who would be Mary’s guardian, nor were you there when they argued ˹about it˺". Muhammad ﷺ was not present when a guardian was chosen by lot in Jerusalem centuries earlier. The detail of the quarrel and the pens is itself offered as proof that the account came by revelation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is news of the unseen that We reveal to you ˹O Prophet˺"**: this is part of the news of the Ghayb, the Unseen, which We reveal and narrate to you, O Muhammad ﷺ. It was unknown to you. Gabriel is sent with it.

**"You were not with them when they cast lots to decide who would be Mary’s guardian"**: you were not present when they cast lots with their pens to decide which of them should be charged with her care and bring her up. You cannot narrate what happened as an eyewitness.

**"nor were you there when they argued ˹about it˺"**: you were not with them when custodianship became a dispute and every claimant argued his superiority. Allah disclosed events as if you had witnessed them.

### How the lots were cast, in the fuller report

Ibn Jarir records ʿIkrimah's account of Maryam's mother carrying the infant, wrapped in cloth, to rabbis descended from Aaron, brother of Musa. They administered Bayt Al-Maqdis then, as others administered the Kaʿbah. She offered the vowed child to them, declaring her free because a menstruating daughter should not enter the Masjid and would not be taken home. Their response turned on paternity: she was their Imam's daughter, and ʿImrān had led prayer and managed sacrificial rituals. Zakariyya replied that her maternal aunt was his wife. Because their hearts could not yield the Imam's daughter so readily, they used the pens with which they wrote the Tawrah for a lottery, and Zakariyya won her care. He was also master, chief, scholar, imam and prophet among them.

ʿIkrimah, as-Suddī, Qatādah, ar-Rabīʿ bin Anas and several others describe the mechanism in the river. The rabbis went into the Jordan and threw their pens in, deciding that the pen which remained afloat and idle would mark the man who would take care of Maryam. The water took all the pens under except Zakariyya's, which stayed afloat in its place. Another description of the prior agreement is that the owner of the pen which flows against the current would be the winner. They had argued first about custody before agreeing on the draw.

### Why the detail itself is the argument

These events belong to a remote past. The Prophet ﷺ had no likely way to see, hear or read them through ordinary channels available to him. The verse says he missed both the reed pens cast by agreement and the preceding quarrel over custody. Revelation therefore supplied the particulars as part of his message, and the account itself testifies to his prophethood. Being told these stories and their meanings in divine speech can be rarer and more perfect than personal witnessing.

### The law of drawing lots

The rule governing qurʿah, drawing lots, follows from the example. In the view of the Hanafi jurists, a lot cannot decide rights that are already known and fixed in Islamic law. Using it there counts as qimār, gambling. If several people jointly own something and a lot is drawn so that one takes the whole thing, that is not allowed. If a child's parentage is disputed, a lot cannot make one man the father. Where rights depend on opinion, lots are permissible. Portions of a joint property can be allotted by lot, A taking the eastern portion and B the western, because that could equally be done by mutual agreement or by a judge's decree. In short, where everyone's rights are equal and the lot only allocates equal portions and decides which portion belongs to whom, the draw is permissible.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ was absent from the quarrel and the casting of pens over Maryam's guardianship, so its details reach him only by revelation (ghayb).
- The reports put Zakariyya's pen afloat in the Jordan while the others sank, after the rabbis of Ārūn's line had argued over their imam's daughter.
- Lots may allocate equal shares, but they may not decide fixed legal rights or a child's parentage.
