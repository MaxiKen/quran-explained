<<<VERSE 3:78>>>
The verse warns that “There are some among them who distort the Book with their tongues” (3:78). They make their own words seem to come from revelation, then knowingly attribute those lies to Allah. The warning includes both changing the wording and twisting its meaning.

### Phrase by phrase

**“There are some among them”** refers to a group among the People of the Book, not every person who follows an earlier scripture. One report identifies Kaʿb al-Ashraf and his companions among those who mislead people by distorting the text.

**“who distort the Book with their tongues”** means they alter words while reciting or explaining scripture. Mujāhid, al-Shaʿbī, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah, and ar-Rabīʿ bin Anas explain this as changing Allah’s words. Another explanation says the distortion can be false interpretation, even when a person cannot remove the original words from Allah’s preserved revelation.

The Torah and Injīl are the earlier books discussed here. Wahb bin Munabbih says they remain as Allah revealed them, while people mislead others through additions, false interpretations, and books they wrote themselves. Another view notes that copies currently in human hands have also experienced additions and deletions. The distinction is between Allah’s original revelation, which He preserves, and the ways people may alter or misrepresent its text and meaning.

**“to make you think this ˹distortion˺ is from the Book”** describes the purpose of the deception. The speaker wants the listener to mistake an invented reading for revelation. This can confuse people who do not know the scripture well.

**“but it is not what the Book says”** exposes the false claim. A person’s words do not become scripture merely because they are read with a religious voice or attributed to a holy book.

**“They say, ‘It is from Allah’”** shows the seriousness of the lie. They attach divine authority to their own words.

**“but it is not from Allah”** denies that attribution. Allah did not reveal what they invented.

**“And ˹so˺ they attribute lies to Allah knowingly”** means they understand that they are misrepresenting revelation. Their speech is not an honest mistake but a deliberate falsehood.

An inward reading applies the warning to those who claim spiritual knowledge on the Ṭarīqa, the path of spiritual discipline, while their hearts have not verified what their tongues say. They embellish words to impress ordinary people and beginners. Religious language does not make a claim true if the speaker has no knowledge of it.

### In short

- Some people distort scripture through altered wording or false interpretation.
- The Torah and Injīl are not the same as later human claims made in their name.
- Knowingly attributing a lie to Allah is a serious offense.

<<<VERSE 3:79>>>
The verse says it is impossible for a prophet to ask people to worship him: “It is not appropriate for someone who Allah has blessed with the Scripture, wisdom, and prophethood” (3:79). Such a person calls people to become devoted servants of the Lord, teaching and studying scripture.

### Phrase by phrase

**“It is not appropriate for someone”** means that no true messenger would contradict the very purpose of his mission by calling people to worship him. This was relevant when Christians from Najrān discussed ʿĪsā (Jesus), and some Muslims asked whether they should prostrate before Muhammad ﷺ instead of greeting him. Neither prophet would ask for worship.

**“who Allah has blessed with the Scripture”** refers to a person given revelation. The scripture is a trust to convey, not a tool for gaining personal devotion.

**“wisdom”** includes understanding, sound judgment, and knowledge of the law, Al-Hukm. A prophet knows the difference between Allah’s command and human desire.

**“and prophethood”** means Allah has chosen him to deliver a message. Allah knows whom to select for this task (6:124). This selection rules out the idea that a prophet would turn people away from Allah toward himself.

**“to say to people, ‘Worship me instead of Allah’”** would be a direct call to kufr, disbelief. No prophet can instruct people to commit shirk by associating him with Allah. The messengers convey what Allah commands and forbid what He forbids.

**“Rather, he would say, ‘Be devoted to the worship of your Lord ˹alone˺’”** means the prophet calls people to become devoted to Allah. All messengers brought this same central teaching: worship Allah alone (16:36; 21:25). The Qur’an asks whether Allah appointed other gods to be worshipped (43:45). It also warns that an angel who claimed to be a god would be punished (21:29).

**“in accordance with what these prophets read in the Scripture and what they taught”** connects learning with practice. The word Rabbāniyyūn describes learned, wise people who teach the Book and study it. Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk explain it as people who are wise, knowledgeable, and forbearing. Knowledge should make people better servants of Allah, not servants of a human teacher.

One inward reading describes the Rabbāniyyūn as people singled out for Allah, with sincere intention, trust, and intimacy. It speaks of being characterized by Allah’s good qualities, while remaining a servant. Abū al-Qāsim Kurragānī says that the traveler remains on the path while acquiring these qualities and has not yet reached the destination. This spiritual description still rejects worship of any prophet or teacher.

The same reading describes the long remembrance of Allah as a sign of spiritual aspiration. It says that the seeker waits for the remembrance to bear fruit and seeks closeness to Allah rather than worldly power. The Prophet ﷺ is called Muṣṭafā, the chosen one, but his distinction does not make him an object of worship. True closeness means servanthood to Allah.

The verse also answers the claim that Ibrāhīm’s religion permitted people to worship human beings. Allah had already instructed Abraham and his descendants to submit to Him (2:132). No prophet, including Abraham, Adam, or Jesus, ever called people to worship him. The messenger’s task is to bring the ummah to Allah, not to make himself its god.

### What does it mean to be Rabbāniyyūn?

They are people who learn scripture, teach it, and put its guidance into practice. Their knowledge leads to faith and service. A true teacher directs people toward Allah, not toward the worship of the teacher.

### In short

- A prophet blessed with scripture and prophethood does not call people to worship him.
- Prophets call people to worship Allah alone and to become learned servants of their Lord.
- Spiritual insight and remembrance deepen servanthood but never make a human being divine.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:80>>>
The verse continues: “And he would never ask you to take angels and prophets as lords” (3:80). No messenger would tell people to worship created beings or to reject Allah after submitting to Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**“And he would never ask you”** means a prophet would not issue a command that contradicts his message. The same applies to a true religious teacher: he does not make people’s worship a condition of following him.

**“to take angels and prophets as lords”** means treating them as objects of worship or as independent sources of divine power. Some Jews elevated ʿUzayr (Ezra), and some Christians elevated ʿĪsā. Some polytheists among Quraysh treated angels as daughters of Allah. The verse rejects these forms of shirk.

A prophet may be honored and obeyed as Allah’s messenger, but he is not worshipped. The Torah, Gospel, and Qur’an all direct people toward worshipping Allah alone. The Sharīʿah commands respect for prophets while denying that any created being shares in al-rubūbiyya, the Lordship that belongs to the Real alone.

An inward reading says that no created thing has independent power to establish good or evil. It uses the word ithbāt for attributing existence or power to something. The believer may honor a messenger and follow his law, but must not give created forms the status of divine lordship. When the lights of tawḥīd appear in the heart, the person stops treating outward appearances as independent powers.

**“Would he ask you to disbelieve after you have submitted”** means that a messenger would not call people away from iman, faith, after they have surrendered to Allah. Submission and worship of created beings cannot coexist. If a claim asks someone to reject Allah’s oneness, that claim is not from a prophet.

One interpretation connects the phrase with the claim that Muhammad ﷺ asked people to love or worship him as Christians worship ʿĪsā. The verse rejects that claim. A messenger asks for obedience to Allah, not worship of himself.

### In short

- Prophets and angels are honored servants, not lords to be worshipped.
- No messenger would command shirk or disbelief after submission to Allah.
- Tawḥīd affirms Allah’s lordship alone while allowing respect for His messengers.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:81>>>
Allah recalls a covenant made with the prophets: “if there comes to you a messenger confirming what you have, you must believe in him and support him” (3:81). Every prophet was to accept a later messenger who confirmed Allah’s revelation and help him.

### Phrase by phrase

**“˹Remember˺ when Allah made a covenant with the prophets”** refers to a solemn pledge taken from the prophets Allah sent, from Adam through ʿĪsā. It concerns their duty to believe in and support a messenger who comes after them with confirmation of what Allah gave them.

**“Now that I have given you the Book and wisdom”** means Allah has already granted revelation and understanding. The prophets are not being asked to abandon their own missions. They are asked to recognize that Allah may send a later messenger who continues and confirms the same truth.

**“if there comes to you a messenger confirming what you have”** is understood by many as referring to Muhammad ﷺ, the final messenger, whose message confirms the earlier scriptures. The prophets were to accept and support him if he appeared in their lifetimes. One report also says the prophets were instructed to describe Muhammad’s qualities to their communities.

A further interpretation says the covenant required every prophet to believe in the messengers who came before and after him. These readings do not contradict one another: all prophets affirm the same revelation from Allah and support one another’s truth.

**“you must believe in him and support him”** means more than private recognition. The prophets were to affirm the messenger and assist his mission. The obligation includes their communities, who were to follow the covenant made through their prophets.

**“He added, ‘Do you affirm this covenant and accept this commitment?’”** asks the prophets to confirm their pledge and accept its binding force. They answer that they do. Allah then makes them witnesses to the covenant.

**“Then bear witness, and I too am a Witness”** means the prophets testify to the agreement, while Allah Himself witnesses it. The pledge is therefore known and binding. Turning away from it is not a minor mistake.

One report from ʿAbd Allāh bin ʿAbbās says Allah never sent a prophet without taking this pledge: if Muhammad ﷺ appeared during that prophet’s lifetime, he would believe in and support him. ʿAlī bin Abī Ṭālib and Ibn ʿAbbās are also named in this explanation. Qatādah and al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī say that the prophets pledged to believe in one another. The report is described with a ḥasan grade.

This pledge follows the earlier covenant in which Allah called the children of Adam to witness that He is their Lord (7:172). The covenant is also connected to the Prophet’s night journey, when he led the prophets in prayer at Bayt al-Maqdis in Jerusalem. The prophets’ gathering signifies their unity in the truth and Muhammad’s place as their leader. He is promised al-Maqām al-Maḥmūd, the praised station of intercession (17:79), which the other prophets will leave for him on the Day of Gathering.

Gabriel and Michael are included in the allusive explanation of the covenant. It describes Gabriel pledging to believe in and help Muhammad, with Michael witnessing the pledge. This expresses the honor given to Muhammad ﷺ before his earthly existence. The same account says that his name and obedience are linked with Allah’s command, while making clear that he remains Allah’s servant and messenger.

### Why did Allah take a covenant from the prophets?

The covenant establishes that revelation is one continuous message. Prophets do not compete with or contradict one another. Each confirms the truth brought by the others, and the final messenger is to be recognized and supported by those who receive his message.

### In short

- Allah took a pledge from the prophets to believe in and support a messenger who confirmed their revelation.
- Many explanations identify this final messenger as Muhammad ﷺ, while also emphasizing the prophets’ mutual faith.
- Allah, the prophets, and their communities are witnesses to the covenant.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:82>>>
The verse gives the consequence of breaking the covenant: “Whoever turns back after this, they will be the rebellious” (3:82). After Allah has made the pledge clear, abandoning it is a departure from obedience and truth.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Whoever turns back after this”** means turning away after the prophets have accepted the covenant and after its evidence has been established. The person cannot claim that the messenger’s truth was hidden.

An inward reading says this includes abandoning the Prophet’s Sunnah and wandering from his path after his signs and miracles have become clear. The covenant asks for belief and support, not merely an outward statement. It is part of the testimony that leads to Muhammad’s praised station of intercession (17:79).

**“they will be the rebellious”** means that they have left the limits of Allah’s command. They reject the covenant and show disobedience after knowing what they agreed to.

### In short

- The covenant is binding after it has been accepted and witnessed.
- Turning away from the messenger after clear evidence is rebellion.
- The Prophet’s Sunnah and path call believers to remain faithful to Allah’s pledge.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:83>>>
The verse challenges those seeking another religion: “Do they desire a way other than Allah’s” (3:83), when every creature submits to Him and all will return to Him? The natural order itself shows that the heavens and earth are under Allah’s command.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Do they desire a way other than Allah’s”** means they seek a religion other than the submission Allah has chosen. The dispute with the People of the Book over Abraham’s religion showed that they rejected the judgment given through revelation. But no one can escape Allah’s authority.

**“knowing that all those in the heavens and the earth submit to His Will”** means all creation is subject to Allah. Believers submit willingly in heart and body. Disbelievers may resist with their beliefs, but their bodies and lives remain under Allah’s power.

The Qur’an says that everything in the heavens and earth prostrates to Allah willingly or unwillingly (13:15). It also describes the shadows of created things inclining in submission and all creatures and angels prostrating (16:48-50). No created being controls its own existence independently.

One report says that when people are asked who created the heavens and earth, they answer, “Allah” (39:38). This admission shows that even those who dispute the faith recognize the Creator’s power.

An inward reading describes a person who looks for a reality other than Allah as chasing a mirage. When the person reaches it, only dust remains. The lights of divine disclosure bring willing submission, while the divine decree overpowers those who resist.

**“willingly or unwillingly”** distinguishes the believer’s conscious submission from the unavoidable submission of creation. The believer obeys by choice. The disbeliever still cannot escape Allah’s command over life, death, and the created world.

**“and to Him they will ˹all˺ be returned”** means everyone will return to Allah after death. He will judge each person according to deeds and distinguish those who submitted willingly from those who rejected the truth.

### In short

- No one can escape Allah’s authority over creation.
- Believers submit willingly, while disbelievers remain subject to Allah even when they resist.
- All will return to Allah for judgment.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:84>>>
The Prophet is instructed to affirm faith in every revelation: “We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us” (3:84). Muslims believe in Allah and all the prophets and scriptures, without rejecting any messenger or dividing revelation into rival communities.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ ‘We believe in Allah’”** declares faith in Allah alone. The Prophet and his community rely on Allah rather than their own strength or power.

**“and what has been revealed to us”** refers to the Qur’an revealed to Muhammad ﷺ. The Muslims accept its teachings and submit to Allah’s guidance.

**“and what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants”** includes the revelations given to Ibrāhīm, Ismāʿīl, Isḥāq, Yaʿqūb, and the tribes, al-Asbāṭ, who descended from Yaʿqūb. The wording embraces the children of Israel and the prophets raised among them.

**“and what was given to Moses, Jesus, and other prophets from their Lord”** includes the Torah, the Injīl, and the revelations given to every prophet. The list names several messengers but does not exclude those not named.

**“we make no distinction between any of them”** means Muslims do not believe in some prophets while rejecting others. It does not mean every prophet had the same rank or mission. It means they all came from Allah and their truth is affirmed.

**“and to Him we ˹fully˺ submit”** means that worship and obedience are directed to Allah alone. Submission includes believing in all prophets and following the final message brought by Muhammad ﷺ.

An inward reading says that believers do not attribute faith to their own lower selves, strength, or effort. They believe by Allah’s teaching and guidance. This humility does not remove responsibility. It recognizes that all ability to obey is a gift from Allah. The universal submission of creation provides the setting for this human confession: all in the heavens and earth submit to Allah (13:15), even the shadows and creatures (16:48-50), and the Creator is acknowledged when people are asked who made the heavens and earth (39:38).

The same principle rejects shirk. Muslims do not place one messenger against another or claim that a messenger’s community can reject later revelation. The way of Ibrāhīm, Mūsā, ʿĪsā, and Muhammad is one in its foundation: worship Allah alone and submit to Him.

### In short

- Muslims believe in Allah and the revelations sent to all the prophets.
- They make no distinction by rejecting some messengers and accepting others.
- Their worship and submission belong to Allah alone.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:85>>>
The verse gives a final rule: “Whoever seeks a way other than Islam, it will never be accepted from them” (3:85). All prophets called people to submission to Allah, and the final form of that submission is the religion brought by Muhammad ﷺ.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Whoever seeks a way other than Islam”** means whoever seeks a religion other than submission to Allah. Islam can be used in the general sense of surrendering to Allah, the foundation shared by all prophets. It can also refer specifically to the final Sharīʿah revealed to Muhammad ﷺ.

The earlier prophets and their communities were Muslims in the general sense: they submitted to Allah and followed the revelation given for their time. Their fundamental belief in Allah was the same, though some subsidiary laws differed. When Allah sent a later prophet, the law for that period became the applicable guidance.

After Muhammad ﷺ was sent, Islam means the final religion revealed through him. Earlier laws are no longer independently binding where the final message has replaced them. The final Sharīʿah remains in force until the Day of Judgment. A hadith says that if Mūsā were alive in Muhammad’s time, he would have to follow him. When ʿĪsā returns near the end of the world, he too will follow the law of Muhammad while retaining his prophethood.

**“it will never be accepted from them”** means Allah will not accept a person’s chosen religion if it contradicts the guidance He has sent. This is not because Allah rejects earlier prophets. Rather, it is because accepting all prophets includes following the final messenger when he has come. The Qur’an states that the religion with Allah is Islam (3:19).

**“and in the Hereafter they will be among the losers”** means that those who reject the final guidance lose their share in the Hereafter. The loss is not merely material. It is the loss of salvation and the punishment of the Fire.

An inward reading compares submission to a tree. The Sunnah is its water, faith its fruit, and Allah the one who plants and nurtures it. A deed that does not follow the Prophet’s Sunnah becomes a cause of regret. This image warns that a claim of Islam without following the Prophet’s guidance cannot bear the fruit of faith.

The Qur’an compares a good word to a good tree whose roots are firm and branches reach heaven (14:24-25). The same reading says that Allah expands a person’s breast to Islam with light from the Sunnah (39:22). The Prophet’s way is therefore not an optional decoration. It nourishes the submission and keeps it alive.

Shāfiʿī is reported to have said in a dream that Allah offered him a wish. The allusive account uses this to speak about the gift of submission and the need to follow the Sunnah. The Qur’an also says that all creation submits to Allah (13:15), that creatures and their shadows bow to Him (16:48-50), and that people acknowledge Him as Creator (39:38). It says Allah named the community Muslims (22:78), and it describes the worship of Allah alone as the religion of the prophets. A person should not separate the name Islam from its required obedience.

### What does Islam mean here?

In its broad sense, Islam is submission to Allah, which all prophets taught. In its specific sense, it is the final religion and Sharīʿah given to Muhammad ﷺ. Both meanings lead to the same conclusion: after the final messenger comes, people must accept the guidance Allah has sent through him.

### In short

- Islam means submission to Allah, shared by all prophets and completed in the final message.
- The Sharīʿah of Muhammad ﷺ is the binding guidance after his prophethood.
- Faith must be joined with the Sunnah, whose guidance nourishes submission.
