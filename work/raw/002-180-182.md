<<<VERSE 2:180>>>
The next injunction concerns the will. "It is prescribed that when death approaches any of you—if they leave something of value—a will should be made in favour of parents and immediate family with fairness. ˹This is˺ an obligation on those who are mindful ˹of Allah˺". The verse commanded that parents and relatives be included in the will, and this was obligatory, on the soundest view, before the verse of inheritance came down. Once Allah legislated fixed shares for the deserving heirs, they took their portions without needing to be named in a will or to be reminded of the dead person's favour.

### Phrase by phrase

**"It is prescribed that when death approaches any of you"**. Literally al-waṣiyyah is an order to do something, whether during the lifetime of the one who gives it or after his death, but in accepted usage it refers to what must be done after death, so it may be translated as will or bequest. "When death approaches" means when its causes appear. The verse is read as prescribing the will for the moment death draws near, so that the dying person should make his testament.

**"if they leave something of value"**. Among the many meanings of the Arabic word khayr is wealth, as in the verse which says of man that he is passionate in his love for good things, where it is agreed to mean wealth. One reading takes it as a large amount of wealth.

**"a will should be made in favour of parents and immediate family with fairness."**. In early Islam, when shares of inheritance were not yet fixed, a dying person could bequeath up to a third of his estate to his parents and relatives in whatever proportions he chose. That third was their right and the rest went to the children. The obligation had three aspects: no shares except those of the children were fixed, so the other relatives' portions were determined by the will. Making such a will was obligatory on the dying person. Bequeathing more than a third was not permitted. "With fairness" means justly, more generously towards the parents, not exceeding the allotted third and not preferring the richer relative.

**"˹This is˺ an obligation on those who are mindful ˹of Allah˺"**. The word rendered "obligation" emphasises what precedes it. The duty is a firm one on the muttaqīn, the God-fearing who ascribe no partner to Allah, and the will is to follow the sacred law (Sharīʿah).

### What was abrogated and what remains

The first of the three aspects was abrogated by the verse of inheritance according to most of the Companions and their successors. Ibn ʿAbbās is reported as saying that the bequest to parents and next of kin was abrogated by the verse "For men there is a share in what their parents and close relatives leave, and for women there is a share in what their parents and close relatives leave—whether it is little or much. ˹These are˺ obligatory shares" (4:7). Ibn Abī Ḥātim relates the same from Ibn ʿUmar and Abū Mūsā, from Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib and al-Ḥasan, from Mujāhid, ʿAṭāʾ and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, and from Muḥammad ibn Sīrīn, ʿIkrimah, Zayd ibn Aslam and al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, and names further holders of abrogation by 4:7. They include Ṭāwūs and Shurayḥ, Qatādah and al-Suddī, and Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān. Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī, al-Zuhrī and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk give the same view. Imam Aḥmad records Muḥammad ibn Sīrīn's account of Ibn ʿAbbās reciting Sūrat al-Baqarah. On reaching this verse, he declared it abrogated. Saʿīd ibn Manṣūr and al-Ḥākim also record this. Al-Ḥākim grades it ṣaḥīḥ by al-Bukhārī and Muslim's criteria. In another narration from Ibn ʿAbbās the verse of inheritance abrogated the will in favour of those who have fixed shares, while the command to bequeath to relatives without a share still held, as al-Jaṣṣāṣ and al-Qurṭubī note. The Ummah's consensus nevertheless excludes an obligation to bequeath to relatives lacking fixed shares. The obligatory character of that bequest is therefore abrogated too. It is now desirable, mustaḥabb, subject to their need.

The obligation itself, the second aspect, was abrogated by the famous mutawātir hadith. This describes transmission through uninterrupted chains from many directions, whose narrators could not have agreed on a lie. The sermon was delivered at the Farewell Ḥajj before some one hundred and fifty thousand Companions. Its point is that Allah assigned each rightful recipient a share, leaving no bequest for an heir. Al-Tirmidhī records it as ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. The Sunan also record it from ʿAmr ibn Khārijah. The narration from Ibn ʿAbbās adds: unless all the heirs permit it. Since Allah fixed the heirs' shares, the testator need not, and indeed may not, make a will in favour of an heir, though if the other heirs allow it, it becomes permissible. Al-Jaṣṣāṣ says the hadith is reported from a group of Companions and accepted unanimously by the jurists, so it counts as an assured report capable of abrogating a verse. Al-Qurṭubī says that scholars agree that an injunction known with full certainty from the Prophet ﷺ, as with mutawātir and well-known reports, is on a par with the Qur'an and must be taken as Allah's command, so abrogation of a verse by such a hadith is no cause for doubt. The hadith excluding a bequest to an heir is formally a solitary report, al-khabar al-wāḥid. Yet it was proclaimed at the Farewell Ḥajj before the largest gathering of Companions. Their consensus and the Ummah's show that they regarded it as absolutely proven. Had they felt the slightest doubt they would never have abandoned a verse of the Qur'an for it.

The third aspect still holds by unanimous approval: no more than a third may be bequeathed, unless the heirs permit more, even the whole. The Ṣaḥīḥayn record the question of Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ, Abū Waqqāṣ's son. With wealth and only a daughter to inherit, he asked the Prophet ﷺ about bequeathing everything, then half. Both requests were refused. A third was allowed, though even that was called much. Leaving heirs wealthy is better than leaving them poor and begging. Al-Bukhārī records that Ibn ʿAbbās recommended that people reduce their bequests from a third to a quarter because of these words. The will should therefore be fair, giving relatives a portion without injustice to the qualified heirs and without extravagance or stinginess.

### Rulings

A will is no longer needed for relatives whose shares the Qur'an fixed, and is not permitted for them without the other heirs' consent, but relatives with no legal share may be bequeathed up to a third. The specific will described in this verse is abrogated, yet making a will remains necessary, especially for someone who owes debts or holds things in trust, which must be covered in it. The Ṣaḥīḥayn record Ibn ʿUmar's account of the Prophet's ﷺ instruction. A Muslim with something to bequeath should not pass two nights without a written will ready. Ibn ʿUmar kept his ready every night after hearing this. Another wording speaks of three nights for one against whom others hold rights. It is recommended, on the general sense of this verse, to bequeath up to a third to relatives who do not inherit, and many verses and hadiths command kindness and generosity to kin. A person may alter or cancel his will concerning the third during his lifetime. After a death the question arises how to deal with the wealth left, and the Islamic rule is to distribute it justly among the heirs, which the God-fearing should do in a spirit of fairness, creating a brotherly atmosphere and preventing litigation and quarrels. Where a family member such as an orphaned grandson does not inherit under the scheme of inheritance, the testator may provide for him from the third, for Islam approves of every lawful device for keeping society free of disputes.

### The testament of the poor

In the devotional reading, the testament of the lords of wealth is one thing and that of the lords of spiritual states another. At life's end, the rich distribute a third of their wealth. The spiritually poor instead bequeath pure states and truthful deeds. The disobedient fear because of their bad deeds. The recogniser's fear is ten times greater, arising from the truthfulness of his deeds and purity of his states. The difference is that the disobedient fears the outcome and dreads punishment, while the recogniser fears the majesty and awareness of the Real. The fear of the recognisers is called awe, and that of the disobedient fear. Fear arises from reports and awe from face-to-face vision. Awe is a fear that places no veil before supplication, no blindfold over insight and no wall before hope, a fear that melts and kills, and until he hears the call "Do not fear, nor grieve" (29:33) its owner finds no ease. Generosity is shown to him, yet the prospect of losing it burns within him. As his light grows, so does his terror of change. Abū Saʿīd Abī al-Khayr was in this state at death, and was asked to say some words of reminder. As he rested his head on death's pillow, those around him sought a final reminder. They addressed him as the qiblah of the burnt, the yearners' model and the world's sun. His answer was only an image: eyes filled with water, liver with fire, hands with wind and head with dust. Bishr al-Ḥāfī had the same state at his departure.

### In short

- Before the verse of inheritance, a dying person had to bequeath up to a third to parents and relatives in fair proportions.
- The verse was abrogated by 4:7 (Ibn ʿAbbās and many successors) and by the Farewell Ḥajj hadith "there is no will for an heir" (al-Tirmidhī), which the Ummah unanimously accepted (al-Jaṣṣāṣ, al-Qurṭubī).
- What remains: no bequest to an heir without the other heirs' consent, up to a third for non-inheriting relatives (recommended, not obligatory), and no more than a third in all (the hadith of Saʿd, the Ṣaḥīḥayn).
- A will covering debts and trusts should always be kept ready (Ibn ʿUmar).
- Inwardly, the poor bequeath pure states rather than wealth, and the recogniser's fear is awe born of vision.

<<<VERSE 2:181>>>
Having prescribed the will, the passage protects it. "But whoever changes the will after hearing it, the blame will only be on those who made the change. Indeed, Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing".

### Phrase by phrase

**"But whoever changes the will after hearing it"**. Whoever alters the testament of the deceased after hearing it and knowing what it contains, whether a witness or a trustee, by adding to it, taking from it, stopping something in it or, obviously, concealing it altogether, falls under this verse.

**"the blame will only be on those who made the change."**. The sin resulting from the altered will rests on those who changed it, while the one who made the will bears no responsibility for what they did. Ibn ʿAbbās and others said that the dead person's reward is preserved for him by Allah and the sin is earned by those who change the will. Here the explicit subject, "those who made the change", stands in place of the pronoun, which emphasises who bears the burden.

**"Indeed, Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing"**. Allah hears the words and the will of the testator and knows the deeds of the trustee, what the beneficiaries or others have altered, and every detail of their situations, and He requites each accordingly. It is also said that He knows the deed of the testator himself, whether he transgressed in his will. Because of the fear of this sin, people used to execute a dead person's will exactly as it stood even when he had exceeded the limits in it, until the following verse was revealed.

### The weight of a will

ʿAbd al-Razzāq reports from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that a man may do the works of the righteous for seventy years and then commit injustice when dictating his will, so that his life ends with the worst of his deeds and he enters the Fire, while another may do the works of the wicked for seventy years and then dictate a just will, so that he ends with the best of his deeds and enters Paradise. Abū Hurayrah then told his listeners to read, if they wished, the verse ending "These are the limits set by Allah, so do not transgress them. And whoever transgresses the limits of Allah, they are the ˹true˺ wrongdoers" (2:229). Changing the provisions of a will is unlawful unless the aim is the fair settlement described next.

In the devotional reading, whoever distorts an utterance takes its evil and harm upon himself, and his punishment is to be denied the fragrance of sincerity. Whoever aids the religion is aided by Allah, and whoever gives aid against it is forsaken by Him.

### In short

- Altering, cutting, adding to or hiding a will after hearing it is a sin borne by the alterer alone, and the deceased keeps his reward (Ibn ʿAbbās).
- Allah hears the testator's words and knows the trustee's deeds, and will requite each.
- A will can decide a life's end for good or ill (Abū Hurayrah, with 2:229).
- Distorting words costs the distorter the fragrance of sincerity.

<<<VERSE 2:182>>>
Verse 182 states the exception to the prohibition on changing a will. "Whoever suspects an error or an injustice in the will and brings about a ˹fair˺ settlement among the parties will not be sinful. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever suspects an error or an injustice in the will"**. Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Mujāhid, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and al-Suddī explained the first word as error. Error here is straying from what is right unintentionally, and injustice or sin is deliberately exceeding the third or singling out a rich person. The errors include cases where an heir indirectly gains more than his fair share, for instance when the will directs that a certain item be sold to him, or where the testator names his daughter's son in the legacy in order to increase the daughter's portion. Such things may arise from softness of heart without thought of the consequences, or from sinful intent. "Suspects" here means knows that the testator has purposely committed a mistake or transgression.

**"and brings about a ˹fair˺ settlement among the parties"**. The executor is permitted to correct the errors and replace the unjust provisions with a better arrangement, so that both Islamic law and the dead person's wishes are respected. He makes peace between the heirs and the testator, or between the testator and the trustee, by commanding that justice be done, advising the testator and rectifying the wrong, and reconciling those who disagree about it, reducing any bequest to no more than a third. This is not counted as altering the will, which is why Allah mentioned it separately, excluding it from the prohibition of the previous verse.

**"will not be sinful."**. There is no blame on him for this. Rather, he is rewarded for setting things right.

**"Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"**. He forgives the deceased who erred or transgressed in his will, and is merciful regarding the testator's deed. It is also said that His mercy appears in granting the dispensation to reduce bequests to a third and so commanding adherence to fairness. He is forgiving towards every servant who repents to Him and compassionate with them.

### Kindness to beginners

In the devotional reading, the verse alludes to one who recognises a weakness in an aspirant or sees a slackness of purpose in a novice, or to a counsellor who speaks with pure sincerity to someone who lacks the capacity for it. Such a person treats the aspirant with indulgence, encouragement, persuasion and leniency, and this is not objectionable, for urging people towards pure sincerity and encouraging what is not yet firmly established in them carries ample reward. Kindness to beginners helps put things right. Their resolve may still be weak and their effort not yet sincere, so they need this patient treatment.

### In short

- Where a will contains an error or a deliberate injustice, such as favouring one heir indirectly or exceeding the third, the executor may correct it and reconcile the parties without sin, and is rewarded for it.
- This correction is not the forbidden alteration of verse 181.
- Allah forgives the testator's error and shows mercy in permitting the adjustment to a third.
- Inwardly, gentle indulgence towards weak novices is part of making things right.
