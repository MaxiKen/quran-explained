<<<VERSE 5:109>>>
The chapter turns to the Day of Gathering: “What response did you receive?” The messengers are assembled and asked how their communities answered them. Their reply is extraordinary: “We have no knowledge ˹compared to You˺! You ˹alone˺ are indeed the Knower of all unseen.”

The verse opens a window onto that Day. It also explains why the answer takes this form, and what it shows about the messengers' feeling for their people.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Consider˺ the Day Allah will gather the messengers"** recalls the Day of Resurrection, when Allah will gather the messengers together. One gloss describes this as happening at a moment of bewilderment, and another notes that the whole world will be assembled on that day.

The particular mention of the prophets carries a meaning. Everyone will be gathered, but the first question will be put to the prophets, so that all creation may see that nobody is exempt from reckoning on that Day.

**"and say, “What response did you receive?”"** is the question. Allah will ask the messengers how the nations to whom He sent them answered and responded to their teaching. When they invited their communities to Allah and His true religion, how did people react? Did they act as commanded, or did they refuse the call and oppose it?

One gloss specifies that they were summoned to proclaim Allah's oneness. Other verses state the same reckoning, that Allah will question those to whom the message was sent and will question the messengers (7:6), and that by His Lord all of them will certainly be called to account for everything they used to do (15:92-93).

**"They will reply, “We have no knowledge ˹compared to You˺!"** is the answer. Mujahid, Al-Hasan Al-Basri and As-Suddi explain it as the result of the terror of that Day. 'Abdur-Razzaq narrates through Ath-Thawri from Al-A'mash that Mujahid gave this explanation, and Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim record it.

Another gloss attributes the answer to the intensity of the situation, and a third to the great terror and fright of the Day, which makes them forget what they knew.

**"You ˹alone˺ are indeed the Knower of all unseen.”"** assigns knowledge to Allah alone. He is the Knower of what is hidden, including what is hidden from the messengers of how people responded. One account describes them as assigning the answer to Allah and saying that He alone knows all things, present and absent.

### What did Ibn 'Abbas say the answer means?

In the report given by 'Ali bin Abi Talhah, Ibn 'Abbas explains that the messengers will say to the Lord most honoured that they have no knowledge beyond what they know, and that even of that, Allah knows those people better than they do.

The response is made out of respect before the Lord most honoured. It means that they have no knowledge compared with His encompassing knowledge. Their knowledge grasped only the outward behaviour of those people, not the secrets of their hearts.

Allah is the Knower of everything, whose knowledge encompasses all things, and their knowledge set beside His resembles having no knowledge at all, because He alone is the Knower of everything hidden.

### How can this answer be correct about people they knew?

A doubt arises. For those born in a community after its prophet had died, the answer is plainly correct, since nobody knows the unseen except Allah. But what of the great majority who professed belief at their prophet's hands, and who kept following before his eyes what they were told to follow, and of the disbelievers who would not listen and even treated the prophets with hostility? How can it be correct to say that there is no knowledge of their faith and deeds?

Al-Bahr al-Muhit records that Imam Razi answered the doubt by distinguishing two things. One is perfect certitude, and the other is conjecture or weighty likelihood. If a person can testify about the faith and deeds of someone sitting beside him, it is on no other basis than likelihood.

Real faith, iman, is a secret of the heart that nobody can ascertain with certainty without divine revelation. Every religious community had groups of hypocrites who professed faith and followed what they were commanded, while their hearts held neither faith nor the real feeling and drive to carry out those commands. All they had was hypocrisy.

Religious laws, like all laws, were applied to what appeared outwardly. Whoever called himself a believer, followed the divinely ordained laws, and against whom no word or deed was proved contrary to faith, was a good believer in the sight of the prophets and their believing communities. Whether he was a true believer at heart or merely a hypocrite did not count, since they had no means of knowing.

The Prophet ﷺ therefore said that we judge by outward deeds while Allah is the custodian of the secrets of hearts.

### What changes on the Day of Gathering?

Under that rule the prophets, their deputies and the learned could confirm, at least in this mortal world, that someone was a true believer on the basis of a favourable opinion drawn from his outward deeds. That mortal world, in which everything turned on opinion and conjecture, has passed.

This is the day of gathering, the fateful day of Resurrection when the dead rise, things are sorted out and realities unfold. Criminals will stand trial and people will testify against them. If they do not confess, other witnesses will be brought, very special and very official ones.

With mouths and tongues silenced, the wrongdoer's hands, legs and skin will be asked to testify, and they will tell everything. That Day a seal will be set on their mouths while their hands will speak and their feet bear witness to all that they did (36:65). People will then discover that parts of their own bodies can act as informants when summoned by the Lord of the universes, and after testimony of that kind no denial remains possible.

That will be a state in which no judgment rests on opinion, estimate or conjecture. Certitude will be the order of the day. Since nobody has real knowledge of another's faith except Allah, the messengers will recognise what the question amounts to. It is not being asked in the world of their experience, where conjecture could answer it, but in the terrifying scene of the Resurrection, where nothing but certitude will serve.

Their answer, that they have no knowledge, means that they lack the certain knowledge required to answer, and it is right and correct.

### What does the answer show about the messengers' affection?

The prophets did hold opinions about whether their communities accepted or refused their call, and they knew facts about their obedience and disobedience. On that basis they could at least have mentioned the apparent attitude as part of their answer, referring certainty of knowledge to Allah alone.

What we see instead is that they said nothing about what they knew and mentioned none of the events that had passed before them. They resigned all of it to divine knowledge and chose silence.

That was a great demonstration of consideration. The prophets are deeply affectionate toward their people and toward Allah's creation generally. They would not choose to make an adverse comment about their community on their own initiative, for fear of bringing their people into trouble. If they had to say it, they would, but here they had the excuse of lacking certain knowledge, and using that legitimate excuse they avoided saying anything against their communities.

One gloss adds that they later answer and testify that they delivered the message to their people, and another that once they have calmed down they proceed to bear witness against their communities. The first response of bewilderment is therefore not the whole of their testimony.

### What will be exposed about communities and their prophets?

Those who embraced faith on the strength of their prophets' teaching later fell away from the true religion and contrived one of their own in the prophet's name. Every group nevertheless treated itself as the community of its prophet, although after deviating from his real teaching it had ceased to have any connection with him.

Jews claim a connection with Musa (Moses) and Christians with 'Isa (Jesus), although their present religions are unconnected with those prophets. That fact has become obscure in this world of trial, and on the Day of Judgment it will all be made clear.

On that Day Allah will assemble all the prophets and, in the presence of their communities, ask them what teaching they gave and how their community received it. It will then be shown to every community, before its own prophet, how far its members contravened his teaching about the religion of Allah, and how they falsely attached their self-made religion to him.

The question addressed to the prophets is therefore really directed at their communities. The prophets will be the first to testify about the good and bad deeds of their people, and it will be a trying moment for those people. They will have been hoping for their prophets' intercession to rescue them from that shattering experience, and instead the question requires the prophets to explain the conduct of the community.

Since the prophets will say nothing but the truth, wrongdoers will fear that the prophets themselves will bear witness against them. Who then, they will wonder, is left to intercede for them?

### What should a person do with this?

A window is opened onto the Day of Doom. Standing there at the places of reckoning are the highest and dearest of Allah, His noble messengers and prophets, all in awe and reverence. If that is their state, what will be happening to everyone else?

People should therefore begin to worry about that Day from this day. This short span of life is a blessing in hand and should be devoted to preparing for the ultimate accounting.

At-Tirmidhi records a hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ says that no son of Adam will move a step on the Day of Resurrection until he is asked five questions. He will be asked about his life and what he used it up in, about his youth and what he spent it in, about his wealth and where he earned it and where he spent it, and about what he did with the knowledge he had.

Allah's mercy and affection are great. He tests people and Himself tells them the questions of the test. The Prophet ﷺ discharged that task by telling his ummah how to appear in it. What remains is to work out the answers and keep them ready for the day of trial. Whoever still fails after being told in advance what the test contains could hardly be more deprived.

### In short

- The messengers are gathered and asked how their communities responded, and the question is really aimed at those communities.
- Their first answer disclaims knowledge, explained as the effect of the Day's terror and as respect before Allah's encompassing knowledge.
- Outward judgment in this world rests on likelihood, while real faith is a secret of the heart known only to Allah.
- On the Day of Gathering certitude replaces conjecture, and bodies testify when mouths are sealed.
- The silence of the messengers shows their affection, and the exposure of self-made religions claimed in their names is part of what that Day will make clear.
