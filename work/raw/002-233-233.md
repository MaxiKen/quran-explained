<<<VERSE 2:233>>>
Divorce does not remove responsibility for a child: "˹Divorced˺ mothers will breastfeed their offspring for two whole years, for those who wish to complete the nursing ˹of their child˺." The verse joins feeding, financial support and consultation. Neither parent may use the child to hurt the other. Decisions about weaning or another nurse should protect the child, respect both parents and ensure that agreed payments are made fairly.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Divorced˺ mothers will breastfeed their offspring for two whole years, for those who wish to complete the nursing ˹of their child˺."** Two full years are the complete nursing period. The emphasis on whole years makes the duration clear. Earlier weaning may be permitted under the conditions later stated. The discussion concerns arrangements during marriage as well as after divorce, though divorce often makes these duties disputed.

**"The child’s father will provide reasonable maintenance and clothing for the mother ˹during that period˺."** The father bears the cost of the nursing mother's needs within the applicable rules. Reasonable provision follows lawful social custom, without wastefulness or stinginess. Her work of feeding the child must not be treated as though it removes the need to support her.

**"No one will be charged with more than they can bear."** The standard recognises ability. A poor father is not charged as though he were wealthy. This limit does not permit him to evade what he can afford, and it does not allow the mother to be burdened beyond her own capacity.

**"No mother or father should be made to suffer for their child."** The child must not become an instrument of revenge. A father may not remove the child to wound the mother. A mother may not refuse necessary feeding merely to create difficulties for the father. Each parent is described in relation to the child, appealing to affection on both sides.

**"The ˹father’s˺ heirs are under the same obligation."** The relevant heir takes on duties of support and avoidance of harm when the father is absent through death. Explanations differ over whether the wording refers to the father's heir, the child's heir or the administration of property inherited from the father. Those distinctions are developed below.

**"But if both sides decide—after mutual consultation and consent—to wean a child, then there is no blame on them."** Fitam means weaning. The parents may agree to end nursing before two years when that serves the child. Neither is entitled to make the decision alone simply to prevail over the other.

**"If you decide to have your children nursed by a wet-nurse, it is permissible as long as you pay fairly."** Another woman may be engaged to provide milk. The arrangement is lawful, but the agreed wages must be paid fully and on time. Any payment already due to the mother for earlier nursing must also be settled kindly.

**"Be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah is All-Seeing of what you do"** Allah sees decisions, payments, outward actions and hidden motives. The child's welfare cannot be sacrificed merely because a parent can conceal the purpose of a demand. Final accountability reaches arrangements made outside public supervision.

### Why does nursing appear within the divorce rules?

Separation often leaves practical questions unresolved. Infants still need food and care. Parents who no longer live together may disagree over who should feed the child, what should be paid or when nursing should end. The verse gives an ordered way to settle these matters without making the child a target of the adults' quarrel.

Both parties' interests are recognised. Mutual counsel and consent replace emotional pressure. The mother is not to exploit the father's concern, and the father is not to exploit hers. Faith calls for applying the guidance rather than seeking a loophole that places every burden on someone else.

The earlier questions of remarriage remain important, but they do not erase duties to children from the former marriage. The parental relationship continues even when the marital relationship has ended. The aim is a solution both can accept within Allah's law, with the infant's welfare at its centre.

### What is the mother's duty, and when may another nurse be used?

The legal explanation here describes nursing as the mother's natural obligation. Refusal without an excuse, merely from hostility or displeasure, is treated as sinful. It also stresses the immediate need after birth: the child must receive the milk necessary for survival.

This does not mean a father may compel her regardless of illness, inability or another valid need. If she has such an excuse, another arrangement should be made. But where the child refuses another woman's breast or any other milk, necessary feeding may require the mother to nurse. The concern is preserving the child's life, not satisfying the father's anger.

A father may also seek another nurse where the mother's milk is judged unsuitable for the child, even if she is willing to nurse. Another explanation considers the mother's wish to remarry as a reason for arranging nursing elsewhere. The parents may agree that the father assumes care under circumstances allowing it, while settling the mother's previous entitlement and arranging future feeding.

The prohibition of harm restrains these arrangements. The father must not take the infant simply to punish its mother. If she accepts the ordinary nursing payment, he should not remove the child as leverage against her. The mother should not hand over a child who has become accustomed to her and refuses other feeding merely to leave the father with an impossible situation.

Mujahid, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Az-Zuhri, As-Suddi, Ath-Thawri and Ibn Zayd are named in the explanation of these restrictions. Custody and feeding are not weapons that become lawful because the marriage has failed.

### How long does nursing create marriage-related kinship?

Milk kinship means that nursing can establish relationships within which marriage is prohibited, such as between a nursing mother and the child she feeds. The majority rule presented here confines this legal effect to nursing before the end of two years. Milk given only after that age does not create the prohibition under that view.

At-Tirmidhi records from Umm Salamah a saying of the Prophet ﷺ connecting the marriage prohibition with nursing at the breast before weaning. He grades it Hasan Sahih, good and authentic. He says that the majority among the Companions and later people of knowledge followed the two-year limit. The report's transmitters are described as meeting the conditions of the Sahihayn, the two authentic collections.

The expression about being at the breast concerns the nursing age. Ahmad records from Al-Bara' bin 'Azib that when Ibrahim, the Prophet's ﷺ son, died, the Prophet ﷺ said that he had died while still nursing and would have someone to nurse him in Paradise.

Ad-Daraqutni also relates from Ibn 'Abbas a report limiting the marriage prohibition to two years. Malik gives a form transmitted through Thawr bin Zayd. A version through Ad-Darawardi, Thawr, 'Ikrimah and Ibn 'Abbas is described as more authentic and adds that nursing after two years is not counted.

A different duration is given from Abu Hanifah. He holds that milk kinship can have its legal effects up to thirty months, or two and a half years. Where a child's weakness justifies continuing within that period, the discussion says there is no sin. Nursing beyond two and a half years is described there as haram, forbidden. The two-year majority rule and this thirty-month position must not be merged into one claim.

### How was nursing an older person understood?

'A'ishah is reported in the Sahih as regarding milk given to an older person as capable of creating the relevant prohibition. 'Ata' bin Abu Rabah and Layth bin Sa'd are named with that view. She applied it where a woman needed a man to be able to enter her household without the ordinary barrier.

Her evidence was the report concerning Salim, the freed servant of Abu Hudhayfah. The Prophet ﷺ directed Abu Hudhayfah's wife to give Salim some of her milk, although he was grown. After that he entered her home freely.

The other wives of the Prophet ﷺ did not agree with the general application. They treated Salim's case as a special allowance. The majority of scholars likewise did not make it a general rule for adults. The report therefore remains in the discussion with its disagreement, not as an undisputed extension of infant nursing to any age.

Ibn Jarir also expresses concern that continuing nursing after the second year could harm the child's body and mind. Sufyan Ath-Thawri relates that 'Alqamah asked a woman nursing beyond that point to stop. These are the concerns stated in the material, not a separately supplied medical assessment.

### Why does the wording describe the father through the child?

The expression explained here is al-mawludi lahu, the one to whom the child is born, rather than simply walid, father. The shorter term occurs elsewhere, including (31:33). Here the fuller description is understood as encouraging acceptance of the duty of support.

Both parents are involved in the child's birth, but the child is ascribed to the father's lineage. The wording reminds him of that relationship as it places expenses upon him. The instruction combines duty with an appeal to parental affection rather than presenting the cost as an unrelated financial imposition.

The same sympathetic tone appears when both parents are told not to suffer because of their child. Neither is treated only as a source of money or labour. The law recognises the ties that make the situation emotionally difficult and uses them to call for responsible conduct.

### How are maintenance and nursing wages distinguished?

During marriage and the relevant post-divorce waiting period, the father is responsible for the mother's maintenance. Under the ruling given here, she does not charge an additional nursing wage during that time. Nursing is her duty, while her needs are already met through the maintenance obligation. Demanding a separate fee in those circumstances is treated as harm to the father.

After divorce and the completion of that waiting period, the former husband's ordinary duty to maintain her has ended. His duty to pay for the child's nursing continues. The divorced mother may then ask for nursing wages at the rate another woman would receive. If she demands more, the father may engage another nurse instead, subject to the child's needs and the prohibition of harm.

Ad-Dahhak explains that a divorced mother who nurses the couple's child is to receive reasonable provision and clothing. The broader instruction is linked with (65:7): the wealthy spend according to their means, while those with limited resources spend from what Allah has given them. The distinctions about timing and payment clarify how that provision is administered.

### What standard of living determines the amount?

If both parents are affluent, maintenance should match that condition. If both are poor, it matches theirs. The disagreement described here concerns different financial standings.

Following al-Khassaf, the discussion in Hidayah gives an intermediate level where the husband is rich and the wife poor: above the poor standard but below the rich one. Al-Karkhi instead makes the husband's status decisive. Fath al-Qadir reports that many jurists gave their ruling on that basis.

Both approaches retain the prohibition of extravagance and stinginess. Reasonable local practice is relevant where it does not contradict Islamic law. The duty cannot be fixed by personal resentment, and it cannot be increased beyond capacity as a means of punishment.

### Who supports the child after the father's death?

One explanation of the heir clause stresses not harming the father's relative, the infant. Mujahid, Ash-Sha'bi and Ad-Dahhak are named with it. Another, described as the majority explanation, requires the heir to provide the mother's nursing support and safeguard her rights as the father did. Ibn Jarir discusses the matter at length.

A further explanation identifies an heir who is also mahram to the child, a relative within a permanently prohibited degree of marriage. Those who would inherit from the child become responsible for support if the father is absent and the child has no property from him. Where several such heirs exist, the duty is shared in proportion to their inheritance shares.

Abu Hanifah applies the principle beyond milk to the continuing support of a minor. The purpose is meeting the child's needs, not nursing alone. The example given is an orphan with a mother and grandfather. Their contribution is divided as one-third from the mother and two-thirds from the grandfather.

The discussion contrasts this support claim with inheritance. A grandfather owes support to an orphaned grandson even though he is not required to maintain his own adult sons. But a grandson does not inherit alongside nearer sons under the rule stated here. The argument connects the nearer relative's priority with the hadith in Al-Bukhari concerning the nearest male.

The grandfather may nonetheless make provision by a will where needed, potentially giving the grandson more than the sons' individual inheritance shares. The distinction aims to protect the child's needs without replacing the inheritance rule with the maintenance rule. Support and inheritance answer different questions.

Another interpretation refers to the young heir administering property inherited from the father and providing for the other parent. These accounts give different explanations of who the heir is and how support is funded. They should not be silently reduced to one arrangement.

### How should weaning be decided?

Early weaning is permitted after real consultation and agreement when it benefits the child. A mother's inability or the child's illness may make completing two years unsuitable. The decision should follow those needs rather than serve as another move in the parents' dispute.

Ath-Thawri emphasises that one parent must not act without consulting the other. The requirement is a mercy: it brings the knowledge and concern of both parents into a decision affecting the infant. The discussion also connects it with (65:6), which directs fair consultation over nursing and permits another nurse where agreement becomes difficult.

Consultation is not fulfilled by merely announcing a decision already imposed. Both agreement and discussion are named. The child is not to pay the price of adults refusing to cooperate.

### What is owed to a wet-nurse?

The nurse's wages should be clearly agreed before the work begins. The agreed amount must then be paid without shortfall, delay or evasion. Parents who withhold it bear the sin of that failure. Hiring another nurse does not erase money already due to the mother for previous feeding and care.

The permission covers an appropriate practical reason for another arrangement. It is not blameworthy merely because someone other than the mother feeds the child. The fairness of payment and the absence of harm are central to its lawfulness.

### What does the command reveal about Allah's mercy?

An inward reflection sees the command to mothers as evidence that Allah's care is greater even than maternal tenderness. He does not merely create a mother's love and leave the child to it. He urges nursing, directs care and places duties around the infant's welfare. His generosity, knowledge and mercy extend beyond human affection.

One account tells of a woman holding her child while baking bread. Hearing that the Prophet ﷺ would pass, she asked about Allah being more merciful to His servants than a mother to her child. When he confirmed it, she pointed out that a mother would not throw her baby into an oven. The Prophet ﷺ wept and spoke of punishment in the Fire for those who refuse to affirm Allah's oneness. No collector or grade is added where the account gives none.

Ka'b 'Ujra relates another teaching. The Prophet ﷺ, also called Mustafa, asked the Companions about someone killed in Allah's path and told them he was in the Garden. He then asked about a deceased person of whom two just witnesses knew only good, and again spoke of the Garden. When asked about someone for whom two just witnesses knew no good, the Companions answered that he was in the Fire. The transmitted passage stops with their response, so no further answer is invented.

The closing reminder brings all these instructions back to Allah's sight. Fairness must hold in the seen and unseen parts of parenting alike. A parent cannot finally defend neglect with an excuse designed only for other people. Allah knows whether a decision protected the child or used the child to hurt someone else.

### In short

- Two full years complete nursing, while earlier weaning requires consultation and agreement about the child's welfare.
- The father provides reasonable support, and payment arrangements change when the mother's waiting period has ended.
- Neither parent may use feeding or custody to injure the other.
- Milk kinship, heir support and maintenance have distinct rules and recorded legal disagreements.
- Another nurse may be hired, but her agreed wages and earlier amounts due must be paid fairly and promptly.
