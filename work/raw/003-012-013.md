<<<VERSE 3:12>>>
The Prophet ﷺ is told to warn the disbelievers: “Soon you will be overpowered.” The warning speaks of defeat in this life and being gathered for punishment in the next. Its first setting is not every disbeliever in every age. The explanations connect it with the opponents of that time, including the Quraysh and the Jews of Madinah, who challenged the Prophet ﷺ after the victory at Badr.

### Phrase by phrase

**“˹O Prophet!˺ Tell the disbelievers”** is a command to Muhammad ﷺ to say the warning openly and clearly. One account says that after returning from Badr, he gathered the Jews in the marketplace of Banu Qaynuqaʿ and urged them to accept Islam before the defeat that had come to Quraysh came to them. They replied that the believers had defeated only inexperienced fighters and claimed that the outcome would be different against them.

Al-Kalbi reports from Abu Salih that Ibn Abbas described Jews of Madinah who recognized the Prophet ﷺ as the unlettered messenger foretold through Mūsā (Moses). They considered following him after Badr but chose to wait for another encounter. When the Muslims suffered a setback at Uhud, they became doubtful and said he was not the promised prophet. They then ended their treaty. In a separate account, Muhammad bin Ishaq bin Yasar relates that after Badr the Prophet ﷺ gathered Jews in Madinah and called them to accept Islam before the warning was fulfilled. Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf then travelled with sixty riders to meet Abū Sufyān and his companions in Makkah, and they agreed to join their efforts against the Prophet ﷺ. These are reports about particular opponents of that time, not every disbeliever in every age.

**“Soon you will be overpowered”** promises defeat in this life. Some explanations specify that those addressed would be killed or taken captive. In the case of the Jews mentioned, the material also describes subjection through the payment of jizya or expulsion. This warning is tied to the opponents of that time, not to every disbeliever without distinction. Another interpretation says that the Meccan idolaters were the direct audience and that Badr was the defeat meant.

**“and driven to Hell—what an evil place to rest!”** points beyond defeat in this world to the gathering in Hell in the Hereafter. The two parts of the warning are distinct: worldly defeat does not replace the final judgment, and the promise of judgment does not make the present consequences unreal. An inward reading calls the Hereafter the ājil, the future that is coming, and contrasts it with present life. Separation from Allah can already impoverish a person, even when weakened perception fails to recognize that loss.

### What was the setting of the warning?

The explanations give more than one immediate setting. One connects the words to the Jews in Madinah, who challenged Muhammad ﷺ after Badr and were warned after he called them to Islam. Another identifies the disbelievers of Makkah as the ones told that they would be defeated. A report connects the warning to the Jews who first considered believing after Badr, then doubted after Uhud, broke their treaty, and joined Abū Sufyān’s side. These accounts differ in who is directly addressed, but all keep the warning within the conflict of that period rather than applying it indiscriminately to every nonbeliever.

An inward reading says that the disbelievers’ future loss is both separation from Allah in this life and the pain of the Hereafter. Their present faculties may be too weak to recognize the first kind of loss. What seems like a life of ease can therefore hide an inward deprivation, while the punishment of the next life brings that loss into full view.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ is told to warn the disbelievers of defeat and the punishment of Hell.
- The warning is connected with the opponents of that time, including the Jews of Madinah and the Quraysh.
- The reports differ on the immediate audience but describe worldly defeat followed by judgment in the Hereafter.

<<<VERSE 3:13>>>
The meeting of two armies at Badr is presented as “a sign for you in the two armies that met in battle” showing that Allah supports whom He wills. The believers were far fewer and less equipped than their opponents, yet the encounter ended in their victory. The verse also describes how each side perceived the other’s numbers. The explanations differ over who saw the other as twice as numerous, and one account says their perceptions changed during the battle. The event is a lesson in trusting Allah rather than judging truth by visible strength alone.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Indeed, there was a sign for you in the two armies that met in battle”** points to Badr. The encounter is described as evidence that Allah would make His religion prevail and His messenger victorious. One account says that after Badr the Prophet ﷺ gathered the Jews in Madinah to warn them that they had seen a sign of his prophethood. Another says the event confirmed that the message he carried was true. The later account of Badr is also linked to the promise that a small, patient group can overcome a larger one (8:66).

**“one fighting for the cause of Allah and the other in denial”** identifies the two sides. One was the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions, fighting in obedience to Allah. The other was the Quraysh army from Makkah. One report names Abu Sufyan, who was associated with the opposing group, while other accounts distinguish his caravan from the army that fought at Badr. The contrast is not simply between two military groups. The verse distinguishes those fighting in faith from those who came in disbelief and, in another account, out of pride and display.

**“The believers saw their enemy twice their number”** has more than one explanation. One reading says the Quraysh thought the Muslims were twice as numerous as they were. This struck fear into them and helped the believers prevail. Another says the Muslims first saw the Quraysh as twice their own number. That sight led them to depend more fully on Allah and ask for His help. A further account says both sides initially saw the opposing force as larger, then each camp appeared smaller to the other when they drew up for battle. This shifting perception encouraged the armies to engage, so that what Allah had decreed would happen.

The explanations give different details of the numbers. One says the Quraysh had about 1,000 men, 700 camels, and 100 horses, while the Muslims had just over 300 men, 70 camels, two horses, six coats of armor, and eight swords. Another gives the Muslim force as 313 and the opposing army as 950. These figures describe a striking difference in manpower and equipment. The report attributed to ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd says the Muslims first found the enemy to be twice their number, then looked again and thought there was not one more enemy fighter than their own side. Surah al-Anfal describes Allah making each group appear small to the other (8:44) and explains that the meeting occurred so a matter already decreed would be brought about (8:42).

**“But Allah supports with His victory whoever He wills”** explains why the weaker side prevailed. Their victory did not come from their larger resources. Allah strengthened them with His aid. The Qur’an recalls that He had already granted victory at Badr when the believers were few (3:123). Another explanation says that the believers saw their opponents as more numerous and therefore turned to Allah with greater trust. In either reading, Allah’s support, not a force’s visible size, determines the outcome.

**“Surely in this is a lesson for people of insight”** calls readers to think about Allah’s wisdom and decision. An extraordinary victory by a small, poorly equipped group over a much stronger force is not to be explained by worldly advantage. It shows that truth is not unsupported, even when its followers appear weak. People of insight are asked to consider what the event reveals about Allah’s power and the limits of human calculation.

### Why do the accounts differ about what each side saw?

The reports describe the moment from different perspectives. Some say the Quraysh saw the believers as twice their number, which brought fear and despair. Others say the Muslims saw the enemy as twice their number, which deepened their reliance on Allah. A further explanation describes both groups at first seeing the other as larger, then seeing the forces as smaller when the battle lines formed. These accounts do not change the verse’s central point: the appearance of numbers was not the measure of the outcome, and Allah supported whom He willed.

The material also gives different counts for the armies and their equipment. It reports approximately 1,000 Quraysh fighters, 700 camels, and 100 horses, against just over 300 believers, 70 camels, two horses, six coats of armor, and eight swords. Another report gives 313 believers and 950 opponents. The variation is retained rather than collapsed into one exact count.

### What made the encounter a sign?

The sign lay in the imbalance between the two forces and the result. The believers were few and had little equipment. Their opponents had far more men and resources. Yet Allah gave victory to the believers, fulfilling the warning that the disbelievers would be overcome. The account describes this as confirmation of Muhammad’s prophethood and proof that Allah can make His religion prevail. It also provides a worldly example of the final distinction between truth and falsehood.

An inward reading says that physical sight alone does not guarantee insight. When Allah covers a person’s inner sight, sharp eyes do not reveal what matters. When He opens a person’s inward perception, limited outward vision does not prevent understanding. The event therefore asks people not only to look at the numbers but to see what Allah’s support made clear.

### In short

- Badr showed that Allah could grant victory to a small and lightly equipped force.
- Explanations differ over which side saw the other as twice its number and over the exact army counts.
- The event was a sign of Allah’s support, a lesson in trust, and evidence that visible strength does not decide the outcome.
