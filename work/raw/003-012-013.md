<<<VERSE 3:12>>>
The Prophet ﷺ is told to say to the disbelievers: "Soon you will be overpowered and driven to Hell—what an evil place to rest!" The defeat is placed in this world and the gathering in the next. The words were heard after Badr by people who still thought that the result in the field said nothing about who was right.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹O Prophet!˺ Tell the disbelievers"**: the warning is given as a direct address. The disbelievers named in the reports that follow are the Jews of Madinah after Badr, and the command runs without restriction as to religion: believers will defeat them and Allah will gather them in the fire of Hell.

**"Soon you will be overpowered"**: you will be defeated in this life. The word can be read as sa-tughlabūn, "you shall be vanquished", or sa-yughlabūn, "they shall be vanquished". For the people addressed the defeat came through killing, capture, imprisonment, the paying of jizya and expulsion.

**"and driven to Hell"**: gathered together on the Day of Resurrection to Hell. Here too the reading runs in two forms, wa-tuḥsharūna, "you will be mustered", or wa-yuhsharūna, "they will be mustered". The mustering is in the Hereafter, and the place they enter is the fire.

**"what an evil place to rest!"**: an evil cradling and an evil resting place, an evil place and outcome.

### The objection raised by the Jews of Madinah after Badr

ʿĀṣim ibn ʿUmar ibn Qatādah related that, after victory at Badr, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ returned to Al-Madinah and summoned the Jews at the market of Banū Qaynuqāʿ. Muḥammad ibn Ishaq bin Yasar preserves the report. The Prophet ﷺ warned them not to let Badr's fate reach them as it had reached Quraysh, and he called them into Islam beforehand. He added that prophethood was no secret to them, since their Scripture and Allah's covenant both mentioned him.

Their reply rejected the warning. Quraysh, they said, were inexperienced men ignorant of warfare, so let him not be deluded by beating them. If Jews fought, he would discover real fighters. This verse followed. ʿIkrimah and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr transmit the account from Ibn ʿAbbās, and a shorter version records the same taunt after the Prophet ﷺ urged the Jews to accept Islam on his return.

### A second report about the Jews of Madinah

Ibn ʿAbbās also reports a change after Uhud. At Badr the Jews of Medina had identified Muhammad ﷺ as the unlettered Prophet announced by Moses, whose description stood in their Scriptures and whose banner would never fall. Some wanted immediate faith, while others counselled waiting for another encounter. Defeat at Uhud reversed them. They denied that he could be the promised prophet, abandoned Islam, and broke a treaty that still had time to run. Then Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf rode toward Mecca with sixty riders, met Abū Sufyān's party, agreed on one common word and purpose, and returned to Medina. Revelation answered them in this verse.

### To which disbelievers does the promise apply?

The warning does not claim that every unbeliever everywhere loses in this world. Its horizon is the generation addressed. Pagans among them fell in battle or were captured. Jews were subdued through deaths, captivity, expulsion and jizya. Doubt dissolves when the promise is confined to those hearers and the fate they actually met.

### The pain they already carry without knowing it

The Real's speech will pass them by later, in ājil, and even now, in the present or ʿājil, living holds no delight for them. Tomorrow brings burning punishment, ḥurqa. Today already brings furqa, absence and separation from God, but dim insight cannot feel the pain of a punishment already begun.

### In short

- The disbelievers addressed after Badr are told they will be overpowered in this world and gathered to Hell in the next.
- The Jews of Madinah answered the call to Islam by calling the Quraysh inexperienced, and after Uhud some broke their treaty and rode with Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf to Abū Sufyān.
- The overcoming named here belongs to the disbelievers of that time, beaten by killing, capture, jizya and expulsion, and not to every disbeliever in every age.

<<<VERSE 3:13>>>
This verse, "Indeed, there was a sign for you in the two armies that met in battle—one fighting for the cause of Allah and the other in denial. The believers saw their enemy twice their number. But Allah supports with His victory whoever He wills. Surely in this is a lesson for people of insight", looks back to Badr as proof of the warning in the verse before it. A small force with very little equipment defeated a force about three times its size, and the seeing of the two armies was itself part of the sign.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, there was a sign for you in the two armies that met in battle"**: this āyah proves that Allah exalts His religion, grants success to His Messenger, makes His word visible and raises faith above rivals. For the Jews addressed, Badr also confirms Muhammad's prophethood. Two camps met there in combat.

**"one fighting for the cause of Allah and the other in denial"**: one camp fought in the cause of Allah, the Muslims. The other fought in disbelief, the idolaters of Quraysh at Badr. The first group fought in obedience to Allah, Muhammad ﷺ and his Companions. The second consisted of disbelievers in Allah and His Messenger, Abū Sufyān and his army, who had come out of Makkah in pride and for show.

**"The believers saw their enemy twice their number"**: the two sides did not see the numbers in the same way, and the material explains the sighting in more than one way. These explanations are set out in the section on the double sighting below.

**"But Allah supports with His victory whoever He wills"**: help is His to assign. He strengthened the side He chose and stood by His allies, and He grants believing servants success now and again on the Day when witnesses rise.

**"Surely in this is a lesson for people of insight"**: a lesson for those who understand, people of vision who are discerning. They should think about Allah's wisdom, decisions and decree. The lesson is also put as a question to the hearers: after this, will you not be warned, and believe?

### The numbers at Badr

The imbalance began with people and animals: Quraysh fielded about 1000 men. Their animals included 700 camels plus 100 horses. Opposing them stood just over 300 Muslims. Muslims brought only 2 horses, carried 8 swords and 6 armours, and shared 70 camels. A precise count sets Muhammad's men at 313, mostly foot soldiers, while Abū Sufyān commanded 950. A tiny, poorly supplied force had nevertheless overturned a much larger one, exactly as promised earlier at Makkah. Observers willing to learn could hardly miss the event.

In the fourteenth year of prophethood Badr supplied this world with an image of the next. Deniers held every advantage of manpower and might, whereas believers possessed almost no worldly power. Yet denial collapsed and faith prevailed decisively. Without divine succour, such reversal across so large a resource gap is impossible. Truth therefore never stands unsupported, and Allah sides with believers. For deniers, the event exposes their real position. They had evidence in the preacher's speech and conduct, but pride sheltered behind elegant rebuttals and invented explanations. Death eventually removes that shelter, and the next world shows those arguments to be empty.

### The double sighting: who saw whom as twice?

The verse's own wording allows two sightings, and both are reported. In one reading, the idolaters thought the Muslims were twice as many as they really were, and Allah made that illusion one of the factors in the victory of Islam over them. In the other, the Muslims saw twice as many idolators as themselves, and Allah still gave them victory over the disbelievers.

ʿAbdullāh bin Masʿūd said, "When we looked at the disbelievers' forces, we found that they were twice as many as we were. When we looked at them again, we thought they did not have one man more than we had." His words stand beside the verse in al-Anfal: "And (remember) when you met, He showed them to you as few in your eyes and He made you appear as few in their eyes" (8:44).

Timing separates the sightings. At first sight, Muslims estimated their opponents at double their actual strength, which drove them toward trust and prayer for help. Quraysh made the mirror error about believers, and terror followed. Believers also clung to another promise: "If there be among you a hundred patient one, they shall over-come two hundred" (8:66). Had the true ratio (roughly three to one) registered, fear might have overwhelmed them. Once ranks closed, perception reversed again. Each army appeared small to the other, daring both to engage, "so that Allah might accomplish a matter already ordained" (8:42). That ordained result separated truth from falsehood, lifted faith over unbelief, and humiliated the deniers. Allah names the outcome himself: "And Allah has already made you victorious at Badr, when you were a weak little force" (3:123).

Jewish onlookers receive a second application. They personally watched unbelievers outnumber Muhammad's Companions twofold, yet Allah still delivered triumph at Badr. Because they never fought, the bare outcome carries the proof.

### Inner sight and outer sight

An inward reading notices perception itself being governed. To finish an affair, Allah can enlarge a few in one people's eyes and shrink a multitude in another's. Cover the inner eye, baṣīra, and keen outer eyes, abṣār, profit nothing. Open the inmost self, and obstructed insight, baṣāʾir, cannot hurt. Armies thought they were reporting numbers, but seeing had already been arranged by the One arranging victory.

### In short

- At Badr about 313 Muslims with 70 camels, 2 horses, 6 armours and 8 swords defeated some 950 to 1000 Quraysh, and that result is the sign.
- Each army saw the other as twice its number at one moment, and as few at another, so that both would fight and the ordained outcome would become clear (8:42, 8:44, 8:66, 3:123).
- The lesson is for people of insight: victory comes from Allah's support, not from numbers, and outer eyes are useless when inner sight is covered.
