<<<VERSE 2:255>>>
Ayat al-Kursi gathers the central truths of Allah's oneness and perfect care: "Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him, the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining." Everything depends upon Him, while He depends upon nothing. Neither sleep, ignorance nor fatigue interrupts His rule. Even intercession and created knowledge operate only through His permission.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him, the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining."** Allah names the One possessing every perfection and free from deficiency. Al-Hayy means the Living, whose life never ends. Al-Qayyum means the One who stands by Himself and sustains all others. The root concerns standing, while the intensive form expresses complete, continual support. Created things require support and cannot share this self-sufficiency.

**"Neither drowsiness nor sleep overtakes Him."** Sinah is drowsiness, while nawm is full sleep. Neither touches Allah. The vastness of what He sustains does not make Him tired or inattentive. Measuring His care by the exhausted human worker would be a false comparison.

**"To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth."** Everything is His possession, creation and servant, subject to His authority. The ownership expressed in the wording leaves no independent part of the universe outside His rule.

**"Who could possibly intercede with Him without His permission?"** No one may speak as an independent claimant over His judgment. Intercession is possible for those He permits, on the terms He permits. A revered person's status does not give that person power to compel Allah.

**"He ˹fully˺ knows what is ahead of them and what is behind them,"** His knowledge covers past, present and future. Explanations also distinguish life here from the Hereafter, states before and after birth, and what people can see from what is hidden. The meanings are compatible in affirming that nothing escapes Him. One application particularly concerns the angels and what they know of this world and the next.

**"but no one can grasp any of His knowledge—except what He wills ˹to reveal˺."** Created knowledge is granted, not independently acquired in defiance of Him. What creatures know of events and what they know of Allah's attributes both depend on what He allows and conveys. None shares His complete knowledge of every particle.

**"His Seat encompasses the heavens and the earth,"** Kursi is explained as the Seat or footstool, distinct in some accounts from the 'Arsh, Throne. Another interpretation understands the phrase as His knowledge encompassing both. The descriptions of its vastness are given below without measuring Allah Himself as though He were a created occupant of a chair.

**"and the preservation of both does not tire Him."** Guarding the heavens, earth and everything between them is effortless for His perfect power. Neither burden nor weariness limits His attention. Another explanation includes preservation of the Throne and Seat without dependence on angels.

**"For He is the Most High, the Greatest"** His height and greatness concern His perfect essence, attributes, power and dominion. The conclusion gathers the preceding perfections into the acknowledgement that ultimate honour and authority belong to Him alone. The related description appears in (13:9).

### Why is this called the greatest verse?

Ahmad records 'Ubayy bin Ka'b being asked by the Prophet ﷺ which verse in Allah's Book was greatest. After initially leaving the knowledge to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, he answered Ayat al-Kursi. The Prophet ﷺ congratulated him, addressing him as Abu Al-Mundhir. Muslim also records the account.

Ahmad's version adds a description of the verse praising the King beside the Throne with a tongue and lips. Muslim does not include that additional portion. The difference in what each report contains must remain visible.

Abu Dharr al-Ghifari also asked about the greatest verse, and Ahmad in Al-Musnad records the same answer. A report from Abu Hurayrah calls it the chief verse of Al-Baqarah and says Satan leaves a house where it is recited. Another report in An-Nasa'i promises that for someone reciting it after every fard salah, obligatory prayer, nothing stands between him and entry into Paradise except death. The explanation associates death with beginning to experience its comfort and tranquillity.

The verse is described as ten complete statements. They form an integrated confession of worship, life, self-sufficiency, ownership, knowledge and power. Its merit is therefore connected with what it teaches, not only with its length or a formula detached from belief.

### What do the reports of night protection describe?

Ahmad records Abu Ayyub keeping dates from which a ghoul repeatedly took food. A ghoul here means a jinn appearing at night. The Prophet ﷺ taught him to call it in Allah's name to answer to His Messenger ﷺ. He caught it, released it on promises not to return and was told that it would come again. After repeated incidents it offered to teach Ayat al-Kursi as protection. The Prophet ﷺ confirmed that this time it had spoken truth despite being a liar. At-Tirmidhi records the account and grades it Hasan Gharib, good and singular in transmission.

Al-Bukhari records a similar but distinct incident from Abu Hurayrah. He guarded the sadaqah, charity, of Ramadan. A thief gathered food, pleaded poverty and dependants and was released. The Prophet ﷺ warned that the thief had lied and would return. The same happened again. On the third night Abu Hurayrah insisted that he would take him to the Prophet ﷺ.

The thief then taught him to recite the whole verse before sleep, promising that Allah would appoint a guard and Satan would not approach until morning. When Abu Hurayrah reported it, the Prophet ﷺ confirmed the teaching and identified the visitor as Satan. The account notes the Companions' eagerness for good deeds. An-Nasa'i also records it in Al-Yawm wa Al-Laylah.

These reports are not merged into one episode with alternating names. Abu Ayyub's dates and Abu Hurayrah's entrusted charity are separate settings. Their shared point is the confirmed benefit of the verse, not trustworthiness of the beings who tried to deceive them.

### What is said about Allah's greatest name?

Asma' bint Yazid bin As-Sakan reports the Prophet ﷺ identifying Allah's greatest name within this verse and the opening of Al 'Imran, (3:1-2). Ahmad records it, as do Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah. At-Tirmidhi grades it Hasan Sahih, good and authentic.

Ibn Marduwyah records from Abu Umamah a report locating the greatest name, with which supplication is answered, in Al-Baqarah, Al 'Imran and Ta-Ha. Hisham bin 'Ammar, described as the orator of Damascus, points to the Living and All-Sustaining names here and in the opening of Al 'Imran, and to the corresponding description in (20:111).

Several early authorities likewise understand the combination Hayy and Qayyum as the great name. 'Ali recalls finding the Prophet ﷺ in prostration during Badr repeatedly calling upon Allah. The printed material does not preserve the words after that introduction, so the missing recitation is not supplied from memory.

Al-Qayyum is treated as an attribute belonging only to Allah. The naming instruction warns against calling a human being simply Qayyum. It particularly cautions against shortening 'Abdul-Qayyum, servant of the All-Sustaining, by discarding the part that expresses servanthood. The theological meaning must not be lost through casual use of a name.

### Why does perfect life exclude sleep?

Created life is interrupted by fatigue, inattention and dependence. Allah's life has none of those defects. The universe's constant support is not an exhausting task requiring recovery. The explanation therefore tells the reader not to imagine Him through an analogy with personal experience.

Abu Musa reports in the Sahih a speech of the Prophet ﷺ saying that Allah does not sleep and that sleep does not befit Him. He lowers and raises the scales. The deeds of day and night are brought before Him in their appointed order. His veil is described as light, or fire, and its removal would expose creation to the burning radiance of His face as far as His sight reaches.

The report enlarges the meaning of uninterrupted care. No time of day, deed or distant creature is beyond His attention. The heaven and earth stand by His command, as (30:25) also states. All approach Him as servants, individually known and counted, as recalled in (19:93-95).

### How can intercession occur under absolute sovereignty?

The preceding warning against depending upon intercession is clarified by the condition of permission. The angels cannot intercede except after Allah gives leave for one with whom He is pleased, as (53:26) and (21:28) state. Neither multitude nor rank gives them an independent claim.

The prophetic account of intercession describes the Prophet ﷺ falling in prostration beneath the Throne for as long as Allah wills. He is then told to raise his head, speak and intercede. A determined number is permitted to enter Paradise. The permission comes first, not as something forced by the intercessor.

Another saying identifies the Prophet ﷺ as first to intercede for the human communities at the Resurrection. This honour is called al-Maqam al-Mahmud, the praised station. It is a bestowed distinction, consistent with Allah's supreme rule rather than an exception to it.

The warning also addresses superficial hopes of salvation. Merely performing outward forms or relying on a religious figure without surrendering one's conduct undervalues the majesty of the final judgment. Ritual form is not a substitute for sincere obedience to the Lord described here.

### What does complete knowledge mean for created knowledge?

The angels' statement in (19:64) recognises that they descend only by the Lord's command and that what lies ahead, behind and between belongs to Him. He is never forgetful. Human distinctions between visible and hidden, past and future do not divide His knowledge.

A person knows only what Allah allows. This is not a claim that learning is useless, but a denial of an independent, unlimited knowing power in any creature. The explanation extends the principle to understanding His attributes: knowledge of Him rests on His disclosure. The passage in (20:110) confirms that creatures do not encompass His knowledge.

Such dependence corrects confidence that a favourite religious figure has automatic access to every hidden matter. It also teaches gratitude for ordinary knowledge. What is granted is real, but its limits remain real too.

### How are the Seat and Throne described?

Waki' relates Ibn 'Abbas identifying the Kursi as the footstool and saying that the Throne cannot be adequately measured by people. Al-Hakim records the statement in his Mustadrak. It is a statement from Ibn 'Abbas, not one he attributes to the Prophet ﷺ. Al-Hakim grades it Sahih according to the criteria of the Two Sahihs while noting that they did not collect it.

Ad-Dahhak relates from Ibn 'Abbas that the seven heavens and earths laid alongside one another would be like a ring in a desert compared with the Kursi. A report from Abu Dharr gives a similar comparison in the Prophet's ﷺ answer. Other narrations compare the Kursi itself with the 'Arsh in the same way. Another image describes the seven heavens as seven silver coins within a metal shield.

These comparisons convey vastness, not a licence to measure Allah by the furniture familiar to people. One explanation describes the Seat and Throne as heavenly bodies vastly greater than the heavens and earth, while asserting that Allah is beyond sitting, standing and spatial placement. Another understands the encompassing Seat as the encompassing of His knowledge.

The discussion also gives the approach of the salaf, the early righteous generations: accept the revealed descriptions without equating Allah's attributes with creation or altering their apparent meaning. The caution against created analogies and the caution against changing the revealed description are both retained. Their purpose is reverence before what human reason cannot fully comprehend.

### What does the inward reading ask of remembrance?

It pictures Allah as the hope of the wounded, care of the orphan, healing of the heart and light of those who affirm His oneness. His existence is beginningless, His splendour endless and His beauty not dependent on another. The poems turn those beliefs into longing, loyalty and relief through remembrance.

Junayd, described as a pir of the tariqah, a spiritual elder of the path, warns against speaking Allah's name while the heart gives its ultimate attachment to something else. The warning says such divided remembrance brings rebuke in both abodes. Delighting in the sound of the name while refusing its claim on the heart is not the intended surrender.

An account of the mi'raj, the ascension, gives an admonition to Muhammad ﷺ about people claiming faith while depending upon another ultimate support. Were they to attend to Allah's kindness and the wonders of His creation, they would not worship anything else. No collector or grading is added where the account supplies none.

The accompanying prayers describe nearness removing sorrow and remembrance easing grief. Allah is addressed as witness, judge and the One who receives every claim. Poetic images call even the confinement of love a garden because the beloved name is there. The imagery is preserved as allusion and longing, not as a bodily portrait or a replacement for the verse's explicit account of sovereignty.

The inward lesson comes back to the opening: none besides Him deserves worship. Knowledge of His sufficiency should make remembrance truthful in heart and tongue alike. The whole verse joins assurance of care with refusal to make an idol of any supposed independent helper.

### In short

- Allah alone deserves worship and sustains every creature without dependence, sleep or weariness.
- Intercession and created knowledge exist only through His permission.
- Reports describe the verse's exceptional merit, protection before sleep and recitation after obligatory prayer.
- Descriptions of the Seat and Throne convey vastness without making Allah comparable to created beings.
- Sincere remembrance should reflect in the heart the sole allegiance confessed by the tongue.

<<<VERSE 2:256>>>
The verse forbids forcing entry into faith: "Let there be no compulsion in religion, for the truth stands out clearly from falsehood." Islam's evidence is to be recognised, not replaced by coercion. The one who rejects false objects of worship and believes in Allah gains a firm support. The strength belongs to the truth held, not to pressure imposed on another person's body.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Let there be no compulsion in religion, for the truth stands out clearly from falsehood."** The right direction has been distinguished from error through plain arguments. Whoever Allah enlightens accepts with certainty. Forcing an outward response cannot make an unwilling heart believe. One explanation specifically applies the prohibition to the People of the Book and Magians after the Arabs' acceptance of Islam, while another gives the instruction a general meaning.

**"So whoever renounces false gods and believes in Allah"** Taghut includes Satan, idols, imagined rivals and what Satan calls people to worship besides Allah. It can function as singular or plural. Rejecting it includes refusing its religious command, worship and claimed authority. Tawhid means affirming Allah's oneness and worshipping Him alone.

**"has certainly grasped the firmest, unfailing hand-hold."** The image is a secure handle or knot attached to a strong support. Mujahid explains it as iman, faith, while As-Suddi identifies it as Islam. The support does not fail. Someone's releasing it is a different matter from its breaking beneath someone who holds it.

**"And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing"** Allah hears the confession and knows deeds, inner belief and the reward He grants. A coerced statement cannot deceive the One who knows the heart. The same knowledge recognises sincere adherence where outward social pressure offers no advantage.

### What situation among the Helpers prompted the verse?

Ibn 'Abbas describes women of the Ansar, the Helpers, whose children had repeatedly died in infancy. They vowed that if a boy survived, they would raise him as a Jew. When Banu An-Nadir were expelled from Al-Madinah, some children of the Helpers were living among them. Their families wanted to keep them and compel a change of religion.

Ibn Jarir records the account. Abu Dawud and An-Nasa'i also transmit it. Sa'id ibn Jubayr says those wishing to leave with the Jews left, and those wishing to accept Islam did so. The occasion concerns a particular community, while the explanation says the indication is general.

Mujahid gives another account about a man of the Helpers compelling a boy called Subayh to become Muslim. Another passage names Mundhir Ibn Sawi al-Tamimi in connection with renouncing false deities. These are the different settings reported, not one family under several assumed names.

As-Suddi also relates a case of Abu'l-Husayn and his two sons involving traders from Syria who came to Madinah to sell oil. The transmitted wording says the sons called the traders to Christianity and that the traders converted before leaving, yet it then describes the father asking for the sons to be summoned. That connection is unclear in the supplied text. The verse is associated with his request, and the Prophet ﷺ is reported to rebuke the two as the first to disbelieve. The unclear account is not silently rewritten into a different conversion story.

### Does the invitation to an unwilling man contradict the rule?

Ahmad records an authentic report from Anas in which the Prophet ﷺ invited a man to accept Islam. The man said he disliked doing so, and the Prophet ﷺ encouraged him to accept despite that feeling.

The explanation distinguishes encouragement from force. The Prophet ﷺ did not compel the man. He invited him to take the step through which Allah could grant sincerity and true intention. The report's chain is described as containing only three transmitters between Ahmad and the Prophet ﷺ, but that shortness does not change the meaning of the act into coercion.

### How are warfare and freedom of belief distinguished?

The legal discussion answers an objection that jihad and fighting appear to contradict this verse. It says their purpose is not compelling people to embrace faith, but checking oppression and disorder. The instruction in (5:64) that Allah does not love those spreading corruption is brought into that argument.

The arrangement of jizyah is also cited. This is the due paid by free non-Muslims under Muslim rule in exchange for the stated protection of life, property and honour. The existence of such protected non-Muslim life is presented as evidence that accepting Islam is not the demanded result of every encounter.

The discussion forbids killing women, children, the aged and disabled even in battle, connecting the restriction with their inability to create the disorder at issue. It also forbids killing those who become law-abiding protected citizens through the jizyah arrangement. Its severe comparison of harmful attackers with dangerous serpents or scorpions concerns the argument about restraining harm, not a permission to treat every non-Muslim as a creature to be killed.

'Umar invited an elderly Christian woman to Islam. When she answered that she was old and near death, he did not compel her. He recited the prohibition of compulsion. The illustration places the restraint within an actual invitation, not only an abstract profession of tolerance.

The central distinction is between an outward act and the qalb, heart. Force can affect the body, but belief is a matter of inward assent. Coercing the former cannot manufacture the latter. The legal reasoning should therefore not be replaced with a claim that forced words constitute sincere faith.

### What does the firm hand-hold represent?

Al-Baghawi records 'Umar explaining jibt as magic and taghut as Satan. The same statement reflects on courage and cowardice, saying that the brave may defend people he does not know while the coward deserts even his mother. It places honour in religion and character rather than Persian or Nabatian ancestry. The definition of taghut encompasses the false worship, invocation and judgment practised in ignorance.

The handle is securely joined to its support. The image shows firmness of adherence and a straight course. Another explanation connects it with the confession that none deserves worship besides Allah and the unending bliss of Paradise. Its security does not mean that abandoning Islam has no consequence. The promise concerns holding, not knowingly letting go.

### What dream illustrated this image?

Qays bin 'Abbad saw a humble-looking man pray two modest units in the mosque. People called him a man of Paradise. Qays followed and asked about it. The man warned against making claims without knowledge, then explained a dream he had told the Prophet ﷺ.

He had seen a green, spacious garden with an iron pole fixed in the ground and reaching the sky. A handle stood at its top. Asked to climb, he said he could not. A helper raised his robe and encouraged him. He climbed, grasped the handle and woke with that impression still present.

The Prophet ﷺ explained the garden as Islam, the pole as its pillar and the handle as the firmest support. He told the man that he would remain Muslim until death. The Companion was 'Abdullah bin Salam. Ahmad records the report, and it appears in the Two Sahihs, with another chain also in Al-Bukhari.

The dream gives the image a personal illustration, while the warning against unsupported claims remains part of the account. The assurance came from the Prophet's ﷺ interpretation, not simply from an observer liking someone's appearance.

### What is the inward application of rejection and belief?

The inward reading compares clear truth and error with day and night. It distinguishes the timeless reality of Allah from the dependent hudud, limits, of created things. The false deity is then applied to anything that distracts a person from the Lord.

Faith is described as the heart's life through Allah. Holding the firm support means respecting commands and prohibitions and travelling the path of the Prophet ﷺ, called al-Mustafa. Inward recognition must have an outward form in obedience. That combination is described as success in both abodes.

The shared warning about insincerity also applies. A person who casually rejects clear arguments while relying on superficial religious forms is following temptation rather than holding fast. Guidance requires sincere recognition, not pressure substituted for conviction.

### In short

- Entry into faith is not secured by coercion, because belief concerns the heart.
- The Helpers' family cases are reported occasions for a rule explained more broadly.
- Rejecting false worship and believing in Allah is the firm hand-hold, not a guarantee for someone who abandons it.
- The discussion distinguishes restraining oppression from forcing religious assent.
- Inward belief should be joined to outward obedience.

<<<VERSE 2:257>>>
Faith brings a protecting relationship with Allah: "Allah is the Guardian of the believers—He brings them out of darkness and into light." False guardians do the opposite, making error attractive and drawing people away from truth. The verse sets these two directions beside their lasting consequences.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah is the Guardian of the believers—He brings them out of darkness and into light."** Wali means guardian, protecting friend and helper. Allah grants success and guidance, bringing believers from ignorance, disbelief, doubt and hesitation to faith and clear knowledge. Iman, faith, is therefore described as the greatest blessing.

**"As for the disbelievers, their guardians are false gods who lead them out of light and into darkness."** Their false supporters include Satan and his helpers, who beautify ignorance and make misguidance seem desirable. Kufr means disbelief. It is described as the greatest misfortune, with darkness in allegiance to the powers that promote it.

**"It is they who will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever"** The false guardians cannot provide the safety they promise. Those described remain in the Fire without death or departure. Their ending exposes the failure of the support to which they entrusted themselves.

### Why is light singular while darkness has many forms?

Truth is one, while falsehood divides into branches and competing paths. The language reflects that contrast. The discussion recalls (6:153), where Allah's single straight path is set against the other paths that separate people from it. Its instruction leads to taqwa, mindfulness of Allah.

Other grammatical illustrations are (6:1), with darknesses and light, and (16:48), with the right and lefts. The point is not that every use of a plural independently establishes a doctrine. These expressions illustrate the contrast between united truth and multiple directions of error.

### What does coming out of light mean?

One explanation treats the wording as a corresponding opposite to Allah's bringing believers out of darkness. Another concerns people who believed in the expected Prophet ﷺ before he came but rejected him after his arrival. They moved away from a truth they had previously acknowledged.

A particular application names 'Abdullah Ibn Salam and his companions among those helped into faith, while Ka'b Ibn al-Ashraf and his companions are named among those led by the devil towards disbelief. These examples illustrate the two directions without narrowing the whole verse to only those individuals.

### What does Allah's friendship include?

The inward reading gathers friend, patron, ruler and caretaker into the description of His care. It connects (47:11), which identifies Him as the believers' patron, and (41:31), which speaks of friends in this life and the next. His support is not limited to a brief earthly advantage.

Yusuf (Joseph) addresses Him as his protecting friend in both worlds in (12:101). The reflection contrasts a promise spoken to those receiving care with a prophet's own declaration of familiarity. It expressly denies that a friend's station thereby exceeds a prophet's: the end of the friend's work is described as the beginning of the prophet's.

Rather, the contrast illustrates tenderness towards the weak. Those unable to make confident claims of nearness receive assurance. A saying associates Allah's presence with hearts broken for Him. Helplessness and humility are not automatically exclusion from His care.

### Why is mistreating a believer taken so seriously?

An ungraded report here describes the Prophet ﷺ comparing destruction of the Ka'bah with denigrating one of Allah's friends, treating the latter as the greater disobedience. A nomad asks who those friends are, and the Prophet ﷺ identifies the faithful through this verse. No collector or independent grading is added to the report.

The image gives gravity to the protecting relationship. A believer's dignity cannot be dismissed merely because the person appears weak. The account ends by beginning another report about a broken-hearted person being brought before Allah at the Resurrection. Its continuation is not supplied in the material and is not invented here.

### In short

- Allah's guardianship leads believers from ignorance and doubt to faith and knowledge.
- False guardians beautify error and lead towards darkness rather than lasting protection.
- The one light of truth contrasts with the many branches of falsehood.
- Allah's care reaches this life and the next, and the inward reflection emphasises His tenderness towards the humble.
