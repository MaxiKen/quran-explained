Verse 151 closes the long discussion of the qiblah by returning to the prayer with which it began. "Since We have sent you a messenger from among yourselves—reciting to you Our revelations, purifying you, teaching you the Book and wisdom, and teaching you what you never knew—". When Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) raised the Kaʿbah they prayed for two things (verses 127 to 129): that Allah accept their founding of the House, and that He raise among their descendants a messenger from among themselves. The qiblah passage has already shown that the first request was granted. Verse 151 now declares that the second was granted too, and the sentence runs on into verse 152, where the response Allah asks for is spelled out.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Since We have sent you a messenger from among yourselves"**. The verse opens with "as" or "just as", the Arabic letter kāf that compares two things. On one reading it connects with the previous verse: just as I perfect My favour on you by giving you the qiblah, so too I have sent among you a Messenger of your own, Muḥammad ﷺ, and the sending of the Messenger is itself part of the perfecting of My grace. Al-Qurṭubī instead connects the comparison with the first words of verse 152, "remember Me", so that the two verses together mean: the ability to remember Allah is as great a blessing as the new qiblah and the coming of the Last Prophet ﷺ, and people must therefore thank Allah so that His favours continue. Mujāhid likewise explained the verse as meaning: so remember Me in gratitude for My favour. Since the Prophet ﷺ was sent in answer to the prayer of the founder of the Kaʿbah, it is no wonder that the Kaʿbah should be appointed his qiblah. Allah reminds His believing servants of what He gave them by sending Muḥammad ﷺ as a Messenger, and the Messenger is from their own lineage, one of themselves.

**"reciting to you Our revelations"**. He recites to them the clear verses of the Qur'an, with its commands and prohibitions. This is the first of the attributes Ibrāhīm named in his prayer, and verse 151 lists them in order: he recites the verses, he teaches the Book and the wisdom, he purifies, and he teaches what his listeners did not know.

**"purifying you"**. He cleanses them of sin, of idolatry and of the worst kinds of behaviour, the ills of the soul and the practices of the Jāhiliyyah, the age of ignorance before Islam. He causes them to grow by directing them to virtue and goodness, by belief in the Oneness of Allah, by zakāh and by charity, and by forbidding them evil and wrong. He takes them out of the darkness of disbelief into the light of faith.

**"teaching you the Book and wisdom"**. The Book is the Qur'an, and the wisdom (ḥikmah) is his Sunnah, the rulings of the Qur'an, the lawful and the prohibited, and the understanding of religion (fiqh).

**"and teaching you what you never knew"**. He teaches them legal rulings, the prescribed punishments, the stories of bygone nations and both worldly and sacred knowledge that they had no access to before the revelation of the Qur'an and the coming of Muḥammad ﷺ, things that could not be found in the earlier divine Books or worked out by individual reason. In the days of ignorance they used to utter foolish things. The blessing of the Prophet's ﷺ message raised them to the rank of the awliyāʾ, the loyal friends of Allah, and the rank of the scholars, so that they came to have the deepest knowledge among people, the most pious hearts and the most truthful tongues. A parallel verse says: "Indeed, Allah has done the believers a ˹great˺ favour by raising a messenger from among them—reciting to them His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom. For indeed they had previously been clearly astray" (3:164). Allah also censures those who failed to value this bounty: "Have you not seen those ˹disbelievers˺ who meet Allah's favours with ingratitude and lead their own people to their doom" (14:28). Ibn ʿAbbās said that the favour of Allah in that verse means Muḥammad ﷺ.

### The answered prayer and the sign of the qiblah

It was at the time of the building of the Kaʿbah that Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl prayed for a prophet to be born among the people of Makkah. The prayer was accepted and the coming of the final Prophet ﷺ, whose focus would be Makkah, was decreed. The messengers who came afterwards used to foretell his arrival. Now that he had come, the Kaʿbah in Makkah was made the direction of prayer for all nations, a sign from Allah that the awaited Prophet ﷺ had arrived. The sincere needed no further proof, but even a sign as conclusive as this did not silence those who gave no thought to Allah. It is those who fear Allah who are guided along the path to Him.

In the devotional reading, the Messenger was sent to open the gates of attainment, because Allah knew beforehand that the hearts of His friends would thirst to meet Him, and there is no way to Him except through the mediation of the messengers. By sending messengers He charged some people with the burdens of the Law, and by sending messengers to others He honoured them with the arts of drawing near. What a difference between one people and another!

### In short

- The verse announces that Ibrāhīm's second prayer (2:129) has been answered: a Messenger from the people's own stock has been sent, which also explains why his qiblah is the Kaʿbah.
- His four tasks are those Ibrāhīm named: reciting the verses, purifying, teaching the Book and the wisdom (the Sunnah), and teaching what no one could otherwise know (cf. 3:164).
- The comparison "as We have sent" links either to the perfecting of favour in verse 150 or to "remember Me" in verse 152: the Prophet ﷺ is the favour, and remembrance and gratitude are the response (14:28).

<<<VERSE 2:152>>>
Having sent the Messenger in answer to Ibrāhīm's prayer, Allah tells people how to respond to so great a gift. "remember Me; I will remember you. And thank Me, and never be ungrateful". People are to remember Allah as the Supreme Benefactor and to thank Him by obeying Him. As long as they do so, He will remember them by showering material and spiritual blessings on them, and the verse ends with the warning not to deny His blessings or disobey Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"remember Me; I will remember you."**. Remember Me through prayer, glorification, acts of obedience and the like, and I will reward you. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī explained: remember Me in what I have commanded you, and I will remember you in what I have bound Myself to do for you, meaning His rewards and forgiveness. One reading is: remember Me in times of ease and I will remember you in times of hardship. Another: remember Me through obedience and I will remember you by granting you Paradise. The remembrance asked for engages the heart and the whole person, and its reward is Allah's approval and protection, since each is repaid according to his deeds. The ḥadīth qudsī recorded by al-Bukhārī, and reported by Imām Aḥmad from Anas, states that Allah said: O son of Adam, if you mention Me to yourself I will mention you to Myself, and if you mention Me in a gathering I will mention you in a gathering of angels, or in a better gathering. If you draw closer to Me by a hand's span I will draw closer to you by a forearm's length, if you draw closer by a forearm's length I will draw closer by an arm's length, and if you come to Me walking I will come to you running.

**"And thank Me"**. Be grateful for the countless favours I have bestowed on you, and show it by obedience. Allah commands that He be thanked and promises still more for thanking Him, as another verse says: "And ˹remember˺ when your Lord proclaimed, 'If you are grateful, I will certainly give you more. But if you are ungrateful, surely My punishment is severe.'" (14:7). Abū Rajāʾ al-ʿUṭāridī related that ʿImrān ibn Ḥuṣayn once came to them wearing a fine silk garment they had never seen him wear before or after, and explained that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had said that when Allah favours someone with a bounty, He likes to see the effect of His bounty on His creation, or, as one of the narrators named Rūḥ transmitted it, on His servant.

**"and never be ungrateful"**. Do not be ungrateful through disobedience, do not stop giving thanks for these blessings, and do not deny them or use them in prohibited ways. Allah is reproaching those who did not give the bounty of the Prophet ﷺ its due, and commanding the believers to affirm that favour and appreciate it by thanking and remembering Him.

### What remembrance is

Dhikr, remembrance, belongs essentially to the heart. Since the tongue is the heart's interpreter, reciting a divine Name or a verse of the Qur'an aloud is also called dhikr, but oral dhikr truly deserves the name only when the heart remembers along with it. The poet Rūmī observed that repeating a divine Name has no efficacy if one is thinking of cows and donkeys while mechanically pronouncing it. Even so, mechanical dhikr without the heart is not altogether futile. When a man complained to the Sufi Abū ʿUthmān of exactly this, he replied that one should thank Allah even for the favour of having drawn at least one organ, the tongue, into His service. Al-Qurṭubī records both anecdotes.

The merits of dhikr are innumerable, and the greatest of them is the assurance given here, that when a person remembers Allah, Allah remembers him. Abū ʿUthmān once claimed to know the moment at which Allah remembers His servants. When his hearers asked how he could know such a thing, he answered from this very promise: a Muslim who remembers Allah is at once remembered by Him, so everyone can know for himself that the instant he turns to Allah in remembrance, Allah remembers him.

### Remembrance as obedience

The verse means that if people remember Allah by obeying His commandments, He will remember them by granting pardon and reward. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr took the remembrance of Allah to mean obedience and submission to Him, saying that whoever has not obeyed Him has not remembered Him, even if he keeps himself outwardly busy with supererogatory (nafl) prayers and recitations of His praise. A hadith supports this fully. Al-Qurṭubī cites it from the Aḥkām al-Qurʾān of Ibn Khuwayz Mandādh: the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever obeys Allah, keeping to the rules of the lawful (ḥalāl) and the unlawful (ḥarām), has truly remembered Him even if he falls short in nafl prayers and fasting, while whoever disobeys the divine commands has in fact forgotten Allah, however many hours he spends in nafl prayer, fasting and praise.

The Sufi master Dhū al-Nūn al-Miṣrī said that whoever remembers Allah in the full sense forgets everything else, and that in return for such total absorption Allah Himself takes charge of all his concerns and gives him something far more valuable for everything he loses. The Companion Muʿādh remarked that for winning deliverance from divine wrath no deed of a human being compares with dhikr. Abū Hurayrah reported a hadith in which Allah Himself declares that He stays with His servant for as long as the servant goes on remembering Him with lips moving in dhikr. Only those who are filled with fear of Allah are free of fear of everything besides Him, and Allah remembers those who remember Him.

### Remembrance of the Friend

In the devotional reading, this is the remembrance of the lovingly kind Friend, the heart's ease and the spirit's food. Remembrance is the polo ball and familiarity with Him the bat. Yearning is its steed and love its field, burning for Him is the rose and recognising Him the garden. Such remembrance makes the Real apparent, for it is joined to the Reality and separate from mortal nature. It is the watering place of the tree of tawḥīd, whose fruit is friendship with the Real, which is why the Lord of the Worlds said that His servant does not cease remembering Him, and He remembering the servant, until the servant is passionate for Him and He for the servant. This is not the remembrance of the tongue that everyone knows. It lives inside the spirit. In later life Abū Yazīd remembered little with the tongue, and when asked about it he said he wondered at this remembrance of the tongue, and wondered more at the stranger, for what business has a stranger in the midst? Remembrance of Him is in the midst of the spirit. One master, in a whispered prayer, asked his Lord how he could possibly remember Him while He Himself was doing the remembering and the servant was crying out from his own forgetfulness. He is both the remembrance and the remembered, and only He helps anyone find Him. Whoever reaches You is done with sorrows, whoever sees You, his spirit laughs. Who in the two worlds has more joy than the one who remembers You?

To the poor man the warning is: you are remembering yourself, so what do you know of remembering Him? Never having travelled, what do you know of the way stations? Never having seen the Friend, how can you know His name and mark? You worship yourself, for whatever you do, you do for your own sake. Pass into the spirit and you will gain dignity. If one day you pass by the street of the Reality and remember Him in your secret core, you will see what no eye has seen, no ear has heard and what has never passed into the heart of any mortal. One of Allah's scriptures is reported to say: My servant, you will remember Me once you have tried out the others, for I am better for you than anyone else. When you have seen and tested them, you will know My worth and My rightful due. Once you have grasped their lack of loving kindness and My kindness and loyalty, you will know that I am more lovingly kind and more useful to you than anyone else. My servant, did I not remember you before you remembered Me? Did I not love you before you loved Me? I wanted you first, and then you wanted Me. My servant, have you turned to another because you are ashamed to face Me? Where are you going? My door is open and My gifts are bestowed on you. As someone put it, all your brightness is from Me, you wander about and then you come back to Me. Take one step on His path and a thousand generosities reach you from Him. From you a little service, from Him much blessing. From you a bit of obedience, from Him great mercy. The Prophet ﷺ alluded to this in the saying from Allah already quoted, that whoever remembers Him in himself is remembered by Him in Himself, whoever remembers Him in an assembly is remembered in a better assembly, and whoever comes to Him walking is met by Him rushing. As for "thank Me", it has been said that gratitude is what arises when one sees the blessings and is grateful in respect of one's own activity.

### In short

- Remembering Allah through obedience, prayer and glorification is met by His remembering the servant with reward, forgiveness, approval and protection, as the ḥadīth qudsī of the hand's span and the forearm promises (al-Bukhārī).
- Gratitude brings increase (14:7) and Allah loves to see the effect of His bounty on His servant. Ingratitude means disobedience or denying and misusing the blessings.
- True dhikr is of the heart, with the tongue as its interpreter. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and the hadith cited by al-Qurṭubī make obedience in ḥalāl and ḥarām the real test of remembrance, above nafl worship.
- Dhū al-Nūn, Muʿādh and Abū Hurayrah's hadith show its fruits: Allah takes charge of the one absorbed in Him, nothing saves from His wrath like dhikr, and He is with the servant whose lips move in remembrance.
- Inwardly, Allah remembered and loved the servant first. Remembrance in the spirit, not merely on the tongue, leads to friendship with Him.
