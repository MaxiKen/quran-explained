<<<VERSE 3:23>>>
The verse asks, "Have you not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures?" (3:23) They claimed to follow revelation, yet when invited to let Allah’s Book settle a dispute, some turned away. The explanations connect this refusal to Jews who knew that the Torah supported the truth but did not want its judgment when it went against their preferences.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Have you not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures?"** (3:23) addresses Muhammad ﷺ and points to people who received the Torah, the Injīl (Gospel), and other scripture. The verse challenges those who claim to follow their books while refusing what those books require. One explanation says their scripture contained knowledge that confirmed Muhammad’s prophethood. Another centers the passage on the Torah’s judgment in a legal dispute.

**“Yet when they are invited to the Book of Allah to settle their disputes”** means that they were called to let revelation decide between them. Al-Suddī reports that al-Nuʿmān ibn Awfā asked the Prophet ﷺ to let their rabbis judge a dispute. The Prophet ﷺ instead called for Allah’s Book to be the judge, but they refused.

Another report, related from Ibn ʿAbbās by Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and ʿIkrimah, says that the Prophet ﷺ entered a Jewish place of study and called its people to Allah. Nuʿaym ibn ʿAmr and al-Ḥārith ibn Zayd asked what religion he followed. He answered that he followed the religion of Ibrāhīm (Abraham). They claimed that Ibrāhīm had been Jewish. The Prophet ﷺ suggested that they consult the Torah to decide the matter, but they refused.

Al-Kalbī connects the dispute to a case of adultery at Khaybar. The Jews asked the Prophet ﷺ for a ruling. He gave the judgment that was found in their own Torah, but they were angry and some refused to accept it. One account says they were called to apply the punishment of stoning for a married man and woman. One account says the fuller discussion of this legal ruling appears later in al-Māʾidah. These reports differ in their immediate occasion, but they share the same point: the people who claimed the scripture turned away when its ruling challenged them.

**“some of them turn away heedlessly”** describes refusal, not ignorance. They did not want to accept the judgment and its consequences. The verse censures people who ask for a ruling but reject it when it does not match their desires.

An inward reading says that the call itself tests people whose refusal is already known to Allah. Their turning away reflects deprivation from the beauty of divine disclosure. The Prophet ﷺ is told to remain patient with the task given to him and to recognize the state of those who refuse.

### Why did they refuse the Book’s judgment?

The accounts give more than one setting, including a dispute about religious identity and a legal case. In each, the Torah is offered as the standard, yet some of those who claim to follow it resist its ruling. The issue is not a lack of access to scripture. It is their unwillingness to accept a judgment that conflicts with their preference.

### In short

- The verse criticizes people who claimed to follow scripture but turned away when asked to accept its judgment.
- Reports connect the refusal with disputes over Abraham’s religion and with a legal ruling found in the Torah.
- The inward reading treats their refusal as a test and calls the Prophet ﷺ to patience.

<<<VERSE 3:24>>>
The verse explains their confidence in turning away: “The Fire will not touch us except for a few days” (3:24). They believed punishment would be brief, then treated themselves as safe. The explanations describe this claim as something they had invented without authority. Their false assurance deceived them and made them bold in rejecting truth.

### Phrase by phrase

**“This is because they say”** introduces the claim. **“The Fire will not touch us except for a few days”** (3:24) refers to their belief that any punishment would be short. One explanation says they believed they would remain in the Fire for seven days, one day for each thousand years of earthly life. Another says they tied the punishment to the forty days their ancestors worshipped the calf. A further account describes them saying they would be punished for only a limited number of days. These are reported versions of the claim, not a statement that the claim is true.

Some held that they were protected because they were descendants of prophets. Others said the punishment would be delayed. The explanations present these beliefs as invented assurances that had no authority from Allah. Their false expectation made them feel secure even while rejecting revelation.

**“They have been deceived in their faith by their wishful lying”** means that the claim itself misled them. They were not relying on a promise Allah had made. They had fabricated the idea that punishment would soon end and then allowed it to reassure them. An inward reading says that they were drawn gradually into greater false confidence, until they presumed they could set the limits of their own punishment. They would eventually discover that their affliction would be increased, not reduced.

### In short

- The people described claimed that punishment in the Fire would last only a short time.
- Explanations give different versions of the number of days and the reason they expected brief punishment.
- The verse calls this belief a deception they invented, not a promise from Allah.

<<<VERSE 3:25>>>
The verse asks the people who believed they were safe to consider the Day “about which there is no doubt” (3:25). Allah will gather them, repay each soul in full for what it has done, and wrong no one. The imagined limit they placed on punishment cannot change the reality of the final judgment.

### Phrase by phrase

**“But how ˹horrible˺ will it be”** expresses the gravity of what awaits them. **“when We gather them together on the Day”** points to the Day of Resurrection. **“about which there is no doubt”** (3:25) affirms that it is certain to come. The people who denied revelation and rejected the messengers will be gathered with everyone else, and their claims about escaping punishment will be exposed. One explanation asks what their condition will be after their lies about Allah, their rejection of messengers, and their killing of prophets and those who called for righteousness are brought to account.

An inward reading describes the Day as so imposing that the minds of those who turned away will be bewildered and their claims cut off. They will face accounting, reproach (ʿitāb), punishment, and the absence of honor and approval (ʿadam al-ikrām). Another mystical distinction says that the gathering of all people for judgment is in the future, while the gathering of those who love Allah occurs in the present moment.

**“when every soul will be paid in full for what it has done”** means that each person receives the full consequence of good and evil actions. Nothing is overlooked, and no one can substitute an invented promise for the account of what they actually did.

**“and none will be wronged”** means that good deeds will not be reduced and sins will not be increased. Allah’s judgment is just. Every person receives what is due without injustice.

### What does full recompense mean?

The verse leaves no room for an assumed exception. A person’s own actions will be weighed, and no one can claim that a small set of days or an inherited status makes the final account irrelevant. Justice means that good is not reduced and evil is not increased.

### In short

- All people will be gathered for a certain Day of Resurrection.
- Each soul will receive the full recompense for its deeds.
- No good deed will be reduced, no sin increased, and no one will be wronged.

<<<VERSE 3:26>>>
The verse teaches the Prophet ﷺ and the believers to pray, “O Allah! Lord over all authorities!” Allah alone gives and removes authority, honors and disgraces whom He wills, and holds all good in His hands. The prayer reminds believers that the rise and fall of nations is not decided by human power alone. It also answers those who mocked the promise that the Muslim community would reach lands then ruled by powerful empires.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ ‘O Allah! Lord over all authorities!’”** teaches praise, gratitude, reliance, and trust. The word Allāhumma means “O Allah.” An inward explanation notes that the final mīm serves in place of the ordinary word used to call someone. The phrase recognizes Allah as the sole owner of sovereignty, with no partner, helper, companion, or rival in rule or creation.

The verse was revealed in a setting where the Prophet ﷺ promised that the Byzantine and Persian lands would come under Muslim rule. The Jews and hypocrites mocked the promise. One report places the words after the conquest of Makkah, when people doubted that Muhammad ﷺ and his community could overcome Persia and Byzantium. Another relates them to al-Ahzab, the Battle of the Confederates, called Khandaq, the Trench, in historical accounts. This followed the disbelievers’ defeats at Badr and Uhud and the growing strength of the Muslims. Arabia’s disbelievers, Jews, and Christians joined together to attack Madinah, and the Prophet’s Companions dug a trench.

In one account, ten men were assigned forty hand-spans of trench, which was several miles long, deep, and wide. Another report describes ten men assigned a forty-cubit stretch. The Companions worked without stopping and on hungry stomachs, with little time even for pressing needs. The Prophet ﷺ worked beside them, and the task was described as difficult even for a modern army engineering team. When the diggers met a hard rock they could not break, Salmān al-Fārisī went to ask him for help. The Prophet ﷺ struck the rock three times. Each strike produced a flash of light. In the first, he said he saw the palaces of Ḥīrah in Persia. In the second, he saw the red palaces of the Byzantines. In the third, he saw the palaces of Ṣanʿāʾ in Yemen. He gave the Companions the news that their ummah, the Muslim community, would prevail in those lands. When the hypocrites heard this, they mocked the hungry workers who were unsure of their own safety but spoke of conquering distant powers. The verse came as an answer and a prayer affirming that all authority belongs to Allah.

Reports about the trench are attributed to al-Baihaqi, Abu Nu'aim, and Ibn Khuzaimah. Another account from Ibn ʿAbbās and Anas ibn Mālik connects the verse to the promise of Persia and Byzantium after Makkah was conquered. A report from Qatādah says that the Prophet ﷺ asked Allah to grant those kingdoms to his community.

**“You give authority to whoever You please and remove it from who You please”** means that sovereignty belongs to Allah. He gives it to one person or people and takes it from others. Human rulers cannot secure it against His will. The Qur’an rejects the assumption that people can decide who should receive Allah’s mercy and prophethood. It asks whether they would distribute their Lord’s mercy themselves (43:31, 43:32), and says Allah knows best where to place His message (6:124). People are also favored differently in this life (17:21).

One explanation says this verse foretells that prophethood would pass from the Children of Israel to an Arab, Qurayshi, Makkan, unlettered prophet, Muhammad ﷺ, the final messenger to humankind and jinn. Allah gave him knowledge of His law and of past and future events. The Muslim community would spread east and west, including Persia, Byzantium, Syria, Iraq, and Yemen, and the religion and law he brought would prevail over other religions and laws. This is presented as Allah’s gift, not as a power the community could claim for itself.

**“You honour whoever You please and disgrace whoever You please”** continues the same point. Allah can raise the poor and humble and remove honor from rulers. One report applies the humiliation to ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl and the people of Persia and Byzantium. Another connects it to Quraysh, who asked why the Qur’an had not been sent to a great man from Makkah or Ṭāʾif (43:31). The answer is that Allah distributes His mercy and chooses whom He wills, not those whom people consider most powerful.

**“all good is in Your Hands”** says that Allah’s acts are good in the wider order of creation. The verse names both giving and removing authority, and both honoring and disgracing, yet it says that all good is in Allah’s hands. An explanation says an event can be unwelcome to one person or nation but serve a wider good for others. The loss of one group may become a gain for another. The poet al-Mutanabbī expressed this as one group’s calamity becoming another’s benefit. The statement does not deny that a person may experience something painful. It places that experience within Allah’s complete wisdom and knowledge.

**“Surely You ˹alone˺ are Most Capable of everything”** confirms that no person can resist Allah’s decision. He can raise the weak to honor and remove power from kings. He can fulfill what appears impossible to people.

### What does the inward reading add?

An allusive reading treats the prayer as a lesson in how to praise Allah. He has no partner in His essence, kingdom, creation, or power. Created beings find honor by humbling themselves before Him. Allah raises whom He wills by drawing them near, enabling them to witness His oneness, and occupying their hearts with Him. He lowers whom He wills by leaving them to distraction and the clamor of the lower self. In this reading, true honor is nearness to Allah, and true disgrace is estrangement from Him. The honor or ʿizz of the servant lies in humility and self-effacement (maḥw). Lasting life (baqā) comes through the surrender and passing away (fanā) of the lower self. The first gleams of intimacy, called ṭawāliʿ, raise a person toward Allah. The night visitors of the lower self, ṭawāriq, pull the person toward distraction.

### In short

- Allah alone gives and removes authority and bestows honor or disgrace.
- The verse is linked to promises about the Muslim community’s future and to the mocking of those promises.
- The trench account describes the Prophet ﷺ receiving visions of Persian, Byzantine, and Yemeni palaces after striking a rock.
- Even painful changes in power are within Allah’s wisdom, and true honor is nearness to Him.

<<<VERSE 3:27>>>
The verse says, “You cause the night to pass into the day and the day into the night.” It presents the changing night and day, life emerging from what appears dead, and the provision Allah gives as signs of His complete power. The same power that governs the world also guides human hearts and changes their states.

### Phrase by phrase

**“You cause the night to pass into the day and the day into the night”** means that Allah lengthens one and shortens the other. The change unfolds across the seasons as the balance between daylight and darkness shifts. The power that governs the heavens and the cycles of time belongs to Allah alone.

**“You bring forth the living from the dead and the dead from the living”** has several examples. A chick comes from an egg, and an egg from a bird. A plant grows from a seed, and a seed comes from a plant. A human being develops from a drop, and a drop comes from a living person. Dates emerge from their seeds and seeds from dates. The phrase can also describe spiritual change: Allah can bring a believer from a disbelieving family or a disbeliever from a believing one. He brought Ibrāhīm (Abraham) from people who worshipped idols, while the son of Nūḥ (Noah) remained an unbeliever. A scholar’s child may be ignorant, and a child of an unlearned person may become learned. Allah can change the state of faith or knowledge as He wills.

Another inward reading treats night passing into day as the light of tawhid, the oneness of Allah, overcoming the darkness of the lower self. Day passing into night can describe the heart’s light being eclipsed by the lower self. The living emerging from the dead can represent hope and renewed closeness after spiritual apathy. The dead emerging from the living can describe a heart that becomes barren after rejecting faith. A verse about those who did not believe the first time is cited in this connection (6:110).

**“And You provide for whoever You will without limit”** means that Allah grants provision in abundance to some and restricts it for others, with wisdom and justice. Provision can mean wealth and sustenance, given without measure or strain. The verse describes Allah’s power to provide beyond what people can calculate. A messenger of truth may lose a home or material support, yet Allah can provide special help without measure.

A report about the merit of these verses says that a person who recites Al-Fātiḥah, Ayat al-Kursi, verse 3:18, and the present verses after each surah will be given a home in Paradise, placed in the Sacred Enclosure, receive mercy seventy times each day, have seventy needs fulfilled, and be protected from every envier and enemy. The report is presented as a promise of Allah’s care and aid.

### How does this power reach the human soul?

The verse moves from the cycles of the heavens to changes in faith, knowledge, and provision. Allah can bring a believer out of an unbelieving family, or allow a believer’s child to reject faith. He can make a learned person from someone uneducated or reverse that state. These examples show that neither belief, knowledge, nor provision lies beyond His control. The inward reading also treats the alternation of night and day as changes in the heart, from light and closeness to darkness and distance.

### In short

- Allah controls the passage of night and day and all changes in the natural world.
- He brings life from what appears dead and can also change spiritual states, bringing a believer from disbelief or allowing a person to turn away.
- He provides without limit and gives support beyond what people can calculate.
