<<<VERSE 3:23>>>
The verse asks, "Have you not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures? Yet when they are invited to the Book of Allah to settle their disputes, some of them turn away heedlessly". They claim to follow the Torah and the Injīl, yet when they are called to refer to those books, with what Allah commanded them about following Muhammad ﷺ inside them, a party turns away with aversion. The censure falls on defiance, and three different occasions are given for the call they refused.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Have you not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures?"**: the Jews addressed already possessed Torah knowledge testifying to Muhammad's prophethood. Their share, a mere portion of the Book, had reached them.

**"Yet when they are invited to the Book of Allah to settle their disputes"**: they are called to the Book of Allah so that it may judge between them in what they differ about. The verb "being called" marks their state at the moment of refusal.

**"some of them turn away heedlessly"**: a party of them turned away, opposed to accepting its rulings, ignoring the judgement because it did not agree with their desires. People who claim to follow the Torah ought to have accepted the Torah's own judgement without hesitation.

### The stoning case at Khaybar

One occasion named is the rejection of stoning by Banū Qurayẓah and Banū al-Naḍīr among the people of Khaybar. They had been given knowledge of what stands in the Torah about stoning and other matters, and they invoked the Scripture of Allah, the Qur'an, to judge between them about stoning the married man and the married woman who had committed adultery at Khaybar, as their own Scripture prescribes. Then a faction from Banū Qurayẓah and the people of Khaybar turned away from the judgement and denied it.

The case is filled out a step further. Two Jews fornicated, and the Jews asked the Prophet ﷺ to adjudicate. He ruled that they be stoned, and they refused. The Torah was brought and consulted, and the same verdict was found in it, so the two were stoned, and the Jews became wrathful. Al-Kalbī put the occasion the same way, as the ruling on fornication at Khaybar about which the Jews asked the Prophet ﷺ, with the fuller account deferred to Surah al-Ma'idah.

### Two more occasions of the same refusal

As-Suddī reports an exchange after the call to Islam. Al-Nuʿmān ibn Awfā proposed rabbis as arbiters. The Prophet ﷺ chose Allah's Scripture instead, and the offer was rejected. Then revelation followed.

Ibn ʿAbbās gave a classroom scene through Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and ʿIkrimah. Inside a Jewish study house, the Prophet ﷺ invited its occupants to Allah. Nuʿaym ibn ʿAmr joined al-Ḥārith ibn Zayd in asking his religion. He answered, "The religion of Abraham." They claimed Abraham as a Jew. "Then let Torah judge between us," he replied. Refusal again brought revelation.

### A call made to people known not to answer

Inwardly the caller is being tested. You must summon people whose refusal We already know. Remain patient under the command and understand their unfortunate condition. Deprived beforehand by Our will, irāda, of beautiful self-disclosure, ḥusn al-tajallī, they become ahl al-tawallī, a people defined by turning from response, ijāba.

The refusal is puzzling on the surface, because recognition should have been easy. Allah's guidance is one and the same as what He gave various prophets at various times in various languages, and the same guidance came to the Final Prophet in the form of the Qur'an. The Qur'an's only difference from the former scriptures lies in having purified God's religion of human interpolations. People who already believe in revealed scriptures should find this call familiar. Those who deny it do so because the call does not seem serious beside their own self-styled concepts, by which they feel protected from the fire of Hell. Even if they fail to recognise the truth, they think, their salvation is not at risk. When the divine scales are set in place, they will learn how deluded that wishful thinking was.

### In short

- People given a share of the Book refused the Book's own judgement in the stoning case at Khaybar, refused the Torah as judge over the religion of Ibrāhīm (Abraham), and refused any judge but their rabbis.
- The refusal came from scholars and leaders who claimed to follow the Torah, and it fell because the judgement did not agree with their desires.
- The caller is told to be patient with people who were always going to turn away.

<<<VERSE 3:24>>>
The verse gives the reason in their own words: "This is because they say, “The Fire will not touch us except for a few days.” They have been deceived in their faith by their wishful lying". What made them bold enough to challenge and defy the truth was a doctrine they invented for themselves, that punishment would be short, measured and over. The verse names the doctrine a lie, and calls it the thing that deceived them in their religion.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is because they say, “The Fire will not touch us except for a few days.”"**: the turning away and rejection happened because they said the Fire would not touch them in the Hereafter except for a certain number of days. They thought the fire of Hell would touch them only briefly, after which they would be forgiven and enter Paradise.

**"They have been deceived in their faith by their wishful lying"**: the lies they used to invent deluded them in their religion. The self-deception kept them on a false creed: they believed the Fire would touch them for a few days for their errors, but they had invented the notion themselves, and Allah gave them no authority to support the claim.

### How the few days were counted

Two counts are given, and they do not agree. One group held that they would be punished for seven days, days of the Hereafter, each day equalling a thousand years of this life, as punishment for the days in which their forefathers worshipped the calf. That makes the few days a direct measure: a thousand years of earthly reckoning for each day of fire. Allah had already exposed the same claim in Surah Al-Baqarah.

The other count makes it forty days. They contended that their forefathers had worshipped the calf for forty days, so punishment, if any, would run for the same period only, forty days, after which it would end. The deception is also explained as a claim that punishment would simply be postponed, and as a firm adherence to their religion, Judaism, held as a guarantee.

Behind both counts stood other forged securities. Self-deception was fed by the belief that they were the progeny of prophets and would therefore escape the fire of Hell altogether. Whatever form the guarantee took, the Fire was made small, and a small fire could be risked.

### Being drawn on by degrees

Inwardly the warning sharpens further. In this world they were drawn onward gradually, istidrāj, until deciding salvation and a reduced punishment for themselves felt legitimate. What passes for understanding is only falsehood. Error mistakes its own pronouncement for a binding determination, ḥukm. The doubled affliction will eventually expose it.

The blindness has a history in this surah too. The Qur'an's call did not arrive as an alien voice. The same guidance had been given to prophets before, and the Qur'an only cleared God's religion of human additions. A person carrying the earlier scriptures should recognise the call quickly. The obstacle is the private concept of safety each person has built for himself. Viewed from inside that concept, denying the truth carries no danger, because the fire has already been shortened by decree. The scales of the next world are what expose the mistake.

### In short

- The Fire was said to touch them only seven days measured at a thousand years each, or forty days matching the worship of the calf, and neither count came from Allah.
- The invented shortness of punishment made denial safe in their own eyes, which is the deception the verse names.
- Being left to build on the lie is itself a punishment, drawn on by degrees until the affliction is doubled.

<<<VERSE 3:25>>>
Then the shortened fire meets its Day: "But how ˹horrible˺ will it be when We gather them together on the Day about which there is no doubt—when every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged". The opening question looks past denial to consequence. What state awaits people who lied against Allah, rejected messengers, murdered prophets and killed scholars devoted to righteousness and resistance to evil? Allah will interrogate them about every part.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But how ˹horrible˺ will it be when We gather them together"**: how will they act, and what will their state be, when We bring them all together after they die. What will their predicament be, and how much will they regret?

**"on the Day about which there is no doubt"**: the Day of Resurrection, which will certainly come. There is no uncertainty in it.

**"when every soul will be paid in full for what it has done"**: every soul, righteous or sinner, from the People of the Scripture and from others, will receive the requital of what it earned, of good or of evil.

**"and none will be wronged"**: no good deed will be diminished and no evil deed increased. There is no oppression by reduction of good actions or addition to evil ones.

### The grandeur of that gathering

The address is called remarkable for the intensity and grandeur it conveys. Perplexity will seize the intellects of deniers. Inmost selves will reel, pretensions will be severed and hearts pulled from concealment while souls climb toward collarbones. Waiting beyond that shock stand accounting and censure, al-ḥisāb wa-l-ʿitāb, torment and punishment, al-ʿadhāb wa-l-ʿiqāb, with neither honour nor approval granted, ʿadam al-ikrām wa-l-ījāb.

There is a second resurrection set beside this one. For disbelievers, resurrection waits until Gathering Day. Lovers, by contrast, begin rising in the present moment. To explain that fully would take too long, but the contrast fixes the verse. For one company the gathering is entirely future and entirely dreadful. For another, its first rising has already begun in life.

### In short

- On a Day with no doubt in it, every soul is paid exactly what it earned, with no good diminished and no evil added.
- The gathering strips away the invented guarantees of the last verse and replaces them with reckoning and censure.
- The lovers have a resurrection that begins in the present moment, but for those who turned away the gathering is dread alone.

<<<VERSE 3:26>>>
The Prophet ﷺ is taught a prayer that answers every boast about power: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O Allah! Lord over all authorities! You give authority to whoever You please and remove it from who You please; You honour whoever You please and disgrace who You please—all good is in Your Hands. Surely You ˹alone˺ are Most Capable of everything.”" The verse was revealed while believers were digging a trench on empty stomachs and being mocked for imagining empires, and it opens with a command to praise, thank, rely and trust.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O Allah! Lord over all authorities!"**: say this while praising your Lord, thanking Him, relying on Him in all matters and trusting in Him. All sovereignty is Yours. Allāhumma means "O God", yā Allāh, the final mīm standing in place of the vocative particle yā.

**"You give authority to whoever You please and remove it from who You please"**: You are the Giver and You are the Taker. Your will is what occurs, and whatever You do not will does not occur. You decide concerning Your creation and do as You will.

**"You honour whoever You please and disgrace who You please"**: You exalt whom You will and abase whom You will, by giving the kingdom or by seizing it, according to His wisdom and justice.

**"all good is in Your Hands. Surely You ˹alone˺ are Most Capable of everything"**: in Your hand and power is good, and exaltation, abasement, sovereignty, spoils, triumph and power are all within that power. He is able to do all things, in every one of those fields.

### Gratitude for a transfer of history

Gratitude is the verse's first lesson for Messenger and ummah alike. Prophethood moved away from the Children of Israel toward an unlettered Arab of Quraysh from Makkah, the final messenger to humans and jinn. He received earlier prophets' finest qualities plus knowledge of Allah, His law, history and the Hereafter beyond any predecessor's endowment. Through his community, moreover, faith would span the world's eastern and western reaches, with his religion and law dominating older laws and faiths.

It also refutes those who thought they could decide matters for Allah. "And they say: 'Why is not this Qur'an sent down to some great man of the two towns (Makkah and Ta'if)?'" (43:31). Allah refuted them: "Is it they who would portion out the Mercy of your Lord?" (43:32), which means that Allah decides for His creation what He wills, without resistance or hindrance from anyone. He has perfect wisdom and unequivocal proof in it, and He gives prophethood to whom He wills. "Allah knows best with whom to place His Message" (6:124). "See how We prefer one above another (in this world)" (17:21).

### The trench, the rock and the three lights

The background is the battle of the Confederates, al-Ahzab, called the Trench, Khandaq. The disbelievers of Makkah had suffered repeated defeat at Badr and failure at Uhud, and Muslims were growing stronger. Disbelievers of Arabia, Jews and Christians joined in a united front to attack Madinah in a conclusive battle aimed at eradicating Islam and Muslims. The Prophet ﷺ decided in consultation with his Companions to dig a trench around parts of Madinah to block an unhindered attack.

Narrations in al-Baihaqi, Abu Nu'aim and Ibn Khuzaimah say the digging was allotted in stretches of forty hand-spans to groups of ten men. The trench ran several miles and was fairly deep and wide, so the enemy could not cross it, and it had to be finished in the shortest time. The Companions worked non-stop on hungry stomachs, finding it hard to leave even for pressing needs. The Prophet ﷺ took part in the digging like everybody else. In one stretch the diggers struck a huge rock they could not break, and they sent Salman al-Farisi to report it and ask for instructions. A named work party anchors the same episode: Salmān, Ḥudhayfah ibn al-Yamān, al-Nuʿmān ibn Muqarrin al-Muzānī, ʿAmd ibn ʿAwf and six Helpers shared forty cubits. Flint defeated their steel and would not break.

The Prophet ﷺ came to the spot, took the pickaxe in his hands and struck. The rock shattered into pieces, and from it rose a streak of light that lit the area far and wide. He said, "In this light, I see the palaces and buildings of Hirah in the country of Persia." He struck again and a second beam rose: "In this light, I was shown the red palaces and buildings of the Byzantinians." At the third strike, "In this I was shown the great palaces of San'a in Yemen." Then he said, "I share the good news given by Jibra'il with you that my community of Muslims will prevail over all these countries."

The hypocrites of Madinah ridiculed what they heard. Here were people scared of the enemy, digging trenches without food or rest, unsure of their own lives, yet dreaming of running over Persia, the Byzantine empire and Yemen. A conquest-of-Mecca setting is also reported by Ibn ʿAbbās and Anas ibn Malik. Following the city, Muhammad ﷺ promised Persia and Byzantium to his nation. Hypocrites and Jews scoffed at the distance between aspiration and power, asking how Mecca and Medina could possibly lead to such mighty kingdoms. Qatādah adds that the Prophet ﷺ had requested those kingdoms for his nation. Other settings place ʿAbdullāh Ibn Ubayy Ibn Salūl questioning sovereignty after Mecca fell, or Quraysh challenging a prophet to match Persia's silk-couched king with visible dominion.

Appearing as a prayer, the verse turns this back on the mockers. Perfect power is displayed in the rise and fall of nations, and the verse hints that the prophecy will come to pass and Persia and the Byzantine lands will fall to Muslims. The enemies had never learned the lesson of earlier wielders of power. They judged events from the material angle, while all power and government sits in the hand of Allah, with whom lie honour and disgrace. He is not prevented from seating the poor and meek on thrones or wresting power from kings, so it should not be difficult for Him to choose ragged believers digging trenches to rule Persia, Syria, Iraq and Yemen. In the direct reading, sovereignty goes to Muhammad ﷺ and his followers and is withdrawn from the people of Persia and Byzantium, Muhammad ﷺ is exalted, and ʿAbdullāh Ibn Ubayy Ibn Salūl, Persia and Byzantium are abased.

### Why the verse names good and not evil

After giving and taking, honouring and disgracing, the text says only that good is in His hand. One might expect evil, sharr, to be paired with it, but the choice points to something real. A calamity for one person or nation may be unwelcome to them and yet, from the wider angle of the whole community of nations, not be evil at all. The Arab poet al-Mutanabbī put it briefly: the calamities of one group are the gains of another. Looked at from its relation to the Creator of the universe and in the perspective of the totality of experience, nothing is really evil in itself. In the wisdom, power and consideration of the created universe as a whole, everything can be called khair, good, as the verse puts it.

### How the Real teaches praise

Allāhumma itself teaches praise. Describe Me, says the sense, with the exaltation due to Me: Master of the kingdom. No essence partner, kingdom sharer, creating competitor, helper or companion stands beside You, so your kingship becomes known. Human kings expect humility from subjects. Seized earthly kingdoms belonged to people arrogant toward Him. Before the Real, self-abasement or tadhallul beautifies creatures, tajammul. Effacing themselves in Him, maḥw, supplies honour and might, ʿizz, while surviving in Him through annihilation, fanāʾ, supplies their remaining, baqāʾ.

Then exaltation and abasement unfold in pairs. Forsaking abases, while Your essential might exalts. Guidance into witnessing You and declaring Your oneness (tawḥīd) exalts. Denial and deprivation abase. Nearness brings good fortune, while estrangement follows turning away. Intimacy lifts one person whereas distraction from You lowers another. Exaltation can erase the lower self's controlling elements, just as abasement surrenders someone to its clamour. Intimacy sends out gleaming risings, Ṭawāliʿ, while the lower self sends night visitors, Ṭawāriq. Expansion through You, basṭ, contrasts with constriction away from You, qabḍ.

### In short

- Sovereignty is given and torn away, honour granted and disgrace imposed, all by Allah, who cannot be resisted (43:31-32, 6:124, 17:21).
- At the Trench the Prophet ﷺ split a rock and saw Hirah, the red palaces of the Byzantines and Sanʿa in three lights, and hypocrites who laughed at hungry men digging were answered with this prayer.
- "Good is in Your hand" because what ruins one nation can be another's gain, and nothing, seen whole, falls outside His good disposal.

<<<VERSE 3:27>>>
The prayer moves from thrones to the sky: "You cause the night to pass into the day and the day into the night. You bring forth the living from the dead and the dead from the living. And You provide for whoever You will without limit.”" The same hand that gives kingdoms lengthens and shortens every day of the year, takes life out of lifeless things and lifeless things out of life, and sets provision where no calculation can follow it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You cause the night to pass into the day and the day into the night"**: lengths are transferred between darkness and light until balance is reached, then transferred again until one dominates. Seasons (spring, summer, autumn and winter) enact the exchange year-round. Sun and moon, indeed all spaces and heavenly bodies, operate by His command.

**"You bring forth the living from the dead and the dead from the living"**: plant yields seed and seed yields plant. Egg produces chicken and chicken produces egg. Date and date-stone exchange places, and believer and disbeliever can each emerge from the other.

**"And You provide for whoever You will without limit.”"**: You give whomever You will innumerable wealth and provision while depriving others, in wisdom and justice. Provision is given without reckoning, without straitness or inconvenience, and in abundance to whomever He chooses.

### The wider meanings of living and dead

Examples multiply in both directions. Eggs arise from birds and beasts, sperm arises from people, and fruit and dried grains arise from trees and plants. Opposite them, sperm can become a human infant, an egg a chick and a seed a tree. A living chicken may emerge from a dead egg while a dead grain leaves a living spike.

Broadly, life and death also contrast scholar with ignorant, complete with imperfect, and Muslim believer with kāfir disbeliever. Absolute power governs spiritual changes alongside physical ones. A staunch disbeliever can become a perfect believer, an ignorant person a scholar and a kāfir a Muslim. Reversal also occurs: believers can slide into disbelief while rustic people rise into learning. Ibrāhīm (Abraham) came from an idol-worshipper, but the son of Nūḥ (Noah) stayed unbelieving. Literacy does not run predictably either, since scholars, ʿālim, may father illiterate sons and illiterate men scholarly sons. The demonstration descends deliberately from cosmos to human soul.

### The provision that comes without measure

In the vaster universe Allah performs the alternation of darkness and light daily and on a colossal scale, bringing life from dead elements and causing living things to die. Nothing would be surprising if the same power appeared among human beings. Honour and power also lie in His hand. Those treated as insignificant by the leaders of the age can be raised to the highest eminence, but only those who regard such gifts as purely His gifts deserve them. The most undeserving are those who treat rank as their own acquisition.

A truth-preacher can lose home and livelihood yet remain directly guarded by Allah and specially sustained. Ordinary provision tracks effort. A messenger of truth receives without measure.

### The merit attached to these two verses

Al-Baghawī transmits a promise attached to a prescribed recitation combining Sūrat al-Fātihah, Āyat al-Kursī, the witness verse (3:18) and this pair on sovereignty (26-27). Whoever recites them receives Paradise as an abode, a place in the Sacred Enclosure, mercy seventy times daily, seventy needs fulfilled, safety from envier and enemy, and prevailing over both. Witness and sovereignty are paired because both rest on disposal belonging to Allah alone.

### Night, day and the states of the heart

Cosmic alternation maps onto the heart. Night yields to day when tawḥīd's light takes authority and erases the lower self's dark traces. Day sinks into night when hearts' suns seem eclipsed and morning indefinitely delayed. Life leaves death when listlessness vanishes, union's pledge recovers youth and heartfelt promises freshen. Death leaves life when discontent's tree sprouts thorns, hopeless people find no goodness or fragrance, and eyes and hearts fall into the confusion named in "just as they did not believe in it the first time" (6:110).

Provision beyond reckoning spares labour, kadd, exertion, jahd, a sweating brow, jabīn, and a toiling right hand, yamīn. Night becomes repose and refreshment, rāḥa, and daylight becomes delight and joy, bahja. Special gifts, karāmāt, fill the hours, while moments become approaches, qurubāt. Tongue, lisān, cannot inventory the favours, nor explanation, bayān, sound their depth. Kingship receives its closing lesson here. Anyone convinced he owns a kingdom wakes rudely when told that Allah seizes kingdoms at will, sees extinction threatening his rule and learns that self-abasement preserves it better than pompous delusion. In the truest sense a king is undistracted from witnessing the real King.

### In short

- Night and day exchange their lengths through the seasons, life and death exchange places in seed, egg, sperm and grain, and provision falls without measure.
- Living and dead also mean scholar and ignorant, believer and disbeliever: Allah brought Ibrāhīm out of an idol-worshipper and left the son of Nūḥ in unbelief.
- A hadith joins this verse to the witness verse of 3:18 with promises of Paradise, mercy seventy times a day, and protection from envier and enemy.
