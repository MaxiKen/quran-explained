<<<VERSE 5:4>>>
After the prohibitions comes a positive answer about lawful food: "They ask you, ˹O Prophet,˺ what is permissible for them ˹to eat˺." Allah permits what is good and allows game caught by properly trained hunting animals. The catch counts as the hunter's when the animal has been sent under the required conditions and holds the prey for him, rather than taking it for itself.

The permission is not a release from religious responsibility. The hunter must mention Allah's name, observe the conditions and remain mindful of Him. The knowledge used to train an animal is itself something Allah has taught human beings.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They ask you, ˹O Prophet,˺ what is permissible for them ˹to eat˺."** The Companions ask the Messenger Muhammad ﷺ about lawful food, particularly game obtained through trained dogs or falcons. One account names the questioners as Zayd Ibn Muhalhil al-Ta'i and 'Adiyy Ibn Hatim al-Ta'i. Another account connects the question with an order concerning dogs, described below.

**"Say, “What is good and lawful."** At-tayyibat means good, pure things. The previous verse excluded foods harmful to the body, religion or both, apart from necessity. This answer states the positive principle. Muqatil includes what Muslims are allowed to eat and provision obtained through lawful means. The goodness of food therefore concerns more than its appeal to appetite.

**"Also what is caught by your hunting animals and birds of prey"** includes trained dogs and hunting birds such as falcons. The explanation also mentions wild cats and cheetahs. Beasts use their teeth or claws, and birds use their talons. Their catch can be lawful even if it dies before the hunter arrives, provided the conditions are fulfilled.

**"which you have trained as instructed by Allah."** The animal must be taught to respond to the hunter, pursue the prey when sent and leave it when restrained. Its skill is not simply uncontrolled predatory instinct. Allah has given people the knowledge through which they can train it to work on their behalf.

**"So eat what they catch for you"** makes the purpose of the catch decisive. The animal is to hold the game for its master rather than eat it for itself. If it begins consuming the prey, that conduct contradicts the required training. If the hunter reaches the quarry alive, he must slaughter it properly.

**"but mention the Name of Allah over it ˹first˺.”** The principal explanation is to say bismillah, in Allah's name, when sending the animal after game. The instruction also applies to releasing an arrow. Other explanations relate the words to slaughtering the game or to mentioning Allah when eating. They are not reasons to omit the invocation when the hunt begins.

**"And be mindful of Allah. Surely Allah is swift in reckoning"** commands obedience to the rules of permission and prohibition. The hunter must avoid carrion and other unlawful food. Allah's reckoning is swift, and the warning is also explained through the severity of His punishment. A lawful pastime must not become a reason to neglect prayer or another obligation.

### What makes food good and pure?

The broad principle concerns things wholesome in themselves and suited to human nature, without a defect introduced through harm or a breach of Islamic law. But human preference alone cannot reliably apply that principle in every case. The general rule therefore comes with revealed instructions explaining its application.

Surah Al-A'raf describes the Prophet ﷺ as permitting good things and forbidding what is filthy. Muqatil's explanation includes lawful earnings as well as the things acquired. An otherwise permitted food does not make an unlawful way of obtaining it acceptable.

Ibn Abi Hatim reports that Az-Zuhri was asked about drinking urine for treatment. He answered that it was not among at-tayyibat. That response is retained as his answer to the particular question, rather than expanded into a medical discussion not given here.

Training also explains why an animal's hunt can count for a human being. It has learned to act for its master. In that limited sense, it becomes a substitute for him in obtaining the prey. The permission is tied to this learned behaviour, not simply to the fact that a person owns a predatory animal.

### What does jawarih mean?

Al-jawarih refers to creatures that hunt or obtain prey. One explanation derives it from jarh in the sense of earning. An Arabic expression can describe someone as obtaining something good for his family, or describe a caretaker as the person who earns for another. The Qur'an uses the related word for what people do or earn during the day (6:60).

Another meaning of the word concerns wounding. This becomes important in the legal requirement that the animal should kill through a wound rather than by the force of a crushing blow. The two explanations draw attention to different aspects of the hunt: obtaining something for the master and the manner in which it is killed.

Ibn 'Abbas includes trained birds as well as trained beasts. Ibn Abi Hatim records the explanation, with similar statements from Makhul, Khaythamah, Yahya bin Abi Kathir, Tawus and Mujahid. The majority view among the Companions, their followers and the Imams includes dogs and falcons within the permission.

Ibn Jarir reports that 'Adi bin Hatim asked the Messenger ﷺ about a falcon's catch and was told to eat what it caught for him. He also records Ibn 'Umar allowing the catch of trained birds if the hunter reaches it before the bird eats from it. The majority explanation treats trained birds like trained dogs, since both catch through claws or similar hunting means.

### How is proper training recognised?

A trained dog goes after the quarry when released and brings or keeps it for its owner without starting to eat it. A falcon must respond when called back, even during pursuit. These behaviours show that the creature hunts under direction rather than independently for its own meal.

The description also includes returning after being sent, stopping when restrained and holding the catch without eating it. One legal gloss gives three successful demonstrations as the minimum by which this training is established. A single claim that an animal is trained is not the whole test.

Mukallibin describes the trainers or those releasing trained creatures after prey. It derives from language about training dogs, but its use extends to other hunting animals. The related explanation of taklib includes training and sending after game. The grammatical form describes the people's condition as trainers, while the following expression describes their teaching the animals. The sense of sending is also reported in the discussion through al-Qurtubi.

Training must govern the particular hunt. If the dog eats the prey or the falcon refuses to return when called, the animal has acted against the behaviour on which the permission depends. The dead game is then not treated as something held for the hunter under these conditions.

### Which conditions make the dead catch lawful?

Four conditions are set out in the explanation. The hunting creature must be properly trained. The hunter must deliberately send it, rather than merely discover a catch it pursued on its own. It must retain the prey for him instead of eating it. And Allah's name must be pronounced when it is released.

When these conditions are met, the game may be eaten even if it dies before reaching the hunter. A separate slaughter is not needed for an animal lawfully killed in this manner. If the conditions are absent, the catch is not lawful unless it can still be properly slaughtered while alive.

Abu Hanifah adds the requirement that the hunting animal actually wound its prey. This is connected with the sense of jawarih as creatures that wound. An explanation likewise excludes game killed merely by the weight of the beast's blow rather than by its claws. Killing through a permitted hunting wound is distinguished from beating or crushing to death.

The concession concerns wild game outside human possession and control. Once a wild animal has been captured, it must be properly slaughtered. Its being a wild species does not allow a person to treat a captive animal as though it were still inaccessible quarry.

### What did the Prophet ﷺ tell 'Adi bin Hatim?

The Two Sahihs record 'Adi's question about sending trained hunting dogs while mentioning Allah's name. The Prophet ﷺ allowed him to eat what they caught, even if they killed the game. But he excluded the case in which another dog joined the hunt. Allah's name had been invoked over the dog that was sent, not over that other dog.

Another wording tells the hunter to slaughter the prey if he reaches it alive. If he finds it dead and his dog has not eaten from it, he may eat it, because the dog's lawful hunting has fulfilled the slaughter. If the dog has eaten, the Prophet ﷺ warned that it may have caught the prey for itself rather than for the hunter.

'Adi also asked about the mi'rad, an implement used in hunting. The answer distinguished a wound from a fatal blow. What its sharp edge killed could be eaten. What its broad surface killed could not, because the animal had been beaten to death. The hunting permission does not remove the distinction between lawful killing and carrion.

Reports in the Sahihs of Al-Bukhari and Muslim also place a properly released arrow, accompanied by Allah's name, alongside the catch of a trained hunting creature. The means differ, but the hunt remains subject to the stated religious conditions.

### When is Allah's name mentioned?

Abu Tha'labah relates in the Two Sahihs that the Prophet ﷺ instructed the hunter to mention Allah's name when sending the dog or shooting the arrow. The invocation belongs to the act that begins the pursuit. It is not postponed simply because the prey will only be reached later.

Ibn 'Abbas likewise explains the verse as saying Allah's name when sending the hunting animal. His reported explanation adds that forgetfulness brings no blame. That qualification should not be confused with deliberately treating the invocation as unnecessary.

Another explanation concerns eating. The Prophet ﷺ taught his stepson 'Umar bin Abu Salamah to begin with Allah's name, use his right hand and eat from the part before him. The Two Sahihs record the instruction. Mentioning Allah at the meal belongs to the same grateful recognition, even though it is a different occasion from releasing the hunter's animal.

Al-Bukhari records 'A'ishah describing a question about meat brought by recent converts. The recipients did not know whether Allah's name had been said over it. The Prophet ﷺ told them to mention Allah's name and eat. This report concerns that uncertainty about the meat brought to them. It is not presented as permission to ignore known unlawful slaughter.

### What question followed the order about dogs?

Abu Rafi reports that the Prophet ﷺ ordered dogs to be killed. People then asked what use of those animals remained lawful to them. The verse permitting the catch of trained hunting creatures is related as the answer. Al-Hakim records the narration in his Sahih.

The fuller account begins with Jibril (Gabriel) coming to the Prophet ﷺ but not entering, despite being given permission. When the Prophet ﷺ came out and asked why, Jibril explained that they did not enter a house containing a picture or a dog. A search found a puppy in one of the rooms.

Abu Rafi says he was then instructed to kill dogs in Madinah. On reaching al-Awali, he found a woman keeping one for protection. He initially spared it out of compassion. When he reported this, he was told to return and kill it, and did so. A group then asked the Prophet ﷺ what was lawful for them concerning this class of animals. He did not answer immediately, and the verse was revealed.

This is the event as reported in the account attached to the verse. Its point here is the question about permitted hunting use. It does not supply a general instruction for readers to harm animals or an account of every later ruling concerning dogs.

### Why end a hunting ruling with accountability?

Permission must be used within obedience. Hunting for enjoyment does not excuse missing salah, the prescribed prayer, or neglecting another necessary duty. Taqwa means keeping Allah in mind when applying both the permission and its limits.

The verse therefore directs attention beyond skill, success and appetite. Allah taught human beings the knowledge they use, gives the permission under which they eat and will call them to account. Gratitude for the gift includes refusing to turn it into a reason for disobedience.

### In short

- Allah permits good food and game caught on the hunter's behalf by properly trained beasts and birds.
- The creature must be deliberately sent, under the stated invocation and training conditions. Its eating the quarry or hunting independently changes the ruling.
- Game reached alive must be slaughtered. Dead game is permitted only when the conditions for lawful hunting are met, not when it died from a crushing blow or an uncertain joint hunt.
- Allah's name is said when sending the hunting creature or arrow, and also remembered when eating. The reports preserve their particular qualifications about forgetfulness and uncertainty.
- Hunting must not displace prayer or other duties. The permission remains under Allah's swift reckoning.
