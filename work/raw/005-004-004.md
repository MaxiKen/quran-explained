<<<VERSE 5:4>>>
This verse answers a question that the believers had asked the Prophet ﷺ. They asked what they were allowed to eat. The verse replies: "They ask you, ˹O Prophet,˺ what is permissible for them ˹to eat˺." The answer is that what is good and lawful is permitted, and so is what trained hunting animals and birds of prey catch for their owners, provided the owner mentions the Name of Allah when he sends them out. The believer is to eat that game, having first mentioned the Name of Allah over it. The verse ends with a reminder: "And be mindful of Allah. Surely Allah is swift in reckoning." The two halves belong together. The verse first widens the permission, and then it places a condition on it, which is that the believer must remember Allah both at the beginning of the hunt and at the meal.

The verse follows the list of forbidden food in 5:3, and the phrase about what has been earned points back to 6:60, where Allah says He knows what you have earned by day. Having named what is forbidden, the Qur'an now names what is lawful, so that the believer knows both sides of the rule. The material says the question was about hunting with trained dogs and falcons, and this verse is the answer. A report in the material says the question was asked when Gabriel, who had been granted permission to enter, would not enter a house that had a picture or a dog in it, and a puppy was found there. Abu Rafi', who was sent to kill the dogs in Madinah, spared a woman's dog and was told to kill it, and then people asked what was lawful for them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They ask you, ˹O Prophet,˺ what is permissible for them ˹to eat˺."** The question was put to the Prophet ﷺ. The material names the people who asked as Zayd bin Muhalhil al-Ta'i and 'Adi bin Hatim al-Ta'i, both of whom were fishermen. Another report tells of a time when the Prophet ﷺ had ordered the killing of dogs in Madinah. People then asked what was lawful for them from a community whose dogs they had been ordered to kill. This report is given as the occasion for the verse.

**"Say, 'What is good and lawful.'"** The Prophet ﷺ is told to answer. The material explains the word at-tayyibat as "the good things". Muqatil said it covers everything Muslims are allowed and every kind of provision that is earned legally. Al-Qurtubi and the commentary tradition take it in the same way, as what is clean, sound and suitable. The material notes that in Surat al-A'raf the Prophet ﷺ is described as allowing good things and forbidding filthy things. Az-Zuhri was asked about drinking urine for medicine and said that this is not one of the good things. Ibn Abi Hatim also recorded this statement.

**"Also what is caught by your hunting animals and birds of prey which you have trained as instructed by Allah."** The Arabic word for these animals is jawarih, which comes from a root meaning to earn or to gain. The Arabs would say that someone earned something good for his family in this sense. The material says the word covers trained hunting dogs, falcons, wild cats and any beast or bird that can be trained to hunt. Ibn 'Abbas said this, and Khaythamah, Tawus, Mujahid, Makhul and Yahya bin Abi Kathir were reported to agree. The majority of the Companions, the next generation and the imams held that game caught by trained birds of prey is lawful in the same way as game caught by trained dogs, because birds of prey catch with their claws, just as dogs catch with their teeth. Ibn 'Umar, in a report of Ibn Jarir, held that a falcon's catch is lawful only if the hunter reaches it alive, and otherwise not. 'Adi bin Hatim asked the Prophet ﷺ about game caught by a falcon. He said: whatever the falcon catches for you, eat it.

The material gives a clear test for a trained animal. It must catch the game with its claws or teeth, not kill it by the force of a blow alone. The training is shown by the animal's habit of bringing the prey to its master and not eating it. Ibn Jarir and others hold that the animal must hold the prey until the owner arrives. The lawfulness of the game is then agreed among the scholars, even if the animal has killed it.

**"training them as hounds"** describes the training. The material explains that the animal must be sent after the game, and when it catches the game it holds it for its owner and does not take a bite for itself. The material lists four conditions. First, the dog or falcon must be taught and disciplined, so that when it is released it catches the game and brings it back, and does not eat it. A falcon must return when called, even while chasing. If the dog eats the prey, or the falcon does not return, the game is no longer the owner's and may not be eaten. Second, the animal must be released by the owner's choice, not left to chase game on its own. The word used for training, mukallibin, is from the verb meaning to set a dog against game, and the commentary tradition takes it as a sending out. Third, the animal must bring the game to its owner and not eat it, as the phrase "what they hold for you" shows. Fourth, the owner must say the name of Allah when he releases the animal. When these conditions are met, game that is dead when it reaches the hunter may be eaten without a second slaughter. If they are not met, the game must be slaughtered before it can be eaten. One scholar, Abu Hanifah, adds a fifth condition, which is that the animal must have wounded the prey. The material says the word jawarih hints at this, since the word also means animals that wound. The material adds that wild game caught and held in captivity is not lawful without slaughter.

The material cites the report of 'Adi bin Hatim in the two Sahih collections. He asked the Prophet ﷺ whether he could send his hunting dog with the name of Allah, and the Prophet ﷺ said that if the dog catches the game and it is still alive, he must slaughter it. If the game is dead, and the dog did not eat from it, the hunter may eat it, because the dog has completed its slaughter. If the dog eats from the game, the hunter must not eat it, because the Prophet ﷺ said he fears that the dog caught it for itself. In another report, the Prophet ﷺ said that if the dog catches with the sharp edge the game may be eaten, and if it is killed by the broad side it may not. The material also records that if another dog joins the hunt, and the hunter did not mention the name of Allah over that second dog, the game may not be eaten.

**"so eat of what they catch for you, but pronounce the Name of Allah over it"** is the central command. The material says it means: when you send the animal out, say "In the Name of Allah". Ibn 'Abbas said that if you forget, there is no harm. Abu Tha'labah, in the two Sahih collections, reported that the Prophet ﷺ told the hunter to mention Allah's Name when he sends his dog, and also when he shoots an arrow. The verse is also read as a command to say the Name of Allah when eating, and the material cites the report in the two Sahih collections in which the Prophet ﷺ taught his stepson 'Umar bin Abi Salamah to say the Name of Allah, to eat with his right hand, and to eat from the part of the dish nearest to him. 'A'ishah, in a report collected by al-Bukhari, said that some people recently converted from disbelief brought meat, and the believers did not know whether the Name of Allah had been mentioned over it. The Prophet ﷺ said: mention the Name of Allah over it and eat it.

**"And be mindful of Allah."** The verse returns to taqwā. The material explains that this means keeping the commands of Allah and staying away from what He forbids. The believer who hunts with a trained animal, or who eats its catch, is still under the law of Allah.

**"Surely Allah is swift in reckoning."** The verse ends with a warning that Allah reckons quickly. One reading explains that the phrase is a reminder that His punishment is severe for those who neglect what He has commanded.

### Why the trained animal is lawful

The material explains the reasoning. A trained hunting animal is lawful because it hunts on behalf of its master. It has learned to act according to human purpose, and so it stands in the place of its master. The material says that things that nature regards as clean and well formed, and animals whose nature fits the human nature, are lawful, provided no defect has appeared in them for reasons of health or of the law. The human mind cannot always apply this principle correctly, so the Qur'an gives the principle together with its right application.

### The hunt and the prayer

The material ends with a warning. Hunting with a trained animal is permitted, but it is not allowed to neglect the prayer and other duties for the sake of the pleasure of chasing game.

### In short

- The verse answers a question about what is lawful to eat, and it says that the good things are lawful.
- Game caught by trained dogs or birds of prey is lawful, provided the animal was trained, was released by its owner, did not eat the catch, and was sent out with the Name of Allah.
- The believer must say the Name of Allah when sending the animal and again when eating.
- The verse ends with a call to be mindful of Allah, because Allah reckons swiftly.
