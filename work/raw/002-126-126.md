<<<VERSE 2:126>>>
Verse 126 records the prayer Ibrāhīm (Abraham) made for the place where he had left his family, and Allah's answer to it. "And ˹remember˺ when Abraham said, “My Lord, make this city ˹of Mecca˺ secure and provide fruits to its people—those among them who believe in Allah and the Last Day.” He answered, “As for those who disbelieve, I will let them enjoy themselves for a little while, then I will condemn them to the torment of the Fire. What an evil destination!”" Ibrāhīm always carried out Allah's commands without losing a moment and was ready for every kind of sacrifice in His way, of worldly goods, of wife and children, of his own likes and dislikes. Yet love for one's family is not only a natural urge but a divine command, and here he prays for the well-being of his family in this world as much as in the next.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember˺ when Abraham said, “My Lord"**. Remember, Prophet, when Ibrāhīm prayed to his Lord. The prayer opens with the word Rabb, which means the One who gives nurture, and this teaches the proper way of praying, for the address itself draws Allah's mercy and grace on the one who prays. In ancient times Arabia was the most arid land on earth, its sands and bare rocks unfit for any cultivation and wholly exposed to attack from outside. Four thousand years ago Ibrāhīm was commanded to take his family into an uncultivable valley in the part of Arabia known as the Ḥijāz and settle them there, and he bowed to the will of Allah without the slightest hesitation. Having done so, he prayed in effect: my Lord, I have done as I was commanded. Now hear the call of Your servant.

**"make this city ˹of Mecca˺ secure"**. Free of fear and disturbance, a place where no one meets with evil: safe from slaughter and plunder, from calamities, and from the domination of unbelievers. The first thing he asked was that the barren desert where he had left his family under divine command become a city, so that his wife and son would not feel lonely and their daily needs would be easily met. The same prayer occurs in the chapter named after him: "My Lord! Make this city ˹of Mecca˺ secure, and keep me and my children away from the worship of idols" (14:35). There, however, the Arabic says "the city", al-balad, while here it says "a city", baladan. The difference probably arises because the prayer reported here was made when the place was still a desert and Ibrāhīm wished a city to grow in it, before the Kaʿbah was built, whereas the prayer in the other chapter was made a second time after the House had been built and people lived around it. Near the end of that chapter he says, "All praise is for Allah who has blessed me with Ishmael and Isaac in my old age" (14:39), which shows that the second prayer came after the birth of Isḥāq (Isaac), who was born thirteen years after Ismāʿīl (Ishmael).

**"and provide fruits to its people"**. Different kinds of fruit, all types of crops, bestowed on those who live there. The surrounding land could grow nothing, and before this the place had neither vegetation nor water.

**"those among them who believe in Allah and the Last Day"**. Those who believe in the resurrection after death. The phrase stands in place of "its people" and singles out the believers for mention in the request, in keeping with Allah's earlier words that His covenant does not reach the wrongdoers. This is an example of Ibrāhīm's rectitude. The opening of his prayer for the prosperity of the people of Makkah seems to include unbelievers as much as believers. But when he had earlier prayed for all his descendants without distinction, asking for the position of imāmah (leadership), Allah had answered that the prayer would be granted for the faithful but not for the unjust, the mushrikūn, those who associate partners with Him. The fear of Allah and the anxiety to obey Him completely were so deeply rooted in the heart of His Friend that even while praying for his people's prosperity the earlier proviso came to his mind, and he at once added that he was asking only for the believers. Ibn Abī Ḥātim narrated that Ibn ʿAbbās explained it in these terms: Ibrāhīm asked Allah to grant sustenance to the believers only.

**"He answered, “As for those who disbelieve, I will let them enjoy themselves for a little while"**. Allah was pleased with his rectitude and told him that worldly prosperity would be given to the disbelievers too. Ibn Jarīr related that Ubayy ibn Kaʿb said these are Allah's words, not Ibrāhīm's, and Mujāhid and ʿIkrimah explained it the same way. In Ibn ʿAbbās's account Allah revealed: I will also provide for the disbelievers just as I provide for the believers. Would I create something and not sustain and provide for it? I shall allow the disbelievers a little delight and then force them to the torment of the Fire, and what an evil destination. Ibn ʿAbbās then recited: "We provide both the former and the latter from the bounty of your Lord. And the bounty of your Lord can never be withheld" (17:20). Ibn Marduwyah recorded this, and similar statements from ʿIkrimah and Mujāhid. The verb is read both umtiʿuhu and umattiʿuhu, and the enjoyment is sustenance in this life, a little, for the length of his life. The Qur'an says the same elsewhere: those who fabricate lies against Allah will never succeed, theirs is a brief enjoyment in this world, then their return is to Allah and He will make them taste the severe punishment for their disbelief (10:69-70). The Prophet ﷺ is told not to let their disbelief grieve him, for "We allow them enjoyment for a little while, then ˹in time˺ We will force them into a harsh torment" (31:23-24). And were it not that people might all become one community of disbelievers, Allah would have given the homes of those who disbelieve silver roofs and stairways, gates and thrones and ornaments of gold, "Yet all this is no more than a ˹fleeting˺ enjoyment in this worldly life. ˹But˺ the Hereafter with your Lord is ˹only˺ for those mindful ˹of Him˺" (43:33-35).

**"then I will condemn them to the torment of the Fire. What an evil destination!”"**. After the delight the disbeliever enjoyed in this life, I will force him and drive him to the Fire in the Hereafter, with no escape, and an evil place of return it is. In the next world only the faithful will prosper, while the disbelievers receive nothing more than the punishment due to them. The verse shows that Allah gives the disbelievers respite and then seizes them in a manner that befits His greatness and power: "Many are the societies whose end We delayed while they did wrong, then seized them. And to Me is the final return" (22:48). The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record that no one is more patient than Allah on hearing abuse: people attribute a son to Him while He grants them sustenance and health. The Ṣaḥīḥ also records that Allah gives the unjust person respite until, when He seizes him, He never lets him go, and the Prophet ﷺ then recited, "Such is the ˹crushing˺ grip of your Lord when He seizes the societies entrenched in wrongdoing. Indeed, His grip is ˹terribly˺ painful and severe" (11:102).

### How the prayer for security was answered

Allah granted the request. He made Makkah a sanctuary in which no human blood is shed, no injustice is done to anyone, no prey is hunted, and no part of which is ever deserted. He said, "Whoever enters it should be safe" (3:97), and "Have they not seen how We have made ˹Mecca˺ a safe haven, whereas people ˹all˺ around them are snatched away?" (29:67). Muslim recorded that Jābir said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade anyone to carry weapons in Makkah, and the hadiths forbidding fighting in the sacred area are given under the previous verse. Makkah became a thriving city, which Muslims now visit as pilgrims in their millions every year, and no unbeliever has ever conquered it or held sway over it. The Qur'an itself tells the story of the People of the Elephant (Aṣḥāb al-Fīl), who were destroyed for venturing to invade it. The city has also been safe from slaughter and plunder. Even before Islam, the Arabs of the age of ignorance, for all their unbelief and idolatry, respected the Kaʿbah and its surroundings as a matter of creed. Vengeful as they were, they would never take revenge while the enemy remained inside the Ḥaram. The people of Makkah were respected throughout Arabia, and the caravans trading between Makkah and Syria or Yemen were never molested. Allah gave security even to the birds and animals of the Ḥaram by forbidding all hunting there, and birds and animals visibly feel safe inside it and are not frightened of people. Islam emphasised and enforced this sanctity still further. As for the bloodshed that took place in the Ḥaram at the hands of al-Ḥajjāj ibn Yūsuf or the Qarāmiṭah, that was the work of people who called themselves Muslims, not an invasion by unbelievers. If a man sets fire to his own house it does not disprove the rule that the house is secure against outsiders. Such incidents have been very rare since the days of Ibrāhīm, and the dreadful fate of those who dared to pollute the House of Allah is known. In short, Allah made the city so secure in answer to this prayer that even the Dajjāl (the Antichrist) will have no power to enter it. The land of the Ḥijāz has remained an abode of peace and plenty to the present day.

### How the prayer for fruits was answered

Although the land around Makkah cannot be cultivated, Allah in answer to the prayer made the neighbouring city of Ṭāʾif very fertile and rich in fruit, which began to come to Makkah. According to certain Israelite traditions, Ṭāʾif was originally situated in Syria and was moved to its present place by the archangel Jibrāʾīl (Gabriel) at Allah's command. Fruits also arrived with travellers from as far away as Syria.

There is wisdom in the form of the prayer. Ibrāhīm did not ask for the land of Makkah to be made fertile. He asked, in effect, that fruits be brought to it from elsewhere. He probably intended that his descendants should not become absorbed in agriculture, because his purpose in founding the settlement, as his other prayer says, was that his people should keep up the prayer. He wanted the essential work of his descendants to be guarding the House of Allah and devoting themselves to worship. Otherwise he could as easily have prayed for Makkah itself to be fertile, and Allah would have granted it. The point is clearer still in the word thamarāt, the plural of thamarah, fruit, which appears again where Allah asks whether He has not established for them "a safe haven ˹in Mecca˺ to which fruits of all kinds are brought as a provision from Us" (28:57). If the fruits of trees are meant, the word "brought" is enough to show that Allah did not promise to grow them in Makkah but to send them from other places. But that verse does not speak of the fruits of all kinds of trees. It speaks of the fruits of all kinds of things, and in ordinary usage "fruit" means the product of a thing or an activity. The word therefore covers not only the produce of trees but the products of every craft and industry, everything needed to sustain human life. Anyone can see that Makkah has neither agriculture nor industry, yet enjoys the benefits of both as much as any prosperous city in the world.

### Why this prayer was granted

In the devotional reading, the request was answered because it was not adulterated by the servant's worldly concern. Ibrāhīm was not asking for his own portion (ḥaẓẓ) but for the right (ḥaqq) of his Lord. Because he observed the required courtesy in seeking provision, asking it for those who believed, it was granted to them, and to those who did not believe as well. When, on the other hand, he spoke about leadership and asked for his offspring without permission, that request was refused and he was told that the covenant does not reach the wrongdoers.

### What the verse asks of a believer

The believer's whole life should be an act of worship. In whatever far corner of the world he lives he should remember at all times that one day he must return to his Lord. He should harm no one, and should treat the earth he lives on as a place of worship, kept free of all pollution. Physically in this world, his heart and mind should stay fixed on Allah, and his life should revolve around Him alone in submission. True religion makes demands on everyone, and sometimes it even requires that one's children be taken to a place as arid as Ibrāhīm's valley. Whatever it entails, a person should faithfully carry out Allah's commands first, and only then pray that his efforts be made to succeed. It may well be that Allah will make springs gush from a dry desert and lush crops rise from barren land. Whatever splendour one is granted in this life does not of itself mean that Allah considers one fit to lead mankind. Everything given to a person is a means of testing him, and no one is exempt from Allah's trial. Leadership means being chosen by Allah to represent Him among the nations, and it belongs only to those He has found fit to represent His religion on earth.

### In short

- Ibrāhīm prayed that the barren valley become a secure city with fruits for its believing people, and Allah granted both: Makkah became a sanctuary no unbeliever has ever conquered (3:97, 29:67), and its fruits come to it from Ṭāʾif and beyond (28:57).
- Remembering that leadership had been denied to the wrongdoers among his offspring, he limited his request to the believers. Allah replied that He provides for the disbelievers too (17:20), but their enjoyment is brief and ends in the Fire (10:69-70, 31:23-24, 43:33-35, 22:48, 11:102).
- Ibrāhīm asked for fruits to be brought rather than for fertile land, so that his descendants would be keepers of the House rather than farmers.
- The prayer was granted because it sought Allah's right and not the servant's own portion.
