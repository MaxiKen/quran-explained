<<<VERSE 7:136>>>
After sign upon sign, Pharaoh's people still rebelled and overstepped every limit, so the final punishment came: "So We inflicted punishment upon them, drowning them in the sea for denying Our signs and being heedless of them". The sixth punishment is called rijz in the Qur'an, meaning a plague or pestilence. More than seventy thousand Egyptians are reported to have died in it. They were relieved of it through Mūsā's (Moses') prayer, and again broke their promise as before. When they showed no sign of understanding, the last punishment, death, finally seized them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So We inflicted punishment upon them"**: Allah took retribution from them once and for all, when the time appointed for their destruction came.

**"drowning them in the sea"**: the word al-yamm means salt water, the sea. Allah drowned them in the sea that Mūsā had parted by Allah's power, through which he and the children of Israel had passed. Leaving behind their lands, houses and possessions, Pharaoh and his soldiers went into the sea in pursuit of Mūsā and his people. When all of them were inside, the sea closed over them and every one of them drowned.

**"for denying Our signs"**: because they rejected Allah's nine signs and disbelieved in them, turning away from a truth beyond any doubt.

**"and being heedless of them"**: they paid no attention to them and did not reflect on them.

### Why do people reject plain signs?

Punishment fell on the peoples to whom prophets were sent because they denied Allah's signs (āyāt). Those who believed in the prophets, on the other hand, earned special help from Allah. They stayed patient under every kind of provocation from those around them, and remained steadfast on the path of Allah.

The "signs" are the arguments that establish the truth as true. But a person's ego and pride can keep him from accepting them. Instead of attending fully to the arguments, he turns his attention to the preacher who presents them. He cannot answer the arguments. But the one presenting them is not a figure established by history, nor is he wrapped in worldly glory, so it is easier to mock the presenter than his arguments. He feels that to listen to the arguments would be to place himself beneath the preacher. So, to keep his pride and his sense of superiority over the caller (dāʿī), he will not concede that the truth stands above him.

Here lies the real test for every human being. In this world Allah shows Himself indirectly, through signs and reason. In the Hereafter He will show His Being directly, and then no one will dare to deny Him. But only faith that comes from a person discovering the truth while Allah is still unseen has any value.

### In short

- After the plague and their broken promises, Pharaoh and his army chased Mūsā into the parted sea and were drowned.
- They perished because they denied Allah's signs and gave them no thought.
- Pride makes people mock the messenger rather than answer his arguments, and faith counts only while Allah is unseen.

<<<VERSE 7:137>>>
The previous verses told of Allah's warnings to Pharaoh's people. This verse tells of their shameful end and of the success of the Israelites: "And ˹so˺ We made the oppressed people successors of the eastern and western lands, which We had showered with blessings. ˹In this way˺ the noble Word of your Lord was fulfilled for the Children of Israel for what they had endured. And We destroyed what Pharaoh and his people constructed and what they established". Allah gave the people who were thought weak, the children of Israel, the eastern and western parts of the land as an inheritance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹so˺ We made the oppressed people"**: literally "the people who were considered weak", those humiliated by Pharaoh and his people and oppressed through bondage. The verse does not say "the people who were weak", but "those who were taken to be weak". This implies that those who have Allah's support can never be truly weak, however they may look. In the end people come to see that they are not weak, because all honour and dignity belong to Allah.

**"successors of the eastern and western lands"**: literally "We made them inherit". The word "inheritance" shows that, as a son is the rightful heir to his father's land and wealth, the Israelites became the rightful heirs to the land and wealth of Pharaoh's people. "East" and "west" are plural in the Arabic, perhaps for the different points of sunrise and sunset in winter and summer. All the experts in tafsīr say "the land" means the land of Egypt and Syria, which came under Israelite rule after Pharaoh's people and the Amalekites were destroyed. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and Qatādah said it refers to ash-Shām, Greater Syria. One explanation lists the land of Jerusalem, Palestine, Jordan and Egypt, and another the lands of the Levant.

**"which We had showered with blessings."** This describes the land, which Allah blessed with water and trees, and with the best crops and fruits. The Qur'an calls Syria the land of barakah, blessing, and several traditions call Egypt a land of blessing too. The caliph ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb called the Nile the prince of rivers. ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar said that Egypt holds nine of the ten parts of blessing, and the tenth is spread over the rest of the earth. Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ records this.

**"˹In this way˺ the noble Word of your Lord was fulfilled for the Children of Israel"**: the fair word of your Lord came true, O Messenger. One explanation says it was His promise to reward them with Paradise or with victory. Mujāhid and Ibn Jarīr said it refers to this promise:

> "But it was Our Will to favour those who were oppressed in the land, making them models ˹of faith˺ as well as successors and to establish them in the land; and through them show Pharaoh, Hamân, and their soldiers ˹the fulfilment of˺ what they feared" (28:5-6).

**"for what they had endured."** Because they bore patiently the persecution of their enemy, or, as some say, because they held firmly to their religion.

**"And We destroyed what Pharaoh and his people constructed"**: Allah destroyed what Pharaoh and his people had produced, their farms, buildings, castles and towns. "What they constructed" may also mean their evil schemes against Mūsā (Moses).

**"and what they established"**: Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid said it means what they built. The verb can be read yaʿrishūn or yaʿrushūn, what they raised up. It covers the tall buildings and palaces they put up, and the trees and vines they planted.

### Which promise was fulfilled?

The verse shows how the promise of Allah and His Messenger came true, as it always does. The promise may be the one Mūsā made to his people earlier in this surah: "Perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy and make you successors in the land" (7:129). Or it may be Allah's promise to the Israelites in Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ, quoted above. In fact the two are one, since Mūsā's promise came from Allah's.

### Why was patience rewarded?

This favour to the people of Israel was the reward for their patience, as the verse says, "for what they had endured". It quietly suggests that any people or person who follows the same example, at any time and in any place, will receive the same reward. When Mūsā promised them rule over the land, he had insisted that steadfastness, patience and seeking Allah's help were the only key to success.

Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said the verse teaches that when someone is not strong enough to defend himself against an enemy, the best road to success is patience. When a wronged person tries to take revenge himself by wronging his opponent, Allah leaves him to manage his own affairs, and he may succeed or fail. But when a person asks Allah for help against what others inflict on him and stays patient, Allah opens the door of success for him.

It is not right to claim that the Israelites showed no patience because, when Mūsā urged them to be patient, they complained that they had been persecuted before and after he came. First, their patience under constant persecution by Pharaoh and his people is a proven fact. Second, their words may not have been a complaint at all, but simply an expression of grief.

### Does the promise extend to this Ummah?

As Allah fulfilled His promise of rule over the land to the Israelites, He made a similar promise to the community of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in Sūrat an-Nūr: "Allah has promised those of you who believe and do good that He will certainly make them successors in the land, as He did with those before them" (24:55). As the Israelites saw Allah's promise fulfilled in their rule over the land, the Muslim Ummah saw it fulfilled in its rule over most of the earth, as Rūḥ al-Bayān notes.

### What comes next?

With Pharaoh and his people gone, the story now turns to the Israelites. The coming verses tell of their triumph and good fortune, and then of the insolence they showed in spite of Allah's many blessings. These verses also console the Prophet ﷺ in his grief at the stubbornness of the disbelievers, by showing how the earlier prophets were troubled by their own peoples.

### In short

- Allah made the Israelites, once thought weak, heirs to the blessed lands of Egypt and Syria. Those with Allah's support are never truly weak.
- This fulfilled the promise made through Mūsā and in Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ, as a reward for their patience.
- Patience and seeking Allah's help bring success, while taking revenge on one's own leaves a person to himself.
- Allah destroyed Pharaoh's buildings and gardens, and made a similar promise of succession to this Ummah.
