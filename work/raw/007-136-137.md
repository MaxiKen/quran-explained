<<<VERSE 7:136>>>
The end of the people of Pharaoh comes in one sentence. "So We inflicted punishment upon them, drowning them in the sea for denying Our signs and being heedless of them"

### Phrase by phrase

**"So We inflicted punishment upon them"** says that the retribution was taken from them once and for all. They had rebelled and gone past every limit, and the signs had been set in front of them one after another without any effect on them. When the appointed time for their destruction came, the punishment was released on them and there was nothing left to hold it back.

**"drowning them in the sea"** names the punishment. The sea spoken of here is the salt water, and it was the same water that Musa split by the power of Allah, so that he and the Children of Israel walked through it dry. The people of Pharaoh followed them into the parted water. When the whole of the army was inside, the sea closed over them and every one of them drowned.

Before that end, one sign had come to them that was heavier than the rest. The sixth punishment is given in the Book as the rijz, which means plague or pestilence, and the number of the Egyptians who died in it is put at more than seventy thousand. They were relieved of it through the prayer of Musa, and they broke the promise they had made as they had broken every promise before it. When it was clear that nothing would bring them to their senses, the final punishment arrived, which was death. They left their lands and their houses and everything they owned behind them and rode out after Musa and his people, and they were drowned in the sea.

**"for denying Our signs and being heedless of them"** gives the reason. They called the nine signs a lie, and they were heedless, in the sense that they never turned the matter over in their minds. The signs of Allah had passed in front of their eyes and they took no lesson from any of them.

### What this end tells the reader

The passage closes the account of a people who were given more warning than any other people in the surah, since they had nine signs of the clearest kind, and the door of return was held open for them through every one. What was left at the end was the sea, and what the verse establishes is that the punishment follows on the denial and not on anything else. The people themselves had read their own troubles as a bad omen of the messenger, and the last of their troubles was the answer to the prayer he had made against them.

### In short

- The punishment was taken from them once and for all at the appointed time.
- The sea was the same water Musa had split by the power of Allah, and it closed over the people of Pharaoh while the Israelites watched.
- Before it came the plague, in which more than seventy thousand of them died, and the promise they made at its lifting was broken like the others.
- Their lands and their possessions were left behind when they rode out after the messenger.
- The reason given is the denial of the signs and the heedlessness that kept them from reflecting on them.

<<<VERSE 7:137>>>
The other side of the same event is now told, and the promise that stood behind it. "And ˹so˺ We made the oppressed people successors of the eastern and western lands, which We had showered with blessings. ˹In this way˺ the noble Word of your Lord was fulfilled for the Children of Israel for what they had endured. And We destroyed what Pharaoh and his people constructed and what they established"

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹so˺ We made the oppressed people successors"** names the people who came out of the whole affair with the land. The words describe them as those who were taken to be weak, and not as the weak. The difference carries a point of its own. A people whose support is Allah are never weak in the real sense. Their outward condition may look otherwise for a time, and the end of the matter makes the truth of it plain to everyone. Dignity and honour belong to Allah, and He gives them to whom He wills.

**"successors of the eastern and western lands"** describes what they were given. The word used here for succession is the word for inheritance. The choice is deliberate, since an heir takes what comes to him by right, and the point of it is that the Israelites were the real inheritors of the land and the wealth of the people of Pharaoh, just as a son is the inheritor of what his father left. The east and the west are put in the plural, which may be a way of naming the points of sunrise and sunset as they shift through the winter and the summer. Verse 137 of this surah opens on that sentence. Experts in the explanation of the Book say that the land meant is the land of Jerusalem, Palestine, Jordan and Egypt, and that these came under the rule of the Israelites after the people of Pharaoh and the Amalekites were destroyed.

**"which We had showered with blessings"** names what the land itself was. The blessing spoken of is barakah, and the blessing was in the water and in what grew, the fields and the trees. The Book calls Syria a land of blessing, and Egypt is named as blessed in a number of traditions. The Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab called the Nile the prince of all rivers. Abdullah ibn Umar said that Egypt carries nine parts of blessing out of ten, and that the tenth part was divided out over the whole earth. The report is carried in Al-Bahr Al-Muhit.

So the verse says in the end that a people who had been counted weak and abject were given the rule of lands that had been held by men of arrogance, and that the promise of Allah and His messengers came out true, as it always does.

**"In this way the noble Word of your Lord was fulfilled for the Children of Israel for what they had endured"** names the promise and the reason.

The promise meant here is either the one Musa had spoken to his people in verse 129, that their Lord would destroy their enemy and make them successors in the land, or the promise given to the Israelites in Surat Al-Qasas, where Allah says that He wished to show favour to those who were oppressed in the land, and to make them leaders and inheritors, and to establish them in the land, and to let Pharaoh and Haman and their hosts receive from them the very thing they feared (28:5-6). The two are one and the same in substance, since the promise Musa gave to his people was drawn from the promise of Allah.

The favour was the reward of their patience, which is why the verse says that it was for what they had endured. Patience in distress is what brought it, and there is an indication inside that, that any people or any person who follows the same example, at any time and in any place, will receive the same reward.

**What patience does when a person is outmatched.** Al-Hasan Al-Basri drew a lesson out of the verse. When a person is not strong enough to defend himself against an enemy, the way to success is to hold steady. If a man who has been wronged tries to take his revenge with his own hand, and does wrong to his opponent in return, Allah leaves him to himself, and the affair ends in success or in failure as it happens. When he asks Allah for help against the wrong done to him instead, and waits, Allah opens the door of success on him.

**The same promise for the community of Muhammad ﷺ.** The promise of rule over the land was fulfilled for the Israelites, and a promise of the same kind was given to the community of the Prophet ﷺ in Surat Al-Nur, where the Book says that Allah has promised those of them who believe and do good deeds that He will give them succession in the earth as He gave it to those before them (24:55). As the Israelites saw the word of Allah come true in their sovereignty over the land, the Muslim ummah saw it come true in sovereignty over a great part of the earth. That reading is recorded in Ruh al-Bayan.

**A note on the complaint of the Israelites.** It is not right to say that the Israelites did not observe patience, since when Musa asked them to hold steady they said that they had been persecuted before he came to them and after he came. Their patience under the constant persecution of the people of Pharaoh is a proven fact, and the words in that place may have been an expression of grief rather than a complaint.

**"And We destroyed what Pharaoh and his people constructed and what they established"** returns to the other side. What they produced is what they built, such as their agriculture and their buildings, and in one reading the words cover the designs and the schemes they worked out against Musa. What they established is what they raised high, which is read as their mansions and their trees. Ibn Jarir, Mujahid and Ibn Abbas said that the word means what they built. Qatadah said of the whole sentence that what they produced came to nothing, and what they had raised was left standing empty. Al-Hasan Al-Basri said of the same words that the buildings and the works of a people who oppose a messenger do not outlast the messenger. So the verse ends with the houses and the crops of the great ones of the earth brought down, and the tents of the people who had been counted weak set up in the land.

### Why the passage is placed here

The events told up to this point have been about the destruction of Pharaoh and his people. The verses that follow describe the victory and the success of the Israelites, and then their insolence in the face of everything Allah had given them. The whole passage also carries a comfort for the Prophet ﷺ in the face of the stubbornness of the people who refused him, since it shows how the messengers before him were treated by their own peoples, and what came of it in the end.

### What the denial of signs comes to

The punishment fell on the communities that denied the signs of God, while the believers were given a special help from Him on account of their patience under the provocations of the people around them, and their standing firm on the path of God.

The word for signs carries the sense of the arguments that establish the truth as truth. What holds a person back from them is his own pride. Instead of giving his full attention to the argument in front of him, he turns his attention to the man who is presenting it. He cannot answer the argument, and since the person making it is not a figure of established standing and carries no worldly glory, the easiest road for the proud man is to make fun of the presenter rather than the proof. He tells himself that to sit and listen would be to make his own status lower than that of the preacher, and so, to keep his rank, he refuses to admit the superiority of the truth.

That is the real point at which a human being is being tested. In this world God makes Himself known indirectly, through signs and through reason, while in the Hereafter He will make His being known directly, and then no one will dare to deny Him. Only that faith has value which comes from a person's own discovery of the truth while God is as yet unseen.

### In short

- The people who were treated as weak were made the inheritors of the eastern and western lands that had been blessed.
- They are called those taken as weak rather than the weak, since a people with Allah as their support are not weak in the real sense.
- The word used is inheritance, which makes them the true inheritors of the land and wealth of the people of Pharaoh, and the land meant is Egypt and Syria.
- The promise was fulfilled for them because of their patience, and the same promise was given to the community of Muhammad ﷺ in Surat Al-Nur (24:55).
- What the people of Pharaoh built, planted and raised high was destroyed, and the word of the Lord stood for the people who had endured (28:5-6).
- The denial of the signs comes from pride, which turns a person's attention from the proof to the man who brings it, and only the faith of a person's own discovery, while God is unseen, has value.
