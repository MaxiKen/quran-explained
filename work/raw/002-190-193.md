<<<VERSE 2:190>>>
With the calendar settled, the injunction on fighting follows. "Fight in the cause of Allah ˹only˺ against those who wage war against you, but do not exceed the limits. Allah does not like transgressors". The whole Muslim community agrees that jihād and fighting were prohibited before the emigration to Madīnah. Every verse of that period told the Muslims to bear patiently the pains inflicted by the disbelievers and even to ignore and forgive where they could. After the emigration the first command to fight came through this verse, as al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and others say. Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq instead identifies the verse permitting the wronged to respond to those fighting them. Most Companions and their successors name this Sūrat al-Baqarah verse as the first command to fight. The verse Abū Bakr identifies was also among the earliest on the subject. It may be called the first permission.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Fight in the cause of Allah ˹only˺ against those who wage war against you"**. After the Prophet ﷺ was prevented from visiting the House in the year of Ḥudaybiyyah, he made a pact with the disbelievers that he would return the following year and they would vacate Makkah for three days. As the believers prepared for ʿumrah with him, they feared a broken agreement and an attack by Quraysh. They were reluctant to fight in iḥrām, within the Sacred Precinct or during sacred months. The verse answered those fears. Abū Jaʿfar al-Rāzī conveys al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas's report from Abū al-ʿĀliyah. He identifies this as Madīnah's first verse about fighting. Afterwards, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ fought his attackers and spared non-combatants, until Sūrat Barāʾah was revealed. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said the same and added that the verse was later abrogated by "kill the polytheists ˹who violated their treaties˺ wherever you find them" (9:5), or by the opening verse of immunity in the same sūrah. This is not plausible, however, since "those who wage war against you" simply directs the fighting at the enemies actually fighting Islam and its people, as in "together fight the polytheists as they fight against you together" (9:36). Fighting in the cause of Allah means fighting in obedience to Him to raise His religion and His word, whether in the Sacred Precinct or elsewhere, against the disbelievers who fight you to turn you from His religion. The fighting referred to is that which took place in the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime, when the Makkan opponents, not content with expelling him and his Companions from their home town, prevented them from living and working peacefully in Yathrib and repeatedly attacked them without provocation, so that they had to fight in self-defence. Once aggression has been committed believers are obliged to defend themselves, but initiating hostilities is not permitted, only defensive war, and even that must be openly declared by an established state.

**"but do not exceed the limits."**. Fight for Allah's sake and do not become transgressors by committing what is forbidden. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī includes mutilation and theft of spoils among transgressions. He also forbids killing women, children and elderly non-combatants, and killing priests or residents of houses of worship. Burning trees and killing animals without real benefit are included. Ibn ʿAbbās, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz share this view, alongside others. Muslim records from Buraydah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to fight for Allah's sake against those who disbelieve in Him, but not to steal from the spoils, betray, mutilate, kill a child or kill those who dwell in houses of worship. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from Ibn ʿUmar that a woman was found dead in one of the Prophet's ﷺ battles and he then forbade the killing of women and children, and there are many other hadiths to this effect. Since the command is restricted to those who come to fight, people who do not fight may not be killed in jihād: women, children, the very old, priests and monks devoted to quiet religious pursuits, the physically handicapped, and casual labourers who work for the disbelievers but do not go out to fight with them. The jurists therefore also rule that if a woman, an old man or a religious person actually takes part in the fighting or helps the enemy in any way, killing them becomes permissible, because they then fall among "those who wage war against you". The Prophet's ﷺ battle orders explain the injunction well. Abū Dāwūd records Anas's account of instructions to departing Companions. They were to set out in Allah's name, adhering to the Messenger's ﷺ community. They must spare the old and weak, young children and women. Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq gave Yazīd ibn Abī Sufyān the same instructions when sending him to Syria, adding the prohibition of killing the devout, the monks and priests, and the labourers employed by the disbelievers, especially when they do not fight.

**"Allah does not like transgressors"**. He does not love those who initiate fighting, in the Sacred Precinct or anywhere else, or who overstep the bounds He has set and made sacred.

### Killed by the sword of struggle

The recognisers and chevaliers read this fighting as a spiritual stage for travellers and a state of lovers. Passing through its gate requires being slain by struggle's sword on the Sharīʿah's path and burned in love's fire. Do not suppose that fire is only the lamp you know or that killing is only the state you know. Those killed by the Real are one thing and those killed by the throat another, and burning in the fire of punishment is one thing and burning in the fire of love another. A great master describes discovering the smoke of a burning brand where he had expected a lamp. In friendship, he says, the sin belongs to the slain, while the judge shelters the adversary. Bewilderment in union is itself the path. Whoever is drowned in Him seeks Him still more. One day Shiblī went into the desert and saw forty men, distracted and impassioned, each lying with a brick under his head and his life at his throat. Moved by tenderness, he asked Allah what He wanted of them. Pain burdened their hearts and their haystacks had been set alight. Would He now slay them all with jealousy's sword? His innermost core was told: I will kill them, and once I have killed them I will pay their blood-money. Asked what that was, the answer came that whoever is killed by the sword of My majesty has the vision of My beauty as his blood-money. The blood-money of one killed by the hand is dinars, and of one killed by passion, seeing.

### In short

- This is the first Madinan command to fight, revealed before the make-up ʿumrah following Ḥudaybiyyah, when Muslims feared treachery from Quraysh (Abū al-ʿĀliyah, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas). Fighting had been forbidden in Makkah.
- It permits fighting only against those who actually fight the Muslims, and the claim that 9:5 abrogated it is not plausible (cf. 9:36).
- Transgression means mutilation, theft of spoils, and killing women, children, the old, monks and labourers (al-Ḥasan, Buraydah, Ibn ʿUmar), unless they join the fighting.
- Only defensive, declared war is allowed. Allah does not love aggressors.
- Inwardly, the lovers are slain by the sword of struggle, and their blood-money is the vision of His beauty (Shiblī).

<<<VERSE 2:191>>>
Verse 191 was revealed after Ḥudaybiyyah, when the Prophet ﷺ decided, under the terms of the treaty, to travel to Makkah with his Companions for the make-up ʿumrah he had been stopped from performing the year before. "Kill them wherever you come upon them and drive them out of the places from which they have driven you out. For persecution is far worse than killing." It continues: "And do not fight them at the Sacred Mosque unless they attack you there. If they do so, then fight them—that is the reward of the disbelievers". On the eve of the journey the Companions worried that the disbelievers could not be trusted to keep a treaty. If they turned aggressive as before, what were the Muslims to do? The verse gave them permission to meet aggression by killing the attackers wherever they found them and, if they could, to drive them out as they themselves had been driven out.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Kill them wherever you come upon them"**. If they start the fight, kill them wherever you find them, in the Sacred Precinct or elsewhere. Spend your energy fighting them as they spend theirs fighting you.

**"and drive them out of the places from which they have driven you out."**. Expel them from Makkah as they expelled you, by way of equal recompense. This was carried out after the conquest of Makkah.

**"For persecution is far worse than killing."**. Throughout the Makkan period, Muslims were restrained from fighting and repeatedly told to forgive. Before this verse, the Companions consequently believed that killing disbelievers was bad and forbidden. The comparison with fitnah corrects that misconception. Killing is a terrible evil. Yet the Makkan disbelievers persisted in kufr and shirk and barred Muslims from religious duties, including Ḥajj and ʿumrah. The permission to kill them is explained as averting this greater evil. The word fitnah here means disbelief and polytheism and the prevention of Muslims from their worship, as al-Jaṣṣāṣ, al-Qurṭubī and others state. Because jihād involves shedding the blood of men, Allah points out that these men are committing disbelief, associating partners with Him and hindering from His path, which is a far greater evil and more disastrous than killing. Abū Mālik explains the comparison as condemning the disbelievers' acts. The explanation that shirk is worse comes from Mujāhid and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, and from Abū al-ʿĀliyah and ʿIkrimah. Al-Ḥasan and Qatādah give it too, as do al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk. Their idolatry is more serious than slaying them in the Sacred Precinct or in iḥrām, the very thing the Muslims so greatly feared. Oppression that stops believers from worshipping and drives them back to disbelief is worse than killing.

**"And do not fight them at the Sacred Mosque unless they attack you there."**. The words "wherever you come upon them" might seem to include killing within the Ḥaram. The next sentence restricts that general wording. Fighting must not begin near the Sacred Mosque, including its Makkan environs, unless the enemy attacks there first. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record that the Prophet ﷺ said that Allah made this city a sanctuary on the day He created the heavens and the earth, and that it remains sacred by His decree until the Day of Resurrection. He was granted permission to fight there for only one hour of a day. Its trees must not be cut and its grass must not be uprooted. Anyone citing his fighting there must be reminded that Allah permitted His Messenger ﷺ, not them. This refers to the conquest of Makkah by force and the deaths of some polytheists at al-Khandamah. Beforehand, he had announced safety for those who shut their doors. Those entering the Mosque or Abū Sufyān's house were also safe. Within Makkah's Ḥaram, even birds and animals must not be killed, much less human beings. If someone begins killing there, however, the other party may defend itself. The jurists agree on this exception. One legal reading confines this prohibition on initiating combat to the Sacred Mosque and its precincts. Elsewhere, it regards defensive jihād as necessary and initiating jihād as valid.

A different reading permits only defensive war. It places the fighting in the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime, when his Makkan opponents expelled the Muslims and repeatedly attacked their new settlement at Yathrib, Madīnah. On that reading, Muslims must defend themselves against aggression, but may not begin hostilities. Even defensive war must be publicly declared by an established state. Before the make-up ʿumrah in 7 AH, the Companions feared combat within the sacred precincts. They knew fighting there was prohibited. The verse answers that concern: honour the Ḥaram, but defend yourselves if the disbelievers attack within it.

**"If they do so, then fight them—that is the reward of the disbelievers"**. If they attack you there first, you may fight and kill them to stop their aggression, and such killing and expulsion is the requital of disbelievers, for whom death is the reward. A variant reading has "slaying" rather than "fighting" in all three verbs. On this basis the Messenger of Allah ﷺ took the pledge from his Companions under the tree at Ḥudaybiyyah to fight, after Quraysh and their allies Thaqīf and others joined together to stop the Muslims from reaching the Sacred House. Allah then stopped the fighting before it began: "He is the One Who held back their hands from you and your hands from them in the valley of ˹Ḥudaibiyah, near˺ Mecca, after giving you the upper hand over ˹a group of˺ them" (48:24), and He spared Makkah because of "believing men and women, unknown to you" whom the Muslims "might have trampled" (48:25).

### Expel love of the enemy first

In the devotional reading, the command means that it is incumbent on you to show enmity to His enemies just as it is incumbent to befriend and assist His friends, sparing them not even for ties of kinship. Expel first your love and alliance with them from your hearts, and then drive them from the lands of Islam so that they are diminished. "Persecution is worse than killing" also alludes to the heart's ordeal of veiling. This is graver than bodily suffering through spiritual exertion. Losing the heart's life is more serious than losing the body's. Bodies live by what suits and is familiar to them, but the heart lives only through Allah, so remaining apart from Him is far worse than letting go of one's spirit and life. The Sacred Mosque clause also alludes to protecting time with Allah from bodily and lower-self attributes. These may intrude even through voluntary acts of obedience. A distracting preoccupation must be removed by every possible means. No attachment should remain to block the heart from Him.

### In short

- Revealed before the make-up ʿumrah of 7 AH, the verse allowed the Muslims to kill attackers wherever found and to expel the Makkans as they had been expelled, later fulfilled at the conquest.
- Fitnah, meaning kufr, shirk and barring Muslims from worship, is worse than killing (Abū Mālik, Mujāhid, Qatādah), which removed the Companions' scruple about fighting.
- Fighting may not be initiated in the Sacred Mosque and its precincts, a sanctuary since creation in which even animals are safe, except in defence, which the jurists unanimously allow. One legal reading allows initiating jihād elsewhere, while another allows only defensive war declared by an established state.
- The pledge under the tree rested on this permission, but Allah held back both sides (48:24-25).
- Inwardly, expel love of the enemy from the heart first, for separation from Allah is worse than losing one's life.

<<<VERSE 2:192>>>
After the permission to fight comes the door to peace. "But if they cease, then surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful".

### Phrase by phrase

**"But if they cease"**. The command to stop applies when the polytheists cease their fighting in the Sacred Area and accept Islam in repentance. They turn to Allah, leaving disbelief and the association of partners with Him. The Muslims must then stop fighting them. The Companions had feared Makkan treachery during the ʿumrah of 7 AH, and this verse completes the answer given to them: the fighting permitted in defence ends as soon as the aggressors desist.

**"then surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"**. Allah will forgive them their sins even if they had previously killed Muslims in His Sacred Area, for His forgiveness covers every sin, however enormous, once the sinner repents. He forgives those who turn to Him with regret and asks nothing of them for the disobedience they committed before, and He is merciful to those who die in a state of repentance, compassionate and not quick to punish.

### Quieting the lower self

In the devotional reading, when the clamour of your thoughts and the enemies within your lower self (nafs) cut you off from Allah and jostle you, give up the chatter of the lower self and stop its combative motion, for those who have been asked to guard their innermost secrets should have no business with the exertions of the lower selves in their various forms of opposition.

### In short

- Once the aggressors stop fighting and disbelieving, the Muslims must stop fighting them.
- Allah forgives even those who shed Muslim blood in the Ḥaram if they repent, for no sin exceeds His forgiveness.
- He is merciful to the penitent and slow to punish.
- Inwardly, when the lower self's clamour cuts one off from Allah, its chatter and combat must be stilled.

<<<VERSE 2:193>>>
Verse 193 states the goal of the fighting and its limit. "Fight against them ˹if they persecute you˺ until there is no more persecution, and ˹your˺ devotion will be to Allah ˹alone˺. If they stop ˹persecuting you˺, let there be no hostility except against the aggressors".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Fight against them ˹if they persecute you˺ until there is no more persecution"**. Fight them, if they begin fighting you, whether in the Sacred Precinct or not, until fitnah is no more. Fitnah here means shirk, according to Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan, and likewise Qatādah, al-Rabīʿ, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, al-Suddī and Zayd ibn Aslam: until there is no association of partners with Allah in the Sacred Precinct and no one is prevented from Allah's path. Al-Bukhārī records Nāfiʿ's account of Ibn ʿUmar's refusal to join the conflict involving Ibn al-Zubayr. Two visitors complained of the people's shortcomings. Why, they asked, did the son of ʿUmar, a Companion of the Prophet ﷺ, not go out? He replied that Allah had forbidden him to shed his brother's blood. They invoked the command to fight until persecution ended. He answered that their earlier fighting had ended fitnah and established religion for Allah alone. The visitors instead wanted fighting that would restore fitnah and turn religion towards others. ʿUthmān ibn Ṣāliḥ adds another question addressed to Ibn ʿUmar as Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān. Why did he alternate Ḥajj and ʿumrah across the years, yet leave jihād despite Allah's encouragement? Ibn ʿUmar replied that Islam is built on five: faith in Allah and His Messenger, the five prayers, fasting Ramaḍān, paying zakāh and the Ḥajj to the House. The man cited this verse alongside "if two groups of believers fight each other, then make peace between them. But if one of them transgresses against the other, then fight against the transgressing group" (49:9). Ibn ʿUmar recalled their fighting during the Messenger's ﷺ lifetime. Islam was then weak, and believers faced killing or torture for their religion. Once Islam became strong, that fitnah ended. Asked about ʿAlī and ʿUthmān, he affirmed Allah's forgiveness of ʿUthmān. The questioners, he said, disliked that forgiveness. He described ʿAlī as the Messenger's ﷺ cousin and son-in-law. Pointing towards ʿAlī's house, he showed its closeness to the Prophet's ﷺ. Ibn ʿUmar thus explained the verse as referring to the coercive religious system of the ancient world. In this reading, war against fitnah was temporary and tied to a specific purpose. The Islamic revolution replaced religious persecution with intellectual freedom. With that fitnah ended, Qur'anic permission for violence applied only where self-defence became inevitable.

**"and ˹your˺ devotion will be to Allah ˹alone˺."**. So that the religion of Allah prevails over all others, all worship is for Him alone and none is worshipped beside Him, and Islam and the worship of Allah reign in the Sacred Precinct, the path of Allah becoming the dominant way of life. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs convey Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī's report of a question put to the Prophet ﷺ. One man fights from bravery, another to show off. Which fights in Allah's cause? His reply identifies the one whose purpose is to make Allah's word uppermost. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs also record his command to fight until people declare that none but Allah has the right to be worshipped. That declaration protects life and property from him, except by the right of the law. Their reckoning remains with Allah.

**"If they stop ˹persecuting you˺, let there be no hostility except against the aggressors"**. If they abandon shirk and their fighting of the believers, cease warfare against them, for whoever fights them afterwards commits injustice, since aggression may only be launched against the unjust. This is the sense of Mujāhid's statement that only combatants are to be fought. Or the meaning is that once they abandon their injustice, which is shirk, no aggression may be started against them, "aggression" here meaning retaliation and fighting, as in "if anyone attacks you, retaliate in the same manner" (2:194), "The reward of an evil deed is its equivalent" (42:40) and "If you retaliate, then let it be equivalent to what you have suffered" (16:126). Those who desist are not wrongdoers and may be shown no enmity. ʿIkrimah and Qatādah said that the wrongdoer is the one who refuses to declare that there is no god but Allah. One reading takes it as those who start the fight against you in the Sacred Precinct, where you are not allowed to kill them once they stop.

### Combat against the lower self

In the devotional reading, the verse alludes to combat against the lower selves, for your worst enemy is the nafs within you. Hold fully to the disciplines of the spiritual path until nothing remains of the traces of carnal humanity. Submit the nafs and the heart, qalb, to Allah. No inward opponent should remain to defend itself or follow the lower self. Nor should it independently manage its affairs or exercise free choice in any state. The vicissitudes of life will then flow over you as He wills and you will be effaced from every choice except what comes with the decree. When the self surrenders there is no enmity except towards the masters of holding back, while those who uphold the right of Allah's command are far from any obligation to compel others.

### In short

- Fighting continues only until fitnah, meaning shirk and religious persecution, ends and worship is for Allah alone (Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and others).
- Ibn ʿUmar refused to join the strife of Ibn al-Zubayr's time, explaining that fitnah had been fought when Islam was weak and no longer existed (al-Bukhārī, with 49:9).
- Fighting in Allah's cause means fighting so that His word is uppermost (Abū Mūsā), and the declaration of faith protects life and property.
- Once the enemy desists there may be no hostility except against aggressors, retaliation being limited to the equivalent (2:194, 42:40, 16:126).
- Inwardly, the real war is against the nafs, until it surrenders and the servant is effaced in the decree.
