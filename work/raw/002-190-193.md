<<<VERSE 2:190>>>
With the calendar settled, the injunction on fighting follows. "Fight in the cause of Allah ˹only˺ against those who wage war against you, but do not exceed the limits. Allah does not like transgressors". The whole Muslim community agrees that jihād and fighting were prohibited before the emigration to Madīnah. Every verse of that period told the Muslims to bear patiently the pains inflicted by the disbelievers and even to ignore and forgive where they could. After the emigration the first command to fight came through this verse, as al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and others say. A narration from Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq names as the first the verse granting leave to those who are fought against because they were wronged, but according to most Companions and their successors this verse of Sūrat al-Baqarah was the first command to fight, while the verse Abū Bakr named, being among the very earliest revealed on the subject, could be called the first permission.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Fight in the cause of Allah ˹only˺ against those who wage war against you"**. After the Prophet ﷺ was prevented from visiting the House in the year of Ḥudaybiyyah, he made a pact with the disbelievers that he would return the following year and they would vacate Makkah for three days. As he and the believers prepared for the ʿumrah they feared that Quraysh would break the agreement and start fighting, and they were averse to fighting while in iḥrām, in the Sacred Precinct and during the sacred months, so this was revealed. Abū al-ʿĀliyah said, as Abū Jaʿfar al-Rāzī relates from al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, that this was the first verse about fighting revealed in al-Madīnah, and that from then on the Messenger of Allah ﷺ fought only those who fought him and left non-combatants alone, until Sūrat Barāʾah came down. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said the same and added that the verse was later abrogated by "kill the polytheists ˹who violated their treaties˺ wherever you find them" (9:5), or by the opening verse of immunity in the same sūrah. This is not plausible, however, since "those who wage war against you" simply directs the fighting at the enemies actually fighting Islam and its people, as in "together fight the polytheists as they fight against you together" (9:36). Fighting in the cause of Allah means fighting in obedience to Him to raise His religion and His word, whether in the Sacred Precinct or elsewhere, against the disbelievers who fight you to turn you from His religion. The fighting referred to is that which took place in the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime, when the Makkan opponents, not content with expelling him and his Companions from their home town, prevented them from living and working peacefully in Yathrib and repeatedly attacked them without provocation, so that they had to fight in self-defence. Once aggression has been committed believers are obliged to defend themselves, but initiating hostilities is not permitted, only defensive war, and even that must be openly declared by an established state.

**"but do not exceed the limits."**. Fight for Allah's sake and do not become transgressors by committing what is forbidden. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said that transgression includes mutilating the dead, stealing from the spoils, killing women, children and old people who take no part in the fighting, killing priests and the residents of houses of worship, burning trees and killing animals without real benefit, and Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān and others held the same. Muslim records from Buraydah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to fight for Allah's sake against those who disbelieve in Him, but not to steal from the spoils, betray, mutilate, kill a child or kill those who dwell in houses of worship. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from Ibn ʿUmar that a woman was found dead in one of the Prophet's ﷺ battles and he then forbade the killing of women and children, and there are many other hadiths to this effect. Since the command is restricted to those who come to fight, people who do not fight may not be killed in jihād: women, children, the very old, priests and monks devoted to quiet religious pursuits, the physically handicapped, and casual labourers who work for the disbelievers but do not go out to fight with them. The jurists therefore also rule that if a woman, an old man or a religious person actually takes part in the fighting or helps the enemy in any way, killing them becomes permissible, because they then fall among "those who wage war against you". The Prophet's ﷺ battle orders explain the injunction well. Anas reports in Abū Dāwūd that he sent the Companions out with the words: go in the name of Allah, keeping to the community of the Messenger of Allah, and do not kill anyone old and weak, any young child or any woman. Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq gave Yazīd ibn Abī Sufyān the same instructions when sending him to Syria, adding the prohibition of killing the devout, the monks and priests, and the labourers employed by the disbelievers, especially when they do not fight.

**"Allah does not like transgressors"**. He does not love those who initiate fighting, in the Sacred Precinct or anywhere else, or who overstep the bounds He has set and made sacred.

### Killed by the sword of struggle

In the language of the recognisers and the path of the chevaliers, this killing and fighting is another way station of the travellers and another state of the lovers. So long as you have not been killed by the sword of struggle in the path of the Sharīʿah and burned by the fire of love, you will not be allowed through this gate. Do not suppose that fire is only the lamp you know or that killing is only the state you know. Those killed by the Real are one thing and those killed by the throat another, and burning in the fire of punishment is one thing and burning in the fire of love another. A great master said that he had not known that this was the smoke of the burning brand, having fancied that wherever there is fire there is a lamp, nor that in friendship the sin belongs to the slain and the judge gives refuge to the adversary, nor that bewilderment in union is the path and that whoever is drowned in Him seeks Him all the more. One day Shiblī went into the desert and saw forty men, distracted and impassioned, each lying with a brick under his head and his life at his throat. Tenderness rose in his breast and he asked Allah what He wanted of them, having laid the burden of pain on their hearts and set fire to their haystacks, whether He would now kill them all with the sword of jealousy. His innermost core was told: I will kill them, and once I have killed them I will pay their blood-money. Asked what that was, the answer came that whoever is killed by the sword of My majesty has the vision of My beauty as his blood-money. The blood-money of one killed by the hand is dinars, and of one killed by passion, seeing.

### In short

- This is the first Madinan command to fight, revealed before the ʿumrah of Ḥudaybiyyah, when Muslims feared treachery from Quraysh (Abū al-ʿĀliyah, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas). Fighting had been forbidden in Makkah.
- It permits fighting only against those who actually fight the Muslims, and the claim that 9:5 abrogated it is not plausible (cf. 9:36).
- Transgression means mutilation, theft of spoils, and killing women, children, the old, monks and labourers (al-Ḥasan, Buraydah, Ibn ʿUmar), unless they join the fighting.
- Only defensive, declared war is allowed. Allah does not love aggressors.
- Inwardly, the lovers are slain by the sword of struggle, and their blood-money is the vision of His beauty (Shiblī).

<<<VERSE 2:191>>>
Verse 191 was revealed after Ḥudaybiyyah, when the Prophet ﷺ decided, under the terms of the treaty, to travel to Makkah with his Companions for the make-up ʿumrah he had been stopped from performing the year before. "Kill them wherever you come upon them and drive them out of the places from which they have driven you out. For persecution is far worse than killing." It continues: "And do not fight them at the Sacred Mosque unless they attack you there. If they do so, then fight them—that is the reward of the disbelievers". On the eve of the journey the Companions worried that the disbelievers could not be trusted to keep a treaty. If they turned aggressive as before, what were the Muslims to do? The verse gave them permission to meet aggression by killing the attackers wherever they found them and, if they could, to drive them out as they themselves had been driven out.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Kill them wherever you come upon them"**. If they start the fight, kill them wherever you find them, in the Sacred Precinct or elsewhere. Spend your energy fighting them as they spend theirs fighting you.

**"and drive them out of the places from which they have driven you out."**. Expel them from Makkah as they expelled you, by way of equal recompense. This was carried out after the conquest of Makkah.

**"For persecution is far worse than killing."**. Throughout the Makkan period the Muslims were kept from fighting and repeatedly told to forgo and forgive, so much so that before this verse the Companions believed that killing disbelievers was bad and forbidden. To remove this misconception it was said that fitnah is worse than killing: killing is a terrible evil, but more terrible is what the Makkan disbelievers have done by persisting in kufr and shirk and by stopping the Muslims from their religious duties, from Ḥajj and ʿumrah, and it is to avert this greater evil that killing them is permitted. The word fitnah here means disbelief and polytheism and the prevention of Muslims from their worship, as al-Jaṣṣāṣ, al-Qurṭubī and others state. Because jihād involves shedding the blood of men, Allah points out that these men are committing disbelief, associating partners with Him and hindering from His path, which is a far greater evil and more disastrous than killing. Abū Mālik explained that what you disbelievers are doing is worse than killing, and Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ʿIkrimah, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said that shirk is worse than killing. Their idolatry is more serious than slaying them in the Sacred Precinct or in iḥrām, the very thing the Muslims so greatly feared. Oppression that stops believers from worshipping and drives them back to disbelief is worse than killing.

**"And do not fight them at the Sacred Mosque unless they attack you there."**. Since the general words "wherever you come upon them" might suggest that killing was allowed even within the Ḥaram, the next sentence restricts them: do not initiate fighting near the Sacred Mosque, which includes all its environs in Makkah, unless they start fighting you there. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record that the Prophet ﷺ said that Allah made this city a sanctuary on the day He created the heavens and the earth, and that it remains sacred by His decree until the Day of Resurrection. Fighting in it was made lawful for him only for an hour of a day, its trees are not to be cut nor its grass uprooted, and if anyone cites the fighting the Messenger of Allah ﷺ did there, they should be told that Allah permitted His Messenger and did not permit them. He was referring to the conquest of Makkah by force, when some polytheists were killed at al-Khandamah after he had proclaimed that whoever shut his door was safe, whoever entered the Mosque was safe and whoever entered the house of Abū Sufyān was safe. In the Ḥaram of Makkah it is not permissible to kill even a bird or an animal, let alone a human being, yet this verse shows that if someone begins killing within the sacred precincts the other party may fight back in defence, a point on which the jurists agree. It also shows that the prohibition on initiating fighting is confined to the Sacred Mosque and the precincts around it, while elsewhere, just as defensive jihād is necessary, initiating jihād is also valid. When the Companions were to accompany the Prophet ﷺ to Makkah in 7 AH for the make-up ʿumrah, they feared that any fighting would have to take place within the sacred precincts, where it is not permitted, and this verse answered that doubt: the sanctity of the Ḥaram must be observed, but if the disbelievers begin fighting within it the Muslims may fight back.

**"If they do so, then fight them—that is the reward of the disbelievers"**. If they attack you there first, you may fight and kill them to stop their aggression, and such killing and expulsion is the requital of disbelievers, for whom death is the reward. A variant reading has "slaying" rather than "fighting" in all three verbs. On this basis the Messenger of Allah ﷺ took the pledge from his Companions under the tree at Ḥudaybiyyah to fight, after Quraysh and their allies Thaqīf and others joined together to stop the Muslims from reaching the Sacred House. Allah then stopped the fighting before it began: "He is the One Who held back their hands from you and your hands from them in the valley of ˹Ḥudaibiyah, near˺ Mecca, after giving you the upper hand over ˹a group of˺ them" (48:24), and He spared Makkah because of "believing men and women, unknown to you" whom the Muslims "might have trampled" (48:25).

### Expel love of the enemy first

In the devotional reading, the command means that it is incumbent on you to show enmity to His enemies just as it is incumbent to befriend and assist His friends, sparing them not even for ties of kinship. Expel first your love and alliance with them from your hearts, and then drive them from the lands of Islam so that they are diminished. "Persecution is worse than killing" alludes to the ordeal that comes to hearts from the misfortune of veiling, which is worse than what comes to the bodily selves from the exertion of the spirit, for the passing away of the heart's life is graver than the passing away of the body's. Bodies live by what suits and is familiar to them, but the heart lives only through Allah, so remaining apart from Him is far worse than letting go of one's spirit and life. The verse about the Sacred Mosque alludes to not disturbing your time with Allah by admitting the attributes of the bodily and lower self, even in the form of supererogatory acts of obedience, and if a noisy preoccupation distracts you from Him, remove it by every possible means so that no attachment remains to block you.

### In short

- Revealed before the make-up ʿumrah of 7 AH, the verse allowed the Muslims to kill attackers wherever found and to expel the Makkans as they had been expelled, later fulfilled at the conquest.
- Fitnah, meaning kufr, shirk and barring Muslims from worship, is worse than killing (Abū Mālik, Mujāhid, Qatādah), which removed the Companions' scruple about fighting.
- Fighting may not be initiated in the Sacred Mosque and its precincts, a sanctuary since creation in which even animals are safe, except in defence, which the jurists unanimously allow. Elsewhere jihād may be initiated.
- The pledge under the tree rested on this permission, but Allah held back both sides (48:24-25).
- Inwardly, expel love of the enemy from the heart first, for separation from Allah is worse than losing one's life.

<<<VERSE 2:192>>>
After the permission to fight comes the door to peace. "But if they cease, then surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful".

### Phrase by phrase

**"But if they cease"**. The command to stop applies when the polytheists cease their fighting in the Sacred Area and accept Islam in repentance. They turn to Allah, leaving disbelief and the association of partners with Him. The Muslims must then stop fighting them. The Companions had feared Makkan treachery during the ʿumrah of 7 AH, and this verse completes the answer given to them: the fighting permitted in defence ends as soon as the aggressors desist.

**"then surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"**. Allah will forgive them their sins even if they had previously killed Muslims in His Sacred Area, for His forgiveness covers every sin, however enormous, once the sinner repents. He forgives those who turn to Him with regret and asks nothing of them for the disobedience they committed before, and He is merciful to those who die in a state of repentance, compassionate and not quick to punish.

### Quieting the lower self

In the devotional reading, when the clamour of your thoughts and the enemies within your lower self (nafs) cut you off from Allah and jostle you, give up the chatter of the lower self and stop its combative motion, for those who have been asked to guard their innermost secrets should have no business with the exertions of the lower selves in their various forms of opposition.

### In short

- Once the aggressors stop fighting and disbelieving, the Muslims must stop fighting them.
- Allah forgives even those who shed Muslim blood in the Ḥaram if they repent, for no sin exceeds His forgiveness.
- He is merciful to the penitent and slow to punish.
- Inwardly, when the lower self's clamour cuts one off from Allah, its chatter and combat must be stilled.

<<<VERSE 2:193>>>
Verse 193 states the goal of the fighting and its limit. "Fight against them ˹if they persecute you˺ until there is no more persecution, and ˹your˺ devotion will be to Allah ˹alone˺. If they stop ˹persecuting you˺, let there be no hostility except against the aggressors".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Fight against them ˹if they persecute you˺ until there is no more persecution"**. Fight them, if they begin fighting you, whether in the Sacred Precinct or not, until fitnah is no more. Fitnah here means shirk, according to Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan, and likewise Qatādah, al-Rabīʿ, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, al-Suddī and Zayd ibn Aslam: until there is no association of partners with Allah in the Sacred Precinct and no one is prevented from Allah's path. Under this verse al-Bukhārī records from Nāfiʿ that two men came to Ibn ʿUmar during the conflict of Ibn al-Zubayr and said that people had fallen into shortcomings, and asked what prevented him, the son of ʿUmar and a Companion of the Prophet ﷺ, from going out. He replied that Allah had forbidden him to shed his brother's blood. When they quoted "Fight against them until there is no more persecution", he answered that they had fought until there was no more fitnah and the religion was for Allah alone, whereas these men wanted to fight until there was fitnah and the religion was for other than Allah. ʿUthmān ibn Ṣāliḥ adds that a man asked Ibn ʿUmar, addressing him as Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, why he performed Ḥajj one year and ʿumrah the next while abandoning jihād despite knowing how Allah encouraged it. Ibn ʿUmar replied that Islam is built on five: faith in Allah and His Messenger, the five prayers, fasting Ramaḍān, paying zakāh and the Ḥajj to the House. When the man cited "if two groups of believers fight each other, then make peace between them. But if one of them transgresses against the other, then fight against the transgressing group" (49:9) and this verse, Ibn ʿUmar said that they had done that in the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, when Islam was weak and a man was tried in his religion by killing or torture, and that once Islam grew strong there was no more fitnah. Asked about ʿAlī and ʿUthmān, he said that Allah had forgiven ʿUthmān but the questioners disliked His forgiving him, and that ʿAlī was the cousin and son-in-law of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, pointing with his hand to show how close ʿAlī's house stood to the Prophet's ﷺ. Ibn ʿUmar thus explained the verse as referring to the coercive religious system of the ancient world. The war against fitnah was temporary and limited to its purpose, and once the Islamic revolution replaced religious persecution with intellectual freedom, fitnah had been ended and the Qur'an gave no permission for violence except where self-defence became inevitable.

**"and ˹your˺ devotion will be to Allah ˹alone˺."**. So that the religion of Allah prevails over all others, all worship is for Him alone and none is worshipped beside Him, and Islam and the worship of Allah reign in the Sacred Precinct, the path of Allah becoming the dominant way of life. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī that the Prophet ﷺ was asked about a man who fights out of bravery and another who fights to show off, which of them fights in Allah's cause, and he replied that whoever fights so that Allah's word is uppermost fights in Allah's cause. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs also record his words that he was commanded to fight people until they declare that none has the right to be worshipped but Allah, and whoever says it has protected his life and property from him except by the right of the law, his reckoning being with Allah.

**"If they stop ˹persecuting you˺, let there be no hostility except against the aggressors"**. If they abandon shirk and their fighting of the believers, cease warfare against them, for whoever fights them afterwards commits injustice, since aggression may only be launched against the unjust. This is the sense of Mujāhid's statement that only combatants are to be fought. Or the meaning is that once they abandon their injustice, which is shirk, no aggression may be started against them, "aggression" here meaning retaliation and fighting, as in "if anyone attacks you, retaliate in the same manner" (2:194), "The reward of an evil deed is its equivalent" (42:40) and "If you retaliate, then let it be equivalent to what you have suffered" (16:126). Those who desist are not wrongdoers and may be shown no enmity. ʿIkrimah and Qatādah said that the wrongdoer is the one who refuses to declare that there is no god but Allah. One reading takes it as those who start the fight against you in the Sacred Precinct, where you are not allowed to kill them once they stop.

### Combat against the lower self

In the devotional reading, the verse alludes to combat against the lower selves, for your worst enemy is the nafs within you. Hold fully to the disciplines of the spiritual path until nothing remains of the traces of carnal humanity. Submit the nafs and the heart (qalb) to Allah so that no opponent remains within you to protect itself, to take instruction from the lower self, to manage its own affairs or to exercise free choice in any state. The vicissitudes of life will then flow over you as He wills and you will be effaced from every choice except what comes with the decree. When the self surrenders there is no enmity except towards the masters of holding back, while those who uphold the right of Allah's command are far from any obligation to compel others.

### In short

- Fighting continues only until fitnah, meaning shirk and religious persecution, ends and worship is for Allah alone (Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and others).
- Ibn ʿUmar refused to join the strife of Ibn al-Zubayr's time, explaining that fitnah had been fought when Islam was weak and no longer existed (al-Bukhārī, with 49:9).
- Fighting in Allah's cause means fighting so that His word is uppermost (Abū Mūsā), and the declaration of faith protects life and property.
- Once the enemy desists there may be no hostility except against aggressors, retaliation being limited to the equivalent (2:194, 42:40, 16:126).
- Inwardly, the real war is against the nafs, until it surrenders and the servant is effaced in the decree.
