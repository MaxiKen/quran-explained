<<<VERSE 5:64>>>
"˹Some among˺ the Jews said, “Allah is tight-fisted.” May their fists be tied and they be condemned for what they said. Rather, He is open-handed, giving freely as He pleases. That which has been revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ from your Lord will only cause many of them to increase in wickedness and disbelief. We have stirred among them hostility and hatred until the Day of Judgment. Whenever they kindle the fire of war, Allah puts it out. And they strive to spread corruption in the land. And Allah does not like corruptors"

This verse answers a slander of some Jews, who said that Allah is tight-fisted. It shows that the words are a curse on the speakers, that Allah's hands are open and His giving is full, and that the revelation increases the hostility of the disbelievers. The verse ends by saying that Allah does not love those who spread corruption.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Some among the Jews said, 'Allah is tight-fisted.'"** The material explains that the speakers meant that Allah is a miser who does not spend what He has. The verse says this is a slander. Ibn 'Abbas explained that they did not mean His hand was literally tied, but that He was stingy. Mujahid, Ikrimah, Qatadah, As-Suddi and Ad-Dahhak gave the same meaning. The material cites 17:29, which forbids the believer to be tied in stinginess or to stretch the hand out in extravagance. Ikrimah said the verse was revealed about Finhas, one of the Jews, who said that Allah is poor and they are rich. Abu Bakr struck him for it.

**"May their fists be tied and they be condemned for what they said."** The material says the curse is the answer to their slander. The Jews are stingy, envious and cowardly, and their humiliation is the fulfilment of the curse. The material cites 3:112, which says that disgrace is put over them.

**"Rather, He is open-handed, giving freely as He pleases."** The material explains that both hands of Allah are outstretched, and that He spends as He wills. Abu Hurayrah narrated a hadith that the right hand of Allah is full and that no spending decreases what He has. The hadith is recorded in the two sahihs.

**"That which has been revealed to you will only cause many of them to increase in wickedness and disbelief."** The material says that the revelation brings blessing to the believers and a calamity to their enemies. It increases the believers in faith and good works, and increases the disbelievers in envy and in denial. The material cites 41:44, which says the Qur'an is guidance and healing for those who believe, and 17:82, which says the Qur'an increases the wrongdoers in nothing but loss.

**"We have stirred among them hostility and hatred until the Day of Judgment."** The material explains that their groups and sects will never be united, because they have not agreed on the truth and have opposed the Prophet ﷺ.

**"Whenever they kindle the fire of war, Allah puts it out."** The material says that every plot they make against the Prophet ﷺ is extinguished by Allah, and their evil returns to harm them. The material reads this as Allah keeping their designs in check through divisions among them.

**"And they strive to spread corruption in the land. And Allah does not like corruptors."** The material says it is their habit to cause mischief on earth. Allah does not love those who do so.

### The Jews of Madinah

The material gives the background of the verse. The Jews of Madinah had wealth and influence, and when the Prophet ﷺ came they remained unmoved, because they wanted to keep their power and the offerings they received. They turned against the call of truth, and as a punishment their wealth was reduced. In their frustration they spoke these words. The material says the reports on this came from the Lubab, transmitted from Ibn 'Abbas, which Al-Tabarani and Abu al-Shaykh recorded.

### In short

- Some Jews slandered Allah by calling Him tight-fisted, and the verse answers with a curse on them and a description of His open hands.
- The revelation brings blessing to the believers and an increase in envy and denial for the disbelievers.
- Allah does not love those who spread corruption in the land.

<<<VERSE 5:65>>>
"Had the People of the Book only been faithful and mindful ˹of Allah˺, We would have certainly absolved them of their sins and admitted them into the Gardens of Bliss"

This verse gives the promise that faith and taqwa would bring forgiveness and Paradise to the People of the Book.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Had the People of the Book only been faithful and mindful of Allah."** The material explains that the People of the Book are the Jews and Christians. The verse says that faith and taqwa are the way to their salvation. The material notes that the Qur'an calls them to faith in the Prophet ﷺ, and that their refusal is the cause of their loss.

**"We would have certainly absolved them of their sins."** The material says that the expiation of sins is the forgiveness of Allah. It would have removed the dangers they faced and granted them their goals.

**"And admitted them into the Gardens of Bliss."** The material says this is the reward of Paradise for those who believe and fear Allah. The material notes that the promise in this verse is about the Hereafter, and that the next verse adds a promise for this world.

### In short

- Faith and taqwa would have brought the People of the Book forgiveness of their sins and entry into Paradise.
- The promise is for the Hereafter, and the material notes that the next verse extends it to this life.
- The refusal of the People of the Book was the cause of their loss.

<<<VERSE 5:66>>>
"And had they observed the Torah, the Gospel, and what has been revealed to them from their Lord, they would have been overwhelmed with provisions from above and below. Some among them are upright, yet many do nothing but evil"

This verse promises that if the People of the Book had kept the Torah, the Gospel and the revelation sent to them, they would have been given provision from above and below. It then says that some of them are upright, but many do evil.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And had they observed the Torah, the Gospel, and what has been revealed to them from their Lord."** The material says the Torah and the Injil were the guidance of the earlier prophets, and that they testify to the truth of the Prophet ﷺ. The verse calls on the People of the Book to follow them and not to alter them.

**"They would have been overwhelmed with provisions from above and below."** The material explains that provision from above and below means ample and constant sustenance. The material cites 7:96, which says that if the people of the towns had believed and feared Allah, He would have opened blessings from heaven and earth. It also cites 7:159, which speaks of a community among the people of Musa who guided others by truth and established justice.

**"Some among them are upright."** The material says some of the People of the Book were moderate and on the right path. They were the ones who first held to the Torah and Injil, and then believed in the Qur'an and the Prophet ﷺ. The material cites 57:27, which says that Allah gave the reward to those among the followers of Isa who believed.

**"Yet many do nothing but evil."** The material says the majority were evil in their conduct. The verse is not a judgement on all of them, but on most.

### The promise and the grade of the middle course

The material explains that this promise is not a rule that sustenance always follows faith, but a promise made to a particular group under particular conditions. Some believers in the time of the Prophet ﷺ received it in full, such as the Negus, the king of Abyssinia, and Abdullah ibn Salam. The material says that the general rule is that the pure life is promised to the believers, and it may come as abundance or as outward hardship, as it did with the prophets and the righteous.

The material cites 35:32-33, which speaks of three classes of the people of the Book: those who wrong themselves, those who follow a middle course, and those who are foremost in good deeds. The middle course is given to this community, and the foremost are above it.

### In short

- The verse promises that the People of the Book would have had ample provision if they had kept the Torah, the Gospel and the revelation.
- Some of them are upright, and the material cites 7:159 and 57:27 for those who believed.
- The verse does not make a general rule, but it describes a promise to a particular group, and it cites 35:32-33 for the three classes.
