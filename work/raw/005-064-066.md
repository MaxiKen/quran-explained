<<<VERSE 5:64>>>
A blasphemous claim is quoted and answered: “Allah is tight-fisted.” May their fists be tied and they be condemned for what they said. The reply does not merely deny the statement. It affirms the opposite, that both of Allah's Hands are wide open and that He gives as He wills.

The verse then turns to the effect of revelation upon those who resent it, to the enmity placed among them and to their repeated attempts at war and corruption. Each statement answers something the claim implied.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Some among˺ the Jews said, “Allah is tight-fisted.”"** reports the words of particular people, not of every Jew. The statement is explained as meaning that Allah is a miser who does not spend from what He has, not that His Hand is literally bound.

**"May their fists be tied and they be condemned for what they said."** answers them in their own image. Their hands are described as restrained from good deeds and from spending in good causes, and they are cursed for the words they uttered.

**"Rather, He is open-handed, giving freely as He pleases."** affirms Allah's generosity. His favours are ample, His bounty unlimited and He owns the treasures of everything. The dual form of the word for hand is explained as indicating abundance, since the most a wealthy person can give freely is with both hands.

**"That which has been revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ from your Lord will only cause many of them to increase in wickedness and disbelief."** describes the effect of the Qur'an on those who reject it. The same revelation increases believers in faith, good works and beneficial knowledge.

**"We have stirred among them hostility and hatred until the Day of Judgment."** states that their hearts are never united. Their groups and sects remain opposed to one another.

**"Whenever they kindle the fire of war, Allah puts it out."** describes plots that fail and turn back on those who made them. One explanation says that whenever they unite to kill the Prophet ﷺ in disobedience, Allah causes them to split and oppose each other.

**"And they strive to spread corruption in the land. And Allah does not like corruptors"** names their persistent effort at mischief, described as preventing people from the Prophet ﷺ and calling to other than Allah. Allah's dislike of the corruptors means that He will punish them.

### What exactly did the claim assert?

'Ali bin Abi Talhah reports Ibn 'Abbas explaining that they did not mean a hand literally tied. They meant that Allah is a miser who does not spend from what He possesses, and Allah is far holier than what they attribute to Him. Mujahid, 'Ikrimah, Qatadah, As-Suddi and Ad-Dahhak are reported with the same explanation.

Another verse forbids tying the hand to the neck like a miser and stretching it to its utmost reach like a spendthrift, since both lead to blame and severe poverty (17:29). That wording describes stinginess through the image of a bound hand, which is why the phrase here is read as an accusation of miserliness.

The claim also included saying that Allah is poor while they are rich. 'Ikrimah reports that the verse was revealed about Finhas, one of the Jews, and the earlier statement of his that Allah is poor and we are rich is recalled, along with Abu Bakr striking him for it. Another account names Finhas Ibn 'Azura'.

A fuller report describes two people, Nabbash son of Qays and Finhas, chief of the Jewish tribe of Qaynuqa', uttering words of affront about Allah, with the opening verse revealed on that account. It is given in Al-Lubab from Ibn 'Abbas, narrated by Al-Tabarani and Abu Al-Shaykh. These differing identifications are preserved rather than merged.

### Why would people say such a thing?

One explanation places the words in a change of circumstances. The Jews of Madinah had been given wide means and wealth. When the Prophet ﷺ came and they heard the call of Islam, they remained unmoved, being more concerned with communal leadership and the offerings they received through their self-perpetuating customs.

As a consequence of that stance, their affluent condition was turned into material straitening and they faced poverty. Frustrated and off guard, they began to say that the divine treasure seemed to be running short or that Allah had chosen to be miserly. Another account describes their circumstances becoming straitened after their denial of the Prophet ﷺ, though they had been the wealthiest of people.

A further explanation adds a motive. When the Qur'an stressed spending in charity and giving a loan to Allah, they made that the object of ridicule, saying that God is a pauper whose subjects are rich and whose hands are empty these days. They knew well that Allah is above any shortage, so the words were aimed at the Prophet ﷺ and the Qur'an rather than at a literal claim about divine need.

On that reading the real argument was that he was not a true prophet and the Qur'an not a book from Allah, since a genuine revelation would have had different content. Such a claim shows an insensitivity to the spirit of religion rather than a reasoned objection.

Refusing the Qur'an's guidance was also not a simple refusal. It was effectively a claim that they already possessed salvation and needed no further guidance. A conceited mentality of that kind develops extreme egotism, and such people are unwilling to give up their ego even in ordinary dealings. The result is that society falls prey to mutual differences and enmity.

### What is the answer given?

The verse says that what they wished against Allah returns upon them. Their own hands are bound and they are cursed. The explanation adds that what Allah said occurred, describing them as miserly, envious, cowardly and greatly humiliated, and cites the statement that indignity has been put over them (3:112).

Another passage asks whether they have any share in dominion, in which case they would not give people even the slightest thing, and whether they envy people for what Allah has given them of His bounty, when He had already given the family of Ibrahim (Abraham) the Book and wisdom and a great kingdom. The reference is not numbered in the account and is therefore given without a citation.

The curse is explained in one gloss as punishment through the imposition of the capitation tax, and in another as punishment reaching them in the Hereafter and in this world in the form of disgrace.

### How is Allah's generosity described?

His bounty is unlimited and He owns the treasures of everything. Any good reaching His servants comes from Him alone, without partner. He has created what people need by night and day, while travelling and at home, in every situation.

Another passage says that He gave people of all that they asked for, and that if they tried to count Allah's blessings they could never do so, while man is an extreme wrongdoer and ungrateful. The statement is given without a verse number in the account.

Ahmad ibn Hanbal reports through 'Abdur-Razzaq from Ma'mar from Hammam bin Munabbih that Abu Hurayrah narrated the Messenger ﷺ saying that Allah's right Hand is perfectly full and that no amount of spending reduces what He has, though He spends by night and day. He asked how much Allah has spent since creating the heavens and the earth, and said that it has not decreased what is in His right Hand.

The same report describes His Throne as being over the water, and in His other Hand the hold by which He raises and lowers. It also gives Allah's words, spend and I will spend on you. The hadith is recorded in the Two Sahihs.

Giving and withholding are both acts of wisdom. Allah is independent and free of need while being master of all means. He extends provision for whom He wills in His wisdom and narrows it for whom He wills in His wisdom, and no objection can be raised against that.

### Why does revelation increase some people in rejection?

The bounty given to the Prophet ﷺ is a calamity for his enemies. The more revelation increases believers in faith and knowledge, the more those disbelievers increase in envy toward him and his ummah, in exceeding ordained limits and in denial.

Two other verses make the same point. The Qur'an is described as a guide and healing for believers, while for those who disbelieve there is heaviness in their ears and blindness, and they are as though called from a distant place (41:44). Another says that Allah sends down in the Qur'an what is healing and mercy for believers, and that it increases wrongdoers in nothing but loss (17:82).

One explanation attributes the increase to envy, another to their disbelief in the revelation itself. Neither treats the Qur'an as the cause of rejection in the sense of compulsion. It is the response of a resentful heart to a truth it will not accept.

### What does the enmity among them mean?

Their various groups and sects remain in enmity and hatred because they do not agree upon the truth and because they opposed and denied the Messenger ﷺ. Every sect among them is opposed to the others, and the enmity is described as showing itself in killing and destruction.

The practical effect is explained as protection for the Muslims. Differences inside their own sects mean they would neither dare to wage open war nor succeed in a conspiracy. The failure of open war is described in the extinguishing of the fire of war, and the failure of sinister plotting in their running about the earth spreading mischief.

### What is the deeper disturbance they cause?

A prophet's call asks people to adopt the religion of obedience to Allah that everything else in the universe already follows. That is what keeps a peaceful balance in the world, and it is described as islah, setting things right.

Those who place obstacles in the way of the prophetic call therefore seek disturbance in Allah's world. Yet a person has freedom only over his own inner turmoil. Nobody has that right over another's fate, which is why the work of a messenger is to convey rather than to compel.

### In short

- The claim that Allah is stingy is explained as an accusation of miserliness, and particular speakers are named in differing reports.
- The answer affirms open-handed generosity, with giving and withholding both acts of wisdom.
- The words arose from lost wealth and lost leadership, and were aimed at discrediting the Prophet ﷺ and the Qur'an.
- Revelation increases the resentful in rejection while increasing believers in faith and knowledge.
- Enmity among their sects, the failure of their wars and their striving for corruption are described together with Allah's dislike of corrupters.

<<<VERSE 5:65>>>
The verse states what was withheld and what remains open: "We would have certainly absolved them of their sins and admitted them into the Gardens of Bliss." The condition is faith and taqwa. Had the People of the Book believed and guarded themselves, their past would have been wiped away and their end would have been delight.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Had the People of the Book only been faithful and mindful ˹of Allah˺"** sets the condition. Faith is explained as belief in Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, and mindfulness as avoiding the sins and prohibitions they had committed. One gloss describes believing in Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an and repenting of the previous path.

**"We would have certainly absolved them of their sins"** promises removal of what they had done, even if those sins were many. Another explanation describes the dangers being removed from them and their objectives granted.

**"and admitted them into the Gardens of Bliss"** gives the end. The gardens are described as full of bliss in the Hereafter, with delights that never end.

### Why is this stated as a condition that was not met?

The admonition recalls that the people of the Torah and the Injil gained nothing from the direction those books gave or from what the honoured prophets taught. When worldly greed took hold, they set aside things that should never have been set aside, and ran into straitened circumstances in this world as well.

The verse then keeps the door open. If they turn to the ways of faith and godliness even now, Allah can forgive their past sins and admit them to gardens of bliss. The unfulfilled condition is a statement about their choice, not a closure of the offer.

### What does reverence for Allah do to a person?

The root of waywardness is described as reckless bravado. Once a person fears Allah, he immediately grasps the power coming from Him and that bravado vanishes. The same fear makes him understand what Allah's will is, and he accepts it there and then.

Impure impulses are then driven out. When a person makes himself wholly attentive to Allah, he becomes entitled to Allah's attention. Allah washes away his human weaknesses and, after his death, lodges him in the flourishing gardens of Paradise.

Psychological weaknesses are the evils that stop a person advancing toward Paradise, but with Allah's help they can be overcome and the goal of lasting bliss reached. That is the process this verse promises in summary form: belief, mindfulness, forgiveness and admission.

### In short

- Faith and taqwa are the stated condition for forgiveness and admission to the gardens of delight.
- The promise remains open to those who turn now, despite the earlier failure to benefit from guidance.
- Reverence for Allah removes bravado, clarifies His will and drives out impure impulses.
- Human weaknesses obstruct the way to Paradise, but divine help overcomes them.

<<<VERSE 5:66>>>
The promise is widened from the Hereafter to this life: "And had they observed the Torah, the Gospel, and what has been revealed to them from their Lord, they would have been overwhelmed with provisions from above and below." Obedience to revelation is connected with abundance rather than with loss.

The verse ends with a distinction that must not be flattened. Some among them are upright, while many do nothing but evil. Both statements are true of the same community.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And had they observed the Torah, the Gospel"** uses a word meaning to establish and make something stand upright, not merely to act in accordance with a book. Iqamah signifies carrying the teachings out fully and correctly, with no increase, decrease or shortcoming.

What is meant to stand straight must stand perfectly straight, established and not tilting. The explanation adds that this includes demonstrating the description of Muhammad ﷺ contained in those books, and implementing what is in them.

**"and what has been revealed to them from their Lord"** is read as the Qur'an revealed after them. Another reading takes it as setting out for others what Allah had already made plain to them in those two books. A third reading takes it as believing that every scripture and every messenger comes from their Lord.

**"they would have been overwhelmed with provisions from above and below."** describes provision descending from the sky and growing from the earth, that is rain from above and vegetation and fruit from beneath their feet. Above and beneath indicate sustenance available easily and constantly, pouring forth from every place.

A parallel verse says that if the people of the towns had believed and been mindful, Allah would have unlocked for them the blessings of sky and land (7:96).

**"Some among them are upright, yet many do nothing but evil"** distinguishes a moderate, just community from the majority. Their uprightness is described as implementing the Torah and believing in the Prophet ﷺ, and the evil of the many as concealing his description.

### Why is worldly provision promised here but not in the previous verse?

The earlier verse promised blessings in the Hereafter. This one extends the promise to worldly comfort as well. The reason suggested is the malpractice of altering and distorting scripture out of taste for the temporal and insatiable greed for money.

That greed was their main obstacle to obeying the Qur'an and the Prophet of Islam, despite clear signs of their truthfulness. They feared that becoming Muslim would end their leadership in the community and stop the offerings and gifts they received as religious high priests.

The promise removes that fear. If they truly believe and act righteously, nothing will be taken from their worldly wealth and comfort. It will instead be increased. The offer answers a specific anxiety rather than stating a general economic law.

### Is extended provision a rule for every believer?

No. The promise was made to the Jews present at the time of the Prophet ﷺ, who were its direct addressees. Those who accepted the message received these blessings in full, such as Najashi, the Negus, king of Abyssinia, that is Ethiopia, and 'Abdullah ibn Salam.

It does not follow that provision is always extended for faith and always narrowed for its absence. The purpose here is not to state a general rule but to make a promise to a particular group under particular conditions.

The general promise of a good and pure life is universal, but it may take the form of extended means or of means that are outwardly constricted. The blessed prophets and men of Allah did not all have extended sustenance at all times, yet they all had the purest of lives.

### Who are the upright among them?

The word used describes a moderate community on the middle course. The explanation identifies people who were first Jews or Christians and then believed in the Qur'an and the Prophet ﷺ and entered Islam.

One account names 'Abdullah Ibn Salam and his followers, the monk Bahira, the Negus with his followers, and Salman al-Farisi with his companions. Another names a just community implementing the Torah, such as 'Abdullah ibn Salam and his companions.

Those described as evil are named in one account as Malik Ibn al-Sayf, Abu Yasir, Judayy Ibn Akhtab, Sa'id Ibn 'Amr and the two men called Ka'b, Ibn al-Ashraf and Ibn Asad, with their evil identified as concealing the description of Muhammad ﷺ. Naming individuals is part of the historical report and does not turn the verse into a verdict on every member of a community.

### Why did so few recognise the truth?

When a call to truth goes out, prominent people in the existing order feel disturbed. They fear that complying will jeopardise their economic interests and high positions. That attitude is described as narrow-mindedness.

They forget that what they fear has appeared only to test their entitlement to Allah's blessings. Worthiness is decided by receptiveness to the call, not by self-centred devices. Rejecting it in order to keep high status is itself the act that rules out meriting Allah's favour.

Communities carrying divine scriptures tend to form a self-made religion through additions and deletions. As years pass, its members grow so used to it that they take it for the genuine religion of Allah. When the plain and true religion is then presented, they find it unfamiliar and are disturbed by it.

That was the condition of Jews and Christians, and a large majority of them were not impressed by the truth of Islam. Only a few, such as the Negus, king of Abyssinia, and 'Abdullah ibn Salam, were already on the path of moderation and understood the truth immediately. They came forward as though they had already been moving in that direction and were simply joining the caravan of Muslims to continue their journey.

### Where does the middle course stand among the grades?

The explanation connects this community with the grades described elsewhere. Allah gave them the highest grade of the middle course, which is also given to this ummah, and above it stands the grade of those foremost in good deeds.

One passage describes the Book being given as an inheritance to chosen servants, of whom some wrong themselves, some follow a middle course and some are foremost in good deeds by Allah's permission, which is a great grace. They enter gardens of Eden, adorned with bracelets of gold and pearls, wearing silk (35:32-33).

Two other passages are cited in the same connection. Among the people of Musa (Moses) there is a community leading with truth and establishing justice by it (7:159), and those among the followers of 'Isa (Jesus) who believed were given their due reward (57:27).

These references show that the upright group named here is part of a wider pattern in the scriptures. Upright minorities have existed within earlier communities, and their existence does not cancel the criticism of the majority.

### In short

- Establishing the Torah, the Gospel and the later revelation means carrying them out fully, without addition, subtraction or shortfall.
- Obedience is promised abundance from the sky and the earth, answering the fear that faith would cost them their livelihood.
- The promise was addressed to a particular group at a particular time, and is not a rule that every believer receives extended provision.
- Some among them were upright and believed, while many concealed the truth. Both statements stand together.
- Self-made religion makes the plain truth feel unfamiliar, which is why so few recognised it immediately.
