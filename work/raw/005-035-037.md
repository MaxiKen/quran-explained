<<<VERSE 5:35>>>
"O believers! Be mindful of Allah and seek what brings you closer to Him and struggle in His Way, so you may be successful"

This verse gives three commands to the believers. They are to fear Allah, to seek the means of nearness to Him, and to strive in His way. The material explains that the verse comes straight after the rules on robbery and the spreading of disorder in 5:33 and 5:34, and before the rule on theft. Between those rules the Qur'an speaks about fear of Allah, worship and striving, and this placement is deliberate. The material reads the verse as a call to the heart before the law is applied to the body.

The material also says that the Qur'an does not simply state a penalty as a cold command. It first softens the heart with the fear of Allah and the Hereafter, so that a person gives up crime from inside. This is why the words of fear of Allah come again and again after the rules of crime and punishment.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers!"** The address is to those who have faith. The material says that the command is for the people who already accept the message, and it asks them to act on what they have accepted.

**"Be mindful of Allah."** The material explains taqwa as fear of Allah that stops a person from crime both in public and in private. It is the one thing that can really hold back a person who is able to act in secret. The verse begins with this because every other command depends on it. Some scholars read taqwa in the wider sense of obeying what Allah commands and keeping away from what He forbids.

**"And seek what brings you closer to Him."** The word wasilah is a means of approach. The material explains that it comes from a root that means to make effort to become close to someone. It is used for the closeness that comes through longing and love. Its meaning is broad. Anything that brings a servant nearer to the one he worships is a wasilah. Ibn 'Abbas, Abu Wa'il, al-Hasan, Qatadah, Mujahid, 'Ata' and As-Suddi read it as the means of approach. Ibn Zayd gave a similar reading. Qatadah explained it as seeking nearness to Allah by obeying Him and doing what pleases Him. Ibn Jarir reported the same view. The material says that this means seeking nearness through faith and good deeds.

The material also records a hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ described wasilah as a high rank in Paradise, above which there is no rank. It is reported by Jabir bin 'Abdullah in a narration that Al-Bukhari recorded. Muslim recorded that the Prophet ﷺ told the believers to ask for wasilah for him after they answer the adhan, and that whoever does so will earn his intercession. The material says that the highest rank is the Prophet's own, and that every believer may seek it through his love for him. The material also notes that the path to Allah is built on love, and love grows through following the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ. It cites the verse in which Allah says that if people love Him they should follow the Prophet, and that Allah will love them. The more closely a person follows the Sunnah in worship, dealings and conduct, the closer that person comes to Allah.

The material also records that Umar asked Allah for rain in a time of drought through the intercession of al-Abbas, and that the prayer was answered. The Prophet ﷺ taught a blind companion to pray with the Prophet as his wasilah. The material treats these as examples of how a person may ask Allah through the good standing of the righteous.

**"And struggle in His Way."** The material says that jihad is mentioned separately even though it is part of good deeds, to show its higher standing. There is a saying of the Prophet ﷺ that jihad is the highest peak of Islam. The separate mention also answers a possible confusion. The earlier verses forbid spreading disorder on the earth, and jihad could look like disorder to someone who does not understand it. The phrase "in His Way" marks the difference. Robbery and rebellion aim at personal gain. Jihad aims to raise the word of Allah and to end oppression.

**"So you may be successful."** The success promised here is both in this world and the next. The material ties it to the nearness of Allah and to the reward of Paradise, which is a place of comfort and safety for those who strive in His way.

### The wasilah and the believer

The material draws a distinction. The highest rank of guidance belongs in a special way to the Prophet ﷺ, who prayed for it. The lower ranks are open to all believers. The material says that the call to seek the wasilah is therefore a call to every believer to seek nearness through what is commanded. It mentions that the great reformers used the wording of love and longing in this word to show that nearness grows with love for Allah and His Messenger.

### Three steps toward nearness

The material gives a fuller reading of how nearness is reached. It mentions three things that open the way to it: taqwa, good deeds and righteous company. The material says that love of the righteous is also a cause of Allah's pleasure, so that praying to Allah through them is a permitted act. This reading draws on the same report about Umar and al-Abbas.

### In short

- The verse calls the believers to fear Allah, to seek nearness to Him through faith and good deeds, and to strive in His way.
- The wasilah is a means of approach, and its highest form is a rank of Paradise that belongs to the Prophet ﷺ.
- Jihad is named separately to mark its higher standing, and to show that it is not the disorder that the earlier verses forbid.

<<<VERSE 5:36>>>
"As for the disbelievers, even if they were to possess everything in the world twice over ˹and offer it all˺ to ransom themselves from the punishment of the Day of Judgment, it would never be accepted from them. And they will suffer a painful punishment"

This verse describes the fate of those who reject faith. The material says it turns from the call to nearness in the previous verse to the state of those who have turned away from it. The disbelievers are shown offering everything they own, and twice as much, to escape the punishment of the Day of Judgment. It is not accepted from them.

The material says the verse is a warning against kufr and shirk, and against the grip of wealth and worldly desire. It is a rebuke of the greed that takes people away from what is halal and haram, and that keeps them busy with gathering property.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for the disbelievers."** The word kufr means to reject or cover over the truth. The material says that the verse speaks of the rejection of the message. It also refers to shirk, the joining of partners with Allah in His divinity. Both states are named because both cut a person off from the way of nearness.

**"Even if they were to possess everything in the world twice over."** The material explains that the picture is of a person who owns all the wealth of the earth, and then the same again. The image is chosen to show that no amount of wealth can buy a release from the punishment.

**"To ransom themselves from the punishment of the Day of Judgment."** The material says that a ransom is something given in exchange for freedom. On the Day of Judgment, there is no exchange of this kind. The verse says that no ransom will be accepted. The material also says that a disbeliever will be certain of the punishment, and will not be able to evade it.

**"It would never be accepted from them."** The phrase is final. The material explains that the punishment is not lifted by any payment. The door of repentance is closed at that point, because the time for acting in this world has passed.

**"And they will suffer a painful punishment."** The punishment is described as painful. The material says that this is a punishment of pain and not only of fear, and that it is the lasting result of a life spent in heedlessness.

### The rebuke of gathering wealth

The material explains that people often commit sins in order to meet their own needs and those of their families. Because wealth brings comfort, they seek it without distinguishing between what is halal and what is haram. The verse shows that this search is empty in the end. Comfort gained in a short life is never enough, and more always follows. This race for gold and greed comes to an end on the Day of Judgment, when the wealth cannot be used. The material says that the verse is a cure for this condition. Once a person understands that wealth cannot buy him out of the punishment, he has a reason to turn away from sin.

### In short

- Those who reject the truth and join partners with Allah cannot escape the punishment by offering wealth.
- The verse uses the image of double wealth to show how complete the loss is.
- The greed for wealth that makes people indifferent to halal and haram is what the verse warns against.

<<<VERSE 5:37>>>
"They will be desperate to get out of the Fire but they will never be able to. And they will suffer an everlasting punishment"

This verse completes the picture of the fate of the disbelievers. The material says it describes their state in the Fire. They long to leave, but they cannot.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They will be desperate to get out of the Fire."** The material explains that the word for desperate describes a longing that is full of anguish. The people in the Fire will want to leave, because the pain is severe. The material says that every time the flames lift them toward the upper parts of Hell, the angels of punishment strike them with iron bars and drive them back down.

**"But they will never be able to."** The denial is firm. The material says that there is no way out, and no means of escape or self-rescue.

**"And they will suffer an everlasting punishment."** The material says that this punishment is eternal, and that the people in it will never depart from it or avoid it. The verse is read alongside a hadith reported by Anas bin Malik and recorded by Muslim and An-Nasa'i. In it, a man from the people of the Fire is asked whether he would ransom himself with the earth's fill of gold. He says yes. Allah tells him that he was asked for less and did not do it, and he is ordered into the Fire.

### In short

- The people of the Fire long to leave, but they are driven back and cannot escape.
- The punishment is everlasting, and no ransom or wish can change it.
- The hadith of Anas shows that a request for a smaller thing in this world, once refused, leads to the Fire.
