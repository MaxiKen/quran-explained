<<<VERSE 5:35>>>
Between the laws concerning violent robbery and theft, believers are called to change the motives from which conduct grows: "Be mindful of Allah and seek what brings you closer to Him". Avoiding crime is not merely a response to police or punishment. It belongs to a life directed toward Allah through obedience, love and sustained effort.

The verse joins three commands: taqwa, seeking nearness and striving in Allah's way. Success comes through that combination. A person must leave what Allah forbids, pursue what pleases Him and remain ready to exert himself for His cause.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers!"** addresses those who believe in Allah and follow His Messenger ﷺ, accepting Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an. The address itself is also understood as generous and gentle, inviting people who have answered revelation into greater closeness.

**"Be mindful of Allah"** calls for taqwa, guarding oneself against His displeasure through obedience. When mentioned beside positive acts of worship, it particularly stresses avoiding prohibitions. Faith must govern hidden conduct as well as what others can see.

**"and seek what brings you closer to Him"** commands seeking wasilah, a means of approach. Ibn 'Abbas explains it as nearness or access. The means here include faith, good deeds and obedience that Allah loves, not an approach independent of His command.

**"and struggle in His Way"** calls for jihad, striving for Allah's cause. The explanations include struggle in obedience and fighting the hostile unbelieving enemy for the elevation of the religion. The words in His Way distinguish that purpose from personal gain and violent disorder.

**"so you may be successful"** promises the hoped-for good and escape from what is feared. The explanations connect success with faith, deliverance from wrath and punishment, and lasting happiness in Paradise.

### What does wasilah mean?

The word denotes a means by which someone draws near. A lexical distinction is made between related forms written with sin and sad: the form here carries the sense of seeking closeness with longing and love, whereas the other can describe connection more generally. Jauhari and Raghib al-Isfahani are cited in explaining that distinction.

The early explanations name obedience, faith and righteous action. Mujahid, Abu Wa'il, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi and Ibn Zayd give the sense of approach or nearness. 'Ata' and others likewise connect it with obedience. Qatadah explains it through doing what pleases Allah.

Hudhayfah is reported by Hakim as identifying wasilah with nearness and obedience. Ibn Jarir transmits related explanations. Another verse describes even those invoked by others as seeking access to their own Lord (17:57). They too need His favour rather than possessing independent power over Him.

### Is wasilah also a particular rank in Paradise?

Yes. The word also names the highest rank of Jannah, Paradise, identified with the dwelling of the Messenger ﷺ and described as nearest to Allah's Throne. This particular use does not cancel its broader meaning of seeking nearness through obedience.

Al-Bukhari records Jabir bin 'Abdullah reporting the prayer to be said after the adhan, the call to prayer. It asks Allah, Lord of the perfect call and the established prayer, to grant Muhammad ﷺ wasilah, excellence and the promised praiseworthy station. The Prophet ﷺ promises his intercession on the Resurrection to the person who makes that prayer.

Muslim records 'Abdullah bin 'Amr bin Al-'As reporting that listeners should repeat the caller's words, then ask Allah to bless the Prophet ﷺ. Whoever asks one blessing for him receives ten from Allah. They should then ask for wasilah, a rank available to only one servant, whom the Prophet ﷺ hopes to be. Asking it for him is connected with receiving his intercession.

Ahmad's Musnad also contains a report described as sahih, authentic, that names wasilah as a rank with none above it and calls believers to pray that the Prophet ﷺ receive it.

### How can every believer seek something particular to the Prophet ﷺ?

The distinction is between the supreme degree and the wider path of nearness. The highest station belongs particularly to the Prophet ﷺ, while lesser degrees remain open to believers. The explanation compares this with guidance: its highest perfection is particular, but others can receive its preliminary and intermediate degrees.

Love for Allah and His Messenger ﷺ is central to that approach. Mujaddid Alf-Thani and Qadi Thana'ullah of Panipat emphasise the longing contained in the word. Love grows through following the Sunnah, the Prophet's ﷺ words, deeds and pattern of life.

The Qur'an connects loving Allah with following His Messenger (3:31). That following extends beyond ritual worship to transactions, character, dealings with others and social conduct. Nearness is not separated from the way a person actually lives.

### How are the prophets and righteous people connected with this approach?

The explanation includes loving and keeping company with prophets and righteous people among the means of Allah's pleasure. On that basis, it also accepts praying to Allah while making them a wasilah.

It cites 'Umar seeking rain during famine with 'Abbas as a means of approach, after which the prayer was answered. It also recalls the Prophet ﷺ teaching a blind Companion to ask Allah through Muhammad ﷺ, the Prophet of mercy.

These examples are presented as supplication to Allah. The account does not turn the righteous into independent possessors of the requested gift. It explains their place through love, prayer and Allah's acceptance. No further legal dispute or additional report is supplied here beyond the material being explained.

### Why is striving mentioned separately from other good deeds?

Jihad already belongs among good works, but is singled out because of its high place. A prophetic saying calls it the peak of Islam. The separate mention also prevents confusion with the armed corruption condemned earlier.

Robbery and rebellion seek personal power, wealth or other wrongful ends. Striving in Allah's way is described as elevating His word and removing oppression. Similar outward exertion does not make the purposes or legal character identical. The qualifier in His Way is essential, not optional language that any violent actor can simply claim.

The reward described is enduring comfort in secure, beautiful dwellings of Paradise. Their inhabitants will not die, lose their youth or see their garments wear out. The promised happiness is not another fragile worldly advantage.

### How does the inward reading develop these commands?

The address is heard as a gentle invitation from the One whose kindness gives hope even to the disobedient. All comes under His command (4:78), and neither deliverance nor nearness can be achieved without His guidance and favour.

Seeking approach therefore includes reverence for commands, respect for prohibitions, tenderness toward people and service to Allah. Voluntary worship and care for the heart deepen that response.

Three practices are named for voluntary striving: remembering that Allah sees, protecting one's days from spiritual harm and recognising one's poverty when standing exposed before Him. They foster watchfulness, worship and humility respectively.

Another three nurture the heart: listening to knowledge, limiting unnecessary social mixing and reducing endless wishes. Listening places a person in the company associated with angels. Withdrawal from harmful company protects against the sins it encourages. The praise of that withdrawal is an inward counsel about guarding conduct, not an assertion that every isolated person is incapable of sin.

The fullest nearness will be realised in the Hereafter. In this life, righteous action may bring an inward taste of it through devotion and effort for Allah's cause. The moral call placed among legal passages thus reaches beyond fear of an earthly penalty to love of the One obeyed.

### In short

- Taqwa restrains forbidden conduct, while wasilah directs believers toward obedience and loving nearness to Allah.
- Wasilah also names the Prophet's ﷺ special rank in Paradise, for which believers are taught to pray.
- Love of Allah is joined to following the Sunnah in worship and ordinary dealings.
- Striving in His Way is distinguished from violent disorder by its obedience and purpose, not merely by a claim made for it.
- Watchfulness, knowledge, humility and care for others nurture the inward life alongside outward duties.

<<<VERSE 5:36>>>
No accumulation of wealth can purchase deliverance after the time for obedience has passed: "it would never be accepted from them. And they will suffer a painful punishment". The verse imagines the disbelievers possessing the whole world's wealth and another equal amount. Even that impossible ransom would not undo their rejection on the Day of Judgment.

The warning follows the invitation to seek Allah's nearness. What brings a person near is faith and obedience, not a reserve of possessions large enough to negotiate away accountability later.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for the disbelievers"** concerns those who reject Allah and His messengers. A brief gloss names rejection of Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an. The warning continues into the next verse's description of their lasting punishment.

**"even if they were to possess everything in the world twice over ˹and offer it all˺"** imagines total earthly wealth doubled. The size of the offer removes the thought that the problem is simply insufficient payment. No material sum changes the judgment.

**"to ransom themselves from the punishment of the Day of Judgment"** describes the attempt to buy release once the punishment is unmistakably before them. The wealth once pursued for comfort would then seem worth surrendering entirely for escape.

**"it would never be accepted from them."** The proposed exchange fails. The Day of Judgment is not another market in which possession gives the purchaser power over the outcome.

**"And they will suffer a painful punishment"** states what remains after the ransom is refused. The threat is real suffering, not merely disappointment at the loss of possessions.

### Why expose the futility of wealth in this way?

People often sin to satisfy their own wants or the wants of their families. Those desires can turn into an endless pursuit of money without distinguishing halal, the lawful, from haram, the forbidden. The hoped-for final sufficiency keeps moving away as more possessions create further desires.

The verse brings that pursuit to its end. A short life spent collecting comfort cannot make its owner secure against the next life. Even if someone could own far more than any actual person possesses, the collection could not serve as a substitute for faith.

The warning therefore attacks the assumption behind sinful accumulation. It does not say that all property is itself wrong. It shows the futility of crossing Allah's limits for wealth imagined to be the ultimate protection.

### What does the hadith about an earthly ransom show?

Anas bin Malik reports the Prophet ﷺ describing a person from the Fire being asked about his dwelling. He calls it the worst dwelling. Asked whether he would offer an earth full of gold to escape it, he says that he would.

He is then told that something much less had been required of him, and he had not done it. He is returned to the Fire. Muslim and An-Nasa'i record the report.

The contrast is between the immense price he is now willing to pay and the obedience he earlier refused. The account does not supply a new opportunity to bargain. It exposes the false priorities of the earlier life.

### How does this warning connect with the preceding condemnation of disorder?

The world is presented as an ordered creation in which beings fulfil their assigned functions rather than intrude destructively upon one another. Inanimate things and animals follow the order given to them. Human beings receive guidance through the prophets but also possess a temporary freedom of choice.

They can misuse that freedom by rebelling and disturbing the safety of others. Armed terror and open warfare against the divine command intensify that wrongdoing. The warning against disturbance is therefore connected with the invitation to live according to the order Allah has given.

Kufr, disbelief, and shirk, associating partners with Allah, are named alongside the ruinous consequences of sin. The passage is intended to awaken repentance while action can still change. The imagined ransom comes only after that opportunity has been neglected.

### In short

- Even twice the world's wealth cannot ransom the disbelievers from the final punishment.
- Possessions pursued without regard for lawful limits are not protection against accountability.
- The hadith contrasts a later willingness to surrender everything with an earlier refusal of far less demanding obedience.
- The warning is given now so that the response can be faith and reform, not a futile bargain after judgment.

<<<VERSE 5:37>>>
The people just described will want escape desperately: "They will be desperate to get out of the Fire but they will never be able to." Their wish does not become a path out. The verse makes explicit that the punishment threatened for these disbelievers is enduring rather than a hardship that eventually expires.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They will be desperate to get out of the Fire"** describes a longing born from the severity of the pain. They seek a change of condition and relief from what surrounds them. The desire is intense, but desire alone gives them no power over the sentence.

**"but they will never be able to."** denies departure. Their attempts do not succeed, and the punishment is not evaded by moving from one place to another within it.

**"And they will suffer an everlasting punishment"** confirms its permanence. The glosses describe it as continuous, without interruption or final release. This concerns the disbelievers identified in the preceding verse.

### How are their attempts to escape described?

The account depicts the flames lifting them toward the upper part of Hell. When they seek escape in anguish, the angels of punishment strike them with iron bars and they fall back into its depths. The image explains why the wish to depart is continually frustrated.

Their suffering is therefore not only severe but inescapable. The ransom denied in the preceding verse and the exit denied here close two imagined avenues of deliverance: buying release and finding a way out.

The warning belongs beside the earlier invitation to obedience and nearness. It is given before the judgment, when turning to Allah remains the response being urged. It is not a command for people to inflict such suffering on others.

### In short

- The disbelievers described will long to escape the Fire but will not leave it.
- Their punishment is stated to be lasting and uninterrupted.
- The warning exposes the failure of both ransom and escape, urging a response to guidance before judgment.
