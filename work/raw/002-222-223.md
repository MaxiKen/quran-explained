<<<VERSE 2:222>>>
The verse answers a question about menstruation and married life: "They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about menstruation." It forbids intercourse during the monthly period, then permits it after the bleeding has ended and the woman has washed. The restriction concerns intercourse, not sharing a home, food, affection or companionship. It ends by joining bodily cleanliness to repentance: Allah loves people who return to Him and keep themselves clean.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about menstruation."** The Companions asked how husbands should treat their wives during their periods. Menstruation is the blood that naturally comes from the womb at certain times. One account identifies Abu'l-Dahdah as the questioner. Other accounts describe a wider question prompted by the practices of the Jews around them.

**"Say, “Beware of its harm!"** The word adha describes harm, discomfort or an ailment. Here it is also explained as the uncleanness associated with the blood and the prohibition of intercourse at that time. The rule concerns harm to both husband and wife. It does not make the woman's natural condition a sin.

**"So keep away, and do not have intercourse with your wives during their monthly cycles until they are purified."** Keeping away means avoiding intercourse, specifically the sexual organ affected by menstruation. It does not command the husband to withdraw from his wife in every other part of life. The prohibition lasts through the period of bleeding. The wording that follows explains the further requirement of cleansing.

**"When they purify themselves, then you may approach them in the manner specified by Allah."** After the bleeding ends, the woman performs ghusl, the full ritual bath. Intercourse may then resume through the vagina, not through a forbidden alternative. Ibn 'Abbas distinguishes the two parts of the sentence: the first concerns freedom from blood, while the second concerns washing with water. Another explanation stresses resuming intercourse at the permitted time, rather than during menstruation.

**"Surely Allah loves those who always turn to Him in repentance and those who purify themselves.”"** Repentance means returning from disobedience to Allah, even after a sin has been repeated. Purification includes washing away physical uncleanness and keeping clear of forbidden sexual acts. Allah's love brings honour and reward. A person who has broken the rule through ignorance or carelessness must repent, not treat the offence as something beyond return.

### Why was the question asked?

Anas described a practice among the Jews in which menstruating women were excluded from ordinary household company. People would not share meals with them or mingle with them at home. Another telling describes their being sent out of the house, with eating and drinking together also avoided. The Companions asked the Prophet ﷺ what they should do. Ahmad recorded the account, and Muslim also transmitted it.

The answer drew a clear boundary. The Prophet ﷺ allowed ordinary contact and affection, while excluding intercourse. The Jews who heard this objected that he opposed their customs whenever he heard about them. Usayd bin Hudayr and 'Abbad bin Bishr then asked whether they should go further and have intercourse during menstruation too. The Prophet's ﷺ expression changed. They left thinking he was angry with them. Afterwards, when a gift of milk arrived, he sent them some. That kindness reassured them that he was not angry with them personally.

The incident guards against two opposite mistakes. One is to isolate a woman because of a natural bodily condition. The other is to imagine that rejecting such isolation removes the actual prohibition. Neither is the answer given here. Ordinary companionship remains, but the stated sexual limit remains too.

A report from Jabir also links the questions behind this passage to a claim about intercourse from a rear position and children being born cross-eyed. Some of the Ansar, the Helpers, asked about both that claim and menstruation. The two answers belong together, but their subjects are distinct. Verse 222 sets the limit during menstruation. Verse 223 explains the freedom of position within permitted vaginal intercourse.

### What affection remains permitted?

Most scholars understood the Prophet's ﷺ answer to permit affection and enjoyment short of intercourse. Abu Dawud records through 'Ikrimah that one of the Prophet's ﷺ wives described his covering her sexual organ before intimate contact during her period.

'A'ishah answered the same practical concern when Masruq came to her. He felt too shy to ask. She reminded him that she was his mother and he her son. When he asked what a husband might enjoy during his wife's period, she excluded the sexual organ, not the woman herself. Abu Ja'far bin Jarir relates the report. The same position is given from Mujahid, Ibn 'Abbas, 'Ikrimah and Al-Hasan.

Other reports describe a covering more broadly. Maymunah bint Al-Harith Al-Hilaliyah said that the Prophet ﷺ asked a wife to wear an izar before intimate contact during her period. An izar is a cloth covering the lower part of the body. This appears in the Two Sahihs, with the wording given by Al-Bukhari. 'A'ishah reported similar conduct. When 'Abdullah bin Sa'd Al-Ansari asked what was allowed during menstruation, the Prophet ﷺ pointed to what lay above the izar. Ahmad, At-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah and Abu Dawud record that answer. These reports preserve the practical care taken around the forbidden act.

Sharing a bed, eating together and normal physical closeness are permitted. 'A'ishah described washing the Prophet's ﷺ hair while menstruating. He would rest his head in her lap and recite the Qur'an. A report in the Sahih describes her drinking from a cup and passing it to him. He drank with his mouth at the place hers had touched. She also ate meat and passed it to him, and he ate where she had eaten. These are not gestures of avoidance. They show the difference between the specific restriction and the continuing warmth of married life.

### What does purification require?

The stopping of blood and the act of washing are distinguished in the verse. Ibn 'Abbas explains the first as the end of bleeding and the second as cleansing with water. Muqatil bin Hayyan, Al-Layth bin Sa'd, Mujahid, Al-Hasan and 'Ikrimah give the same explanation.

The rule presented here requires the bath before intercourse resumes. Where water cannot be used, tayammum, the permitted dry purification with earth or sand, takes its place. Thus the end of bleeding alone is not treated as the whole instruction. The words about cleansing must also be observed.

Two readings of the expression for becoming pure are mentioned: yathurna and yattahharna. The fuller form has absorbed one consonant into the next. The discussion brings out the sense of cleansing after menstruation has ceased. The language is not simply a command to wait a certain time and ignore purification.

The phrase about approaching as Allah has directed is explained in two connected ways. Ibn 'Abbas and Mujahid identify al-farj, the vagina, as the permitted place. Abu Razin, Ad-Dahhak and 'Ikrimah emphasise the permitted time, when the wife is pure. These readings do not turn another bodily opening into an alternative during the prohibited period. The fuller treatment of that issue belongs to the next verse.

### Does the natural condition mean spiritual distance?

Menstruation is something Allah has decreed for the daughters of Adam. A bodily condition that may cause embarrassment or aversion is not necessarily chosen by the person who experiences it. It must not be confused with a moral fault. The suspension of ritual prayer at that time is also a divine command, not punishment for a woman's sin.

Prayer is intimate conversation with the Lord. Being directed to leave that particular bodily act for a time does not close every form of nearness. Dhikr, remembrance of Allah with heart and tongue, remains possible. A saying of the Prophet ﷺ relating Allah's words describes His presence with those who remember Him. The image is of another carpet of nearness, open even when the carpet of formal prayer is not used.

The final words also admit several inward applications. Repenting from dhunub, sins, can be joined to cleansing the heart from 'uyub, its defects. One may turn away from an error and also abandon the thought that one's own repentance independently secures salvation. Another reading pairs leaving forbidden things with loosening attachment to familiar comforts and distractions from Allah.

Repentance is pictured as seeking the water of forgiveness. Purification is pictured as washing in shame and humility, with a broken heart rather than self-satisfaction. Another pairing contrasts returning from error with waking from heedlessness. In a further reading, the person stops admiring the act of repentance itself and recognises that events remain within Allah's originating decree. These are inward readings of the closing promise. They do not remove the concrete commands about bodily conduct and washing.

### In short

- Menstruation forbids intercourse, not meals, companionship or ordinary affection between spouses.
- The natural condition is not a sin, and the suspension of prayer does not prevent remembrance of Allah.
- The bleeding must end and purification be performed before intercourse resumes in the permitted way.
- Allah loves repeated repentance and cleanliness, both outwardly and in the heart.

<<<VERSE 2:223>>>
The verse explains permitted marital intimacy through the image of cultivation: "Your wives are like farmland for you". It allows different approaches and positions while keeping intercourse in the place associated with conception. It also puts intimate life within a larger purpose: good deeds, the hope of righteous children, mindfulness of Allah and preparation to meet Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Your wives are like farmland for you,"** The comparison concerns the place where offspring may begin. Ibn 'Abbas explains the image as the place of pregnancy. Just as cultivated land produces a crop, the wife's womb is associated with the growth of children. Sexual pleasure is therefore not presented as the only purpose of intimacy. The image points to lawful offspring as well.

**"so approach them ˹consensually˺ as you please."** The permitted freedom concerns the manner and direction of approach, not a change of the place of intercourse. It may be from the front or behind while remaining vaginal intercourse. The time restriction explained in verse 222 still applies. Different positions are allowed within those limits.

**"And send forth something good for yourselves."** This includes acts of obedience that will benefit a person after death. It also includes the intention of drawing near to Allah through lawful married life, asking for righteous offspring and remembering Allah before intercourse. Saying bismillah means beginning in Allah's name.

**"Be mindful of Allah, and know that you will meet Him."** Freedom in married life does not remove accountability. The commands and prohibitions concerning women must be observed, including the restrictions on intercourse during menstruation and on anal intercourse. Human beings will stand before Allah at the Resurrection and receive the outcome of their deeds.

**"And give good news to the believers"** The Prophet ﷺ is told to announce the happy end of faith joined to obedience. Those who respect Allah's limits are promised Paradise. Their lasting blessings include the joy of meeting their Lord and seeing His blessed Face.

### What mistaken belief did the verse correct?

Jabir reported that the Jews claimed a child would be cross-eyed if conceived through vaginal intercourse from a rear position. The verse answered this claim by permitting different approaches to the same place. Al-Bukhari records the report through Ibn Al-Munkadir. Abu Dawud and Muslim also transmit it. Ibn Abu Hatim gives the account through Muhammad bin Al-Munkadir from Jabir bin 'Abdullah. Ibn Jurayj adds the Prophet's ﷺ explanation that either approach is allowed when penetration remains vaginal.

Ahmad records from Ibn 'Abbas that some of the Ansar asked about this manner of intercourse. The Prophet ﷺ allowed different approaches while retaining that same boundary. Another report gives the household setting in more detail. The Muhajirun, the emigrants, came to Al-Madinah and married women of the Ansar. Their accustomed sexual positions differed from those of their new wives. The Ansari women had been influenced by the Jewish practice and the claim about cross-eyed children.

One woman refused the unfamiliar practice until she could ask the Prophet ﷺ. She told Umm Salamah what had happened. When the Prophet ﷺ arrived, the woman felt shy and left, so Umm Salamah explained the question. He asked for the woman to be called back, recited the verse and made clear that the permitted place was one opening, the vagina. Ahmad records this account, and At-Tirmidhi also transmits it with the grade Hasan, meaning good.

The story reached the discussion through Hafsah bint 'Abdur-Rahman bin Abu Bakr, whom 'Abdullah bin Sabit approached despite his own embarrassment. The shyness in these reports did not make the practical question unworthy of an answer. The answer removed a superstition while defining the actual boundary.

### Does freedom of position permit anal intercourse?

No. The image of cultivated land identifies the place of conception, not every possible opening. The permission includes standing or sitting, kneeling or lying down, with either spouse above or beside the other, and approaching from the front or rear. It does not include penetration of the anus. This distinction also explains how the phrase about approaching from behind must be read.

An-Nasa'i records an explicit rejection of a claim attributed to Ibn 'Umar. Abu An-Nadr asked Nafi' about people saying that he transmitted permission for anal intercourse from Ibn 'Umar. Nafi' denied the allegation. What Ibn 'Umar had actually described was the practice of Quraysh: vaginal intercourse from a rear position. When they married Ansari women after moving to Al-Madinah, disagreement over this position led to the question answered by the verse. The chain of this report is described as authentic.

A second account gives Ibn 'Umar's reaction to the unambiguous question. Sa'id bin Yasar Abu Hubab asked him about intercourse in a woman's rear. Ibn 'Umar first asked what was meant. When the question was clarified as anal intercourse, he rejected it with the question of whether a Muslim would do such a thing. Darimi records this in his Musnad through Abu Muhammad 'Abdullah bin 'Abdur-Rahman. Its chain is described as authentic. It is not permission concealed in a vague expression, but an express refusal.

Malik bin Anas was also asked directly by Isma'il bin Ruh. He pointed to the place of pregnancy as the place of intercourse and told him to use only the vagina. When told that people attributed permission to him, Malik firmly called the attribution false. Abu Bakr bin Ziyad Naysaburi relates the exchange. The address Abu 'Abdullah in that conversation refers to Malik.

Several prophetic reports reinforce the prohibition. Ahmad records from Khuzaymah bin Thabit Al-Khatami that the Prophet ﷺ repeated three times that Allah is not shy of the truth, then prohibited anal intercourse with women. Ibn Majah and An-Nasa'i also collect it. A report from Ibn 'Abbas warns that Allah will not look at a man who has anal intercourse with either a man or a woman. An-Nasa'i transmits it, as does At-Tirmidhi, who grades it Hasan Gharib, good and singular in its transmission. Ibn Hibban includes it in his Sahih, and Ibn Hazm also calls it authentic.

Another report, from 'Ali bin Talaq, states that the Prophet ﷺ forbade anal intercourse and connected plain speech about the matter with Allah's not being shy of the truth. Ahmad records it. At-Tirmidhi also gives it and grades it Hasan.

The rejection is also reported from many of the salaf, the earliest Muslim generations. Among them are Tawus and 'Ata, Sa'id bin Jubayr and Sa'id bin Musayyib, Abu Salamah and 'Ikrimah, 'Urwah bin Az-Zubayr, Mujahid bin Jabr and Al-Hasan. Together with the majority of scholars they strongly condemned the practice. Some used the severe word kufr, unbelief, in rebuking it. That severity belongs to the account as given. It should not be erased by confusing vaginal intercourse from behind with anal intercourse.

### What can be sent ahead through married life?

The instruction includes obedience more broadly, not only one phrase spoken at one moment. Acts that obey Allah and avoid His prohibitions become provisions for the life to come. Intercourse itself can carry the intention of lawful closeness and the hope of pious children. Righteous offspring are one explanation of the good sent ahead.

Ibn Jarir relates from 'Ata, or through him from Ibn 'Abbas, an explanation about mentioning Allah's name before intercourse. Al-Bukhari records from Ibn 'Abbas a prayer taught by the Prophet ﷺ. It asks Allah to protect the couple from Satan and to protect whatever child He grants them. The report says that if a child is decreed from that union, Satan will not be able to harm the child.

This locates the act within remembrance, intention and responsibility. It is not enough to ask whether a deed brings immediate pleasure or worldly benefit. The greater and lasting side of life must be kept in view. An earthly gain that damages the everlasting life leaves a person with loss rather than success. After death, deeds will be weighed in Allah's justice. A person cannot escape the consequence merely because the action once seemed useful in this world.

### The soul's share and the heart's purity

An inward reading distinguishes the soul from the heart. The soul belongs to the lower world, with water and dust as its origin. The heart is described as a subtle gift from the higher world, founded in light. The soul's ordinary condition is absence from contemplation, while the heart's task is witnessing. The Prophet ﷺ, called Mustafa, is quoted as saying that every heart is between two of Allah's fingers.

Allah has kindly given the soul companionship suited to its nature. The permission of marriage appears in (4:3), while rest, love and mercy between spouses appear in (30:21). The soul finds its lawful share with one like itself. The heart, however, must not descend into dependence on created things or let attachment to them displace its attention to Allah. Its inner secret is to be cleansed of everything that competes with Him.

That inward purity is connected with Allah's love for those who cleanse themselves in (2:222). It is also linked to the men who love to be purified in (9:108). The bodily permission and the heart's discipline concern different aspects of the person. Lawful companionship need not be confused with giving the heart's ultimate allegiance to anything besides Allah.

The same reflection distinguishes impurity belonging to a thing's very nature from impurity acquired afterwards. Its illustration is a corpse: even immersion in the ocean a thousand times does not change an impurity described as intrinsic. An originally clean thing that becomes soiled can, by contrast, be washed. Within acquired impurity, a lighter kind is removed with water alone, while a heavier kind requires water and earth. The distinction gives the inward lesson its force: cleansing requires recognising what kind of condition is being addressed, rather than imagining every impurity is removed in exactly the same way.

### In short

- The farmland image permits different positions within vaginal intercourse, not anal intercourse or intercourse during menstruation.
- The verse rejects the claim that approaching from behind causes a child to be cross-eyed.
- Remembering Allah, seeking righteous children and obeying His limits give married life a purpose beyond immediate pleasure.
- Meeting Allah makes private conduct accountable, while believers who obey Him receive good news of Paradise.
