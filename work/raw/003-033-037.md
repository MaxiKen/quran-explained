<<<VERSE 3:33>>>
The verse says, “Indeed, Allah chose Adam, Noah, the family of Abraham, and the family of ’Imrân” (3:33). It describes selected people and households whom Allah raised above others of their time. The next verses turn to the family of ʿImrān and the birth and upbringing of Maryam (Mary).

### Phrase by phrase

**“Indeed, Allah chose Adam, Noah”** names two prophets selected for their missions. Adam was created by Allah, taught the names of things, and given a place in Paradise before being sent down according to divine wisdom. Allah commanded the angels to bow before him. Nūḥ (Noah) was made a messenger to people who worshipped idols. He called them openly and privately for a long period, but many turned further away. He prayed against them, and Allah drowned those who rejected him. Those who followed the religion sent to Nūḥ were saved.

**“the family of Abraham”** includes the prophet Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and his descendants. One explanation says the family was chosen because prophethood continued among them, including Muhammad ﷺ, the final prophet.

**“and the family of ’Imrân”** refers here to the family of Maryam and her son ʿĪsā (Jesus). One explanation identifies ʿImrān as Mary’s father. Another explanation distinguishes this ʿImrān from the person associated with Mūsā (Moses) and Hārūn (Aaron). The verse introduces the account of Maryam, whose birth and upbringing are described next.

**“above all people ˹of their time˺”** means Allah favored these people and households over others of their age by choosing them for prophethood and guidance. The wording does not say that their status came automatically through family descent. An inward reading says that being chosen is a gift from Allah, not something inherited merely through lineage.

### Why are these households named together?

The verse links Adam, Nūḥ, Ibrāhīm’s family, and ʿImrān’s family as people chosen to carry guidance. One explanation says that the names were also a comfort to Muhammad ﷺ when people doubted his prophethood. The passage is about Allah’s choice and the prophets’ mission, while the family of ʿImrān leads directly into the account of Maryam and ʿĪsā.

### In short

- Allah chose Adam, Nūḥ, the family of Ibrāhīm, and the family of ʿImrān above people of their time.
- Their distinction came through Allah’s choice and prophethood, not simply through ancestry.
- The mention of ʿImrān introduces the story of Maryam and ʿĪsā.

<<<VERSE 3:34>>>
The verse says, “They are descendants of one another” (3:34). It links the chosen people in the previous verse through family descent and a shared way of worshipping Allah. Their message centers on Allah’s oneness and doing what is right. Allah hears what people say and knows their actions.

### Phrase by phrase

**“They are descendants of one another”** can mean that some were ancestors of others and that the chosen households were connected through family lines. It also points to continuity in their faith and conduct. The prophets and their followers called people to worship Allah alone and to do good.

An inward explanation says that sharing descent does not itself make a person chosen. Being selected is something Allah grants, not an automatic result of kinship. Another explanation says that people in these families passed noble character and virtue from one generation to another.

**“And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing”** means He hears what people say and knows what they do and intend. He knows who follows His way and who only claims a connection to those He chose.

### In short

- The chosen people are connected by descent and by a shared call to Allah’s oneness.
- Family connection alone does not guarantee that a person is chosen.
- Allah hears people’s claims and knows their actions and intentions.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:35>>>
The verse recalls the prayer of the wife of ʿImrān: “My Lord! I dedicate what is in my womb entirely to Your service” (3:35). She vows that her child will be devoted to worship and service at the sacred place in Jerusalem. She asks Allah to accept the vow, knowing that He hears prayer and knows her intention.

### Phrase by phrase

**“˹Remember˺ when the wife of ’Imrân said”** introduces the mother of Maryam. One report gives her name as Ḥannah bint Fāqūdh. She had been unable to have children and prayed to Allah after seeing a bird feeding its young. Allah granted her a child. Muhammad bin Ishaq relates this account of Hannah, the wife of ʿImrān. She made the vow while pregnant, not yet knowing whether she would give birth to a boy or a girl. Her husband ʿImrān died while she was still pregnant, according to one report.

**“My Lord! I dedicate what is in my womb entirely to Your service”** describes a vow to free the child from ordinary worldly work and devote the child to worship and service at Bayt al-Maqdis, the sacred place in Jerusalem. In the religious practice described for earlier prophets, a child could be dedicated to this service. The mother expected a boy, whom she thought would be able to fulfill the role.

An inward reading understands the consecrated person as someone freed from being occupied by created things and devoted wholly to Allah. It says that the mother’s vow expressed sincerity, even though she did not know the child’s sex or what the child’s future would be.

**“so accept it from me”** is a request that Allah receive the vow. The mother does not assume that her intention alone guarantees acceptance. She asks for it as a gift.

**“You ˹alone˺ are truly the All-Hearing, All-Knowing”** recognizes that Allah hears the prayer and knows what is in the womb and the intention behind the vow. She asks the One who knows her purpose to accept it.

### The child’s later dedication and the Sunnah of naming

The surrounding account also discusses what happens after a child is born. The Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ includes naming a newborn on the day of birth. Al-Bukhari and Muslim report that the Prophet said a son had been born to him and that he named him Ibrāhīm, after his father. They also report that Anas brought his newborn brother to the Prophet, who softened a date, placed some in the baby’s mouth, and named him ʿAbd Allāh. These reports show that a child can be named promptly, alongside the mother’s earlier dedication and prayer.

A report from Qatādah, from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, from Samurah bin Jundub describes the ʿaqīqah: a newborn boy is connected to a sacrifice on his behalf until the seventh day, when he is named and his hair is shaved. Aḥmad and the compilers of the Sunan collections recorded the report, and At-Tirmidhi graded it ṣaḥīḥ. Another narration says that blood is offered on his behalf. The explanation notes that this wording is more famous and established than the wording that simply says a sacrifice is offered. These are later practices concerning a newborn, distinct from the mother’s vow to dedicate the child to service at Bayt al-Maqdis.

### In short

- The wife of ʿImrān prayed for a child and vowed to dedicate the child to worship and service in Jerusalem.
- She made the vow while pregnant and did not yet know she would give birth to a girl.
- She asked Allah to accept the vow because He hears prayer and knows intention.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:36>>>
The verse records the mother’s response: “My Lord! I have given birth to a girl” (3:36). She had hoped for a boy who could serve at the sacred place, so she expresses her surprise. She names the child Maryam and asks Allah to protect her and her offspring from Satan.

### Phrase by phrase

**“When she delivered, she said, ‘My Lord! I have given birth to a girl’”** shows that the child was not what her mother expected. The explanation says she thought a boy would be better suited to the dedicated service because of differences in strength and the effects of menstruation. In that earlier religious practice, only boys were expected to serve in this way. The verse then says that Allah knew best what she had delivered, marking Allah’s knowledge of Maryam’s future and honor.

**“and the male is not like the female”** records the mother’s understanding of the difference in suitability for the particular service she had vowed. An inward reading says that, outwardly, a boy and girl differ, but Allah accepted Maryam and made signs and miracles appear through her and her son. Her being a girl did not prevent Allah from honoring her beyond what her mother had imagined.

**“I have named her Mary”** gives the child her name. The passage is also cited as evidence that a mother may name her child and that a name may be given on the day of birth. The mother’s vow concerns the child’s upbringing and dedication, and one explanation says that a mother has a measure of guardianship over that education and training.

**“and I seek Your protection for her and her offspring from Satan, the accursed”** is the mother’s prayer for Maryam and her descendants. She asks Allah to protect them from Satan’s evil. One report from Abū Hurayrah says that every newborn is touched by Satan and begins crying, except Maryam and her son ʿĪsā. ʿAbd al-Razzāq recorded the report. It is presented as an answer to the mother’s request for protection.

### What does the mother’s response teach?

The mother’s surprise does not mean her prayer was rejected. She made the vow sincerely, and Allah knew what the child would become. One inward reading says that Maryam’s acceptance produced signs that would not have appeared if the child’s sex had been the only measure of her suitability. The mother continues to entrust Maryam and her descendants to Allah’s protection.

### Other teachings connected with birth

Muhammad bin Ishaq’s account identifies the mother as Hannah, wife of ʿImrān. The reports surrounding this birth also discuss the Sunnah of naming a child. Al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Two Sahihs, record that the Prophet named a son Ibrāhīm after his father. They also relate that Anas brought his newborn brother, whom the Prophet named ʿAbd Allāh. These reports are distinct from the mother’s naming of Maryam, but they support naming a child at birth.

Another report, transmitted from Qatādah through al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī from Samurah bin Jundub, discusses ʿaqīqah. It says that a newborn boy is tied to a sacrifice on his behalf until the seventh day, when he is named and his hair is shaved. Aḥmad and the compilers of the Sunan recorded it, and At-Tirmidhi graded it ṣaḥīḥ. An alternate wording says that blood is offered on his behalf and is described as the more famous, established version. These teachings explain practices related to a child’s birth, while the verse itself emphasizes Maryam’s name and the mother’s supplication.

### In short

- The mother expected a boy but gave birth to Maryam, a girl.
- She named Maryam and asked Allah to protect her and her descendants from Satan.
- Allah knew Maryam’s future and accepted her dedication despite her mother’s surprise.

<<<VERSE 3:37>>>
The verse says, “So her Lord accepted her graciously and blessed her with a pleasant upbringing” (3:37). Allah accepts the mother’s dedication of Maryam, cares for her, and appoints Zakariyyā (Zachariah) as her guardian. At the place of worship, Zakariyyā finds her supplied with food that appears outside its season. Maryam says that it comes from Allah, who provides without limit.

### Phrase by phrase

**“So her Lord accepted her graciously”** means that Allah received Maryam and her mother’s dedication with favor. One explanation says He accepted her in place of the boy her mother expected. He made her beloved among people and caused the righteous to care for her. An inward reading says that Allah’s gracious acceptance kept Maryam preserved and obedient, even though people would later say hurtful things because of her and her son.

**“and blessed her with a pleasant upbringing”** means that Allah gave Maryam excellent character and guided her to knowledge, religion, and righteous conduct. One report describes her as growing unusually quickly. Another explains the phrase as Allah nurturing her throughout every stage of life and hour.

**“entrusting her to the care of Zachariah”** describes Zakariyyā becoming Maryam’s guardian. Her mother brought her to the caretakers and worshippers at Bayt al-Maqdis and told them of her vow. They wanted custody because ʿImrān, Maryam’s father, had been a leader at the sanctuary. Some explanations say Zakariyyā was the husband of Maryam’s maternal aunt. Another describes him as her brother-in-law. A hadith in the two major collections compares the maternal aunt to a mother, supporting the closeness of the relationship.

The people did not agree at first, so they drew lots. One report says twenty-nine caretakers cast their pens into the Jordan. They agreed that the pen which remained steady and rose to the surface would identify the guardian. Zakariyyā’s pen did so, and he took charge of Maryam. Another account says her mother asked the caretakers to take her in, Zakariyyā argued that he was best suited because his wife was her aunt, and the group resolved the dispute by lots.

Zakariyyā arranged a private room for Maryam near the sanctuary, where she could worship. Some reports say he arranged for a wet nurse. Others say that Maryam did not need one. The explanations differ on these details, while agreeing that she grew under his care.

**“Whenever Zachariah visited her in the sanctuary, he found her supplied with provisions”** describes a sign of Allah’s care. Zakariyyā would find Maryam provided with food. The explanations say that she received summer fruit in winter and winter fruit in summer. One account says that Gabriel brought the food to her.

**“He exclaimed, ‘O Mary! Where did this come from?’”** shows Zakariyyā’s surprise at the provision and his question about its origin.

**“She replied, ‘It is from Allah’”** gives Maryam’s answer. She does not claim the food as her own achievement. She recognizes Allah as its giver.

**“Surely Allah provides for whoever He wills without limit”** means that Allah gives freely, beyond ordinary measure or seasonal expectation. The provision at the sanctuary shows His care for Maryam and His ability to provide as He wills.

### Reports that explain the guardianship and provision

Muhammad bin Ishaq and Ibn Jarir both report that Zakariyyā was married to Maryam’s maternal aunt. Another report describes him as her brother-in-law. A report about the relationship between Yaḥyā (John) and ʿĪsā says they were maternal cousins. The Two Sahihs also preserve the Prophet’s ruling about ʿAmārah, daughter of Ḥamzah: she was placed with her maternal aunt, the wife of Jaʿfar bin Abi Talib, and he said that a maternal aunt is like a mother. These reports are cited to explain the closeness of Maryam’s aunt and Zakariyyā and why he was a suitable guardian.

The account of the lots is given in more than one form. In one telling, the caretakers cast their pens in the Jordan and agreed that the one which stayed firm and surfaced would identify Maryam’s guardian. Zakariyyā’s pen did so. In another telling, those who served at the sanctuary first disputed who should care for Maryam, then agreed that lots would settle the matter. Both versions place the decision in Allah’s care rather than presenting Zakariyyā as taking custody by force.

Several early interpreters explain the unusual food as seasonal fruit appearing at the opposite time of year. The explanation names Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah, Saʿīd bin Jubayr, Abū Ash-Shaʿthāʾ, Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Qatādah, al-Rabīʿ bin Anas, ʿAṭiyyah al-ʿAwfī, and al-Suddī among those who reported this. One account says Gabriel brought it. The word miḥrāb here means the sanctuary or worship room where Maryam stayed. An inward reading says she could be found there, devoted to worship, and that her excellent upbringing included being kept obedient and protected. It connects this protection with Maryam’s request for refuge in the Compassionate One (19:18), and with the righteous fruit of her story, ʿĪsā.

A saying attributed to Dāwūd (David) advises: “If you see someone seeking Me, become a servant to him.” The word ṭāliban means a seeker. The saying is used to illustrate Zakariyyā’s service to Maryam as one who sought Allah. These reflections portray her care as a responsibility and honor, not merely a dispute over custody.

### Why was Zakariyyā chosen as her guardian?

The explanations differ about his exact family relationship to Maryam. Some say he was married to her maternal aunt, while another calls him her brother-in-law. They agree that he was a righteous prophet and caretaker at the sanctuary, and that the lots selected him when others also sought custody. His guardianship gave Maryam access to his knowledge and righteous conduct.

### In short

- Allah accepted Maryam’s dedication and gave her a good upbringing.
- Zakariyyā became her guardian after the caretakers settled their dispute by lots.
- Maryam received food out of season and recognized Allah as its provider.
