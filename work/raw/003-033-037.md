<<<VERSE 3:33>>>
The story of Maryam and her son begins with election: "Indeed, Allah chose Adam, Noah, the family of Abraham, and the family of ’Imrân above all people ˹of their time˺". Four names and households are placed above the nations, and the last of them is the family into which Maryam will be born. The Prophet ﷺ is being comforted with predecessors, because disbelievers had doubted his prophethood, and the names prepare the reader for ʿĪsā, who follows immediately in the narrative.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, Allah chose Adam, Noah, the family of Abraham, and the family of ’Imrân above all people ˹of their time˺"**: Allah preferred these households over the people of the earth, and over all the people of their own times. He preferred their very selves by making prophethood reside in them and in their progeny.

### What the choosing involved in each case

Allah chose Adam by creating him with His Hand and breathing life into him, commanding the angels to prostrate before him, teaching him the names of everything and letting him dwell in Paradise before sending him down from it out of His wisdom. He honoured Adam by that prostration of the angels.

He chose Nūḥ (Noah) as earth's first messenger, sent when idols were worshipped and partners were associated with Allah. Nūḥ preached day and night, publicly and privately, across an immense period, but distance only grew. His supplication followed, the flood drowned his people, and survival belonged solely to his followers.

He chose the household of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) by placing prophethood in his children, and the household includes the master of all mankind and the Final Prophet, Muhammad ﷺ. He chose the household of ʿImrān, the father of Maryam, mother of ʿĪsā, and the choice is also a choice of Jesus through his mother. ʿĪsā is from the offspring of Ibrāhīm, as will be shown in Surah Al-An'am.

When the Jews heard this, they said, "We follow the religion of Adam, we are Muslims", and the answer named choice after choice in Islam: Adam preferred with Islam, Nūḥ with Islam, the children of Ibrāhīm with Islam, and the family of ʿImrān, that is Moses and Aaron, with Islam, outranking contemporaries. Another view denies that this ʿImrān was father to Moses and Aaron.

The brief reference serves the Prophet ﷺ directly. Since disbelievers would not obey him because they doubted his prophethood, names of past prophets were cited to remove the doubt. ʿĪsā is singled out for fuller identification because he will come again to this world and work with the followers of the Prophet ﷺ, so Muslims need to know who he is.

### In short

- Adam, Nūḥ, the family of Ibrāhīm and the family of ʿImrān were chosen above the people of their times.
- The honour includes creation, prostration, first messengership, prophethood in Abraham's children, and Maryam's line.
- The names console the Prophet ﷺ and prepare for the story of ʿĪsā.

<<<VERSE 3:34>>>
The next verse ties the households together: "They are descendants of one another. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing." The chosen are not scattered favourites. They form one line of descent and one pattern of religion, and Allah hears both the claims made about them and the truth of those who actually follow them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They are descendants of one another"**: the seed of one is offspring from another. Some follow the religion of the others, and the offspring of some are the descendants of others. These prophets and their followers were one family in declaring Allah's Oneness and doing good, and they passed noble character and virtues to one another.

**"And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing"**: statements from servants reach His hearing and their deeds fall under His knowledge, so selection remains His. Applied to Jewish claims of being Allah's children, beloved people and followers of His religion, He also knows their punishment and recognises genuine followers.

### Chosen is given, not inherited by blood alone

Adam and his seed are made of the same substance, yet the characteristic of being chosen, iṣṭifāʾ, comes from Allah and not from lineage or relation. The family connection is real, but it is not the cause of election. These houses are singled out because Allah set prophethood in their progeny and sustained Oneness and virtue through them. Descent explains how one messenger can be offspring of another. It does not explain why one is chosen, and the verse joins the genealogy directly to hearing and knowing so that the cause stays with Allah's choice rather than with human pedigree.

### In short

- The prophets and their followers are one continuous family in descent, in the declaration of Oneness and in inherited virtue.
- Allah Hears the claims people make about these families and Knows who actually follows their religion.
- Election itself, iṣṭifāʾ, is a gift from Allah, not a product of lineage.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:35>>>
A vow opens Maryam's story: "˹Remember˺ when the wife of ’Imrân said, “My Lord! I dedicate what is in my womb entirely to Your service, so accept it from me. You ˹alone˺ are truly the All-Hearing, All-Knowing.”" The woman was Hannah, the mother of Maryam, and she made the vow before she knew whether the child was male or female. The dedication expected a servant of the sanctuary, and the prayer asks only for acceptance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Remember˺ when the wife of ’Imrân said"**: remember, O Muhammad, when Hannah, the mother of Mary, spoke. She is introduced by her husband's name, while the coming verses show that the vow, the naming and the prayer of refuge are hers.

**"My Lord! I dedicate what is in my womb entirely to Your service"**: my unborn child is vowed as a consecrated offering, to serve Jerusalem's Sanctuary. Earlier prophetic codes allowed one child to be released from worldly work and assigned wholly to Allah, worshipping Him and attending His House without worldly chores. The newborn was expected to serve Bayt Al-Maqdis, Jerusalem's mosque, in God's way.

**"so accept it from me. You ˹alone˺ are truly the All-Hearing, All-Knowing.”"**: You hear my supplication and You know my intention. He is the Hearer of prayers and the Knower of the answer and of what lies in the womb. She did not know then whether she would bear a male or a female.

### How Hannah came to make the vow

Muḥammad bin Ishaq says childlessness preceded the vow. Hannah watched a bird feeding its chick, longed for offspring and begged Allah for a child. Her supplication was accepted, intercourse with her husband followed, and pregnancy began. A second narration makes her elderly before the longing, prayer and first awareness of conception. ʿImrān died while she was still pregnant.

When she became aware of her pregnancy, she made the child over to worship and service of Bayt Al-Maqdis. Her motherly authority is itself noted here. A mother holds a measure of guardianship over her child in education and training. She would never have vowed if she had no right of guardianship, and the naming in the next verse proves that a mother also has the right to give her child a name.

### In short

- Hannah, long childless, prayed for offspring and vowed the unborn child entirely to the service of Bayt Al-Maqdis.
- She expected to dedicate a servant of the sanctuary without knowing the child's sex, while ʿImrān died during the pregnancy.
- The vow implies a mother's guardianship in training and makes her the one who seeks acceptance directly from Allah.

<<<VERSE 3:36>>>
Birth confounds the vow's expectation: "When she delivered, she said, “My Lord! I have given birth to a girl,”—and Allah fully knew what she had delivered—“and the male is not like the female. I have named her Mary, and I seek Your protection for her and her offspring from Satan, the accursed.”" Hannah had hoped for a boy because only males were consecrated for service. She apologised for a girl, and Allah accepted this very girl and made her distinct from every other girl in the world.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When she delivered, she said, “My Lord! I have given birth to a girl,”"**: when she was delivered of what was in her womb, it was a baby girl. She had been hoping for a boy, because only males were consecrated to the service of God.

**"and Allah fully knew what she had delivered"**: what she bore was already perfectly known to God. A variant reading makes the words Hannah's confession: "God knows very well what I have given birth to."

**"and the male is not like the female"**: the male she asked for is not as the female bestowed on her. He was designed for the service of God, while she might seem unsuitable in strength and in the commitment to worship Allah and serve the mosque in Jerusalem, because of lesser physical ability, her private parts and the effects of menstruation. In the simpler phrasing, the male is not like the female in service and in weakness.

**"I have named her Mary"**: she gave her the name Maryam. A mother has the right to name her child, and the ayat testifies that a newborn may be named on the day of birth, as part of the law of those before us.

**"and I seek Your protection for her and her offspring from Satan, the accursed”"**: I crave Your protection and safety for her and for her seed, her children, from the outcast and accursed Satan. The offspring meant is ʿĪsā.

### Naming a child on the day of birth

The Prophet's Sunnah confirms the practice. He announced, "This night, a son was born for me and I called him by my father's name, Ibrahim." Both al-Bukhārī and Muslim preserve it. Anas bin Malik's newborn brother likewise received a date chewed by the Prophet ﷺ placed in his mouth and was named ʿAbdullāh, and other infants were named on their birth day.

Naming belongs with another rite. Through Qatādah and al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Samurah bin Jundub reports: "Every new born boy held in security by his 'Aqiqah, until his seventh day, a sacrifice is offered on his behalf, he is given a name, and the hair on his head is shaved." Aḥmad and the Sunan collectors preserve the hadith, and at-Tirmidhī graded it Ṣaḥīḥ. A second wording, "and blood is offered on his behalf", is described as more famous and established, and Allah knows best.

### The one child Satan could not touch

Allah accepted Hannah's supplication for refuge. ʿAbd ar-Razzāq recorded that Abū Hurayrah said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ declared, "Every newly born baby is touched by Shaytan when it is born, and the baby starts crying because of this touch, except Maryam and her son." Abū Hurayrah then said, "Read if you will: 'And I seek refuge with You for her and for her offspring from Shaytan, the outcast.'" The same statement is reported by the two Shaykhs, al-Bukhārī and Muslim: "Every new-born is touched by Satan and begins life by crying except for Mary and her son."

### Consecrated means free of every other master

A consecrated person, muḥarrar, owes bondage to no creature. God's prior decree frees such a one from preoccupation with anything besides the Real across every state. Hannah felt shame when the promised consecration produced a girl apparently unable to serve. Yet God's knowledge of the child exposed the comparison: maleness and femaleness differ outwardly, but acceptance caused miracles to flow from Maryam. Her plea succeeded in both daughter and son. Their story saved one company and destroyed another through trial, fitna. Even her prayer for refuge has an inward edge: protection was sought from God against any Satanic element hiding in her own speech.

Acceptance left visible effects in Maryam and her son. Through the pair, one company found salvation while another met destruction and trial, fitna. Even the refuge clause turns inward: she asked God to guard her speech itself from anything Satanic.

### In short

- Hannah apologised for a girl, expecting only boys to serve, yet Allah accepted Maryam and set her apart from every other girl.
- Maryam was named at birth, a practice the Prophet ﷺ confirmed, and naming is joined to the ʿaqīqah, its sacrifice and the shaving of the head on the seventh day.
- The prayer for refuge was answered visibly: Maryam and her son are the exception to Satan's touch at birth.

<<<VERSE 3:37>>>
The acceptance becomes visible at once: "So her Lord accepted her graciously and blessed her with a pleasant upbringing—entrusting her to the care of Zachariah. Whenever Zachariah visited her in the sanctuary, he found her supplied with provisions. He exclaimed, “O Mary! Where did this come from?” She replied, “It is from Allah. Surely Allah provides for whoever He wills without limit.”" Maryam grows under a prophet's care, in a room in the sanctuary, with food that does not follow the seasons.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So her Lord accepted her graciously and blessed her with a pleasant upbringing"**: He received Maryam from her mother with goodly acceptance as a result of the vow. He made her grow in a good manner, making her conduct becoming and her mannerism delightful, and He made her well liked among people. She would grow in a day by as much as a newborn grew during a year. She accompanied the righteous so she could learn righteousness, knowledge and religion.

**"entrusting her to the care of Zachariah"**: Allah made Zakariyya her sponsor and guardian for her benefit, so she could learn from his tremendous knowledge and righteous conduct. A variant reading makes the direction explicit: kaffalahā, "He gave Zakariyya charge of her", with God as the subject of the verb.

**"Whenever Zachariah visited her in the sanctuary, he found her supplied with provisions"**: every time he entered the miḥrāb, the sanctuary, the room and most noble seat in the temple where she worshipped, he found sustenance already with her.

**"He exclaimed, “O Mary! Where did this come from?” She replied, “It is from Allah. Surely Allah provides for whoever He wills without limit.”"**: she said, still very young, "From God. He sends it to me from Paradise." God provides abundantly, without reckoning and without consequence, for whomever He wills, in season or out of it.

### How Zakariyya became her guardian

To fulfil the vow, Maryam's mother brought her to Bayt Al-Maqdis and addressed its caretakers and worshippers, including Zakariyya. The dedicated child could not remain at home, so she asked them to receive her. Before the priests who kept Jerusalem's Holy House she declared, "This here before you is the dedication I offered." 

Guardianship became a contest because the imam's daughter had arrived. ʿImrān, the mosque's imam, had died during the pregnancy, although fatherhood and office would have made him the natural guardian. Zakariyya argued kinship: his wife was Maryam's maternal aunt and nearest female relative after mother. The others rejected automatic preference. Twenty-nine men went to the River Jordan and cast quills, stipulating that the one that stayed fast and surfaced would win. Zakariyya's quill did exactly that.

Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Jarir call Zakariyya the husband of Maryam's maternal aunt. Another description makes him her brother-in-law, supported by the Ṣaḥīḥ phrase, "I saw John and 'Isa, who are maternal cousins." Ibn Ishaq's version is judged plausible, meaning an aunt raised Maryam. Two Sahihs show the same principle in the Prophet's decision that ʿAmārah bint Ḥamzah live with her maternal aunt, Jaʿfar bin Abi Talib's wife: "The maternal aunt is just like the mother." 

Zakariyya built a ladder-accessed gallery room inside the temple, visited by nobody else. Reports disagree on nursing: one uses a wet-nurse, another says Maryam needed no suckling and soon sat and walked. Her dwelling stood beside the mosque in a good house. Zakariyya locked it when leaving and unlocked it upon returning, while supplying food, drink and oil.

### Fruit out of season

One repeated observation about provisions assigns seasons in reverse. As-Suddī, ʿAtiyyah al-ʿAwfī, ar-Rabīʿ bin Anas, Qatādah, ad-Daḥḥāk, Ibrāhīm an-Nakhaʿī, Abū ash-Shaʿthāʾ, Saʿīd bin Jubayr, ʿIkrimah and Mujāhid all describe summer fruits appearing in winter and winter fruits in summer. Corn culms and out-of-season grapes illustrate the inversion. Another telling credits Gabriel with bringing them. God gives continuously, in abundant measure and without inconvenience to whomever He chooses.

### What gracious acceptance looked like

Maryam received more than her mother had even wished. Gracious acceptance, qabūl ḥasan, may mean a singular obedience entrusted to her as God entrusts His friends, awliyāʾ. Contemporaries marvelled that so excellent a trust, ḥusn, belonged to a girl. Alternatively, graciousness lay in perfect care despite advance knowledge of slander that would touch God's name because of her. Enemy shaming does not concern Him. Passion can even savour blame when it occasions mention of the Beloved, careless of whoever speaks.

Acceptance also protected her from sin and error, ʿiṣma, until her later declaration, "Lo! I seek refuge in the Compassionate One from you! If you fear God" (19:18). Growth became straight obedience, constant preference for His pleasure, and eventually a fruit named ʿĪsā. A prophet's guardianship formed part of the gift, since Zakariyya supervised and protected her affairs. A revelation to Dāwūd (David) says: "If you see someone seeking Me, become a servant to him," in Arabic, in raʾayta lī Ṭāliban fa-kun lahu khādiman. Another sign is location: Maryam could only be found in the sanctuary, the miḥrāb. Servants who remain where worship occurs are rare and precious.

### In short

- Maryam was accepted, grew beautifully among the righteous, and was placed with Zakariyya, her maternal aunt's husband, after twenty-nine keepers cast quills in the Jordan.
- In her sanctuary room he found summer fruit in winter and winter fruit in summer, and she answered that it was from Allah, who provides without limit.
- Her gracious acceptance meant unique obedience, preservation, a prophet's guardianship, and a life found only in the miḥrāb.
