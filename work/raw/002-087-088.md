<<<VERSE 2:87>>>
Verse 87 sums up the long history of guidance offered to the Israelites and their response to it. "Indeed, We gave Moses the Book and sent after him successive messengers. And We gave Jesus, son of Mary, clear proofs and supported him with the holy spirit." Then comes the charge: "Why is it that every time a messenger comes to you ˹Israelites˺ with something you do not like, you become arrogant, rejecting some and killing others".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We gave Moses the Book"**. Allah gave Mūsā (Moses) the Torah, and the Jews changed and distorted it, defied its commands and altered its meanings. The verse reminds the Israelites how Allah in His mercy forgave their transgressions again and again and provided them with every means of guidance, beginning with a divine Book.

**"and sent after him successive messengers"**. After Mūsā, Allah sent messengers and prophets who followed his law. He revealed the Torah with guidance and light, and by it the prophets who submitted to Allah judged for the Jews (5:44). As-Suddī reported Abū Mālik's explanation that the verb qaffaynā means "We made succeed", while others said "We made follow". Both are possible, since Allah also says that He sent His messengers in succession (23:44). Among these prophets were Yūshaʿ (Joshua), Dāwūd (David), Zakariyyā (Zechariah) and Yaḥyā (John the Baptist), all of whom told the Jews that it was not enough to revere the Torah as a holy book, for its teachings had to be practised.

**"And We gave Jesus, son of Mary, clear proofs"**. Last in the line of the prophets of the Children of Israel came ʿĪsā (Jesus), who was sent with some laws that differed from parts of the Torah. For that reason Allah supported him with miracles: bringing the dead to life, shaping birds from clay and breathing into them so that they became living birds by Allah's leave, healing the sick, the blind and the leper, and foretelling the unseen, as Ibn ʿAbbās described. His clear proofs also included commands, prohibitions, and the Gospel itself, irrefutable evidence of his prophethood. All these signs testified to his truthfulness and to the truth of what he was sent with.

**"and supported him with the holy spirit"**. Rūḥ al-Qudus is Jibrīl (Gabriel). This is Ibn Masʿūd's explanation of the verse, and it is also the view of Ibn ʿAbbās, Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb, Ismāʿīl ibn Khālid, as-Suddī, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, ʿAṭiyyah al-ʿAwfī and Qatādah. Allah says of the Qur'an that the trustworthy spirit brought it down (26:193-194), and that the holy spirit brought it down from your Lord with the truth (16:102). The Arabic construction annexes the noun to its qualifying adjective, so that rūḥ al-qudus means "the sanctified spirit", and Jibrīl is so described because of the sanctity of ʿĪsā, whom he accompanied wherever he went. The poet and Companion Ḥassān ibn Thābit called him, in a couplet preserved in the hadith, "Jibrīl, the messenger of Allah, who comes to us, the holy spirit, the incomparable". Allah strengthened and helped ʿĪsā through Jibrīl in several ways: he was conceived through the breath of the angel, Jibrīl protected him from Satan's touch at his birth, the angel accompanied him constantly to defend him against the hostility of the Jews, and through him ʿĪsā was raised to the heavens.

**"Why is it that every time a messenger comes to you ˹Israelites˺ with something you do not like"**. Still you refuse to be upright. Whenever a messenger came with truth that your souls did not desire, that did not agree with your hearts and your religion, you reacted the same way. The question is a rebuke. The Children of Israel grew more defiant and envious of ʿĪsā and would not accept a difference from even one part of the Torah, although he told them that he confirmed the Torah and would make lawful some of what had been forbidden to them (3:50).

**"you become arrogant"**. You disdained to follow him, full of pride against the truth and looking down on the messengers of Allah. The Prophets commanded the Jews what differed from their desires and opinions, and they upheld the rulings of the Torah that the Jews had changed, which is why belief in them came so hard.

**"rejecting some and killing others"**. Some they called liars, such as ʿĪsā, and later Muḥammad ﷺ, and some they killed, such as Zakariyyā and Yaḥyā. The verbs are in the present tense, narrating past events as if they were happening now. Az-Zamakhsharī remarked that Allah did not say "killed" in the past tense because the Jews would still try to kill the Prophet ﷺ in the future, by poison and by magic. During his final illness the Prophet ﷺ said that he still felt the effect of the food he had eaten at Khaybar, and that now his aorta was being cut. Al-Bukhārī and others record this hadith.

### Jibrīl as the holy spirit in the Sunnah

Al-Bukhārī records from ʿĀʾishah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ set up a minbar in the mosque from which Ḥassān ibn Thābit defended him in verse, and that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ prayed, "O Allah, aid Ḥassān with Rūḥ al-Qudus, for he has defended Your Prophet." Abū Dāwūd records it in his Sunan, and at-Tirmidhī, who graded it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. Ibn Ḥibbān records in his Ṣaḥīḥ that Ibn Masʿūd heard the Prophet ﷺ say: Rūḥ al-Qudus has told me that no soul will die until it has used up its provision and its term, so have taqwā of Allah and seek your sustenance in the most fitting way.

### Why a people who revered the Torah killed its prophets

The Torah was God's Book revealed to the Jews, but gradually they stopped treating it as a source of guidance and came to regard it as a sacred relic of national heritage and a symbol of ethnic superiority, more a guarantee of salvation than a guide to earning it. The prophets who arose after Mūsā all insisted that believing in the sacredness of scripture is futile unless its message is lived out every day. The very people who held the Torah in such esteem could not tolerate these exhortations, because self-interest and worldly ambition ruled their lives while they passed themselves off as bearers of the true faith. As long as religion consolidated their worldly standing they welcomed it, but the pure message of truth looked to them like a threat to the hegemony they had built on religion. Egoism stood in the way of true religiosity. Instead of being the first to believe, they rejected the message and the messenger, scorned and persecuted God's prophets, called them impostors and even killed them. Their stubbornness in the worship of their own desires meant that whenever the prophets brought injunctions that did not please them, they denied the prophets, even ʿĪsā, or killed them outright, as they did Zakariyyā and Yaḥyā.

The devotional reading finds the root of it in one word. Allah delivered His address to them and made one messenger follow another, all calling to the same thing, but they listened to the callers with the ear of whim (hawā). Whatever their lower selves found pleasant they accepted, and whatever their whims found troublesome they rejected. They were marked by this self-serving desire and, worse, became its servants (ʿabadū). The attributes of the worshipper (ʿābid) became the thing worshipped (maʿbūd). What a terrible affliction lies upon them!

### In short

- Allah gave the Torah to Mūsā (Moses), sent a succession of prophets who judged by it (5:44, 23:44), and finally sent ʿĪsā (Jesus) with miracles and the Gospel.
- The holy spirit who supported ʿĪsā is Jibrīl (Gabriel), as Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah and others held, and as 26:193-194 and 16:102 show, and the Prophet ﷺ asked that Ḥassān be aided by the same spirit.
- Whenever a messenger brought what they disliked, the Israelites responded with arrogance, calling some liars and killing others, among them Zakariyyā and Yaḥyā.
- The present tense "killing" points forward to their attempts on the Prophet's ﷺ life, as az-Zamakhsharī noted, and the whole pattern springs from listening to revelation with the ear of whim.

<<<VERSE 2:88>>>
Verse 88 records the Jews' own excuse and Allah's answer to it. "They say, “Our hearts are unreceptive!” In fact, Allah has condemned them for their disbelief. They have but little faith".

### Phrase by phrase

**"They say, 'Our hearts are unreceptive!'"**. The Jews said this to the Prophet ﷺ mockingly. The word ghulf is the plural of aghlaf, something wrapped up in a cover, so the claim is that their hearts are encased and cannot take in what he says. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq reported Ibn ʿAbbās explaining it as "our hearts are screened". Mujāhid said "covered". ʿIkrimah said "there is a stamp on them". Abū al-ʿĀliyah said "they do not comprehend". Mujāhid and Qatādah reported that Ibn ʿAbbās read the word in a way that gives a different sense: our hearts are vessels already full of every kind of knowledge, and have no need of the knowledge you bring, Muḥammad. ʿAṭāʾ held this view too. In other words, our hearts can absorb any knowledge, yet they prove incapable of comprehending yours. The Israelites were arguing that their stubbornness was no fault of theirs, since nothing said to them could reach them or make sense to them. Scholars note the sarcasm in it. The Jews meant that their hearts were so well protected against Islam that it could never touch them, and this was their way of congratulating themselves on being staunch in their belief. The saying resembles the words of other deniers, that their hearts are veiled against what the Prophet calls them to, their ears are deaf, and a barrier stands between them and him (41:5).

**"In fact, Allah has condemned them for their disbelief"**. The word "nay" introduces the rebuttal. It is not as they claim. Allah has cursed them, removing them far from His mercy, expelling them from it, depriving them of every kind of righteousness and degrading them for their unbelief, which is not the result of any defect in their hearts. He has sealed their hearts as a punishment for their disbelief, and it is this, not firmness of faith, that keeps Islam out. What they boast of is a damnation, for they deny Islam, which is now the true religion, and cling to a religion that has been abrogated. In Sūrat an-Nisāʾ Allah says the same: for their saying "our hearts are unreceptive", it is Allah who has sealed their hearts for their disbelief, so they believe only a little (4:155).

**"They have but little faith"**. The Arabic adds an extra particle to emphasise how little. Scholars differ on what this littleness means here and in the parallel verse. Qatādah understood it as "only a few of them believe". Others said that their faith is minute, because they do believe in the Resurrection and in the reward and punishment that Mūsā (Moses) foretold, yet this faith does not benefit them, since it is overshadowed by their rejection of what Muḥammad ﷺ brought. Others held that the Jews did not truly believe in anything, and that "little is what they believe" means they do not believe at all, like the Arabic idiom "hardly have I seen the like of this", meaning never. One early gloss offers both: they believe neither much nor little, or, as it is also said, they do not believe in what you say at all.

### Why a little faith does not count

The little faith the Jews possessed concerned doctrines common to Islam and Judaism, such as belief in Allah and in the Day of Judgement. But they did not accept Muḥammad ﷺ as a prophet or the Qur'an as the word of Allah, so their faith was incomplete, and since a little faith is not acceptable they turn out to be unbelievers. When the verse calls this little faith īmān, it does so only in the lexical sense, for īmān means total certitude, even if it attaches to some things and not others. From the standpoint of the Sharīʿah such partial faith cannot be called īmān at all. The Sharīʿah accepts as valid only the faith that affirms with certainty each and every thing it requires one to affirm.

### Pretension bares its fangs

The devotional reading adds that if anything in them had been free of pretension, finding meanings in God's signs and scripture would have become easy. But when the truth must be recognised, the fangs of pretenders are bared. As the saying goes, when tears run down the cheeks, the one who truly weeps is told apart from the one who only pretends.

### In short

- "Our hearts are unreceptive" was a mocking boast, read by Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah and Abū al-ʿĀliyah as covered, stamped or uncomprehending, or, in Ibn ʿAbbās's other reading, as already full of knowledge.
- Allah's answer is that the real cause is His curse and seal upon them for their disbelief, as 4:155 and 41:5 confirm.
- Their "little faith" is read as a few believers, as a faint belief in Allah and the Last Day that cannot save them, or as no faith at all.
- Partial faith is not faith in the Sharīʿah's sense, which requires certain affirmation of everything it demands.
