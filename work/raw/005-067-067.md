<<<VERSE 5:67>>>
After the long account of hostility and distortion, the Messenger ﷺ is addressed directly: "O Messenger! Convey everything revealed to you from your Lord. If you do not, then you have not delivered His message." The command is complete, and so is the promise attached to it. Allah will protect him from the people.

The verse holds two things together. Nothing may be withheld because it is unwelcome or dangerous. Nothing need be feared from those who hate the message, because protection belongs to Allah rather than to guards.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O Messenger!"** addresses Allah's servant and Messenger Muhammad ﷺ by his title of office. The command is given to him as the one entrusted with revelation, and he fulfilled it in the best manner.

**"Convey everything revealed to you from your Lord."** One gloss lists what this included: speaking against their goddesses, denouncing their religion, fighting them and inviting people to Islam. Nothing was excluded because it was difficult or provocative.

**"If you do not, then you have not delivered His message."** makes the obligation indivisible. Ibn 'Abbas, in the report given by 'Ali bin Abi Talhah, explains that concealing even one revealed verse means the message has not been conveyed. A variant reading speaks of messages in the plural, and the explanation given is that concealing part of it is concealing all of it.

**"Allah will ˹certainly˺ protect you from the people."** promises protection, aid, support and victory over his enemies. He is told not to fear or grieve, since none of them will be able to touch him with harm. One gloss describes the protection as being from the Jews and others, and another as being from people who might try to kill him.

**"Indeed, Allah does not guide the people who disbelieve"** separates the Messenger's ﷺ duty from the outcome. He conveys, and Allah guides whom He wills. One explanation says He does not guide to His religion anyone who is not fit for it, and another describes those not blessed with guidance as people who have no desire to be guided.

### Did the Prophet ﷺ conceal anything?

Al-Bukhari records 'A'ishah saying that whoever tells you Muhammad ﷺ hid any part of what Allah revealed to him is uttering a lie, and she cited this verse. Muslim collects it in the Book of Iman, and At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i collect it in the books of Qur'anic commentary in their Sunan.

Al-Bukhari gives the short form here and the full narration elsewhere in his book. In the Two Sahihs 'A'ishah also says that if Muhammad ﷺ had hidden anything of the Qur'an, he would have hidden the verse reproaching him for concealing in himself what Allah would bring to light and for fearing people when Allah had a better right to be feared. That reference is given without a number in the account and is therefore described rather than cited.

Al-Bukhari also records Az-Zuhri's summary of the arrangement: the message comes from Allah, its delivery belongs to the Messenger ﷺ, and submission to it belongs to us. Each party has a distinct role, and none of them may be transferred to another.

### How was the delivery publicly attested?

The ummah of Muhammad ﷺ testified that he conveyed the message and fulfilled the trust when he asked them during the largest gathering of his life, in his speech at the Farewell Hajj. More than forty thousand Companions were present.

Muslim records Jabir bin 'Abdullah reporting the Messenger ﷺ saying on that day that the people would be asked about him, and asking what they would reply. They answered that they bore witness that he had conveyed, fulfilled and given sincere counsel. He kept raising his finger toward the sky and then pointing it at them, saying, O Allah, did I convey, twice.

The great address of that Hajj is described as both a constitution for Islam and the parting will of a prophet more affectionate and caring than a father or mother. After his instructions he asked whether he had conveyed their religion to them. When the Companions confirmed it, he called on them to be witnesses.

He also told those present to carry his message to those absent. The absent include people then alive who were not in that gathering and those not yet born. The means of reaching them was the spread of knowledge of the religion, a duty discharged by the great efforts of the Companions and their successors.

### What effect did that charge have on the transmission of hadith?

Under the influence of that parting instruction, the Companions treated his words and deeds as a weighty trust from Allah. They took care that not one sentence spoken by him should remain unconveyed to his community, and this holds under normal conditions.

Where someone withheld a particular hadith for a special reason or compulsion, he made a point of narrating it before his death to at least some of those around him, so that the trust would not remain a burden on him. A report about Mu'adh in the Sahih of Al-Bukhari is given as an example, that he narrated a hadith at the time of his death so that he would not be sinful for failing to pass on a trust.

This explains why the verse matters for the whole tradition that follows it. The obligation of complete delivery did not end with the Prophet ﷺ. It shaped how his Companions understood narration itself.

### Why did the command need such emphasis?

The preceding sections had described at length the crookedness, obstinacy and plotting of Jews and Christians. Two effects on a human messenger were possible. He might feel disappointed or constrained, so that the work of conveying slowed or fell short somewhere.

Alternatively, if he devoted himself single-mindedly to his mission without regard for hostility, harm or injury, the worst hardships at the hands of his adversaries were likely. The verse answers both risks at once. He is commanded emphatically to convey everything revealed to him, all of it, without hesitation.

Whether someone likes or dislikes it, accepts or refuses it, is not to worry him in his mission. Along with the command comes the good news that those disbelievers will be unable to harm him, because Allah Himself will protect him.

### What did the Messenger ﷺ say about the weight of this charge?

Al-Hasan reports the Prophet ﷺ saying that when Allah sent him with the message he felt oppressed by it, because he knew some people would give him the lie. He was apprehensive of Quraysh, the Jews and the Christians, and this verse was revealed.

Another account through Al-Hasan describes the charge as appearing very frightening when he was given the duty of conveying Allah's message, since those around him were bound to falsify and oppose him. When the verse came down, he was at peace.

The fear named here is not doubt about the truth of the message. It is the natural apprehension of a person told to announce things that powerful people will hate, before any guarantee of protection has been given.

### How was he guarded before this verse?

Ahmad records 'A'ishah describing the Prophet ﷺ as vigilant one night while she was beside him. Asked what was the matter, he said he wished a righteous man from his Companions would guard him that night.

Suddenly they heard the clatter of arms. He asked who was there, and the newcomer answered that he was Sa'd bin Malik, that is Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas, and had come to guard him. 'A'ishah says the Prophet ﷺ then slept and she heard the sound of his sleep. The hadith is in the Two Sahihs.

Another narration begins with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ being vigilant one night after he came to Al-Madinah, that is after the Hijrah and after his marriage to 'A'ishah was consummated in the second year of the migration.

A further account through 'A'ishah says the Prophet ﷺ was guarded until this verse was revealed, after which he raised his head from the room and told the people to go away, since Allah would protect him. At-Tirmidhi records it and calls it gharib, singly transmitted. Ibn Jarir records it as well, and Al-Hakim in his Mustadrak says its chain is sahih, authentic, though the two leading collections did not record it.

Another report through 'Ikrimah from Ibn 'Abbas says the Messenger ﷺ used to be guarded, with Abu Talib sending men from Banu Hashim every day for that purpose until this verse was revealed. When his uncle then wanted to send protectors with him, he said that Allah had protected him from jinn and human beings.

A further narration names Sa'd and Hudhayfah coming together to keep watch, and describes the Prophet ﷺ sleeping so deeply that 'A'ishah heard his snoring, after which the verse came and he put his head out from the collar of his garment to tell the people they could leave.

### What changed after the revelation?

Reports say that before this verse some Companions used to stay around him for protection, in the city and on journeys. After its revelation he relieved them all, since no security arrangements were needed any longer. Allah had taken that responsibility Himself.

One account says that after the verse no one dared to harm the Prophet ﷺ during his efforts to spread Islam. It adds that casual injury received in jihad battles is not contrary to this. The promise concerns the completion of the mission, not the absence of every hardship in combat.

### Which occasion is reported for the revelation?

The reports differ. Some connect the verse with the night watch and the end of guarding, as described above. Others connect it with the general command to convey, including the difficult matters of denouncing false worship and inviting people to Islam.

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri is also reported as saying that the verse was revealed on the day of Ghadir Khumm concerning 'Ali ibn Abi Talib. That report is preserved here as one transmitted account of the occasion.

These differing reports do not change the command itself. Whether the immediate occasion was a particular announcement or the ending of the guard, the obligation stated is complete delivery of everything revealed, and the protection promised covers the one who delivers it.

### What does the verse teach a person who conveys the religion?

Arabian society at the time was not irreligious. It was founded on religion, and many people held positions of prominence and leadership in its name. High religious standing was a mark of social status and a source of pride.

The Prophet ﷺ nevertheless endured the worst opposition, because a self-made religion had taken the place of the real religion of Allah. Centuries of tradition had produced seats of power and centres of vested interest. A call for pure religion therefore felt like an attack on the status of those who held it, and they feared the destruction of the old order that gave them prominence.

That situation is hard on a preacher. Open religious activity becomes equivalent to a struggle against the established religious authorities of the day. If he makes no compromises, he expects severe reaction, ridicule, degradation, impoverishment, violent action and the loss of all support and assistance.

Two paths then appear. He can take worldly considerations into account, in which case successful communication of Allah's word becomes impossible. Or he can disregard those constraints, in which case Allah's promised help enables him to concentrate on the task.

The promise given is that if he devotes himself fully to conveying, divine help will be sufficient against whatever difficulties the addressed community creates. The one who carries Allah's will should simply trust in Allah.

Opposition is natural and must be faced, but its sphere is limited by Allah's law concerning the trial of human beings. It can never happen that opponents gain enough control to stop the work or prevent its completion. Achieving the goal of that work is Allah's plan, so it will be completed. Acceptance by those addressed is their own responsibility, and it takes effect only to the extent they desire it.

### How does the closing statement complete the argument?

Guidance is not the messenger's to produce. Two other verses are cited with this one: guidance is not upon him, but Allah guides whom He wills (2:272), and his duty is only to convey while the reckoning belongs to Allah (13:40).

That is why the verse can demand complete courage and complete honesty at the same time. The Messenger ﷺ is responsible for delivery, not for the outcome in other people's hearts. Refusing to guide those who disbelieve is a statement about their choice, and it releases him from measuring his success by their acceptance.

### In short

- Everything revealed must be conveyed, and concealing one verse means the message has not been delivered.
- 'A'ishah's reports and Az-Zuhri's summary establish that nothing was hidden and that each party has its own role.
- The Farewell Hajj gathering provides the public testimony of the community that he conveyed, fulfilled and advised sincerely.
- Before this verse he was guarded by Companions and by men sent from Banu Hashim. Afterward he dismissed the guards on the strength of Allah's protection.
- Reports differ on the immediate occasion, including one connecting it with Ghadir Khumm, while the command and the promise remain the same.
- Whoever conveys the religion faces vested interests and must trust Allah's help, since acceptance belongs to those addressed and guidance belongs to Allah.
