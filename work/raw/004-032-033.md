<<<VERSE 4:32>>>
After the rules on inheritance, in which a male receives the share of two females, this verse deals with the feelings those differences can stir up: "And do not crave what Allah has given some of you over others. Men will be rewarded according to their deeds and women ˹equally˺ according to theirs. Rather, ask Allah for His bounties. Surely Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things."

The verse came as an answer to several remarks from women of the time. Aḥmad recorded that Umm Salamah, also written Umm Salmah and Umm Salama, said, "O Messenger of Allah, men go out to fight and we do not, and we receive half of the inheritance." Allah then revealed the words "do not crave what Allah has given some of you over others". At-Tirmidhī also recorded it. Her point was not an objection. She wished she could be a man and have the merits that belong to men. Other women said that if they had been men they would have joined the jihād and had its reward. Another woman asked the Prophet ﷺ, "A man gets a double share in inheritance, and a woman's witness counts as half of a man's. Does that mean that we get half the reward for all our acts of worship?" The verse answers both kinds of remark. Its first part answers Umm Salamah, and the words "men will be rewarded according to their deeds" answer the second woman.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And do not crave what Allah has given some of you over others"**. Allah forbids a person to crave the wealth, mount or wife of his brother, or anything that his brother has. Instead, one should ask Allah for abundant bounty, and say, "O Allah, give us the like of what You gave him, or better," leaving the matter to Allah. Another explanation says the verse was about Umm Salamah, who said, "If only it were prescribed for us as it is for men, so that we might be rewarded as they are." Allah forbade such talk. The merits in which He has made one group excel another are such things as praying in congregation, attending the Friday prayer, joining military expeditions, jihād, and enjoining good and forbidding evil. The "some of you" and "others" in the verse are men and women. A further explanation says Allah forbids what leads to mutual envy and hatred, in worldly matters or in religion.

The ruling is a wide one. The verse closes the door on craving things, traits and merits that Allah has given to others, because effort cannot gain them. When a person finds himself short in wealth, comfort, looks, merit or knowledge, he naturally feels a surge of envy, and he wants to be at least equal to the other person or ahead of him. But some merits have nothing to do with a person's own effort. They are gifts of nature, such as being a man, being born into a prophet's family or a ruler's, or being born beautiful. A person who lacks them cannot get them, however long he tries. A woman cannot turn into a man. A person cannot choose to be born into the family of a prophet, nor change his build, nor become tall and handsome.

When a man finds himself unable to match the other, his inner self starts toying with the thought that it would be wonderful if the other person lost those blessings too, so that they would be equal or he would be better off. This is envy. It is a shameful and damaging trait, and it causes many quarrels, disturbances, killings and ruin in the world.

**"Men will be rewarded according to their deeds and women ˹equally˺ according to theirs"**. Each person will earn the wages of his own works. There is a reward if the deeds are good and a punishment if they are evil. Others explain it as a share from the reward for what men have earned in the struggle and so on, and for women, from what they have earned by obeying their husbands and guarding their chastity. Another says that for men the reward comes from works of goodness, and for women from works of goodness done in their homes. One report from Ibn ʿAbbās, through al-Wālibī, says this part of the verse is about inheritance, and that each person will receive his due share. A short explanation puts it simply: neither is rewarded except through what they earn.

In the same way, the later part of the verse supports another point. Effort made to acquire real merits is not wasted. Each man and woman will receive a return for their own work.

**"Rather, ask Allah for His bounties"**. Do not wish for what others were given over you, since the decision has been made and will come to pass, and wishing does not change it. Instead, ask Allah for His bounty. He says that He will give it, since He is the Most Generous and the Most Giving. One short explanation says to ask Him for success and protection, and another says to ask for whatever you need.

The ideal conduct is this. When you see someone excel in one of life's graces, do not wish to match him in that particular excellence. Ask Allah for graces and blessings from Him. His grace takes different forms for different people. For some, wealth is a blessing, because they might have fallen into sin and disbelief if they had become poor. For others, the grace lies in the very poverty they are in, because if they became rich they might have sunk into thousands of sins. In the same way, for some the grace is power and influence. For others, the unnoticed and forgotten, the grace shows itself in that very state, since power and influence might have drawn them into many sins. So when you ask Allah, do not ask for some fixed kind of life. Ask for His grace, so that He, in His boundless wisdom, opens the doors of His blessings for you.

**"Surely Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things"**. The arrangement Allah has made is the essence of wisdom and justice. Every person who is created and maintained in any condition is in an arrangement that matches what wisdom and justice demand. But man does not fully know the results of what he does. Only Allah knows how fitting it is to keep a person in a given condition. Allah knows who deserves this life, and He gives him wealth, and who deserves poverty, and He makes him poor. He knows who deserves the Hereafter, and He guides him to the deeds that will help him succeed in it, and He knows who deserves failure, and He keeps him from righteousness and what leads to it. He also knows where merit is deserved and what each person is asking for, and He is the Knower of good and bad, reward and punishment, success and failure.

### Striving for good is different from craving

Verses of the Qur'an and sayings of the Prophet ﷺ urge people to compete in good deeds. That is an open field, where one may look at the record of merit that others have left and be encouraged to reach it. It concerns deeds within a person's control, which can be gained by learning, training and work. If someone is impressed by another person's intellectual gifts, practical life and excellent character, he may strive to gain those qualities. That is a desirable and praiseworthy thing, and the verse does not contradict it.

The verse also removes a mistake that a lot of people make without realising what they do. Some are so devoted to acquiring merits that cannot be acquired that they ruin their otherwise peaceful lives in this world. If it gets worse, and they are consumed by envy, which is to wish, "If I don't have this blessing, may it be taken from the other person," then they destroy their chance of salvation in the next life through the grave sin of envy.

There is another kind of person who has no courage, avoids action or has no sense of honour and shame. Such people do not even try to gain the merits that can be gained. When someone gives them good advice, they blame fate and destiny, which is only a cover for their own timidity and laziness. The verse settles this with a wise and just rule. A person's effort cannot gain merits that are outside his control, such as being high-born or handsome. Such things should be left to fate, and one should be content with Allah's decree and thankful to Him. Craving more than that is pointless, ineffectual and meddlesome, and it leaves nothing but sorrow. As for merits that can be acquired by effort, wishing for them helps only when it is backed by the necessary struggle. The verse promises that the effort will not be wasted and that each person will be repaid for his or her work.

### Be pleased with what you were given

Anyone whom Allah chose to be a man should be grateful, and anyone He chose to be a woman should be pleased to be exactly that. Had she been a man, she might have failed in the duties placed on men and ended up a sinner. A person made beautiful should be even more thankful, and a person with plainer looks should not grieve, but think of the good that may be hidden behind it. Beauty does not guarantee good, and it may land its owner in some unwelcome trial or corruption.

A true descendant of the Prophet ﷺ should thank Allah for the great blessing of that tie. A person who does not have it should not worry about it or long for it. It cannot be gained by effort, and if someone still insists on craving it, all he will get is sin, a broken heart and an aching soul. Rather than longing for a line of descent one lacks, a person does better to look to his own deeds and make them good and righteous. Once a person has built up such spiritual strength, he can get ahead of those with a higher lineage.

### Where the crimes of property and life begin

Earlier verses forbade unjust use of someone's property and unjust taking of life (4:29). Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ points out that this verse aims at closing the very source of those crimes. Theft, robbery and other unlawful ways of seizing property, and killing and destruction, all spring from one particular feeling. A person finds someone else ahead of him in wealth or material gains. He first feels a pinch in his heart and wants to catch up or to become superior. The strength of this desire then pushes him to commit these crimes. The Qur'an stops these crimes at their root by stopping the desire for what cannot be acquired.

### Why people differ

Differences between individuals in this world follow Allah's plan of creation. Some are less gifted in body and mind than others. Some are born into favourable conditions and some into unfavourable ones. Some have great resources, and some barely get by. The ones with less become jealous of those who are above them, and this leads to envy, hostility and conflict in social life. It is wrong to judge oneself or others only by outward things. They matter only in this world, and a person receives them only to leave them behind. What really matters is success in the Hereafter, which has nothing to do with worldly possessions. It depends on deeds done in this world for the sole purpose of seeking Allah's pleasure. So the wise course is to shield oneself from envy, to pray to Allah for strength, and to keep directing one's efforts toward success in the Hereafter.

Wherever people live together, as a family or as a country, someone has to lead. The authority rests undisputedly with one person. In Allah's plan for this world, man has been made head of the family and has been given the inborn abilities needed for this duty. The biological and physical differences between men and women fit that plan. Those who want to go against it will only cause distortion and discord. Nature will go on making men and women according to the divine scheme, whereby men are given the qualities needed as protectors and guardians, and women the quality of cooperation that lets them assist them without harm to the faculties nature gave them. But when men and women are handed contradictory roles in social activity, the result will be nothing but distortion in social life.

### The inner reading

Abū Bakr al-Kattānī said, "Whoever thinks he will arrive without effort is a wisher, and whoever thinks he will arrive by effort is a drudge." Whoever imagines he will reach the goal without toil is a wisher. As the saying goes, the incapable are those who follow their own whims and wish for Allah. Whoever imagines that he will reach the Sought by toiling and seeking is a drudge.

Shaykh al-Islām Anṣārī said:

> "He is not found by seeking, but the seeker finds. Until he has found Him, he will not seek. Whatever can be found by seeking is worth little. The servant finds the Real before the seeking, but for him seeking is the first step. The knower finds seeking from finding, not finding from seeking. In the same way, the obedient person finds obedience from self-purification, not self-purification from obedience, and finds the cause from the meaning, not the meaning from the cause. O Allah, since finding You comes before the seeking and the seeker, I seek because restlessness has overpowered me. The seeker is seeking, and the Sought was obtained before the seeking. This is a most marvellous business. More marvellous still, finding has become ready cash when seeking has not yet begun. The Real is seen, but the curtain of exaltedness is in place."

You are an ocean of loveliness, a ripple of beauty, the rule of generosity, the heart of life. The lovers who look for You burn with longing, like Alexander searching for the water of life.

Another reading of the verse is this: you wish for the rank of the masters without travelling their path, holding to their conduct and practising their practices. You want the condition of the great, yet you have not walked their road. You look for the Kaʿbah of union with eyes that have never struggled. You see how fortunate the friends are, but you have not seen what they went through. Your trouble is that you wish to be like those who took pains. You imagine that the pen of the covenant wrote the mark of friendship on the hearts of the passionate for nothing, with no effort. Yet every moment they have been wounded in spirit and heart, and they have drunk a cup mixed with poison. How many nights, to see You, I have been struck on the head by the dogs of Your street! But not everyone is fit for His wounds, and not everyone is worthy to suffer His pain.

May Allah's mercy be on those chivalrous ones who made their spirits the target of the arrow of His trial and knew their hearts as the litter for His burden of pain! In that trial and sorrow they hum this tune:

> If my spirit is worthy to suffer Your pain, this share of passion's good fortune is enough for me.

Yes, everyone's wound is in proportion to his faith, and everyone's burden in proportion to his strength. The stronger someone is, the heavier his burden. That is the hidden meaning of the verse where He says... and the passage breaks off there.

### In short

- Do not crave what Allah gave to others. The merits that are gifts of nature, such as being a man or a woman, one's looks, one's lineage and one's rank, cannot be gained by effort, and craving them breeds envy and many crimes.
- Each of men and women is rewarded for what he or she earns. Striving in deeds within one's reach is praised, and that effort is never wasted.
- Ask Allah for His bounty, His grace and not some fixed state of life, since only He knows what is good for you.
- The verse was revealed in answer to Umm Salamah's wish that women could join the fighting, and to a woman's question about whether her worship earns half the reward.
- Inwardly, the one who sits and wishes and the one who thinks effort alone gets him there are both wrong, and the Real is found before the seeking.

<<<VERSE 4:33>>>
The next verse repeats the law of inheritance and ends the old practice of inheriting by pledge: "And We have appointed heirs to what has been left by parents and next of kin. As for those you have made a pledge to, give them their share. Surely Allah is a Witness over all things." Some women, on learning that a man's share is the same as two women's, wished they were men so as to get the double portion. The law was therefore restated, and the verse says in few words that every share in it rests on perfect wisdom and justice. Human reason cannot take in all the factors that make for order or disorder in the created world, so it cannot reach exactly the elements of wisdom considered in fixing Allah's laws. So whoever has been given a share should be pleased with it and grateful.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And We have appointed heirs to what has been left by parents and next of kin"**. The Arabic word is mawālī. Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, as-Suddī, Zayd ibn Aslam, Qatādah, Abū Ṣāliḥ, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās, among others, said that it means heirs. Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported to have said that it means relatives. Ibn Jarīr remarked that the Arabs call a cousin a mawlā. The words "what has been left by parents and next of kin" mean what a person inherited from his parents and family. So the sense is: to every one of you, people, We have appointed relatives, such as your children, who will inherit later what you inherited from your own parents and relatives. Another explanation says that Allah has appointed heirs, relations, for each man and woman, to receive what parents and kinsmen leave.

**"As for those you have made a pledge to, give them their share"**. Those with whom you have a pledge of brotherhood were to be given their share of the inheritance, so as to honour the pledges that had been made, with Allah as witness. In the early days of Islam, this was the practice. It was later replaced when the Muslims were told to fulfil the pledges they had already made and to refrain from making new ones. One explanation says that these were allies with whom, before Islam, people had made agreements of mutual help and inheritance. They were to receive their portion, a sixth. Later it was abrogated by the verse that says that blood relatives are nearer to one another, and by 33:6. That verse says: "blood relatives are more entitled ˹to inheritance˺ than ˹other˺ believers and immigrants, unless you ˹want to˺ show kindness to your ˹close˺ associates ˹through bequest˺." Where other heirs exist, an agreement between two parties no longer touches the inheritance in any way.

Al-Bukhārī recorded that Ibn ʿAbbās explained it this way. When the emigrants came to Madīnah, an emigrant would inherit from the Anṣārī who was his brother, while the Anṣārī's own relatives would not, because of the bond of brotherhood the Prophet ﷺ had made between the emigrants and the Anṣār. When the verse "And to everyone We have appointed heirs" came down, it cancelled that pledge as far as inheritance goes. Ibn ʿAbbās said that the verse about those you have made a pledge to remained valid for cooperation and good advice, while inheritance was taken out of it. It became permissible to leave something in a will to a person who had previously had the right to inherit.

A different account is given about adoption. In the pre-Islamic period, people used to adopt men and youths, and set aside part of their wealth for them as they would for their own children. Allah ended this. It is not abrogated, though, if a man gives them something from the third of his wealth. Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab said that the verse was revealed about those who adopted men who were not their natural sons and then made them heirs. Allah ordered that something be set aside for them in the bequest, while the division of the estate was assigned specifically to the heirs who were immediate or close relatives. He denied adopted sons a share in the estate and allowed them a portion of the bequest instead.

**"Surely Allah is a Witness over all things"**. Allah is aware of all your circumstances and works. He has witnessed every one of you when you gave these pledges and promises.

### Keeping faith

In the beginning of Islam, Allah made relationships formed by mutual commitment equal to the ties of blood in deciding inheritance. That ruling was later replaced, but the rule to respect one another stayed. If this is how Allah treats a pledge between two men, what of the pledge a servant makes with Allah? He said: "Among the believers are men who have proven true to what they pledged to Allah" (33:23). People have recited the line: "Truly, those who have died to the religion of whims find the decree of death a sweet watering place."

### In short

- Allah appointed heirs for everyone from what parents and close relatives leave, and the shares He set rest on perfect wisdom and justice, so each person should be content with his.
- In early Islam, those bound by a pledge of brotherhood or alliance had a share of inheritance. That was later replaced, and the pledge remained valid only for help and good advice, with a bequest from a third of one's wealth still possible.
- Adopted sons are not heirs, but they may be given something in a will.
- Inwardly, if pledges between people are to be respected, so much more is the pledge a servant makes to Allah.
