<<<VERSE 4:32>>>
A verse born from a woman's question: "And do not crave what Allah has given some of you over others. Men will be rewarded according to their deeds and women ˹equally˺ according to theirs. Rather, ask Allah for His bounties. Surely Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things". The halves and doubles of the inheritance law had raised it, and the answer governs every heart that has ever looked at another's portion.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And do not crave what Allah has given some of you over others"** forbids the wish itself, in worldly affairs or religion, for what Allah has preferred some with, because such wishing breeds mutual envy and hatred. A man should not covet the wealth, the mount or the wife of his brother, nor anything his brother possesses. The decree has come to pass, and wishing does not change it.

**"Men will be rewarded according to their deeds and women ˹equally˺ according to theirs"** means each person earns wages for works of their own, a reward if the deeds are good and punishment if they are evil, as Ibn Jarīr explains. No one is rewarded except through what they earn, and one reading ties the line back to the inheritance itself, each person receiving the due share, as al-Wālibī reported from Ibn ʿAbbās.

**"Rather, ask Allah for His bounties"** redirects the desire. The ideal conduct, on seeing someone excel in a grace of life, is not to wish oneself at par, but to pray to Allah for blessings from Him. The word for ask is read wa-s’alū, or wa-ṣalū. The instruction to the tongue is: "O Allah, provide us with the like of, or better than, what You have provided him with", and then to consign the matter to Allah.

**"Surely Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things"** closes the door. Allah knows who deserves this life and gives him riches, and whoever deserves poverty He makes poor. He knows who deserves the Hereafter and directs him to the deeds that make him successful in it, and whoever deserves failure, He prevents him from righteousness and what leads to it.

### The women's question

Imam Aḥmad recorded that Umm Salamah said: "O Messenger of Allah, men go to battle, but we do not go to battle, and we earn one-half of the inheritance that men get." At-Tirmidhī also recorded this hadith. Her purpose was not objection. She wished she were a man, to deserve the merits restricted to men, and other women wished the same: had they been men, they could have ridden out with the fighters and earned what the fighters earn in jihād. Another woman asked the Prophet ﷺ whether the double share of men in inheritance and the half weight of a woman's witness meant that women would receive half the reward for all their acts of worship. The verse came down carrying both answers: "do not crave" answered Umm Salamah, whose name appears in the reports as Umm Salama and Umm Salmah, and "men a share... women a share" answered the woman's arithmetic. The things in which men were preferred are named: praying in congregation, attending the Friday prayer, joining military expeditions, jihād, enjoining good and forbidding evil.

### The unacquirable

The verse forbids coveting precisely those things that cannot be acquired by effort. When a person finds themselves lacking in wealth, comfort, beauty, merit or knowledge, envy surges up and urges them to draw level or push ahead. But some excellences have nothing to do with effort. They are blessings of nature: being a man, being of a prophet's family, being born to rulers, being born beautiful. A woman cannot become a man. No one can choose a prophet's household, change a physique, or collect chosen features by any quantity of medicine and treatment. Finding themselves incapable of matching the other, the inner self begins to wish the blessings snatched away from the other too, so that both stand equal or the other lower. That is envy, a shameful and damaging trait, the cause of endless disputes, disturbances, killings and ravages.

The verse's counsel is rest, not resignation into sin. Whoever Allah elected to be a man should be grateful. Whoever He elected to be a woman should be pleased with being just that, for had she been a man she might have failed the responsibilities placed on men and ended a sinner. The beautiful should be grateful, and the lesser in looks should not grieve, for beauty does not guarantee good and might land its bearer in trial or corruption. The true descendant of the Prophet ﷺ should thank Allah for the connection, and whoever lacks it should neither worry nor pine, since it cannot be acquired and the coveting earns only a broken heart and an aching soul. The best policy is one's own doings. Let them be good and righteous, and a person so strengthened stands ahead of those with higher lineage.

### The open field of deeds

The verse does not close competition. Other verses and the sayings of the Prophet ﷺ exhort people to excel each other in good deeds, an open field where looking at another's trail of merit is right, because these are deeds within a person's control, acquirable by learning, training and working. Whoever is impressed by another's intellect, practical life or excellent morals may strive to acquire the same, and that is praiseworthy. The verse's own second half supports it: effort in acquiring merits does not go waste, and every man and woman will get a return, a share of what they worked for.

Two errors are removed on the way. Some ruin an otherwise peaceful life in devotion to merits that cannot be acquired, and if they go further into envy, wishing the blessing away from the other, the chances of salvation in the life to come stand destroyed by the grave sin. Others lack courage or shame and do not even try for acquirable merits, and when counselled they blame destiny and fate, camouflaging their own timidity and inertia. The verse's rule sorts them out. What man's effort cannot reach is resigned to fate, with contentment and gratitude. What effort can reach is pursued, with the promise that the share of the work will not be wasted.

### Sealing the crimes at the root

At-Tafsīr al-Baḥr points out, referring back to the prohibitions of verse 29, that this verse aims to seal the very root of those crimes. Theft, robbery and the other illegitimate ways of taking property, and the killing and destroying of people, all sprout from one emotional attitude: finding the other ahead in wealth and material things, feeling the pinch, wishing to draw level or rise above, and then being pushed by the intensity of that desire into the crime. The Qur'an blocks the crimes by blocking the desire for the unacquirables at its root.

### Ask for the grace, not for a fixed thing

The grace of Allah wears a different face on every person. For some, wealth is the blessing, for were they paupers they might fall into sin and disbelief. For others, the grace lies in their very straitened circumstances, for were they rich they might succumb to a thousand sins. For some the grace is power and influence, and for others, the unremembered and the unattended, the grace manifests in exactly that state, since a look at reality would show them that power might have caused their fall. So the instruction stands: do not ask Allah for some fixed quality of life, ask for His grace, and He in His infinite wisdom opens the doors of His blessings. The arrangement He has made is the quintessence of wisdom and justice, and only He knows how suitable each condition is for the one kept in it.

### The inner reading

Abū Bakr Kattānī drew the verse onto the road of the seekers. The one convinced that arrival comes without expended effort is a wisher. The one convinced that arrival comes only through effort is a drudge. Whoever fancies he will reach the goal without toil is a wisher, and "the incapable are those who follow their own caprice and wish for God." Shaykh al-Islām Anṣārī said:

> He is not found by seeking, but the seeker finds. Until he finds Him, he will not seek. Whatever can be found by seeking is of little worth. The servant finds the Real before the seeking, but for him seeking is the earliest step. The recognizer finds seeking from finding, not finding from seeking. O God, since finding You is before the seeking and the seeker, I am seeking because unsettledness has overpowered me. The seeker is seeking and the Sought is obtained before the seeking. This is a most marvelous business.

Another reading of the verse takes the wishers to task: they want the station of the masters without traveling the masters' path or clinging to their conduct. They want the state of the great ones, but they have not gone on the great ones' road. They search for the Kaʿbah of union with eyes that have not undertaken struggle, and they see the great good fortune of the friends without seeing their tribulation. Their trouble is wishing to be like those who have taken trouble. The lovers seeking God are full of longing like Alexander seeking the water of life. Not just anyone is suited for His wounds, and the chevaliers who made their spirits the target of His trial's arrow hum in the midst of it that their spirits must be worthy of His pain, for that share of passion's good fortune suffices. The wound of each is measured by his faith, and the burden of each by his strength. The stronger the strength, the heavier the burden carried.

### In short

- The wish for what others were given is forbidden because it changes nothing and ripens into envy.
- Each is rewarded by their own earning, and the field of acquirable merit stays open to every man and woman.
- What cannot be acquired, sex, lineage, looks, is resigned to destiny with gratitude, and the pain spared by each state is known only to Allah.
- Coveting is the root of the property crimes and killings of verse 29, and the verse cuts it there.
- Ask not for a fixed thing but for His grace, which wears a different face for every person.
- On the inward road, wishing without toiling is the mark of the wisher, and the wound of the friends is bought with tribulation, never with wishing.

<<<VERSE 4:33>>>
The inheritance law set down, the verse seals its wisdom and remembers a brotherhood that once inherited: "And We have appointed heirs to what has been left by parents and next of kin. As for those you have made a pledge to, give them their share. Surely Allah is a Witness over all things".

### Phrase by phrase

**"And We have appointed heirs to what has been left by parents and next of kin"** states that for everyone there are appointed heirs of what parents and relatives leave. From Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān down through Ad-Daḥḥāk, as-Suddī, Zayd ibn Aslam, Qatādah, Abū Ṣāliḥ, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Mujāhid, the appointed ones, the mawālī, are read as heirs. Ibn ʿAbbās said the same, and was also reported to give mawālī the sense of relatives. Ibn Jarīr noted that the Arabs call the cousin a mawla. To all people, then, Allah appointed relatives who will later inherit what they themselves inherited from their own parents and family.

**"As for those you have made a pledge to, give them their share"** concerned the allies with whom covenants of mutual assistance and inheritance were made before Islam, and in the first years of the faith.

**"Surely Allah is a Witness over all things"** includes the pledges themselves. Allah witnessed everyone when the promises were given.

### The brotherhood that inherited

When the emigrants came to Al-Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ established bonds of brotherhood between them and the Anṣār, and the emigrant inherited from his Anṣārī brother, while the latter's own relatives did not, because of the pledge. The emigrant and his ally even stood together in jihād. Al-Bukhārī recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās that when this verse was revealed, it cancelled the pledge of brotherhood regarding inheritance. What survived of "give them their due portion" was the duty of cooperation and mutual advice, with inheritance excluded, while leaving a bequest to the person who had held the right before stayed open. In the pre-Islamic era people used to adopt men and youth and allocate them a portion of wealth as they would their own children, and Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab, the reporting authority for the occasion, said the verse was revealed about those adopters. Their due was apportioned in the bequest alone. The division of the estate went specifically to the immediate family and close relatives. The allies' share in that first period was a sixth, and the whole ruling of the pledge-inheritance was later abrogated by the verse on the womb-relatives (33:6). If other heirs exist, a pledge or mutual contract no longer affects the inheritance in any manner, though a gift to a former ally from a third of the wealth remains open.

So the shares stand on wisdom, as the previous verse insisted to Umm Salmah and the women who asked. Human reason cannot encompass the totality of factors holding the order of the universe together, and so cannot compute the wisdom inside the shares Allah determined. Whoever has been allotted whatsoever share should be pleased with it and grateful.

Read inwardly, the verse honours the covenant itself. At the faith's beginning He had ranked relationships of mutual commitment as equals of kinship ties in inheritance. That ruling passed away, but the rule of respect between the parties stayed. If that is the manner of commitments between men, what of the mutual covenant with God, of whom His servants say with the words of the Qur'an that they are men true to the covenant they made with Him (33:23)? Those who have died to the religion of whims, it has been said, find the decree of death a sweet watering place.

### In short

- Heirs are appointed for everyone from parents and near kin, and the shares rest on a wisdom reason cannot audit.
- The pledge-brotherhood of the emigrants and the Anṣār once carried inheritance, and the verse ended that, keeping cooperation, advice and bequests.
- Adopted men and allies receive from the bequest, not the fixed shares of the estate.
- Allah witnessed every pledge, and the covenant made with Him outlasts the ruling that passed away.
