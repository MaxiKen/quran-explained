<<<VERSE 3:18>>>
The surah’s opening established tawhid, the oneness of Allah. This verse returns to it and declares that “Allah ˹Himself˺ is a Witness that there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him.” The angels and people of knowledge bear witness as well. Their testimony points to Allah’s oneness, while the statement that He maintains creation in justice describes the order of His rule and sacred law. The verse repeats that there is no god except Him and names Him Almighty and All-Wise.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Allah ˹Himself˺ is a Witness”** means that Allah has made His oneness clear through signs and proofs. His existence, attributes, power, and creativity are visible in creation. The testimony, or shahadah, is not an uncertain human claim. Allah is the most truthful and just witness. He also bears witness to the revelation sent to Muhammad ﷺ (4:166).

A report tells of two Jewish scholars who came from Syria to Madinah. The place matched a description in the Torah of where the final prophet would live. They heard of a pious man called a prophet and went to meet Muhammad ﷺ. His appearance reminded them of the descriptions in their scripture. They asked whether he was Muhammad and then Ahmad. He said that both names applied to him. They asked who the greatest witness in Allah’s Book was. This verse was revealed as the answer, and the two men accepted Islam.

**“that there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him”** is the truth to which Allah bears witness. He alone is Lord and God. Every creature belongs to Him, depends on Him, and needs Him. Allah needs nothing from anyone. The verse repeats the declaration later to emphasize it.

**“and so are the angels and people of knowledge”** names two further groups who testify to Allah’s oneness. The angels carry out His commands. People of knowledge include the prophets, believers, and those who know Islam. The term ulū al-ʿilm, “people of knowledge,” can also include anyone who uses sound inquiry and reflection on the universe to recognize Allah’s unity, even if their knowledge was not acquired through formal religious study. Their testimony is a great honor, since it is joined to the testimony of Allah and the angels. Allah does not need their testimony. Its honor is for them.

Aḥmad recorded a hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ recited this verse at ʿArafāt and then said, “My Lord, I too bear witness to this.” Another report says that whoever recites it and then makes this declaration will be admitted to Paradise. The report describes Allah as promising that He will keep His promise.

**“He is the Maintainer of justice”** means that Allah constantly upholds His creation and acts with justice in everything. The universe’s order is presented as a sign of a single planner and of a Lord who places things rightly. If Allah maintains justice in the larger universe, human beings too are accountable to live by justice. The created world itself is in submission to His appointed order, and people are called to submit their lives to His plan.

**“There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise”** repeats the testimony and describes Allah as invincible in His power and wise in His actions, commands, and decisions. He cannot be overcome, and His wisdom governs what He creates and legislates.

### Why are the angels and people of knowledge named as witnesses?

Their testimony confirms the oneness that Allah has already made clear. The angels know Allah’s command and carry it out. The people of knowledge recognize the signs and proclaim the truth. The verse gives particular honor to those who know, while also widening the term to include people whose careful reasoning leads them to recognize Allah. Their witness is not needed to establish Allah’s unity. Rather, Allah gives them the honor of recognizing and affirming it.

### Reports about reciting the verse

A report says that after reciting the verse at ʿArafāt, the Prophet ﷺ added that he too bore witness. Another report from al-Aʿmash says that a person who recites the verse and follows it with that declaration will be admitted to Paradise. A report from Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī says that reciting Ayat al-Kursi (2:255), this verse, and the passage about Allah’s ownership (3:26) brings forgiveness and care for seventy needs, the least of which is forgiveness.

### An inward reading of the witness

An allusive interpretation pictures Allah bearing witness to Himself before any created being existed. There was no Adam, heaven, earth, light, darkness, visible creation, unseen creation, or human understanding. Allah has no beginning and will have no end. Nothing is outside His power, yet His reality cannot be grasped by imagination or human reasoning. He is the First, the Last, the Outward, and the Inward, and His own knowledge encompasses His essence and attributes.

The angels, prophets, and people of knowledge are honored when their testimony is joined to His. This does not mean Allah needs support from created witnesses. The honor comes from Allah’s generosity in enabling them to know Him. No one can fully comprehend His kingdom or the perfection of His divinity. Knowledge of Him is itself a gift. Allah singles out for His mercy whom He wills (2:105).

### In short

- Allah, the angels, and people of knowledge bear witness to Allah’s oneness.
- Allah maintains creation with justice and is Almighty and All-Wise.
- Reports describe the verse’s recitation at ʿArafāt and its use in supplication.
- An inward reading says that Allah’s knowledge of Himself is complete and that human knowledge of Him is a gift.

<<<VERSE 3:19>>>
The verse says that “Allah’s only Way is Islam.” Islam means submission to Allah. The explanations discuss both the shared submission taught by every prophet and the final way brought by Muhammad ﷺ. They also explain that disputes among people who received scripture arose after knowledge came to them, driven by envy and rivalry rather than a lack of evidence.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Certainly, Allah’s only Way is Islam”** uses a word that can mean religion or way. At its broadest, Islam is submission to Allah, belief in His oneness, the Last Day and final reckoning, Paradise and Hell, every prophet and messenger from Adam onward, and the commands they brought. In this sense, the followers of Nūḥ (Noah), Ibrāhīm (Abraham), Mūsā (Moses), and ʿĪsā (Jesus) were Muslims when they submitted to Allah and obeyed the messenger sent in their time. The Qur’an says that Allah assigned the same religion to Nūḥ and the prophets who followed him (42:13). Nūḥ said that he had been commanded to be among those who submit (10:72). Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) prayed to be made Muslims (2:128), and the disciples of ʿĪsā said they bore witness that they submitted (3:52). The Torah was revealed to Mūsā, and the Injīl to ʿĪsā.

The word can also refer specifically to the religion and shariah, the sacred law brought by Muhammad al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ. One explanation distinguishes the enduring shared faith from the particular laws given to different communities. It says that once Muhammad ﷺ was sent as the final prophet, earlier laws were superseded and the way that remains binding is the one taught by the Qur’an and Sunnah. The well-known hadith of Jibrīl explains Islam in this specific sense. The Holy Qur’an says that anyone seeking a way other than Islam will not have it accepted (3:85). Although the explanations begin with two possible meanings, both conclude that people are called to submit to Allah and follow the messenger sent to them. For the community addressed after Muhammad ﷺ, this means following his message.

An inward interpretation describes submission in three stages. The first is a declaration that protects a person’s life and property. The next is true belief, following the Sunnah, and acting faithfully. The highest is surrendering oneself to Allah’s decree without protest or turning away. Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl’s prayer to be made submitters (2:128), Ibrāhīm’s response to the command “Submit” (2:131), and Yūsuf’s (Joseph’s) prayer to die in submission (12:101) are given as examples of this complete surrender.

**“Those who were given the Scripture did not dispute ˹among themselves˺ out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them”** means that their disagreements arose after revelation and evidence had reached them. They did not differ because guidance was absent. Envy, hatred, rivalry, and fear of losing authority led some to oppose even what they knew to be true. One explanation says that some feared losing their influence among the community. Their disputes were therefore acts of transgression against knowledge.

**“Whoever denies Allah’s signs, then surely Allah is swift in reckoning”** warns that rejecting Allah’s revelations brings judgment. Some explanations say the reckoning begins after death, in barzakh, the state between death and resurrection. The full account of deeds will take place on the Day of Judgment, when denial and disputation are exposed. The verse says Allah is swift in reckoning. Nothing about a person’s denial is hidden from Him.

### What does “Islam” mean here?

The word can mean submission to Allah shared by all prophets, or the specific religious law brought by Muhammad ﷺ. The explanations preserve both senses. Earlier communities were Muslims when they followed the revelation sent to their prophet. The religious laws could differ across prophetic eras, and earlier laws were later superseded. In the period after Muhammad ﷺ, the message brought by him is the way to submit to Allah.

One explanation rejects the idea that all religions can be treated as equally valid paths to salvation merely because a person does good deeds. It calls kufr, disbelief in a basic principle of Islam, rebellion against Allah. It argues that submission and obedience to Allah are essential, and that denying a basic principle of Islam cannot be made acceptable by other good actions or outward morality. It cites a verse saying that some people’s deeds will have no weight on the Day of Judgment (18:105). This is presented as a firm claim about the meaning of the verse, not as an account of every disagreement over the word Islam.

### Why did people who had scripture dispute?

The verse says the dispute followed knowledge. The explanations attribute it to envy and rivalry, including fear of losing power and influence. Some people knew what their scripture said about Muhammad ﷺ but resisted accepting his prophethood. Their opposition was not caused by a lack of evidence, but by a refusal to accept its consequences.

### In short

- Islam means submission to Allah, a call shared by all prophets.
- The explanations also use Islam for the final way brought by Muhammad ﷺ, whose message is followed after his coming.
- Those who had scripture disputed after knowledge reached them because of envy, rivalry, and attachment to authority.
- Allah’s reckoning is certain, and rejection of His signs has consequences.

<<<VERSE 3:20>>>
The verse tells the Prophet ﷺ what to say if people dispute with him: “I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers.” It then calls the People of the Scripture and the unlettered people to submit as well. If they do and follow the Prophet’s sacred law, they are guided and set on the path. If they turn away, the Prophet’s duty is only to deliver the message. Allah sees every servant and will judge their response.

### Phrase by phrase

**“So if they argue with you ˹O Prophet˺”** refers to disputes about Allah’s oneness and the truth of Islam. The surah has already answered the claims made against it. The Prophet ﷺ is told not to be consumed by arguments that do not change the truth.

**“say, ‘I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers’”** means that the Prophet ﷺ and those who follow him devote their worship to Allah alone and have accepted His guidance. One explanation highlights the word “my face” in the underlying expression, since the face is the most honored part of a person. Once it turns in submission, the rest of the person follows. The followers accept the same faith and way. The Prophet ﷺ also describes his way as a call to Allah, which he and those who follow him carry out with clear knowledge (12:108).

**“And ask those who were given the Scripture and the illiterate ˹people˺, ‘Have you submitted yourselves ˹to Allah˺?’”** extends the call to Jews and Christians, as well as the Arab idolaters who were not given a scripture. The question is an invitation to submit sincerely and follow the message brought by Muhammad ﷺ.

**“If they submit, they will be ˹rightly˺ guided”** means that accepting Allah’s message and following the Prophet ﷺ leads them from error to guidance.

**“But if they turn away, then your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺”** comforts the Prophet ﷺ. He is responsible for conveying Allah’s guidance, not for forcing people to accept it. Their return and reckoning belong to Allah. One explanation notes that this statement preceded the later command to fight, so the immediate duty described here is to deliver the message.

**“And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants”** means He fully knows who accepts and who rejects, and will judge each person accordingly. His knowledge includes their actions, intentions, and response to the call. His perfect wisdom means that He is not questioned for what He does, while His servants will be questioned (21:23).

### Is the message meant for all people?

The explanations say Muhammad ﷺ was sent to all people, not only to one group. The Qur’an calls him a messenger to all humanity (7:158) and describes him as a warner to all peoples (25:1). Two hadith collections and others report that he sent letters to rulers and communities, including Arabs and non-Arabs, People of the Scripture and those without a scripture.

Muslim records Abū Hurayrah’s report that the Prophet ﷺ warned that anyone from his ummah, Jewish or Christian, who hears his message and dies without faith faces the Fire. The Prophet ﷺ is also reported to have said that he was sent to all peoples, described in one saying as the red and black, and that earlier prophets were sent to their own people while he was sent to all humanity.

### What is the Prophet’s duty when people refuse?

The verse separates the Prophet’s duty from the response of his audience. He conveys the message clearly. Whether people accept or reject it is not his responsibility to control. Allah sees what they do and will decide with full knowledge. An inward reading applies Allah’s taṣrīf, His directing of circumstances, to the difference between people’s states. The caller should continue advising in public and private, while not allowing the varied responses of people to unsettle the heart. The task is to counsel and deliver, while Allah governs the outcome.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ and his followers submit to Allah alone and invite others to do the same.
- The call is addressed to the People of the Scripture and to those without a revealed scripture.
- If people refuse, the Prophet’s duty is still to convey the message. Allah sees and judges every servant.
- Muhammad ﷺ is described as a messenger to all humanity.

<<<VERSE 3:21>>>
Following the call to submit in the previous verse (3:20), this verse condemns those who deny Allah’s signs, kill prophets unjustly, and kill people who call for justice. It announces “a painful punishment” and leads into the description of wasted deeds and helplessness (3:22). The explanations connect these actions with repeated rejection of revelation and arrogance toward those who called people to truth and fairness.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Indeed, those who deny Allah’s signs”** refers in one explanation to people who reject the message of Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur’an. It also includes those who deny earlier revelation, such as the Injīl. Their rejection is part of a larger refusal to accept messengers and follow what Allah revealed.

**“kill the prophets unjustly”** condemns killing messengers who had committed no crime and were calling people to the truth. Another reading of the verb can mean fighting against the prophets. The verse stresses that they were opposed without right.

A report attributed to the Prophet ﷺ and narrated by Ibn Abī Ḥātim says that the Children of Israel killed forty-three prophets at one time. One hundred and seventy righteous elders stood up and urged them to uphold justice, and they too were killed that same day. The report illustrates the severity of the rejection described.

**“and kill people who stand up for justice”** refers to those who call others to fairness and just conduct. One explanation describes them as people who encourage moderation in actions and morals. Another says they called people to Allah’s oneness. Killing those who urge justice is presented as an extreme act of arrogance and defiance.

**“give them good news of a painful punishment”** uses the language of good news sarcastically. The punishment is painful and humiliating. The explanations say that rejection of truth and arrogance toward people lead to disgrace in this life and punishment in the Hereafter. An inward reading describes the offenders as having been abandoned to deprivation, or khidhlān, then moved from one state of disgrace to another and finally to punishment.

### Why is punishment called good news?

The phrase is deliberately ironic. These people expected to prevail, yet the message delivered to them is a painful and humiliating outcome. Their deeds and status will not protect them from the consequence of rejecting truth and opposing those who call for justice.

### In short

- The verse condemns denial of revelation, unjust killing of prophets, and killing those who call for justice.
- A report describes forty-three prophets and one hundred and seventy righteous elders being killed on the same day.
- The “good news” of punishment is sarcastic and announces humiliation and pain.

<<<VERSE 3:22>>>
The verse says that the people described just before it (3:21) have deeds that “are wasted in this world and the Hereafter,” and that “they will have no helpers.” Good works cannot compensate for rejecting Allah’s signs and committing grave injustice. They will receive no reward for deeds that lacked faith, and no one will protect them from punishment.

### Phrase by phrase

**“They are the ones whose deeds are wasted in this world and the Hereafter”** means their good actions have become invalid and will bring no lasting benefit. The explanations include acts of charity and kindness to relatives among the works that fail when they are not joined to faith. Their denial, killing, and rejection of justice undo the value they expected their deeds to have.

An inward reading says that they have lost Allah’s help in both abodes. Their works do not reach their hopes in this life, and they find no success or realization of their hopes in the next. The rejection of Allah’s power and the failure to bear witness to His oneness leave them without support.

**“And they will have no helpers”** means there will be no protector who can save them from Allah’s punishment. Their position, allies, and good works cannot rescue them.

### In short

- Their deeds bring no reward in this life or the next because they rejected faith and justice.
- No helper or protector can save them from Allah’s punishment.
