<<<VERSE 3:18>>>
The surah now returns to the Oneness with a list of witnesses: "Allah ˹Himself˺ is a Witness that there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—and so are the angels and people of knowledge. He is the Maintainer of justice. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise". Allah's own testimony comes first, and Allah is sufficient as a witness. The angels and the possessors of knowledge join a testimony they did not create, and the repetition fixes the point before the question of religion is taken up in the next verse.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah ˹Himself˺ is a Witness that there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him"**: Lordship and godhood belong to Him alone over every created thing. All else serve Him, depend on Him and need Him, whereas He is rich beyond needing anyone. Proofs and signs have made this plain to creation. It is the greatest testimony possible, affirming Allah's sole right to worship and the perfection of justice across creation and sacred law alike. Legislative and universal signs converge on His divinity.

**"and so are the angels and people of knowledge"**: angels affirm it. So do possessors of knowledge among prophets and believers, by conviction and by speech. Angels stay near Allah and execute His instructions, testifying with knowledge and sight that He alone merits worship. Ulū al-ʿilm, people of knowledge, are first the prophets and those formed by Islam's knowledge.

**"He is the Maintainer of justice"**: Justice governs every act by which He maintains creation. The Arabic participle, qāʾiman, stands in the accusative as a circumstance, governed by an implied sense of being alone in the upholding: He alone is upholding His creatures with justice, constantly and uniquely.

**"There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise"**: the statement is repeated for emphasis. The Almighty does not submit to weakness, because of His might and greatness, and cannot be overpowered, while the All-Wise is wise in His creation, planning and legislation, in all His statements, actions and decrees. Allah says the same of His own witnessing elsewhere: "But Allah bears witness to that which He has sent down (the Qur'an) unto you (O Muhammad ﷺ)" (4:166).

### The honour given to the possessors of knowledge

Al-Ghazālī found in this verse a great honour for the scholars, because Allah mentions their witness together with His own and with that of the angels. The phrase may also be taken more widely. It may mean everyone who, by inquiry on sound intellectual principles or by reflecting on this universe, reaches knowledge of the unity of Allah, even if he is not a scholar by formal training in the traditional sense. The testimony does not change in either case. It is borne by the Being whose being and attributes, and the manifestations of whose power and creativity across the world, are themselves open signs of His Oneness, and it is confirmed by His messengers and Books.

### Why two Jewish scholars asked for this verse

The verse is also called the verse of shahādah, witness. Al-Baghawī reports that two leading Jewish scholars came to Madinah from Syria. Looking at the township, they told one another that its appearance matched the prophecy in the Torah that the last of the prophets would live there. They heard that a very pious man lived there whom people called a prophet, and they went to see the Prophet ﷺ. His appearance reminded them of every attribute the Torah had predicted. They said to him, "You are Muhammad?" He said, "Yes." They said, "You are Ahmad?" He said, "Yes, I am Muhammad and Ahmad." Then they said they had one question, and if he answered it correctly they would embrace Islam. He told them to ask. They asked, "Which is the greatest witness in the Book of Allah?" This verse was revealed as the answer, he recited it to them, and both embraced Islam at once.

Reports attach promises to its recitation. In the Musnad of Aḥmad, the Prophet ﷺ recited this verse at ʿArafat and then said, "And to that, O my Lord, I too am a witness." Al-Aʿmash relates that whoever recites the verse and says those words will be addressed by Allah to the angels on the Last Day in these terms: My servant has made a promise while I am foremost in keeping a promise, so let My servant be admitted into Paradise. Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī narrates that the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever recites Āyat al-Kursī, "Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him" (2:255), the verse Shahidal-lāhu (3:18) and the verse of the kingdom up to "without measure" (3:26-27) will have all his sins forgiven, be admitted to Paradise, and have seventy needs taken care of, plenty of them, the simplest being forgiveness itself.

### The witness before there was anything to witness with

An inward reading reaches behind creation itself. Self-witness belongs to the Real: He celebrates Himself and attests His attributes fittingly, proclaiming existence, independence, self-subsistence and permanence. Back then no creature could refuse, ignore or recognise anything. Intellect had not arrived, heavens and space did not exist, and neither brightness nor shadow had appeared. Adam was absent, as were universe, air, land, sea, light and darkness. Still the Lord of the Worlds spoke in majestic measure, witnessing a unity without peer. He remains unchanged and everlasting. First and Last, Outward and Inward, He knows beings and non-beings. As Outward He dominates through compelling greatness; as Inward He escapes imaginative reasoning about "how". 

Creaturely witness therefore adds no missing support. Its gift is felicity for witnesses themselves. Angels, prophets and knowers receive astonishing eminence when Allah joins their testimony to His own. Only His exaltedness truly recognises Him and grasps His unity; heaven, earth, both worlds, Paradise and Hell cannot measure His kingdom's permanence or divinity's perfection. "Who knows You? It is You who know You, You. No one knows You. You alone know You." He chose their felicity, set them apart, caressed them and opened recognition to them. "And God singles out for His mercy whomsoever He will" (2:105).

The universe makes the same confession in action. It is run by its Master through unseen agents, the angels, and it presents itself as one completely unitary system, which indicates a single Planner. Every part stands in a state of surrender, performing its function according to the appointed plan, and that order, in which everything is in its right place, proves that its Lord loves justice and righteousness. The surrender required of people is the same performance, now chosen.

### In short

- Allah, the angels and the possessors of knowledge witness that none deserves worship but Him, and He upholds creation in justice.
- The verse honours the scholars by joining their witness to His, though His own witness needed no support, and it answered two scholars from Syria who asked for the greatest witness in the Book.
- Before any created thing existed, the Real witnessed to the Real, and He remains the First and the Last, the Outward and the Inward.

<<<VERSE 3:19>>>
The next verse names the religion that follows from that testimony: "Certainly, Allah’s only Way is Islam. Those who were given the Scripture did not dispute ˹among themselves˺ out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them. Whoever denies Allah’s signs, then surely Allah is swift in reckoning". The first sentence closes every other road. The second explains why the road was refused by people who had already received Scripture. The third sets the reckoning in motion.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Certainly, Allah’s only Way is Islam"**: there is no religion accepted with Allah from any person except Islam. Islam here names submission, obedience to the commands of Allah in complete surrender, believing in all the prophets of all times and in what they brought, and acting accordingly. It includes obeying all the messengers up to Muhammad ﷺ, who finalised their commission and closed all paths to Allah except through himself. The verse is also read, with anna in place of inna, as a continuation of the testimony before it: shahida Llāhu, God bears witness, anna ad-dīna ʿinda Llāhi al-islām, that religion with God is Islam.

**"Those who were given the Scripture did not dispute ˹among themselves˺ out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them"**: the Jews and Christians divided in religion, some affirming God's Oneness and others rejecting it. They did so only after Allah had sent messengers and revealed Books with the proofs that should have prevented it. The cause was transgression and baghy, resentful envy, hatred and enmity between them, in which some wronged others and some defied even those they hated when the hated were right. They split into groups and sects after evidence had been established, out of envy and greed for the world.

**"Whoever denies Allah’s signs, then surely Allah is swift in reckoning"**: whoever rejects what Allah sent down in His Book will be punished for his rejection, reckoned for his denial and tormented for defying His Book. The swiftness can be felt at once, because reckoning begins soon after death, in the state called barzakh. The detailed accounting waits for the Day of Judgment, when a person answers for his doings in the minutest detail.

### One dīn, many laws

The word dīn has several senses in Arabic, and one of them is "the way". In the Qur'an's own usage it stands for the principles and injunctions common to all prophets, from Adam to the last of them, Muhammad al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ. Subsidiary injunctions, which differed by age and community, are called sharīʿah, minhāj or, in later terms, madhhab. Allah says: "Allah made you follow the same religion with which He bound Nuh (and other prophets)" (42:13).

That shared dīn affirms Allah as perfect in Being and attributes, flawless, and alone worthy of worship, with belief held inwardly and spoken outwardly. It also affirms judgment's Day, final accounting, reward and punishment, Paradise and Hell. Every messenger sent by Him and every commandment carried by them belong inside it, again believed in heart and confessed by tongue.

At root Islam means handing oneself over to Allah in obedience. Anyone who believed his own age's prophets and obeyed Allah through them could rightly be called Muslim, with Islam as his religion. Nūḥ (Noah) said, "I have been commanded that I be from among the Muslims" (10:72). Ibrāhīm (Abraham) described himself and his community the same way: "Our Lord, make us Muslims, the submitting ones to Thee, and from our progeny a community of Muslims submitting to Thee" (2:128). The disciples of ʿĪsā said, "And bear witness that we are Muslims" (3:52). Since Allah sent Muhammad ﷺ, whoever meets Allah by a path other than his meets Him by one that will not be accepted. "And whoever seeks a religion other than Islam, it will never be accepted of him" (3:85).

In its second sense the word names the law brought finally by the last prophet, which abrogated all previous ways and stands until the Day of Judgment, and which the Prophet ﷺ explained in the well-known hadith of Jibrīl (Gabriel). Both readings end in the same place. In the age of Nūḥ, Islam was what Nūḥ brought. In the age of Ibrāhīm, what Ibrāhīm brought. In the age of Mūsā (Moses), Islam took the form of the tablets of the Torah and his teachings. In the age of ʿĪsā (Jesus), it came as the Injīl and his teachings. After the last prophet, the injunctions of earlier codes that were abrogated no longer count as Islam at all, and Islam is what took shape on the pattern given by the Qur'an and the Sunnah.

### Why good deeds outside submission do not save

These verses cut at the root of an approach that places Islam and kufr, disbelief, on one footing in the name of tolerance, and claims that every faith in the world, Judaism, Christianity or even paganism, can be a source of salvation provided its followers do good deeds and keep good morals. The material calls this a veiled attempt to empty Islam of substance and to make it something that can be moulded to fit any religion, even disbelief. Light and darkness are not the same, and it is absurd to suppose that Allah loves disobedience and rebellion as He loves obedience and submission. Whoever denies even one basic principle of Islam is, without any shadow of doubt, a rebel against Allah, however impressive his other deeds and formal morality may look. Salvation in the Hereafter depends first on obedience to Allah and His Messenger. For those who remain deprived of it, "We shall not assign weight to their deeds on the Day of Judgment" (18:105).

### Why the People of the Book disputed a prophet they recognised

After declaring the religion, the verse explains the dispute over the prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ. The People of the Book did not act from doubt. Through their own Scriptures they knew the truth of the matter, but they feared losing ground to the Muslims. Malice toward Muslims, love of power and influence, and an arrogant self-image as traditional leaders drove the altercations. The exposition of what stood in their Scripture had already come to them, and the difference broke out after that knowledge, through transgression among themselves. Where the truth is refused in that way, the reckoning is swift.

### Three stations of submission

Approved religion means looking toward Allah as servants, worshipping by His decree, seeking His approval and returning to Him. Its submission has three stations. First comes public acknowledgement protecting blood and property. The speaker's neck escapes the sword and wealth remains his regardless of conformity, hypocrisy, following or innovation. Right belief, loyalty in deeds and adherence to Sunnah mark the second. Surrender crowns the path at the third station, pleasing God and sheltering recognition. It casts a person onto the Real's high threshold, approves His decree without protest or aversion, follows Him and treats His decision with reverence.

That third station is what Abraham asked for himself and Ishmael: "Our Lord, make us submitters to Thee!" (2:128). It is what he answered when it was said to him, "Submit!", and he said, "I submit to the Lord of the worlds" (2:131). And it is alluded to in the words told of Joseph: "Receive me as a submitter and join..." (12:101).

### In short

- The only religion accepted with Allah is Islam: one dīn of submission shared by every prophet, while the laws that carry it changed by age and were completed in Muhammad ﷺ.
- The People of the Book split only after knowledge came, through envy, malice and love of position, so their deeds outside submission carry no saving weight (18:105).
- Reckoning is swift: it begins in barzakh after death and is completed in detail on the Day of Judgment.

<<<VERSE 3:20>>>
When argument continues after recognition, the Prophet ﷺ is told what to say: "So if they argue with you ˹O Prophet˺, say, “I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers.” And ask those who were given the Scripture and the illiterate ˹people˺, “Have you submitted yourselves ˹to Allah˺?” If they submit, they will be ˹rightly˺ guided. But if they turn away, then your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺. And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants". The answer to the dispute is not more dispute. It is a declaration, an invitation to both audiences at once, and a limit placed on the caller's responsibility.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So if they argue with you ˹O Prophet˺, say, “I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers.”"**: when Jews and Christians dispute tawḥīd, His Oneness, do not trade argument indefinitely. State the stance. My worship is purified for Allah alone, alongside no partner, rival, offspring or companion, and believers with me share that religion and creed. Literally the claim is "I have surrendered my countenance". Wajh, the face, is picked for its nobility because bodily surrender soon follows facial surrender and submission. Allah puts the same claim in the Prophet's mouth elsewhere: "Say (O Muhammad ﷺ): 'This is my way; I invite unto Allah with sure knowledge, I and whosoever follows me...'" (12:108).

**"And ask those who were given the Scripture and the illiterate ˹people˺, “Have you submitted yourselves ˹to Allah˺?”"**: say to the Jews and Christians, and to the unlettered Arab idolaters, "Have you too surrendered?", which carries the force of a command: Submit! The caller is sent to both groups at once, to the People of the two Scriptures and to the pagan Arabs, with one religion, one way and one law.

**"If they submit, they will be ˹rightly˺ guided. But if they turn away, then your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺"**: surrender like yours brings guidance out of error. Turning away sends accounting, return and destination back to Allah. Your obligation stops at delivery.

**"And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants"**: He has perfect knowledge of who deserves guidance and who does not. He guides whom He wills and allows whom He wills to stray, with perfect wisdom and unequivocal proof in all of it, because "He cannot be questioned for what He does, while they will be questioned" (21:23). He sees those who believe and those who do not, and He requites His servants for their deeds. This address was given before the command to fight them was revealed.

### A message to all mankind

Similar verses plainly prove Muhammad's message reaches all creation. Allah says, "Say (O Muhammad ﷺ): 'O mankind! Verily, I am sent to you all as the Messenger of Allah'" (7:158), and "Blessed be He Who sent down the criterion to His servant that he may be a warner to the 'Alamin (mankind and Jinn)" (25:1). Hadith collections beyond the Two Sahihs also preserve reports that letters went out during his lifetime to earth's kings and assorted peoples. Arabs and non-Arabs, scriptured communities and illiterate audiences all received the summons exactly as he had been commanded.

ʿAbd ar-Razzāq recorded that Abū Hurayrah said the Prophet ﷺ declared, "By He in Whose Hand is my soul! No member of this Ummah, no Jew or Christian, hears of me but dies without believing in what I was sent with, but will be among the people of the Fire." Muslim also recorded that hadith. The Prophet ﷺ said, "I was sent to the red and the black." And he said, "A Prophet used to be sent to his people, but I was sent to all mankind."

The surah began by confirming the Divine Oneness and refuting the Trinity, and the disputations of the People of the Book have continued after the truth was proved conclusively. The advice given here is that their acceptance or rejection is no measure of the believer's own standing. A believer declares that he and those with him have entered Islam with no doubt about its truth. The mission itself was for the disputers' own good. If they submit they are rightly guided, and if they keep a hostile attitude, the Prophet ﷺ is comforted: his duty was conveying the message of Allah and His commandments, and the rest belongs to Allah, who is in full sight of what His servants do.

### How to look at people who refuse

An inward discipline hides inside delivery's command. View disputants through Allah's free disposal, taṣrīf, so their unequal stages and differences cannot unsettle your own state. Behind varied treatment stands one decisive power, qudra, assigning each person the lot He chose. Counsel publicly in public; privately witness His disposal concerning them. Give the tongue to advice while clearing the heart of their chatter and withdrawing the inmost self from watching them. You were assigned delivery, never management of outcomes. Initiating and bringing affairs about belongs to Him.

### In short

- The answer to continued argument is a declaration: I and my followers have submitted to Allah, and the same submission is put to the People of the Scripture and the unlettered alike.
- Submission means guidance, and refusal leaves reckoning to Allah, since the Messenger's duty ends at delivering the message and this was said before the command to fight.
- The call is universal, to kings and peoples, Arabs and non-Arabs, as the letters and the hadith in Muslim affirm.

<<<VERSE 3:21>>>
The warning turns historical: "Indeed, those who deny Allah’s signs, kill the prophets unjustly, and kill people who stand up for justice—give them good news of a painful punishment". The phrase "good news" is sarcasm. Behind it stand the past crimes of the People of the Book, the killing of prophets who brought them nothing but the truth, and the killing of ordinary people whose offence was to enjoin justice.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, those who deny Allah’s signs"**: disbelief in Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an is meant, and, among the Jews, refusal of Injīl and Qur'an as well. People of the Book are rebuked for past and recent transgression, defying messengers, rejecting truth and refusing obedience.

**"kill the prophets unjustly"**: they killed many prophets when the prophets conveyed to them what Allah had legislated, without cause or criminal behaviour on the prophets' part, since the prophets only called them to truth. The word is also read yuqātilūna, "they fight against" the prophets. In one explanation the slayers are widened to those who ally themselves with the slayers of the prophets, wrongfully, when the prophets had committed no crime.

**"and kill people who stand up for justice"**: they also kill those of mankind who believed in the prophets and enjoin equity, meaning Allah's divine Oneness, and moderation in deeds and morals. This is the worst type of arrogance. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Kibr (arrogance) is refusing the truth and degrading people."

**"give them good news of a painful punishment"**: announce to them a painful and humiliating torment, a doom that will spread out to their hearts. The good tidings are sarcastic ridicule, and the fāʾ in fa-bashshirhum, "so give them good tidings" works like the answer to a condition: if they disbelieve, then give them this news.

### Forty-three prophets in one day

A report transmitted in Rūḥ al-Maʿānī from Ibn Abī Ḥātim has the Prophet ﷺ explaining this verse with a number. The Banū Isrāʾīl slew forty-three prophets at one and the same time. Then one hundred and seventy pious elders stood up to ask them to uphold justice, and they slew those too on the same day. The event is given as forbidden to them in advance: forty-three prophets killed, and a hundred and seventy devout worshippers who forbade the act, each killed immediately. That pattern, taken together, drew an answer. Rejected truth plus contempt for creatures brought disgrace and humiliation now, with humiliating torment reserved later.

### A perpetual turning away

Those bound to disappointment, khidhlān, and branded with deprivation, ḥirmān, are told that Allah's turning away from them is perpetual. He has already determined their transfer from one abode of disgrace, hawān, to another: from disappointment and deprivation to punishment and fires, nīrān. The inward logic matches the history. A true argument is also one of Allah's signs, and someone who will not bow to a true argument will not bow to God. Deniers refuse the truth not because it is unclear to them but because acknowledgement would concede the standing of the one who brings it, and jealousy and arrogance do not allow that. In the extreme, they want to exterminate the caller himself.

### In short

- The crimes named are denial of the signs, killing prophets without right, and killing those who enjoin justice, and the punishment announced as "good news" is ridicule.
- A report puts forty-three prophets and a hundred and seventy elders who forbade the killing into a single day.
- Arrogance, defined by the Prophet ﷺ as refusing truth and degrading people, is answered by disgrace here and humiliation hereafter.

<<<VERSE 3:22>>>
The account closes with an audit: "They are the ones whose deeds are wasted in this world and the Hereafter. And they will have no helpers". Nothing they built survives the transfer. Their works fail in both abodes, and when the punishment comes, no one steps between them and it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They are the ones whose deeds are wasted in this world and the Hereafter"**: even charitable spending and kindness toward relatives count as failed and invalid across both worlds. Nothing remains on their ledger, because such deeds lack consequence without faith. People guilty of denial, killing and injustice gain no benefit here and receive no reward later.

**"And they will have no helpers"**: no protectors from the chastisement, and no one who will save them from Allah's punishment.

### Why the works failed

The failure is measured in two currencies. Today they are granted no success in their works, tawfīq bi-aʿmāl. Tomorrow their hopes go unrealised, taḥqīq li-āmāl. That is because they lost Allah's help in both abodes and did not bear witness to His might and power. The plans such people lay against the callers of truth, to censor their words and silence them, fail in the same way. When the scales of justice are set up, the actions that had convinced them of success and salvation turn out to have no value at all. They will enter the next world divested of all support.

### In short

- Charity and kindness to kin cannot rescue deeds built on denial and murder, so their works are wasted here and hereafter.
- No helper will protect them from the punishment.
- The loss is twinned: no success granted to their works today, and no hopes realised tomorrow.
