<<<VERSE 2:232>>>
The verse protects a divorced woman's lawful choice after her waiting period: "do not prevent them from re-marrying their ex-husbands if they come to an honourable agreement." A former marriage that ended after one or two divorces may be renewed by a new contract. Relatives must not prevent it merely because they resent the earlier divorce. Allah knows the couple's needs and the consequences of the rules He gives better than those who would substitute anger or imagined honour for His command.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When you have divorced women and they have reached the end of their waiting period,"** Unlike the preceding verse, this concerns an 'iddah, waiting period, that has finished. The old marriage has ended. The husband can no longer simply revoke his divorce, but a new marriage is possible where fewer than three divorces have occurred.

**"do not prevent them from re-marrying their ex-husbands if they come to an honourable agreement."** The instruction addresses guardians who obstruct remarriage. It is also understood broadly to warn former husbands and others against preventing a divorced woman from lawfully marrying, whether her former husband or someone else. Renewing the former marriage requires a new nikah, marriage contract, and mahr, marriage payment. Mutual agreement must accord with Islamic law, not merely with personal preference.

**"This is enjoined on whoever has faith in Allah and the Last Day."** Iman, faith, has consequences for conduct. Belief in Allah's commands and in final accountability should move a person to accept the instruction even where family resentment pulls in another direction. Falling short of the command exposes a disorder in the practice of that belief.

**"This is purer and more dignifying for you."** Allowing lawful marriage helps keep hearts free from hostility and conduct free from impurity. It protects the couple's honour and removes occasions for suspicion or misconduct. Refusing a marriage without a valid reason does not necessarily preserve the dignity the guardian thinks he is defending.

**"Allah knows and you do not know"** Allah knows the couple's attachment, their need for one another and the true outcomes of allowing or blocking the marriage. Human judgment may mistake wounded pride or financial advantage for benefit. The command calls for trust in knowledge greater than such limited calculations.

### What happened to Ma'qil's sister?

The principal account concerns Ma'qil bin Yasar Al-Muzani. He had given his sister in marriage, but her husband divorced her and allowed the waiting period to finish. Afterwards the man wanted to marry her again, and she wanted to return to him. Ma'qil refused because he felt that his earlier kindness had been answered with humiliation.

He told the man that he had honoured him by arranging the marriage, only for him to divorce her. He swore that she would never return to him. The man was not described as bad, and the woman wished to renew the marriage. The obstacle was therefore the brother's resentment, not the couple's refusal or a stated legal barrier.

Al-Bukhari records the account. It is also transmitted by Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi, Ibn Abu Hatim, Ibn Jarir, Ibn Marduwyah and Al-Bayhaqi through Al-Hasan from Ma'qil. At-Tirmidhi calls the report authentic. Al-Hakim is also named in connection with the occasion of revelation.

When Ma'qil heard the verse, he accepted it as his Lord's command. He summoned the man and arranged the remarriage, overcoming his earlier anger. Ibn Marduwyah adds that he would make compensation for breaking the oath. The command to allow the marriage took precedence over preserving a vow founded on resentment.

One account identifies the sister as Jamilah and her former husband as 'Abdullah Ibn 'Asim. Another telling describes the husband as a cousin who had received her hand after Ma'qil declined other suitors. After the divorce and completed waiting period, he returned among the men proposing marriage. The point remains the same across those details: both wanted a lawful new marriage, while her brother tried to deny it because of the past.

A similar incident is reported concerning a cousin of Jabir ibn 'Abdullah. The prohibition addressed his opposition too, and he obeyed it. These accounts show the change demanded from relatives, not merely a rule announced for other households.

### Is the rule only about this one family?

Ibn 'Abbas explains it as the case of a husband divorcing once or twice, the waiting period ending and both former spouses later wanting remarriage while the family prevents it. Masruq, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Az-Zuhri and Ad-Dahhak give the same account of its setting.

The family may resent the man. A former husband may resent the woman's marriage to someone else and consider it an affront to his honour. A guardian may even hope to withhold consent until marriage brings him financial gain. None of these motives supplies a valid reason to deny a free woman's lawful marriage.

The protection is therefore wider than permission to return to one particular former husband. It warns against obstructing lawful choice without an admitted legal ground. The reference to mutual honourable agreement defines the protection. It does not make all objections forbidden regardless of the proposed arrangement.

### What is said about a guardian's role?

One legal explanation draws from the verse the necessity of a wali, a marriage guardian such as a father, brother or adult son. Ibn Jarir and At-Tirmidhi are named with this inference. The command restraining the guardian assumes a role that must be exercised properly, not abolished through resentment.

Two hadith formulations are brought into that explanation. One says that a woman does not give another woman away in marriage or give herself away, and uses a severe comparison with adultery for self-arranged marriage in that sense. Another requires a mature guardian and two trustworthy witnesses for a valid marriage. These formulations are preserved as the legal argument given, without attaching a collector or grade not supplied for them here.

The discussion also explicitly protects the consent of a sane woman who has reached puberty. The words about the two agreeing mean that she cannot be given in marriage without her consent or permission. Guardianship is not a licence to replace her willing agreement with force.

Another legal emphasis discusses the family's right to object where a woman seeks marriage outside recognised compatibility without the guardian's permission, or accepts a payment below mahr al-mithl, the customary comparable marriage payment in her family. In that account, the family's interests are affected and the consent is not regarded as satisfying all legal requirements. These qualifications belong alongside the command against unjust obstruction. They must not be erased, but neither may resentment be disguised as a valid ground merely by calling it family concern.

### When is intervention justified?

The condition is an honourable agreement within the shariah, Islamic law. If one party does not agree, or is being compelled, people around them may intervene. An agreement to live together without marriage is not the lawful agreement protected here. Nor is a direct new marriage between a couple after three divorces while ignoring the further conditions.

A proposed marriage to another man during the waiting period is likewise outside the permission. Relatives, and other Muslims within their ability, should prevent unlawful arrangements of these kinds. The discussion calls such prevention wajib, obligatory. It distinguishes protection against a real violation from opposition to a marriage that satisfies the law.

The distinction is crucial. The verse does not tell families to abandon responsibility. It tells them not to turn responsibility into domination. A valid objection must concern the actual conditions, not the humiliation someone feels about a past divorce or the profit he hopes to extract.

### Why is allowing lawful marriage described as purity?

Blocking marriage can injure the woman and deny her right. It can also place chastity under strain. If those prevented from marrying fall into wrongdoing, the people who created the unjust obstacle share responsibility for their conduct. The discussion warns that consequences may include discord, violence and ruin well before final judgment.

Forcing someone into marriage with a person she does not want can bring a different pattern of harm: continuing hostility, divorce or khul', compensated release sought by the wife. What is defended as family honour may become the cause of the very conflict the guardian hopes to avoid.

The couple may retain affection because of their earlier intimacy. Keeping them apart without lawful cause can create suspicion, while an honourable marriage protects their conduct. Purity therefore concerns hearts and practical circumstances together. It is not achieved merely by making an outward display of family control.

### Why is imagined advantage not enough?

A guardian may suppose that refusal preserves prestige. He may hope to obtain money by delaying agreement. But short-term calculation cannot establish what will benefit the couple or the family in the end. The attempted gain may produce quarrels, financial loss or even danger to life.

Allah's instruction already accounts for interests that human beings see only partially. The person opposing it does not acquire better knowledge by being confident in his anger. The words about Allah's knowing expose the weakness of calling self-interest wisdom.

The inward application calls guardians to abandon the fierceness associated with jahiliyyah, the age of ignorance. Rage at a woman's choice is not proof of sincere care. If the guardian once accepted the husband as a suitable spouse, he must not now oppress her simply because resentment has taken hold. Subduing the lower self is a harder and more sincere struggle than directing its anger at someone weaker.

### How does the verse teach obedience to law?

The command is followed by three connected motives. Faith and the Last Day call a person to accountability. Purity explains the good that obedience brings and the harm that opposition can cause. Allah's superior knowledge challenges the supposed advantage offered by disobedience.

This method does more than publish a rule and threaten an earthly penalty. It aims to make obedience intelligible and acceptable within the heart. The Qur'an's instructions are repeatedly accompanied by reminders to fear Allah and to recognise His knowledge of conduct. They prepare a person to observe the command even where no police officer or witness can enforce it.

The discussion presents the Qur'an as guidance for all generations until qiyamah, the Resurrection. Its legal limits and punishments are joined to sympathetic persuasion, moral instruction and warning. The purpose is that people avoid deserving punishment, not merely that an authority have grounds to inflict it afterwards.

Law, police and armies alone are described as insufficient for lasting reform. Fear of Allah and awareness of final accountability give an inward reason to refrain from wrongdoing when external supervision is absent. A person's secret and public behaviour can then be brought into agreement. The family case illustrates that wider principle: the command should govern the guardian's private intention as well as his publicly stated decision.

### In short

- After one or two divorces and a completed waiting period, the former spouses may marry again through a new lawful contract.
- Relatives must not block a mutually wanted marriage merely out of anger, pride or greed.
- A woman's consent is essential, while objections must concern real legal grounds rather than resentment.
- Allowing lawful marriage protects dignity and purity better than unjust obstruction.
- Allah knows the consequences of His commands beyond the limited calculations of those resisting them.
