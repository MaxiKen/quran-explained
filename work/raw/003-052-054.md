<<<VERSE 3:52>>>
When ʿĪsā sees that his people reject him, he asks, “Who will stand up with me for Allah?” (3:52). His close disciples answer that they believe in Allah and have submitted. They offer to help him convey Allah’s message and ask him to bear witness to their faith.

### Phrase by phrase

**“When Jesus sensed disbelief from his people”** means that ʿĪsā (Jesus) recognized the continued rejection of the Children of Isrāʾīl, also called Banī Isrāʾīl or the Israelites. One explanation says he saw that they wanted to kill him. He did not ask for helpers at the beginning of his mission, but after opposition became clear. He had already undertaken the work alone in obedience to Allah.

**“he asked, ‘Who will stand up with me for Allah?’”** is a request for people to help convey Allah’s message, not to serve ʿĪsā for personal gain. Mujāhid explains it as asking who will follow him to Allah. Another explanation understands him to be asking who will help him communicate the message. The disciples answer when support is needed for the cause of Allah. They say plainly that their help is for the sake of Allah and His message.

**“The disciples replied”** refers to al-Ḥawāriyyūn, the close followers of ʿĪsā. One explanation gives their number as twelve. The word ḥawārī is connected with purity or whiteness. They are called disciples because of the sincerity and purity of their hearts, or because of their white clothing. An inward reading describes them as āthār al-ʿināyah, signs of divine care, and āthār al-takhṣīṣ, marks of special selection. The word can also mean a helper or loyal supporter. Al-Qurṭubī explains the title in this sense.

**“We will stand up for Allah”** means they will help Allah’s religion and support ʿĪsā in conveying it. They are not claiming to help Allah in His power. They offer their own effort as servants.

**“We believe in Allah”** is a declaration of faith and of Allah’s oneness. The disciples accept the message and recognize ʿĪsā as Allah’s messenger.

**“so bear witness that we have submitted”** asks ʿĪsā to testify that they have surrendered to Allah. Their submission includes believing in Allah and following the messenger He sent.

A hadith connects this language of helpers with the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. During the Hajj season, before the Hijrah, he asked who would give him asylum so he could convey the speech of his Lord. Quraysh had prevented him from doing so. The Ansar gave him refuge, comforted him, and protected him from his enemies. He later migrated to them. Their support resembles the disciples’ support for ʿĪsā.

The Two Sahihs record that during the battle of al-Aḥzāb, the Prophet ﷺ called for people to fight, and al-Zubayr stepped forward. When he asked again, al-Zubayr came forward a second time. The Prophet said that every prophet has a ḥawārī, and that al-Zubayr was his ḥawārī. The report shows that the word can mean a sincere and courageous companion.

Ibn Abī Ḥātim records that Ibn ʿAbbās explained the request to be counted among the witnesses as a request to join the ummah of Muhammad ﷺ. The report is given with a good chain. The disciples’ faith is thus connected to the wider community that testifies to Allah’s oneness and to the truth of His messengers.

### Why did ʿĪsā ask for helpers at this point?

The request comes when rejection becomes active hostility. It does not mean that a worthy mission must first build a party or secure power. ʿĪsā began by obeying Allah, then sought support when the need arose. The disciples’ answer shows that sincere believers can help a messenger carry the message through danger.

### In short

- ʿĪsā asks for help conveying Allah’s message after his people reject him.
- The disciples declare faith, submission, and readiness to support the cause of Allah.
- Their example is compared with the Ansar and with al-Zubayr, the Prophet’s ḥawārī.

<<<VERSE 3:53>>>
The disciples continue with a prayer: “Our Lord! We believe in Your revelations and follow the messenger” (3:53). They ask Allah to count them among those who bear witness. Their testimony joins belief in the revelation, obedience to ʿĪsā, and affirmation of Allah’s oneness.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Our Lord!”** addresses Allah directly. The disciples turn to Him after declaring their readiness to help ʿĪsā. Their commitment is ultimately for Allah, not for personal advantage.

**“We believe in Your revelations”** means that they accept what Allah has revealed. The Injīl (Gospel) is named in the explanations as the scripture given to ʿĪsā. They regard it as revelation from Allah and affirm its truth.

**“and follow the messenger”** refers to following ʿĪsā. The disciples’ faith is not only a claim: they obey the messenger and accept the religion he conveys. Another explanation says that they follow the religion of Allah’s messenger.

**“so count us among those who bear witness”** asks Allah to include them with those who testify to the truth. This witness includes affirming Allah’s oneness and the truthfulness of His messenger. One explanation says that Ibn ʿAbbās understood this as a prayer to be included in the ummah of Muhammad ﷺ, whose members bear witness to the messengers who came before them. They are joined to the line of Banī Isrāʾīl who accepted the Gospel and followed ʿĪsā. An inward reading says Allah raised ʿĪsā as a prophet and walī, a close friend of Allah.

The disciples’ prayer follows their offer of support in the previous verse. Their words show that the helper’s role is grounded in belief and obedience. They do not only stand beside ʿĪsā against opposition. They follow him as Allah’s messenger and ask Allah to make their testimony sincere.

The comparison with the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ continues to clarify what faithful support looks like. Before the Hijrah, during Hajj, Quraysh blocked him from conveying the message, while the Ansar offered protection and refuge. The Prophet later migrated to them, and they formed a community that supported his mission. At al-Aḥzāb, al-Zubayr repeatedly answered the call for help. The Two Sahihs preserve the Prophet’s statement that al-Zubayr was his ḥawārī. This example links the disciples’ testimony with the later ummah that believes and bears witness.

The word ḥawārī means a sincere companion or helper. The disciples were said to be twelve, and their name is explained through purity of heart or white clothing. What matters in their prayer is not their number or appearance, but that they believe in the revelation, follow the messenger, and submit to Allah.

### What does it mean to be among the witnesses?

The disciples ask to be included among those who affirm the truth Allah has revealed. Their witness is both belief and action: they accept the Gospel, follow ʿĪsā, and declare their submission to Allah. This is the path of sincere faith, not mere association with a prophet.

### In short

- The disciples believe in Allah’s revelation and follow ʿĪsā.
- They ask to join those who testify to Allah’s oneness and the truth of His messengers.
- Their support is expressed through both prayer and action.

<<<VERSE 3:54>>>
The verse says, “And the disbelievers made a plan ˹against Jesus˺, but Allah also planned” (3:54). The leaders among the Children of Israel conspire against ʿĪsā, while Allah counters their scheme and saves His prophet. Allah is the best of planners.

### Phrase by phrase

**“And the disbelievers made a plan ˹against Jesus˺”** refers to the people who plotted to arrest and kill ʿĪsā. They accused him of misleading people, dividing families, opposing the ruler, and changing the Torah. They persuaded a disbelieving king that ʿĪsā was a threat and urged him to order his arrest. In the account, the leaders among the Children of Israel intended to seize and crucify him.

**“but Allah also planned”** means that Allah acted with a subtle counter-plan to frustrate their evil purpose. The explanation says that Allah raised ʿĪsā to heaven and made another man resemble him. The people seized and crucified the man they mistook for ʿĪsā, while Allah saved His prophet. The plotters thought they had succeeded, but their confusion and ignorance hid what Allah had done.

The word makr means a hidden or subtle plan. Its moral character depends on its purpose. A plan for evil is blameworthy, while a plan that defeats wrongdoing and protects a prophet is good. In everyday language, the word may carry a narrower sense of conspiracy, but its Qur’anic use here is broader. The Qur’an says that an evil plan only besets its perpetrator (35:43). Allah’s plan is not unjust. It answers the scheme of the disbelievers and brings their own wrongdoing back upon them.

One explanation cautions against interpreting the verse through the ordinary negative meaning of the word. It says that Allah is called Khayr al-Mākirīn, the best of planners, because His subtle response overcomes every secret design. The phrase does not attribute wrongdoing to Allah. It describes His ability to defeat a hostile plan with perfect knowledge and justice.

The explanations describe the Children of Israel’s accusation that ʿĪsā intended to change the Torah and lead people away from obedience to the ruler. Their allegations included claims that he was an illegitimate son. These were accusations made by his enemies, not facts accepted about him. They sought the support of political power when argument and distortion did not stop his message.

Ibn ʿAbbās explains another form of divine planning: when people increase in disbelief and sin, Allah may continue to give them blessings until they become reckless, then seize them from where they do not expect it. This gradual granting of ease can become a trial rather than a sign of approval.

A report about Abū Dardā illustrates this warning. Someone had tormented him, and Abū Dardā prayed that the person be given a healthy body, long life, and great wealth. The account calls this a severe supplication because such blessings may lead a heedless person to forget the next life and be destroyed by arrogance. It is a reminder that worldly ease can become part of a person’s trial.

The struggle of ʿĪsā also recalls the support promised to other messengers. In verse 52, his disciples offered to help him. Before the Hijrah, at Hajj, Quraysh blocked Muhammad ﷺ from conveying his message, but the Ansar gave him refuge and later protected him. At al-Aḥzāb, al-Zubayr came forward when the Prophet called for help. The Two Sahihs preserve the Prophet’s statement that al-Zubayr was his ḥawārī. The ummah’s path is to stand with truth even when influential people plot against it.

### How can Allah be “the best of planners”?

The verse contrasts an unjust secret plot with Allah’s perfect response. The enemies plan harm, while Allah protects His prophet and exposes the failure of their design. The word is understood through its purpose and outcome: evil planning belongs to the disbelievers, while Allah’s plan is wise, just, and victorious.

### In short

- The disbelievers conspired to arrest and kill ʿĪsā.
- Allah defeated their plan, saved His prophet, and left the plotters in confusion.
- Allah’s subtle plan is just and wise, while evil planning returns upon its perpetrator.
