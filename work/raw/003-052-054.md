<<<VERSE 3:52>>>
Rejection hardens into a plot, and a small company steps forward: "When Jesus sensed disbelief from his people, he asked, “Who will stand up with me for Allah?” The disciples replied, “We will stand up for Allah. We believe in Allah, so bear witness that we have submitted.”" ʿĪsā had presented his call to Banī Isra'il with miracles proving his prophethood. They remained adamant, rejected him, and moved toward physical hostility, yet a first group of followers declared its creed before its prophet.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When Jesus sensed disbelief from his people"**: persistence in disbelief and misguidance had become clear. He knew their unbelief, saw murder being intended, and repeatedly heard rejection.

**"he asked, “Who will stand up with me for Allah?”"**: who will be my helpers in Allah's cause, against His enemies? Mujāhid commented, "Who would follow me to Allah?" It appears more likely that he was asking who would help him convey the message of Allah.

**"The disciples replied, “We will stand up for Allah. We believe in Allah, so bear witness that we have submitted.”"**: the Ḥawāriyyūn said, "We are the helpers of Allah." They were his closest disciples and intimates, the first to believe in him, twelve in number.

### Helpers before the Hijrah, and a hawārī for every prophet

The Prophet's life supplies a later match. Before the Hijrah, during Hajj season, he asked, "Who will give me asylum so that I can convey the Speech of my Lord, for the Quraysh have prevented me from conveying the Speech of my Lord." The search ended with the Anṣār, who sheltered him, received his migration, comforted him and guarded him from enemies. Jesus likewise found Israelites who believed, supported him and followed the light sent with him.

Ḥawārī in Arabic means "support". A report in the Two Sahihs describes Az-Zubayr stepping forward twice when fighters were sought at Al-Ahzab, after which the Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Every Prophet has a Hawari, and Az-Zubayr is my Hawari."

A hadith cited through al-Qurtubi gives the wider gloss: every prophet has a ḥawāriyy, a sincere companion, and Zubayr is mine.

The name itself is explained from whiteness. Ḥawāriyy, singular of al-Ḥawāriyyūn, derives from ḥawar, whiteness. The title was given to the sincere disciples of ʿĪsā because of their sincerity, the purity of their hearts, or their white dress. They were twelve men of pure white complexion, though some say they were called ḥawāriyyūn because they were bleachers of clothes, qaṣṣārūn.

An important note follows from the timing. ʿĪsā looked for helpers in Allah's way only when he sensed hostility from disbelievers, not at the first stage of his call. He rose to the task alone, obeying his Lord's command without first setting up a party. When the need came, he found a group around him. Every worthy mission is said to demand that determination and courage.

### What it costs to stand with a rejected caller

The religious leaders of the Israelites had refused to believe. In any society, leading lights control resources and hold the religious seats, becoming the representatives of religion in people's eyes. If such people reject someone, he is deprived of life's resources and, even after losing everything for truth, remains a heretic to the crowd. Supporting a preacher of truth in that atmosphere is the hardest of tasks. It testifies to his veracity amid suspicion and opposition and sides with truth while it stands alone and unsupported.

Truth in its purity unsettles lives built on untruth, particularly those decorated with truth's label to secure public honour. Such leaders suppress the call, turn crowds against its bearer through misconstructions, and eventually reach for power against both call and caller. God's succour nevertheless remains with the preacher, allowing his mission to finish. Blocking Paradise-bound people from its path is treated as their greatest evil.

Inwardly, the disciples' answer is sharpened further. When the message reached them and people differed, some believing and most calling him a liar, Jesus learned that prophethood spares no one from affliction and enemy oppression. He separated his heart from them and held to his goal, qaṣd. His question then asks who will help him in exclusive devotion to God's true reality and sincere dedication to His goal. Those touched by the effects of providence, āthār al-ʿināya, and chosen through the effects of selection, āthār al-takhṣīṣ, answered that he should bear witness to them in truth and let nothing from them cast doubt on him.

### In short

- Sensing hardened disbelief, ʿĪsā asked for helpers to convey Allah's message, and twelve disciples answered as helpers of Allah.
- The Prophet ﷺ made the same request before the Hijrah and found the Anṣār, and he named Az-Zubayr his ḥawārī at Al-Ahzab in the Two Sahihs.
- Standing with a rejected caller means testifying to truth while it is alone, which is why obstructing Paradise's path is counted the worst opposition.

<<<VERSE 3:53>>>
The disciples repeat their declaration as prayer: "˹They prayed to Allah,˺ “Our Lord! We believe in Your revelations and follow the messenger, so count us among those who bear witness.”" What they said to their prophet in the previous verse, they now say to their Lord. The creed does not change when the audience does.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹They prayed to Allah,˺ “Our Lord! We believe in Your revelations"**: we believe in what You have revealed, in the Scripture, that is the Gospel. Their faith is in Allah and in what He sent down.

**"and follow the messenger"**: we follow him whom You sent, the religion of the Messenger Jesus, the messenger sent to them.

**"so count us among those who bear witness.”"**: enrol us with witnesses to Your Oneness and Your Messenger's truthfulness, people who attest truth and believe in You and Your messengers. Another gloss asks for enrollment among earlier forerunners who witnessed before them.

### Among the community of Muhammad

Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded that Ibn ʿAbbās explained "write us down among those who bear witness" as "among the Ummah of Muhammad." This hadith has a good chain of narration. The same meaning is reached from the other direction in the simpler gloss, which can mean "make us of the community of Muhammad ﷺ." A small company in Israel therefore prays to be enrolled with witnesses who had not yet come, and the phrase opens their local stand into the wider community of faith.

These were people with no material expectations from their children and no envy at goodness in others. Their happiness rested on dedication and devotion to God's cause, on children kept safe from Satan and enrolled among believers. Through their good influence their children controlled their selves and joined those who truly remembered God, choosing the right way and abandoning the path that leads astray. Such people are provided for out of God's special mercy.

### The fate closing around the prayer

The rest intensified dissension and enmity and turned to plotting. Their scheming rebounded when God made them taste its evil. Crucifixion and killing Jesus existed in imagination, born from ignorance and confusion, while God had raised His prophet, nabī, and friend, walī. His enemies earned banishment and cursing instead. That was God's scheming with them.

### In short

- The disciples ask to be written with the witnesses to Oneness and to their messenger's truthfulness.
- Ibn ʿAbbās, in a report with a good chain, reads the witnesses as the community of Muhammad ﷺ.
- Around the prayer the majority plots and imagines a crucifixion, while God raises His prophet and friend.

<<<VERSE 3:54>>>
Two plans meet: "And the disbelievers made a plan ˹against Jesus˺, but Allah also planned—and Allah is the best of planners". The Jews planned arrest and crucifixion. Allah's counter-plan saved His prophet, turned the plot back on the plotters, and left them certain they had succeeded. The verse's key word, makr, needs care, because Allah is called the best of those who practise it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And the disbelievers made a plan ˹against Jesus˺"**: Israel's disbelievers conspired around defamation and crucifixion in an attempt to kill ʿĪsā. One description assigns an assassin to carry out the murder. Their aim was simply to kill Jesus.

**"but Allah also planned"**: against them, Allah placed Jesus's likeness on the would-be killer and raised Jesus heavenward while the substitute died. On the attempted murder's occasion they were left inside misguidance. He also wished death for their man Tatianos.

**"and Allah is the best of planners"**: no schemer is stronger in will, none knows Jesus more completely, and no plot against enemies can be more severe than His.

### How the plot unfolded, and how it was answered

The Jews complained about ʿĪsā to a king who was a disbeliever. They claimed he misguided people, discouraged obedience to the king, caused division, and separated a man from his own son, adding other lies they will carry on their necks, including calling him an illegitimate son. The king grew furious and sent men to capture ʿĪsā, torture him and crucify him.

His home was surrounded, leaving capture apparently certain. God rescued him by raising him from the house into heaven and transferring his image to a man inside. In darkness the unjust intruders mistook that man for ʿĪsā, seized and humiliated him, crucified him and crowned him with thorns. Deception thus turned on the deceivers. The Prophet was saved and raised, while the plotters remained disordered inside transgression's darkness, convinced of success. Hard, truth-defiant hearts and enduring disgrace followed them until Resurrection Day.

The machinery is filled in a little more in the next verse and in the reports around it. They sent one of their own to arrest him, and Allah changed that man's face to resemble ʿĪsā while ʿĪsā was raised to the heavens. The person was crucified under mistaken identity. The Jews had convinced the ruler that ʿĪsā was a heretic bent on changing the Torah and making apostates of everybody, and the ruler ordered the arrest. The subtle and secret move made by Allah countered their evil plans more effectively than open force would have done.

### Makr: a subtle and secret move

Arabic makr is a move made subtly and secretly. Purpose determines its moral quality: good if good, bad if bad. Hence the restriction in "And evil plan besets none but its perpetrator" (35:43). Everyday Urdu usage limits makr to conspiracy, evil plans and strategy, so that idiom must not govern the Arabic. Allah can therefore be Khair al-Mākirīn, the best of those who make moves.

Another account explains divine deception through blessings. Ibn ʿAbbās said God increased blessings as unbelief and sin increased, drawing people into total recklessness. Immense unbelief followed as heads turned toward rebellion and misguidance. Only then were they taken gradually from an unknown direction.

A tradition adds the paradox of a harmful blessing. Someone tormented Abū Dardā, and Abū Dardā prayed, "Lord God, bestow upon him a healthy body, long life, and great wealth!" An intelligent man who ponders those words will know that this is the worst of supplications. Given these gifts, recklessness and heedlessness can make a person heedless of the next world until he is destroyed.

### What the ascension claim protects

The miraculous ascension of ʿĪsā, his life in the heavens and his later descent form the Muslim community's shared belief. Some sects, contrary to that belief, are said to deny all three and to exploit these verses' words and meanings in opening distortion's doors. The defence rests on the plain sequence: an arresting party entered, a likeness was taken, and the prophet was raised. Allah's plan leaves the plotters holding a body that is not his.

### In short

- The plot was a complaint to a disbelieving king, a house surrounded at night and a crucifixion performed on the wrong man.
- Makr is a secret move, good or bad by its purpose, and Allah's move saved ʿĪsā, cast his likeness on the assassin and hardened the plotters in disgrace (35:43).
- Ibn ʿAbbās reads divine deception as increased blessings drawing the wicked into recklessness until they are taken unawares.
