<<<VERSE 5:83>>>
"When they listen to what has been revealed to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflowing with tears for recognizing the truth. They say, “Our Lord! We believe, so count us among the witnesses"

This verse describes a group of people who, on hearing what was revealed to the Messenger, see the truth and weep. They say that they believe and ask to be counted among the witnesses.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When they listen to what has been revealed to the Messenger."** The material explains that the occasion was the recitation before the Negus. Ja'far ibn Abi Talib recited the revelation to the king and his court, and the listeners heard the Qur'an in its plain sense. The material reports that they recognised in it the description of Muhammad ﷺ that was in their own scripture, the Injil.

**"You see their eyes overflowing with tears for recognizing the truth."** The material says the tears were a sign of an inner change. The truth had reached the heart, and the listeners were moved to tears. The material says this was not a matter of words alone, but a turning of the whole being towards the truth.

**"They say, Our Lord! We believe, so count us among the witnesses."** The material explains that they declared belief in Allah, His Book and His Messenger. They asked to be among the witnesses, that is among the community of Muhammad ﷺ who bear witness to the truth.

### The reading of the Qur'an and the heart

The material explains that the words of the Qur'an were a living sign, and that the listeners identified themselves with the truth until they lost their separate identity in it. It says that pride is the main barrier to accepting the truth, and that those who are free of pride are not as strongly opposed to it.

### The Negus and his delegation

The material explains that a group of about forty men came from Abyssinia to Madinah, sent by the Negus. When the Prophet ﷺ recited Surah Ya-Sin to them, they wept and said it was like what had been revealed to Isa. The material cites 57:27, which speaks of the compassion and mercy that God placed in the hearts of the followers of Isa, and 3:199, which describes the People of the Book who humble themselves before Allah. It cites 28:52-53, in which those given the Scripture say they believe in the Qur'an and were Muslims before it, and 28:55, in which they say they do not seek the ignorant. It also cites 27:93, which says that the Prophet ﷺ is told to say that Allah's signs will be shown to them, and they will know them.

### In short

- The listeners recognised the truth in what was recited to them, and their eyes filled with tears.
- They declared their belief and asked to be counted among the witnesses.
- The Negus and his delegation are the historical setting, and the verse describes the inner change that the truth caused.

<<<VERSE 5:84>>>
"Why should we not believe in Allah and the truth that has come to us? And we long for our Lord to include us in the company of the righteous.”"

This verse gives their answer to those who blamed them for believing. There is nothing to stop them from believing in Allah and the truth that has come to them, and they long to be included with the righteous.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Why should we not believe in Allah and the truth that has come to us?"** The material explains that this is their answer to the reproaches of the people who opposed them. The truth that had come was the Qur'an and the Messenger, and there was nothing to stop them from believing in it once the conditions for faith were present.

**"And we long for our Lord to include us in the company of the righteous."** The material says that the word for long is a supplement to the word for believe. They hope that Allah will admit them to Paradise among the righteous.

### The reproach against them

The material says that the Jews reproached those who became Muslims. The reply shows that their belief has a clear ground. The material says that this is a model for anyone who turns to the faith and is blamed by his own people. He does not need to justify himself beyond the truth that has come to him.

### In short

- They answered the reproach by saying there was no reason not to believe in Allah and the truth.
- They hoped to be among the righteous in Paradise.
- The reply is a model for anyone who is blamed for accepting the truth.

<<<VERSE 5:85>>>
"So Allah will reward them for what they said with Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there forever. And that is the reward of the good-doers"

This verse says that Allah will reward them for what they said with Gardens beneath which rivers flow, where they will remain forever. It calls this the reward of the good-doers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So Allah will reward them for what they said."** The material explains that the reward is for their declaration of faith, which they made freely from their hearts. The words they spoke were an expression of their whole being.

**"With Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there forever."** The material describes the Gardens as places of trees and dwellings, with rivers of water, milk, wine and honey. It says they will remain in Paradise for ever, and never leave it.

**"And that is the reward of the good-doers."** The material says the good-doers are those who testify to the Oneness of Allah, and that some reports say the good-doers are those who are kind in speech and deed.

### In short

- Their declaration of faith is rewarded with Gardens beneath which rivers flow.
- They will remain there forever.
- The good-doers are those who testify to the Oneness of Allah and are kind in speech and deed.

<<<VERSE 5:86>>>
"As for those who disbelieve and reject Our signs, they will be the residents of the Hellfire"

This verse contrasts the reward of those who believe with the fate of those who disbelieve and reject Allah's signs. They will be the residents of the Hellfire.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who disbelieve and reject Our signs."** The material explains that the disbelief is the rejection of the signs that Allah sent, which are the revelation and the Messenger. The material says that the Jews of the time, and the polytheists, were of this kind, and that their denial was the cause of their loss.

**"They will be the residents of the Hellfire."** The material says that the verse ends with a plain statement that they will remain in the Fire. The material sets this beside the reward of the previous verse, so that the believers and the deniers are shown as two different ends.

### The meaning of the contrast

The material says that the verses from 82 to 86 together show that the people who receive the truth with humility are rewarded, and those who deny it are punished. The contrast is not between two peoples, but between two responses to the same message.

### In short

- The reward of the believers is set against the fate of those who reject the signs of Allah.
- Those who disbelieve and deny the revelation will be the residents of the Fire.
- The contrast is between the people who see the truth and the people who deny it.
