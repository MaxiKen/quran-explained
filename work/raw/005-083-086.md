<<<VERSE 5:83>>>
The response of that group is described in detail: "When they listen to what has been revealed to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflowing with tears for recognizing the truth." Their hearing produces a visible effect, and then words. They ask to be counted among the witnesses.

The tears are not sentimental. They mark recognition of something they already knew from their own scripture.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When they listen to what has been revealed to the Messenger"** describes them hearing the recitation. One gloss specifies that they heard Ja'far ibn Abi Talib reciting what had been revealed to the Messenger ﷺ. Another describes the Prophet ﷺ reciting Surah Yasin to the delegation of the Negus from Abyssinia.

**"you see their eyes overflowing with tears"** is the visible effect of hearing. The delegation wept and submitted to Islam, saying how close this was to what used to be revealed to 'Isa (Jesus).

**"for recognizing the truth"** gives the cause. They recognised the truth, and one gloss explains that recognition as finding the traits and description of Muhammad ﷺ in their own scripture. Their hearts were soft, and they wept in submission because they knew what 'Isa had brought.

**"They say, “Our Lord! We believe"** is their declaration, their shahadah. It is explained as belief in Allah, in His Book and in His Messenger Muhammad ﷺ, and as accepting the truth of the Prophet ﷺ and the Book. Another gloss connects it with the good news they already held about the coming of Muhammad ﷺ.

**"so count us among the witnesses”** asks to be inscribed among the witnesses. One explanation says it means being made part of the community of Muhammad ﷺ that believes. Another describes the witnesses as those who affirm their acceptance of the truth and testify to it.

A further account explains the request as asking to be recorded among the people of this community who will stand as witnesses against others on the Day of Rising. These readings are not in conflict about the substance. All describe a request to be enrolled with those who attest the truth.

### What happened at that hearing?

The delegation came to the Prophet ﷺ and he recited the Qur'an to them. They wept, submitted and embraced Islam. The fuller account of the Negus, the migration to Abyssinia and the composition of the delegation is given with the preceding verse, and is not repeated here.

What this verse adds is the inner event. Hearing became recognition, recognition became tears, and tears became a spoken request to be counted with the believers.

### Why are such words rewarded?

Paradise is declared here as the reward for certain words. The question is what those words were that entitled their speakers to eternal Paradise.

What they said represented and expressed their whole being. It was the sound of their personality flowing out. They heard Allah's message in such a way that they found the entire truth enshrined in it, and it penetrated their hearts and minds.

It caused a revolution in them, changing the whole focus of their wishes and aspirations until every barrier of prejudice and interest broke down. They identified themselves with the truth so completely that they lost their separate identity and became its witnesses, and becoming a witness meant that truth took root inside them.

For them the Qur'an was never simply a book. It stood as a living sign of the Lord of the universe. That divine experience was expressed in words, but it was not simply a matter of words. It was a turmoil and an earthquake affecting their whole existence, so much so that tears flowed from their eyes.

### What is the significance of words here?

Words spoken about reality do more than make a statement. They are the highest form in which a person gives meaningful shape to his actions and feelings. In the known universe only human beings have the capacity to grasp the refinement and significance of words and to use them to best effect.

Words are therefore the best of what a person has, and a word is eloquent action. When a person demonstrates and voices his servitude to Allah in words, a certain claim to Paradise is established for him. That is why the reward in the verse that follows is given for what they said.

### What does the inward reading draw from this verse?

It notes that faith is to be heard, seen, recognised, spoken and done. Their hearing shows that it is heard. The overflowing eyes show that it is seen. Recognition of the truth shows that it is recognised. Their speaking shows that it is spoken.

The verse a little later describes the recompense of those who do good beautifully, and that shows faith is also to be acted upon. Hearing comes first in the order, because hearing is where it begins. A servant hears the truth and it gladdens him. He accepts it, enters into work and acts upon it. Allah, Lord of all worlds, is pleased with a group holding every one of these traits.

Three marks of recognition are named, and this group is said to hold all three perfectly: weeping, supplication and approval. The weeping is for disloyalty, the supplication is for bestowal, and the approval is of the decree.

Whoever claims recognition without these traits is not truthful in his claim. He is not counted among those who recognise Allah and has no standing among the knights of the path and the religious.

The Pir of the Tariqah is quoted distinguishing two kinds of recognition. That of common people comes by hearsay, and that of the elect by face-to-face vision. The recognition of common people arises out of Allah's munificence, while that of the elect arises from what they find.

Concerning the recognition of common people, Allah describes them hearing what was sent down to the Messenger (5:82-86). Concerning the recognition of the elect, He says that He will show them His signs (27:93). Hearing is praise of the people of the Shariah, and the showing of signs is a felicitation of the people of the Haqiqah.

A warning is attached to both. Whoever speaks of the Shariah and turns his back on it in any respect becomes a heretic. Whoever speaks of the Haqiqah and looks at himself in any respect becomes one who associates partners with Allah.

### What prevents people from this recognition?

The strongest reasons for not accepting the truth are ego and pride. People carrying hidden pride react most violently to the call toward truth. They refuse it whatever the cost, and can end up as its most dangerous opponents.

Those not filled with pride may be flawed by other evils, but they cannot oppose the truth to that extent. That is why the preceding verse named the absence of arrogance as the reason this group was nearest in love to the believers.

### In short

- Hearing the revelation produced tears, recognition and a spoken request to be enrolled among the witnesses.
- The recognition is explained as finding the description of Muhammad ﷺ in their own scripture.
- Their words expressed a change of the whole person, which is why Paradise is given for what they said.
- Faith is heard, seen, recognised, spoken and acted upon, with weeping, supplication and approval as the marks of recognition.
- Pride is what prevents this response, and its absence is what allowed it here.

<<<VERSE 5:84>>>
The believers' words continue as an answer to criticism: "Why should we not believe in Allah and the truth that has come to us?" The question is rhetorical. Nothing stands in the way of faith, and they add a hope to be admitted with the righteous.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Why should we not believe in Allah and the truth that has come to us?"** is their reply to those who blamed them for believing. One account says their own people reproached them, and another says Jews reviled them for their Islam.

The truth that came to them is explained as the Qur'an and as the truth reaching them from scripture and the Messenger ﷺ. Nothing prevents faith when its prerequisites are present, and that is the force of the question.

**"And we long for our Lord to include us in the company of the righteous.”"** adds hope to belief. The hoping is described as attached to their believing, and the company they ask for is the righteous among the community of Muhammad ﷺ, admitted with the believers into Paradise.

One explanation describes that hope as entering the Garden in the company of the prophets and of those who followed them in obeying Allah and dreading His punishment. Another states the wish as being admitted by their Lord along with the righteous people.

### Why is the reply phrased as a question?

They were being reproached for something that carries no rational obstacle. The question turns the reproach back on the reproachers. What reason is there not to believe, when the truth has come and its conditions are met, and when the shahadah they gave asks for nothing but the truth?

That form of answer also records their confidence. They do not defend themselves by minimising the truth or by claiming a private exception. They ask why belief should be surprising at all.

### What does the hope add to the belief?

Belief is not presented as complete without a direction. They believe, and they hope to be admitted with the righteous. The hope keeps their faith social rather than self-sufficient, since the company they want is the company of obedient people.

The same verse group describes such Christians elsewhere as among the People of the Scripture who believe in Allah and in what was revealed to the Prophet ﷺ and in what was revealed to them, humbling themselves before Allah (3:199). Others are described as given the Scripture before this Book, believing in it and saying that they were already Muslims before it came (28:52-53), and as not seeking the company of the ignorant (28:55).

Those descriptions match the words here. The hope to be admitted with the righteous is the same posture as humbling themselves before Allah and refusing the company of the ignorant.

### In short

- The question answers reproach by asking what obstacle to faith could exist once truth has come.
- The truth named is the Qur'an and what reached them through scripture and the Messenger ﷺ.
- Their hope is admission with the righteous, described as the upright among the Prophet's ﷺ community.
- Parallel passages describe the same people as humbling themselves before Allah and refusing the company of the ignorant.

<<<VERSE 5:85>>>
The answer to those words is a reward: "So Allah will reward them for what they said with Gardens under which rivers flow". The reward corresponds to the declaration. It is permanent, and it is described as the reward of those who do good.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So Allah will reward them for what they said"** ties the reward to their statement. One gloss explains it as Allah decreeing this for them because they gave their shahadah, their declaration of faith, voluntarily. They were not compelled, and the declaration was their own.

The reward is given for embracing faith and for recognising and believing the truth. Their words were the outward form of that recognition, which is why the reward is expressed in terms of what they said.

**"with Gardens under which rivers flow"** describes the reward. The rivers are described in one account as flowing beneath its trees and dwellings, and as rivers of water, milk, wine and honey. Another describes gardens with palaces and trees overlooking flowing streams.

**"to stay there forever."** They will remain in Paradise forever, never dying and never being removed from it.

**"And that is the reward of the good-doers"** identifies who receives it. The good are explained as those who testify to Allah's oneness, and another explanation describes them as those who are kind in speech and action. A third describes them as virtuous through believing.

Another account describes the good-doers as people who pursue the truth and comply with it in every place, at every time and through whoever brings it. A further explanation describes doing good as following that truth and yielding to it with no restriction and no condition attached.

### Why is the reward tied to words?

The verse that follows the declaration describes Paradise as given for what they said. Their words were not empty formulas. They carried the whole weight of a changed allegiance, and the reward corresponds to that.

This does not make speech a substitute for action. The description of the good-doers includes obedience to the truth wherever it is found, and the inward reading of the preceding verse lists acting upon faith as its completion.

### What does following the truth wherever it is found mean?

It means accepting the truth without regard for the identity of the person who brings it or the community it comes from. The people rewarded here recognised the Qur'an as continuous with what they had received, and they submitted.

That is the quality the description stresses. Their reward is not for belonging to a particular group but for recognising and obeying the truth when it stood before them, without condition and without reservation.

### In short

- The reward answers their declaration and is given because they professed faith voluntarily.
- Gardens with flowing rivers and permanent dwelling are described, with rivers of water, milk, wine and honey in one account.
- The good-doers are explained as those who testify to Allah's oneness, who are kind in speech and action, and who obey the truth wherever they find it.
- The reward is tied to words that carried a real change of allegiance, not to speech in place of action.

<<<VERSE 5:86>>>
The contrast is stated in a single sentence: "As for those who disbelieve and reject Our signs, they will be the residents of the Hellfire." The verse that has just described a permanent reward now describes a permanent loss, and the difference between the two groups lies in their response to the same signs.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who disbelieve"** concerns disbelief in Allah. The group is set against those just rewarded, and no middle position is described between the two outcomes.

**"and reject Our signs"** adds denial of the revelations. One gloss identifies them as Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, and another describes the denial as defiance and opposition to the signs.

**"they will be the residents of the Hellfire"** states their end. They are described as the people of the Fire and its companions, entering it and remaining without leaving, and as its owners and folk.

### Why is this verse placed here?

It completes the comparison begun with the two groups described earlier in this passage. The delegation that wept and asked to be enrolled among the witnesses received gardens. Those who denied the signs receive the Fire.

The same signs were available to both. The difference was not in the evidence but in the response to it. That is why the preceding verses stress recognition, humility and the absence of pride.

### What does the permanence of both outcomes show?

The reward is described as dwelling forever, and the punishment as residence without exit. Neither outcome is provisional, and neither depends on communal identity.

The verse therefore closes the passage by removing any expectation that a religious label settles the matter. What settles it is belief in Allah, acceptance of His signs and the conduct that follows.

### In short

- Disbelief in Allah and denial of His signs lead to residence in the Fire.
- The signs denied are identified with Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, and the denial is described as defiance.
- The verse completes the comparison with the rewarded group, showing that both outcomes are permanent.
- The same signs were before both groups, so the difference lies in the response rather than in the evidence.
