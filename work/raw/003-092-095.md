<<<VERSE 3:92>>>
Righteousness is reached through the wallet's dearest contents: "You will never achieve righteousness until you donate some of what you cherish. And whatever you give is certainly well known to Allah". After the refusal of a disbeliever's ransom, believers are taught acceptable charity. Its measure is not the size of the gift, but whether what is given is loved, needed and sincerely intended.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You will never achieve righteousness until you donate some of what you cherish"**: By no means shall you attain Al-Birr unless you spend of that which you love. Wakiʿ reported that ʿAmr bin Maymun said attaining Al-Birr means attaining Paradise. It is also glossed as the reward of piety, its honour and Paradise, or as reliance on Allah and God-fearingness. Voluntary alms are named in one reading as the road to piety's reward.

**"And whatever you give is certainly well known to Allah"**: whatever of good you spend, big or small, Allah knows it well, just as He is aware of your intentions, whether you intend His Countenance or other people's praise. He will requite it accordingly, and everyone will be rewarded accordingly.

### What birr means

The literal and real meaning of birr is the perfect fulfilment of someone's rights. It is also used for charity, performance of good deeds and nice conduct. Barr is a person who fulfils, fully and totally, all rights due on him. The Qur'an calls prophets good to parents in this sense: "good to my mother" (19:32), and "good to his father and mother" (19:14). The plural, abrār, the righteous, is frequent: "Surely the righteous shall drink of a cup the mixture of which is camphor" (76:5); "Surely the righteous shall be in bliss, upon furnished thrones gazing" (83:22); while the opposite pole is named: "And surely, the sinners shall be in hell ablaze" (82:14). The antonym of birr is fujūr, disobedience and sinfulness.

A report from Abu Bakr has the Prophet ﷺ say:

> "Stick to speaking the truth because truth is a companion of birr (righteousness) and both these are in Paradise; and abstain from lying because that is a companion of fujur (sin) and both these are in Hell."

It appears in al-Adab al-Mufrad of Imam al-Bukhari, in Ibn Majah and in the Musnad of Ahmad.

Verse 2:177 lists belief in Allah and the Last Day and a whole set of deeds and classes all of them as birr. Within that wide range, the most preferred form of righteousness named here is to spend the most cherished things in Allah's way. Perfect fulfilment of Allah's rights and total self-unburdening from them cannot come unless we spend what we hold dear. This has also been translated as perfect good, excellence in doing good, and the great merit. To sum up: entering the ranks of the abrār depends on sacrificing in Allah's way what one holds dear.

### Abu Talhah's garden, and the Companions who surveyed their hearts

The first addressees surveyed what they loved most and brought requests to spend it. Imam Ahmad reported that Anas bin Malik said Abu Talhah had more property than any other among the Ansar in Al-Madinah. The most beloved of his property was Bayruha' garden, in front of the Messenger's mosque. Sometimes Allah's Messenger ﷺ would go to the garden and drink its fresh water.

When these verses were revealed, Abu Talhah presented his intention:

> "O Allah's Messenger! Allah says, 'By no means shall you attain Al-Birr, unless you spend of that which you love.' No doubt, Bayruha' garden is the most beloved of all my property to me. So I want to give it in charity in Allah's cause, and I expect its reward and compensation from Allah. O Allah's Messenger! Spend it where Allah makes you think is feasible."

The Messenger ﷺ answered:

> "Well-done! It is profitable property, it is profitable property. I have heard what you have said, and I think it would be proper if you gave it to your kith and kin."

Abu Talhah accepted the suggestion and distributed that garden among his relatives and cousins. This hadith was recorded in the Two Sahihs. It teaches that charity is not restricted to the poor and needy: giving to family and relatives is equally charitable and meritorious.

Details of the place are preserved in the other direction. Abu Talhah was the richest person among the Ansar of Madinah, owner of a fruit farm across the mosque with a well called Bi'r Ha'. In place of the farm there is now, in front of Bab al-Majidi, a building known as Iṣṭifā Manzil where pilgrims to Madinah stay; in a corner north-east from it, Bi'r Ha' still exists by the same name. A translator notes that the building and well have now been demolished due to the extension of the Mosque. The Holy Prophet ﷺ used to visit this farm once in a while and drink water drawn from Bi'r Ha', and He liked the water from this well.

Sayyidna Zayd ibn Haritha brought a horse he loved above everything he owned, wishing to spend it in Allah's way. The Prophet ﷺ accepted it, then handed it to Zayd's son Sayyidna Usamah. Disappointment followed, because Zayd thought his own household had received the charity back. The Prophet ﷺ reassured him that Allah Almighty had accepted that charity of his (Ibn Jarir, and others).

Freedom also became a gift. Sayyidna ʿUmar freed the bond-maid he liked most for Allah's sake. Sayyidna ʿAbdullah ibn ʿUmar likewise freed a bond-maid he loved in Allah's name. The Two Sahihs also recorded that ʿUmar said, "O Messenger of Allah! I never gained possession of a piece of property more precious to me than my share in Khaybar. Therefore, what do you command me to do with it?" The Prophet ﷺ said, "Retain the land to give its fruits in Allah's cause." 

### Rulings drawn from the verse

The word birr here is inclusive of all charity, obligatory or voluntary.

1. Some commentators take the spending to mean obligatory charity, zakah, while others take voluntary, Nafl, supererogatory charity. The consensus of respected researchers is that its sense is general and includes both. The recorded initiatives of the Companions cited above bear witness that their giving was voluntary. The outcome is that whatever has to be given, zakah or voluntary charity, attains perfect excellence only when what is liked and loved is given. Not the spare and worthless things picked out to get rid of the burden of charity as if it were a punitive tax. Another verse takes this up clearly: "O those who believe, spend of the good things you have earned and of what We have brought forth for you from the earth and do not opt for a bad thing spending only from there while you are not going to accept it at all unless you close your eyes to it..." (2:267). Picking out the bad and useless for charity is unacceptable; acceptable charity with full merit is what is spent out of dear belongings.

2. Moderation is hinted in the word "from", min, in "some of what you cherish". The purpose is not to spend away everything one likes and loves en masse in Allah's way. Spending from the good things, in the measure that has to be spent, brings the full reward of charity.

3. Sincerity counts, not price. Even a tiny beloved item can earn birr when given sincerely. Hasan al-Basri said that a single date-fruit alone, offered for Allah's pleasure, is enough for the promised merit.

4. Poor people are not excluded. On the outside, those with little to spend appear deprived of this great good. But birr can be achieved by other means as well: ʿIbādah, worship, Dhikr, remembrance, Tilawah of the Qur'an and abundance of nawafil, supererogatory prayers. The poor and needy can therefore also become recipients of this good, as Hadith narrations explain clearly.

5. "What you love" means what is working for you and needed by you, not what is spare and useless. The favoured servants feed others, in the words of (76:8), "the favoured servants of Allah feed the..." in spite of their love for the food. And they are further described: "They (the favoured servants of Allah) prefer others over their ownselves even though they themselves are in need" (59:9).

6. Spending the extra and spare is not devoid of merit. Entry into perfect good and the ranks of the righteous depends specially on spending cherished possessions. But no charity is devoid of merit, whether one spends the cherished or the extra. What is prohibited is the habit of always picking out the extra and the bad. A person who spends good things he likes in charity and also gives away extras, leftover food, used clothing, or defective utensils or articles in use, commits no sin thereby and definitely earns reward for those too. In addition, by spending the cherished, he achieves the great good and entry into the ranks of the righteous.

Allah is fully aware of the real quality of what a person spends. He knows whether it is dear to him, and whether he is spending it for Allah's good pleasure or for pretence and publicity. A verbal claim to be spending what one cherishes is not enough. The all-Knowing and all-Aware One keeps vigil, cognizant of the secrets of the heart, watching and knowing the true worth of the spending in His way.

### Some of what you love, or everything you love

The phrase is mimmā, min mā, "of what" you love, and min is the particle for dividing something into portions. Start from the greater desire. Wanting al-Bārr, the One who is All-Goodness, means expending everything loved. Wanting birr, piety and goodness, means expending some portion of what is loved. Worldly expenditure of the loved leads to what is sought from the Real. Attachment to lower-self fortunes, man kāna marbūṭan bi-ḥuẓūẓi nafsihi, blocks fortune in the Lord's nearness, lam yaḥẓu bi-qurbi rabbihi. If loved things have to be spent even for birr, preferring worldly fortunes above Him bars the road to al-Bārr.

Motives divide spenders. Contentment with His knowing motivates some. Others hope to defend themselves from affliction and tribulations, while still others seek reward and compensation. A lover's image has him deliberately walking weakly, hoping news of his suffering will bring her message. He trembles, through what seeking the sublime has taught, that one day she may mention his qualities before Salmā.

### In short

- Birr is the perfect fulfilment of rights, and its most preferred form in this verse is spending what you cherish, not what you discard (2:177, 2:267, 19:32, 19:14, 76:5, 83:22, 82:14).
- Abu Talhah gave Bayruha' to his kin, ʿUmar retained Khaybar's land for its fruits, and others freed the slaves they loved most.
- Quality is measured by sincerity and need, not price: one date given sincerely suffices, the poor can reach birr by worship and remembrance, and even spare goods carry reward when the cherished are also given.

<<<VERSE 3:93>>>
The refutation returns to dinner: "All food was lawful for the children of Israel, except what Israel made unlawful for himself before the Torah was revealed. Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Bring the Torah and read it, if your claims are true.”" Jews objected that Muhammad ﷺ claimed Abraham's creed yet ate camel meat and drank its milk, which they held unlawful for Abraham. The verse answers that all food was lawful until Jacob forbade one thing to himself, and then dares them to produce their own book.

### Phrase by phrase

**"All food was lawful for the children of Israel"**: all food that is lawful for Muhammad and his community today was lawful for the Children of Israel, the sons of Jacob. Before the Torah was revealed, all types of food were allowed.

**"except what Israel made unlawful for himself before the Torah was revealed"**: save what Israel, Jacob, forbade himself by way of vows, in days before the Torah was revealed to Moses. Jacob forbade himself the meat and milk of camels.

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Bring the Torah and read it, if your claims are true.”"**: Produce the Torah and read it unto us, where they are made forbidden, if you are truthful in your claim. Bring the Torah now and recite it, so that the truth of what you say may become clear. They failed to produce the Torah and knew they were liars, since there was nothing in the Torah to substantiate their claim. They were stupified and did not bring it.

### What Jacob forbade, and why

The Jews said to the Prophet: "You claim that you follow the creed of Abraham but Abraham did not eat camel's meat nor drink its milk." The response revealed that camel's meat was forbidden to Jacob himself when he was afflicted with sciatica, ʿirq al-nasā. He vowed that if he were cured, he would not eat of it again, and so it was forbidden him. The Torah was not revealed until long after Abraham, so camel was not unlawful in Abraham's time as they claimed.

Ibn ʿAbbās narrates in an authentic report that Jacob suffered from sciatica (Al-Hakim and al-Tirmidhi). He vowed to abandon what he liked most in what he ate if Allah Almighty cured him of the disease. Camel meat was what he liked most, so he abandoned it when cured. Then, it so happened that this prohibition which started because of a vow continued among the Bani Isra'il as a divine injunction. Their code recognized a vow as a cause of unlawfulness for something lawful, just as in our own Shari'ah a vow may make permissible acts obligatory. A vow that renders things unlawful is really an oath; in our Shari'ah it is not permissible to keep such an oath, and breaking it with Kaffarah becomes obligatory, in accordance with: "why do you forbid what Allah has made lawful for you?" (66:1).

Abu Rawq and al-Kalbi said this was revealed when the Prophet ﷺ said, "We follow the religion of Abraham." The Jews responded: "How can this be so when you eat the meat of camels and drink their milk." He said: "That was lawful for Abraham and so we make it lawful too." The Jews said: "Everything that we take for unlawful today was unlawful to Noah and Abraham and so on and so forth until our time." To refute them, Allah revealed this verse. In the exchange as the Jews themselves retell it afterward, they claimed nothing was self-forbidden: whatever is forbidden for us today, such as camel meat, was already forbidden on all prophets from Adam to Moses, and only Muhammad makes such things lawful. And they claimed those things were also forbidden in the Torah. Hence the challenge.

For Allah's sake, Isra'il forbade himself the things he found most delightful. That practice was allowed under his period's law, making its placement after the previous verse apt. Our Law allows spending what we like and covet in obedience, not prohibiting what Allah allowed. Scripture supports it: "And gives his wealth, in spite of love for it" (2:177), and "And they give food, in spite of their love for it" (76:8).

### Four questions answered, and the answer that divided the room

Imam Ahmad records, through Ibn ʿAbbās, a delegation of Jews asking to be told things only a Prophet would know. The Prophet ﷺ invited their questions, but first took a pledge like the one Ya'qub took from his children: if they recognised the truth of his answers, they would follow him in Islam. They agreed.

The Prophet took their covenant that they will follow him if he answers these questions, and they agreed. He said:

> "I ask you by He Who sent down the Tawrah to Musa, do you not know that Isra'il once became very ill? When his illness was prolonged, he vowed to Allah that if He cures His illness, he would prohibit the best types of drink and food for himself. Was not the best food to him camel meat and the best drink camel milk?"

Affirmation followed, and the Messenger ﷺ called Allah to witness against them before continuing:

> "I ask you by Allah, other than Whom there is no deity (worthy of worship), Who sent down the Tawrah to Musa, do you not know that man's discharge is thick and white and woman's is yellow and thin? If any of these fluids becomes dominant, the offspring will take its sex and resemblance by Allah's leave. Hence, if the man's is more than the woman's, the child will be male, by Allah's leave. If the woman's discharge is more than the man's, then the child will be female, by Allah's leave."

A second affirmation brought a second call for Allah's witness, and then:

> "I ask you by He Who sent down the Tawrah to Musa, do you not know that the eyes of this unlettered Prophet sleep, but his heart does not sleep?"

A third affirmation brought a third witnessing. Then came the decisive request: 'Tell us now about your Wali among the angels, for this is when we either follow or shun you.' His answer named Jibril as his Wali, the one bringing revelation, and as the Wali of every Prophet Allah sent. Rejection followed at once: 'We then shun you. Had you a Wali other than Jibril, we would have followed you.' On that, Allah, the Exalted revealed, "Say: 'Whoever is an enemy to Jibril...'" (2:97).

### Abrogation inside their own law

The second objective is a refutation of any denial of abrogation, naskh. Start with Jacob's marriage: two sisters could be wives at the same time, then the Tawrah prohibited the practice. Earlier, Adam's sons could marry Adam's daughters, before that too was forbidden. Ibrahim's law allowed female servants as companions alongside a wife, as Hajar was taken while Sarah was married to him; the Tawrah later prohibited this. Food tells the same story. Noah on leaving the ark could eat all types of animals. Isra'il then forbade camel meat and milk for himself, his children imitated him, and the Tawrah later prohibited that food and added several more prohibitions. Every example sits inside the Tawrah, and every one is a Naskh, an abrogation. Let the Jews now consider what Allah legislated for ʿĪsā, and whether it falls in that category. Why do they not follow ʿĪsā in this regard? Instead they defied and rebelled against ʿĪsā and against the religion Allah sent Muhammad ﷺ with.

The Jews would ask on the same pattern why Islam differs from former revealed religions on the lawful and unlawful, such as camel and rabbit meat made illegal by their self-made laws while licit in Islam, and how a revealed religion could command prayer toward the Kaʿbah instead of al-Bayt al-Maqdis, Jerusalem, the direction of prayer in the teachings of all prophets until then. They refused to believe that God could reveal a religion in which the Kaʿbah was the direction for prayer.

### Before any command: a kindness, and the two schools of effort

In their original state, things receive no declaration of permission or prohibition from God. No divine ordinance, ḥadd, exists concerning them in that state, and this is liberality and kindness from the Real. The state lasts until a command or law arrives. God later eased precepts of religious obligation, aḥkām al-taklīf, for advanced disciples, ahl al-nihāya. They adopt what is easiest so they can perfect what comes to them from precepts of the hearts, aḥkām al-qulūb, since difficulties incumbent on their hearts are harder for them. Novices, ahl al-bidāya, receive difficulty in tasks and litanies, adopting the more onerous way so as to empty their hearts of thoughts. Whoever thinks otherwise is in error.

False claims are alluded to in "Whoever invents falsehood against God". People of false claims and errors absolve themselves and attribute false notions, hawājis, to God, but God is free of these. Incoming thoughts, khawāṬir, can be distinguished from false notions, hawājis, only by a rare servant.

### In short

- All food was lawful until Jacob, suffering sciatica, vowed away the camel meat he loved most, and the Torah cannot be produced to prove otherwise.
- Abrogation is proved from inside the Torah itself: Noah's universal permission, Jacob's vow, Adam's marriages, Hajar's companionship and two sisters all changed.
- The four questions were answered under covenant, and the refusals turned on one name: Jibril, the Wali of every prophet (2:97, 66:1).

The passage ends by turning the hearer somewhere positive: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Allah has declared the truth. So follow the Way of Abraham, the upright—who was not a polytheist.”" Allah has spoken the truth in what He conveyed and legislated in the Qur'an, and the religion of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) legislated there is the perfect way. **"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Allah has declared the truth"**: Say, O Muhammad: God has spoken the truth in this matter just as He has in all that He has related. ### The same command elsewhere

"Say: 'Truly, my Lord has guided me to a straight path, a right religion, the religion of Ibrahim, the Hanif, and he was not of the idolaters'" (6:161). "Then, We have sent the revelation to you (saying): 'Follow the religion of Ibrahim, the Hanif, and he was not of the idolaters'" (16:123).

Abraham's creed, milla, is complete abandonment to God: al-khurūju ilā Allāh bi-l-kulliya, and surrender to His decree without holding back in any way, wa-l-taslīmu li-ḥukmihi min ghayri an tabqā baqiyya. To affirm anything other than God in accounting for what comes into being is idolatry, shirk.

### In short

- Allah has spoken the truth about Jacob, Abraham and the lawful and unlawful.
- Abraham's way is to be followed as the complete and perfect path of surrender (6:161, 16:123).
- His creed inwardly is total abandonment to God and surrender to His decree without reserve.
