<<<VERSE 6:60>>>
"He is the One Who calls back your souls by night and knows what you do by day, then revives you daily to complete your appointed term."

The rest of the verse says that to him everyone returns at the last, and that he will then tell them what they used to do. The verse that follows the keys of the unseen does not argue about what Allah knows. It points at something everybody does twice a day, and draws the conclusion from it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He is the One Who calls back your souls by night"** Sleep is a taking away, and the same verb covers death itself. He brings death to his servants during the sleep of the night, since sleep is a minor death. The same verb covers death itself, and another verse spells out the distinction: it is Allah who takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that do not die during their sleep, and he keeps the souls for which he has ordained death, and sends the rest on for a term appointed (39:42). Both kinds of taking are named together, and that is the point of the wording here. The verse about Isa uses the same idea, where Allah says: "O Isa, I will take you and raise you to Myself" (3:55).

**"and knows what you do by day"** The deeds and actions performed in the day are not outside the knowledge claimed in the previous verse. The verse covers movements and idleness, day and night. It is the same to him whether a person conceals his speech or declares it openly, whether he hides by night or goes about freely by day (13:10).

**"then revives you daily to complete your appointed term"** He raises you again, that is, wakes you up in the day, on the reading of Mujahid, Qatadah and As-Suddi. The appointed term is the life span of each person. One gloss adds the reason for the return, namely that you carry out your tasks until the fixed period is over. Another reading takes the term to be the point at which both your lifespan and your provision are finished.

**"To Him is your ultimate return"** On the Day of Qiyamah, after death.

**"then He will inform you of what you used to do"** This is not raw information. He will reward you, good for good and evil for evil, and the telling and the settling of the account come in the same breath.

### Sleep as a daily rehearsal

The example is chosen because nobody has to be told that it works. Every night and every morning a person witnesses his own rising from a simulated death, and the verse argues from that to the collective death and the collective rising after it. The Supreme Being who can do the first can do the second. With his most perfect power, this is how it shall be, and so the verse ends with the return, the reckoning of deeds, and then the rewards and the punishments.

A related note adds that sleep suspends the body in the way death would, and that this is stated in a hadith as well as in the verse. The world, on this reading, has been built to supply practical examples of the very things people are being asked to believe. A person who does not close his eyes and screen off his mind with false veils will find demonstrations of them across the universe. Branches emerge from a trunk and leaves sprout from the branches, but their joints are different, because the Creator knows that the branches have to stay attached while the leaves have to detach and fall. If it were otherwise, the leaves would never separate, and the whole system of bringing new life to the tree each year would be upset. A seed, once sown, finds in the soil around it all the ingredients from which it will grow into a full tree. So if he attends to the state of a leaf and of a seed, the claim that he is inattentive to the state of a human being makes no sense.

### Why night and day are a mercy

The same pairing appears elsewhere in the Qur'an as an argument from design. It is out of his mercy that he made the night and the day, so that people may rest in one of them and seek his bounty in the other (28:73). And the night was made as a covering, and the day as a means of livelihood (78:10-11). Read next to this verse, the arrangement is not merely kind. It is legible. The hours that take a person away and give him back to himself are the same hours in which his deeds are being noted.

### What the messengers' knowledge does not prove

The discussion of the unseen in the previous verse needs one guard rail here. Allah had given his messengers, and the last of them above all, knowledge of many unseen things that surpassed what all the angels and the other prophets were given. That does not narrow the gap at all. The knowledge of no one can be equal to Allah's, and it never can be. Otherwise this would be the same excess the Christians committed in their reverence for their prophet, when they began to equate him with Allah. In the language of the Qur'an that is shirk, and the verses about the keys and about sleep are both there to close that door.

### In short

- Sleep is a taking of the soul that stops short of death, and the verse uses it because everybody experiences the return.
- The knowledge claimed for the day and the night is the same knowledge stated in the verse about the keys, now applied to the person himself.
- The appointed term is the end of a lifespan, and the return after it is followed by being told what one used to do, which means being repaid.
- Honouring a prophet past the point of creatureliness is named here as the excess that turns into shirk.

<<<VERSE 6:61>>>
He reigns supreme over all of His creation, and sends recording-angels, watching over you. When death comes to any of you, Our angels take their soul, never neglecting this duty

One sentence covers the whole of a life: who is above whom, who is written down, and who does the collecting.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He reigns supreme over all of his creation"** The divine name here is the Vanquisher, the one who controls everything, so that all things are subservient to his grace, greatness and majesty. He is described as dominant and irresistible, to whom everything is subjugated, and as above his servants in a manner that befits his majesty. That last phrase is doing real work: the being above them is not a place or a posture.

**"and sends recording-angels, watching over you"** These are guardians over mankind. For every person there are angels in succession, before and behind him, guarding him by the command of Allah (13:11), and watching his deeds and recording them (82:10). Two receivers take what a person says, one sitting on the right and one on the left, and not a word does he utter but there is a watcher ready by him (50:17-18). One account has it that there are two angels in the day and two in the night, and that what they record is both the good and the bad.

**"When death comes to any of you, Our angels take their soul"** The appointed hour is when a person's lifespan ends and he is dying, and the angels in charge of that job are called "our messengers", which makes the taking of a soul an errand rather than a seizure. Ibn Abbas and several others held that the Angel of Death is helped by angels who pull the soul from the body. Once it reaches the throat, he captures it. A variant reading of the verb lets the sentence say that they take him fully, or that they cause him to die, and both senses point at the same act.

**"never neglecting this duty"** They do not fall short of what they have been commanded, and they do not delay the time of death by a fraction. They guard the soul of the dead person and carry it wherever Allah wills. If he was among the righteous it goes up to Illiyyin, and if he was among the wicked and the sinners it goes down to Sijjin.

### What the angels say at the bedside

A hadith recorded by Ahmad, transmitted from Abu Hurayrah, describes the same moment from the other side. The angels attend a dying person. If he was righteous, they say to his soul: O pure soul from a pure body, come out honoured, and take the good news of rest and contentment from a Lord who is not angry at you. They keep saying it until the soul leaves the body. Then they raise it toward heaven and ask that its door be opened. Something is asked at the door: who is this? The answer names the soul. The reply is a welcome, an entry with honour, and the same good news again. This goes on until the soul reaches the heaven above which there is nothing. If the dying person was evil, the angels say: O wicked soul from a wicked body, come out in disgrace. They tell it to expect boiling fluid, a fluid dark and murky and intensely cold, and other torments of the same kind, all together, matching what it did. This is repeated until the soul leaves. It is raised up, and the door is asked to open. The answer is no welcome and a return in disgrace, and the doors of heaven are not opened for it. Then it is thrown down until it comes back to the grave. There the righteous man is made to sit up, and is told as before. The evil man is made to sit up too, and is told the same kind of thing.

The detail worth noticing is the change of voice. In the verse the angels are "our messengers", and in the hadith they are the ones who speak, and in both cases the person dying is the one being addressed. Nothing in either text puts the honour or the disgrace of the soul in the hands of its family, its rank or its wealth.

### In short

- Allah's being above his servants is stated with a qualifier, so that supremacy is not imagined as a position.
- The recording angels are guardians in succession, and the record they keep is of words and deeds, good and bad.
- The taking of the soul is done by angels with assistants, on a description transmitted from Ibn Abbas, and it is never late by a fraction.
- The hadith of the pure soul and the wicked soul shows what is said at the moment of death, and that the doors of heaven open on the soul's character and not on its pedigree.

<<<VERSE 6:62>>>
"Then they are ˹all˺ returned to Allah—their True Master. Judgment is His ˹alone˺. And He is the Swiftest Reckoner."

Three clauses, and they close the whole passage about the unseen, the leaf, the sleep and the soul.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then they are all returned to Allah"** This is read in two ways. It can be the return of each soul when its death comes, and it can be the return of the whole creation on the Day of Judgement, when they are presented to him and he subjects them to his just decision. The two are not in competition, since the verse is about where everything ends up.

**"their True Master"** He is their master and their possessor, the true one, the eternal one, the just. Another reading takes "their Lord, the Just" as a claim about who is entitled to be worshipped: he is their real master, whom they ought to have worshipped and did not worship as he ought to be worshipped. On that reading the sentence also says something about the alternatives, which is that objects of worship other than Allah are void.

**"Judgment is his alone"** The decree that will be carried out in their case belongs to him and to nobody else, which answers the demand made four verses back for a punishment delivered on the challengers' timetable. Say: those of the earlier times and the later ones will all be gathered together for an appointed day of a known time (56:49-50). They will be gathered so that not one of them is left behind (18:47), and the judgement will be made without any weight being lost, and then it is said: and your Lord wrongs no one (18:49).

**"And He is the Swiftest Reckoner"** He reckons with the whole of creation swiftly. One gloss, resting on a hadith to that effect, puts it as half a day of the days of this world, and another says he takes all of them to account in a single day. The two agree in what matters: nothing queues up in front of him, and no case waits while another is heard.

### The question the last clause answers

A reader could stop at "Judgment is his alone" and ask a practical question. How is it possible for one God to take the reckoning of age-long deeds of billions of human beings, and to remember each of them? The answer the wording gives is that the question itself is the error. Reasoning about what Allah does by analogy with what people do is ignorance, and he does it in no time. The clause is not there to reassure a nervous accountant. It is there to remove a comparison.

What is left, at the end of the passage, is a pair of descriptions that hold each other in place. He is the Just, so the reckoning will not be bent. He is the swift, so it will not be postponed, and the delay that made the mockers confident was never a reprieve that he was struggling to administer.

### The master who is also the guardian

The word translated as master carries more than ownership. Before this passage began, the same surah told the Prophet to greet the poor believers with peace, on the ground that his Lord had taken mercy upon himself. That is the shape of the sentence here too: he is the sovereign and the judge of judges, and something besides that. He is their master and guardian, and he comes to their help whenever they need him. The reckoning and the mercy were both written down, and the verse names the second before it names the first.

### In short

- The return is both the individual one at death and the collective one at the Judgement, and the verse is comfortable with both.
- Judgment belongs to Allah alone, which is the standing answer to those who wanted the torment scheduled to their convenience.
- Speed is asserted against a presumption of scale: billions of lives do not make a queue for the one who knows the leaf and the grain.
- "Their True Master" keeps guardianship inside the verdict, so that the passage about knowledge and power ends where the passage about mercy began.
