<<<VERSE 5:41>>>
"O Messenger! Do not grieve for those who race to disbelieve—those who say, “We believe” with their tongues, but their hearts are in disbelief. Nor those among the Jews who eagerly listen to lies, attentive to those who are too arrogant to come to you. They distort the Scripture, taking rulings out of context, then say, “If this is the ruling you get ˹from Muḥammad˺, accept it. If not, beware!” Whoever Allah allows to be deluded, you can never be of any help to them against Allah. It is not Allah’s Will to purify their hearts. For them is disgrace in this world, and they will suffer a tremendous punishment in the Hereafter"

This verse is a word of comfort to the Prophet ﷺ and a warning to the believers. It describes a group of hypocrites who showed a Muslim face in Madinah, and a group of Jews who listened to the Prophet ﷺ in order to carry his words back to their leaders. The material says the verse opens the next part of the chapter, which returns to the People of the Book after a short break. Its subject is the Jewish tribes who lived near Madinah, and the cases they brought to the Prophet ﷺ.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O Messenger! Do not grieve."** The address is one of consolation. The material explains that the Prophet ﷺ was grieved by those who rushed to reject the message. The verse tells him that their rejection is not his failure, and that he is not responsible for their hearts.

**"Those who race to disbelieve."** The material reads this as a description of those who hurry toward kufr, the rejection of the truth. They are described as racing because they do not hesitate to reject it, and they seek out every opportunity to do so.

**"Those who say, 'We believe,' with their tongues, but their hearts are in disbelief."** The material identifies these as the hypocrites. It says that the hypocrites professed faith in words while their hearts held no faith at all. Ibn Ubayy and his followers are named in the material as the leaders of this group. The grammar of the verse ties "with their tongues" to the word "say," so the claim of faith is spoken and not believed.

**"Nor those among the Jews who eagerly listen to lies."** The material says that the Jews are described as listeners who are eager to hear falsehood. The verse does not say they hear the truth. It says they gather falsehood and bring it back with them. The material mentions Banu Qurayzah and Ka'b and his followers as the Jewish side of the group.

**"Attentive to those who are too arrogant to come to you."** The material explains that the more prominent among them refused to come to the Prophet ﷺ in person, out of pride. They sent inferiors to listen to him, and those inferiors carried his words to their leaders. The material says that this is why the verse speaks of listeners on behalf of others.

**"They distort the Scripture, taking rulings out of context."** The material explains the phrase as the changing of words from their places, by altering their meanings after they have understood them. The Torah is the Scripture in view. The material says the change was made knowingly.

**"If this is the ruling you get from Muhammad, accept it. If not, beware!"** The material reports that this was said by the leaders to their followers. The sense is that the followers should accept the Prophet's ruling only if it suits them, and should reject it if it does not.

**"Whoever Allah allows to be deluded, you can never be of any help to them against Allah."** The material explains that no one can protect a person whom Allah has left in his delusion. It notes that a trial can mean a test, and that the verse is saying the Prophet ﷺ cannot save such people by his own effort.

**"It is not Allah's Will to purify their hearts."** The material explains that the purification of the heart means cleansing from treachery, scheming and persistence in kufr. It says that these people have shut themselves out of this purification by their own choice.

**"For them is disgrace in this world, and they will suffer a tremendous punishment in the Hereafter."** The material explains the disgrace as humiliation in this world, and the Hereafter punishment as greater than the worldly one. It notes that the Jewish tribes were later made to suffer for their conduct.

### The background of the verse

The material explains that the verse grew out of two cases between the Jewish tribes near Madinah. The first concerned killing and retaliation, and the second concerned adultery and its punishment.

Before Islam, the tribes of Banu Qurayzah and Banu Nadir lived near Madinah. Banu Nadir were the stronger and wealthier. They forced Banu Qurayzah into a harsh agreement on the blood money for killing. If a man of Banu Nadir killed a man of Banu Qurayzah, there was no retaliation, and only a payment of dates was due. If a man of Banu Qurayzah killed a man of Banu Nadir, the killer was put to death and a larger payment was taken. The material lists other unequal terms, such as two men of Banu Qurayzah being killed for one of Banu Nadir, and two hands or ears being taken for one. Banu Qurayzah were too weak to refuse.

When the Prophet ﷺ came to Madinah, these tribes had not become Muslims and were not bound by any treaty with the Muslim community. The material says that they still knew the Torah pointed to the Last Prophet. They used the Islamic principle of equality as a support in their dispute with Banu Nadir. Banu Qurayzah refused to pay the doubled blood money, and Banu Nadir threatened war. The two sides agreed to take the case to the Prophet ﷺ. The material says Banu Nadir then sent a delegation of their hypocritical allies to learn his judgement before he gave it. These men were told to accept his ruling only if it favoured Banu Nadir.

The material gives a second case, from Khaybar. A married couple from a noble Jewish family had committed adultery. The Torah prescribed stoning, but their people hoped for a lighter punishment. They sent the couple to Banu Qurayzah, asking them to bring the case to Muhammad, with the same condition: accept the ruling if it is light, reject it if it is heavy.

### The Torah and the verse of stoning

The material gives the account of the Jewish adulterers, which Al-Bukhari and Muslim record. It was reported by Ibn 'Umar, who said that the Jews came to the Prophet ﷺ and told him that a man and a woman among them had committed adultery. He asked what the Torah says about stoning. They said it prescribes only exposure and flogging. Abdullah bin Salam said they were lying, and that the Torah mentions stoning. The reader hid the verse with his hand, but when Abdullah bin Salam told him to remove it, the verse on stoning was revealed. The Prophet ﷺ ordered the two to be stoned. Ibn 'Umar said that he was among those who stoned them, and that he saw the man shielding the woman from the stones with his body.

The material gives another report from Abu Dawud, in which the Prophet ﷺ sat on the Torah, placed it on the pillow, and said that he trusted it and the One who revealed it. He asked for their most learned man. The material also gives a report from al-Bara' bin Azib, who said that the Jewish man had been flogged and darkened with coal, and that the Prophet ﷺ asked a Jewish scholar to confirm the Torah's rule. The scholar said the Torah prescribes stoning, but that the notables had stopped it and replaced it with flogging and blackening the face. The Prophet ﷺ said that he was the first to bring back the command that had been suspended, and he ordered the Jewish man to be stoned.

The material explains that the Prophet ﷺ asked them about the Torah not out of honour for their book, but because Allah commanded him to do so. It made them admit what they had hidden. Their coming to him was motivated by desire, hoping for a lighter punishment, and not by belief in his judgement.

### The judicial process for non-Muslims

The material explains that these Jews were not Dhimmis of the Islamic state, nor did they believe in its law. They had a pact of no war with the Prophet ﷺ. Because of this, he had the choice to turn away or to judge between them. Abu Bakr al-Jassas, in his work on the rulings of the Qur'an, reconciled the two verses on this point. The choice in the verse applies to people who are under a pact but are not citizens. The command to judge applies to citizens of the Islamic state, who owe allegiance to its law.

The material says that in either case the Prophet ﷺ judged according to the law revealed by Allah, and not according to the desires of the parties. In matters of common law, such as the punishment for killing or adultery, the rule is the same for everyone. The material gives the example of the hand of the thief, which applies to all citizens. But personal and religious matters of non-Muslims are left to their own religion. The Prophet ﷺ did not interfere with the marriages of the Magians, the Jews and the Christians, and he accepted them as valid. When non-Muslim citizens bring a case to a Muslim judge by agreement, the judge rules by his own law.

### The four traits of the group

The material ends its explanation with four evil traits of the group the verse describes. The first is to race toward disbelief. The second is to listen eagerly to lies. The third is to distort the Scripture from its context. The fourth is to put personal desire above the command of Allah. The material says that the Muslim is warned against these traits, and that they reject the truth.

The material also draws a broader lesson. A person who places his personal interests above the truth, and who distorts the message to justify himself, loses the capacity to accept the truth. The servant of Allah who carries the message should not be discouraged by opposition, because the efforts of opponents are not against the messenger but against Allah, and so they cannot succeed.

### The reports on the reason for the verse

The material gives several accounts of the reason for revelation. Some say it was revealed about the hypocrites and the Jews, and the subject of the blood money. Others say it was about the adulterers of Khaybar. The material says that Ibn 'Abbas, Al-Bara' bin Azib, Hudhayfah bin Al-Yaman, Abu Mijlaz, 'Ikrimah and Al-Hasan Al-Basri all held that the verse concerned the People of the Book, and that Al-Hasan added that it applies to the Muslims as well. The material also reports that Ibrahim and Mansur took the verses as applying to the Children of Israel, and that Allah accepted them for this Ummah.

The material says that the verses on the rulings in this chapter are related to these events, and that the lesson of the verse is clear. Its language is a comfort to the Prophet ﷺ, a warning to the believers, and a judgement on those who used the Scripture to suit their desires.

### In short

- The verse comforts the Prophet ﷺ and warns against the hypocrites and Jews who used his words for their own purposes.
- It describes two cases, the blood money of Banu Qurayzah and Banu Nadir and the adultery of the Jewish couple, and shows how the Torah's stoning rule came back to light.
- Judgement under a pact or for citizens follows the law of Allah, and the traits the verse names must be avoided by the believer.
