<<<VERSE 5:41>>>
The Messenger ﷺ is consoled in the face of people who treat revelation as something to manipulate: "Do not grieve for those who race to disbelieve". Some profess belief without inward faith. Others listen eagerly to falsehood and seek a judgment only if it confirms the result they have already chosen.

Their problem is not the absence of information. They displace known rulings and make desire the condition of acceptance. The verse exposes that conduct, limits the Prophet's ﷺ responsibility for their response and warns of disgrace and punishment.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O Messenger! Do not grieve for those who race to disbelieve"** comforts Muhammad ﷺ when their opposition causes him pain. They hurry into rejection, preferring their desires and opinions to Allah's command. Their conduct does not mean he has failed to convey the message.

**"those who say, “We believe” with their tongues, but their hearts are in disbelief."** describes hypocrisy. Their words claim what their inward state denies. One gloss names 'Abdullah Ibn Ubayy and his followers among those meant.

**"Nor those among the Jews who eagerly listen to lies"** concerns the people described by this conduct, not a statement that every Jewish person is identical. They welcome false claims and receive their leaders' distortions as something to follow.

**"attentive to those who are too arrogant to come to you."** is explained in two related ways. They listen obediently to absent leaders, or attend the Prophet's ﷺ gathering to carry information back to those leaders. The latter remain away while their agents gather material for them.

**"They distort the Scripture, taking rulings out of context"** means changing the understood meaning or practical application after knowing its proper place. The accounts particularly discuss the punishment of adultery and unequal treatment in cases of killing. A brief gloss also includes alteration of the Prophet's ﷺ description in the Torah.

**"then say, “If this is the ruling you get ˹from Muḥammad˺, accept it. If not, beware!”"** reveals their instruction to the delegation. A desired ruling is to be accepted, while an unwelcome ruling is to be rejected. The authority of revelation is being made dependent on preference.

**"Whoever Allah allows to be deluded, you can never be of any help to them against Allah."** speaks of fitnah, trial or being left in delusion. Other glosses include exposure of their condition and persistence in disbelief. The Messenger ﷺ cannot independently overturn Allah's judgment upon a heart that rejects guidance.

**"It is not Allah’s Will to purify their hearts."** describes their being left without the purification they have resisted through treachery, scheming and deliberate rejection. The warning follows the account of their chosen conduct, not an excuse for it.

**"For them is disgrace in this world, and they will suffer a tremendous punishment in the Hereafter"** joins present humiliation with the Fire. Reported forms of worldly disgrace include exposure, expulsion, killing in the historical conflict and subjection to jizya. These are explanations of the narrated consequence, not instructions for private action against a community.

### What are the two principal reported occasions?

The accounts concern Jewish groups around Al-Madinah and their attempts to obtain a favourable judgment from the Prophet ﷺ. One concerns killing, qisas, proportionate retaliation, and diyah, blood money. The other concerns adultery and its prescribed punishment.

One explanation initially favours the adultery account, while the fuller treatment recognises that both disputes may underlie this sequence of verses. Their shared feature is conditional acceptance of judgment. The parties want a prophetic decision when it serves their purpose, but not when it challenges an entrenched advantage.

The detailed reports therefore need not be flattened into a single event. They explain different forms of the same conduct, with the later laws about judgment and retaliation fitting the broader sequence.

### How had the two tribes made blood money unequal?

Banu An-Nadir were stronger and wealthier than Banu Qurayzah and had imposed unequal terms upon them. In the report from Ibn 'Abbas recorded by Ahmad, the weaker group's dead received fifty wasaq while the stronger group's dead received a hundred. The supplied account describes the payment as gold, but its parenthetical weight explanation is not consistent with another account's measure, so no modern conversion is attempted here.

A more detailed version gives seventy wasaq of dates against one hundred and forty. It also describes unequal retaliation: the stronger tribe could demand a man for a woman, two men for one, a free person for a slave, and two corresponding limbs for one limb injured. These are reported terms of domination, not rulings approved by the verse.

The figures differ, but both accounts describe one life being valued at twice another on the basis of power. The weaker tribe had accepted those terms through fear, not because they represented equal justice.

After the Hijrah, the migration, a member of Qurayzah killed someone from An-Nadir. The stronger tribe demanded its enhanced payment. Qurayzah resisted, pointing out their common ancestry, religion and homeland. They would no longer accept that weakness should halve the value of their people.

### Why did they approach the Prophet ﷺ?

The dispute nearly produced war. Elders advised taking it to Muhammad ﷺ. The weaker party expected that his justice would not preserve the imposed inequality. The stronger party also recognised that an impartial decision would probably go against its privilege.

It therefore sent people ahead to discover his likely ruling. These intermediaries outwardly associated with Islam but secretly served their former allies. They were instructed to accept only a decision granting the demanded advantage.

Allah disclosed the plan to the Messenger ﷺ. The account connects this with the warning against racing into disbelief and with the ensuing passages on judgment (5:42, 5:44, 5:45, 5:47).

Ibn Jarir records Ibn 'Abbas identifying the parties as Bani An-Nadir and Bani Qurayzah and describing the Prophet ﷺ as making their blood money equal. Ahmad, Abu Dawud and An-Nasa'i also record the account through Abu Ishaq. Al-Baghawi supplies the fuller version of the unequal pact.

The principle is that power cannot make one group's blood worth more than another's. The historical discussion compares such distinctions with later racial inequality in law, using the comparison to expose privilege rather than to introduce a new contemporary legal survey.

### What happened in the adultery case?

Malik reports from 'Abdullah bin 'Umar that a Jewish man and woman accused of adultery were brought to the Prophet ﷺ. He asked what their Torah prescribed. They answered with public exposure and flogging rather than the ruling they had concealed.

'Abdullah bin Salam challenged the answer and asked for the Torah. When it was opened, the reader covered the relevant passage with his hand and read the material before and after it. Ibn Salam told him to remove his hand. The concealed ruling on stoning then became visible.

The Prophet ﷺ ordered the judgment carried out. Ibn 'Umar reports seeing the man trying to shield the woman with his body. Al-Bukhari and Muslim collect the report, and the account also preserves Muslim's wording of Ibn 'Umar's presence among those carrying out the sentence.

Another Al-Bukhari narration describes a respected reader blind in one eye. The Prophet ﷺ calls for the Torah to be brought and read truthfully (3:93). On exposing the passage, the reader acknowledges that its knowledge had been hidden among them.

These accounts centre deliberate concealment. The dispute is not presented as an innocent lack of access to the rule. The people carrying the book know the relevant passage and attempt to prevent its application.

### What does the visit to Al-Midras add?

Abu Dawud records Ibn 'Umar describing an invitation to the Quff district. The Prophet ﷺ went to the house of Al-Midras, where he was addressed as Abu Al-Qasim and asked to decide the case.

A cushion had been arranged for him. When the Torah was brought, he placed it on that cushion and expressed belief in it and in the One who had revealed it. He then asked for their most learned person, and a young man was brought. The narration continues with the same case.

The explanation does not take this as surrendering the authority of the final revelation to any altered practice. It says the Prophet ﷺ acted by Allah's command and required them to acknowledge the ruling they were concealing. His judgment agreed with the preserved instruction, exposing the contradiction in their conduct.

### How is Ibn Surya's testimony described?

A fuller account places the accused couple among the people of Khaybar and describes them as belonging to influential families. Hoping for a lighter penalty, they sent the case and the couple to their associates in Banu Qurayzah. The delegation, named as including Ka'b ibn Ashraf and others, was to accept only the desired result.

When they asked the Prophet ﷺ about a married couple committing adultery, he first asked whether they would accept his judgment. They said yes, but recoiled when the judgment was stoning. Jibra'il is reported as directing him to seek Ibn Surya as an arbitrator.

The account identifies Ibn Surya as a learned young man from Fidak, fair in complexion and blind in one eye. The delegation acknowledged his exceptional knowledge. Summoned and placed under a solemn oath, he confirmed that the Torah contained the same penalty.

He said that without the oath and fear of the Torah's consequence for a false statement, he would have concealed it. The confession therefore exposed both the actual rule and the willingness to hide it.

### Why had a lighter punishment replaced it?

Ibn Surya explained that a prince had committed adultery and been spared. When an ordinary offender later faced stoning, his supporters demanded that the prince first receive the same treatment. To avoid that confrontation, leaders agreed upon a lighter penalty for everyone.

The replacement involved beating and public humiliation, with faces blackened. Other accounts describe a hundred lashes and parading the offenders on a donkey facing backward. Those details describe the substituted practice, not the revealed ruling they claimed to uphold.

Al-Bara' ibn 'Azib reports another occasion in which the Prophet ﷺ saw a Jewish man who had been flogged and blackened with coal. The people initially claimed this was their scriptural law. A learned man, questioned by an oath invoking Allah who gave the Torah to Musa (Moses), admitted that stoning had been replaced after notables were spared and ordinary people objected.

The Prophet ﷺ declared himself the first to restore the command after its suspension and ordered the sentence. The report connects the event with the instruction to accept a desired ruling and avoid an unwelcome one. Its later applications to the judgment verses belong to those verses' own explanations.

### What makes this more than a search for another legal opinion?

The criticism concerns knowingly displacing a recognised rule to protect wealth, status, relatives or clients. The delegation did not come willing to hear and follow the truth. It wanted another religious authority to certify a predetermined preference.

One expectation was that Islam's concessions and ease might produce a lighter sentence. If so, the parties hoped to obtain the benefit without appearing to alter the Torah themselves. If not, they intended to reject the decision. The apparent search for arbitration concealed refusal to submit to any unwelcome outcome.

This explains the comfort given to the Prophet ﷺ. He need not measure the success of his mission by whether such people approve his answer. Their refusal exposes their own criterion, not a defect in the message.

### How does the discussion distinguish outsiders from protected citizens?

The legal explanation distinguishes non-Muslims living under a no-war agreement from dhimmis, protected non-Muslim members of the Islamic polity. One historical account describes the tribes at an earlier stage as having no treaty obligation to follow Islamic law. The later legal discussion describes a non-aggression pact but not the full relationship of protected citizenship.

For parties outside that protected relationship, the Prophet ﷺ was given a choice whether to hear the case. For protected citizens, safeguarding rights and removing injustice is a responsibility of the government. A responsible official cannot simply refuse to provide justice because the parties are not Muslims.

Abu Bakr al-Jassas reconciles the passage allowing a choice with the later command to judge by distinguishing these relationships. The first concerns people with the limited pact, and the second concerns people for whom the Muslim administration is legally responsible.

A different explanation treats the earlier choice as later abrogated. That dispute and the specific wording of the judicial command belong more fully to the next verse. Here the distinction shows why the narrative does not establish one undifferentiated rule for every non-Muslim litigant.

### Does a common criminal law require identical personal religious law?

The account separates public criminal justice from personal religious matters. The examples at issue are homicide, blood money and adultery. In the legal explanation, common criminal rules are not changed according to the litigant's wealth or communal identity.

It does not follow that every personal religious practice must be administered as though the people were Muslims. The discussion says the Prophet ﷺ did not impose Muslim restrictions on wine and swine upon non-Muslims in the same manner, and recognised their marriages rather than subjecting each existing union to every Muslim marriage requirement.

It mentions Magians of Hajar and Jews and Christians of Najran and Wadi al-Qura coming under Muslim protection. It also reports differing marriage practices, including close-kin unions among Magians and marriages without the Muslim conditions of witnesses or 'iddah, the waiting period, among the other communities. Those are historical descriptions in the legal account, not approval of such marriages for Muslims or a complete description of those communities in every time.

The stated approach leaves personal and religious affairs to their own law and permits judges from their own religion to handle them. If both parties voluntarily choose a Muslim judge as arbitrator, he decides according to the law he is appointed to uphold. Consent to his arbitration does not oblige him to give whichever answer the parties prefer.

### How does distortion close the heart?

The narrative describes an alliance of hypocrites protecting worldly interests and religious leaders protecting status. Some considered it beneath them to attend the Prophet's ﷺ gatherings, but sent others to listen and report. The information could then be given a misleading meaning for a campaign against the message.

Repeatedly placing self-interest above truth produces a disposition unable to welcome correction. The warning about hearts not being purified is explained through that deliberate abandonment of guidance. They turn away, and are left in the falsehood they choose.

The Messenger ﷺ can convey, warn and make the facts clear. He cannot force sincere belief or independently purify a heart against Allah's judgment. The lesson for someone conveying the religion is therefore steadfastness without assuming responsibility for every opponent's refusal.

Nor does the criticism belong only to people in the historical narrative. The patterns themselves are dangerous: listening eagerly to convenient lies, misreporting an opponent, displacing known words and accepting authority only when it agrees with desire. Those are the habits from which a believer must guard himself.

### In short

- The Messenger ﷺ is consoled against hypocritical belief and deliberate manipulation of revelation.
- The reports connect the verse with concealed adultery rulings and unequal blood money, while preserving differences in their details.
- Seeking judgment only on condition of a favourable answer is not sincere submission to truth.
- The legal discussion distinguishes public justice, protected citizenship and personal religious law.
- Deliberate distortion hardens the heart. The duty to convey guidance does not give anyone independent power to force another person's faith.
