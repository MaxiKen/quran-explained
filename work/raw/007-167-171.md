<<<VERSE 7:167>>>
Allah reminds His Prophet ﷺ of a declaration He made about the Jews: "And ˹remember, O Prophet,˺ when your Lord declared that He would send against them others who would make them suffer terribly until the Day of Judgment." The verse then balances its warning with hope: "Indeed, your Lord is swift in punishment, but He is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful". This verse and the next describe two punishments given to the Israelites. The first is that Allah will keep sending, until the Day of Judgement, people who punish and disgrace them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember, O Prophet,˺"** Mention, O Messenger.

**"when your Lord declared"** The word is taʾadhdhana. Mujāhid said it means "declared", while others said "ordained". Allah made it known in clear terms, in words that carry the force of an oath.

**"that He would send against them others"**: against the Jews.

**"who would make them suffer terribly"**: who would humiliate and humble them in this life and afflict them with a degrading torment. This was for their disobedience, their defiance of Allah's commands and Law, and their tricks for getting round His prohibitions. Al-ʿAwfī narrated from Ibn ʿAbbās that this torment includes disgrace and the payment of the jizyah, the tribute tax. One explanation says the reference is to Muhammad ﷺ and his Ummah, who imposed the capitation tax and other measures on them.

**"until the Day of Judgment."** For as long as the world lasts.

**"Indeed, your Lord is swift in punishment,"** He is swift to requite those who disobey Him, defy His Law and refuse to believe in Him, and sometimes punishes them in this worldly life.

**"but He is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"**: forgiving to every servant who repents and turns back to Him, and merciful to those who believe and obey.

### How has this declaration been fulfilled?

It is reported that Mūsā (Moses) made the Jews pay a land tax for seven or thirteen years, and that he was the first to do so. Allah later sent Sulaymān (Solomon) against them. After him came Nebuchadnezzar, who killed some of them, took others captive and imposed the jizyah on them. They fell under the humiliating rule of the Greeks, then the Chaldeans, and later the Christians, who subdued and disgraced them and made them pay tribute. They went on paying this tax to the Magians until the Prophet ﷺ was sent. When Islam came, they came under his authority and paid the jizyah to him as well. History records that they have been dominated and treated with contempt by one people after another, down to this day.

It is also said that in the last age the Jews will side with the Dajjāl, the False Messiah. The Muslims, together with ʿĪsā (Jesus) son of Maryam, will kill them. This will happen shortly before the end of this world.

### Why are warning and mercy joined?

This verse mentions both punishment and mercy, so that no one falls into despair. Allah often places encouragement and warning side by side, so that hearts always hold both hope and fear.

### Why was their punishment so severe?

When a great reward is promised for a task, the punishment for failing in it is equally great. This is true of a community made the bearer of a holy Book. Allah raised the Jews to this rank. Besides promises of blessings in the Hereafter, they were given extraordinary benefits in this world. But they kept on disobeying, committing irreligious acts in the name of religion. So Allah removed them from their high position. It was decreed that as long as the world lasts they would taste Allah's punishment, with whatever awaits them in the Hereafter still to come.

This does not mean they would never see good fortune until the Last Day. As these verses themselves make clear, they would have spells of good times. But even these would be a kind of curse, since they would give them the chance for more insolence and so make them liable to more punishment.

### In short

- Allah declared that He would keep sending, until the Day of Judgement, people who would humble the Jews for their disobedience and tricks.
- History bears this out, from the tax under Mūsā through Nebuchadnezzar to the jizyah paid in the Prophet's time.
- A people raised to bear a holy Book is punished all the more severely for betraying it.
- The verse joins swift punishment with forgiveness and mercy, so that hearts hold both fear and hope.

<<<VERSE 7:168>>>
The second punishment follows: "We dispersed them through the land in groups—some were righteous, others were less so." Then come their trials: "We tested them with prosperity and adversity, so perhaps they would return ˹to the Right Path˺". Allah broke the Jewish people into fragments scattered across the world, so that they could not hold together as one solid nation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We dispersed them through the land in groups"** The verb means to cut into pieces. "Groups" renders umam, the plural of ummah, a community or party. Allah split them into separate nations, sects and factions across the earth, after they had been united. He also said: "And We said to the Children of Israel after Pharaoh, “Reside in the land, but when the promise of the Hereafter comes to pass, We will bring you all together.”" (17:104).

**"some were righteous,"** Some were rightly guided and upheld the rights of Allah and of His creatures. They followed the commandments of the Torah faithfully and did not distort them as others did. One explanation identifies them as the tribes beyond the river al-Raml. On another view, the righteous are those who believed in the Prophet ﷺ and followed the guidance of the Qur'an once it was revealed.

**"others were less so."** Some were moderate, and some went to excess in disobedience. Those "less so" include disbelievers and wicked people, and those who stubbornly disobeyed their prophets and even killed them. On the second view, they are those who believed the Torah to be Allah's word, yet disobeyed it or distorted its commands, selling their eternal salvation for petty worldly gains. One explanation takes them as the remaining believers among the children of Israel, and another as their disbelievers. The jinn described themselves in the same terms: "Among us are those who are righteous and those who are less so. We have been of different factions" (72:11).

**"We tested them with prosperity and adversity,"** With good things and bad: times of ease and difficulty, eagerness and fear, health and affliction. The good things were fertile land, abundance and comfort. The bad things were drought, failed harvests and hardship, or the persecutions and disasters that run through their history, or a famine sent as punishment.

**"so perhaps they would return ˹to the Right Path˺"**: so that they might turn back from their wickedness and repent of their transgression and disbelief.

### Why is scattering a punishment?

This verse shows that being united as a nation is a blessing from Allah, while being broken into scattered pieces is a punishment He sends. Muslims, for their part, have always had the blessing of a collective identity, recognised in the world as an organised people. From Madinah in the time of the Prophet ﷺ to this day, they have held independent rule in various parts of the earth. Muslim lands stretching from the Far East to the West are clear proof of this.

The Jews, by contrast, have been persecuted by one people after another from the time of Sulaymān (Solomon) onward. Their captivity under Nebuchadnezzar, their persecution by later kings, and their defeat at the hands of the Prophet ﷺ and his Caliph ʿUmar al-Fārūq are clear examples.

Their gathering in Palestine in the last age does not contradict this. The Prophet ﷺ foretold in authentic traditions that ʿĪsā (Jesus) will come down from heaven before the end of the world, in the land of Syria, and that the Christians will then accept Islam. He will fight the Jews, as mentioned under the previous verse. Allah does not summon those who have earned His punishment through police or other agents. The causes He creates drive them to the place of their end. Their gathering in one land spares ʿĪsā the trouble of seeking them across the world.

### A law for every community

This law of Allah was not for the Jews alone. It applies equally to the later community raised, after the Jews were removed, to the high rank of Allah's witnesses. If Muslims find themselves dominated by those who deny the truth and by polytheists, and their groups scattered, they must turn back to Allah. That state is a sign that they have earned His displeasure.

### What are prosperity and hardship for?

Both kinds of test were used on them. They were given prosperity and wealth to see whether they would thank their Lord. When they failed that test, they were made to suffer the many punishments described in the preceding verses. Their thinking and conduct became so twisted that in prosperity they said what Allah records: "Indeed, Allah has heard those ˹among the Jews˺ who said, “Allah is poor; we are rich!”" (3:181). And in hardship: "˹Some among˺ the Jews said, “Allah is tight-fisted.”" (5:64).

The verse also implies that prosperity and poverty, joy and suffering, are sent to people as tests of the faith and love of the created for the Creator. People of understanding should not set real store by either, since both are temporary and must end. So it is unwise to grow arrogant in prosperity or despondent in poverty. A Persian poet said that happiness brought him no real gain and sorrow did not make him weep, for both came only as guests to his steadfast spirit.

### The inner reading: the exiles of the path

For those who taste these realities, the verse alludes to the travellers among the community, the exiles of the Ṭarīqah. They wander the world from one region to another and from one cave to another. They hide their spiritual state from people and struggle for their religion against the harm others would do it. Al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ pointed to this when he said that a time will come when the religious will not stay safe unless they keep away from people. You will see them like travellers fleeing from people, now in the mountains, now in the desert.

They rush and wail across the world, in mountain monasteries and desert caves, lost in the ocean of thought, and they say of themselves to all: "No home, no possessions." The story of the Companions of the Cave bears witness to this. So does the story of the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr in the cave: "he was only one of two. While they both were in the cave, he reassured his companion, “Do not worry; Allah is certainly with us.”" (9:40).

Their travel and exile is also explained as yearning. Through most of their days and states, those who yearn have no settled place or rest. Perhaps they will reach somewhere they can see a trace of the Friend, or ask someone about Him. In this sense they have sung: our traces will point to us after we are gone, so look at our traces.

The Pir of the Ṭarīqah said: "O Allah, the homeland of Your exiles is exile, so when will this end?" How, he asked, can one You are testing deserve relief, and how can one whose homeland is exile ever reach home? "The yearner is slain by friendship, and the shroud of those slain by friendship is the vision of You."

"We tested them with prosperity and adversity" means: We tested them with pleasure and disappointment. Pleasure does not deceive them, and disappointment does not turn them away from Allah. They have before them a concern more important than their own pleasure or disappointment. Among people they are only on loan, and to themselves they are strangers. They are free of attachment, their hearts joined to their Protector and their innermost selves aware of Him. With the tongue of poverty and in a state of brokenness, they keep saying: "O Lord, we have come to Your threshold as servants. Raise us up if You wish, and if You wish, bring us low."

### In short

- Allah's second punishment of the Israelites was to scatter them across the earth, for unity is a blessing and scattering a punishment.
- Among them were the righteous, the moderate and the wicked.
- Allah tested them with prosperity and hardship so they might return, but many answered with insolence in both states.
- The same law applies to the Muslims, who must turn back to Allah if they find themselves scattered and dominated.
- In the inner reading, the exiles of the path wander in yearning, unmoved by pleasure or disappointment.

<<<VERSE 7:169>>>
Verse 169 turns to the generation that came after the mixed one, and it was worse still: "Then they were succeeded by other generations who inherited the Scripture." Their attitude to the Book is laid bare: "They indulged in unlawful gains, claiming, “We will be forgiven ˹after all˺.” And if similar gain came their way, they would seize it." Allah confronts them with their own pledge: "Was a covenant not taken from them in the Scripture that they would not say anything about Allah except the truth?" They knew better: "And they were already well-versed in its teachings." The verse ends by setting the next life against this one: "But the ˹eternal˺ Home of the Hereafter is far better for those mindful ˹of Allah˺. Will you not then understand"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then they were succeeded by other generations"** In the time of Mūsā (Moses), many of the Jews feared Allah and did righteous deeds. After that generation of righteous and unrighteous people came another in which there was no goodness. The verb khalafa means to succeed someone, acting as his deputy in his absence. The noun khalf, with a still lām, usually means a successor marked by wickedness, unlike the good ways of his elders. Qatādah said: by Allah, this is an evil generation. Allah says the same in another verse: "But they were succeeded by generations who neglected prayer and followed their lusts and so will soon face the evil consequences" (19:59).

**"who inherited the Scripture."** The word comes from inheritance. They received the Torah from their forefathers, after their prophets and messengers, and studied it. This was the job Allah had entrusted to them by His command. One explanation adds that they took the Scripture and hid the traits and description of Muhammad ﷺ found in it.

**"They indulged in unlawful gains,"** The Arabic speaks of the ʿaraḍ of "this lower world". ʿAraḍ means something bought with money, or sometimes anything owned. Using it for wealth shows that whatever is owned in this world is temporary. As the opposite of jawhar, "substance", it means an accident, which needs something else in order to exist, as colour needs a thing to appear in. The word for "lower", adnā, comes either from a root meaning nearness or from one meaning lowliness, in which case it describes something base. Mujāhid said they would consume anything they could in this life, lawful or unlawful, and still wish for forgiveness. They took bribes and other unlawful gains to hide the description of Muhammad ﷺ, to distort Allah's Scripture and to give judgements against what was revealed.

**"claiming, “We will be forgiven ˹after all˺.”"** They convinced themselves that Allah would forgive what they had done. One explanation reports them saying that sins committed by night would be forgiven by day, and sins committed by day would be forgiven at night. They kept hoping in Allah while deceiving themselves.

**"And if similar gain came their way, they would seize it."** If the same kind of unlawful gain came to them again, they would take it every time, even declaring it lawful. They hope for forgiveness while repeating the same offence and persisting in it. Yet the Torah holds no such promise for those who persist in sin. Nothing stops them, for whenever they get the chance they consume, whether it is allowed or not.

**"Was a covenant not taken from them in the Scripture"** "The covenant of the Scripture" means the covenant in the Scripture. The question is really an affirmation: a covenant was certainly taken from them. Allah took a pledge from them to proclaim the truth to people and not hide it, without distorting or changing anything.

**"that they would not say anything about Allah except the truth?"** Ibn Jurayj reported that Ibn ʿAbbās identified the untruth here as their claim of forgiveness for sins they go on committing without repentance.

**"And they were already well-versed in its teachings."** They had read what was in it, so why did they ascribe lies to it, claiming it promised forgiveness for persisting in sin? Their failure to act on the Scripture came not from ignorance but from full knowledge, which made their disobedience greater. One explanation says they studied the traits and attributes of Muhammad ﷺ in it. Another says they studied its rulings on the lawful and the unlawful but did not act on them.

**"But the ˹eternal˺ Home of the Hereafter is far better for those mindful ˹of Allah˺."** Paradise and its eternal bliss are far better than any passing gain they were given as a bribe. They are for those who have taqwā, following what Allah commands and avoiding what He forbids. Such people guard against disbelief, idolatry, indecency, bribery and altering the Prophet's description in the Torah. Allah says, in effect: My reward, and what I have, is better for those who avoid what is forbidden, abandon their lusts and are active in obeying their Lord. This both encourages them to seek His immense reward and warns them of His severe torment.

**"Will you not then understand"** Do these people, who prefer this life to what is with Allah, have no sense to restrain them from their foolish and extravagant ways? Do they not realise that this world is vanishing and the Hereafter lasts forever, and so prefer it? The verb can be read "Do they not understand?" or "Do you not understand?"

### The judges who took bribes

As-Suddī said that every time the children of Israel appointed a judge, he would take bribes. The best of them met and made one another promise not to take bribes. Yet when one of them took a bribe for a judgement and was asked why, he replied, "I will be forgiven." His people would rebuke him for it. But when he died or was replaced, his successor took bribes as well. So Allah says that if those who rebuked him had their own chance at worldly gain, they would seize it too.

### How did they trade the Book?

Allah says elsewhere: "˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when Allah took the covenant of those who were given the Scripture to make it known to people and not hide it, yet they cast it behind their backs and traded it for a fleeting gain. What a miserable profit" (3:187).

Among the early Jews there were two groups, the righteous and the wrongdoers. But the later generations who inherited the Torah did the opposite of their elders. They turned the Book into an object of trade and a means of earning a living. For money they would twist and distort the text of the Torah. Gradually they made the world the goal of their lives and piled up wealth without caring whether it was lawful. They still read the Holy Book, but through interpretations of their own making they coloured it so that Allah Himself seemed to support their rebellious way of life. Their callousness grew until they took comfort in declaring: we are a pious community and the descendants of prophets, and Allah will forgive us for the sake of His favourites.

### Can one sin and count on forgiveness?

Despite a sin as grave as distorting the Book of Allah, they claimed His forgiveness. The very next words expose this: offered money to change another passage, they would readily take it. Allah's forgiveness is always open to those who recognise their error and turn to their Lord in repentance. They show remorse for what they have done and firmly resolve not to repeat it. To persist in sin while claiming forgiveness is sheer self-deception.

Then comes the question whether they had not made a covenant with Allah to say nothing about Him but the truth. Indeed they had, and they read this pledge in their own Book. Only their impudence and obstinacy keep them from grasping the truth of the Hereafter, which belongs to those who fear Allah.

### In short

- A later generation inherited the Torah but used it for gain, taking bribes to distort it and to conceal the Prophet's description.
- They claimed forgiveness while persisting in the same sins, though the Torah promises no such thing.
- They broke a covenant to say only the truth about Allah, and did so knowingly, which made their sin greater.
- Forgiveness is for those who repent sincerely, and the Hereafter is far better for the mindful than any passing gain.

<<<VERSE 7:170>>>
Not all of them were like this, and Allah honours the faithful: "As for those who firmly abide by the Scripture and establish prayer—surely We never discount the reward of those acting righteously". The previous verse (7:169) mentioned the covenant taken from the religious leaders of the Israelites in particular. They were to change nothing in the Torah and say nothing about Allah but the truth, yet they broke that pledge and distorted the Torah for petty sums. This verse continues the theme. Some of their religious leaders do follow the Torah truthfully and hold firmly to its guidance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who firmly abide by the Scripture"**: those who hold fast to the Book, carry out its commands and keep away from what it forbids. They declare lawful what it makes lawful and unlawful what it forbids, and they bring into the open the traits and description of Muhammad ﷺ. Allah praises them because their Book directs them to follow His Messenger Muhammad ﷺ. The verb can be read yumassikūn or yumsikūn. One explanation names ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his companions as examples.

**"and establish prayer"**: who perform the prayer as it should be performed, at its appointed times, the five daily prayers.

**"surely We never discount the reward of those acting righteously"** Allah does not let the reward of those who do good in deed and word go to waste. Grammatically, this sentence is the predicate of alladhīna, "those who". An explicit noun, al-muṣliḥīn, "the righteous" or "reformers", stands in place of a pronoun, so the meaning is "their reward". This assures such people that they will not lose their reward. Those who have been true to their faith and met its demands are the righteous among them.

### Which Book, and why "hold fast"?

The Book most probably means the Torah. It may also mean all of Allah's revealed books, such as the Torah, the Injīl and the Qur'an. Simply keeping Allah's Book and showing it respect, without following its commands, does not fulfil the purpose for which it was revealed. That is why the verse says "hold fast", and not "take it" or "read it". Holding fast to the Book means following all of its rulings and commands.

### Why is prayer singled out?

Of all the Torah's teachings, the verse mentions only the establishment of prayer, the ṣalāh. Prayer is the most important and purposeful of the acts Allah has commanded. It is the most basic expression of faith in Allah and obedience to Him. Praying regularly makes a person able to keep up other acts of worship more willingly and easily. The Prophet ﷺ called prayer the pillar of Islam, on which the whole structure rests. Whoever sets it up properly has built the whole edifice of his faith on firm ground. Anyone who does not pray regularly is of no worth to Allah, even if he seems always to be repeating His name, sees unusual visions or performs extraordinary feats.

### What does holding fast look like?

This happens in every prophet's community. At first its members are pious and mindful of Allah, but in later generations that spirit fades and they become like other worldly people. Religion is still among them. The Holy Book is still read and taught, but as a national heritage, not as the real fulfilment of a pledge to Allah. They follow the ways of the world and, in practice, forget the Hereafter. Heedless of right and wrong, they make their desires their religion. Yet they pride themselves on being the best of communities, the followers of Allah's favourite, the heirs of the Book, sure of salvation by virtue of their belief in the oneness of Allah.

The truth is that a person must hold fast to Allah's Word and pray regularly. The sign that he holds fast to Allah's Book and prays sincerely is that he becomes a maintainer of peace and harmony, one of the muṣliḥūn. Closeness to Allah's Book and His worship are what set a person against anyone who disturbs the peace.

### In short

- Allah honours those who hold fast to their Book, act on it and establish prayer, such as ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his companions.
- Holding fast means following the Book's commands, not merely keeping and revering it.
- Prayer is singled out because it is the pillar of the religion.
- The sign of true devotion is that a person becomes a maker of peace.

<<<VERSE 7:171>>>
Allah recalls an earlier moment when the Israelites were made to take their covenant seriously: "And ˹remember˺ when We raised the mountain over them as if it were a cloud and they thought it would fall on them." With it came a command: "˹We said,˺ “Hold firmly to that ˹Scripture˺ which We have given you and observe its teachings so perhaps you will become mindful ˹of Allah˺.”" This incident, when the Israelites were made to promise obedience to the law of the Torah, is also mentioned in Sūrat al-Baqarah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember˺ when We raised the mountain over them"** Mention, O Muhammad, when Allah raised the mountain above the Israelites after they refused to accept what was in the Torah. The word here, nataqnā, means to pull or lift up. In Sūrat al-Baqarah the word is rafaʿnā, "We raised", and so Ibn ʿAbbās took it to mean lifting high. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported that Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as raising the mountain, as another verse testifies: "We raised the Mount over them ˹as a warning˺ for ˹breaking˺ their covenant" (4:154). Saʿīd ibn Jubayr reported from him that the angels raised the Mount over their heads. One explanation says it was wrenched up from its roots and fixed above their heads.

**"as if it were a cloud"** The word is ẓullah, a sunshade or canopy. The mountain spread over them like a cloud, casting its shadow.

**"and they thought it would fall on them."** They were certain it would fall on them if they did not accept the Scripture, for Allah had threatened that it would. They had at first refused its rulings as too burdensome, but then they accepted them.

**"˹We said,˺ “Hold firmly to that ˹Scripture˺ which We have given you"** Take what Allah revealed to you, the Torah, firmly, seriously and earnestly, and act on it with diligence.

**"and observe its teachings"** Remember what is in it by acting on it, and do not forget it. One explanation says: remember the reward and punishment it contains. Others say: recall its commands and prohibitions, or act on what it makes lawful and unlawful.

**"so perhaps you will become mindful ˹of Allah˺.”"**: so that you may fear Allah and obey Him, guard yourselves against His wrath and torment, and believe in Him.

### What happened at the mountain?

Al-Qāsim ibn Abī Ayyūb narrated, through Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, that Ibn ʿAbbās said Mūsā (Moses) later set out with them for the Sacred Land. He took the Tablets with him once his anger had passed, and commanded them to keep the orders Allah had given him to deliver. But these orders weighed heavily on them, and they would not carry them out until Allah raised the mountain over them like a canopy. That was when the angels lifted the mountain over their heads. An-Nasāʾī collected this report.

Another account says that when Mūsā came back from Mount Sinai with the Torah, they found in it many injunctions they considered too hard, and showed they were unwilling to obey. Allah commanded the angel Jibrīl (Gabriel) to raise Mount Sinai over their town, whose area is said to have been three square miles. When the mountain hung over their heads, they fell down in prostration out of fear and promised to obey the law of Mūsā, the Torah. Yet later events saw them stray from that Law again and again.

Receiving the commandments at the foot of the mountain was meant to impress on them that a pledge to Allah is a very serious matter. If they failed to keep its conditions, they should remember who the other party was. He is the Majestic Being who could, if He willed, bring the mountain down on them.

### Was this not compulsion in religion?

The incident raises a question. Why were the Israelites forced to promise obedience, when the Holy Qur'an declares: "Let there be no compulsion in religion" (2:256)? The answer is simple. No disbeliever has ever been forced to accept the faith, and no one is permitted to use force for that purpose. But those who have entered the covenant of faith of their own free will, and later turn from Allah's commands to escape their obligations, must be made to keep the rules of the Law. If they abandon their duty, they are liable to the penalties the Law prescribes. That is exactly what happened here. They had already believed in Allah and His Prophet and entered the covenant of faith. What they refused was obedience to the law of the Torah, and they were rightly made to fulfil their commitment.

### In short

- When the Israelites refused the burdensome rulings of the Torah, Allah raised the mountain over them like a canopy.
- Certain it would fall, they fell in prostration and promised to obey, though they later strayed again.
- They were told to hold the Scripture firmly and act on it so that they might become mindful of Allah.
- This was no compulsion into faith, since they had already believed and were being held to a covenant they had made.
