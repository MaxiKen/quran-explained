The verse tells the Prophet to recall a declaration made against the Israelites. "And ˹remember, O Prophet,˺ when your Lord declared that He would send against them others who would make them suffer terribly until the Day of Judgment." The verse then names two of the attributes of Allah. "Indeed, your Lord is swift in punishment, but He is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"

### Phrase by phrase

**"when your Lord declared"** turns on the word the verse uses. Which one authority read as meaning that Allah made the matter known. And others read as meaning that He ordained it. Either way the sentence carries the weight of a vow that Allah took upon Himself, and what follows it states what the vow contained.

**"that He would send against them others who would make them suffer terribly until the Day of Judgment"** names the punishment. The people meant are the Jews, and the torment is one that carries humiliation. The reason for it is given as their disobedience, their defiance of the orders of Allah, and the tricks they used to get around the prohibitions laid on them.

**"Indeed, your Lord is swift in punishment, but He is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"** closes the verse with two of the names of Allah. The swiftness in punishing belongs to those who disobey Him and defy His law. The forgiveness and the mercy belong to those who turn back to Him. The two are put together in one sentence so that no hearer falls into despair. And the Book often joins encouragement and warning in this way so that the heart keeps both hope and fear.

### The humiliating subjection

The humiliation named in the verse has taken visible forms in the history of the people. Ibn 'Abbas explained it through the tribute that the Jews were made to pay, and the report of that explanation came through Al-'Awfi. The disgrace of being under the rule of others, and the payment of the jizyah, are counted as part of the terrible suffering the verse speaks of.

The payment itself reaches back to Musa. It is reported that he required the Jews to pay the production tax for seven years. And in another report for thirteen. And that he was the first to lay such a charge on them. After him the people fell under one power after another. They were ruled by the Greeks of the line of Kushdanin, then by the Chaldeans. And later by the Christians. And those rulers disgraced them and made them pay the tribute tax. When Islam came and the Prophet was sent, they came under his power and paid the jizyah again.

One account names the rulers who were sent against them. Allah sent Solomon against them, and after him Nebuchadnezzar, who killed some of them and took others of them captive, and who imposed the jizyah on those who remained. They went on paying that tax to the Magians until the time when the Prophet was sent, and he imposed it on them as well. Another account states the position plainly, that the people who lay the cruel torment on them are the Prophet and his community. And that the tax named in the reports is the capitation tax that was laid on them.

The sign of the declaration is plain in the present state of the people. They have been dominated and treated with disdain by others, and history has recorded one persecution after another, from the time of the Prophet Solomon down to the modern age. Their imprisonment under Nebuchadnezzar, their treatment at the hands of the kings who came after him. And their defeat at the hands of the Prophet and of Umar al-Faruq are the examples that are given. A later persecution in the second world war is given in the same record as a fresh instance of the same declaration.

The political standing some of them hold today does not change that reading. The state that was set up in Palestine has been described as a piece of the world powers, created by them for their own political aims against the Muslim ummah. And as a military base of America. Its existence depends on the aid its masters provide, and on the day that aid stops it will not be able to hold its ground for long. The people remain under the rule of others in the sense that matters.

### What comes before the end of the world

The declaration has a future side to it as well. In the last age the Jews will support the False Messiah, and the Muslims, together with Isa the son of Mary, will fight them and kill them. That event belongs to the time just before the end of this world.

The verse then gives the two names of Allah that frame the whole matter. The swiftness in punishment is not a denial of mercy, and the mercy is not a licence to go on as the people were going on. The door of return stays open for the one who repents, and the punishment stays ready for the one who does not.

### In short

- The declaration was that Allah would keep the Jews under powers that would humiliate them until the Day of Judgment. And the reason given is their disobedience, their defiance of His law and the tricks they used.
- Ibn 'Abbas explained the humiliating torment as the disgrace of subjection together with the payment of the jizyah. And Musa is reported to have been the first to lay the production tax on them.
- One account names Solomon and after him Nebuchadnezzar as the rulers sent against them. And it says the tax went on being paid until the Prophet was sent and imposed the jizyah on them himself.
- The record of the people has been one of domination and persecution. And the state set up in Palestine is described as a base of the world powers rather than a sign that the declaration has lapsed.
- The verse closes with swift punishment for those who defy Allah and forgiveness with mercy for those who turn back. And the two are joined so that the heart of a hearer holds both hope and fear.

<<<VERSE 7:168>>>
The next verse states the scattering of the same people. "We dispersed them through the land in groups—some were righteous, others were less so. We tested them with prosperity and adversity, so perhaps they would return ˹to the Right Path˺"

### Phrase by phrase

**"We dispersed them through the land in groups"** tells how the people were left. The words mean that Allah divided them into communities and sects and scattered them across the earth. The Book states it elsewhere in the words that after Musa had died the Children of Israel were told to dwell in the land. And that when the final promise came near they would be brought out as a mixed crowd gathered from different nations (17:104).

**"some were righteous, others were less so"** describes the mixture inside the scattered people. Some of them were guided aright and some were not righteous. The teachers who worked through this passage of the Book include Mujahid, Qatadah and As-Suddi. And their comments belong with the generations that came after. The jinn made the same declaration about themselves, saying that among them were some that were righteous and some contrary to that. And that they were groups having different ways (72:11).

**"We tested them with prosperity and adversity"** names the two sides of the trial. The good means times of ease, plenty and comfort. And the evil means drought, poor harvest and hardship, along with fear and affliction. The two were used in turn on the same people.

**"so perhaps they would return ˹to the Right Path˺"** gives the purpose of the trial. The testing was meant to bring the people back to their Lord. And the hope named in the verse is the hope that they would turn from their wrongdoing.

### What the scattering means

The word used for the dispersal carries the sense of breaking something into pieces. Its plural is the word for a community or a party. So the verse describes a people who were broken into fragments and spread over the earth. The old unity of the people was a favour and the fragmentation is a punishment.

Being joined together as a single body is a blessing from Allah. And being broken into parts that stand apart from one another is a punishment from Him. The Muslims have held the opposite of that state for most of their history. Their own entity began in Al-Madinah in the time of the Prophet ﷺ. And it has been recognised as an organised people in the world since then. Islamic countries stand from the far east to the west, and their presence is given as the plain evidence of that fact.

### The righteous and the ones who were not

The two groups named in the verse are described in more than one way. The righteous among the people were those who followed the commands of the Torah faithfully and did not try to change or distort them. The ones described as other than that include the disbelievers and those who went on disobeying their prophets in an obstinate way. And even killed some of them.

Another reading of the two groups turns on the coming of the Prophet. The righteous are those who believed in him and followed the guidance of the Book that was revealed to him. While the others are those who held to the Torah as the word of Allah but disobeyed it or changed what it commanded. And who in that way sold their eternal salvation for petty gains of this world.

One account of the division gives the scattered people in numbers. It says that nine tribes and a half settled beyond the river al-Raml. While the second group are the remaining believers among the Children of Israel. Another reading of the same words takes the second group to be the disbelievers among them.

### Prosperity and hardship as the two tests

The good and the evil named in the verse came on the people one after another. Wealth and ease were given to them so that it might be seen whether they showed gratitude to their Lord. And when they failed that test they were made to undergo the punishments that the earlier verses of the surah describe.

Their way of thinking had gone so far that they spoke against Allah in both states. In the time of their prosperity they said that Allah was poor and that they were rich (3:181). In the time of their poverty they said that the hand of Allah was shackled (5:64). The two sayings are recorded in the Book as the fruit of the same perversion.

The trial has a wider meaning than the history of one people. Prosperity and poverty, happiness and suffering, are sent to human beings as a test. So that the faith of the created in the Creator and the love of the created for the Creator may be judged. Neither state should carry much weight with a person of understanding, since both are temporary and both come to an end.

A line of poetry is quoted on the point, that neither the happiness that came to a man brought him real benefit, nor did the sorrow make him weep. Since both came as guests to a resolute spirit.

### The warning that the trial carries for the community of the Prophet

The law at work in the verse is not confined to the Jews. The reward for carrying out a task is great when the task is great. And the punishment for failing it is equally great. The Jews had been raised to the position of bearers of the Book. And they were given promises of bounties in the world to come and extraordinary benefits in this world along with them. What they did was to go on disobeying, and to commit their irreligious acts in the name of the religion. The Book had been entrusted to them as a job. And the result of their conduct was that they were removed from that high position. And it was settled that they would go on experiencing the punishment of Allah for as long as the world lasts.

That does not mean that they were given no good days at all. The verses themselves say that there were intervals of good fortune. Those intervals were themselves a kind of curse on the people, in that they gave them the chance to grow more insolent and so become liable for more punishment.

The same law applies to the ummah that was raised to the position after the Jews were removed from it. If Muslims find that they are dominated by those who deny the truth and by those who join partners with Allah. And if their groups have become scattered, then the finding itself calls them back to Allah. Since it shows that they have drawn down His displeasure.

### The travellers and their inward reading

One account reads the scattering as an image of the people of the path who travel the earth. The verse is taken as an allusion to the travellers of the community, the exiles of the Tariqah. Who wander from region to region and from cave to cave to hide their state from people and to struggle for their religion against the harm others do them.

The saying of Al-Mustafa is quoted for that reading. He said that a time would come when those who are religious would not stay safe unless they kept away from people. And that they would be seen like travellers fleeing from people, sometimes in the mountains and sometimes in the desert. The description given of them is that they rush and run and wail across the world, in mountain monasteries and in desert caves, entirely effaced in the ocean of thought. And that they recite on behalf of everyone that they have no home and no possessions.

Two events from the Book are given as witnesses to that reading. The first is the story of the Companions of the Cave. And the second is the stay of the Prophet and of Abu Bakr in the cave. The Book says of that second event that the two were in the cave. And that the Prophet told his companion not to grieve. Since Allah was with them (9:40). Abu Bakr is given the title of the second of the two in that event. And the story is quoted as proof that a believer may be driven into a cave and still be under the care of his Lord.

Another meaning is given for their travelling as well, which is that they were yearning. These people spend most of their days without settledness and without ease. And the hope that keeps them moving is that they may reach some place where they see the mark of the friend, or meet someone who can tell them about the friend.

The prayer of one of the men of the path is quoted on the point, that the homeland of the exiles of Allah is exile and that the question is when that business will end. He asks how the man being tested by his Lord can be thought worthy of relief. And how the man whose homeland is exile can ever reach home. He says that the one who yearns is slain by friendship. And that the shroud of those slain by friendship is the vision of the Beloved.

That reading takes the testing of the verse in an inward sense. The people described are tested with pleasure and with disappointment. And they are neither deceived by the pleasure nor turned away from their Lord by the disappointment. In front of them stands a concern greater than either their pleasure or their disappointment. They are on loan among people, and they are strangers among themselves. Their hearts are joined to their Patron. And with the tongue of poverty and the attribute of brokenness they say that they have come to the Threshold as servants. And that if He wills He may exalt them and if He wills He may lay them low.

### The descent of Isa and the gathering in Palestine

The persecution of the people has gone on without a break. History has recorded that they were laid low by one people after another from the time of Sulayman to the present age. And their treatment by the rulers of later times has followed the same line.

The present state of Palestine raises a question, and the answer given to it rests on the prophecies. The people must come together in one place in the last age. The Prophet ﷺ informed the community that Isa would come down from the heavens before the end of the world, that all the Christians would enter Islam. And that he would fight the Jews and put them to death. The land named for his descent is Syria, and he will make war on the Jews from there.

The people who are due to answer for their conduct are not brought in by police or by any other agency. They are driven to the place of their death by the causes that the will of Allah sets in motion. The gathering of the Jews in Palestine is put in that light. Since it saves Isa the trouble of seeking them out in different parts of the world.

Their present political power is called a delusion that takes in only those who do not follow the ways of the world. The so called state of Israel has been described as a common camp ground of the big powers such as America, Russia and England. It depends on the aid of its masters for its existence, and it has to serve their aims. The people who live under that arrangement are still in real servitude and are deprived of free rule in the true sense of the words.

### In short

- The people were broken into communities and scattered over the earth. And the Book states the same scattering in the words that after Musa they were told to dwell in the land and would later be brought out as a mixed crowd from different nations (17:104). While the jinn said of themselves that among them were the righteous and those contrary to them (72:11).
- Some of the people were righteous and some were otherwise. And the scattering is itself read as a punishment. Since being joined in one body is a blessing of Allah and being broken into parts is a sign of His displeasure. As the Muslims knew from their own entity that began in Al-Madinah.
- The trial came through prosperity and through hardship. And the people spoke against Allah in both states, saying in their wealth that Allah was poor and they were rich (3:181). And saying in their poverty that the hand of Allah was shackled (5:64).
- One inward reading takes the scattering as the state of the travellers of the Tariqah, the exiles who wander to hide their state. And the saying of Al-Mustafa and the stay of the Prophet with Abu Bakr in the cave are quoted for it (9:40).
- The record of the people runs from the time of Sulayman through Nebuchadnezzar and later persecutions to the defeat at the hands of the Prophet and of Umar al-Faruq. And their rule in Palestine is described as a base of the big powers rather than a settled sovereignty.

The question asks whether the people who preferred this life to what is with Allah have any sense at all to stop them from their foolish and extravagant ways.

### The words the verse uses

Several words in the passage carry meanings that sharpen it. The word for a successor is used in the form that carries the sense of a deputy or a person trusted with another's work in his absence. And the form used in the verse denotes such a deputy marked by wickedness. As opposed to the good practice of his elders. The word for inheritance is a derivative of the word for the state of being an heir. And the word for the gains carries the sense of something that is bought with money.

The word for nearness lies behind one of the terms in the passage as well, and the feminine form of the same word is used. The same form may also be taken from the root that signifies disgrace, and in that case the word refers to something disgraced.

The final question in the verse has a second reading that turns on the person addressed. The covenant named in the verse was a pledge not to say anything about Allah except the truth. It was taken from the people when the Book was given to them. And its content included the duty of making the Book known and clear to mankind and not hiding it. Their conduct went against that pledge at the point that mattered most, since they ascribed to the Book a promise of forgiveness for the man who persists in sin.

The teaching of the passage on forgiveness is plain. The forgiveness of Allah is always there for the one who sees his error, turns to his Lord in repentance, shows remorse for what he has done. The Book named in the verse is most probably the Torah. And even if unusual visions come to him and extraordinary acts are seen from him.

The prayer also trains a person for the rest of the worship. Offering it regularly makes a man capable of being regular in the other acts of worship more willingly, and with more ease.

There is a further sign attached to holding fast to the Word of God and to saying the prayer sincerely. The sign is that the person has become a maintainer of peace and harmony among people. Communion with the Book of God and with His worship is what sets a man against the one who disturbs the peace.

The report says that Musa later set out with the people for the Sacred Land. He took the Tablets along with him once his anger had subsided. And he commanded the people to hold to the orders that Allah had given him to deliver to them. Those orders fell heavily on the people. And they were unwilling to carry them out until Allah raised the mountain over them in the shape of a canopy. Which happened when the angels lifted it above their heads. An-Nasa'i collected that report. The narrator of it in the chain was Ayyub.

### The promise that was taken under the mountain

The incident explains why the pledge taken at the mountain carried such weight. The people had been given commands they found hard, and they showed their unwillingness to obey them. When the mount was held over their heads they fell down in prostration out of fear and gave their promise to obey the law of Musa.

The report of the town gives its size. The area of the settlement is said to have been three square miles, and the Archangel Gabriel was commanded to raise the Mount of Sinai over it.

The passage lays down a lesson about the seriousness of a pledge. The conditions were made such that the people felt the mountain was about to fall on them. And the purpose was to impress on them that giving a pledge to God is a very serious matter. If they did not fulfil the conditions of the pledge, they were to remember that the other party to it was the majestic Being who could cause the mount to fall on them by His will.

The events that followed are recorded in the same account. The people promised their obedience at the mountain, and the later record of them shows them deviating from the law again and again.

### The question of compulsion in faith

The incident raises a question, since the Book declares elsewhere that there is no compulsion in the religion (2:256). The answer rests on the difference between the two situations. None of the disbelievers was ever forced to accept the faith, and no one is allowed to use force for that purpose. The people under the mountain were not in that position. They had already entered the covenant of faith by their own free will, and they had believed in Allah and in His prophet. When they later moved away from the commands of Allah simply to escape the commitments they had taken on, they were held to those commitments.

The ruling that follows from the incident is that a person who has taken the covenant is liable to the punishments that the law prescribes for the duties he abandons. The compulsion in the story was not a compulsion to believe. It was the holding of a people to a pledge they had already made freely.

### In short

- The mountain was raised over the heads of the Israelites in the shape of a canopy. And they were certain that it would fall on them. And the Book states the same event in the words about the covenant made at the mountain (4:154).
- Ibn 'Abbas read the word of the verse as a raising up. And Sa'id bin Jubayr reported the same from him with the detail that the angels lifted the mount over the heads of the people.
- The report that came through Al-Qasim bin Abi Ayyub and was collected by An-Nasa'i tells how Musa brought the Tablets from Sinai after his anger had cooled. And how the commands fell heavily on the people until the mountain was raised.
- The settlement under the mountain was three square miles across, and the account says the Archangel Gabriel was commanded to raise the Mount of Sinai over it.
- The command that came with the raising was to hold firmly to the Scripture and to observe its teachings, so that the people might become mindful of their Lord.
- The answer to the question about compulsion is that no one is forced to accept the faith. While those who freely take the covenant and then desert it are held to what they promised.
