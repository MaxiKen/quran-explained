<<<VERSE 5:70>>>
The chapter returns to a long history: "Indeed, We took a covenant from the Children of Israel and sent them messengers." Allah bound them to hear and obey, and He kept sending reminders. The verse then states what happened whenever a messenger brought something they did not want.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We took a covenant from the Children of Israel"** recalls pledges to hear and obey Allah and His messengers. One gloss describes the covenant taken in the Torah concerning Muhammad ﷺ, including the undertaking not to associate partners with Allah. Another describes it as a covenant to believe in Allah and His messengers.

**"and sent them messengers."** The sending was repeated. Allah raised reformers among them to remind them of their pledge as their conduct deteriorated.

**"Whenever a messenger came to them with what they did not desire"** gives the condition. The test was not whether the message was unfamiliar but whether it agreed with their hearts and their desires. They broke their pledges and followed their lusts instead of the law.

**"they denied some and killed others"** states the two responses. Whatever part of the law suited them they took, and whatever did not conform to their desires they abandoned.

### Who is named in these two responses?

The denial is explained as directed at 'Isa (Jesus) and Muhammad ﷺ, and the killing at Zakariyah (Zechariah) and Yahya (John). Firm promises to listen and follow were broken by ignoring what the messengers brought, by disbelieving some and murdering others.

One grammatical observation notes that a present tense is used for the killing in order to narrate past events as though occurring now and to match the verse endings. The form does not change the historical claim.

### What happened when their arrogance passed all limits?

The pledge was taken through Musa (Moses) and was kept for only a few days before their behaviour began to deteriorate. Their waywardness and arrogance increased until they silenced the admonishers themselves and killed many people.

Allah then subjected them to the rule of Nebuchadnezzar, ruler of Babylon, described as king of Babylonia from 605 to 562 before the common era. Their holy city of Jerusalem was attacked and destroyed in 586 before the common era, and they were taken to his country as bonded labour. The Old Testament book of Daniel is referred to in the account of their enslavement in Babylon.

The purpose of that subjugation is explained elsewhere as a testing period. Light punishment was meant to awaken moral sense, and where devotion to Allah was aroused the punishment ceased.

### In short

- The covenant required hearing and obeying Allah and His messengers, and reminders were sent repeatedly.
- Their response depended on desire rather than on truth, so some messengers were called liars and others were killed.
- Zakariyah and Yahya are named among those killed, and 'Isa and Muhammad ﷺ among those denied.
- Their arrogance brought subjugation under Nebuchadnezzar and the destruction of Jerusalem in 586 before the common era.

<<<VERSE 5:71>>>
The verse explains how such conduct becomes habitual: "They thought there would be no consequences, so they turned a blind eye and a deaf ear." Assuming that nothing would follow, they became unable to see or hear the truth. Allah then turned to them, and many of them returned to the same state.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They thought there would be no consequences"** describes the assumption behind the behaviour. They supposed no fitnah, that is no trial or punishment, would reach them for denying and killing the messengers. Another reading of their thought is that their hearts would not be corrupted by what they did.

**"so they turned a blind eye and a deaf ear."** They became wilfully blind and did not see guidance, and deaf so that they did not hear the truth deep in their hearts. Being unable to see or hear the truth meant they could not be guided by it.

**"Yet Allah turned to them in forgiveness ˹after their repentance˺"** records that they disbelieved, then believed and repented, and Allah forgave them. His turning toward them followed their turning toward Him.

**"but again many became blind and deaf."** The relapse came after a period of time. The wording says many of them, not all, and one gloss adds that some died in that state.

**"And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do"** closes the verse. He has perfect knowledge of what they do, of who among them deserves guidance and who deserves misguidance, and He will requite them for it.

### What historical sequence is described?

After the destruction and exile, their hearts were softened and they sought Allah's pardon. He helped them through Cyrus, king of Iran, who captured their region in 539 before the common era by attacking and defeating the Chaldeans. He freed them from exile and allowed them to return and settle in their own country.

Another account describes them being sent back to Jerusalem from Babylon after a long time through the help of some Persian kings who secured their release from the disgrace imposed by Nebuchadnezzar. They repented and turned to making amends, and Allah accepted their repentance.

They then received a new lease of life and progressed considerably. After a short time they fell back into recklessness and arrogance. Allah sent prophets and reformers to warn them once more, and still they would not come to their senses.

They went so far as to kill Yahya, that is John, and a man they understood to be 'Isa. They were also set on killing Zakariyah and 'Isa himself. Divine wrath then broke upon them. In the year 70 of the common era the Roman emperor Titus defeated them, attacking their land and destroying them, and after that they could never stand on their own.

### Why does the verse mention Allah's turning to them?

Because the mercy was real and was followed by a real relapse. The account does not present the forgiveness as a reason for confidence that consequences never arrive. It shows that a people given a second chance can waste it.

Their assumption that no harm would come of breaking covenants and killing prophets is what blinded them. The verse records that the assumption was false, first in exile and later in destruction.

### In short

- Assuming that nothing would follow their crimes made them blind and deaf to guidance.
- Allah turned to them after their repentance, and the historical account ties this to the release under Cyrus in 539 before the common era.
- Many relapsed, killed Yahya and sought to kill 'Isa, and were destroyed under Titus in the year 70 of the common era.
- Allah sees what they do and will requite them, and the wording says many rather than all.

<<<VERSE 5:72>>>
A claim is stated and answered with the words of the one it exalts: "Those who say, “Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary,” have certainly fallen into disbelief." The verse then gives the consequence of associating partners with Allah, and it answers the claim with what the Messiah himself told his people to worship.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who say, “Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary,” have certainly fallen into disbelief."** names particular Christian sects, described in one account as the Monarchites, Jacobites and Nestorians, and in another as the claim of the Nestorians. They said this even though 'Isa made known that he was Allah's servant and messenger.

**"The Messiah ˹himself˺ said"** introduces his own words to the Children of Israel, and those words are “Worship Allah—my Lord and your Lord.” That is his call to tawhid, affirming Allah's oneness. The first words he spoke as a baby in the cradle were that he is the servant of Allah. He did not say that he is Allah or that he is the son of Allah.

He said that he is a servant of Allah, that He gave him the Scripture and made him a prophet (19:30), and that Allah is his Lord and their Lord, so they should worship Him alone, which is the straight path (19:36). As a man, after being sent as a prophet, he commanded them to worship his Lord and their Lord alone without partners.

**"Whoever associates others with Allah ˹in worship˺ will surely be forbidden Paradise by Allah."** states the consequence for one who associates partners and dies in that state. Allah forbids Paradise to him and makes the Fire his abode.

**"Their home will be the Fire. And the wrongdoers will have no helpers"** adds that there is no help from Allah and no one to support or protect them from the state they will be in. Another verse says Allah does not forgive partners being set up with Him, but forgives anything short of that to whom He wills (4:48).

The dwellers of the Fire are described as calling to the dwellers of Paradise for water or anything Allah has provided, and being told that Allah has forbidden both to the disbelievers. A report in the Sahih describes the Prophet ﷺ having someone proclaim that only a Muslim soul will enter Paradise, with another narration saying only a believing soul.

### How did miracles produce this error?

Allah granted 'Isa the power to perform extraordinary miracles so that people would recognise him as a prophet and have faith in him. The outcome was otherwise. He had been sent purely for guidance, but seeing his miracles, Christians came to hold that he was divine and that God had taken flesh in him.

The Jews, for their part, ignored him and relegated him to the position of a magician and conjurer. One group denied him, and the other was moved by him to establish polytheistic beliefs. The same person became the occasion of two opposite errors.

### What does the wider history of scripture-bearers show?

Communities carrying divine scriptures later developed the belief that they were Allah's special people and would face no reckoning whatever they did. Clear statements against such beliefs exist in Allah's teaching, but they became blind and deaf to them.

They built barriers of self-made beliefs and invented stories around themselves, so that divine warnings went unnoticed. The history of the Jews shows that subjugation by enemies was appointed as a testing period, and that light punishment was meant to awaken moral sense.

Where devotion to Allah was aroused, the punishment ceased. Where it was not, Allah cast them away and did not turn His attention to them again. The verse's warning about Paradise being forbidden belongs to this same pattern of consequence.

### In short

- The claim that Allah is the Messiah is contradicted by the Messiah's own call to worship Allah as his Lord and their Lord.
- His first words in the cradle named him a servant given the Scripture and made a prophet.
- Associating partners with Allah forbids Paradise and makes the Fire the abode, with no helper against it.
- Miracles intended to establish prophethood were misread into divinity, while another group reduced him to a magician.
- Scripture-bearing communities that assume immunity from reckoning build barriers that keep divine warnings out.

<<<VERSE 5:73>>>
A second claim is named: "Those who say, “Allah is one in a Trinity,” have certainly fallen into disbelief." The verse answers it in the shortest possible form. "There is only One God." It then warns that if they do not stop, a painful punishment will reach those among them who disbelieve.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who say, “Allah is one in a Trinity,” have certainly fallen into disbelief."** Mujahid and several others say the verse was revealed about the Christians in particular. As-Suddi and others say it concerns taking 'Isa and his mother as gods besides Allah, which makes Allah the third of a trinity.

One account names the claim as that of the Marqusiyyah, holding that there is a father, a son and a holy spirit. Another explains the phrase as referring to the Messiah, Ruh al-Quds, the Holy Spirit, and Allah, or to the Messiah, Maryam (Mary) and Allah, with all three taken as God.

The formulation is then described as making Allah a one third partner with them, so that all three become one and that one is three. This unity in trinity, three persons in one godhead, is identified as the common belief of Christians.

**"There is only One God."** There are not many worthy of worship. There is one God without partner, Lord of all creation and of everything that exists, the only God for the dwellers of the heavens and the earth, with no son and no partner.

**"If they do not stop saying this"** requires them to desist from what they say. One gloss describes it as repenting from the claim and professing His oneness.

**"those who disbelieve among them will be afflicted with a painful punishment"** warns of torment in the Hereafter, shackled and tormented, described as the Fire, with pain reaching their hearts. The wording says those among them who disbelieve, and one gloss identifies them as those fixed upon unbelief.

The discussion says this belief is supported neither by reason nor by revelation, and that it is explained in circuitous and ambiguous language. When it is not understood, it is declared to be a reality beyond comprehension.

That is offered as the reason the verse answers with a plain sentence rather than an argument. Against three persons in one godhead, the reply is simply that there is no god but One God.

As-Suddi connects the claim with the question Allah will put at the end of the chapter, asking 'Isa son of Maryam whether he told people to worship him and his mother as two gods besides Allah, to which he answers, glory be to You (5:116). The link shows that taking him and his mother as divine is what makes Allah a third in the count.

### What is the relation to the preceding verse?

Both verses describe disbelief arising from an excess in reverence. The first names the claim that Allah is the Messiah, and this one names the claim of a third of three. The warnings that follow, including the call to repent in verse 74 and the description of the Messiah and his mother in verse 75, answer them together.

The two claims are therefore not treated as unrelated errors. They are two forms of the same departure from the worship the Messiah himself commanded.

### In short

- The claim of a third of three is named as disbelief by particular sects and formulations, including father, son and holy spirit.
- The answer is that there is one God without partner, son or equal.
- Desisting and professing oneness are required, and a painful punishment is warned for those fixed in unbelief.
- The claim is criticised as supported by neither reason nor revelation and explained in ambiguous language.
- It belongs with the preceding verse as two forms of the same excess in reverence.

<<<VERSE 5:74>>>
After such warnings the door is left open: "Will they not turn to Allah in repentance and seek His forgiveness?" The question is a rebuke and an invitation at once. Allah is All-Forgiving and Most Merciful, even toward people who have said what these verses have just described.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Will they not turn to Allah in repentance and seek His forgiveness?"** calls them to repent of the claims they have made and to ask forgiveness by affirming Allah's oneness. The interrogative form is intended as a rebuke, not as doubt about whether repentance is possible.

**"And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"** describes Him as forgiving toward whoever repents and believes, whatever the sin may have been, even disbelief, and as merciful toward whoever dies repentant. He is compassionate toward the believers.

### What does this show about Allah's generosity?

The verse shows how generous, kind and merciful Allah is toward His creatures. They had committed a grave sin and invented a lie and a false allegation, yet He still calls them to turn back so that He may forgive them.

Allah forgives those who sincerely repent to Him. The invitation is not withdrawn because the offence was great. Its greatness is precisely why the offer of forgiveness matters.

It stands between the warning of painful punishment and the description of the Messiah and his mother that follows. The order keeps the reader from treating the preceding verses as a final sentence upon people still alive and still able to turn.

### In short

- Allah forgives whoever repents and believes, even after disbelief, and is merciful to whoever dies repentant.
- The invitation shows generosity toward people who invented a grave falsehood.
- It is placed before the argument so that the reasoning reaches people who can still turn.

<<<VERSE 5:75>>>
The argument is drawn from the plainest facts about the two people exalted: "The Messiah, son of Mary, was no more than a messenger." Messengers had come and gone before him. His mother was a woman of truth. Both of them ate food. The verse ends in wonder at how clear signs are made and how people are still deluded.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Messiah, son of Mary, was no more than a messenger."** He is like the prophets before him, one of Allah's servants and one of His honourable messengers. Another verse says he was nothing more than a servant upon whom Allah bestowed favour and made an example for the Children of Israel (43:59).

**"˹Many˺ messengers had ˹come and˺ gone before him."** Prophets came into the world, completed their mission and passed away. They were not eternal, and eternity is the mark of godhood. Being human like them, the Messiah was not eternal either, and so he cannot be what Allah is.

One gloss draws the inference directly. Prophets passed away ahead of him, and his own passing followed the same course. A god would not pass away, so the claim answered in verse 72 fails on that fact alone.

**"His mother was a woman of truth."** She believed in Allah with complete trust in Him. The word used means extremely truthful, and it is described as the highest rank given to her.

**"They both ate food."** They needed nourishment and the relief of bodily needs. They were therefore servants like other servants, not gods. The reasoning from eating is developed below.

**"See how We make the signs clear to them, yet see how they are deluded ˹from the truth˺"** expresses amazement. The signs are made unequivocal and plain, and they are the signs of Allah's oneness and of the falsehood of extremism in giving lordship to another. The delusion consists of the opinions, misguided ideas and claims they hold after that clarification.

### Why does eating disprove divinity?

Whoever needs to eat and drink depends on almost everything in the world. The earth, the air, the water, the sun and the animals cannot be described as things he does not need.

The reflection is directed inward. There is the grain of food outside a person, and then its journey begins at entry into the stomach and continues to digestion. Every factor involved, directly and indirectly, must be considered, and then there is a chain of effects generated by eating whose extent no one can precisely trace.

By pointing out that the Messiah and his pious mother used to eat, the Qur'an refers to that endless chain of needs. Neither of them was exempt from needing food and drink, a fact established by observation and by authentic narration that even Christians do not deny.

Whoever is not free of the need for food and drink is not free of dependence on anything in this world. How then could a human being, dependent like all human beings on a chain of causation for survival, be God? The argument is described as strong and clear, understandable by the educated and the uneducated alike, because eating and drinking are contrary to godhood.

A qualification is added. Not eating is not by itself proof of godhood, since otherwise the angels would all be gods. The argument from food shows dependence, and dependence is what excludes divinity.

Another gloss gives further reasons from the same fact: a being who eats is compound and fallible, and produces the waste a body produces. Such a being cannot be God.

### Was Maryam a prophet or a saint?

The question is debated. The complimentary use of the word meaning truthful indicates a godly person rather than a prophet, because on an occasion of compliment the higher rank is what gets mentioned. Had she held the station of prophethood, the word used for her here would have been the feminine form of prophet, but the word used is the one denoting truthfulness, which is the station of sainthood or godliness. This is abridged from a discussion in Ruh al-Ma'ani.

According to the majority of Muslim scholars, prophethood has never been given to women, that mission being particular to men. The verse cited for this, in Surah Yusuf, says that Allah sent before him only men to whom He revealed, from among the peoples of the towns (12:109).

The debate is reported rather than closed by assertion. What both readings agree on is that she was honoured, truthful and not divine.

### In short

- The Messiah was a messenger who passed away like the messengers before him, and eternity is not his attribute.
- His mother is described as extremely truthful, which is the highest rank given to her and not a claim of prophethood.
- Their need for food shows dependence on the whole chain of causes, which is incompatible with divinity.
- Not eating is not itself proof of godhood, since the angels do not eat.
- The signs are made plain, and the delusion lies in the claims held after that clarification.

<<<VERSE 5:76>>>
The question is put directly to the worshipper: “How can you worship besides Allah those who can neither harm nor benefit you?” The verse then contrasts that object of worship with Allah, who hears and knows. The comparison is between a thing without power and the One who has it all.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “How can you worship besides Allah"** is addressed to the children of Adam who worship other than Allah, the Christians being one example among them. It admonishes people who set up rivals with Him and worship idols, monuments and false deities, which deserve no degree of divinity. It is a rebuke against shirk, associating partners with Allah, in the form this passage has discussed since verse 72.

**"those who can neither harm nor benefit you?"** describes what cannot ward off harm from you or bring you benefit, in this world or in the Hereafter. The question is meant as a disavowal rather than as a request for information.

**"And Allah ˹alone˺ is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing.”"** He hears what His servants say and knows all things. One gloss specifies that He hears their claims about 'Isa and knows their punishment, and another that He hears their sayings and knows their circumstances.

### Why is worship of anything else irrational?

How could anyone worship inanimate objects that do not hear, see or know anything, and have no power to bring harm or benefit even to themselves, let alone to others, instead of worshipping Allah? Such a god is in reality very weak, while Allah is high above any weakness.

Only a Being who is Himself dependent on nobody and who has power to benefit or harm can be worthy of worship. Food is the most significant symbol of human dependence, and whoever depends for his food depends for everything. Whoever takes food is a wholly dependent being, and such a being cannot be God.

The same reasoning applies to conferring benefit and inflicting harm. Someone receiving benefit or suffering harm is an event requiring the coordination of universal factors. No ordinary human being can organise such factors, so no human being can raise himself to a position where he is treated as worthy of worship.

### What lies behind venerating a created being?

When a person places someone at the heart of his reverence and affection, what he really feels is that this person enjoys high standing in Allah's world and will be of use to him before Allah. All such hopes are described as false.

In this world of test and trial, the fact that everything other than Allah is helpless is not absolutely clear, which is why people fall into that misapprehension. In the Hereafter, when the truth is disclosed, a person will see that every support he relied on besides Allah was wholly without value.

Nothing is hidden from Allah, and He will repay accordingly. The verse therefore ends where the argument ends, with hearing and knowledge belonging to Him alone. A related verse in Surah Yusuf recalls that those sent before the Prophet ﷺ were men given revelation from among the peoples of the towns (12:109), which is another way of placing 'Isa and Maryam among servants rather than among objects of worship.

### In short

- What cannot harm or benefit is not worthy of worship, and the question is a disavowal rather than an enquiry.
- Dependence for food shows dependence for everything, which excludes divinity.
- Benefit and harm require the coordination of universal factors that no human being controls.
- Hopes placed in a venerated created being are false, and their falsity becomes plain in the Hereafter.
- Allah alone hears what people say and knows what they do, and will repay them.

<<<VERSE 5:77>>>
The chapter now states the principle that explains both errors: "Do not go to extremes in your faith beyond the truth". Excess in religion is crossing the limits the religion itself has drawn, in belief and in action. The verse also forbids following the vain desires of people who went astray earlier and misled many.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, “O People of the Book!"** addresses Jews and Christians. One gloss identifies the addressees as the people of Najran, and another as the Christians in particular. The instruction is given to their generations as well, up to the Last Day.

**"Do not go to extremes in your faith beyond the truth"** uses a word meaning to cross limits. Ghuluww, excess in religion, is crossing the limits drawn by religion in matters of belief and deed.

The example given is homage to prophets. There is a limit to it, and the furthest one may go is to take them as the best among Allah's creation. Going past that limit and calling those same prophets God or a son of God is excess in belief.

**"nor follow the vain desires of those who went astray before ˹you˺."** names the cause of the error. They followed their teachers, the advocates of misguidance who came before their time. One gloss calls them their forefathers who went astray formerly on account of their extremism.

**"They misled many and strayed from the Right Way.”"** describes leaders who led others astray from guidance and themselves missed the plain road. Moving away from the right path, they lost it.

### What is the other form of excess?

The previous verses described the tyranny and injustice of Bani Isra'il toward the prophets sent to them, falsifying some and killing others. This verse exposes another aspect of the same crookedness. Not content with one extreme, they reached for another, committing excess in reverence by making Allah's messengers nothing short of God.

The section of Bani Isra'il who said that Allah is exactly 'Isa son of Maryam thereby became disbelievers. Elsewhere the Jews are censured for the same kind of excess, in the statement that the Jews said 'Uzayr (Ezra) is the son of Allah while the Christians said the Messiah is the son of Allah. That passage is described without a verse number here because the account gives none.

Their behaviour toward the prophets was therefore a mixture of opposites. They could be neglectful and irreverent enough to falsify or kill them, and they could push an exaggerated reverence so far as to name them God or a son of God.

A group behaviour swinging between excess and deficiency is read as a mark of ingrained brutality. A well known Arab saying is cited to the effect that an ignorant person never stays at moderation but goes either to excess or to deficiency. Excess means crossing the limit, and deficiency means falling short of one's duty.

The two extremes may have come from two different groups among them, or one group may have acted in both ways toward different prophets, falsifying and killing some while equating others with God.

### How does Allah make Himself known?

The most significant truth of life is that Allah alone created and owns every universe of existence together with all it holds, and rules over it. He is One. To Him belongs all that exists and to Him belongs command, and He alone must be obeyed.

Man, however, is a lump of clay come alive, still wrapped in layers of materialism. How can he reach the One so pure, and how can he learn what Allah wills and what He wants from him? In His grace Allah appointed two channels of knowledge through which a person can learn what Allah likes and dislikes and what he must do and avoid.

The first is divine scripture, standing as law and guidance for the whole of humanity. The second is those special and welcome servants chosen from among human beings and sent as practical models of Allah's likes and dislikes and as practical keys to His Books. In religious terminology they are called messenger or prophet.

The reason given for the second is that a book, however exhaustive, can never be sufficient for the complete grooming of a human being. It takes a person to be the natural groomer and nurturer of another person. Allah's practice has therefore been to allow two orders to flourish in the mission of reforming and training people in the art of living: the Book of Allah and the men of Allah, including the blessed prophets and then their deputies, the scholars and the spiritual guides.

### Where have people gone wrong about the men of Allah?

Concerning this second order, people have fallen into excess and deficiency since ancient times. The different sects that sprang up within religions are described as products of this error of approach.

In some places those men were inflated beyond the required limit, reaching the outer frontiers of worshipping them. In others they were wholly ignored and bypassed with the declaration that the Book of Allah is enough for us. That declaration is apparently true, but by misinterpreting it so as to negate the importance of the prophets they made it a slogan.

At one extreme a messenger of Allah, and even spiritual guides and ascetics, were taken to be knowers of the unknown and seers of the unseen, as though they possessed Allah's own attributes. People began by worshipping living guides and ended by worshipping their graves. At the other extreme even a messenger of Allah was given the status of a mere courier or postman.

The essential message is therefore that whoever insults the standing of the prophets is no less culpable in disbelief than whoever lifts them far past the bounds set for honouring them and calls them Allah's equal.

### What is religion in this light?

This verse, with its command not to be excessive in faith, is a preface to that subject, and it stands beside the prohibition of shirk discussed with the preceding verse. It makes clear that the religion is really a set of limits and restrictions. Falling short inside those bounds is a crime, and so is stepping over them.

As it is a grave sin to insult Allah's messengers and their deputies, so it is a far graver sin to take them to be owners of Allah's own attributes or equal sharers with Him. The guidance given here to the People of the Book and to their generations until the Last Day has the status of a basic principle, since the slightest deviation from it can lose a person forever.

### Is deep intellectual research a form of excess?

The word meaning unjustly appears alongside the command not to be excessive. Investigative scholars of Qur'anic commentary say it is used for emphasis, because excess in religion is always unjust and the possibility of its being just does not exist.

Al-Zamakhshari and others nevertheless identify two kinds of excess at this place. The first is the unjust and false kind prohibited here. The second is a just and permissible kind, for which he names deep intellectual research, such as the method of the scholastic theologians of Islam in matters of faith or of Muslim jurists in matters of rulings. On his view this too is excess, but excess that is just and permissible.

Most scholars, on the considered view, hold that such research does not come within the definition of excess at all. The practical conclusion given is that deep insight and detailed investigation into questions of the Qur'an and Sunnah should stay within the limits established from the Prophet ﷺ and from the Companions and their successors. If it does, it is not excess, and whatever does reach the limit of excess is blameworthy here as well.

### How did this excess arise historically?

To the early followers of 'Isa he was a man sent from Allah, human and a prophet. As that faith travelled out of Syria it met the philosophy of Egypt and Greece, and those who accepted it entered the fold while under the influence of the philosophical thought of the time.

From internal causes and external factors a new era began, in which efforts were made to describe the faith in the philosophical style then current. The philosophers of Egypt and Greece were dominant in the civilised world of those days, and their thinking greatly influenced its intelligentsia.

Greek philosophers had formed an imaginary concept of the universe and interpreted reality on three levels: existence, life and knowledge. Those ideas impressed the Christian theologians, who hoped to win over the intellectuals of the day and so set about reshaping their faith along contemporary lines.

They offered an interpretation relating the faith in essence to those three levels, hoping that people could equate it with their own way of thinking and so accept it. Religious reality was said to manifest itself in a trinity, with existence as the Father, life as the Son and knowledge as the Holy Spirit.

To complete the religion interpreted in this way, many ideas were imported into it, such as the belief that 'Isa was the embodied manifestation of the word, and that sin had come upon all humanity after Adam's descent, requiring God's son to make atonement by submitting to his own crucifixion. So it was that, by the fourth century of the common era and shaped around Egyptian, Greek and Roman ideas, the faith now known as Christianity took form.

This is the historical account supplied in the material. It is presented as an explanation of how an excess in reverence acquired a philosophical form, not as a survey of every Christian doctrine or community.

### What is the path the verse commands?

At the end of the verse they are told not to follow the desires and whims of a people who had already gone astray and had made others go astray. The reason is given at once: those people moved away from the right path and lost it.

That straight road lies in the middle, between going too far and falling short. Two things are therefore achieved. Verse 77 points out the fatal error of crossing over the norm or lagging far behind it, and it stresses the need to stay firm on the straight road of moderation.

### In short

- Excess in religion means crossing the limits drawn by religion in belief and action, and the limit in honouring prophets is to regard them as the best of creation.
- Bani Isra'il combined two extremes, killing and falsifying some prophets and elevating others to divinity, including the claim about 'Uzayr and about the Messiah.
- Allah makes Himself known through His Books and through the men He sent as practical keys to those Books, and both extremes concerning those men are condemned.
- Deep investigation within the limits established from the Prophet ﷺ, the Companions and their successors is not excess, and the majority do not class it as such.
- The historical account traces how reverence for 'Isa acquired a philosophical trinitarian form, and the verse commands the path of moderation.

- The account of how Christian doctrine took philosophical form is reported as the supplied explanation and expressly limited, so that it is not read as a survey of every Christian community or doctrine. The 'Uzayr statement is described without a verse number because the material gives none.
