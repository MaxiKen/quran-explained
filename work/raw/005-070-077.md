<<<VERSE 5:70>>>
"Indeed, We took a covenant from the Children of Israel and sent them messengers. Whenever a messenger came to them with what they did not desire, they denied some and killed others"

This verse recalls the covenant that God made with the Children of Israel and the way they dealt with the messengers who came to them. Whenever a messenger brought what did not suit their desires, they denied some and killed others.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We took a covenant from the Children of Israel and sent them messengers."** The material explains that the covenant was the pledge to believe in God and in His messengers and to act rightly. God sent prophets to them as a continuing mercy, and each one called them back to that pledge.

**"Whenever a messenger came to them with what they did not desire."** The material says this means any message that did not agree with their wishes or their religion as they had shaped it. The verse describes a habit. They accepted what suited them and rejected what did not.

**"They denied some and killed others."** The material gives the examples of Zechariah and Yahya, whom the material names as killed by them. It says that the present tense used for killing is a way of describing past events as if they were happening now, so that the verse keeps its rhythm.

### The history of the covenant

The material gives an account of their history. They broke the pledge again and again, and the people who were punished were the ones who had turned away. It says that after a period of humiliation under the king of Babylon in 586 BC, they were freed under Cyrus in 539 BC, and returned to Jerusalem, and repented. Then they went back to their old ways, and they killed Zechariah and Yahya and planned to kill Isa. The material says the verse is a warning that the same pattern can repeat.

### In short

- God made a covenant with the Children of Israel and sent them messengers to remind them of it.
- They rejected messengers whose message did not suit them, and some were denied and some were killed.
- The verse is a warning that disregard of the covenant brings its own result.

<<<VERSE 5:71>>>
"They thought there would be no consequences, so they turned a blind eye and a deaf ear. Yet Allah turned to them in forgiveness ˹after their repentance˺, but again many became blind and deaf. And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do"

This verse describes how they assumed there would be no consequences for what they did, and so became blind and deaf to the truth. It then says that God turned to them in forgiveness after they repented, and that many of them became blind and deaf again.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They thought there would be no consequences."** The material says they believed no harm would come to them for denying and killing the messengers. The phrase can be read as a trial or punishment that would fall on them. The material reads it as a belief that the punishment would never come.

**"So they turned a blind eye and a deaf ear."** The material explains that the blindness and deafness are a state of the heart. They did not see the guidance or hear the truth that was in their hearts, so they were unable to follow it.

**"Yet Allah turned to them in forgiveness after their repentance."** The material says God accepted their repentance after they had returned from their error, and forgave them.

**"But again many became blind and deaf."** The material explains that many of them returned to the same condition after they were forgiven, and died in it. The verse does not say all of them.

**"And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do."** The material says God knows their denials and their killings, and will repay them for them.

### In short

- They assumed no consequences would follow, and they became blind and deaf to the truth.
- God forgave them when they repented, but many of them fell back into the same state.
- God sees all that they do.

<<<VERSE 5:72>>>
"Those who say, “Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary,” have certainly fallen into disbelief. The Messiah ˹himself˺ said, “O Children of Israel! Worship Allah—my Lord and your Lord.” Whoever associates others with Allah ˹in worship˺ will surely be forbidden Paradise by Allah. Their home will be the Fire. And the wrongdoers will have no helpers"

This verse declares those who say that Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary, to be in disbelief. It recalls that the Messiah himself told the Children of Israel to worship Allah alone, and it says that whoever associates others with Allah will be forbidden Paradise.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who say, Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary, have certainly fallen into disbelief."** The material explains that this is the claim of those who attributed divinity to Isa. It is a claim about Allah that is far beyond what Isa himself taught.

**"The Messiah himself said, O Children of Israel! Worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord."** The material explains that Isa called his people to worship Allah alone. The material cites 19:30, where Isa says that he is a servant of Allah who has given him the Scripture and made him a prophet, and 19:36, where he says that Allah is his Lord and theirs, and that they should worship Him.

**"Whoever associates others with Allah will surely be forbidden Paradise."** The material says that association in worship is the one sin that Allah does not forgive, as the verse 4:48 says. Whoever dies in that state will have Paradise forbidden to him and the Fire as his home.

**"And the wrongdoers will have no helpers."** The material says that no one can save them from the state they will be in.

### Isa as servant and the mother of Isa

The material says that Isa was like the earlier prophets, a servant of Allah and a messenger. It cites 43:59, which describes him as only a servant on whom Allah bestowed favour and whom He made an example for the Children of Israel. The material says the first words Isa spoke in the cradle were that he is the servant of Allah. It also reports that the Prophet ﷺ had someone announce to the people that only a believing soul would enter Paradise.

### In short

- Those who claim that Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary, have fallen into disbelief.
- Isa told his people to worship Allah alone, and he called himself a servant.
- Association in worship is the sin that leads to Paradise being forbidden, and the wrongdoers have no helpers.

<<<VERSE 5:73>>>
"Those who say, “Allah is one in a Trinity,” have certainly fallen into disbelief. There is only One God. If they do not stop saying this, those who disbelieve among them will be afflicted with a painful punishment"

This verse declares that those who say Allah is one of three in a Trinity have fallen into disbelief. It affirms that there is only One God, and warns that if they do not stop, a painful punishment will fall on those who disbelieve among them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who say, Allah is one in a Trinity, have certainly fallen into disbelief."** The material explains that the claim is the belief in God as one of three, with Isa and his mother named as gods alongside Allah. Mujahid said that the verse was revealed about the Christians in particular, and As-Suddi said that it was about taking Isa and his mother as gods. The material cites 5:116, where Allah asks Isa whether he told the people to take him and his mother as two gods besides Allah, and he says that Allah is far beyond that.

**"There is only One God."** The material says this is a plain statement of tawhid. There are not many worthy of worship, and He has no partner.

**"If they do not stop saying this, those who disbelieve among them will be afflicted with a painful punishment."** The material says the warning is a threat of a painful torment in the Hereafter. The verse gives them the chance to stop.

### In short

- Those who say that Allah is one of a Trinity have fallen into disbelief.
- There is only One God, with no partner.
- The warning is that a painful punishment will come if they do not stop.

<<<VERSE 5:74>>>
"Will they not turn to Allah in repentance and seek His forgiveness? And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"

This verse calls them back to repentance and forgiveness. It asks them why they do not turn to Allah and seek His forgiveness, and it reminds them that He is All-Forgiving and Most Merciful.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Will they not turn to Allah in repentance and seek His forgiveness?"** The material explains that the question is a rebuke for what they say about Isa. The way back is to repent and ask for forgiveness for the claim that they have made.

**"And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful."** The material says that Allah forgives the one who repents, and is merciful to him.

### In short

- The verse is a call to repent and ask for forgiveness.
- The rebuke is a question, and the answer is that Allah forgives those who repent.
- The mercy of Allah is open to those who turn back to Him.

<<<VERSE 5:75>>>
"The Messiah, son of Mary, was no more than a messenger. ˹Many˺ messengers had ˹come and˺ gone before him. His mother was a woman of truth. They both ate food. See how We make the signs clear to them, yet see how they are deluded ˹from the truth˺"

This verse says that the Messiah, son of Mary, was only a messenger like those before him. It notes that his mother was a woman of truth, and that both of them ate food, which shows that they were human. It ends by saying that the signs are made clear, yet people turn away from them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Messiah, son of Mary, was no more than a messenger. Many messengers had come and gone before him."** The material explains that the prophets came and passed away, and that Isa likewise passed away. This shows that he was not eternal, which is the mark of divinity.

**"His mother was a woman of truth."** The material says the word here is siddiqah, meaning a woman of complete truthfulness and faith. The material notes that this is a rank of sainthood and not of prophethood, since the Qur'an does not use the word nabiyyah for her. It cites 12:109, which says that messengers were only men sent from among the people of the towns.

**"They both ate food."** The material says that eating shows dependence. A being that needs food depends on the whole chain of causes, and so cannot be God. The material says that not eating would not prove divinity either, since that would make the angels gods.

**"See how We make the signs clear to them, yet see how they are deluded from the truth."** The material says that the signs have been explained clearly, and yet they turn away from them, which is a wonder.

### The reasoning of the verse

The material says that the verse answers the claim of Isa's divinity with three points: he had a beginning, he passed away like the messengers before him, and he depended on food like every human being. These are plain signs, and the verse asks the reader to consider how they still turn away.

### In short

- The Messiah was only a messenger, and the messengers before him passed away.
- His mother was a woman of complete truth, and neither of them was a god, since both ate food.
- The signs are made clear, and it is strange that people still turn away.

<<<VERSE 5:76>>>
"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “How can you worship besides Allah those who can neither harm nor benefit you? And Allah ˹alone˺ is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing.”"

This verse tells the Prophet ﷺ to ask them how they can worship what cannot harm or benefit them, while Allah alone hears and knows all things.

### Phrase by phrase

**"How can you worship besides Allah those who can neither harm nor benefit you?"** The material explains that the question is a rebuke. The idols and the deified persons cannot prevent harm or bring benefit, in this world or the Hereafter.

**"And Allah alone is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing."** The material says that the one who hears their claims about Isa, and knows their state, is Allah alone. The worship is owed only to Him.

### Who is addressed

The material says the verse addresses the people of the Book who attributed godhood to Isa, and in particular the Christians who worshipped him and his mother. It says that the rebuke applies to every worship of what cannot benefit or harm.

### In short

- The question is a rebuke: why worship what cannot harm or benefit you?
- Allah alone hears and knows everything.
- Worship belongs to Him alone.

<<<VERSE 5:77>>>
"Say, “O People of the Book! Do not go to extremes in your faith beyond the truth, nor follow the vain desires of those who went astray before ˹you˺. They misled many and strayed from the Right Way.”"

This verse tells the People of the Book not to go to extremes in their faith beyond the truth, and not to follow the desires of earlier people who went astray, misled many, and strayed from the right way.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not go to extremes in your faith beyond the truth."** The material explains that the word for extremes means crossing the limits set by religion, in belief or in practice. It names two kinds of extremes that Bani Isra'il fell into. One was excess, by raising prophets to divinity. The other was deficiency, by rejecting them or killing them. The material says the wise path is the middle course.

**"Nor follow the vain desires of those who went astray before you."** The material says that those who went astray before were the forefathers and the earlier teachers, who led many others astray. Following their desires means taking their path instead of the truth.

**"They misled many and strayed from the Right Way."** The material says that the middle path is the right way, and those who left it lost their path.

### The limits of religion

The material explains that religion is a set of limits. It is a crime to fall short of them, and it is equally a crime to go beyond them. Honouring the messengers is also limited. The highest one may go is to regard them as the best of creation. To call them divine or sons of God is excess. The material says the same applies to the reverence of the righteous, and that the Book and the messengers are the two sources of knowledge through which people learn what Allah loves.

The material says that Zamakhshari and others divided extremes into two kinds: the unjust extreme that is forbidden, and a just extreme such as deep and careful study of the faith, which the majority of scholars do not count as excess at all. The material says that study remains within the limits drawn by the Prophet ﷺ, his Companions, and their successors.

### The path of Isra'il in this verse

The material says the verse is addressed to the Children of Israel as well, and warns them not to follow the desires of a people who had gone astray. The verse thus asks for moderation between the two extremes, which is the straight path.

### In short

- The People of the Book are told not to go beyond the truth in their faith.
- Religion is a set of limits, and both excess and deficiency are errors.
- The straight path is the middle course, and the verse warns against following those who strayed before.
