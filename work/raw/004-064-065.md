<<<VERSE 4:64>>>
"We only sent messengers to be obeyed by Allah’s Will. If only those hypocrites came to you O Prophet after wronging themselves seeking Allah’s forgiveness and the Messenger prayed for their forgiveness, they would have certainly found Allah ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful." This verse states two things. First, every messenger Allah sent was sent to be obeyed by Allah's leave, so obedience to a messenger is obedience to Allah's command. Second, it addresses hypocrites who wronged themselves: had they come to the Prophet after their wrongdoing, confessed, sought Allah's forgiveness, and had the Prophet prayed for them, Allah would have accepted their repentance and shown mercy. The verse thus joins authority and mercy. Rejecting the messenger's judgment is ruin, but humble repentance through him opens forgiveness.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We only sent messengers to be obeyed by Allah’s Will."** means Allah did not send any messenger except that people obey the rules he sets, in accordance with Allah's will and command. The phrase "by Allah's leave" means no one obeys except by Allah's leave, and the prophets are obeyed only by those whom Allah directs to obedience. Mujahid explained it this way. Another verse says Allah fulfilled His promise when the believers were killing their enemy with His permission (3:152), that is, by His command, decree and will. Since obedience is by Allah's leave, opposing the messenger's rules is treated like the way of the disbelievers.

**"If only those hypocrites came to you O Prophet"** refers to sinners and evildoers who commit errors and mistakes. They are directed to come to the Messenger after wrongdoing.

**"after wronging themselves"** describes their state: they have been unjust to themselves.

**"seeking Allah’s forgiveness"** means they should beg Allah for forgiveness in the Prophet's presence.

**"and the Messenger prayed for their forgiveness"** means they should ask him to supplicate to Allah to forgive them.

**"they would have certainly found Allah ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful"** means Allah would forgive them and award them His mercy and pardon.

### Why messengers are sent

A prophet is not sent to the world to raise a group of followers who praise him and cover him in glory. A prophet comes so that people may learn how to live and put it into practice. A person should be so thoroughly committed to it that even in controversial situations where conflicting interests have caused strained relations, one will not fail to obey the Prophet's teachings. The true believer suppresses his ego and conscientiously follows the guidance of the Prophet. He willingly accepts his ways, even if they are detrimental to his interests and run counter to his way of thinking. He remains mentally alert to any inadvertent mistake on his part, soon realising that he had strayed from the Prophet's path to the path of Satan. He immediately repents, mends his ways and seeks forgiveness. By contrast, a person who cannot adhere to the straight path of faith when the dictates of faith clash with his feelings and desires cannot be expected to remain steadfast in harder trials. At such trying times one has to give proof of one's faith by making sacrifices for it.

From the first to the last of this section is an allusion to the greatness of Mustafa's rank in Allah's sight. It is one of the robes of honour given to him by Allah, because He has raised the intermediary and made his decree equal to His own decree. Just as approving of the Real's decision brings about the certainty of those who affirm tawhid (the oneness of Allah), in the same way, approving of the Messenger's decree brings about the faith of the faithful. The passage then lists parallels: obeying the Messenger is obeying the Real, disobeying him is disobeying the Real, his words are the Real's revelation, his explanations are the Real's road, his acts are the Real's argument, his Shari'ah (sacred law) is the Real's creed, his decree is the Real's religion, and following him is friendship with the Real. Thus He says, "Follow me; God will love you" (3:31). The passage then urges the servants to travel in his tracks if they want God to give them access to His friendship and approve of their servanthood, for he is God's Messenger. They should bind the belt of following him, receive his decrees in spirit and heart without resistance, put forth their body, put down their neck, throw themselves into the decree, and not give narrowness and tightness any access to themselves.

### Conditions for accepted repentance

For repentance to be accepted, two things are required: the sinner must present himself before the Prophet, and the Prophet must pray for his forgiveness. One reason given is that they violated the great station of prophethood bestowed by Allah on His noble Messenger and caused him undue pain by disregarding the decision he had given. So, in order that their repentance over their crime becomes acceptable, the condition that they present themselves before the Prophet and that he prays for their forgiveness was imposed on them.

Although this verse was revealed in the background of a particular incident relating to the hypocrites, yet its words yield a general ruling. Anyone who presents himself before the Prophet and the Prophet prays for his forgiveness will definitely be forgiven. The presence before the Prophet during his blessed life in this mortal world holds the same effect even today. A visit to the sacred precincts of the Mosque of the Prophet, together with presenting oneself before the blessed resident of the sanctified mausoleum, falls within the jurisdiction of this rule.

Sayyidna Ali reported that three days after the Messenger of Allah was buried a villager came and fell down close to the blessed grave. Weeping bitterly, he referred to this verse and addressed the grave, saying that Allah had promised in this verse that a sinner who presented himself before the Rasul of Allah and whom the Rasul elected to pray for would be forgiven, and that he was now presenting himself so that he might be blessed with that prayer. People present at the time say that a voice coming from the sanctified mausoleum rang out with the words: "You have been forgiven." (al-Bahr al-Muhit)

The same idea is expressed in other words. If those who built the counter mosque and Hatib had wronged themselves by so building it and turning away from the Prophet, yet had come to him to repent, asked forgiveness of Allah, and asked forgiveness of the Messenger, they would have found Allah Forgiving and Merciful. If, when they had wronged themselves by seeking the judgment of the false idol, they had come to the Prophet repentant and asked forgiveness from Allah and the Messenger had asked forgiveness for them, they would have found Allah Relenting and Merciful. If, when they wronged themselves by committing sins, they had come to the Prophet confessing what they had done and regretting it, repenting from what they did, and asked for forgiveness from Allah, and the Prophet had asked for forgiveness for them, they would have found Allah Forgiving and Compassionate with them.

### The incident that made Umar's action correct

The universal principle stated at the start of the verse means that any messenger sent by Allah was sent so that everyone obeys the rules set by him in accordance with Allah's will and command. In that case, anyone who opposes the rules set by the Messenger will be treated like a disbeliever. Therefore, whatever Sayyidna Umar did turned out to be correct.

The same principle underlies the incident in which Umar killed a man who rejected the Prophet's judgment. That story is told in the next verse, where the revelation "But no, by your Lord, they can have no faith" is connected to it.

### In short

- Every messenger was sent to be obeyed by Allah's leave, so opposing a messenger's rules is treated like the way of the disbelievers.
- Hypocrites who wronged themselves should have come to the Prophet, confessed, sought Allah's forgiveness, and asked him to pray for them.
- Such repentance would have been accepted, because Allah is Forgiving and Merciful.
- The verse yields a general ruling: anyone who presents himself before the Prophet and the Prophet prays for his forgiveness will be forgiven.
- The same principle underlies Umar's action against the man who rejected the Prophet's judgment.

<<<VERSE 4:65>>>
"But no! By your Lord, they will never be true believers until they accept you O Prophet as the judge in their disputes, and find no resistance within themselves against your decision and submit wholeheartedly." This verse is an oath by Allah that no one becomes a true believer until he accepts the Prophet as judge in his disputes, finds no resistance in himself against the Prophet's decision, and submits fully. It declares that refusing the Prophet's judgment is kufr (disbelief), and makes his judgment the criterion between faith and disbelief. It follows the previous verse: after stating that messengers are sent to be obeyed, it swears that hypocrites who sought other judges have no faith. It also extends the Prophet's authority beyond his lifetime, for after him his Shari'ah is his verdict.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But no!"** is an emphatic denial of the hypocrites' claim that they believe.

**"By your Lord"** is the ultimate oath, by Allah Himself, and by the age of Muhammad.

**"they will never be true believers"** means they will not believe in secret nor deserve to be called believers when alone.

**"until they accept you O Prophet as the judge in their disputes"** means they must refer for judgments to the Messenger during his lifetime, and to the sacred law after his death, in every disagreement between them.

**"and find no resistance within themselves against your decision"** means no dislike, no doubt, no inhibition, no constraint and no tightness in the heart.

**"and submit wholeheartedly"** means accepting the ruling completely and faithfully, outwardly and inwardly, with total submission and without objection.

### Why refusing the Prophet's judgment is disbelief

This verse projects a powerful view of the highly exalted station of the Prophet. Nobody can become a Muslim unless he accepts the verdict of the Prophet willingly, calmly and fully, so that not the slightest strain remains in his heart because of this verdict. The Prophet himself exercises God-given authority over his community in his capacity as the Messenger of Allah. He is thus responsible for adjudicating in every dispute that may arise. The authority he exercises in making decisions certainly does not depend upon his being made a judge by some litigants. Muslims were asked to make the Prophet their judge because people are generally not satisfied with judgments delivered by someone appointed by the government. They prefer an arbitrator they themselves choose to have. But the Prophet is not only the ruler of a state. He is a Messenger of Allah, protected against sins by Him, sent as mercy for the worlds, and a loving father for his community too. The compassionate instruction is that whenever a situation of conflict arises in social or religious matters, the contesting parties are duty-bound to make the Prophet the arbitrator and judge and have him give the final verdict. Once the verdict has been given by him, let them all accept it whole-heartedly and act accordingly.

### The Prophet's authority extends to all times

Acting in accordance with this command is not restricted to the blessed times when the Prophet graced this mortal world. After his passing, the verdict of the sacred Law he left behind is nothing but his own verdict. Therefore the rule reigns supreme right through the Last Day: guidance should be sought by turning directly to the Prophet during his blessed lifetime, and after him one must turn to his Shari'ah, which is, in all reality, a return to him.

### The stream of Az-Zubayr

Al-Bukhari recorded that Urwah said: Az-Zubayr quarrelled with a man about a stream which both of them used for irrigation. Allah's Messenger said to Az-Zubayr, "O Zubayr! Irrigate (your garden) first, and then let the water flow to your neighbor." The Ansari became angry and said, "O Allah's Messenger! Is it because he is your cousin?" On that, the face of Allah's Messenger changed colour because of anger and he said, "Irrigate (your garden), O Zubayr, and then withhold the water until it reaches the walls (surrounding the palms). Then, release the water to your neighbor." Thus, Allah's Messenger awarded Az-Zubayr his full right after the Ansari made him angry. Before that, Allah's Messenger had given a generous judgment, beneficial for Az-Zubayr and the Ansari. Az-Zubayr said, "I think the following verse was revealed concerning that case: But no, by your Lord, they can have no faith, until they make you judge in all disputes between them."

### The man who rejected the Prophet's judgment

Al-Hafiz Abu Ishaq Ibrahim bin Abdur-Rahman bin Ibrahim bin Duhaym recorded Damrah's report about two men who took their dispute to the Prophet, and he issued a judgment to the benefit of whoever among them had the right. The person who lost the dispute said, "I do not agree." The other person asked him, "What do you want then?" He said, "Let us go to Abu Bakr As-Siddiq." They went to Abu Bakr and the person who won the dispute said, "We went to the Prophet with our dispute and he issued a decision in my favor." Abu Bakr said, "Then the decision is that which the Messenger of Allah issued." The person who lost the dispute still rejected the decision and said, "Let us go to Umar bin Al-Khattab." When they went to Umar, the person who won the dispute said, "We took our dispute to the Prophet and he decided in my favor, but this man refused to submit to the decision." Umar bin Al-Khattab asked the second man, who agreed. Umar went to his house and came out holding aloft his sword. He then struck the head of the man who rejected the Prophet's decision with the sword and killed him. Consequently, Allah revealed, "But no, by your Lord, they can have no faith."

This explains why a person who is not satisfied with the decision given by the Prophet in every dispute and in every problem or issue is not a Muslim. The heirs of the person killed went to the court of the Prophet with a suit against Umar accusing him of having killed a Muslim without a valid legal reason. It is important to note that when the case was presented before the Prophet the following words came out spontaneously from his blessed lips: "I never thought Umar will ever dare killing a believing Muslim." The higher authority, when approached with an appeal against the decision of a lower authority, should not take sides with his subordinate authority. Instead, it should come up with a decision based on justice and fairness alone, as it has been illustrated above where the Prophet expressed his displeasure over the decision of Umar before the verse was revealed and had a bearing on this incident. Once this verse was revealed, the reality came out in the open: the person killed was not a believing Muslim, as confirmed by this verse.

### The scope of the Prophet's judgment

The expression "in the disputes which arise" settles that it is not restricted to dealings and rights alone. It covers articles of belief, ideas and many other theoretical problems. Given such a wide spectrum of intellectual and social activity, things could reach the undesirable limits of mutual differences in outlook and conduct when confronting a certain problem. When this does happen, it is the duty of a Muslim not to continue mutual disputation. Instead, both parties should revert to the Prophet, and when he is not with them personally, they must revert to his Shari'ah to search for the solution of their problems.

### Self-assessment and the use of concessions

Doing something which was provenly said or done by the Prophet with a heavy heart is a sign of weakness in faith (iman). For example, there are occasions when the Shari'ah has allowed the performance of salah (prayer) by making tayammum (dry ablution) in place of the regular wudu (ablution) with water. Now, when a person feels uneasy and not so satisfied with the idea of making the symbolic tayammum in place of a regular wudu he would very much like to do, then he should not take this attitude of his as a sign of taqwa (being mindful of Allah) or piety. Instead, he should take it to be caused by some spiritual sickness. No one can be more muttaqi, more God-fearing than the Prophet. If a person looking for the satisfaction of his heart does not favour the form and condition in which the Prophet allowed people to perform their prayers while sitting, and in which he himself performed them, and instead elects to undergo unbearable hardship by insisting on standing, then he should be sure that there is some sickness in his heart. But if someone whose pain or discomfort or need is not that acute elects not to use the leave (rukhsah) given to him by the Shari'ah and, instead of that, chooses the path of high determination (azimah), that is then correct in accordance with the very teaching of the Prophet. But feeling any strain in utilising the leaves granted by the Shari'ah as some sort of absolute rule is no taqwa. It is for this reason that the Prophet has said: the way Allah is pleased with what is done with high determination, so is He pleased with what is done by using leaves.

The report is cited from at-Bahr al-Muhit, and al-Muhit is named there. People say the same. 

### The Prophet's authority in the Qur'an

Details given above clearly show that the Prophet was not only a reformer and moral leader but also a just ruler, an authority of such class and majesty, in that his judgment was declared to be the very criterion of belief and disbelief, a distinguishing feature between iman (faith) and kufr (disbelief), as the incident relating to Bishr, the hypocrite, shows. To make this prophetic station more explicit, Allah Almighty has, at many places in His sacred Book, made the obedience of the Prophet mandatory along with His own due obedience incumbent on all believers. Thus the Qur'an says: "Obey Allah and obey the Messenger" (3:32, 3:132, 4:59, 5:92, 24:54, 47:33, 64:12). Then, elsewhere it says: "Whoever obeys the Messenger, he really obeys Allah" (4:80). A little deliberation in these verses unfolds the radiant grandeur of his authority. A practical demonstration became manifest after Allah Almighty sent to him His Law so that he can decide cases in accordance with it. Therefore it was said: [Q 4:105 "that is, 'We revealed to you the …'].

Then they will not find in themselves any tightness from what you decree and will surrender with full submission. The passage then marvels at the Prophet's rank and good fortune, his nobility and excellence. It asks who, from the era of Adam until today, has had such complete bounty and well-arranged work, such celestial exaltedness and god-like luster. It says that after five hundred and some years the pillar of his Shari'ah's good fortune is flourishing. His branches are blooming. His wood is yielding fruit. His eminence is elevated. His decree is predominant. His splendor is in this world, and his fame in the next. Each heart has a lamp from him. Each tongue has a burning brand from him. Each heart has a splendor from him. Each head has a call from him. Each spirit has a place for him. It asks whether you fancy that there is none of God's gentleness, or that above the world's beauties there is no king, or that the spirits of good men have no familiarity with the beauty of the dust of your feet, or that when you lift up your tresses from your cheek the lamp of the spheres has no brightness from you. [DS 821-22]

The best method of general ibadat, following the sunnah (the Prophet's practice), meaning acts of worship, remembrance of Allah, self-allotted voluntary recitations made privately in specified hours of the morning or night, prayer to Allah that peace and blessings be on the Prophet (tasbih), and glorification of Allah, is the same as what used to be the routine of the Prophet himself and which was, after him, followed consistently by his noble Companions. All Muslims must, as a matter of personal obligation, discover them from the authentic and sound narrations of hadith so that they can make them an integral part of their daily routine of life.

### In short

- Allah swears that no one is a true believer until he accepts the Prophet as judge in his disputes and submits to his decision without resistance.
- Refusing the Prophet's judgment is kufr, and his judgment is the criterion between faith and disbelief.
- The Prophet's authority as judge applies during his lifetime and, after him, through his Shari'ah.
- The stories of Az-Zubayr's stream and of the man killed by Umar illustrate the verse.
- The verse also teaches self-assessment: feeling strain in using the concessions granted by the Shari'ah is a sign of weak faith.
