<<<VERSE 4:64>>>
The station of the Messenger stated as a universal principle: "We only sent messengers to be obeyed by Allah's Will. If only those ˹hypocrites˺ came to you ˹O Prophet˺—after wronging themselves—seeking Allah's forgiveness and the Messenger prayed for their forgiveness, they would have certainly found Allah ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful". A messenger is not sent to be praised and covered in glory, but to be obeyed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We only sent messengers to be obeyed by Allah's Will"** means his obedience was ordained for those he is sent to, all of them to follow the rules he sets, by Allah's will and command. "By Allah's leave" means none shall obey except by His leave, as Mujāhid read, for the prophets are only obeyed by whomever Allah directs to obedience. The like appears where Allah says He fulfilled His promise to the believers when they were killing the enemy with His permission (3:152), by His command, decree, will, and the superiority He granted.

**"If only those ˹hypocrites˺ came to you ˹O Prophet˺—after wronging themselves—seeking Allah's forgiveness"** points the sinners and evildoers, in their errors and mistakes, toward the Messenger ﷺ. They are to seek Allah's forgiveness in his presence and ask his supplication for them.

**"and the Messenger prayed for their forgiveness"** adds the second of two conditions binding their repentance: presenting themselves before the Prophet ﷺ, and his prayer for them. The reason reaches back to the crime: they violated the great station of prophethood and caused him undue pain by disregarding his decision, so the repentance over that crime requires his presence and his prayer.

**"they would have certainly found Allah ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful"** is the outcome: forgiveness with mercy and pardon awarded. Rather than false oaths and interpretations, confession and presentation would have served them better, and Allah would certainly have accepted their repentance.

### The word that guards his path after him

Though revealed in the background of the hypocrites' incident, the verse's words yield a general ruling: anyone who presents himself before the Prophet ﷺ, with his prayer for forgiveness, is forgiven. And the presence before him holds the same effect today as it did in his blessed lifetime, for the visit to the sacred precincts of his Mosque and the presenting of oneself before the blessed resident of the sanctified mausoleum fall within the jurisdiction of the rule. ʿAlī tells of the villager who came three days after the burial, fell down close to the blessed grave weeping bitterly, and recalled this very verse: Allah has promised that a sinner presenting himself before the Rasul, with the Rasul's prayer for his forgiveness, is forgiven, so here I am, presenting myself. A voice rang out from the mausoleum in answer: you have been forgiven, as al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ relates.

The verse's judgment on the opponent is equally general. The one who stands against what the Messenger has set is dealt with as disbelievers are dealt with, which is why what ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb did turned out correct. Al-Ḥāfiẓ Abū Isḥāq Ibrāhīm ibn Duhaym, in his tafsīr, has the report from Damrah: two men took their dispute to the Prophet ﷺ, and he gave judgment to the benefit of whoever had the right. The loser said he did not agree, and proposed going to Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq, who answered that the decision was what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had issued. Still refusing, the man proposed ʿUmar, and when the winner recounted the story and the loser concurred, ʿUmar went into his house, emerged holding aloft his sword, and struck the head of the man who rejected the Prophet's ﷺ decision. Then the verse came down.

### The inner reading

From first to last the passage alludes to the greatness of Muṣṭafā's rank in God's sight, one of the robes of honour given him. God lifted away the intermediary and set the Messenger's decree beside His own. As approval of the Real's decision settles the certainty of the tawḥīd-voicers, approval of the Messenger's decree settles the faith of the faithful. Obeying the Messenger is obeying the Real, disobeying him is disobeying the Real, his words are the revelation of the Real, his explanations the road of the Real, his acts the argument of the Real, his shariah the creed of the Real, his decree the religion of the Real, and following him friendship with the Real. Thus He says: "Follow me, God will love you" (3:31). To the master of masters, the paragon of engendered beings, the centre where the circle of new arrivals turns, the word goes out: tell My servants that whoever wants God's friendship and the approval of his servanthood should travel in your tracks, bind the belt of following, receive your decrees in spirit and heart without resistance, put forth the body, put down the neck, and throw himself into the decree, giving narrowness and tightness no access.

### In short

- Messengers are sent to be obeyed, and obedience itself comes only by Allah's leave.
- The hypocrites' repentance had two conditions, presence before the Prophet ﷺ and his prayer, and the villager at the grave found both, and the word of forgiveness.
- The general ruling stands past his lifetime, and the visit to his sanctuary falls under it.
- ʿUmar's sword against the rejecter was confirmed by the verse, and the inner reading makes the Messenger's decree equal to the Real's own.

<<<VERSE 4:65>>>
The oath that measures belief: "But no! By your Lord, they will never be ˹true˺ believers until they accept you ˹O Prophet˺ as the judge in their disputes, and find no resistance within themselves against your decision and submit wholeheartedly". The ultimate oath, by His Glorious, Most Honourable Self, and a definition of faith sworn upon it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But no! By your Lord"** is the oath Allah swore by Himself, opening with the strongest negation the language holds.

**"they will never be ˹true˺ believers"** is the verdict on the claim: nobody becomes a believer unless the Prophet's ﷺ verdict is accepted willingly, calmly and fully, with not the slightest strain left in the heart over it. His judgment is the very criterion of belief and disbelief, the distinguishing feature between īmān and kufr, as the incident of Bishr showed.

**"until they accept you ˹O Prophet˺ as the judge in their disputes"** states his office. He is the exerciser of God-given authority over his community, responsible for adjudicating every dispute that arises, and his authority to decide does not depend on being made a judge by some litigant. People are generally more satisfied with an arbitrator they choose than with a judge appointed by government. But he is not only a ruler of state. He is a Messenger of Allah, protected against sins, sent as mercy for the worlds, and a loving father for his community. The instruction is therefore compassionate. In every conflict of social or religious matters, the contesting parties must make him arbitrator and judge, accept the verdict wholeheartedly, and act accordingly.

**"and find no resistance within themselves against your decision"** allows no hesitation, inhibition, constraint or doubt regarding what he decides.

**"and submit wholeheartedly"** demands total submission, inwardly and outwardly, without rejection, denial or dispute.

### The stream and the two authorities

Al-Bukhārī recorded that ʿUrwah said that az-Zubayr quarrelled with a man about a stream both used for irrigation. The Prophet ﷺ said: "O Zubayr, irrigate your garden first, and then let the water flow to your neighbour." The Anṣārī became angry and said: "O Allah's Messenger, is it because he is your cousin?" The face of the Messenger ﷺ changed colour, and he said: "Irrigate, O Zubayr, and withhold the water until it reaches the walls surrounding the palms, then release it to your neighbour." So he gave az-Zubayr his full right when the Anṣārī provoked him, having first given a generous judgment beneficial to both. Az-Zubayr said he thought the verse was revealed concerning that case.

The rule extends to all times. Acting on this command is not restricted to his blessed lifetime, for after him the verdict of the sacred Law he left is nothing but his own verdict, and turning to his shariah is, in all reality, a return to him. The disputes covered are not restricted to dealings and rights alone, as al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ notes: they cover articles of belief, ideas and theoretical problems, and when mutual differences in views go to undesirable limits, the duty is not continued disputation but reverting to him, and after him to his shariah.

The verse also settles the appeal that followed ʿUmar's stroke. The heirs of the killed man sued ʿUmar before the Prophet ﷺ for killing a Muslim without valid legal reason, and the words came spontaneously from his blessed lips: he never thought ʿUmar would ever dare kill a believing Muslim. The higher authority, approached against the decision of a lower one, should not side with his subordinate but decide by justice and fairness alone, as the Prophet's ﷺ displeasure over ʿUmar's decision illustrated, before the verse bore on the incident. Once the verse was revealed, the reality stood open: the person killed was not a believing Muslim.

### The self-assessment of the heart

To do with a heavy heart what the Prophet ﷺ provably said or did is a sign of weakness in faith. The shariah has allowed ṣalāh by tayammum in place of the regular wudu with water. Whoever feels uneasy with the symbolic tayammum, longing for the regular wudu, should not mistake the attitude for taqwā, for it is a spiritual sickness. Who can be more God-fearing than the Prophet ﷺ? If the form in which he allowed prayer while sitting, and himself prayed sitting, does not find favour with someone, and he insists on standing through unbearable hardship, there is sickness in his heart. Conversely, one whose need is not acute may decline the leave, rukhṣah, and take the path of high determination, ʿazīmah, which is correct by his very teaching. But feeling strain in using the granted leaves is no taqwā. The way Allah is pleased with what is done with high determination, He is pleased with what is done by using leaves. The same holds for worship generally. The best method of adhkār, the remembrance of Allah, the aurād and durūd and tasbīḥ, is what was the routine of the Prophet ﷺ himself and his Companions after him, and Muslims must find these out from the authentic narrations of hadith, the sunnah itself, and make them part of daily life.

Many places in the Qur'an make his obedience mandatory beside its own, with the formula of obeying Allah and the Messenger together (3:32, 3:132, 4:59, 5:92, 24:54, 47:33, 64:12), and the word that whoever obeys the Messenger really obeys Allah (4:80). He was sent the Law so that he could decide cases by it (4:105), and the whole radiant grandeur of his authority hangs on those verses.

### The inner reading

"Do you not know that all the work has been bound to my tracks, and both houses attached to my street?" What a rank and good fortune, what a nobility and excellence! From Ādam's (Adam) era until that day, none had such bounty, such well-arranged work, such exaltedness and such lustre. After 500 and some years, the pillar of his shariah's good fortune flourished, branches blooming and wood yielding fruit, eminence elevated, decree predominant. His splendour is in this world, his fame in the next. From him each heart has a lamp, each tongue a burning brand, each head a call, each spirit a place. There is gentleness of God above what the fancies suppose, and the spirits of good men in their passion do have familiarity with the beauty of the dust of his feet, and when the lamp of the spheres shines, it shines from him.

### In short

- Faith is sworn upon submission to his judgment, with no residue of strain in the heart.
- The stream of az-Zubayr and the sword of ʿUmar are the verse's two occasions, and the appeal between authorities was decided by fairness, not favour.
- After him, his sacred Law is his own verdict, and reverting to it covers beliefs and ideas as much as property and rights.
- A heavy heart at his proven practice is sickness, not piety, and the leaves he granted carry Allah's pleasure as the high determination does.
- Inwardly, all the work is bound to his tracks, and both houses attached to his street.
