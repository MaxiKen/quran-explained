<<<VERSE 3:1>>>
The surah opens with three letters and nothing else, "Alif-Lãm-Mĩm". What they mean is known best to Allah, and the verses that follow them work out what they point to. The second verse names Allah alone as worthy of worship, the third announces the Book sent down to the Prophet ﷺ, and the fourth warns of the punishment that waits for those who reject the signs of Allah. The letters are a threshold, and what stands behind them is the first statement of the surah's subject and the first argument for it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Alif-Lãm-Mĩm"**: three letters, and the first thing a reader meets in this surah. The same three letters open al-Baqarah, and their placement was dealt with there. One view is that they expose how weak the Arabs were. They had the same letters that make up their own speech, and still they could not bring a Qur'an like this one. Another view is simply that Allah knows best what He means by these letters. The surah does not explain them here, and the detail of such words of hidden meaning is given later in the section.

### What each letter alludes to

**The Alif** alludes to the way Allah is enough for you, in every situation you are in. People are held by carelessness and have not lost the road of right action and right thinking. Allah is the One who starts in motion whatever puts you back on the road, and no one else is enough for you in what helps you there. He does not wait for you to ask. Even if you have no idea what state you are in, and even if you do not perceive it, His sufficiency is already there. And He gives without your seeking.

**The Lām** alludes to the kindness, luṭf, that Allah shows you at the finest and most hidden level, so fine that you cannot see the spot where His favor strengthens you.

**The Mīm** alludes to how the stream of divine determination, taqdīr, fits in with the circumstances the friends of Allah ask for. Nothing stirs in this world, and nothing shows itself, unless it brings good pleasure to them. Going too far is not the issue when we say that in His words "Every day He is upon some matter" (55:29), what the matter is, is the coming about of what the friends desire.

### Why the surah begins with letters that carry no meaning on their own

These disconnected letters run against the ordinary customs of human speech. It is said that by listening to them every concept disappears from the hearts, whether a known written custom or something the mind has invented, and whether the concept comes out of necessity, Ḍarūra, out of sensory perception, ḥiss, or out of independent reasoning, ijtihād. Once hearts are emptied in that way, and innermost selves are purified of what is familiar and customary, the name Allah reaches a heart that is sanctified from everything besides Him, and an innermost self that is clean of every mode of inquiry, kayf.

### A report from Ibn ʿAbbās

Ibn ʿAbbās said that Allah told them of the coming of the delegation of Najran. The letters have also been read as an oath, in which Allah swears that He is One and has neither child nor partner.

### The Greatest Name in this verse and in 2:255

A hadith says that the Greatest Name of Allah is contained in two verses, the verse of Ayat Al-Kursi (2:255) and this opening of the surah, "Alif-Lãm-Mĩm. Allah! None has the right to be worshipped but He, the Ever Living, the One Who sustains and protects all that exists" (3:1-2). The letters themselves were explained at the beginning of al-Baqarah, and the meaning of al-Hayyul al-Qayyūm was explained at the verse of Ayat Al-Kursi.

### In short

- Three letters open the surah, and their meaning is left with Allah.
- They show the limits of human speech, since the same letters make up ordinary words and cannot make a Qur'an.
- Each letter points to a different aspect of Allah: His sufficiency, His kindness and His ordering of events.
- A hadith places the Greatest Name of Allah in this opening and in the verse of Ayat Al-Kursi (2:255).

<<<VERSE 3:2>>>
The second verse states the Oneness of Allah in plain words: "Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining". It is the first full statement of the surah's subject, and it works in two moves. It declares, and then it argues, because the two names that follow the declaration are the argument.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah!"**: the name itself opens the verse, and a statement of the Oneness has to begin there.

**"There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him"**: here the Oneness of Allah is stated as a categorical declaration. The sense of it is that nothing at all is worthy of worship apart from Allah. In the terms of Islamic theology, the letters that came before gave a rational proof, and this verse gives a reported proof. A rational proof rests on argument. A reported proof rests on a verse of a divine book, on a declaration made by an authority, or on a report narrated by a trustworthy person.

**"the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining"**: al-Hayyul al-Qayyūm. The first is the One who never dies and is not evanescent. The second is the Self-subsistent who has no beginning. These two names carry the rational proof of the verse.

### What worship demands of the one who is worshipped

To worship someone is to stand before that person in complete submission and humility. So the one who is worshipped has to stand at the top of honour and power, and has to be perfect in every respect.

The conclusion is plain. A thing that cannot hold its own being, and has to depend on somebody else even to exist, can hardly claim honour or power of its own.

### Nothing that depends on another can be worshipped

Everything else in this world is ruled out by the same test. Be it an idol carved out of stone, or water, or a tree, or an angel, or an apostle, a thing that cannot start itself and cannot hold itself together is in no condition to be worshipped. Only the One who was alive before everything, who is present now, and who will go on living and sustaining, can be worshipped. That Being can only be Allah. Nobody else is worthy of worship.

### The Ever-Living is the one who never takes His eyes off you

Allah alone is worthy of worship. He lives perfectly, without any death or deficiency, and He exists by Himself, so He is not in need of any of His creation. The creation exists only through Him and is always in need of Him. He does not get drawn away so that He becomes too busy for you, and He does not fail to attend so that you are left with nothing. He watches, raqīb, over the deepest states of your inner self. Alone or among people, His watchfulness is the same, and in whatever condition you find yourself, He is your Beloved, ḥabīb.

### The words of hidden meaning

The Alif Lam Mim at the head of the first verse belongs to a special set of words used by the Qur'an, words of hidden meaning known as mutashabihāt. The true meaning of these is kept between Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, and the section clears it up a little further on. The answer to the doubts that disbelievers nurse comes at the end of this section, where the verse turns to what is hidden from sight and to the shaping of every person in the womb.

### In short

- The verse declares that nothing is worthy of worship except Allah, and then argues the point from two of His names.
- Worship requires perfection, and nothing that depends on another being can be perfect in the way worship requires.
- Idols, water, trees, angels and apostles are all ruled out by that test.
- Allah is the Ever-Living and All-Sustaining, and He watches the believer in every state.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:3>>>
The third verse turns from the name of Allah to His sending down a Book: "He has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book in truth, confirming what came before it, as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel". The point of the verse is that the Book is not a new claim. It arrives with the truth, and it confirms what was sent down before it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book in truth"**: the Book was sent down with truth, which means there is no doubt or suspicion that it comes from Allah. Allah revealed it with His knowledge, and the angels testify to that fact, and Allah is sufficient as a witness. The Book was sent to you, Muhammad ﷺ, and Gabriel was sent with it, so that truth might be shown apart from falsehood.

**"confirming what came before it"**: this means the previous Books, sent down to the servants and prophets of Allah. Those Books testify to the truth of the Qur'an, and the Qur'an in turn bears witness to what they held, among them the glad tidings of Muhammad's prophethood and of the descent of the Glorious Qur'an. Even though the books were sent to the messengers before you, the remembrance, dhikr, of you was not left out of any of them. The one who spoke for them said that the pages of their absent beloveds carry his name on their title page, and just as the lights of the prophets were extended through you, everything revealed was adorned with the remembrance of you. Briefly put, one can say that the Oneness of Allah is not peculiar to the Qur'an or to the Prophet of Islam. Long before, Allah sent down Books such as the Torah and the Injil, and sent prophets of His own, and every one of them taught this and carried it as the message of his mission. When the Qur'an came, it simply confirmed their truth. It put forward no new claim that anyone had to struggle to understand or accept.

**"as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel"**: the Torah was sent to Mūsā (Moses) son of ʿImrān, and the Injil to ʿĪsā (Jesus) son of Mary, before the Qur'an was revealed. Whenever the Qur'an speaks of the Torah and the Injil it means the original books revealed to Moses and Jesus, so these words should not be confused with the Old and New Testaments of today. The Qur'an itself was revealed in instalments, with true stories and just laws that agree with the earlier books, and there is no contradiction between them all.

### In short

- The Book was sent down with truth, and Allah's knowledge and the angels' testimony stand behind it.
- It confirms the earlier Books, and they in turn confirm it, including the glad tidings of the Prophet ﷺ.
- The Torah went to Mūsā son of ʿImrān and the Injil to ʿĪsā son of Mary, and the Qur'an adds no new claim.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:4>>>
The fourth verse finishes the account of the Books: "previously, as a guide for people, and ˹also˺ revealed the Standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺. Surely those who reject Allah’s revelations will suffer a severe torment. For Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment". The first half describes what the earlier Books did and what the last Book does. The second half warns.

### Phrase by phrase

**"previously, as a guide for people"**: before the Qur'an, and before Muhammad ﷺ, the Torah and the Gospel were sent down as guidance for people, guiding the Children of Israel from error and leading those who followed these two Books in their own time. The word used for the sending down of the Torah and the Injil is different from the one used for the Qur'an, and the reason given is that the Qur'an comes in instalments, while the two Books were revealed in one go.

**"and ˹also˺ revealed the Standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺"**: the criterion, al-furqān, tells misguidance, falsehood and deviation apart from guidance, truth and piety. It is the standard that separates the lawful from the unlawful, and it works through the signs, plain evidences and clear proofs it contains and through its explanations. It is mentioned after the three Scriptures so that it embraces all revealed Scripture besides them. All these books held guidance and directed people to the best of this world and the afterlife.

**"Surely those who reject Allah’s revelations will suffer a severe torment. For Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment"**: those who disbelieve in the signs of Allah denied and refused them, and for them is a severe torment on the Day of Resurrection. Their chastisement is the ignominy of the veil, though they do not perceive it. Allah is Mighty, victorious in His affair, so that nothing can stop Him from carrying out His promise and His threat. He is Lord of Retribution with a severe punishment for those who disobey Him, the like of which none can do. Everyone seeks what is precious, but most do not find it, and Allah is Precious, ʿazīz, to His friends and Lord of Retribution to His enemies.

### The Oneness of Allah is confirmed by what these verses say of Him

The last part of this section strengthens the doctrine of the Oneness on the ground of two attributes. Encompassing knowledge and unlimited power are strong proofs of the One who possesses eternal, all-encompassing knowledge, and whose power controls everything. Anyone with imperfect knowledge and limited power cannot be entrusted with this exalted station. The signs of Allah referred to here, and the doom that follows rejecting them, are aimed in this context at the delegation of Najran, and the doom is heavy both in this world and in the next.

### In short

- The Torah and the Gospel came before, as guidance, and the Qur'an came in instalments as the criterion.
- The criterion separates truth from falsehood, and it embraces all revealed Scripture.
- Rejecting the signs of Allah brings a severe torment, and Allah is Mighty and able to punish.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:5>>>
The fifth verse is short and wide: "Surely nothing on earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah". After the Book and its warnings, the surah turns to the One who sent them down, and describes Him as the one whose knowledge has no gaps at all.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Surely nothing on earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah"**: nothing in the earth or in the heavens is hidden from His watch. He has perfect knowledge in the heavens and the earth. In this context the earth holds the story of the delegation of Najran, and the heavens hold the business of the angels, and neither is hidden from Him. He knows everything, whether it is open or hidden. Nothing, no existent thing at all, is hidden in heaven and earth from Allah, because of His knowledge of universals and particulars. Heaven and earth are named here because the senses of people do not reach beyond them.

### Every breath is counted

A servant does not breathe a single breath without Allah counting it, muḥṣīh. No particle is found in the heaven or the earth unless He brought it into being and sustains it, mubdiʿ. And no quality or attribute belongs to anyone unless He is the governor of it, mutawallī. That is the level of ordinary people, ʿumūm. The elect, khuṣūṣ, stand higher: nobody puts a need to Him without His meeting it, qāḌīhā, and nobody meets a calamity without His sufficing for it, kāfīhā.

### Why only Allah can give guidance

Only Allah can tell us the truth about Himself, about His being and about His attributes, and He has set that out plainly in His Book, so that a reader who is keen to know Him finds real light there. Only He can guide anyone. The right course for a person can be settled by no one but the One who has known people from their birth to their death, and who knows what went before the birth and what lies beyond the death. So it is Allah alone who can set the nature of people inside the whole universe and decree the kind of conduct that will secure their success in this world and the next, since a sound human mind can only be one in step with the rest of the world and in accord with the larger order of the cosmos. And it is Allah alone who has both the knowledge and the power to say what people should be aiming at in the present world. On that basis, the only sensible thing for a person to do is to trust in the Maker and to take His guidance whole, with complete confidence.

### In short

- Nothing on earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah, and He knows universals and particulars.
- Every breath is counted, every particle is created by Him, and every need is met by Him.
- Only He can settle the right course of people, because only He knows the whole of their life and the whole of the universe.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:6>>>
The sixth verse closes the opening section: "He is the One Who shapes you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise". The verse does two things at once. It names Allah as the one who forms every human being, and it rules out every other claimant to worship.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He is the One Who shapes you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills"**: He forms you in the wombs however He wishes, a man or a woman, attractive or otherwise, happy or unhappy, short or tall, fair or ugly, blessed or doomed, white or black or anything else. It is by His supreme wisdom that billions of men and women have been given clear and distinguishable features, so that each one is a person distinct from all the others.

**"There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him"**: He is the Creator, and so He is the only deity worthy of worship, without partners. His is the perfect might, wisdom and decision, and no one else has any of it. Nobody else deserves the love and reverence of worship. He is Mighty in His kingdom and cannot be overpowered, and He is Wise in His planning and legislation.

### Why this verse answers the claim that ʿĪsā is divine

One can say that the verse settles the question of ʿĪsā, the son of Mary. He is a servant, created, like everybody else that Allah creates. Allah made him in his mother's womb and shaped him as He willed, and his creation moved from one stage to the next, as Allah said, "He creates you in the wombs of your mothers, creation after creation in three veils of darkness" (39:6). So on what ground could ʿĪsā be a god, as the Christians claim?

### The reports on the form of the Creator

In a sound report MuṣṬafā said, "God created Adam in His form, and his height was sixty cubits". It has also been narrated as "in the form of His face". Some who follow interpretation turned the referent of the pronoun away from Allah and rejected the outward sense, on the ground that their religion rests on interpretation and denial of the outward meaning. Those who follow the sunnah, whose way is to hear, to accept and to surrender, leave interpretation aside and keep the outward sense. Their view is that the pronoun in that report points to Allah, that no investigation or interpretation is allowed here, and that imagining a likeness is a mistake, since Allah has no equal in any of His attributes. On the question of vision, many reports describe the Real as having a shining face and form. Ibn ʿAbbās narrates that MuṣṬafā said, "I saw my Lord in the most beautiful form." In the narrative of Abū Umāma al-Bāhilī, MuṣṬafā said, "My Lord was shown to me in the most beautiful form. He said, 'O Muḥammad!' I said, 'Here I am, obeying Thee.' About what are the Higher Plenum disputing?" The narrative of Jābir ibn Samura is this: "Surely God disclosed Himself to me in the most beautiful form." The narrative of Anas is this: "My Lord came to me in the most beautiful form." Anas also said that among the favors Allah will grant Adam on the Day of Resurrection is this: He will say, "Did I not bestow upon you My form?" Ibn ʿAbbās said that Moses was angry with the Children of Israel, and when he came down with a prohibition he told them, "Drink this, you donkeys!" Allah asked him whether creatures made in His own form could be likened to donkeys. It is well known, the reports of the resurrection say, that MuṣṬafā said, "God will come to them in other than the form in which they recognize Him."

### In short

- Allah shapes every person in the womb as He wills, and no one else has that power.
- That is why ʿĪsā is a created servant like the rest of us, and cannot be a god.
- The reports on the form of the Creator are read, by those who follow the sunnah, as referring to Allah, with no likeness imagined.
