<<<VERSE 3:1>>>
The surah opens with the three separate letters, “Alif-Lãm-Mĩm.” Their meaning is not settled in the explanations gathered here. One view leaves their full meaning with Allah. Another understands them as a challenge: the Qur’an is made from letters familiar to its hearers, yet they cannot produce a book like it. A report attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās connects the letters with the coming account of the Najran delegation. Another interpretation hears them as an oath affirming that Allah is One, with no child or partner.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Alif-Lãm-Mĩm”** is not expanded into an agreed ordinary sentence. Some say Allah knows best what He means by these letters. Another explanation says they point to the limits of human power: the Qur’an uses the same letters that form ordinary speech, but people cannot match it. A report from Ibn ʿAbbās links them to the account of the delegation from Najran. It is also said that the letters form an oath declaring Allah’s oneness and denying that He has offspring or a partner.

### What can the letters mean?

The explanations preserve several readings rather than forcing one. The letters may be among the Qur’an’s hidden expressions, whose full meaning is known to Allah. They may also challenge those who hear the Qur’an to produce something like it from the very letters they use themselves. A further reported interpretation treats them as an oath by which Allah declares His unity and freedom from offspring or partnership. The report from Ibn ʿAbbās associates them with the story of the Najran delegation, which the surah returns to later (3:61).

### An inward reading of the letters

An allusive interpretation gives each letter a spiritual association. Alif points to Allah’s sufficiency for a person in every state, even before that person recognizes a need or asks for help. Lām points to His subtle kindness, which may work beyond a person’s awareness. Mīm points to harmony between what Allah decrees and what His close servants seek. This reading also treats the unfamiliar letters as a means of loosening the heart’s attachment to ordinary concepts, sensory impressions, and independent reasoning. When the heart is emptied of these, it can turn wholly toward Allah. This is an inward reflection, not a claim that the letters have one agreed lexical translation.

### In short

- The verse names three letters without giving one agreed explanation of their full meaning.
- They have been read as hidden knowledge, a challenge to imitate the Qur’an, an oath of Allah’s oneness, or a reference to the Najran account.
- An inward reading associates the letters with Allah’s sufficiency, subtle kindness, and the heart’s surrender to His decree.

<<<VERSE 3:2>>>
The verse declares that “There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining.” Allah alone deserves worship. He is perfectly living and does not die or fade, while everything else depends on Him for existence and support. This dependence is offered as a reason that created things, however revered, cannot be gods: what needs another to exist cannot have the independent power that worship requires.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Allah!”** names the One who is the subject of the declaration. The sentence does not present several possible objects of worship. It identifies Allah as the One to whom worship belongs.

**“There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him”** states the oneness of Allah. Worship involves complete humility and submission, so its object must possess the highest power and perfection. Created things cannot sustain their own existence. This applies to idols made of stone or water, to trees, and even to angels and messengers. They depend on their Creator and therefore are not worthy of worship. Allah alone is independent and deserves it.

**“the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining”** describes why He alone has this right. The Ever-Living does not die or pass away. The All-Sustaining exists without beginning and maintains all that exists, while creation always needs Him. The pairing is also understood as saying that Allah is never distracted from a person’s state and never inattentive to it. Whether a person is alone or among others, Allah watches over the inward state.

### Why dependence rules out other gods

The reasoning begins with what worship means. A worshipper submits to the one regarded as supreme in honor and power. Anything that cannot bring itself into existence or keep itself alive has no independent claim to that position. The world and all its inhabitants are sustained by Allah. Their dependence points away from worship of created things and toward the One who lives eternally and sustains them all.

### A report about Allah’s greatest name

A report discussed in connection with the opening verses says that Allah’s greatest name is found in these verses. The passage also points back to the verse of the Throne (2:255), where the same description of Allah as Ever-Living and Sustainer appears. The central point here remains the declaration that Allah alone is worthy of worship.

### In short

- Allah alone deserves worship because He is the Ever-Living and the One who sustains everything.
- Every created thing depends on Him, so no created being can share His right to worship.
- Allah’s watchfulness extends to a person’s inward state in solitude and in company.

<<<VERSE 3:3>>>
The verse says that Allah revealed the Book to the Prophet ﷺ “in truth, confirming what came before it,” and also revealed the Torah and the Gospel. The Qur’an is presented as revelation from Allah, not a human invention. Its message of Allah’s oneness continues the guidance brought by earlier prophets. Its agreement with earlier revelation is a further sign that the call is one, even though the books were sent at different times.

### Phrase by phrase

**“He has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book in truth”** means that Allah sent the Qur’an to Muhammad ﷺ. It came with truth and has no doubt in its origin. The revelation is described as coming with Allah’s knowledge, with angels bearing witness to it. One explanation says Gabriel brought the Book to the Prophet ﷺ.

**“confirming what came before it”** means that the Qur’an affirms the earlier revealed books and the truth they carried. Those books also bear witness to the Qur’an, including the glad news of Muhammad’s prophethood. Another explanation stresses that the Qur’an’s doctrine of oneness is not a new claim that contradicts previous prophets. The earlier message and the Qur’an agree in their call to Allah.

**“as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel”** points to the books sent to Mūsā (Moses) and ʿĪsā (Jesus). They were revealed before the Qur’an. One explanation distinguishes their revelation as a single event from the Qur’an’s repeated, gradual revelation. The names here refer to the original books revealed to those prophets, not to later books bearing similar names.

### What does confirmation mean?

The Qur’an’s confirmation is not merely that earlier scriptures existed. It affirms the truth in the revelation given before it, including the prophets’ shared call to worship Allah alone. The Qur’an also serves as a witness to that earlier truth. One inward reading describes the Qur’an as fulfilling the promise of Muhammad ﷺ found in earlier books. It pictures his remembrance as present within those revelations and treats his mission as completing the line of prophetic guidance.

### In short

- The Qur’an is described as true revelation from Allah, delivered to Muhammad ﷺ.
- It confirms the truth of earlier revelation and shares the prophets’ call to Allah’s oneness.
- The Torah and Gospel were revealed before the Qur’an to Mūsā and ʿĪsā, while the Qur’an came in stages.

<<<VERSE 3:4>>>
The verse completes the account of earlier revelation and describes the Qur’an as a criterion that distinguishes truth from falsehood. It also warns those who reject Allah’s revelations of severe punishment. The closing names Allah as Almighty, whose power cannot be resisted, and as able to carry out retribution. Revelation is therefore both guidance and a standard by which claims and actions are judged.

### Phrase by phrase

**“previously, as a guide for people”** continues the description of the Torah and Gospel: they came before the Qur’an and guided people in their time. One explanation specifies that they guided the Children of Israel away from error. Another treats the revealed books together as guidance toward what is good in this life and the next.

**“and ˹also˺ revealed the Standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺”** names the criterion. Some explanations understand it broadly as the revealed scriptures that distinguish truth from falsehood. Others identify it with the Qur’an, which clarifies what is lawful and unlawful and separates guidance from error. Its clear signs and explanations make the distinction plain.

**“Surely those who reject Allah’s revelations will suffer a severe torment”** warns those who deny and unjustly refuse the revelations. One explanation places the punishment on the Day of Resurrection. Another says it comes in this world and the next, and specifically connects the warning in this setting with the delegation from Najran.

**“For Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment”** means that no one can overpower Him or prevent His promise and warning from being carried out. His power is complete, and His punishment for those who reject His signs and oppose His messengers is severe.

### How is the criterion understood?

The explanations differ in how widely they apply the word “Standard.” It can refer to all revealed scripture, since revelation distinguishes truth from falsehood. It can also refer specifically to the Qur’an’s clarification of what is right, including lawful and unlawful conduct. Both readings make the same point: people need Allah’s guidance to judge rightly, and rejecting it has consequences.

### In short

- Earlier scriptures guided their people before the Qur’an was revealed.
- The “Standard” is understood as revealed scripture generally or the Qur’an’s clear distinction between right and wrong.
- Those who reject revelation face severe punishment, and Allah has the power to carry out His warning.

<<<VERSE 3:5>>>
The verse states, “Surely nothing on earth or in the heavens is hidden from Allah.” It presents Allah’s knowledge as complete, reaching every existent thing and every detail. The heavens and earth are named as the widest domains people can perceive, not as limits on what He knows. This knowledge also means that no breath, need, or hardship is outside His awareness.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Surely nothing on earth or in the heavens”** includes every existent thing in both realms. Allah knows universals and particulars, and no particle exists there without Him as its originator and creator. A person does not draw a single breath without it being known to Him.

**“is hidden from Allah”** means His knowledge misses nothing. He governs every quality and attribute. One inward explanation distinguishes the needs of ordinary people from those of the spiritual elect: every person’s need is known, and Allah fulfills a need and suffices in calamity. The point is not that some things are concealed from Him, but that His knowledge and care reach every state.

### In short

- Allah knows every existent thing in the heavens and earth, from the largest to the smallest.
- No breath or personal need is hidden from Him.
- An inward reading adds that He meets needs and suffices through hardship.

<<<VERSE 3:6>>>
Allah is the One who “shapes you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills.” The verse joins His creative power to His sole right to be worshipped: He forms each person, and He alone is Almighty and All-Wise. The explanations describe many forms and conditions, including male and female, different appearances, and different destinies. The verse also answers claims that ʿĪsā (Jesus) is divine: he was formed in his mother’s womb, like other human beings, by Allah’s will.

### Phrase by phrase

**“He is the One Who shapes you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills”** means that Allah creates and gives form before birth. He makes people male or female, tall or short, handsome or otherwise, and gives them differing conditions. One explanation also mentions differing complexions and whether a person will be happy or miserable. The variety of human beings, including their distinct features, is presented as a sign of His power and wisdom.

**“There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him”** follows from the fact that He alone creates. ʿĪsā was formed in a womb and developed through stages, as other people do. The verse cited in this connection describes creation in the womb through successive stages and three veils of darkness (39:6). A created servant cannot be the Creator.

**“the Almighty, All-Wise”** means that Allah’s power cannot be overcome and that His acts and decisions have wisdom. He chooses the forms and conditions of those He creates.

### What do the reports about Allah’s form mean?

The material also discusses reports using the language of Allah’s form. One report described as sound says that Allah created Adam in His form and gives Adam’s height as sixty cubits. Another version says “in the form of His face.” The explanations preserve a disagreement over how to understand such reports. Some interpreters turn the pronoun away from Allah and interpret the wording, while others accept its outward wording and refuse to speculate about how it applies. Those who take the outward wording also insist that Allah has no equal and that imagining likeness between Him and creation is an error.

Other reports are cited without a grading here. Ibn ʿAbbās is said to have reported that the Prophet ﷺ saw his Lord in the most beautiful form. Abū Umāma al-Bāhilī relates a longer account in which the Prophet ﷺ is addressed and asked what the Higher Plenum are disputing. Jābir ibn Samura and Anas are also named in reports describing the Lord appearing in the most beautiful form. Anas further reports that Allah will remind Adam of the form He bestowed on him. The longer account is said to be discussed elsewhere.

A report attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās also tells of Mūsā speaking angrily to the Children of Israel and calling them donkeys when they objected to a prohibition. Allah then rebuked him for comparing creatures He created in His form with donkeys. The material also says that reports about the Resurrection describe Allah coming to people in a form other than the one by which they recognize Him. These reports are presented alongside the warning not to imagine that Allah resembles His creation.

### In short

- Allah gives each person form in the womb and does so with power and wisdom.
- Because Allah creates, He alone deserves worship. The formation of ʿĪsā in a womb is given as evidence that he is created, not divine.
- Reports about Allah’s form are interpreted differently, while the explanations that accept their outward wording warn against imagining any likeness to creation.
- Several reports describe the Prophet ﷺ seeing Allah in a beautiful form, and the material distinguishes a sound report from other reports whose grading it does not state.
