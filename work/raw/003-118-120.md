<<<VERSE 3:118>>>
> “O believers! Do not associate closely with others who would not miss a chance to harm you. Their only desire is to see you suffer. Their prejudice has become evident from what they say—and what their hearts hide is far worse. We have made Our revelations clear to you, if only you understood” (3:118)

The verse warns believers against making people who are actively hostile their trusted confidants. It speaks about those whose words and conduct show a desire to harm the community, not about every person of another faith. Believers are called to use discernment while upholding justice and the protection of people under covenant.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Do not associate closely with others who would not miss a chance to harm you”** cautions against giving sensitive trust to people who have shown that they intend to undermine the believers. The Arabic term bitanah refers to an intimate confidant, someone admitted to private affairs and trusted with hidden plans. The image is of an inner lining close to the body. The warning concerns access and trust, not ordinary kindness, trade, neighborliness, or fair treatment.

Reports connect the verse with believers in Madinah who maintained close personal ties with Jewish neighbors and hypocrites. Some relationships came from kinship, friendship, treaties, and life as neighbors. Ibn Abbas and Mujahid relate that the verse warned against sharing confidential matters with people who might use them to turn believers away from their faith or expose their plans. The Qur’an’s concern is the demonstrated risk of betrayal, not a license to suspect someone merely because they are Jewish, Christian, or otherwise different.

**“Their only desire is to see you suffer”** describes the hostile intention of the people in view. The verse calls believers to notice patterns of conduct and words. It does not say that every disagreement proves enmity. People can disagree honestly, and neighbors of different faiths can cooperate and care for one another. A believer should distinguish a real threat from prejudice or rumor.

The verse also should not be used to deny the rights of protected non-Muslims. Islamic law includes obligations toward people under a treaty or dhimmah. The Prophet ﷺ warned against harming a person protected by agreement. A report from Ibn Masʿud says that the Messenger of Allah would stand against one who wronged a protected person. Security screening for sensitive responsibilities is not the same as denying someone’s basic rights or mistreating them.

**“Their prejudice has become evident from what they say”** means that words may reveal hostility already present in someone’s conduct. Believers should listen carefully and make sound judgments. Public insults, attempts to expose confidential information, or efforts to provoke conflict may indicate that a person is not a safe confidant. But a single disagreement should not be exaggerated into proof of a hidden plot.

**“and what their hearts hide is far worse”** reminds believers that Allah knows intentions more fully than people do. Humans must act on evidence, not pretend to know the unseen. A person may set a prudent boundary without claiming certainty about another’s heart. The verse teaches awareness while leaving ultimate knowledge to Allah.

**“We have made Our revelations clear to you, if only you understood”** calls believers to use revelation and reason together. The guidance is clear enough to distinguish trust from naïveté, but it does not abolish compassion or justice. Believers should be careful with confidential matters while remaining truthful, fair, and kind in their dealings.

### Trust, counsel, and public responsibility

A hadith from Abu Saʿid describes how prophets and leaders may have advisers who encourage good and others who encourage harm. Al-Bukhari and An-Nasaʾi transmit this report. It teaches people to choose counsel carefully, including within their own community. A Muslim should not trust someone merely because they share a label, nor condemn someone simply because they do not.

Another report describes ʿUmar ibn al-Khattab being asked to appoint a Christian scribe for sensitive state affairs because the man was skilled. ʿUmar declined because of the confidentiality involved. This historical decision concerns access to confidential government matters. It should not be turned into a universal rule excluding non-Muslims from ordinary work or public service. The Prophet’s teaching about protecting a person under treaty remains an essential limit.

Al-Isfahani and al-Qurtubi explain bitanah as someone admitted to a person’s secrets and private affairs. This supports a practical distinction: the verse governs intimate confidence and sensitive access, not every social relationship. The believers of Madinah could have neighbors, relatives, and trading partners from other communities while still safeguarding information that could put people at risk.

### In short

- The verse warns against trusting people whose hostility is evident in words and actions.
- It does not condemn every person of another religion or prohibit fair social relations.
- Confidential trust must be balanced with justice and protection of people under treaty.

<<<VERSE 3:119>>>
> “Here you are! You love them but they do not love you, and you believe in all Scriptures. When they meet you they say, “We believe.” But when alone, they bite their fingertips in rage. Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “˹May you˺ die of your rage!” Surely Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart” (3:119)

The verse describes believers who wish good for people who oppose them, while those opponents conceal resentment. It praises the believers’ faith in all revelation and reminds them that Allah knows what is hidden in hearts.

### Phrase by phrase

**“You love them but they do not love you”** describes the sincere goodwill believers can feel toward people who do not return it. Muslims may hope that others recognize the truth and receive guidance. The warning is directed at a hostile group whose conduct shows resentment, not every member of the People of the Book. The verse does not command hatred of an entire community.

**“and you believe in all Scriptures”** means that Muslims affirm the revelations Allah sent to earlier prophets, including the Torah and the Gospel in their original revelation. They believe in Moses and the other messengers, as well as Muhammad ﷺ. Their faith is not selective. This gives them a basis for recognizing truth wherever it is found, while also accepting the Qur’an’s guidance.

**“When they meet you they say, ‘We believe’”** describes the words of people who pretend to share the believers’ faith while concealing opposition. A person’s claim should be judged with fairness, but repeated conduct may reveal that their words are not sincere. The verse warns believers not to be naïve about a demonstrable pattern of deception.

**“But when alone, they bite their fingertips in rage”** is a vivid image of intense frustration and envy. It depicts the private anger of the hostile people in view when they see the believers’ faith and solidarity. The image is not an instruction for Muslims to retaliate. It exposes resentment and reassures believers that hidden hostility does not escape Allah’s knowledge.

**“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ ‘May you die of your rage!’”** is a declaration that the opponents’ anger will not stop Allah’s guidance or take away what He grants the believers. It is a rhetorical rebuke, not a command for Muslims to harm anyone. The Prophet ﷺ is told to answer their spite by affirming Allah’s control over the outcome.

**“Surely Allah knows best what is hidden in the heart”** means that Allah knows both the believers’ sincere concern and the opponents’ concealed resentment. People cannot see every intention, so they should avoid pretending to know what Allah alone knows. The verse calls for prudence based on conduct and humility about what remains hidden.

### In short

- Believers may wish good for people who do not return their affection.
- Muslims affirm the revelations sent to earlier prophets as well as the Qur’an.
- Hidden resentment is known to Allah, and the response is not personal retaliation.

<<<VERSE 3:120>>>
> “When you ˹believers˺ are touched with good, they grieve; but when you are afflicted with evil, they rejoice. ˹Yet,˺ if you are patient and mindful ˹of Allah˺, their schemes will not harm you in the least. Surely Allah is Fully Aware of what they do” (3:120)

The verse describes the reaction of hostile opponents to the believers’ changing circumstances. They are saddened by the believers’ good fortune and pleased by hardship. The believers are told to respond with patience and taqwa, trusting that Allah knows every scheme.

### Phrase by phrase

**“When you ˹believers˺ are touched with good, they grieve”** means that the opponents in view dislike seeing the believers gain safety, strength, or success. Their reaction reveals envy and hostility. The verse does not say that every person outside Islam rejoices at Muslim hardship. It describes those already shown to be antagonistic in the surrounding passage.

**“but when you are afflicted with evil, they rejoice”** refers to setbacks such as loss, fear, or defeat. The Battle of Uhud is one example of a difficult moment when some opponents were pleased by the believers’ hardship. The verse helps Muslims understand that not everyone who offers advice or claims friendship has their well-being at heart. It also cautions them against responding to hostility by becoming unjust themselves.

**“if you are patient and mindful ˹of Allah˺”** gives the response. Sabr is steadiness through hardship, restraint from wrongful retaliation, and persistence in doing what is right. Taqwa is awareness of Allah that keeps the believer within His limits. Patience is not passivity in the face of harm. It can include seeking lawful protection, acting wisely, and continuing to fulfil responsibilities without surrendering to fear or rage.

The Qur’an connects patience and taqwa with divine help in battle (3:125), recounts Joseph’s patience and mindfulness in hardship (12:90), and closes this chapter by urging believers to persevere and remain mindful of Allah (3:200). A hadith from Abu Dharr points to Allah’s promise of a way out for the person who fears Him (65:2). These teachings show that patience and taqwa guide personal, social, and political conduct.

**“their schemes will not harm you in the least”** does not promise that believers will never suffer injury or loss. It assures them that hostile plots cannot defeat Allah’s purpose or take away the ultimate reward of faith. The believers may need to respond to real danger, but they do not need to panic or imitate the wrongdoing of their opponents.

**“Surely Allah is Fully Aware of what they do”** means that no action or hidden plan is outside Allah’s knowledge. He sees the believers’ patience and the opponents’ conduct, and He will judge with justice. The believer can therefore remain careful without becoming consumed by suspicion.

### In short

- Hostile people may resent believers’ good fortune and rejoice at their hardship.
- Patience and taqwa help believers respond without injustice or despair.
- Allah knows every scheme and will not let hostility defeat His purpose.
