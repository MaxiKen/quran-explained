<<<VERSE 6:22>>>
"˹Consider˺ the Day We will gather them all together then ask those who associated others ˹with Allah in worship˺, 'Where are those gods you used to claim?'" The surah has just said that the wrongdoers will not succeed. Now it shows the trial where that verdict is passed, and the question that will be put to the people of shirk. Nobody is left out of the gathering. Disbelievers and the things they set up for themselves as objects of worship are collected together, and then the question is asked: where are the partners you claimed, the ones you worshipped and expected to intercede for you? Where are the resolvers of your problems, and why do they not come to help you in the hour that tests you?

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Consider˺ the Day We will gather them all together"** is the Day of Resurrection, and the command to "consider" it is addressed to the Prophet ﷺ, as a warning to his people. Not one of them is left behind.

**"then ask those who associated others ˹with Allah in worship˺"** carries the answer to a small question of wording. The particle "then" between the gathering and the questioning signals delay. The interrogation does not begin the moment they are assembled. They will go on standing in that state of wonder and confusion for a long stretch of time, and the reckoning will start after that gap has passed.

**"Where are those gods you used to claim?"** is a rebuke, and it asks for the claimed partners to be produced. The same challenge is put in Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ: "And remember the Day when He will call to them and say: Where are My partners whom you used to claim?" (28:62).

### How long the waiting lasts

A hadith reports the Prophet ﷺ saying: what will happen to you when Allah gathers you on the plains of the Resurrection as arrows are gathered in their quiver, and you stay in that state for fifty thousand years? Another narration says that everyone will tarry in the dark that day for a thousand years, unable even to speak to one another. That version was reported by al-Ḥākim in his al-Mustadrak, and by al-Baihaqī. The same pair of numbers stands in two verses of the Qur'an. In Sūrat al-Maʿārij: a day whose measure is fifty thousand years (70:4). In Sūrat al-Ḥajj: a day in the reckoning of your Lord is like a thousand years of your counting (22:47). The difference is explained by the weight of it. That day will be long in proportion to how hard the pain is, and the levels of pain differ, so it looks like fifty thousand years to some people and like a thousand to others.

### Why the question is asked at all

Allah knows what they claimed and what they worshipped, and He knows how they will answer. The question is not for information. It is exposure: the people who handed the work of godhood to things that cannot answer are made to ask after them in front of everyone.

### In short

- The verse opens the scene of the great trial, with everyone gathered and the claimed partners conspicuously missing.
- "Then" marks a delay: long standing in confusion before the questioning and the reckoning begin.
- The delay is described in two lengths, a thousand years and fifty thousand, and the difference belongs to the severity each group feels.
- The challenge to produce the partners, asked here and in 28:62, is a rebuke rather than a request for news.

<<<VERSE 6:23>>>
"Their only argument will be: 'By Allah, our Lord! We were never polytheists.'" After the question in verse 22 comes the answer, and the answer is a lie sworn on Allah's own name. The verse calls their reply a fitnah, and that single word carries both things that are happening here. Their answer is itself a trial, an ordeal that testifies against them. And it is also infatuation: these people were devoted to idols and to self-made objects of worship, they even slaughtered sacrifices for them, and on this day all that devotion has nowhere left to go but denial.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Their only argument will be"** means that no plea is left to them, no answer and no excuse at all. The grammar of the sentence allows the word to be read either as their quarrelling or as their apology, and both senses come to the same thing: they will be driven to this.

**"By Allah, our Lord!"** is an oath taken by the great name of the very One they are denying before. "Our Lord" can be read as a description of Allah and as a direct address, and either way the shamelessness is the point. It was the most terrifying of days, the day of being paid back, with the awesome proofs of their Lord's power and authority standing around them, and still they had the nerve to lie to His face.

**"We were never polytheists"** is the denial of associationism and of disbelief itself, and the answer is given with a "then" in front of it, which shows that even this was produced after a long period of deliberation.

### Why they are allowed to lie

Most commentators take the reply for something the liars did not think about either before or after. It is the heat of the awe around them and the collapse of their own nerve, a state in which a person says things he does not mean. But if you look at how that Day is described generally, another sense appears. Allah gave them the freedom to speak as they used to speak in the mortal world, so that everyone present could see their full profile, and learn that these people did not stop at disbelief and polytheism but were unmatched as liars too, since they would not give up lying even on a day this horrifying. A hint of that lies in Sūrah al-Mujdalah: "the day they all swear to Him as they swear to you" (58:18). Just as they swore falsely in front of the believers, so they will not hesitate to swear falsely in front of the Lord of all the worlds.

Then, when they have sworn their way out of the shirk and the kufr, Allah in His absolute power has their mouths sealed with silence, and His order goes out to the limbs of their bodies: now speak, and testify to everything they did. That is when it emerges that their own hands and feet and eyes and ears had been a kind of secret service, planted with them all along, and they will tell, and tell everything. ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī gave the verse in ibn-Kathīr's account a harder sense: there is nothing left to protect them from the affliction that will be put on them. Sūrat Yā Sīn states it: "On that Day We put a seal on their mouths, and their hands will speak to Us, and their feet will testify to what they were earning" (36:65). And elsewhere: "they will not conceal anything from Allah" (4:42). Ibn ʿAbbās explained that verse this way: they will lie as much as they can, and swear falsely, but once their own hands and feet testify, they will have no nerve left to say anything wrong at all.

The wrongdoer is left free to make whatever statement he likes before the Judge of all judges, even a lie, just as he lied in mortal life, and nobody snatches that right off him. Allah tears the façade of the lie down with His own power, through the testimony of the man's own body parts, so no advocate is needed. This is why lying will not work after death. Think of an entrance examination. After death the first test a person sits is taken by the two angels, Munkar and Nakir. In the hadith, when they ask a kāfir who his Lord is and what his religion is, he says only: oh, I do not know. A true Muslim answers: Allah is my Lord, and Islam is my religion, and I accepted Your oneness. The kāfir's answer is the one he will not be able to improve on by talking his way out. Nobody will have any courage to lie in that earlier test, otherwise a disbeliever could have given the Muslim's answer. The reason is that the examiners there are angels who do not know the unseen and who cannot make hands and feet testify. If lying were an option there, the angels would simply act on the answer given, and the whole arrangement would come apart. The test of the Resurrection is different, because the questions and the answers pass before Allah Almighty, and no lie works there even if a person tries it.

### Who these people were

Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ and Māzhari report from some elders a description of which liars are meant. They are people who never openly declared some created thing to be God or God's deputy. In what they did, though, they assigned all the powers of godhood to created beings: they went to them with their needs, made offerings in their names, and asked them for sustenance, for health, for children and for everything they wanted. Such people did not think of themselves as associating partners with Allah, which is why on the Day of Resurrection they will swear that they never did. In the end Allah Almighty exposes their disgrace.

### Does Allah speak to them or not

A question is raised by the verse, because some passages of the Qur'an state that Allah will not speak to the wrongdoers and the sinners, while this verse plainly shows an address being made. Two answers are given. The address here is not one that honours them or accepts their prayers, and what is denied in those other verses is that kind of standing and acceptance. It is also said that the questioning on that Day may reach them through the angels, and that the verse which denies divine speech has an indirect address in view.

### In short

- Their defence is a false oath taken on the name of the One they are answering, and it is the last thing their own argument offers them.
- Fitnah names both the ordeal they are in and the infatuation they are now denying.
- The lie is permitted to stand on the record only so that limbs, and then silence, demolish it.
- The same freedom to lie does not exist in the trial of the grave, where the examiners cannot be answered by words alone.
- The liars exposed here are people who never called a creature God, and behaved as if they had.

<<<VERSE 6:24>>>
"See how they will lie about themselves and how those ˹gods˺ they fabricated will fail them." The Prophet ﷺ is told to look at it. The lie they tell on the Day of Judgement turns back on them, and the fabricated partners are simply gone when they are needed. On another reading the address is to the angels.

### Phrase by phrase

**"See how they will lie about themselves"** means that the curse of what they say falls on their own selves. They have written the punishment of their own lie onto themselves by telling it. **"and how those ˹gods˺ they fabricated will fail them"** means that what they used to coin has vanished, absent from before them, useless, and those they worshipped on the strength of a claim leave them to their plight. The word for fabrication here opens two readings. One takes it as the partners they invented in the mortal world, whose unreality was exposed on the Day of Resurrection. The other takes it as the false oath they swear on that Day, the fabrication that their own limbs then uncover. A further reading connects it instead with the story they used to tell about their objects of worship in this world, for example the claim that "we do not worship them in order to make them gods for us, but so that they may intercede for us with Allah and bring us closer to Him" (39:3). That fabrication, too, was exposed on the Day, because nobody interceded for them in their hour of trial and nobody got their punishment reduced one step.

### Are the idols there or not

The verse appears to say that at the time of this question and answer the false objects of worship will not be present. But the Qur'an also says: "gather those who did wrong and what they used to worship" (37:22), which sounds as though they will be there. The answer is that their absence, in the sense this verse gives, means only that they are unavailable, as partners, as sharers, or as intercessors. They will be present, and will be unable to help the people who backed them in any way whatsoever, and so there is no contradiction between the two verses. It is also possible that everything is gathered at one time and in one place first, and then separated, and the question asked after the separation.

### Why a lie cannot be given up

Verse 24 carries a warning about a habit. These people were given free speech on the Day of Judgement, and they used it to deny their shirk with a false oath. There is a hint in that, and the hint is that lying is an evil habit and never easy to shake off. The same mouths that swore falsely in front of the believers in this world could not stop themselves on the day that disgraced them in front of the whole creation. That is why lying is condemned hard in Qur'an and hadith, with serious warnings attached, and why liars are cursed at many places in the Book. The Prophet ﷺ said: stay away from lying, because a lie is the partner of sin, and a lie and a sin both go to Jahannam. Ibn Ḥibbān recorded that in his Ṣaḥīḥ. He was once asked which deed takes a person to Hell, and he said: a lie, and this is in the Musnad of Aḥmad. On the night of the Ascent into heaven, the Miʿrāj, the Prophet ﷺ saw a man whose mouth was being split open at the corners, which then closed and returned to how they were, and then were split open again, on and on, all through the Day of Qiyamah. He asked the Angel Jibrāʾīl al-Amīn who it was, and the answer came: he is a liar. In another narration in the Musnad of Aḥmad he said that a person does not become a complete Muslim until he abandons lying altogether, to the point that he does not lie even as a joke or for fun. Al-Baihaqī and others report, with sound authority, that the character of a Muslim may carry other faults, but not breach of trust and not lying. And a further hadith says that lying cuts a person's provision.

### In short

- The verse tells the Prophet ﷺ to watch the lie collapse onto the heads of the liars.
- "What they used to coin" can mean the idols, the invented oath, or the excuse of intercession quoted from 39:3.
- The partners are present on that Day and absent where it counts, which settles the surface tension with 37:22.
- Lying is described as a habit that survives even the Day of Judgement, and it is condemned in the Qur'an, in hadith, and in the vision of the Ascent.
- A lie also costs a person his provision, which is a worldly penalty on top of the next one.

<<<VERSE 6:25>>>
"There are some of them who ˹pretend to˺ listen to your recitation ˹of the Quran˺, but We have cast veils over their hearts—leaving them unable to comprehend it—and deafness in their ears. Even if they were to see every sign, they still would not believe in them. The disbelievers would ˹even˺ come to argue with you, saying, 'This ˹Quran˺ is nothing but ancient fables!'" The scene moves back from the Day of Judgement to the court of the Prophet ﷺ, where some people sit and listen. The verse explains why the recitation does not reach them, and it insists that the fault is not in the evidence: signs of every kind could be produced and it would change nothing.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There are some of them who ˹pretend to˺ listen to your recitation"** means that they come to hear him recite, and the hearing does them no good. Those named in the reports include They include Abū Sufyān ibn Ḥarb and al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, an-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith and Umayyah ibn Khalaf, ʿUtbah and Shaybah the sons of Rabīʿah, and al-Ḥarth ibn ʿĀmir. Another account gives ʿUtbah and Shaybah sons of Rabīʿah, and Umayyah and Ubayy sons of Khalaf, and says that they listened to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ while he recited, and then asked an-Naḍr about it.

**"but We have cast veils over their hearts"** means covers put on the hearts so that they do not understand the Qur'an, and **"deafness in their ears"** means a heaviness in the ears that keeps them from hearing what would benefit them and from hearing truth and guidance with any willingness to take it in. This covering is the consequence of their own stubbornness and turning away.

**"Even if they were to see every sign, they still would not believe"** means they will not believe in any sign, proof, clear evidence or token they witness, because their comprehension is not sound and their judgment is not fair. The demand that every proof be produced was put by al-Ḥarth ibn ʿĀmir. The example of these people, elsewhere in the Qur'an, is a herd animal being shouted at, which hears nothing but the shouting and the calling (2:171). And: if Allah had known of any good in them, He would have made them listen (8:23).

**"The disbelievers would come to argue with you, saying, 'This is nothing but ancient fables!'"** means that when they come to dispute about what has been revealed in the Qur'an, and he informs them, they say: what Muḥammad brought us was taken from the books of the people before us, that is, copied. An-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith is named as the one who said it. The word for fables, asātīr, is the plural of ustūrah, a tale.

### The man who was asked what the Prophet was saying

Ibn ʿAbbās gave the exchange behind the verse. Abū Sufyān ibn Ḥarb and his companions came to listen, and they said to an-Naḍr: O Abū Quṭaylah, what is Muḥammad saying? He answered: by the One who has made this House, the Kaʿbah, His own, I do not know what he is saying. I only see his lips moving and muttering something. As for the content, it is old wives' tales about nations that came and went, the same sort of story I used to tell you. An-Naḍr talked a great deal about bygone nations, and the people of Makkah, Quraysh among them, would listen to what he told them. It was then that Allah revealed this verse. So the verdict "ancient fables" was the report of a man who had just admitted that he could not hear a word of what was being recited.

### How prejudice finishes the job

In this world of trial a person never lacks openings for pretexts, and he will find a justification for whatever suits him. Some people appear to listen to the Qur'an, but where there is prejudice from the outset, the truth lands on deaf ears and never touches the heart. They raise mental barriers, and those barriers leave them ignorant of their duties even after the duties have been explained to them in detail. No argument moves them, however plainly it is set out. Whatever religious question they allow past the barriers is admitted only so that it can be disputed. Their aim is not to hear and understand anything that would change how they act, and so the shirk stays untouched, so they never lay hold of the side of the question that matters. They are quick to twist a religious matter, and they hunt inside every moral question for facts they can misstate, so that they and their followers can feel they are on the right path.

### In short

- Listening is real and the benefit is nil, because the veils and the heaviness are put there by Allah as the consequence of their own refusal.
- The people named as listeners are leaders of Quraysh, and one of them reported that he could make out nothing except moving lips.
- Producing every sign would not help them, since their problem is comprehension and judgment, not evidence.
- "Ancient fables" is the accusation of the people who were themselves busy with old stories.
- Prejudice builds its own barriers and turns argument into a tool for resisting guidance.

<<<VERSE 6:26>>>
"They turn others away from the Prophet and distance themselves as well. They ruin none but themselves, yet they fail to perceive it." This verse sums up a double manoeuvre. They block other people from the Prophet ﷺ and from the Qur'an, and they stay away themselves, so they combine both evils: they get no benefit and they let nobody else get any. The harm they are doing lands on their own heads, and they do not notice.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They turn others away from the Prophet"** means discouraging people from following the truth, from believing in Muḥammad ﷺ and from obeying the Qur'an. **"and distance themselves as well"** means they themselves keep clear of him, and do not let themselves benefit the way they refuse to let others benefit. **"They ruin none but themselves"** means they destroy only themselves by this action, and its harm touches them alone, **"yet they fail to perceive it"** means the fact does not register with them at all. They also carry the same weight of sin as the people they are warning off.

### Who the verse is about

Early authorities such as ad-Daḥḥāk, Qatādah and Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥanafiyyah took it about the common disbelievers of Makkah, who kept people away from listening to the Qur'an and following it while they stood off from it themselves. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reports the same from Ibn ʿAbbās: they obstruct people from believing in Muḥammad ﷺ. Much the same was relayed from Qatādah, Mujāhid and ad-Daḥḥāk and from several others, and al-Suddī agreed. The disbelievers of Quraysh, on this telling, used to avoid meeting the Prophet ﷺ and used to put people off coming to him.

A second reading takes the pronoun back to the Prophet ﷺ rather than to the Qur'an, and makes the verse about his respected uncle Abū Ṭālib, who stopped people from hurting his nephew and even supported him, but would not believe in the Qur'an or follow it. Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported to have held this, and it was the view of ʿAṭāʾ ibn Dīnār and of al-Qāsim ibn Mukhaymarah, reported through Saʿīd ibn Abī Hilāl, and another reading names Abū Ṭālib as the one who warned people off harming him while not believing in him himself. The report of al-Walībī from Ibn ʿAbbās goes the other way, back to the unbelievers of Makkah.

### The poetry of Abū Ṭālib

Muqātil set the verse in a scene. The Quraysh assembled at Abū Ṭālib's place, meaning to get at the Prophet ﷺ, and he went to his uncle to invite him to Islam. Abū Ṭālib said:

> By Allah, they will not reach you, for all their numbers,
> until I am laid in the ground and buried.
> So carry on with your business, unobstructed,
> and take the good news and a settled mind.
> You have brought a religion which is most certainly
> one of the best religions on the earth.
> Were it not for fear of blame and of insult,
> you would have found me a firm believer in it.

The lie will be uncovered that same Day, in the words quoted under verse 22: they will say that those partners have vanished from us, and that they never called on anything at all before Allah (40:73-74). That, on this account, is what was revealed about: they warn people off and stay off themselves. The harm of the arrangement returns to the one who set it up, though he does not see it.

### In short

- Keeping others away and staying away yourself is described as a single act of self-destruction with two hands.
- One reading names the leaders of Quraysh, who avoided the Prophet ﷺ and blocked people from him.
- Another reading names Abū Ṭālib, who defended him with his power and refused him with his belief.
- The verse's last clause is the sharpest one: they do not even perceive that the ruin they are building is for themselves.
