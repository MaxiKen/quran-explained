<<<VERSE 4:153>>>
The impossible demand: "The People of the Book demand that you ˹O Prophet˺ bring down for them a revelation in writing from heaven" (4:153). The verse sets the demands of the Jews beside the demands their fathers made of Mūsā, and the comparison is not in their favour.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The People of the Book demand that you ˹O Prophet˺ bring down for them a revelation in writing from heaven"** records what the Jewish chiefs asked of the Holy Prophet ﷺ: a book sent down from heaven, all written there, like the Torah that came to Mūsā, and then they would believe him. Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī, as-Suddī and Qatādah say they asked for a book to come down to them from heaven just as the Tawrah was sent down to Mūsā. Ibn Jurayj adds that they asked for books sent down addressed to so-and-so among them, testifying to the truth of what he was sent with. One report names Kaʿb and his followers, and another says they asked for a book containing all that was good for them and all that was bad, their rewards and their punishments. The occasion is reported plainly: they said to the Prophet ﷺ that if he was truly a prophet, he should bring them an actual book from heaven as Mūsā did, and the verse was revealed about them. The demand was a device to drag and stall, born of stubbornness, defiance, rejection and disbelief, for their chronic urge to take an opposite stand made them offer one excuse after another. By revealing the verse, Allah let His Prophet ﷺ see the real state of affairs, giving him comfort and peace of mind against a people used to harassing the prophets of Allah. The disbelievers of Quraysh asked for similar things, as Sūrat al-Isrāʾ relates: "And they say: We shall not believe in this Qur'ān" (17:90).

**"They demanded what is even greater than this from Moses, saying, “Make Allah visible to us!”"** reaches back to the fathers who went further still. Their forefathers said, "O Mūsā! We shall never believe in you until we see Allah plainly" (2:55), demanding with their own eyes a sight of Him, and a thunderbolt from heaven destroyed them for their wickedness, their rejection of Mūsā and their audacity toward Allah. The event is explained in Sūrat al-Baqarah: they were struck by the bolt while they looked, then raised up after their death so that they might be grateful. The asking itself was blameworthy, for they impudently demanded the vision, and there was no excuse for it after the miracles had come to them. They were not seeking the vision for instruction, nor as a means to confirm faith, nor were they driven to it by intense longing. It showed a lack of manners. Even if the system of nature were altered to show them the realities, the display would last only a time, and the freedom that led them to rebelliousness would go on misusing itself as before.

**"So a thunderbolt struck them for their wrongdoing"** — the Fire burnt them, death seizing them as punishment when they harassed Mūsā with the demand.

**"Then they took the calf for worship after receiving clear signs"** — the al-Bayyināt, the tremendous miracles and unequivocal proofs they witnessed at the hand of Mūsā in Egypt, together with the demise of their enemy Firʿawn and his soldiers when they all drowned in the sea. Yet soon after, passing a people who worshipped idols, they said to Mūsā, "Make for us a god as they have gods" (7:138). The story of the calf is told in Sūrat al-Aʿrāf (7) and Sūrat Ṭā Hā (20) after Mūsā went to meet his Lord, and when he returned, Allah decreed that those who had not worshipped the calf should kill those who had, for their repentance to be accepted. They complied, and Allah resurrected them afterwards. Though fully cognizant of clear proofs of Allah being One and free of partners in His Divinity, the Israelites stooped lowest of the low, taking a calf as their object of worship and bypassing their own genuine Creator.

**"Still We forgave them for that ˹after their repentance˺"** — He left them and did not exterminate them, for Allah remained forbearing when the occasion demanded that they be eliminated.

**"and gave Moses compelling proof"** — authority and power, the staff and the white hand, evident sway over them, for when he commanded them to slay themselves in repentance they obeyed him. The unjust, however, always finds an explanation of Allah's signs by which to refute them and continue a life of transgression far from His ways. A prophet is a man among human beings, appearing before the people as a common man, and it is in his utterances that he gives clear arguments proving his status as Allah's messenger. Man's trial demands that he believe without seeing and discover the inner realities, so believing after seeing makes no sense.

### The inward reading of the clear authority

The verse holds two unseemly acts: the demand to be shown Allah, and the worship of the calf after clear signs were manifest. There is an allusion to those content with the calf as their object of worship, for while submitting themselves to it, how can they bear witness to the Real? The innermost selves of those people were untouched by deeper knowledge, so they clung with their intellects to a limited thing and let it be their object of worship. The clear authority given to Mūsā is a manifest proof, indeed a light that preserved him from declaring any likeness to God or stripping Him of His attributes through taʿṭīl. It is actualization and declaring God incomparable, neither stripping Him of His attributes nor comparing Him to His creation. It is said to be the power to hear the divine speech without intermediary, and it is said to belong to this community in the future, in their abiding in the state of meeting God. In the tradition of the vision, the Prophet ﷺ said:

> You will not be gathered together in one group in seeing Him.

### In short

- The demand for a book from heaven was a stalling device, answered by the record of the fathers who demanded to see Allah plainly and were struck by the thunderbolt.
- The calf was worshipped after clear signs in Egypt and the drowning of Firʿawn, yet Allah forgave after their repentance and gave Mūsā compelling proof.
- Sight is not the door of faith, for the trial of man asks for belief without seeing.

<<<VERSE 4:154>>>
The terms of the covenant: "We raised the Mount over them ˹as a warning˺ for ˹breaking˞ their covenant and said, “Enter the gate ˹of Jerusalem˺ with humility.” We also warned them, “Do not break the Sabbath,” and took from them a firm covenant" (4:154). The verse lists the binding commands and the standing pledge.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We raised the Mount over them ˹as a warning˺ for ˹breaking˞ their covenant"** tells of the people who flatly refused to acknowledge the canonical law of the Torah, refraining from implementing its rulings and refusing what Mūsā brought them from Allah. So Allah uprooted the Mount of Ṭūr and fixed it above their heads, suspended over them, and they were ordered to prostrate, which they did, even looking above while prostrating for fear that the mountain might fall on them. The Book recalls it: "When We raised the mountain over them as if it had been a canopy, and they thought that it was going to fall on them. We said: Hold firmly to what We have given you" (7:171). The extraordinary situation was created by God on Mount Ṭūr to take a solemn covenant from the Jews.

**"and said, “Enter the gate ˹of Jerusalem˺ with humility”"** — the gate of the city, of Jericho, and they were to enter Bayt al-Quds while bowing and saying Ḥiṭṭah, meaning: O Allah, take from us our sin of abandoning jihād. They defied the command in word and action, entering the House while crawling on their rear ends and saying ḥinṭah, a wheat grain, in shaʿrah, the hair. That was the cause of their wandering in the desert of Tīh for forty years.

**"We also warned them, “Do not break the Sabbath”"** — the command to honor the Sabbath and honor what Allah prohibited on that day, and one reading of the warning is simply: do not act unjustly. A variant reading of the prohibition is lā taʿaddū, the original tāʾ assimilated with the dāl, close in meaning to lā taʿtadū. The transgression was fishing during it, catching fish on the day of Sabbath, a command from Him that was not to be transgressed.

**"and took from them a firm covenant"** — a strong covenant, regarding Muḥammad ﷺ as one gloss has it. They rebelled, transgressed and committed what Allah prohibited by using deceit and trickery, as Sūrat al-Aʿrāf relates in asking about the town that was by the sea. They contravened every single command one after the other, breaking the solemn pledge with Allah, who had enjoined them to enter their synagogue with humility, pray to God in all humbleness and submissiveness, and keep their struggle for livelihood strictly within the bounds He set. So Allah put disgrace on them in the mortal life of the world, and the worst punishment awaits them in the Hereafter. The same pattern had already run through the earlier verse, from the miracles in Egypt to the calf, from Firʿawn drowned in the sea to the demands of Quraysh in Sūrat al-Isrāʾ, yet the People of the Book had the al-Bayyināt before their eyes and broke every term.

### The signs that did not avail

The more signs were given to them outwardly, the more denial and rejection there was in their hearts, for receiving more communications did not benefit them, since the eyes of their hearts were not open to witnessing them. God most high said:

> But signs and warners do not avail a folk who will not believe. (10:101)

This is the case with all the prophets, and the history of the Israelites furnishes ample proof of it.

### In short

- The Mount was raised over a people refusing the Torah's rulings, and the shadow of it hovered until they accepted the covenant.
- The gate was to be entered bowing with Ḥiṭṭah, but they crawled and mocked, and forty years of wandering followed.
- The Sabbath was broken by fishing, the firm covenant broken by deceit, and signs did not avail hearts that would not see.
