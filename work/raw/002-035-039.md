<<<VERSE 2:35>>>
The story of Ādam (Adam) moves from honour to trial. His superiority had been shown to the angels and acknowledged by them, Iblīs had been condemned for his pride, and now Ādam and his wife received a home. "We cautioned, 'O Adam! Live with your wife in Paradise and eat as freely as you please, but do not approach this tree, or else you will be wrongdoers.'" The verse contains an unlimited permission and a single prohibition. Everything in the Garden was theirs, with one exception, and that exception became the test. Since Iblīs had been disgraced on Ādam's account, he had a score to settle, and the following verse tells how he used this one forbidden thing to bring about their fall.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We cautioned, 'O Adam!'"** is Allah speaking directly to Ādam. Al-Ḥāfiẓ Abū Bakr ibn Mardawayh reported that Abū Dharr asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ whether Ādam was a prophet, and he answered that he was, a prophet and also a messenger, one whom Allah addressed directly, and this verse is an instance of that address.

**"Live with your wife in Paradise"** shows that Ḥawwāʾ (Eve) had already been created before Ādam entered the Garden, as Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq pointed out. He related that after Allah had finished rebuking Iblīs and had taught Ādam the names of everything, Ādam fell asleep. According to the People of the Book and scholars such as Ibn ʿAbbās, Allah took one of the ribs from his left side and caused flesh to grow in its place while he slept, unaware, and from the rib He created Ḥawwāʾ as a woman, so that she might be a comfort to him. When he woke and saw her beside him he is reported to have said, "My flesh and my blood, my wife," and he found rest in her. When Allah had joined them in marriage and given him this comfort, He addressed him with the words of this verse. Some scholars conclude from the order of events that Ādam was created and the angels prostrated to him somewhere outside Paradise, and that he was brought there afterwards. The words of the Qur'an do not rule out the other possibility, that everything happened inside Paradise and that he was simply told later where he was to live.

The wording also carries legal meaning. In granting permission to eat and in forbidding the tree, Allah used the dual form and addressed both husband and wife. But in assigning the dwelling, He did not address them both. He said to Ādam, "Live, you and your wife." From this difference jurists derive two principles: the husband is responsible for providing his wife with a dwelling, and in the matter of residence the wife follows the husband and lives in the house where he lives. The verb "live" (uskun) itself suggests a temporary stay rather than ownership. Allah did not say that Paradise had been given to them, only that they should dwell there, for He knew that events were coming that would require them to leave it. Besides, the right to own a home in Paradise is earned through faith and good deeds and comes only after the Day of Judgment. The jurists have derived from this that if a man invites someone to live in his house, the guest acquires neither ownership of it nor a right to stay permanently.

**"and eat as freely as you please"** is translated from the Arabic raghadan, which describes provision that one does not have to work for and that never runs out or falls short. Their life was wholly free of care. They were allowed to eat of its food without restriction, from anywhere in the Garden and whenever they wished. Because the verb "eat" is in the dual, the wife is not subordinate to her husband in the matter of food. She may eat whatever she needs or likes, just as he may. And because they were allowed to eat "wherever you will," a person has the right to move freely from place to place according to his needs and wishes.

**"but do not approach this tree"** is an emphatic way of saying "do not eat from it." Allah did not want them to eat its fruit, and as a precaution He forbade them even to go near it. From this the jurists derive a basic principle of Islamic law: whatever is likely to serve as a means or instrument of sin is forbidden along with the sin. Some things are not forbidden in themselves, yet when there is a real danger that using them will draw a person into an unlawful act, they too become forbidden.

What was the tree? Neither the Qur'an nor the hadith names it. Some scholars said it was wheat, others the grapevine, barley, the date palm or the fig tree. Some said it was a tree whose fruit relieves the eater of the need to answer the call of nature, and others that it was the tree from which the angels eat so as to live forever. Another description calls it the tree of knowledge, comprising every type and kind. Imām Abū Jaʿfar ibn Jarīr gave what is held to be the correct position: Allah forbade Ādam and his wife a certain tree in Paradise and they ate from it, but we do not know which tree it was, because neither the Qur'an nor the authentic Sunnah tells us. It may have been barley, a grape or a fig, but knowing this brings no benefit, just as not knowing it brings no harm. Ar-Rāzī took the same position, and al-Qurṭubī likewise saw no need to specify what the Qur'an has left unspecified. Even the Bible does not name the tree. The popular idea that the fruit was an apple arose from nothing more than a pun in Latin, where the word malum means both "apple" and "evil."

**"or else you will be wrongdoers"** means transgressors who have overstepped a limit, and also those who harm themselves, for the loss that followed fell on them.

### How could a prophet disobey?

Ādam had been forbidden the tree and warned against his enemy Satan, and yet he ate from it. On the surface this looks like a sin, while the Qur'an, the hadith and reason all establish that prophets are protected from sin. The four great Imāms of law and all reliable scholars agree that every prophet is protected from all sins, major and minor. A few have suggested that prophets are not protected from minor sins, but the majority reject this. The protection is necessary because prophets are sent as guides. If a guide could break Allah's commands and sin, even in small matters, people could no longer trust his word or his example, and if the prophets themselves cannot be trusted, guidance becomes impossible.

The Qur'an does, however, relate a few incidents that appear to show a prophet committing a fault and drawing Allah's displeasure, and Ādam's eating from the tree is one of them. The agreed explanation is that whenever a prophet errs, it is through a misunderstanding or simple forgetfulness, never through deliberate and wilful defiance of a command. A mujtahid, a scholar qualified to work out by analogy the ruling of a case not explicitly covered by the Qur'an or the hadith, is rewarded by Allah for his effort even when his conclusion is mistaken. A prophet's error is always of this kind, or is due to oversight, and is therefore pardonable and not a sin in the technical sense. Moreover, a prophet is under Allah's protection and can never slip or forget in matters that concern his prophetic and legislative function, but only in personal matters. The rank of the prophets is so exalted, however, that even a small oversight in a great man is treated as a great error. That is why such slips are described in the Qur'an as "sins" and meet with Allah's displeasure, although they are not sins in their nature.

As for Ādam's own error, several explanations have been offered. The first is that a particular tree was pointed out to him as forbidden, but the prohibition was meant to cover every tree of that kind, not that one tree alone. The hadith records a comparable case. The Prophet ﷺ once held up a piece of silk and some gold and said that these two were forbidden to the men of his Ummah, and the ban plainly covers silk and gold as such, not merely the pieces in his hand, though someone might imagine otherwise. Ādam supposed that only the one tree shown to him was forbidden. Satan exploited this misunderstanding, swore to him that as a well-wisher he could never advise anything wrong or harmful, and assured him that the forbidden tree was a different one from the tree he was urging him to pick from. The second explanation is that Satan suggested that the prohibition had applied only for a period after Ādam's creation, as infants are kept from heavy food until they grow, and that now that Ādam was stronger the ban had been lifted. The third is that when Satan told him that eating the fruit would guarantee him eternal bliss in Paradise, Ādam simply forgot the prohibition, and the Qur'an lends weight to this when it says, "And indeed, We once made a covenant with Adam, but he forgot, and so We did not find determination in him" (20:115). Whichever explanation is right, the essential point stands: Ādam did not wilfully disobey Allah. What he did was an oversight, or the kind of mistake a mujtahid can make. It was not properly a sin, but because of his closeness to Allah and his rank as a prophet even this lapse was treated as serious and called a sin, and when he repented and asked pardon, Allah forgave him.

### The two paths

When Allah made Ādam stand before the angels and Iblīs and tested them all with the command to bow, He was giving the first man a practical demonstration of the two paths that would lie open to his descendants. They could follow the example of the angels and bow to God's command even when it meant bowing to someone they considered beneath them, or they could be proud like Satan and refuse. This is the test every human being faces throughout life. On earth a person is constantly presented with the same two alternatives: to bow before truth and justice in all he does, as the angels bowed, or to let arrogance and contempt govern him and deny others their due, as Satan did. The forbidden tree then shows the second half of the lesson: how a person goes astray by letting Satan deceive him into crossing the limits God has set.

### A reading for the inner life

The Sufis have preserved several encounters with Iblīs that turn on this verse. It is said that Iblīs once met Ādam and told him: understand that your face has been made white and mine black, but do not let that delude you. We are like an almond tree planted by a gardener. The tree bears fruit, the fruit goes to the grocer, and some of it is sold to a happy customer and some to a grieving one. The grieving man blackens the almonds and scatters them on the coffin of his dead, while the happy man mixes them with sugar and hands them out white in his joy. I am the black almonds scattered over coffins, and the almonds given out in happiness are the work of your good fortune. But remember that the gardener is one, and we drank from the same stream. Whoever falls among the roses will smell roses, and whoever falls into the gardener's thorns will have his eyes pierced by them.

Dhu'l-Nūn al-Miṣrī said that out in the desert he came upon Iblīs, whose head had not risen from prostration in forty days. He said to him, "Poor wretch, after rejection and the curse, what is all this worship?" Iblīs answered, "O Dhu'l-Nūn, I may have been dismissed from servanthood, but He has not been dismissed from lordship."

Sahl ibn ʿAbdallāh at-Tustarī said that he came across Iblīs one day and said, "I seek refuge in God from you." Iblīs replied, "O Sahl, if you seek refuge in God from me, I seek refuge in God from God. If you say you seek help against the hand of Iblīs, I say I seek help against the hand of the All-Merciful." Sahl asked him why he had not prostrated to Ādam, and Iblīs said, "Leave these foolish words. If I have a road to the Presence, show it to me. Do you want to pin the blame on me? Just now I was at Ādam's grave. I made a thousand prostrations there and rubbed the dust of his grave on my eyes, and in the end I heard the call: 'Do not take the trouble. We do not want you.'" Then he handed Sahl a piece of writing and vanished as Sahl began to read it. On it was written: "Though I erred, destiny did not. Blame me if you wish, Sahl, or leave me be."

Abū Yazīd al-Basṭāmī said that he asked God to show him Iblīs and found him in the sanctuary at Makkah, speaking clever words. He said, "Wretch, with all this cleverness, why did you hold back from the command of the Real?" Iblīs said, "That was a command of trial, not a command of desire. Had it been a command of desire, I would never have held back." Abū Yazīd asked whether it was opposition to the Real that had brought him to this. Iblīs said, "Opposition is one opposite set against another, but God has no opposite. Conformity is one like beside another like, but God has no like. Do you imagine that my conformity with Him comes from me and my opposition from me? Both come from Him, and no one has any power over Him. And despite all that has happened, I hope for mercy, for He has said, 'My mercy encompasses everything' (7:156), and I am a thing." Abū Yazīd objected that the verse goes on to make godfearing a condition. Iblīs said, "Conditions are for someone who does not know how things will turn out. Nothing is hidden from this Lord." Then he disappeared. These stories are not told to excuse Iblīs. They are told to show that worship without submission to Allah's choice is worth nothing, and that no one should rest on his own obedience.

### In short

- Allah settled Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ in Paradise with free and effortless provision and forbade them a single tree, which neither the Qur'an nor the Sunnah identifies and which there is no benefit in identifying.
- The wording yields legal principles: the husband provides the home and the wife lives where he lives, the wife eats as freely as the husband, people may move about freely, and whatever leads to sin is forbidden like the sin.
- Prophets are protected from sin. Ādam's eating was a misunderstanding or forgetfulness (20:115) exploited by Satan's oath, not wilful disobedience, and it is called a sin only because of his exalted rank.
- The prostration and the tree together set out the two paths before every human being: bow to Allah's command like the angels, or refuse like Satan.

The test did not last. "But Satan deceived them, leading to their fall from the blissful state they were in, and We said, 'Descend from the heavens to the earth as enemies to each other. You will find in the earth a residence and provision for your appointed stay.'" Satan kept whispering to them until they ate from the tree they had been forbidden, and the life of effortless ease came to an end. They were sent down to the earth, warned that enmity would spoil life there, and told that their stay on earth would itself be temporary.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But Satan deceived them, leading to their fall"** translates fa-azallahumā, "caused them to slip." The Arabic allows two readings of "from it." It may mean "from Paradise," so that Satan caused them to be removed from it, which is how ʿĀṣim ibn Abī an-Najūd recited the verse, and there is a variant reading, fa-azālahumā, that means exactly that, "he removed them from it." Or it may refer to the tree, in which case the verse means that he tripped them by means of it, as al-Ḥasan and Qatādah explained. The sense then is "because of the tree," just as the Qur'an says elsewhere that a person is "turned away from it" (51:9), meaning turned aside from the truth by some cause. Satan accomplished this by asking them whether he should show them the tree of immortality and a kingdom that never fades, and by swearing to them by God that he was giving them sincere advice, "I am truly your sincere advisor" (7:21), so they ate from it.

The choice of the word "slip" is itself significant. It shows that the error of Ādam (Adam) and Ḥawwāʾ (Eve) was not the kind of disobedience that technically counts as a sin. It arose from a misunderstanding that Satan engineered. They ate because they had been cleverly deceived.

**"from the blissful state they were in"** refers to the clothing, the spacious dwelling and the comfortable provision they had enjoyed. The Qur'an says that Satan "brought them out" of this state, but in fact they left under a divine command. Because Satan served as the means and the intermediary, the action is attributed to him. His role gives him no independent power whatsoever, and any notion of two rival powers in the universe is ruled out completely.

**"and We said, 'Descend from the heavens to the earth as enemies to each other'"** is addressed to Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ and all who would come from them, and on some accounts also to the serpent and the peacock. Who exactly is meant by "enemies to each other" depends on whether Satan was still in the heavens. If he had not yet been expelled from the skies, he is included in the address, and the meaning is that the enmity between Satan on one side and Ādam, Ḥawwāʾ and their descendants on the other would continue on the earth. If, as some scholars hold, he had already been cast out, then the address is to Ādam, Ḥawwāʾ and their progeny, and the implication is a double punishment: banishment from Paradise and the pain of seeing enmity arise among their children through their wronging one another, which no parent can bear easily.

**"You will find in the earth a residence and provision for your appointed stay"** promises a dwelling place, a livelihood from the earth's produce, and a limited span of life, until death and until the Day of Resurrection. The earth was to be a temporary dwelling that they would also have to leave, which meant that they would never find complete peace of mind there.

### Iblīs and Shayṭān

In the previous episode the Qur'an called him Iblīs, a name from the root balasa, "to be disappointed," meaning one who has lost all hope of Allah's grace. Here he is called ash-Shayṭān, from the root shaṭana, "to be far away," meaning one who has been removed far from Allah's mercy. Iblīs is a personal name. Shayṭān names a kind. When the Qur'an says ash-Shayṭān with the definite article it always means Iblīs, but the common noun shayṭān, and its plural shayāṭīn, covers the whole kind, which includes both human beings and jinn. It is also possible that the word is connected with the root shayṭ, which means an excess of anger and rage.

### How did Satan reach them?

If the Paradise from which Ādam was expelled was in the heavens, as the majority of scholars hold, how could Iblīs enter it after Allah had already expelled him for refusing to prostrate? The majority answer that Satan was indeed barred from Paradise but that there were times when he slipped in secretly. The Tawrāh, for example, says that Iblīs hid inside the mouth of the serpent and entered that way. Some scholars suggest that he misled Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ while he was on his way out of Paradise. Others, including az-Zamakhsharī, say that he was already on the earth and tempted them while they were still in heaven. He may never have met them at all, but planted the suggestion in their minds from a distance, something Satan is always able to do and of which hypnotism offers a faint example. Or, being one of the jinn, who have been given certain powers denied to human beings, he may have taken the form of a serpent or of something else and so got in. Perhaps it was because of this disguise that Ādam did not recall Allah's warning that Satan was his enemy. Since Satan swore to them that he was their well-wisher, it seems that he really did meet them and speak with them face to face. Al-Qurṭubī gathered at this point several useful hadiths about snakes and the ruling on killing them.

### Reports about the descent

Ibn Abī Ḥātim narrated that Ubayy ibn Kaʿb reported the Messenger of Allah ﷺ saying that Allah created Ādam tall, with thick hair, like a date palm heavy with branches. When he ate from the tree his covering fell away and the first thing exposed was his nakedness. Seeing it, he ran through Paradise until his hair caught in a tree, and as he struggled to free himself the Most Merciful called to him, "Ādam, are you running away from Me?" Hearing the words of the Most Merciful, he said, "No, my Lord, but I am ashamed."

Al-Ḥākim recorded that Ibn ʿAbbās said that Ādam lived in Paradise only for the stretch of time between the ʿAṣr prayer and sunset, and al-Ḥākim judged this report ṣaḥīḥ by the standard of al-Bukhārī and Muslim, though they did not include it. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded Ibn ʿAbbās saying that the place where Allah set Ādam down was called Dahnā and lay between Makkah and aṭ-Ṭāʾif. According to al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Ādam came down in India and Ḥawwāʾ at Jeddah, that Iblīs came down at Dustumaysān, some miles from Basra, and that the serpent came down at Aṣbahān. Muslim and an-Nasāʾī recorded that Abū Hurayrah reported the Messenger of Allah ﷺ saying that of all the days on which the sun rises, Friday is the best: Ādam was created on it, admitted to Paradise on it, and sent out of it on it.

### A reading for the inner life

Consider the wonder of it. First Allah caressed His servant and put his small affairs in order. Then He threw him into turmoil, undid what He had arranged for him, and rebuked him on top of it. The Pīr of the Ṭarīqah prayed: O God, You show Your friends to their enemies and hand the poor over to heartache and grief. You make them ill and then nurse them Yourself. You make them helpless and then heal them Yourself. You made Ādam from dust and then dealt so beautifully with him. You wrote his happiness at the top of the ledger and made him Your guest in Paradise. You seated him in the garden of approval and made a pact with him not to eat the wheat, while hiding in Your unseen knowledge that he would eat it. Then You put him in prison and made him weep for years. Your all-compelling power does what compellers do and Your lordship does what lords do, and all Your rebukes and all Your war are aimed at Your friends.

The Pīr was asked whether Ādam was more complete in this world or in Paradise. He answered that he was more complete in this world, because in Paradise he came under suspicion through his own self, while in this world he came under suspicion through passion. And he warned against thinking that Ādam was taken out of Paradise because of any lowliness in him. The real cause was how grand his aspiration was. Passion's petitioner came knocking at the door of Ādam's breast and said: the beauty of meaning has been unveiled, and you are still sitting in the abode of ease. Ādam saw an infinite beauty beside which the beauty of the eight paradises was nothing, and his high aspiration tightened its belt and said: if you are ever going to fall in love, it must be with that. Then came the command: Ādam, you have stepped into passion's street now, so leave Paradise, which is the house of ease.

### In short

- Satan made them slip, either out of Paradise or by means of the tree, through a whisper about immortality and a false oath of goodwill (7:21). The word "slip" shows that their fault was a deception-induced error, not a wilful sin.
- They were sent down with the warning of mutual enmity, whether between Satan and humanity or among human beings themselves, and told that the earth would be a temporary home with provision until death.
- Scholars differ on how Satan reached them in a Paradise he had been expelled from, whether by stealth, through the serpent, from a distance or on his way out.
- Reports describe Ādam's height, the brevity of his stay, the places where each of them came down, and Friday as the day of his creation, admission and departure.

A long line of early scholars identify the words with the prayer recorded in Sūrat al-Aʿrāf. Similar reports come from al-ʿAwfī, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Saʿīd ibn Maʿbad on the authority of Ibn ʿAbbās, and al-Ḥākim recorded this report in his Mustadrak from Ibn Jubayr from Ibn ʿAbbās, stating that its chain is ṣaḥīḥ although al-Bukhārī and Muslim did not record it.

**"Surely He is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful"** gives the reason. Allah forgives whoever regrets his error and returns to Him. He overlooks misdeeds and shows mercy to everyone who dies repentant. The same promise runs through the Qur'an: "Do they not know that Allah alone accepts the repentance of His servants and receives their charity, and that Allah alone is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful" (9:104), and, "Whoever commits evil or wrongs themselves then seeks Allah's forgiveness will certainly find Allah All-Forgiving, Most Merciful" (4:110), and, "And whoever repents and does good has truly turned to Allah properly" (25:71). These verses testify that Allah forgives the sins of all who repent, out of His kindness and mercy toward His creatures. There is no god but Allah, the Most Forgiving, the Most Merciful.

### A reading for the inner life

Notice that Allah speaks here with the lid on. He says that Ādam received words, but He does not disclose what passed between them, so that the secrets of love would not spill into the open and love's story would stay concealed. A poet once said to his beloved, "Halt," and she answered only, "Qāf," the first letter of the word. She did not say "I have halted," which would have given the secret away to the watchers, nor "I will not halt," which would have wounded the lover's heart. Though the ordinary language of commentary does not say so, the people of allusion suggest that the words were like what lovers say at parting: when you leave me, do not forget my covenant, and even if your awareness of me should fade one day, do not prefer another to me. O Ādam, keep Our compact in mind, and choose no one over Me. The tongue of Ādam's state replied: my heart travels with You and keeps You company, so where else could its love attach? When You are a heart's spirit and its very awareness, how could that heart forget to remember You?

### Phrase by phrase

**"We said, 'Descend all of you!'"** repeats the command of verse 36 in order to attach to it what follows about guidance. The address includes Ādam, Ḥawwāʾ and, on some accounts, the serpent, the peacock and Satan, and through Ādam it reaches his whole progeny. Allah had already announced that He would send down Books and raise prophets and messengers for his descendants.

**"Then when guidance comes to you from Me"** promises that guidance will come whenever and as often as Allah sends it. Abū al-ʿĀliyah said that al-hudā, the guidance, means the prophets, the messengers, the clear signs and the plain explanation. In short, it is a Book and a prophet.

**"whoever follows it"** means whoever accepts what is in Allah's Books and what His messengers bring, believing in Him and acting in obedience to Him.

**"there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve"** is explained in complementary ways. They will have no fear about the Hereafter and no grief about what they left behind in this world. Or they will have no fear of any torment to come and no grief over what has passed. Some say the negation is perpetual: no fear will ever touch them once death itself is slain, and they will never grieve once Hell is shut. The parallel passage in Sūrat Ṭā Hā says, "Then when guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows My guidance will neither go astray in this life nor suffer in the next" (20:123), which Ibn ʿAbbās explained as: he will not be misguided in this world nor miserable in the Hereafter. Those who follow the guidance will be admitted to Paradise.

### Two rewards that cover everything

The verse promises two things to those who follow divine guidance: no fear and no grief. Fear is the anxiety felt in anticipating some future trouble or pain. Grief is the sorrow felt at losing something valuable or failing to obtain something desired. Between them these two rewards cover every possible form of comfort and peace, since all distress is either dread of what may come or sorrow over what has gone.

The Arabic makes a subtle distinction between the two. For fear it uses a noun in a general statement, literally "no fear upon them." For grief it uses a verb with a pronoun as its subject, literally "they will not grieve." The implication is that complete freedom from every sense of loss is possible only for the friends of Allah, the awliyāʾ, who follow divine guidance in all its details. For everyone else, emperor or billionaire, grief at the loss of something valued or the frustration of a desire is unavoidable, being part of the very scheme of things. The friends of Allah need not grieve because they have annihilated their own desires and their very will in total submission to the will of Allah. The Qur'an tells us that the people of Paradise will thank Allah for taking away all regret and sorrow: "Praise be to Allah, Who has kept away from us all causes of sorrow" (35:34). Some measure of sorrow is therefore inevitable for every human being except those who have perfected and secured their relationship with Allah. The word "saints" is only a weak approximation of awliyāʾ Allāh, "the friends of Allah," a concept that bears only a faint resemblance to the Christian idea of a saint, which is why "friends of Allah" or "men of Allah" is the better rendering.

It should be made clear that the grief denied to the friends of Allah is grief over worldly losses and frustrated worldly desires. In anxiety about the next world, in fear of Allah and in deep awe before His glory, they are far ahead of everyone else. The Prophet ﷺ himself often appeared worried and deep in thought, not from fear of any worldly loss or trouble, but out of concern for his Ummah and awe before the divine majesty. Nor does the verse mean that prophets and the friends of Allah do not feel the instinctive human fear that frightening things arouse. The Qur'an itself relates that when the staff of Mūsā (Moses) became a serpent, "Moses concealed fear within himself" (20:67). That was an instinctive, physical fear, and the incident belongs to the earliest days of his prophethood, for when Allah said "Do not be afraid," the fear vanished altogether. It can also be explained differently: his fear did not come, as it does with ordinary people, from expecting harm from the serpent, but from the possibility that so extraordinary an event might lead the Israelites into misguidance. On that reading the fear was not worldly at all but concerned the next world.

### A reading for the inner life

Bad manners on the carpet get a guest shown the door. When Ādam misbehaved at the fountain of nearness, Allah said, "Descend, some of you enemies to others, and in the earth is a dwelling for you," after Ādam had enjoyed a place of nearness as his dwelling and his enjoyment for a time. They had lived in ease, and in the end they returned to want, and when they became poor they clung to poverty, hoping for reward, and even when things were easy again they hurried back to that state. Yet at the very moment Allah expelled Ādam from the Garden and sent him down to the earth, He gave him the good news that He would restore him to his former condition if his heart inclined to return. That good news is this verse: "when guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows it, there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve."

### In short

- After the pardon the descent was repeated, no longer as punishment but as the honoured mission of vicegerency accompanied by the promise of guidance, meaning Books and prophets.
- Whoever follows that guidance is promised freedom from fear about the future and grief over the past, the two forms that all distress takes, as 20:123 confirms.
- Total freedom from grief belongs only to the friends of Allah, who have surrendered their own will. Their fear and awe of Allah, and even instinctive human fear like that of Mūsā (20:67), are not what the verse denies.

<<<VERSE 2:39>>>
The promise of the previous verse has a counterpart. "But those who disbelieve and deny Our signs will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever." The guidance that was promised to the children of Ādam (Adam) divides them into two groups. Those who follow it have neither fear nor grief, and those who reject it have the Fire without end. With this verse the first address to humanity, delivered at the moment of its descent to the earth, is complete.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But those who disbelieve and deny Our signs"** means those who reject the guidance when it comes, refusing to believe in Allah's Books and calling His scriptures and His messengers liars. The "signs" are His revelations and proofs, the Book and the prophet sent with it. Allah does not show Himself plainly in this world. He reveals Himself through His signs, and in this way He tests His creatures. Only those who are able to read His signs will find Him, and those who deny the signs have failed the test for which they were sent to the earth. The one who brings the message of truth stands before people as Ādam stood before the angels, and whoever is too proud to acknowledge him is following in Satan's footsteps.

**"will be the residents of the Fire"** describes them as the rightful owners of Hell, its proper inhabitants, the people to whom it belongs as a home belongs to those who live in it.

**"They will be there forever"** means that they will remain in Hell perpetually, never dying and never being released, and will find no way out of it. The parallel verse in Sūrat Ṭā Hā puts the same warning in terms of this life as well as the next: "But whoever turns away from My Reminder will certainly have a miserable life, then We will raise them up blind on the Day of Judgment" (20:124), and that misery stands opposite the security promised to those who follow the guidance (20:123). The pairing in these two verses, fear and grief removed for the believer and the Fire made permanent for the denier, is the pattern that the rest of the Qur'an will fill in.

### Favours without gratitude

Those who receive Allah's favours without gratitude, and who pay no attention to affirming the truth (taṣdīq) and realising it in themselves (taḥqīq), will have a painful punishment deferred until the end, and a separation from Allah that is already upon them in the here and now. The disbeliever's loss, in other words, does not wait for the Hereafter. The Fire is the final form of a distance that began the moment the signs were denied.

### In short

- Those who disbelieve and deny Allah's signs, His Books and His messengers, are the people of the Fire and will remain there forever, neither dying nor leaving.
- Allah tests people through His signs rather than by appearing openly. To reject the signs is to fail the test, as 20:124 confirms with its promise of a miserable life and a blind resurrection.
- Ingratitude for favours brings punishment later and separation from Allah now.
