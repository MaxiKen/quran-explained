<<<VERSE 2:40>>>
With this verse the surah turns to a new audience. "O children of Israel! Remember My favours upon you. Fulfil your covenant and I will fulfil Mine, and stand in awe of Me alone." Up to this point Sūrat al-Baqarah has spoken about the Qur'an itself, warned the open deniers and the hypocrites, called everyone to worship Allah alone, presented the Qur'an as an inimitable miracle, and told the story of Ādam (Adam) to show Allah's power and wisdom. Now it addresses the People of the Book directly, and it continues to do so, with the Jews especially in view, all the way to verse 123. The tone at the start is friendly and persuasive: a reminder of noble ancestry, a list of blessings, a call to honour an old agreement, and a warning to fear no one but Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O children of Israel!"** means the descendants of Yaʿqūb (Jacob), for Israel is his second name. Abū Dāwūd aṭ-Ṭayālisī recorded that ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās said that a group of Jews came to the Prophet ﷺ, and he asked them, "Do you know that Israel is Yaʿqūb?" They said, "Yes, by Allah," and he said, "O Allah, bear witness." Aṭ-Ṭabarī recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās that the word Israel means "the servant of Allah." It is a Hebrew word. Some scholars have observed that among the prophets only the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ has several names, apart from Yaʿqūb, who has two, Yaʿqūb and Isrāʾīl. The Qur'an addresses the Jews here not as the children of Yaʿqūb but as the children of Israel, so that the very title reminds them that they are the children of "the servant of Allah" and should follow their father in worshipping and obeying Allah alone. The address is a way of saying: O children of a pious, righteous servant of Allah who obeyed Him, be like your father and follow the truth, much as one might say, "Son of a generous man, be generous," or, "Son of a scholar, seek knowledge." The Qur'an uses the same device when it says, "O descendants of those We carried with Noah in the Ark! He was indeed a grateful servant" (17:3). The address "O children of Israel" is repeated at the beginning and near the end of the long passage that follows, a familiar rhetorical means of making an appeal persuasive. The passage opens briefly, with four verses inviting them to Islam and three urging good deeds, before the long and detailed address begins.

**"Remember My favours upon you"** calls them to gratitude. Mujāhid said that Allah's favour to the Jews was that He made water gush from the rock, sent down manna and quails, and saved them from slavery under Pharaoh. Abū al-ʿĀliyah said the favour is that prophets and messengers were raised among them and Books were revealed to them. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq reported that Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as Allah's support for them and their forefathers in rescuing them from Pharaoh and his people. Others list the Scripture and the messenger, the escape from Pharaoh and from drowning, the parting of the sea, the clouds sent to shade them, and the honey and quails. The verse resembles what Mūsā (Moses) said to his people: "O my people! Remember Allah's favours upon you when He raised prophets from among you, made you sovereign, and gave you what He had never given anyone in the world" (5:20), meaning anyone of their own time. The favours were bestowed on their forefathers, but the descendants inherited them, and the right response is gratitude expressed through obedience.

**"Fulfil your covenant and I will fulfil Mine"** speaks of an agreement with two sides. The covenant that Allah took from them concerned the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ: that when he was sent they would believe in him and follow him, and that in return Allah would grant them what He had promised and lift from their necks the chains and burdens laid on them because of their errors. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Qatādah and Mujāhid identified the covenant with the one described in Sūrat al-Māʾidah, which the Tawrāh also records: "Allah made a covenant with the Children of Israel and appointed twelve leaders from among them and then said, 'I am truly with you. If you establish prayer, pay alms-tax, believe in My messengers, support them, and lend to Allah a good loan, I will certainly forgive your sins and admit you into Gardens under which rivers flow'" (5:12). That covenant mentions prayer and alms, but its most important clause is faith in all of Allah's messengers, which includes the Prophet ﷺ, and so, according to Ibn ʿAbbās as cited by Ibn Jarīr, the covenant here means believing in the Prophet ﷺ and obeying him. Other scholars identify the covenant with a pledge recorded in the Tawrāh itself: Allah would send a great prophet from the descendants of Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) whom all peoples would obey, and that whoever obeyed him would have his sins forgiven, enter Paradise and receive a double reward. Ar-Rāzī gathered several of the announcements made by earlier prophets about the coming of Muḥammad ﷺ. Abū al-ʿĀliyah put it more generally: Allah's covenant with His servants is that they embrace Islam and hold to it. As for Allah's side, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported that Ibn ʿAbbās said "I will fulfil Mine" means "I will be pleased with you and admit you to Paradise," and as-Suddī, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, Abū al-ʿĀliyah and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said the same. The verse quoted from Sūrat al-Māʾidah makes this clear: Allah forgives the sins of those who keep the covenant and admits them to Paradise. It also includes His promises of a good life in this world and a beautiful reward on the Day of Judgment.

**"and stand in awe of Me alone"** means fear Me and no one else. Ibn ʿAbbās said it means: fear the punishment I may send upon you as I sent it upon your forefathers, such as the transformation into apes. The verse thus begins with encouragement and ends with warning, in the hope that they would come back to the truth, follow the Messenger ﷺ, obey what the Qur'an commands and forbids, and believe in it. The warning had a particular point. The scholars of the Israelites were afraid that if they spoke the truth about the Prophet ﷺ they would go against popular feeling and lose both their followers and their income, so these verses urge them to speak the truth without fear, since Allah alone deserves to be feared.

### Who the People of the Book were

In the time of the Prophet ﷺ the disbelievers and hypocrites fell into two kinds. On one side were the idolaters, who had no religious learning, were mostly unlettered, and followed the customs of their forefathers, such as the people of Makkah in general, whom the Qur'an calls the ummiyyūn. On the other side were those who believed in the earlier prophets, knew the earlier revealed Books such as the Tawrāh and the Injīl, and were regarded as educated. Some of them followed Mūsā but did not accept ʿĪsā (Jesus) as a prophet, and these were the Jews. Others followed ʿĪsā but did not believe that Mūsā, as a prophet, was protected from sin, and these were the Christians. Because they believed in the Tawrāh (Torah) or the Injīl or both, these two groups are called in the Qur'an Ahl al-Kitāb, the People of the Book. Being educated, they were respected and trusted by those around them and their opinion carried great weight. If they came to the straight path, others could be expected to follow. The Jews were the dominant group in Madīnah and its surroundings, and since Sūrat al-Baqarah is a Madīnan surah, after dealing with the idolaters it addresses the People of the Book in a special way.

The greatest favour God grants a people is to send them His prophet and so open the road to eternal salvation, and before the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ, the last of the prophets, this favour had been the privilege of the Children of Israel. In time, however, their religion ceased to be a matter of conscious conviction and deteriorated into a set of lifeless rituals handed down from one generation to the next. The coming of the Prophet ﷺ brought the reality to light. Those whose feeling for the truth was still alive recognised his prophethood and followed him, while those whose religion had become merely inherited found his message strange and rejected it. Given the clear prophecies in the Tawrāh of an Arab prophet to come, recognising him should not have been difficult, but believing in him was against their worldly interests. Over centuries a religious establishment had grown up in which they held pride of place, honoured as the heirs of prophets and holy men, looked to for spiritual leadership, and supported by tributes and offerings all year round. It seemed to them that accepting the Arab Prophet would end their privileged position and demolish the whole structure of their livelihood.

### Rulings and lessons

Al-Qurṭubī observed that when Allah asks the Israelites to worship and obey Him, He reminds them of the blessings He has showered on them, whereas He asks the followers of the Prophet ﷺ to do so without mentioning His blessings at all: "remember Me; I will remember you" (2:152). This is a subtle sign of the superiority of this Ummah. The Muslim community has a direct relationship with Allah. It begins by recognising the Benefactor, and through knowing Him comes to recognise His bounties. Other peoples begin with the bounties and only through them reach a knowledge of the Benefactor.

The verse also shows that keeping an agreement one has entered into is obligatory and breaking a promise is forbidden, as another verse states outright: "O believers! Honour your obligations" (5:1). A hadith reported by Muslim adds that promise-breakers, before their final punishment in the next world, will be humiliated before all humanity when it is gathered on the Day of Judgment, for a flag will be planted as a mark of shame beside each one who committed this sin, and the greater the betrayal, the higher the flag.

### A reading for the inner life

The verse alludes to the Real's gentleness, generosity and loving kindness toward His servants. He reminds them of His favour as if to say: I am the generous Lord, the grateful, the forgiver of servants. I have come forward with kindness in the face of every disloyalty, I invite My servants to praise Me in spite of all their offences, and I ask them to be grateful for My blessings. So He says to the Children of Israel: remember My blessings, be grateful for them and recognise your duty toward Me, so that you may deserve increase and enjoy a good name and good fortune.

The Children of Israel and this community were addressed very differently. To them He said, "Remember My blessings." To this community He said, "Remember Me" (2:152). To them He gave blessings, and to this community He gave companionship. The one group He kept at a distance from Himself through the witnessing of blessings, and the other He kept close to Himself by stipulating love. As a lover might say: I travelled to You seeking the highest things, while others travelled seeking a comfortable life. The Pīr of the Ṭarīqah prayed: O God, the one who really works is the one whose work is with You, and the one who really has a friend is the one whose friend is like You. How could anyone who has You in both worlds set You aside? The wonder is that the one who has You weeps more than anyone. The one who has not found weeps at not finding, so why does the one who finds weep too? And yet, when someone has a friend like You, any complaint from him is a disgrace.

"Fulfil your covenant and I will fulfil Mine" belongs to a family of verses in which Allah answers the servant's step with His own. "Call upon Me, I will respond to you" (40:60). "Remember Me; I will remember you" (2:152). It is as if He said: My servant, you open one door and I will open another. Open the door of turning back and I will open the door of good news, for those who turn to Allah "will have good news" (39:17). Open the door of spending and I will open the door of replacement, for "whatever you spend in charity, He will compensate you for it" (34:39). Open the door of striving and I will open the door of guidance, for "those who struggle in Our cause, We will surely guide them along Our Way" (29:69). Open the door of seeking forgiveness and I will open the door of forgiving, for whoever "seeks Allah's forgiveness will certainly find Allah All-Forgiving, Most Merciful" (4:110). Open the door of gratitude and I will open the door of increase, for "if you are grateful, I will certainly give you more" (14:7). My servant, come back to My covenant and I will come back to yours.

God's covenants with His servant are said to be many, and whenever the servant is loyal in one of them, the Lord of the Worlds answers with a loyalty of His own. The first is that the servant pronounces the words of the shahādah, and its counterpart from the Lord of the Worlds is the protection of his life and property, as in His words that whoever says "there is no god but God" has placed his wealth and his property under My protection. At the final stage, the servant guards both thoughts and gaze. Allah answers with what He has prepared for the righteous: gifts beyond anything seen by an eye, heard by an ear or imagined in a human heart. Between that beginning and this end lie many intermediate covenants. In each of them the servant contributes deeds and words, and God contributes a reward beyond reckoning. One of the people of this path put some of them this way: keep My covenant by presence at the gate, and I will keep yours with great reward. Keep My covenant by guarding the secrets, and I will keep yours with My beautiful kindnesses. Keep My covenant by preferring no other to Me, and I will keep yours by never holding back My gentleness and good. Keep My covenant with beautiful striving, and I will keep yours with constant contemplation.

### In short

- The surah now addresses the Jews of Madīnah as "children of Israel," the children of Yaʿqūb, whose name means "servant of Allah," reminding them of their ancestry so that they might follow their father in obedience.
- The favours are the rescue from Pharaoh, the parted sea, the shading clouds, manna and quails, water from the rock, and above all prophets and Books (compare 5:20).
- The covenant is the pledge of 5:12 to believe in and support Allah's messengers, which binds them to believe in the Prophet ﷺ, in return for forgiveness and Paradise. Keeping agreements is obligatory (5:1).
- Allah addresses this Ummah with "remember Me" (2:152) rather than "remember My favours," a sign of its direct relationship with Him, and every step the servant takes toward Allah is answered by a step from Him.

<<<VERSE 2:41>>>
Having reminded the Children of Israel of their covenant, the verse now spells out what it requires of them in the present. "Believe in My revelations which confirm your Scriptures. Do not be the first to deny them or trade them for a fleeting gain. And be mindful of Me." Four commands follow one another: believe in the Qur'an, do not lead the way in rejecting it, do not sell the truth for worldly profit, and keep your duty to Allah. Each of them answers a temptation that the Jewish scholars of Madīnah actually faced.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Believe in My revelations which confirm your Scriptures"** refers to the Qur'an, which Allah sent down to Muḥammad ﷺ, the unlettered Arab prophet, through Jibrīl (Gabriel), as a bringer of good news, a warner and a light. It contains the truth from Allah and confirms what was revealed before it in the Tawrāh and the Injīl: the Oneness of Allah, prophethood, the description and traits of Muḥammad ﷺ, and some of the prescribed laws. Abū al-ʿĀliyah explained it as: O People of the Book, believe in what I have sent down that agrees with what you have, for they found the description of Muḥammad ﷺ written in the Tawrāh and the Injīl. Mujāhid, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and Qatādah said the same. Since the Qur'an was sent to confirm the essential teachings of the Tawrāh, the covenant itself obliged the Israelites to believe in it. What the Qur'an confirms about the Tawrāh and the Injīl is that they are Books of Allah as originally revealed. The distortions introduced into them over time are no part of the original texts, so no question arises of the Qur'an confirming those additions.

**"Do not be the first to deny them"** is explained by Ibn ʿAbbās as: do not be the first to disbelieve in the Qur'an and in Muḥammad ﷺ when you know more about him than anyone else. Abū al-ʿĀliyah said: do not be the first of the People of the Book to disbelieve in Muḥammad ﷺ after hearing that he has been sent, and al-Ḥasan, as-Suddī and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said likewise. Ibn Jarīr held that "them" refers to the Qur'an mentioned at the start of the verse. Both views are correct, because the two are bound together: whoever disbelieves in the Qur'an has disbelieved in Muḥammad ﷺ, and whoever disbelieves in Muḥammad ﷺ has disbelieved in the Qur'an. "The first" means the first among the Children of Israel, for there were people of Quraysh and of the Arabs generally who rejected the Prophet ﷺ before the People of the Book did. The verse speaks of the Children of Israel in particular because the Jews of Madīnah were the first of them to be addressed by the Qur'an, so their rejection would make them the first of the People of the Book to reject it.

Disbelief is the gravest sin whether one is the first to commit it or the last, so why single out being first? Because the first to deny bears not only the sin of his own denial but the additional burden of misleading everyone who follows his example, and so earns a multiplied punishment. Those who come after will lean on those who went before and the leaders will carry the followers' sins. Do not, in other words, establish disbelief as a continuing practice, for the responsibility of the one who starts a practice is greater than that of the one who merely imitates it. From this a general rule follows: whoever is in any way responsible for others falling into sin shares the burden of their sin, and whoever helps others toward a good deed shares in its reward. Many verses of the Qur'an and many hadiths repeat this point.

**"or trade them for a fleeting gain"** means: do not exchange faith in My signs and belief in My Prophet for the life of this world and its pleasures, which are small and bound to end. The signs in question are especially the passages describing Muḥammad ﷺ in their Book. Do not suppress them for fear of losing what you hope to earn from the lowly people among you, whether income, position or leadership. Do not prefer your cheap worldly share to the grandeur of Allah's truth. The Arabic word āyāt means both "signs" and "verses," and in context the prohibition is plain: no one may take people's money for twisting or hiding the verses of Allah's Book to please them or to serve their worldly interests. On this there is complete consensus in the Ummah.

**"And be mindful of Me"** means have taqwā of Me, fear Me and no one else in this matter. Ibn Abī Ḥātim reported that Ṭalq ibn Ḥabīb defined taqwā as acting in obedience to Allah, by a light from Allah, hoping for Allah's mercy, and avoiding disobedience to Allah, by a light from Allah, fearing Allah's punishment. Here the command warns the People of the Book against deliberately hiding the truth, spreading its opposite, and opposing the Messenger ﷺ.

### Wages for teaching the Qur'an

The verse does not deal with the question of accepting a wage for teaching the Qur'an or reporting its verses correctly, but the question is important in its own right and the jurists differ on it. Imām Mālik, Imām al-Shāfiʿī and Imām Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal consider such wages permissible. Imām Abū Ḥanīfah and some other jurists held them impermissible, because the Prophet ﷺ forbade using the Qur'an as a means of livelihood. Circumstances later changed radically. In earlier times those who taught the Qur'an received a living allowance from the Bayt al-Māl, the public treasury of the Islamic state. When Islamic society fell into disorder, through Western imperialism among other factors, they lost that support. Teaching the Qur'an to children is a full-time occupation, and the teachers could not take up other demanding work without breaking the essential chain by which the Word of Allah is passed from one generation to the next. For this reason the Ḥanafī jurists ruled that accepting wages for teaching the Qur'an is permissible, and according to the Hidāyah, the well-known manual of Ḥanafī law, this is the ruling to be given in these times. Later jurists extended the permission to related duties such as leading the prayer, calling the adhān, and teaching hadith and fiqh, since these are connected with teaching the Qur'an and the survival of Islam depends on them equally. This is set out in ad-Durr al-Mukhtār and in ash-Shāmī's commentary on it.

Ash-Shāmī, the famous Ḥanafī scholar, explained at length in that commentary and in his own book Shifāʾ al-ʿAlīl that the later jurists allowed wages for teaching the Qur'an only because of an essential religious need, without which the whole Islamic order would be disturbed, and that the permission is therefore limited to such needs. It follows that paying or receiving wages for reciting the Qur'an in order to pass on the reward to the dead, or for some worldly purpose, remains forbidden, since it meets no essential religious need. Both the reciter who takes such wages and the person who pays them commit a sin, and since the recitation earns no merit, there is nothing to transfer to the dead. Ash-Shāmī refers to many authoritative works, including Tāj ash-Sharīʿah, al-ʿAynī's commentary on the Hidāyah, and the marginal notes of Khayr al-Dīn al-Ramalī on al-Baḥr ar-Rāʾiq, and cites al-Ramalī in particular to the effect that paying for the recitation of the Qur'an beside a grave or elsewhere in order to transmit its reward to the dead was never reported from the Companions, their successors or the great scholars of the early centuries, and is therefore an innovation (bidʿah) in religion.

### A reading for the inner life

The belief that this verse calls for is of more than one kind. The generality of believers believe on the basis of reasoned proof, inferring the truth by argument. The elite believe through clear evidence, having been drawn near, for the Real has drawn close to them and so they believe in Him. And the final stage of belief is direct witnessing, which belongs to the elect of the elect. As for the closing words, those who fear Allah's punishment are many, but those who stand in awe of Him through knowing and seeing Him are rare.

### In short

- The Children of Israel are commanded to believe in the Qur'an, which confirms the Tawrāh and the Injīl as originally revealed, including their descriptions of the Prophet ﷺ.
- They must not be the first of the People of the Book to reject it, because whoever leads others into sin carries their sins as well as his own, just as whoever leads others to good shares their reward.
- Concealing or twisting Allah's verses for money, position or popularity is forbidden by consensus, and the verse warns them to fear Allah rather than their followers.
- The separate question of wages for teaching the Qur'an divides the jurists, with the Ḥanafīs permitting it out of necessity while forbidding paid recitation for the dead as an innovation.
