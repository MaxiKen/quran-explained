<<<VERSE 7:59>>>
The chapter now leaves the general argument and begins the stories of the prophets. "Indeed, We sent Noah to his people. He said, “O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him. I truly fear for you the torment of a tremendous Day.”" The section opens the eighth ruku of the surah, and from here to nearly the end the subject is the messengers and the communities they were sent to.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We sent Noah to his people"** marks the beginning of the stories. After Allah had mentioned the story of Adam at the start of this surah, He began to mention the prophets, one after another, and He began with Nuh, since he was the first messenger sent to the people of the earth after Adam.

His line is given in the books that record descent. He was Nuh bin Lamak bin Matushalakh bin Khanukh, and Khanukh, as they say, was the prophet Idris. Idris was the first person to write letters with the pen, and the descent runs from him through Barad and Mahlil, then Qanin, Yanish and Shith, and so to Adam, peace be upon them all. That descent is given by Muhammad bin Ishaq and by others of the imams who document lineage.

The beginning of the story is placed in the generations before him. Allah had mentioned the story of Adam first, and the decades after Adam are described in the reports as a long stretch in which the descendants held to the oneness of God. In a tradition from Abdullah ibn Abbas, the change began with the dead. When righteous people among them died, their people built places of worship over their graves and made images of them, so that the memory of their righteousness and their devotion would stay fresh and they could imitate them. As time passed they made statues of them, and later still they worshipped those idols, naming them after the righteous dead. The names given are Wadd, Suwa', Yaghuth, Ya'uq and Nasr. When this practice had spread and grown, Allah sent Nuh as a messenger, and it was through his mission that the new world was populated after the flood, which is why he is sometimes called the junior Adam.

The verse proves that his mission was to his own people and not to all the world. They lived in Iraq. They were outwardly civilised, and they were caught up in shirk. Disbelief as a public matter did not exist before them, since Adam was a prophet but his time was not the time for confrontation with error, and most of the injunctions given to him concerned the settling of the land and the management of human needs. The confrontation with kufr and with shirk began with Nuh. In terms of the mission of prophethood and of a code of laws, he is the first messenger of Allah, and his struggle with a crooked people lasted nine hundred and fifty years, at the end of which all but a few of them were drowned.

**"O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him"** is the call itself, and it is built of three parts. The first invites them to worship Allah, which is the essence of the matter. The second persuades them to leave off shirk and kufr, which had spread among them like an epidemic. The third warns them, as the next words do.

**"I truly fear for you the torment of a tremendous Day"** is the warning. The Day is the Day of Resurrection, and the torment spoken of is what awaits them if they meet Allah while they are joining others to Him. The words may also take in the punishment of this world, which for them came as the flood.

### How the story is placed in the surah

The surah up to this point has dealt with three things, the oneness of God, the messengership, and the life to come, which are the basic principles of the religion. They were proved and affirmed in many ways, people were urged to follow them, and they were warned against taking a stand against them. Then, as a natural sequel, the tricks of Satan that lead people away were described. What follows now is a description of some of the prophets and their communities, and it runs for about fourteen sections.

The pattern of those sections is the same in each. The messengers invited their communities to the same universal principles and to the same articles of faith. They gave good news of reward to those who believed and warned of punishment and a sad end for those who refused. Out of that come hundreds of rulings, major and minor, by way of corollary. The communities of the present are given the chance to learn from the end of the communities of the past, and for the Prophet ﷺ himself the record became a comfort, since this is how matters had gone with all the prophets before him.

The date of Nuh in relation to the other early figures is given in several reports. He is in the eighth generation of Adam. In a narration of Ibn Abbas that appears in the Mustadrak of Hakim there is an interval of ten generations between Adam and Nuh, and the same has been reported by Tabarani on the basis of a hadith of the Prophet ﷺ narrated by Abu Dharr. A generation is generally counted as a hundred years, which makes a thousand years between the two. Ibn Jarir reported that the birth of Nuh came eight hundred and twenty six years after Adam died. The Book states plainly that he reached the age of nine hundred and fifty, and a hadith gives the age of Adam as nine hundred and sixty. On those figures the span from the birth of Adam to the death of Nuh comes to two thousand eight hundred and fifty six years. Some narrations give his real name as Shakir, others as Sakan, and still others as Abd al-Ghaffar. Whether his time came before or after Idris is a matter of debate, and the majority of the Companions held that Nuh came before Idris, as is recorded in Al-Bahr Al-Muhit. A narration of Ibn Abbas in the Mustadrak of Hakim has the Prophet ﷺ saying that Nuh was given prophethood at the age of forty and lived for sixty years after the flood.

### What is drawn from the community and its end

The reason for their refusal is given plainly in the Book. It was very hard for them to accept that a man who looked like them had been chosen to deliver God's message on His behalf. Compared with the great figures of the past, whose religion they thought they were following and whose greatness history had settled, Nuh was a man of their own time with no glory attached to his name, and he looked ordinary to them. So they did not accept him, and they did not hesitate to call the prophet of their age foolish and misguided, since in their view he had turned away from the way of their great ones.

None of the things that should have moved them did so. His good will towards them, the strength of his arguments, his reasoning, and his firmness on the path of truth all left them where they were. Once he had conveyed the message in full and they had rejected it, the community was drowned in the great flood. The reason for the punishment was that they had called the signs of God false and denied them. What they wanted was for God's message to reach them through some established figure rather than an ordinary man. In the sight of God that was blindness. He has given human beings insight so that they may recognise the truth in the shape of signs, and not through something they can hold. Those who cannot do that are blind in the sight of God although their eyes are open, and such people have no share in His grace.

### In short

- Nuh was the first messenger sent to the people of the earth after Adam, and his call was the call of every prophet after him.
- The first idol worship began when images and places of worship were put up over the graves of righteous people, and the idols were named after them.
- His people were civilised in outward ways and sunk in shirk, and they were warned of the torment of the Day of Resurrection, and of the punishment of this world as well.
- Nuh is the junior Adam in the sense that the world was populated again through those who were saved with him.
- The stories that begin here all carry the same articles of faith, and they are set out so that later communities may take warning from the end of earlier ones.

<<<VERSE 7:60>>>
The first answer to the call comes from the men who held power among the people. "But the chiefs of his people said, “We surely see that you are clearly misguided.”" The word used for them means the chiefs, the headmen and the empowered elders of a community.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But the chiefs of his people said"** names the speakers. They are the leading men, and alongside them the general public and the great ones of the people are included by the same term in other readings of the verse.

**"We surely see that you are clearly misguided"** is the charge. What they meant by it was that he was trying to pull them away from the faith of their forefathers, and that the ideas he brought about rising on a last day and being rewarded or punished were nothing but superstition.

That answer carries a mark of its own. It is the manner of evil people to call the righteous misguided, as the Book will say of other communities in more than one place, since the call to abandon idols that their forefathers had worshipped strikes at everything they hold. The Book records the same attitude elsewhere. It says of one set of people that when they saw the believers they said that these had surely gone astray (83:32), and it records of another set that when they were not guided by the Qur'an they called it an ancient lie (46:11). Other verses carry the same theme.

It was also said of this community that the charge of foolishness and misguidance was not their only objection, since they had a further doubt about the man himself. That doubt is set out in Surah Al-Mu'minun, and it belongs with the answer given in the verses that follow.

### In short

- The leaders of the people were the first to answer, and their answer was an accusation rather than an argument.
- They charged Nuh with clear error, meaning that he had left the religion of their forefathers.
- Calling the righteous misguided is the habit of those who are themselves in the wrong, and the Book notes the same habit in other communities.

<<<VERSE 7:61>>>
The charge is answered without heat. "He replied, “O my people! I am not misguided! But I am a messenger from the Lord of all worlds"".

### Phrase by phrase

**"O my people! I am not misguided"** begins the reply with an address that keeps the bond intact. The word he denies is the more general of the two words for error, which makes the denial wider in its reach than a denial of the narrower word would have been.

**"But I am a messenger from the Lord of all worlds"** gives the ground of his standing. Nuh is not bound by the customs of his people, which rest on ignorance. What he brings them comes from the Lord of all that exists, the Lord and King of all things, and everything he tells them is under the guidance of his Lord.

### Answering a charge without heat

The way the answer is put is itself a lesson. The words carry the diction that belongs to a prophet. What he leaves behind is a trail for everyone who carries the call, for preachers and for reformers. He does not answer anger with anger. He takes the doubts they have and removes them in the simplest words he can find. His mission is to convey what Allah has sent him with, and what he conveys is for their good and not for any benefit of Allah and not for any personal interest of his own.

The phrase about the Lord of all the worlds also strikes at the practice of shirk, that is to say at the joining of any partner to Him. When the sense of that phrase is understood, no god or goddess of any kind stands as a valid object of worship. Their doubts about the punishment of the last day came from a lack of awareness on their part, and he, by contrast, had been given certainty about it.

The further doubt of the community is set out in Al-Mu'minun. There they argued that he was only a human being, a man like themselves who ate and drank and slept and woke as they did. They asked how they could take such a man as their leader, and said that if Allah had wanted to send them a message He should have sent angels with it, whose greatness would have impressed them. From where they stood, all they saw was one of their own community who wanted to set himself above them (23:24).

### In short

- The reply denies the charge and gives the reason for his mission in the same breath.
- He speaks to them as his people, and answers the accusation without matching it in temper.
- The phrase about the Lord of all the worlds by itself removes any partner from worship.
- The second line of objection, that he was only a man like them, is answered in the verses that follow and is set out at length in Al-Mu'minun.

<<<VERSE 7:62>>>
He then describes what the mission consists of. "conveying to you my Lord’s messages and giving you ˹sincere˺ advice. And I know from Allah what you do not know".

### Phrase by phrase

**"conveying to you my Lord’s messages"** is the first part of a messenger's work. What he passes on are the messages of his Lord, with the commands and the prohibitions of his Lord explained in them.

**"and giving you sincere advice"** is the second part. The counsel of Nuh is meant for their good, and he warns them of the torment and calls them to repentance and to faith.

**"And I know from Allah what you do not know"** is the third part. He knows from Allah what they do not know of the torment that waits for them if they do not believe, and that knowledge came to him by revelation.

### The testimony of the gathering at Arafah

Those three things are the description of a messenger, since he conveys the message in plain and eloquent words, offers sincere advice, and knows about Allah. Nobody else can compete with the prophets in that combination. To mark it, the Prophet ﷺ put a question to his Companions at Arafah, at the largest gathering of theirs there ever was. He asked them what they would say when they were asked about him. They answered that they would testify that he had conveyed the message and delivered it and given sincere advice. He then raised his finger towards the sky and brought it down towards them, saying, O Allah, bear witness, O Allah, bear witness. Muslim recorded that report in his collection.

The reply also settles the doubts about the last day. Their uncertainty came from a want of awareness, while he had been given certainty on the matter. In the same breath his call is cleared of any personal interest. The mission was for their good alone, and it served no benefit of Allah and no advantage of his own.

### In short

- The work of the messenger is summarised in three parts, the conveying of the messages, sincere counsel, and knowledge of Allah.
- The Prophet ﷺ asked his Companions on the Day of Arafah what they would testify about him, and called Allah to witness their answer, and that report is in the collection of Muslim.
- The doubts the people held about the last day came from their own ignorance, since the messenger had been given certainty about it.
- He sought nothing for himself, and everything he brought was for the good of the people he was sent to.

<<<VERSE 7:63>>>
He puts the wonder back to them as a question. "Do you find it astonishing that a reminder should come to you from your Lord through one of your own, warning you, so you may beware and perhaps be shown mercy?”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do you find it astonishing that a reminder should come to you from your Lord"** takes up their surprise directly. The answer is that the matter is not surprising at all. That Allah sends down revelation to a man among them is mercy, kindness and compassion towards them, since the warning is meant to save them from the torment, and that is why the reminder comes.

**"through one of your own"** touches the real objection, which was that the messenger was a man from among them. The point is worked out at length. Allah is the Absolute Master, and giving someone the rank of prophet or messenger is His own affair. He does it when He wills, and no one can have it otherwise. Beyond that, a little thought shows that the purpose for which a prophet is sent to guide human beings can be achieved only through a human being, and that this is a mission on which angels cannot be sent.

### Why the messenger had to be one of them

The reason is what prophethood is for. Its aim is to bring people to believe in Allah and obey Him, and to save them from the hard end that follows from opposing what He has laid down. That can only happen when someone of their own kind stands before them as a working model of the conduct asked of them, and shows them that it is possible to worship Allah and keep His injunctions while still living a human life with all its needs and desires. If angels had brought the call and set their own model before people, the answer would have been easy. Angels have no human desires, they do not go hungry or thirsty, they do not sleep and they do not tire, so how could people ever be like them? But when one of their own kind, with all the ordinary human traits and desires, walks among them as a perfect model of obedience, no excuse is left to them.

**"warning you, so you may beware and perhaps be shown mercy"** gives the purpose of the whole arrangement, that is to say the reason why a warner is sent at all. A warner whose warning can move people has to be one of their own kind, a human being with human qualities. So the answer here covers every community that raised the same doubt about the human character of its messenger. It is a matter for regret that in spite of the clarifications the Book gives, some people go so far as to deny the humanness of the Prophet ﷺ himself. The trouble with the ignorant is that they will not accept the superiority of anyone from their own kind, and so they nurse a grudge against the friends of God and the scholars of their own time and look down on them for being their contemporaries. That has always been the trademark of the ignorant.

Their other objection, that they were being asked to follow a man and not an angel, has been answered along the same lines. In Al-Mu'minun the doubt is given in their own words, and it is the doubt of a community that could not see how Nuh, a man like themselves, could be raised above them (23:24).

### In short

- The wonder they felt is turned back on them, since sending a man among them is a mercy rather than a strange thing.
- The rank of prophethood is given by Allah to whom He wills, and no one can take it or refuse it.
- A human messenger is the only kind who can serve as an example for human beings, since an angel's example would leave people an easy excuse.
- The warning comes so that the people may guard themselves and find mercy.
- The same refusal to accept a contemporary as a leader has been the habit of the ignorant in every age.

<<<VERSE 7:64>>>
The story closes with the response of the people and the end that came on them. "But they rejected him, so We saved him and those with him in the Ark, and drowned those who rejected Our signs. They were certainly a blind people".

### Phrase by phrase

**"But they rejected him"** records what the people did with all of that. They held to their rejection and their opposition, and only a few of them believed.

**"so We saved him and with him those in the Ark"** is the deliverance. When the flood came, the believers were kept from drowning and from the torment by being brought into the ship. The story in full, with the account of the drowning of the people, is given in Surah Hud and in Surah Nuh, and what appears here is the gist of it as the occasion requires.

**"and drowned those who rejected Our signs"** is the judgement. Their drowning is put down to the fact that they called the signs of God false. The Book says of them elsewhere that because of their sins they were drowned and then admitted into the Fire, and that they found none to help them instead of Allah (71:25).

**"They were certainly a blind people"** is the verdict on them. The blindness spoken of is blindness to the truth, since they could neither recognise it nor find the way to it.

The timing of the punishment carries a lesson. One of the early teachers said that when the flood overtook the people of Nuh they were at the peak of their numbers and their power, and the lands and mountains of Iraq were becoming too small for their growing population. It is the standing practice of Allah to give the disobedient time, and then to send the punishment at the moment when they have reached the highest point of their numbers, their power and their wealth, and have grown drunk with their own condition.

In this way the enemies were paid back, and the messenger was saved with those who believed in him, while the men who opposed him and denied him were destroyed. The Book says elsewhere that He will indeed make His messengers victorious (40:51). That is Allah's Sunnah with His servants, in this life and in the Hereafter, that the good end, the victory and the triumph belong to those who are mindful of Him.

The numbers of those who were saved are given in the reports. Eighty men were saved with Nuh in the ship, and one of them was Jurhum, who spoke Arabic. That statement came through Ibn Wahb on the authority of Ibn Abbas, and Ibn Abi Hatim collected it. It was also narrated with a continuous chain reaching Ibn Abbas. Some narrations say that the Ark held forty men and forty women. After the flood, the place in Mosul in Iraq where they came to settle became known as Thamanun, which means eighty.

### What the story of Nuh contributes

The brief account placed here carries three lessons. The first is that the call of all the prophets and their basic articles of faith were one and the same. The second is that Allah supports His appointed messengers in ways that are full of wonder, so that Nuh was in no danger to his safety even when a flood had risen as high as the peaks of the mountains around him. The third is that calling the prophets of Allah false amounts to inviting the punishment of Allah. That warning still stands. The communities of the past were overtaken because they treated the messengers as liars, and the same end can overtake those who take the same road now. The better course is not to become heedless and free of fear of that.

One more thing is marked by the closing words. The people wanted the message to be brought to them through some great and established figure rather than an ordinary man. In the sight of God that was blindness, since He has given human beings insight so that they may know the truth in the shape of signs, and not through anything they can see and touch. Whoever cannot do that is blind in the sight of God even with his eyes open, and such a person has no share in the grace of God.

### In short

- They held to their rejection, and only a few of their people believed.
- The believers were saved in the Ark, and those who called the signs of God false were drowned and then brought into the Fire.
- Their blindness was blindness to the truth, since they could not recognise it or find their way to it.
- The punishment came when the community was at the height of its numbers and power, which is the way of Allah with those who persist in denial.
- The account is placed here to show that the call of all the prophets was one, that Allah guards His messengers, and that treating them as liars brings down the punishment of Allah.
