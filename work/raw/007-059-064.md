<<<VERSE 7:59>>>
The surah now opens a long series of prophets' stories, beginning with Nūḥ (Noah): "Indeed, We sent Noah to his people. He said, “O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him. I truly fear for you the torment of a tremendous Day.”" From its start up to this point, the surah has set out the basic principles of Islam: tawḥīd (Allah's oneness), risālah (messengership) and the Hereafter. It has proved them in different ways, urged people to follow them and warned against opposing them, and shown how Shayṭān leads people astray. After Allah told the story of Ādam (Adam) at the beginning, He now tells the stories of the prophets in order, from the first to the later ones.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We sent Noah to his people."** The Arabic word laqad, "indeed", answers an oath that is left unstated. The verse shows that his mission was to his own people alone, not to all mankind. They lived in Iraq. They were outwardly civilised but sunk in shirk, associating partners with Allah. Allah sent him to call them to Allah's oneness and to give up worshipping anything besides Him.

**"He said, “O my people! Worship Allah"**: worship Allah alone and declare His oneness. This first sentence is the call to worship Allah, the heart of the whole principle.

**"you have no other god except Him."** You have no true god but the One I am calling you to. This second sentence urges them to give up shirk and kufr, which had spread among them like an epidemic. In the Arabic, "other" can be read ghayrihi, describing "god", or ghayruhu, standing in its place.

**"I truly fear for you the torment of a tremendous Day.”"** I fear you will be struck by it if you do not believe and keep worshipping others besides Him. He meant the punishment of the Day of Resurrection, should you come before Allah still joining partners with Him. This third sentence warns of the great punishment they would face if they refused. It could mean the punishment of the Hereafter, and also the punishment of the Flood in this world.

### Why does the series of prophets begin with Nūḥ?

From the eighth section (rukūʿ) almost to the end of the surah comes the account of several prophets and their peoples, about fourteen sections in all. Its subject is the shared principles of tawḥīd, messengership and the Hereafter. Every prophet called his people to these universal principles. He gave good news of reward to those who believed, and warned of punishment and a bitter end for those who refused. Hundreds of basic and secondary rulings come up along the way. Later peoples could learn from the fate of earlier ones. For the Prophet ﷺ it was a comfort to know that the other prophets had met the same treatment.

The first prophet in the chain was Ādam (Adam), but his was not a time of struggle against disbelief and error. Most of the laws given to him concerned settling the earth and managing human needs, and disbelief and disbelievers did not yet exist. The struggle against kufr and shirk began with Nūḥ. In terms of the prophetic mission and a code of law, he was Allah's first rasūl, the first messenger to the inhabitants of the earth after Ādam. Also, after the great Flood drowned the world, the only survivors were Nūḥ and those with him in the Ark, and the new world was peopled through them. That is why he is also called the second Ādam. So he comes first in this account, which tells of his nine hundred and fifty years of struggle against the crooked ways of most of his people. In the end all but a few believers were drowned.

### Who was Nūḥ, and when did he live?

His lineage is given as Nūḥ son of Lamak, son of Matūshalakh, son of Khanūkh. Khanūkh, as they claim, was the prophet Idrīs (Enoch), the first person to write with a pen. Idrīs's own line runs back through Barad, Mahlīl, Qanīn, Yānish and Shīth to Ādam. Muhammad ibn Isḥāq and other experts in genealogy recorded this lineage. Whether Nūḥ came before or after Idrīs is debated. Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ reports that most of the Companions held that Nūḥ came before Idrīs.

Nūḥ was in the eighth generation after Ādam. Al-Ḥākim's Mustadrak reports from Ibn ʿAbbās that ten qarn separated Ādam and Nūḥ. Al-Ṭabarānī reports the same from a hadith of the Prophet ﷺ narrated by Abū Dharr. A qarn generally means a hundred years, so by this narration the gap was a thousand years. Ibn Jarīr reported that Nūḥ was born eight hundred and twenty-six years after Ādam's death. The Qur'an states plainly that Nūḥ lived nine hundred and fifty years, and a hadith says Ādam lived nine hundred and sixty. That makes a total of two thousand eight hundred and fifty-six years from Ādam's birth to Nūḥ's death. Some narrations give Nūḥ's real name as Shākir, others as Sakan, and others as ʿAbd al-Ghaffār. Al-Ḥākim's Mustadrak also reports from Ibn ʿAbbās that the Prophet ﷺ said Nūḥ was made a prophet at forty and lived sixty years after the Flood.

### How did idol worship begin?

For about a thousand years after Ādam, his descendants held to the belief in Allah's oneness. Then, as ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās and several other scholars of tafsīr explained, some righteous men died. Their people built places of worship over their graves and made images of them, to remember their righteousness and devotion and to copy them. As time passed they made statues of them, and later worshipped those idols, naming them after the righteous men: Wadd, Suwāʿ, Yaghūth, Yaʿūq and Nasr. Gradually these great men took on the status of gods among the people of ancient Iraq. When this evil had spread so far, Allah sent Nūḥ as His messenger to reform them, and all praise is due to Him.

### In short

- After Ādam's story, the surah begins the stories of the prophets with Nūḥ, the first messenger sent to confront shirk and the "second Ādam" through whom the world was repeopled.
- Idol worship began when people made images of righteous men to remember them, and later worshipped them.
- Nūḥ called his people in Iraq to worship Allah alone, to give up shirk, and to fear the punishment of a tremendous Day.
- He lived nine hundred and fifty years. Accounts give about a thousand years between Ādam and Nūḥ.

<<<VERSE 7:60>>>
The leading men of his people answered him with scorn: "But the chiefs of his people said, “We surely see that you are clearly misguided.”" The word for chiefs, malaʾ, means the headmen, nobles and powerful elders of a community.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But the chiefs of his people said"**: the leaders, notables, commanders and great men of his people, the respected elders of their council, spoke for the rest.

**"“We surely see that you are clearly misguided.”"** We think you are plainly wrong in what you say, O Nūḥ (Noah). They said this because he called them to give up the idols they had found their forefathers worshipping. In effect they told him that he was trying to pull them away from their ancestral faith. They added that his talk of rising on a Last Day to be rewarded or punished was mere superstition.

### Why do the wicked call the righteous misguided?

This is the way of evil people. They see the righteous as the ones who have gone astray. Allah says elsewhere, "And when they saw the faithful, they would say, “These ˹people˺ are truly astray,”" (83:32). He also says:

> "The disbelievers say of the believers, “Had it been ˹something˺ good, they would not have beaten us to it.” Now since they reject its guidance, they will say, “˹This is˺ an ancient fabrication!”" (46:11).

There are other verses on the same theme. Nūḥ's people did not hesitate to call the prophet of their time foolish and misguided. In their eyes he had left the religion of their great forefathers.

### In short

- The chiefs of Nūḥ's people accused him of plain error for calling them away from their ancestors' idols.
- They also dismissed his warnings of resurrection and judgement as superstition.
- Evil people have always seen the righteous as the ones who are astray.

<<<VERSE 7:61>>>
Their words were harsh, but Nūḥ (Noah) answered them as a prophet should: "He replied, “O my people! I am not misguided! But I am a messenger from the Lord of all worlds". He did not meet anger with anger. He tried to clear away their doubts in the simplest words, leaving a model for everyone who calls others to the truth, every preacher and reformer.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He replied, “O my people! I am not misguided!"** Nūḥ told the leaders of his people that there was nothing wrong with him, and no foolishness in him. He was not in error, as they claimed. The Arabic word ḍalālah here is more general than aḍ-ḍalāl, "misguidance". So denying it denies more: not even a trace of error is in him.

**"But I am a messenger from the Lord of all worlds"**: I am rightly guided by my Lord, and I am His messenger to you, the messenger of the Lord and King of all things. He is my Lord, your Lord and the Lord of all creation.

### What does "Lord of all worlds" answer?

Nūḥ was saying that their charge of error was false. Admittedly, he did not follow the customs of their forefathers, which were rooted in ignorance. Instead he had been sent as a messenger from the Lord of all the worlds. That one phrase strikes decisively at shirk. Once its meaning is grasped, no god or goddess of any kind can stand as a rightful object of worship.

### In short

- Nūḥ answered his people's insults calmly, a model for everyone who calls others to the truth.
- He denied even the smallest trace of error, and declared himself a messenger of the Lord of all worlds.
- Calling Allah "Lord of all worlds" leaves no room for any other object of worship.

<<<VERSE 7:62>>>
Nūḥ (Noah) goes on to describe his mission, completing the reply that runs across these two verses (7:61-62): "conveying to you my Lord’s messages and giving you ˹sincere˺ advice. And I know from Allah what you do not know". Everything he told them came under his Lord's guidance. His only task was to deliver Allah's message, which was for their own good. It brought no benefit to Allah and served no interest of his own.

### Phrase by phrase

**"conveying to you my Lord’s messages"**: I deliver only the revelation Allah has sent me with, explaining His commands and prohibitions. The verb can be read ublighukum or uballighukum.

**"and giving you ˹sincere˺ advice."** I want only good for you. I urge you to follow what Allah has commanded and earn its reward. I warn you against what He has forbidden and the punishment for disobeying Him. I call you to repentance and faith.

**"And I know from Allah what you do not know"**: I know, through the revelation Allah has given me, the punishment that awaits you if you do not believe. Your doubts about the punishment of the Last Day come from not knowing, while Allah has given me certainty about it.

### What marks a true messenger?

These are the qualities of a messenger. He conveys the message in plain yet eloquent words, gives sincere advice, and knows Allah. In these no other people can compete with the prophets.

Muslim recorded in his Ṣaḥīḥ that on the Day of ʿArafah, when the gathering was larger than ever, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked his Companions what they would say when they were asked about him. They said they would testify that he had conveyed the message, delivered it and given sincere advice. He then kept raising his finger toward the sky and lowering it toward the people, saying, "O Allah, bear witness. O Allah, bear witness."

### Why did they object that he was only a man?

Nūḥ's people raised another doubt, recorded in Sūrat al-Muʾminūn. To them he was simply a mortal, eating and drinking, sleeping and waking just as they did. How could they take him as their leader? If Allah had wanted to send them a message, He would have sent angels, whose rank and grandeur would have impressed them. All they saw was a man of their own race and people who wanted to lord it over them:

> "But the disbelieving chiefs of his people said ˹to the masses˺, “This is only a human like you, who wants to be superior to you. Had Allah willed, He could have easily sent down angels ˹instead˺. We have never heard of this in ˹the history of˺ our forefathers" (23:24).

The answer to this doubt comes in the next verse.

### In short

- Nūḥ's only task was to deliver Allah's messages and advise his people sincerely, for their good and not his own.
- He knew from revelation what they did not: the punishment awaiting them if they refused to believe.
- Clear delivery, sincere advice and knowledge of Allah are the marks of every messenger. The Prophet ﷺ had his Companions bear witness that he had conveyed the message.
- His people objected that he was only a man and that angels should have been sent.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:63>>>
Nūḥ (Noah) answers his people's surprise that a man like them should bring a message: "Do you find it astonishing that a reminder should come to you from your Lord through one of your own, warning you, so you may beware and perhaps be shown mercy?”" Do not wonder at this. It is nothing odd that Allah should send revelation to a man from among you, out of mercy, kindness and compassion for you.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do you find it astonishing"**: indeed you do find it odd, and in effect you deny it.

**"that a reminder should come to you from your Lord"**: the reminder is prophethood, an admonition, revelation and good teaching from your Lord.

**"through one of your own"**: through the tongue of a man from among you, a human being like you. One explanation adds: a man you know well, who grew up among you, who has never been a liar or misguided, and who has not come to you from some other people.

**"warning you"**: so that he may warn you of punishment if you do not believe.

**"so you may beware"**: so that you may fear Allah, obey Him, and avoid His punishment by worshipping none but Him.

**"and perhaps be shown mercy?”"** So that his warning may lead you to stop opposing him, and you may receive mercy through faith and be spared the torment.

### Why must a messenger be human?

The argument is that it is no cause for surprise if a human being is made Allah's messenger. First, Allah is the Absolute Master. Choosing someone for prophethood or messengership is His alone, and He does it as He wills. No one may dare insist otherwise.

Second, a little thought shows that the purpose of sending a messenger to guide human beings can only be served by a human being. It is a task angels cannot perform. The aim of prophethood is to bring people to believe in and obey Allah, and to save them from the bitter results of defying His commands. That is possible only when someone of their own kind sets before them a living model of that conduct. He must show them that one can worship Allah and keep His commands while still meeting ordinary human needs and desires.

Had angels brought the call and set the example, people could easily have said that angels have no human desires. They never feel hunger or thirst, never sleep and never tire, so how could anyone become like them? But when one of their own kind, with all the usual human traits and desires, walks among them as a perfect model of obedience to Allah, they are left with no excuse. The words "warning you, so you may beware" point to this. The one whose warning can make people fear must be of their kind, a full embodiment of human nature like them.

The disbelievers of many peoples have raised this same doubt, questioning whether a human being could be a prophet or messenger. This is the Qur'an's answer to every such doubt. It is sad that despite so many clear statements in the Qur'an, some people go as far as denying the humanity of the Prophet ﷺ.

### Why do people reject a messenger they know?

The ignorant fail to see this truth. They simply refuse to accept that someone of their own kind could stand above them. That is why they nurse hatred for the friends of Allah (awliyāʾ) and the scholars (ʿulamāʾ) of their own time, and look down on them because they are contemporaries. This has always been the mark of the ignorant.

Nūḥ's people were the same. They found it very hard to accept that a man who seemed just like them had been chosen to deliver Allah's message. They compared him with the great men whose religion they claimed to follow, whose greatness was fixed in the records of history. Beside them, Nūḥ, their contemporary with no glory attached to his name, looked like an ordinary man. His goodwill, his strong arguments, his reasoning and his firm stand on the path of truth did not move them. So they refused to accept him, and they would not take up the way of righteousness and fear of Allah (taqwā). All they could say was that their forefathers knew better.

### In short

- Nūḥ told his people there was nothing odd in a reminder coming from their Lord through a man from among them.
- Choosing a messenger is Allah's right alone. Only a human can show by example that obedience is possible alongside human needs.
- An angel's example would leave people an excuse, and a human's leaves them none.
- The ignorant despise the righteous of their own time and revere only great names from the past, as Nūḥ's people did.

<<<VERSE 7:64>>>
When Nūḥ's (Noah's) fatherly, well-meaning answers made no impression on his heartless people, Allah sent the punishment of the Flood: "But they rejected him, so We saved him and those with him in the Ark, and drowned those who rejected Our signs. They were certainly a blind people". The full story of Nūḥ, the drowning of his people and the rescue of those in the Ark comes in Sūrah Hūd and Sūrah Nūḥ. This verse gives the gist that suits the context here.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But they rejected him"**: they ignored his good counsel completely and did not believe in Allah. They insisted on denying and opposing him, and only a few of them believed, as another verse states. In the end Nūḥ asked Allah to destroy the disbelievers.

**"so We saved him and those with him in the Ark"**: Allah saved Nūḥ and the believers with him from drowning and from the punishment, by letting them take refuge in the Ark (the fulk, the ship).

**"and drowned those who rejected Our signs."** He drowned those who denied His Scripture and His messenger Nūḥ, by means of a great flood, as their punishment. Another verse says, "So because of their sins, they were drowned, then admitted into the Fire. And they found none to help them against Allah" (71:25).

**"They were certainly a blind people"**: blind to the truth, unable to recognise it or find their way to it. Their hearts were blind to guidance, and they disbelieved in Allah.

### When does Allah's punishment come?

Zayd ibn Aslam said that when the Flood overtook Nūḥ's people, they were at the height of their numbers and power. The lands and mountains of Iraq could hardly hold them. It has always been Allah's way to keep giving the disobedient respite. He sends His punishment when they have reached the peak of their numbers, power and wealth, and are drunk, as it were, on their success.

### How many were in the Ark?

Reports differ on how many were in Nūḥ's Ark. According to a report that reached Ibn Wahb, Ibn ʿAbbās put the number rescued with Nūḥ on the ship at eighty men. One of them was called Jurhum, and he spoke Arabic. Ibn Abī Ḥātim collected this, and it is also narrated from Ibn ʿAbbās with a continuous chain. Some narrations say the eighty were made up of forty men and forty women. After the Flood they settled at a place near Mosul in Iraq, which became known as Thamānūn, "Eighty".

### What does Nūḥ's story teach?

Here Allah tells how He took vengeance on His enemies, saved His messenger and those who believed in him, and destroyed their disbelieving enemies. He says elsewhere, "We certainly help Our messengers and the believers, ˹both˺ in this worldly life and on the Day the witnesses will stand forth" (40:51). This is Allah's way (sunnah) with His servants in this life and the next: the good end, victory and triumph belong to those who fear Him.

The brief account of Nūḥ at this point teaches three things.

1. The call of all the earlier prophets, and their basic articles of faith, were one and the same.
2. Allah supports His chosen messengers in astonishing ways. They were in no danger even when surrounded by a flood rising to the mountain peaks.
3. Denying Allah's noble prophets invites His punishment. The warning still stands. As earlier peoples were punished for denying their prophets, a similar fate could overtake their modern counterparts, so they would do well not to grow careless and unafraid.

### What kind of blindness is meant?

When Nūḥ had fully conveyed the divine message and his people rejected it, they were drowned in the great Flood. The reason was that they had denied and belied Allah's signs. They wanted Allah's message to come through an established, celebrated figure, not an "ordinary man". In Allah's eyes that was blindness. Allah gave human beings insight so that they would recognise the truth in the form of signs, not through some tangible proof. Those who do not recognise the truth in its signs are blind in Allah's eyes, though they have eyes. Such people have no share in Allah's grace.

### In short

- Nūḥ's people persisted in denial, so Allah saved him and the believers in the Ark and drowned the rest.
- Allah gives the disobedient respite and then seizes them at the height of their power and pride.
- About eighty people were saved, among them Jurhum, who spoke Arabic.
- Victory belongs to the messengers and the God-fearing. Those who will not see the truth in Allah's signs are truly blind.
