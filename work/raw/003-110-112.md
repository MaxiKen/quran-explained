The title is given with its grounds: "You are the best community ever raised for humanity". The rank is not a bare compliment. It rests on work done for other people, and on faith, and it can be lost by whoever drops that work.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You are the best community ever raised for humanity"**: The community of Muhammad ﷺ is the most righteous and the most useful of communities for mankind. Abu Hurayrah explained that they are best for people because captives brought in chains later enter Islam. The same sense, that they are the best of peoples for the people, is reported from Ibn 'Abbas and from others including 'Ata', 'Ikrimah, Mujahid and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas.

**"you encourage good, forbid evil, and believe in Allah"**: Al-Ma'ruf, the recognised good, is all that Islam has ordained, and Al-Munkar, the recognised evil, is all that Islam has forbidden. In one reading, enjoining right conduct means enjoining the oneness of Allah and following Muhammad, and forbidding indecency means forbidding disbelief, idolatry and opposition to the Messenger. Belief here covers Allah, all revealed scriptures and all messengers.

**"Had the People of the Book believed, it would have been better for them"**: Belief in what was sent down to Muhammad would have been better for Jews and Christians than the state they remain in, better in this world and in the Afterlife. Only a few of them believe in what Muhammad came with.

**"Some of them are faithful, but most are rebellious"**: Among the faithful are 'Abd Allah ibn Salam, given also as Abd Allah ibn Salam and 'Abdullah Ibn Salam in the reports, and his companions and followers. Most are described as evil-livers and disbelievers who break their pledges, people who do not stay within the sacred way of life and the sacred law of Allah. The majority follow deviation, disbelief, sin and rebellion.

### Why this community is called the best

One ground given elsewhere is moderation, in the words "And in the same way We made you a moderate Ummah (community)" (2:143). Moderation is presented as a mark visible across this community's collective life, in Surah al-Baqarah.

The ground named in this verse is different. This community was raised to dispense good to all beings created by Allah. Its charge is concern for the spiritual and moral reformation of others. Past communities were also enjoined to bid the Fair and forbid the Unfair, as authentic hadith show. Yet several of them had no jihad as a religious obligation, so that mission could be carried out only by heart and by tongue. The followers of Muhammad ﷺ have a third means, the power of the hand, which includes the forms of jihad, and the enforcement of Islamic laws through government belongs to it as well.

In other communities, the distinguishing features of religion were gradually worn away by general inertia, and the obligation of enjoining good stood forsaken. Of this community, by contrast, it was prophesied:

> In this Ummah, there shall be right through the day of Doom, a group of people which will remain firm and stick to (the task of) bidding the Fair and forbidding the Unfair.

Belief in Allah is the second distinguishing feature. Belief as such was common to all past prophets and their communities, so the distinction lies in degree. The perfection of belief given to the community of Muhammad ﷺ is held to be of a class of its own compared with past communities.

### Attestations of the rank

### A conditional praise

Whoever acquires these qualities has a share in this praise. Whoever does not is likened to the People of the Scriptures whom Allah criticised for not forbidding one another the evil they committed (5:79).

- A minority of the People of the Book, such as 'Abd Allah ibn Salam, believed, while most remained in rebellion and shirk.
