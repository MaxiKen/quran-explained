The story of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) now reaches the building of the House of Allah. "And ˹remember˺ when We made the Sacred House a centre and a sanctuary for the people ˹saying˺, “˹You may˺ take the standing-place of Abraham as a site of prayer.” And We entrusted Abraham and Ishmael to purify My House for those who circle it, who meditate in it, and who bow and prostrate themselves ˹in prayer˺". The Jews denied the Prophet ﷺ on the assumption that prophethood could not go outside their own clan, scoffed at the Muslims for turning towards the Kaʿbah in prayer, and regarded the Hajj as no more than a custom of the ignorant Arabs. The previous verse had shown that leadership could never go to the unjust, even among Ibrāhīm's descendants, which knocks away the basis of their vanity. This verse reminds them that the Kaʿbah was built under divine command by Ibrāhīm himself with the help of his son Ismāʿīl (Ishmael). The Hajj and the facing of the Kaʿbah were therefore instituted by Allah. The Prophet ﷺ, a direct descendant of Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl, is reviving the Abrahamic way, which the Jews too must follow.

In a few words the verse sketches the history of the rebuilding of the Kaʿbah, the qualities of the House of Allah and of the city of Makkah. The respect that must be paid to this sacred place. The Qur'an returns to the subject again and again, and the chapter devoted to the pilgrimage gives the fullest account, where Allah appointed for Ibrāhīm the place of the House, told him to associate nothing with Him, to purify the House for those who circle it, stand, bow and prostrate. To proclaim the pilgrimage so that people would come from every distant pass (22:26-27).

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember˺ when We made the Sacred House a centre"**. Remember, together with Muḥammad, when We appointed the House, the Kaʿbah. The word rendered "centre", mathābah, comes from a root that means to come back. It denotes a place to which people return again and again. Ibn ʿAbbās explained that people do not stay at the House. They visit it, go home, and come to visit it again. It is a place of visitation to which people flock from every direction, a place to which they turn and for which they long, a means of connecting their hearts to Allah. Al-Qurṭubī reports Mujāhid as saying that no one ever has enough of visiting the Kaʿbah. Each time a person comes back with a greater longing to return and see it again. Some scholars remarked that one sign of an accepted Hajj is that the pilgrim finds in his heart, on returning home, a stronger desire to present himself at the House again. Those who have been there confirm it: each visit increases the thirst instead of quenching it. Makkah has no beautiful landscape, no easy access and no worldly comforts to offer. Yet millions are drawn there annually, an attraction described as miraculous. This is because Allah accepted the prayer of His Khalīl (close friend) Ibrāhīm when he asked Him to make people's hearts eager to visit the House.

**"and a sanctuary for the people"**. A place of safety from the injustice and attacks that befall other places. Abū al-ʿĀliyah said: safe from enemies and armed conflict. In the Jāhiliyyah (the age of ignorance before Islam) people were often victims of raids and kidnapping, while those living around the Sacred Mosque were safe and nobody was carried off from there. Mujāhid, together with ʿAṭāʾ, as-Suddī, Qatādah and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas all explained the phrase as meaning that whoever enters it is safe. A man might come across his father's killer there and still not raise a hand against him. Those who visit the House are safe even if they had committed evil acts. This honour flows from the honour of the one who first built it, the Khalīl of ar-Raḥmān. "The House" here means not only the Kaʿbah itself but the whole area of the Mosque around it, which is called the Ḥaram. The Qur'an uses "Kaʿbah" and "House of Allah" in this wider sense elsewhere: the phrase about "an offering to reach the Kaʿbah" (5:95) must mean the Ḥaram. This is because the verse concerns animal sacrifice and no sacrifice may be offered inside the Kaʿbah itself. So the verse means that the whole Ḥaram is a place of peace, in which shedding blood and taking revenge are forbidden. In the devotional reading, everyone who seeks refuge at that House in veneration and respect, repenting of his sins, is secure from the punishment of the Hereafter as well.

**"˹You may˺ take the standing-place of Abraham as a site of prayer"**. The verb is read as a command, ittakhidhū, and also as a statement, ittakhadhū, "they took". It is addressed to the community of Muḥammad ﷺ: take the Maqām of Ibrāhīm as a place of prayer, the stone he stood on while building the House, the founding stone, so that you perform two units of prayer there after circling the Kaʿbah. One early gloss paraphrases it as "a place by which you turn in prayer". The stone miraculously received the print of his foot, as al-Bukhārī's collection records. Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported to have said that the whole Ḥaram is the Station of Ibrāhīm. He probably meant that the two rakʿahs (units of prayer) which this verse enjoins after ṭawāf (circling the Kaʿbah) may validly be offered anywhere within the Ḥaram, and most of the jurists accept this. The Prophet ﷺ himself explained the command by word and deed on his last Hajj. After finishing his ṭawāf he reached the Station, which stands some yards from the Kaʿbah, recited this very verse, and prayed two rakʿahs on the far side of the stone with his face towards the Kaʿbah, as Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim records. From this the jurists inferred that a person who cannot find room close to the Station may stand at any distance from it, so long as the Kaʿbah and the Station are both in front of him. The verse shows that the two rakʿahs after ṭawāf are wājib (necessary), as al-Jaṣṣāṣ and Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī held, while offering them specifically behind the Station is a sunnah (the Prophet's ﷺ practice). There is no bar to praying them elsewhere in the Ḥaram. This is because the Prophet ﷺ is reported to have offered them near the door of the House. So did Ibn ʿAbbās, as al-Jaṣṣāṣ notes. In his "al-Manāsik" Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī says that someone unable to pray them behind the Station may validly pray them wherever he can within the Ḥaram, or even outside it. That is exactly what happened to Umm Salamah, one of the Prophet's ﷺ wives: on her last Hajj she could not pray them inside the Ḥaram and did so only once she was outside the city of Makkah. Most jurists, apart from Imam Mālik, hold that someone compelled to pray them outside the Ḥaram owes no animal sacrifice in compensation.

**"And We entrusted Abraham and Ishmael"**. Literally, We made a covenant with them, We gave Our ʿahd. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said this means Allah ordered them to purify it of every filth and impurity, so that none should ever touch it. Ibn Jurayj asked ʿAṭāʾ what Allah's ʿahd is. He answered: His command. The command to settle near the House and keep it clean, mentioned in the pilgrimage chapter (22:26-27), had at that time been addressed to Ibrāhīm alone, because his son was still an infant. Its purpose then was not to begin rebuilding the Kaʿbah but to place his wife and son in that locality so that a human settlement would take shape. This verse repeats the command to keep the House clean. Now it is addressed to Ismāʿīl as much as to his father. This is because the son had grown into a young married man and could share in the task.

**"to purify My House"**. From idols and graven images, said Ibn ʿAbbās in the report of Saʿīd ibn Jubayr. Mujāhid and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr added: from idols, sexual activity, false witness and sins of every kind. The command covers physical and outward dirt as much as inner filth, kufr (unbelief) and shirk (associating partners with Allah), and impurities such as greed, lust, envy, pride, vanity and hypocrisy. People come to this place to remember Allah in an atmosphere of peace and solitude. Therefore, everything contrary to that purpose is banned. The expression "My House" shows that the command applies to mosques generally. This is because all mosques are the houses of Allah, "houses which Allah has ordered to be raised" (24:36), and many hadiths order that mosques be kept pure and free of filth. The Prophet ﷺ said that mosques are established for the purpose they were built for, the worship of Allah alone. Al-Qurṭubī reports that the second caliph ʿUmar once heard a man shouting in the mosque and rebuked him for forgetting where he was. Due respect to a mosque means not speaking loudly and above all not saying anything the Sharīʿah forbids. Those who enter must keep body and clothes free of dirt, filth and even bad smells, and keep their hearts free of shirk, hypocrisy, pride, malice and greed. The Prophet ﷺ told people not to enter a mosque after eating raw onion or garlic, and forbade very small children and the insane to be brought in, for fear of their polluting it.

**"for those who circle it, who meditate in it, and who bow and prostrate themselves ˹in prayer˺"**. Ṭawāf around the House is a well-established rite. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said "those who circle it" are the strangers who come from outside Makkah, while "those who meditate in it", those who stay in retreat (iʿtikāf), are the people who live in the area of the Sacred House, and Qatādah and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas explained it the same way. "Those who bow and prostrate" are those who pray. The two words are the plurals of rākiʿ, one bowing, and sājid, one prostrated. Ibn ʿAbbās said that once it is a place of prayer it includes everyone described as bowing and prostrating, and ʿAṭāʾ and Qatādah gave the same explanation. One gloss reads it as those who perform the five daily prayers, whatever country they come from. Here the faithful bow and prostrate themselves, and their greatest mark of respect for their Creator is the circling of the Kaʿbah. For those who come from outside to perform the Hajj, ṭawāf carries greater merit than prayer. The verse also makes clear that it is entirely permissible to pray inside the House of Allah, whether the prayer is farḍ (obligatory) or nafl (voluntary), as al-Jaṣṣāṣ observed.

### How Makkah came to be a sanctuary

Several hadiths say that Allah made Makkah sacred before He created the heavens and the earth. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: The city’s sanctity dates from Allah’s creation of earth and heavens. Therefore, it is a sanctuary until the Day of Resurrection by Allah's decree. Fighting in it was not lawful for anyone before me. It was made lawful for me for a few hours of one day. Its thorny shrubs are not to be uprooted, its game not to be chased, nothing dropped in it to be picked up except by someone who announces it publicly. Its trees are not to be cut. Al-ʿAbbās said: except the idhkhir, the lemon-grass, Messenger of Allah, for our goldsmiths and our graves. He replied: except the lemon-grass. That is Muslim's wording. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs have a similar hadith from Abū Hurayrah. Al-Bukhārī one from Ṣafiyyah bint Shaybah.

Abū Shurayḥ al-ʿAdawī told ʿAmr ibn Saʿīd, when the latter was sending armies against Makkah: Commander, allow me to tell you something the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said on the day after the conquest of Makkah. My ears heard it, my heart took it in and my eyes saw him as he said it. He praised and thanked Allah and said that Allah, not the people, made Makkah a sanctuary. Therefore, no one who believes in Allah and the Last Day may shed blood in it or cut down its trees. If anyone argues that fighting there is permitted because the Messenger fought in it, tell him that Allah permitted His Messenger and did not permit you. Allah permitted me only for a few hours of that day. Today its sanctity is as it was before, and let those present inform those absent. When asked what ʿAmr had answered, Abū Shurayḥ said: he told me, "I know this better than you, Abū Shurayḥ. The Sacred House gives no protection to a sinner, a murderer or a thief." Al-Bukhārī and Muslim both collected this.

Ibn Jarīr narrated from Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that Ibrāhīm made Allah's House a sacred area and a safe refuge, and that he himself had made what lies between the two sides of al-Madīnah a sacred area, so that its game may not be hunted nor its trees cut. An-Nasāʾī and Muslim also recorded this. There is no contradiction between the hadiths that say Allah made Makkah a sanctuary when He created the heavens and the earth and those that say Ibrāhīm made it one, because, before building the House, together with Ibrāhīm proclaimed Allah’s decree of Makkah’s sanctity. In the same way the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was recorded as the Final Prophet when Ādam (Adam) was still clay, and yet Ibrāhīm prayed, "Our Lord! Raise from among them a messenger" (2:129). Allah accepted the prayer although He knew in advance that it would come about by His decree. When the Companions asked the Prophet ﷺ how his prophethood began, he said: I am the supplication of my father Ibrāhīm and the good news of ʿĪsā (Jesus) son of Maryam, and my mother saw a light go out from her that lit up the castles of ash-Shām (Syria).

### What the sanctuary protects. What it does not

The ban on bloodshed in the Ḥaram was one of the remnants of the way of Ibrāhīm that survived into the Jāhiliyyah. Every kind of fighting or bloodshed, individual or collective, was held forbidden inside the sanctuary, so much so that a man would not take revenge even if he met the killer of his brother or father there. The Islamic Sharīʿah preserved this rule. It was lifted only for the Prophet ﷺ on the day Makkah was conquered, only for a few hours, and was reimposed for ever straight afterwards, as he himself announced in his address that day, which al-Bukhārī records. Here no one may harm even the lowliest living creature, for Makkah has been made a sacred place of worship for all time.

A man who commits, inside the precincts, a crime for which the Sharīʿah lays down a fixed punishment (ḥadd) or allows retaliation (qiṣāṣ) gets no sanctuary from the Ḥaram. By consensus, as al-Jaṣṣāṣ and al-Qurṭubī record, such a criminal is duly punished. This is because the Qur'an itself says that if they fight you inside the Sacred Mosque, fight them (2:191). The jurists differ about a man who commits a crime outside and then takes refuge in the Ḥaram. Some would punish him there in the prescribed manner. Imam Abū Ḥanīfah held that if such men could escape punishment this way the Ḥaram would become an easy refuge for every kind of criminal and disorder would prevail, yet out of regard for the sanctity of the place the criminal is not punished inside it. He is forced to come out. Then the prescribed punishment is carried out.

### The stone Ibrāhīm stood on

Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said that the Maqām is the stone on which Ibrāhīm, Allah's Prophet, stood, and a mercy from Allah: he stood on it while Ismāʿīl handed him the stones for the Kaʿbah. As-Suddī said it is the stone that Ismāʿīl's wife placed under Ibrāhīm's feet when she washed his head. Al-Qurṭubī mentioned this but considered it unauthentic, though others preferred it. It was reported from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, together with Qatādah and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas. Ibn Abī Ḥātim reported Jābir's description of the Prophet's ﷺ Hajj: when the Prophet ﷺ had performed ṭawāf, ʿUmar asked him, "Is this the Maqām of our father?" He said yes. ʿUmar said, "Should we take it as a place of prayer?" and Allah revealed the words of this verse. Al-Bukhārī headed a chapter with this phrase, explaining "centre" as meaning that they return to it repeatedly, and under it he gave the report of Anas ibn Mālik about ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb's saying that he agreed with his Lord in this matter. Ibn Jarīr narrated from Jābir that after the Messenger of Allah ﷺ kissed the Black Stone he went round the House three times at a fast pace and four times at a slow pace, then went to the Maqām of Ibrāhīm, with the Maqām between him and the House, where he offered two rakʿahs. This belongs to the lengthy account preserved in Muslim’s Ṣaḥīḥ. Al-Bukhārī recorded that ʿAmr ibn Dīnār heard Ibn ʿUmar say that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ completed seven circuits of the House before offering two rakʿahs behind the Maqām.

All these texts show that the Maqām is the stone Ibrāhīm stood on while building the House. As the walls rose, Ismāʿīl brought his father a stone to stand on and handed him the building stones. Ibrāhīm would set them on the wall, and whenever he finished one side he moved to the next, until the House was complete. His footprints remained visible in the stone, and the Arabs knew this in the Jāhiliyyah, which is why Abū Ṭālib said in his poem known as al-Lāmiyyah that Ibrāhīm's footprint, made with his bare feet on the stone, was still to be seen. The Muslims saw them too. Anas ibn Mālik said that he could still see Ibrāhīm’s footprints, including the toes, in the Maqām, but that the prints wore away because people rubbed the stone with their hands. In earlier times the Maqām stood close to the wall of the Kaʿbah. Today it stands next to al-Ḥijr, on the right of those entering through the door. When Ibrāhīm finished building he either placed the stone beside the wall or simply left it where it last stood. He was commanded to pray next to it after ṭawāf. Therefore, it is understandable that the Maqām stands at the final point of construction. The Leader of the Faithful, ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, moved it back from the wall during his caliphate. He is one of the two men the Messenger of Allah ﷺ meant when he said to imitate the two who come after me, Abū Bakr and ʿUmar. He is the one whose view about praying at the Maqām the Qur'an confirmed, which is why none of the Companions objected when he moved it. ʿAṭāʾ said that ʿUmar moved the Maqām back. Mujāhid said he was the first to move it to where it now stands. ʿĀʾishah is reported, through an authentic chain, to have said that in the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and of Abū Bakr the Maqām was right beside the House. The account also states that ʿUmar moved it during his reign.

### Was the Kaʿbah built from nothing?

The revelation reached Ibrāhīm while he lived in Syria that Allah would show him the site of the Kaʿbah, which he was to build and keep clean for those who would gather there for the Hajj and the prayer. The earlier verse told how Jibrāʾīl (Gabriel) led him to the desert where Makkah now stands, where the remains of the earlier structure of the Kaʿbah survived only as a mound. How he was commanded to leave his wife and infant son there and return to Syria. He left at once, but was naturally anxious for them. Therefore, when he was out of earshot he prayed for them with the prayer recorded in the chapter named after him: "My Lord! Make this city ˹of Mecca˺ secure" (14:35). Further that he had settled some of his offspring in a barren valley near the Sacred House so that they might keep up prayer, asking Allah to make people's hearts incline to them and to provide them with fruits so that they would be thankful (14:37). He knew that the command to keep the House clean meant freedom from inner filth, shirk and kufr, as much as from outward dirt. Therefore, on leaving he prayed first that the place be made safe and peaceful and second that he and his children be protected from idol-worship. The Friend of Allah had reached that degree of knowledge where a man sees himself as nothing and makes no move without realising that everything, even his inclinations, rests in Allah's hand. Therefore, he turned to Allah for help in carrying out the command itself. There is a further subtlety: since Allah had commanded respect for His House, some people might one day, out of sheer ignorance, begin to worship the Kaʿbah itself. That is why he prayed specifically for protection from shirk. Out of love for his wife and son he then prayed for them to be provided with fruits in that uncultivable land. A hadith in al-Bukhārī's collection tells in detail how Jibrāʾīl made the spring of Zamzam flow, how people of the tribe of Jurhum came and settled there, and how Ismāʿīl married a woman of that tribe. The full story is told under the verse about raising the foundations.

Every verse of the Qur'an on the Kaʿbah either says that Allah Himself indicated its location or reports the command to keep the House clean. None suggests that a new building would occupy a different site, which itself shows that the Kaʿbah already existed in some form. Hadith and history confirm it. The earlier structure had either been destroyed in the Flood of Nūḥ (Noah) or raised into the heavens, leaving its foundations buried. Therefore, Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl were not its original founders but raised a new building on the old foundations. As to who founded it first and when, there is no fully authenticated hadith. Certain narrations coming from the People of the Book say that the angels founded it even before Ādam came down to the earth. The account also states that Ādam built it a second time or renovated it. That structure stood until the Flood destroyed it and left it a mound, which is how Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl found it. Since then the Kaʿbah has undergone alterations but has never been completely demolished. Before the Prophet ﷺ was commissioned, the Quraysh of Makkah rebuilt it afresh. He himself took part in that work.

### A House of stone that draws hearts

In the devotional reading, it is a House that Allah created from stone yet attached to eternity. Looking at the House only through the eye of creation cuts a person off, while those who look with far-reaching vision arrive and connect. It is said that the House was built of stone, but of a stone that attracts hearts as a magnet attracts iron. What kind of house is it? A house whose shade keeps whoever comes to it in the courtyard of security. A house that gives whoever glances at it the good news of forgiveness. A house around which subtleties circle the heart of whoever circles it, one circling answering another, for "Is there any reward for goodness except goodness?" (55:60). A house on which nothing spent to reach it is lost, and from which nothing withheld brings profit. Whoever visits it forgets every other place of visitation and leaves his own country behind. No distance to it counts as too far. Visiting it is not to be neglected for fear of unpleasant events or harmful attacks. This is because the House holds no harm for the lifeblood of the poor. Whoever holds back from visiting it lacks noble-heartedness (futuwwah) or is deficient in love (maḥabbah). The heart of one who stays away from it is harder than stones. When the rays of its lights fall on someone he forgets suns and moons. The wonder is not those who stay away but how those who come to it can bear to leave. As for the Station of Ibrāhīm, a servant stood upon his feet in Allah’s service. His footprints were made a qiblah for all Muslims, to honour him for all time until the Resurrection.

### In short

- The Kaʿbah was built under Allah's command by Ibrāhīm with Ismāʿīl. Therefore, the Hajj and the Muslim qiblah are Abrahamic institutions. The Prophet ﷺ is their rightful heir, which answers the Jews' scorn.
- The House is a "centre", a place people return to with ever greater longing. A sanctuary: the whole Ḥaram is a place where blood may not be shed, a rule Allah decreed from the creation of the heavens and the earth, which Ibrāhīm proclaimed and which was lifted only for the Prophet ﷺ for a few hours on the day of the conquest.
- The sanctuary shelters no criminal: a crime committed inside is punished by consensus, and for one who flees there after a crime outside, Abū Ḥanīfah held that he is forced out and then punished.
- The Maqām is the stone Ibrāhīm stood on while building, marked with his footprints. The Prophet ﷺ prayed two rakʿahs behind it after ṭawāf, which is wājib wherever it is offered and sunnah at that spot, and ʿUmar, whose suggestion the verse confirmed, later moved the stone back from the wall.
- Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl were commanded to purify the House of idols, sin and inner filth for those who circle it, stay in it and pray in it, a command that extends to every mosque.
