Allah now names the people who will receive the mercy promised in the previous verse: "“˹They are˺ the ones who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, whose description they find in their Torah and the Gospel." The verse then lists what this Messenger does: "He commands them to do good and forbids them from evil, permits for them what is lawful and forbids to them what is impure," and he "relieves them from their burdens and the shackles that bound them." It ends with the condition for success: "˹Only˺ those who believe in him, honour and support him, and follow the light sent down to him will be successful.”"

The people who truly deserve the full mercy and favour that Mūsā (Moses) asked for his people, and who truly meet the conditions of the previous verse, are those who follow the unlettered Messenger of Allah ﷺ. While describing them, the verse names some distinctive qualities of the Prophet ﷺ. It then requires not only belief in him but also following his commands and his practice. So, together with belief in Allah, following the Sunnah and the Sharīʿah of the Prophet ﷺ is necessary for lasting success in the Hereafter. This is how Muhammad ﷺ was described in the books of the earlier prophets. They gave their nations the good news of his coming and commanded them to follow him. His description was still plain in their books, as their rabbis and priests well knew.

### Phrase by phrase

**"“˹They are˺ the ones who follow the Messenger,"** They are the ones just described, who follow the religion of the Messenger, Muhammad ﷺ.

**"the unlettered Prophet,"** The Prophet who could neither read nor write, and who was taught only by revelation from his Lord.

**"whose description they find in their Torah and the Gospel."** They find him there by name and by description, with his traits, in the Torah revealed to Mūsā and the Gospel revealed to ʿĪsā (Jesus).

**"He commands them to do good and forbids them from evil,"** He commands decency, belief in Allah's Oneness and fine character. He forbids disbelief, harming others, and whatever sound minds and sound natures know to be bad.

**"permits for them what is lawful"**: literally "the good things". He makes clear to them that things in their scripture are lawful, such as the meat and milk of camels, sheep and other animals. He permits good things that had been forbidden to them under their law. He also makes lawful the baḥīrah, sāʾibah, waṣīlah and ḥām, animals the idolaters had banned by their own invention, prohibitions that only made life hard for themselves. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported this from Ibn ʿAbbās. In all, he allows good food, drink and relationships that do no harm.

**"and forbids to them what is impure,"** He forbids foul things such as carrion, the flesh of the pig, blood, liquor and all the prohibited animals. He forbids riba, and foods they treated as lawful though Allah had forbidden them. Impure earnings are included too, such as gambling, interest and bribes. Some scholars count immorality and bad manners among impure things as well.

**"and relieves them from their burdens and the shackles that bound them."** He came with gentleness and an easy religion, lifting the hard rules laid on earlier peoples. The details follow below.

**"˹Only˺ those who believe in him,"** Those who believe in Muhammad ﷺ, from among the Israelites and others, such as ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and his followers.

**"honour and support him,"** They respect and revere him, and help him against the disbelievers who were his enemies, even with the sword.

**"and follow the light sent down to him"**: the Qur'an, sent down with Jibrīl (Gabriel), along with the revelation the Prophet ﷺ delivered to people, his Sunnah. They hold lawful what it makes lawful and unlawful what it forbids.

**"will be successful.”"** They will succeed in this life and the Hereafter. They will be saved from Allah's wrath and punishment, gain what they hope for and be safe from what they fear.

### Why is the Prophet ﷺ called "unlettered"?

The phrase "the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet" names three qualities of the Prophet ﷺ. The Arabic word ummī means someone who has not learned to read and write. The Qur'an calls the Arabs ummiyyīn because, in general, they had little to do with reading and writing. Not being able to read and write is plainly not a merit in itself. It is a sign of a lack. Yet, unusual as it may seem, this very lack became a sign of the Prophet's perfection of the highest degree, given the matchless knowledge, wisdom and learning he taught and showed.

When an educated person shows perfect wisdom and wise conduct, it is seen as the fruit of his education. When such perfections, of the highest degree a human can imagine, come from someone who could not even read and write, it is a miracle as clear as daylight. It is all the more remarkable that he lived forty years in Makkah al-Mukarramah with no chance to learn. Then, at exactly forty, he suddenly began to speak words of such wisdom, in such language and style, that no one in the world could match even the smallest part of them. That speech is the Qur'an. Its challenge stands: "And if you are in doubt about what We have revealed to Our servant, then produce a sûrah like it and call your helpers other than Allah, if what you say is true" (2:23).

The Arabs were so proud of their eloquence that they called all other peoples ʿajam, "the mute", yet they could not meet this challenge. That is clear proof of the Qur'an's miraculous nature. It plainly testifies that the Prophet ﷺ was Allah's true Messenger and the Qur'an is Allah's true word. Being unlettered may be a defect in others, but in him it is praiseworthy. The verse lists it alongside his being Messenger and Prophet, which shows it too was a great quality of his.

He was unlettered so that no one could doubt his honesty. Had he been educated, people would have suspected that he composed everything himself and attributed it to Allah. According to Jewish tradition, the scriptures had also predicted an unlettered Final Prophet, which would make it easy for people to recognise him as sent by Allah.

### What does it mean to "find him" in the Torah and the Gospel?

This is the fourth quality the verse mentions. The verse does not say they will find his qualities or signs written in their books. It says, literally, that they will find him written there. This hints that his qualities would be described in the Torah and the Gospel so vividly and in such detail that reading them would be like seeing the Prophet ﷺ himself. The Torah and the Gospel are named, and not the Zabūr, the book revealed to Dāwūd (David), because these were the books the Jews and the Christians believed in. The Zabūr also contained his description.

As far as the Israelites knew, every prophet who had come had been from their own people, without exception. But according to Allah's plan, the last prophet was to come from the children of Ismāʿīl, and Allah had already told the Israelites so through their prophets. They were given this news so that when the last prophet appeared, they would not face a great trial. They could recognise him at once and rally to his cause. Many such prophecies still survive in their books.

The present versions of the Torah and the Gospel have been greatly changed and distorted, and have lost their originality and reliability. Even so, they still contain passages that point to the Prophet ﷺ. Moreover, the Qur'an declared in clear terms that the Torah and the Gospel described him. Had this claim been false, the Jews and Christians of his time would have used it against the Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims. Yet neither group ever tried to refute it. That itself shows that the Torah and the Gospel of that time contained clear references to him.

Some of the qualities described in those books are reported in the Qur'an itself, with reference to the Torah and the Gospel. Others are reported in the hadith, by people who read them in those books and later accepted Islam because of those descriptions.

### The dying boy who recognised him

Imām Aḥmad recorded that Abū Ṣakhr al-ʿUqaylī heard from a bedouin who had brought a milk camel to sell in Madinah in the Prophet's lifetime. Having sold it, the bedouin decided to go and meet this man and hear from him. He found the Prophet ﷺ walking between Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, and followed them. They came to a Jewish man reading from an open Torah. He was grieving over his son, a very handsome boy, who lay dying.

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked the father, by the One who sent down the Torah, whether he did not find his description and his coming in his book. The father shook his head. His son said, "Yes indeed, by the One who sent down the Torah! We find your description and your coming in our book." The boy then bore witness that none is worthy of worship but Allah and that he was Allah's Messenger. The Prophet ﷺ told his Companions to take charge of their brother and keep the Jew away from him. He himself saw to the boy's funeral and led the funeral prayer. This hadith is sound, and a similar hadith from Anas in the Ṣaḥīḥ supports it.

Al-Baihaqī gives a version from Anas in Dalāʾil al-Nubuwwah. In it, the Jewish boy used to serve the Prophet ﷺ. When the boy fell ill, the Prophet ﷺ visited him and found his father reciting the Torah beside his bed. The father denied the description, the boy affirmed it and declared his faith, and the Prophet ﷺ told his Companions to give him an Islamic funeral, since he had died a Muslim.

### The creditor who tested him

ʿAlī reported that the Prophet ﷺ owed some money to a Jewish man. The man came demanding it back, speaking harshly, and said he would not let the Prophet go until he was paid. The Prophet ﷺ said the man had a right over him, and sat down beside him, saying he would not leave until the man willingly let him go. He stayed there a whole day and night, praying Ẓuhr, ʿAṣr, Maghrib, ʿIshāʾ and the next morning's Fajr in that place. The Companions were deeply upset and quietly rebuked the Jew to make him release the Prophet ﷺ. Sensing this, the Prophet ﷺ asked what they were doing. They said they could not bear to see him held by a Jew. He replied that his Lord had forbidden him to wrong anyone under a covenant.

The next morning the Jew, deeply moved, came and declared his faith in Allah and His Messenger. He explained that he had held the Prophet ﷺ only to test the qualities the Torah gave him, and he pledged half his wealth in Allah's cause. He was a wealthy man, so this was a large sum for the Muslims. The description he had read was this. Muhammad, son of ʿAbdullāh, would be born in Makkah, his place of migration would be Madinah and his realm Syria. He would not be harsh by temper or rough in speech. He would not raise his voice in the markets, and he would keep far from indecency. The man said he had found all of this true of him. Al-Baihaqī reported the incident in Dalāʾil al-Nubuwwah.

### How did the earlier books describe him?

Ibn Jarīr recorded that ʿAṭāʾ ibn Yasār met ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr and asked him about the description of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in the Torah. He said, "Yes, by Allah! He is described in the Torah just as he is described in the Qur'an." The Torah, he said, contains the words "O Prophet! We have sent you as a witness, and a deliverer of good news, and a warner" (33:45), and calls him a refuge for the unlettered. It says: You are My servant and Messenger. I have named you al-Mutawakkil, the one who trusts in Allah. He is not harsh or coarse. He does not use foul language in the markets, and does not repay evil with evil, but forgives and pardons. Allah will not take his soul until He has straightened the crooked religion through him, so that people declare that none is worthy of worship but Allah. Through him He will open sealed hearts, deaf ears and blind eyes.

When ʿAṭāʾ later put the same question to Kaʿb, the reply matched ʿAbdullāh's to the letter. Al-Bukhārī recorded this from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr, in one version as far as "forgives and pardons". It was common in the speech of the Salaf to call the books of the People of the two Scriptures "the Torah", as some hadiths also show. Allah knows best.

ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, a great scholar of the Jews, reported almost the same words from the Torah, as Ibn Saʿd recorded in his Ṭabaqāt, ad-Dārimī in his Musnad and al-Baihaqī in Dalāʾil al-Nubuwwah. In his version the Prophet ﷺ is sent as a witness over all the nations, a bearer of good news to the righteous, a warner to wrongdoers and a protector of the unlettered Arabs. He neither quarrels nor shouts in the markets, and Allah will not let him die until he has set the crooked straight and brought people to believe in Allah alone.

Imām al-Baghawī reported from Kaʿb al-Aḥbār that the Torah described the Prophet ﷺ as Allah's Messenger and chosen servant. He is neither harsh in temper nor unpleasant in speech. He does not shout in the markets, and he does not repay a wrong with a wrong, but forgives or overlooks. He is born in Makkah and migrates to Ṭaybah, which is Madinah, and his realm is Syria. His people are ḥammādūn, "constant praisers", who praise Allah in ease and in hardship. They say the takbīr as they climb on their journeys, and watch the shadows to know the times of prayer. He wears a cloth around his waist and keeps his hands and feet clean with wuḍūʾ. His caller to prayer raises his voice high in the air. In battle his people stand in rows as close as their rows in prayer. At night their recitation is heard like the humming of bees.

Ibn ʿAsākir and Ibn Saʿd recorded, with a full chain of narrators, that Sahl, the freed slave of Khuthaimah, said he had read this description in the Gospel (Injīl). He would be neither short nor tall, fair in colour, with his hair in two locks. Between his shoulders would be the seal of prophethood. He would not accept charity, would ride a camel, milk his own goat and wear patched clothes. He would be of the line of Ismāʿīl, and his name would be Aḥmad.

Al-Baihaqī reported in Dalāʾil al-Nubuwwah from Wahb ibn Munabbih, a great expert in the earlier scriptures, that Allah revealed to Dāwūd in the Zabūr: A prophet named Aḥmad will come after you. I will never be displeased with him, and he will never disobey Me. I have forgiven him all his slips and omissions. I have enjoined on his people the voluntary and obligatory prayers I enjoined on the earlier prophets. On the Day of Judgement they will come before Me with a light like the light of the prophets.

The revelation went on: O Dāwūd, I have favoured Muhammad and his people above the followers of other prophets, with six concessions I gave to no one before. They will not be punished for slips made in error. I will forgive their unintended sins when they ask My forgiveness. What they spend willingly in My way, I will repay many times over, even in this world. Whoever says "We belong to Allah and to Him we return" when distress strikes, I will turn that distress into blessing and mercy and a guide to Paradise. And I will answer their prayers, sometimes by giving what they ask, and sometimes by storing it up for them for the Day of Judgement.

These are only a few of hundreds of such reports, which scholars have gathered in separate volumes. Among them is Shaykh Raḥmatullāh Kairānwī of India, who treated the subject thoroughly in his book Iẓhār al-Ḥaqq. He gave specific examples of such descriptions in the current versions of the Bible, even though these have lost their originality through countless changes and distortions.

### What do "good" and "evil" mean here?

The Arabic word for good, maʿrūf, literally means something recognised, acknowledged or known. The word for evil, munkar, means unfamiliar or unrecognised. So maʿrūf refers to acts known and recognised in Islam, and munkar to acts not recognised in it. This shows that only what the people of the first century of Islam recognised counts as a good deed. Anything else is munkar, rejected. Any act not approved by the Companions and their followers cannot be considered a good deed, however positive or appealing it looks. That is why the authentic traditions call such acts bidʿah, innovation.

Commanding good and forbidding evil was a quality of all the prophets, as it had to be, since that is the very purpose of their being sent. So there must be a reason it is named here as a special mark of the Prophet ﷺ. A little reflection on his way of preaching, speaking to each person according to his state of mind, shows the difference. Bedouins who knew nothing but herding camels and goats were addressed so simply and plainly that they grasped even complex and abstract ideas without effort. He received delegations from the great powers of the day, Rome and Persia, and from other tribes, and they were struck by how easily he made his point clear. The gift Allah gave him of moving people by his words was miraculous, and even his enemies admitted it. The Torah's words about opening blind eyes, deaf ears and sealed hearts through him may also refer to this effective way of teaching.

ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd said that whenever you hear Allah say "O you who believe!", give it your full attention, for it is either a good you are being commanded or an evil you are being forbidden. The greatest of these commands and prohibitions is that Allah sent the Messenger ﷺ to call people to worship Him alone, without partners, and to forbid the worship of anything besides Him. This is the message Allah sent with every messenger before Muhammad ﷺ, calling every community to worship Allah and shun false gods (16:36).

### What are the burdens and shackles?

The Arabic word iṣr means a burden so heavy that it stops a person from moving. Aghlāl is the plural of ghull, a shackle that binds a criminal's hands to his neck. Both refer to punishments. They were not religious requirements in themselves. These hard rules included the following:

- Cloth stained with impurity had to be cut away, not simply washed.
- Spoils taken in jihad against disbelievers were not lawful for them. It is reported that fire came down from heaven and burned them up.
- Hunting on the Sabbath, Saturday, was forbidden.
- The limbs used in committing a sin had to be cut off.
- A killer had to be put to death in return, whether the killing was deliberate or accidental, with no option of paying compensation.
- Repentance required killing oneself.
- Breaking their pledges made good things unlawful for them, and other hardships included tearing their clothes.

These heavy commands are what the verse calls burdens and shackles. The Prophet ﷺ would cancel them and replace them with commands that are easy to carry out. He described this side of the Sharīʿah when he said that he was leaving them an easy, practical law, neither burdensome nor open to going astray. In another tradition he said that Islam is easy. A hadith reported through many routes has him say that he was sent with the easy way of pure monotheism, the ḥanīfiyyah.

When he sent Muʿādh and Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī as governors to Yemen, he told them to bring good news and not drive people away, to make things easy and not hard, and to cooperate and not quarrel. Abū Barzah al-Aslamī, his Companion, said that he had accompanied the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and seen how easygoing he was. The nations before had been given hard laws, while Allah made the law broad and easy for this Ummah. So the Prophet ﷺ said that Allah has forgiven his Ummah the thoughts that pass through their minds, as long as they do not speak of them or act on them. He also said that his Ummah has been forgiven unintentional mistakes, forgetfulness and what they are forced to do.

That is why Allah guided this Ummah to pray: "“Our Lord! Do not punish us if we forget or make a mistake. Our Lord! Do not place a burden on us like the one you placed on those before us. Our Lord! Do not burden us with what we cannot bear. Pardon us, forgive us, and have mercy on us." (2:286). Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim records that after each supplication in this prayer, Allah the Exalted answered that He would accept it.

### Why does the verse require honour along with obedience?

The verse ties success to four conditions: believing in the Prophet ﷺ, revering him, readily helping him, and following the commands of the Qur'an.

The word for honour here, ʿazzarūhu, comes from taʿzīr. Lexically it means to hold someone back from something out of love and affection, and to protect him. ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās explained it as reverence. Al-Mubarrad said it means the highest degree of reverence. So lasting success belongs to those who not only love and respect him but are always ready to help him against his enemies. In his lifetime, helping him meant helping him in person. After his passing, helping his message and supporting the Sharīʿah takes the place of helping him.

The words "honour and support him" come between "those who believe in him" and "follow the light". This placement shows that the obedience owed to the Prophet ﷺ is not like obedience to a high official, given out of necessity. It is obedience in the true sense, born of love and respect, so that a person loves and reveres him enough to make obeying him the dearest thing to him. The Prophet ﷺ stands in many relations to his people. He is their prophet, their master, their commander and the object of their deep love, all at once. Being above all of them in knowledge, wisdom and social and moral conduct, he also calls for deep respect.

The Prophet ﷺ is perfect in every one of these respects, so Muslims must honour all of them. He is to be believed as a prophet, obeyed as a commander, revered as a man of great knowledge and wisdom, and loved and cherished for his great kindness to them. Obeying him is a religious duty for Muslims, because the purpose of his being sent is left unfulfilled without it.

In his case Allah added further duties. Believers are to "support and honour him" (48:9). They are told: "O believers! Do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet, nor speak loudly to him as you do to one another, or your deeds will become void while you are unaware" (49:2). And: "O believers! Do not proceed ˹in any matter˺ before ˹a decree from˺ Allah and His Messenger." (49:1). This warns Muslims not to speak ahead of the Prophet ﷺ in his presence. Sahl ibn ʿAbdullāh took it to mean that people should not start talking before he speaks, and should stay silent while he talks. Another verse is understood to forbid calling the Messenger as people call one another: "Do not treat the Messenger’s summons to you ˹as lightly˺ as your summons to one another." (24:63). The Qur'an warned the Muslims that all their good deeds would be wasted if they showed him disrespect.

### How did the Companions honour him?

The Companions, who spent most of their time with the Prophet ﷺ, kept to the respect the Qur'an taught. It is reported that after this verse came down, Abū Bakr spoke to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ as quietly as if telling him a secret. ʿUmar al-Fārūq did the same.

ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ said that no one on earth was dearer to him than the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, yet he could never bring himself to look at him fully. If asked to describe him, he could not, because he had never looked at him enough.

At-Tirmidhī reported from Anas that whenever the Prophet ﷺ came into a gathering of his Companions, they lowered their eyes out of respect. Only Abū Bakr and ʿUmar would raise their eyes to him and smile at him.

ʿUrwah ibn Masʿūd was once sent to Madinah to spy on the Muslims. Back in Makkah he reported that he had seen the courts of great kings, of Rome and Persia, and had met the Negus. Yet nowhere in the world had he seen anything like the respect and veneration the Muslims had for their prophet. He told them he did not think they would ever defeat such people.

Al-Mughīrah ibn Shuʿbah reported that the Companions thought it disrespectful to call the Prophet ﷺ from outside his house. They knocked on his door with their fingernails so as not to make a loud noise. Even after his passing, they avoided loud talk in his mosque and did not raise their voices even when preaching there. Many could not hold back their tears when he was mentioned. This remarkable love and respect let them share a large part of prophetic perfection and reach the highest religious rank after the prophets.

### What is the light, and why follow both?

The Qur'an is called a light. Light needs no outside proof of its existence. In the same way the Qur'an is its own proof that it is divine, Allah's word. Its supreme wisdom and eloquence are as plain as sunlight, all the more because it came from an unlettered man. Light is not only bright in itself but brightens the dark, and the Qur'an lit up a humanity lost in darkness.

The verse begins with "the ones who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet" and ends with "follow the light sent down to him". The first phrase calls for obeying the Prophet ﷺ, the second for obeying the Qur'an's commands. So lasting salvation depends on obeying both the Qur'an and the Sunnah, and obeying the Prophet ﷺ is achieved only by following his way of life, the Sunnah.

### Why is believing in a new prophet so hard?

The real spirit of religion is fear of Allah and concern for the Hereafter. In the later generations of a prophet or reformer, that inner spirit cools and form becomes all-important. At such a time Muhammad ﷺ came with religion in its pure form, cleared of every human addition and alteration. When a prophet appears, the greatest virtue is to believe in him. This faith is not the ritual recital of a few words. It means leaving a lifeless structure of rituals for a living religion that speaks directly to human nature.

A person's ties to the old religious structure rest only on historical tradition and ancestral custom. Accepting the religion of a new prophet takes a conscious decision. He leaves lifeless rituals behind and chooses reality. This looks simple, but in every age it has proved the hardest step to take, because people find it so hard to see beyond appearances.

### In short

- The full mercy asked for by Mūsā belongs to those who follow the unlettered Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, whose description the Torah and the Gospel contained.
- His being unlettered made the Qur'an's wisdom and eloquence an undeniable sign of his truth.
- He commands what Islam recognises as good, permits the pure, forbids the impure, and lifts the heavy burdens of earlier laws.
- Success requires believing in him, honouring and supporting him out of love, and following the Qur'an and his Sunnah.
- The Companions' deep reverence for him raised them to the highest rank after the prophets.
