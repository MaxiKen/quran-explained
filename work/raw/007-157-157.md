### Phrase by phrase

**"the ones who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet"** describes the people who deserve the perfect mercy. That mercy, and the complete favour of it, is what Musa had asked for on behalf of his own nation. The people who fulfil the conditions set out in the verse before this one are, in truth, those who follow the Messenger of Allah. The verse names him with the word that means a man who does not know the art of reading and writing.

The Book refers to the Arabs as the unlettered ones, because the Arabs in general had very little to do with reading and writing. Being unable to read and write is, in itself, plainly not a quality. It is an indication of a shortcoming. What makes the word remarkable here is that the same lack, which is a defect in others, has become for this Prophet a sign of the highest perfection.

A demonstration of wisdom and sound practice coming from a man of letters is taken as the fruit of his education. The same things, and many more of the same order, stand as a proof as clear as daylight. They come from a man who did not know how to read and write. It is more striking still that he lived in Makkah el-Mukarramah for forty years without ever having an occasion to learn. Then, at the age of forty, he began to speak in words of such wisdom and such a style. No one in the world could produce a match for the smallest part of them. That is a witness standing on its own, that he was truly the Messenger of Allah and that the Book he brought is truly the word of Allah. In another place the Book puts the challenge to the whole world to produce a surah to match it (2:23). The inability of the Arabs to answer it, in a nation as boastful of its speech as they were, is proof of the miraculous character of the Book.

The quality of being unlettered, then, may be a defect for other men, and for this Prophet it is a praiseworthy attribute. In the same way, the words of the verse calling him a messenger and a prophet are witness enough that his being unlettered was itself a sign. Not a weakness in the claim made for him.

### The description in the earlier books

**"whose description they find in their Torah and the Gospel."** is the verse's claim about the earlier Books. The Gospel here is the book revealed to Jesus, and the Torah is the book revealed to Musa. What is found in them is not merely a set of attributes or a set of indications. The words say that they find him in their books, and that carries an implicit meaning of its own. The qualities named in the Torah and the Gospel are described in such detail that seeing them written there is like seeing the Prophet himself.

The mention of the Torah and the Gospel, without a reference to the Zabur, the Book revealed to Dawud, is because the Jews and the Christians believed in those two books. The Zabur also carried the description of the Prophet, as the reports that follow make clear.

The present copies of the Torah and the Gospel have passed through great changes and distortions, and have lost the quality of being original and trustworthy. Even so, they still carry expressions that point to him. Beyond that, the Book stated in definite terms that those two books contained his description. The Jews and the Christians of his own time would have used the claim against him if it could have been shown to be false. and neither group ever tried to refute it. That silence is itself a witness that the Torah and the Gospel of that period carried clear references to him.

Some of the attributes named in those books have been reported in the Book itself. Others have come down in the hadith through men who read them in those books before they entered Islam.

Ibn Jarir recorded that Al-Muthanna said that 'Ata' bin Yasar met 'Abdullah bin 'Amr and asked him about the description of the Messenger of Allah in the Torah. He answered, by Allah, that he is described in the Torah just as he is described in the Book. He is a witness to them, one who brings good news and one who gives warning (33:45). He is a safe refuge for the unlettered ones. The description continued in his words. He is My servant and My Messenger, he is called Al-Mutawakkil, the one who trusts in Allah, he is neither hard nor harsh, and he does not utter foul speech in the markets. Nor does he return an evil deed with one of the same kind. Rather, he forgives and he forgoes. Allah will not end his life until He has straightened through him the crooked religion, so that they proclaim that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah. Through him He will open sealed hearts, and deaf ears, and blind eyes.

'Ata' then said that Ka'b was asked the same question by him, and his answer did not differ from the answer of 'Abdullah by a single letter. Al-Bukhari recorded it from 'Abdullah bin 'Amr. He mentioned that our Salaf were in the habit of calling the Books of the People of the Two Scriptures the Tawrah, as some hadith support.

The People of the Book had been told of the coming of the last prophet by their own prophets. The report of it was left in their books so that, when he appeared, they would recognise him readily and gather to his cause. It was also foretold that he would be unlettered, so that no one could raise a doubt about his credibility. A man with schooling would have been suspected of inventing what he brought and attributing it to God. To the knowledge of the Israelites, whichever prophets had come had all, without exception, been raised from their own community. By the plan of God the last prophet was to be raised from the children of Isma'il. This had already been conveyed to them through their prophets. Many of the predictions on the subject are still to be found in their books.

### The reports of those who read the description

Several reports have come down about people who read the description and tested it against the man himself.

Imam Ahmad recorded that Abu Sakhr Al-'Uqayli said that a bedouin told him he had brought a milk-producing camel to Al-Madinah during the lifetime of the Messenger of Allah. After he had sold it he said to himself that he would meet the man and hear from him. The man came across him walking between Abu Bakr and 'Umar. He kept behind them until they reached a Jewish man, who sat reading from an open copy of the Tawrah. The man was mourning a son of his who was dying, and the boy was one of the most handsome of children. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked the father whether he did not find the description of him and of his advent in his book. He put the question to him by the One who had sent down the Tawrah. The man nodded his head in the negative. His son said that the truth was otherwise, by the One who sent down the Tawrah. He said that they found his description and his advent in their book. He bore witness that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah. He also bore witness that this man is the Messenger of Allah. The Prophet ﷺ told the companions to take the boy from the Jew, and he took care of the funeral himself and led the prayer over him. That hadith is sound, and it is supported by a similar report in the Sahih narrated from Anas.

Al-Baihaqi reported the same kind of event in his Dalail al-Nubuwwah. The wording there has Anas telling of a Jewish boy who was in the service of the Prophet ﷺ. When the boy fell sick the Prophet ﷺ came to his house, and there he found the father reciting parts of the Torah beside the bed. The Prophet ﷺ asked him, by the pledge of Allah, whether he found in the Torah a description of his coming as a prophet. The father said no, and the son at once said that his father was wrong, and that they found him and his attributes mentioned in the Torah. Then he bore witness that there is no god but Allah and that he is the Messenger of Allah. When the boy died, the Prophet ﷺ told his companions to carry out the funeral rites for him as a Muslim.

Ali reported an affair of a different kind. The Prophet ﷺ owed some money to a Jew. The man asked for it back and told him in a harsh tone that he would not let him go until he was paid. The Prophet ﷺ said that the man had a right over him. He sat down beside him and said that he would not leave until the man let him go willingly. He stayed there a whole day and night, and offered the midday, the afternoon, the sunset and the night prayers, and the dawn prayer of the next day. The companions were greatly annoyed at the behaviour of the Jew, and they began, quietly, to press him to let the Prophet ﷺ go. The Prophet ﷺ, who had some idea of what they were doing, asked them what it was. Their answer was that they could not bear the sight of the Prophet ﷺ standing held by a Jew. He told them that his Lord had forbidden him to do wrong to anyone under a covenant.

Next morning the Jew came to him, deeply impressed by what he had seen. He declared that he bore witness that there is no god but Allah, and that this man is the Messenger of Allah. After that declaration he said that he had detained the Prophet ﷺ for no other purpose than to test the truth of the attributes he had read about him in the Torah. He gave half of his wealth away for the sake of Allah. The description he said he had read was this. The man's name was Muhammad the son of 'Abdullah, his birth would be in Makkah, the place of his migration would be Madinah, and his country would be Syria. He would be neither a man of harsh temperament nor a man of rough speech, and he would make no noise in the markets. It said that he would be far from the acts of immodesty. The report comes through Al-Baihaqi in Dalail al-Nubuwwah.

Al-Baghawi reported from Ka'b Ahbar a description that the Torah carried. Muhammad is the Messenger and the chosen servant of Allah, neither harsh nor disagreeable in speech. He does not cry out in the markets, and he does not return ill for ill. It said also that he forgives or passes over the wrongdoing of others. His birthplace is Makkah, his migration is to Taybah, which is Madinah, and his country is Syria. His people will be a praising people, who praise Allah in comfort and in trouble. They say the takbir when they rise in their travels, and they watch the shadows to work out the times of their prayers. They wear a sheet of cloth round the waist, and they keep their hands and feet clean with the wudu. The man who calls them to prayer will raise his voice in the air. In the field of battle they will stand in lines as close as they stand in prayer. At night their recitation will be heard like the humming of bees.

Ibn 'Asakir and Ibn Sa'd cited a full chain of narrators. Sahl, the freedman of Khuthaimah, said that he had read the following description of the Prophet ﷺ in the Injil. He would be neither short nor tall, of a fair colour, with his hair in two locks. He would carry the seal of prophethood on his body between the shoulders. He would not accept alms, he would ride a camel, he would milk the goat with his own hands, and he would wear patched clothes. He would be of the line of Isma'il, and his name would be Ahmad.

Ibn Sa'd in his Tabaqat, Darimi in his Musnad and Al-Baihaqi in Dalail al-Nubuwwah reported from 'Abdullah bin Salam, who had been a great scholar among the Jews. He reported that the Torah contained this description. O Prophet, We have sent you to bear witness over every nation on the earth, as a bearer of glad tidings to the righteous, as a warner to the wrongdoers. As a protector of the unlettered Arabs. You are My Messenger and My servant. I have named you Al-Mutawakkil, the one who trusts in Allah. You are neither harsh in temperament, nor quarrelsome, nor one who cries out in the markets. You do not return evil in exchange for evil. Rather, you forgive and you pass over the wrongdoing of others. Allah will not let him die until the crooked has been set right and the people have come to believe in Allah alone. That will not come to pass until blind eyes see, deaf ears hear and sealed hearts are opened. A similar description is carried in the Sahih of Al-Bukhari from Amr ibn al-'As.

Al-Baihaqi also reported in his Dalail al-Nubuwwah from Wahb ibn Munabbih, a great scholar and a man learned in the early books. Ruh al-Ma'ani carried that report. The wording is that Allah revealed to Dawud in the Zabur that a prophet would come after him whose name would be Ahmad. Allah would never be displeased with him, he would never disobey his Lord, and his errors and omissions had been forgiven. His people had been given the optional and the obligatory prayers that had been laid on the earlier prophets. On the Day of Judgement they will come before their Lord carrying a light like the light of the early prophets. Dawud was told that Muhammad and his people had been given excellence over the people of the other prophets. They had been favoured further with six concessions that were not given to the peoples before them. They will not be punished for inadvertent omissions. Their unintentional sins will be forgiven if they ask for forgiveness. The money they spend willingly in the way of their Lord will be repaid to them many times over in this world as well. When they are struck by distress and they say that they belong to Allah and that to Him they are returning. Their distress will be turned into a blessing and a mercy and a means of guidance towards Paradise. Their prayers will be answered, at one time by handing them the very thing they asked for and sometimes by storing the prayer to be granted on the Day of Judgement.

The reports of this kind have been gathered by scholars into separate volumes, and what has been cited here is a small part of what they collected. The book Izhar al-Haqq dealt with the subject thoroughly. It gave specific examples of such descriptions still found in the present versions of the Bible, even after all the changes and distortions they have been through.

### He commands them to do good and forbids them from evil

**"He commands them to do good and forbids them from evil,"** names the work he does among the people he is sent to. The word for the good he commands carries the sense of what is known and acknowledged. The word for the evil he forbids carries the sense of what is strange and not recognised.

Among the greatest of these commands is that Allah sent the Messenger ﷺ to order the worship of Him alone. No partner is to be joined to Him, and the worship of anything besides Him is forbidden. That is the message with which every messenger was sent before him. The Book says it in the words that a messenger was raised in every ummah, calling people to worship Allah and to keep away from false deities (16:36).

The way this command is understood has consequences. What was recognised as good by the people of the first century of Islam is what is taken as a good deed. Anything else falls on the side of the rejected. Any act that was not approved by the companions and their students cannot be counted as a good deed, however agreeable or positive it may look to a person. On that basis the authentic hadith called such acts innovated ones.

The attribute of commanding good and forbidding evil was common to all the prophets. The very purpose of their being raised was to guide people to what is good and to keep them from what is evil. So there must be something distinctive in the way it is said of this Prophet. A little reflection on the way he spoke to people according to the state they were in makes the distinction clear. The Arab bedouins, who knew nothing beyond grazing camels and goats, were addressed in such simple and candid terms that they understood matters of a complex and speculative kind without effort. He received delegations from the powers of the age, from Rome and Persia and from the other tribes. They were struck by the directness of his approach and by how easily his point came across. His God-given ability to reach people in his speech was something his enemies themselves acknowledged. The earlier books carried the same thing in the promise that through him blind eyes would see, deaf ears would hear and sealed hearts would open. That verse has been taken as a reference to the way he made matters understood.

### He permits the good things and forbids the impure

**"permits for them what is lawful and forbids to them what is impure,"** describes what he does with the law itself. He makes lawful the good things that the people had been forbidden, and he makes unlawful the things that are foul. One report from Ibn 'Abbas, carried through Ali bin Abi Talhah, says that the good things include the kinds of livestock the people had declared off limits for themselves. The same report says that the impure things include the flesh of the pig and riba. They include foods that they had treated as lawful although Allah had forbidden them.

The first part of it means that many things which were good in themselves had been made unlawful for the Israelites as a punishment. Those same things were made lawful for the people of this Prophet. The fat of a properly slaughtered animal is one of them. It had been forbidden for the Israelites, and it was made lawful for the Muslims. The examples given for the impure things include blood, dead animals, liquor, and the animals that are forbidden. They also include the means of earning that are not permitted, such as gambling, riba, and bribes. Some have added immorality and bad manners to the list, and one of the works on the subject, Al-Siraj Al-Munir, is cited on the point.

### He relieves them of burdens and shackles

**"and relieves them from their burdens and the shackles that bound them."** is the lifting of the load that had been laid on the nations before. The word used for the burden points to a weight heavy enough to stop a man moving. The word used for the shackles is the plural of a word for the iron that binds the hands of a criminal to his neck. Both words point to things that were, in themselves, a punishment and not a religious requirement.

The examples given are of that kind. The Israelites were required to cut away the piece of cloth that had impurity on it, and were not allowed simply to wash it. The spoils taken in a jihad from the enemies of religion were not lawful for them. It is reported that a fire used to come down from heaven and burn them up. They were not allowed to hunt on the Sabbath. A human organ that had been involved in a sin had to be cut off. A murderer had to be killed in retaliation whatever the nature of the killing, whether deliberate or by mistake. There was no concession of paying a ransom in place of the killing. All of that has been taken as the burden and the shackles spoken of in this verse. The Prophet ﷺ came to set such things aside and to replace them with commands that can be carried out without hardship.

The Messenger ﷺ described this side of the law himself. He said that he was leaving among a people a law that is easy and practicable, neither burdensome nor such as would lead them astray. In another report he said plainly that the religion is easy. When he sent Mu'adh and Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari to Yemen as commanders, he told them to bring glad tidings and not drive people away, and to make matters easy and not difficult. He said too that they should agree with one another and not fall into disagreement among themselves.

Abu Barzah Al-Aslami, a companion of the Prophet, said that the Messenger of Allah was with him on a journey and that he saw how light the man's way was. The nations before had things made difficult for them in their laws, and Allah made the law wide and easy for this ummah. Out of that came the word of the Messenger of Allah. Allah has forgiven his ummah for what passes within themselves, as long as they do not speak it or act on it. The word that his ummah has been forgiven for unintentional errors, for forgetfulness, and for what a man is driven to do under duress. This is why Allah has guided this ummah to a supplication that asks the Lord not to punish its people if they forget or fall into error. It asks Him not to lay on them a burden like the burden laid on those before them, and not to place on them a load greater than they can bear. The same supplication asks for pardon, forgiveness and mercy, and it names Him as its Patron and Protector (2:286).

It is recorded in Sahih Muslim that Allah answered every one of those requests with the words that He would accept it. A supplication whose every request is granted is the plainest sign that the load on this people has been made light.

Now the verse says that the Prophet ﷺ came with leniency and with an easy religion. The well-known report carries the words that he was sent with the easy way of the one who turns to his Lord alone.

Abdullah bin Mas'ud gave a rule to the believers on this matter. He said that when a person hears the words, O you who believe, he should give them his full attention. What follows is either a good that he is being commanded to do or an evil that he is being forbidden.

### Those who believe, honour, support and follow the light

**"˹Only˺ those who believe in him, honour and support him. Ollow the light sent down to him will be successful.”"** sets out four conditions. Uccess is tied to all of them.

The first is belief in him. The next is reverence of him. The word used for reverence is derived from a root that carries the sense of stopping a person, out of love and affection, from doing something, and of guarding him. Ibn 'Abbas explained it as reverence, and one of the early authorities on the language said that it denotes the highest degree of reverence there is. The third is help. In the lifetime of the Prophet ﷺ the help spoken of was given to him in person. After his departure the same help is given to his message, and the support given to the sacred law stands in the same place. The fourth is the following of the light, which is the Book sent down with him and the revelation that he delivered to mankind. The light has been taken as the Book itself, since it was sent down with Gabriel.

What the light means is worth setting out. Light does not need an outside proof to show that it exists, and the Book is in the same position. It is its own evidence that it is from Allah, since the highest order of wisdom and eloquence is in it, as plain as the light of the sun. It was spoken by a man who could not read or write. In another respect the Book is unlike any other light, since light not only shows itself but makes other things bright. The Book has brought out of darkness a people who had been lost in it.

There is a further point in the way the verse is built. It opens with those who follow the Messenger and it closes with those who follow the light that was sent down with him. The first phrase lays on the people the obedience of the Prophet ﷺ and the second lays on them the obedience of the Book. Eternal success is therefore tied to both of them together, and obedience to the Prophet ﷺ is reached only by following the way of his life, which is the sunnah. Between those two phrases sits the command to honour and to help him. The point of that arrangement is that the obedience owed to the Prophet ﷺ is not the obedience a man gives to a high official out of necessity. It is an obedience that comes out of love and reverence.  A person should carry as much love and reverence for him as will make that obedience the dearest thing to him. The Prophet stands to his people in more than one relation. He is a prophet, a teacher and a commander. He is an object of deep love as well. The fulfilment of the demands of all of those positions falls on the believer.

The companions took this seriously, and the reports show how. In another verse the words come that the people should help him and honour him (48:9). In another, the believers are told not to raise their voices above the voice of the Prophet ﷺ and not to speak to him in a loud voice as they speak to one another. The warning is that their works may come to nothing while they are not aware (49:2). In another, they are told not to go ahead of Allah and His Messenger (49:1). Sahl ibn 'Abdullah took that to mean that a person should not begin to speak before the Prophet of Allah speaks, and should keep silent when he is speaking. In another, they are told not to treat the calling of the Messenger among them as the calling of one of them to another (24:63). The warning attached to it is that a failure of respect towards him can bring a man's good deeds to nothing.

The companions who had his company most of the time kept to that instruction. It is reported that after the revelation of the verse Abu Bakr used to speak to the Messenger of Allah in a low voice. He spoke the way a man does when he is telling a secret. Umar al-Faruq did the same. Amr ibn al-'As said that no one on the face of the earth was dearer to him than the Messenger of Allah. He said at the same time that he could not bring himself to look at him directly. He said that if he were asked to describe him he would find it difficult, since he had never looked at him long enough to give a description. Anas reported that whenever the Prophet ﷺ came into the assembly of the companions they kept their eyes down out of respect. He said that only Abu Bakr and Umar raised their eyes and smiled at him.

The respect of the Muslims for their Prophet was noticed by those who came from outside. Urwah ibn Mas'ud was once sent to Madinah to spy on the Muslims. When he returned to Makkah he reported that he had seen the courts of great kings, of the king of Rome and of Persia. He had met the Negus as well. The reverence he saw in the hearts of the Muslims for their prophet was something he had never seen anywhere in the world. He told his people that they would never succeed against such a people. Mughirah ibn Shu'bah reported the same care in another form. When the companions wanted the Prophet ﷺ and he was inside his house, they would not call out to him from the street. They knocked at his door with their fingernails, so as to make no loud sound. Even after he had left them, the companions avoided raising their voices in his mosque, and they avoided it in the sermons given there as well. When the Prophet ﷺ was mentioned among them, a number of them could not help weeping.

It was that love and that reverence which let the companions take a large share of the perfection of prophethood. It gave them the highest religious standing there is, next to the prophets themselves.

### The spirit of the religion and the shape of it

The verse also speaks to a matter that comes up in every age. The real spirit of the religion is the fear of God and a concern for the Hereafter. In later generations, after a prophet or a reformer has gone, the inner spirit grows less ardent and the greatest weight comes to be placed on the outward form. The Prophet ﷺ brought the pure religion, free of the additions that people had made to it.

When a prophet appears, the greatest virtue for a person lies in believing in him. That faith is not the reciting of a few words as though it were a ritual. It means leaving behind a structure of rituals that has lost its spirit and taking up a living religion that speaks directly to human nature. A man's link with the older structure rests on nothing more than inherited tradition and the customs of his people. When he accepts a new religion, he makes a conscious decision, and he leaves the shell behind for the reality. That looks like a simple matter, and in every age it has proved the hardest step for people to take, since people find it hardest to see past appearances.

There is a further thing in the verse that was noted in the report of 'Abdullah bin Salam. The people of the earlier books had been told of the coming of the last prophet. The purpose was that they should not face a great trial when he came, but should recognise him readily and gather to his cause. Many of the predictions on the matter are still there in their books. The early Muslims who came from those communities read them and recognised him before they entered Islam. The obligation on the one who reads them is the same in every age, that he should take the man when he finds him described.

### In short

- The people who receive the perfect mercy are those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet. He had no schooling, and that lack became by his wisdom and eloquence a sign of the highest perfection rather than a defect.
- The verse claims that his description is found in the Torah and the Gospel, and the claim was never refuted by the Jews and the Christians of his own time. The reports gathered by Al-Baihaqi in Dalail al-Nubuwwah, by Al-Baghawi, by Ibn Sa'd and by Ibn 'Asakir give the descriptions from those books. They name Makkah as his birthplace, Madinah as the place of his migration and Syria as his country.
- He commands the good and forbids the evil, which is the call to the oneness of Allah that every messenger was sent with. He makes lawful the good things that had been forbidden to the Israelites as a punishment, and makes unlawful the impure things, among them riba and the earnings that are not permitted.
- The burdens and the shackles he lifted are the hard requirements laid on the nations before, and he came with a law that the Prophet ﷺ himself described as easy. His ummah was forgiven for what passes within them, for error, and for what a man is driven to do against his will. That is what the supplication of Al-Baqarah asks and what Sahih Muslim records the answer to.
- Success is tied to four things together, belief in him, reverence for him, help given to him and following the light sent down with him. The reverence placed in the middle is what turns obedience into love, as Abu Bakr and Umar and the rest of the companions showed in their conduct towards him.
