Every road ends at the same gathering: "Whether you die or are martyred—all of you will be gathered before Allah". The distinction is not destination but the state in which a person arrives there.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whether you die or are martyred—all of you will be gathered before Allah"**: And whether you die or are killed, if you die, read in both ways muttum or mittum, or are slain in holy struggle or otherwise, on a journey or in settlement, in a fighting expedition, verily unto Allah you shall be gathered, mustered in Hereafter, to God and to none other than Him. He will requite you. Death in whatever form, or killing in Allah's path, ends in the same return. All come back to Allah alone for reward measured by deeds. Wa-la-in's lām is again for oaths. Gathering after death is the setting named.

Whoever dies or is killed will return to Allah, Exalted and Most Honored. He will reward him if he has done good deeds, or will punish him for his evil deeds. The present gloss says He will reward you according to what you have done. Forgiveness and mercy of the previous verse are the reward carried by those who die in His way.

The inward reading finds sweetness for a traveller whose destination is God. Hardships sweeten like honey when the road ends in Him. Returning to Him outranks staying behind with anything else. Knowing God makes sacrifice of spirit, rūḥ, ruh, better than a life spent otherwise. Blessing leaves whatever a servant puts before God. This world is one offer. The final outcome is the other.

Frame numbers are 154, verse 154, and 156, verse 156, with Say, say, as speech artifact.

Gathering before Allah levels the distinction between home death and battle death. Both are gathered to the same Judge. That is why regret counterfactuals lose force. Staying cannot move a person outside the gathering, and going cannot move him outside decree. What changes is reward, forgiveness and mercy or punishment for evil deeds, according to what was done.

### In short

- Whether dying naturally or killed in the holy struggle or otherwise, it is to God, and none other, that all shall be mustered in the Hereafter.
- He will requite according to deeds, rewarding good and punishing evil.
- Since destination is God, journey's hardships become sweet to one who knows Him.
