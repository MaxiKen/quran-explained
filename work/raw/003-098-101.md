<<<VERSE 3:98>>>
“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O People of the Book! Why do you deny the revelations of Allah, when Allah is a Witness to what you do?”” (3:98). The verse calls the People of the Book to account for rejecting Allah’s revelations even though their actions are never hidden from Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺”** directs the Prophet ﷺ to speak plainly. The question is a rebuke, but it is also an invitation to reconsider the evidence. The command to say it places the issue before the People of the Book and the wider community, so the claim and response are not left obscure.

**“O People of the Book!”** addresses communities who received earlier revelation, including the Torah and the Gospel. They had a history of prophets, scripture, and knowledge of Allah’s guidance. The passage is not a statement that every individual among them acted alike. It challenges those who knowingly rejected the truth and resisted the message after recognizing its signs.

The surrounding verses have discussed Abraham, the Kaʿbah, and the pilgrimage, along with questions about the Torah and Gospel. Some people of the Book disputed the change of qiblah from Bayt al-Maqdis toward Makkah and claimed that earlier scripture supported their position. This verse asks them to take Allah’s revelations seriously instead of using inherited claims to obscure the evidence.

**“Why do you deny the revelations of Allah”** asks why they refuse the signs and message Allah has sent. In the immediate setting, the revelations include the Qur’an and the prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ. Earlier scriptures had given signs of a coming messenger, and the verse challenges those who recognize the truth yet deny it. From Adam onward, the prophets called their people to Allah, and the revelation is ruh, guidance that gives life to the heart. The reports surrounding this passage, including an account transmitted by Ibn Ishaq, also show how disputes could be used to keep people from recognizing the message. It does not criticize honest questions or the sincere search for understanding. It condemns knowingly turning away from clear guidance.

**“when Allah is a Witness to what you do?”** means that Allah sees their rejection, their words, and their attempts to conceal or distort what they know. No public performance of piety can hide a person’s real intention from Him. The Qur’an also reminds people that wealth and children cannot benefit anyone on the Day of Judgment (26:88). Allah’s witness is a warning that evasion will not remove accountability, and a comfort to believers who are treated unjustly because they hold to the truth.

### In short

- The People of the Book are challenged over knowingly denying Allah’s revelations.
- Allah knows what people say, conceal, and do.
- The warning concerns deliberate rejection, not sincere inquiry.

<<<VERSE 3:99>>>
“Say, “O People of the Book! Why do you turn the believers away from the Way of Allah—striving to make it ˹appear˺ crooked, while you are witnesses ˹to its truth˺? And Allah is never unaware of what you do.”” (3:99). The verse condemns efforts to obstruct people from Allah’s way and to misrepresent it, even though those responsible know the truth.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Why do you turn the believers away from the Way of Allah”** describes efforts to stop people from following Islam. This can happen through misleading claims, pressure, or presenting faith as something other than what Allah revealed. The path is Allah’s religion and worship, not a human faction’s private possession. The verse warns those who deliberately prevent others from accepting guidance.

Some reports connect the verse with people who invited ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir and other believers to return to Judaism. The point is not that all dialogue across religious communities is forbidden. The warning concerns a deliberate attempt to pull believers away from the faith by concealing evidence or exploiting their vulnerability. Reports transmitted by Ibn Ishaq describe Shas ibn Qays stirring old divisions among the Anṣār, a way of turning people from the path without debating the message itself. The revelation is ruh, spiritual guidance that should bring hearts toward Allah rather than toward rancor. A person may discuss differences honestly without trying to make the truth appear false.

**“striving to make it ˹appear˺ crooked”** means trying to portray Allah’s straight way as distorted or unreliable. A crooked path does not lead where it promises. Those who misrepresent the religion may elevate peripheral disputes while concealing its central call to worship Allah alone, follow the prophets, and live with justice. The verse does not forbid asking how a ruling is understood. It rebukes a purposeful effort to twist the message.

**“while you are witnesses ˹to its truth˺”** makes their conduct more serious. They have knowledge of the evidence from scripture and know that the messenger’s call is not an invention. Their responsibility is not merely to recognize truth privately but to avoid obstructing others from it. Knowledge should lead to fairness, not manipulation.

**“And Allah is never unaware of what you do”** repeats the warning that hidden plans and public arguments are both known to Him. Allah may give people time to reconsider, but delay is not approval. The verse reassures believers that those who obstruct them will be held to account, even when they seem influential or persuasive.

### How does the warning apply?

The verse asks believers to recognize when disagreement is sincere and when someone is intentionally trying to turn them from their faith. It does not ask them to respond with hostility or to treat every member of another community as an enemy. Rather, believers should remain grounded in knowledge, hear arguments carefully, and refuse to distort another person’s position in return. A message is defended by truthfulness and good conduct, not by imitation of the very crookedness being condemned.

### In short

- Allah’s way must not be deliberately misrepresented or blocked.
- Those who know the truth have a duty to be honest about it.
- Believers should distinguish sincere discussion from calculated pressure.

<<<VERSE 3:100>>>
“O believers! If you were to yield to a group of those who were given the Scripture, they would turn you back from belief to disbelief” (3:100). The verse warns believers not to let people who seek to undermine their faith direct them away from what they know to be true.

### Phrase by phrase

**“O believers!”** addresses the Muslim community directly. Faith is a gift that needs care and steadfastness. The warning follows the challenge to those who obstruct the way of Allah, and tells believers not to cooperate with efforts that would undo their faith.

**“If you were to yield to a group of those who were given the Scripture”** does not prohibit ordinary dealings, respectful conversation, or learning from people of other religions. It cautions against following a group’s religious pressure or accepting claims that knowingly pull believers away from Islam. Earlier debates about the Torah and the Gospel form part of the background, but the warning concerns the effect of yielding to pressure that undermines faith. The word “group” also matters. The verse does not condemn every Jew or Christian. It describes those who are intent on turning believers back from faith, whether from envy, hostility, or a desire to prevail in dispute.

A report explains the warning through an attempt to reopen conflict between the Aws and Khazraj, two tribes among the Anṣār. Before Islam they had fought one another, but Allah joined their hearts as an ummah. A man named Shas ibn Qays, described in another account as Shammas ibn Qays, resented their friendship. He arranged for someone to recite old poems that praised one tribe over the other. The verses stirred memories of pre-Islamic rivalry, and anger rose until the two sides were close to taking up arms.

When the Prophet ﷺ heard what was happening, he came to them and reminded them that he was among them and that Allah had united them through Islam. He asked whether they wanted to return to kufr after receiving faith. The companions recognized that they had been provoked, put down their weapons, embraced, and wept. The story shows how a person can be drawn toward disbelief not only by a direct theological argument but also by anger, tribal pride, and a desire for revenge. Ibn Ishaq transmits the account, and another version includes the narrators Ayyub and al-Mu'ammil. One report says that the Prophet ﷺ intervened before the two sides fought. The episode is a warning about following the path of provocation rather than the guidance given to the community. Allah reminds believers that the Messenger called them to faith and that they had accepted a covenant with Him (57:8). The Qur’an is ruh, a source of spiritual life that steadies the community when agitation threatens to divide it.

**“they would turn you back from belief to disbelief”** describes the danger of accepting such influence. The warning is not a prediction that every conversation will cause a believer to lose faith. It calls for discernment about whose counsel one follows and whether it strengthens justice and truth or revives hostility and doubt. Allah tells believers elsewhere that some people wish to turn them back after they have believed (2:109). Their response should be knowledge, patience, and reliance on Allah rather than fear or suspicion of everyone outside their community.

### How can believers guard their faith?

The incident shows that social unity can be attacked through selective memories and inflammatory speech. Old grievances may be real, but reviving them to provoke violence does not repair them. The Anṣār had moved from rivalry to brotherhood, and Islam gave them a shared direction. They were saved from renewed conflict by remembering Allah’s favor, listening to the Prophet ﷺ, and choosing reconciliation.

The passage also warns against letting secondary disputes replace the foundations of faith. Taqwa, awareness that one is accountable to Allah, should guide the believer’s priorities. If a community turns every difference into a test of loyalty, it becomes easier for an outside provocateur to divide it. A believer should examine claims, ask trustworthy people, and hold to what Allah has revealed without surrendering faith to anger or pressure.

### In short

- Do not follow pressure that would draw you away from faith.
- The verse warns against a hostile group, not every person among the People of the Book.
- Remembering Allah’s gift of unity can prevent old rivalries from returning.

<<<VERSE 3:101>>>
> “How can you disbelieve when Allah’s revelations are recited to you and His Messenger is in your midst? Whoever holds firmly to Allah is surely guided to the Straight Path” (3:101)

The verse marvels that believers could abandon faith while revelation is being recited among them and the Prophet ﷺ is teaching them. It then points to the protection found in holding firmly to Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**“How can you disbelieve”** is a question of astonishment and warning. The preceding verse described how people might be pressured toward disbelief. This verse reminds believers of the powerful means Allah has given them to remain firm. It does not claim that a believer can never stumble. It calls them back to the reasons they have for faith and to the help available when they seek it.

**“Allah’s revelations are recited”** means that the community hears the Qur’an and receives Allah’s guidance. The revelations clarify belief, worship, right conduct, and the consequences of rejecting guidance. Their recitation is not merely sound. It calls people to understand, remember, and act. A person should respond to the Qur’an with reflection and obedience rather than treating it as familiar words that require no change.

**“His Messenger”** refers first to the companions who lived with the Prophet ﷺ and could ask him directly for explanation and guidance. His presence among them was an extraordinary blessing. They could hear the Qur’an from him, see how he practiced it, and receive correction when they were confused. For later generations, the Prophet is no longer physically among them, but the Qur’an and his reliably transmitted teaching remain sources of guidance. This difference should not make later believers feel abandoned. It gives them a responsibility to learn faithfully and seek sound knowledge.

### How do believers hold fast after the Prophet’s time?

The verse recalls the moment when the Aws and Khazraj nearly fought after someone stirred up their old rivalry. The Prophet ﷺ came between them and recited the warning. Their response was to stop, put down their arms, and reconcile. A report from Ibn ʿAbbas connects the incident with this verse and the following command to hold fast together (3:103). Another report associates the reminder with Mu'adh ibn Jabal and his companions. The incident shows that revelation can interrupt a dangerous cycle when people listen to it and remember the Messenger’s guidance. Allah also reminds the believers that the Messenger invites them to faith and has taken their covenant (57:8).

Another report describes the companions asking which believers had the most remarkable faith. They first mentioned angels and prophets, who receive direct signs and revelation. The Prophet ﷺ then spoke of people who would come later, believe in what they read in books, and hold to faith without having seen him. This report encourages later believers, who do not have the Messenger physically in their midst, to remain faithful to the revelation they have received.

**“Whoever holds firmly to Allah”** means seeking His protection, remaining attached to His guidance, and relying on Him rather than on one’s own strength. Holding firmly includes affirming Allah’s oneness, following what He has commanded, avoiding what He has forbidden, and asking for His help. Reliance is not passivity. It means taking the means available while trusting that guidance and success come from Allah.

An inward reflection adds that even a person’s ability to hold fast is a gift from Allah. It describes ʿirfān as a deeper recognition of Allah that should make a believer more humble, not self-satisfied. Guidance begins with His favor and continues through His help. The believer should therefore avoid pride in their own steadfastness and keep asking Allah to preserve their faith. Sincere reliance also means leaving behind shirk, the idea that anything besides Allah independently controls one’s ultimate fate.

**“is surely guided to the Straight Path”** promises direction to the one who clings to Allah. The straight path is the way of truth and obedience, protected from the crookedness described in the previous verse. The promise encourages believers to remain connected to Allah through revelation, prayer, remembrance, and trust. This is the path of steadfastness that guards faith without turning it into pride or hostility. It also prepares the community for the next verse’s instruction to hold together and not divide.

### In short

- Revelation and the Messenger’s teaching are means of protection from disbelief.
- Holding firmly to Allah includes following His guidance and relying on Him.
- Steadfastness is a gift to seek with humility, and it leads to the Straight Path.
