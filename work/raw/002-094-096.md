<<<VERSE 2:94>>>
Verse 94 proposes a simple test of the Jews' claim that the next world belongs to them. "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “If the ˹eternal˺ Home of the Hereafter with Allah is exclusively for you ˹Israelites˺ out of all humanity, then wish for death if what you say is true!”" If Paradise is certainly yours, the verse reasons, you should be eager to reach it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺"**. Allah instructs His Prophet ﷺ to put this challenge to the Jews.

**"If the ˹eternal˺ Home of the Hereafter with Allah is exclusively for you ˹Israelites˺ out of all humanity"**. If Paradise in Allah's keeping is purely and specifically yours, as you allege, and not for the believers in Muḥammad ﷺ and his Companions or for anyone else. The Jews claimed the blessings of the next world as their private reserve, meant for no other people. Other verses record the same claim from the Jews and from the Christians: that the Fire will touch them only for a number of days (2:80), that none will enter Paradise except a Jew or a Christian (2:111), and that they are the children of Allah and His beloved (5:18). What they meant was that since their faith was the true one, they were sure of salvation: the repentant and forgiven admitted to Paradise from the start, the sinners released from Hell after a few days' punishment, and the obedient welcomed like sons and beloved friends.

**"then wish for death"**. Ask for death. The Arabic ties both conditions to the verb "wish", so that the sense is: if you speak truly in claiming that it is yours, you will naturally incline to what is yours, and since the road to it is death, long for death. Wish for it and ask for it so that you may reach that place quickly and rest from the burdens and worries of this life. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq related from Ibn ʿAbbās that the meaning is "invoke Allah to bring death on the lying camp", whether the Muslims or the Jews. The Jews declined the Messenger's ﷺ offer. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk likewise reported Ibn ʿAbbās explaining "wish for death" as "pray for death".

**"if what you say is true!"**. If you are truthful in your claim. This challenge was aimed not at the Jews of every age but specifically at the Prophet's ﷺ contemporaries, who denied him out of envy and malice despite recognising him as a genuine prophet.

### What would have happened had they wished

ʿAbd ar-Razzāq narrated from ʿIkrimah that Ibn ʿAbbās said: had the Jews invoked Allah for death, they would have perished. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded Saʿīd ibn Jubayr relating from Ibn ʿAbbās that had the Jews asked for death, every one of them would have choked on his own spittle. These statements have authentic chains back to Ibn ʿAbbās. Ibn Jarīr records a report of the Prophet ﷺ concerning that wish. According to it, the Jews' demand for death would have killed them and revealed their places in the Fire. Someone invoking that curse against Allah's Messenger would have found both family and possessions gone on returning home. Had they made that wish on that day, not a Jew would have been left alive anywhere on earth.

### A test that needed only the tongue

Apart from improper expressions like "sons of Allah", claims of this kind are in themselves correct, provided they are made by people who follow a true and valid faith. The Jews, like the Christians, were still following a faith that had been abrogated and was no longer valid, and that nullified their claim. The Qur'an refutes it repeatedly in different ways, and this verse adopts a special method. The ordinary way of settling a dispute is a discussion in which both sides present their arguments, but the Jews knew they could not win and shied away from it. So the Qur'an proposes an unusual trial requiring no great knowledge or understanding, only a little strain on the tongue. If the Jews were so sure that the blessings of the next world were theirs, let them declare that they wished to die, and that declaration would establish them as sincere. If they refused the challenge, it would show them to be liars. The Qur'an also predicted that they would never have the courage to go through with it.

### The same challenge in Sūrat al-Jumuʿah and at Najrān

Sūrat al-Jumuʿah gives a similar challenge in (62:6-8). If the Jews claim to be Allah's chosen people out of all humanity, they should wish for death. Their deeds prevent them from doing so. The death they flee will nevertheless overtake them and return them to the Knower of the seen and unseen. Because they claimed to be Allah's sons and beloved ones, and said that only Jews or Christians would enter Paradise, they were called on to invoke Allah to destroy the lying party, themselves or the Muslims. When they declined, everyone was sure they were in the wrong, for people certain of their claims would have accepted, and their lies stood exposed. In the same way the Messenger of Allah ﷺ called the delegation of the Christians of Najrān to a mutual curse after refuting them in a debate in which they had shown stubbornness and defiance. Allah told him to say to whoever disputed about ʿĪsā (Jesus) after knowledge had come: come and bring our children and yours, our women and yours, ourselves and yourselves, and then sincerely invoke Allah's curse on the liars (3:61). When the Christians heard the challenge, some said to each other, "By Allah, if you do this with this Prophet, not one of you will be left with an eye that blinks." So they made peace and paid the jizyah in humiliation, and the Prophet ﷺ accepted it and sent Abū ʿUbaydah ibn al-Jarrāḥ back with them as a trustee. Allah also commanded His Prophet ﷺ to tell the polytheists that whoever is entrenched in misguidance, the Most Compassionate will give him plenty of rope (19:75), that is, whichever of us has deviated, may Allah increase and prolong his deviation. This mutual invocation (mubāhalah) is called a "wish" here, because every just person wishes that Allah would destroy the unjust opponent debating with him, especially when he holds clear proof of the truth he calls to. And the mubāhalah involves invoking death on the unjust party because, for disbelievers, life is the greatest prize, above all when they know the evil destination that awaits them after it.

### Longing for death as a sign of faith

The devotional reading notes that one sign of ardent longing (ishtiyāq) is to desire death while enjoying the best of health. Whoever is confident beyond all doubt that the Garden is his longs for it ardently. The Jews did not long for the Garden, and Allah said that they never would, and the fulfilment of this was a miracle for the Messenger ﷺ, for it turned out as He had said. In this there is good news for believers who long for death: forgiveness is theirs, for Allah bestows that longing only so that it may be realised when they reach the Garden. As was said of old, for the negligent, shame will be enough on the Day of Meeting.

### In short

- If Paradise were exclusively the Jews', they should have longed for the death that leads to it, and they were invited to pray for death on whichever party was lying.
- Ibn ʿAbbās and the early authorities say that had they done so, they would have died on the spot, and Ibn Jarīr relates the Prophet's ﷺ word that they would have seen their seats in the Fire.
- The test required only words, yet the Jews, who knew the truth of Islam in their hearts, dared not take it, which is itself evidence for Islam.
- The same mubāhalah logic appears in 62:6-8, in the challenge to the Christians of Najrān (3:61), who chose the jizyah instead, and in 19:75.

<<<VERSE 2:95>>>
Verse 95 announces the outcome before the test is even taken. "But they will never wish for that because of what their hands have done. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of the wrongdoers".

### Phrase by phrase

**"But they will never wish for that"**. They will never ask for death. Given their hostility to the Prophet ﷺ, one might have expected them to seize on so simple a challenge, but in their heart of hearts they knew that the Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims stood for the truth and that they themselves were champions of falsehood, in fact unbelievers. They refused the trial because they were struck with awe, afraid that the moment they voiced the wish, death would come and they would be sent to Hell. Their refusal is itself evidence of the truth of Islam.

**"because of what their hands have done"**. Because of what they themselves had done in the religion of Judaism: disbelieving in Allah, rejecting His prophets, distorting His scriptures, and rejecting the Prophet ﷺ, the consequence of their mendacity. They knew that they recognised him and disbelieved in him anyway.

**"And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of the wrongdoers"**. He knows the evildoers, the disbelievers among them and among others, and He will requite them. Each person will be given what he has earned.

### Could they have wished in secret?

No one should suppose that the Jews perhaps accepted the challenge and wished for death silently in their hearts. First, the Qur'an itself predicts that they would never wish for it. Second, had they been able to wish it in their hearts, they would certainly have declared it aloud, since their victory lay in exactly such a declaration, and it offered the easiest possible way of refuting the Prophet ﷺ, yet they did not take the opportunity. Nor can one suppose that they did make a declaration that simply went unreported. The opponents of Islam have always outnumbered its adherents, and had any such thing happened they would have trumpeted it to the world to show that the Jews had passed the test the Qur'an itself proposed.

### In short

- The Jews never wished for death, exactly as the verse foretold, because their own deeds told them what awaited them.
- Their refusal to take so easy a test is an argument for Islam, and no secret or unreported acceptance is conceivable.
- Allah knows the wrongdoers and will repay each according to what he has earned.

<<<VERSE 2:96>>>
Verse 96 explains the refusal. "You will surely find them clinging to life more eagerly than any other people, even more than polytheists. Each one of them wishes to live a thousand years. But even if they were to live that long, it would not save them from the punishment. And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do".

### Phrase by phrase

**"You will surely find them clinging to life more eagerly than any other people"**. The sentence opens with an oath-particle in Arabic: you will certainly find the Jews the most covetous of mankind for life, eager to remain in this world even if that life is miserable and lowly. They are greedy for a longer life because they know their evil end and that their only reward with Allah is total loss. This world is a prison for the believer and a paradise for the disbeliever, so the People of the Book want to put off the Hereafter as long as they can. They will nevertheless meet what they are trying to escape.

**"even more than polytheists"**. Even greedier than the Arab idolaters, who reject the Resurrection and the reckoning. The Jews know that their journey ends in the Fire, while the idolaters do not believe even in that. The polytheists, who have no divine book, would naturally love a long life, since for them physical life was the be-all and end-all. The Jews acknowledged the next world, and even imagined that they alone deserved its joys, yet no one wished to stay in this world as long as they did. That very desire shows how hollow their pretension was, and that they themselves knew the fires of Hell awaited them, so that physical life, for as long as it lasted, was a kind of protection against their final fate. The Jews know what humiliation awaits them in the Hereafter for ignoring a truth they recognised. This is the basic weakness that kept them from accepting the challenge of the preceding verses.

**"Each one of them wishes to live a thousand years"**. Any one of them would love to be granted a life of a thousand years. The Arabic particle law, "if only", its clause spells out the object of the verb "would love".

**"But even if they were to live that long, it would not save them from the punishment"**. The granting of such a life would not budge him from the chastisement of the Fire. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq related Ibn ʿAbbās explaining that a long life will not save them from torment. ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said that the Jews are the most eager for this life and wish they could live a thousand years, but living a thousand years will not save them from torment any more than Iblīs's long life benefited him, since he was a disbeliever. No length of life, however great, would put any distance between them and Allah's punishment.

**"And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do"**. Allah knows what His servants do, good or evil, and will repay each accordingly. He sees their contraventions and rebellion, and what they hide about the description and traits of Muḥammad ﷺ. Nothing is hidden from Him. A variant reading has "what you do".

### What their history proves

That the Israelites were never really concerned with truth is proved by their own history. They had killed their own prophets, such as Zakariyyā (Zechariah) and Yaḥyā (John the Baptist), for no other reason than that these prophets criticised their way of life and tried to bring them back to the divine path, a fact recorded in their own scripture in Nehemiah 9:26. They had watched Mūsā (Moses) perform undeniable miracles, but as soon as he left them for forty days on Mount Sinai they began worshipping the golden calf, since only his personal authority had kept them in check. When the Mount was raised threateningly over their heads they agreed for a time, as Exodus 19:5, 8, 16 and 18 records, to do all that the Lord had spoken, but afterwards most of them went back to their disobedient ways. Had they truly been seeking God, their attention would have been fixed entirely on the life after death, whereas in fact they, more than anyone, were obsessed with love of the present world.

### Love of life as heedlessness

The devotional reading explains that love of this worldly life is the result of heedlessness (ghaflah) of God, and the worst heedlessness is their excessive love of remaining in this world. The believer's state is the opposite. The heedless and the insolent are eager for life only because they know how they have failed in obedience, just as a runaway slave has no wish to be brought back to his master. For believers, the passage of death to the One from whom only good is expected is better than remaining among those from whose evil they are never safe. Prolonging life in the face of certain death holds no value for them, since death comes unannounced and life is cut short. What is coming is near, and when a life span is complete nothing can fend off the assault of the appointed time from the shoulders of hope.

### The occasion that follows

The verse leads into the next passage. ʿAbdullāh ibn Ṣūryā asked the Prophet ﷺ, or, in another report, ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, which angel brings down the revelation. Told that it was Jibrīl (Gabriel), he said: "He is our enemy, because he brings chastisement. Had it been Mīkāʾīl (Michael) we would have believed, for he brings fertility and security." The verses that follow were revealed in answer to this.

### In short

- The Jews cling to life more than anyone, even more than idolaters who deny the Resurrection, because they know what awaits them.
- A thousand years of life would not move them an inch away from the punishment, as Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Zayd ibn Aslam explained, no more than Iblīs's long life helped him.
- Ibn Ṣūryā's objection to Jibrīl, which the verse leads into, caps the picture. Their own history, prophets killed, the calf, the broken pledge at Sinai, shows a people more attached to this world than to truth.
- Love of this life is heedlessness of Allah. The believer, expecting only good from Him, does not fear the journey to Him.
