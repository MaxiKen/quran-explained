<<<VERSE 4:66>>>
The counterfactual that answers the taunt: "If We had commanded them to sacrifice themselves or abandon their homes, none would have obeyed except for a few. Had they done what they were advised to do, it would have certainly been far better for them and more reassuring". The verses before it closed the file of Bishr, who first proposed Kaʿb ibn Ashraf and then refused the Prophet's ﷺ true decision. Verse 66 answers what the Jews said in the affair's wake.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If We had commanded them to sacrifice themselves or abandon their homes"** states what Allah knows of what has not occurred, and how it would be if it did. Even if people were commanded to commit what they were prohibited from, most would not submit, for their nature is to dispute orders.. The reference reaches back to the Children of Israel, Banī Isrāʾ in the Arabic, who were commanded to kill one another so their repentance would be accepted. The command was severe, and they obeyed it to the loss of seventy thousand. The other charge was to go forth from their dwellings empty-handed as was decreed for them..

**"none would have obeyed except for a few"** counts the doers. The innocent killing the guilty, or the leaving of homes, would have found few hands. One reading names among them the leader Thābit ibn Qays ibn Shammas, the Anṣārī..

**"Had they done what they were advised to do"** means doing what they were commanded and refraining from what they were prohibited, the repentance and sincerity they were exhorted to.

**"it would have certainly been far better for them and more reassuring"** weighs the outcomes. Better than disobedience, it brings a firmer conviction of faith, as as-Suddī read the strengthening, more real in this world and better in the Hereafter..

### The taunt and the answer

When the news of Bishr's affair got around the city of Madinah, the Jews chided the Muslims. What sort of people are you, they asked, believing in a Messenger of Allah yet not accepting his decisions?? Look, they said, we were commanded to kill each other for our repentance, a severe command, and we obeyed it, seventy thousand of our people killed. Your lot, given such a charge, would have refused it. Then the verse came down with its conditional. Had these hypocrites, or common people generally, been charged with something severe, the self-killing or the migration like the Banī Isrāʾ's, very few, being what they are, would have obeyed..

This is a stern warning for those who take the solutions of their disputes outside the Law and Way of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. It is at the same time an answer to the chiding, for the state of affairs fits the hypocrites and certainly not those firm in faith.. When the verse was revealed, someone among the noble Companions said: Allah did not put us through this trial. The Prophet ﷺ answered: there are people in my community in whose hearts faith is entrenched firm like mountains. Ibn Wahab says the speaker was Abū Bakr. By another narration, when Abū Bakr heard the verse he said that had this command been prescribed, by God he would have been the first to sacrifice himself and his family to obey. And by some narrations the Prophet ﷺ said that had the command to self-kill or migrate come, Umm ʿAbd, ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd, would have acted accordingly. As for migration itself, the Companions had already proven it, leaving homes, properties and businesses in Makkah for Madinah.

### In short

- The counterfactual exposes the disputing nature: a severe command would have found few doers among them.
- The Jews' boast of seventy thousand killed is answered: the trial fits the hypocrites, not the firm.
- Abū Bakr's pledge to be first, and Ibn Masʿūd's name, stand against the few, and the Companions' actual migration had already answered the leaving of homes.

<<<VERSE 4:67>>>
The reward held out: "and We would have granted them a great reward by Our grace". One line of promise, tied to the obedience the previous verse found so rare.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We would have granted them"** follows the ladder: had they confirmed themselves in what they were admonished to do, the giving would have followed.

**"a great reward by Our grace"** is from Us, as the verse's Arabic has it, and the great reward is Paradise, the abundant reward in the hereafter. In the reading by allusion, the great reward is the thawāb ʿaẓīm given from Us, with an enduring gift, ʿaṭāʾ muqīm, rendered besides.

### The giving behind the verse

The promise sits inside a passage thick with reports. Behind it stand the Anṣārī's sadness, Thābit ibn Qays ibn Shammas, and Rabīʿah ibn Kaʿb al-Aslamī, who brought the water for wudu at the tahajjud hours. Jibrīl's descents run through it, and so does the hadith only Aḥmad recorded. ʿAmr ibn Murrah al-Juhanī's report of the five prayers, the zakah and the fasting of Ramaḍān stands beside the great commentary's chains through Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ash-Shaʿbī and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas. Al-Ḥāfiẓ Abū ʿAbdullāh al-Maqdisī gathered all of it in his book Ṣifat al-Jannah, and Abū Bakr ibn Marduwyah before him, with Muslim and al-Bukhārī carrying the central reports. The reward itself needs no narrator: it is the great wage from Us. This great reward, promised here at the end of verse 67, is described in detail in verses 69 and 70, which unfold the ranks it holds, and verse 68 shows the road that reaches it.

### In short

- The great reward is Paradise, given from Us, not earned.
- Read inwardly, the ʿaẓīm reward comes with an enduring gift for the obedient.

<<<VERSE 4:68>>>
The road itself: "and guided them to the Straight Path". Better for them, then rewarded from Us, and now guided, in this life and the Hereafter.

### Phrase by phrase

**"guided them"** means that had they stood firm in the obedience they were admonished to, Allah would have made them firm in this world upon an established religion He is pleased with.

**"to the Straight Path"** is the ṣirāṭ mustaqīm, the path that leads to Him and to His Paradise, the straight path laid out in the Book of God and the sayings of the Prophet.

### The question the verse answers

The path was promised, and the question came at once. Some Companions asked the Prophet ﷺ how they would see him in Paradise when he would be in the highest stations and they lower than him. The following verse came down in answer, with this one standing as its doorstep. The whole run of reports stands behind it. There is the Jews' taunt of the killing command, and the Anṣārī with Thābit ibn Qays ibn Shammas. The migration from Makkah to Madinah proved the leaving of homes. Jibrīl's descents, the tahajjud and the water for wudu, the zakah and the fasting of Ramaḍān in ʿAmr ibn Murrah al-Juhanī's report that only Aḥmad carries, all belong to it. So do the chains of Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ash-Shaʿbī, ar-Rabīʿ and as-Suddī, gathered in the Ṣifat al-Jannah of al-Ḥāfiẓ Abū ʿAbdullāh al-Maqdisī. Ibn Jarīr and al-Bukhārī hold the reports, and verse 69 carries the answer to the Companions' question.

### In short

- The guided path is an established religion in this world and the road to Paradise in the next.
- The Companions' question about seeing him ﷺ in the high stations is what the next verse answers.

<<<VERSE 4:69>>>
The answer to every longing heart in the passage: "And whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger will be in the company of those blessed by Allah: the prophets, the people of truth, the martyrs, and the righteous—what honourable company". A universal promise after the special addressees of the verses before, and the most beloved hadith of the Companions standing under it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger"** means implementing what they commanded and avoiding what they prohibited, obeying Allah regarding the obligations and the Prophet ﷺ regarding the prophetic practices.

**"will be in the company of those blessed by Allah"** means Allah will grant him a dwelling in the Residence of Honour, placed in the company of the prophets and those lesser in grade.

**"the prophets"** enjoy the support of the Divine power, like someone seeing something from a close range, so that Allah says of such a one: "Do you argue with him over what he sees?" (53:12).

**"the people of truth"** are the ṣiddīqīn, lexically the very truthful. In the Islamic terminology they are the Companions of a prophet who excel all others in submission, enjoying the highest status of piety after the prophets, like Abū Bakr, who believed in the very initial stage without hesitation or hostility.. They are people close to the prophets in spiritual excellence, like someone seeing from a distance. Somebody asked ʿAlī whether he had seen Allah Almighty. He said he could not worship something he had not seen, then added that though people have not seen Allah with their eyes, their hearts have seen Him through the realities of faith..

**"the martyrs"**, the shuhadāʾ, sacrificed their lives and wealth in the way of Allah, a word not translated by martyr, since martyr is also applied to those killed in ethnic or racial wars. In the stations of knowledge they recognize their objective through the chain of reasons and proofs, with no access to direct vision, like someone seeing something in a mirror from close range. It is very similar to Ḥārithah's word that he seemed to see the Throne of his most sublime Lord..

**"the righteous"** are the ṣāliḥīn, who hold themselves, outwardly and inwardly, to only the good and the right. Their objective is reached by following the precepts of the sharīʿah, like someone seeing something in a mirror from a distance, the level of "and if you cannot see Him, then He sees you anyway"..

**"what honourable company"** promises the pleasures of Paradise: seeing them, visiting them, being in their presence, even with the difference of stations.

### The occasions of the promise

Ibn Jarīr recorded that Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said that an Anṣārī man came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ feeling sad. Being asked, he told the whole of it: his days and nights were spent looking at the Prophet's ﷺ face and sitting by him, and tomorrow the Prophet would be raised with the prophets, out of their sight.. He said nothing in answer, until Jibrīl came down with this verse, and he sent the good news to the man. The mursal narrations run from Masrūq, ʿIkrimah, ʿĀmir ash-Shaʿbī, Qatādah and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas. Abū Bakr ibn Marduwyah recorded a different chain from ʿĀʾishah. A man told the Prophet ﷺ he was dearer to him than himself, his family and children, and that remembering him at home he could not wait to look at him. Contemplating his death, he knew the Prophet ﷺ would be housed with the prophets in Paradise's highest stations, and feared he might never see him there.. The Prophet ﷺ did not answer until the verse came down. Al-Ḥāfiẓ Abū ʿAbdullāh al-Maqdisī collected it in his Ṣifat al-Jannah, commenting that he saw no problem with the chain.

Ibn ʿAbbās says the verse came down concerning Thawbān, whom the love of God's Messenger had wasted and weakened, bending his back and yellowing his face. Asked whether night-waking or discipline had done it, and then whether he was desiring, Thawbān wept: he did not know how the nights passed until day came and he saw him again. Wishing for him by day was the veil of his idleness, aching for him by night the chamber of his wakefulness, separation from him the adornment of his sorrow. The hardest sorrow was that in the next world the Messenger would be in the highest of the High Chambers, and he would be held back from seeing him. Mid-conversation arrived Gabriel, the Presence's messenger, the courier of mercy, with the verse, and God's Messenger recited it, gladdened his heart and laid the balm on his woundedness. Yes, it takes a pain like this for a balm like that to appear. Without burning, no way out opens. Without drowning in the ocean of remembrance, the shore of security is never reached by the hand of "Remember Me, I will remember you" (2:152). The pain of Ishmael calls for the burn of Abraham.

### The forms of the meeting

The meeting in Paradise takes forms. Paradise's inmates see the strata above them as stars are seen, by the Muʾatta's report from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī. The strata above come down to those below, to meet and sit with them, as Ibn Jarīr reported from ar-Rabīʿ. And those below may be permitted upward visits. And those below may be permitted to visit the higher strata, as the Prophet ﷺ gave many the glad tidings of being with him in Paradise. In Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, Rabīʿah ibn Kaʿb al-Aslamī, who used to spend nights at the Prophet's house, brought his water for wudu and the miswak at the hour of tahajjud. Being told to ask, he asked for his company in Paradise.. Anything else, said the Prophet ﷺ, and being told no, he said: then help me fulfil this wish by performing many prostrations, the sujūd of plenty of voluntary prayers. By the Musnad of Aḥmad, from ʿAmr ibn Murrah al-Juhanī, a man testified to the unity of Allah and the messengership, prayed the five, gave the zakah and fasted Ramaḍān. The Prophet ﷺ said the one dying in that state is with the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs on the Day of Resurrection, as long as, finger raised for the condition, his parents never met disobedience from him.. Only Aḥmad recorded this hadith. And at-Tirmidhī adds the businessman who is truthful and trusty to the prophets', the ṣiddīqīn's and the shuhadāʾ's company.

Love is the way of nearness. On being asked about a person who loves a people without achieving their standards of deeds, the Prophet ﷺ said: one is with those whom he loves, in the mutawātir reports of al-Bukhārī from many Companions. Anas said Muslims were never happier than with this hadith. In another narration he named his loves, the Messenger of Allah, Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, hoping to be resurrected with them though his actions fell short of theirs.. The company is not restricted to any colour or race. In al-Muʿjam al-Kabīr, al-Ṭabarānī reported from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar that a man from Ethiopia came and asked whether, believing and doing as the Prophet ﷺ did, he could have the honour of being with him in Paradise. The answer: definitely yes, and by the Power whose hands held his life, the black race will become fair and beautiful in Paradise, radiating from a distance of a thousand years. Then the Ethiopian asked whether his eyes would see the blessing the Prophet's blessed eyes would witness. Hearing "definitely yes", he wept until he died in that state, and the Prophet ﷺ performed his last rites, bathing, shrouding, the funeral prayer and the burial, with his own blessed hands.. The exchange with this brother from Ethiopia carried the recitation of the opening of Sūrat ad-Daḥr, spelled Ad-Daher in one report, that man has passed through a period of being nothing at all (76:1). It carried too the report of the one hundred and twenty-four thousand virtues written for "Pure is Allah and praised is He". These stand beside the mountain-crushing deeds that still leave man's virtues no match for Allah's blessings, unless He elects to be generous..

### The ranks and their convergence

Some commentators say all four ranks can be found in one person, for the Qur'an itself names a prophet a siddīq. It says of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) "surely he was a man of truth, a prophet" (19:41), of Yaḥyā "a prophet from among the righteous" (3:39), and of ʿĪsā that he shall speak to the people in the cradle and in middle age and be one of the righteous (3:46).. The attributes overlap as the scholar's attributions do, the commentator, the ḥadīth scholar, the jurist and the historian converging in one person. By common practice, a person is identified with the attribute dominating his life-work. So the ṣiddīqīn mean the most illustrious Companions, the shuhadāʾ the martyrs of Uḥud, and the ṣāliḥīn the general run of righteous Muslims.. Imām Rāghib al-Isfahānī determined all four ranks different, a view the al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ, the Rūḥ al-Maʿānī and the Mazharī carry. On this reading, Allah classified true Muslims in four ranks, appointing stations of precedence, and inducing common Muslims not to lag behind in reaching for them.. Prophethood itself can never be acquired by effort, but its company can, and the path to it runs through obedience and love.

### In short

- The obedient are promised the company of the four ranks, with seeing, visiting and presence despite the difference of stations.
- The occasions are the longing of the Anṣārī, of Thābit ibn Qays and of Thawbān, whose yellowed face drew Gabriel down with the verse.
- The sujūd of many prostrations, the five prayers with zakah and Ramaḍān fasting, and the truthful business all earn the company, and love is its shortest road.
- One is with those whom he loves, said the mutawātir hadith, and no Companions were ever happier with any word.
- The ranks may converge in one person, and their gradations are gradations of the knowledge of one's Lord.

<<<VERSE 4:70>>>
The closing attribution: "This is Allah's favour, and Allah fully knows ˹who deserves it˺" (4:70). The company of verse 69 is not a wage earned. It is a bounty given, and the Giver knows His own gifts.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is Allah's favour"** names the origin of the whole gift. The origin is Allah's mercy, for He it was who made them suitable for it, and their good deeds were not the cause. The being-with is bounty, favour unhooked from every cause, merit and reason. What comes to them, and what happens through them, is the disbursement of His bounty, and it originates in His generosity. He favoured them with it, and they did not earn it through their obedience.

**"and Allah fully knows"** means His knowledge suffices. He knows those who deserve guidance and success. He knows the states of the people, and He will reward them all accordingly. He suffices as Knower of the reward of the Hereafter, so trust what He has told you, for none can tell like One Who is aware.

### Not a wage

The verse closes the promise of the passage with a correction of the ledger. Reading the four ranks of verse 69, a hearer might begin to count his deeds against them, as if the prophets' company were a price paid at a scale. The verse lifts the arithmetic away. The station of prophethood itself is never acquired by effort and struggle. What effort can reach is the company of the prophets, and even that arriving is mercy: He made them suitable for it before they walked a step of the way. The suitableness, not the stride, is the gift.

So the Anṣārī who grieved that he would lose the Prophet's ﷺ face was not owed an answer. The man in ʿĀʾishah's report, who loved him dearer than self and family, could not claim one. Thawbān's yellowed face and bent back could not purchase the descent of Gabriel. The answers came down anyway, verse after verse, because the Giver gave them. What the reports preserve is not a transaction but a tenderness.

### The key and the Giver

Obedience to al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ was made the key that opens the stations of prophethood, truthfulness and martyrdom, in a manner suitable for the community and appropriate to his high rank. A key, though, is not the treasure. It opens what the owner allows, and the owner here is Allah, whose favour the verse now names. The believer turns the key by prayer, zakah, fasting and the prostrations Rabīʿah was told to multiply, and the door swings open on what was never his to demand.

Read inwardly, the same order holds. He detached the situation of the honoured ones from every cause, merit and reason. Their arrival is the disbursement of His bounty, and their standing among the ranks is His generosity at work. The masters of the inward road hear in the close of the passage what they heard at its start: nothing comes from the servant, everything comes from the Lord.

### The Knower of hidden love

The closing name of Allah carries a special comfort. He suffices as Knower of the love of Thawbān, and of the honour and reward bestowed upon him in Paradise. The love was hidden. No scale weighed it, no ledger held it, and Thawbān himself could only stammer of nights that would not pass. The All-Knowing had weighed it already, and the answer had his name on it.

So it is with every longing the passage records, the Anṣārī's evenings, the man who could not wait to look at him again, the Ethiopian's weeping. Allah fully knows who deserves what He gives, of every heart in Madinah and Makkah and beyond, and the verse hands the whole matter over to that knowledge. No one is lost from a register that complete.

### In short

- The company is bounty, not wage, detached from merit and cause, and given by mercy.
- Obedience to al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ is the key, and the favour turns it.
- Prophethood is never earned by effort, but its company is promised to the obedient.
- The All-Knowing Giver needs no auditor of His gifts, and no hidden love is lost to Him.
