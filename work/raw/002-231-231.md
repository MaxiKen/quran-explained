<<<VERSE 2:231>>>
Divorce and reconciliation must both be handled honourably: "either retain them honourably or let them go honourably." The verse addresses the point when a revocably divorced wife's waiting period is nearly over. Her husband may restore the marriage or let the separation become complete. He must not reclaim her simply to hurt her, extend her confinement or extract money. Allah knows the purpose behind an outwardly legal act.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When you divorce women and they have ˹almost˺ reached the end of their waiting period,"** The opportunity for return is still open because the 'iddah, the waiting period, has not quite finished. The command concerns one or two revocable divorces, not the situation after a third divorce. One explanation locates this point before the ritual bath following the third menstruation.

**"either retain them honourably or let them go honourably."** Retaining means returning to real married life with kindness and respect for the wife's rights. Releasing means leaving the divorce to become complete at the end of the period, without fighting, insults or unjust demands. Honour is required in both choices. It is not a condition reserved for the choice the husband prefers.

**"But do not retain them ˹only˺ to harm them ˹or˺ to take advantage ˹of them˺."** A right of revocation cannot be used to keep a woman captive. Prohibited purposes include extending her waiting period, preventing her from marrying, forcing her to pay for release or reclaiming her only to divorce her again. The words concern intention as well as visible procedure.

**"Whoever does that surely wrongs his own soul."** Harm to the wife also exposes the husband to sin and punishment. His ability to use a legal power does not protect him from the consequences of defying the command that governs it.

**"Do not take Allah’s revelations lightly."** Allah's laws are not a game. Contradicting their purpose through manipulative conduct is one form of mockery. Another is pronouncing marriage, divorce or release and then trying to escape their consequences by saying that the words were only a joke.

**"Remember Allah’s favours upon you as well as the Book and wisdom He has sent down for your guidance."** His favours include Islam, the sending of His Messenger ﷺ and the revelation of clear guidance. The Book is the Qur'an. Al-Hikmah, wisdom, is explained as the Prophet's ﷺ Sunnah, his legal teaching and practice, and as the rulings distinguishing lawful from unlawful. Remembering these gifts means acting gratefully on their instruction.

**"Be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things"** Taqwa here requires obeying what Allah commands and avoiding what He forbids. He knows public conduct, hidden intentions and secret harm. Nothing in the husband's purpose or the couple's circumstances lies outside that knowledge.

### What abuse does the verse forbid?

A man could divorce his wife, wait until she was nearly free, then reclaim her only to prevent her marrying someone else. After another divorce, he would repeat the process and prolong the waiting period again. Ibn 'Abbas, Masruq, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Ar-Rabi' and Muqatil bin Hayyan identify this as the conduct prohibited here.

The injury is not confined to physical violence. Keeping someone in prolonged uncertainty or using legal forms to confine her can itself be harm. One explanation specifically includes pressure to obtain compensation from her. A claim to have followed the outward sequence cannot excuse a purpose that the verse expressly forbids.

After a decision to separate, reconciliation should still be considered where it is genuinely possible. But considering reconciliation is different from exploiting the possibility of return. The husband must intend to leave old anger behind, repair the relationship and give his wife her rights. If that intention is absent, he should allow the separation to finish.

The inward instruction makes the same demand in the language of companionship. Anger and stubborn quarrelling must be relinquished. Either part without roughness or maintain the relationship with real faithfulness. Neither keeping nor releasing provides a legitimate setting for cruelty.

### Why should revocation have witnesses?

The discussion connects return with (65:2), which directs the use of two trustworthy witnesses and requires truthful testimony for Allah's sake. Two trustworthy Muslims should witness the revocation. This protects the facts of what occurred and provides evidence if the wife disputes a claim about return.

Without witnesses, a husband might claim after the waiting period has ended that he had already revoked the divorce before its expiry. That claim could be driven by selfishness or temptation rather than truth. Witnesses help close this route to injustice. The instruction is therefore a practical safeguard, not merely ceremonial decoration.

A valid return during the waiting period does not require a fresh marriage contract. But the absence of that requirement does not mean it should happen without clarity, evidence or a sincere purpose of restoring married life.

### What does honourable release involve?

If anger remains after the time allowed for reflection, continued marriage may not be the right course. Separation should then be completed according to the shariah, Islamic law, rather than made an occasion for retaliation. A quarrel between two people can otherwise spread to two families and harm them in this life and the next.

The wife must not be deprived of what she is owed. The mahr, her marriage payment, is not to be recovered without the valid grounds already explained. Nor should the husband demand another payment merely because she needs freedom from his refusal to live properly with her.

The discussion sets out the provision due during the 'iddah: she remains in the family home and receives her maintenance. If intercourse has occurred, an unpaid marriage payment must be paid in full. Where divorce occurs before intercourse, half the payment is due in the case described. These are presented as obligations, not optional gestures that can be withdrawn in anger.

Beyond those claims, a parting gift of cash, clothing or another benefit is commended. The discussion points ahead to (2:241), which speaks of provision for divorced women. Some claims are obligatory and others belong to generous treatment. Together they show that ending a contract need not turn the parties into enemies.

Kindness at departure is consistent with the goodwill once shown during marriage. Separation ends a particular relationship, not every duty of humane conduct. The husband should seek an end to disputes and failures of mutual rights, not an opportunity to disgrace or wound his former wife.

### Why is the oppressor said to injure himself?

The wife is truly harmed, but the man also brings consequences upon himself. In the Hereafter, cruelty must be answered and the oppressed person's claim addressed. A person cannot assume that escaping an earthly remedy settles the matter before Allah.

The discussion also reflects on consequences in this life. Injustice may disgrace its author or return upon him through misfortunes, whether or not he recognises the connection. Shaykh Sa'di's Persian couplet is paraphrased through an image of a weapon missing its victim and turning back against the attacker. The apparent victory of oppression can become the oppressor's own injury.

The Qur'an thus gives more than a bare penal rule. It explains the wisdom of the command, appeals to conscience and describes what is lost by disobedience. The reminders of Allah and final accountability are part of the law's instruction, not unrelated devotional additions.

### What happens if someone claims to have been joking?

One explanation of treating revelation lightly is acting against its commands while claiming respect for their outward wording. Another concerns explicit statements whose consequences the speaker later tries to dismiss as amusement.

Abu al-Darda' describes a pre-Islamic practice of pronouncing divorce or freeing an enslaved person and then saying it was only a joke. The ruling given here does not accept that excuse. Al-Hasan, Qatadah, 'Ata' Al-Khurasani, Ar-Rabi' and Muqatil bin Hayyan likewise describe people who divorced, married or declared someone free and then denied seriousness.

A prophetic report from Ibn al-'Abbas, recorded by Ibn Marduwayh, names divorce, emancipation and marriage as matters whose serious and joking forms have the same effect. The same instruction is also transmitted from 'Ubadah ibn al-Samit. Another wording from Abu Hurayrah names marriage, divorce and revocation of divorce.

The examples are concrete. A man and woman who complete proposal and acceptance before witnesses cannot simply dismiss the marriage as a game. A divorce pronounced in clear words takes effect even where the speaker claims that he was joking. A revocation is likewise not emptied of consequence by that claim. The historical example of freeing an enslaved person follows the same principle: the freedom is not revoked by saying the declaration was playful.

These rules concern the deliberate use of the relevant clear acts and expressions. They are not an erasure of the earlier distinction between an unintended slip of speech and a chosen statement. The point is that solemn institutions cannot be deliberately enacted and then casually denied.

### What warning was given to the Ash'ari tribe?

Ibn Jarir relates from Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari that the Prophet ﷺ became angry concerning conduct among the Ash'ari people. Abu Musa asked about that anger. The Prophet ﷺ objected to a man moving lightly between declarations that he had divorced and taken back his wife. He said that this was not the appropriate way for Muslims to handle divorce and directed attention to the prescribed period.

Masruq connects such mockery with the man who repeatedly divorces and returns to prolong his wife's wait. The legal words themselves must not become toys. The gravity of marriage requires more than confidently uttering them.

### What form of divorce leaves room for repair?

Where no alternative remains, the basic method commended here is one clear, revocable divorce. This preserves the possibility of return. Wording that produces an immediate irrevocable separation, called al-talaq al-ba'in, should not be chosen merely out of anger. Reaching three divorces should also be avoided, because a straightforward new marriage between the former spouses then becomes haram, unlawful.

The verse speaks generally of divorcing women while giving instructions applicable to revocable divorce. The discussion sees this as an indication that this is the ordinary approved form, with other forms carrying disapproval. It does not turn permission for divorce into permission to discard the safeguards around it.

### In short

- Return before the waiting period ends must restore genuine companionship, not prolong harm.
- Witnesses protect the facts of revocation and prevent false claims after the period has expired.
- Honourable release includes paying what is due and treating the former wife with dignity.
- Clear acts of marriage, divorce and revocation cannot be dismissed as jokes.
- Allah knows the hidden purpose behind legal conduct, and injustice ultimately injures its author too.
