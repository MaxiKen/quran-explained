<<<VERSE 7:172>>>
Allah recalls a moment before human history began: "And ˹remember˺ when your Lord brought forth from the loins of the children of Adam their descendants and had them testify regarding themselves." He put a question to them: "˹Allah asked,˺ “Am I not your Lord?” They replied, “Yes, You are! We testify.”" And He closed off any future excuse: "˹He cautioned,˺ “Now you have no right to say on Judgment Day, ‘We were not aware of this.’" This verse and the next describe the great covenant that the Creator made with all of His creation even before they took on their existence. It is known as the covenant of Alast.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember˺ when your Lord brought forth"** Mention, O Muhammad, when your Lord took out their seed on the Day of the Covenant.

**"from the loins of the children of Adam"** In the Arabic, "from their loins" (min ẓuhūrihim) is an inclusive substitute for "from the children of Adam" (min banī Ādama), repeating the same preposition. Allah brought them out of one another's loins, all of them ultimately from the loins of Adam, offspring after offspring, in the way they multiply.

**"their descendants"** The word is dhurriyyah. Imām Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī said it comes from a root meaning "to create", and the Qur'an uses it in that sense in a number of verses. So dhurriyyah means all who are created. The covenant therefore included every human being, since all are the progeny of Adam.

**"and had them testify regarding themselves."** They bore witness that Allah is their Lord and King and that no one but He deserves worship. Allah set up proofs of His Lordship for them and gave them the faculty of reason. He made acknowledging that He created them and is their Lord part of their innate nature. He created them on this fiṭrah. As another verse says: "Let there be no change in this creation of Allah." (30:30). The two reports quoted under 7:30 make the same point. One, in the two Ṣaḥīḥs from Abū Hurayrah, says that every child is born on the fiṭrah. In the other, which Muslim recorded from ʿIyāḍ ibn Ḥimār, Allah says He created His servants pure in faith, ḥunafāʾ.

**"˹Allah asked,˺ “Am I not your Lord?”"** This was the pledge Allah took from all humankind. Its aim was to have them acknowledge that He is their Nourisher and Sustainer, the Lord of all created beings.

**"They replied, “Yes, You are! We testify.”"** We know and acknowledge that You are our Lord. Allah then told the angels to bear witness over them, and told them to bear witness over one another.

**"˹He cautioned,˺ “Now you have no right to say on Judgment Day,"** The testimony was taken so that they could not say otherwise. The verb can be read "lest they say" or "lest you say". Allah said, in effect, that He asked them this so that they would not deny His proofs on the Day of Judgement.

**"‘We were not aware of this.’"** "This" is tawḥīd, the oneness of Allah, and the covenant. They cannot claim that they knew nothing of it, or that no pledge was taken from them. The pledge Allah took lit a candle of faith in their hearts. With a little reflection they can easily recognise Him as their Lord, so the excuse of ignorance will be of no use to them on the Day of Judgement.

### Was the testimony spoken or shown?

Allah made them testify about themselves both by their condition and in words. Testimony is sometimes given in words, as in: "They will say, “We confess against ourselves!”" (6:130). At other times a person's state is itself the testimony. Allah says: "It is not for the polytheists to maintain the mosques of Allah while they openly profess disbelief." (9:17). That verse means their disbelief bears witness against them, not that they literally testify against themselves there. Another example is: "and they certainly attest to this—" (100:7), meaning by their deeds.

Asking works the same way. It is sometimes done in words and sometimes through a person's situation. Allah says: "And He has granted you all that you asked Him for." (14:34).

### What do the hadiths add?

Several hadiths tell how Allah drew Adam's offspring out of his loins and set some on the right side and some on the left. Imām Aḥmad recorded from Anas ibn Mālik that the Prophet ﷺ said a man of the Fire will be asked on the Day of Resurrection whether he would give the whole earth as ransom if he owned it. He will say yes. Allah will tell him that He asked far less of him while he was still in Adam's loins: to associate nothing with Him. But he insisted on associating others with Him. This is also recorded in the two Ṣaḥīḥs.

Commenting on this verse, at-Tirmidhī recorded a hadith from Abū Hurayrah. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that after creating Adam, Allah wiped his back, and every soul He would create from him until the Day of Resurrection fell out of it. Allah placed a gleaming light between the eyes of each, on their foreheads, and showed them to Adam. Adam asked who they were, and Allah said they were his offspring. Adam was pleased by the light of one man and asked about him. Allah said he was a man from the later generations of his offspring, named Dāwūd (David).

Adam asked how long Dāwūd would live, and was told sixty years. He said: My Lord, I give him forty years of my own life. When Adam's life came to its end and the angel of death came for his soul, Adam said: do I not still have forty years left? The angel replied: did you not give them to your son Dāwūd? Adam denied it, and so his offspring deny. Adam forgot, and his offspring forget. Adam erred, and his offspring err.

At-Tirmidhī graded this hadith ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ and said it was reported through several chains from Abū Hurayrah. Al-Ḥākim also recorded it in his Mustadrak. He said it is ṣaḥīḥ by Muslim's criteria, though they did not record it.

Imām Mālik, Abū Dāwūd, at-Tirmidhī and Imām Aḥmad reported from Muslim ibn Yasār that some people asked the Caliph ʿUmar al-Fārūq about this verse. ʿUmar said the same question had been put to the Prophet ﷺ, and he heard his answer. Allah first created Adam, then passed His hand over his back and drew out all the righteous descendants who would come into being. He said: I have created these for Paradise, and they will do the deeds of its people. Then He passed His hand over Adam's back again, and all his wicked descendants appeared. He said: I have created these for the Fire, and they will do the deeds of its people.

One of the Companions asked: if Allah has already decided people's fate, what use are good deeds? The Prophet ﷺ replied that whoever is created for Paradise takes up the deeds of its people and dies doing them. Whoever is created for the Fire falls into the deeds of its people and dies doing them. Since no one knows which group he belongs to, he should put all his effort into the deeds of the people of Paradise and hope to be among them.

The Tradition reported by Imām Aḥmad from the Companion Abū al-Dardāʾ adds that those who came out the first time were shining white, and those who came out the second time were dark. These and similar hadiths show that Allah brought Adam's offspring out of his loins and separated the people of Paradise from the people of the Fire.

### From Adam's back, or from his children's?

The hadiths describe Adam's children coming out of Adam's own back. The verse describes them coming out of the backs of his children. There is no real conflict. Adam's direct offspring are described as coming from his back, and the generations after them as coming from the backs of his descendants.

### Spirits alone, or bodies too?

This suggests that the children of Adam who came out of his back were not spirits alone. They also had some kind of body made of fine elements. Nourishing, the work of a Lord, concerns the body, which is brought from one stage to the next. Spirits need no such nourishment, since they remain in one state from the moment of their creation. The hadiths support this. Their mention of colours, and of brilliance on the forehead, requires some material form, since spirits plainly have no colour.

### How could all humanity fit in one place?

One may wonder how all the human beings to be created until the Last Day could gather in one place. The tradition from Abū al-Dardāʾ answers this. They did not appear in their usual size but as tiny as small ants. In an age of scientific knowledge, it should be no surprise that a full-sized person can be reduced to the size of an ant. Science has found a system like the solar system at work around the nucleus of an atom. Books of hundreds of pages can be reduced to a small dot on microfilm. So there is nothing surprising in Allah, the All-Powerful, reducing them to the size of ants on this occasion.

### When and where was it taken?

ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās answered this, as Imām Aḥmad and an-Nasāʾī reported through authentic chains. The covenant was taken when Adam was sent down from heaven to earth. The place was the valley of Naʿmān, the plain of ʿArafāt near Makkah. Another explanation places it in that valley on the Day of ʿArafah, where the multitude looked like small ants.

### How could the unborn recognise their Lord?

This pledge was taken before any human being except Adam had been created. So how could Adam's children have the knowledge and reason to acknowledge Allah as their Lord and Nourisher? Recognising that seems to need the experience of being nourished, which was impossible before coming into the world.

The answer is simple. Allah has power to do anything He wills. If He could make every human being appear in the size of an ant, He could far more easily give them enough knowledge and reason to recognise their Lord. He brought them forth in a small size, with body and soul and every function a complete human being needs. Reason and understanding, the most important of those functions, must have been included.

### What use is a covenant no one remembers?

One more question remains. What value can a covenant have when it came before people's actual creation and they do not remember it on earth? Some individuals have in fact remembered it. The great spiritual master Dhū al-Nūn al-Miṣrī said that he remembered this covenant as clearly as if he were hearing it at that very moment. Some elders are said to have remembered even the people standing near them. Such cases are rare, though, and do not answer the question.

The real answer is that many acts work by their very nature, whether or not anyone remembers or understands them. They leave their mark naturally. One clear example is the Muslim practice of saying the adhān in a newborn's right ear and the iqāmah in his left. The adhān is the call to ṣalāh, a bold declaration that Allah is One, has no partner and is the Greatest. The iqāmah repeats it and adds that the prayer is about to begin.

The baby neither understands this call nor remembers it as an adult. Its wisdom is to revive the pledge he made with Allah and to sow the seed of faith in his heart by repeating the covenant's message in his ears. Its effect can be seen in every Muslim, even one who does not practise. He is proud to call himself a Muslim and would hate to lose that identity. The command to recite the Qur'an even to those who know no Arabic may have the same reason. The impact of its words lights up their hearts and renews their faith in Allah.

The wisdom of the covenant is likewise to sow the seed of faith in every human heart. That seed draws nourishment from the soil of the heart whether people are aware of it or not. Its fruit is the love and reverence for the Creator that is part of human nature. This love may take wrong forms, such as worshipping idols or created beings. Yet all worship, true or false, is in itself an expression of love and reverence for the Creator. Billions of people hold this love for Allah and express it in worship shaped by their knowledge or their ignorance. Only a few, absorbed in worldly pursuits, have dulled their natural understanding and forgotten the pledge they made.

### Why does Allah record what He already knows?

Allah created all the worlds, the heavens and the earth and whatever lies between them. His infinite wisdom and all-embracing knowledge designed this universe so perfectly that no room remains for doubt or question. He created everything according to wise laws. Following them leads to eternal success and lasting peace, while turning from them brings the punishments Allah has set.

His knowledge and wisdom would have been enough to decide the fate of every creature. He had no need for angels to watch and record His servants' deeds, nor to weigh those deeds in the Balance on the Day of Judgement. He knows every deed, even His servants' hidden thoughts and intentions, with no possibility of a wrong judgement.

But His grace and perfect justice chose that no one should be punished without documentary proof of his sins. The evidence will be such that the sinner has no choice but to admit what he did. So He appointed angels to record every act of each person: "not a word does a person utter without having a ˹vigilant˺ observer ready ˹to write it down˺" (50:18). According to Ibn ʿAbbās, everything good or bad about a person is recorded by his guardian angels: "Every matter, small and large, is written ˹precisely˺" (54:53). Then, on the Day of Judgement, the Balance will be set up to weigh everyone's good and bad deeds. Those whose good deeds weigh heavier will be saved, and those whose bad deeds weigh heavier will be punished.

When Allah, the Best of Judges, holds His court on that Day, He will also call witnesses to each person's deeds. Some wrongdoers will try to discredit them. Then Allah will make the person's own limbs speak and testify against him. The very places where he did his deeds will bear witness too. In the end he will find no way to deny the witnesses and will confess his evil deeds: "And so they will confess their sins." (67:11).

### How has Allah helped people to obey?

Allah, the Most Kind and Loving, did not leave His servants with bare laws and regulations. Out of kindness He gave them complete guidance through His prophets and His Books, to save them from eternal punishment. Loving parents make it compulsory for their children to go to school every morning. But they also make sure everything the children need is ready in time, so that keeping the rule is as easy as possible. Allah, who is beyond all comparison, is more loving and kind to His servants than parents are to their children. He did not merely lay down laws but made them a source of real guidance. With His commands, He also taught how to carry them out with ease and readiness.

Besides sending His messengers and Books, He appointed many angels to help and guide people to the right path. He also scattered plain signs of His might and wisdom everywhere, so that people could use their own observation and understanding to tell right from wrong and to remember their Creator. He repeatedly invites them to reflect on the signs spread around them: "There are ˹countless˺ signs on earth for those with sure faith, as there are within yourselves. Can you not see" (51:20-21).

This verse speaks of two kinds of signs, outer and inner. The outer signs are everywhere: the heavens and the earth, the oceans and mountains, plants and trees, fruits of many colours and tastes. All of them point unmistakably to the Lord. The inner signs include the way human beings are reproduced, their bodies, their thoughts and intentions, and their feelings of joy and sorrow. A little reflection on these signs leads a person to the Creator and moves him to thank Him.

### What other covenants has Allah taken?

Another way Allah moved people to act righteously was to have them enter covenants with Him through His prophets. The Holy Qur'an mentions a number of such covenants, made with different people in different circumstances. The prophets were made to promise that they would convey Allah's message to their people, whatever hardship or reproach they met. This pious group did convey His message as faithfully as possible and sacrificed all they had in doing so. The people of every prophet, in turn, were made to promise to obey him, and in some cases to spend all their energy carrying out particular commands. Some kept their promise and some did not.

The most significant of these is the covenant all the prophets made concerning the Holy Prophet ﷺ. They pledged to follow the last of the prophets and to help him whenever they had the chance: "˹Remember˺ when Allah made a covenant with the prophets, ˹saying,˺ “Now that I have given you the Book and wisdom, if there comes to you a messenger confirming what you have, you must believe in him and support him.”" (3:81). By implication, this pledge binds the followers of every prophet to believe in the Holy Prophet ﷺ, follow him and support his aims. It also binds them to follow the law given by the last of the prophets.

### What is a pledge of allegiance worth?

The traditional pledge of allegiance, the bayʿah or pledge of discipleship that a guide takes from devoted followers, is modelled on this way of Allah. Prophets, their companions and spiritual leaders have all taken such pledges from their followers. The Holy Qur'an mentions the Bayʿat al-Rizwān: "Indeed, Allah was pleased with the believers when they pledged allegiance to you ˹O Prophet˺ under the tree." (48:18). The Holy Prophet ﷺ took such pledges from his Companions on many occasions. The Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah is a famous covenant made with the Anṣār of Madinah before the migration.

The Sufi custom of taking a pledge from followers is a covenant to practise Allah's commands regularly and to strengthen faith through frequent remembrance of their Lord. Swearing allegiance in this way has many benefits and draws barakah, blessing, from Allah. After pledging himself to a Shaykh, a spiritual master, a follower feels more willing to carry out his religious duties and more careful to seek Allah's pleasure.

This also clears up a misconception common among people of little knowledge, that placing one's hand in the hand of a Shaykh is enough for salvation in the Hereafter. That notion is entirely wrong. The pledge is a commitment to follow practical guidance as the Shaykh instructs. Merely placing one's hand in his is of no use at all. In fact, it betrays the pledge and may bring on Allah's wrath.

### The inner reading: the first day of friendship

In the tongue of the Reality, the verse holds another hint and another taste. It alludes to the beginning of the friends' states, and to the binding of the compact of friendship with them on the first day, in the beginningless past, when the Real was present and the Reality was there. A poet sang: "How blessed were Laylā and those nights when we used to meet her!" What a fine day was the day the foundation of friendship was laid, and how exalted the hour when its compact was made! Whoever desired on that first day will never lose that desire. Whoever yearned when union with the Friend came has come to know life's crown and the direction of all his days. Another line runs: "How blessed was the time of Your covenant, without which my heart would have no room for longing!"

The command came: "remind them of Allah’s days ˹of favour˺" (14:5). These servants, Allah says, have forgotten His covenant and busied themselves with others. They are to be reminded of the day their pure spirits made the covenant of friendship with Him, when He anointed their yearning eyes with the kohl of "Am I not your Lord?"

O needy one! Remember the day when the spirits of the friends drank the wine of passion for Allah from the cup of love in the assembly of intimacy. The angels drawn near, of the Higher Assembly, said that these were a people of high aspiration. The angels themselves had never tasted this wine nor caught its scent. Yet the clamour of these beggars rose as high as Capella, crying, as the verse has it, "Are there any more?" (50:30). As the poet says: "Of that wine, which my religion does not forbid, my lips will never be dry until I return to nonexistence." One day the paragon of the world and master of the children of Adam ﷺ said of a mountain: this is a mountain that loves me and that I love.

### In short

- Before their earthly life, Allah drew all of Adam's descendants from their fathers' loins and had them testify that He is their Lord.
- The hadiths describe them drawn from Adam's back as tiny beings, with body and reason, at Naʿmān near ʿArafāt, divided into the people of Paradise and of the Fire.
- Though forgotten, the covenant sowed a seed of faith in every heart, which is why the excuse of ignorance will not be accepted.
- Allah, who knows all, still records deeds and calls witnesses out of justice, and He guides through prophets, Books and signs.
- The covenant of Alast stands at the head of the covenants Allah took through the prophets, and of every true pledge of allegiance.

<<<VERSE 7:173>>>
The covenant also closes off a second excuse, that of blind imitation: "Nor say, ‘It was our forefathers who had associated others ˹with Allah in worship˺ and we, as their descendants, followed in their footsteps." The excuse ends in a plea: "Will you then destroy us for the falsehood they invented?’”" No one can shelter behind the errors of his forefathers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Nor say, ‘It was our forefathers who had associated others ˹with Allah in worship˺"** They would claim that their fathers were idolaters before their time, and that it was their fathers who broke the covenant and pledge before them.

**"and we, as their descendants, followed in their footsteps."** They were only their offspring, small and weak, coming after them, and they simply copied them.

**"Will you then destroy us for the falsehood they invented?’”"** Would Allah punish them for what the idolaters among their forefathers did, when they set up idolatry and broke the covenant? They would plead: "O our Lord! Will You destroy us for what our ancestors did, and make our deeds worthless because they associated others with You?" They would insist they bore no sin, since they only followed their ancestors and had no knowledge.

### Why is imitation no excuse?

The verse denies them any refuge in the claim that they were unaware of the truth. They might argue that, as their forefathers' offspring, they only followed their path, so they should not be punished for their forefathers' error. The verse answers that they will not be punished for what their forefathers did. It was their own fault and their own indifference that brought them to ruin.

The covenant had sown the seed of truth in their hearts. So it was not hard for them to see that stones they had carved into idols, or fire, trees and other created things, could never be their Creator or help them in need. They cannot use such arguments, since they were made to testify within themselves to the oneness of Allah, tawḥīd. The reminder given through the tongue of Muhammad ﷺ, who brought the miracle, serves every soul as a call to remember that testimony within itself.

### The voice of inner nature

If an animal is taken from its parents and raised in an unfamiliar setting, it still keeps the traits of its species when it grows up. Its behaviour in everything springs from instinct. The same is true of a human being's awareness of Allah. The consciousness of a Creator and Lord is planted so deeply in the human soul that it is never lost.

But there is one difference between animals and humans. Animals cannot go against their nature. They are driven to do whatever their inner nature urges. A human being is different. This awareness lies in his nature, yet he is entirely free to act, even while his mind and conscience show him right and wrong. So he has the power and the choice either to follow his inner voice, or to ignore it and do as he likes.

It is in these moments of moral choice that a person is on trial, and on their outcome Paradise or Hell is decided. Whoever heeds Allah's voice, obeying what He commands in nature's silent language, passes the test. The doors of Paradise will be opened to him after death. Whoever ignores Allah's voice speaking through nature will be guilty in His sight, and Allah will ignore him just as he ignored that voice.

### In short

- No one may excuse his idolatry by saying he only followed his forefathers.
- Each person is answerable for his own choices, because the covenant planted the truth of tawḥīd in every heart.
- Unlike animals, humans are free to follow or ignore their inner voice, and that choice is their test.

<<<VERSE 7:174>>>
Verse 174 closes this passage: "This is how We make our signs clear, so perhaps they will return ˹to the Right Path˺". Allah sets out His signs in detail so that people may turn back to the pledge they made and acknowledge Him as their Lord.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is how We make our signs clear,"** In the Qur'an Allah explains the story of the Covenant, and He makes His other signs clear in the same way. Just as He made His signs and proofs clear to earlier nations, He makes the lessons clear to these people, the Quraish.

**"so perhaps they will return ˹to the Right Path˺"**: so that they may reflect on them and turn back from disbelief and idolatry to the original Covenant. They may then repent of worshipping others alongside Allah and come to believe in Him alone and worship Him alone, as they had agreed long before they broke their promise.

### Who is left without an excuse?

Allah has made His signs plain through these detailed verses to make it easy for people to return to their pledge. Whoever applies his mind will find the truth and so escape everlasting punishment.

The voice of inner nature is itself evidence that Allah exists. So no one now has the excuse of ignorance. No one can say he is only doing what has been done since ancient times. A person brings the awareness of Allah with him from birth, and it survives every change in his circumstances. In view of this, what excuse does anyone have for following the path of misguidance?

### In short

- Allah explains the Covenant and His other signs in detail so that people may return to Him.
- The Quraish, like every nation, were shown these signs so that they would leave idolatry for worship of Allah alone.
- With the awareness of Allah born in every person, no one is left with an excuse for misguidance.
