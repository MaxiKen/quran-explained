The question and the answer follow. "˹Allah asked,˺ “Am I not your Lord?” They replied, “Yes, You are! We testify.”" The verse closes with the reason for the testimony. ### Phrase by phrase

**"when your Lord brought forth from the loins of the children of Adam their descendants"** describes the bringing out of every human being from the loins of his ancestors. One generation came out of the loins of the next, and the whole line of them came out of the loins of Adam. They came offspring after offspring, in the way that human beings multiply. The reading says that they appeared like small ants in the valley of Na'man on the day of Arafah, because of how many they were.

**"and had them testify regarding themselves"** is the testimony the verse is built on. Their witness against themselves was that Allah is their Lord. He is their King, and no deity is worthy of worship except Him. Allah set up proofs of His Lordship before them and gave them the faculty of reason, and then brought the acknowledgment out of their own mouths. Allah created them on the fitrah. Which is the way He built them. As the Holy Qur'an says in the words that the face should be set towards the religion as a man of pure faith. And that this is the fitrah of Allah with which He created mankind. And that there is to be no change in the creation of Allah (30:30).

**"Am I not your Lord?" They replied, "Yes, You are! We testify."** is the exchange itself. One account adds what was said to the angels at that moment, that they were to be witnesses over the people and that the people were to be witnesses over one another.

**"Now you have no right to say on Judgment Day, 'We were not aware of this.'"** gives the purpose of the whole event. The pledge lit the lamp of faith in the hearts of the people, so that a little reflection would be enough for them to recognise their Lord. On that day the plea of ignorance will not help them, since the testimony was taken from them before they were sent into the world.

### What the testimony means

The testimony was taken in two ways at once. It was taken in words, and it was taken by the state the people were made in. The Book shows both kinds elsewhere. The words of testimony are used in the passage where the people say that they bear witness against themselves (6:130). Testimony is also given by a man's own condition, as in the words about those who join partners with Allah. The verse says that it is not for them to maintain the mosques of Allah, since their own disbelief is a testimony against them (9:17). That verse means that their disbelief testifies against them, rather than that they stand and speak the words. Another verse of the same kind says of man that he is a witness to it by his own deeds (100:7).

Asking works in the same two ways. It is sometimes put in words and sometimes in the situation a person is placed in. The Book says that Allah gave the people of all that they asked for (14:34). And that covers the requests a man makes in speech and the needs that are met without a word being spoken.

### The reports of the event

What the verses describe is known as the covenant of Alust. It is the great covenant that the Creator made with all His created beings before they took the form in which they exist.

The reports that follow the event are of two kinds. One kind describes the bringing out of the descendants from Adam himself. The other describes the bringing out of the descendants from the loins of the children of Adam, which is what the verse says. There is no conflict between the two. The direct children of Adam came out of the back of Adam, and the people who came after them came out of the backs of his descendants.

The report of the first kind came through Muslim bin Yasar. Some people asked Umar al-Faruq about the meaning of the verse. And he said that the same question had been put to the Prophet ﷺ and that he had heard the answer. The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah first created Adam, then placed His hand on his back and drew out all the righteous among his descendants who were to come into being. Of them He said that they were created for Paradise, and that they would act righteously as those who deserve Paradise act. The second hand was laid on the back of Adam again. Out came all the wicked among his descendants who were yet to come into being. Of them He said that they were created for the Fire, and that they would act wickedly as those who are led to the Fire act.

A man among the companions asked the Prophet ﷺ why people were still asked to do good deeds when the matter had already been settled. The Prophet ﷺ answered that the one created for Paradise begins to do the deeds that make a man worthy of Paradise and dies in that state. While the one created for the Fire goes into the acts that make him liable for the Fire and dies doing the deeds that lead him there. The lesson drawn from the answer is that a man who does not know which group he belongs to should put all his effort into the deeds that are the mark of the people of Paradise. And should hope to be counted among them.

The report was carried by Malik, by Abu Dawud, by Tirmidhi and by Imam Ahmad. The same event is given in another form in the Two Sahihs. Abu Hurairah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that every child is born on the fitrah. And that it is his parents who make him a Jew, a Christian or a Zoroastrian. The Prophet ﷺ compared the matter to animals born with whole bodies, and asked whether any of them is seen with a nose cut off at birth.

Muslim recorded that Iyad bin 'Himar said that the Prophet ﷺ said that Allah said He had created His servants as people of the oneness. And that the devils had come to them and turned them from their religion and made unlawful what He had made lawful.

The Tradition that Imam Ahmad reported adds a detail about colour, and the companion named at the head of its chain is Abu al-Darda. The people who appeared first were fair, and those who appeared on the second occasion were black. The companion Abu Hurairah reported the same description through Tirmidhi, and his report adds one more detail. Every child of Adam who was to come into the world, to the last of them, carried a kind of brilliance on his forehead.

The colour and the brilliance point to something in the report that is worth noticing. A spirit has no colour, so the people who came out at that moment were not spirits alone. They had a fine kind of body given to them for the occasion. Since the nourishing that the verse speaks of has to do with a body that passes from one state to another. Spirits do not need that kind of care, since they stay as they were created.

### The report about Adam and Dawud

Another report belongs to the same event. At-Tirmidhi recorded that Abu Hurayrah said that the Prophet ﷺ said that when Allah created Adam, He wiped Adam's back and every person He would create from him until the Day of Resurrection fell out of it. A glimmering light was placed between the eyes of each of them, and they were then shown to Adam. Adam asked who they were, and Allah said that they were his offspring. Adam saw a man among them whose light he liked and asked who he was. Allah told him that the man belonged to the later generations of his offspring, and that his name was Dawud. Adam asked how long the man would live, and Allah said sixty years. Adam said that he would give forty years of his own life to the man. When the end of Adam's life came and the angel of death came to take his soul, Adam said that he still had forty years left. He was told that he had given them to his son Dawud. Adam denied it, and his offspring followed him in the denial. Forgetting came over Adam, and it came over his offspring with him. A mistake was made by Adam, and his offspring followed him into it.

At-Tirmidhi said of that report that it is hasan and sound, and that it came through various chains of narration. The name at the head of those chains is Abu Hurayrah, and the report goes back to the Prophet ﷺ. Al-Hakim also recorded it in his Mustadrak, and he said that it is sound according to the criteria of Muslim, although the two did not record it.

Other reports of the same family add that Allah separated the people of Paradise from the people of the Fire at that moment. The report of Anas bin Malik, recorded by Imam Ahmad, has the Prophet ﷺ saying that a man from the people of the Fire will be asked on the Day of Resurrection whether he would pay the whole of what is on the earth as a ransom if he owned it. He will say that he would. Allah will say that He asked him for something far less than that while he was still in the loins of Adam. Which was that he should not join anything with Him in worship. The man insisted on joining others with Him. That report is recorded in the Two Sahihs.

### Why the covenant was taken

The record that Allah keeps is complete without any help from His creatures. His knowledge takes in everything, including the hidden thoughts and intentions of His servants, with no possibility of a wrong judgement. His grace and His perfect justice, however, chose that no one should be punished without the evidence of his own deeds being put before him, in a way that leaves the sinner no choice but to acknowledge what he did.

So He appointed angels to record every act a person does. The Book says that not a word does a man utter but there is a watcher by him ready to record it (50:18). The note attached to that verse in one of the studies of al-Qur'an al-Karim, volume 4, page 969, adds a saying of Ibn 'Abbas. His words are that the guardian angels of a mortal record all that he does, whether the deed is good or evil. The Book adds in another place that everything small and great is written down (54:53). On the Day of Judgement the Balance will be set to weigh the good and the bad deeds of all people. The ones whose good deeds weigh heavy will be given salvation, and the ones whose bad deeds weigh heavier will be punished.

The court of the Day of Judgement will also call witnesses. Some of the wrongdoers will deny what the witnesses say against them. Then their own organs will be given the power to speak and will bear witness to what the man did. The places where the deeds were done will also come forward as witnesses, until the man finds no way to deny the testimony and confesses his evil deeds. The Book refers to it in the words that they will confess their sins (67:11).

None of this was left to law and regulations alone. Allah out of His kindness gave His servants complete guidance through His prophets and His Books, so that they might be saved from eternal punishment. The likeness used for it is the care of kind parents. Who make it necessary for their children to go to school every morning and also make sure that the children have what they need before the time comes. Allah, who is free of any likeness to His creatures, is kinder to His servants than parents are to their children. He did not only set out the laws but made them a source of real guidance. And along with the commands He taught the people how to carry them out with ease and readiness.

Beyond the messengers and the Books, He set a great number of His angels to help people and guide them to the right path. He also placed clear signs of His power and His wisdom all around them. So that their own observation and understanding would be enough to tell right from wrong and to remind them of their Creator. The Book says that on the earth there are signs for those who have firm faith. And that there are signs in their own selves as well. And then asks whether they do not see (51:20-21). The external signs are all around, in the heavens and the earth, the oceans, the mountains, the plants and the trees, and the fruits of different colours and tastes. The internal signs are in the make of a human being, in the way people are reproduced, in their thoughts, their intentions and their feelings of happiness and sorrow. A little reflection on either kind leads a person to the Creator and makes him grateful to Him.

### The pledges taken at different times

Another arrangement made by Allah for the guidance of people was to bring them into covenants with Him through His prophets. The Book refers to a number of such covenants, made with different people in different circumstances. The prophets themselves were made to promise that they would convey the message of Allah to their people regardless of the difficulties and the reproach they met. That group of prophets kept the promise as faithfully as was possible and gave up all they had in the work.

The people of every prophet were made to promise that they would obey their prophet, and in some special cases they were bound to spend their energy on particular commands. Some of them kept the promise and others did not.

The most significant of those covenants is the one in which all the prophets were made to enter regarding the last Prophet. They were bound to follow him and to help him whenever an occasion for it came. The Book mentions that covenant in the words that Allah made the prophets take a pledge about the messenger who would come to confirm what they had. And that they were to believe in him and help him (3:81). One consequence drawn from that pledge is that the followers of the earlier prophets are bound by it. So that they are to believe in the last Prophet, follow him and support him in what he set out to do.

The practice of taking a pledge from those who follow a teacher comes out of this same arrangement. A pledge of this kind is called a bay'ah. And it is the way the prophets, their companions and the spiritual leaders took the commitment of those who came to them. One such pledge is mentioned in the Book, in the words that Allah was well pleased with the believers when they pledged their allegiance under the tree (48:18). That event is known as the pledge of al-Rizwan. The Prophet ﷺ took a pledge of that kind from his companions on many occasions. The pledge of al-'Aqabah was a well known covenant made before the migration with the helpers of Al-Madinah.

The customary way of the Sufis. Who take a pledge from those who follow them, is a covenant taken for practising the commands of Allah regularly and for strengthening belief in Him by frequent remembrance of their Lord. Swearing allegiance to a teacher carries many advantages and draws barakah from Allah. A person who has given his pledge to a spiritual master feels more willing and more inclined to carry out his religious obligations. And he is more careful in seeking the pleasure of Allah.

The same discussion clears away a false notion that is common among people of little knowledge. Putting one's hand into the hand of a teacher or a spiritual leader is not by itself enough for salvation in the world to come. The pledge is a promise to follow the practical guidance the teacher gives. Nothing but the placing of a hand in a hand is of no use at all. And a person who stops there has turned away from the pledge he made. Which may bring down the displeasure of Allah on him.

### What the word for the descendants carries

The verse uses the word for the children of Adam in the phrase that opens the covenant. According to Imam Raghib al-Isfahani, that word is derived from the root that signifies creating. The Book uses the word in a number of places with that sense.

The word therefore covers all created human beings, since the covenant of eternity took in all the descendants of Adam. Nobody who was to come into the world until the end of time stood outside it.

### The questions the event raises

The discussion of the covenant brings up two questions. The first is the time and the place of the event. The second is how the children of Adam could have had the knowledge and the reason to acknowledge Allah as their Lord and Nourisher, when the experience of being nourished comes only after a person has entered the world.

The first question is answered in a report from 'Abdullah ibn 'Abbas, carried through sound sources by Imam Ahmad and Nasa'i. His report places the event at the moment Adam was sent down from the heavens to the earth. The place was the valley of Nauman, which is known as the plain of Arafat near Makkah.

The second question has a plain answer. Allah has the power to do whatever He wills. The same power that made all human beings appear in the size of an ant could more easily give them the knowledge and the reason they needed to recognise the Lord who nourishes them. They appeared with body and soul together in a small size, and with every physical function that a complete human being needs. Reason and understanding are among the most important of those functions, so they were included.

The size of the people at that moment was explained in the report of the companion Abu al-Darda. Who said that they did not appear with their usual size. And that they appeared in the size of a small ant. The matter is not strange even by the measure of the age of scientific knowledge. Science has established that a system like our solar system works in and around the nucleus of an atom. Books of hundreds of pages can be reduced to a small dot by micro filming. It should be no surprise, then, that Allah the All-Powerful reduced the people to the size of an ant on that occasion.

### The question of a covenant that is not remembered

One question is left open by the reports. What value can be attached to a covenant taken before the creation of human beings, when the people who entered it do not remember it after they come into the world?

The first thing to note in the answer is that some individuals have remembered the occasion. The spiritual leader Dhul Nun al-Misri said that he remembered the covenant as clearly as he was hearing it at that moment. Some of the elders reported that they remembered the people who had stood near them at the time. Such cases are rare, and they do not answer the question by themselves.

The real answer is that many acts are effective by their own nature, without anyone remembering them or understanding them. Their effect is imprinted on a person as a matter of course.

The practice of saying the adhan in the right ear of a new born child and the iqamah in his left ear is a plain example of an act of that kind. The adhan is the call to the salah. And it is a bold declaration that Allah is one, that He has no partners. And that He is the greatest of all. The iqamah is the same declaration with the addition that the prayer has been set ready for the people to join. The baby does not understand the meaning of the call and does not remember it when he becomes an adult. The wisdom behind the practice is to revive the pledge he took with Allah. And to plant the seed of faith in his heart by repeating the covenant in his ears. The effect of the act is plain in every Muslim person, even one who is not a good Muslim in practice. He takes pride in calling himself a Muslim and he dislikes being cut off from that identity.

The command to recite the Book even to people who do not know Arabic belongs to the same order. Their hearts are enlightened by the effect of the words, and their faith in Allah is renewed by them.

The wisdom behind the covenant is of the same kind. It sowed the seed of faith in the heart of every human being. That seed takes its nourishment from the soil of the human heart whether or not a person is aware of it. The fruit of the seed shows itself in love and respect for God, and that love and respect is part of human nature. The expression of that love may take unjust forms, such as the worship of false gods, of idols, or of created beings. Whether that worship is rightly directed or wrongly directed, it carries the same love and respect for the Creator within it. The billions of people who live in the world have that respect and love for Allah given to them. And they express it through their worship as their knowledge or their ignorance directs them. Nothing needs to be said about the few who have worn down their natural understanding by following the things of this world until they forgot the pledge they made with Allah.

The Prophet ﷺ stated the matter in a report already quoted, that every child is born on the fitrah and that his parents turn him to their religion. Another report of the Prophet ﷺ says that Allah said He created His servants as people of pure faith, and that they were then led astray by the influence of Satan.

### The two excuses the verse closes off

The words that the hearers are not to say on the Day of Judgement that they were not aware of the matter closes off one excuse. The pledge had lit the lamp of faith in the hearts of the people, and a little reflection was enough to recognise the Lord. The plea of ignorance will not avail on that day.

The next verse closes off a second excuse, which is the plea that the people were only following their forefathers.

### The inward reading of the covenant

One reading takes the event in the tongue of the inner reality and finds in it an allusion to the state of the friends of Allah. It speaks of where their states begin. The compact of friendship was bound with them on the earliest of all days, in the era of the Beginningless. At that hour the Real was present, and the reality was there.

The prayer of longing that goes with that reading is quoted in its own words. How blessed were Layla and the nights when those who loved met with Layla. What a fine day was the day the foundation of friendship was laid. What an exalted time was the time the compact of friendship was made. Desire was awakened in some of them on the first day, and such desire is never forgotten. Those who yearned at the hour of union with the Friend know the crown of life, and they know the direction their days are to take.

The command that goes with the reading is the word of Allah to His Prophet to remind the people of the days of God (14:5). The servants of Allah had forgotten His covenant and busied themselves with other things. The reminder was to take them back to the hour their pure spirits bound themselves in friendship with Him. While their yearning eyes were anointed with the collyrium of the question, Am I not your Lord.

The scene drawn for that day is the assembly of intimacy. Where the spirits and the very persons of the friends drank the wine of passion for their Lord from the cup of love. The near angels of the Higher Plenum are described as saying that these are a people of high aspiration. And that they themselves had never tasted that wine and had not found even its scent. The roaring and the shouting of those beggars rose to Capella, and the Book gives their cry in the words that ask whether there is any more (50:30).

The poetry of that longing is quoted with the reading. The lines say that the lips of the speaker will not stay dry from that wine, which his religion does not forbid, until he returns to nonexistence. The same passage recalls the day when the master of the children of Adam said of a mountain that it loved him and that he loved it.

That reading takes the verse as a description of a bond that a person carries in his deepest self. And it stands beside the plain meaning. Which is that the descendants of Adam were brought out and made to testify to the Lordship of their Creator.

### In short

- The verse describes the bringing out of every descendant of Adam from the loins of his ancestors. And the testimony taken from them that Allah is their Lord and King, with no deity worthy of worship except Him. And that testimony is the first binding of the people to the oneness of Allah.
- They were created on the fitrah. And the Prophet ﷺ said that every child is born on the fitrah and that his parents turn him to their own religion. While another report has Allah saying that He created His servants as people of the oneness and that the devils turned them away.
- The testimony was taken in words and by the state the people were made in. As the Book shows in the words that they bear witness against themselves (6:130), in the words about those who join partners with Allah (9:17). And in the words that a man is a witness to his own conduct (100:7).
- The reports tell of the drawing out of the righteous and the wicked, of the appearance of the people in the size of an ant, of the fair colour and the black colour named in the Tradition of Abu al-Darda, of the brilliance on the foreheads in the report of Tirmidhi. And of the forty years Adam gave to his son Dawud and then denied.
- The event belongs with the other pledges Allah took through His prophets, among them the covenant that all the prophets would follow and help the last Prophet (3:81) and the pledge of al-Rizwan under the tree (48:18). And the inward reading takes the covenant as the bond of friendship placed in the spirits of the friends of Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Nor say, 'It was our forefathers who had associated others ˹with Allah in worship˺ and we, as their descendants, followed in their footsteps.'"** sets out the plea. The people would say that their fathers were the ones who broke the pledge and took partners with Allah. And that they themselves were only their descendants, small and weak after them. Who followed their example.

**"Will you then destroy us for the falsehood they invented?"** is the question that goes with the plea. The people would ask whether they were to be punished for what the idolaters among their forefathers did. And whether their own actions were to be counted for nothing because of the falsehood of others.

### Why the plea cannot stand

The verse stops the people from taking refuge in the claim that they were unaware of the truth. The plea that they were the descendants of their fathers and only followed their path does not carry. And the verse shows why. The people are not punished for the acts of their forefathers. It was their own fault and their own careless attitude that brought the disaster on them.

The covenant had sown the seed of truth in their hearts. It was not difficult for them to reach the conclusion that the stones they shaped into idols with their own hands, or the fire, or the trees, or any other created thing, could not be their creator and had no power to help them in their need.

The same point is put in another way. The awareness of a Creator and a Lord was placed in the soul of a human being so deeply that he never loses it. That awareness is the seed of the oneness of Allah. And it stays in place even when a person worships something else. An animal separated from its parents and raised in a strange place still keeps the marks of its species. And its behaviour in all its dealings comes from its instinct. Human beings hold their God-consciousness in the same way. There is one difference between an animal and a human being. Animals are not able to go against their nature and are compelled to do what their nature urges on them. A human being is free in the matter of action. The awareness is placed in his nature. And at the same time his mind and his conscience point out to him what is right and what is wrong.

Human beings therefore have the power to follow that inner voice. And the power to ignore it and do as they please. The occasions of that choice are the places where a person is on trial. And the decision about Paradise or the Fire rests on the result. A person who listens to the voice of God and does what He tells him through the silent language of nature passes the test. And the doors of Paradise are opened for him after death. A person who ignores the voice of God speaking at the level of nature is held guilty. And he is passed over by God in the measure that he passed over the voice.

That voice of the inner nature is itself evidence of the existence of God. Nobody is left with the excuse of ignorance. And nobody can say that he is only doing what has been done since ancient times. A person brings the awareness of God with him from his birth. And it stays with him through every change in his circumstances. Against that, the question is what excuse anyone has for following the path of misguidance.

### The way the reminder reaches a person

The reminder of the covenant reaches each person by the tongue of the one who carries the miracle. And one reading adds that the reminder serves the same purpose for every soul. Which is to bring it back to what it acknowledged within itself.

The dialogue in the verse is put in a form that shows the reasoning of the plea. So that a hearer can see how little weight it carries. The people of the plea say that the error belongs to those who came before them. And that they themselves carry no sin because they had no knowledge of what was done. The verse answers that the knowledge was given to them in their own nature. And that the pledge was taken from them in person.

### In short

- The verse closes off the plea that the people were only following their forefathers. Who had joined others with Allah before them. And that they were small and weak descendants after them.
- The people are not punished for the acts of their forefathers. And the verse lays the fault on their own carelessness. Since the covenant had already sown the seed of truth in their hearts.
- The stone shaped by a man's own hands, the fire, the trees and every other created thing could not be taken for a creator once a person gave the matter any thought.
- The awareness of a Creator is placed in the human soul so deeply that it is never lost. And the animal separated from its parents shows the same thing in the marks of its species.
- A human being differs from an animal in being free to follow that inner voice or to ignore it. And the choice is where he is tried and where the decision about Paradise or the Fire is made.
- The voice of the inner nature is itself evidence of the existence of God. So that no one is left with the excuse of ignorance or with the plea that he was only following the custom of the ancients.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is how We make our signs clear"** states the way the Book works. The verses are explained in detail. And the story of the covenant is set out in full, just as the signs and the evidences were made clear for the nations that came before. Verse 174 is the closing word of the passage that began with the bringing out of the descendants of Adam.

**"so perhaps they will return ˹to the Right Path˺"** gives the purpose. The aim is that the people of Quraish should turn away from the worship of partners and come to the belief that Allah is one and to the worship of Him alone. As they once agreed before they broke their promise. The return aimed at is the return to tawhid. And to the covenant of the oneness that their own selves had acknowledged. The aim is also that people should reflect on the signs and come back from their disbelief. And that they should return to the covenant they made at the beginning.

### What the closing words ask of a hearer

The verse places the whole matter inside the reach of an ordinary person. The signs have been made clear. And the covenant has been told. And the pledges taken from earlier peoples have been recorded so that the reader can see how they were kept and how they were broken.

Anyone who applies his reason to what has been set out can find the truth and save himself from an everlasting punishment. The making clear of the signs is itself a favour to the hearer. Since it removes the ground of every excuse and leaves the way back open for whoever wants to take it.

### In short

- The verse states the way the Book works. Which is that the signs are explained in detail. And it names the purpose. Which is that people should return to the right path.
- The return aimed at is a turning from joining others with Allah to belief in Him alone. And a coming back to the covenant that was made at the beginning. As the people had once agreed before they broke their promise.
- The story of the covenant is told in full so that a reader may reflect on the signs. And a person who applies his reason to what has been set out can find the truth and save himself from an everlasting punishment.
