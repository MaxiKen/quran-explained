<<<VERSE 2:97>>>
Verse 97 answers the Jews' newest excuse, that the angel who brought the Qur'an was their enemy. "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Whoever is an enemy of Gabriel should know that he revealed this ˹Quran˺ to your heart by Allah’s Will, confirming what came before it—a guide and good news for the believers.”" The angel is only a messenger, and the message he carries is exactly what a divine Book should be.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ 'Whoever is an enemy of Gabriel'"**. Imām Abū Jaʿfar ibn Jarīr aṭ-Ṭabarī recorded that the scholars of Tafsīr agree this verse and the next were revealed in response to the Jews, who claimed that Jibrīl (Gabriel) was their enemy and Mīkāʾīl (Michael) their friend. The saying is traced to ʿAbdullāh ibn Ṣūriyā in particular. Let whoever is an enemy to Jibrīl die in his exasperation, for an enemy of Jibrīl is automatically an enemy of Allah, and he is misguided. ʿIkrimah explained that the elements Jibr, Mīk and Isrāf all mean "servant", while īl means "Allah". Others say that īl means "servant" and the word attached to it is a name of Allah, since īl is the constant element in such compounds, just as ʿabd is the constant element in ʿAbdullāh, ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān, ʿAbd al-Malik, ʿAbd al-Quddūs, ʿAbd as-Salām, ʿAbd al-Kāfī and ʿAbd al-Jalīl while the remainder varies, and so too with Jibrīl, Mīkāʾīl, ʿAzrāʾīl and Isrāfīl. Allah knows best.

**"should know that he revealed this ˹Quran˺ to your heart"**. Whoever is Jibrīl's enemy should know that he is Rūḥ al-Qudus, who brought down the glorious Reminder (dhikr), the Qur'an, to your heart from Allah. He is therefore a messenger from Allah, and whoever takes one messenger as an enemy has taken all the messengers as enemies. Jibrīl did not choose his missions himself but acted on his Lord's command. The angels say that they descend only by the command of your Lord (19:64), and the Qur'an is a revelation from the Lord of all worlds, brought down by the trustworthy spirit upon the Prophet's heart (26:192-194).

**"by Allah’s Will"**. By Allah's leave and command. Allah sent Jibrīl to the Prophet ﷺ with the Qur'an.

**"confirming what came before it"**. Affirming the earlier Scriptures, the Torah and the Injīl (Gospel), and affirming the oneness of Allah that they taught.

**"a guide and good news for the believers"**. Guidance for their hearts away from error, and the good news of Paradise, which belongs exclusively to the believers, the bliss Allah has prepared for them. Allah says of the Qur'an that it is guidance and healing for those who believe (41:44), and that He sends it down as a healing and mercy for the believers, though it only increases the wrongdoers in loss (17:82).

### ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and the three questions

Al-Bukhārī records from Anas ibn Mālik that ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām was out working his land when news reached him that the Prophet ﷺ had arrived in al-Madinah. He came to him and said: "I am going to ask you about three things that no one but a Prophet knows. What is the first portent of the Hour? What is the first meal the people of Paradise will eat? Why does a child resemble its father, and why its maternal uncle?" Allah's Messenger ﷺ replied, "Jibrīl has just told me the answers." ʿAbdullāh said, "He, of all the angels, is the enemy of the Jews." The Messenger ﷺ recited this verse, then said that the first portent of the Hour would be a fire gathering people from the east to the west, that the first meal of the people of Paradise would be the extra lobe of the liver of a fish, and that if the man's discharge comes first the child resembles the father, while if the woman's comes first it resembles her side of the family. ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām declared, "I testify that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah and that you are the Messenger of Allah." He then said: "Messenger of Allah, the Jews are liars. If they learn of my conversion before you ask them about me, they will slander me." When the Jews came, ʿAbdullāh went inside the house. The Messenger ﷺ asked them, "What kind of man is ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām?" They answered, "The best of us and the son of the best of us, our master and the son of our master." He asked, "What would you say if he embraced Islam?" They said, "May Allah save him from that." ʿAbdullāh then came out before them, bearing witness that there is no god but Allah and that Muḥammad is Allah's Messenger. At once they said, "He is the worst of us and the son of the worst of us," and went on abusing him. Ibn Salām said, "This is what I feared, Messenger of Allah." Only al-Bukhārī records this hadith with this chain, and al-Bukhārī and Muslim both record it from Anas through another chain.

### Why the Jews hated Jibrīl

The Jews' perversity was fertile in excuses for denying the Prophet ﷺ. When they learned that the angel Jibrīl had brought the Qur'an, they said that he was their enemy, since he had always been the bearer of the harsh commandments and the catastrophic punishments sent down on them, and so they would not accept a Book that came by his hand. They added that they could have accepted it had Mīkāʾīl brought it down, for he is associated with rain and divine mercy. The background is that in ancient times the Israelites, through their own rebelliousness, brought severe punishments upon themselves, and it was God's way to warn them in advance through the prophets, who acted on the words of Jibrīl. The lesson was that people must follow God's commandments or face His wrath. Instead of heeding the warnings, the Israelites came to see Jibrīl as their enemy because he was always the one who gave notice of impending doom. So when the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ proclaimed that he received his revelation through Jibrīl, they said: so it is our old enemy, who always brings commands from heaven against us, and no wonder the Scriptures, which are the Israelites' birthright, are now being delivered to a man of another tribe. But angels, rarefied creatures that they are, hold no grudges against mortals. They descend only by divine command to carry out the tasks their Creator assigns, in this case revealing the Scripture to the prophet God Himself chose. Given the clear proofs that the Arab Prophet had received the same revelation as Ibrāhīm (Abraham), Mūsā (Moses) and ʿĪsā (Jesus), and that it tallied with the earlier scriptures, the Israelites had no grounds for rejecting it. Their reaction sprang from insincere, unrestrained lives ruled by self-interest, empty convention and racial prejudice, with religion kept up only superficially to maintain the pretence that they were faithfully treading God's path.

The Qur'an's refutation is simple. Jibrīl is only a messenger who, acting as a messenger, brought the Qur'an down to the Prophet ﷺ under divine command. Why be so particular about the messenger and never look at the Book itself? The Book confirms the earlier Books of Allah, guides people and gives good news to the believers, and since that is what divine Books are for, the Qur'an is evidently a divine Book and must be obeyed. To reject it out of hostility to Jibrīl is sheer stupidity or outright perversity. The devotional reading puts the same point as a question: whoever thinks Jibrīl brought no good, what good is greater than the Qur'an that came down through him?

### "To your heart": words as well as meanings

Ashraf ʿAlī Thānavī warned in his Bayān al-Qurʾān against a grave error that could be drawn from the phrase "revealed to your heart", namely the conclusion that Allah revealed only the meanings of the Qur'an and not its words. The supposition is baseless, because many verses of the Qur'an name Arabic as the vehicle of revelation for this particular Book, which should leave no room for doubt. Moreover, the heart perceives words as much as meanings. The heart is the perceiving agent, and the ears and other organs are only its instruments. In the state of revelation especially, the outer senses go dormant, and the words themselves reach the heart directly, bypassing the ears. Although the nature of revelation cannot be properly understood, a mundane analogy helps: in dreams we hear words and remember them afterwards even though our external hearing was suspended while we dreamt. So revelation of meanings to the heart in no way excludes revelation of the words. In any case, relying on one's own conjecture against a clear and definite statement of the Qur'an is a sin and an intellectual dishonesty.

### How the revelation came down

The devotional reading dwells on the phrase in another way. Magnanimous and beautiful is the Qur'an that Jibrīl brought down from the All-Merciful: the repose of the spirit of the friends of God, the healing of sick hearts, a mercy to the faithful, and that is why Allah says he sent it down "upon your heart", as elsewhere He says that the trustworthy spirit brought it down upon the Prophet's heart (26:193-194). When Jibrīl conveyed the pure revelation he came sometimes as a man and sometimes as an angel. Verses about the permitted and the forbidden, and the explanation of laws and rulings, he brought in human form, and on those occasions the heart was not mentioned. Thus Allah says that He sent down upon you the Book (3:7), and asks whether it is not enough for them that We sent down to you the Book which is recited to them (29:51). But whenever the subject was love, the attributes of passion and the intimations of friendship, he came as an angel, spiritual and subtle, and joined with the heart of Muṣṭafā ﷺ, conveying the Qur'an secretly to his secret core, unperceived by anyone else. When he left the realm of the heart, Muṣṭafā ﷺ would say that he broke away while he was still aware of him. It is said that when the Prophet ﷺ was immersed in contemplation during this speech, the revelation descended first to his heart, as the verse says, then passed from his heart to his understanding and his hearing, and then, for the sake of the people's shares, he came down from companionship's summit to the lowland of service. Such is the level of the elect. With certain other people revelation descends first on the hearing, then on the understanding, then on the heart, climbing from the lowness of struggle to the height of contemplation. This is the level of wayfarers and desirers. How far apart the two are!

### In short

- The Jews, led by Ibn Ṣūriyā, called Jibrīl (Gabriel) their enemy and Mīkāʾīl (Michael) their friend, and used this as a pretext to reject the Qur'an.
- Jibrīl is a messenger acting only by Allah's command (19:64, 26:192-194), so enmity to him is enmity to all messengers and to Allah.
- ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām's three questions, answered through Jibrīl, led him to Islam and exposed the Jews' lies, as al-Bukhārī records.
- The Book Jibrīl brought confirms earlier Scripture and is guidance and good news for believers (41:44, 17:82), and "to your heart" means words as well as meanings, received by the heart as the true perceiver.

<<<VERSE 2:98>>>
Verse 98 widens the principle. "Whoever is an enemy of Allah, His angels, His messengers, Gabriel, and Michael, then ˹let them know that˺ Allah is certainly the enemy of the disbelievers". Loyalties among the angels cannot be divided, because they all serve one Lord.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever is an enemy of Allah, His angels, His messengers"**. Whoever takes Allah, His angels and His messengers as enemies has made himself Allah's enemy. His messengers include both angels and men, for Allah selects messengers from both angels and people (22:75). Whoever believes in one messenger must believe in all, and whoever rejects one has rejected them all. Allah pronounced those who deny Him and His messengers and say "we believe in some and disbelieve in others" to be disbelievers (4:150), and the same applies to those who take Jibrīl (Gabriel) as an enemy.

**"Gabriel, and Michael"**. Although both belong to the angels who are messengers, Jibrīl and Mīkāʾīl (Michael) are named specifically, because the verse was revealed in support of Jibrīl, who carries Allah's messages to His Prophets, and because the Jews had set Jibrīl down as their enemy and Mīkāʾīl as their friend. Allah's reply is that an enemy of either one is an enemy of the other, and of Allah. The two names are a particular added to a general, "His angels". Several readings of the names are recorded: Jibrīl, Jabrīl, Jibraʾīl, Jabraʾīl, Mīkāl, Mīkāʾīl. Mīkāʾīl too sometimes descended to some of Allah's Prophets, though far less often than Jibrīl, whose task this chiefly was, while Isrāfīl is entrusted with blowing the Trumpet for the Resurrection on the Day of Judgement. The Ṣaḥīḥ records that whenever the Messenger of Allah ﷺ woke at night he would pray: "O Allah, Lord of Jibrīl, of Mīkāʾīl and of Isrāfīl, Creator of the heavens and the earth, who knows the seen and the unseen, it is You who judge between Your servants in what they differ over. Guide me by Your leave to the truth about which they differ, for You guide whom You will to a straight path."

**"then ˹let them know that˺ Allah is certainly the enemy of the disbelievers"**. Allah says "of the disbelievers" rather than "of them" to make their status plain. To make an enemy of Allah's friend is to make an enemy of Allah, and whoever makes an enemy of Allah will find Allah his enemy and will lose in this life and the Hereafter. Al-Bukhārī records from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ related Allah's words: whoever takes a friend of Mine as an enemy, I have declared war on him. So Allah was angry with those who took Jibrīl as an enemy. Allah is the rival of the disbelievers among the Jews and among others, just as Jibrīl, Mīkāʾīl and all the believers are enemies of those who oppose them, and certain defeat awaits whoever has Allah as his rival. Those who deny or oppose the angels and the prophets will meet their due punishment in the other world.

### ʿUmar among the Jews

Ash-Shaʿbī related that ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb said: "I used to go to the Jews while they studied the Torah, amazed at how the Qur'an agreed with the Torah and the Torah with the Qur'an. Once they said to me, 'ʿUmar, there is no one we like more than you.' I asked why, and they said it was because I visited them and mixed with them. I told them I came only out of amazement at how the parts of the Book of Allah strengthen one another, the Qur'an agreeing with the Torah and the Torah with the Qur'an. One day as I sat with them, Allah's Messenger ﷺ passed behind me. The Jews said, 'There is your man, go to him. He has just reached an alley of Madinah.' I turned to them and said, 'I adjure you by Allah and by the Scripture revealed to you, do you know that he is Allah's Messenger?' Their chief told them, 'He has implored you by Allah, so tell him.' They said, 'You are our chief, you tell him.' The chief said, 'We know he is Allah's Messenger.' I said, 'Then you are headed for the worst destruction, knowing that he is Allah's Messenger and refusing to follow him.' They said, 'Among the angels we have one enemy and one ally.' I asked who, and they said, 'Our enemy is Jibrīl, the angel of harshness and coarseness, burden and hardship, and our ally is Mīkāʾīl, the angel of tenderness, gentleness and ease.' I said, 'Then I bear witness that Jibrīl cannot be at enmity with the allies of Mīkāʾīl, nor can Mīkāʾīl be the ally of the enemies of Jibrīl. Both of them, and all who are with them, are enemies of whoever takes either as an enemy, and allies of whoever takes either as an ally.'" The verse was revealed in confirmation of this.

### Hostility to the angels is hostility to Allah

To be hostile to the angels, including Jibrīl and Mīkāʾīl, or to the prophets, amounts to hostility to Allah Himself, and this verse brands the enemies of the angels and prophets as disbelievers by declaring that Allah is the enemy of the disbelievers. The devotional reading reflects on the honour this confers. The Jews claimed that Jibrīl brought no good and that they did not love him, and that had it been Mīkāʾīl they would have believed. The Real exposed their lie, for what good is greater than the Qur'an that came down through him? Then He declared that whoever is an enemy to Jibrīl and Mīkāʾīl has God for an enemy. The messenger sent by the Beloved to His highly esteemed beloved, Muḥammad ﷺ, has a noble station and a weighty rank. The enmity of the disbelievers does no harm to Jibrīl, for the Real is his protector (walī), and whoever is Jibrīl's enemy has God as his enemy. How mighty and glorified is that station, and how grand is His exaltedness!

### In short

- Enmity to Allah, His angels or His messengers, angelic or human (22:75), is a single enmity, since believing in some and rejecting others is disbelief (4:150).
- Jibrīl and Mīkāʾīl are named because the Jews tried to set one against the other, and Allah answers that an enemy of either is an enemy of both and of Him.
- ʿUmar confronted the Jews with their own knowledge of the Prophet ﷺ and refuted the Jibrīl-Mīkāʾīl excuse before the verse came down.
- Allah declares war on whoever makes an enemy of His friend, as the ḥadīth in al-Bukhārī says, and the enemies of the angels cannot hurt them, for Allah is their protector.
