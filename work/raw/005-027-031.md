<<<VERSE 5:27>>>
"Relate to them in truth ˹O Prophet˺ the story of Adam’s two sons—how each offered a sacrifice: Abel’s offering was accepted while Cain’s was not. So Cain threatened, “I will kill you!” His brother replied, “Allah only accepts ˹the offering˺ of the sincerely devout"

This verse begins the story of the two sons of Adam, Habil (Abel) and Qabil (Cain). The Prophet ﷺ is told to recount it truthfully. Each brother offered a sacrifice to Allah, and only Habil's was accepted. Qabil threatened to kill him, and Habil replied that Allah accepts only from those who are God-fearing. The material explains that the story is told as a lesson about envy, injustice and the acceptance of deeds, and that it is set against the earlier account of the cowardice of the Children of Israel. The story condemns unjust killing and shows that its consequences are severe in this life and the next.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Relate to them in truth, O Prophet, the story of Adam's two sons."** The material explains that the word "truth" is a principle for every historical account. The narrative must have nothing false in it, nothing dubious, and nothing added or removed. The material cites the same word of truth in other places: 3:62, about the account of 'Isa, and 18:13 and 19:43, where the account of the people of the cave and of 'Isa are called true. It also cites 19:34, where 'Isa is described as a word of truth. The material says the Qur'an's careful choice of the word truth shows that a narrator must be exact. It also argues that the Prophet ﷺ, who could not read or write, told these events exactly, which is evidence that the revelation came from Allah.

The material notes that most authorities take "the two sons of Adam" to mean the two real sons, Habil and Qabil, though the words could also cover every human being descended from Adam. The Companions and early scholars are reported to have said that the account is meant for the envious and unjust among the people.

**"How each offered a sacrifice: Abel's offering was accepted while Cain's was not."** The material explains that the sacrifice was made to draw nearer to Allah. The sign of acceptance was a fire from the sky that consumed the offering, and an offering that was not consumed remained unaccepted. Habil owned flocks and offered the best of a spring lamb. Qabil was a farmer and offered grain. The fire consumed Habil's offering and left Qabil's untouched. The material gives the background: at that time every child of Adam was born as a twin, a son and a daughter, and marriage was allowed between a son of one birth and a daughter of another. The beautiful daughter of one birth was to marry the son of the other, and Qabil wanted the beautiful sister for himself. Adam proposed the offering as a way of settling the dispute.

**"So Cain threatened, 'I will kill you!'"** The material says that the threat came from envy, after the failure had shamed him. It cites the report that envy is the cause of the threat, because Habil had received a blessing that Qabil had not.

**"His brother replied, 'Allah only accepts the offering of the sincerely devout.'"** The material explains that Habil answered with a principle: acceptance depends on taqwa. If Qabil had been God-fearing, his offering would have been accepted. The material says this also contains the cure for envy. When a person sees another blessed, he should look at his own shortcomings and repent, not wish to take the blessing away.

### Acceptance depends on sincerity and taqwa

The material draws the principle of the verse out in full. Deeds are accepted only when they come from taqwa and sincerity. The material cites Ibn Mas'ud, who said that if he were certain Allah had accepted one of his deeds, he would not give it up for the whole world. It cites Abu al-Darda', who said that one accepted prayer would be better for him than the world and all in it. It also cites 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz, who wrote that no deed is accepted without taqwa, and 'Ali, who said that a deed done with taqwa is never small. The material also says that 'Amir ibn 'Abdullah wept at death, because he did not know whether his worship had been accepted.

### In short

- The story of Habil and Qabil is to be told truthfully, as the Qur'an tells the stories of 'Isa and of the cave.
- The sacrifice of Habil was accepted because of his taqwa, and Qabil's was not.
- Habil's reply gives the principle that deeds are accepted only from the God-fearing, and it is also a cure for envy.

<<<VERSE 5:28>>>
"If you raise your hand to kill me, I will not raise mine to kill you, because I fear Allah—the Lord of all worlds"

This verse gives Habil's reply to the threat. If Qabil raises his hand to kill him, Habil will not raise his hand against Qabil. He gives his reason: he fears Allah, the Lord of all the worlds. The material says that Habil was the stronger of the two, but fear of Allah restrained him. The material links the verse to a saying of the Prophet ﷺ about two Muslims who meet with swords, and to the teaching on patience in the face of fitnah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you raise your hand to kill me."** The material notes that Habil did not reply with anger. He spoke with peace and principle, and he had sympathy for his brother.

**"I will not raise mine to kill you."** The material explains that Habil refused to imitate the crime. He did not want to earn the same sin as his brother. He chose patience and endurance.

**"Because I fear Allah, the Lord of all worlds."** The material says that fear of Allah stopped Habil's hand, and that the only being worthy of fear is Allah, since no one can benefit or harm without His will. Abdullah bin 'Amr is reported to have said that Habil was the stronger, but his fear of Allah restrained his hand.

### The teaching on killing between believers

The material cites the saying of the Prophet ﷺ, recorded in the Two Sahihs, that when two Muslims meet with their swords, both the killer and the killed will be in the Fire. The Companions asked about the one who was killed, and the Prophet ﷺ said that he too had intended to kill his companion. The material cites Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas, who, at the start of the trouble that befell 'Uthman, reported that the Prophet ﷺ said it is better to sit idle in a time of fitnah than to stand up in it. When asked what to do if someone enters one's home to kill him, Sa'd reported that the Prophet ﷺ said to be like the pious son of Adam. At-Tirmidhi recorded this, with similar reports from Abu Hurayrah, Khabbab, Abu Bakr, Ibn Mas'ud, Abu Waqid and Abu Musa.

### In short

- Habil refused to raise his hand against his brother, because he feared Allah.
- The material says that fear of Allah, not weakness, restrained him.
- The verse teaches that a believer must not answer the threat of killing with killing, and that restraint is better in a time of fitnah.

<<<VERSE 5:29>>>
"I want to let you bear your sin against me along with your other sins, then you will be one of those destined to the Fire. And that is the reward of the wrongdoers.”"

This verse gives the rest of Habil's reply. He says that he would rather Qabil bear the sin of killing him, along with his other sins, so that he would be among the people of the Fire. It ends by saying that this is the reward of the wrongdoers. The material explains that this is a warning, and that Habil's statement is not a wish for the Fire, but a statement of what the crime would bring.

### Phrase by phrase

**"I want to let you bear your sin against me along with your other sins."** The material says that Habil, whom the story calls Abel, is speaking. The material explains that the words mean the sin of killing Habil, added to Qabil's earlier sins. Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi and Qatadah are reported to have said the same. The material notes that Habil does not wish to take on that burden himself.

**"Then you will be one of those destined to the Fire."** The material says that the Fire is the place of the one who commits this crime. Habil is describing the end of his brother's path, not praying for it.

**"And that is the reward of the wrongdoers."** The material explains that this is the general rule: those who wrongfully transgress are rewarded with the Fire. The verse makes a general principle out of the particular case.

### The burden of unjust killing

The material says that Adam's first son carries a share of every unjust killing that follows, because he was the first to commit murder. It cites a saying recorded by Ahmad and the group of collectors, except Abu Dawud, and a report of 'Abdullah bin 'Amr, who said that the son of Adam who killed his brother would be the most wretched of men. The material says that a sin of killing brings punishment in this world as well, and that transgression and cutting the ties of kinship are sins that are punished quickly.

### In short

- Habil told his brother that he would rather bear no share in the sin, and that the killer would carry the sin of the killing with his other sins.
- The reward of wrongdoers is the Fire, and this verse states the general rule.
- The first murderer carries a share in every unjust killing that follows, and the punishment for such crimes begins in this life.

<<<VERSE 5:30>>>
"Yet Cain convinced himself to kill his brother, so he killed him—becoming a loser"

This verse tells what Qabil did. His self urged him on, and he killed his brother, and so he became one of the losers. The material explains that the verse shows the inner struggle that leads a person to crime, and that the loss he suffered was both in this life and in the next.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Yet Cain convinced himself to kill his brother."** The material explains that Qabil's self made the killing seem reasonable. The material notes that Shaytan played a part in the crime, and that Adam, in the account, was the father of both men. One report says that Shaytan showed Qabil how to kill, by placing an animal's head on a rock and crushing it with another stone, and that Qabil did the same to his brother. A report from Zayd bin Aslam, passed on by his son 'Abdur-Rahman, says that Qabil was twisting his brother's neck, not knowing how to kill him, until Shaytan told him to strike his head with a stone.

**"So he killed him, becoming a loser."** The material explains that Qabil lost in this life and in the next, and that no loss is greater than this. The material also notes that Qabil did not know what to do with the body, and carried it on his back because he was the first person to die on earth.

### The voice of conscience

The material reads the verse with the teaching of conscience. A question arising in a person's conscience is a test. A person who answers the voice of conscience succeeds, and a person who hides behind false excuses to silence it fails. The material says that Qabil first felt that his action was unjustified, but his envy did not cool, and he invented reasons until he found rest in them and killed his brother.

The material also says that the verse shows the path of sin. A man who buries his bad intention in his heart does not have to bury a body afterwards.

### In short

- Qabil's self urged him to kill his brother, and he did so.
- He became one of the losers, both in this life and the next.
- The verse teaches that the conscience is a test, and that a person who silences it with excuses has failed.

<<<VERSE 5:31>>>
"Then Allah sent a crow digging ˹a grave˺ in the ground ˹for a dead crow˺, in order to show him how to bury the corpse of his brother. He cried, “Alas! Have I ˹even˺ failed to be like this crow and bury the corpse of my brother?” So he became regretful"

This verse tells how Allah taught Qabil what to do with his brother's body. He sent a crow that dug a grave for another dead crow, and Qabil learned how to bury his brother. He cried out that he had failed to be like the crow, and he became regretful. The material says that the regret came after the crime, not before, and that the animal taught the man a lesson in how to bury the dead.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then Allah sent a crow digging a grave for a dead crow."** The material explains that Qabil did not know how to bury his brother. Several reports say that two crows fought, one killed the other, and the living crow dug a grave and buried the dead one. Ibn 'Abbas is reported to have said that a crow came to a dead crow and covered it with sand until it was hidden in the ground.

### Regret after the crime

**"In order to show him how to bury the corpse of his brother."** The material explains that the lesson was practical. The animal taught the man, and the material draws the point that a person can know less than a bird about burying the dead. One reading says Qabil had to bury his brother's naked body, and the material notes that the lesson concerned covering the body.

### The lesson of the crow

**"He cried, 'Alas! Have I even failed to be like this crow and bury the corpse of my brother?'"** The material explains that Qabil was sorry that he had failed to do what the crow had done, but was not sorry for the killing itself. The material says that his regret was for his inability to bury his brother, not for his crime.

**"So he became regretful."** The material says that al-Hasan al-Basri explained the regret as Allah making him feel sorrow for the loss he had earned. The material notes that regret is the end of those who commit sins.

### In short

- Allah taught Qabil how to bury his brother by sending a crow to bury another crow.
- Qabil regretted his failure to bury his brother, but his regret was not for the killing itself.
- The verse shows that regret follows sin, and that the lesson came from an animal.
