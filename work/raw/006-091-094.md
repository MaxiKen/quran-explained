<<<VERSE 6:91>>>
"And they have not shown Allah His proper reverence when they said, “Allah has revealed nothing to any human being.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Who then revealed the Book brought forth by Moses as a light and guidance for people, which you split into separate sheets—revealing some and hiding much? You have been taught ˹through this Quran˺ what neither you nor your forefathers knew.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Allah ˹revealed it˺!” Then leave them to amuse themselves with falsehood"

A claim about revelation is met with one question, and the question is put to people who have a book of their own.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And they have not shown Allah His proper reverence"** They did not glorify Allah as he ought to be glorified. The reading of the inner sense takes the sentence in three parts. He was not recognised the way recognition is owed to him. He was not described the way his description deserves. He was not held in awe the way awe is his due. No one has recognised him as he is worthy of being recognised, and no one has known him as he is worthy of being known. They encompass nothing of his knowledge (2:255), and you have been given no knowledge except a little (17:85).

This is the charge the verse opens with, and it is heavier than the sentence that follows it. A man who has no proper notion of who Allah is can say anything about what Allah does or does not send. The grandeur that belongs to him was simply not reckoned with.

**"when they said, Allah has revealed nothing to any human being"** Some said this verse came down about the Quraysh, and others said it came down about certain Jews, and the context of the verse supports that second reading. If the speakers were the idolaters of Makkah, the matter is obvious, since they believed in no book and no prophet at all. If they were Jews, then what they said was the product of anger and irritation, and it contradicted the dictates of their own religion. The claim is not a doubt. It is a flat denial of the whole idea of books and messengers. One report names the man who said it, Malik ibn al-Sayf, a Jew, and he claimed that Allah had not revealed any Scripture to a human being. Another account says that when this was reported to the Jews of his own community, they were displeased with him, and he was removed from his position of religious authority. Imam al-Baghawi is the one who transmits that.

The same refusal is answered in other verses too. "Is it a wonder to mankind that we inspired to a man from among themselves, saying: warn mankind" (10:2). And: "nothing stopped people from believing when the guidance reached them, except their words: has Allah sent a man as a messenger?" And: "if there were on the earth angels walking about in peace and security, we would have sent down to them from heaven an angel as a messenger" (17:94-95).

**"Who then revealed the Book brought forth by Moses, a light and guidance for people"** Say to the one who denies that revelation happens: who sent down the Torah, which you and everyone else know Allah sent down to Musa the son of ʿImran? He sent it as a light and a guidance. It sheds light on the answers to disputes that people fall into, and it guides a man out of the darkness of doubts. The question is not asked to learn something. Whoever denies that Allah speaks to human beings has to explain the book he already accepts, and the argument is content to wait for that explanation.

**"which you split into separate sheets, revealing some and hiding much"** You made the Torah into separate sheets, copied from the original and then altered, changed and distorted as you wished, and you said: this is from Allah, meaning that you took it to be part of the book he revealed, when it is not from him at all. The word for sheets is the plural of a word for a single page or paper. One note explains the practice behind it. A book kept in loose sheets can have any sheet taken out whenever it is wanted, and that leaves a man free to deny what the sheet enjoins. The example given is the passages of the Torah that described the signs and the attributes of the Messenger of Allah. Those are the sheets that were left out. What you show, in other words, is much of what contains nothing about the description of the Prophet, and what you hide is the description itself.

The reading of the three verbs here is open. Each of them can be taken as third person, so that the Jews are being spoken about, or as second person, so that they are being addressed to their faces. The ending alūnahu belongs to the first of these, and the second changes only the person. What you show is much of what says nothing about the description of the Prophet, and what you hide is that description. Nothing in the argument turns on the choice.

**"You have been taught what neither you nor your forefathers knew"** Consider who sent this Qur'an down. In it Allah taught these people the story of the nations before them and the story of what is still to come, when neither they nor their ancestors had a line of it. One reading takes the address as being to the Arabs. Another takes it as being to the Jews, taught through the Qur'an what neither they nor their fathers had in the Torah, because what they were confused about and quarrelling over has now been made plain in it. Legal rulings, punishments, the lawful and the unlawful, and the description of the Prophet are what they were taught by it, and none of that had reached them before through the Torah or through the Injil.

**"Say: Allah! Then leave them to amuse themselves with falsehood"** Say: Allah sent it down. There is no other correct answer to the question. If they answer it, well. And if they will not answer, the point stands without them. Then leave them to play in their ignorant and misguided state, to amuse themselves with quibbling and lying, until the true news comes to them from their Lord. Then they will know whether the good end belongs to them or to the fearful servants of Allah.

### What it means to read a book page by page

The verse's charge about splitting the sheets is worked out in one commentary as a disease of hearing, not of copying. Such people call attention to some teachings of God and deliberately omit others. They listen to and recite the verses about reward, and pass over the verses about the deeds that earn reward. They single out whatever supports the petty details of their politics, and ignore whatever instructs in quiet reform. They elaborate the verses that let them display their talent for hair-splitting, and skim past the verses that set out the eternal realities of religion. Verses that hint at their superiority are given frequent public airing, and verses that mention their responsibilities are neglected. People who treat the book of God this way develop a brazen disregard for the truth. They get into frivolous and trivial discussion, then they bicker, and then they make contradictory statements. No real co-operation can be expected from them. And the question is put plainly: if people will not do justice to God's Book, how will they be just with human beings?

The same fault is what makes the call ineffective. A caller, however perfect he is, reaches a heart only when the listener carries a fearing heart in his breast and treats the Hereafter as a matter of the utmost gravity. Where those two qualities are missing, the one who gives the call can do him no good at all.

### Leave them

The last words of the verse are taken by the people of the inner way as a rule for the heart rather than a dismissal of an opponent. "Say: Allah, then leave them" is read as an eloquent allusion to solitariness. It means: make your aspiration one-pointed, recognise the Real as one, keep the heart turned toward him, and set the others aside. What has a man seized by his love to do with others? Majestic is the unity, and how can there be any finding? Holy is the self-sufficient, and how can there be any arrival? He is known, and yet taking him in with knowledge is absurd. He is seen, and yet perceiving his attribute is impossible. He withdrew his own description and measure, so that nothing exalted could overreach his exaltation and no intellect could take in his scope. And what does water and dust have in common with the one who was, and is, and will be? In this work, this world and the next are a single wall, and for the recognizer to talk about the two of them is defect and shame. Al-Shibli said to one of his companions: "Stick to God, leave everything else, and be with him."

### In short

- The verse begins by measuring the men by what they think of Allah, and only then by what they say.
- One question settles the case: the book you already believe in had to come from somewhere.
- Splitting a scripture into sheets is described as a way of keeping the right to deny what is inconvenient.
- Both audiences are addressed in the sentence about being taught what neither they nor their fathers knew, and the entry gives both readings.
- The command to leave them to their play closes the argument, and the inner reading turns it into an instruction about the heart.

<<<VERSE 6:92>>>
"This is a blessed Book which We have revealed—confirming what came before it—so you may warn the Mother of Cities and everyone around it. Those who believe in the Hereafter ˹truly˺ believe in it and guard their prayers"

The book answers the charge about books, and its address is wider than the town it first reaches.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is a blessed Book which We have revealed"** This is the Qur'an, and it is blessed because it contains mercy and forgiveness for whoever believes in it. Gabriel brought it down. The argument in the previous verse ended with the Torah, and now the book in front of them is described in the same terms, so that the comparison is made on their own ground.

**"confirming what came before it"** It agrees with the Torah, the Gospel, the Psalms and every earlier scripture on Allah's oneness, and on the description of Muhammad. The confirmation is the proof of its truth. One comment draws the reason this book came so late. The earlier two were sent for the children of Israel, and for the other branch, the children of Ismaʿil, the Arabs living around Umm al-Qura, no prophet and no book of guidance had come until then. This Qur'an was sent for them in particular and for the whole world in general.

**"so you may warn the Mother of Cities and everyone around it"** The Mother of Towns is Makkah, and Makkah al-Mu'azzamah is called Umm al-Qura by the Qur'an itself. The name means the root and the foundation of all towns and habitations. Two reasons are given for it. According to the historical narratives, the land mass of the earth first began to appear from that place. And it is the qiblah, the central point of attraction for every act of worship in the world. Those around it are the people of Makkah and all the lands round about, which takes in the east, the west, the north and the south of it, that is, the whole world, the Arabs and the rest of the children of Adam, Arab and non-Arab alike. The same range is stated elsewhere. "Say: O mankind, I am sent to you all as the Messenger of Allah" (7:158). "That I may therewith warn you and whoever it reaches" (6:19). "And for whichever of the sects rejects it, the Fire is their promised meeting place" (11:17). "Blessed is he who sent down the criterion to his servant, so that he would be a warner to all the worlds" (25:1). And: "say to those who were given the Scripture and to the unlettered: will you submit?" And: "if you do, you are rightly guided, and if you turn away, your duty is only to convey the message, and Allah sees his servants" (3:20). It is recorded in the two Sahīhs that the Messenger said: "Every prophet was sent only to his own nation, and I have been sent to all people." That is one of the five things he was given which no one before him had been given.

**"Those who believe in the Hereafter truly believe in it"** Belief in this blessed book, the Qur'an, belongs to the people who believe in Allah and in the Last Day. Those who believe in life after death, and in the delight of Paradise, put their faith in it. A person who believes in the Day of Judgement is moved by the fear of Allah, and that fear lets him reason it out, and once he is convinced he steps past the constraints of ignorant custom and takes what is true.

**"and guard their prayers"** They are constant in guarding their salah. They perform what Allah ordered, offering the prayers complete and on time, at the appointed hour for the five daily prayers. They keep up their prayer by maintaining its requirements, its essentials and its recommended acts, at the times fixed by the sacred law.

### The disease the verse is treating

The verse's pairing of belief in the Hereafter with prayer carries an admonition against a habit these people were known for. They accepted what they liked and rejected what they did not like, opening a front against whatever they had rejected, with a typical lack of concern. That is the effect of not believing in the Hereafter. A little thought shows that a lack of concern for the Last Day is the mother of every spiritual disease. Kufr and shirk and sin and disobedience all come from there. When a believer in the Hereafter slips into a mistake or a sin, he feels a pinch in his heart, and sooner or later he repents and resolves to keep away from it. Fear of Allah and concern for the Last Day are the channels that make a human being what he should be, and make him able to refuse crime and disobedience by a conscious choice. That is why no surah of the Qur'an, and perhaps no ruku either, is left without a reminder of it. The comment on this passage ends with the prayer the verse invites: "O Allah, make all our concerns a single concern, the concern for the Hereafter."

### In short

- The book is called blessed before it is called confirmed, and its confirmation of earlier scripture is offered as evidence rather than as a claim.
- The address to the Mother of Cities is argued from the name of the place, and the whole world is inside "everyone around it".
- A hadith in the two authentic collections is the proof that this message is general, since earlier prophets were sent to their own nations alone.
- Belief in the Hereafter is what makes a person capable of believing in this book and of guarding the prayer, and it is presented as the cure of the fault in the previous verse.

<<<VERSE 6:93>>>
"Who does more wrong than the one who fabricates lies against Allah or claims, “I have received revelations!”—although nothing was revealed to them—or the one who says, “I can reveal the like of Allah’s revelations!”? If you ˹O Prophet˺ could only see the wrongdoers in the throes of death while the angels are stretching out their hands ˹saying˺, “Give up your souls! Today you will be rewarded with the torment of disgrace for telling lies about Allah and for being arrogant towards His revelations!”"

Three claims are named, and then the scene that answers them is shown as a vision.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Who does more wrong than the one who fabricates lies against Allah"** No one is more unjust than a man who lies about Allah. The lie can be a claim that he has partners or a son. It can be a claim that Allah sent him as a prophet when he did not. The verse's question form is deliberate, since nobody is supposed to be able to produce a worse case.

**"or claims, 'I have received revelations', although nothing was revealed to them"** Or he says: it is revealed to me, and he has not been inspired with anything at all. ʿIkrimah and Qatadah said that this part of the verse came down about Musaylimah al-Kadhdhab, the liar, who claimed prophethood. One gloss attaches the first part of the verse to Malik ibn al-Sayf, the man who said that Allah had revealed nothing to any human being, and the second part to Musaylimah.

**"or the one who says, 'I can reveal the like of Allah's revelations'"** And whoever says: I will reveal the like of what Allah has revealed, is putting his invented words beside revelation. This, in one account, is ʿAbdullah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh. The same arrogance appears in another verse about the mockers of the Quraysh: "And when our verses are recited to them, they say: we have heard this before, and if we wished we could say the like of this."

**"If you could only see the wrongdoers in the throes of death"** The conditional has no answer stated in the verse, and the sense of the missing answer is: you would see a terrifying sight. The wrongdoers here are described in one gloss as the idolaters and the hypocrites, and it is said that what is meant is the day of Badr, since that day is called a day of judgement in its own right. They are in the hardships, the agonies and the afflictions of death, and the angels are stretching out their hands against them, beating them, until their souls leave their bodies. Two other verses use the stretching out of hands in the same sense. "If you stretch your hand against me to kill me" (5:28). "They stretch forth their hands and their tongues against you with evil" (60:2). Ad-Dahhak and Abu Salih both said that the outstretched hands here are hands of torment. Another verse states it outright: "how will it be when the angels take away the souls of those who disbelieve, smiting their faces and their backs" (8:50).

**"Give up your souls!"** Deliver your souls to us, so that we may seize them. When a disbeliever is near death, the angels bring him the good news of torment, vengeance, chains, restraints, the Fire, boiling water, and the anger of the Most Merciful. His soul scatters through his body and refuses to come out, and they go on beating him until it does.

The scene at the hour of death is the subject of many reports passed down in a way that rules out error on the part of the transmitters. The commentary leaves them to another verse, the one where Allah says that he keeps the believers firm with the word that stands firm, in this life and in the Hereafter (14:27).

**"Today you will be rewarded with the torment of disgrace"** Today you are paid the doom of degradation, utterly humiliated, because you said about Allah other than the truth, claiming prophethood and inspiration falsely, and because you turned away from his signs with arrogance. You thought yourselves too great to believe in the Qur'an and in the man it came to.

### The inward reading of the same verse

On the tongue of commentary, the lies in question are the lies of Musaylimah and al-Ansi, and that has been explained. On the tongue of allusion, and on the tasting of the people of the path, the verse carries another secret. It rests on the report of al-Mustafa, that every verse has an outward face and an inward one. The reality of that secret is this. Whoever claims the recognition of Allah is in truth a forger, because the majesty of the unity is pure and has no need of recognition from water and dust. The masters of the tariqah said: "Whoever remembers has forged, whoever shows patience has been audacious, and whoever recognises is cut off." Junayd said: "My greatest sin is my recognition of him." He meant the fancy that he had recognised Allah the way it is fitting to recognise him, with nothing left out of his reality and no limit crossed in his height. Such recognition does not come from a son of Adam, and neither his understanding nor his imagination reaches it. It belongs only to the Lordhood's own knowledge, since in reality he recognises himself and knows himself. The attributes of a thing that arrives have no access to eternity. Whatever comes out of the compass of power into the world of ignorance is imprisoned by change, and changing things have no access to stability. Whoever lets himself be guided by gazing and inference is walking in his own tracks, and a man walking in his own tracks is simply deceived. Anyone who hopes to arrive at recognition is really after his own share, and a person chasing his own share is nourishing himself and bowing down to himself. The command that follows from this is blunt: quit being the companion of self-fed habit-worshippers, and kiss the dust at the feet of the men who have disowned themselves. The son of Adam was not, and then he came to be. Whatever was not and then was is unbeing by its own nature, so how should knowledge of being arrive out of it? He stands between two stretches of nothing, and in himself he is nothing at all, and nothing cannot bring everything about. Good fortune does not come by artifice, and recognition does not come by causes. Felicity does not come by worship, and recognition does not come by being sufficient unto yourself. Al-Shibli said: "You did not recognise him with your recognition." What had happened instead, he went on, was that they had "perceived him with their knowledges and their opinions", and "formed a notion of him with their imaginations and their understandings". And his last word: "You measured him with your beliefs and your hearts, and all of that is created like you, and it turns back on you."

### In short

- The three claims in the verse are three different crimes, and each of them is tied to a named man in the reports.
- The threat is shown rather than argued, which is what the unanswering "if you could only see" is for.
- The beating of the soul out of the body is described in the same terms in another verse, and the hands the angels stretch out are glossed as hands of torment.
- The inward reading makes the verse a warning against claiming to know Allah at all, and it treats even recognition as a possible lie.

<<<VERSE 6:94>>>
"˹Today˺ you have come back to Us all alone as We created you the first time—leaving behind everything We have provided you with. We do not see your intercessors with you—those you claimed were Allah’s partners ˹in worship˺. All your ties have been broken and all your claims have let you down.”"

The scene shifts from death to what comes after it, and the address is now to a man with nothing in his hands.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You have come back to Us all alone as We created you the first time"** This will be said to them on the day of return. They come singly, each one alone, with no family, no possessions and no children, as they were created at the first, barefoot, naked and uncircumcised. Another verse gives the same address: "they will be brought before your Lord in rows, and it will be said: now you have come to Us as We created you the first time" (18:48). The sense is that as he began their creation, so he brings them back, and they had used to deny the Resurrection and rule out that it could happen. So this is the Day of Rising.

**"leaving behind everything We have provided you with"** The wealth and the money a man collects in this life, all of it he leaves behind him. It is recorded in an authentic collection that the Messenger of Allah said: "The son of Adam says: my money, my money! What part of your money do you actually have, other than what you ate and so used up, what you wore and so wore out, and what you gave in charity and so it remains? The rest you depart from and leave to the people." Al-Hasan al-Basri said something to the same effect about the same verse. On the Day of Rising, he said, the son of Adam will be brought as though he were a golden chariot, and Allah will ask him: where is what you collected? He will answer: Lord, I collected it and left it as intact as it ever was. Allah will say: where is what you sent ahead for yourself of good deeds? And the man will know that he sent nothing ahead for himself. Ibn Abi Hatim recorded this statement from him, and al-Hasan then recited this very verse.

**"We do not see your intercessors with you, those you claimed were Allah's partners"** This is a rebuke aimed at the rivals, the idols and the images that people worshipped in this life, believing that they would be useful to them here and at the Resurrection, if there is a Resurrection. They are not in sight. The claim they made is examined on the spot. It was claimed that these gods had a share in their worship, and that they were partners in that sense, and now the intercessors are missing from the room. Allah calls them while the rest of creation listens: "where are my partners whom you claimed?" (28:62). And: "it will be said to them: where are those you used to worship instead of Allah? Can they help you, or help themselves?" (26:92-93). And: "it will be said: call upon your partners, and they will call upon them, but they will give them no answer" (28:64).

**"All your ties have been broken"** Now you and they have been cut off from each other. The other reading of the same words takes the meaning to be that all the connections, the means and the ties between you and them have been severed. One variant recitation reads it as an adverbial phrase, so that the bond between you is what has been cut. And all that you used to claim, your hope of benefit from the idols and the rivals you worshipped beside Allah, has vanished and abandoned you. Their false deities go away from them, as another verse says, and their invented intercession fails them (10:30).

Three further statements belong to this severing. "When those who were followed declare themselves innocent of those who followed them, and they see the punishment, all their relations are cut off from them. And those who followed will say: if only we had one more return, we would disown them as they have disowned us. Thus Allah will show them their deeds as regret for them, and they will never come out of the Fire" (2:166-167). "Then when the Trumpet is blown, there will be no kinship among them that day, and they will not ask about one another" (23:101). "You have taken idols instead of Allah, and the love between you is only in the life of this world. On the Day of Resurrection you will disown each other and curse each other, your abode will be the Fire, and you will have no helper" (29:25). And in the assembly of judgement: "on that day We will gather them all together, then We will say to the people of shirk" (10:28), until the end of the passage, where their invented gods vanish from them.

### Why words fail at this point

The haughtiness that made a man say what he said in the previous verse came from his position in the world and from the goods available to him. He forgot that every blessing sent his way is there to test him, and only for a finite period. When the time of death arrives, all of it is snatched from him suddenly, and what follows is a lone existence, like the one he came into the world with. In that stage there is no wealth and no position, no companions and no intermediaries. There is nothing but himself and his Maker. The things he was proud of will not stand between him and God's punishment.

A great many people work their way through life on the strength of words. Everyone has a vocabulary for making his own existence look justified and his own conduct look sound. He paints the path he is on as straight, and as running directly to the right ends. But when the revolution of the Hereafter unveils what things actually are, the meaninglessness of all that speech will be the most obvious thing in the scene.

### In short

- Coming back alone is described in the same terms as coming into existence: no family, no property, and no cover of rank.
- Leaving wealth behind is illustrated by a prophetic report about the three things a man actually keeps from it, and by al-Hasan al-Basri's picture of the reckoning.
- The missing intercessors are examined as a factual claim, and the question asked in front of all creation is where they are.
- Every tie of the world, blood, alliance and love, is shown as severed at exactly the point where it was supposed to help.
- The verse closes the argument about revelation by showing that speech about oneself does not survive the scene it is spoken into.
