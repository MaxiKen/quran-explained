<<<VERSE 5:54>>>
The prohibition just stated is followed by a promise that removes any fear of depending on it: "Whoever among you abandons their faith, Allah will replace them with others who love Him and are loved by Him." Islam does not survive because a particular group protects it. Allah does.

The verse then describes the people He raises in place of those who turn back. Their qualities are not incidental praise. They identify who is welcome and dear in Allah's sight, and what a person must become if he is to stand against disbelief and apostasy.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Whoever among you abandons their faith"** speaks of turning back from Islam to disbelief. One gloss identifies the tribes meant as Asad, Ghatafan and some people from Kindah and Murad, and the replacement people as the people of Yaman. Another reads the wording as notification of what Allah knew would happen, since some did apostatise after the Prophet's ﷺ death.

**"Allah will replace them with others who love Him and are loved by Him."** The replacement is certain. Similar statements elsewhere say that if people turn away, Allah will exchange them for others unlike them, and that He can remove a creation and bring a new one, which is not difficult for Him (14:19-20).

**"They will be humble with the believers but firm towards the disbelievers"** describes the qualities of complete believers. They are gentle with their believing brothers and allies and stern with enemies and adversaries. Al-Fath describes those with the Messenger ﷺ as severe against disbelievers and merciful among themselves (48:29).

**"struggling in the Way of Allah"** adds sustained effort for the faith. Devotion in seclusion and a merely gentle or merely hard temperament are not enough for this task. It requires feeling and fervour for establishing the religion.

**"fearing no blame from anyone."** No obstacle holds them back from obedience to Allah, from setting up His law, from fighting His enemies or from commanding good and prohibiting evil. Neither an opponent who blocks them nor a critic who reproaches them turns them from that path.

**"This is the favour of Allah. He grants it to whoever He wills."** These qualities are Allah's gift, not a person's own achievement. Success in the work is not produced by planning, strength or numbers alone.

**"And Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing"** describes His favour as ever extending and His knowledge as perfect concerning who deserves it. One gloss explains the wording as generous in giving and knowing to whom He gives.

### How can Allah's love for a person be something he can act upon?

The first quality has two parts. Loving Allah is, to a degree, within a person's reach. Even where emotional love is not immediately present, intention and determination to work for the One loved can be chosen.

The causes of natural love are also within reach. Meditating on Allah's greatness and majesty, conceiving His perfect power and recalling His authority and blessings generates love in the heart.

The other part, Allah's love for the person, appears to lie outside human choice. Yet the Qur'an supplies its means. The Messenger ﷺ is told to say that whoever loves Allah should follow him, and Allah will then love him (3:31).

Following the Sunnah becomes the pivot of life, habitually and in every department of it. That is the promised cause of Allah's love. The same verse is then read as identifying the group able to meet the challenge of kufr and apostasy: those who follow the Sunnah conscientiously, neither falling short of the Shariah's injunctions nor introducing deeds contrary to it.

### What do humility and firmness mean here?

The word translated humble can be read as the plural of either of two forms. One means low or despicable, and the other soft and tractable, easily led. Most explanations take the second sense here: these people are soft with Muslims.

That softness extends to disputes. They can be mollified and will set a dispute aside even when they are in the right. An authentic hadith records the Prophet ﷺ guaranteeing a home in the middle of Paradise to whoever abandons a dispute despite being correct. The practical meaning is that they do not quarrel with Muslims over their rights and dealings.

The word translated firm is the plural of a term meaning dominant, strong and hard. These people cannot be controlled or manipulated by the enemies of Allah and His religion.

Combining the two gives the essential point. Their love and dislike, friendship and enmity, are not for their own persons or rights. They are for Allah, His Messenger ﷺ and His religion. When they fight, it is not against obedient servants of Allah but against those hostile and disobedient to Him.

The Messenger ﷺ is described as the smiling fighter, smiling toward his allies and fighting his enemies. The balance is not indifference. It is the correct placement of gentleness and strength.

### What does refusing to fear blame require?

Whoever leads a movement meets two kinds of obstacle. One is the strength of the opponent. The other is defamation by his own people. Experience shows that determined people can endure arrest, imprisonment, beating and torture, yet falter when blamed and defamed by those closest to them.

That is why this quality is stated so plainly. The verse considers it sufficient to say that such people continue their striving without regard for blame. Their concern is Allah's pleasure and His blame, not the opinion of created beings.

Ahmad records Abu Dharr saying that his intimate friend, the Messenger ﷺ, commanded him to do seven things. He was told to love the poor and stay near them, to look at those below him rather than above him, to maintain ties of kinship even when relatives cut them, to ask nothing of anyone, to say the truth even when it is bitter, and not to fear anyone's blame for Allah's sake.

The seventh was to repeat often that there is no strength or power except from Allah, words described as a treasure beneath the Throne. The report is confirmed in the Sahih. Its list joins humility toward the poor with truthfulness and independence from people's approval.

A related saying in the Sahih states that a believer is not required to humiliate himself. Asked how that happens, the Prophet ﷺ answered that it is by taking on trials he cannot bear. Refusing to fear blame is therefore not the same as recklessly courting every confrontation.

### How is this connected with a deeper reading of love?

An inward explanation calls the verse an allusion for those who know and good news for the faithful. The allusion is that Allah is the guardian and defender of the religion of submission and that it will always remain. What does the religion lose if some people turn away?

If some turn back, the Lord of might brings others who embrace it with spirit and heart and nurture it joyfully. Through them Allah preserves the signposts of His commands and the foundations of His prohibitions, and adorns the carpet of the Shariah with their places.

Their love is described as inscribed upon them. Faith is written in their hearts (58:22), and their inward eye is lit with the lamp of recognition, so that a person is upon a light from his Lord (39:22). Allah is their nurturer, the lap of prophethood their cradle, gentleness the lodge of their gaze and awe the resting place of their aspiration.

Another passage says that if some disbelieve, Allah has already entrusted the message to a people who will not disbelieve in it (6:89). A prophetic statement adds that a group among his people will never cease supporting the truth, that those who oppose them will not harm them, and that Allah's command will then come.

For the faithful there are glad tidings here. A person who does not turn back is reckoned with the friends, the people of love and faith. The order of the wording matters. Allah's love is affirmed first, then the servants' love, showing that a servant does not love until Allah loves him. Wasiti said that the Fire was nullified when Allah mentioned His love for them in the words describing a people whom He loves and who love Him.

### Which trial did this verse announce?

Most explanations treat the verse as a prophecy of the trial of apostasy and as good news for the group that would overcome it. Its germs had already spread toward the end of the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime. After his death it became an epidemic across the Arabian Peninsula.

Musaylimah al-Kadhdhab, the liar, was the first to claim prophethood alongside the Prophet ﷺ. He was bold enough to send the Prophet's ﷺ emissaries back with a threat that he would have killed them had the rule protecting envoys not forbidden it. The Prophet ﷺ did not live to wage jihad against him.

Aswad al-'Ansi, chief of the Mudhhaj tribe in Yaman, also announced prophethood. The Prophet ﷺ ordered his appointed governor of Yaman to fight him. He was killed, and the Prophet ﷺ died the day after that night. News of his death reached the Companions at the end of Rabi' al-Awwal.

Tulayhah ibn Khuwaylid, chief of Banu Asad, made a similar claim. Groups from these three tribes had turned away during the Prophet's ﷺ final illness. The news of his death released the storm. Across Arabia, seven tribes in various regions rose against the faith and its governing authority, withholding the zakah that Islamic law required them to pay to Abu Bakr As-Siddiq, the caliph of that time. Refusing the alms tax was not treated as a private opinion about wealth. It amounted to a refusal of the authority of the law itself.

### How did Abu Bakr respond?

Responsibility for the community fell on Abu Bakr at a moment of shock and rebellion together. 'A'ishah describes her father's grief after the Prophet's ﷺ death as something that would have crumbled lofty mountains into dust had it been laid upon them. Allah gave him patience and fortitude, and he prevailed.

Rebellion could be met only by force, but the situation was dangerous. He consulted the Companions, and none initially approved a hard line. The fear was that internal warfare would leave the young community exposed to outside powers.

Allah then strengthened his heart and settled him on jihad. He delivered an eloquent khutbah, a sermon, that opened the Companions' hearts to the same course. His words are reported as saying that whoever turns back after becoming Muslim and rejects the Prophet's ﷺ injunctions and the law of Islam must be fought.

He added that even if all jinn and human beings, and all the trees and rocks of the world, came against him and he had no comrade, he would still place his own neck on the stake and carry out that jihad alone. He then mounted his horse and began to move.

The Companions came forward, made Siddiq al-Akbar sit down again and quickly drew up a battle plan with fronts assigned and departure times set. Khalid ibn al-Walid was sent to Yamamah against Musaylimah, who had grown powerful in his area. The fighting was hard, but Musaylimah was ultimately killed at the hands of Wahshi, and his people repented and returned to Islam.

Khalid also went against Tulayhah ibn Khuwaylid, who escaped to another region. Allah later gave him the ability to repent, and he came back into Islam. Word that Aswad al-'Ansi was dead and that his followers had given up reached Madinah near the close of Rabi' al-Awwal, which was the first month of Abu Bakr's caliphate.

That was the first news of victory to reach him in those circumstances. From then on the Companions were given clearer victories on every front against the tribes that had refused zakah.

### Whom does the verse identify?

'Ali, Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Ad-Dahhak and Qatadah are reported as saying that the verse was revealed about Abu Bakr As-Siddiq. On that reading he was the first of the promised people who would rise to defend Islam at Allah's command.

That does not exclude others. Elders who pointed to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari or other Companions are not really in contradiction. Al-Hakim al-Naysaburi records in his Sahih that the Prophet ﷺ said of the promised people that they are people like him, pointing to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari.

The soundest and safest position is that all these honoured people are included, and with them every Muslim until the Day of Resurrection who confronts disbelief and apostasy according to the Qur'an's commands. The description is of qualities, not of a single closed group in one year.

The historical demonstration then confirms the promise of the verse that follows. The qualities listed were visibly present in Abu Bakr and the Companions with him: Allah's love for them, their love for Him, gentleness with Muslims and firmness against the enemies of faith, and jihad in Allah's way without fear of blame.

### What is the personal test in all this?

The most critical moment in a person's life is when sacrifice is required to remain resolutely on the path of Islam. Such occasions arise so that a person may clearly confirm or reject his faith.

Allah wants that proof not only in comfortable conditions but when a person must restrain his emotions or face danger to his life and property. Only by passing that test does he become entitled to be counted among Allah's faithful servants.

Proof at such a moment validates a person's earlier deeds. Failing it renders them valueless. The test in this world is a test of intention, and intention is proved by disregarding danger and taking the first step toward Allah. His help then comes.

Someone who does not prove his intention and does not take that step is a transgressor in Allah's sight and cannot expect help given without his own part. The verse's promise is therefore not a reason for passivity. It is a reason for confidence in Allah while doing what He has commanded.

### In short

- Islam is protected by Allah. Apostasy by some people does not endanger the religion, and He raises others in their place.
- The replacement people love Allah and are loved by Him, and that love is pursued by following the Sunnah in every part of life.
- They are gentle with believers and firm against the enemies of faith, and their loyalty is for Allah rather than for personal rights.
- They strive without fearing blame, including blame from their own people, and they do not humiliate themselves by seeking trials they cannot bear.
- The trial of apostasy after the Prophet's ﷺ death, and Abu Bakr's response to it, are reported as the fulfilment of this promise, which also extends to every later Muslim who confronts disbelief.

<<<VERSE 5:55>>>
After forbidding one kind of alliance, the verse names the alliance that is real: "Your only guardians are Allah, His Messenger, and fellow believers". Every other bond is perishable. Loyalty to the Messenger ﷺ is not separate from loyalty to Allah, and the believers described here are marked by prayer, alms and humility.

The positive statement answers the preceding prohibition. A person is not left with a rule about what to avoid. He is told where genuine protection and companionship are found.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Your only guardians are Allah, His Messenger"** identifies the real friend, protector and guide of a Muslim in every condition. Allah alone can be that friend at all times. Fidelity to the Prophet ﷺ is connected with Allah and not separated from Him.

**"and fellow believers"** adds the sincere companions of a believer. One explanation describes this as the protection, help and intimate companionship available to a believer, in contrast to what was forbidden earlier.

**"who establish prayer and pay alms-tax"** describes them by two obligations. They fulfil salah punctually, observing its etiquettes and conditions, and give zakah from their wealth. Prayer is among the most important pillars of Islam because it worships Allah alone without partners, while zakah is the right of other people and help extended to the needy and poor.

**"with humility"** translates the word for bowing. Its interpretation is discussed below, and the difference matters for understanding the character of these guardians.

### What does the final word mean?

Some leading explanations take it as the technical bowing that is a pillar of prayer. On that reading the phrase follows the mention of prayer in order to distinguish Muslim prayer from the prayer of others, since Jews and Christians also pray but their prayer has no bowing of that kind.

Most explanations instead take the word in its lexical sense of bowing as modesty and humility. Abu Hayyan in Tafsir al-Bahr al-Muhit and Al-Zamakhshari in al-Kashshaf adopt that meaning, and Tafsir Mazhari and Tafsir Bayan al-Qur'an follow it. These people do not become proud of their good deeds. Their natural disposition is modesty.

A third discussion rejects a literal reading in which alms are handed over during the bowing of prayer. It observes that, if that were the meaning, giving alms while bowing would be the best way to give them, and no scholar from whom rulings are taken says so. It therefore explains the phrase as attending prayer in congregation in the mosques and spending in charity on the various needs of Muslims.

Another gloss reads it as humility or as performing voluntary prayers. The readings are not mutually exclusive in their moral point: the guardians described are people of regular worship who are not arrogant about it.

### What is reported about 'Abdullah Ibn Salam?

When 'Abdullah Ibn Salam said that his own people had shunned him, this verse was revealed. Another account names him and his companions, including Asad, Usayd and Tha'labah Ibn Qays, and describes them as shunned by the Jews.

The context explains the comfort in the wording. Someone cut off from his former community is told where his real protection lies. The believers named in the explanation include Abu Bakr and his companions, described as establishing the five daily prayers, giving the poor due from their wealth and bowing in congregation with the Prophet ﷺ.

### What is reported about 'Ali and the ring?

Some narrations say this sentence was revealed about 'Ali. He is described as praying, and when a needy person asked him for something while he was bowing, he took a ring from his finger and passed it to him rather than delaying until the prayer ended.

That eagerness toward good is described as pleasing to Allah and as being praised by this wording. The chain of authority for the narration is a debated matter among scholars and hadith specialists, and the material records that debate rather than settling it silently.

Even if the narration is accepted, its meaning is not that guardianship belongs to one person alone. It shows that those deserving the close friendship of Muslims are ordinary Muslims who fulfil prayer and zakah particularly and regularly, and that 'Ali is especially deserving among them.

Two prophetic reports support that standing. One says that whoever counts the Prophet ﷺ as a friend should count 'Ali as a friend, recorded by Ahmad. Another asks Allah to befriend whoever befriends him and to treat as an enemy whoever shows him enmity.

The explanation suggests he may have been given this honour because a future trial was unveiled to him, and he knew that people would nurse enmity toward him and even rise in rebellion, as happened with the Khawarij.

### Does the wording apply only to him?

No. Even if the verse is connected with that event, its wording is general. It includes all the Companions and all Muslims, and is not a specific command about one individual.

When Imam Baqar was asked whether the words about the believers in this verse mean 'Ali, he answered that 'Ali too is included under the believers and falls within the verse's scope. That answer preserves his honour without narrowing the text.

The point matters for how the verse is used. It establishes where a believer's loyalty belongs, not a restriction of guardianship to a single person.

### What does an inward reading add?

Abu Sa'id Khurraz describes how Allah takes a servant as a friend. He opens for him the door of remembrance. When the servant takes pleasure in remembrance, He opens the door of nearness, raises him to the sitting place of intimacy and seats him on the throne of tawhid, affirming Allah's oneness.

The veils are then lifted from him. He is placed in the house of being alone with Allah, and majesty and tremendousness are disclosed to him. When the servant's sight falls upon that majesty, he remains without himself, becoming a servant in annihilation, falling under Allah's protection and rid of his soul's claims.

The first kindness is described as keeping the servant in remembrance, so that he turns from his own work to Allah's work, from remembering himself to remembering Allah, and from love of himself to love of Allah. Once he is at rest in that remembrance and love, Allah brings him near.

The marks of nearness are the sweetness of obedience, dislike of disobedience, withdrawal from people and pleasure in seclusion. The servant becomes a stranger to himself, one with Allah, consumed within himself, having reached his Patron.

The same passage carries a longing prayer of the Pir of the Tariqah, asking to be delivered from the trouble of Eve and Adam, freed from the bonds of existence and nonexistence, and to take ease with the Beloved in the sitting place of intimacy. It ends by asking how long there will be talk of Adam's attributes and creation, and of the world's time and eternity.

That inward language does not cancel the outward description of the verse. It explains what the guardianship of Allah feels like in a heart that has been turned away from everything else. Remembrance, obedience and humility are its visible marks.

### In short

- Real guardianship belongs to Allah, His Messenger ﷺ and sincere believers, not to alliances forbidden earlier.
- Those believers are marked by regular prayer, zakah and humility rather than pride in their deeds.
- The final word is read as ritual bowing, as modesty, or as congregational prayer and charitable spending.
- The reports about Ibn Salam's shunning and about 'Ali's ring are preserved with the debate over the latter's chain, and the wording remains general.
- The inward description of remembrance and nearness explains the character of a person whose loyalty is truly Allah's.

<<<VERSE 5:56>>>
The conclusion is short and decisive: "then it is certainly Allah’s party that will prevail." Whoever takes Allah, His Messenger ﷺ and the believers as protectors joins a side that cannot be defeated. The promise is not conditional on numbers, resources or the strength of the opposing alliance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever allies themselves with Allah, His Messenger, and fellow believers,"** describes the choice set against the forbidden alliance. One explanation adds that this means seeking their help and support. Another describes it as accepting the allegiance of Allah, His Messenger ﷺ and the faithful believers.

**"then it is certainly Allah’s party that will prevail"** states the outcome. The party of Allah is described as His soldiers, namely Muhammad ﷺ and his Companions, victorious over their enemies. Another explanation says that whoever affiliates with Allah, His Messenger ﷺ and the believers will be helped and assisted, and that they are the victors because of that help.

The wording is explained as placing the party of Allah where an implied statement about them would stand, since they belong to His party and are His followers. The sense is that victory belongs to them because Allah supports them, not because of anything in their own strength.

### What else does the Qur'an say about this party?

A related passage records Allah's decree that He and His messengers will be victorious, and describes Allah as all powerful and almighty. It then says that no people who believe in Allah and the Last Day will be found befriending those who oppose Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, even if those are their fathers, sons, brothers or kindred.

For such believers, faith has been written in their hearts and they have been strengthened with a proof from Allah Himself. They will be admitted to gardens beneath which rivers flow, dwelling there forever, with Allah pleased with them and they with Him. They are Allah's party, and it is His party that will be successful.

That passage joins the outward promise of victory with the inward condition of the heart. Loyalty to Allah is tested most sharply where family ties pull the other way. Success in this life and the next belongs to those who accept the allegiance described here.

### How was this fulfilled in history?

The events after these verses were revealed confirmed the statement. The Companions overcame the powers that threatened them. Internal revolts rose against Abu Bakr, and Allah gave him mastery over every one of them. The great powers of that age arrayed their threatening force against 'Umar al-Faruq, and Allah wiped them from the earth. The account adds that as long as these injunctions were dutifully observed by the caliphs and the Muslims after them, including refraining from close bonds of friendship with non-Muslims, they remained victorious in the struggle of life.

That is a historical claim about a particular period, not a formula guaranteeing success to any group that adopts the label. The condition attached to it is obedience, not merely identification.

### What kind of people prevail?

The description of the faithful here is inward as well as outward. If a person does not fulfil the requirements of faith after embracing it, he has turned away from the religion in Allah's sight. The truly faithful are those whose whole being is pervaded by faith until their relation with Allah reaches the level of love.

Fulfilling the objectives of Islam should be so dear to them that their hearts hold only sympathy and kindness toward their brothers in faith. Their powers and talents are never turned against other Muslims.

Islamic life is purposeful and therefore a life of struggle. A Muslim's mission is to convey Allah's religion to His servants, guiding people away from the path leading to the Fire and toward the path leading to Paradise. Carrying out that task brings difficulties and humiliation.

Two groups then emerge: those whose hearts are set on this world and those whose focus is the Hereafter, with a continuing struggle between them. The test is that a person relies completely on Allah in every difficulty, caring for no one except Allah, and continues his spiritual journey until the day he stands accountable.

Such people centre their attention on Allah. They are steadfast in prayer, give zakah and deal with others on the basis of mutual goodwill. Their bowing before Allah shows that there is no egoism or arrogance in their worldly affairs. They adopt humility and do what Allah desires on every occasion.

The account concludes that when people of this type gather in considerable numbers in any place, they are destined to become dominant in that region. Victory is described as the natural outcome of that character, not as a prize for whoever merely claims the name.

### In short

- Alliance with Allah, His Messenger ﷺ and the believers places a person in the party that prevails.
- Victory comes from Allah's support rather than from numbers or resources.
- The related passage ties that success to refusing alliances that oppose faith, even with close relatives.
- Historical fulfilment is reported in the victories of Abu Bakr and 'Umar al-Faruq, conditioned on continued obedience.
- The prevailing people are marked by love of Allah, humility, prayer, alms and goodwill toward other Muslims.
