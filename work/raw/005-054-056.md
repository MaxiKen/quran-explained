<<<VERSE 5:54>>>
"O believers! Whoever among you abandons their faith, Allah will replace them with others who love Him and are loved by Him. They will be humble with the believers but firm towards the disbelievers, struggling in the Way of Allah; fearing no blame from anyone. This is the favour of Allah. He grants it to whoever He wills. And Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing"

This verse speaks to the believers about the danger of apostasy. It says that if some turn away from their faith, Allah will bring a people who love Him and whom He loves. These people are humble towards the believers, firm against the disbelievers, and strive in the way of Allah without fearing the blame of anyone. The verse is both a warning and a promise. It says that the religion does not depend on any one group, and that Allah is able to raise up others.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Whoever among you abandons their faith."** The material explains that the verse speaks to the Muslims. It warns that turning back from faith is possible, and that such a person has turned away from the religion and from its obligations.

**"Allah will replace them with others who love Him and are loved by Him."** The material says the promise is that Allah brings a people in their place, whom He loves and who love Him. The love is both ways. Al-Hakim, in his collection of sound reports, records that the Prophet ﷺ pointed to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari and said that the people will be like him. The material notes that the verse was also read as a prophecy of the apostasy that followed the death of the Prophet ﷺ, and of the people who fought it.

**"Humble with the believers but firm towards the disbelievers."** The material explains that the Arabic word for humble also means easy to deal with and gentle. These people are soft with their brothers in faith and firm against the enemies of Allah. The material says that their love and enmity are for Allah, His Messenger and His religion, and not for their own persons or interests. Al-Basri, Dahhak and Qatadah are reported to have said that the verse was about the first of these people, and the material reads it as applying to others who follow in the same path.

**"Struggling in the Way of Allah, fearing no blame from anyone."** The material explains jihad as the struggle to establish the true faith, which requires both devotion and fervour for the mission. The material says that the leader of a movement faces the force of the adversary and the criticism of his own people. The verse says that these people do not care for blame.

**"This is the favour of Allah. He grants it to whoever He wills."** The material says that these qualities are a gift of Allah, given to those who deserve them.

### The apostasy after the Prophet ﷺ

The material gives a history of the apostasy. Musaylimah al-Kadhdhab claimed prophethood in his lifetime, and the Prophet ﷺ did not live to fight him. Aswad al-Ansi of Yemen made the same claim, and he was killed the day after the Prophet ﷺ died. Tulayhah ibn Khuwaylid of Banu Asad also claimed prophethood. Groups from these tribes turned away during the last illness of the Prophet ﷺ, and after his death seven tribes refused to pay zakah to the caliph.

The material says that Abu Bakr al-Siddiq faced great grief, and that his patience and courage carried the community through. He consulted the Companions, and none of them agreed to war against the rebels at first, since the danger was that foreign powers would invade the new state. Abu Bakr gave a speech that said he would fight anyone who separated prayer from zakah, even alone. The Companions then rallied around him, and the army was organised. Khalid ibn Walid was sent to Yamamah against Musaylimah, who was killed by Wahshi. His followers repented. Khalid later fought against Tulayhah, who fled and later returned to Islam. News of Aswad's death reached Madinah in Rabi al-Awwal, the first victory of the Siddiqi caliphate, and afterwards the Companions won against the tribes that refused zakah.

The material says that the end of the verse was fulfilled in this history, since the party of Allah prevailed. It adds that the qualities named in the verse were present in Abu Bakr and the Companions with him.

### The lesson for every age

The material says the most sound view is that the verse includes Abu Bakr and his companions, and also every believer who confronts disbelief and apostasy until the Last Day. Abu Musa al-Ash'ari and others are named in some reports, and the material says these do not contradict the first view.

The material notes that one who has embraced Faith and does not meet its demands has turned away in the sight of Allah. The people of true faith have their whole being filled with faith, so that their relation with Allah is one of love. Their concern for the cause of Islam makes them kind to their brothers, and they never turn their power against them. The verse calls them to a life of struggle, and the material says that such people, when they meet together in numbers, are destined to become dominant in their land.

### Seven counsels and the humble believer

The material records a report from Ahmad, which Abu Dharr gave, that his intimate friend the Prophet ﷺ commanded him to do seven things. He was to love the poor and sit near them. He was to look at those below him and not above him. He was to keep the ties of kinship even when they cut them. He was not to ask anyone for anything. He was to speak the truth even when it was bitter, and to fear no blame for the sake of Allah. He was to say often, there is no power and no strength except with Allah, since these words are a treasure under the Throne. The material also gives a hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ said that the believer should not humiliate himself, and when asked how a person does that, he said by taking on trials he cannot bear. The material says the humble believer is humble in the right way, not in weakness.

The material says that the qualities of the people in the verse are a grace from Allah. Those who have them received them from His bounty. The verse also calls them to be people of the Qur'an and the Sunnah, and to keep their hearts from the fitnah of turning away.

### The strength from Allah

The material links the verse to 58:22, which says that Allah strengthens the believers with a ruh from Himself. It also links the verse to 48:29 of Surah al-Fath, which describes the Companions as firm against the disbelievers and merciful among themselves. These qualities of the Companions are a model for every believer who faces the same trial.

### The Khutbah of Abu Bakr

The material records the khutbah of Abu Bakr al-Akbar, called As-Siddiq, after the Prophet ﷺ died. He said that whoever turns back from the injunctions of the Prophet ﷺ and the law of Islam, it is his duty to wage jihad against them. He said that even if all the jinn and humans and all the trees and stones were gathered against him, and he had no comrade to help him, he would stand alone and carry the struggle. The Companions then rose with him and set out the battle plan. The material says that the words of this khutbah opened the hearts of the Companions to jihad against the apostasy.

### In short

- The verse warns that people may turn away from faith, and promises that Allah will bring a people in their place whom He loves.
- These people are gentle with the believers, firm against the disbelievers, and fear no blame in the way of Allah.
- The verse was fulfilled in the wars of apostasy under Abu Bakr, and it applies to every believer who stands firm in the same way.

<<<VERSE 5:55>>>
"Your only guardians are Allah, His Messenger, and fellow believers—who establish prayer and pay alms-tax with humility"

This verse sets out who the believers should take as guardians. It names Allah, His Messenger and the believers who establish prayer and pay zakah with humility. It follows the warning against friendship with the Jews and Christians.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Your only guardians are Allah, His Messenger, and fellow believers."** The material explains that the believer's real loyalty is to these three. The Arabic word is walī, which means guardian, ally and friend. The material says that this is the true friendship that a believer owes.

**"Who establish prayer and pay alms-tax with humility."** The material explains that the believers in view establish the five daily prayers and pay zakah, and that they bow in prayer with the Prophet ﷺ in congregation. The verse describes them as humble, since they give their wealth for the needy and bow before Allah.

### The occasion of revelation

The material records that Abdullah bin Salam and his companions were shunned by the Jews after they embraced Islam. Abdullah bin Salam said to the Prophet ﷺ that their people had abandoned them. The verse was revealed in answer. It told them that their guardian is Allah, His Messenger and the believers, and not the Jews.

The material also reads the verse in the light of the Qur'an's call to love the Prophet. It cites the verse on following him, which says that Allah will love those who follow him, as stated in 3:31. The material says that love of Allah grows through following the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.

### In short

- The believer's guardians are Allah, His Messenger and the believers.
- The believers meant are those who establish prayer, pay zakah and bow in humility.
- The verse was revealed after Abdullah bin Salam was shunned by the Jews, and it told him that his real allies are the believers.

<<<VERSE 5:56>>>
"Whoever allies themselves with Allah, His Messenger, and fellow believers, then it is certainly Allah's party that will prevail"

This verse closes the passage with a promise. Whoever takes Allah, His Messenger and the believers as allies belongs to the party of Allah, and that party will prevail.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever allies themselves with Allah, His Messenger, and fellow believers."** The material explains that this is a call to take the right allies. The alliance is with the three named in the previous verse.

**"Then it is certainly Allah's party that will prevail."** The material explains that the party of Allah is the group that follows His Messenger and the believers. The verse promises that they will be victorious, as the Qur'an promises elsewhere. The material cites 14:19 and 20, which say that if Allah wills, He can replace a people with a new creation, and 48:29, which describes the companions of the Prophet ﷺ as severe towards disbelievers and merciful among themselves.

### The meaning of bowing

The material explains the word for bowing in the verse. Some scholars read it as the bowing position of the prayer, which is a basic pillar of salah. The material says that the verse names prayer and then the bowing, to show that the prayer of the believers is different from the prayer of the Jews and Christians. Others, Abu Hayyan in al-Bahr al-Muhit and al-Zamakhshari in al-Kashshaf, took the word in its lexical sense of bowing in modesty and humility. The material notes that the same view is taken in Tafsir Mazhari.

The material also gives a report that the verse was revealed about Ali. He was in prayer and had bowed in ruku when a needy man asked him for help. Without finishing his bowing, he took a ring from his finger and gave it to the man. The material says that the report shows how quickly a believer may help the needy, and that the verse was revealed about this act. Ali's love for Allah and His Messenger is also cited, in a hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever he is a friend to, Ali is also a friend to him.

### In short

- Whoever allies with Allah, His Messenger and the believers belongs to the party of Allah.
- The party of Allah is promised victory, and the material says this was seen in the Companions' wars of apostasy.
- The believer's loyalty is to the faith and its people, not to those who oppose it.
