<<<VERSE 2:267>>>
Charity should express respect, not the disposal of things the giver despises: "O believers! Donate from the best of what you have earned and of what We have produced for you from the earth." Allah asks for pure and wholesome expenditure. Giving Him what one would reluctantly accept oneself contradicts that purpose, even though He needs none of the possessions involved.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Donate from the best of what you have earned"** The instruction concerns the good things acquired through work, including money such as gold and silver. Ibn 'Abbas explains it through pure, honest wealth. Tayyib can mean good in quality or halal, lawfully acquired. Both meanings are discussed, with a distinction over whether this particular word itself establishes the requirement of lawful earnings.

**"and of what We have produced for you from the earth."** Grain, fruit and other produce are gifts of Allah even when people cultivate them. The expression also supports the obligation attached to harvests. Labour does not make the crop independent of its Creator.

**"Do not pick out worthless things for donation,"** The prohibition is against deliberately selecting the inferior when good property is available. The verb tayammamū is explained as intending or aiming at something. It is not a demand that someone who possesses only poor-quality goods produce what they do not own.

**"which you yourselves would only accept with closed eyes."** The recipient should not be treated by a standard the giver would reject. Closing the eyes means overlooking a deficiency reluctantly, not necessarily shutting them physically. Another explanation considers a debt repaid with inferior property: the creditor would normally insist on compensation for the shortfall.

**"And know that Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy"** Allah does not depend on donations. He is praised in His being, actions and decisions. He accepts sincere giving, rewards little and multiplies generous expenditure, while the creatures remain dependent on Him.

### What happened with the dates?

Ibn Jarir records from Al-Bara' bin 'Azib that the instruction was revealed concerning the ansar, the Helpers in Madinah. At harvest they hung branches of dates on a rope between pillars in the Prophet's mosque. Poor emigrants could eat from them.

Some people included inferior dates among the good bunches. One account says they thought this permissible. Another says they expected the poor-quality bunches to pass unnoticed among the many gifts. The verse corrected that choice: a person should not offer what they would dislike receiving.

Another report from Jabir concerns zakah al-Fitr, the poor-due at the close of fasting. The Prophet ﷺ commanded a sa', a measure, of dates, and a man brought poor-quality dates. The verse is connected with that act too. A further account refers to a man in Medina owning dates called Hashaf.

These reported occasions agree on the relevant problem without requiring every detail to describe one incident. Inferior produce was being chosen despite the availability of something better. The donors' public appearance as benefactors did not make the choice acceptable.

### Does good mean quality or lawfulness?

The immediate circumstances favour an explanation concerned with quality. Some people had good dates but deliberately brought bad ones. Others explain tayyib through lawfulness, since something is not completely good if unlawfully acquired.

On the first reading, the rule about lawful earnings must also be established through other evidence. On the second, it is already contained in this word's general meaning. The practical conclusions belong together, but the reasoning differs and should remain distinct.

Ibn 'Abbas says Allah is pure and good and accepts what is pure and good. The explanation therefore rejects dishonest wealth as well as intentionally degraded gifts. A charitable destination is not a means of purifying wrongdoing at acquisition.

The exception concerns someone without good-quality property, not a person choosing unlawful income. Someone whose available goods are poor can still give from them and have the gift accepted. The contrast in the verse assumes good things have been earned or produced and inferior things are knowingly selected instead.

### Why compare the giver with a creditor?

Ibn 'Abbas explains the words through a person owed something who receives less than the rightful amount. Unless willing to waive the difference, that person demands it be made good. Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Jarir transmit this explanation.

The challenge exposes inconsistency. A person protects personal rights carefully, then offers Allah something deficient as though He were less worthy of honour. His freedom from need is a reason to recognise the gift's moral purpose, not an excuse to give contemptuously.

The discussion also includes a cross-reference written as (4:92) in connection with this point. Its number is retained as supplied rather than silently replaced with another reference.

### What ruling is drawn concerning family earnings?

Some scholars infer from what you have earned that a father may eat from his son's earnings. Al-Qurtubi supports the discussion with a hadith describing children as a good part of one's earnings and permitting eating from their income.

That is an associated legal inference, not a claim that the children themselves are disposable property. The explanation places it alongside the verse's language of acquisition. No additional collector or grading is supplied for that report here.

### How are land dues explained?

Abu Hanifah uses the general reference to produce brought from the earth to infer that 'ushr is wajib, obligatory, on every yield of 'ushri land, large or small. The command to pay the crop's due at harvest in (6:141) is also cited in support.

'Ushr is explained as the tenth or twentieth part, while kharaj means land tax. Both can resemble taxes when collected by an Islamic state, but their religious character differs. 'Ushr is monetary worship as well as a land levy, and is therefore also called zakah al-ard, the land's zakah. Kharaj is described as a tax without that aspect of worship.

The explanation connects 'ushr with Muslims, who are obligated to perform this worship, and kharaj with non-Muslims, who are not so obligated. It treats these as technical distinctions requiring further fiqh, legal understanding, for their detailed application.

There is also a distinction in timing. Zakah on gold, silver and commercial goods becomes due after a year, while the produce's 'ushr becomes due at harvest. If the land produces nothing, 'ushr falls away. Zakah on qualifying monetary and commercial assets remains due at year's end even without trading profit.

Detailed regional land rules are outside the present explanation. The discussion points towards further treatment, including questions concerning Pakistan and India, without supplying those rulings in this verse.

### Why command gifts when Allah has no need?

The purpose includes narrowing the distance between rich and poor. People receive practical help, while the giver learns piety. The principle is illustrated through (22:37): sacrificial flesh and blood do not reach Allah, but devotion does.

His bounty never runs out. Whoever gives pure property deals with a Lord who is neither poor nor unjust and who can multiply the return. He remains worthy of praise whether a servant gives much, little or nothing.

The inward reading asks a person to compare what they reserve for themselves with what they offer for Allah. Precious possessions are often kept for worldly enjoyment while things of little value are assigned to devotion. Yet what Allah has given the servant is immense, and what the servant offers is small.

Even that small act is enabled by His favour. He forgives, accepts, compensates and praises it, attributing it to the person as a deed. The gift comes from Him and is then named a reward. Recognising this generosity should produce reverence instead of careless selection.

### In short

- Give lawful, wholesome property rather than knowingly choosing the inferior.
- A person with only poor-quality goods is not treated like someone withholding better goods.
- The date reports illustrate the duty to respect recipients.
- The verse supports discussion of harvest dues and their distinction from ordinary zakah and kharaj.
- Allah needs no donation, but people need its help and the giver needs its lesson in piety.

<<<VERSE 2:268>>>
Two invitations compete for the giver's trust: "The Devil threatens you with ˹the prospect of˺ poverty and bids you to the shameful deed ˹of stinginess˺, while Allah promises you forgiveness and ˹great˺ bounties from Him." The choice concerns more than a financial forecast. It concerns whether fear will obstruct obedience or trust in Allah will guide the use of what He has given.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Devil threatens you with ˹the prospect of˺ poverty"** Satan uses the fear of future loss to make people cling to possessions and withhold charity. Someone need not see him directly to experience the influence of that suggestion. Turning away from Allah's promise because of such fear reveals the character of the thought.

**"and bids you to the shameful deed ˹of stinginess˺,"** Fahsha' is explained through withholding the poor-due and obstructing charity. Another explanation broadens it to sins, forbidden acts and immoral behaviour. Satan urges refusal of good and the pursuit of evil together.

**"while Allah promises you forgiveness and ˹great˺ bounties from Him."** Forgiveness answers the evil urged by Satan, and bounty answers the threatened poverty. The explanation includes replacement, increased provision and reward in the Hereafter. Allah knows how the gift was acquired, intended and spent.

**"And Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing"** His treasury is not exhausted. Open deeds, hidden purposes and every servant's condition are within His knowledge. Fear based on imagining Him powerless or unaware has no sound basis.

### How are the two influences recognised?

Ibn Abi Hatim records from 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud a saying of the Prophet ﷺ distinguishing Satan's influence from an angel's. The former threatens an evil outcome and rejects truth. The latter promises good and affirms truth.

Someone experiencing the good invitation should recognise it as coming from Allah and thank Him. Someone experiencing the other should seek refuge in Allah from Satan. The Prophet ﷺ then recited this verse. At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i also record the narration in their tafsir collections.

The instruction turns an inward experience into a practical response. Gratitude strengthens trust when the thought encourages obedience. Seeking refuge resists a fear that urges rejection of the divine promise. It does not require claiming direct sight of the unseen tempter.

### Why can luxury coexist with fear of poverty?

The reflection describes Satan first magnifying personal requirements until a person believes every earning belongs to comfort and luxury. Once actual needs are satisfied, another desire appears: ostentatious expenditure. The person feels successful while wealth is absorbed in display.

Such spending can be extravagant while charity is still refused through fear. The issue is not simply whether money leaves the hand, but the direction in which it is spent. Satan's invitation reserves it for the self and its reputation.

The alternative recognises wealth as belonging to Allah. Actual needs receive their due, while surplus serves weaker people and the cause of His religion. The giver hopes not to be deprived of Allah's favour when standing empty-handed before Him, and aligns personal effort with His mission.

### What is real wealth in the inward reading?

Satan's poverty is described as separation from Allah. His own harvest was burned, so he wants others to lose theirs. Allah's promise reflects His generosity, while Satan's threat reflects his impoverished state. The principle is linked with (17:84), that each acts according to their own manner.

The Lord's invitation is recalled through (14:10), calling towards forgiveness. Satan's invitation in (35:6) leads his party towards the blaze. These are opposed destinations, not equally trustworthy counsels.

Avarice and restless appetite for this world are therefore inward poverty. Contentment and seeking the afterworld are wealth. Contentment frees a person from dependence on creatures, allows inward communion with Allah here and opens hope for the garden, generosity and vision in the next life.

Shaykh al-Islam Ansari distinguishes wealth of property, disposition and heart. Lawful property is a trial, unlawful property a curse and excess a punishment. Wealth of disposition consists in contentment, satisfaction and chivalrous generosity. Wealth of heart means an aspiration beyond this world, a desire beyond the next world's rewards and yearning for the vision of the Patron.

The contrast corrects the fear's measure. Having much is not the same as being free of need, and giving is not automatically impoverishment. The heart's dependence and destination matter too.

### In short

- Satan uses fear of loss to obstruct charity and encourage wrongdoing.
- Allah promises forgiveness and bounty with complete knowledge of the giver.
- Thank Allah for invitations towards truth and seek refuge from suggestions rejecting it.
- Wealth serving only luxury and display can coexist with deep inward poverty.
- Contentment and devotion are forms of wealth beyond possessions.

<<<VERSE 2:269>>>
The ability to recognise and follow the better invitation is itself a gift: "Allah grants wisdom to whoever He wills. And whoever is granted wisdom is certainly blessed with a great privilege." Wisdom joins understanding with sound action. It is not merely clever speech or the possession of money, but a way of seeing and responding rightly.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah grants wisdom to whoever He wills."** Al-Hikmah has several related explanations. Ibn 'Abbas describes knowledge of the Qur'an, including its permissions, prohibitions, parables, clear and less clear passages, and what abrogates or is abrogated. Other explanations emphasise profitable knowledge leading to righteousness and correctness in speech, action and opinion.

**"And whoever is granted wisdom is certainly blessed with a great privilege."** The gift contains abundant good and leads towards lasting happiness. One explanation identifies it with prophethood granted to Muhammad ﷺ. Others include the broad understanding and practice through which people obey Allah rightly.

**"But none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason"** Sound minds and good comprehension allow a person to receive admonition. The expression concerns benefiting from signs and their implications, not merely hearing words. A grammatical explanation notes the assimilation of letters in yadhdhakkar, the verb for taking heed.

### Do the meanings of wisdom contradict each other?

Nearly thirty explanations are gathered in the discussion called al-Bahr al-Muhit. They are described as close rather than contradictory. Hikmah is related to ihkam, completing an act or statement with its proper requirements. Placing each thing where it belongs expresses that idea.

In (2:251), concerning Dawud (David), the gift of kingdom and wisdom is explained through prophethood, where that right placement is accomplished ideally. In (3:164), teaching the Book and wisdom is explained by the Companions and Successors through hadith and sunnah.

Raghib al-Isfahani distinguishes the attribution to Allah from its attribution to creatures. For Allah it concerns comprehensive knowledge and perfect creative action. For a person it concerns rightly guided knowledge of what exists and corresponding conduct.

Other expressions include true knowledge, righteous action, the true word, understanding religion, correct judgment and fear of Allah. A hadith is cited saying that real wisdom is fear of Allah, without an additional collector or grade here. The broad explanation takes this verse's abundant good as embracing these related meanings rather than excluding all but one.

### What kinds of good may be wished for?

Ahmad records from Ibn Mas'ud a saying of the Prophet ﷺ allowing admiration in two cases: someone given wealth who spends it rightly, and someone given wisdom who judges and teaches by it. Al-Bukhari, Muslim, An-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah also record the hadith.

The desire is for comparable good, not for the other person's blessing to be removed. Both examples concern a gift put to proper use. Wealth serves righteousness, and understanding serves judgment and instruction. Possession alone does not complete either virtue.

### Why is wisdom joined to giving?

A person spending as Allah commands demonstrates understanding of the Qur'an. Being so absorbed in money that Allah's cause is neglected is described as folly. Wisdom recognises that financial interests should not block participation in good.

An open mind can receive even an argument that self-interest would prefer to dismiss. The connection here is the recognition that the divine promise is more trustworthy than Satan's threat. The broader shared reflection on release from self-centred limitations continues with the following verses rather than being repeated in full here.

### What are wisdom's reality and fruit inwardly?

Its reality is recognising what suits an affair, putting things in their places and understanding people in their circumstances. It sees a statement's likely end from its beginning and recognises an inward meaning through its outward expression.

Its fruit is balanced conduct: tenderness in dealings with people, fear joined with hope in dealing with oneself, and awe joined with intimacy before Allah. It avoids making one side of those relationships erase the other.

Wisdom is pictured as light adorning the tongue with remembrance, the heart with right reflection and the limbs with right action. Speech then reaches hearts, thought rises like a falcon and action surrenders personal desire to Allah's pleasure.

Poetry imagines strong wings for the peacock of the Throne, a joyful nest and steeds coming from the divine city. These images portray release towards the unseen, not instructions about earthly animals. Further imagery joins the severity of passion and need with the gentleness of union and joy. Black locks become swords of severity, while sweet lips kindle candles of gentleness.

The final address praises the perfection that restores those who lost and the beauty that provides for the destitute. The movement between awe and comfort belongs to the same wisdom that keeps remembrance steady whatever arrives.

### In short

- Wisdom joins truthful understanding with fitting action.
- Its related meanings include Qur'anic knowledge, sunnah, sound judgment, righteousness and fear of Allah.
- Wealth and wisdom become admirable when used for good and shared through instruction.
- An open mind can recognise what attachment to money obscures.
- The inward reading pictures wisdom as light balancing action, reflection, fear, hope, awe and intimacy.
