<<<VERSE 5:8>>>
"O believers! Stand firm for Allah and bear true testimony. Do not let the hatred of a people lead you to injustice. Be just! That is closer to righteousness. And be mindful of Allah. Surely Allah is All-Aware of what you do"

This verse sets a rule of justice that holds even towards an enemy. The material says it repeats in substance a command given in the chapter of women, but in a different order and for a different reason. The verse says that hatred of a people must not push a believer into injustice. It asks him to stand firm for Allah as a witness who bears true testimony, to be just, and to be mindful of Allah, who knows what he does.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Stand firm for Allah and bear true testimony."** The material says the phrase "stand firm for Allah" means standing for the truth for the sake of Allah, not for people or for reputation. The earlier verse in the chapter of women, 4:135, has the same command with the words in a different order, asking believers to stand for justice as witnesses for Allah. The material explains that putting "for Allah" first here closes the door on personal feeling. Abu Hayyan gives a delicate reason for the change of order between the two verses, and the material follows it. The Holy Qur'an uses the same testimony language in more than one place, and the word shahadah in the material is wider than testimony in a court. A person who stands for Allah will stand for justice, and the one who stands for justice will stand for none but Allah. In 4:135, where the concern is self, parents and kin, justice is put first so that no kinship can pass for service to Allah.

**"Do not let the hatred of a people lead you to injustice."** The material explains that there are two main causes that keep people from justice. One is taking the side of oneself, friends or relatives, which 4:135 addresses. The other is enmity against someone, which this verse addresses. The material cites the account of the Companions in the Two Sahihs, through An-Nu'man bin Bashir, in which his father gave him a gift and his mother refused to accept it unless the Prophet ﷺ were made a witness. The Prophet ﷺ asked whether he had given the same to all his children. When he said no, the Prophet ﷺ told him to fear Allah and treat his children equally, and said he would not witness an injustice. The father then took back the gift. The material says this is the same principle at a smaller scale: a believer must not let his feelings change his fairness.

**"Be just! That is closer to righteousness."** The material notes that the word "closer" is read by some as meaning that justice is in fact the right course and not merely a nearer one. It says that no other course is open to a believer, so "closer" amounts to "is" in its effect. The material also records that some of the female Companions told 'Umar he was harsher than the Prophet ﷺ, and it notes that they meant the manner of his reproof and not the Prophet's conduct.

**"And be mindful of Allah."** The material says that taqwa means keeping the commands and avoiding the prohibitions in all matters, including justice and injustice.

**"Surely Allah is All-Aware of what you do."** The verse closes by reminding the believer that Allah sees every act, and that He will repay him for it.

### Justice and the duty of testimony

The material links this verse to the duty of giving evidence. It cites 2:283, which says that concealing testimony is a grave sin, and 2:282, which says that neither the scribe nor the witness should be made to suffer. The material says that bearing testimony is wajib, an obligation, and that the verses in Surat al-Baqarah make concealing it a grave sin. It then argues that in modern courts, repeated summonses and long delays can make the witness a victim, and that a simpler procedure would restore the duty to testify without harm. The material widens the idea of testimony beyond the court. A medical certificate, a mark on an examination paper, a certificate of qualification and a vote are all forms of testimony, and a false one is a grave sin.

### The vote as testimony, recommendation and agency

In the sunnah and in the Holy Qur'an the word shahadah has a broad sense, and the material applies that sense to the vote. The material gives the vote three meanings. It is testimony that the candidate is worthy. It is also a recommendation, for which 4:85 says that whoever makes a good recommendation shares in its reward and whoever makes a bad one shares in its burden. And it is an agency, because the voter appoints a representative in matters that belong to the whole nation. The material concludes that a vote for an unworthy candidate is false testimony, an unjust recommendation and an improper agency all at once. It says that every Muslim, man and woman, must investigate a candidate before voting.

### In short

- The verse asks the believer to stand firm for Allah, to bear true testimony, and to be just even towards those he dislikes.
- Enmity must never push a believer into injustice, and taqwa must guide every judgment.
- The material links the verse to the duty of honest testimony in court and in daily life, including the vote.

<<<VERSE 5:9>>>
"Allah has promised those who believe and do good ˹His˺ forgiveness and a great reward"

The verse gives a promise to the believers who do good deeds. It gives two rewards, forgiveness and a great reward, and the material says the great reward is Paradise. It follows directly from the call to justice in 5:8, so that the promise is given to those who carry out that justice.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah has promised those who believe and do good."** The promise is given to those who believe and who act on that belief by doing good. The material says that faith and good deeds are linked, so the promise does not go to belief alone or to deeds alone. It notes that the believer's good deeds are part of the means by which Allah's mercy reaches him.

**"His forgiveness and a great reward."** The material explains that the forgiveness is pardon for sins, and that the reward is Paradise. It adds that the believer is not admitted to Paradise purely on account of his deeds, but on account of Allah's mercy and favour. The deeds are the path that Allah has set for that mercy, so the believer should thank Allah for the whole of it.

### Mercy and deeds

The material says that a verse of promise is a comfort to the believer. No one can claim that his deeds alone have earned him Paradise, because even the one who does good remains in need of Allah's pardon. The verse therefore presents the work and the mercy together, so that the believer neither despairs of Allah's forgiveness nor relies on his own action alone. The material links this to the wider teaching that the path of righteousness has to be walked with humility.

### The reward and the path

The reward is described as great, and the material says that its greatness is part of Allah's generosity. It says that the believer is given the promise so that he will continue in good work, and that the forgiveness is what allows him to stand before Allah without fear. The verse thus gives the believer hope and a clear direction at the same time.

### In short

- The promise is given to those who believe and do good, and it includes forgiveness and a great reward.
- Deeds are the path to that reward, but the reward itself comes from Allah's mercy and favour.

<<<VERSE 5:10>>>
"As for those who disbelieve and deny Our signs, they are the residents of the Hellfire"

The verse is the counterpart of the promise in 5:9. It names those who reject faith and deny the signs of Allah, and it says that they are the people of the Fire. The material reads the two verses together, so that the choice between them is plain.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who disbelieve and deny Our signs."** The "signs" are the proofs and revelations of Allah. The material says that in the world Allah makes His presence known through signs in the form of arguments that cannot be refuted. A person who hears a clear argument and answers it with empty wordplay instead of accepting it is denying the signs, even if he never uses the word. The verse is aimed at the one who rejects the truth after it has been made clear.

**"They are the residents of the Hellfire."** The verse ends with the fate of those who deny the signs. The material says the Fire is the place where they are held, and it says that the verse warns those who hear the truth and refuse it.

### Two paths

The material reads 5:9 and 5:10 together. One group believes and does good, and receives forgiveness and a great reward. The other denies and receives the Fire. The material says that the arrangement of the two verses makes the choice clear. It also says that the signs of Allah are open to the reasoning mind, so the one who denies them cannot claim that he had no means of knowing. The material notes that the signs include both the arguments of the Qur'an and the evidence in the world around the person.

### Denial after clarity

The material says that the denial is the decisive point. A person who has not heard the truth is not in the same position as one who has heard it and turned away. The verse does not speak of ignorance but of denial after the signs have come, and that is why the warning is so direct.

### In short

- Those who deny Allah's signs, after the truth has been shown to them, are the people of the Fire.
- The verse is a warning, and it stands in contrast to the promise in the verse before it.

<<<VERSE 5:11>>>
"O believers! Remember Allah’s favour upon you: when a people sought to harm you, but He held their hands back from you. Be mindful of Allah. And in Allah let the believers put their trust"

The verse calls the believers to remember a favour from Allah. It recalls a time when a people wanted to harm them, and Allah held back their hands. It ends by calling the believers to be mindful of Allah and to put their trust in Him. The verse follows 5:7, where the covenant of hearing and obeying is recalled, so that the favour of protection is placed alongside the covenant.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Remember Allah’s favour upon you."** The material says the favour is protection from harm. It explains that the verse asks the believer to recollect Allah's protection with his heart and tongue, as an act of gratitude.

**"When a people sought to harm you, but He held their hands back from you."** The material records several reports of this protection. One account, given by Jabir, tells of a man from the tribe of Muharib named Ghawrath bin al-Harith. He told his people that he would kill the Prophet ﷺ. He came to him and found him sitting with his sword in his lap. He asked to see the sword, took it, and raised it to strike. Each time, Allah stopped him. The man asked whether the Prophet ﷺ was not afraid of him, and the Prophet ﷺ replied that Allah would protect him. The man returned the sword, and this verse came down. In another report, Jabir describes the Prophet ﷺ resting under a tree after a journey, with his sword hung on a branch. A Bedouin took the sword and asked who would save him now. The Prophet ﷺ answered "Allah," and the man repeated the question two or three times, and each time the answer was the same. The man then returned the sword without harm. Abdur-Razzaq reported this through Ma'mar and al-Zuhri, and the material notes that Ma'mar said Qatadah used to mention that some Arabs had sent this man to kill the Prophet ﷺ.

The material also gives a second setting. Muhammad bin Ishaq, Mujahid and 'Ikrimah are reported to have said the verse was revealed about the Banu an-Nadir, who plotted to drop a stone on the Prophet ﷺ when he came to them for help with a blood payment. Allah told him of the plot, and he returned to Madinah. The Banu an-Nadir were then expelled after a siege. Another reading takes the verse as referring to a plot by a people who wanted to kill the believers. The material says the reports differ, and it presents them side by side without choosing among them.

**"Be mindful of Allah."** The material says the believer must not think his protection is a reason to relax. He must remain mindful of Allah in keeping His instructions and avoiding His prohibitions.

**"And in Allah let the believers put their trust."** The material explains that trust in Allah is what the believer relies on when he is threatened. It says that Allah suffices those who trust Him and protects them from the plots of others.

### Favour and the covenant

The material links the verse to 5:7, where the believers are reminded of the covenant of hearing and obeying. It also cites 57:8, which asks the believers why they do not believe when the Messenger calls them to their Lord and has taken their covenant. The material says that remembering Allah's favour leads to gratitude, and that gratitude leads to obedience. The protection the verse describes is therefore a reason for the believers to keep the covenant they made.

### Protection and trust

The material says that the believer who has been protected must still act. Trust in Allah does not mean doing nothing. It means taking the right means and then relying on Allah for the result. The protection in the verse is a sign that Allah is able to hold back those who plot against His servants, and the believer's trust is the response to that certainty.

### In short

- The verse reminds the believers of Allah's protection from people who wanted to harm them.
- The reports of this protection are many and they differ on the occasion, but they agree that Allah held back the hands that were raised against the believers.
- The believer responds to this favour with taqwa and trust in Allah.
