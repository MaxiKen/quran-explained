<<<VERSE 5:8>>>
Allah commands believers to act for His sake and uphold justice: "O believers! Stand firm for Allah and bear true testimony." The instruction reaches the hardest case, an enemy whose conduct has caused anger or injury. Hatred must not become permission to distort evidence, withhold another person's right or inflict a new wrong.

Faithfulness to the covenant includes both devotion to Allah and fair dealings with people. Justice gives a person what is due, neither more nor less according to personal affection or resentment. The verse makes that standard a duty before the One who knows every action.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Stand firm for Allah"** means persist in fulfilling His rights and seeking His pleasure. The motive is not reputation, popularity or the approval of people. A person standing for Allah cannot rightly use that claim to excuse oppression.

**"and bear true testimony."** Witnesses must speak honestly and justly. Shahadah, testimony, helps those deciding a matter reach a sound judgment. It must not be turned into a weapon for a preferred side or hidden when its truth becomes inconvenient.

**"Do not let the hatred of a people lead you to injustice."** The command includes hostile disbelievers as well as anyone personally disliked. A specific gloss mentions resentment toward Shurayh Ibn Shurahbil and warns against injustice to the pilgrims of Bakr Ibn Wa'il. The wider instruction is to refuse aggression even where grievance is real.

**"Be just! That is closer to righteousness."** Taqwa is awareness of Allah expressed through obedience. Justice belongs to it, while oppression contradicts it. "Closer" does not offer injustice as another acceptable religious option. It emphasises the relationship between fairness and genuine fear of Allah.

**"And be mindful of Allah. Surely Allah is All-Aware of what you do"** repeats the inward safeguard. No act of fairness or unfairness is hidden from Him. He will repay the person according to what was actually done, not according to the justification offered to other people.

### Why compare this with the command in Surah Al-Nisa'?

The instruction there places justice prominently and requires truthful witness even against oneself, parents and relatives (4:135). Here the words first emphasise standing for Allah and then justice, before forbidding hatred from producing unfairness. Abu Hayyan's explanation, printed with a shortened name in this passage, relates the different emphasis to two common obstacles.

One obstacle is affection for oneself and one's circle. A person may imagine that protecting family or friends is itself an act for Allah. Placing justice first prevents that claim from excusing partiality. A concession that violates justice cannot be justified merely by naming a good relationship.

The other obstacle is hostility. A person who begins with the requirement to stand for Allah must recognise that His claim reaches the treatment of enemies too. Hatred cannot set aside a duty owed to Him. Both word orders lead to the same requirement, but approach different temptations.

The two verses therefore prohibit opposite forms of bias. Neither protecting a friend nor injuring an enemy is a sufficient reason to depart from the truth. They also require people to supply honest evidence rather than make a judge or other decision-maker work without the facts.

### Does nearer to piety imply that injustice has some piety?

No. The comparative expression stresses the right course without making the opposite course acceptable. Another cited Qur'anic expression describes the people of Paradise as having the better dwelling and resting place. It does not need to grant goodness to the alternative in order to express their good position.

A similar point appears in words spoken by some female Companions to 'Umar. They called him more stern than the Messenger ﷺ. Their meaning was to describe 'Umar's sternness, not to attribute harshness to the Prophet ﷺ. Language of comparison does not always imply that both sides share the quality in the same way.

Here justice is the course required by taqwa. The person cannot choose oppression and then argue that it is merely a little less close to piety. The verse has already forbidden that choice.

### Can a witness refuse to endorse an unjust gift?

An-Nu'man bin Bashir reports in the Two Sahihs that his father gave him a gift. His mother, 'Amrah bint Rawahah, would not agree unless the Messenger ﷺ witnessed it. His father therefore approached the Prophet ﷺ.

The Prophet ﷺ asked whether all his children had received an equivalent gift. When the answer was no, he instructed him to fear Allah and treat his children equally. He refused to witness injustice. An-Nu'man's father then withdrew the gift.

The report shows that testimony is not an empty ceremonial signature. A person asked to attest something must consider whether the act being endorsed is just. The Prophet's ﷺ refusal connects the witness's responsibility with the rights of those affected.

### Why must witnesses also be protected from harm?

The Qur'an forbids concealing testimony and describes the concealing heart as sinful (2:283). Giving true evidence is therefore wajib, a binding duty. Yet that duty is accompanied by an instruction that neither scribe nor witness should be harmed (2:282). A system demanding evidence must not punish honest people for supplying it.

The social criticism here describes repeated police and court appearances, pointless delay and hostile examination designed to break witnesses rather than establish truth. The witness loses working time and faces trouble for an event he did not cause. After such an experience, he may resolve never to come forward again.

The result is a destructive cycle. People who actually saw an event avoid being identified, and fabricated witnesses fill the gap. Courts then decide on the material presented to them, however unreliable. The passage expresses the severity of this failure by saying that not even five or ten percent of cases in the system being criticised reach a just outcome. That is its polemical assessment of the setting it describes, not a new statistical claim about courts today.

It attributes much of the procedure to the inherited British colonial system and contrasts it with the simpler, faster justice it describes in the Hijaz and elsewhere. The practical proposal is to restructure evidence-taking around the two Qur'anic duties together: the person who knows the truth must speak, and the authorities must avoid unnecessary hardship to him.

Witnesses should be treated well and released as soon as their evidence has been properly recorded. Truthful testimony is less likely to disappear where giving it does not threaten a person's livelihood. Protecting witnesses serves justice rather than weakening it.

### Does testimony only mean speaking in court?

No. Shahadah in the Qur'an and Sunnah is broader than a formal court appearance. It includes statements on which others rely to make decisions about rights, qualifications or responsibilities.

A medical certificate declaring someone unfit for duty or fit for retirement on health grounds is testimony. If it knowingly misstates the person's condition, it becomes false evidence. The fact that it appears on a familiar administrative form does not remove its moral seriousness.

Marking an examination also testifies to a student's performance. Deliberately changing the marks, or carelessly giving an inaccurate assessment, falls under the same warning. The people affected depend on the marker's honesty and attention.

A graduation certificate or professional testimonial states that its recipient has the ability the document claims. If that ability is absent, the signatories have endorsed something untrue. Their responsibility cannot be dismissed as routine paperwork.

These examples extend the verse's demand into ordinary institutions. The witness must consider what his statement actually tells others, not simply whether the statement carries the formal label of testimony.

### What does the discussion say about voting?

It treats a vote as having three aspects. First, it is testimony that the candidate is, according to the voter's informed judgment, capable and trustworthy enough to represent people. A vote cast for someone known to lack those qualities misstates that judgment.

Second, it is shafa'ah, a recommendation. The Qur'an attaches a share of the consequences to both good and bad recommendations (4:85). Supporting a deserving person can therefore bring a share in the resulting good. Helping an undeserving person into office can bring a share in the wrong made possible through that support.

Third, the vote is wakalah, appointing an agent or representative. It does not concern only the voter's private property, where he alone might bear the loss. The representative acts in matters involving the community's shared rights. Reckless appointment therefore affects others as well as the voter.

The criticism is directed against treating elections as mere contests of winning and losing. Money, pressure, casual friendships and promises cannot turn an unfit candidate into a fit one. A voter who knowingly endorses the unworthy participates in false testimony, an unjust recommendation and misuse of shared authority at once.

The resulting duty is investigation. Men and women should consider the candidate's competence, suitability, honesty and reliability before voting. Negligence or indifference does not make the choice morally empty. The explanation praises support for a worthy person and warns of responsibility for the predictable wrongdoing of an unworthy one.

### What makes justice part of the covenant?

The previous verse explained the inward pledge of hearing and obeying (5:7). Here that pledge takes the form of fair treatment. A person must give another what is actually deserved, rather than what anger or affection would prefer.

Bitter memories are a particular test. They can make unfairness feel like loyalty to a past injury. The verse requires the believer to resist that movement and remain faithful to Allah. Justice toward an enemy is not forgetfulness of the wrong. It is refusal to answer it with a fresh violation of the covenant.

### In short

- Stand for Allah, not for reputation or faction, and give honest evidence.
- Neither affection for friends nor hatred of enemies permits injustice.
- True witnesses have a duty to speak, while authorities have a duty not to harass or needlessly burden them.
- Certificates, examination marks and votes can all carry the responsibility of testimony.
- Allah knows what is actually done. Fairness must therefore govern both public decisions and private motives.

<<<VERSE 5:9>>>
Allah answers faithful obedience with a promise: "Allah has promised those who believe and do good ˹His˺ forgiveness and a great reward". Belief and righteous action are joined, and the promised outcome joins pardon with Paradise. The declaration gives hope after the demand for difficult, impartial justice.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah has promised"** makes the assurance His own. It is not a wish based on uncertain human approval. Allah does not fail His promise.

**"those who believe and do good"** describes people who believe in Allah and His messengers and act in obedience. A gloss specifies belief in Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, together with the duties between servants and their Lord. Accepting Allah's guidance must bear fruit in conduct.

**"˹His˺ forgiveness"** promises pardon for sins. One explanation describes their sins being forgiven in this world. The promise is not limited to overlooking outward faults before other people. It concerns their standing before Allah.

**"and a great reward"** means Paradise. The reward is an expression of Allah's generosity and mercy toward His believing servants.

### Is Paradise purchased by good deeds?

The explanation distinguishes a cause from an independent entitlement. Good deeds are the way Allah has appointed toward His mercy, pardon and acceptance. They matter, but they do not place Him under a debt payable apart from His favour.

Paradise is therefore entered through His mercy, even though righteous actions are the appointed means of receiving it. The ability to follow that way and the mercy granted at its end both come from Allah. Gratitude, rather than self-sufficiency, is the proper response to the promise.

Allah makes His guidance known through signs and arguments. Those who accept that guidance, rather than evade it, are described as deserving the promised reward. Their response is faith expressed in good action, not simply clever speech about religion.

### In short

- Allah promises forgiveness and Paradise to those whose faith is joined with righteous conduct.
- His promise is dependable, while the reward remains His mercy rather than an independent debt owed to human achievement.
- Good deeds are the means He has appointed toward pardon and acceptance.

<<<VERSE 5:10>>>
The contrasting outcome is stated plainly: "As for those who disbelieve and deny Our signs, they are the residents of the Hellfire". Rejection of Allah and His revelations has a consequence different from the forgiveness and reward promised to believing obedience.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who disbelieve"** describes rejection of Allah. The verse does not treat unbelief as equivalent to the faithful response described immediately before it.

**"and deny Our signs"** includes denial of His revelations and the evidence through which His guidance is made known. A gloss specifically names Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an as what is being rejected.

**"they are the residents of the Hellfire"** describes enduring residence, not a passing encounter. The explanation compares their attachment to the Fire with the continuing association of a companion with his companion. They enter as a consequence of disbelief and rejection and remain there.

### What does denying a sign involve?

The warning includes encountering Allah's clear argument and answering it with verbal evasions instead of acceptance. Cleverness in speech does not change the truth of what has been shown. Refusing the evidence while obscuring that refusal with words is treated as denial of His signs.

The punishment is explained through Allah's perfect justice, wisdom and judgment. He does not wrong anyone or make a mistaken decision. The contrast with the preceding promise presents both mercy toward believing obedience and justice toward deliberate rejection.

### In short

- Denial concerns Allah and the signs through which His guidance is made known.
- Evasive argument does not turn rejection into acceptance.
- The Fire is described as the abiding outcome of that rejection, under Allah's just judgment.

<<<VERSE 5:11>>>
Allah asks believers to remember protection as a gift: "O believers! Remember Allah’s favour upon you: when a people sought to harm you, but He held their hands back from you." An enemy's intention did not become the outcome the enemy wanted. The believers' safety therefore calls for gratitude, obedience and reliance on Allah, not confidence in their own independence.

The reports connect the reminder with more than one attempted attack on the Prophet ﷺ and his community. They should be retained as different accounts rather than forced into a single incident.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Remember Allah’s favour upon you"** calls for remembrance by both heart and tongue. The addressed believers include Muhammad ﷺ and his Companions. Their safety and deliverance from hostile plans belong among the favours to be acknowledged.

**"when a people sought to harm you"** describes a real intention to attack or kill. The image of hands reaching toward them expresses an attempt to put hostility into action. Several accounts identify different groups or incidents.

**"but He held their hands back from you"** means that Allah prevented the intended harm. His protection included turning the attackers away and placing fear in their hearts. A dangerous situation did not lie beyond His knowledge or power.

**"Be mindful of Allah."** Gratitude for safety must take the form of obeying His commands and avoiding His prohibitions. Taqwa, awareness of Allah, is required after deliverance as well as during danger.

**"And in Allah let the believers put their trust"** directs reliance to Him alone for religious and worldly needs. The explanation promises that He suffices for those who trust Him and protects them against the evil plans of others.

### What happened when a man took the Prophet's ﷺ sword?

Abdur-Razzaq records Jabir describing a halt during a journey. People spread out under trees for shade. The Prophet ﷺ hung his weapon on a tree and rested. A bedouin seized it, raised it toward him and asked who could protect him now.

The Prophet ﷺ answered that Allah would protect him. The man repeated his question two or three times and received the same answer. He eventually lowered the sword or returned it to its sheath. The Prophet ﷺ called his Companions and told them what had happened while the man sat beside him. He did not punish him in this account.

Ma'mar reports that Qatadah connected the event with Arabs who wanted the Prophet ﷺ killed and sent the man for that purpose. Qatadah would then cite this reminder of Allah restraining hostile hands. The story is also mentioned in the Sahih, where the man's name is given as Ghawrath bin Al-Harith.

Another report from Jabir gives a different form of the encounter. Ghawrath ibn al-Harith, a man from Muharib, offered before people of Banu Ghatafan and Muharib to assassinate Muhammad ﷺ. He approached the Prophet ﷺ while the sword lay in his lap and asked to see it. The Prophet ﷺ handed it over.

The man drew it and moved it about, but Allah restrained him whenever he meant to strike. He asked whether the Prophet ﷺ feared him, especially now that he held the weapon. The Prophet ﷺ replied that Allah would protect him. Ghawrath put the sword back into its sheath and returned it. This account also connects the verse with the failed attack.

The differences concern how the weapon came into the man's hands and how the encounter unfolded. The point they share is reliance on Allah in an immediate danger and the failure of the intended harm. The details need not be silently merged.

### What was the plot involving Bani An-Nadir?

Muhammad bin Ishaq bin Yasar, Mujahid and 'Ikrimah connect the verse with a plot by Bani An-Nadir. The Prophet ﷺ had gone to them seeking help with the blood money for two people killed by Muslims. While he sat beneath a wall, they planned to drop a stone onto his head.

The task was assigned to 'Amr bin Jihash bin Ka'b. Allah informed His Prophet ﷺ of the plan. He left for Madinah, and his Companions followed afterward. The intended attack was therefore prevented before it could be carried out.

The account goes on to say that Allah commanded the Prophet ﷺ to expel Bani An-Nadir. He besieged their area and required them to leave Madinah. This is the continuation attached to that particular report, not part of the separate sword incident.

### Are other groups mentioned?

A brief explanation identifies the people intending harm as Banu Qurayzah. Another names Quraysh. Neither supplies a detailed incident alongside that identification. These should remain distinct notices rather than being turned into an invented combined narrative.

The variety of accounts does not change the practical instruction. Believers are to recognise that Allah protected them from an attack their opponents intended. Remembering deliverance should deepen their dependence on Him and their fidelity to His commands.

### What is the proper response to protection?

The verse does not end with praise of the believers' strength. It ends with mindfulness and trust. Safety can be received wrongly if it produces forgetfulness, pride or the assumption that obedience no longer matters.

The instructed response holds the past and present together. Remember the favour already received, fulfil the duties now required and entrust what lies ahead to Allah. The protection of the Messenger ﷺ is among the concrete reminders through which the community is taught that reliance.

### In short

- Allah's prevention of an intended attack is a favour to remember with heart and tongue.
- Reports connect the verse with the man who took the Prophet's ﷺ sword and with the plot of Bani An-Nadir. Brief glosses name other groups without supplying further detail.
- The accounts retain their differences while sharing the theme of harm restrained by Allah.
- Gratitude for deliverance means continued obedience and reliance on Allah in both religious and worldly affairs.
