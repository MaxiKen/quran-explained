<<<VERSE 7:138>>>
After their miraculous victory over Pharaoh and his people, and the ease that followed, the Israelites began to show the ignorance that wealthy people show when given a life of luxury. Allah records what the ignorant among them said to Mūsā (Moses) once they had crossed the sea and seen His signs and His great power: "We brought the Children of Israel across the sea and they came upon a people devoted to idols." At once they asked, "O Moses! Make for us a god like their gods." His answer was blunt: "He replied, “Indeed, you are a people acting ignorantly". It was their first impertinent request.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We brought the Children of Israel across the sea"**: Allah brought them safely across when Mūsā struck the sea with his staff and it parted.

**"and they came upon a people devoted to idols."** They passed by a people who clung to their idols, worshipping them constantly instead of Allah. In the Arabic, "devoted" is read yaʿkufūn or yaʿkifūn. Some scholars of tafsīr said these people were from Canaan, or from the tribe of Lakhm. One explanation calls them al-Raqīm, remnants of the people of Ibrāhīm (Abraham). Ibn Jarīr said they worshipped idols made in the shape of cows. This later influenced the children of Israel when they worshipped the calf.

**"They demanded, “O Moses! Make for us a god like their gods.”"** Show us a god, an idol to worship, just as these people have idols to worship.

**"He replied, “Indeed, you are a people acting ignorantly"**: you do not know Allah's greatness and majesty, or that He is free of any partner or anything like Him. You are ignorant of the rights due to Allah alone: belief in His oneness and the reverence owed to Him, and of how unfitting it is to ascribe partners to Him. You repay Allah's grace to you with words like these. One explanation says: you do not know Allah's command.

### Why do people long for a visible god?

Having crossed at the Red Sea's northern tip into the Sinai Peninsula, the Israelites then made their way southward along the coast. On the way they saw a people engaged in idol worship, and some of the Israelites asked Mūsā to make them an idol.

The greatest human weakness is attaching importance to what can be seen, and so failing to give one's attention to the unseen God. That is why people cannot detach themselves from things visible to the naked eye. The unenlightened bow down before gods of stone and metal. The civilised give the same devotion to a particular personality, a community, a cultural structure, or something similar.

### In short

- Soon after crossing the sea, some Israelites asked Mūsā for an idol like those of a people they passed.
- Those people worshipped cow-shaped idols, which later shaped the Israelites' worship of the calf.
- Mūsā called them ignorant of Allah's greatness and of His sole right to worship.
- People's weakness for the visible draws them to idols of stone, or to revered personalities and structures.

<<<VERSE 7:139>>>
Mūsā (Moses), deeply troubled by their ignorance, went on: "What they follow is certainly doomed to destruction and their deeds are in vain.”" All the effort these people put into their worship was going to waste.

### Phrase by phrase

**"What they follow is certainly doomed to destruction"**: these idol-worshippers are ruined, and their way, their idolatry, will be destroyed.

**"and their deeds are in vain.”"** All their works and devotion are false, worthless and in error, because they worship other than Allah.

### Did anything similar happen in the Prophet's ﷺ time?

Imam Abū Jaʿfar ibn Jarīr reported from Abū Wāqid al-Laythī that the Companions set out from Makkah with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ for Ḥunayn. Some disbelievers had a lote tree near which they used to stay, and on which they hung their weapons. It was called Dhāt Anwāṭ. When the Companions passed a huge green lote tree, they asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to appoint a Dhāt Anwāṭ for them, like the one the disbelievers had. He swore by the One in whose hand was his soul that they had said just what Mūsā's people said to him. Then he recited this verse and the one before it.

### Why could Mūsā never grant their request?

When Mūsā said that all the idol-worshippers' devotion would be wasted, he meant this. He had been sent to turn people to the worship of Allah alone. Carving idols for his own followers was unthinkable.

### In short

- Mūsā declared that the idol-worshippers' way was doomed and all their devotion worthless.
- The Prophet ﷺ likened some Companions' request for a sacred tree at Ḥunayn to the Israelites' request for an idol.
- A prophet sent to call people to the one God could never make idols for them.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:140>>>
Mūsā (Moses) pressed home his reproach: "He added, “Shall I seek for you a god other than Allah, while He has honoured you above the others?”" How could he seek for them any god but Allah, when Allah had given them excellence over all the peoples of their time?

### Phrase by phrase

**"He added, “Shall I seek for you a god other than Allah"**: shall I command you to worship another lord, besides the one true God? In the Arabic, abghīkum, "shall I seek for you", stands for abghī lakum.

**"while He has honoured you above the others?”"** He has preferred you above all the peoples of your time, above all creatures then, because you surrendered to Him. You have already seen His great signs. He destroyed your enemy for you, made you leaders in the land and established you there. Those who believed in Mūsā were superior to all the people of that age.

### What did their "superiority" mean?

The superiority of the Israelites over the rest of humanity is not meant here in a racial sense. It refers to their mission. It carries the same meaning as the Qur'an's description of the community of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ as "the best community". Allah's practice is to entrust His Book to one community, which then carries His message to the rest. Long ago that duty belonged to the Israelites. After the coming of the Final Prophet ﷺ, it passed to the Muslim ummah.

### In short

- Mūsā asked how he could seek another god for them when Allah had raised them above the peoples of their time.
- Their superiority was one of mission, as carriers of Allah's Book, not of race.
- That role later passed to the community of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, called "the best community".
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:141>>>
Mūsā (Moses) reminded the children of Israel of Allah's blessings, above all their rescue from Pharaoh's tyranny and the humiliation they had suffered: "And ˹remember˺ when We rescued you from the people of Pharaoh, who afflicted you with dreadful torment—killing your sons and keeping your women. That was a severe test from your Lord". He reminded them too of the honour and the justice they had seen against their enemy, when they watched him disgraced, drowned and utterly destroyed. The same subject is told in Sūrat al-Baqarah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember˺ when We rescued you from the people of Pharaoh"**: remember, O children of Israel, when Allah saved you from Pharaoh and his people. One reading has anjaynākum, "We rescued you", and another anjākum, "He rescued you".

**"who afflicted you with dreadful torment"**: who burdened you with the worst kind of suffering and humiliation.

**"killing your sons"**: slaughtering your little sons.

**"and keeping your women."** Letting your grown women live, to use them as servants.

**"That was a severe test from your Lord"**: literally "a tremendous trial". The deliverance was a tremendous blessing from your Lord, a test to see whether you would be thankful. Or the suffering itself was a tremendous trial. Either way it was a grace or a hardship from your Lord. So will you not take the warning and stop saying what you are saying?

### Why remind them of their suffering now?

The verse reminds them of their pitiful state and their persecution by Pharaoh, when their sons were killed and their daughters kept alive as maidservants. Allah freed them from that disgraceful punishment through His prophet. Could they now show their Lord such ingratitude, setting up lifeless stones beside Him as gods? They must repent to Allah for their transgression.

### Was Pharaoh's oppression a punishment?

The power Pharaoh had to oppress the Israelites was a test for them, not a punishment. Believers are put through such trials to shake them and wake them up, as if by a shock. It then becomes clear who turns away from Allah's true religion when faced with hard times, and who holds firmly to it through patience in adversity.

### In short

- Mūsā reminded the Israelites how Allah saved them from Pharaoh, who killed their sons and enslaved their women.
- Both the suffering and the rescue were a great test from their Lord, a test of patience and of gratitude.
- Having been freed by Allah, asking for idols was gross ingratitude that called for repentance.
- Oppression of believers is a trial that shows who stays steadfast and who falls away.
