<<<VERSE 7:138>>>
The people are on the far shore with the sea behind them, and the first thing they ask for is an idol. "We brought the Children of Israel across the sea and they came upon a people devoted to idols." Then the request and the answer come. "They demanded, “O Moses! Make for us a god like their gods.” He replied, “Indeed, you are a people acting ignorantly"

### Phrase by phrase

**"We brought the Children of Israel across the sea"** recalls the crossing that had just been told. They went over the northern end of the sea and came to the peninsula of Sinai, and then they travelled south along the bank.

**"and they came upon a people devoted to idols"** describes what they saw on the way. The people in question are said by some of those who explained the Book to have been from Canaan, and by others to have been of the tribe of Lakhm. In one account they are called al-Raqim, and are described as a remnant of the people of Abraham. What they were given to was the worship of idols, and they stayed near them in devotion.

Ibn Jarir commented on this place that the people were worshipping idols they had made in the shape of cows, and that this is what influenced the Children of Israel afterwards, when they worshipped the calf. So the request made here was the seed of what came later, and the verse records where it was planted.

**"They demanded, “O Moses! Make for us a god like their gods.”"** is the request itself. It came from the ignorant among the Children of Israel, and it came after they had crossed the sea and seen the signs of Allah and His great power with their own eyes. What they wanted was a god they could see, since the God they had been brought to is not seen with the eye.

**"He replied, “Indeed, you are a people acting ignorantly”"** is the answer of Musa. What he was telling them was that they did not know the greatness and the majesty of Allah, nor His freedom from any partner and from anything that resembles Him. In another reading the sense is that they were ignorant of what was due to Him alone in His oneness, and of what may not be ascribed to Him. It was their repayment of His favour to them that brought the words out of the messenger.

### What was behind the request

The people of Israel had come out of the greatest victory of their history and had entered a life of ease and comfort, and then they began to show the marks of ignorance, in the way that people who are given wealth and luxury begin to show them. The first impertinent request they made was this one, that a god be made for them like the gods of the people they had seen being worshipped along the road.

There is a weakness of the human being underneath it, and it is worth naming. A person attaches importance to appearances, and because of that he cannot give his attention to the God who is not seen. He cannot detach himself from the things that the eye can take in. The unenlightened bow down before gods of stone and of metal, while people who count themselves advanced give their devotion to a personality, or a community, or a structure of culture. The form changes with the times, and the devotion stays where it was.

### The report of the lote tree

A report carried by Ibn Jarir from Abu Waqid Al-Laythi puts the whole verse in front of the community of the Prophet ﷺ. He said that they had left Makkah for the battle of Hunayn in the company of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. Some of the disbelievers had a lote tree whose shade they used to stay in, and on which they would hang their weapons, and the tree was called Dhat Al-Anwat. So when the companions passed by a large green lote tree, they said to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that he should appoint for them a Dhat Al-Anwat like the one the others had.

He said, by the One in whose hand my soul is, that they had said just what the people of Musa said to him, and he repeated their words, that he should make for them a god as the others had gods, and that his answer had been that they were an ignorant people and that what the others followed was doomed and their deeds were in vain.

The report is a mirror held up to the ummah that had the Messenger of Allah among them, and it shows that the inclination recorded in this verse is not a thing confined to one nation or to one age.

### In short

- The Children of Israel crossed to the peninsula of Sinai and travelled south along the bank of the sea.
- On the way they came upon a people given to idols, said to be from Canaan, or from Lakhm, or named al-Raqim as a remnant of the people of Abraham.
- Ibn Jarir said the idols were shaped like cows, and that this is what influenced the Children of Israel later in the affair of the calf.
- They asked for a god they could see, after they had watched the signs of Allah and His power at the sea.
- Musa answered that they were ignorant of the greatness of Allah and of His freedom from every partner.
- The report of Abu Waqid Al-Laythi at Hunayn shows the same inclination in the companions' question about the lote tree called Dhat Al-Anwat, and the Prophet ﷺ answered it with this very verse.

<<<VERSE 7:139>>>
The messenger finishes his answer with the fate of the people they admired. "What they follow is certainly doomed to destruction and their deeds are in vain.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"What they follow is certainly doomed to destruction"** is a sentence about the end of the people themselves. The way they were on was going to be destroyed, and the destruction meant here is of the religion they held and of the community that held it.

**"and their deeds are in vain"** speaks to the work they had done. Everything they had put into that worship was error, and no part of it stayed with them as a gain. In one reading the words say plainly that what they were doing was a waste and a losing of themselves.

**Why the messenger said it in that order.** He had already told his people that they were ignorant, and the next thing was to show them what sort of thing they were asking to be given. If he had refused the request and said nothing else, the desire would have stayed in them. What he did was to name the end of the very people they wanted to copy, and there is no argument against a model that is on its way to ruin.

The words also turn the attention of the Children of Israel back on themselves, since they were being asked to look at a nation whose worship was about to be extinguished, and to want for themselves what was already lost.

### In short

- He told them plainly that the way of the people they admired was going to be destroyed.
- Everything those people had done in that worship was error, and none of it stayed with them.
- The answer turns them away from the model by naming the end of that model.
- The request is refused with a reason, so that the desire behind it is uprooted rather than simply denied.

<<<VERSE 7:140>>>
He puts the question to them in a form that answers itself. "He added, “Shall I seek for you a god other than Allah, while He has honoured you above the others?”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Shall I seek for you a god other than Allah"** is put as a question with an answer already built into it, and in another reading the sense is whether he should command them to worship another lord. The thing they were asking of him was the one thing a messenger cannot do, since a messenger comes to turn people from the worship of the created to the worship of the Creator.

**"while He has honoured you above the others?"** is the argument that closes the matter. In one report the words mean that they had been preferred over the peoples of their own time, and the token of it was the destruction of their enemy and their being made leaders in the land and established in it. Moses put the question to them so that the contrast between what they had and what they were asking for would stand out on its own.

**How the honour is understood.** The superiority spoken of here is not a thing of race or of birth. What it refers to is the mission. The Book speaks of a community as the best community raised up for people, and that is the office meant here as well. The way of God with these things is to choose a community to carry His Book, and then to use that community to carry His message to the other nations. In the ages before, that office was held by the Children of Israel, and after the coming of the last Prophet ﷺ the same mission was given to the Muslim ummah. So the honour was a trust placed on their shoulders, and what they were doing on the road beside the sea was spending it.

### In short

- The question is put so that its own answer stands in it, since a messenger cannot call people to any god but Allah.
- The reason follows at once, that Allah had given them honour above the other peoples of their time.
- One report names the tokens of it as the destruction of their enemy and their establishment in the land.
- The honour meant is the mission and not race or birth, and the Book speaks in the same sense when it calls a community the best community raised up for people.
- That office was carried by the Children of Israel before, and after the coming of the last Prophet ﷺ it was given to the Muslim community.

<<<VERSE 7:141>>>
The place they had been brought out of is put in front of them once more. "And ˹remember˺ when We rescued you from the people of Pharaoh, who afflicted you with dreadful torment—killing your sons and keeping your women. That was a severe test from your Lord"

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember˺ when We rescued you from the people of Pharaoh"** calls them back to the shame they had been brought out of. They had been under the tyranny of the king and his people, and had gone through humiliation and disgrace at their hands. They had also watched their enemy being destroyed in front of them and meeting that end in the water.

**"who afflicted you with dreadful torment"** names what the torment was in the plainest terms. The worst of the chastisement laid on them was the killing of their sons. Their women were kept alive, and the purpose of that was service, since they were made to serve the Egyptians as servants. The sons here are the small children.

**"That was a severe test from your Lord"** names what the whole experience had been. In one reading the words mean that the rescue itself was a tremendous favour from their Lord, and in another that there was a great trial in the punishment itself. The two readings belong together. What the people went through was a test, and the rescue that followed was a favour, and both of them came from the same hand.

The sense of the trial is set out in one report, that being saved from the people of Pharaoh was a great test from their Lord, to see whether they would be thankful. That is the use the verse puts the memory to. The people had been rescued from slaying and from servitude, and they were now free on the far shore of the sea. They were being reminded of what had come before, so that the memory would put the request they had just made in its true light.

### What the years of oppression were for

The opportunity the king had of oppressing the Children of Israel was itself a test for them, and not a punishment. Believers are put through such things so that they may be shaken and woken up. It then becomes clear who among them turns away from the religion of God because the circumstances have become hard, and who holds on to it by patience when the days are dark. The same subject was set out in the explanation of Al-Baqarah, the second surah of the Book. The story of the Israelites is told there at length, and the same reminder is given to them.

### Where the passage is going

Musa had found the Children of Israel on the far side of the sea asking for a god they could see. The account of the reminder given to them in Al-Baqarah carries the same lesson. The reminder given here takes them back over the years of the sons they had lost, and the years of service they had been made to give. It sets all of that against the thing they were now asking their Lord for. The verses after these go on to the account of the mountain, where the same people are given more of the signs and the same inclinations appear again.

### In short

- The Israelites are sent back to the memory of the years they spent under the tyranny of the people of Pharaoh.
- The worst of the torment named is the killing of their sons and the keeping of their women as servants.
- What they went through was a severe test, and in one reading the rescue itself was the great favour from their Lord.
- The trial was to see whether they would be thankful, and the reminder of it is set against the request they had just made.
- The oppression they suffered was a test for them and not a punishment, and believers are tried in that way so that it may be seen who holds on with patience.
