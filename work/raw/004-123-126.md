<<<VERSE 4:123>>>
The end of boasting: "˹Divine grace is˺ neither by your wishes nor those of the People of the Book! Whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly, and they will find no protector or helper besides Allah" (4:123). The verse arose from a contest of boasting and cut it short with a scale.

### The dispute of the communities

Qatādah said the Muslims and the People of the Scriptures once mentioned their own virtues to each other. The People of the Scriptures said that their Prophet came before the Muslims' Prophet and their Book before the Muslims' Book, so they had more right to Allah. The Muslims answered that rather they had more right to Allah, for their Prophet was the Final Prophet and their Book superseded all the Books before it. Then Allah sent down the verse, and His later words backed the Muslims' side of the argument against the opponents of other religions. Similar statements are attributed to as-Suddī, Masrūq, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk and Abū Ṣāliḥ. Al-ʿAwfī reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that the followers of the various religions disputed, each naming the best of its own, the Christians their man, the Jews naming Mūsā, the Muslims naming the Last of them. One report has the adherents of the Tawrah, the adherents of the Injīl and the adherents of other religions sitting together, each claiming to be better than the others, when the verse came down. The ruling inside it is general: no religion is accepted on the strength of wishful thinking or mere hopes. The accepted religion relies on what resides in the heart and is made truthful through actions. A claim does not attain its object merely by being uttered, nor is every claimant to the truth considered such until the claim gains merit with proof from Allah. Safety is not acquired by wishing, for either side. The key is obeying Allah and following what He legislated through the words of His honourable Messengers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly"** sets the scale, and the Qur'ān repeats it: "So whosoever does good equal to the weight of an atom, shall see it. And whosoever does evil equal to the weight of an atom, shall see it" (99:7-8). When these verses were revealed they became hard on many of the Companions. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded that ʿĀʾishah said she told the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that she knew the hardest āyah in the Qur'ān, and when he asked which, she named this one, and he answered:

> That is what strikes the believing servant, even the problems that bother him.

Ibn Jarīr and Abū Dāwūd also recorded this hadith. Saʿīd ibn Manṣūr recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the verse was hard on the Muslims, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told them:

> Be steadfast and seek closeness. Everything that afflicts the Muslim, even the thorn that pierces his skin and the hardship he suffers, will be an expiation for him.

Imām Aḥmad collected this wording through Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah, and Muslim, an-Nasāʾī and at-Tirmidhī also recorded it. The reports multiply the comfort. Abū Hurairah's version has the Companions grieved that the minutest evil in their deeds would be requited, and the answer that they should not worry but keep doing what they could, for whatever hardship or pain afflicts them makes amends, down to the thorn that pinches a foot. Another narration says that any sorrow, pain, sickness or anxiety afflicting a Muslim becomes an expiation of sins. Abū Bakr's case stands in the Jāmiʿ of at-Tirmidhī and in the record of Ibn Jarīr: when the Prophet ﷺ recited the verse to him, he felt as if his back was broken, and being asked what was the matter, submitted that hardly anyone among them could claim to have done nothing bad, so who could hope to go unscathed if every evil deed had to be requited. He was told that he and his believing brothers need not worry, for the worldly hardships they face make amends for their sins, and in another narration he was asked whether he did not get sick, whether distress and sorrow never tested him, and on his answer that all this did happen, was told that this is the requital of whatever evil may have been done. In a hadith of Abū Dāwūd, ʿĀʾishah reports that a discomfort faced in fever, a pain however it comes, even the pinch of a thorn, all become expiation, down to the small effort of someone looking for a thing in one pocket and finding it in another. The division of the requital runs between the two abodes: the believers are recompensed in this world or after death before entering Paradise, the unbelievers in the Hereafter before entering the Fire, while the People of the Scripture wished their day's sins forgiven by night and their night's sins by day.

**"and they will find no protector or helper besides Allah"** closes the escape, for no relative will benefit and no defender save from the torment. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported from Ibn ʿAbbās the one exception: unless he repents and Allah forgives him.

The wider teaching reads the verse against the hopes of every community. When the People of the Book become engrossed in the world, they do not deny Allah or the Hereafter outright. They believe in these realities in the formal sense while devoting all effort to worldly acquisition, very serious about honour and glory here. For Paradise, wishful thinking alone is believed to suffice, whether the blessing of some saint, the membership of a certain group, or the reciting of words and phrases, cheap formulae and superficial acts hoped to save from the Fire and usher into Heaven. Such wishful thinking, in whatever beautiful words couched, will not come to the rescue. The system of Allah is established on realities, not wishes, and in His court everyone will be judged exactly in accordance with his actions, with nothing but the law of justice He Himself promulgated to influence His decisions.

The inward reading gives the same law in images. Whoever plants colocynth will not harvest roses and jasmine, and whoever drinks deadly poison will not find the taste of honey. The duty of service, once squandered, leaves no firm standing on the carpet of nearness. Whoever is branded with misfortune will not be provided with the best of the best, and whomever the divine decree rejects has no helper in creation. Yet whoever labours in Allah's service will not be kept from attaining His blessing. Whomever He has made rich in seeking Him, He honours in finding Him, and whomever He has made to drink from the cup of longing for Him, He brings to the intimacy of meeting Him.

### In short

- The boasting of Muslims and People of the Book over Prophets and Books was answered with one scale, the deed.
- The āyah weighed heavy on the Companions until the Prophet ﷺ taught that every affliction, down to a thorn, is an expiation.
- Abū Bakr's breaking back was steadied by sickness and sorrow as requital, and ʿĀʾishah counted even the search through pockets.
- Repentance remains the one exception to the friendless requital.
- Neither saint nor formula purchases Paradise, and the colocynth yields no roses.

<<<VERSE 4:124>>>
The other scale-pan: "and have faith will enter Paradise and will never be wronged ˹even as much as˺ the speck on a date stone" (4:124). The verse answers the previous severity with a mercy as exact.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But those who do good—whether male or female—and have faith"** sets the one condition under all the good: deeds that issue forth from true faith. The doing is of acts of obedience, that which lies between the servant and his Lord, and the faith is genuine in the one who holds it, male or female alike. Allah mentions His kindness, generosity and mercy in accepting the good deeds of His servants on condition that they embrace the faith, and the reading of the verb runs both ways, they shall be admitted and they shall enter, the receiving and the coming meeting in one word.

**"will enter Paradise and will never be wronged ˹even as much as˺ the speck on a date stone"** measures the justice. Nothing of their righteous deeds will be withheld, not the weight of a naqīr, the speck on the back of a date-stone, and the rewards will not be diminished even by the size of the dint in a date-stone.

### The measures of smallness

The Qur'ān keeps a set of weights for the minute. The naqīr is the speck on the back of a date-stone, the Fatīl the scalish thread in the long slit of a date-stone, and the Qiṭmīr the thin membrane stretched over the date-stone, and all three have their places in the Book. The point of the weighing is comfort: the justice that requites an atom's evil also guards an atom's good, and the believer's account is kept to a measure finer than anything the hand can hold. He who combines faith with practice enters Paradise fully rewarded, and the entry is not a bare admittance but a payment in full, every deed counted, nothing docked, nothing shorted, down to the thread in the slit.

The hint inside the verse divides the communities. Good deeds may exist among the People of the Book and other non-Muslims, yet with an unsound faith those deeds find no acceptance. The Muslims, with sound faith and good deeds, are the successful ones, standing above the others.

### In short

- Faith is the condition under every good deed, and Paradise receives both the man and the woman who meet it.
- The speck on the date stone is the outer limit of the wronging, and no reward is cut even by the thread in the slit.
- Good deeds without sound faith find no acceptance, while sound faith with good deeds stands above every community.

<<<VERSE 4:125>>>
The best religion: "And who is better in faith than those who ˺fully˺ submit themselves to Allah, do good, and follow the Way of Abraham, the upright? Allah chose Abraham as a close friend" (4:125). The verse names the criterion and then its living proof.

### Phrase by phrase

**"who ˺fully˺ submit themselves to Allah"** means the surrender of the face, and the face is the whole personality. The verse calls the same person a muḥsin, a beautiful-doer, for the surrender and the beauty of the deed are one station. Turning the face toward Allah is turning with the whole existence, acting for His good pleasure with all the sincerity at one's command, without ever contaminating the deed with hypocritical or materialistic motives.

**"do good"** names the second half of the criterion: the way of acting must itself be correct. The correct way is no invention of the self. It is the way of Islam pure and unalloyed, standing on the injunctions of Almighty Allah and the teachings of His noble Messenger ﷺ. Two conditions therefore govern the acceptance of any deed, sincerity of intention and soundness of action, accordance with the Sharīʿah and the Sunnah. Sincerity relates to the heart, the innermost human dimension, and conformity to the Sharīʿah relates to the exterior. The deed becomes outwardly correct by following the Sunnah and inwardly correct by sincerity, and whoever fulfils both finds his exterior and interior perfectly synchronized. When sincerity is lost, the doer becomes a hypocrite showing off for people, and when the Sharīʿah is not followed, he becomes an ignorant, wicked person. Lacking either pillar, the deed becomes null and void, and whoever combines both performs the deeds of the faithful believers, whose best deeds are accepted and whose errors are erased.

### The two downfalls

The history of nations and faiths reveals that all deviant groups have either lacked sincerity or not acted right, and these are the very two groups identified in sūrat al-Fātiḥah as those who deviated from the straight path. Those referred to as incurring wrath are the people who lack sincerity, victims of desires, and those called astray are the people who have not acted right, victims of doubts. Everyone understands the first condition, the need for sincerity. The second, good conduct as following the Sharīʿah, is the condition many Muslims ignore, thinking a good deed a good deed wherever done, though the Qur'ān and the Sunnah make it clear that good conduct depends exclusively on the teachings of the Prophet ﷺ and on following his example. Doing less and doing more are both crimes: the offering of three rakʿahs of Ẓuhr instead of the prescribed four is a crime, and so is the offering of five, a sin of the same nature. The condition set by Allah and His Messenger ﷺ for any act of worship is final, and additions or restrictions from one's own side, or forms other than the one laid out, are impermissible no matter how attractive the surface. All new alterations in the established religion, the innovations the Prophet ﷺ declared straying into error, are of this nature, and the ignorant perform them with sincerity as worship worthy of reward, while the deed goes waste or becomes sinful in the light of the Sharīʿah. Hence the stress on good conduct in the Book: "So that He tests you as to who among you is good in deed" (67:2), said in sūrat al-Mulk, where the words are good in deed, not outnumbering in deeds, for the good deed is precisely the deed done by the Sunnah's measure. The proper effort, spoken of in the verse that says whoever desires the Hereafter and strives for it with due striving (17:19), is the same: intended purely for the Hereafter and carried out as the Prophet ﷺ explained by word and deed, with any shift, increased or decreased, outside acceptance. In all the deeds of ṣalāh, sawm, ḥajj, zakāh, charity, the dhikr of Allah, the blessings on the Prophet and the salām, the way is to find how the Messenger of Allah did them and what instructions he left.

**"and follow the Way of Abraham, the upright"** names the pattern. The hanīf is the one who, intentionally and with knowledge, avoids shirk, goes attentively to the truth, and allows no one to hinder him or stop him from it, inclining away from all religions to the upright religion, the creed that accords with the creed of Islam and its Oneness. The command was given: "Follow the religion of Ibrahim the Hanif, and he was not of the Mushrikin" (16:123), and the claim is defined: "Verily, among mankind who have the best claim to Ibrahim are those who followed him, and this Prophet" (3:68), for "Ibrahim was an Ummah, obedient to Allah, a Hanif, and he was not one of the Mushrikin" (16:120).

**"Allah chose Abraham as a close friend"** crowns the pattern. The grade of Khalīl is the highest grade of love, the ultimate closeness to Allah that the servants seek, and Ibrāhīm attained it through obedience to his Lord, who tried him with certain commands and he fulfilled them (2:124). Al-Bukhārī recorded from ʿAmr ibn Maymūn that when Muʿādh came back from Yemen he led the people in the Fajr prayer and recited this verse, and one of the men present commented that surely the eye of Ibrāhīm's mother had been comforted. Ibrāhīm bears the name Khalīl for the great love his Lord bore him, love earned through the acts of obedience that Allah loves and prefers. In the Two Ṣaḥīḥs, Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī said that in his last speech the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> O people! If I were to take a Khalīl from the people of the earth, I would have taken Abū Bakr ibn Abī Quḥāfah as my Khalīl. However, your companion is the Khalīl of Allah.

From Jundub, from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr and from ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd comes the same word: Allah chose His Prophet as His Khalīl, even as He chose Ibrāhīm as His Khalīl.

### The inward reading

In this verse the Lord of the Worlds praises the self-purifiers, showing His pleasure with self-purification in deeds. Muṣṭafā was the first to dress the Kaʿbah of deeds in that cloak, and his word was that deeds are only through intentions. Self-purification does the work of colour in jewels. A jewel without the cape of colour is a worthless stone, and deeds without self-purification are to knock oneself out without hitting the mark. Maʿrūf Karkhī used to beat himself with a whip, saying: O soul, purify thyself and be delivered. It has been said that knowledge is the seed and deeds the planting, with self-purification the water. By self-purification the work is done, in it lies salvation, and in it endless felicity, yet it is exalted, coming down just anywhere to no one and showing its face to just no one, for the Exalted Lord said that it is one of His secrets, deposited in the hearts of those whom He loves among His servants. Of its hiddenness there is a story. A worshipper among the Children of Israel was told that in a certain place there was a tree that people worshipped, and for the sake of Allah and the zeal of the religion he grew angry, took a mattock on his shoulder and set out to dig the tree up by its roots. Iblīs met him on the road in the attributes of an old man, asked where he was going, and heard the errand. He told the worshipper to go back and busy himself with his worship, for this would not be done by his hand, then struggled with him and fell, and the worshipper sat on his chest. Iblīs begged to be let go, promising beautiful words, and once released, said: O worshipper, Allah has prophets. If this tree needed to be dug up, He would command a prophet to dig it up.

### In short

- The best religion is the full surrender of the self, the good deed and the way of Ibrāhīm the upright.
- Sincerity and correctness are the two pillars, and either loss names its people, the hypocrites of showing off and the astray of self-invented ways.
- Good conduct means the Sunnah, no less and no more, for three rakʿahs and five rakʿahs of Ẓuhr offend alike.
- Ibrāhīm, who fulfilled the commands, holds the highest grade of love, and the Prophet ﷺ would have taken no earthly friend but Abū Bakr.
- Self-purification is the colour of the jewel, a secret deposited in the hearts of the beloved, as the worshipper with the mattock learned.

<<<VERSE 4:126>>>
The possession of everything: "To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is Fully Aware of everything" (4:126). The verse closes the passage with the ground the whole scale stands on.

### Phrase by phrase

**"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth"** means that everything and everyone are His property, His creatures and His servants, with the dominion of all of it in His hand alone. No one exists who could avert His decision or question His judgment, and what He does, He is never asked about, for His might, ability, fairness, wisdom, compassion and mercy place Him beyond accounting. His is the kingdom in the heavens and the earth, and His planning runs over everything He created.

**"And Allah is Fully Aware of everything"** means that His knowledge holds all things, and nothing concerning His servants is hidden from Him. Not even the weight of an atom escapes His observation in the heavens and the earth, nor anything smaller or bigger than that, and His power and His planning surround His creation as His knowledge does.

### The three admonishments

The inward reading counts the refrain. Three times in these verses it is said that to Allah belongs everything in the heavens and the earth, and each is an admonishment for a different group, specific to a designated meaning. Each of the three sayings carries a designated meaning. The first is an admonishment for the common Muslims, the second for the worshipful servants and the godwary, the third for the sincerely truthful and the elect. First, He says for the common Muslims that everything in heaven and earth is His possession and kingdom, all His creation and artisanry, His knowledge reaching all. He has made certain rightful dues incumbent among them and set apart obligatory acts, so they are to comply with the rightful due of women, orphans and the weak, put the commands into action, and strive to give comfort and achieve peace. Whether they do good or bad, whether at peace or war, they should know that in reality He knows and He sees, for all is His creation and His artisanry, and how can the creation and the artisanry be concealed from their Maker? "Does He who created not know, while He is the Gentle, the Aware?" (67:14).

The teaching that hangs on the verse reaches every soul in its hiding place. Allah is the Lord of the universe, the possessor of all kinds of power, yet He has chosen to remain invisible in this world, and man commits all kinds of evil because he does not see Him, assuming freedom to do as he pleases. The believer who is better in faith is the one like Ibrāhīm, who reserves his loyalty exclusively for his Lord and carries out his affairs in the world with justice and modesty, scrupulously avoiding injustice and arrogance. And were a person to realise that human beings are utterly powerless, he would experience the same state of utter helplessness that awaits on the Day of Judgement, when all the realities are laid bare.

### In short

- Everything in the heavens and the earth is His property and creation, and no one averts His decision or questions His judgment.
- Not an atom, nor smaller nor bigger, escapes His observation.
- The refrain speaks three times, to the common Muslims, the worshipful, and the elect, and the first hearing is a charge to the rights of women, orphans and the weak.
