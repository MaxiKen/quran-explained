<<<VERSE 3:75>>>
Trust in money divides the People of the Book into two kinds: "There are some among the People of the Book who, if entrusted with a stack of gold, will readily return it. Yet there are others who, if entrusted with a single coin, will not repay it unless you constantly demand it. This is because they say, “We are not accountable for ˹exploiting˺ the Gentiles.” And ˹so˺ they attribute lies to Allah knowingly". After breach of faith in belief, breach of trust in property is named, and the verse refuses to paint a whole community with one brush.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There are some among the People of the Book who, if entrusted with a stack of gold, will readily return it"**: Scripture's people include a man who repays a Qintar, a great amount of wealth, without problem, hardship or temptation to declare it lawful. Smaller sums obviously follow. ʿAbdullāh Ibn Salām and his companions are the named example: a man entrusted ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām with 1200 plates of gold, and they were returned.

**"Yet there are others who, if entrusted with a single coin, will not repay it unless you constantly demand it"**: another man keeps even a dinar, one silver coin, unless the owner stands over him without leaving for a minute. Departure invites denial. More than a dinar is still less safe. Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf denied a Quraysh man's entrusted dinar.

**"This is because they say, “We are not accountable for ˹exploiting˺ the Gentiles.”"**: their stated doctrine excuses betrayal and seizure of illiterate people's property, the Arabs. Religion supposedly suffers no harm when unlettered property is eaten, because Allah allegedly permitted it. Injustice toward anyone of another religion became lawful in their conviction, and God was made its source.

**"And ˹so˺ they attribute lies to Allah knowingly"**: they tell a lie against Allah while they know it, a lie and word of misguidance they invented. Unless they had a right to it, Allah would not have allowed them this money.

### A test case: chickens and sheep taken in battle

ʿAbd ar-Razzāq records Saʿsaʿah bin Yazid's question to Ibn ʿAbbās about battlefield capture of Ahl Adh-Dhimmah property, such as chickens and sheep. The captors regarded confiscation as sinless. Ibn ʿAbbās answered that they were repeating the People of the Book's claim about illiterates. Once Jizyah is paid, their property is forbidden except what they willingly surrender.

From "unless you keep standing over him", Imam Abu Hanifah derived a lender's right to pursue the borrower until receiving his due, as al-Qurtubi noted.

### Can a disbeliever's good deed be praised?

Some in this verse are praised for trustworthiness. If the trustworthy are those among the People of the Book who had embraced Islam, the praise poses no problem. But if they are People of the Book in the absolute sense, including non-Muslims, a question arises: when no act of a kāfir, a disbeliever, is acceptable, why praise it?

The answer separates acceptability from praise. An action's acceptance with Allah is one question, whether observers praise it is another. Praise does not produce acceptance. Good in a disbeliever still counts as good to a degree: reputation in the mortal world and reduced punishment in the Hereafter benefit him. Prejudice and short-sightedness are rejected, and an adversary's excellence receives open-hearted appreciation.

The inward reading agrees that moral quality varies beneath a shared religious failure. Trust of religion, amāna of dīn, may be broken while proper human interactions are respected. Sincere dealing earns no reward for a disbeliever but can lessen punishment, since detailed laws hold disbelievers accountable. Lesser sin means less suffering than the worst offenders, though punishment remains perpetual. Judgment is not theirs, and the words about Gentiles settle nothing.

The positive picture is completed by a description of chosen servants. Being chosen to represent God's religion is not based on belonging to a certain community. The recipient is one selected for spiritual virtues, attached to God and fearing Him until He becomes his Keeper and Lord in the true sense. One who enters a covenant with his Creator cannot go back. Those who fulfil trusts and keep promises are showered with blessings, while the careless in trusts and promises are of no value in God's eyes.

### In short

- A Qintar returned by ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and a dinar denied by Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf mark the two kinds of trustee.
- "No accountability toward the Gentiles" is a lie invented against Allah, and it does not cover even a dhimmī's chickens once jizyah is paid.
- A disbeliever's honesty can be praised without being accepted, earning reputation here and lighter punishment there.

<<<VERSE 3:76>>>
The short verse answers with the opposite portrait: "Absolutely! Those who honour their trusts and shun evil—surely Allah loves those who are mindful ˹of Him˺". Yes, there is duty toward the Gentiles. And yes, covenant-keeping, not community membership, is what Allah loves.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Absolutely! Those who honour their trusts"**: Nay, duty in this regard is incumbent. A covenant made personally, or God's covenant itself, is fulfilled by restoring trusts and other obligations. Allah's chosen keeper honours pledges made with his Lord as well as pledges made with people.

**"and shun evil"**: God-fearing protects pledges from treachery and reversal. Disobedience is refrained from, obedience performed, and Allah's promise to believe in Him and His Messenger is kept alongside promises to honour human trusts. Instructions are fulfilled and prohibitions avoided.

**"surely Allah loves those who are mindful ˹of Him˺"**: Allah loves the Muttaqin, the pious. "He loves them" means He will reward them. The overt noun al-muttaqīn, the God-fearing, replaces the pronoun.

### The covenant meant, and the Sunnah's constant stress

Fulfilment here first targets the covenant Allah took from the People of the Book to believe in Muhammad ﷺ when he was sent, a covenant also taken from all prophets and their nations. Avoiding prohibitions, obeying Him and adhering to the Shari'ah sent with the Final Messenger ﷺ, master of all mankind, fulfil it. Qur'an and Sunnah stress fulfilled commitments again and again.

### Who is loved, and who is shamed

Those who hold to the covenant deserve to be united in friendship, honour and the garden. Those who are in error deserve aversion, ignominy and shame. The loved person is chosen for spiritual virtues. A servant attached to God and fearing Him can have Him as Keeper and Lord in the fullest sense of those words. God's chosen servants fulfil their trusts and do not break their promises, and people such as these are showered with God's blessings.

### In short

- Yes: covenant-keeping and God-fearing are the loved qualities, against the claim of no duty.
- The pledge chiefly meant is the covenant to believe in Muhammad ﷺ, alongside every trust between people.
- Covenant-keepers win friendship, honour and the garden, while promise-breakers earn aversion and shame.

<<<VERSE 3:77>>>
The warning against covenant-breaking is total: "Indeed, those who trade Allah’s covenant and their oaths for a fleeting gain will have no share in the Hereafter. Allah will neither speak to them, nor look at them, nor purify them on the Day of Judgment. And they will suffer a painful punishment". Whoever prefers the small things of a short life to fulfilling what he promised Allah sells the Hereafter at the cheapest price in the passage.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, those who trade Allah’s covenant and their oaths for a fleeting gain"**: a small worldly price is purchased at the cost of Allah's covenant and oaths. Distortion of the Prophet's descriptions and God's Torah covenant, together with false witness and false selling oaths, instantiate the trade. God's name is invoked mendaciously.

**"will have no share in the Hereafter"**: they shall have no portion in Paradise, no share or part in the Hereafter's rewards. Small gain in their living has consumed it.

**"Allah will neither speak to them, nor look at them, nor purify them on the Day of Judgment"**: no kind or nice speech, no merciful look, and no purification from sins and impurities await them. Jewish status brings no exoneration or peace of mind. Wrath is the mode in which He addresses them, and their order is toward Fire.

**"And they will suffer a painful punishment"**: painful doom awaits them, severe pain spreading into their hearts.

### The five warnings and the right of another servant

An ʿahd, pledge or covenant, is what is settled between parties after mutual discussion and binding on both. A waʿdah, promise, issues from one side only. So ʿahd is bilateral and waʿdah unilateral, and breaking a bilateral covenant draws the heavier charge.

Five warnings are counted against those who break their solemn pledge. They will have no share, no lot, in the blessings of heaven. Allah will not speak to them with glad tidings. Allah will not look at them mercifully on the Day of Doom. Their sins against a servant's right will not simply be forgiven: breach of trust wastes another human being's due, and God does not forgive what a human being owes to a human being. This is ḥaqq al-ʿabd, the right of a servant of Allah. A grievous punishment awaits them.

A hadith scales the point down: false-oath usurpation of a Muslim's right makes Hell compulsory even over an insignificant green tree bough. Muslim reports it.

### The hadiths gathered under this verse

Abu Dharr's report in Imam Ahmad says:

> "There are three persons whom Allah will not speak to, look at on the Day of Resurrection or purify, and they shall taste a painful torment. I said, 'O Messenger of Allah! Who are they, may they gain failure and loss?' He said, repeating this statement thrice, 'The Musbil (man whose clothes reach below the ankles), he who swears while lying so as to sell his merchandize and the one who gives charity and reminds people of it.'"

Muslim and collectors of the Sunan also record it.

ʿAdi bin ʿAmirah Al-Kindi tells of a land dispute in Ahmad involving Imru' Al-Qays bin ʿAbis of Kindah and a man from Hadramut. With no evidence from the Hadrami, an oath was required from Imru'. The Hadrami warned that swearing would lose his land by the Lord of the Ka'bah. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Whoever swears while lying to acquire the property of others, will meet Allah while He is angry with him." Raja' adds that the verse was then recited. Imru' asked what forfeiting gained. "Paradise" was the answer, so he witnessed that all the land was yielded. An-Nasa'i also records it. Accounts also attach the verse's revelation to ʿAbdan Ibn al-Ashuʿ and Imra' al-Qays and their dispute, as well as to the Jews.

ʿAbdullah reports another saying in Imam Ahmad: "Whoever takes a false oath to deprive a Muslim of his property will meet Allah while He is angry with him." Al-Ash'ath recognised his own land dispute with a Jewish man in it. He had no evidence. The Jew was asked to swear. He predicted an immediate false oath and lost property. Revelation followed. The Two Sahihs record the hadith. In a fuller report, al-Ashʿath ibn Qays heard Abu Abd al-Rahman relating this and said, "This verse was revealed about me." Bukhari narrates it from Abdan, from Abu Hamzah, from al-A'mash.

Abu Hurayrah reports in Imam Ahmad three more people Allah shall not speak to, look at, or purify on Resurrection Day, with painful torment: a man who withholds surplus water from a wayfarer, a man who swears falsely after ʿAsr prayer to complete a sale, and a man who pledges allegiance to an Imam and obeys only when given something. Abu Dawud and At-Tirmidhi record it too, and At-Tirmidhi graded it Hasan Sahih.

### A life that chooses the world at every fork

On embracing the faith, a person enters a covenant with God that he will obey Him and fulfil all the responsibilities God's law imposes, living among human beings under self-constraint. Such a life can be lived only by one who has risen above worldly interests. Whoever overlooks the divine pact whenever his self suffers or his interests are endangered barters the Hereafter at a paltry price. At every choice between world and Hereafter he prefers the world. How can one who values the Hereafter so little deserve God's mercies there?

Inwardly, caprice, hawāhum, wins over final outcome, ʿuqbāhum, and desires, munāhum, win over harmonious agreement with their Protector, mawlāhum. Present pleasure has cost both abodes and left them apart from the Real. Worldly pleasure, ḥaẓẓ, brought tribulations they fail to recognise, and their punishment is made to remain beyond time's count.

### In short

- Selling the covenant and oaths for a small gain forfeits any share in the Hereafter and draws silence, averted mercy, no purification and painful torment.
- False oaths over property stand at the centre of the hadiths, from Imru' al-Qays's land case to al-Ashʿath's disputed plot, and even a green bough counts.
- The habit beneath it is constant: at every fork the world is preferred, and caprice is chosen over the final outcome.
