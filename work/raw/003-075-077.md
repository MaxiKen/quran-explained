<<<VERSE 3:75>>>
The verse distinguishes between individuals: “There are some among the People of the Book who, if entrusted with a stack of gold, will readily return it” (3:75). Others will not return even a single coin unless the owner keeps demanding it. Their excuse that Arabs are not protected by their rules is a lie against Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**“There are some among the People of the Book”** means the verse is not describing every Jew or Christian. It identifies different kinds of people among them, including some who are trustworthy and some who are not.

**“who, if entrusted with a stack of gold, will readily return it”** describes a person who faithfully returns a large trust. One report identifies ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his companions as an example. A man entrusted Ibn Salām with 1200 plates of gold, and he returned them. His conduct is praised even though the discussion distinguishes worldly trustworthiness from acceptance of faith.

Praising a good deed does not necessarily mean that the person has received salvation. A good action can earn a good reputation in this world and may reduce punishment, even if the person is a kāfir, a disbeliever who has not accepted faith. This verse shows that Islam can recognize a virtue in someone outside the Muslim community without treating every aspect of that person’s belief as correct.

**“Yet there are others who, if entrusted with a single coin, will not repay it unless you constantly demand it”** describes a different kind of person. A report identifies Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf as one who refused to return a dinar entrusted to him by a man from Quraysh. The owner had to remain with him and insist on receiving what was owed.

**“This is because they say, ‘We are not accountable for ˹exploiting˺ the Gentiles’”** reports the excuse some used to justify taking the property of Arabs. They claimed that their religion did not hold them accountable for cheating people outside their community. This is a false claim, not a permission Allah gave them.

**“And ˹so˺ they attribute lies to Allah knowingly”** means they invent a religious justification for dishonesty while knowing it is false. Allah does not allow a person to take property without right merely because the owner belongs to another community.

Abdur-Razzaq records an exchange in which a man asked Ibn ʿAbbās about taking chickens and sheep from non-Muslims under Muslim protection during a battle. The man said they believed there was no sin in taking the property. Ibn ʿAbbās answered that this was the same false claim the People of the Book made. If people pay the jizyah, their property remains protected. It may not be taken unless they willingly give it.

The verse also supports the right of a creditor to keep pursuing a borrower until the debt is repaid. Abū Ḥanīfah derived this ruling from the words about having to keep standing over a person to recover what is owed. The wording is about insisting on a rightful claim, not permission to harass someone who has already paid.

### What is the difference between praising a person and approving their faith?

A person outside Islam may possess a real virtue, such as returning a trust. Recognizing that virtue is not the same as declaring every belief correct or promising salvation. The verse praises honest conduct while warning against using religious difference as an excuse for theft. It rejects prejudice in both directions: neither should a good deed be denied simply because of the person, nor should a false religious claim make dishonesty acceptable.

### In short

- Some People of the Book are trustworthy, while others cheat and refuse to return property.
- The verse condemns the claim that Arabs or Gentiles may be exploited without sin.
- Jizyah does not make a person’s property available to others.

<<<VERSE 3:76>>>
The verse corrects the excuse: “Those who honour their trusts and shun evil—surely Allah loves those who are mindful ˹of Him˺” (3:76). A person is accountable for promises and trusts, and Allah loves those who keep them and avoid wrongdoing.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Absolutely!”** rejects the claim that people may cheat those outside their community. There is a duty to return what belongs to another person.

**“Those who honour their trusts”** means those who fulfil a covenant with Allah and keep agreements with other people. A pledge to Allah includes faith in Him and His messenger, Muhammad ﷺ. A trust between people includes returning property and fulfilling what was promised.

**“and shun evil”** means being mindful of Allah by avoiding betrayal and other prohibitions. A person should not treat a promise as optional when it becomes inconvenient. The Sharīʿah sent with Muhammad ﷺ requires both faith and trustworthy conduct.

**“surely Allah loves those who are mindful ˹of Him˺”** means Allah rewards those who keep their commitments and avoid sin. The muttaqīn, those mindful of Allah, fear violating a trust and act with obedience.

The verse says that a promise made by one person and a covenant binding both parties require honesty. This duty is reinforced by the Prophet’s Sunnah and the Sharīʿah. The next verse warns of the serious outcome for those who trade Allah’s covenant and their oaths for a small worldly gain.

### In short

- A trust and a pledge must be honoured.
- Taqwā includes avoiding betrayal and fulfilling what is owed to others.
- Allah loves the muttaqīn who keep their commitments.

<<<VERSE 3:77>>>
The verse warns of those “who trade Allah’s covenant and their oaths for a fleeting gain” (3:77). They sacrifice their obligations for a small worldly benefit and will have no share in the Hereafter. Allah will not speak to them kindly, look upon them with mercy, or purify them, and they will face painful punishment.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Indeed, those who trade Allah’s covenant and their oaths”** means those who break what they have pledged to Allah and use oaths falsely to obtain another person’s property. The covenant includes accepting the truth and following the messenger whose description they know from the Torah and other scriptures. An oath is not a tool for turning a false claim into a right. The verse says that such a person has no lot, no share, in the Hereafter.

One explanation says the covenant is an agreement made between parties that both must honour. It differs from a promise made by only one side. Both have moral weight, but the verse especially condemns breaking solemn commitments and invoking Allah’s name to deceive.

**“for a fleeting gain”** means a small benefit in this short life, such as a piece of land, goods in a sale, or property obtained through a false oath. The gain may appear valuable to the one taking it, but it is temporary compared with what is lost in the Hereafter.

**“will have no share in the Hereafter”** means that they will receive none of the reward prepared for the righteous. They have chosen worldly advantage over their final outcome, ʿuqbāhum. Their oath may win a dispute in the eyes of people, but it cannot change what Allah knows.

**“Allah will neither speak to them”** means He will not address them with words of kindness or glad tidings on the Day of Judgment. This is a sign of His anger toward their dishonesty.

**“nor look at them”** means He will not look upon them with mercy. They will stand before Him without the favor they hoped their worldly gain would secure.

**“nor purify them on the Day of Judgment”** means He will not cleanse them of their sins or make them worthy of the Garden. One explanation says that violating the rights of another person is especially serious because Allah does not simply forgive what one person owes another without justice being done.

**“And they will suffer a painful punishment”** completes the warning. The five consequences are loss of the Hereafter’s reward, no kind speech from Allah, no merciful regard, no purification, and painful punishment.

### Hadith warnings about false oaths and broken trusts

A report from Abū Dharr says there are three kinds of people whom Allah will not speak to, look at with mercy, or purify on the Day of Resurrection, and who will suffer a painful punishment. They are a man whose garment hangs below his ankles, a person who swears falsely to sell merchandise, and someone who gives charity but reminds the recipient of it. Aḥmad recorded the report, and Muslim and the Sunan compilers also transmitted it.

Another report concerns a dispute over land between Imruʾ al-Qays bin ʿĀbis, a man from Kindah, and a man from Hadramut. The Prophet ﷺ asked the claimant from Ḥaḍramawt for evidence, but he had none. Imruʾ al-Qays was then asked to swear. The other man warned that, by the Lord of the Kaʿbah, a false oath would cost him his land. The Prophet said that whoever swears falsely to take another person’s property will meet Allah while Allah is angry with him. Imruʾ al-Qays asked what he would gain by giving up the dispute. The Prophet answered that he would gain Paradise, and Imruʾ al-Qays relinquished the land. An-Nasāʾī recorded this report.

A report from ʿAbd Allāh, known as Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, says that whoever takes a Muslim’s property through a false oath will meet Allah while He is angry with him. Al-Ashʿath said that the verse was revealed concerning his own dispute with a Jewish man over land. He complained to the Prophet ﷺ, who asked whether he had evidence. He did not. The other man was asked to swear, and al-Ashʿath feared that a false oath would make him lose his property. The Two Sahihs recorded the report.

Abū Hurayrah reports another warning about three kinds of people. One refuses a wayfarer some of the water he has. Another swears falsely after the afternoon prayer to complete a sale. The third gives allegiance to a Muslim ruler but only fulfils the pledge when given something in return. Abū Dāwūd and At-Tirmidhī recorded the report, and At-Tirmidhī graded it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ.

These reports show that the verse is not limited to disputes between religious communities. The Prophet’s Sunnah also warns against these actions. False oaths, dishonest sales, withholding basic help, and conditional loyalty all violate obligations. The warning applies whenever a person uses Allah’s name to take what is not rightfully theirs.

### Why is a small gain called a trade?

The person exchanges a lasting share in the Hereafter for a temporary benefit. The exchange is deeply unequal. A piece of land or profit from a sale cannot compensate for losing Allah’s mercy and facing punishment. A truthful person may even give up an immediate advantage, as Imruʾ al-Qays did, to preserve a claim to the Garden.

### In short

- A false oath cannot make another person’s property lawful.
- Allah warns of five consequences for those who trade His covenant and their oaths for gain.
- Hadith reports apply the warning to dishonest sales, land disputes, charity, and broken pledges.
