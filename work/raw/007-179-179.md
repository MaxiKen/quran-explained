### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We have destined many jinn and humans for Hell."** gives the measure of the loss before the verse sets out its cause. The words mean that Allah made a share in the Fire for a great number of the jinn and of humankind. The reports read it in this way. They were set out for the Fire by the sort of deeds that its people do. The work of the creation was known to Allah before any of it existed. A lot of the jinn and of humankind have been made for Hell, since the means of guidance were given to them in full and they did not use them to seek it.

**"They have hearts they do not understand with, eyes they do not see with, and ears they do not hear with."** states where the refusal led. The senses were made for them as a means of gaining guidance, and no benefit was taken from them.

Other passages of the Book speak of other groups in the same way. Of a former people it is said that hearing, sight and hearts were assigned to them, and that none of it availed them, since they used to deny the signs of Allah (46:26). Of the hypocrites it is said that they are deaf, dumb and blind, and so they do not return to the right path (2:18). Of the disbelievers it is said that they are deaf, dumb and blind, and so they do not understand (2:171).

The reports are careful with words of that kind. Such people are not deaf, dumb or blind in their bodies. The loss falls on them in relation to the guidance, and in that respect alone. One passage states that if Allah had known of any good in them, He would have made them listen, and that even then they would have turned away in aversion (8:23). Another states that it is not the eyes which grow blind, but the hearts which are in the breasts (22:46). Another states that when a person turns away blindly from the remembrance of the Most Gracious, a devil is appointed as his intimate companion. Such a companion holds him back from the path while he thinks that he is guided aright (43:36-37).

**"They are like cattle."** gives the likeness for a people with three losses. They do not take the truth in with their hearing, they do not grasp it with their understanding, and they do not find the guidance with their sight. Grazing cattle take no benefit from their senses beyond what sustains their life in this world, and the people described here stand in the same place.

One passage sets out the likeness as a picture. The example of those who disbelieve is that of a man who shouts at animals that hear nothing but a call and a cry (2:171). When such people are called to the faith, they are like cattle that hear the voice of their shepherd and cannot make out what he is saying.

**"In fact, they are even less guided!"** takes the likeness a step further. Cattle still answer the call of their shepherd, though they do not understand his words, and the people described here do not answer at all. Cattle also fulfil the purpose and the service for which they were made. The disbeliever was made to worship Allah alone, in tawhid, and he disbelieved in Allah and joined others with Him in worship. On that measure, the people who obey Allah are more honourable than some of the angels, while cattle are better than the people who disbelieve in Him.

**"Such ˹people˺ are ˹entirely˺ heedless"** names their condition. They are neglectful of the matter of the world to come and they deny it. They are oblivious to faith in Allah and to the Last Day.

### The decree that stands behind the words

The words about Hell raise the matter of the decree, and the reports answer it in plain terms. Allah knew the work of every creature before the creature existed. He set that work down in writing and kept the record with Him, and the record was made fifty thousand years before the heavens and the earth were created.

Muslim recorded that 'Abdullah bin 'Amr narrated from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ a word on the same subject. The Messenger ﷺ spoke of the destination and the due measurement of the creation. Both were decided fifty thousand years before the heavens and the earth were created, and the throne of Allah was over the water at that time. Many hadiths speak on this subject, and the matter of Al-Qadar is of the highest importance. The verse is not the place to set out the whole of it, and the account leaves the question there.

### The question the words raise, and the answer

The words of the verse deny that such people have hearing, sight and understanding, and a question follows from that. In daily life the same people are not blind, deaf or insane. They see, hear and understand the things around them like anyone else. How is the denial to be squared with the plain fact?

The answer turns on the way the creation is arranged. Everything created carries an amount of sensibility that matches the need of its purpose. Nothing that is called inanimate is wholly without life. The stones and the earth were given so little of it that it can hardly be detected, since no duty of growth or movement was laid on them. Human reason itself asks for some kind of perception in the things called inanimate, since there must be a way of communication between the Creator and what He created. A lord who cannot communicate with his creatures for want of sensibility in them would not be their Lord at all. Allah does communicate with all that He created, the living and the lifeless, and that shows a kind of sensibility in the lifeless as well.

The plant kingdom comes next in the order. Its purpose needs growth and nourishment, and its share of perception is greater in degree and matches that need. The animal kingdom follows, and its form of life is more elaborate and more complex. Animals grow and are nourished, and beyond that they move about the earth, seek their food, guard themselves from danger and bring forth their young. The life given to them is greater and more visible than what came before. Their powers are still measured to their task, and they have as much as helps them feed themselves and defend against their enemies.

The human being comes last in the order, and his case is the most serious. To the functions already named he adds the duty of recognising his Creator and the Lord of the universe, of seeking His pleasure by following His commands, and of keeping away from what He dislikes. He is required to contemplate the creation, to use his reason to tell right from wrong, to turn from evil and to do good for the sake of his Lord. He has also been given wide scope to make his life more purposeful and his rank more elevated, and when he advances on that road he can rise above the ranks of the angels. He is answerable for his acts. Eternal peace is promised to him for good, and punishment and distress for evil. He has been trusted with the highest and most perfect degree of life, and the point of it is that he should be busied with what his life was made for. His powers of understanding, hearing and seeing are not meant to be spent on material things alone, the way the animals do.

On that footing the human faculties of understanding, hearing and seeing should work differently from the faculties of the animals. If a man makes no special use of them, he belongs among those who have eyes but do not see, ears but do not listen, a heart but no understanding. The special use of them is telling right from wrong, doing good and keeping away from evil. That is why the Book calls such people deaf, dumb and blind. Their physical ability was never in question. The Book itself affirms that they have knowledge of worldly things, in the verse which says that they know the outward side of this life while they are heedless of the world to come (30:7).

Another passage speaks of the destruction that came on the people of 'Ad and of Thamud, and says of them that they could see clearly (29:38). Those peoples were drawn in by the influences of Satan and went into the evil deeds that brought them to their ruin. They kept the use of their powers within the narrow range of worldly gain, serving the needs of the body, and they stayed heedless of what the spirit demands. A people may make great advances in science and technology. They may conquer space and explore the moon and the other planets, fill the sky with their satellites and tame the forces of nature for their many needs. All of it serves the physical and material life and goes no further to the peace of the spirit. The Book calls such people deaf, dumb and blind because what they heard, saw and understood belonged to this world alone.

The verse closes its picture with the cattle, who put all their effort into the service of their bodily desires, and then adds that such people are much more misled. They are far more unwise than the cattle. An animal is not bound by the laws of the Shari'ah, carries no reward and no punishment for its acts, and is required only to serve the needs of the body, which it does well. A human being is answerable for everything he does, and he will be rewarded or punished according to what he has done. If a man keeps his effort to the material gains of this world and makes no proper use of his faculties, he is less prudent and less wise than the animals.

### The reports on the heart, the eye and the ear

The reports attach one word to each faculty in the verse. The hearts do not understand the truth, the eyes do not see the truth, and the ears do not hear the truth. The eyes do not perceive the proofs of the power of Allah with a perception that carries reflection with it. The ears do not hear the signs or the admonitions in a way that leads to thought and to heed.

The people described are like cattle in their failure to understand, to perceive and to listen, and they are further astray than cattle. Cattle seek what benefits them and keep away from what harms them. Those who are described here walk towards the Fire out of nothing but obstinacy. They are the heedless, the ones who are oblivious to the matter of the Hereafter and who deny it.

One account puts the matter of the making first. A great number of people from humankind and from the jinn were made for the Hellfire, because of the knowledge that they would do the deeds of the people of Hell. Their hearts fail them on the question of benefit and harm, and they cannot tell which of the two lies in a thing. Their eyes are turned away from the signs of Allah, both in their own selves and upon the horizons, and no lesson is taken from what is seen. Their ears are closed to the verses of Allah, and no reflection follows what is heard. Such people are like cattle in the smallness of their sense. The verse puts them further off the mark than the beasts are. Faith in Allah and in the Last Day never enters their mind.

### The truth each person must find

The truth is a thing a man has to discover for himself. Allah has given every person a heart, eyes and ears, and a man arrives at the truth by using them. The one who does not use them will be unable to find the truth, however near it stands. The discovery is the conscious and deliberate act of the man himself. Only the one who has kept the doors of his heart open for it will understand the truth. Only the one whose eyes are not covered with veils of his own making will see it. Only the one who has not blocked his ears will hear its voice. People of that kind recognise the voice of the truth and give themselves up to it.

A man in the opposite case stays without sense, like the animals. The weightiest arguments cannot move him. A flash of the light of Allah appears before him and he cannot see it. A melody of the divine song reaches his ears and he is held back from hearing it. The truth is there for those who are awake. For a heedless man, no truth is truth.

### In short

- The words mean that Allah made a share in the Fire for a great number of the jinn and of humankind. Such people were set out for it by the sort of deeds its people do. The work of the creation was known to Allah before any of it existed, and He set it down in writing in a record kept with Him. That record was made fifty thousand years before the heavens and the earth were created. Muslim recorded from 'Abdullah bin 'Amr that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ spoke of the destination and the due measurement of the creation. Both were decided at that time, with the throne of Allah over the water.
- The hearts, eyes and ears of such people take no benefit from the guidance they were made for. The Book describes them as deaf, dumb and blind in relation to that guidance alone, as it does of the former people who denied the signs (46:26), the hypocrites (2:18) and the disbelievers who do not understand (2:171). The likeness of the cattle is then given for a people who do not take the truth in, do not grasp it and do not see the guidance. It is enough to say that the verse puts them further off than the cattle are, since cattle answer their shepherd and fulfil the purpose for which they were made. The disbeliever was made for tawhid and turned away from it.
- Everything created has been given an amount of sensibility that matches the need of its purpose. That share runs from the stones and the earth, whose sensibility can hardly be detected, through the plants and the animals, to the human being, who carries the duty of recognising his Creator and is answerable for his acts.
- If a man makes no special use of his understanding, hearing and seeing for telling right from wrong, he belongs among those who have eyes but do not see and ears but do not listen. The Book affirms that such people know the outward side of this life while they are heedless of the world to come (30:7). It also told of the people of 'Ad and of Thamud, who could see clearly and yet were destroyed (29:38). The cattle put all their effort into the service of their bodily desires, and the men described here are more misled than they are. An animal is not bound by the laws of the Shari'ah and carries no reward or punishment for its acts. A human being is answerable for everything he does.
- The truth is a discovery each man must make for himself with the heart, eyes and ears he has been given, and for a man who has gone heedless, no truth is truth.
