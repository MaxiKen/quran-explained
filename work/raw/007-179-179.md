<<<VERSE 7:179>>>
The previous verse traced guidance and loss back to Allah. This one describes those who end in loss: "Indeed, We have destined many jinn and humans for Hell." Their faculties are intact, yet unused: "They have hearts they do not understand with, eyes they do not see with, and ears they do not hear with." So the verse judges them: "They are like cattle. In fact, they are even less guided! Such ˹people˺ are ˹entirely˺ heedless". Many jinn and humans are bound for Hell. They have all the means of guidance, hearts, eyes and ears, but never use them to seek it. Had they used these faculties properly, they could have found the right path.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We have destined many jinn and humans for Hell."** Allah has set aside a share of the Fire for many jinn and humans, and Hell is their lot. He prepared them for it through their doing the deeds of its people. He made many of them for the Fire because He knew they would do the deeds of the people of Hell.

**"They have hearts they do not understand with,"** They do not use their hearts to grasp the truth, or to see what will benefit them and what will harm them.

**"eyes they do not see with,"** They do not see the proofs of Allah's power with the kind of sight that leads to reflection. They do not look at Allah's signs within themselves and on the horizons so as to learn from them.

**"and ears they do not hear with."** They do not listen to Allah's verses, signs and warnings in a way that leads them to think and take heed. They do not benefit from the senses Allah gave them as a means of guidance.

**"They are like cattle."** In failing to understand, see and listen, they are like grazing cattle, which use their senses only for what keeps them alive in this world. They neither hear nor understand the truth, nor see the guidance. Allah gives a similar example: "The example of the disbelievers ˹not responding to the Messenger’s warning˺ is like a flock not comprehending the calls and cries of the shepherd." (2:171). When such people are called to faith, they are like cattle that hear only their shepherd's voice without grasping what he says.

**"In fact, they are even less guided!"** They are further astray than cattle. Cattle at least answer their shepherd's call, even without understanding it. Cattle seek what benefits them and keep away from what harms them, while these people head for the Fire out of sheer obstinacy. Cattle also fulfil the purpose and service for which they were created. The disbeliever's purpose was the worship of Allah alone, in tawḥīd, yet he chose disbelief and gave Allah partners. Hence the obedient among people rank above some angels, whereas the disbelievers rank below cattle.

**"Such ˹people˺ are ˹entirely˺ heedless"**: oblivious to faith in Allah and the Last Day, and to the matter of the Hereafter, which they deny.

### Were they created for Hell?

When Allah willed to create His creation, He knew what they would do before they existed. Fifty thousand years before creating the heavens and the earth, He recorded all of it in a Book that is with Him. Muslim recorded from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that Allah fixed the destinies and measures of creation fifty thousand years before He created the heavens and the earth, when His Throne was over the water. Many other hadiths deal with this subject. The question of al-Qadar, the divine decree, is of the greatest importance, but this verse is not the place for a full discussion of it.

The verse also teaches that a person's final end is a divine secret, known to no one. Yet there are clear signs from which one may form a judgement. The people of Hell can be recognised by one mark: they do not use their faculties of sight, hearing and understanding as they should. Allah gave people these faculties so that they could gain knowledge and come to know their Lord. The people of Hell spend them on worthless aims. They are plainly heedless of the real aim of human life, which is to use one's abilities to seek guidance and earn eternal comfort and peace in the Hereafter.

### Deaf, dumb and blind, but only to guidance

Allah says something similar of earlier nations: "And We gave them hearing, sight, and intellect. But neither their hearing, sight, nor intellect were of any benefit to them whatsoever, since they persisted in denying Allah’s signs." (46:26). Of the hypocrites He says: "They are ˹wilfully˺ deaf, dumb, and blind, so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺" (2:18). And of the disbelievers: "˹They are wilfully˺ deaf, dumb and blind so they have no understanding" (2:171).

They are not actually deaf, dumb or blind, except where guidance is concerned. Allah says: "Had Allah known any goodness in them, He would have certainly made them hear. ˹But˺ even if He had made them hear, they would have surely turned away heedlessly" (8:23). And: "Indeed, it is not the eyes that are blind, but it is the hearts in the chests that grow blind" (22:46). The one who turns from remembrance of the Most Merciful is given a devil as a companion, who keeps him from the path while he thinks he is guided: "And whoever turns a blind eye to the Reminder of the Most Compassionate, We place at the disposal of each a devilish one as their close associate who will certainly hinder them from the ˹Right˺ Way while they think they are ˹rightly˺ guided" (43:36-37).

### If they can see and hear, why does the Qur'an deny it?

Someone may say that the Qur'an denies these people hearing, sight and understanding. Yet in real life they are not blind, deaf or mad. Like other people, they see, hear and understand the things around them. How can the two be reconciled?

The answer calls for a fresh look at the things and creatures around us. Everything created has as much awareness as its purpose requires. The things called inanimate are not wholly without awareness or life. Stones and soil, which have no task of growing or moving, have so little sensitivity that it can hardly be detected. Reason requires that even these have some kind of perception or life, since there must be some means of communication between the Creator and what He creates. A lord who could not communicate with what he made, because it had no awareness, would not be worthy of divinity. Allah does communicate with everything He has created, living or not, which shows that even lifeless things have some kind of awareness.

Plants come next. Their purpose calls for growth and nourishment, so they are given more perception, or life, matching their needs. Next comes the animal kingdom, where life is more developed and complex. Besides growing and feeding, animals must move from place to place, seek their food, protect themselves from danger and, above all, reproduce. So they are given more life, and more visibly, than the others. Yet they have only as much power as they need to feed themselves and guard against dangers and enemies.

Last of all comes the human being. Beyond all these functions, and above them, he has the duty of recognising his Creator, the Lord of the universe. He is to seek His pleasure by following His commands and avoiding what He dislikes. He must reflect on creation and use his reason to tell right from wrong, avoiding evil and doing good to please his Lord. He is also given vast scope to make his life more purposeful and raise his standing. As he progresses, he can rise higher than the angels. He is accountable for his deeds, promised eternal peace for good deeds and everlasting punishment for evil ones. So he has been entrusted with the highest and most perfect degree of life, to spend it on what suits the purpose of his life. He is not meant to waste his special faculties of understanding, hearing and sight on material things alone, as animals do.

So human understanding, hearing and sight should be distinct from those of animals. If a person does not use them for their special purpose, telling right from wrong, doing good and avoiding evil, he is rightly counted among those who have eyes but do not see, ears but do not hear, and hearts but do not understand. That is why the Holy Qur'an calls such people "deaf, dumb and blind".

### What did ʿĀd and Thamūd see?

The verse does not deny their physical ability to see, hear and understand. The Holy Qur'an even affirms their knowledge of worldly matters, in the verse quoted under 7:8 (30:7). Speaking of the destruction of the people of ʿĀd and of Thamūd, it says: "Satan made their ˹evil˺ deeds appealing to them, hindering them from the ˹Right˺ Way, although they were capable of reasoning" (29:38).

Lured by satanic influences, ʿĀd and Thamūd threw themselves into the evil deeds that destroyed them. They kept their abilities for base worldly gains, such as serving their bodily needs, and ignored the demands of the spirit. People may make great advances in science and technology. They may conquer space, explore the moon and other planets, fill the sky with sophisticated satellites and harness the forces of nature for their many needs. Yet all of this only serves their physical, material life. It does not reach beyond, to the search for spiritual peace and comfort.

Such achievements of science and technology serve the body alone, and in this respect do not set a person apart from an animal. That is why the Holy Qur'an calls such people deaf, dumb and blind. They heard, saw and understood only what belonged to this world. They did not put these faculties to the use that could lead them to eternal success and peace.

### Why are they worse than cattle?

The verse likens them to cattle because they spend all their effort on their bodily desires. It then adds that they are even further astray, far less wise than cattle. Animals are not bound by the laws of the Sharīʿah and receive no reward or punishment for what they do. They are required only to meet their physical needs, and they do so efficiently. Human beings, by contrast, are answerable for everything they do in this world and will be rewarded or punished accordingly. If they confine their efforts to worldly gain and fail to use their faculties properly, they are certainly more foolish than animals.

### Who finds the truth?

Truth is something each person must discover for himself. Allah has given everyone a heart, which is the mind, along with eyes and ears, and a person reaches the truth by using them. Whoever does not use them will never find the truth, however close it may be. Discovering the truth is each person's own conscious and deliberate act.

Only someone who keeps the doors of his heart open will understand the truth. Only someone who has not covered his eyes with artificial veils will see it. Only someone who has not stopped his ears will hear its voice. Such people recognise the voice of truth and surrender to it. Whoever is the opposite will stay mindless like the animals, and even the weightiest arguments will not move him. Flashes of Allah's brilliant light will appear before him, but he will not see them. The melody of the divine song will reach his ears, but he will be kept from hearing it. The truth is there for those who are awake. For the heedless, no truth is the Truth.

### In short

- Many jinn and humans are bound for Hell, as Allah knew and wrote long before creation, because they leave their hearts, eyes and ears unused for guidance.
- They are not physically blind or deaf. Like ʿĀd and Thamūd, they use their faculties only for worldly ends, which is no better than an animal's use.
- Humans hold the highest degree of life and are accountable under the Sharīʿah, so wasting their faculties puts them below cattle.
- Truth must be sought with an open heart, eyes and ears, and it is found only by those who are awake.
