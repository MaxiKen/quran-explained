<<<VERSE 4:142>>>
The counterfeit worship: "Surely the hypocrites seek to deceive Allah, but He outwits them. When they stand up for prayer, they do it half-heartedly only to be seen by people" (4:142). The passage now draws the hypocrite's portrait from the inside, beginning with the deception that cannot work.

### Phrase by phrase

**"the hypocrites seek to deceive Allah"** cannot succeed, for there is no doubt that Allah is never deceived, having perfect knowledge of the secrets and what the hearts conceal. The attempt itself comes of their ignorance, scarce knowledge and weak minds. Once successful in deceiving people, with Islamic law as a cover of safety, they imagine a like standing with Allah on the Day of Resurrection. On that Day they will swear to Him they were on the path of righteousness, thinking the oath will benefit them. One gloss names them again, ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy and his followers, disbelieving in Him and opposing Him in secret while thinking they deceive Him. The beginning of sūrat al-Baqarah has said it: "They think to deceive Allah and those who believe" (2:9).

**"but He outwits them"** means the luring further into injustice and misguidance, and the prevention of the truth in this life and on the Day of Resurrection. The Book shows the scene: "On the Day when the hypocrites men and women will say to the believers: Wait for us! Let us get something from your light! It will be said: Go back to your rear! Then seek a light!" (57:13), until "And worst indeed is that destination" (57:15). A hadith states it briefly:

> Whoever wants to be heard of, Allah will make him heard of, and whoever wants to be seen, Allah will show him.

**"they do it half-heartedly only to be seen by people"** describes their conduct with the most honoured, best and righteous act of worship, the ṣalāh. They stand in laziness because they neither truly intend it nor believe in it, have no humility in it and do not understand it, and that is the outward attitude alone. The heart behind it has no sincerity, the showing off aimed at closeness to the watchers. From the prayers that can be hidden, the ʿIshāʾ and the Dawn, both prayed in darkness, they are often away. In the Two Ṣaḥīḥs, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said of them:

> The heaviest prayers on the hypocrites are the ʿIshāʾ and Dawn prayers. If they know their rewards, they will attend them even if they have to crawl. I was about to order someone to pronounce the adhan for the prayer, then order someone to lead the prayer for the people, then order some men to collect fire-wood, then I would burn the houses around men who did not attend the compulsory congregational prayer.

Another narration adds the pull of the table: a meat-covered bone, or a pair of rib-morsels, would have brought any of them in. It adds, too, that only the women and children in the houses kept the homes from being burned around their occupants.

**"hardly remembering Allah at all"** means that during the prayer they feel no humbleness and pay no attention to what they recite, inattentive and jesting, avoiding the good they were meant to receive. Imām Mālik reported, through al-ʿAlāʾ ibn ʿAbdur-Raḥmān, from Anas ibn Mālik, the word of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ:

> This is the prayer of the hypocrite, this is the prayer of the hypocrite, this is the prayer of the hypocrite. He sits watching the sun until when it goes down between the two horns of the devil, he stands up and pecks out four rakʿahs, without remembering Allah during them except little.

Muslim, at-Tirmidhī and an-Nasāʾī also recorded it, and at-Tirmidhī called it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. One note draws the line of the censure. The laziness rebuked here is doctrinal laziness, the sloth toward the articles of faith born of lacking true conviction. Laziness despite correct belief stands outside the censure, and if it comes of some excuse, sickness, fatigue or sleepiness, it is not even blameworthy. Without excuse, it is.

The inward reading sees the trick and its mirage. Hypocritical trickery displays conformity outwardly on the path, while the creed inwardly clings to association. The trickery of the Real toward them is the imagined salvation they count as entitlement. When the covering lifts, the certainty arrives: what they took for drink, sharāb, was a mirage, sarāb, for "there will appear to them from Allah that which they had never reckoned" (39:47). And the sign of hypocrisy stands exposed: energy when one can be seen by others, resolve lost when the opportunity to be seen passes.

### The religion beneath

The teaching beneath names the religion of such people. Those who have not surrendered themselves to Allah succumb to worldly interests, associating with anyone, religious or irreligious, by whom those interests are served. Their words and deeds, however religious in appearance, aim not at pleasing Allah but at showing others they too are believers, for opportunism is their real religion, though they are at pains to appear God-worshipping.

### In short

- The deception of Allah is attempted under the cover of the law and answered by the lifting of every cover on the Day.
- The heaviest prayers on the hypocrites are those prayed in darkness, and the burning of houses was only withheld for the women and children inside.
- The hypocrite's prayer is pecked out at the sun's setting, with no remembrance in it.
- The laziness rebuked is doctrinal, not the fatigue of the faithful, and the mirage awaits every entitlement imagined.

<<<VERSE 4:143>>>
The wavering: "belonging neither to these ˹believers˺ nor those ˹disbelievers˺" (4:143). The verse completes the portrait with the stance that belongs to neither camp.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Torn between belief and disbelief"** names the swaying. The hypocrites belong neither to the believers inwardly or outwardly, nor to the disbelievers inwardly or outwardly, keeping the believers outwardly and the disbelievers inwardly.

**"belonging neither to these ˹believers˺ nor those ˺disbelievers˺"** covers the fits of doubt, leaning toward these sometimes and toward those sometimes: "Whenever it flashes for them, they walk therein, and when darkness covers them, they stand still" (2:20). Mujāhid read the two parties as the Companions of Muḥammad ﷺ and the Jews. Ibn Jarīr recorded from Ibn ʿUmar that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> The example of the hypocrite is the example of the sheep wandering between two herds, sometimes she goes to one of them, and sometimes the other, confused over whom she should follow.

Muslim also recorded it. **"And whoever Allah leaves to stray, you will never find for them a way"** closes the verse. The word of the Book runs: for him you will find no walī, a guiding friend, to lead him to the right path (18:17), for whomsoever Allah sends astray, none can guide him. Those led astray from the paths of safety never find a guide to direct them or a saviour. There is none who can resist the decision of Allah. Of what He does He is never asked, while they all will be asked.

### The inward price

The inward reading adds the price of the wavering. The least worthy among created beings are those who remove the vest of servanthood and have not found the way to true freedom. They hold not even a sliver of precious nobility, nor an agreeable way of living, ʿīshatu ḥanīyya, in their heedlessness.

### In short

- The wavering heart keeps a foot in each camp and belongs to neither.
- The sheep between two herds is the standing image of it, and the light comes and goes for such people.
- Whom Allah sends astray has no guiding friend, and the vest of servanthood once removed leaves neither nobility nor ease.

<<<VERSE 4:144>>>
The proof offered: "O believers! Do not take disbelievers as allies instead of the believers. Would you like to give Allah solid proof against yourselves" (4:144). The command returns with the reason attached.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not take disbelievers as allies instead of the believers"** forbids the whole alliance, the friendship and association with the disbelievers, the advising of them, the intimacy with them, and the exposing of the believers' secrets to them. The Book has said it with the warning:

> Let not the believers take the disbelievers as friends instead of the believers.

No help from Allah comes to whoever does it, in any way, save under a feared danger, and Allah warns you against Himself, meaning His punishment.

**"Would you like to give Allah solid proof against yourselves"** asks the question that turns the choice sober. The manifest Sultan is the proof that warrants receiving the torment, as Ibn ʿAbbās said, for the word Sultan in the Qur'ān means proof, and his statement has an authentic chain. So also read ʿIkrimah with Mujāhid, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk with Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, as-Suddī, an-Naḍr ibn al-ʿArabī and Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī. One gloss reads the address over the outward believers, ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy and his followers, seeking power from the Jews, the proof becoming a clear reason for action against them.

### The gravest of sins in the precepts of love

The inward reading weighs the friendship above the other sins. The admonition was repeated and the command to separate from enemies confirmed, to strengthen the prohibition and end every excuse. The heading above carries its finding. The friendship was warned against over and above other oppositional acts because it involves a preference for something other than the object of adoration. Preferring another to the beloved is the gravest of sins in the precepts of love. When the heart becomes engaged with others in the place reserved for the Real, the outcome cannot but be bad, and the punishment promised is to be left to one's own devices and to what was chosen in siding with the disbelievers. What a wretched substitution. It is the same with all who remain apart from the Real: He leaves them with created beings, doubling the affliction by their remaining apart from the Real and remaining with created beings, and both are severe punishments.

### In short

- Alliance, advice, intimacy and exposed secrets are all inside the prohibition.
- The Sultan given is a proof, and by it the torment is warranted.
- The friendship is graver than other opposition, for it sets another beloved in the place of the Beloved, and the punishment is to be left with created beings.

<<<VERSE 4:145>>>
The depth: "Surely the hypocrites will be in the lowest depths of the Fire" (4:145). The sentence of the wavering is pronounced.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Surely the hypocrites will be in the lowest depths of the Fire"** sentences ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy and his followers, with every hypocrite after them, for their evil, betrayal and scheming against the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions. Ad-darak al-asfal, the lowest depth, means the bottom of the Fire, as Ibn ʿAbbās said. Other scholars held that the Fire has ever lower depths, just as Paradise has ever higher grades, the placing to occur on the Day of Resurrection, due to their tremendous kufr.

**"you will never find for them any helper"** names the abandonment. Ibn Jarīr recorded from ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd that they will be inside coffins of Fire that surround them, closed and sealed. Asked about the hypocrites, Ibn Masʿūd said that they will be placed in coffins made of fire and shut into them at the Fire's lowest depth, with no helper found to save them from the misery and the painful torment.

The inward reading reads the depth downward into the heart. The hypocrite is not one sincerely seeking protective trust, for īmān, being true to the trust, is what brings protection, and the one who holds true to it is saved by it from the Fire. That which causes the falling to the lowest level cannot be called faith. It is verified, it is said, by the words that Allah is the best of schemers (3:54), said again in the other place (8:30), His scheming above all scheming. Once the hypocrites' scheming with the believers stood exposed, their punishment ran more severe than that of the open disbelievers. Of the tradition that each meets Allah in the state he kept, it is said that He transfers them from the present state to one like it: lowest in being cut off today, lowest in the Fire tomorrow. The depth of their cutting off now is the name of īmān they bear while holding not even a sliver from Allah, the greatest of afflictions. Of the reason for the level, one saying is the company they kept here with the greatest name of Allah without respect, and another, the presence without propriety in what they said, and by this they were driven far away.

### In short

- The lowest depth is the bottom, and the coffins of fire are closed and sealed on their occupants.
- What saves from the Fire is the trust held true, and the name of faith without a sliver of it is the greatest affliction.
- Company with the greatest name, kept without respect or propriety, ends in the farthest removal.

<<<VERSE 4:146>>>
The door out: "except those who repent, mend their ways, hold fast to Allah, and are sincere in their devotion to Allah; they will be with the believers" (4:146). After the sentence comes the exemption, for the sense of fear generated by an impending punishment usually becomes the reason for early repentance in a good-natured person.

### Phrase by phrase

**"except those who repent"** opens the exemption. Whoever among the hypocrites repents in this life finds the repentance and the sorrow accepted, if it is sincere and followed by righteous deeds, all the while depending on the Lord. The showing off is replaced with sincerity, so that the good deeds benefit, even if they were minute.

**"they will be with the believers"** places them, on the Day of Resurrection, among the company they once feigned, and **"Allah will grant the believers a great reward"** crowns the company.

The statement about making the faith pure for Allah carries the meaning of sincerity. A deed acceptable to Allah must be free of hypocrisy and exclusively for His sake and pleasure, and the jurists have given the meaning of the mukhlis, the sincere one, in one line:

> A sincere person is one who acts for Allah alone and does not like that people praise him for it. One rendering hears the holding fast as the holding of the belief in Allah's divine Oneness inwardly, and the purity as the purifying of that belief for Him alone.

### The preposition of accompaniment

The inward reading counts the conditions. Not all of them are ordinarily required to repent of a sin. For hypocrites renouncing hypocrisy they are necessary, because of the stubborn nature of their disbelief. For those who meet the conditions He said "with the believers," the preposition of accompaniment rather than of belonging. The allusion is to an imperfection of rank, even after the sincere amendment of the past. Regarding its meaning they have recited: apologies are easy, and what a difference between apologies and thankfulness. The repentance here means renouncing the hypocrisy, amending in the sincerity of faith, and holding fast to Allah by absolving the self of its own power and strength, bearing witness to the blessing of God in the guidance and the deliverance. The religion is made purely God's when the deliverance is regarded as His grace, not the earning of the newfound faith nor the credit of the renounced past.

### In short

- The exemption stands open in this life: repentance, amendment, reliance and purity replace the showing off with sincerity.
- The sincere one acts for Allah alone and dislikes the praise of people for it.
- The preposition of accompaniment keeps the rank imperfect, and the deliverance is grace, not earning.

<<<VERSE 4:147>>>
The question that clears the account: "Why should Allah punish you if you are grateful and faithful? Allah is ever Appreciative, All-Knowing" (4:147). The verse stands among those that evoke hope.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Why should Allah punish you if you are grateful and faithful?"** is a denial in the form of a question. Allah is too Rich to need anyone, and He only punishes the servants for their sins. He would not punish the thankful who correct their actions and believe inwardly in His divine Oneness, in Him and His Messenger.

**"Allah is ever Appreciative, All-Knowing"** gives the reason twice over. He appreciates those who appreciate Him, has knowledge of those whose hearts believe in Him, and gives them perfect reward.

### The two tokens

The inward reading unfolds the two tokens. He has appointed two things as protection from punishment, thankfulness, shukr, and belief, īmān, and they are simple and light qualities. Thankfulness is an assertion, a word said, and belief is a state. When the words and the state both please Him, He makes the way easy for the servant. Praise is sound only from one who is faithful, for it is an act of willing obedience, and the obedience of those without faith is not genuine. The belief meant is the belief of the end, for salvation belongs to those who at the end of the day believe, so that the meaning is: no abiding chastisement for the thankful in their present state who believe in the end. It is said that the thankful believer attests one thing above all: salvation comes through God, not through the thankfulness or the belief. Thankfulness, it is said, is the witnessing of the blessing from God, and belief the seeing of God within it, as if He said that whoever knows the blessing as from God should not let its witness cut him off from the Giver. The word rendered Appreciative, All-Knowing reads in the other form, Allāh shākirun ʿalīmun, and the rendering of the first reads shākiran ʿalīman. His thankfulness means the commending of the servant and the bearing of witness to him in what he does. The true sense of thankfulness is praise of the beautiful actor, the muḥsin, by the mention of his beautiful action, the iḥsān. The servant gives thanks to God by praising Him for His beautiful action toward him, which is His blessing, and the Lord gives thanks to the servant by praising him for his beautiful action, which is his willing obedience.

### In short

- Punishment answers sin alone, and gratitude with faith closes the account.
- Thankfulness is a word, belief a state, and both together make the way easy.
- Thankfulness sees the blessing, belief sees the Giver in it, and the Appreciative commends the beautiful act of the obedient.

<<<VERSE 4:148>>>
The exception of the wronged: "Allah does not like negative thoughts to be voiced" (4:148), "except by those who have been wronged. Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing". The verse permits one voice and discourages the rest.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah does not like negative thoughts to be voiced"** means, in the explanation of Ibn ʿAbbās, that no one should invoke Allah against anyone else unless wronged, in which case the invocation against the wrongdoer is allowed, though patience is better.

**"except by those who have been wronged"** draws the permission's line. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī commented that one should not invoke curses against whoever wronged him, but rather make the supplication:

> O Allah! Help me against him and take my right from him.

In another narration he allowed the wronged one to invoke without transgressing the limits. ʿAbdul-Karīm ibn Mālik al-Jazarī drew the line of retaliation: a curse may be answered with a curse, but a lie may not be answered with a lie, for "whoever takes revenge after he has suffered wrong, for such there is no way of blame against them" (42:40). Abū Dāwūd recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> Whatever words are uttered by those who curse each other, then he who started it will carry the burden thereof, unless the one who was wronged transgresses the limit.

The occasions set the verse in life. One record ties it to Abū Bakr, who was verbally abused by a man, and another to a man who sought hospitality with some people and, receiving no proper hospitality from them, complained, whereupon the verse came down giving him dispensation to complain. The permission granted here is bounded in the very next breath, for the following verse, 149, opens the higher door beyond it.

### The etiquette of correction

The teaching beneath sets the etiquette of correction. In life one comes to know one's shortcomings and faults through the people around one, but Allah disapproves of public criticism that defames the person. Everyone has the right to give counsel with the motive of reform, but the counselling should be done in private, not in public, and if something must be said publicly to check a vice from spreading, the wrongdoer should not be named, the reference kept in general terms. Day in and day out Allah overlooks people's crimes, and His servants too should follow that divine principle. The oppressed may tell of the wrong, yet patience and forgiveness remain better. The victim thereby shows he cares for the loss of the Hereafter above any loss here, the thought of the greater loss rendering the small one insignificant.

The inward reading deepens the etiquette. What the wronged one says about his wronger is not evil in the true sense, by the permission of God, yet letting the matter drop is the good thing, for the requital of an evil is an evil like it, and whoever pardons and reconciles has his reward with God (42:40). Whoever knows his Protector hears grows ashamed of free voice for the urgings of the lower self. The evil out loud, it is said, is what a person hears within himself when he thinks ill of others, and the elect take responsibility in their inward thoughts for what, heard from ordinary people, might be held against them. On the exception the readings divide: some say not even the wronged one, and others that the wronged one has the right to mention who intentionally wronged him. Those who do not prefer the praise of the Real over defaming fellow creatures are cheated of the present moment, and those who look at created beings with the eye of connection to the Real see servants of God and speak no ill of them. One man said to his companion that out of respect he would bear him the lowliest service, beyond what he would bear for his own son, and when such commitment stands between human beings, the servant's regard for refined behaviour toward his Protector is worthier still. Among ordinary people, the utterance of evil out loud is their description of Allah in ways for which they have no permission or divine sanction.

### In short

- The wronged may raise the voice, by supplication, complaint or equal retaliation, and never with a lie.
- The beginner of the cursing carries the burden, and patience remains better than all permission.
- Abū Bakr's abuse and the refused guest set the verse in life.
- Counsel goes private, public correction stays unnamed, and the elect answer even for thoughts.

<<<VERSE 4:149>>>
The higher door: "Whether you reveal or conceal a good or pardon an evil" (4:149), "surely Allah is Ever-Pardoning, Most Capable". The two verses together give a law to eradicate injustice and oppression from the world.

### The law of the two verses

This is no dictatory formulation of the usual worldly kind. It is a law of persuasion and awe. The victim of injustice may protest against the perpetrator or go to a court of law to seek redress, the basic demand of justice and a source of the eradication of crimes. Alongside the option stands the restriction of sūrat an-Naḥl: if a person inflicts injustice on you, you may retaliate for it, on condition that the retaliation not exceed the unjust aggression, for else it is you who turn unjust (16:126). An injustice, in short, is never returned by another injustice, and the retaliation to injustice, where made, runs through justice alone. With this course comes the instruction that revenge, though permissible, is not the better part, for observing ṣabr, patience, and forgiving is better.

The verse also teaches that the oppressed person who goes about complaining of his injustice is not counted under backbiting, ghibah, which is ḥarām, for the oppressor himself gave the victim the opening to complain. On the one hand, then, the Holy Qur'ān allows the option of even retaliation, and on the other, by teaching the oppressed high morals, forgiveness and forgetting, and by setting the great gain of the life-to-come before him, it persuades the sacrifice of a little of the legitimate right. To do good openly or keep it secret, or to overlook an evil deed, is better, for Allah is All-Forgiving, All-Powerful. Primarily the verse concerns the forgiving of injustice, but by mentioning the doing of good, openly or secretly, the hint is given that the overlooking is great, and whoever elects it becomes deserving of the mercy and forgiveness of Almighty Allah.

The ending carries its own lesson. Allah is powerful in the absolute sense, able to punish whomsoever He wills, yet He is All-Forgiving. A human being, who does not have much power or choice, may simply lack the capability of taking revenge, and for such a person overlooking and forgiving are far more desirable. The principle of the Qur'ān thus upholds justice by permitting equal retaliation and exhorts the aggrieved to forgive and forgo, and the result is described in the Book: "Good and evil are not equal. Repel evil with what is better, and your enemy will become like a close friend" (41:34). Court decisions and revenge do play a role in checking injustice, but the parties to a dispute are usually left saddled with long-term grievances and abhorrence that breed future disputes. The lesson in morality of the Qur'ān patches up the quarrelling parties instead, till even old enmities change into close and lasting friendships. From here the passage moves to verses 150, 151 and 152, where the verdict is given that whoever believes in Allah but not in His messengers, or believes in some and disbelieves in others, is no believer but an open disbeliever, with no road to salvation in the Hereafter.

### The praise of the Throne and the pardon

Among the attributes of Allah stands this one, that His servants are forgiven and pardoned by Him though He is able to punish, and the word of the heavens matches. It was reported that some of the angels who carry the Throne praise Him in the words:

> All praise is due to You for Your forbearing, even though You have perfect knowledge.

Others supplicate:

> All praise is due to You for Your forgiving, even though You have perfect ability to punish. An authentic hadith states:

> No charity shall ever decrease wealth, and Allah will only increase the honour of a servant who pardons, and he who is humble for Allah's sake, then Allah will elevate his grade.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whether you reveal or conceal a good"** reads two ways in the inward record. To show good is to take on the good manners found in the law, the ādāb of the sharīʿa, and to conceal it is to recognize the truth in the precepts of the deeper reality.

**"or pardon an evil"** is to adopt from Allah what He has entrusted, in the most beautiful ways of behaving.

**"surely Allah is Ever-Pardoning, Most Capable"** means He is Pardoning of your defects and Powerful in causing you to attain what you love and to recognize the true nature of what you seek. It is said that whoever shows good becomes an example for humankind in what he practises and proposes, from which others may be guided. It is said that whoever conceals it, content with only His knowing it, safeguards himself from the evils of affectation and trusts that whoever acts for Him will be seen and known by Him. Whoever pardons abandons what the lower self calls to, and Allah grants His pardon for what he does, holding also the power to afflict with what the oppressor is afflicted. This warns against heedlessness in witnessing the favour, and alerts to the seeking of refuge from the loss of protection, ʿiṣma, and from abandonment into discord and trial. The final counsel runs in threes. Well-doers are shown good outwardly. Those who spare you their evil receive loyalty and inward prayer. The badly-doing are pardoned with generosity and graciousness. The pardon found from Allah will match what you yourself do, for your sins are the more numerous. He has the power to give you in favour and blessings what vengeance can never attain.

### In short

- Protest and court are permitted, retaliation is hemmed by equality, and patience with forgiveness stands above both.
- The complaint of the oppressed is not backbiting, for the oppressor himself opened the door.
- The pardoner is honoured, the humble elevated, and the angels of the Throne praise the forbearing knowledge and the able forgiveness.
- Repelling evil with what is better turns an enemy into a close friend, and the old enmities of the pardoning become friendships that last.
