<<<VERSE 2:262>>>
The promise of multiplied reward is protected by how the giver treats the recipient: "Those who spend their wealth in the cause of Allah and do not follow their charity with reminders of their generosity or hurtful words" receive their reward from Allah. Giving for His pleasure means refusing to turn assistance into a claim over another person's dignity.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who spend their wealth in the cause of Allah"** They give in obedience and seek His pleasure. A report identifies 'Uthman Ibn 'Affan and 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf as examples connected with the verse's revelation. Their expenditure is presented as generosity directed towards Allah rather than a purchase of praise.

**"and do not follow their charity with reminders of their generosity or hurtful words"** Sadaqah, charity, must not become a tool for reproach. Reminders can be spoken or conveyed through actions. Harm includes treating a recipient as disgraced and revealing the assistance to people from whom the recipient would prefer to keep it private.

**"they will get their reward from their Lord,"** The return belongs with Allah, who Himself rewards the deed. It is not dependent on the recipient's applause, gratitude or continuing submission to the giver.

**"and there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve"** They need not fear the coming horrors of resurrection. Nor will they grieve for children, worldly adornments and pleasures left behind, because the bounty awaiting them is better. The words also express freedom from anxiety about the future and regret over the past.

### What is reported about the two gifts?

Al-Kalbi says the verse concerns 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf and 'Uthman ibn 'Affan. 'Abd al-Rahman brought four thousand silver coins to the Prophet ﷺ for distribution. He explained that he owned eight thousand and had retained half for himself and his family. He described the other half as a loan to his Lord.

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ prayed that Allah bless both what he had given and what he had retained. The report does not blame him for keeping provision for his family. It honours the sincere expenditure and the retained amount together.

'Uthman undertook to equip those lacking equipment for the Battle of Tabuk. The account describes one thousand camels supplied with their equipment and saddle blankets. He also gave a well he owned in charity.

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri says he saw the Prophet ﷺ lift his hands in supplication for 'Uthman. He asked Allah to be pleased with 'Uthman ibn 'Affan, declaring his own pleasure with him. His hands remained raised until dawn. This too is given as part of the report connecting the verse with 'Uthman's expenditure.

### Why are reminders so serious?

Muslim records from Abu Dharr a warning of the Prophet ﷺ about three kinds of people facing painful punishment at the Resurrection. Allah will not speak to them, look at them favourably or purify them. They include someone who continually reminds others of gifts, someone who lets clothing extend below the ankles, and someone who uses a false oath to sell merchandise.

The warning places reproachful giving among grave offences. A charitable act does not provide unlimited credit against subsequent humiliation of its recipient. The receiver's vulnerability is not permission to exercise power through repeated reminders.

Even a seemingly favourable remark can inflict injury. Announcing that one restored another person's affairs makes the recipient's private need part of a public story about the giver's importance. Genuine generosity refrains from both obvious insults and such self-advertisement.

### Where does the giver look for appreciation?

A person seeking worldly prestige naturally watches for recognition here. A person seeking Allah's reward has another measure. The gift is not regarded as a personal favour requiring repayment in compliments.

If recipients fail to respond graciously, the sincere giver does not use that failure to justify cruelty. Hope in Allah's complete reward makes human gratitude less controlling. If another request cannot be met, a gentle explanation is still possible. Awareness that Allah hears the words helps preserve their kindness.

### What does it mean to give without retaining control?

The inward reading asks how something can truly be given while the giver continues to claim authority and rights over it. Proclaiming one's good deed keeps attention fixed on personal achievement. Pointing it out to the recipient imposes a burden that the material gift was supposed to relieve.

Instead, the giver witnesses Allah's favour in enabling the generosity. The ability to help is itself a gift. This changes the direction of gratitude: rather than demand gratitude from the poor person, the giver recognises a reason to thank Allah.

The rule therefore protects both sides. The recipient is not made captive to a benefactor, and the benefactor is not held captive by the need to be admired.

### In short

- Give for Allah's pleasure without using assistance to reproach or humiliate.
- The reported gifts of 'Abd al-Rahman and 'Uthman illustrate generous expenditure.
- The reward is with Allah, not dependent on a recipient's appreciation.
- Remembering Allah's enabling favour frees the gift from a continuing claim of control.

<<<VERSE 2:263>>>
A gift is not good merely because money changes hands: "Kind words and forgiveness are better than charity followed by injury." When giving would be accompanied by contempt, a respectful response and patience are better. Allah has no need of the money, and His forbearance teaches the giver not to punish people for their weakness or discourtesy.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Kind words"** These are compassionate speech and an appropriate response to a request. They include supplication for a Muslim and speaking well of a brother in his absence. The person unable to give can explain gently rather than rebuff the petitioner.

**"and forgiveness"** This means overlooking wrong done through words or actions. A persistent or ill-mannered request can provoke anger, but the answer should not turn that irritation into humiliation.

**"are better than charity followed by injury."** Money followed by reproach or ridicule can cause more damage than benefit. A kind refusal preserves dignity where a contemptuous gift wounds it. The comparison particularly concerns voluntary sadaqah, charity, not a licence to evade obligatory duties.

**"And Allah is Self-Sufficient, Most Forbearing"** Allah needs neither the servants nor their voluntary expenditure. He pardons, forgives and delays punishment rather than immediately striking the person who gives harmfully. The opportunity to correct one's conduct should not be mistaken for approval of the harm.

### What if the request is unreasonable?

The explanation describes someone unable to give under difficult circumstances. An appropriate excuse is far better than giving unwillingly and then inflicting pain. Likewise, if a petitioner behaves rudely, overlooking the offence is better than treating assistance as an opportunity for revenge.

A person may be displeased at apparent ingratitude. Remembering Allah as Ghani, free of need, and Halim, forbearing, helps correct that reaction. The giver acts for personal good through obedience, not because Allah needs the gift. Forgiving and moving past discourtesy better reflects that purpose.

This does not require pretending that rude behaviour is pleasant. It requires refusing to make the petitioner's weakness an occasion for cruelty. The amount offered cannot make hurtful speech harmless.

### How can words themselves help?

A generous reply can make a believer happy even when financial help is unavailable. Prayer and honourable mention extend goodwill beyond the immediate exchange. Forgiveness removes a further burden instead of attaching one to a gift.

The preceding warning, recorded by Muslim from Abu Dharr, shows the danger of habitually reproaching people for one's generosity. Here the positive alternative is made plain: preserve kindness in speech and overlook faults. Financial resources are not the only means of responding well.

### What inward application is given?

The allusive reading sees honourable speech also as acknowledging weakness and sin before Allah. His forgiveness in response is better than alms polluted by proud reminders and injury. The giver needs mercy rather than a platform for displaying superiority.

Giving for Allah requires attention to the unseen rather than merely the visible transaction. The heart's purpose matters alongside the money. Humble speech and patience can therefore reveal a sounder devotion than an impressive donation accompanied by contempt.

### In short

- A gentle response and forgiveness are better than a humiliating gift.
- If unable to give, explain respectfully rather than deriding the petitioner.
- Allah needs no charity, and His forbearance calls the giver towards patience.
- Acknowledging one's own need of forgiveness counters pride in generosity.

<<<VERSE 2:264>>>
The giver is warned to preserve the deed rather than erase it through pride: "O believers! Do not waste your charity with reminders ˹of your generosity˺ or hurtful words." An impressive outward donation can conceal a purpose directed only at people's praise. The verse pictures such expenditure as a thin covering of soil on rock, swept away by a downpour.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Do not waste your charity with reminders ˹of your generosity˺ or hurtful words,"** The address calls people of faith to protect their sadaqah, charity. Reminding recipients of the favour and causing pain can destroy its reward. One explanation also includes conceit that regards obedience as a favour done to Allah.

**"like those who donate their wealth just to show off and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day."** The outward act seeks a reputation for kindness, public praise or some worldly advantage. Its doer does not seek Allah's pleasure or the lasting reward and judgment of resurrection. The explanation identifies the hypocrite who displays giving while lacking the belief that should animate it.

**"Their example is that of a hard barren rock covered with a thin layer of soil hit by a strong rain"** Safwan means smooth rock. The covering may look like fertile ground, but beneath it there is no soil capable of sustaining the crop. Wabil means a heavy downpour, powerful enough to remove that superficial layer.

**"leaving it just a bare stone."** The rain does not create the underlying barrenness. It exposes what was there. In the same way the apparent abundance of showy deeds cannot conceal their lack of lasting reward before Allah.

**"Such people are unable to preserve the reward of their charity."** Nothing remains to harvest. The possessions spent in this life cannot yield the hoped-for return in the Hereafter when the motive and subsequent conduct destroy the expenditure's worth.

**"Allah does not guide ˹such˺ disbelieving people"** They refuse guidance towards conduct that pleases Him and benefits them. Another gloss explains the statement through their lack of reward. The warning includes those who show off while associating partners with Allah.

### Does the warning concern only open unbelief?

The comparison explicitly describes someone without faith in Allah and the Last Day. That does not make display harmless when practised by someone otherwise believing in the Hereafter. A Muslim spending purely for fame or hypocritical self-promotion likewise receives no reward for that expenditure.

The additional description of unbelief also hints at how unsuitable such motivation is to real faith. Hypocrisy and the hunger for praise expose something wrong in the heart's relation to Allah. Belief should direct hope towards His acceptance, not merely towards visible spectators.

The act can therefore be ruined in different ways. It may begin as display, or the giver may subsequently destroy its reward through reminders and harm. Ad-Dahhak expressly applies the rock image to someone following charity with such conduct.

The warning recorded by Muslim from Abu Dharr gives the seriousness of reproachful giving. The point here is not that the material assistance never reached the recipient, but that the giver's reward does not survive the injurious conduct.

### Why does the rock image expose superficial piety?

A covering of earth can make a stone look productive. Similarly, a person can wear an outward appearance of devotion without letting it penetrate the whole being. The appearance lasts only while the ego remains unchallenged.

If a person seeking help speaks rudely, or if the giver's pride is wounded, anger may suddenly exceed all bounds. That encounter resembles the rain washing the soil away. The response reveals what a public display had hidden.

True giving prioritises the unseen world over the visible one. Its aim is not exhausted by how the transaction looks today. That loftier vision opens the way towards recognition of Allah and frees the gift from the need to control the audience.

### What does it mean that guidance is withheld?

The explanation states that Qur'anic guidance is offered universally. The problem is not that certain people are excluded from hearing it in the first place. They disregard it and go further by mocking it.

As a result they are deprived of taufiq, the ability and enabling grace to accept guidance. Their chosen refusal leaves them unable to benefit from what was presented. The closing warning concerns that persistent rejection, not a defect in the guidance itself.

### Why must obedience not be counted as a favour to Allah?

Ibn 'Abbas explains the prohibition also as not treating one's charity as a favour towards Allah. In the inward reading, the servant entered the path through Allah's protection and enabling grace. The heart's opening, the success of obedience and the comfort brought to poor people all depended on Him.

To stand before Him advertising those acts as personal credits reverses the truth of dependence. The servant has received favour in being enabled to do good. The proper response is gratitude, not a demand that Allah recognise an imagined debt.

Bara' ibn 'Azib reports seeing the Prophet ﷺ on the Day of the Moat recite words of Ibn Rawaha. The verses acknowledge that without Allah they would not have found guidance, given charity or prayed. They ask for tranquillity and steadiness when meeting the enemy.

That recollection expresses dependence in both devotion and danger. Without divine care, the way towards tawhid, Allah's oneness, would not have been found. Without His enabling, righteous action would not have occurred.

### What does the inward warning about self-regard mean?

A person who magnifies personal obedience and continually looks back upon it loses the path of servanthood. The language of spiritual chivalry calls such fixation a kind of idol worship, because the self becomes the object occupying the heart. Looking back at one's achievement is described as duality instead of undivided attention to Allah.

A poetic image says that even repeated martyrdom, a hundred times in a day, would not remove that inner idol if the person remained absorbed in the self. The severity belongs to the allusive warning about pride, not a new legal ruling about every believer who remembers a deed.

The application then returns to ordinary charity: do not impose a burden of obligation on those who ask. Gratitude for Allah's enabling and respect for the poor person meet in the same act. Both turn the giver away from self-display and towards the purpose for which the gift was offered.

### In short

- Reminders, humiliation and display can erase charity's reward.
- The rock's thin covering pictures apparent piety without a lasting foundation.
- A believer too must reject spending merely for fame.
- Allah's enabling grace should be acknowledged rather than treating obedience as a favour done to Him.
- Respect for recipients and hope in the unseen protect the gift's purpose.
