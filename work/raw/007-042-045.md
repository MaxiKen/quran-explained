<<<VERSE 7:42>>>
Having described the wretched, Allah now describes the happy: "As for those who believe and do good—We never require of any soul more than what it can afford—it is they who will be the residents of Paradise. They will be there forever". Their hearts believed, and their limbs and senses did good. They stand in contrast to those who denied Allah's signs and were arrogant toward them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who believe and do good"**: those who have faith in their Lord, in Muhammad ﷺ and in the Qur'an. They do good actions between themselves and their Lord, to the best of their ability.

**"We never require of any soul more than what it can afford"**: Allah does not ask more effort of any soul than it is able to give. Grammatically this clause is a parenthesis. "Those who believe and do good" is the subject, and its predicate, "it is they who will be the residents of Paradise", follows after the interruption. The interruption itself carries the message: faith and acting on it are within everyone's reach.

**"it is they who will be the residents of Paradise."** The believers are its rightful people. They will enter it.

**"They will be there forever"**: living there for ever, never dying and never being moved out.

### Is the condition for Paradise too hard?

Belief and good deeds are the condition for entering Paradise. Yet Allah, in His mercy, added at once that He burdens no one beyond their capacity. The point is that good deeds are not so hard as to be beyond any man or woman. Across every area of life, the rulings of the Sharīʿah have been made easy and adaptable. Every injunction has its allowances and concessions, made with sickness, weakness, travel and other human needs in mind.

Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ explains why the reassurance comes here. When people were commanded to do good deeds, they might have found the command heavy, since doing everything good and required, everywhere and in every condition, is beyond anyone's power. These words remove that worry. Allah's injunctions take account of the different stages of human life, and they suit the conditions of every situation, time and place. With so much consideration built into them, acting on them should not be hard at all.

### In short

- Those who believe in their hearts and do good with their limbs will live in Paradise forever.
- Allah never asks more of any soul than it can give, so faith and good deeds are within everyone's reach.
- The Sharīʿah is built with concessions for sickness, weakness, travel and other needs, suited to every time and place.

<<<VERSE 7:43>>>
Two states of the people of Paradise are described here: hearts cleansed of every grudge, and tongues full of gratitude. "We will remove whatever bitterness they had in their hearts. Rivers will flow under their feet. And they will say, “Praise be to Allah for guiding us to this. We would have never been guided if Allah had not guided us. The messengers of our Lord had certainly come with the truth.” It will be announced to them, “This is Paradise awarded to you for what you used to do.”" Their bliss is made complete when Allah removes all hatred and resentment from their hearts.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We will remove whatever bitterness they had in their hearts."** The word is ghill: envy and hatred, resentment or enmity, any spite that existed between them during their worldly life.

**"Rivers will flow under their feet."** Beneath their palaces, dwellings and couches in the Hereafter run rivers of wine, water, honey and milk.

**"And they will say, “Praise be to Allah for guiding us to this."** They say this once they have settled in their homes. One view says it is when they reach the spring of life. All praise and thanks belong to Allah, who led them to this home. Another explanation is that He guided them to the deeds whose reward is this Paradise, and enabled them to do them.

**"We would have never been guided if Allah had not guided us."** Had Allah not enabled us, we could never have done those deeds. Another explanation is that when they see the favour Allah did them in giving them faith, they say: we would never have been guided to the religion of Islam had Allah not guided us. In the Arabic, the answer to "if not" is left unsaid, because the clause before it already implies it.

**"The messengers of our Lord had certainly come with the truth.”"** The messengers brought a truth beyond doubt, with glad tidings, reward and favour. Their promises of good to the righteous proved true, and so did their warnings to the wrongdoers.

**"It will be announced to them, “This is Paradise awarded to you for what you used to do.”"** Literally, "you have been made to inherit it". It was given to you out of Allah's mercy, for the good you used to do and say in the world, seeking His face. The particle that introduces the announcement can be read as a softened "that", or as a particle of explanation. That reading holds in all five places here and in the four that follow.

### How will the grudges be removed?

Al-Bukhārī recorded from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ described what happens once the believers are saved from the Fire. They will be held on a bridge between Paradise and the Fire, and the wrongs they did one another in the world will be settled. Once they are cleansed and purified, they will be allowed into Paradise. The Prophet ﷺ swore that each of them would find his home in Paradise more easily than he found his home in the world.

So when the believers have crossed the Ṣirāṭ and their rescue from Jahannam is certain, they will be stopped first so that their malice can be removed. Any grudge, ill will, or burning sense of a right withheld will be settled and washed away for good. They will enter Jannah free of malice, hatred, animosity and every such sickness of the heart. One view holds that this bridge is the last stretch of the Ṣirāṭ, next to Jannah. ʿAllāmah al-Suyūṭī took the same view.

How will rights be settled there, where no one has money? A hadith in al-Bukhārī and Muslim says the payment will be made in the currency of deeds. The wronged person will be given the good deeds of the one who wronged him. If the wrongdoer's deeds run out while rights are still owed, the sins of the wronged will be loaded onto him. In another hadith, the Prophet ﷺ called a person the greatest pauper who did good deeds in the world but neglected people's rights. Through that neglect he lost all his good deeds and stood there empty-handed.

That hadith states the general rule for settling rights and avenging wrongs. It does not mean everyone must go through it. It is possible that no one will demand his due or take revenge, and the grudges will still be removed without any of that. Some narrations say that after crossing the Ṣirāṭ, people will come to a stream and drink from it, and its water will remove all malice from every heart. Imam al-Qurṭubī explained the verse "and their Lord will give them a purifying drink" (76:21) in the same way: this water of Jannah washes every grudge from the heart.

As-Suddī described it more fully. When the people of Paradise are led to it, they will find a tree near its gate with two springs flowing from beneath its trunk. They will drink from one, and all hatred will leave their hearts, for it is the drink of cleansing. They will bathe in the other, and the glow of delight will shine on their faces. After that their hair will never be untidy and they will never be soiled.

After reciting this verse, ʿAlī al-Murtaḍā once said that he hoped ʿUthmān, Ṭalḥah, az-Zubayr and he himself would be among those whose hearts are cleansed of malice before entering Paradise. In the world these noble men had disagreements that went as far as war.

### What will Paradise feel like?

In Hell, people who were friends in the world will hate and curse each other. The atmosphere of Paradise will be completely different. Every heart there will be open to every other, and springs of love and goodwill will rise from each one. For the person in Hell his past will be a bitter memory, and for the person in Paradise a sweet one.

The future life of evil people will begin with hearts that are graveyards of regret and frustration. The good will be different. Their minds will be full of the remembrance of Allah, on whom they rightly relied. They will be glad to find that the message of the bearers of truth was entirely correct. With thanks they will recognise Allah's grace in blessing them and moving them to stand with those who called them to the truth.

### Does anyone earn Paradise by deeds alone?

An-Nasāʾī and Ibn Marduwyah recorded, in Ibn Marduwyah's wording, a hadith from Abū Hurayrah. Every one of the people of Paradise will see the seat he would have had in the Fire. He will say, "Had Allah not guided me!" and it will make him grateful. Every one of the people of the Fire will see the seat he would have had in Paradise. He will say, "If only Allah had guided me!" and it will be a source of anguish to him.

That is why the believers, given seats in Paradise that would have belonged to the people of the Fire, are told they have inherited it for what they used to do. Their good deeds earned them Allah's mercy, by which they entered Paradise and took homes there in keeping with their deeds. This is the right meaning, for the Two Ṣaḥīḥs record the Prophet ﷺ saying that no one's deeds will admit him into Paradise. The Companions asked, "Not even you, Messenger of Allah?" He replied, "Not even me, unless Allah covers me with His mercy and favour."

This shows that no one reaches Jannah by personal effort alone unless Allah's grace makes it possible. Even the effort itself is not fully in a person's control. It too comes only by Allah's grace and mercy.

### Why is entering Paradise called guidance?

Imam Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī, who explained hidāyah (guidance) thoroughly as the Qur'an uses it, said that guidance is a common word with many degrees. In truth, guidance means finding the way to Allah. The degrees of nearness to Allah are many, varied and endless, and so are the degrees of guidance.

Its lowest degree is being rescued from kufr and shirk and having īmān. At that degree a person's direction turns away from the path of error and toward Allah. Then there is the distance between Allah and His servant. Covering it takes a journey, and each stage of that journey has its own degree of guidance. So guidance is a constant need. No one can ever claim to be beyond it, not even Allah's prophets and messengers.

That is why the Prophet ﷺ not only taught his community to pray "Guide us along the Straight Path" but kept making that prayer himself. The stages and ranks of nearness to Allah have no end. Entry into Paradise is called guidance in this verse because it is the final station of guidance.

### The inner reading: hearts made pure

This is the description of the noble ones of the spiritual path (ṭarīqah) and the travellers on the road of the Ḥaqīqah. The Exalted Lord first cleansed their hearts of whims and innovations. Then they could step onto the road of the Sunnah and find their way to the plain texts of Allah's Book and Muṣṭafā's Sunnah. In the verses about His attributes, they gave up their own imaginings and opinions and set aside their own cleverness. They bowed their necks in compliance, accepted what they heard, and took the road of surrender. So they escaped both likening Him to creation and stripping Him of His attributes.

Then He cleansed their hearts again, of this world and its stains. Then recognition lit up their hearts, and wisdom opened its eyes within them. He honoured them with His gaze and took love of the creatures out of their hearts, so they were wholly with Him. They went forward into solitude with Him and turned back from secondary causes to the Causer. They saw One, heard One and reached One. Their tongues were busy with remembrance, their hearts with reflection, and their spirits with love. Their tongues spoke of Him, their hearts whispered to Him in secret, and their spirits rejoiced.

As the verse says: "The moment my heart was captured by Your beauty, it became the slave of Your majestic loveliness. How exalted is the one who sees Your face, how great the one taken into Your keeping."

Know, though, that until the covenant from before time takes hold of your hem, your heart will not take up this work. Until the Real joins Himself to you, this path will not agree with you. Until the Real looks upon you, your heart will not long for Him.

"For what you used to do" is said to soothe the servant's heart and to add to Allah's tenderness toward him. The servant knows that his own careless deeds are not fit for that Threshold. He knows those stations and ranks are no payment for such deeds. Yet by His bounty Allah makes the unworthy worthy and adorns what is unlovely. In this He shows the servant the goodness of His lordship and His loving kindness.

### In short

- Before entering Paradise, the believers' hearts will be cleansed of every grudge, whether by settling rights or by a purifying drink.
- Rights left unpaid are settled in good deeds, so neglecting others' rights can leave a worshipper bankrupt.
- The people of Paradise will thank Allah, knowing they could never have been guided or done good without Him.
- Deeds are the occasion of Allah's mercy, but it is His mercy that admits a person to Paradise.
- Guidance has endless degrees, and entering Paradise is its final station.

<<<VERSE 7:44>>>
Once everyone has taken their place, a conversation takes place across the divide: "The residents of Paradise will call out to the residents of the Fire, “We have certainly found our Lord’s promise to be true. Have you too found your Lord’s promise to be true?” They will reply, “Yes, we have!” Then a caller will announce to both, “May Allah’s condemnation be upon the wrongdoers". The people of the Fire are addressed, rebuked and reproached once they are in their places.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The residents of Paradise will call out to the residents of the Fire"**: when all have entered their places, the people of Paradise will call to the people of the Fire. One explanation says they do it to affirm the truth, and another says to rebuke.

**"“We have certainly found our Lord’s promise to be true."** What our Lord promised us of Paradise, reward and favour has come true, for we have entered it.

**"Have you too found your Lord’s promise to be true?”"** O people of the Fire, have you found true the punishment and humiliation your Lord warned you of?

**"They will reply, “Yes, we have!”"** The disbelievers will admit that what they were promised about Hell has come true.

**"Then a caller will announce to both"**: a crier will proclaim between the two parties, making both hear.

**"“May Allah’s condemnation be upon the wrongdoers"**: the curse of Allah, His punishment, will rest on the wrongdoers, the disbelievers. They will be shut out of Allah's mercy. Its doors were open to them in their worldly life, and they turned away from them.

### How are the people of the Fire reproached?

Allah will punish the disbeliever for the claims he used to make in this life. In Sūrat aṣ-Ṣāffāt, Allah tells of a believer who had a disbelieving companion in the world:

> "Then he ˹and the others˺ will look and spot him in the midst of the Hellfire. He will ˹then˺ say, “By Allah! You nearly ruined me. Had it not been for the grace of my Lord, I ˹too˺ would have certainly been among those brought ˹to Hell˺.” ˹Then he will ask his fellow believers,˺ “Can you imagine that we will never die except our first death, nor be punished ˹like the others˺?”" (37:55-59).

The angels will also rebuke the disbelievers:

> "˹They will be told,˺ “This is the Fire which you used to deny. Is this magic, or do you not see? Burn in it! It is the same whether you endure ˹it˺ patiently or not. You are only rewarded for what you used to do.”" (52:14-16).

### A scene from Badr

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ spoke in the same way to the dead thrown into the well at Badr. He called their leaders by name: "Abū Jahl ibn Hishām! ʿUtbah ibn Rabīʿah! Shaybah ibn Rabīʿah! Have you found what your Lord promised you to be true? I have found what my Lord promised me to be true." He meant that they had met the Fire, and he had been given victory. ʿUmar asked, "Messenger of Allah, are you speaking to people who have become rotting corpses?" He replied, swearing by the One in whose hand his soul was, "You do not hear what I am saying any better than they do, but they cannot answer."

### How could voices carry so far?

In earlier times people asked how this could be. Paradise is above the heavens and Hell at the lowest depth, so how could the voices of one reach the other? In this age of mass communication the question has become a matter of degree. People now know it is possible to see distant things and hear distant voices. What was unclear in ancient times has become easy to grasp through modern experience and observation.

This carries a lesson. If some statement of the Qur'an seems to defy explanation by today's knowledge, no conclusion should be drawn against it. As knowledge grows, it may well become an accepted fact tomorrow, however odd and unknowable it seems today. This does not mean the people of Paradise and Hell will talk by radio or television. It only shows that in Allah's universe there are arrangements that let two people far apart see and speak to each other without difficulty.

### In short

- The people of Paradise will call to the people of the Fire, affirming that Allah's promise came true, and the people of the Fire will have to admit the same.
- A caller will announce Allah's curse on the wrongdoers, who turned away from His mercy when its doors were open.
- At Badr the Prophet ﷺ addressed the dead leaders of Quraysh in the same words, and said they heard him.
- Something in the Qur'an that seems beyond explanation today should not be judged by present knowledge alone.

<<<VERSE 7:45>>>
The caller goes on to describe the wrongdoers on whom the curse falls: "those who hindered ˹others˺ from Allah’s Way, strived to make it ˹appear˺ crooked, and disbelieved in the Hereafter.”" Three crimes are named. They blocked others from the truth, they tried to distort it, and they denied the life to come.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who hindered ˹others˺ from Allah’s Way"**: they turned away from Allah's religion themselves and turned others away from it too. They stood between people and Allah's path, meaning His law and the message of the prophets, and diverted them from His religion and from obeying Him.

**"strived to make it ˹appear˺ crooked"**: they wanted the path of truth to look twisted and winding, or changed, so that no one would follow it.

**"and disbelieved in the Hereafter.”"** They disbelieved in the meeting with Allah and in resurrection after death. They denied it would ever happen and neither accepted it nor prepared for it. That is why they made light of their evil words and deeds, since they feared no reckoning and no punishment. So they were, and are, the worst of people in word and deed.

### Why do some people see no force in the arguments for the Hereafter?

Only someone serious and sincere about an argument can feel how heavy it is. Those who give the Hereafter no importance cannot see the point of the arguments for it. Its reality is backed by very strong arguments, yet their unserious minds can only dig out faults in it. They raise objections of every kind. They fill themselves with suspicion and doubt, and they lead others into disbelief as well. In Allah's eyes such people are guilty in the extreme. They may think themselves entitled to His grace in this world, but in the Hereafter they will suffer His punishment.

However strong and convincing an argument is, clever talk can always cast doubt on it. Most people cannot tell a real argument from sophistry, so they drift away from the truth when they hear such talk. Some people are quite able to understand the truth and still drive others from it with clever words. On the Day of Reckoning, they will be far removed from Allah's grace.

### In short

- The cursed wrongdoers blocked others from Allah's way and tried to make the straight path look crooked.
- Their denial of the Hereafter made them careless of evil, since they expected no reckoning.
- Arguments for the Hereafter carry weight only for those who approach them seriously. Using clever talk to turn people from the truth is a grave crime.
