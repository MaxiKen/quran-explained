<<<VERSE 7:42>>>
The other group now gets its own sentence. "As for those who believe and do good—We never require of any soul more than what it can afford—it is they who will be the residents of Paradise. They will be there forever". The verse sets out two conditions and then states a rule that makes the two conditions reasonable.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who believe"** means what it says about the heart. Their hearts have accepted the truth.

**"and do good"** means what follows from that in the limbs and the senses. Notice what is being set opposite what. The verse before described people who denied the signs of Allah and were arrogant about them. This verse describes the people whose hearts believed and whose bodies then acted.

**"We never require of any soul more than what it can afford"** is the rule that stands between the conditions and the reward. In the Arabic the clause is a parenthetical remark placed between the subject and its predicate, which is the phrase about the residents of Paradise that comes at the end. What the clause does is remove a fear. When human beings were commanded to do good, it might have seemed a heavy thing to them. To do the good thing that is required, in every place and in every circumstance, is more than any person can manage. The doubt is answered here. What Allah asks of anyone is measured against what that man or woman can carry.

This is how the injunctions of the Sharīʿah work in every department of life. They are flexible and easy, and concessions are given in every one of them because of illness, weakness, travel and the other needs of human beings. And in al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ the point of the clause is said to be that the injunctions are made to suit the various stages of human life and the conditions people actually live in, in all times and all places. When the injunctions are built that way, acting on them cannot be difficult.

### The two conditions put side by side with the two faults

After Allah has set out the state of the wretched, He turns here to the state of the happy, and the two descriptions answer each other word for word. On the one side are the people who denied the signs of Allah and were arrogant about them, whose refusal was in the heart and whose arrogance was in the bearing. On the other side are the people whose hearts believed and whose limbs and senses then did good. Nothing is said here of anything hidden or heroic, because the requirement is stated plainly in two parts, and the second part of it is measured against the person it is laid on.

It is worth noticing what the verse does not ask for. It does not ask for a level of performance that lies beyond the individual, and the clause about capacity is placed in the middle of the sentence to make that clear before the reward is even named. That is why the two conditions together are described elsewhere as something easy, and that is the plain sense of a passage which names belief and good deeds as the terms of entry and then adds that no soul is given more than it can bear.

**"it is they who will be the residents of Paradise"** is the predicate that the parenthetical clause interrupted. They are its rightful owners, the people of the garden.

**"They will be there forever"** means the dwelling has no end, with no dying and no being taken out.

### In short

- The two conditions are belief in the heart and good deeds done with the body, which is the exact opposite of the denial and arrogance described in the verses before.
- The clause about capacity is a promise placed in the middle of the sentence, and it answers the fear that the requirement is more than a person can carry.
- The Sharīʿah is built with concessions in every injunction, for illness, weakness, travel and the ordinary needs of life, so what it asks is proportionate to what a person can bear.
- The reward named is the garden itself, and the stay in it has no end.

<<<VERSE 7:43>>>
The verse describes what the people of the garden have been freed from and what they say about it. "We will remove whatever bitterness they had in their hearts. Rivers will flow under their feet. And they will say, “Praise be to Allah for guiding us to this. We would have never been guided if Allah had not guided us. The messengers of our Lord had certainly come with the truth.” It will be announced to them, “This is Paradise awarded to you for what you used to do.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"We will remove whatever bitterness they had in their hearts"** takes away ill feeling of every kind. The word covers envy and hatred, resentment and grudges, and the ill will that people carry against one another in this world.

**"Rivers will flow under their feet"** sets out what they have instead. The rivers flow beneath their dwellings and beneath their seats, and the rivers are of wine, water, honey and milk.

**"And they will say, “Praise be to Allah for guiding us to this."** is what they say once they have settled into their homes. What the word for praise covers here is gratitude. Some explain the words as praise for the guidance that led them to this dwelling and to the spring of life. Others explain it as what they say when they see the favour Allah showed them by giving them faith, so that they say that praise and thanks belong to Allah who guided them to the religion of Islam.

**"We would have never been guided if Allah had not guided us."** is the second half of the sentence. In the Arabic the words are built on a conditional construction with the answer to the condition left out, because the preceding clause already carries it.

**"The messengers of our Lord had certainly come with the truth."** is the third thing they say. The messengers brought truth itself, and with it the glad tidings and the reward.

**"It will be announced to them, “This is Paradise awarded to you for what you used to do.”"** is the cry that greets them in answer to their own words. This is the garden, and it is what they inherit for what they used to do and say of good in the life of this world.

### The bitterness that has to be cleared first

As-Suddī described how the removal is done on the way in. The people of the garden are brought to it and find a tree close to its door, with two springs running from beneath its trunk. They drink from one spring, and all hatred is drawn out of their hearts, because that is the cleansing drink. In the other they bathe, and the brightness of delight shines out of their faces. After that, none of them ever has unkempt hair or becomes dirty.

The first half of the verse is doing real work, and it is worth taking slowly. Believers will have crossed the bridge of Ṣirāṭ, and once their deliverance from Jahannam is certain they will be held back on a bridge that lies between the garden and the fire, so that ill will can be cleared out of them first. If one had a grudge against another, or ill feeling, or a heartache over a right that was lost, all of it will be settled between them and washed away there and then. So when they enter the garden they are free of malice and hatred and animosity and the other disorders of that kind. It appears in one tafsīr that this bridge is the last part of the bridge of Ṣirāṭ, the part closest to the garden, and ʿAllāmah as-Suyūṭī also chose that reading.

Then comes the matter of rights. Nothing can be paid in money for a right that was lost, since nobody there will have any. What is used instead is the currency of deeds. Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī reported the hadith in al-Bukhārī on how that is done, and it is al-Bukhārī above all who carries it. The deed of the one who took the right is handed to the one whose right it was. And if the wrongdoer's deeds run out while rights are still outstanding, then the sins of the person whose right is unsettled are moved across to the account of the one who took it.

In a hadith the Holy Prophet ﷺ called the person who did good deeds in this world but was careless about the rights of people the greatest pauper, because the neglect lost him all his good deeds and left him standing there with nothing.

That is the general rule for settling rights and avenging wrongs in the fire. It is not necessary that everyone should meet that exact situation, and it is possible that ill feeling is removed without any settling of scores at all. Abū Hurayrah reported from the Prophet ﷺ that each of the people of the garden will be shown his seat in the fire and will say that Allah had guided him, and that this will make him grateful, while each of the people of the fire will be shown his seat in the garden and will wish that he had been guided, and that this will be his anguish. Some narrations describe what happens when people have crossed the bridge: they arrive at a stream and drink from it, and the property of that water is that it takes every trace of ill will out of their hearts. Al-Qurṭubī explains the verse about those who are given a pure drink (76:21) in the same way, that the grudges in the hearts are washed out by it.

ʿAlī al-Murtadā recited this verse once and then said that he hoped that he and ʿUthmān, Ṭalḥah and az-Zubayr would be among those whose hearts are cleansed of ill feeling before they enter the garden. Those men had disagreed with one another in their lifetime to the point of fighting, and the hope is that the verse covers them all the same.

### What their own words show

When they say that they would never have been guided if Allah had not guided them, they are stating a fact about everyone. Nobody enters the garden on personal effort alone. What makes it possible is the grace of Allah, and there is a further point in that. Even the effort a person makes is not within his own control. That too comes by the grace and the mercy of Allah.

Guidance itself is worth understanding properly. Rāghib al-Isfahānī explained hidāyah, guidance, as it is used across the Qur'an, and his point is that the word is a familiar one that has many degrees. Guidance is another name for finding the way that leads to Allah. Since the degrees of nearness to Allah are many, different and without end, the degrees of guidance are varied and different in the same way.

The lowest degree of guidance is deliverance from kufr and from shirk, along with īmān, faith. A person at that degree has had his direction changed, so that he is turned away from the path of error and towards Allah. After that there is the distance between Allah and His servants, and that distance is a journey with stages to travel. Every stage covered has its degrees, and those degrees are what guidance means. So guidance is a need that never lapses. At no point can a person say that he no longer needs it, and that includes the prophets and the messengers. It is for that reason that the Holy Prophet ﷺ not only taught his people to ask for the straight path but kept making that prayer himself, since the stages and ranks of nearness have no end. And it is for the same reason that entry into the garden mentioned in this verse is itself called guidance, because it is the last station of it.

### What the two destinations are like for the people in them

The position in the fire is that people who were friends in this world hate and curse one another. The air of the garden is the opposite. There every heart is open, and love and goodwill for others rise out of all of them.

There is a difference in memory as well. For the person in the fire, his past becomes a bitter recollection, and the life of the evil begins with a heart that is a graveyard of regret and frustration. For the good, the memory is pleasant. Their minds are full of the remembrance of God, the One they trusted, and they are glad to find that everything the bearers of the truth told them turned out to be exactly right. They recognise the grace of God in having blessed them and inspired them to stand with those who called them to accept the truth.

### The inner reading

There is also an inner reading of the passage, and this is the description of the followers of the path and the travellers on the road of reality. The Exalted Lord begins by making their hearts clean of caprice and of invented practice, so that they can step onto the road of the Sunnah and find their way to the plain text of the Book and to the Sunnah of Muṣṭafā ﷺ. They let go of their own imagination and their own understanding in the matter of the divine attributes, and they set aside the discernment of their own intelligence. They bow their necks in compliance, accept with their hearing, and set out on the road of surrender, and in that way they escape both from likening Allah to His creation and from declaring Him ineffective.

Then, a second time, He cleans their hearts of this world and of its defilement, so that the light of recognising Him shines in them and the eyes of wisdom open in them. He honours them with His gaze. He removes the love of creatures from their hearts, so that they are wholly with Him. They go forward in the reality of being alone with Him, and come back from secondary causes to the One who causes them. They see One, they hear One, and they reach One. Their tongues are busy with remembrance, their hearts with reflection and their spirits with love. They mention Him with the tongue, whisper with the heart, and rejoice in the spirit.

And here is the condition attached to all of it. Until the covenant that has no beginning takes hold of your hem, your heart will not accept this work. Until the Real joins with you, this path will not go along with you. Until the Real gazes on you, your heart will not want Him.

The announcement that this garden is inherited for what they used to do is spoken to soothe the heart of the servant and to increase the kindness shown to him. Otherwise the servant knows perfectly well that his own careless deeds do not deserve a threshold like that, and that those stations and degrees are not the wage for acts such as his. But through His bounty He makes the unworthy worthy and adorns what was displeasing, and in that He shows the servant His good godhood and His loving kindness.

### In short

- The verse names two things for the people of the garden: ill feeling of every kind is taken out of their hearts, and rivers flow beneath them.
- Ill will is cleared on a bridge between the garden and the fire, and the loss of rights is settled in the currency of deeds, as the two Ṣaḥīḥ collections record.
- Their words of praise are words about guidance, and nobody reaches the garden by personal effort alone, since even the effort comes by the grace of Allah.
- Guidance has many degrees, from deliverance out of kufr and shirk to the last station, which is entry into the garden itself, and no one outgrows the need for it.
- The verse is read as a description of hearts made clean of their own caprice, so that the road of the Sunnah and the plain text can be found.

<<<VERSE 7:44>>>
The two groups are placed where they can hear each other. "The residents of Paradise will call out to the residents of the Fire, “We have certainly found our Lord's promise to be true. Have you too found your Lord's promise to be true?” They will reply, “Yes, we have!” Then a caller will announce to both, “May Allah's condemnation be upon the wrongdoers"". The verse gives one question, one answer, and one verdict over both sides at once.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The residents of Paradise will call out to the residents of the Fire"** sets the scene. The call may be read as a statement of the truth or as a rebuke delivered to people who did not believe it.

**"We have certainly found our Lord's promise to be true."** is what the people of the garden say, and what they found true is the reward and the favour that had been promised to them. They have entered it, so the promise is now something they know by being in it.

**"Have you too found your Lord's promise to be true?"** is the question put to the other side. What is meant is the torment and the humiliation that had been promised to them.

**"They will reply, “Yes, we have!”"** is the answer, and it is short. The people of the fire give it when they have all taken their places. Another reading has them adding that they have found what was promised about the fire to be true.

**"Then a caller will announce to both"** describes a herald who proclaims between the two parties, making himself heard by both.

**"May Allah's condemnation be upon the wrongdoers"** is the proclamation. Another reading takes the word for the condemnation as meaning the torment of Allah, so that the announcement is that the torment rests on the wrongdoers, and that it stays with them. And another reading gives the sense of the proclamation: that the wrongdoers are to be removed from the mercy of Allah, because the doors of the mercy were open to them in their life and they turned away from them.

### The question people used to ask

In early times the question was raised how the voices of the people of the garden could reach the people of the fire at all, when the two places are so far apart, one above the skies and the other at the lowest level there is. In the present age of mass communication that question has become relative. It is now known that people can see things far away and hear voices from far away, so what was unclear in earlier times is understandable now.

The lesson drawn from that is stated carefully. If some statement of the Qur'an cannot be analysed in the light of present knowledge, nothing should be concluded against it, because with the growth of knowledge it may become an accepted fact tomorrow. What the modern discoveries show is only that in this universe of God there are arrangements by which two people far apart can see and speak to one another without difficulty. The discoveries do not mean that in the Hereafter the two groups will be connected by radio or television.

### Why the arguments never reached them

Only a person who takes an argument seriously, and means it when he asks, will feel its weight. The people who give the Hereafter no importance cannot see the point of any argument about the life to come. The reality of that life rests on evidence of the strongest kind, and such people, with their minds never properly engaged, do nothing with it but hunt for supposed defects. They raise objections of various kinds, and the result is that they themselves come to hold suspicions and doubts about it and they incline other people towards disbelief as well. Such people are, in the sight of God, guilty in the extreme. In the Hereafter they meet the scourge of God, though in this world they may have counted themselves entitled to His grace.

What follows from that is a warning about clever speech. There is no argument so strong that somebody cannot throw doubt over it with a well-turned phrase. Ordinary people cannot always tell real argument from sophistry, and so they are pulled off the truth by hearing such talk. And the people who are capable of understanding the truth and still use clever words to drive others away from it will be very far from the grace of God on the Day of Reckoning.

### The Prophet ﷺ at the well of Badr

The other half of the verse is about the punishment for that. The messenger of Allah ﷺ stood at the well of Badr and addressed the men thrown into it. He called them by name, Abū Jahl bin Hishām and ʿUtbah bin Rabīʿah and Shaybah bin Rabīʿah and the other leaders of the town, and he asked them whether they had found what their Lord promised them to be true, telling them that he had certainly found what his Lord promised him to be true, which was victory. ʿUmar asked him whether he was addressing people who had become rotten corpses. He answered, by He in Whose Hand is my soul, that they did not hear what he was saying any better than ʿUmar did, but that they could not reply. And the report is in the two Ṣaḥīḥ collections.

The verse and the scene belong together. The question the people of the garden will put to the people of the fire is the same question the Prophet ﷺ put to the men in the well, and the answer in both cases is the same.

### What the Qur'an says of the same moment elsewhere

In Sūrat aṣ-Ṣāffāt, the man in the garden looks down and sees his old companion in the middle of the fire, and says that by Allah the other nearly ruined him, and that had it not been for the grace of his Lord he would have been among those brought forth to it. Then the people of the garden say: are we then not to die any more, except for our first death, and are we not to be punished (37:55-59)?

And the Qur'an says that the disbeliever is punished for the claims he used to make in this life. This is the fire that you used to deny. Is this magic, or do you not see? Taste it, and whether you bear it patiently or impatiently, it is all the same, since you are only being repaid for what you used to do (52:14-16).

### The verdict and who it falls on

The proclamation names them wrongdoers, and the verse that follows says who they are. They held people back from the path of Allah, they wanted to make that path look crooked and winding so that nobody would follow it, and they disbelieved in the Hereafter. They denied that the meeting with Allah would ever take place, and they refused to accept it or believe in it. Because they had no fear of any reckoning or any punishment, they made light of the evil they said and did. That is why they were, and remain, the worst of people in what they say and in what they do.

### In short

- The people of the garden call to the people of the fire and ask whether the promise they were warned with turned out to be true, and the answer is yes.
- A herald proclaims between both parties that the condemnation of Allah rests on the wrongdoers, and they are the ones removed from His mercy.
- The old question about how the two groups could hear each other is answered by noting that distance is no obstacle in the arrangements of God's universe, and nothing should be concluded against a verse because present knowledge cannot yet analyse it.
- The same question was put by the Prophet ﷺ to the men thrown into the well at Badr, and they also could not reply.
- Sūrat aṣ-Ṣāffāt and the passage about the fire the disbelievers used to deny say the same thing about the state of the two groups.

<<<VERSE 7:45>>>
The verse completes the description the herald's words began. "those who hindered ˹others˺ from Allah's Way, strived to make it ˹appear˺ crooked, and disbelieved in the Hereafter."

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who hindered ˹others˺ from Allah's Way"** names what they did to other people. They kept people back from the path of Allah, which is His religion and the obedience He asks for. They turned others away from it.

**"strived to make it ˹appear˺ crooked"** is the second charge, and it is worse than the first. They did not merely stay away themselves. They worked at making the path look crooked and winding, so that nobody seeing it would want to walk it. Another reading gives the sense as wanting the way changed.

**"and disbelieved in the Hereafter"** is the third charge, and it explains the other two. They rejected the resurrection after death and did not prepare for it.

### Why the three charges belong together

The three are put in an order that runs from the outside in. First the harm done to others, by blocking them from the road. Then the deliberate work of making the road itself look unattractive. And behind both of those, the thing that made them possible, which is not believing that there is an account to be given.

Nothing about the punishment is settled yet at this point. The verse before gave the verdict that the condemnation of Allah is on the wrongdoers. This verse names the wrongdoers, and the naming is the evidence for the verdict. There is no wrong that is being punished here other than the three things set out, and the third of them is the root of the other two. A person who does not expect to be asked about what he did is free to block other people and to spoil the road for them, and he does it without a second thought.

### In short

- The verse names the people the verdict has just fallen on, and it names three things they did.
- They blocked others from the way of Allah, they worked to make the way look crooked, and they disbelieved in the life to come.
- The third charge is the root of the first two, since a person who expects no account has no reason to leave the road clear.
- Another reading gives the crookedness as wanting the way changed rather than simply misrepresenting it.
