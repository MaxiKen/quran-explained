<<<VERSE 2:226>>>
A husband cannot use an oath of sexual abstinence to leave his wife indefinitely suspended: "Those who swear not to have intercourse with their wives must wait for four months." This is the rule of ila', an oath to refrain from marital intercourse. It sets a limit to the resulting separation and opens a way back. If the husband resumes the relationship, Allah offers forgiveness and mercy.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who swear not to have intercourse with their wives"** The subject is an intentional oath concerning a wife. A thoughtless phrase is not treated like a solemn commitment, though habitual swearing should itself be avoided. The majority understand this particular rule to concern wives rather than enslaved women.

**"must wait for four months."** The period runs from the date of the oath. An oath with no fixed end, one lasting four months and one lasting longer all raise the question of this limit. The husband must not use a longer oath to deprive his wife of her rights indefinitely.

**"If they change their mind,"** Returning means resuming normal married relations, including intercourse. Ibn 'Abbas, Ash-Sha'bi, Masruq and Sa'id bin Jubayr explain it this way, as does Ibn Jarir. The return requires addressing the oath through kaffarah, the prescribed compensation for breaking it.

**"then Allah is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"** Allah forgives the wrong done to the wife's rights when the husband returns and repents. His mercy includes making a lawful release from the oath available. An angry promise need not become an unchangeable obstacle to repairing the marriage.

### What if the oath lasts less than four months?

An oath of shorter duration is distinguished from one reaching or exceeding the four-month limit. In the ruling presented here, the husband may complete the shorter period. His wife is asked to be patient rather than require him to end it before its agreed term. Once the term ends, intercourse may resume and the nikah, the marriage bond, remains intact. If he breaks the oath instead, compensation is due.

'A'ishah reports in the Two Sahihs that the Prophet ﷺ swore to stay apart from his wives for a month. He returned after twenty-nine days and explained that a lunar month can have twenty-nine days. 'Umar bin Al-Khattab transmits a similar report, also in the Two Sahihs. A month-long separation is therefore not automatically treated as though it were an indefinite abandonment.

Where an oath lasts longer than four months, the wife may demand a resolution once four months have elapsed. One legal explanation requires the husband either to return or to divorce, with the authorities compelling a decision if necessary. Another holds that a divorce itself takes effect at the deadline. The disagreement about that consequence is explained under the next verse. Both explanations reject leaving the wife bound indefinitely by the husband's refusal.

### Why are another person's rights so serious?

The passage gives a decisive command about a spouse's rights. Its inward admonition is that obligations to Allah must also be treated with reverence. If neglecting another person's due receives such weight, the servant must not casually set aside the Lord's commands either.

A warning report pictures a young man brought at the Resurrection after neglecting Allah's rights. He is asked whether he felt shame or feared Allah's anger, and is condemned for treating those rights without respect. Ibn 'Abbas also relates a saying of the Prophet ﷺ in which Allah connects caring for His servant's due with the servant's care for His own due. A saying in the scriptures similarly links honouring Allah with being honoured, and scorning His command with being scorned.

These warnings do not make obligations to other people less important. Allah's own rights are described as resting on leniency, with much forgiven. Claims belonging to other people cannot simply be brushed aside. One admonition says that even someone carrying the reward of seventy prophets would be held back over a plaintiff cheated of half a penny until that plaintiff was satisfied. It is an image of the gravity of unresolved human claims, not permission to measure a small injustice as insignificant.

The rights of wives deserve particular care here. Allah Himself presses their claim against husbands who withhold companionship. The Prophet ﷺ, also called Mustafa, declared that the best people are those who are best to their wives, and described himself as the best to his own wife. The deadline is therefore part of a wider demand: power within marriage must not become a means of neglect.

### In short

- Ila' is a deliberate oath to abstain from intercourse with one's wife.
- A shorter oath and an oath reaching the four-month limit have different consequences.
- Returning to married life involves compensation for the oath and opens the way to forgiveness.
- A wife's rights are serious claims, not matters a husband may suspend indefinitely.

<<<VERSE 2:227>>>
The alternative to returning is a real separation: "But if they settle on divorce, then Allah is indeed All-Hearing, All-Knowing". After the limit attached to the oath, a husband must not leave his wife without either restored companionship or a resolved divorce. Allah hears the words and knows the decision behind them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But if they settle on divorce,"** The words concern a husband who persists in separation rather than returning from his oath. The marriage must not remain a device for withholding his wife's rights. The legal explanations differ over whether the four-month deadline itself ends the marriage or requires an explicit decision afterwards.

**"then Allah is indeed All-Hearing, All-Knowing"** Allah hears the oath and the words of divorce, and knows the husband's resolve and circumstances. A public statement cannot conceal a contrary secret purpose. Nor can a man undo the fact of a divorce merely by denying it later.

### Does divorce occur automatically after four months?

One explanation says no. The phrase about deciding on divorce is understood to require a choice after the waiting period. Malik relates from Nafi' that 'Abdullah bin 'Umar said the passing of four months does not by itself cause divorce. At that point the husband must return or divorce. Al-Bukhari also records the report.

Ibn Jarir relates through Suhayl bin Abu Salih that his father asked twelve Companions about ila'. They all said that no decision was required before the four months ended, but afterwards the husband must retain his wife or divorce her. Ad-Daraqutni also transmits this account. The position is reported from 'Uthman, 'Ali and 'Umar, from Abu Ad-Darda', 'A'ishah and Ibn 'Abbas, as well as Ibn 'Umar. Among later authorities it is given from Al-Qasim, Muhammad bin Ka'b, Tawus, Mujahid, 'Umar bin 'Abdul-'Aziz and Sa'id bin Musayyib.

Another legal explanation holds that where the oath is indefinite, for four months or for longer, completing four months without returning produces one irrevocable divorce. Under that ruling, the husband cannot simply take his wife back under the existing marriage. They may enter a new marriage by mutual consent. An intervening marriage to someone else, called halalah, is not required for that return. If intercourse resumes before the deadline, compensation for the broken oath is due and the marriage remains intact.

These are different accounts of how the separation takes legal effect. They must not be merged into a claim that every view requires an automatic divorce, or that every view requires a fresh pronouncement. Both address the same wrong: using an oath to hold a wife indefinitely without companionship.

### What comfort is there in Allah's hearing?

If a husband no longer wants companionship and has settled on separation, Allah knows his inner state. If regret comes later, he should not conceal what happened by denying the divorce. Allah's knowledge is not altered by that denial.

His hearing also offers comfort to the woman distressed by her husband's words. She may feel deserted, but her pain is not unheard. The closing names are therefore both a warning against dishonest conduct and reassurance that the sorrow caused by separation is known to Allah.

### In short

- The oath must end in restored relations or resolved separation, not endless suspension.
- Scholars differed over whether the four-month deadline itself causes divorce or requires a subsequent choice.
- Allah knows the husband's resolve and hears the distress that separation brings.

<<<VERSE 2:228>>>
The verse regulates the interval after divorce and states a broad rule for married life: "Divorced women must wait three monthly cycles ˹before they can re-marry˺." During the relevant waiting period, the husband may restore a revocably divorced marriage with the intention of reconciliation. Neither spouse is without rights. The verse places mutual obligations under fairness, while assigning men a degree of responsibility, and ends with Allah's might and wisdom.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Divorced women must wait three monthly cycles ˹before they can re-marry˺."** The waiting period is called 'iddah. Here the rule concerns a divorced woman whose marriage has been consummated and who menstruates. She does not enter another marriage during this interval. The word quru', translated as monthly cycles, has been understood as menstrual periods or as intervals of purity. These are distinct legal interpretations, not interchangeable descriptions of the same calculation.

**"It is not lawful for them to conceal what Allah has created in their wombs,"** Pregnancy and menstruation must be reported truthfully. Concealing either could distort the waiting period or affect the recognition of parenthood. The woman must not manipulate the period to make it shorter or longer according to preference.

**"if they ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and the Last Day."** This appeals to faith because such facts are often known only to the woman herself. Other people cannot easily verify them. Belief in Allah's knowledge and the final account must guide truthful disclosure where external checking is difficult.

**"And their husbands reserve the right to take them back within that period if they desire reconciliation."** A husband with the right to revoke the divorce may restore the marriage within the 'iddah. The stated purpose is repairing the relationship, not trapping the woman in another cycle of hurt or lengthening her wait before divorcing her again.

**"Women have rights similar to those of men equitably,"** Each spouse owes the other what is due. The word ma'ruf means what is recognised as right, lawful, fair and considerate. The rule includes maintenance, companionship and conduct that does not cause unjust harm. Mutual obligation is not confined to the rights one party can forcefully demand.

**"although men have a degree ˹of responsibility˺ above them."** This degree is connected with household authority, maintenance, the marriage payment and responsibilities concerning divorce. It does not erase women's claims. One explanation makes it an added reason for men to show patience rather than insist on collecting every last personal entitlement.

**"And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise"** Allah can punish those who violate His commands or neglect a spouse's rights. His wisdom governs what He legislates, plans and decrees. No person's domestic advantage puts him beyond the judgment of the One who established these obligations.

### How are the three cycles understood?

The word qar', whose plural is quru', can refer to menstruation or to purity between periods. Both understandings are mentioned. The extended argument here supports understanding it as menstrual periods.

Ibn Jarir relates an incident through 'Alqamah. A woman told 'Umar bin Al-Khattab that her husband had divorced her and then returned when she had closed her door, removed her clothes and prepared water for a bath. 'Umar asked Ibn Mas'ud for his judgment. He answered that she remained the man's wife as long as she had not reached the point of being permitted to resume prayer. 'Umar agreed. The point concerns the end of the third menstrual period and whether the opportunity to restore the marriage still remained at that stage.

A substantial group of Companions is named with the menstrual-period interpretation. They include Abu Bakr As-Siddiq and 'Ali, 'Uthman and 'Umar, Abu Ad-Darda' and 'Ubadah bin As-Samit, Mu'adh and Ubayy bin Ka'b, Anas bin Malik, Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn 'Abbas and Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari. Later authorities named with it include Muhammad bin Sirin, 'Ikrimah, Tawus, Mujahid, Ibrahim, Aswad, 'Alqamah, Sa'id bin Musayyib, 'Ata' and Sa'id bin Jubayr. It is also given from Al-Hasan, Ash-Sha'bi, Qatadah, Makhul, Muqatil bin Hayyan, Ar-Rabi', As-Suddi, Ad-Dahhak and 'Ata' Al-Khurasani.

A report concerning Fatimah bint Abu Hubaiysh is brought into the linguistic discussion. Abu Dawud and An-Nasa'i record an instruction from the Prophet ﷺ not to pray during her aqra', a related plural form understood there as menstrual periods. If authentic, that report would give clear support to this meaning of the word. Its qualification must remain attached to the argument: Abu Hatim describes a transmitter, Al-Mundhir, as unknown in hadith assessment. Ibn Hibban, on the other hand, includes him in Ath-Thiqat, his collection of trustworthy narrators. The report is therefore not presented as an unqualified proof.

### Does every divorced woman have this waiting period?

No. The rule of three cycles concerns the stated category, not every possible divorce. A woman whose marriage has not been consummated has no such waiting period. A woman who has not begun menstruating, or who has reached menopause, is described as waiting three months. A pregnant woman waits until childbirth. These distinctions are connected with the discussion in surah al-Talaq.

A further historical legal rule is also mentioned for enslaved women, whose period is given as two months according to the Sunnah. These categories must not be folded into the three-cycle rule as though the verse used one calculation for every woman.

An inward reflection sees the interval as respect for the marriage that existed. Even when the bond is severed, the former relationship is not treated as if it never mattered. Another spouse is not immediately put in the former spouse's place. The absence of this waiting period in an unconsummated marriage is connected, in that reflection, with the absence of completed spousal relations.

### Why is the woman's own statement accepted?

Pregnancy and the pattern of menstruation concern facts that a woman usually knows more directly than anyone else. Ibn 'Abbas, Ibn 'Umar, Ash-Sha'bi, Mujahid, Al-Hakam bin 'Utaybah, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas and Ad-Dahhak explain the prohibition as covering pregnancy or menstrual periods.

Because verification from outside is difficult, her statement is relied upon. That reliance is joined to a warning, not suspicion by default. She must say what is true, neither adding to the period nor subtracting from it to obtain a preferred outcome. Faith makes the hidden matter answerable to Allah even where it cannot be easily checked by people.

The inward application also concerns the child. Ending the marital bond does not cancel the parental relationship Allah has established. A divorce between spouses must not be used to sever what remains due to their offspring.

### What kind of return is meant?

The husband referred to is one who still has the legal right to revoke his divorce. The return must occur during the relevant waiting period. It is intended to restore normal married life. Calling his right a better right does not mean that another man has a competing right to marry her during this period. No one else may do so then.

One explanation says that a revocation can take effect even without the wife's agreement. It understands the words about reconciliation as an exhortation directing the husband's purpose, not a separate legal condition determining whether revocation occurs. Another emphasis states the husband's right together with its proper aim: he should want a righteous repair of the marriage. Both preserve the condemnation of taking her back merely to prolong her confinement before another planned divorce.

The history of the divorce rules is also discussed. Before the later limit, a husband could repeatedly divorce and reclaim his wife, even after a hundred separate occasions. The following regulation in (2:229) set the limit and distinguished a divorce that could be revoked from a final separation. This verse's wording must therefore be read within the completed rules rather than used to revive endless revocation.

Another account describes an earlier priority to take back a wife after one or two divorces, even after the waiting period if she had not remarried. It says that the later rule limiting divorce replaced that earlier arrangement. It also describes a former claim over a pregnant wife despite repeated divorces, even a thousand, and connects the replacement of that practice with (65:1). These are accounts of earlier rules and their supersession, not permission to ignore the later limits.

### What does fairness require from both spouses?

The verse gathers a wide range of duties into one brief statement. A wife has claims against her husband just as he has claims against her. Each must give what is due, rather than make the other's failure an excuse for neglect.

Muslim records from Jabir a warning of the Prophet ﷺ to fear Allah concerning women. Marriage is undertaken under Allah's covenant, and sexual relations become lawful through His word. The husband's stated claim is that his wife not admit someone he dislikes to his resting place. The report mentions lenient discipline if that boundary is violated. It also states her claim to reasonable food and clothing. The report's description of discipline is not an unrestricted permission to injure or abuse.

In another report, through Bahz bin Hakim from Mu'awiyah bin Haydah, the Prophet ﷺ explains a wife's claims in everyday terms. Her husband is to feed her when he eats and provide clothing when he provides clothing for himself. He must not strike her face, curse her or withdraw from her except within the home. These restrictions stand beside the duties, not outside them.

Ibn 'Abbas applies reciprocity to personal appearance. He liked to make himself presentable for his wife as he liked her to make herself presentable for him. Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abu Hatim record the saying. Care is not a one-way expectation imposed on the wife alone.

The discussion distinguishes equality of obligation from sameness of every task. Duties may be distributed differently, but the duty to respect each person's rights is mutual. A difference in role does not make one party's neglect harmless or make the other party's claims optional.

### What does ma'ruf add beyond legal rules?

A technically defensible action can still be hurtful in ordinary married life. Ma'ruf brings recognised decency and customary consideration into the rule. It includes what the shariah, Islamic law, permits and what sound custom regards as fair, without undue hardship or excess.

Coldness, indifference and needlessly painful conduct may escape a narrowly worded legal article. They do not escape the command to live fairly. The standard asks whether the other spouse is being made to suffer loss or pain that accepted good conduct would reject. Fulfilling the bare form of a duty while deliberately making life miserable is not the whole of equitable companionship.

Women's rights are named prominently because physical strength and social advantage can enable men to obtain their own demands while weaker claims are neglected. The order therefore calls for men to take the initiative in giving what they owe. The wording is not an invitation to wait until the wife can compel compliance.

A practical lesson follows: each person should attend first to duties rather than only demanding rights. A husband's duties are his wife's rights, and hers are his. When both fulfil their obligations, many conflicts lose their basis. The same criticism is applied more widely to governments and people, authorities and those who challenge them: alertness to claims combined with neglect of duties produces confrontation. Patience, forgiveness and generous overlooking of personal shortfalls can reduce that conflict.

### How is the degree above women explained?

Several explanations are carried together. One connects the degree with men's spending, physical advantage, social position, household management and the obedience owed to them. Another lists distinctions in inheritance, blood money, testimony, reasoning and the expenditure and service associated with the marriage. A further explanation stresses maintenance and the marriage payment. The verse is also read in relation to the description of men as protectors and maintainers in (4:34).

One broad formulation extends male advantage to worldly and otherworldly standing. Another expressly limits the administrative precedence to the ordering of worldly family life. On the latter explanation, it does not mean that every man is better before Allah than every woman. Rank before Him depends on faith and conduct. A woman may rise above many men through worship and obedience. The promise in (16:97) joins believing men and women in the reward of righteous action.

These emphases should not be flattened into an assertion that all spiritual rank follows sex. The legal responsibility of household care and the personal merit of a believer are different questions. The discussion treats men's care as a duty, not merely a privilege they may exercise or discard.

Ibn 'Abbas draws an especially demanding conclusion for men. Because they have been given a degree, they should show greater forbearance. If a wife falls short of giving every due, the husband should be patient and should not withhold her rights in revenge. Precedence is made a reason for generosity rather than harsher insistence.

An inward reflection similarly sets the man's described advantage beside the woman's vulnerability. His expenditure gives him a claim, while her past service gives her a claim. Weakness and human need themselves call for care. The difference cannot rightly become a justification for oppression.

### How does the discussion contrast oppression with responsible care?

The social argument presents women and wealth as things that can support human life when given their proper place, but can become occasions of turmoil when misused. It calls for an ordered system of rights rather than either possession of women or rejection of all family responsibility.

Its historical survey portrays women before Islam as often bought, sold, forced into marriage or treated as inherited property. It describes denial of their inheritance, restrictions on their use of property and husbands spending women's wealth without consent. It also recounts the killing or burial of daughters, rejection of penalties for killing women and the burning of widows with their husbands.

The survey includes claims about European councils denying women a soul or fitness for worship and Paradise. It also gives an account of a decision in France in 586 recognising women's humanity while limiting their purpose to serving men. These are historical claims within the argument as transmitted here, rather than independently established conclusions. Their role in the discussion is to illustrate the contrast it draws between degradation and legally recognised personhood.

Against that picture, the rights described include a woman's ownership of her life and property, her consent to marriage and her entitlement to inheritance. A father or grandfather cannot make an unwanted marriage binding simply by imposing it. A marriage contracted without her consent remains dependent on it and is annulled if she refuses. Her wealth cannot be spent without her approval. After divorce or widowhood she is not a possession to be disposed of by others.

Providing for her and seeking her happiness are described as worship. If her husband denies the rights due to her, an Islamic court can require him to fulfil them or release her through divorce. This is not merely an appeal to voluntary kindness without enforceable claims.

The same social argument criticises the opposite extreme: removing men's duty of care and requiring women to carry livelihood burdens without that support. It links its distribution of responsibility to childrearing, family management and differences in physical capacity. Its strong polemic attributes widespread immodesty, disorder and bloodshed to abandoning these limits. It even conjectures that an inquiry might connect more than half of social crimes with unrestricted relations involving women. That is a conjectural social criticism, not a statistic established by the verse.

The intended contrast is between two failures: reducing a woman to property and stripping away the care owed to her in the name of freedom. An Arab saying illustrates the argument by describing ignorance as swinging between excess and neglect instead of remaining moderate. In this account, new laws, institutions and heavy spending cannot by themselves remedy disorder if desire is left unchecked.

### Why does the Qur'an often use masculine address?

The discussion notes that legal language often addresses men in a masculine form while applying to both sexes. Governments use similar general forms. Separate differences in duty are stated where relevant. The general address does not by itself exclude women from faith, worship or reward.

Two further reasons are suggested: the household responsibility given to men and the value placed on women's concealment or modest reserve. These are offered as explanations of the form of address, not as grounds for denying women's spiritual standing.

Umm Salmah asked the Prophet ﷺ about the lack of explicit mention of women in some passages. The verse in Al-Ahzab naming submitting men and women, believing men and women, and obedient men and women is connected with her question. An-Nasa'i, Ahmad's Musnad and Ibn Jarir are named in this connection. Another report describes Muslim women bringing the same concern to the Prophet's ﷺ wives, noting that men and the wives themselves were mentioned more expressly than other women.

The response explicitly joins both sexes in obedience, nearness to Allah, His approval and the ranks of Paradise. The degree concerning family administration must not erase that shared field of spiritual achievement. The final appeal is to faith and good conduct, with Allah's might warning against injustice and His wisdom governing the responsibilities He assigns.

### In short

- The three-cycle waiting rule concerns a particular category of divorced women, and the meaning of the cycles has two legal interpretations.
- Pregnancy and menstruation must be reported truthfully, with the woman's own knowledge carrying responsibility before Allah.
- Restoring a revocable marriage should repair the relationship, not extend harm or evade the later limits on divorce.
- Both spouses have rights under lawful fairness, and men's responsibility requires care and greater forbearance, not neglect of women's claims.
- Household roles do not make every man spiritually superior to every woman. Faith and conduct determine personal merit before Allah.
