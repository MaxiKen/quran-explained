<<<VERSE 2:276>>>
The apparent arithmetic of gain and loss is reversed: "Allah has made interest fruitless and charity fruitful." Riba can increase a balance while taking away its benefit and blessing. Sadaqah, charity, can reduce what is held while increasing lasting good. The verse asks the reader to judge wealth through its relation to Allah, not its visible quantity alone.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah has made interest fruitless"** The explanation includes actual loss of the property and removal of barakah, its blessed benefit. Riba can also become a cause of punishment here and at resurrection. Increase in an account does not prove increase before Allah.

**"and charity fruitful."** He accepts sincere obligatory and voluntary charity, blesses the giver and multiplies the reward. The return may be tenfold, seven hundredfold or more. Its fruit is not confined to an immediate replacement of the money spent.

**"And Allah does not like any ungrateful evildoer"** The ending is connected with the beginning. Someone dissatisfied with lawful provision seeks other people's wealth by prohibited means. The discussion distinguishes kufr, disbelief expressed through denying the prohibition, from the sin of someone who recognises it as haram, unlawful, but still practises it.

### Why are riba and charity placed together?

Their nature, aims and outcomes are opposed. Charity gives what one owns without demanding a material return from the recipient. Riba takes an increase from another without the countervalue required by the account of lawful exchange.

The charitable giver accepts a reduction for Allah's pleasure and the Hereafter's reward. The riba-taker seeks a prohibited addition to existing capital. Allah can frustrate that greed while making the willingly reduced property fruitful.

The contradiction concerns more than outward possession. Wealth exists to support life, comfort, dignity and those dependent upon a person. Losing its useful effects is a form of loss even when the number remains large. Receiving benefit from modest resources is a form of increase even when the balance is smaller.

### What other references explain the distinction?

The comparison of Al-Khabith, evil or impure things, with At-Tayyib, good and pure things, is recalled in (5:100). Their abundance does not make them equal. The gathering and casting away of evil in (8:37) further shows that quantity alone does not secure acceptance.

In (30:39), a gift aimed at obtaining a greater return from other people's property has no increase with Allah. These references distinguish the purpose and quality of an act from its apparent expansion.

Ibn Jarir compares the statement with Ibn Mas'ud's saying that riba ends in less even when substantial. Ahmad records a similar statement in Al-Musnad. The discussion also gives the prophetic warning that however much riba increases, it ultimately decreases, citing Ahmad and Ibn Majah.

### What happens to a small pure gift?

Al-Bukhari records from Abu Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ described giving even the equivalent of a date from honest resources. Allah accepts only the good and pure. He accepts such charity with His right Hand and raises it for the giver as a person raises an animal until it becomes mountain-sized.

The image gives growth a different measure from compound monetary gain. The starting amount is small, but its accepted return is immense. The purity of its acquisition is expressly part of the promise.

No new explanation of the divine attribute is supplied here. The account preserves the hadith's description without adding a doctrine absent from the material.

### Is the destruction only in the Hereafter?

One explanation emphasises that riba brings no benefit at the final return and may become a curse, while charity becomes an enduring blessing. That consequence is certain within the discussion.

The fuller explanation also sees traces in this life. Wealth may collapse outright, or its pleasures and benefits may be removed. Ma'mar reports hearing from elders that forty years hardly pass before a riba-consumer's wealth is overtaken by muhaq, major destructive loss.

The discussion cites market collapses and failed fortunes as examples, while acknowledging ordinary loss in lawful trade too. Its strongest contrast associates catastrophic reversal particularly with riba and speculative markets. These are the claims of the economic reflection, not an independently established forecast about every person's fortune.

The argument does not require each wrongful account to become visibly empty on the same day. Removal of benefit offers another explanation where outward possession continues.

### Can articles of comfort guarantee comfort?

Gold and silver do not themselves relieve hunger, cool a room or warm a body. They obtain things serving those purposes. The real benefit lies in what a person can use and enjoy, not merely in the metal or balance.

The reflection compares swollen wealth without benefit with swelling in a body. Growth exists, but it is not healthy growth. No one would value bodily swelling simply because measurements increased.

A comfortable house, carefully arranged bed, good mattress, heating and cooling can be purchased. Sleep cannot be bought directly. Illness or anxiety may prevent it despite all those aids. The account mentions a report claiming seventy percent of Americans needed sleeping pills, and notes that even medication can fail.

That figure belongs to the report cited in the reflection, not a current medical statistic established here. Its purpose is to distinguish purchased equipment from the mercy, rahmah, of actual rest.

The same distinction applies to eating, family enjoyment and peace of mind. Someone absorbed in turning ten million into fifteen and then twenty may have little time to enjoy any of it. Factories, ships and business worries occupy the night and day that the wealth was meant to make comfortable.

### Why is wealth not the same as honour?

The social criticism describes lenders who make a livelihood from exploiting poverty. It cites historical moneylenders in India and Jewish lenders in Syria as examples of wealthy people resented for such conduct. The criticism concerns the exploitative acts described, not an inherent quality of an ethnic or religious population.

The account links resentment between labour and capital with socialism, communism and violent confrontation. It argues that outwardly prosperous circles cannot be judged apart from people bearing the costs. A severe man-eater analogy again contrasts the well-fed beneficiary with victims elsewhere.

Those are the reflection's historical and economic claims. Their moral point is that extracting wealth at others' expense does not produce genuine respect. Charity, by contrast, can bring tranquillity to someone with fewer luxury goods and goodwill from those helped.

The account also warns that inherited riba wealth may be lost or fail to bring benefit to descendants. It rejects assuming that a flourishing next generation settles the whole question of blessing.

### What additional warnings are gathered here?

The discussion presents ten prophetic sayings to reinforce the prohibition, without supplying collectors and grades for each one. Those absent details are not added.

One names seven destructive offences: association of partners with Allah, sorcery, unlawful killing, consuming riba, exploiting an orphan's property, fleeing at battle and slandering chaste, unsuspecting believing women. Riba appears among offences of grave religious and social harm.

Another describes the dream of a river of blood where a man trying to emerge is driven back by a stone thrown into his mouth. The explanation identifies him with consuming riba. The full named dream narration has been treated under the preceding verse, while this collection repeats its warning.

Another curses the recipient, payer, writer and witnesses of riba, calling them alike in responsibility for the prohibited arrangement. Helpers who formalise it are not excused merely because they did not consume the added payment.

A further warning names four kinds of people denied admission to Paradise and its bliss: addiction to wine, taking riba, exploiting an orphan's property and disobedience to parents. Another compares a dirham received as riba with thirty-three acts of fornication or adultery, emphasising its severity.

The collection also prohibits sale of fruits before they are fit to eat and says widespread adultery and riba invite punishment upon a town. It associates prevalent riba with famine and prevalent bribery with fear.

An Ascension account describes reaching the seventh heaven and seeing thunder and lightning, followed by people whose stomachs resembled houses containing visible snakes. Jibra'il explained that they had consumed riba. No additional collector or authenticity assessment is provided in this passage.

Another warning calls for abstention from embezzlement of spoils and riba, using severe language about unforgiven sins and the embezzler carrying the stolen property at judgment. The collection finally says a creditor must not accept a gift from the debtor because of the loan.

The point of gathering these warnings is awakening, not supplying a new chain or grading from memory. Recognising the sin as sin is the first protection against calling it lawful. The account describes its wider discussion as supported by seven verses and these ten sayings, while directing fuller research elsewhere.

### What is the inward difference between growth and loss?

Permitted conduct is described as accompanied by good and blessings. Conduct arising from unchecked desire ends in loss. The contrast asks not merely whether resources grow but what sort of life is being formed through acquiring and spending them.

A person was not sent into the world simply to hoard. The trial concerns developing qualities fitting for Paradise. Charity gives resources to needy people for Allah, while usury uses them to exploit. Their different aims cannot be assumed to lead to the same final condition.

### In short

- Riba can lose its property, benefit or blessing despite apparent increase.
- Allah grows sincere charity, even when the starting gift is small.
- Comfort and honour cannot be measured by the luxury goods money purchases.
- The collected warnings reach those assisting riba as well as consuming it.
- The contrast concerns the character and final destination formed through the use of wealth.

<<<VERSE 2:277>>>
After the severe warning comes a promise for obedient believers: "Indeed, those who believe, do good, establish prayer, and pay alms-tax will receive their reward from their Lord." Faith is joined to a life of worship and care. The promised peace answers the fear and disgrace described for prohibited gain.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, those who believe,"** They acknowledge Allah, His messengers and scriptures, and accept His commands, including the prohibition of riba. Gratitude recognises His provision rather than seeking more through rebellion.

**"do good,"** Righteous action puts that recognition into practice. It includes kindness to creation and refraining from prohibited conduct. Faith is not presented here as a substitute for obedience.

**"establish prayer,"** They fulfil salah, particularly the five daily prayers, with what is required for their completion. Establishing it means more than claiming an attachment to its name.

**"and pay alms-tax"** They discharge zakah on their property and direct it to those entitled to receive it. The act joins acknowledgment of Allah's right with help for His servants.

**"will receive their reward from their Lord,"** The honour is kept with Him, in Paradise, rather than exhausted in a temporary reputation. The assurance stands beside the preceding account of rejected gain.

**"and there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve"** They are freed from fear concerning the future and sadness over worldly losses. A reported gloss pictures death slain and the Fire closed, when the promise of safety becomes manifest.

### Why join belief, worship and giving?

The verse gathers inward acknowledgment, righteous conduct, bodily devotion and financial responsibility. It does not make one outward accomplishment excuse deliberate neglect of the rest.

The person thanking Allah through obedience is contrasted with the person refusing His limits to accumulate more. Proper giving seeks the world of Paradise, while exploitation fixes its aim on amassing here. Different purposes form different lives and different final outcomes.

The image of charity raised by Allah, recorded by Al-Bukhari from Abu Hurayrah, belongs fully to the preceding verse's explanation. Here the resulting assurance is gathered into the promise of reward for the believing and practising person.

### What inward consolation is given?

The allusive reading recalls (18:30), the assurance that the wage of those doing beautiful deeds is not wasted. Taking refuge at the gate of divine generosity is pictured as entering shade. A step towards Allah finds a gift, and attachment to His generosity leaves the chamber of sorrows.

The language contrasts communion with the darkness of separation. A spirit recognising the Lord's favour laughs in relief. The Pir of the Tariqah marvels at care appearing in every breath and asks who could have a patron or friend like Allah.

The present signs are described as a message, with the full celebration still to come. The promised robe of honour is the reward with their Lord and freedom from fear and grief. The reflection joins it with repose and ease in (56:89), and the appointed time of union with the Beloved.

Poetry longs for separation to strike its tent and depart from the spirit's desert. The closing image recalls Gabriel with six hundred peacock-like wings unable to go beyond the Lote Tree alongside the Prophet ﷺ. It expresses the exceptional reach described in the reflection without adding an outside narrative or collector.

### In short

- Belief joins righteous conduct, complete prayer and proper zakah.
- The Lord's reward answers the previous warning with hope and security.
- Gratitude is shown through obedience and kindness, not hoarding alone.
- The inward reading celebrates divine care now and the fuller consolation to come.
