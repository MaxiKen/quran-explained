<<<VERSE 7:80>>>
The fourth story in the series is that of the Prophet Lūṭ (Lot): "And ˹remember˺ when Lot scolded ˹the men of˺ his people, ˹saying,˺ “Do you commit a shameful deed that no man has ever done before". Allah sent Lūṭ to call his people to His oneness, to command good and to forbid them from their evil practices, their sin and their wickedness.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹remember˺ when Lot scolded ˹the men of˺ his people"**: and We sent Lūṭ to his people. In the Arabic, "Lūṭan" is explained by the clause that follows, "when he said to his people".

**"˹saying,˺ “Do you commit a shameful deed"**: the shameful deed was sodomy, unnatural relations between men.

**"that no man has ever done before"**: no creature, human or jinn, in all the worlds had ever done it before them. None of the children of Ādam (Adam) had done it before, nor had it even crossed their minds. ʿAmr ibn Dīnār said that before the people of Lūṭ, no male had ever had relations with another male.

### Who was Lūṭ?

Lūṭ was the son of Hārān, son of Āzar (Terah), and the nephew of Ibrāhīm (Abraham), the Friend of Allah and father of the prophets. Both came from the land of Babel, near Basra, in Iraq. Idol worship was common there, and even Ibrāhīm's own family took part in it. Allah sent Ibrāhīm as a prophet to guide them. His people opposed him until matters came to a head with Nimrūd's famous fire, and his own father threatened to throw him out of the house.

Of his whole family, only his wife Sārah and his nephew Lūṭ accepted Islam: "So Lot believed in him" (29:26). With these two Ibrāhīm emigrated to Syria (ash-Shām), leaving his homeland behind. After reaching the River Jordan, he settled in Canaan, near Bayt al-Maqdis, by divine command.

Then Allah made Lūṭ a prophet too, and sent him to guide the people of Sadūm (Sodom), near Bayt al-Maqdis, and the villages around it. The area had five main cities: Sadūm, ʿAmūrah, Admah, Ṣubūyīm and Bāliʿ, also called Ṣawghar. In several places the Qur'an calls them al-Muʾtafikah and al-Muʾtafikāt. Sadūm was their centre and capital, and Lūṭ lived there. The land was fertile and green, rich in every kind of grain and fruit. Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ and other works give these details.

### How did a prosperous people fall so low?

Allah describes human nature in the Qur'an: "Most certainly, one exceeds all bounds once they think they are self-sufficient" (96:6-7). Allah had opened the doors of His blessings to these people too. Driven by this common pattern and soaked in wealth, they went to the furthest limits of luxury and lust. They lost the most basic human sense of honour, dignity and modesty, and with it the very ability to tell good from bad. So they fell into unnatural indecency. Besides being ḥarām and sinful, such acts are repugnant to anyone of sound and decent taste, so much so that even animals do not go near them.

### Why "the shameful deed"?

When speaking of zinā (adultery), the Qur'an says, "It is truly a shameful deed and an evil way" (17:32). There the word fāḥishah, "a shameful deed", is indefinite. Here it is definite, al-fāḥishah, "the shameful deed". This hints that this unnatural act gathers all indecencies into one, and is a far graver crime than zinā.

The verse also says that no one in all the worlds had done it before them. No one, not even the worst of people, had ever thought of such a thing before the people of Sadūm. The Umayyad caliph ʿAbd al-Malik said that had the Qur'an not told of the people of Lūṭ, he would never have imagined that a human being could do such a thing.

### Why is a new sin especially grave?

Their shamelessness is condemned on two grounds.

1. People often fall into sins because of their social conditions, or by blindly following their ancestors. That is no valid excuse in the Sharīʿah, but in ordinary practice such a person may be thought excusable to some degree. A sin that no one has ever committed before, with no special pressure behind it, is a curse of the highest degree.
2. Such an act opens a channel that draws others into the same curse. Whoever invents an evil act or custom bears its sin and punishment himself. He also carries the sin and punishment of everyone who follows his lead, right up to the Last Day.

### The inner reading: each rank has its own indecency

Each person's indecency matches his days and his states. Look at where a person stands on the road of the spiritual journey, and his indecency will be measured to his human nature at that point. People fall into three ranks: the common folk, the chosen few, and the chosen of the chosen.

- For the common folk, indecency is what the Sharīʿah defines, punished by flogging or stoning.
- For the chosen few, it is letting the physical eye linger on this world's pleasures and appetites, on their delight and their glitter, and letting them in, even when they are lawful and free of doubt. For such people the lawful pleasures of this world do more harm than forbidden things do to the common folk. In the words of the Master of the Sharīʿah, the penalty for this indecency is: "Lower your eyes and hold back your hands."
- For the chosen of the chosen, it is turning the heart's attention to anything other than the Real. This is despite the command from the Real: "Say, “Allah ˹revealed it˺!” Then leave them" (6:91).

It is as if He says: My servant, do not look at yourself, but see everything as My act. Do not count your deeds as a favour to Me. Look at the success I give you. Flee from your own traces and see only My love. What has someone seized by His love to do with anyone else? Keep your heart turned toward Him and let the rest go. All the world's uproar is talk of "you" and "me". Give up "me", and the whole world will be your garden. Allah says, "I wonder how anyone who believes in Me can rely on other than Me. If they looked at the fine touches of My kindness, they would worship none but Me."

### In short

- Lūṭ, nephew of Ibrāhīm, emigrated with him to Syria and was sent as a prophet to Sodom and its sister cities near Bayt al-Maqdis.
- Wealth and luxury led the people of Sodom to lose all modesty and invent an unnatural indecency no one had committed before.
- Calling it "the shameful deed" marks it as graver than zinā. Inventing a new sin brings the burden of everyone who follows it.
- In the inner reading, each rank has its own indecency, down to turning the heart toward anything other than Allah.

Lūṭ reminded them of their women: "He said, “O my people! Here are my daughters ˹so marry them˺ if you wish to do so.”" (15:71). They replied that they had no desire for women:

That is, you know we do not want women, and you know what we want with your guests.

For this reason the Companions, the Successors and the mujtahid imams declared this crime and sin far graver than other acts of shame. Imam Abū Ḥanīfah said its punishment should match the punishment Allah sent on the people of Lūṭ, when stones rained from the sky and the ground was turned upside down. So, he said, the offender should be thrown down from a height and stoned from above.

Imam Aḥmad in his Musnad, Abū Dāwūd, at-Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah recorded a hadith from Ibn ʿAbbās about those who commit this act. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever is found doing the deed of the people of Lūṭ is to be put to death, both the one who does it and the one to whom it is done.

### What is "the reformed state" that they broke?

The universe follows a natural scheme, which the Qur'an calls iṣlāḥ, the reformed state. Going against it upsets the balance of things, which the Qur'an calls fasād. Only human beings misuse their freedom to go against the way of nature, and Lūṭ's people did exactly this to the divine order. The natural form of sexual relationship is between husband and wife, and that is the way of iṣlāḥ. Sexual relations between man and man, or woman and woman, cross the limits Allah has set. That is what the Qur'an calls fasād.

### In short

- Lūṭ condemned his people for turning from the women Allah created for them to men, against nature and sense.
- They were transgressors who leapt over every limit Allah set, even the limit of humanity itself.
- The scholars count this sin graver than other indecencies. Abū Ḥanīfah likened its penalty to the punishment of Lūṭ's people, and a hadith from Ibn ʿAbbās orders death for both parties.
- The natural bond of husband and wife is the way of iṣlāḥ. Turning from it is fasād.

<<<VERSE 7:85>>>
The fifth story is that of the Prophet Shuʿayb and his people: "And to the people of Midian We sent their brother Shu’aib." His message began, "O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him. A clear proof has already come to you from your Lord." Then came its demands: "So give just measure and weight, do not defraud people of their property, nor spread corruption in the land after it has been set in order. This is for your own good, if you are ˹truly˺ believers". His people were cheating in trade and making the land a place of disorder, and he came to set them right.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And to the people of Midian We sent their brother Shu’aib."** He was their prophet. Muhammad ibn Isḥāq said the people of Madyan descended from Madyan, son of Ibrāhīm (Abraham). He also said that Shuʿayb was the son of Mīkīl ibn Yashjur, that his name in Syriac was Yathrūn (Jethro), and that he was related to Lūṭ (Lot). Madyan was the name of the tribe and also of a city, near Maʿān on the road from ash-Shām to the Ḥijāz. When the Qur'an tells the story of Mūsā (Moses) elsewhere, it says, "When he arrived at the well of Midian, he found a group of people watering ˹their herds˺" (28:23). It refers to this same place.

**"He said, “O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him."** Declare Allah's oneness and worship Him alone. This is the call of all the messengers. These people too had given themselves over to worshipping created things, forgetting the Being and attributes of Allah and neglecting His rights, so this was the first message given to them.

**"A clear proof has already come to you from your Lord."** Allah has set before you the proofs and evidence of the truth of what I have brought. The clear proofs are the miracles that appeared at Shuʿayb's hands, and Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ describes the forms they took.

**"So give just measure and weight"**: kayl means measure and mīzān means weighing. Give people their rights by measuring and weighing correctly.

**"do not defraud people of their property"**: bakhs means causing someone loss by giving him less than his due. Do not cheat people in buying and selling, or run down their goods to short-change them. They used to treacherously avoid giving full weight and measure. The first part of the verse forbids the particular crime of short measure and short weight. These words then widen the ban to every kind of cutting down, trimming or short-changing of rights, whether in property, honour or anything else. Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ explains it this way.

**"nor spread corruption in the land after it has been set in order."** Do not corrupt the earth with disbelief, disobedience, calling people to other than Allah and giving short measure. It was set right by the sending of messengers, by acts of obedience and by fair measure. The same phrase came earlier in this surah, at 7:56. The physical good order of the earth depends on using things rightly, keeping limits and holding to moderation, fairness and justice. Its spiritual good order depends on a bond with Allah and obedience to His commands. Physical and spiritual disorder appear when these principles are weakened or abandoned. Shuʿayb's people had abandoned them completely, turning the earth into a hotbed of disorder. So they were told to stop deeds that spread corruption through the whole earth.

**"This is for your own good, if you are ˹truly˺ believers"**: declaring Allah's oneness and giving full measure and weight are better for you than your present ways, if you believe what I tell you. If you are seeking faith, hurry to it. If they repented and turned from their evil, only good awaited them, in this life and the next. Success in the Hereafter plainly depends on obeying Allah's commands. In this world too, once people find someone honest in weights and measures and careful of all his customers' rights, his credit and goodwill are established in the market. His trade grows in size and reliability.

### Who were the people of Madyan and al-Aykah?

The Qur'an calls the people to whom Shuʿayb was sent both the people of Madyan and the people of al-Aykah. Al-Aykah means a forest or thicket.

Some commentators say these were two different peoples living in separate settlements. Shuʿayb was sent to one of them first, and when they were destroyed, to the other. Their punishments are also described in different words. The punishment of Madyan is called aṣ-Ṣaiḥah, the blast, in some places and ar-Rajfah, the earthquake, in others. The punishment of al-Aykah is called aẓ-Ẓullah, the canopy of cloud. The people of al-Aykah were first struck by intense heat in their town, which nearly roasted them. Then a dense cloud came over the nearby forest and shaded all of it. Drawn by the shade and the cool breeze, all the people of the town gathered under the cloud. By Allah's arrangement, the criminals walked on their own two feet to the very spot where they were to die, with no warrant of arrest and no gun at their backs. Once they were all there, the cloud rained fire and an earthquake shook the ground, killing them all.

Other commentators say the people of Madyan and the people of al-Aykah were one people, and the three punishments struck them together. First came fire from the cloud, then with it a piercing blast, and then the earthquake shook the earth. This view has been preferred. Whether they were two peoples or one people with two names, what matters is the message of truth Shuʿayb brought them.

Another account says that Madyan was a son of Ibrāhīm by his third wife, Qaṭūrā. His descendants settled on the Arabian coast of the Red Sea. They believed in Allah and claimed to follow Ibrāhīm's religion. But five hundred years after Ibrāhīm they fell into wrong ways. They were a trading people, so their wrongdoing showed in their dealings. They kept no honesty in weights, measures and transactions.

### What do the two kinds of rights demand?

The essence of Islam, the shared call of all the blessed prophets, is the fulfilment of rights. Rights are of two kinds. Some belong directly to Allah, and no visible human gain or loss seems to hang on them, such as ṣalāh and ṣawm. Others are the rights of Allah's servants (ḥuqūq al-ʿibād), which concern people. Shuʿayb's people ignored both and acted against both.

By not believing in Allah and His messengers they violated the rights of Allah. By cutting weights and measures they sliced away at people's rights. On top of that they would sit at the entrances to roads and streets, terrorising travellers, robbing them, and threatening worse if they believed in Shuʿayb. So on Allah's good earth they made disorder the order of the day. Shuʿayb was sent to correct these misdeeds, and he told them three things: worship Allah alone, accept the clear proof, and deal fairly.

### Is short-changing only about scales?

Giving short weight and measure is forbidden, and so is cutting back on anyone's rights. Attacking someone's honour, failing to give someone the respect his rightful station deserves, falling short in obeying those who must be obeyed, and failing to honour those who must be honoured all fall under the crime Shuʿayb's people committed. In his famous sermon (khuṭbah) at the Farewell Hajj, the Prophet ﷺ declared that people's honour is as sacred and inviolable as their blood. That broadly confirms this view.

All of this comes under the Qur'an's words muṭaffifīn and taṭfīf, cheating in measure, wherever they occur. Allah says:

> "Woe to the defrauders! Those who take full measure ˹when they buy˺ from people, but give less when they measure or weigh for buyers. Do such people not think that they will be resurrected for a tremendous Day—" (83:1-5).

The passage closes with "the Day ˹all˺ people will stand before the Lord of all worlds" (83:6).

These verses carry a stern warning and a sure promise, from which we ask Allah to save us. Al-Fārūq al-Aʿẓam once saw a man rushing through his bowing (rukūʿ) and prostration. He told him he had given short measure, meaning he had not given the prayer its due. Imam Mālik recorded this report. In that report, falling short in the prayer is itself called taṭfīf.

Allah has made the world run on perfect justice. In it there is no such thing as taking more from others and giving less in return. Everything works by justice, down to mathematical exactness. Unfair dealing goes against the balanced order (iṣlāḥ) that Allah has set, and it disturbs His well-balanced world.

### Why was Shuʿayb called the orator of the prophets?

Shuʿayb was known for his powerful speech, for which he was called the Orator of the Prophets, or Speaker of the Prophets, a title earned by the eloquence of his words and his counsel. Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ also records this title. His address to his people continues in the verses that follow.

### In short

- Allah sent Shuʿayb, descended from Ibrāhīm through Madyan, to the people of Madyan and al-Aykah.
- He called them to worship Allah alone, to accept the clear proof, and to give full measure and weight.
- The ban on short-changing covers every right, of property, honour, respect and even the due of prayer.
- Corrupting the earth after Allah set it right breaks both its physical and its spiritual order.
- Honesty is better for them in both worlds, bringing Allah's reward and trust in the market.
