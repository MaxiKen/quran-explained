<<<VERSE 2:55>>>
Verse 55 recalls the most brazen of the Israelites' demands. They said, "O Moses! We will never believe you until we see Allah with our own eyes", and the verse records what followed: "so a thunderbolt struck you while you were looking on". The favour being remembered is not the thunderbolt but the resurrection that came after it, which the next verse describes. Allah is saying, in effect: remember My favour in bringing you back to life after you were struck down for asking to see Me directly, something that neither you nor anyone else can bear or attain. That is how Ibn Jurayj summarised the two verses.

### Phrase by phrase

**"when you said"**. The people who spoke were a chosen party of the Israelites, and the moment is fixed by the reports. Mūsā (Moses) had come back from Mount Ṭūr (Sinai) with the Torah and set it before his people as Allah's Book. Some of them had the insolence to answer that they would not believe it unless Allah Himself told them so in so many words. With Allah's permission Mūsā replied that even this condition would be met if they came with him to the Mount. Seventy men were chosen for the purpose, and when they arrived they heard the speech of Allah with their own ears. Then, in their perversity, they produced a new excuse. Hearing was not enough, they said, because they could not be certain whether it was Allah who had spoken or someone else. One account adds that they had gone out with Mūsā to apologise to Allah for the worship of the calf and had heard what He said to them before they made their demand.

ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam tells it this way. Mūsā returned from meeting his Lord carrying the Tablets on which the Torah was written and found that his people had worshipped the calf in his absence. He ordered them to kill one another, they obeyed, and Allah forgave them. Then he said to them: these Tablets hold the Book of Allah, with what He has commanded you and what He has forbidden you. They answered: are we to believe that just because you say it? No, by Allah. Not until we see Allah in the open, not until He shows Himself to us and tells us, this is My Book, so hold to it. Why does He not speak to us as He spoke to you, Mūsā?

**"O Moses! We will never believe you until we see Allah with our own eyes"**. Ibn ʿAbbās explained "with our own eyes", jahratan, as openly and publicly, so that we may gaze upon Allah. An early commentary glosses it as: until we see Him plainly, as you did. The demand was made without a trace of shame, and it asked for what no living being can have in this world, since seeing Allah is beyond the power of a creature in the physical world.

**"so a thunderbolt struck you"**. The ṣāʿiqah has been described as a bolt of lightning, as a terrible cry, and as a fire that burned them while they looked at it. Allah's wrath fell upon them and they died. The next verse confirms the death by naming it.

**"while you were looking on"**. ʿUrwah ibn Ruwaym explained that some of them were struck while the others watched, and then Allah revived those and struck the others, so that each group saw what happened to the other. Another reading is that they died beholding what was happening to them, and another that some of them looked at the others as they fell.

### Mūsā's plea

As-Suddī described the scene after the thunderbolt. The seventy died, and Mūsā stood weeping and calling on Allah: My Lord, when I go back to the Children of Israel, what am I to tell them, now that You have destroyed the best of them? Had You willed, You could have destroyed them and me before this. Will You destroy us for what the fools among us have done? Allah then revealed to Mūsā that the seventy had themselves taken part in the worship of the calf. Later He restored them to life one by one, with the rest looking on as each man rose. That is the background to the words of the next verse.

### What the demand reveals

The devotional reading takes the episode as a lesson about how a servant should approach his Lord. To demand to see the divine Essence, without any gift of grace that would make such a vision possible, is open sacrilege, and it is one of the signs of distance and wretchedness. To bear witness instead to the attribute of divine governance, as it is unveiled through displays of might joined to the kindnesses of nearness, is a mark of communion and a token of happiness. The seventy spoke out of ignorance and without a shred of shame, and so the convulsion and the blast of the thunderbolt overtook them.

### In short

- After hearing Allah's speech at the Mount, seventy chosen Israelites demanded to see Him openly before they would believe, and were struck dead by a thunderbolt.
- Ibn Zayd's account places the demand after Mūsā brought the Tablets and the people refused to accept the Book on his word.
- They were struck in turns, some watching the others, and Mūsā pleaded for his people, learning that the seventy had themselves been calf-worshippers.
- Demanding to see Allah's Essence without His grace is an act of insolence, and the sign of a heart far from Him.

<<<VERSE 2:56>>>
Verse 56 records the favour behind the previous verse: "Then We brought you back to life after your death, so that perhaps you would be grateful". The seventy who had been struck down were raised again, and the reason given is the same as in verse 52, that a people so treated ought to be thankful.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then We brought you back to life after your death"**. The verse speaks of death, which settles that the thunderbolt actually killed them. Ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said that death was their punishment and that they were raised afterwards so that they could live out the rest of their lives, and Qatādah said the same. One early commentary puts it: We revived you after burning you. The reports also explain why Allah answered Mūsā's (Moses') plea. The Israelites had always been suspicious of him, and he feared they would accuse him of leading the seventy to a lonely place and having them killed. He prayed to be spared so vile a charge, and Allah granted his prayer and gave the men new life.

**"so that perhaps you would be grateful"** means so that you might give thanks for this favour of Ours, for the blessings granted to you in this way, and specifically for being brought back to life.

### Were they still bound by the law?

Ibn Zayd's account continues past the resurrection. Mūsā told them to take Allah's Book, and they refused. He asked what was the matter with them, and they said: the matter is that we died and came back to life. He told them again to take the Book, and again they refused, so Allah sent angels who lifted the mountain over them, an event this sūrah recalls a few verses further on. This shows that the Children of Israel were still required to obey the commandments after they were revived. Al-Māwardī, however, recorded two opinions on the question. The first held that since they had witnessed such miracles they were compelled to believe, and so were no longer under obligation. The second held that they remained bound by the commandments, since no responsible adult is ever free of them. Al-Qurṭubī judged the second opinion correct. The fact that the Israelites saw tremendous calamities and wonders did not release them from responsibility. They remained accountable, and he regarded this as plain. Allah knows best.

### The generosity in the pardon

The devotional reading sees in the resurrection a pattern of Allah's dealing with sinners. He restored them to consciousness after the blows of punishment had overwhelmed them, both to carry the decree through to its end and to put into effect His way of pardoning sin. One of the faces of generosity is to draw the veil over the faults of servants, and that is what He did for men who had just demanded to see Him.

### In short

- The thunderbolt killed the seventy, and Allah revived them, partly in answer to Mūsā's fear of being accused of their murder.
- They were raised to complete their lives and to be grateful for the favour.
- Al-Māwardī reports a view that after witnessing such miracles they were no longer under obligation, but al-Qurṭubī and the sequel in Ibn Zayd's account show that they remained bound by the commandments.
- Reviving the sinners after punishment is an instance of Allah's way of pardoning and veiling faults.

<<<VERSE 2:57>>>
Verse 57 moves from the calamities Allah rescued the Israelites from to the comforts He surrounded them with in the desert. It recalls how "We shaded you with clouds and sent down to you manna and quails", with the invitation, "Eat from the good things We have provided for you", and closes with a verdict on their response: "The evildoers certainly did not wrong Us, but wronged themselves".

### Where and when this happened

These favours belong to the years in the wilderness of Tīh. The Israelites were originally a people of Syria who had moved to Egypt in the time of Yūsuf (Joseph) and settled there, while Syria itself had fallen under the rule of a people called the ʿAmāliqah (Amalekites). Once Pharaoh had drowned and peace was theirs, Allah ordered them to march against the Amalekites and win back their homeland. They set out, but as they neared Syria and learned how strong the enemy was, their courage failed and they refused to fight in the jihād. Allah punished this disobedience with forty years of wandering, during which they could not even make their way back to Egypt. The wilderness was not large, a stretch of about ten miles between Egypt and Syria, but they would march a whole day toward Egypt, camp for the night, and wake to find themselves exactly where they had set out. For forty years they wandered in this futile rage and frustration, and that is why the place is called Tīh, a word that means having lost one's way.

It was a bare expanse with no tree or building to shelter them from heat or cold, no food and no clothing. In answer to Mūsā's (Moses') prayer, Allah provided miraculously for every need. When the sun became unbearable He gave them the shade of a thin white cloud. When hunger set in He gave them manna and quails. When they were thirsty He told Mūsā to strike a rock with his staff and twelve springs gushed out, as another passage recounts alongside the clouds, the manna and the quails (7:160). When they complained of the dark nights He set a pillar of light over them that never went out. When their clothing began to wear through He worked a further wonder: their garments neither got dirty nor wore thin, and the children's clothes grew with them. Al-Qurṭubī gathered these reports together.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We shaded you with clouds"** refers to the white clouds that sheltered the Children of Israel from the heat of the sun during the years of wandering. In the long hadith about the trials, an-Nasāʾī records Ibn ʿAbbās saying that Allah shaded them with clouds in those years, and as-Suddī, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, Abū Mijlaz, Ibn ʿUmar and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas are all reported to have said the like. Al-Ḥasan and Qatādah placed it in the desert, where the clouds stood between them and the sun. Ibn Jarīr mentions that several scholars described this cloud as cooler and finer than the kind we know.

**"and sent down to you manna"**. Ibn ʿAbbās, in the report of ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah, said the manna used to come down onto the trees and they ate as much of it as they wished. Qatādah said it was whiter than milk and sweeter than honey, and that it rained down on them the way snow falls, from dawn until sunrise. Each person gathered what he needed for that day, because anything kept longer spoiled, except that on Friday, the sixth day, he gathered enough for two days, since on the Sabbath no one left home to seek food or anything else. All this took place in the wilderness. Scholars distinguish this heavenly manna from the manna familiar to them, a nutritious and sweet substance that can serve as food on its own and becomes a sweet drink when mixed with water, and which changes its character when combined with other foods. One commentary identifies it with the substance called turunjabīn, another describes it simply as honeydew, something sweet produced in abundance that they could easily collect, and another as a sweet drink like honey. The word mann itself means a gift or favour. But manna is not restricted to one thing. The Ṣaḥīḥ records from Saʿīd ibn Zayd that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that truffles (al-kamʾah) are a kind of manna and that their juice is a cure for the eyes. Imām Aḥmad also recorded this hadith, as did the group of compilers apart from Abū Dāwūd, and at-Tirmidhī graded it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. At-Tirmidhī alone recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that the ʿajwah date is from Paradise and is a cure for poison, and that truffles are a form of manna whose juice heals the eye.

**"and quails"**. The salwā, according to Ibn ʿAbbās in the same report, is a bird resembling the quail, and the same view is related from Mujāhid, ash-Shaʿbī, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrimah and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas. ʿIkrimah also described it as a bird of Paradise about the size of a sparrow, and Qatādah said it was like a sparrow. An Israelite could catch as many as he needed for the day, since the meat would otherwise spoil, and on Friday he caught enough for the Sabbath as well. The birds did not flee but gathered around them, so that they could be caught with little effort. Because both the manna and the quails came in this extraordinary way, the Qur'an says that Allah sent them down.

**"Eat from the good things We have provided for you"** means eat of the lawful and wholesome things We have given you. This form of command is a simple grant of permission that also guides to what is good, and the same words appear in the parallel account (7:160). It came with a condition: take what you actually need, and do not store any of it away for days to come. True piety, in the words of one commentator, includes living on the pure things of the earth, and here the pure things were delivered to their door.

**"The evildoers certainly did not wrong Us, but wronged themselves"**. The sense is: We commanded them to eat from what We provided and to perform the acts of worship, but they rebelled. The Israelites disobeyed the instruction about storing, and the hoarded meat began to rot. In this way the harm fell on them, not on Allah. Their ingratitude and disbelief did not touch Allah in the least, but they lost their share of the good and exposed themselves to suffering, since the evil consequences of what they did came back on them. The verse resembles the one addressed to the people of Sheba, who were told to eat of their Lord's provision and to thank Him (34:15). Yet the Children of Israel rebelled, disbelieved and wronged themselves even though they had seen clear signs, tremendous miracles and extraordinary events.

### The Companions of the Prophet ﷺ by contrast

Scholars pause here to point out the superiority of the Companions of Muḥammad ﷺ over the companions of all other prophets, in firmness in religion, in patience and in freedom from arrogance. The Companions travelled and fought with the Prophet ﷺ, as at the battle of Tabūk, in fierce heat and hardship, and never asked him for a miracle, although by Allah's leave one would have been easy for him. When they were hungry they only asked him to pray to Allah for an increase in their food. They gathered what little they had and brought it to him, he prayed for blessing on it and told them each to help themselves, and every vessel they owned was filled. When they needed rain he prayed, a cloud came, they drank, watered their camels and filled their water skins, and when they looked around they found that the rain had fallen only on their camp. That is the finest example of people who accept Allah's decision and follow His Messenger ﷺ.

### The Lord who buys the faulty

The devotional reading hears in "We shaded you with clouds" an allusion to the Lord's gentleness and generosity, and it imagines the Lord of the Worlds pleading with His servant. O hapless child of Ādam, why is there no love in you for Me, when love is My due? Why will you not trade with Me, when I am munificent and bounteous? Why do you not do business with Me, when I give in ample measure? My mercy is not narrow, and I hold back blessings from no one. Consider how I dealt with the Children of Israel, the blessings I heaped on them, the tenderness I showed them in that empty desert. Even when they turned away and broke My command I did not abandon them to ruin. I ordered the mist to shade them, the wind to set roasted birds in their hands, the clouds to rain manna and honey on them, and the pillar of light to brighten their moonless nights. When a child was born in that desert it came into the world with the clothes it needed, and as it grew those clothes never grew too old or too dirty for it, its ornament in life and its shroud in death. Was there any blessing I withheld from them, any tenderness I failed to show? And still they neither knew My worth nor thanked Me for the blessings. Then the plea turns personal. O hapless one, no one wants you as I want you. When you come, no one will buy you as I buy. When you put yourself up for sale, other buyers want only the flawless, while I take the flawed. Others send for the loyal, while I send for the disloyal. Come to Me in old age and I will deck the whole kingdom in your honour. Speak with Me while your youth is at its height and tomorrow, on the Day of Resurrection, I will take you under My shelter. People disobey for a lifetime and then come back in shame, and the answer they hear is: welcome, be at ease, greetings.

### In short

- The clouds, manna and quails belong to the forty years in the wilderness of Tīh, the punishment for refusing to fight the Amalekites for their homeland.
- The manna fell like snow, whiter than milk and sweeter than honey, and the quails let themselves be caught. Each day's portion had to be gathered that day, with a double portion on Friday for the Sabbath.
- After Allah had rescued them, He fed them without labour. The Prophet ﷺ said that truffles are a kind of manna and that their juice heals the eye, a hadith graded ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ by at-Tirmidhī.
- The Israelites hoarded against the command and the food rotted. Their ingratitude harmed only themselves, and did not touch Allah.
- The Companions of Muḥammad ﷺ, who endured hunger and heat at Tabūk without demanding miracles, stand in deliberate contrast to the people of this verse.
