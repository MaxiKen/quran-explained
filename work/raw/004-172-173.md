<<<VERSE 4:172>>>
The servant who is not ashamed: "The Messiah would never be too proud to be a servant of Allah, nor would the angels nearest to Allah. Those who are too proud and arrogant to worship Him will be brought before Him all together" (4:172). The opening statement of the verse puts the question of honor where the Christians misplaced it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Messiah would never be too proud to be a servant of Allah"** answers a taunt. The Christians told the Prophet ﷺ that what he said of Jesus was shameful. Al-Kalbī preserves the fuller dialogue: the delegation of Najrān said, why do you insult our lord? He asked who their lord was, and they said Jesus. What do I say about him? he asked. You say he is the servant and messenger of Allah, they answered. He told them it was not shameful for Jesus to be the slave of Allah. They insisted it was shameful, and this verse was revealed. Qatādah said the same of it: such servants will never be arrogant. Masīḥ will never scorn his servitude, will never not acknowledge it, for being a servant of Allah, devoted to His worship and obedient to His will, is an honor in itself, a nobility of the highest class. Sayyidnā ʿĪsā and the near angels are the best testifiers to the worth of that blessing. How could they spurn such an honor? His first words, as an infant, were: "I am God's servant" (19:30). How, then, is he taken for a god?

**"nor would the angels nearest to Allah"** names the favored angels, the carriers of the Throne, who never go against Allah's instruction, never disregard being His servants. The mention is a splendid digression, refuting those who claim the angels are gods or daughters of God, just as the Christians' claim was refuted before them. It does not imply the angels are superior to the Messiah. God was addressing them in accordance with their beliefs, for they believed in the superiority of angels over human beings.

**"Those who are too proud and arrogant to worship Him will be brought before Him all together"** warns whoever scorns His service and grows proud. Proud, Ibn ʿAbbās said, means insolent, and all such, believers and unbelievers, will He assemble on the Day of Resurrection, judging between them with a justice that cannot wrong.

### The measure of greatness

The worst disgrace imaginable lies in worshipping someone other than Allah. That is what the Christians did in taking Masīḥ as the son of Allah and the object of worship, and what the disbelievers did in taking angels as daughters of Allah and worshipping them along with their idols. For them there is punishment and disgrace. The inward reading adds: how can they disdain His worship when honor and dignity come through worship? How can they be too proud to humble themselves when ruin comes from pride? Servants become beautiful in their humility toward their masters. Prophets and angels have reached the highest stage of awareness of God's greatness and of their own helplessness, and their realization surpasses that of all others. One who has realized his own helplessness has, in essence, found how he relates to God.

### In short

- Servanthood is the honor, not the shame, and the Messiah said so with his first words in the cradle.
- The near angels own the same servitude, which settles the angels-worshippers as it settles the Christians.
- Insolence gathers all the proud before the Judge on a day none escapes.

<<<VERSE 4:173>>>
The two paydays: "As for those who believe and do good, He will reward them in full and increase them out of His grace. But those who are too proud and arrogant, He will subject them to a painful punishment. And besides Allah they will find no protector or helper" (4:173). The verse sorts the assembled crowd into two.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who believe and do good"** joins faith in Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'ān to acts of obedience done with sincerity, in agreement with the sacred law.

**"He will reward them in full"** means wages for deeds paid without reduction, reward in Paradise.

**"and increase them out of His grace"** adds bounty upon bounty, kindness, ample mercy and favor: what no eye has seen nor ear heard, what never entered any man's heart to wish.

**"But those who are too proud and arrogant"** describes refusal of worship and obedience out of arrogance, being too proud even to believe.

**"He will subject them to a painful punishment"** means the chastisement of the Fire with degradation, disgrace and dishonor, earned by arrogance and rebellion. As the Book says elsewhere: "Verily! Those who scorn My worship, they will surely enter Hell in humiliation" (40:60).

**"And besides Allah they will find no protector or helper"** removes every recourse: no relative to benefit them, no friend to ward off the punishment, no helper to shield them from it.

### The deepest pain

The inward reading names the deepest pain: communion with the Beloved, never to be attained again by those who once came to know His majesty. Once what they know becomes indisputable, being left behind will be plain to them. Their sorrow on that day, mourning what slipped from their hands, will outburn every other punishment. The aware person has eyes and reaches his destination. The unaware is blind, and wanders astray until he falls into the pit of abomination.

### In short

- Full wages plus increase, beyond what eye, ear and heart have ever known, await faith with good deeds.
- The proud enter Hell in humiliation, with no protector and no helper besides Allah.
- The sharpest pain is the communion forever missed by those who once knew His majesty.
