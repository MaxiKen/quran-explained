<<<VERSE 2:165>>>
The preceding verses set out Allah's Oneness strongly and positively, with its proofs. Verse 165 turns to the error of those who, despite such clear signs, give others a share in His divinity and treat them as the caretakers of their needs. "Still there are some who take others as Allah's equal—they love them as they should love Allah—but the ˹true˺ believers love Allah even more." The verse continues: "If only the wrongdoers could see the ˹horrible˺ punishment ˹awaiting them˺, they would certainly realize that all power belongs to Allah and that Allah is indeed severe in punishment". Here Allah describes the condition of the polytheists in this life, before turning in the next verses to their destination in the Hereafter.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Still there are some who take others as Allah's equal"**. Among people are disbelievers who set up rivals and equals to Allah, idols that they worship alongside Him, magnifying them and submitting to them. Yet Allah alone is worthy of worship, and He has neither rival nor opponent nor partner. The Ṣaḥīḥayn record that ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ which sin is the greatest, and he answered: to set up a rival to Allah when He alone created you. One view is that the verse refers to leaders whom people took as gods. Another connects its revelation with hypocrites hiding precious wealth, whether burying gold and silver or concealing it in caves.

**"they love them as they should love Allah"**. Their attachment to these objects reaches the proportions of the love that is due to Allah alone, a love like the love sincere believers have for Allah.

**"but the ˹true˺ believers love Allah even more."**. The believers' love for Allah is more intense and more lasting than the disbelievers' love for their idols. Because they love Him, know His greatness, revere Him and believe in His Oneness, they associate nothing with Him in worship but worship Him alone, depend on Him and seek His help in every need. A polytheist may turn away from his self-made god when loss threatens, and when things go badly such people call on Allah alone. The believer trusts Allah in gain and loss alike, keeps His love and pleasure as his lasting possession, and never leaves his Creator whatever the odds. The believers never reject Allah, whereas the others soon abandon their idols for Him when hardship comes.

**"If only the wrongdoers could see the ˹horrible˺ punishment ˹awaiting them˺"**. If those who wronged themselves by taking equals to Allah could see the torment on the Day of Judgement, they would behold a terrible sight. The verb "see" (yarā) is read as active or passive, "they see" or "they are made to see", and on one reading its subject is the listener, on another the wrongdoers themselves, in which case it carries the sense of knowing. The response to "if only" is left unstated. If these people knew the terrible punishment that awaits them for their disbelief and shirk, they would shun the deviation they live by.

**"they would certainly realize that all power belongs to Allah and that Allah is indeed severe in punishment"**. All might, power and the ability to vanquish belong to Allah, and His punishment in the Hereafter is severe. At resurrection the idolaters will directly see this truth. Had they possessed that certainty here, they would have believed rather than appoint rivals to Allah. The verse also contains a subtle hint: the opportunity to correct themselves was there. Their frustrations with their gods could have taught them that these were helpless and that real power rests with Allah. They missed it, and must now learn the hard way, hence the stern warning.

### How people make gods

By nature and circumstance human beings look for an external support, a being that compensates for their helplessness and gives them confidence. To give someone this place in one's life is in effect to accept that being as a deity, and whatever becomes the focus of a person's love, devotion and reverence is held to be his god. Since Allah remains invisible in this world, those who judge by appearances tend to grant a visible being the status that belongs only to the Almighty, usually leaders who become the centre of public attention through some mark of distinction. The vacuum in human nature was meant to be filled by Allah, but many fill it with something else, impressed by visible pomp and splendour, by special talents, by seats sanctified by centuries of tradition, by a large following, or by the halo of legend around public figures, and so come to regard certain persons as possessing extraordinary power. In truth, no one in Allah's universe possesses any power or greatness but Allah. A human being can hold the status of divinity only so long as Allah remains unseen. When He appears on the Day of Judgement everything will change so drastically that people will flee from one another. Those who take pride today in their devotion to great men, thinking they are clinging to a firm rock that will help them in the Hereafter, will find that attachment meaningless, as if it had never existed, and will be left to look back on their lives in helpless regret.

### A love that never turns away

The devotional reading finds complete honour for the faithful and Allah's friends in this verse alone. Even without another verse about them, this declaration would establish their eminence. The Lord of the Worlds describes their love for Him as deeper and fuller than the unbelievers' love for their idols. Every so often the unbelievers set up another idol. Like poor people they are content with something carved of wood, and when they can afford it they take the wooden one down and make another of silver or gold. If their love were real, why would they turn from one to another? A man once met a woman among the recognisers of Allah and told her that his all was busy with her all and that he had lost himself in love for her. She asked why he did not look at her sister, who was more beautiful. He asked where the sister was, wanting to see her. Her answer was to send him away as an idler for whom passion was no fit occupation, since a man whose claim of love was true would have had no interest in anyone else. The love of the faithful is not like that. They never turn away from Allah and never incline to another, for if they did they would never find His like however much they sought. Poor man, Allah has many servants like you, and if something bad is to happen to you it will happen, but how can you turn away, when you will not find a Lord like Him? Shiblī traced a lesson in Sufism to a dog asleep outside a house. Driven away repeatedly by the owner, it repeatedly returned. He thought the dog base. In the story Allah then enabled it to speak, asking where else it could go when this man was its owner. The lover will not leave the Friend for a hundred cruelties, and even if they increase he will not be troubled, for he chose Him over everyone else and would have no excuse for complaining.

### In short

- Despite the signs, some give to others the love that is due to Allah alone, and setting up a rival to Him is the greatest sin (Ibn Masʿūd, Ṣaḥīḥayn).
- The believers love Allah more, because they love Him in hardship and ease and never turn away, while the idolater abandons his god when trouble comes.
- Had the wrongdoers seen in this world what they will see on the Day of Judgement, that all power is Allah's and His punishment severe, they would never have taken equals.
- People deify visible leaders and traditions to fill a need meant for Allah, but every such attachment dissolves on the Day of Judgement.
- Inwardly, true love is constancy: the faithful never exchange the Friend for another, as Shiblī learned from the dog that would not leave its master's door.

<<<VERSE 2:166>>>
Verse 165 ended by saying that Allah is severe in punishment. How severe will now be shown. "˹Consider the Day˺ when those who misled others will disown their followers—when they face the torment—and the bonds that united them will be cut off". The severity of the punishment will unfold when the leaders whom the polytheists followed disown their own devotees, when both witness the torment, and when every bond of leading and following between them is snapped.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Consider the Day˺ when those who misled others will disown their followers"**. The leaders who were followed in disbelief will reject the weak and lowly people who followed them, denying that they ever misled them, once they see the terrors of the Day of Judgement. It is much like what happens in this world when accomplices in a crime wriggle out of it on arrest, going so far as to deny recognising one another. The angels whom the idolaters claimed to worship will declare their innocence of them: "We disassociate ourselves ˹from them˺ before You. It was not us that they used to worship" (28:63), and "Glory be to You! Our loyalty is to You, not them. In fact, they ˹only˺ followed the ˹temptations of evil˺ jinn, in whom most of them had faith" (34:41). The angels thus declare themselves Allah's walī, His loyal protected ones, and not the idolaters'. The jinn too will disown those who worshipped them: "And when ˹such˺ people will be gathered together, those ˹gods˺ will be their enemies and will disown their worship" (46:6), for who could be more astray than one who calls on what cannot answer until the Day of Judgement (46:5). The false gods "will deny their worship and turn against them" (19:81-82). Prophet Ibrāhīm (Abraham) told his people that they had taken idols only to keep harmony among themselves in this life, "But on the Day of Judgment you will disown and curse one another. Your home will be the Fire, and you will have no helper!" (29:25). The lowly will say to the arrogant, "Had it not been for you, we would certainly have been believers", and the arrogant will reply, "Did we ever hinder you from guidance after it came to you? In fact, you were wicked ˹on your own˺" (34:31-33). Satan himself will say after the judgement, "I did not have any authority over you. I only called you, and you responded to me. So do not blame me; blame yourselves" (14:22).

**"when they face the torment"**. Both the leaders and the lowly behold the punishment in the Hereafter.

**"and the bonds that united them will be cut off"**. The pledges and affection, the ties of friendship and kinship that existed between them on earth, collapse. ʿAṭāʾ reported that Ibn ʿAbbās explained the bonds as the friendship, and Mujāhid said the same in a narration of Ibn Abī Najīḥ. When they see Allah's torment, their power and every means of salvation are cut off, with no way of making amends and no escape from the Fire.

### Why the idolaters' love fails and the believers' love holds

In the devotional reading, the unbelievers loved their idols in keeping with caprice and nature, not reality. At resurrection, the first sight of punishment exposes their lack of any secure support. They then abandon their idols. The friendship of the faithful, by contrast, is the fruit of the Real's own friendship, as He says: "who love Him and are loved by Him" (5:54). Difficult paths and trials therefore do not diminish their love or turn them from the Real. They first endure the pains of dying. Their pure spirit is then taken, and years pass while they lie in the dust. Then they are frightened again and again at the resurrection in its various stations, rebuked and shown severity of many kinds, and some are kept for a time in hell. Despite all these tribulations, at every moment they fall further into passion and spend their spirits and hearts even more in friendship for the Real, saying in the language of their state: if You are happy with my grief, give me grief upon grief, and away with a passion that lessens under a hundred cruelties. That is why He said that those who have faith are more intense in love for Allah.

### In short

- The punishment of shirk begins with betrayal: the leaders and false gods, whether angels, jinn, idols or Satan, disown those who followed them (28:63, 34:41, 46:6, 19:82, 29:25, 34:31-33, 14:22).
- Every tie of friendship, kinship and loyalty between leader and led is severed before their eyes, and with it every means of escape (Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid).
- Inwardly, a love founded on caprice collapses at the first sight of torment, while a love born of Allah's love (5:54) only deepens under trial.

<<<VERSE 2:167>>>
The followers now face the leaders who abandoned them. They cry, "If only we could have a second chance, we would disown them as they disowned us." Their wish cannot undo the choices made in earthly life. The verse turns from that futile hope to the regret their deeds now bring.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The ˹misled˺ followers will cry, 'If only we could have a second chance, we would disown them as they disowned us.'"** The weak followers wish to return to the world. They would refuse the allegiance their leaders once demanded and turn the rejection back upon them. They also claim they would abandon the idols and worship Allah alone.

**"And so Allah will make them remorseful of their misdeeds."** Their past actions appear as causes of anguish rather than resources for rescue. Seeing the punishment and their mutual rejection makes the consequences of following falsehood plain.

**"And they will never ˹be able to˺ leave the Fire"** The warning includes leaders and followers alike. For the shirk described here, associating others with Allah, the punishment is an enduring stay. Neither group can escape by blaming the other.

### Why would a return not repair their choices?

The words if only express a wish, with we would disown as its imagined result. The followers want another chance partly to settle the account with those who used them. The explanation also reads their promise of exclusive worship as false: if returned, they would go back to the forbidden conduct.

Their distress therefore does not establish a transformed purpose. The opportunity for obedient choice belonged to the life they have already spent. Regret at the result cannot now replace those deeds.

### How are the lost deeds pictured?

The explanation recalls (25:23), where apparent good is reduced to scattered dust. In (14:18), the deeds of those denying their Lord resemble ashes swept away by a storm. The mirage in (24:39) promises water but yields nothing when approached.

Each comparison reveals a failure at the moment of need. What seemed usable cannot provide the hoped-for benefit. The followers now see not only the punishment but the emptiness of the actions in which they had placed confidence.

### What does the inward reading emphasise?

The devotional reflection speaks of tasting the bitterness of attachment to created things. The companionship once trusted now brings only anguish. Distance from Allah cannot be repaired by the very relationships that helped produce it.

The leaders' rejection reveals that dependence, while the followers' desired revenge remains powerless. Neither offers a way out of the final consequence.

### In short

- The followers wish to reject the leaders who have abandoned them.
- Their promised change is not accepted as proof that they would obey if returned.
- Their deeds are pictured as dust, ashes and a mirage at the time of need.
- Neither leaders nor followers escape the Fire through shifting blame.

<<<VERSE 2:168>>>
Having declared that He alone is God and the Creator of everything, Allah now presents Himself as the Sustainer of all His creation and mentions one of His favours. "O humanity! Eat from what is lawful and good on the earth and do not follow Satan's footsteps. He is truly your sworn enemy". The verse was revealed when some people declared it unlawful to use a camel that had been let loose for the idols. Al-Kalbī, reporting from Abū Ṣāliḥ, said it came down about the tribes of Thaqīf, Khuzāʿah and ʿĀmir ibn Ṣaʿṣaʿah, who forbade themselves certain foods and meats, namely the baḥīrah, al-sāʾibah, al-waṣīlah and al-ḥāmiyah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O humanity! Eat from what is lawful and good on the earth"**. O people, and in the first place O people of Makkah, eat of the meat, vegetables and fruits of the earth, of what is cultivated and of the cattle you raise, everything pure and lawful that Allah has not prohibited and that does no harm to body or mind. The root of ḥalāl means to open a knot, so what is made ḥalāl is something whose restriction has been untied. Ṭayyib, good, covers the clean and the pure in both senses, lawful and naturally wholesome and delicious. The Companion Sahl ibn ʿAbd Allāh said that salvation depends on three things: eating ḥalāl, fulfilling the divine obligations, and following the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.

**"and do not follow Satan's footsteps."**. Khuṭuwāt is the plural of khuṭwah, the distance between the feet in striding, so Satan's footsteps are his ways and methods, the temptations he embellishes and the whisperings by which he misguides his followers, here his suggestion that cultivating land and raising cattle, or certain animals, are unlawful. The Jāhiliyyah practices meant are the baḥīrah, a she-camel whose milk was reserved for the idols so that no one might milk her, the sāʾibah, a she-camel set loose to pasture freely for the idols with nothing allowed to be carried on her, and the waṣīlah, a she-camel freed for the idols because she gave birth to a female at her first and again at her second delivery, together with everything else Satan made attractive to them. Muslim records from ʿIyāḍ ibn Ḥimār that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ related Allah's words: every kind of wealth I have given My servants is lawful for them, and I created My servants upright (ḥunafāʾ), but the devils came to them, led them away from their religion and forbade them what I had allowed. Qatādah and al-Suddī said that every act of disobedience to Allah is among Satan's footsteps, and ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd reports Ibn ʿAbbās as saying that any vow or oath made in anger is among Satan's footsteps, and its expiation is that of a vow.

**"He is truly your sworn enemy"**. His enmity towards you is plain, and no one with sense follows an enemy who wants to harm and mislead him. Allah warns elsewhere: "Surely Satan is an enemy to you, so take him as an enemy. He only invites his followers to become inmates of the Blaze" (35:6), and, of Iblīs and his offspring, "Would you then take him and his descendants as patrons instead of Me, although they are your enemy? What an evil alternative for the wrongdoers ˹to choose˺" (18:50).

### Rulings drawn from the verse

Dedicating animals to idols, or to a saint or anyone other than Allah, is declared unlawful in verse 173 that follows. This verse does not deny the unlawfulness of such a dedicated animal, as some have wrongly supposed. The point is to reject turning permitted animals into forbidden ones through dedication to idols. Leave them as they are and use them for your own benefit. Inventing prohibitions on one's own authority is a grave sin. Dedication to someone other than Allah makes the thing impure, and that impurity makes it unlawful. If someone dedicates an animal to other than Allah out of ignorance or carelessness and wishes to put it right, he should resolve to withdraw from the deed and repent of it, and then the meat of that animal becomes lawful for him.

Polytheism is the worship of something other than Allah in order to satisfy one's feelings of devotion and veneration. Allah is the human being's greatest and most essential need. The desire to worship Him belongs to human nature so deeply that life cannot dispense with Him. Going astray does not mean abandoning God altogether. The real God is replaced by a false one. That is why the Islamic law holds unlawful everything that in any degree diverts a person's natural desire for God in another direction. The polytheists set animals free in the name of their deities and did not use them for food or work, which is tantamount to granting divinity to an object, a prerogative of none but Allah, and dilutes the reverence that should be reserved for Him alone. Satan encourages people to channel their awe and reverence along other paths so that their attachment to Allah is weakened.

In the devotional reading, what is forbidden, however pleasing now, is contaminated in the end, and what is permitted, however hateful now, is wholesome in the end. The pure and permitted is what is acquired without forgetting the Real, what the gatherer obtains for His sake while witnessing Him in every state, and anything that makes you forget the Real or disobey Him is from Satan's footsteps.

### In short

- Allah, the sole Creator, is also the Sustainer, and He has made the good things of the earth lawful. Tribes like Thaqīf and Khuzāʿah had forbidden themselves animals such as the baḥīrah and sāʾibah, and the verse was revealed against this.
- Satan's footsteps are his ways of misguidance, including inventing prohibitions (Muslim from ʿIyāḍ ibn Ḥimār), every act of disobedience (Qatādah, al-Suddī) and angry oaths (Ibn ʿAbbās). He is a declared enemy (35:6, 18:50).
- Dedicating animals to other than Allah is forbidden (verse 173), and the believer's duty is not to make lawful things unlawful on his own authority. Repentance restores the lawfulness of a wrongly dedicated animal.
- Shirk replaces the true God with a false one, and the law blocks everything that diverts devotion from Allah.

<<<VERSE 2:169>>>
Verse 169 explains what following Satan's footsteps leads to. "He only incites you to commit evil and indecency, and to claim against Allah what you do not know". Your enemy Satan commands only what harms you, and worse.

- Satan's suggestions lead only to harmful sin and to indecency, and his whisper in the heart is opposed by the angel's (Ibn Masʿūd).

<<<VERSE 2:170>>>
The idolaters receive a call to Allah's revelation through His Messenger ﷺ. Asked to leave ignorance and misguidance, they instead invoke ancestral practice. "When it is said to them, 'Follow what Allah has revealed,' they reply, 'No! We ˹only˺ follow what we found our forefathers practicing.'" The verse ends: "˹Would they still do so,˺ even if their forefathers had ˹absolutely˺ no understanding or guidance". Ibn Isḥāq reports Ibn ʿAbbās's account of a group of Jews invited to Islam by the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. They refused. Their response was to say that they preferred the practice inherited from their fathers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When it is said to them, 'Follow what Allah has revealed,'"**. When the disbelievers, and the Arab idolaters (mushrikīn) in particular, are told to follow the guidance and light Allah has sent down, the affirmation of His Oneness and the lawfulness He has declared of what is cultivated and of the cattle people raise.

**"they reply, 'No! We ˹only˺ follow what we found our forefathers practicing.'"**. They resist stubbornly, insisting on the customary practice inherited from their fathers, the worship of idols and false deities and the forbidding of the camel let loose and the slitting of the camel's ear for the baḥīrah, because they assumed their forebears had been divinely appointed to follow that way.

**"˹Would they still do so,˺ even if their forefathers had ˹absolutely˺ no understanding or guidance"**. The question is one of repudiation. How could they go on following their fathers in all conditions and to the exclusion of every other way, even if those fathers understood nothing of religion and had no guidance to the practice of any prophet? One reading makes it: even if their fathers understood nothing about the affairs of this world either. Those they imitate had no sound understanding and no guidance to the truth that Allah accepts.

### Blind following and legitimate following

The verse censures the blind following of forefathers, but it also lays down, in the words "no understanding or guidance", the condition that distinguishes forbidden following from the permissible kind. Following the forefathers is censured because they lacked reason and guidance. Guidance means the injunctions revealed openly and clearly by Allah, and reason here means the binding guidance deduced by ijtihād from the recognised sources of the Sharīʿah. Those named in the verse cannot be followed because they had neither a revealed law from Allah nor the ability to derive rulings from His Word. There is a subtle hint in this. If one is satisfied that a certain scholar has perfect knowledge of the Qur'an and Sunnah, and that where no clear and direct text exists he has the expertise of a mujtahid to derive rulings from them by analogy, it is permissible to follow such a scholar. This does not mean obeying his injunctions or his person. It means obeying the injunctions of Allah alone, but since direct and trustworthy knowledge of those injunctions in all their ramifications is not available to most people, they must follow a mujtahid scholar in order to act in accordance with them. Those who hasten to quote verses like this one against following the great mujtahid imams are themselves unaware of their proven meaning.

Al-Qurṭubī said in his comments on this verse that the prohibition refers to following forefathers in false beliefs and deeds. Following them in correct beliefs and good deeds is not included, for Sūrat Yūsuf presents it clearly in the words of Yūsuf (Joseph): "I have shunned the faith of a people who disbelieve in Allah and deny the Hereafter" and "I follow the faith of my fathers: Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob" (12:37-38). Following forefathers in falsehood is forbidden, while in truth it is permissible and indeed desirable. Al-Qurṭubī also took up the question of following the mujtahid imams here, noting that some quote this verse to criticise taqlīd. He agreed that following falsehood was wrong. Following truth was a different matter: it is fundamental to religion and helps protect the Muslims' faith. Someone unable to exercise ijtihād must follow qualified guidance in religious matters.

The devotional reading describes eyes fixed on people of matching form, class, kind and ancestry. Their settled path leads them into the fire. Recognising their ancestors' lack of restraining intelligence and unifying guidance would have led them to resist that hostile example. They would have seen its opposition to Allah's command. Instead, insight was taken from them, along with the proofs that bring certainty.

### In short

- Called to Allah's revelation, the disbelievers, including a group of Jews (Ibn ʿAbbās via Ibn Isḥāq), plead the custom of their fathers, who had neither understanding nor guidance.
- The verse condemns following ancestors in falsehood, not following the guided, as Yūsuf's example shows (12:37-38). Following a qualified mujtahid is really obedience to Allah's law for those who cannot derive it themselves (al-Qurṭubī).
- Inwardly, those who cannot look beyond their own kind lose the light of insight.

<<<VERSE 2:171>>>
Allah now gives a parable of the disbelievers in relation to the one who calls them. "The example of the disbelievers ˹not responding to the Messenger's warning˺ is like a flock not comprehending the calls and cries of the shepherd." The verse concludes: "˹They are wilfully˺ deaf, dumb and blind so they have no understanding". Just as Allah says elsewhere that to those who disbelieve in the Hereafter belong all evil qualities (16:60), so here He likens them to animals.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The example of the disbelievers ˹not responding to the Messenger's warning˺ is like a flock not comprehending the calls and cries of the shepherd."**. In their injustice, misguidance and ignorance, and in their stubborn following of the path of their fathers, the disbelievers and mushrik idolaters facing Muḥammad ﷺ are like wandering camels and sheep. When the shepherd calls them to what benefits them, telling them for instance to eat or drink, they hear only unintelligible sound, a shout and a cry, and understand nothing of what is said. In the same way the disbelievers who listen to an admonition hear the voice but not the meaning that is meant to correct them. Ibn ʿAbbās and Abū al-ʿĀliyah give this explanation, alongside Mujāhid and ʿIkrimah. It is also related from ʿAṭāʾ and al-Ḥasan, Qatādah and ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī, and al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas. The comparison joins the caller and the unresponsive disbelievers. They correspond to a shepherd and a flock that hears the sound without understanding it.

**"˹They are wilfully˺ deaf, dumb and blind"**. They are deaf because they do not hear the truth so as to benefit from it, dumb because they do not utter it and are tight-lipped when it comes to accepting it, and blind because they do not see or recognise its path and way, nor their own benefit and loss. They pretend to be deaf, dumb and blind where the truth and guidance are concerned.

**"so they have no understanding"**. With their vital senses so dulled they comprehend no admonition. They fail to understand Allah's command and the Prophet's ﷺ call. Camels and cattle similarly hear a shepherd without comprehending his words.

### How superstition hardens into deafness

Once something that is not God is accepted as God through a superstitious attitude, many other evils follow. People may assign an animal supernatural powers belonging to Allah alone. They then treat it as a means of nearness to Him and a giver of blessings. A later generation inherits these superstitions as settled beliefs and zealously defends them as the ancestors' sacred way. Rational examination of their beliefs then meets resistance. Each generation deepens the resistance until arguments can no longer be heard or understood. They behave as though sight, hearing and thought were unavailable for considering what is put before them.

In the devotional reading, without the ears of comprehension and acceptance their bodily hearing did them no good. They sank to the level of animals, incapable of attainment, and whoever is content to be like an animal does not have much worth.

### In short

- The disbelievers who ignore the Messenger's ﷺ call are like a flock that hears the shepherd's voice but grasps nothing of it, a reading shared by Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatādah and others (cf. 16:60).
- They are deaf to the truth, dumb in confessing it and blind to its path, and so understand nothing.
- Inherited superstition hardens generation by generation until people lose the very capacity to weigh an argument, and one content to live like an animal has little worth.
