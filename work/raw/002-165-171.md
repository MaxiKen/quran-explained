The preceding verses set out Allah's Oneness strongly and positively, with its proofs. ### Phrase by phrase

**"Still there are some who take others as Allah's equal"**. Among people are disbelievers who set up rivals and equals to Allah, idols that they worship alongside Him, magnifying them and submitting to them. Yet Allah alone is worthy of worship, and He has neither rival nor opponent nor partner. The Ṣaḥīḥayn record that ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ which sin is the greatest, and he answered: to set up a rival to Allah when He alone created you. One view is that the verse refers to leaders whom people took as gods, and another that it was revealed about hypocrites who buried their gold and silver or hid it in caves.

**"they love them as they should love Allah"**. Their attachment to these objects reaches the proportions of the love that is due to Allah alone, a love like the love sincere believers have for Allah.

**"but the ˹true˺ believers love Allah even more."**. The believers' love for Allah is more intense and more lasting than the disbelievers' love for their idols. Because they love Him, know His greatness, revere Him and believe in His Oneness, they associate nothing with Him in worship but worship Him alone, depend on Him and seek His help in every need. A polytheist may turn away from his self-made god when loss threatens, and when things go badly such people call on Allah alone. The believer trusts Allah in gain and loss alike, keeps His love and pleasure as his lasting possession, and never leaves his Creator whatever the odds. The believers never reject Allah, whereas the others soon abandon their idols for Him when hardship comes.

**"If only the wrongdoers could see the ˹horrible˺ punishment ˹awaiting them˺"**. If those who wronged themselves by taking equals to Allah could see the torment on the Day of Judgement, they would behold a terrible sight. The verb "see" (yarā) is read as active or passive, "they see" or "they are made to see", and on one reading its subject is the listener, on another the wrongdoers themselves, in which case it carries the sense of knowing. The response to "if only" is left unstated. If these people knew the terrible punishment that awaits them for their disbelief and shirk, they would shun the deviation they live by.

**"they would certainly realize that all power belongs to Allah and that Allah is indeed severe in punishment"**. All might, power and the ability to vanquish belong to Allah, and His punishment in the Hereafter is severe. Had the idolaters known this in the world as surely as they will see it with their own eyes on the Day of Resurrection, they would have believed and would not have set up equals to Him. The verse also contains a subtle hint: the opportunity to correct themselves was there. Their frustrations with their gods could have taught them that these were helpless and that real power rests with Allah. They missed it, and must now learn the hard way, hence the stern warning.

### How people make gods

By nature and circumstance human beings look for an external support, a being that compensates for their helplessness and gives them confidence. To give someone this place in one's life is in effect to accept that being as a deity, and whatever becomes the focus of a person's love, devotion and reverence is held to be his god. Since Allah remains invisible in this world, those who judge by appearances tend to grant a visible being the status that belongs only to the Almighty, usually leaders who become the centre of public attention through some mark of distinction. The vacuum in human nature was meant to be filled by Allah, but many fill it with something else, impressed by visible pomp and splendour, by special talents, by seats sanctified by centuries of tradition, by a large following, or by the halo of legend around public figures, and so come to regard certain persons as possessing extraordinary power. In truth, no one in Allah's universe possesses any power or greatness but Allah. A human being can hold the status of divinity only so long as Allah remains unseen. When He appears on the Day of Judgement everything will change so drastically that people will flee from one another. Those who take pride today in their devotion to great men, thinking they are clinging to a firm rock that will help them in the Hereafter, will find that attachment meaningless, as if it had never existed, and will be left to look back on their lives in helpless regret.

### A love that never turns away

In the devotional reading, if this were the only verse in the whole Qur'an about the faithful and the friends of Allah, their eminence would be complete, for the Lord of the Worlds is saying that they love Him more intensely and more completely than the unbelievers love their objects of worship. Every so often the unbelievers set up another idol. Like poor people they are content with something carved of wood, and when they can afford it they take the wooden one down and make another of silver or gold. If their love were real, why would they turn from one to another? A man once met a woman among the recognisers of Allah and told her that his all was busy with her all and that he had lost himself in love for her. She asked why he did not look at her sister, who was more beautiful. He asked where the sister was, wanting to see her. Her answer was to send him away as an idler for whom passion was no fit occupation, since a man whose claim of love was true would have had no interest in anyone else. The love of the faithful is not like that. They never turn away from Allah and never incline to another, for if they did they would never find His like however much they sought. Poor man, Allah has many servants like you, and if something bad is to happen to you it will happen, but how can you turn away, when you will not find a Lord like Him? Shiblī said that he learned Sufism from a dog sleeping at the door of a house. The owner came out and kept driving it away, and it kept coming back. Shiblī thought how base the dog was, until Allah gave the dog speech and it said: O Shaykh, where should I go? He is my owner. The lover will not leave the Friend for a hundred cruelties, and even if they increase he will not be troubled, for he chose Him over everyone else and would have no excuse for complaining.

### In short

- Despite the signs, some give to others the love that is due to Allah alone, and setting up a rival to Him is the greatest sin (Ibn Masʿūd, Ṣaḥīḥayn).
- The believers love Allah more, because they love Him in hardship and ease and never turn away, while the idolater abandons his god when trouble comes.
- Had the wrongdoers seen in this world what they will see on the Day of Judgement, that all power is Allah's and His punishment severe, they would never have taken equals.
- People deify visible leaders and traditions to fill a need meant for Allah, but every such attachment dissolves on the Day of Judgement.
- Inwardly, true love is constancy: the faithful never exchange the Friend for another, as Shiblī learned from the dog that would not leave its master's door.

<<<VERSE 2:168>>>
Having declared that He alone is God and the Creator of everything, Allah now presents Himself as the Sustainer of all His creation and mentions one of His favours. "O humanity! Eat from what is lawful and good on the earth and do not follow Satan's footsteps. He is truly your sworn enemy". The verse was revealed when some people declared it unlawful to use a camel that had been let loose for the idols. Al-Kalbī, reporting from Abū Ṣāliḥ, said it came down about the tribes of Thaqīf, Khuzāʿah and ʿĀmir ibn Ṣaʿṣaʿah, who forbade themselves certain foods and meats, namely the baḥīrah, al-sāʾibah, al-waṣīlah and al-ḥāmiyah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O humanity! Eat from what is lawful and good on the earth"**. O people, and in the first place O people of Makkah, eat of the meat, vegetables and fruits of the earth, of what is cultivated and of the cattle you raise, everything pure and lawful that Allah has not prohibited and that does no harm to body or mind. The root of ḥalāl means to open a knot, so what is made ḥalāl is something whose restriction has been untied. Ṭayyib, good, covers the clean and the pure in both senses, lawful and naturally wholesome and delicious. The Companion Sahl ibn ʿAbd Allāh said that salvation depends on three things: eating ḥalāl, fulfilling the divine obligations, and following the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.

**"and do not follow Satan's footsteps."**. Khuṭuwāt is the plural of khuṭwah, the distance between the feet in striding, so Satan's footsteps are his ways and methods, the temptations he embellishes and the whisperings by which he misguides his followers, here his suggestion that cultivating land and raising cattle, or certain animals, are unlawful. The Jāhiliyyah practices meant are the baḥīrah, a she-camel whose milk was reserved for the idols so that no one might milk her, the sāʾibah, a she-camel set loose to pasture freely for the idols with nothing allowed to be carried on her, and the waṣīlah, a she-camel freed for the idols because she gave birth to a female at her first and again at her second delivery, together with everything else Satan made attractive to them. Muslim records from ʿIyāḍ ibn Ḥimār that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ related Allah's words: every kind of wealth I have given My servants is lawful for them, and I created My servants upright (ḥunafāʾ), but the devils came to them, led them away from their religion and forbade them what I had allowed. Qatādah and al-Suddī said that every act of disobedience to Allah is among Satan's footsteps, and ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd reports Ibn ʿAbbās as saying that any vow or oath made in anger is among Satan's footsteps, and its expiation is that of a vow.

**"He is truly your sworn enemy"**. His enmity towards you is plain, and no one with sense follows an enemy who wants to harm and mislead him. Allah warns elsewhere: "Surely Satan is an enemy to you, so take him as an enemy. He only invites his followers to become inmates of the Blaze" (35:6), and, of Iblīs and his offspring, "Would you then take him and his descendants as patrons instead of Me, although they are your enemy? What an evil alternative for the wrongdoers ˹to choose˺" (18:50).

### Rulings drawn from the verse

Dedicating animals to idols, or to a saint or anyone other than Allah, is declared unlawful in verse 173 that follows. This verse does not deny the unlawfulness of such a dedicated animal, as some have wrongly supposed. Its point is that animals Allah has made lawful should not be made unlawful by dedicating them to idols. Leave them as they are and use them for your own benefit. Making things unlawful on one's own authority is a grave sin, and once something is dedicated to other than Allah it becomes impure, and what is impure is unlawful. If someone dedicates an animal to other than Allah out of ignorance or carelessness and wishes to put it right, he should resolve to withdraw from the deed and repent of it, and then the meat of that animal becomes lawful for him.

Polytheism is the worship of something other than Allah in order to satisfy one's feelings of devotion and veneration. Allah is the greatest and most essential need of a human being, and the urge to worship Him is so integral to human nature that no one can live without Him. Going astray does not mean abandoning God altogether. The real God is replaced by a false one. That is why the Islamic law holds unlawful everything that in any degree diverts a person's natural desire for God in another direction. The polytheists set animals free in the name of their deities and did not use them for food or work, which is tantamount to granting divinity to an object, a prerogative of none but Allah, and dilutes the reverence that should be reserved for Him alone. Satan encourages people to channel their awe and reverence along other paths so that their attachment to Allah is weakened.

In the devotional reading, what is forbidden, however pleasing now, is contaminated in the end, and what is permitted, however hateful now, is wholesome in the end. The pure and permitted is what is acquired without forgetting the Real, what the gatherer obtains for His sake while witnessing Him in every state, and anything that makes you forget the Real or disobey Him is from Satan's footsteps.

### In short

- Allah, the sole Creator, is also the Sustainer, and He has made the good things of the earth lawful. Tribes like Thaqīf and Khuzāʿah had forbidden themselves animals such as the baḥīrah and sāʾibah, and the verse was revealed against this.
- Satan's footsteps are his ways of misguidance, including inventing prohibitions (Muslim from ʿIyāḍ ibn Ḥimār), every act of disobedience (Qatādah, al-Suddī) and angry oaths (Ibn ʿAbbās). He is a declared enemy (35:6, 18:50).
- Dedicating animals to other than Allah is forbidden (verse 173), and the believer's duty is not to make lawful things unlawful on his own authority. Repentance restores the lawfulness of a wrongly dedicated animal.
- Shirk replaces the true God with a false one, and the law blocks everything that diverts devotion from Allah.

### Blind following and legitimate following

The verse censures the blind following of forefathers, but it also lays down, in the words "no understanding or guidance", the condition that distinguishes forbidden following from the permissible kind. Following the forefathers is censured because they lacked reason and guidance. Guidance means the injunctions revealed openly and clearly by Allah, and reason here means the binding guidance deduced by ijtihād from the recognised sources of the Sharīʿah. Those named in the verse cannot be followed because they had neither a revealed law from Allah nor the ability to derive rulings from His Word. There is a subtle hint in this. If one is satisfied that a certain scholar has perfect knowledge of the Qur'an and Sunnah, and that where no clear and direct text exists he has the expertise of a mujtahid to derive rulings from them by analogy, it is permissible to follow such a scholar. This does not mean obeying his injunctions or his person. It means obeying the injunctions of Allah alone, but since direct and trustworthy knowledge of those injunctions in all their ramifications is not available to most people, they must follow a mujtahid scholar in order to act in accordance with them. Those who hasten to quote verses like this one against following the great mujtahid imams are themselves unaware of their proven meaning.

Al-Qurṭubī said in his comments on this verse that the prohibition refers to following forefathers in false beliefs and deeds. Following them in correct beliefs and good deeds is not included, for Sūrat Yūsuf presents it clearly in the words of Yūsuf (Joseph): "I have shunned the faith of a people who disbelieve in Allah and deny the Hereafter" and "I follow the faith of my fathers: Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob" (12:37-38). Following forefathers in falsehood is forbidden, while in truth it is permissible and indeed desirable. Al-Qurṭubī also took up the question of following the mujtahid imams here, noting that some quote this verse to criticise taqlīd. As far as following falsehood goes, he said, they are right, but this has nothing to do with following the truth, which is a basic religious principle and a great means of protecting the religion of the Muslims, since one who lacks the ability for ijtihād must rely on following in matters of religion.

In the devotional reading, such people never raise their eyes beyond those who are like them in form, class, kind and ancestry, for they are set on their way, and on that path they have plunged into the fire. Had they known that their ancestors had no intelligence to restrain them and no guidance to unite them, they would have withdrawn from them as hostile and resisted them as opposed to Allah's command. But the lights of insight were snatched from them and they were deprived of the proofs of certainty.

### In short

- Called to Allah's revelation, the disbelievers, including a group of Jews (Ibn ʿAbbās via Ibn Isḥāq), plead the custom of their fathers, who had neither understanding nor guidance.
- The verse condemns following ancestors in falsehood, not following the guided, as Yūsuf's example shows (12:37-38). Following a qualified mujtahid is really obedience to Allah's law for those who cannot derive it themselves (al-Qurṭubī).
- Inwardly, those who cannot look beyond their own kind lose the light of insight.
