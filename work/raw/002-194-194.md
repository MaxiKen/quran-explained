Having answered the Companions' fear of fighting within the Ḥaram, the passage now answers their fear about the month. "˹There will be retaliation in˺ a sacred month for ˹an offence in˺ a sacred month, and all violations will bring about retaliation. So, if anyone attacks you, retaliate in the same manner. ˹But˺ be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah is with those mindful ˹of Him˺". The make-up ʿumrah was to fall in Dhū al-Qaʿdah, one of the four sacred months in which fighting anyone anywhere was forbidden, and the Muslims wondered how they could fight even defensively if the Makkans attacked. The answer is that just as the need for defence makes an exception to the sanctity of the Ḥaram, it makes an exception to the sanctity of the sacred months.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹There will be retaliation in˺ a sacred month for ˹an offence in˺ a sacred month"**. Ibn ʿAbbās and Miqsam relate the exclusion from ʿumrah in the sixth Hijrah year. The account also comes from Qatādah and al-Suddī, from ʿAṭāʾ and al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, and from aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk. The idolaters barred the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his Muslim companions from the Sacred House. This happened in the sacred month of Dhū al-Qaʿdah. They agreed to let him enter the following year, and he did so with his Companions, and Allah allowed him to make up for their treatment with these words. Qatādah describes their entry into Makkah in the following Dhū al-Qaʿdah. They performed ʿumrah and stayed three nights. This repaid the idolaters who had boasted about turning them back at Ḥudaybiyyah. The sacred month of the ʿumrah in the seventh year after the Migration was compensation for the sacred month in which they were stopped in the sixth. Just as they fight you in a sacred month, fight them in it, a reply to the Muslims' sense of the gravity of the matter.

**"and all violations will bring about retaliation."**. Sacred things (ḥurumāt), whatever must be treated as inviolable, the sacred land, the sacred month and the state of iḥrām, demand retaliation in kind when they are violated, one in return for another. There are four sacred months, Dhū al-Qaʿdah, Dhū al-Ḥijjah and Muḥarram, which run consecutively, and Rajab. Even before Islam fighting in them was held unlawful and the Makkan disbelievers observed their sanctity, and in the early years of Islam, through 7 AH, the law remained in force, which is why the Companions were perplexed. Later, by the consensus of the Ummah, the prohibition of fighting in these months was abrogated and permission to fight given, though it remains preferable not to initiate fighting in them without the need to defend. Their sanctity has therefore not been wholly abrogated. It stands like the sanctity of the Ḥaram, and both admit an exception for defence. Imam Aḥmad records Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh's report with an authentic chain. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ refrained from warfare during a sacred month unless attacked first. He then marched out. Otherwise he waited until those months ended. So when he was told at Ḥudaybiyyah that ʿUthmān, whom he had sent as envoy to the polytheists, had been killed, he took the pledge from his Companions under the tree to fight them, fourteen hundred men, and when he learned that ʿUthmān was alive he gave up the fight and returned to peace. Likewise after the battle of Ḥunayn, when Hawāzin took refuge in al-Ṭāʾif, he besieged the city, and Dhū al-Qaʿdah began while the siege continued. Anas relates in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs that forty days passed from the start of Ḥunayn until the Prophet ﷺ returned to al-Madīnah from al-Jiʿrānah. Mounting casualties led him to end the siege without taking al-Ṭāʾif. He returned to Makkah for ʿumrah, starting from al-Jiʿrānah, where the Ḥunayn spoils were divided. This pilgrimage took place in Dhū al-Qaʿdah, in the eighth Hijrah year.

**"So, if anyone attacks you, retaliate in the same manner."**. Whoever commits aggression against you by fighting in the Sacred Enclosure, in iḥrām or in the sacred months, answer him in the manner of his aggression, but do not go beyond what he did. The Muslim response is itself called aggression only because it resembles it in form. This ordains justice even towards the polytheists, as in "If you retaliate, then let it be equivalent to what you have suffered" (16:126). Combat is forbidden within Makkah's sacred precincts and during the sacred months. An attack there permits believers to defend themselves. Defence still excludes atrocities and breaches of Allah's limits. Great wartime provocation does not remove those restraints.

**"˹But˺ be mindful of Allah"**. Fear Allah when avenging yourselves, renouncing aggression and not exceeding what He has permitted, and obey Him out of taqwā.

**"and know that Allah is with those mindful ˹of Him˺"**. He is with the God-fearing by His aid and support in this life and the Hereafter, giving them victory and success.

### When two rights meet

In the devotional reading, the verse alludes to the case when two of Allah's rights face each other: submit the moment to the ruling of the moment and move with its allusions. Preferring either right for even a small worldly advantage veils you from witnessing the Real. It blinds the heart's sight. The fitting choice is the one that more strongly opposes your whims and stands further from personal attraction and pleasure. Allah is with the God-fearing who fear preferring their own whims over what pleases Him, and when they act for Him rather than for themselves He is with them by His help: "If you stand up for Allah, He will help you" (47:7).

### In short

- The ʿumrah of 7 AH in Dhū al-Qaʿdah repaid the exclusion of 6 AH in the same sacred month (Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah).
- Violations of sacred things, the Ḥaram, iḥrām and the four sacred months, may be answered in kind but no further (cf. 16:126).
- The Prophet ﷺ never began fighting in a sacred month (Jābir), as the pledge at Ḥudaybiyyah and the siege of al-Ṭāʾif show. The months' sanctity now admits an exception for defence, like that of the Ḥaram.
- Taqwā governs even retaliation, and Allah is with the God-fearing.
- Inwardly, when two rights of Allah meet, follow the ruling of the moment and never your own portion (47:7).
