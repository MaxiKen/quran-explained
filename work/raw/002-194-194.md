Having answered the Companions' fear of fighting within the Ḥaram, the passage now answers their fear about the month. "˹There will be retaliation in˺ a sacred month for ˹an offence in˺ a sacred month, and all violations will bring about retaliation. So, if anyone attacks you, retaliate in the same manner. ˹But˺ be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah is with those mindful ˹of Him˺". The make-up ʿumrah was to fall in Dhū al-Qaʿdah, one of the four sacred months in which fighting anyone anywhere was forbidden, and the Muslims wondered how they could fight even defensively if the Makkans attacked. The answer is that just as the need for defence makes an exception to the sanctity of the Ḥaram, it makes an exception to the sanctity of the sacred months.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹There will be retaliation in˺ a sacred month for ˹an offence in˺ a sacred month"**. Ibn ʿAbbās, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Suddī, Qatādah, Miqsam, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and ʿAṭāʾ said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ set out for ʿumrah in the sixth year of the Hijrah and the idolaters barred him and the Muslims with him from the Sacred House during the sacred month of Dhū al-Qaʿdah. They agreed to let him enter the following year, and he did so with his Companions, and Allah allowed him to make up for their treatment with these words. Qatādah relates that the Muslims entered Makkah in Dhū al-Qaʿdah of the next year, performed the ʿumrah and stayed three nights, and that the idolaters, who had boasted of turning them back at Ḥudaybiyyah, were thus repaid. The sacred month of the ʿumrah in the seventh year after the Migration was compensation for the sacred month in which they were stopped in the sixth. Just as they fight you in a sacred month, fight them in it, a reply to the Muslims' sense of the gravity of the matter.

**"and all violations will bring about retaliation."**. Sacred things (ḥurumāt), whatever must be treated as inviolable, the sacred land, the sacred month and the state of iḥrām, demand retaliation in kind when they are violated, one in return for another. There are four sacred months, Dhū al-Qaʿdah, Dhū al-Ḥijjah and Muḥarram, which run consecutively, and Rajab. Even before Islam fighting in them was held unlawful and the Makkan disbelievers observed their sanctity, and in the early years of Islam, through 7 AH, the law remained in force, which is why the Companions were perplexed. Later, by the consensus of the Ummah, the prohibition of fighting in these months was abrogated and permission to fight given, though it remains preferable not to initiate fighting in them without the need to defend. Their sanctity has therefore not been wholly abrogated. It stands like the sanctity of the Ḥaram, and both admit an exception for defence. Imam Aḥmad records from Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh, with an authentic chain, that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would not fight in a sacred month unless he was attacked first, in which case he marched out, and otherwise waited until the sacred months ended. So when he was told at Ḥudaybiyyah that ʿUthmān, whom he had sent as envoy to the polytheists, had been killed, he took the pledge from his Companions under the tree to fight them, fourteen hundred men, and when he learned that ʿUthmān was alive he gave up the fight and returned to peace. Likewise after the battle of Ḥunayn, when Hawāzin took refuge in al-Ṭāʾif, he besieged the city, and Dhū al-Qaʿdah began while the siege continued. Anas relates in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs that forty days passed from the start of Ḥunayn until the Prophet ﷺ returned to al-Madīnah from al-Jiʿrānah. When casualties mounted he lifted the siege before taking al-Ṭāʾif, went back to Makkah, performed ʿumrah from al-Jiʿrānah, where he divided the spoils of Ḥunayn, and this ʿumrah fell in Dhū al-Qaʿdah of the eighth year of the Hijrah.

**"So, if anyone attacks you, retaliate in the same manner."**. Whoever commits aggression against you by fighting in the Sacred Enclosure, in iḥrām or in the sacred months, answer him in the manner of his aggression, but do not go beyond what he did. The Muslim response is itself called aggression only because it resembles it in form. This ordains justice even towards the polytheists, as in "If you retaliate, then let it be equivalent to what you have suffered" (16:126). Fighting in the sacred months or within the precincts of Makkah is forbidden, but if opponents commit aggression there, believers may defend themselves, and even then they must abstain from atrocities and overstep none of the bounds set by Allah, however great the provocation of war.

**"˹But˺ be mindful of Allah"**. Fear Allah when avenging yourselves, renouncing aggression and not exceeding what He has permitted, and obey Him out of taqwā.

**"and know that Allah is with those mindful ˹of Him˺"**. He is with the God-fearing by His aid and support in this life and the Hereafter, giving them victory and success.

### When two rights meet

In the devotional reading, the verse alludes to the case when two of Allah's rights face each other: submit the moment to the ruling of the moment and move with its allusions. Beware of preferring one over the other for the sake of your worldly portion, even a little, for you will be veiled from witnessing the Real and the sight of your heart will be blinded. Whatever is nearer to what opposes your whims and further from what attracts and pleases you is in itself the more fitting. Allah is with the God-fearing who fear preferring their own whims over what pleases Him, and when they act for Him rather than for themselves He is with them by His help: "If you stand up for Allah, He will help you" (47:7).

### In short

- The ʿumrah of 7 AH in Dhū al-Qaʿdah repaid the exclusion of 6 AH in the same sacred month (Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah).
- Violations of sacred things, the Ḥaram, iḥrām and the four sacred months, may be answered in kind but no further (cf. 16:126).
- The Prophet ﷺ never began fighting in a sacred month (Jābir), as the pledge at Ḥudaybiyyah and the siege of al-Ṭāʾif show. The months' sanctity now admits an exception for defence, like that of the Ḥaram.
- Taqwā governs even retaliation, and Allah is with the God-fearing.
- Inwardly, when two rights of Allah meet, follow the ruling of the moment and never your own portion (47:7).
