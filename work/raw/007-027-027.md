<<<VERSE 7:27>>>
The address turns again to every child of Ādam, this time as a warning: "O children of Adam! Do not let Satan deceive you as he tempted your parents out of Paradise and caused their cover to be removed in order to expose their nakedness. Surely he and his soldiers watch you from where you cannot see them. We have made the devils allies of those who disbelieve". People should guard against Shayṭān's deception in everything they do and in every situation. Otherwise he may put them through the same trial he brought on their parents, Ādam and Eve.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O children of Adam! Do not let Satan deceive you"**: do not let Iblīs make you slip in obeying Me or lead you astray. Do not follow him, lest you fall into his temptation. He deceives by making disobedience look beautiful. He tempts people to stop covering their nakedness with clothing, or to abandon the clothing of mindfulness, taqwā.

**"as he tempted your parents out of Paradise"**: he made eating from the tree look good to Ādam and Eve, and the result was their expulsion from Paradise. Iblīs plotted to move Ādam from the home of comfort to the home of hardship and toil, which is this life.

**"and caused their cover to be removed"**: the verb yanziʿu, "stripping", describes the state they were in as they left. He took away their robe, which one explanation calls the robe of light. He caused Ādam's private parts to be uncovered after they had been hidden from him.

**"in order to expose their nakedness."** His aim was to show them their shameful parts. This points to a deep hatred, for Ādam and for all humankind.

**"Surely he and his soldiers watch you"**: the word for his soldiers is qabīl, which means a company or group. A family group bound together is called a qabīlah, a tribe, and looser groups are called qabīl. So the meaning is "he and his tribe", his army.

**"from where you cannot see them."** One explanation gives the reason as "because your breasts are their dwellings". Another explains it by their airy bodies, or by their having no colour. Either way, Shayṭān is an enemy who sees you with his helpers while you do not see them. That makes the chance of falling for their deception quite strong, so always be on your guard against him and his tribe.

**"We have made the devils allies of those who disbelieve"**: the devils are made friends, helpers and companions of those who do not believe in Allah, in Muhammad ﷺ and in the Qur'an. Against those who have faith and do good, the devils have no way in.

### An enemy from the very beginning

The warning against Iblīs and his followers rests on history. Allah reminds Ādam's children how old his hatred is, going back to their first father. That enmity was never something they could afford to forget. A similar verse says:

> "Would you then take him and his descendants as patrons instead of Me, although they are your enemy? What an evil alternative for the wrongdoers ˹to choose˺" (18:50).

### How can anyone guard against an unseen enemy?

Other verses make clear that Shayṭān's cunning web is very weak against people who keep turning to Allah and stay on guard against his tricks. The end of this verse points the same way. If the devils are made the friends and guardians of those who disbelieve, then those who believe should find it no hardship at all to keep clear of their snares.

Some righteous elders said that the defence against an enemy who sees us, while we cannot see him, is simple. Come under Allah's protection. He sees these devils and watches how they move and act, but they cannot see Him. The clothing of taqwā, described under the previous verse, also shields a person from Shayṭān's attacks.

### Can people ever see the jinn?

The statement that human beings cannot see Shayṭān describes the normal course of things. If someone saw them by way of an exception to the usual pattern, that would not contradict the verse. Authentic hadith narrations, for example, tell of jinn who came to the Prophet ﷺ, asked him questions and accepted Islam.

### In short

- Allah warns all of Ādam's children to guard against Shayṭān at all times, lest he do to them what he did to their parents.
- His ancient enmity showed when he had Ādam and Eve expelled from Paradise and stripped of their covering.
- He and his forces see people while people cannot see them, so the danger is real.
- Yet his web is weak against those who turn to Allah, and he is the ally only of those who disbelieve.
- Normally humans do not see the jinn, though authentic hadiths tell of jinn who came to the Prophet ﷺ and accepted Islam.
