<<<VERSE 7:27>>>
The story is left behind and the warning becomes personal. "O children of Adam! Do not let Satan deceive you as he tempted your parents out of Paradise and caused their cover to be removed in order to expose their nakedness. Surely he and his soldiers watch you from where you cannot see them. We have made the devils allies of those who disbelieve". The verse names the enemy, describes what he did once, and tells the reader where the two of them stand now.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O children of Adam! Do not let Satan deceive you"** is the admonition, and the address returns to all the children of Adam as it was in the previous verse. The deceiving here is the making of disobedience look beautiful. The warning is that they should not let him do to them what he did to their two parents. The line is followed by a warning not to be tricked into either of two things: into dropping the covering of the body, or into dropping the garment of mindfulness.

**"as he tempted your parents out of Paradise"** is the example that gives the warning its force. He made the tree look worth eating from, and what followed was that the two of them were carried out of the garden. What he wanted, and what he got, was to move the father of mankind out of the dwelling of comfort and into the dwelling of hardship and fatigue, which is this life.

**"and caused their cover to be removed in order to expose their nakedness"** is the second thing he did. The robe that was torn off them was the robe of light. One reading gives the detail that their private part had been hidden from Adam before, and that uncovering it is what showed how deep the hatred ran. Another reading explains why the two of them cannot see him, and says that their breasts are his dwelling places.

**"Surely he and his soldiers watch you from where you cannot see them"** states the position the reader is in. The word for his soldiers means his company or his group, and a joined family group takes its name from the same root. The sense of the line is that the enemy and his accomplices do see you, and you do not see them. Other verses have made clear as well that the snare of Shaytan is far too weak to hold a person who keeps turning to Allah and keeps his guard up. What the end of this verse says, that the devils are made the friends and guardians of those who do not believe, also shows that the believers should find it no great difficulty to stay out of that net.

One gloss explains the invisibility in physical terms. They have ethereal bodies, or they are without colour. Another reading of it, given in Rūḥ al-Maʿānī, puts it in terms of general conditions and habit, which is to say that the statement describes the ordinary run of things. If a human being were to see them against the ordinary run, that would not count as a contradiction of it. That is what happened when the jinn came to the Holy Prophet ﷺ, asked him questions and embraced Islam, and this appears in authentic narrations of hadith.

**"We have made the devils allies of those who disbelieve"** is the closing statement, and it explains why the guard has to be kept up. The disbelievers did not simply fall into the net. The allies were given to them.

### How the guard is kept

Some righteous elders gave a simple answer to the problem of an enemy who sees us while we cannot see him. The way to defend against him is to come under the protection of Allah, since He sees these satans and watches how they move and act, and they cannot see Him.

The garden is the whole of the story, and it has already been told. Allah warns the children of Adam about Iblīs and those who follow him by setting out the old enmity he holds against the father of mankind. Iblīs schemed to get Adam out of Paradise and to uncover his private part after it had been hidden from him, and that is exactly what deep hatred does. And there is a verse that puts the exchange in its proper light: "Will you then take him and his offspring as protectors and helpers rather than Me, while they are enemies to you?" (18:50).

### In short

- The warning is put as a personal matter: do not let him do to you what he did to the two who came before you.
- He brought them out of the dwelling of comfort and into the dwelling of hardship, and he stripped away the covering that had kept them hidden.
- He and his company see the reader and the reader does not see them, which is what makes the danger real and the guard necessary.
- The defence named is to come under the protection of the One whom the satans cannot see, and the promise attached to taqwā is that his snare is weak for anyone who keeps turning to Allah.
