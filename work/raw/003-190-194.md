<<<VERSE 3:190>>>
> “Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of the day and night there are signs for people of reason” (3:190)

The verse invites people to reflect on the ordered creation around them. The heavens, earth, and recurring changes between night and day are signs that point to Allah’s power, wisdom, and oneness. The wise do not merely observe these patterns. They let them lead to gratitude, worship, and accountability.

### Phrase by phrase

**“in the creation of the heavens and the earth”** includes the vast sky, the land beneath it, and the varied forms of life and matter within them. The expanse of the heavens and the stability of the earth invite thought about the One who brought them into being. Mountains, seas, plants, animals, minerals, colours, scents, and tastes all have qualities that people can notice and use.

The verse does not ask people to know every mechanism of the universe before they believe. It asks them to consider that an ordered world is not self-explanatory. The existence, organization, and usefulness of creation direct a thoughtful person beyond the objects themselves to their Creator.

Some reports describe the Quraysh asking the Prophet ﷺ for a spectacular sign, including that Mount al-Safa be turned into gold. Other accounts recall the signs associated with Moses, such as his staff and the shining hand, and the miracles of Jesus, including healing the blind and the leper. The verse redirects attention from demanding a spectacle to the signs already present in the heavens and earth. A person who refuses to reflect may remain unconvinced even if a requested wonder occurs.

**“and the alternation of the day and night”** points to their succession and changing lengths. Night gives way to day, and day returns to night. Their duration also varies over the year and across places on earth. These patterns make human life possible and remind people that creation is governed by order beyond their control.

The Arabic word ikhtilāf can suggest both alternation and difference. The two meanings fit the signs described here: day follows night, while their lengths and qualities vary. The changes are repeated, but not produced by human beings. Their regularity invites reflection on the wisdom and power of the One who sustains them.

**“there are signs”** means that creation points toward its Maker. A sign is not the same as the thing it points to. The beauty of a landscape, the usefulness of rain, or the predictable return of daylight is not itself Allah, but each can remind a person of His power and care.

The Qur’an warns that people may pass by signs in the heavens and earth while turning away, and that some acknowledge Allah while still associating partners with Him (12:105-106). It also says that signs will be shown in the horizons and within people themselves (41:53). Reflection should therefore lead beyond admiration of created things to recognition of the Creator.

**“for people of reason”** refers to those who use their minds honestly. Reason is not merely technical skill or the ability to gain wealth. It includes drawing a sound conclusion from what one observes and living in a way consistent with that conclusion.

The verse’s account of wisdom joins thought to faith. People can explain many natural processes, but explanation does not remove the question of why there is an ordered creation at all. The signs encourage gratitude and humility, not pride in claiming to know everything.

The word āyah can mean a verse, a miracle, a proof, or a sign. Here the plural “signs” points to evidence in the created world. The same word is used for revelations that guide people, so the believer reads both the world and the Holy Qur’an as invitations to recognize Allah. This does not mean treating every natural event as a private message about one person’s future. It means seeing the world as dependent on its Creator and using reason within the limits of evidence.

The changes between night and day also give people practical lessons. Night offers rest, while daylight permits travel and work. Their lengths shift with the seasons, and their cycle continues without human control. A person who reflects may feel small before this order and grateful for the ordinary rhythms that sustain life. That humility is one mark of sound reason.

Creation includes beauty and difficulty, abundance and scarcity. The verse does not promise that every created thing will feel pleasant to every person. It says that the world has signs of divine power and wisdom. Human beings may not understand each detail, but they can respond by seeking knowledge, using creation responsibly, and remembering that they will answer for their actions.

### The report about the request for a sign

A report from Ibn Abbas describes people of Quraysh asking Jews about the signs Moses brought and Christians about the miracles of Jesus. They then asked the Prophet ﷺ to turn al-Safa into gold. The response was to point them to the signs already visible in the creation of the heavens and earth and in the alternation of night and day. The report underscores that the request was not necessarily an honest search for truth. Someone who has already decided not to believe can always demand one more sign.

The people from Makkah asked for something striking, yet the visible order of creation was already before them. The same distinction applies to other prophetic signs. Moses, Jesus, and Yaqub were messengers, each supported by guidance appropriate to his people. The existence of a sign does not force faith upon someone determined to evade its meaning.

This does not make asking questions or seeking evidence wrong. The verse itself encourages reflection. The distinction is between a sincere question that is open to an answer and a demand used to postpone belief indefinitely.

### From signs to worship

Contemplation is meant to shape a person’s response. The heavens and earth are not only subjects for study. They are signs that lead to tawhid, recognition that Allah alone is Creator and worthy of worship. The signs are helpers for the mind, but a person still has to consider what they mean and respond honestly.

The Qur’an calls the attentive person to say that creation points beyond itself to its Maker. The people of reason recognize Allah’s sovereignty and then remember Him through dhikr. In this way, thought leads to worship rather than stopping at admiration of nature.

The Prophet’s own practice joined reflection to salah. Reports from ʿAʾishah, transmitted by Ibn Hibban and Ibn Marduwyah, describe his night worship and his deep response to the verses about creation. He made wudu, stood in prayer, and wept while reflecting on the signs. Another report in Al-Bukhari describes the Prophet rising at night, reciting these verses, and praying before the dawn adhan. These narrations show that reflection was not an abstract exercise for him. It moved him to gratitude and worship.

The prayer reports also clarify that salah follows the worshipper’s capacity. The Prophet taught that a person prays standing when able, sitting when unable to stand, and lying on a side if unable to sit. This guidance is part of the Shariah and does not lessen the worship of someone whose health limits their posture. The contemplation of creation should make people more grateful for the ability and guidance Allah gives them, not contemptuous of those who have less strength.

Creation also bears signs that support knowledge of certainty, al-yaqīn. The outward world can lead to knowledge through evidence, while reflection on the self deepens recognition. This does not mean every natural observation immediately settles every philosophical question. It means that patient reflection can strengthen a person’s awareness of Allah and their responsibility to Him.

### In short

- The heavens, earth, and alternation of night and day are signs of Allah’s power and wisdom.
- Reason should lead from observing creation to recognizing and worshipping its Creator.
- The verse calls for reflection on signs already present, not endless demands for spectacle.

<<<VERSE 3:191>>>
> “˹They are˺ those who remember Allah while standing, sitting, and lying on their sides, and reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth ˹and pray˺, “Our Lord! You have not created ˹all of˺ this without purpose. Glory be to You! Protect us from the torment of the Fire” (3:191)

The people of reason remember Allah in every posture and reflect on the creation around them. Their contemplation leads to prayer: they affirm that creation has purpose, glorify Allah, and ask to be protected from the Fire.

### Phrase by phrase

**“those who remember Allah while standing, sitting, and lying on their sides”** describes remembrance that continues in different circumstances. A person may remember Allah with the tongue, in the heart, through prayer, or by noticing His signs. The three postures encompass the changing conditions of ordinary life.

The phrase does not mean that every kind of formal prayer is performed in every posture without regard to ability. The Prophet ﷺ taught that a person prays standing when able, sitting when unable to stand, and reclining when unable to sit. Imran bin Husayn reports this teaching. The verse praises remembrance in all states, while the rules of salah take account of capacity and the form of worship.

The word dhikr means remembrance. It includes praising Allah, reciting revelation, making supplication, and keeping awareness of Him while going about one’s responsibilities. Remembrance should not be limited to a particular place or hour. The verse teaches a heart that returns to Allah in ease and difficulty.

**“and reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth”** means to consider the signs around them and what those signs reveal about their Creator. The faithful do not stop at seeing beauty or usefulness. They ask what the created order tells them about power, wisdom, mercy, and the purpose of human life.

Reflection on creation is an act of worship when it leads to knowledge of Allah and better conduct. It is not a command to speculate about Allah’s essence, which human reason cannot encompass. People can contemplate what Allah has made and the guidance He has revealed, while accepting that His being is beyond their grasp.

The Qur’an says that signs are found both in the horizons and within people themselves (41:53). The outward world and the human self both invite reflection. The goal is not simply to collect facts, but to recognize that life and the universe are not independent of Allah. The Holy Qur’an warns against passing by signs while turning away (12:105-106).

**“Our Lord! You have not created all of this without purpose”** is the conclusion drawn from reflection. The believers reject the idea that creation is a meaningless accident with no moral consequence. Allah created in truth and wisdom. People are accountable for what they do with the life and resources entrusted to them.

This conviction changes how a person views hardship and death. If creation has purpose, suffering is not the whole story and death is not the final end. Allah will judge wrongdoers and reward those who act righteously. The universe is not a game in which justice never arrives.

**“Glory be to You!”** declares Allah exalted above frivolity and imperfection. The believers affirm His perfection while admitting the limits of their own understanding. Their praise is not just a phrase. It follows from seeing order and purpose in creation.

**“Protect us from the torment of the Fire”** is the prayer that follows their reflection. They recognize that their actions matter and ask Allah to save them from the consequence of wrongdoing. They seek protection by turning to Allah and by doing the good deeds that He has guided them toward.

### Remembrance and reflection together

The verse brings together dhikr and thought. Remembrance keeps the heart directed toward Allah, while reflection considers the signs He placed in creation. Remembrance without thought can become inattentive repetition. Thought without remembrance can become pride in one’s own intelligence. Together, they lead to humility, worship, and moral responsibility.

Some spiritual teachers distinguish between remembrance spoken by the tongue and remembrance present in the heart. They describe complete remembrance as awareness that shapes a person’s inner life and conduct. In the Tariqah tradition, this is sometimes expressed as remembrance dwelling in the spirit, not only on the tongue. This is a useful reflection, but it does not make outward worship unimportant. The verse includes both the heart’s orientation and embodied prayer.

The verse also does not require a person to abandon work, family, or ordinary responsibilities in order to remember Allah. Standing, sitting, and lying are postures found throughout ordinary life. A believer can fulfil daily duties while remembering the One to whom those duties are owed. The signs associated with Isa and other messengers also point beyond the miracle to Allah, who alone gives power and guidance. The wise say that remembrance should lead them back to Him in every condition.

The prayer “You have not created this without purpose” is a confession of faith, not a claim that every mystery has been solved. People may not understand why a particular hardship occurs or why a natural process takes the form it does. They can still trust that Allah’s creation has wisdom beyond what they currently see.

### The Prophet’s night prayer

Reports describe the Prophet ﷺ reflecting on these verses during the night. Ibn Abbas stayed at the home of his aunt Maymunah and saw the Prophet rise during the night, look toward the sky, recite the verses beginning with the creation of the heavens and earth, and then prepare for prayer. He performed wudu, used a tooth-stick, and prayed. When Bilal later called the adhan, the Prophet ﷺ performed the dawn prayer and led the people.

A report from Aʿishah, transmitted through ʿAta ibn Abi Rabah and Ibn Umar, describes the Prophet asking to spend the night in worship. He performed wudu and prayed, weeping as he stood, bowed, and prostrated. When Bilal came to alert him for the dawn prayer, he asked why the Prophet was weeping when Allah had forgiven him. The Prophet replied that the verse had been revealed that night and asked how he could not weep after hearing it. He warned against reciting the passage without reflecting on its meaning.

The reports do not make night prayer obligatory for every believer. They show the Prophet’s gratitude and contemplation. When Bilal gave the adhan, the Prophet ﷺ turned from voluntary night worship to lead the dawn salah. A person may draw near to Allah through voluntary prayer according to their capacity, while maintaining the obligations and responsibilities Allah has placed upon them.

Imran bin Husayn’s report about praying while standing, sitting, or reclining is also recorded by Al-Bukhari. Ibn Marduwyah preserves the account from Ibn Umar and Aʿishah about the Prophet’s night prayer and tears. Ibn Abbas’s report about staying in Maymunah’s home supplies another glimpse of the same practice. These reports agree that worship adapts to the person’s ability and that reflection on revelation can deepen gratitude.

### Reflection has limits

People are encouraged to study the signs in creation, but should not pretend that reason can encompass Allah’s essence. One may learn from what Allah made and from the revelation He sent. Speculation beyond the evidence can lead to confusion rather than knowledge.

The spiritual language of tawhid also points to a practical truth: knowing that Allah is One should make a person direct worship and ultimate trust to Him alone. It should not be reduced to a slogan while the person’s conduct is governed by greed, vanity, or fear of people.

Abu Sulayman al-Darani described how even an ordinary sight can remind a person of Allah’s blessing and provide a lesson. Bashar al-Hafi said that sustained reflection on Allah’s greatness would make disobedience difficult. These sayings point back to the verse’s practical aim: contemplation should change the way a person lives.

Abu ʿAli Daqqaq asked Abu ʿAbd al-Rahman Sulami (Abu Abd al-Rahman) whether remembrance or reflection was more complete. Sulami answered that remembrance concerns Allah, while reflection is an act of the created mind. Their discussion distinguishes the two without setting them against one another. Remembrance keeps a person oriented toward Allah, while reflection on creation is one way of recognizing His power.

The poet Rumi expressed the limit of human thought by saying that the most thoughtful people discover how far their understanding extends and where it must become humble. The Holy Qur’an invites people to reflect on creation, not to claim that their minds can encompass the Creator. This is the balance of knowledge: seek understanding of the signs, and acknowledge what remains beyond human reach.

### Wisdom in everyday life

The passage also gives a practical measure of reason. A wise person does not merely accumulate information or seek social praise for intelligence. They remember Allah, reflect on creation, and use what they learn to worship and act justly. Some jurists used this meaning when interpreting a will that left a lot of property “to the wise”: the beneficiaries should be people of sound faith and conduct, not simply those who pursue worldly resources. The teaching reminds believers that knowledge is measured by its fruit in life.

### In short

- Remembrance of Allah belongs in every state, while formal prayer follows the rules of ability.
- Reflection on creation should lead to worship, humility, and awareness of purpose.
- The believers praise Allah and ask for protection from the Fire because they know their choices matter.

<<<VERSE 3:192>>>
> “Our Lord! Indeed, those You commit to the Fire will be ˹completely˺ disgraced! And the wrongdoers will have no helpers” (3:192)

The believers recognize the seriousness of the Hereafter. They ask Allah to protect them from the Fire, because those condemned to it are disgraced and no one can rescue the wrongdoers from His judgment.

### Phrase by phrase

**“those You commit to the Fire will be completely disgraced”** means that entering the Fire is a profound humiliation. A person may have enjoyed status in the world, but that reputation cannot shield them from the truth of their deeds. The verse presents the believers’ fear of the final outcome and their appeal for Allah’s mercy.

**“And the wrongdoers will have no helpers”** means that no created being can override Allah’s judgment or rescue those who knowingly persisted in injustice. The term “wrongdoers” identifies those who chose wrongdoing. The verse does not deny that people can help one another in this life, but it reminds them that no worldly alliance can replace Allah’s mercy on the Day of Judgment.

The believers’ prayer is not despair. They turn to Allah because they know He can forgive and protect. Their fear of the Fire moves them toward obedience rather than hopelessness.

### Fear, hope, and accountability

This supplication follows the reflection on creation. The believers recognize that the world has purpose, that Allah is perfect, and that their own actions matter. They do not ask for protection while intending to continue wrongdoing. Their plea is joined to an effort to obey the guidance and Shariah Allah has revealed.

The word “disgraced” points to more than physical pain. A person may lose worldly status, but the deepest disgrace is to be exposed as someone who rejected truth and harmed others. The believers ask Allah to protect them from that outcome and to preserve them among those who receive His mercy.

The statement that wrongdoers will have no helpers does not deny that people can support one another in this life. It means that no created helper can overrule Allah’s judgment or erase the consequences of deliberate injustice. The Qur’an warns that people can pass by signs without accepting them (12:105-106). The prayer asks for divine protection from the final consequence of that refusal.

The Prophet ﷺ modelled fear of accountability alongside gratitude. Reports about his night prayer describe him making wudu, standing in salah, and weeping while reciting the verses about creation and the Fire. Al-Bukhari transmits the account of Ibn Abbas staying in Maymunah’s home, while Ibn Marduwyah records a report from Aʿishah and Ibn Umar about the Prophet’s devotion. His tears were not hopelessness. They were a sign of reverence and deep awareness of the Day of Judgment.

A spiritual reflection also uses the Fire as an image for separation from Allah, while the direct meaning remains the punishment of the Hereafter. Such inward language can remind the believer that turning away from Allah is itself a loss. It should not be used to erase the literal warning or to claim that the Fire is only a metaphor.

The prayer helps believers keep a balanced view. They do not feel secure merely because they have good deeds, and they do not despair because they have sinned. They ask Allah for protection, repent, and make use of the means He has provided. The faithful can offer one another help and counsel in this life, while recognizing that ultimate rescue belongs to Allah.

### In short

- The Fire is a state of disgrace for those condemned to it.
- No worldly helper can override Allah’s judgment.
- The believers ask Allah for protection with hope in His mercy.

<<<VERSE 3:193>>>
> “Our Lord! We have heard the caller to ˹true˺ belief, ˹proclaiming,˺ ‘Believe in your Lord ˹alone˺,’ so we believed. Our Lord! Forgive our sins, absolve us of our misdeeds, and allow us ˹each˺ to die as one of the virtuous” (3:193)

The people of reason respond to the call of faith by believing. They then ask Allah to forgive their sins, erase their misdeeds, and let them die among the righteous. Their reflection becomes a lived commitment and a humble prayer.

### Phrase by phrase

**“We have heard the caller to true belief”** refers to the messenger who called people to faith in Allah. The caller is understood as Muḥammad ﷺ, who conveyed revelation and invited people to worship Allah alone. Hearing the message here means receiving it attentively, not merely hearing sounds.

**“‘Believe in your Lord alone,’ so we believed”** records their answer. They accept the call and follow the guidance it brings. Their faith is not a claim that they are already perfect. The rest of the prayer shows that believers still seek forgiveness and help to remain steadfast.

**“Our Lord! Forgive our sins”** asks Allah to cover and pardon their wrongs. They do not excuse themselves or rely on their own deeds as though these made them independent of mercy.

**“absolve us of our misdeeds”** asks Allah to remove the effects and record of their errors. Some explanations distinguish between major sins and lesser misdeeds, while others understand the two expressions as reinforcing the plea for forgiveness. In either reading, the believers ask for complete pardon.

**“and allow us each to die as one of the virtuous”** asks to be gathered with al-Abrar, the righteous. It is a prayer for a good end, not a claim that the speakers already possess an unquestionable rank. They ask Allah to keep them faithful until death and include them among those whose lives were marked by obedience.

### Faith, repentance, and hope

The prayer moves from hearing to believing, then from belief to repentance and hope for a righteous end. Faith does not make a person immune to mistakes. It gives them a reason to return to Allah when they fail and to seek a better course.

The believers ask for forgiveness before asking to die among the righteous because they recognize their dependence on Allah. They know that righteous companionship and a good end are gifts. Their responsibility is to believe, act well, and keep asking for guidance.

The caller’s message is not a private path available only to a select group. The Qur’an invites people to faith and makes its call public. Those who hear it are responsible for responding honestly. The believers in this prayer affirm that they have accepted the call and ask Allah to let them remain faithful.

### The response to the caller

The caller is understood as the Prophet ﷺ, and the message is the call of the Holy Qur’an to believe in Allah alone. The believers’ answer is not merely that they heard the words. They follow the sacred law that the message brings and seek to live by it. Their faith, iman, is a response to Allah’s guidance, not a claim that they can save themselves.

A spiritual interpretation connects iman with aman, security. The believer receives security by Allah’s favour and responds by affirming Him. This reminds the reader that faith is both a gift and a responsibility. It calls for trust, obedience, and care for what Allah has entrusted to each person.

The prayer for forgiveness also has two complementary parts. The believers ask Allah to cover their sins and to remove the consequences of their misdeeds. They do not treat repentance as a way to escape accountability while continuing the same conduct. They ask for forgiveness and for the strength to change.

Their request to die with al-Abrar, the righteous, is a request for a faithful ending. It includes the hope of being gathered with prophets and the virtuous, but it also asks for the practical ability to remain among them through good action. A believer does not know the time of death, so the prayer asks Allah to preserve faith through every stage of life.

Remembrance links this prayer back to the wise people described in the preceding verse. They think about creation, recognize the Creator, and respond to the call with humility. Their knowledge of tawhid leads to supplication, while their awareness of their own failures leads them to seek forgiveness. This combination protects them from both arrogance and despair.

### In short

- The believers hear the call to worship Allah alone and accept it.
- They seek forgiveness rather than claiming perfection.
- They ask Allah to keep them faithful and gather them with the righteous at death.

<<<VERSE 3:194>>>
> “Our Lord! Grant us what You have promised us through Your messengers and do not put us to shame on Judgment Day—for certainly You never fail in Your promise.” (3:194)

The believers ask Allah to grant them the good He promised through His messengers and to spare them disgrace on the Day of Resurrection. Their prayer expresses trust in Allah’s promise and humility about whether they themselves will remain worthy of it.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Grant us what You have promised us through Your messengers”** means the believers ask for the mercy, forgiveness, and Paradise promised through revelation. They are not accusing Allah of breaking His word. They ask to be among those who receive the promise.

The messengers conveyed Allah’s promises and taught people how to seek them through faith and obedience. The believers ask for help to remain on that path. They want the promised reward, but they know that their own efforts do not make them independent of Allah’s generosity.

**“and do not put us to shame on Judgment Day”** asks Allah to spare them the disgrace of being exposed as hypocrites or condemned for their sins. The prayer is for protection from a painful reckoning and from the humiliation of being turned away from the promised reward.

Their request is not a denial of accountability. In the previous prayer they asked for forgiveness and to die among the righteous. Here they ask that Allah complete His favour and not disgrace them at the resurrection. Humility is consistent with hope.

**“for certainly You never fail in Your promise”** affirms Allah’s truthfulness and reliability. The believers know that His promise is certain. Their uncertainty concerns themselves: they do not presume that they have already earned the outcome. They ask Allah to help them remain faithful and to make them recipients of what He promised.

### A prayer of dependence

The repeated address “Our Lord” expresses humility and nearness. The believers return to Allah with each request because they know that guidance, forgiveness, a good end, and the reward of the Hereafter all depend on Him.

The prayer also teaches believers how to hope. They trust Allah’s promise without becoming complacent about their own conduct. They take the means of obedience, repent when they fall short, and ask Allah to preserve them until the end of life.

### Trust in the promise

The believers ask for what Allah promised through His messengers, not for an entitlement they can demand independently. They know that Allah’s promise is certain, while their own perseverance is not guaranteed unless He helps them. Their repeated “Our Lord” expresses dependence and humility.

The promised good includes forgiveness, safety from the Fire, and entry into Paradise. The messengers who conveyed it include Isa and the prophets before Muḥammad ﷺ. The believers ask not to be put to shame on the Day of Resurrection because they know that a public reckoning will expose every deed. They ask for mercy before that day, and for Allah to help them live in a way consistent with the promise.

This prayer does not imply that Allah might fail to fulfil what He promised. The uncertainty lies in whether a particular person will remain among those who qualify for the promised reward. The supplicants ask to be included among them and to be kept faithful until death. Their words join certainty about Allah’s truthfulness with humility about their own standing.

The repeated address also models how to ask Allah for both spiritual and final good. A believer may request guidance in daily life, forgiveness for past errors, and a good end in the Hereafter. No one needs to pretend they have already earned these gifts. The prayer acknowledges that Allah’s mercy and assistance are necessary at every step.

The promise through the messengers also links earlier revelation to the present community. Each messenger called people to worship Allah and promised accountability and reward. Believers who follow Muḥammad ﷺ inherit that call and ask Allah not to let them fall short of it. The story of creation and the prayers of the wise lead to the same end: live with purpose, worship the Creator, and hope to meet Him without disgrace.

### In short

- The believers ask to receive the reward promised through the messengers.
- They seek protection from disgrace on the Day of Resurrection.
- They trust Allah’s promise while remaining humble about their own standing.
