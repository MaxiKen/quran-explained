<<<VERSE 4:94>>>
The caution of the fighter: "O believers! When you struggle in the cause of Allah, be sure of who you fight. And do not say to those who offer you ˹greetings of˺ peace, “You are no believer!”" (4:94). The verse came down over killings that should never have happened, and it fixed a rule of the Sharīʿah that holds wherever Islam is professed.

### The occasions of the verse

Imām Aḥmad recorded, through ʿIkrimah from Ibn ʿAbbās, the case of a man of Banū Sulaym who was tending a flock of sheep and passed by some of the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ. He said the salām to them. They said to each other that he had only said it to protect himself from them, then they attacked him and killed him, and brought his sheep to the Prophet ﷺ, and the verse was revealed to its end. At-Tirmidhī recorded it in his chapter on tafsīr, calling it ḥasan, and noting that it is also reported from Usāmah ibn Zayd. Al-Ḥākim recorded it as well, judging its chain sound though the compilers did not collect it. Al-Bukhārī has the shorter form: a man was tending his sheep, the Muslims caught up with him, he said As-Salāmu ʿAlaykum, and they killed him and took his sheep. Ibn ʿAbbās said that the goods of this world were those sheep, and he recited the word: peace.

Imām Aḥmad also recorded the expedition to Idam, from al-Qaʿqāʿ ibn ʿAbdullāh ibn Abī Ḥadrad, on the authority of his father ʿAbdullāh ibn Abī Ḥadrad. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ sent them out, and ʿAbdullāh rode in a group that counted Abū Qatādah, al-Ḥārith ibn Rabʿī and Muhallam ibn Juthāmah ibn Qays among its men. At Idam a man passed them on his camel, ʿAmr ibn al-Aḍbaṭ al-Ashjaʿī, and he greeted them with the salām as he went by. The group did not attack him, but Muhallam ibn Juthāmah, because of some previous problem with him, killed him and took his camel. When they returned and told the Messenger of Allah ﷺ what had happened, a part of the Qur'ān came down about them. Only Aḥmad recorded this hadith.

A third report, in one school's record, concerns Usāmah ibn Zayd. His victim was Mirdās ibn Nuhayk al-Farārī, a fellow believer. And al-Bukhārī recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās the word the Prophet ﷺ spoke to al-Miqdād:

> You killed a believing man who hid his faith with disbelieving people, after he had announced his faith to you. Remember that you used to hide your faith in Makkah before.

That is the shorter version, without a complete chain. A longer version with a connected chain stands in the record of al-Bazzār. There, an expedition went out under the authority of al-Miqdād ibn al-Aswad by the Prophet's ﷺ command. At the designated place everyone had dispersed save one man of much wealth, who pronounced his witness that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah. Al-Miqdād killed him regardless. One of the group rebuked him: the man had just testified that there is no god but Allah, and the rebuker swore to carry the story to the Prophet ﷺ. Back in Madinah, the report was made, and the Prophet ﷺ said:

> Summon al-Miqdād before me. O Miqdād! Did you kill a man who proclaimed: there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah? What would you do when you face: there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah, tomorrow?

The verse was revealed, and he said to al-Miqdād that the man had been a believer who hid his faith among disbelieving people and announced it to them, yet was killed by men who had themselves once hidden their faith in Makkah. Other incidents are reported with reference to this verse, and the respected scholars of tafsīr admit no contradiction among the narrations, since the whole set of events could stand behind the revelation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! When you struggle in the cause of Allah, be sure of who you fight"** carries a general rule for all Muslims: nothing is to be done summarily on guess alone, for acting on conjecture lands a person in trouble. The restriction to going out in the way of Allah is there because the events occurred in travel, or because doubts crop up in places never visited before, unlike the city of residence where people and things are familiar. Otherwise the rule is general, in travel and at home alike. The word of the verse is read two ways, discriminate and ascertain, and the sense is to tell the believer from the unbeliever before the blow falls. The Prophet ﷺ set the balance in a hadith:

> To act sensibly is from Allah, and to rush through is from Shayṭān.

So al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ cites it.

**"And do not say to those who offer you ˹greetings of˺ peace, “You are no believer!”"** lays down what the greeting establishes. Taken as the technical salām, the Muslim greeting, the words fit the first event most suitably. Taken as submission and surrender in the literal sense, they fit both events equally, and the reading of the word then covers the one who declares the profession of faith, the shahādah, which is an indication of being a Muslim. Either way, the instruction holds. In the case of one who professes Islam, no Muslim may interpret the declaration as hypocrisy without certain knowledge and proof, and no one may dig into his inward state on suspicion, waiting for a definite proof of certitude before the law applies to him. In the battles, some Companions could not hold this fine line. People presented themselves as Muslims, and some Companions took the profession for a lie, killed them and collected their belongings as spoils. Allah condemned the practice, though an admonition was counted sufficient for them, since the Companions did not know the rule clearly until that time.

**"seeking a fleeting worldly gain"** diagnoses the weakness behind the false step, the desire to acquire wealth through spoils. They looked at the man, saw the flock or the camel or the wealth, and read his words as a trick to save his life and property.

**"Instead, Allah has infinite bounties ˹in store˺"** turns the eyes away from the spoil. With Allah are plenteous spoils, which render a person free of any need to kill for property. The pure wealth with Allah is far better than what was acquired, and He has a tremendous reward for whoever stays his hand from a fellow believer.

**"You were initially like them then Allah blessed you ˹with Islam˺"** closes the argument with memory. They used to be in the same situation, hiding the faith from their people, just as the shepherd hid his, as Saʿīd ibn Jubayr commented in the record of ʿAbdur-Razzāq. Their own lives and property had been protected simply upon professing the faith, feeling safe with the believers because of the attestation that there is no god but Allah and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah, before the Emigration. The Qur'ān had already reminded them: "And remember when you were few and were reckoned weak in the land" (8:26). They were never asked to prove their Islam, never asked to let their hearts be examined, never ordered to produce evidence before being counted Muslims. The simple recital of the kalimah was enough. The duty that follows from memory is plain: take anyone who recites the kalimah as a Muslim.

**"So be sure!"** returns the command with weight: discriminate, lest a believer be killed, and treat those entering the religion as they were once treated. The word also carries a hint, for the Jews and the Christians all claimed to be believers, so some investigation is meant, not none.

**"Indeed, Allah is All-Aware of what you do"** ends with the threat and the warning, as Saʿīd ibn Jubayr stated, for nothing done is hidden from Him and He requites.

### When a Muslim may not be called a kāfir

The ruling that comes from this verse concerns the one who professes Islam, whether by reciting the kalimah or by joining himself to some marked feature of Islam, the adhan and the ṣalāh among them. Such a person is to be treated as a Muslim. Muslims deal with him as with any other Muslim, and they do not wait and worry over whether the embrace was wholehearted or done out of expediency. Even his doings are not a criterion of expulsion. Someone may skip the ṣalāh, not fast, and be involved in all sorts of sins, and still no one has the right to say he has left the pale of Islam or to treat him as a disbeliever. The great Imām Abū Ḥanīfah said that no sin makes the people of the Qiblah kāfirs by our tongue. A similar statement runs in the narrations: never call the people of the Qiblah kāfirs, however sinful and given to evil they may be.

The boundary nonetheless stands. A person may profess Islam and attest to faith, then disgrace himself by uttering words of disbelief, prostrating before an idol, denying an injunction of Islam that is absolute and obvious, or taking up a religious way or mark of the disbelievers. Such a person is declared a kāfir on the proof of his deeds, and this is the hint carried by the word of caution in the verse. Musaylimah the Liar is the standing case. He professed the creed and practised the adhan and the ṣalāh, and in his adhan his muezzin said the testimony that there is no god but Allah, followed by the testimony that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah. Yet he also claimed to be a prophet and a messenger receiving revelations, an open denial of the binding injunctions of the Qur'ān and the Sunnah. On that basis the Companions declared him an apostate by consensus, and a jihād force was sent against him.

The correct course therefore runs: take everyone who recites the kalimah and adheres to Islam, one of the people of the Qiblah who turn to the direction of the Holy Kaʿbah in prayer, as a Muslim. His heart is not ours to inspect. Let Allah be the judge. When you see one professing faith yet doing what contradicts it, take him to have apostatised only on the condition that the deed be absolutely and certainly contrary to the dictates of faith, admitting no other probability or interpretation. The terms Kalimah-reciter and people of the Qiblah are technical terms applied solely to one who, after professing Islam, does nothing by word or deed that can be classed as infidelity.

The passage also casts its law wider over crime. It lays down the premeditated and unpremeditted modes, but it suggests ways of dealing with other serious crimes of the public realm. As it is a duty not to deprive others of life, so it is commanded not to disgrace people, rob them, deprive them of livelihood, or victimise them in any way that seriously disturbs their peace of mind. Harm done by mistake calls for accepting the blame quickly, with repentance before Allah and compensation for the loss of the one affected. Harm intended deliberately ranks as the equivalent of deliberate homicide, differing only in its degree of gravity.

### The inner reading

When you strike forth on the path of Allah, look clearly. Read by allusion, the journey of the wayfarer is a journey toward one of the friends of Allah, that he may be the intimate of his days and the witness of his heart and spirit. However far a traveller goes, he is never at ease from seeking, and never holds back the foot of effort. The friends are the favourites of the Exalted Threshold, accepted in the Presence of Divinity, and not everyone sees them, nor does every eyesight perceive them. When found, give ear, and when seen, hold fast, for hearts grow bright by witnessing them and felicity without end sits in their company. The pīr of the ṭarīqah, Junayd, was once asked to weigh two cycles of voluntary prayer against an hour in the presence of the poor. He took the hour with the poor, for that witnessing is the love of Allah, who has made it incumbent on Himself to love those who love each other in Him and visit each other in Him. Bringing that love to hand is an obligatory act, and leaving an obligatory act aside for a supererogatory one is not the work of the clever, nor the conduct of the chevaliers.

### In short

- The verse answered real killings: the shepherd of Banū Sulaym, the camel rider at Idam, the wealthy man of the Miqdād expedition, and Mirdās ibn Nuhayk al-Farārī killed by Usāmah ibn Zayd.
- No action on guess, in travel or at home, for haste belongs to Shayṭān and discernment to Allah.
- The salām, or the profession of faith, establishes Islam on its face, and no one may dig for hypocrisy without proof.
- With Allah are plenteous spoils, and the memory of one's own hidden faith should close the hand.
- The people of the Qiblah are never called kāfir for sin, apostasy comes only on certainty, and Musaylimah, who prayed and yet claimed revelation, marks the boundary.
- The traveller of the path looks clearly for the friends of Allah, whose witnessing outranks voluntary prayer.
