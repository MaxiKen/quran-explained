<<<VERSE 7:12>>>
The question in this verse has already been asked of the angels, and only one of them has a problem with it. Allah asked, "What prevented you from prostrating when I commanded you?" He replied, "I am better than he is: You created me from fire and him from clay."

### Phrase by phrase

**"What prevented you from prostrating when I commanded you?"** is put to Iblīs, and the form of it asks for a reason. Ibn Jarīr read the question as meaning: what was it that held you back from prostrating once the order had been given. That reading is sound, and Allah knows best.

**"I am better than he is"** is the reply of Iblīs, and the ground it offers is his own worth. The excuse he gave is worse than the crime he committed. Shaytan refused because he held that a better being cannot bow to a lesser one, and the question in his mouth becomes: so how could You order me to prostrate to him?

**"You created me from fire and him from clay"** is the comparison on which the whole refusal rests. It also marks the first use of qiyas, the argument by analogy, in the history of the creation.

### The comparison rests on the wrong basis

The cursed one weighed the material each of the two was made of, and he ignored the honour that had been given instead. That honour is that Allah formed Adam with His own Hand and breathed life into him. The command that Iblīs was resisting is stated elsewhere in the Qur'an, and the words there are "So when I have fashioned him and had a spirit of My Own ˹creation˺ breathed into him, fall down in prostration to him" (38:72).

The claim that fire outranks mud was also false in its own terms. Mud carries the qualities of wisdom, forbearance, patience and assurance, and mud is the ground in which plants grow, flourish, increase and provide good. Fire carries the qualities of burning, recklessness and hastiness. So the material that Shaytan was made of sent him to his ruin, while the material that Adam was made of was the reason he came back to Allah in repentance, lowliness, obedience, surrender to the command, admission of the fault and a request for pardon.

Weighing origin settles nothing. The question was never which substance was the finer one. It was which of the two had been ordered, and an order is not a comparison.

### The first to argue by analogy

Ibn Jarīr recorded that Al-Ḥasan commented on the words "You created me from fire, and him you created from clay". His comment was that Iblīs used qiyas, and that he was the first one to do so. The statement has an authentic chain of narration.

Ibn Jarīr also recorded Ibn Sīrīn on the same point. His comment was that Iblīs was the first to use qiyas, and that the sun and the moon would not be worshipped at all if it were not for qiyas. This statement too has an authentic chain of narration.

Al-Ḥasan's observation is the sharpest thing said about this verse. A true fact was taken, that Iblīs and Adam differ in origin, and it was used to settle a question of rank that it had no power to settle. Muslim recorded that ʿĀʾishah reported the Messenger of Allah ﷺ as saying that the angels were made from light, that Shaytan was made from a smokeless flame of fire, and that Adam was made from what has been described to you. The report is worth pausing over. The difference in origin is real, and a difference in origin is still not the measure of anything.

### The pride behind the excuse

There are moments when Allah chooses a messenger to carry His word to a people, and the people cannot readily accept that the man is a guide. To accept it would mean accepting that they themselves had failed to reach the truth, and that they were therefore the lesser. This is the moment the devil works on, because this is the moment he stirs jealousy and pride. A man puffed up with the thought that he is better will not grant his brother any superiority, and in the sight of Allah that man is walking the path of Satan.

Two destinations follow from two attitudes. The one who walks the road of jealousy and pride earns the fate of Hell. The one who stays low and humble shows that he has earned a place in the gardens of Paradise. And so the whole question is a question about a person's response to what someone else has been given, because nothing anyone holds comes from himself. To grant another person superiority is to grant that the way Allah distributed His gifts was right. To deny it is to deny the propriety of His decree. And when a man defers to another on the strength of some truth, he is not bowing to a mere man. He is answering the command of his Lord, and the other's personal standing has nothing to do with it.

This event has been treated before, in the section of Surah Al-Baqarah that deals with Adam and with Shaytan, and some of the questions raised here were answered there.

### How he found the words to speak

It is worth asking how Iblīs managed to say any of this at all. He said it in the most august presence conceivable, before the Master of all honour, where the awe and the majesty of the occasion would not let an angel or a prophet or a messenger draw breath. The answer given is that this was a stern and cutting manifestation of Divine wrath. When Iblīs was rejected, a veil (ḥijāb) was drawn over his sight. He grew blind to the grandeur and the awe of the One he was addressing, and insolence settled over him completely.

That is the same blindness in a different dress. His own self became the screen between him and the greatness in front of him, and pride keeps that screen in place for anyone who takes the same road.

### In short

- The question asks for a reason, and the reason offered is that Iblīs was made of the better substance.
- The comparison is on the wrong basis, because it weighs origin and ignores the honour given, and it also has the facts backwards about mud and fire.
- He was the first to argue by analogy, and the report from ʿĀʾishah shows that a real difference in origin is still not the measure of anything.
- Jealousy and pride are what lie behind the excuse, and to deny another's superiority is to deny the propriety of Allah's own decree.
- A veil came over his sight, which is why he could speak at all in a presence like that.

<<<VERSE 7:13>>>
Allah said, "Then get down from Paradise! It is not for you to be arrogant here. So get out! You are truly one of the disgraced."

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then get down from Paradise!"** The reason attached to the command is that he defied the order and disobeyed his Lord. The scholars of tafsīr take the place he is sent down from to be Paradise itself. It could also mean the particular status he held in the utmost highs. One gloss adds that it is also said this means come out of angelic form, and another takes it as go forth from the earth.

**"It is not for you to be arrogant here"** means that showing pride in this place is not yours to do, in the form of an angel, before the children of Adam.

**"So get out! You are truly one of the disgraced"** is the sentence, and the punishment runs against the intention. What he intended was to establish his own rank, and what he gets is the loss of it. A gloss reads the words as: you are among those who are abased, and the humbling is that you are punished.

### The reversal

Iblīs set out to prove that he was the greater of the two, and he is sent away as the least of all. He is told that this place is not appropriate for him to be proud in, because it belongs to those who are good and pure-hearted, and that he is disgraced and humiliated even though he counts himself superior to Adam. The words addressed to him are contemptible and abased.

There is something in the order of the scene that deserves notice. The question comes first. Then the excuse, in full. Then the dismissal. In another arrangement the dismissal would be an answer to nothing at all. Here the reason for the sentence is left standing in the record in front of the reader, and the sentence is not the first thing heard.

### What the expulsion actually takes away

The angelic form is what the readings converge on. Iblīs was among the angels and held a rank of his own, and that rank is what he was proud of, and it is what he set against the clay of Adam. So the expulsion strips the form away. The angelic form goes, and what is left is a being outside, refused, and counted among the disgraced.

The other phrase makes the same point from the opposite direction. The arrogance was the arrogance of an angel directed at the children of Adam, and it was committed in Paradise. So the order to leave Paradise is also the order to stop being what he had been pretending to be.

### Two commands, one judgement

In this stretch of the surah the Shaytan is told to leave the heavens twice over. There is first the word to go, and the word that he is of the low. Then comes the command to get out, condemned and disgraced. The first of the two is perhaps a proposal, and the second is its implementation. They are not two sentences of the same kind, and the distance between them is the distance between a judgement announced and a judgement carried out.

### In short

- The command is to leave Paradise, and the ground of it is that he defied the command and disobeyed.
- The punishment reverses what he was after, so the one who claimed to be greater is named among the abased.
- The angelic form and the rank that went with it are what the expulsion takes away.
- The expulsion is spoken twice, once as a judgement announced and once as a judgement carried out.

<<<VERSE 7:14>>>
He appealed, "Then delay my end until the Day of their resurrection."

### Phrase by phrase

**"He appealed"** shows who is speaking and what kind of speech it is. Iblīs asks, and what he asks for is a delay. The gloss on the request is that it means give me more time, up to the day when the dead are raised out of their graves. He is described there as accursed, and as a being who has no wish to die. Another rendering has it as postpone my affair until the day when these people are brought back to life.

**"my end"** is the term he wants postponed, and the wording is that of a request rather than of a claim.

**"until the Day of their resurrection"** names the day. The "their" refers to the children of Adam, and the day is the day on which they are raised. The purpose he attaches to the request is stated plainly elsewhere: so that he may go on tempting and misguiding whichever of the children of Adam he is able to reach.

### Why he asked, and when

The prayer is a strange one, and it is made at the worst possible moment. Iblīs makes it at the very time when he stands under the wrath of Allah, with his refusal just exposed and his excuse just dismissed. What he asks for is not forgiveness and not mercy. He asks for time, and the time has a purpose attached to it.

The purpose is worth weighing. He asks for the day of resurrection, so that the work of misguiding can continue until the last day of all. The one who has just been told that he is disgraced and hated asks to live on until every human being has been raised, so that not one of them is left without his attention.

### Can the prayer of a disbeliever be accepted?

The question comes from the verse "Calling upon Him ˹alone˺ is the truth" and its closing words, "The calls of the disbelievers are only in vain" (13:14). The usual reading is that a disbeliever's prayer is not answered. Yet here is Iblīs, the foremost disbeliever of all, and his request is granted. The difficulty is obvious, and the answer is straightforward.

In this mortal world, even a disbeliever's prayer can be answered. It was answered for Iblīs himself. In the life to come it will not be answered at all. The verse that speaks of calls being in vain belongs to the life to come and has nothing to do with this world.

So the grant given to Iblīs is not a kindness shown to his prayer and not an answer to a need. It is a decree, and the decree goes against him in the end, as the next verse shows.

### In short

- The request is for time and not for mercy, and the purpose attached to it is to keep misguiding the children of Adam until the last day.
- It was made at the worst possible moment, by one who had just been dismissed and disgraced.
- A disbeliever's prayer can be answered in this world, and this one was, but the answer in the life to come is no.
- What follows is a decree rather than a favour, and the decree turns against the one who asked.

<<<VERSE 7:15>>>
Allah said, "You are delayed ˹until the appointed Day˺."

### Phrase by phrase

**"You are delayed"** is the answer to the request, and the answer is a grant. The gloss reads the words as: you are given respite until the trumpet is blown. Another makes it plainer: the respite runs to the first blast of the trumpet, when every created being dies and the Creator alone remains. The same term is named in another verse as the day of an appointed time (15:36-37).

**"˹until the appointed Day˺"** is the limit set on the grant. The day is fixed and known to Allah, and it is not the day that Iblīs asked for.

### How long the respite was

The prayer he made asked for a lease of life running to the Day of Resurrection. The words of the answer say only that respite is given. Read against the question, that looks like a grant of exactly what was asked. What the verse does not state clearly is whether the term is the Resurrection as he requested, or something else. And there is a reason to think it is something else, because another verse puts the words "until the appointed Day" (15:38) in this very place.

The upshot is that his prayer was answered, and answered only in part. What he received was a fixed term preserved in Divine knowledge, and not the day of Resurrection or Qiyamah.

A report from as-Suddī supports this reading. Allah did not give Iblīs respite until the Day of Resurrection. He gave him a term whose hour is appointed, and that is the day when the Horn is blown for the first time, when every being in the heavens and the earth is stunned, and dies.

### Two days, and not one

The prayer that Iblīs made was for the time of the second blast, when the dead are raised. That is what is called al-Baʿth. Had the grant matched the request, Iblīs would have been alive at that moment. But the facts would not have allowed it. Nothing is alive at that moment except the Ever-Living and Self-Sustaining One, and that is the moment when the great promise stands fully manifested: "Every being on earth is bound to perish. Only your Lord Himself, full of Majesty and Honour, will remain ˹forever˺" (55:26-27). Iblīs was not to live to see it. So the term of the grant was changed to the day of the Horn, and it was given at that. Death then becomes the rule for the whole of creation. Iblīs dies with the rest, and when the others are raised he is raised too.

This also clears up a doubt that could arise from "Every being on earth is bound to perish" (55:26), which is closely tied to this prayer. The apparent contradiction is resolved.

The substance of it is that al-Baʿth and al-Waqt al-Maʿlum are two separate days. One commentary holds that they are indeed two separate days. From the first blast of the Horn until the entry into Paradise or Hell is one long day in which many things happen at different times. Because of what happens in it, that day can be named after any of those events. It can be called the day of the blast, the day of perishing, the day of resurrection and the day of recompense. On this account every difficulty in the passage falls away.

### In short

- The answer is a grant of respite, running to the blast of the trumpet when every created being dies.
- The prayer was granted only in part. What was given was a fixed appointed term, and not the day of Resurrection that was asked for.
- The raising of the dead and the appointed time are two separate days, and Iblīs was given the second and not the first.
- Because he would have been alive on the day when nothing is alive but the Ever-Living One, the term was changed, and the tension with "Every being on earth is bound to perish" (55:26) is resolved.

<<<VERSE 7:16>>>
He said, "For leaving me to stray I will lie in ambush for them on Your Straight Path"

### Phrase by phrase

**"For leaving me to stray"** is the ground on which everything that follows is built. The sense is: since You have sent me astray. Ibn ʿAbbās read it as "You misguided me". Others gave it as, since You ruined me, I will set myself in wait for the servants whom You will bring out of the offspring of the one for whose sake I was expelled. One gloss gives the force of the words as: for Your sending me astray.

**"I will lie in ambush for them"** means he will sit in wait against the children of Adam. The word is the word for a hunter at his post.

**"on Your Straight Path"** is the road itself. It is the path of truth and the way of safety, the path that leads to You, and the religion of Islam. Mujāhid said the straight path is the truth.

### The road he chose to ambush

The reply assumes that the object of the whole dispute is a road, and it assumes it so completely that the rest of the speech depends on it. The sense is that he will misguide them off that road so that they do not worship Allah alone, and the reason he gives is that he was sent astray himself. The path of truth and the way of safety are named as one thing, and to be sent astray from a road is to be sent astray from a destination.

The word also covers the religion. What is being ambushed is not a route on a map. It is a way of life, and the ambush is set across it.

### The ambush at every stage

Imam Aḥmad recorded from Saburah bin Abī al-Fakih that he heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say that Shaytan set himself in wait for the son of Adam upon all his paths. He took his post on the path of Islam and asked: will you enter Islam and leave the religion of your fathers? The son of Adam disobeyed him and entered Islam. So he took his post on the path of the migration, and asked: will you leave your land and your sky? The parable of the one who migrates is that of a horse in its strength and stamina. The son of Adam disobeyed him and migrated. So he took his post on the path of the struggle, the struggle against one's own self and the struggle with one's wealth, and asked: suppose you go out to fight and you are killed? Your wife will then marry another man and what you owned will be split up. The son of Adam disobeyed him and waged the struggle all the same.

The shape of the report is worth noticing. The ambush is not set at one point on the road. It is set at every point of it, and at each point the temptation is the one that fits that point. At the beginning the lure is the old ways. In the middle it is the comfort of staying where you are. At the end it is the price that will be paid. Each time the argument arrives dressed as a reasonable question.

The promise that comes with it does not vary. If a man among the children of Adam does this and then dies, the promise from Allah is that he enters Paradise. If he is killed, the same. If he drowns, the same. If a fall from an animal breaks his neck, the same. The list of ways to die is deliberately long, and the promise does not change with any of them.

### In short

- The speech rests on one grievance, that he was sent astray, and everything else is a response to it.
- The straight path is the path of truth and the way of safety, which is the religion itself, and the ambush is set across it.
- The ambush is set at every stage of the way, and the temptation at each stage fits that stage.
- The promise of Paradise does not depend on how a person dies, and the list of deaths is long on purpose.

<<<VERSE 7:17>>>
I will approach them from their front, their back, their right, their left, and then You will find most of them ungrateful."

### Phrase by phrase

**"I will approach them from their front"** means he raises doubts in them about the life to come. The content of the doubt is spelled out: that there is no Hell, no Paradise, no resurrection and no reckoning ahead of them.

**"their back"** means he makes them more eager for this life, telling them that the world lasts for ever, bidding them to pile up and hold back out of stinginess, and ordering them to corrupt.

**"their right"** is the side of religion. There he confuses those who are guided until they leave the guidance, and makes the road look fair to those already astray so that they stay astray.

**"their left"** is the side of pleasures and delights.

**"and then You will find most of them ungrateful"** is the count he takes at the end. Ibn ʿAbbās read the words as meaning those who single Allah out in worship. The opposite of ungrateful here is grateful in the sense of acknowledging the One God, so the claim is that most of them will not acknowledge Him.

### The four sides are not a boundary

The verse has him naming four directions: the front, the rear, the right and the left. The purpose is not to set a limit. The sense is that the attack comes from every side and from all directions together, and for that reason an attack from above or from beneath the feet does not contradict it at all. There is also a report that Shayṭān travels within a human being along the blood vessels, so that he comes to hold the whole body. That report does not contradict it either.

So the four are simply the four that can be named, and what they describe is the whole person. What is left out is not a small remainder. Two directions are left out, and a third direction is left out as well, the one inside the body of the man being addressed.

### What each side does

The four are not four attempts of the same kind. One works on the future, and its instrument is doubt about whether there is a future at all. One works on the present, and its instrument is the belief that the present will last for ever, which is what makes hoarding and corruption look reasonable. One works on religion, and its instrument is confusion for the guided and reassurance for the misguided. One works on appetite, and its instrument is pleasure.

Between them they cover a person completely, because each attacks a different part at the same time. Doubt about the future takes away the reason to obey. Belief in an endless present takes away the reason to give. Confusion about religion takes away the reason to choose. And pleasure takes away the desire for anything else.

### The guess that came true

On Iblīs's part the claim was no more than a guess. It nevertheless came out exactly right. The Qur'an says of him, "Indeed, Iblîs' assumption about them has come true, so they ˹all˺ follow him, except a group of ˹true˺ believers" (34:20). And it was never a matter of power, because he has no authority over them. The test is only to separate the one who believes in the Hereafter from the one who doubts it, and the Lord is watchful over all things (34:21).

A hadith on this subject teaches the believer to ask for protection from every side at once. Imam Aḥmad narrated that ʿAbdullah ibn ʿUmar said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to recite this supplication often in the morning and when night came:

> O Allah, I ask You for well-being in this life and the Hereafter. O Allah, I ask You for pardon and well-being in my religion, my life, my family and my wealth. O Allah, cover my errors and quiet my fear in times of difficulty. O Allah, guard me from in front of me, from behind me, from my right, from my left and from above me. I seek refuge in Your greatness from being struck down from below me.

Wakīʿ explained the closing phrase, about being struck down from below, as a reference to earthquakes. Abū Dāwūd, an-Nasāʾī, Ibn Mājah, Ibn Ḥibbān and al-Ḥākim all collected this hadith, and al-Ḥākim said its chain is authentic.

### In short

- The four sides describe a totality rather than a boundary, and the directions left unmentioned are above, below and inside the man himself.
- Each side works on a different part of a person, and all of them work at once.
- The claim was a guess on Iblīs's part, and it proved true, save that he has no authority over the believers.
- The supplication asks for protection from in front, behind, right, left, above and below, which answers the verse's four sides with six.

<<<VERSE 7:18>>>
Allah said, "Get out of Paradise! You are disgraced and rejected! I will certainly fill up Hell with you and your followers all together."

### Phrase by phrase

**"Get out of Paradise!"** is the second expulsion, and it carries the curse with it. The statement brings together the cursing, the driving out, the banishment and the turning away from the highest stations.

**"You are disgraced and rejected!"** renders the two words that describe his condition. Ibn Jarīr said the first of them means disgraced, and the second means the one who is put at a distance, that is, banished and driven out. Ibn ʿAbbās gave the sense as belittled and despised. As-Suddī said hateful and expelled. Qatādah said cursed and despised. Mujāhid said driven out and banished. Ar-Rabīʿ bin Anas assigned banishment to the first word and belittlement to the second. One authority said that no being is known to be in this condition except one, and a well-known chain gives the sense of the first word as despised.

**"I will certainly fill up Hell with you and your followers all together."** is the sentence of requital. The "you" is Iblīs together with his offspring, and with people as well. In the address, those who are present carry more weight than those who are absent, and the sense of the whole line is the sense of a condition: whoever follows you will be punished.

### What the two words take away

Each of the two words is a denial. One says he has been made small. The other says he has been put far away. Together they remove both size and nearness, and once both are gone there is nothing in him that anyone could want. What is described is not a pain to be suffered somewhere. It is a standing condition, and the condition is contempt.

The second expulsion has an order of its own as well. The first command to leave the heavens was perhaps a proposal. This one is its implementation. The words "get out" are used again, and what has been added to them is the consequence: the Fire is to be filled.

### What the Fire is filled with

The rest of the passage sets out the purpose. The command is to go, and then the promise: "Be gone! Whoever of them follows you, Hell will surely be the reward for all of you—an ample reward" (17:63). Then comes the list of how the filling is done. "And incite whoever you can of them with your voice, mobilize against them all your cavalry and infantry, manipulate them in their wealth and children, and make them promises." And the line closes with the fact that undoes all of it: "But Satan promises them nothing but delusion" (17:64).

Every instrument in that list is either a kind of speech or a kind of pressure. The voice, the cavalry, the infantry, the wealth, the children and the promises all work on a person from outside. And the thing being promised is a delusion, so that what is finally filled is a Fire full of people who were talked into it by a lie.

The ground of it all is the last line of the same passage: "You will truly have no authority over My ˹faithful˺ servants. And sufficient is your Lord as a Guardian" (17:65). The instruments belong to Iblīs and the outcome does not.

### In short

- The two words used against him deny him both size and nearness, so that contempt is all that is left of his standing.
- The second expulsion carries out the first, and what it adds is the consequence, that the Fire is to be filled.
- The Fire is the reward of those who follow him, and the reward is described as ample.
- The means of filling it are the voice, the cavalry, the infantry, the wealth, the children and the promises, and every one of them is delusion.
