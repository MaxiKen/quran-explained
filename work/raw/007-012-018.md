<<<VERSE 7:12>>>
Allah now questions Iblīs about his refusal: "Allah asked, “What prevented you from prostrating when I commanded you?” He replied, “I am better than he is: You created me from fire and him from clay.”" The question is a rebuke, and the answer lays bare the root of Iblīs's fall: pride built on a false comparison. The event was told earlier in Sūrat al-Baqarah, where some of its aspects were discussed. Other questions it raises are taken up here.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah asked, “What prevented you from prostrating when I commanded you?”"** means: what stopped you and held you back from prostrating after I had ordered you to? Ibn Jarīr gave this meaning, and it is sound. Allah knows best. In the Arabic, the word allā is made of an and lā, and the lā is extra, so it does not turn the sentence negative. The question was asked as a reproach.

**"He replied, “I am better than he is:"** This excuse is worse than the crime itself. Shayṭān meant that one who is better cannot bow down to one who is lower. "So how can You order me to prostrate before him?"

**"You created me from fire and him from clay.”"** His reasoning was that fire is better and greater than clay, and that fire devours mud. The cursed one looked at what each was made of, not at the honour given to Ādam. Allah had created Ādam with His own Hand and breathed life into him. Shayṭān set up his comparison against a plain command: "fall down in prostration to him" (38:72).

So Shayṭān alone broke ranks with the angels by refusing to prostrate. He became, in the Arabic expression, ablasa from mercy, which means he lost all hope of gaining Allah's mercy. He fell into this error through his false comparison.

### Was fire really better than clay?

His claim that fire is nobler than clay was also false. Clay has the qualities of wisdom, forbearance, patience and steadiness. It is where plants take root, flourish and multiply, giving good to others. Fire has the qualities of burning, recklessness and haste. So the stuff Shayṭān was made of led him to ruin, while Ādam's origin led him back to Allah. Ādam returned in repentance, humility and obedience, submitting to Allah's command, admitting his error and asking for forgiveness and pardon.

Muslim recorded that ʿĀʾishah reported the Messenger of Allah ﷺ as saying:

> "The angels were created from light, Shayṭān from a smokeless flame of fire, and Ādam from what has been described to you."

### The first to reason by analogy

Ibn Jarīr recorded a comment of al-Ḥasan on the words "You created me from fire and him from clay". Iblīs, he said, used qiyās, reasoning by analogy, and nobody had reasoned that way before him. The report of this comes through an authentic chain. Ibn Jarīr also recorded that Ibn Sīrīn said, "The first to use qiyās was Iblīs. Would the sun and the moon ever have been worshipped, if not for qiyās?" This too has an authentic chain.

### How could he speak like that before Allah?

Iblīs dared to speak in the most majestic Presence imaginable, before the Master of all honour. There the awe of the occasion would not let even angels, prophets and messengers draw breath. How did he do it? Scholars (ʿulamāʾ) say it was a grave and cutting display of Allah's anger. Because of his refusal, a veil (ḥijāb) came over his sight. He became blind to the greatness and awe of Allah, and irreverence settled over him entirely.

### The same pride today

Sometimes Allah chooses a messenger to carry His word, yet people find it hard to accept him as their guide. Accepting him would mean admitting that they were lesser, since they had failed to reach the truth themselves. On such occasions Shayṭān stirs up jealousy and pride. Puffed up with the thought "I am better", a person refuses to recognise that his brother stands above him. In Allah's sight this is walking Shayṭān's path. Whoever walks the path of jealousy and pride earns Hell. Whoever stays humble shows that he deserves a home in the gardens of Paradise.

Everything a person receives comes from Allah. To admit someone else's superiority is really to admit that Allah's gifts were rightly given. To deny the superiority of a gifted person is like denying Allah's own decree. Likewise, giving way to another person because the truth is on his side is not bowing to a mere human. He bows to Allah, in answer to His command, and not because of the other's personal standing.

### Why do the words differ from one telling to another?

This story appears in the Qur'an in several places, and the wording of the questions and answers differs each time, though the event is one. Whenever the main event is told the subject stays the same, and there is no need for the words to match exactly. A report can be given by its meaning. Where subject and sense are one, differences of wording do not matter.

### In short

- Allah's question was a rebuke: nothing should have kept Iblīs from obeying a direct command.
- Iblīs defended himself by comparing fire with clay, ignoring the honour Allah gave Ādam, and his excuse was worse than his crime.
- He was the first to use analogy against a clear command, and his pride cost him all hope of mercy.
- The same thought, "I am better", still keeps people from accepting truth from someone Allah has favoured.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:13>>>
Allah answers Iblīs's pride with expulsion: "Allah said, “Then get down from Paradise! It is not for you to be arrogant here. So get out! You are truly one of the disgraced.”" Iblīs was sent down because he defied Allah's command and disobeyed Him. The humiliation he received was the exact opposite of the greatness he claimed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah said, “Then get down from Paradise!"** The sense is: "because you defied My command and disobeyed Me". According to the scholars of tafsīr, the place meant is Paradise, the Garden. It is also said to mean "go down from the heavens". It could refer as well to the special rank Iblīs held in the highest heights. One more explanation is "come out of your angelic form".

**"It is not for you to be arrogant here."** It is not right for you to show pride here. Paradise is no place for pride, for it is a home for those who are good and pure of heart. One explanation reads it as: you may not parade your pride, in an angel's form, before the children of Ādam.

**"So get out!"** Leave it. Some say this means leave your angelic form, and some say go out from the earth.

**"You are truly one of the disgraced.”"** You are among the abased and contemptible, humiliated by being punished. You are disgraced and humbled, even though you count yourself superior to Ādam. This was a just reward for his evil intent, since he was given the opposite of what he aimed at. He reached for greatness through arrogance and received disgrace.

### Was Iblīs expelled twice?

In these verses Shayṭān is ordered out of the heavens twice. The first order is here: "So get out! You are truly one of the disgraced." The second comes a few verses later: "Get out of Paradise! You are disgraced and rejected!" (7:18). Perhaps the first is the decision and the second is its carrying out.

### In short

- Iblīs was sent down from Paradise, or from his high rank, for defying Allah's command.
- Paradise is a home for the good and the pure-hearted, with no room for arrogance.
- His punishment fitted his crime: he wanted greatness and was given disgrace.
- He was ordered out twice, perhaps first by decision and then in fact.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:14>>>
Even under Allah's anger, Iblīs asked for something: "He appealed, “Then delay my end until the Day of their resurrection.”" Disgraced and expelled, the cursed one asked for a reprieve. He did not want to die, and he wanted time to lead Ādam's children astray.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He appealed, “Then delay my end"** means give me respite, postpone my affair. He made this request at the very moment he stood under Allah's anger. It was an odd prayer indeed: a lease of life.

**"until the Day of their resurrection.”"** That is, until the day people are raised from their graves, the Day of Rising. His purpose, in one explanation, was: "so that I can tempt and mislead whomever of Ādam's children I am able to." Accursed as he was, he did not want to die. The cursed one remembered his enmity and asked for respite until the Day of Judgement. The same exchange appears in another surah:

> "Satan appealed, “My Lord! Then delay my end until the Day of their resurrection.” Allah said, “You will be delayed" (15:36-37).

### What exactly did he ask for?

Iblīs was asking for life until the second blowing of the Horn, when all the dead will be raised. That day is called Yawm al-Baʿth, the Day of Raising. Whether he was given exactly that is taken up under the next verse.

### In short

- Even while under Allah's anger, Iblīs asked to be spared death until the Resurrection.
- He wanted that time to tempt and mislead as many of Ādam's children as he could.
- What he asked for was life until the second blowing of the Horn, the Day of Raising.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:15>>>
Allah granted the request, on His own terms: "Allah said, “You are delayed ˹until the appointed Day˺.”" He gave Shayṭān what he asked for out of His wisdom. It was His decision and decree, which nothing can block or resist. None can overturn His judgement, and He is swift in reckoning.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah said, “You are delayed"** means you are among those given respite.

**"˹until the appointed Day˺.”"** The respite lasts until the Trumpet is blown. That is the first blast, when every created being dies and the Creator alone remains. Another verse speaks of "the day of an appointed time", and this too is explained as the time of the first blast.

### How long is the respite?

Here Allah's reply is short. Given the question and the answer, one might understand that the respite runs to the Resurrection, as Iblīs asked. But the verse does not say so clearly. At the matching place in another surah the words are "until the appointed Day" (15:38). On the surface this means he was not given respite until the Day of Qiyāmah. He was given it until a particular time kept in Allah's knowledge. So his prayer was answered, but not completely.

Ibn Jarīr reported a narration from as-Suddī that supports this. Allah did not give Iblīs respite until the Day of Resurrection. He gave it until a day whose time is fixed: the day the Horn is first blown, when all in the heavens and on earth fall stunned and dead.

Iblīs had asked to live until the Day of Raising, Yawm al-Baʿth. Had that been granted as asked, he would have been alive at a moment when nothing lives except the Ever-Living, the Self-Sustaining (al-Ḥayy al-Qayyūm). That is when this great promise will be plainly fulfilled: "Every being on earth is bound to perish. Only your Lord Himself, full of Majesty and Honour, will remain ˹forever˺" (55:26-27). Iblīs was therefore not to live through that moment. Instead the term was changed to "the day the Horn is blown" (yawma yunfakhu fī aṣ-ṣūr), and granted in that form. Death will then cover the whole creation, and Iblīs will die too. When all the others are raised, he will be raised with them.

This clears the doubt that his prayer might raise against the verse "Every being on earth is bound to perish" (55:26). The two do not conflict. The Day of Raising and the Day of the Appointed Time (Yawm al-Waqt al-Maʿlūm) are two separate days. Iblīs asked for the first, and was given the second.

Ashraf ʿAlī Thānavī, in his Bayān al-Qurʾān, preferred the view that these are two separate days. He also explained that from the first blowing of the Horn until people enter Paradise or Hell will be one long day. Different things will happen at different times within it, so the day can be named after any of them: the day of the Horn, the day of perishing, the day of Resurrection, the day of Recompense. This clears up all the difficulties.

### Can a disbeliever's prayer be answered?

The verse "The calls of the disbelievers are only in vain" (13:14) is usually taken to mean that a disbeliever's prayer is never answered. This event seems to say otherwise. The answer is that in this world even a disbeliever's prayer can be answered, so much so that the prayer of the foremost disbeliever, Iblīs, was granted. In the Hereafter a disbeliever's prayer will not be answered. That verse concerns the Hereafter and has nothing to do with this world.

### In short

- Allah granted the reprieve by His wisdom and decree, which no one can resist.
- The respite runs to the first blast of the Trumpet, not to the Resurrection, so Iblīs will die with all creation and be raised with them.
- This fits the verse that every being on earth will perish.
- In this world even a disbeliever's prayer may be answered. The verse calling their prayers vain refers to the Hereafter.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:16>>>
Sure now that he had his reprieve, Iblīs went on in defiance and rebellion: "He said, “For leaving me to stray I will lie in ambush for them on Your Straight Path". He blamed Allah for his own error and swore to block the way of Ādam's children to Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He said, “For leaving me to stray"** renders aghwaytanī, which means "as You have sent me astray". Ibn ʿAbbās said it means "You have misguided me". Others explained it as "since You caused my ruin". In the grammar, fa-bi-mā aghwaytanī means "by Your sending me astray". The bāʾ is the particle of an oath, and the words that follow are the oath's answer. In other words: because You made me fall into error, through my not obeying Your command to bow to Ādam.

**"I will lie in ambush for them"**: for the children of Ādam. Others give his words as: "I will sit in wait for Your servants whom You will create from the offspring of the one You expelled me for." His aim is to turn them aside and mislead them from the path, just as he himself was misled.

**"on Your Straight Path"**: the path of truth and the way of safety, the path that leads to You, the religion of Islam. Mujāhid said the straight path means the truth. Iblīs meant that, since Allah had sent him astray, he would draw people off this path until they no longer worshipped Allah alone.

### How does Shayṭān lie in wait?

Imam Aḥmad recorded that Saburah ibn Abī al-Fākih heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ describe how Shayṭān sits in wait for the son of Ādam on all his paths.

1. He sat on the path of Islam and said, "Will you become a Muslim and abandon your religion and the religion of your forefathers?" The son of Ādam disobeyed him and accepted Islam.
2. So he sat on the path of hijrah, migration for Allah's cause, and said, "Will you emigrate and leave your land and your sky?" Yet the emigrant (muhājir) is like a horse in its stamina. He disobeyed him and emigrated.
3. So he sat on the path of jihad, striving with one's self and one's wealth, and said, "If you fight, you will be killed, your wife will marry another and your wealth will be shared out." He disobeyed him and strove.

Whoever of Ādam's children does this has Allah's promise to enter Paradise. The promise holds whether he dies, is killed or drowns, or whether his mount throws him and breaks his neck.

### In short

- Once he had his reprieve, Iblīs blamed Allah for his own error and swore to mislead Ādam's children.
- He lies in wait on the straight path, the path of truth that leads to Allah, to stop people worshipping Him alone.
- He sits in turn on each path of good: Islam, hijrah and jihad. Whoever defies him and keeps going is promised Paradise.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:17>>>
Iblīs completes his threat: "I will approach them from their front, their back, their right, their left, and then You will find most of them ungrateful.”" He means he will come at people from every side. He will turn them away from the path of good and draw them onto the path of evil.

### Phrase by phrase

**"I will approach them from their front"**: ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported Ibn ʿAbbās as saying this means raising doubts about the Hereafter. Another explanation spells it out: telling them there is no Hell, no Paradise, no resurrection and no reckoning.

**"their back"**: making them keener on this life. He persuades them that this world will last forever, urges them to hoard and hold back out of stinginess, and orders them to spread corruption.

**"their right"**: confusing them in their religion. He confuses the guided until they abandon guidance. To the misguided he makes their error look fair, so that they stay in it.

**"their left"**: luring them into sins, through pleasures and delights. He makes their low desires seem beautiful to them.

**"and then You will find most of them ungrateful.”"** Ibn ʿAbbās said the thankful are those who worship Allah alone. Thankful here means believing in Him. Most of them, or as one explanation has it, all of them, will fail to be thankful. That will be because of the denial and ingratitude Iblīs leads them into.

### Why does he not mention "above"?

Al-Ḥakam ibn Abān reported from ʿIkrimah that Ibn ʿAbbās said, "He did not say he would come from above them, because mercy comes down from above." In another report Ibn ʿAbbās said he cannot come at them from above, for then he would stand between the servant and Allah's mercy.

### Four sides, or every side?

The four directions are not meant as a limit. The sense is that Shayṭān's attack comes from every side and direction, covering every path of good and evil. On this reading, the chance that he leads people astray from above or from beneath their feet does not contradict the verse. Nor does the hadith that Shayṭān can enter the human body, flow through its veins and arteries, and take command of the whole of it.

### Iblīs's guess came true

Shayṭān's words were only a guess, a mere assumption. Still, what happened matched his guess, as Allah says:

> "Indeed, Iblîs’ assumption about them has come true, so they ˹all˺ follow him, except a group of ˹true˺ believers. He does not have any authority over them, but ˹Our Will is˺ only to distinguish those who believe in the Hereafter from those who are in doubt about it" (34:20-21).

### A prayer for protection on every side

That is why a hadith encourages seeking refuge with Allah from Shayṭān's lures in every direction. According to ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar, in a report from Imam Aḥmad, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ often made this supplication in the morning and at nightfall. He asked Allah for well-being in this world and the Hereafter. He asked for pardon and well-being in his religion, his worldly life, his family and his wealth. He asked Allah to cover his faults and to calm him in times of fear. He asked to be guarded from in front, from behind, from his right, from his left and from above. And he sought refuge in Allah's greatness from being killed from beneath him.

Wakīʿ explained that being killed from beneath refers to earthquakes. The hadith was also collected by Ibn Ḥibbān, Abū Dāwūd, Ibn Mājah, al-Ḥākim and an-Nasāʾī. Al-Ḥākim said its chain is ṣaḥīḥ.

### In short

- Iblīs vowed to attack from every side: doubts about the Hereafter, love of this world, confusion in religion and the pull of sin.
- The four directions stand for every direction, though one report notes he left out "above", where mercy comes down.
- His forecast that most people would be ungrateful was a guess, but it came true, except for a group of true believers.
- The Prophet ﷺ prayed morning and evening for protection from every side.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:18>>>
Allah passes final sentence on Iblīs: "Allah said, “Get out of Paradise! You are disgraced and rejected! I will certainly fill up Hell with you and your followers all together.”" Here Allah stresses His curse on Shayṭān. He expels and banishes him, driving him away from the highest heights.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah said, “Get out of Paradise!"** means go down from Paradise, O Shayṭān.

**"You are disgraced and rejected!"** In the Arabic this is madhʾūman madḥūrā. The meanings given for the pair are close to one another.

- Ibn Jarīr said madhʾūm is "disgraced", and madḥūr is "distanced", that is, banished and expelled.
- In one report Ibn ʿAbbās said "despised". In the report of ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah he said "belittled and despised".
- As-Suddī said "hateful and expelled". Qatādah said "cursed and despised". Mujāhid said "expelled and banished".
- Ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said madhʾūm means banished and madḥūrā means belittled.
- Other glosses are "rebuked and kept far from good" and "humiliated and removed from Allah's mercy".

ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said, "We know of no one who is both madhʾūm and madhmūm, disgraced and condemned, except one."

**"I will certainly fill up Hell with you"**: in the Arabic, the lām in la-man ("as for whoever follows") either marks the start of the sentence or introduces an oath. The oath's answer is "I will fill Hell". The sentence carries the sense of punishment implied by "whoever": whoever follows you, I will punish him. "With you" means you, your offspring, and the people who follow. In this address those present are given precedence over those absent.

**"and your followers all together.”"** These are the jinn and humans who follow and obey him and go against Allah's command, the disbelievers among both.

### What can Shayṭān do, and what can he not?

Another passage gives the same sentence on Iblīs. It also shows what he is allowed to do, and where his power ends:

> "Allah responded, “Be gone! Whoever of them follows you, Hell will surely be the reward for all of you—an ample reward. And incite whoever you can of them with your voice, mobilize against them all your cavalry and infantry, manipulate them in their wealth and children, and make them promises.” But Satan promises them nothing but delusion. ˹Allah added,˺ “You will truly have no authority over My ˹faithful˺ servants.” And sufficient is your Lord as a Guardian" (17:63-65).

He may call people with his voice, rally his forces against them, take a share in their wealth and children, and make them promises. But his promises are only delusion, and he has no authority over Allah's faithful servants. Hell is for him and for those who choose to follow him, and Allah is enough as a guardian for the rest.

### In short

- Allah drove Iblīs out of Paradise, disgraced, despised and banished from His mercy.
- Hell will be filled with him and with every jinn or human who follows him against Allah's command.
- Shayṭān can call, tempt and make empty promises, but he has no authority over Allah's faithful servants.
