<<<VERSE 4:160>>>
The foods withdrawn: "We forbade the Jews certain foods that had been lawful to them for their wrongdoing, and for hindering many from the Way of Allah" (4:160). The passage turns from belief to dinner table, and the punishment fits a people who bent the law itself.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We forbade the Jews certain foods that had been lawful to them"** states that because of the injustice and transgression of the Jews, demonstrated by committing major sins, Allah prohibited some of the lawful, pure things previously allowed to them. Their wrongdoing, in the Arabic, is of some of those of Jewry, not all. Two readings of the prohibition are given. It could be one of decree only, meaning that Allah let the Jews falsely interpret their Book, changing and altering the information about what was allowed, so that out of exaggeration and extremism in religion they prohibited some things for themselves. Or it could mean that in the Tawrah itself, Allah prohibited things allowed before, for all food had been lawful to the Children of Israel before the Tawrah was revealed: "except what Isrāʾīl made unlawful for himself before the Tawrah was revealed" (3:93), namely the meat and milk of the camel. Later, many things were prohibited in the Tawrah, as sūrat al-Anʿām (6) recounts: to those who are Jews every beast with undivided hoof was barred them, and the fat of ox and sheep likewise, saving what clings to their backs or entrails or is mingled with bone. Thus were they recompensed for their rebellion.

**"for their wrongdoing"** explains a difference. The good things forbidden carried no physical or spiritual harm in themselves. In the Sharīʿah brought by Muḥammad al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ, what is known as ḥarām is forbidden for one or the other harm it brings. With the Jews it was the reverse: pure things were taken from them as punishment for transgression and disobedience.

**"and for hindering many from the Way of Allah"** names a double obstruction: themselves and others were alike turned from the truth, a behavior carried from the past into the present. Obstructing good became a standing feature of theirs. Enemies of the messengers they were and remained, killing many of the prophets, denying Muḥammad ﷺ and ʿĪsā (Jesus), peace upon them, and barring the very mention of Allah's religion. One mark of a self-styled religion is the self-styled sign: a group that invents its own rules of lawful and unlawful, observes the outward forms with great care, and takes worldly benefit from ways God forbade, while putting obstacles in the path of the divine caller. Such people are bracketed with the patently irreligious, however religious the forms they keep.

### The inward measure

Embarking on prohibited things necessitates the forbidding of licit things. When gentleness and generosity reach a servant, the cause is his keeping of the Sharīʿah's outward and his reverence for it in spirit and heart, through which he attains rest in whispered prayer and the soft favors that union brings. If harshness and severity appear instead, it is because he looked with denial on the sanctuary of the Sharīʿah and put the religion's prohibited things to work in following the commanding soul. Whoever holds back from the outward of the Sharīʿah finds the beauty of the Ḥaqīqah veiling its face from him. Were the Sharīʿah taken away, the turning wheel would halt. Were the religion taken away, the Twins would leave the sky.

### In short

- Pure foods were withdrawn as punishment, not for any harm in the foods, whether by the Tawrah's ruling or by their own tampering hands.
- The camel of Isrāʾīl and the hoof and fat of sūrat al-Anʿām mark the span of the prohibition.
- Hindering others from the way runs in the family history, and self-styled law-making is its signature.

<<<VERSE 4:161>>>
The crooked gain: "taking interest despite its prohibition, and consuming people's wealth unjustly. We have prepared for the disbelievers among them a painful punishment" (4:161). The charge sheet adds the money crimes.

### Phrase by phrase

**"taking interest despite its prohibition"** recalls a ban already given: Allah had forbidden them ribā, and the prohibition stood written in the Torah. Yet they took it, making usury lawful for themselves through various kinds of tricks, ploys and cons.

**"and consuming people's wealth unjustly"** means devouring men's substance by false pretences, through transgression and bribery, through bribes offered in adjudication, taking wealth without any legitimate right.

**"We have prepared for the disbelievers among them a painful punishment"** singles out the disbelievers among the Jews, and the pain of what is prepared will reach their very hearts.

The inward reading keeps the measure proportional: forbidden acts breed the loss of what was allowed. Let a man trespass with his outward self and the permitted states he once enjoyed grow closed to him, while the subtle kindnesses once received in his innermost self are quietly taken back.

### In short

- Ribā was forbidden in their own Book, and clever devices do not launder it.
- Bribery and false pretences devour wealth, and the punishment prepared for it is felt in the heart.
- Wrongdoing closes the doors that obedience had opened, outward and inward alike.

<<<VERSE 4:162>>>
The exception: "But those of them well-grounded in knowledge, the faithful ˹who˺ believe in what has been revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ and what was revealed before you—˹especially˺ those who establish prayer—and those who pay alms-tax and believe in Allah and the Last Day, to these ˹people˺ We will grant a great reward" (4:162). After the censure, the door.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those of them well-grounded in knowledge"** describes people firm in the religion and full of beneficial knowledge, established in knowledge, a subject treated before in sūrat Āl-'Imrān (3). The verse, said Ibn ʿAbbās, came down about ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and about Thaʿlabah bin Saʿyah, Zayd bin Saʿyah and Asad bin ʿUbayd, men who entered Islam and held true to what Allah sent Muḥammad ﷺ with. They were of the People of the Book who, when the Holy Prophet ﷺ came, found in him fully and unmistakably all the qualities their scriptures had prophesied of the Last among prophets, and believed, like ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, Usayd and Thaʿlabah. In one reading the knowledge meant is knowledge of the Torah, and the believers meant are all the believers, the Emigrants and the Helpers.

**"the faithful ˹who˺ believe in what has been revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ and what was revealed before you"** marks them out: the Qur'ān and every scripture before it they believe, the Torah and the Gospel among them, even though the Jews around them refused to believe.

**"˹especially˺ those who establish prayer"** means the diligent, those who perfect their five daily prayers. The Arabic phrase wa'l-muqīmīna l-ṣalāta stands in the accusative as a laudative, and it is also read in the nominative.

**"and those who pay alms-tax"** reads as the zakah due on wealth and property, or as those who purify themselves, or as both together. Allah knows best.

**"and believe in Allah and in the Last Day"** joins belief that no deity deserves worship except Allah to belief in resurrection after death, with reward or punishment for good and evil deeds.

**"to these ˹people˺ We will grant a great reward"** names Paradise, an immense and abundant reward. One reading of the verb is We shall surely give, another He shall surely give.

### What salvation turns on

The great reward is promised for faith and righteous deeds together. As for salvation as such, it depends on the correction of the essentials of doctrine, subject to one condition: that one's life ends with the blessing of īmān. Not every Jew stood outside this door. Those not caught up in human interpolations, who discovered the original divine religion, who rose above prejudice, the blind imitation of their community's ways and opportunism, found no obstacle to recognizing the truth and surrendering to it. They could see the truth despite every kind of mental block, and these are the people ushered into the Paradise God prepared for them.

### The three knowledges

The inward reading hears the phrase "well-grounded" as a ladder. The firmly rooted in knowledge are those who have gathered its varieties, the knowledge belonging to the Sharīʿah, to the Ṭarīqah, and to the Ḥaqīqah. The Sharīʿah's knowledge is gained by learning. The Ṭarīqah's by practice. The Ḥaqīqah's is simply found, arriving as a gift. Concerning knowledge of the Sharīʿah, He says: "Ask the folk of the remembrance" (16:43). Concerning knowledge of the Ṭarīqah, He says: "Seek the means of approach to Him" (5:35). Concerning knowledge of the Ḥaqīqah, He says: "We taught him knowledge from Us" (18:65). He turned knowledge of the Sharīʿah over to a teacher, knowledge of the Ṭarīqah over to a guide, and knowledge of the Ḥaqīqah He kept to Himself. Whoever fancies he has no need of a teacher for the Sharīʿah is a heretic. Whoever claims the Ṭarīqah is possible without a guide is a tempter. Whoever names any teacher of the Ḥaqīqah besides the Real is deluded. It has been said that the firmly rooted learn the Sharīʿah and put it into practice through self-purification until they perceive the Ḥaqīqah in their secret core. Thus Muṣṭafā said:

> When someone acts on what he knows, God will bequeath him knowledge of what he does not know.

Knowledge not practiced is wasted and becomes an argument against its holder. Knowledge practiced becomes an argument to his benefit, and the knowledge of the Ḥaqīqah reaches him as a gift.

### In short

- ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his fellows, named by Ibn ʿAbbās, stand inside the door the censure closed.
- Prayer, zakah, and belief in Allah and the Last Day are the entry lines, and the reward is Paradise.
- The firmly rooted climb by three knowledges, the learned law, the practiced path, and the gift found in the secret core.
