<<<VERSE 6:56>>>
Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “I have been forbidden to worship those you invoke besides Allah.” Say, “I will not follow your desires, for I then would certainly be astray and not one of those ˹rightly˺ guided.”

Two sentences of refusal, put in the mouth of the Prophet, and both begin with the same command: say.

### Phrase by phrase

**"I have been forbidden"** The prohibition is located in the Qur'an. Nothing is being invented here for the sake of argument. The one who refuses has been told to refuse, and he says so out loud.

**"to worship those you invoke besides Allah"** The ones addressed are worshipped by being called on, and the word for worship covers the calling, the hoping and the relying that go with it. The idols of the Arabs are the plain case, and the sentence does not stop with them.

**"I will not follow your desires"** The desires named are the wish that he stop preaching Allah's oneness, the wish that the idols be honoured, and the wish that a man like Salman and his fellow believers be pushed away from his sitting so that the chiefs can be pleased. To grant any of that would be following them, not leading them.

**"for I then would certainly be astray and not one of those guided"** The order of the two halves is worth keeping. Going astray comes first. Losing guidance is the description of the state he has entered, and not a separate punishment added afterwards. The sense is that he is astray from the path of truth, and so is not guided to it at all. Whoever follows his desires without proof from Allah is in that condition, whoever he is.

### What the refusal is worth

This is the answer to the demand made at the start of the passage. The chiefs would accept the message only if the Prophet moved the poor believers out and produced the sign they asked for. Both requests are answered with a single principle: there is a line he will not cross, and it is not his line to move. What he worships is settled by revelation, and what he wants is settled by the same revelation. A prophet who negotiates with his own desires would be indistinguishable from the people he is warning.

One commentator notes that the address here is aimed at ʿUyaynah and his folk, that is, at the men who were making the demands. The point of the verse is wider than the occasion, since it states the standing condition of anyone who claims to be guided while he chases what he likes.

### The wishful thinking that becomes worship

There is a slower kind of idolatry than bowing to a stone, and it is worth naming here because the verse cuts it off at the root. What a man wants to be true, and takes to be real, is far too often promoted into something he worships. Sometimes, to dodge the consequences of having neglected his duties, he takes somebody as a favourite of Allah and expects that man to stand up for him before his Lord and to put in a word for him. Sometimes he imagines that this man has a charismatic or supernatural greatness of his own, and that attaching himself to the man will make up for what he has failed to do. Sometimes, out of sheer easy-goingness, he invents a god who is easy of access and can be pleased with small things. All of that is supposition, and a supposition can never lead anybody to reality.

What makes the position worse is that such people then challenge those who have rallied to the real Lord of the Universe. They dare to put it to the believers in this form: if all this greatness and majesty belong to the one Allah you represent, then make his retribution come down on us, and we should like to see it. They talk like that because they see themselves surrounded by worldly glory, while the people who call others to Allah's oneness own almost nothing. They forget that their comforts are the fruit of their devotion to this world, and that the missionaries of Allah are empty-handed precisely because their yearning for the Hereafter has kept them from stooping to the level of that devotion.

### In short

- The Prophet's refusal is not a bargaining position but a report of what he has been forbidden.
- Following desires is named as the alternative to following revelation, and going astray is described as the shape of that alternative.
- Pushing weak believers out of the sitting would have been one of those desires, and it is refused in the same breath as idolatry.
- Wishful thinking about holy men, intercession and an easy god is treated here as a kind of worship, and it is refused on the same grounds.

<<<VERSE 6:57>>>
Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Indeed, I stand on a clear proof from my Lord—yet you have denied it. That ˹torment˺ you seek to hasten is not within my power. It is only Allah Who decides ˹its time˺. He declares the truth. And He is the Best of Judges.”

The claim made here is not that he is certain and they are wrong. It is that he is standing on something, and that something is not himself.

### Phrase by phrase

**"I stand on a clear proof from my Lord"** A clear proof is a clear statement, something visible in what has been revealed, and the phrase is read as: I have full insight into my matter and my religion. It is the opposite of the desires named in the previous verse. He is not relying on what suits him, and he is not relying on them.

**"yet you have denied it"** The denial is aimed at the Qur'an and at Allah's oneness. The proof has been put in front of them, and they have passed it by.

**"That torment you seek to hasten is not within my power"** They were asking for the punishment to arrive now, as a test of the claim. The answer is that it was never in his hand to hand out. He is a warner, not an executioner.

**"It is only Allah Who decides"** The ruling on whether to punish at once or to respite belongs to him alone. If he wills, he punishes in response to their demand, and if he wills, he gives them time, out of his great wisdom.

**"He declares the truth. And He is the Best of Judges"** One reading takes the verb as: he relates the truth, so that the sentence means that the report he gives is true and his judgement is just, and that he commands only what is right. On either reading the superlative closes the argument: in the reckoning between his servants there is nobody better, and nobody to appeal past.

### Who was asking, and why

According to al-Kalbi, the ones this verse came about are al-Nadr ibn al-Harith and the chiefs of Quraysh. They used to joke with the Prophet and ask him to bring the chastisement on them, the one he had promised. That mocking request is what the verse takes apart. It assumes that the proof offered by a messenger and the power to enforce it belong in the same hand, and they do not. The same assumption produced the demand in the previous verse, that the poor believers be cleared away, since wealth and rank were taken as evidence that Allah was pleased.

What the chiefs had hold of was the ordinary glamour of this world, and the messengers of Allah had nothing of it. Their standing did not come from what they had, and the reckoning of the two did not depend on their permission.

### In short

- A clear proof from Allah is set against desires, and the difference between the two is the whole content of the verse.
- The torment they demanded is not in the Prophet's hand, and the decision about its timing is not negotiable.
- A variant reading makes the verse say that Allah relates the truth, so that his report and his judgement are both in question.
- The mockery of al-Nadr ibn al-Harith and his folk shows what the verse is answering: a demand that proof and punishment arrive on the challengers' schedule.

<<<VERSE 6:58>>>
Say ˹also˺, “If what you seek to hasten were within my power, the matter between us would have already been settled. But Allah knows the wrongdoers best.”

The verse finishes the thought of the one before it and then hands the case over.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If what you seek to hasten were within my power"** The supposition is that the punishment were in his possession, which it is not. On that reading the clause is not a boast but a reductio: the thing they keep asking for belongs to somebody else.

**"the matter between us would have already been settled"** If it were his, he would have brought it down at once and been rid of the trouble, and they would have been destroyed. One reading adds the motive: he would have hastened it so that he might find rest.

**"But Allah knows the wrongdoers best"** He knows them, and he knows best how long the respite should run for each of them and when to punish. Settling the matter is not delayed because it is uncertain.

### What the linkage does

The verses before this one answered the demand for punishment with the words "the best of judges", and this verse repeats the point with "Allah knows the unjust best". Immediately after that, the description of Allah's knowledge and power takes over, and the passage turns to what is actually in his hand. The movement of the wording is the argument: the case is removed from the Prophet's shoulders and placed where it belongs, and then the verse states who is qualified to hold it.

One report records that the punishment al-Nadr ibn al-Harith asked for did come, and that he was killed after being taken captive on the day of Badr. That is not offered here as proof that every demand gets an answer on the spot. The point of the verse is that timing was never in the Prophet's control, and one man's death at Badr does not make a schedule for anybody else.

### In short

- The hypothetical is the whole argument: if the torment were his to give, the case would have been closed already.
- The delay is attributed to knowledge, not to inability, and the respite has a measured end that only Allah knows.
- The verse closes the sequence about the demanded sign and hands the decision to the one who judges.
- Badr is named in the reports as the day the asked-for punishment overtook one of those who demanded it.

<<<VERSE 6:59>>>
"With Him are the keys of the unseen—no one knows them except Him. And He knows what is in the land and sea."

That is where the verse begins. It goes on to name a leaf falling, a grain in the darkness of the earth, and everything green or dry, and it closes by saying that all of it is written in a perfect Record. After three verses in which the Prophet hands the decision over, this verse states what the one who holds the decision actually knows. It is the widest claim about Allah in this passage, and it is made in four images.

### Phrase by phrase

**"With Him are the keys of the unseen"** The word translated as keys is plural, and its singular can be read two ways. Maftah, with an a, means a treasury, and miftah, with an i, means a key. Both senses are carried by the plural, so some translators write treasures and some write keys, and the outcome is the same, since owning the keys to a treasury is owning the treasury. The phrase "with him" means that they are possessed and held by him alone. He has the exclusive power to bring what they open into existence, and to make it manifest when he decides. Placing "with him" before the rest of the sentence marks that restriction. The next clause then states it outright.

**"no one knows them except him"** The hint becomes a plain statement, driven home so that there is no room for an exemption. The unseen here has a defined sense, which the next section takes up, and most of the doubts raised against the verse are doubts about something else.

**"And He knows what is in the land and sea"** The creatures in the sea and on the land are included, and so is whatever he destroys in them. Naming the two of them is a way of naming the whole universe with everything in it. It is like saying morning and evening for all the time, or east and west for the whole world. Animals, plants and inanimate things are all inside that knowledge.

**"Not even a leaf falls without His knowledge"** He knows the movement of things that have no life at all. What then of the living, and above all of those on whom the divine laws have been laid, human beings and jinn? Every part of the leaf's history is within his knowledge: before it falls, when it falls, and after it falls. He knows how many times it swings and spins in the air, where it lands, and through what circumstances it passes. That a leaf's fall is picked out is also fitting. The fall is the end of its growth and its botanical life, and its last condition stands as a mirror for all the rest.

**"nor a grain in the darkness of the earth"** A grain sown in a field, or buried in the deep dark belly of the earth under a rock, is not outside that knowledge. The order is deliberate. First comes the thing that falls where anybody can see it. Then comes the thing hidden underground.

**"or anything green or dry"** Wet and dry cover every state a thing can be in, and nothing of that kind is left out. In the Arabic, the negation is carried onto the grain clause and onto the wet and the dry by wa-la, and not. The closing exceptive, illa fi kitabin mubin, is read as standing inside the earlier illa ya'lamuha, but he knows it. One affirmation therefore runs through several images.

**"but is written in a perfect Record"** A written book is a manifest book, and what is written stays preserved, leaving nothing to chance, to mistake or to forgetting. Some take the Record to be al-Lawh al-Mahfuz, the Preserved Tablet, where the precise measure and the time of each thing are noted. Some take it to be the divine knowledge itself. The comparison holds either way, because that knowledge is not built on conjecture.

### The five keys

The clause about the keys is read through a hadith that al-Bukhari recorded. In it the Prophet counted the keys of the unseen as five, and said that nobody but Allah knows them. They are the knowledge of the Hour, the descent of the rain, what is in the wombs, what a person will earn tomorrow, and the land in which he will die. The same five are recited in a verse of the surah of Luqman, where all of them are named as Allah's alone (31:34). One reading takes the keys to be the stores of the unseen themselves: rain, vegetation and fruit, and the descent of the punishment they kept asking for, which overtook one of its demanders on the day of Badr. The same knowledge that holds the stores is what makes a leaf and a grain reportable.

### What is not the unseen

Much of the unease around this verse comes from taking al-Ghayb in a loose sense. On that reading it means whatever is simply absent from my knowledge, whether or not somebody else has means of getting at it. Once the word means that, all sorts of practices look like counter-examples. There is astrology, the mathematical divination of fortunes, divination by figures and lines drawn in the sand, and palmistry, all of them harnessed to guess at future events. There is the person who seems to learn things about the future through illumination or inspiration. Then there are the weather forecasters. They examine the drift, power and velocity of the monsoons and announce rain and storms weeks ahead, and are then treated as though they had stepped into the territory of the unseen. Common people reach for these cases and start to doubt the verse, since observation appears to show that others acquire this knowledge too.

The answer given is that the Qur'an's usage is narrower, and the doubts evaporate once it is respected. Knowledge that has to be acquired through means and instruments is not knowledge of the unseen. The forecaster got his information only when the substance of the event had already come into existence and was available to be read. The only difference between him and an ordinary observer is that the sign is not open yet to everybody, and reveals itself to experts through instruments. When it grows stronger it becomes common observation too. That is why a forecaster cannot announce rain two months ahead. The solid evidence of that rain has not presented itself yet. It is also why no physician can tell from a pulse what a patient swallowed a year ago, or will swallow a year hence, because that does not habitually leave a mark. These are things whose existence is foretold by examining traces. Once the traces exist, the matter is observation and not the unseen, however refined the equipment is.

The same holds for the claim that a hidden event can be computed. A man works from existing data and announces that sunrise will be at five forty-one, or that an eclipse will fall on such and such a date. That is something like reading out the arrival board for trains and planes. Determining a time from the speed of something already perceptible is not knowing the unseen. As for astrology and its relatives, the claim to know by that route is deception. One true guess in a hundred false ones is not knowledge.

There is a further refinement. Awareness of this kind stays in the class of estimation and calculation, and the real word for knowledge is reserved for certitude, which none of these methods produce. That is why the records of such predictions are full of errors, and why an honest audit of the whole lot of them comes out badly.

And when part of what belongs to the unseen is given to a prophet through revelation, or to a righteous servant through illumination or inspiration, it has stopped being unseen in the language of the Qur'an. Such material is called news of the unseen, or reports of the unseen, and not the unseen itself. That is the wording of the verse: "That is some of the reports of the unseen which We reveal to you" (11:49). So when this verse states that no one knows the keys except Allah, there is no exception to be carved out of it.

Medicine is put to the test here in a concrete way. When the X-ray was invented it was hoped that the sex of a fetus could be settled, and it did not serve the purpose, and it carried its own harms. Specialists who study the fetus in section view, or who float it in water to read it digitally, are still helpless in the first stretch of pregnancy, when nothing can be known. Even the genetic tests cannot work before a certain stage, and that stage is a matter of observation. Later, predictions are made which are technical approximations at best, sometimes right, sometimes misread, and this is not the certitude the verse is talking about.

### Two attributes no creature shares

The distinctive feature of Islam among the religions is belief in Allah's oneness, and that oneness is not limited to counting him as one. It is believing in him as unique and without equal in all his attributes of perfection, and refusing to take any created being as a sharer in them. Life, knowledge, power, hearing, seeing, will, creation and sustenance belong to that list, and two of them stand out: knowledge and power. His knowledge embraces every existent and every non-existent, open and secret, large and small, atoms and particles, and his power surrounds and controls everything fully and conclusively. The verse before this one and the one after it are both about these two attributes. The tradition names them al-Ilm al-Muhit, the knowledge that encompasses everything, and the knowledge of the unseen besides.

Put the other way round, the claim that an angel's or a messenger's knowledge is all-encompassing hands that creature the status of Allah. In Qur'anic terms that is shirk, and it is the aspect of associating partners that surah Al-Shuʿara puts on the lips of the idolaters on the day of Qiyamah: "By Allah, we were in manifest error when we held you as equals with the Lord of the Worlds" (6:97), and the verse after it belongs to the same confession. That is why the clause about the keys is placed exactly where it is. It is not decoration. It is the line that keeps oneness intact.

The practical conclusion drawn from these two attributes is striking. If a person were to believe in them strongly and completely, and start to imagine them as present before him, then he could not even think of committing a crime. Here is somebody who, in every state, in word and deed, in rest and in movement, is conscious that an All-Knowing, All-Aware, All-Powerful one is watching over him. He knows him outside in and inside out, and he is aware of what the heart intends and what the mind lets pass through it. On that assumption, how could he take even a tiny step towards disobeying his All-Powerful Master? In religious terminology this is known as istihdar, and it has been called the philosopher's stone of the moral life. The verse has been called a sovereign prescription. It is the kind of belief that makes a person a model human being and keeps him one. Its effect is not a theory about the universe. It is a discipline, and a person may say that a discipline is all religion ever asked of him.

### The knowledge that leaves nothing out

Other verses confirm that this all-encompassing knowledge, from which neither a particle nor its condition is excluded, belongs to Allah alone. In the surah of Luqman it is said that Allah will bring forth a thing the weight of a mustard seed, though it be inside a rock or anywhere in the heavens or the earth. The reason given is that Allah is subtle and aware (31:16). In Ayat al-Kursi, the verse of the Throne in surah al-Baqarah, it is said: "They encompass nothing of his knowledge except what he wills" (2:255). In surah Yunus nothing the weight of an atom in the earth or in heaven is said to be hidden from your Lord (10:61). And in surah At-Talaq it is said that Allah encompasses all things in knowledge (65:12). The same point is made about the treasures: "We have treasures of everything, but We send them down in a particular measure" (15:21).

Two sentences in this verse therefore do two different jobs, and they are ordered with a system. The first states the knowledge of the unseen, which nothing else shares. The rest describe ilm al-shahadah, the knowledge of what is present and existing, in a way that lets no particle stand outside it. That is the shape of the claim: a knowledge that nothing fresh or dry escapes, and a power that owns the keys.

### In short

- The keys of the unseen are both the treasuries and the means of access to them, and both senses belong in the wording.
- The verse is read through the hadith of the five keys, and the five are what no creature can get at.
- Forecasts, instruments and computations are not counter-examples, because they read traces that already exist, and what Allah discloses to a prophet stops being unseen.
- Allah's knowledge of the leaf, the grain and everything wet or dry is a claim about the whole universe, recorded in a Preserved Tablet rather than left to chance.
- The verse is a prescription as much as a description. The discipline it aims at is simple, and whoever really holds these two attributes before him cannot bring himself to sin.
