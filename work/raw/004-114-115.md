<<<VERSE 4:114>>>
The good whisper: "There is no good in most of their secret talks" (4:114), "except those encouraging charity, kindness, or reconciliation between people". The passage turns from the theft and the conspiracies of cover-up to the uses counsel can be put to.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There is no good in most of their secret talks"** means the talks people hold with each other and the plans they make for transitory worldly gain, unmindful of the concerns of the life-to-come, are devoid of good. One gloss reads the secret conferences as those of the clan of Tuʿmah. A hadith sets the measure: speech harms its speaker somehow, whatever it is, save the remembrance of Allah, the bidding of the Fair and the forbidding of the Unfair. The bidding of the Fair, the command of what is good, covers every action the Sharīʿah counts good and those who know it patently recognize. The forbidding of the Unfair, Nahy ʿan al-Munkar, covers every action the Sharīʿah counts undesirable, squarely unrecognized by those who know. The bidding includes all imperatives and motivations through which people are led to what is good and right, and helping the oppressed, assisting the needy with loans and guiding the lost all fall under the broad category.

**"except those encouraging charity, kindness, or reconciliation between people"** names the talk that carries real good. Charity and reconciliation belong to that wider category, yet receive their own mention, for their benefit travels from person to person and lifts the collective life of the community. These two fields cover what is most essential about public service, bringing benefit to Allah's creation and protecting people against pain and loss, for charity spreads benefits among people and reconciliation stops mutual pain and loss. The scholars of tafsīr agree that sadaqah, charity, carries a general sense here, running from the obligatory zakāh through voluntary giving to anything that brings a person benefit. The glosses keep the case in view: the encouragement of alms to the poor, the kindness that gives a loan, and the reconciliation between Tuʿmah and Zayd ibn al-Samin, the Jew.

The merit of peacemaking stands in the records. Imām Aḥmad recorded that Umm Kulthūm bint ʿUqbah, one of the immigrant women who pledged allegiance to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, heard him say:

> He who brings about reconciliation between people by embellishing good or saying good things, is not a liar.

She said she never heard him allow what people say of lies except in three cases: in war, in bringing peace between people, and in the man's invented compliments to his wife and her speech to her husband. The Group recorded this hadith, with the exception of Ibn Mājah. Imām Aḥmad also recorded from Abū ad-Dardāʾ that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> Should I tell you what is better than the grade of fasting, praying and sadaqah? Bringing reconciliation between people. Spoiling the relationship between people is the destroyer.

Abū Dāwūd and at-Tirmidhī recorded it also, at-Tirmidhī calling it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. The parallel reports run the same way: an act far superior in rank to fasting, prayers and charity, namely the removal of discord, the making of peace between two persons and the elimination of bickering, and the warning that mutual discord among people is what really shaves, for it does not shave the head but shaves off the faith.

**"seeking Allah's pleasure"** sets the condition. The deeds of bidding charity and fair action and reconciliation are worthwhile and acceptable only when undertaken sincerely and exclusively for the pleasure of Allah, awaiting the reward with Him, the Exalted and Most Honored, without any trace of personal or material interest.

**"We will grant them a great reward"** answers the sincerity with an immense, enormous and tremendous reward, a vast wage in Paradise.

### The call and its answer

The verse has its setting in the fate of every genuine call to truth. Such a call amounts to the establishment of Allah's balance of justice on the earth, and everyone feels himself being weighed in its scale, which removes the veneer from a person and leaves him as he actually is. Those exposed this way are infuriated to an extreme, and the preacher of Allah's word bears the brunt. Finding themselves valueless in the scales of truth, such people are overcome with haughtiness and pride, and they oppose and obstruct whatever casts doubt on their reputation as lovers of truth. Rumours fly against the call and the caller. Others are dissuaded from extending financial help. Every effort is made to keep Allah's servants from rallying around the bearer of the divine message, by planting doubts about what he carries. To those who have kept their consciences alive, Allah gives the ability to surrender before the preacher of His word, to extend full support and mould their lives by his guidance. Speech, their God-given faculty, goes to the open acknowledgement of truth. They urge the spending of time and wealth on the mission, encourage charity and welfare works, and labour to persuade people to quench old enmities and settle grievances. Truth acknowledged, a positive psychology wakes as a natural result, and its disposition runs to positive deeds.

### The inward reading

Three things in this verse hold the best of the servants' deeds: charity, the honourable, and the making-wholesome of the people. The good of them is not specified for one individual, for its profit reaches others. The wonder is not that a person should open a door for himself. The wonder and the chivalry are that he should open a door to himself for another. Pīr Bū ʿAlī Siyāh put it plainly: to please yourself is a small thing, for the real work lies in pleasing another. Muṣṭafā alluded to it in the word that the worst of men is he who eats alone. Charity itself runs to three sorts. With the possessions, it gives comfort to the poor by spending blessings on them. With the body, it takes up for them the duty of service. With the heart, it stays loyal to the beauty of intention and the consolidation of aspiration. There is also charity toward the rich: acting with munificence toward them, not exposing one's need to them, taking back one's hope from their charitable gifts, and coveting nothing from them. Where charity, the honourable and the making-wholesome gather in one person, veneration itself stands from head to foot, the oyster shell of the mysteries of lordhood, welcomed by the witnesses to the divinity. Men will speak of him as the sincerely truthful, and on the morrow his gathering will be with the sincerely truthful. This is the tremendous wage the Exalted Lord has promised.

### In short

- Most secret talk serves the transitory gain and holds no good, save the word of remembrance and the bidding of the Fair.
- Charity, kindness and reconciliation stand out for reaching beyond the speaker, and charity here includes the zakāh and every benefit given.
- Reconciliation outranks fasting, prayer and charity, embellished words in its service are no lie, and discord shaves off faith.
- Sincerity for Allah's pleasure alone makes the deed acceptable, and its wage is tremendous.
- The best deeds open a door for another, and their doer is gathered with the sincerely truthful.

<<<VERSE 4:115>>>
The breach: "And whoever defies the Messenger after guidance has become clear to them and follows a path other than that of the believers" (4:115). The verse names the crime that closes every door of return, and it rests on the authority of the whole community.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And whoever defies the Messenger after guidance has become clear to them"** speaks of the deliberate turn, the choosing of a way other than the Law revealed to the Messenger once the truth stands clear, apparent and plain. The defiance is a breach, and one gloss keeps the episode in view: Tuʿmah opposing regarding Allah's divine Oneness and His judgement, preferring idolatry, the religion of the people of Makkah, to the religion of the believers.

**"and follows a path other than that of the believers"** names a conduct closely related to the contradiction of the Messenger ﷺ. The contradiction could take the form of opposing a text of the Qur'ān or the Sunnah, or of opposing what the Ummah of Muḥammad ﷺ has agreed on. When they all agree on something, that Ummah stands immune from error, a miracle that increases their honour on account of the greatness of their Prophet.

### The authority of consensus

Two crimes are identified in the verse as great and as causes of consignment to Hell. The first is opposing the Messenger of Allah, which is obviously kufr and a terrible curse. The second is the violation of the universal Muslim consensus, the abandoning of something carrying the agreement of all Muslims and the opting for another way against them. This shows that the consensus of the Muslim community is a valid religious authority. Following the injunctions of the Qur'ān and the Sunnah is obligatory, and by the same token, a universal agreement of the Muslim Ummah carries an equally obligatory following, opposition to it being a grave sin. The hadith of the Prophet ﷺ runs that the hand of Allah is on the Muslim community, and the one who splits off from it is himself split off for Hell. Imām al-Shāfī was once asked whether the validity of the authority of the consensus of the Muslim Ummah could be proved from the Holy Qur'ān. He devoted three full days to the recitation of the Qur'ān, completing three recitals during each day and night, and the proof he finally came up with was this verse. When he cited it before the scholars, they all agreed that the proof was sufficient to confirm the authority of ijmāʿ, the consensus of the Muslim community.

**"We will let them pursue what they have chosen, then burn them in Hell"** describes the punishment as a handing over. Going down the wicked path draws its own punishment: the evil way is made to look good in the heart, beautified, so that the temptation deepens. He is turned over to what he turned to, made a leader of the misguidance he followed, left in this world to what he chose and admitted in the Hereafter into the Fire where he burns. The parallels of the Qur'ān run the same course: "Then leave Me Alone with such as belie this Qur'ān. We shall punish them gradually from directions they perceive not" (68:44). "So when they turned away, Allah turned their hearts away" (61:5). "And We shall leave them in their trespass to wander blindly" (6:110). The destination is assembled: "Assemble those who did wrong, together with their companions" (37:22). And the criminals "shall see the Fire and apprehend that they have to fall therein, and they will find no way of escape" (18:53).

**"what an evil end"** seals it: an evil destination, an evil journey's end, an evil return.

The inward reading carries the same law into the life of the heart. Thoughts that come from the Real are His emissaries to the servant, and whoever opposes the subtle directives the inner path demands draws down the punishments of hearts, blinded eyes among them, shut to the perception of what is right. Resisting consensus in the outward religion casts a person out of the fold, and resisting recognized truth, once the path has become clear, works the same falling away.

### In short

- Defying the Messenger after clear guidance, or following a path other than the believers', is the breach that seals the Fire.
- The Ummah's agreement is a miracle and an authority, and al-Shāfī found its proof in this very verse after three days of recitation.
- The punishment is a handing over: the evil path made beautiful in the heart, then the Fire.
- The parallels run from gradual punishment to the assembly of the wrongdoers with their companions.
- In the inward life, resisted directives blind the eyes to the right, as resisted consensus leaves the fold.
