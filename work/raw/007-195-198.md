<<<VERSE 7:195>>>
Verse 195 presses the question home with the faculties of the body. "Do they have feet to walk with? Or hands to hold with? Or eyes to see with? Or ears to hear with? Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Call upon your associate-gods and conspire against me without delay”" The idols have nothing of that equipment, and the Messenger ﷺ is told to invite their worshippers to bring every partner they have against him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do they have feet to walk with? Or hands to hold with? Or eyes to see with? Or ears to hear with?"** The question is asked to reject the claim, not to seek news. The objects that the idolaters call upon have none of these members, while the men who bow to them have all of them. The note on this verse says that the aim of the question is the comparison of the two sides, since the worshipper is the more complete of the two in his being.

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Call upon your associate-gods and conspire against me without delay”"** is the challenge given to the Messenger ﷺ. He is told to invite the idolaters to gather all their gods and to plot against him in whatever way they choose, and to give him no time at all. The words carry his confidence in the One who protects him.

### The witnesses from the earlier passage

The witnesses that stand behind these words are the same ones the earlier verses brought forward. The people of Hud challenged their prophet and called on their gods against him, and Hud answered them with his trust in the Lord who holds the forelock of every living creature (11:54-56). Ibrahim, the friend of Allah who is known as Al-Khalil, argued with his people over the idols they had carved. He broke them and left the largest of them standing (21:58, 37:93, 37:95), and his father heard from him that the idols neither hear nor see nor avail anything (19:42). He told his people that what they and their fathers worshipped were enemies to him except the Lord of all that exists (26:75-78), and he declared himself clear of what they worshipped except the One who created him, and he left that word as a legacy among his offspring (43:26-28). The parable of the fly belongs to the same series, since the objects they call upon could not create a fly even if they all gathered for it, and could not take back what a fly might snatch away (22:73-74). The call to them dies where it is made, since if they are invoked they do not hear the call (35:14).

The reports of the young men named Mu'adh belong to the same story. They came to Islam in Al-Madinah and set about the idols of their people, and the idol of Al-Jamuh was one of the objects they handled. The man who owned it took his lesson from what he saw, and he died at Uhud. The same lesson reached the people of Makkah when the challenge of the Messenger ﷺ was first put to them. It runs through all of these accounts, that what is called upon besides Allah holds neither help nor harm.

### In short

- Verse 195 puts the idols to the test of the body, since they have no feet, hands, eyes or ears, while the men who worship them have all of these.
- The question is a rejection of the claim and not a request for news, and the comparison shows that the worshipper is the more complete of the two.
- The Messenger ﷺ is told to invite them to call on all their associate-gods and to conspire against him without giving him any delay.
- The witnesses of the earlier verses carry the same conclusion, from the words of Hud to the words of Ibrahim, and the accounts of the idols of Al-Madinah, including the idol of Al-Jamuh, left no support to those who trusted in them.

<<<VERSE 7:196>>>
Verse 196 states where the Prophet ﷺ places his trust. "“Indeed, my Protector is Allah Who has revealed this Book. For He ˹alone˺ protects the righteous"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, my Protector is Allah"** The word translated as protector also carries the sense of helper for the one who is under its care. The Prophet ﷺ declares that the One in charge of his affairs is Allah, and that he has no need of anyone besides Him.

**"Who has revealed this Book"** The Book here is the Qur'an. The reports note that the One who is his protector is the same Lord who sent the Book down to him, and one of the accounts adds that the Scripture was brought by Jibril.

**"For He ˹alone˺ protects the righteous"** The righteous, in the reading given by Ibn 'Abbas, are all those who set nothing beside Allah. The prophets and the faithful among the Muslims are included in that word.

### What the verse settles

The Prophet ﷺ is told to declare that he does not fear the hostility of his opponents in the least. Allah, who revealed the Book to him, is his protector and his helper. Among all the attributes of his Lord, the verse singles out the revealing of the Book, and there is a reason for that. The only ground of their hostility toward him was his invitation to the message of the Qur'an. Since the Lord who sent that Book down stood with him, he was certain of help and protection.

The last part of the verse gives a rule that reaches past the Prophet ﷺ. The reports state it in plain terms. Allah does not only help and protect His messengers, who hold a special favour from Him. He helps and protects every Muslim who acts righteously. The opposition of an enemy therefore does no real harm to such a person. In most cases he is made to triumph over those who oppose him in this world. If he is not given that triumph, and if he appears to be defeated, the loss does not touch the aim of his life. His failure in the world is in truth his success, since the object of his life is to seek the pleasure of Allah and to obey Him in every affair. A failure that comes from Allah draws him nearer to that object.

### In short

- Verse 196 has the Prophet ﷺ declare that Allah, who revealed the Book, is his protector, and that the same Lord protects the righteous.
- The word for protector also means helper, and the Prophet ﷺ needed no one besides Allah.
- One account names Jibril as the one who brought the Scripture down.
- Ibn 'Abbas read the righteous as all those who set nothing beside Allah, and the prophets and the faithful among the Muslims fall under the word.
- Because the hostility of the opponents was aimed at the message of the Book, the verse names the revealing of the Book among the attributes of Allah, and it gives the rule that Allah helps and protects every righteous Muslim, so that apparent defeat in this world does not touch the true aim of a believer's life.

<<<VERSE 7:197>>>
Verse 197 restates the weakness of the objects that the idolaters call upon. "But those ˹false gods˺ you call besides Him can neither help you nor even themselves.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"those ˹false gods˺ you call besides Him"** names the idols and every other being that is given the place of a god. The verse applies to the whole class of such objects.

**"can neither help you nor even themselves"** gives the emptiness of the worship given to them. They have no power to bring benefit to those who serve them and no power to keep harm from them. They cannot even defend their own existence. If a thing cannot take care of itself, what can it do for anyone else?

The teachers draw a question out of this. If these objects are utterly incapable and cannot do a single thing, how can a mind turn to them for help instead of turning to Allah? Another comments on the challenge to the Prophet ﷺ to fear the plots of his enemies, and asks how any concern for them could be justified when their gods can do nothing at all.

### The wider class of false gods

One of the reflections on the passage points out that the fault is not limited to the idols that the Arabs knew. It covers every being that receives the worship and the longing of the heart. A person may attach those feelings to his country, to his community, or to some living or dead figure, when they belong to the one and only God. All of that comes into the same class, and the verse applies to all of it.

The passage then takes the objects one by one. None of them has power of its own. None has a leg, a hand or an eye that it made for itself. Every owner of a leg was given a leg, and if the leg is taken away, he cannot fashion another one. The same holds for the hand and for the eye. Every owner of a hand was given a hand, and if it is lost, a new hand cannot be made. Every owner of an eye received it as a gift, and if the eye is gone, no effort can produce another.

Allah alone is the Sustainer and the Saviour of the human being. In this world He remains unseen. In the world to come He will reveal Himself, and then a person will see with his own eyes that Allah alone was the provider and the supporter of all that lives. It was only from a want of wisdom that people attributed that care to supposed deities. Those associates of theirs have no power to look after themselves, and the question of what they could do for others answers itself. Allah alone helps His servants in this world, and He it is who will help His faithful servants in the world to come.

### In short

- Verse 197 states that the false gods called upon besides Allah can neither help their worshippers nor help themselves.
- The objects of such worship have no power to bring benefit or to keep harm away, and they cannot defend their own existence, so a mind that turns to them is turning to something dead.
- The same fault extends beyond the idols of Arabia to every being that receives the longing and worship due to Allah alone, whether a country, a community or a person living or dead.
- None of these objects has a leg, a hand or an eye that it made for itself, since every such gift was given by Allah, and once taken away it cannot be restored by any power of the creature.
- Allah alone is the Sustainer and the Saviour, unseen in this world and revealed in the world to come, and it is He who helps His servants and will help His faithful servants in the life after death.

<<<VERSE 7:198>>>
Verse 198 returns to the silence of the idols when they are called. "If you ˹idolaters˺ call them to guidance, they cannot hear. And you ˹O Prophet˺ may see them facing towards you, but they cannot see"

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you ˹idolaters˺ call them to guidance, they cannot hear"** The call to guidance is the call to the truth and to the right path. Nothing of it reaches the idols, because they are not living but dead. A dead thing has no hearing, and no call of any kind can enter it.

**"And you ˹O Prophet˺ may see them facing towards you, but they cannot see"** draws the picture that the eye takes in. The figures stand with faces turned, and they look in the direction of the one who stands before them. The look is a shape cut in stone or metal. The eyes are drawn or carved, and they perceive nothing at all.

### Why the verse speaks of them as seeing

The reports explain the form of the sentence. The idols have no sight, and yet the words are put in the way a person would be described. They are made in the shape of human beings, with eyes drawn on the face. Such a figure gives the impression of looking at the one who stands before it, since its eyes are open and turned. The Book therefore treats them as if they had a mind and could look, and then denies the sight. One reading explains the matter in the same way, that a person may see such a figure staring at him, and that the stare is only the shape of a stare, with nothing behind it.

### The statues and their makers

The reports describe how these objects came to be. People used to make statues in the form of human beings and animals. The statues were given hands and feet and eyes. The hands could not hold, the feet could not walk, and the eyes, though they were painted, could not see. The figures were lifeless objects, fixed where they were placed and unable to move from the spot.

From that follows the lesson of the whole passage. A thing that cannot hear a call cannot answer it. A thing that cannot see cannot know the one who stands before it or the need that he brings. The call of guidance is given to the living Lord of all, who hears and sees and answers, and the idols are on the other side of that line.

### In short

- Verse 198 states that if the idolaters call the idols to guidance, no hearing comes of it, and that the Prophet ﷺ may see the figures facing him while they see nothing.
- The call to guidance means the call to the truth and the right path, and no part of it reaches the idols, since they are not living but dead.
- The reports explain that the figures are shaped like human beings and given eyes, so they look as though they are staring at the one before them, and the verse treats them in that manner only to deny that any sight is there.
- People used to make statues in the likeness of humans and animals, with hands and feet and eyes, and those figures could not hold, walk or see, being lifeless objects that could not move.
- A thing that cannot hear cannot answer a call, and the guidance belongs to the living Lord who hears and sees and answers, not to the shapes that stand facing the worshipper.
