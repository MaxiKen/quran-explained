<<<VERSE 2:229>>>
Divorce cannot be used as an endless threat followed by repeated reclamation: "Divorce may be retracted twice, then the husband must retain ˹his wife˺ with honour or separate ˹from her˺ with grace." The verse limits revocable divorce, commands decent treatment whether the marriage continues or ends, and protects the wife's property. It also permits khul', a separation in which a wife offers compensation when the couple cannot maintain the duties of marriage. These are Allah's boundaries, not opportunities for either spouse to exploit the other.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Divorce may be retracted twice,"** The divorce meant here is one after which the husband may restore the marriage during the 'iddah, the prescribed waiting period. He may divorce once and return, and a second time and return. A third divorce does not carry the same right of revocation. The permission is not an invitation to pronounce all three at once.

**"then the husband must retain ˹his wife˺ with honour or separate ˹from her˺ with grace."** Continuing means restoring genuine married life with good companionship and without harm. Releasing means allowing separation to take its course while giving the woman her rights. One explanation concerns letting the waiting period finish after a first or second divorce. Another includes a third divorce, which must likewise be accompanied by kindness rather than injustice.

**"It is not lawful for husbands to take back anything of the dowry given to their wives,"** Mahr is the marriage payment belonging to the wife. A husband cannot pressure her into returning it, cancelling an unpaid claim or surrendering gifts as the price of being released. His decision not to remain married does not turn her property back into his.

**"unless the couple fears not being able to keep within the limits of Allah."** The exception concerns a breakdown in which the spouses fear that they cannot fulfil the rights Allah has established between them. A wife may be unable to live with her husband because of deep aversion or differences of temperament. The question is whether married life can continue within its duties, not whether a husband can profit from making it intolerable.

**"So if you fear they will not be able to keep within the limits of Allah, there is no blame if the wife compensates the husband to obtain divorce."** People who know the couple or are related to them can consider their situation. Where the fear is real, the wife may offer compensation for release, and the husband may accept it. Neither is blamed for this genuine arrangement. Its freedom from blame must not be extended to money extracted through cruelty.

**"These are the limits set by Allah, so do not transgress them."** The rules governing halal, what is lawful, and haram, what is unlawful, in marriage are boundaries fixed by Allah. Both the right of return and the exception allowing compensation have limits. Neither may be detached from fairness and used for the opposite purpose.

**"And whoever transgresses the limits of Allah, they are the ˹true˺ wrongdoers"** Overstepping these boundaries wrongs the other person and oneself. The transgressor exposes himself to Allah's anger and judgment. Having a legal power does not place its holder above the law that gave it.

### Why was repeated revocation restricted?

An earlier practice allowed a husband to divorce and reclaim his wife repeatedly while her waiting period continued, even after a hundred divorces. That created a means of keeping a woman bound without offering settled married life. The new restriction removed the right to revoke after a third divorce.

Abu Dawud records Ibn 'Abbas explaining the change in connection with (2:228). A husband had previously been able to take back his wife even after divorcing her three times. The rule that revocable divorce is twice replaced that practice. An-Nasa'i also collects the report.

'Urwah relates a particular form of abuse. A man told his wife that he would neither release her nor truly take her back. His plan was to divorce her and then reclaim her whenever the waiting period was nearly over. She reported this to the Prophet ﷺ, and the verse was revealed. Ibn Abu Hatim records the account, and Ibn Jarir, also called At-Tabari, transmits it too.

Ibn 'Abbas explains the command after two divorces as a call to fear Allah concerning the third. The husband must either keep his wife kindly or let her go without infringing her claims. The limit is not simply a numerical procedure. It removes the possibility of turning repeated divorce into captivity.

### Why is marriage more than an ordinary contract?

Nikah, marriage, has a contractual aspect: people undertake obligations through agreement. But it is also worship and Sunnah, following the Prophet's ﷺ way. The Muslim community, the ummah, recognises that it is more than a transaction like a sale, loan or payment. Abu Hanifah and several other jurists emphasise that its character as worship takes precedence over its commercial resemblance.

This explains why mutual willingness alone does not supply every condition. Some categories of people may not marry one another at all. The discussion also requires witnesses to be present for proposal and acceptance. Unlike an ordinary transaction, where witnesses may become necessary chiefly when a dispute arises, marriage is not made valid here merely by the couple privately agreeing and never later disputing it. Public announcement is the Sunnah. Other rules of conduct may be obligatory or recommended, but the basic point remains that marriage has a sacred and public form.

Its dissolution is correspondingly regulated. Ending an ordinary bargain may immediately release both parties to make another. Talaq, divorce, carries residual responsibilities and a waiting period. The wife cannot marry someone else during that interval, and the husband's obligations are not all erased by pronouncing a word.

The intended commitment is lifelong. Its breakdown can affect not only two spouses but children, later generations, extended families and even wider communities. The instructions therefore first seek to prevent avoidable separation. Spouses should try to understand one another and talk through disagreements. Advice, warning and restraint are part of the effort to resolve conflict. Where the dispute becomes serious, an arbitrator from each family can work towards reconciliation. Keeping the effort within people close to both parties may prevent wider publicity from hardening the conflict.

None of this means a marriage must continue regardless of its condition. Reconciliation sometimes fails, and a relationship intended as comfort becomes a punishment for both. In that situation a regulated separation can offer relief. The law does not simply declare the bond unbreakable in every circumstance.

### What safeguards surround the power of divorce?

The legal arrangement described here gives the husband the direct power of talaq. Its rationale associates that assignment with expected deliberation, foresight and forbearance, and contrasts them with the danger of temporary emotion controlling a decision. This is the reasoning offered for the distribution of powers, not a cancellation of the wife's means of release.

A woman may bring cruelty or other grounds before a judge qualified under Islamic law, prove her case and obtain annulment or divorce. She is not required to endure her husband's wrongdoing without recourse. Khul' supplies another route under the conditions stated in this verse.

The permission of divorce is also restrained by strong disapproval of unnecessary use. A hadith quoted in this discussion describes divorce as the most detested of lawful things. It is presented as a solution to serious necessity, not a routine response to passing displeasure.

The timing rules give room for reflection and protect the wife from an unnecessarily prolonged wait. Divorce during menstruation is forbidden in the explanation given here. So is divorce during a tuhr, an interval of purity, in which intercourse has occurred. A menstrual period already under way would not count towards the subsequent waiting calculation, while intercourse in the interval of purity raises the possibility of pregnancy and a wait continuing until childbirth.

Waiting for the proper time can also allow anger to subside. Forgiveness and repair may make the intended divorce unnecessary. The regulation is therefore not only a calculation after a decision. It slows the path towards an irreversible rupture.

A first or second clear divorce leaves a possibility of ruju', revocation, during the waiting period. If the husband restores the relationship then, the existing marriage remains. If he does not, the waiting period ends and the separation becomes final. After the third divorce he no longer has that right of return, and mutual willingness alone does not immediately make a new marriage between them lawful. The further conditions are treated in (2:230).

### May three divorces be pronounced together?

The staged structure is used as evidence against combining three pronouncements in one act. An-Nasa'i records from Mahmud bin Labid that the Prophet ﷺ heard of a man doing this and stood in anger. He objected to Allah's Book being treated as a plaything while he was still among them. A man present reacted by asking whether he should kill the offender. The reported question conveys the intensity of the scene, not an instruction here to carry out such an act.

The rule keeps opportunities for repair from being swept away in one outburst. Even when separation is unavoidable, taqwa, mindfulness of Allah, requires justice and kindness. The most emotional circumstances do not suspend those demands.

### When may money be returned?

A husband may not neglect his wife's rights, refuse to release her and then exploit her distress to demand property. Returning the mahr, waiving it or handing over gifts under that pressure is not the voluntary compensation allowed here. The prohibition is connected with (4:19), which forbids treating women harshly to recover what was given to them.

A genuinely willing gift is different. The discussion connects that case with (4:4), which permits accepting something a wife freely gives with a good heart. Consent is not manufactured by making married life unbearable.

Khul' addresses another distinct case. Where a wife dislikes her husband or finds their temperaments incompatible, she may fear that she cannot fulfil her marital duties. The difficulty can concern his appearance or character, but it need not involve an accusation that his religion or behaviour is bad. If both recognise that the marriage cannot be maintained within Allah's limits, compensation for release is permitted.

The material describes returning what he had given as mahr or gifts, or offering money as the agreed means of release. The verse removes blame from both the woman who offers it and the husband who accepts it under the stated conditions. It does not remove the earlier prohibition against coercion.

Different readings of the wording preserve the same central concern. The ordinary form says that the two fear failure to maintain the limits. A passive form describes fear being held concerning them, and second-person forms address the two directly. The grammatical discussion concerns who is described as fearing and maintaining the limits, not a different permission to extract wealth.

### What happened with the wife of Thabit bin Qays?

Several reports connect this permission with Thabit bin Qays bin Shammas and his wife. The names given in those accounts differ and should remain visible. Ibn Jarir names Habibah bint 'Abdullah bin Ubayy bin Salul. Another account calls her Jamilah, daughter of 'Abdullah Ibn Ubayy Ibn Salul, identified as chief of the hypocrites. Malik's Muwatta' gives the name Habibah bint Sahl Al-Ansariyah.

In Malik's account, the Prophet ﷺ was going out to the dawn prayer and found Habibah at his door in the darkness. She explained that she could no longer remain with Thabit. When Thabit arrived, the Prophet ﷺ told him what she had said and treated her speaking about the difficulty as permissible. She said that everything he had given her was still with her. The Prophet ﷺ told him to accept it. He did, and she remained at her family's home. Ahmad, An-Nasa'i and Abu Dawud also report this account.

Al-Bukhari records from Ibn 'Abbas that Thabit's wife did not criticise his religion or his character. Her fear was of kufr within Islam, explained here as failing to acknowledge and fulfil his rights. The Prophet ﷺ asked whether she would return his garden. She agreed, and he instructed Thabit to take the garden and divorce her once. An-Nasa'i also records the report.

The distinction matters. Release was not limited to a case where a woman could prove that her husband was irreligious or morally corrupt. A serious inability to continue the marriage and honour its obligations could itself require a lawful way out.

### Is every request for divorce treated alike?

No. Permission for release in genuine difficulty stands beside a warning against requesting divorce without justification. Ibn Jarir records from Thawban a saying of the Prophet ﷺ that a woman who asks her husband for divorce without a reason will be denied the fragrance of Paradise. At-Tirmidhi also records it and grades it Hasan, meaning good.

That warning must not erase the case just described, in which the Prophet ﷺ permitted the woman to state her inability to continue and directed the separation. The accounts distinguish groundless disruption from a real fear that the marriage cannot be lived rightly.

At-Tirmidhi also records the waiting period following a khul' obtained by Rubayi' bint Mu'awwidh bin 'Afra'. The Prophet ﷺ instructed her to wait for one menstrual period. That report concerns this form of release and is not simply replaced by assuming that every kind of separation has an identical waiting calculation.

### What does keeping Allah's limits mean?

A hadith described here as authentic warns against crossing Allah's limits, neglecting His commands or committing what He has prohibited. It adds that some matters have been left without a ruling as mercy, not forgetfulness, and warns against probing them in that spirit. No extra collector is attached here where none is supplied for that report.

The warning gives the divorce rules their proper scale. They are neither arbitrary obstacles to relief nor devices by which a more powerful spouse can engineer suffering. Their purposes must be respected alongside their forms.

### Why does separation receive such a sorrowful inward reading?

An allusive reading sees the spacing of divorce as a warning against hurrying towards final separation. It connects the pain of parting with the praise in (13:21) for those who join what Allah has commanded to be joined. The permission to separate does not make the breaking of familiarity and companionship something to celebrate.

One account pictures angels made half of fire and half of snow, with Allah's power holding these opposites together. Their glorification praises the One who joins them and asks Him to bring believing hearts into harmony. The image urges reconciliation without denying the legal permission when reconciliation fails.

A story among the Sufis, spiritual wayfarers, gives another image of separation. A pir, a spiritual elder, meets an unfamiliar figure in the desert beside water and growing plants. The figure names himself Abu Murra, the Father of Bitterness. He says that the water is his tears and that the plants grow from them. He weeps in separation over the days of union, finding even their memory a small comfort in deprivation. The accompanying verse laments how hopes of companionship can end in estrangement.

A further story concerns Hasan ibn 'Ali after divorcing a wife. He sent her dower of 40,000 dirhams in the hope of comforting her. She set it before her and cried over the inadequacy of wealth when her companion was no longer beside her. The closing image compares such consolation to offering sweets where a snakebite calls for an antidote. Money may discharge a material claim without curing the grief of separation. That is why release must be gracious, not merely financially calculated.

### In short

- Revocable divorce is limited to two occasions, preventing endless divorce and reclamation.
- Whether retaining or releasing a wife, a husband must respect her rights and must not use cruelty to recover her property.
- Khul' permits genuine compensation for release when the marriage cannot be lived within Allah's limits.
- Divorce has safeguards, stages and opportunities for repair. Its permission does not make careless or coercive use acceptable.
- Keeping Allah's boundaries requires fairness in purpose as well as compliance in form.
