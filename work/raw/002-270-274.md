<<<VERSE 2:270>>>
Neither a hidden gift nor a private promise escapes Allah: "Whatever charities you give or vows you make are surely known to Allah." His knowledge assures the sincere person that good is not lost. It also warns someone who withholds obligations or spends wrongly that appearances cannot conceal the truth.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whatever charities you give"** The wording embraces every expenditure, small or large. Allah knows whether it is halal, lawfully acquired, directed towards obedience and offered sincerely. He also knows spending in sin, hypocrisy, inferior property and gifts spoiled by reproach. Calling something sadaqah, charity, does not conceal its defects.

**"or vows you make"** Nadhr is a vow, a commitment to an act not previously required of the person. The discussion includes monetary worship and bodily worship. Vows may be unconditional or dependent on an event, fulfilled or unfulfilled. Their full reality is known to Allah.

**"are surely known to Allah."** The sincere act seeking His pleasure receives reward. The comprehensive wording also encourages observance of the conditions and limits. No one can rely on a public reputation when private intention or conduct contradicts it.

**"And the wrongdoers will have no helpers"** Those rejecting revelation, associating partners with Allah, withholding duties or transgressing His limits have no protector against His punishment. The warning concerns the failure to obey, not the fear that an unnoticed sincere gift has been forgotten.

### Why join expenditure and vows?

Both involve an undertaking towards Allah, but their outward forms vary. Money may be distributed publicly while a vow remains known only to the person making it. His knowledge encompasses both, including whether the undertaking was honoured.

The purpose is not merely to announce that records exist. It is to awaken responsibility. The giver should observe the conditions, and the person making a vow should not treat a solemn commitment as an empty utterance.

Nor can support from others override divine judgment. Helpers may conceal conduct from people, but cannot conceal it from Allah or protect a person from His anger at resurrection.

### What does awareness of His knowledge offer inwardly?

The allusive reading distinguishes people moved by warning of punishment, people encouraged by reward and those whose deepest reassurance is being known by Allah. For the latter, His attentive knowledge is itself an intimate promise.

It recalls (52:48), commanding patience under the Lord's judgment and assuring the servant of His watchful care. Opposition to inward covenants is described as a danger to that relationship. The aspirant should therefore guard the heart's fidelity, not only the public form of the undertaking.

The shared reflection on wisdom adds that self-centred interests can prevent clear sight. Considering Allah's cause as one's own helps a person look beyond those limitations. An open mind recognises what attachment to possessions can obscure.

### In short

- Every expenditure and vow is known in its intention, form and fulfilment.
- Allah rewards sincere obedience and sees defects hidden by appearances.
- Wrongdoing cannot be sheltered from Him by human helpers.
- Remembering His watchful care encourages inward as well as outward fidelity.

<<<VERSE 2:271>>>
Charity does not require publicity to have value: "To give charity publicly is good, but to give to the poor privately is better for you." Secrecy can protect both the giver's sincerity and the recipient's dignity. Yet public giving remains good, and circumstances can make it the better way to serve a sound purpose.

### Phrase by phrase

**"To give charity publicly is good,"** A declared gift can be a righteous act. Open giving is not the same as giving for display. Its worth depends on purpose and conduct. One explanation particularly relates the public gift to obligatory alms, while another includes voluntary charity.

**"but to give to the poor privately is better for you,"** Concealment protects against boasting and spares the recipient public embarrassment. One gloss identifies the poor with the people of al-Suffah, the Platform. The gift's being hidden from people does not hide it from Allah.

**"and will absolve you of your sins."** Charity can bring forgiveness as well as a raised rank. The benefit is not restricted to secret gifts. Its mention here reassures someone who sees no worldly recognition that forgiveness is a real and great gain.

**"And Allah is All-Aware of what you do"** He knows outward action and inward intention together. Readings of the preceding verb express either He will absolve or We will absolve, with grammatical differences concerning how the clauses are joined. The promise remains Allah's forgiveness, not the audience's verdict.

### Is secrecy always preferable?

One explanation applies a general preference for secrecy to obligatory and voluntary charity alike. Besides reducing the danger of riya', display, it protects the recipient and does not reveal the giver's resources. It therefore has practical benefits as well as religious ones.

Other explanations prefer making obligatory zakah manifest so that others may follow the example and the payer is not falsely accused of withholding it. They distinguish that from the ordinary preference for concealing voluntary sadaqah.

Both discussions recognise circumstances in which publicity serves a legitimate purpose. Encouraging imitation of a good act or removing an accusation can make an open gift better. That does not abolish the basic reason for secrecy where no such need exists.

The Prophet ﷺ also compares audible Qur'anic recitation with disclosed charity and quiet recitation with concealed charity. The comparison acknowledges both forms while directing attention to their different circumstances and benefits.

### Who is shaded through hidden charity?

The Two Sahihs record from Abu Hurayrah a hadith of the Prophet ﷺ about seven kinds of people receiving Allah's shade when no other shade exists. One gives so secretly that the left hand does not know what the right has given.

The others include a just ruler and a youth raised in worship. There is a person whose heart remains attached to the mosques after leaving them. Two people love each other for Allah and meet and part on that basis. Another remembers Him alone and weeps. Another refuses the invitation to illicit intercourse from a beautiful woman of noble standing because of fear of Allah.

The range joins private restraint, public responsibility, love and remembrance. Hidden charity belongs to a broader life in which obedience is not dependent on being watched by people. Its vivid description concerns concealment, not an anatomical claim about separate knowledge in the hands.

### What question is reported at revelation?

Al-Kalbi says the Companions asked the Prophet ﷺ whether giving secretly or openly was better after hearing the instruction about expenditure. This verse answered their question. The report explains why the comparison is made without condemning every open gift.

### What inward distinction is drawn?

The allusive reading compares an open gift with publicly showing companionship with Allah. That can be generous and good. Protecting the intimacy from unnecessary intermediaries, however, preserves the conditions of love and raises the structure of communion.

Suhba means companionship, widad love and wusla communion in this reflection. The analogy adds an inward application to the practical instruction. It does not require abandoning the visible help owed to poor people.

### In short

- Public charity can be good, while secrecy commonly protects sincerity and dignity.
- Sound reasons, such as encouraging others or clearing an accusation, can favour publicity.
- Scholarly explanations differ over the ordinary public treatment of obligatory alms.
- Forgiveness and Allah's knowledge give a hidden gift lasting value.
- The shaded giver illustrates devotion independent of an audience.

<<<VERSE 2:272>>>
Charity must not become a means of withholding kindness until another person accepts faith: "You are not responsible for people’s guidance ˹O Prophet˺—it is Allah Who guides whoever He wills." The Prophet ﷺ conveys and explains the truth. Allah alone brings acceptance to the heart. Giving for His pleasure benefits the giver even when the recipient has not embraced the religion.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You are not responsible for people’s guidance ˹O Prophet˺"** The duty is to communicate the message, not to create belief by personal power. The verse is connected with reluctance to help non-Muslim relatives and with withholding voluntary alms in the hope of prompting conversion.

**"it is Allah Who guides whoever He wills."** Acceptance is His special prerogative. The messenger's honoured station does not transfer that divine power to him. Calling towards truth and granting inward guidance remain distinct.

**"Whatever you ˹believers˺ spend in charity, it is for your own good"** The recipient receives help, and the giver receives the reward of obedience. Allah needs none of the property. The principle resembles (41:46), which says that righteous conduct benefits the person doing it.

**"as long as you do so seeking the pleasure of Allah."** Seeking His Face is explained through seeking His pleasure and reward, not a temporary worldly objective. Al-Hasan Al-Basri includes expenditure on oneself when its purpose is obedience.

**"Whatever you donate will be paid back to you in full, and you will not be wronged"** The smallest sincere expenditure is not omitted. The emphasis rules out diminished reward and injustice. Another gloss adds that the person's sins are not increased through the gift.

### What occasions are reported?

An-Nasa'i records from Ibn 'Abbas that the believers disliked giving charity to relatives who associated partners with Allah. They asked and were allowed to give, with this verse revealed concerning the question.

Another account names Asma', daughter of Abu Bakr, or Asma', daughter of Abu'l-Nadir, asking about relatives who did not follow the religion. Both names occur as alternatives in the explanation. It also refers to poor people among the idolaters and the people of the Book.

The instruction rejects withholding voluntary assistance simply to force a religious response. The giver should pursue the reward of sincere kindness without claiming responsibility for producing the recipient's faith.

### Which legal categories are distinguished?

The legal discussion identifies the charity here as nafl, voluntary giving. It permits giving to a dhimmi, a non-Muslim protected resident under the category described, while distinguishing obligatory zakah. Al-Mazhari says zakah is not given to anyone except a Muslim and is not meant by this passage.

The same discussion permits other kinds of obligatory and voluntary sadaqah to dhimmis, but excludes the category harbi from receiving any kind of sadaqah. These are the distinctions stated in that legal explanation, not a claim that every non-Muslim recipient belongs to one undifferentiated category.

Thus the general encouragement of kindness and its particular legal treatment should not be collapsed. The verse's assurance of reward is explained through voluntary charity, while zakah retains its specified conditions.

### What if the giver unknowingly chooses the wrong recipient?

'Ata' Al-Khurasani says someone giving for Allah is not asked to bear the recipient's wickedness. The explanation concerns a sincere gift unintentionally reaching a righteous, evil, deserving or undeserving person. Sound intention is not defeated by an honest mistake.

The Two Sahihs record from Abu Hurayrah the story of a man who resolved to give charity at night. Unknowingly he gave first to an adulteress. People discussed it the next morning, and he praised Allah and resolved to try again.

The second gift unknowingly reached a rich person. After another morning of public comment, he praised Allah and gave again. The third reached a thief. He again thanked Allah despite discovering the recipient's identity.

He was then told that the gifts had been accepted. The woman might be helped to abstain from adultery. The rich man might be prompted to give from his own wealth, and the thief might turn from stealing.

The account does not encourage deliberate carelessness about recipients. It demonstrates that a sincere mistake can still carry reward and even unforeseen benefit. The giver is responsible for personal purpose and effort, not omniscient knowledge of every beneficiary.

### Why is an open mind part of giving wisely?

The shared reflection describes the confinement caused by personal interests. An argument may be true while a closed mind refuses to perceive it. Devoting resources to Allah's cause can loosen that confinement and permit a clearer view of realities beyond one's immediate advantage.

Its language of rising towards divine independence, sufficiency and wisdom pictures a widening consciousness, not identity between the creature and Allah. The point is seeing beyond want and self-interest so that things can be recognised more truly.

### How is the Prophet's honour distinguished from divine guidance?

The inward address celebrates Muhammad ﷺ through the praiseworthy station, the firmly tied banner, noble degrees, high stations and pleasing practices. It calls him leader of those coming first and last and denies that anyone equals him.

Yet the same address reserves inward guidance entirely to Allah. Even that unmatched prophetic honour does not make him the originator of faith in another heart. He calls, while Allah guides. The distinction protects both his mission and Allah's unique authority.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ explains the truth, while Allah alone grants inward acceptance.
- Voluntary charity need not be withheld until relatives accept faith.
- The legal explanation distinguishes such giving from zakah and other specified categories.
- Sincere gifts are rewarded fully, including gifts reaching unsuitable recipients unintentionally.
- Serving Allah's cause can free perception from the limits of self-interest.

<<<VERSE 2:273>>>
Some of the most deserving people are also the easiest to overlook: "˹Charity is˺ for the needy who are too engaged in the cause of Allah to move about in the land ˹for work˺." Their restraint can look like wealth to someone who does not know them. The giver is invited to notice real need without requiring a humiliating public appeal.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Charity is˺ for the needy who are too engaged in the cause of Allah to move about in the land ˹for work˺."** The explanation refers to poor emigrants residing in Al-Madinah without sufficient resources. It particularly identifies the people of al-Suffah, living by the Prophet's mosque. One account describes about four hundred emigrants devoted to studying the Qur'an and preparing for the struggle.

**"Those unfamiliar with their situation will think they are not in need ˹of charity˺ because they do not beg."** Modest conduct and restraint conceal their poverty. Clothes and speech can suggest comfort to someone who has not investigated. Lack of asking is not proof that resources are sufficient.

**"You can recognize them by their appearance."** The discerning person recognises marks of need, exertion and humility. The instruction encourages attention to circumstances rather than dependence on a spoken demand.

**"They do not beg people persistently."** Some explanations take the wording as excluding importunate asking while not necessarily excluding every request. The stronger explanation says they do not ask at all, so persistence does not arise. Al-Qurtubi describes that complete restraint as the agreed interpretation in the discussion he gives.

**"Whatever you give in charity is certainly well known to Allah"** He knows the money and intention, and no quiet help is lost. Its full reward is kept for resurrection, when it is urgently needed.

### What does inability to travel mean?

The wording about moving through the land concerns seeking livelihood through trade and travel. Related forms are used in (4:101) concerning travel and shortening salah, prayer, and in (73:20) concerning sickness, journeys seeking Allah's bounty and fighting in His cause.

Here the religious commitment restricts the opportunity to earn. The description therefore reaches people unable to take other work because of their service and preoccupation, not only someone whose physical feet cannot move.

The shared financial reflection warns against treating personal comforts as the only rightful destination of income. Recognising these people's need corrects that narrowed view and permits wealth to support a necessary service beyond one's own household.

### Who is the needy person in the hadith?

The Two Sahihs record from Abu Hurayrah a saying of the Prophet ﷺ distinguishing the person who goes around receiving a date, mouthful or meal from someone with more hidden need. The latter lacks enough to live upon, is not recognised as needy and does not ask people for assistance.

Ahmad also records the hadith from Ibn Mas'ud. Its purpose is to direct attention towards someone who may receive nothing precisely because of restraint. The charitable system should not see only the loudest request.

Ahmad records from Abu Sa'id that his mother sent him to ask the Prophet ﷺ for help. On arrival he sat down and heard an instruction about satisfaction, modesty and contentment. Allah would enrich, make decent and suffice the person adopting those qualities. Asking while already having sufficient means was described as begging.

Abu Sa'id remembered his camel, Al-Yaqutah, worth more than the small amount in question, and left without requesting anything. Abu Dawud and An-Nasa'i record the same wording. The report treats unnecessary asking differently from genuine unprovided need.

### Does good clothing disqualify a poor person?

Al-Qurtubi says a faqir who does not own the nisab, the threshold of zakah, is not made wealthy merely by wearing costly clothes. Zakah may be paid to that person. A visible possession should therefore not be mistaken for a complete measure of financial sufficiency.

This sits beside the instruction to recognise need by signs. Appearance can inform judgment, but an unexamined impression can mislead. The passage asks for discernment rather than an automatic rule equating respectable dress with wealth.

### What legal use of signs is mentioned?

The discussion permits rulings based on circumstantial evidence. Its example concerns a body found wearing a zunnar, a waist-cord associated with certain non-Muslim religions, and bearing the additional sign of being uncircumcised. In that described case it says the body is not buried in the Muslim graveyard.

The example is supplied as an application of inference from visible signs, not as a reason to disregard the living person's known faith. Other references concerning recognition include (48:29), marks upon faces, and (47:30), recognition through the tone of speech.

### What spiritual restraint is pictured?

The inward reading calls this description a balm for the broken-hearted. The servants have halted at Allah's decree, keeping their souls obedient, hearts attentive to recognition, spirits at ease in love and secret cores awaiting vision.

Their absorption in the Exalted Lord is pictured as leaving no room for self-centred acquisition. The reflection recalls (24:37), people whom trade does not divert from remembrance, and the enduring abode in (40:39). It includes some Helpers as well as emigrants.

Their watchword is remembrance and their covering love. Despite material constraint, their aspiration is not fixed on other people. Not asking creatures is interpreted as trust. Not asking even from Allah is interpreted, in this particular allusive description, as complete approval of His decree.

That describes a spiritual station rather than cancelling the general legitimacy of supplication. The outward duty remains to recognise and support the poor, whose dignity should not prevent assistance.

### In short

- Seek out people whose religious service limits earning and whose restraint hides need.
- Respectable appearance or silence does not prove financial sufficiency.
- The hadith draws attention to poverty overlooked because its sufferer does not ask.
- The instruction supports discerning use of circumstances rather than superficial assumptions.
- The inward reading pictures trust and acceptance without removing the community's duty to help.

<<<VERSE 2:274>>>
Generosity is not confined to a special hour or a single visible occasion: "Those who spend their wealth in charity day and night, secretly and openly" receive their reward from Allah. The description joins every time and both forms of giving under one purpose. What matters is sincere obedience, not the search for fame.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who spend their wealth in charity day and night,"** They continue to give as circumstances require. There is no restriction of ordinary charity to daylight or night-time. Opportunities to help should not be lost because they fall outside a preferred schedule.

**"secretly and openly"** Both can be rewarded. Secret giving is generally preferable where publicity serves no pressing good, while open giving can be better when a sound need calls for it. Neither form excuses self-promotion.

**"their reward is with their Lord,"** Its safekeeping and return are with Allah at resurrection. The charitable expenditure includes proper spending on one's family, not only gifts outside the home.

**"and there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve"** Allah's favour frees them from fear about the coming future and grief over what they left in the world. The explanation connects this promise with the earlier assurance in (2:38).

### What revelation reports are given?

One account connects the verse with 'Ali Ibn Abi Talib. Another report, transmitted with reference to Ibn 'Asakir, says Abu Bakr gave forty thousand dinars, divided into ten thousand by day, ten thousand by night, ten thousand publicly and ten thousand secretly. Some identify that expenditure as the occasion, while recognising other views.

Another account gives owners of horses maintained for Allah's cause. Abu Umamah, Abu'l-Darda', Makhul, al-Awza'i and Rabah ibn Zayd share that explanation. Their expenditure continues by night and day, publicly and privately, as the horses need care.

The account excludes keeping them for pride or as security for debts considered impossible to repay. The aim must be Allah's cause rather than display or that misuse. Ibn 'Abbas likewise explains the expenditure through fodder supplied to the horses.

A further prophetic saying in that report states that devils do not bewitch someone with a noble horse in the house. It is included as reported material, without adding a grading absent from the account.

### What reward is described for maintaining a horse?

Asma' bint Yazid reports a saying of the Prophet ﷺ that someone maintaining a horse solely for Allah's pleasure finds its conditions and care recorded among good deeds. The description includes being fed or hungry, watered or thirsty, and even its urine and dung.

The transmission includes 'Abd al-Hamid ibn Bahram. Another report from Jabir, transmitted through Makhul and 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Yazid, compares expenditure on such a horse with extending the hand in charity. These names identify the reported accounts without reproducing the entire chains.

The example shows how a continuing practical responsibility can become sustained giving. Ordinary care is not spiritually empty when its purpose is righteous.

### How is family expenditure charity?

The Two Sahihs record the Prophet ﷺ telling Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas that spending while seeking Allah's pleasure raises the person's degree, including food placed in the wife's mouth. The intimate household example prevents generosity from being restricted to public donations.

Ahmad records from Abu Mas'ud that a Muslim's expenditure on family, undertaken in expectation of Allah's reward, is written as charity. Al-Bukhari and Muslim also record the hadith.

The family is therefore not a rival to devotion. Proper provision with sincere intention is itself an avenue of devotion. Attention to dependants belongs alongside help for others.

### Why support those working for religion?

The financial reflection particularly commends supporting people whose full-time religious service leaves no time to earn. Different occupations shape different skills and attention. A successful businessperson understands commercial complexities, while a person devoted to religious work develops a different concentration.

Society needs both kinds of activity. A division of labour allows people with resources to reserve a share for those unable to provide for themselves because of that service. The religious worker need not demand or expect payment, while the person with money recognises a responsibility to support the work they have not undertaken personally.

The proposed exchange seeks Allah's pleasure, not fame for either group. Helping a peaceful religious struggle that brings no public laurels is particularly free of worldly attractions. Quiet support can therefore express the verse's devotion as fully as a celebrated project.

### What continues when possessions are exhausted?

The inward reading pictures a servant giving without slackening while wealth remains. When it is gone, remembrance and witnessing do not stop. Ruh, spirit, becomes the next image of surrender, not an instruction to injure the body.

Spending property in religion according to the shariah is described as the faithful person's work. Surrendering the spirit's self-centred claims in contemplation of the Patron's majesty and beauty is described through haqiqah, inward reality, as the work of spiritual chivalry.

Poetic language of wagering away possessions and then spirit conveys that total dedication. The reflection ends by contrasting the servants' effort with the far greater generosity of Allah towards them. Giving therefore returns to gratitude rather than a claim that the servant has done enough to place Him in debt.

### In short

- Charity is welcome by day or night, secretly or openly, when sincerity governs it.
- Different reports illustrate the verse through individual gifts and maintaining horses for Allah's cause.
- Proper family expenditure with expectation of reward is also charity.
- Supporting dedicated religious service allows a sincere division of responsibilities.
- Inward devotion continues through remembrance even when material resources end.
