<<<VERSE 7:90>>>
Allah shows how deep the disbelief, rebellion and defiance of Shuʿayb's people ran: "The disbelieving chiefs of his people threatened, “If you follow Shu’aib, you will surely be losers!”" It was a misleading statement by the arrogant chiefs, said among themselves or to their followers, as Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ reports from ʿAṭāʾ.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The disbelieving chiefs of his people threatened"**: the leaders and important people who refused the call to believe in Allah's oneness. Some of them said it to others, and they said it to the lowly among the people, warning them against Shuʿayb and his faith. They swore it, for in the Arabic la-in, "if", carries the lām of an oath.

**"“If you follow Shu’aib"**: if you follow his religion and his way, and leave the way of life of our ancestors.

**"you will surely be losers!”"** You will be ignorant dupes and will certainly come to ruin.

### What was their real crime?

Shuʿayb's people were not guilty of denying Allah. Their guilt was misrepresenting Him. They credited to Allah a religion He had never sent. This has been the position of the peoples of all the prophets. They were no different from those to whom Allah had earlier revealed His religion. But by their own changes and additions they turned it into something else. They made Allah's religion into the religion of their desires and went on calling it Allah's religion.

The religious leaders of such a time, backed by the people and holding power, know they cannot win an argument with a prophet. Yet they do not want anyone to rob them of their importance, so they look for a way to silence him. The chiefs' threat to their own followers was exactly such a way.

### In short

- The disbelieving chiefs swore to their people that following Shuʿayb would bring them ruin.
- Their real guilt was claiming that a religion of their own making came from Allah.
- Unable to answer a prophet, such leaders try to silence him and frighten others away.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:91>>>
The punishment came for their threat to drive out Shuʿayb and his followers: "Then an ˹overwhelming˺ earthquake struck them and they fell lifeless in their homes". Here the punishment is called an earthquake (rajfah, zalzalah). Other verses describe it in other words.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then an ˹overwhelming˺ earthquake struck them"**: the violent quake seized them, together with the loud cry announcing their punishment.

**"and they fell lifeless in their homes"**: in the morning they lay dead in their dwellings, their town and their encampments, fallen on their knees and faces.

### How do the different descriptions fit together?

Allah mentions their end again in Sūrah Hūd:

> "When Our command came, We saved Shu’aib and those who believed with him by a mercy from Us. And the ˹mighty˺ blast overtook the wrongdoers, so they fell lifeless in their homes" (11:94).

That verse mentions the blast because they had mocked Shuʿayb, saying, "Does your prayer command you that we should abandon what our forefathers worshipped" (11:87). So it was fitting to mention the cry that silenced them, after they had mocked his ṣalāh. In Sūrat ash-Shuʿarāʾ, Allah says, "So they rejected him, and ˹so˺ were overtaken by the torment of the day of the ˹deadly˺ cloud" (26:189). That was because they had challenged Shuʿayb: "So cause ˹deadly˺ pieces of the sky to fall upon us, if what you say is true" (26:187).

So all these forms of punishment struck them on the Day of the Cloud, Yawm aẓ-Ẓullah. First came a dark cloud over them, holding fire, flames and a terrible light. Then a cry came down from the sky, and a tremor shook them from beneath. Their souls were taken, their lives ended and their bodies lay still.

### What did Ibn ʿAbbās say about the day of the cloud?

ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās explained how the earthquake and the cloud came together, as Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ records. First a heat fell on them so fierce that it was as though a door of Hell had opened toward them. They could not breathe, and neither shade nor water gave relief. They went down into cellars, but it was hotter there. With nowhere left to turn, they fled the city for the nearby forest. There Allah sent a thick cloud, with a cool breeze beneath it. Half-crazed by the heat, they all rushed into its shade. Then the whole cloud turned to fire and rained down on them, and the earthquake came too and reduced them to ashes. So the punishment of the earthquake and the punishment of the cloud fell on them at the same time.

Some commentators suggest another possibility: that Shuʿayb's people were in separate groups, some struck by the earthquake and others destroyed by the cloud. The question of whether Madyan and al-Aykah were one people or two was discussed under 7:85.

### In short

- The earthquake struck Shuʿayb's people for threatening to expel him and the believers, and they lay dead in their homes.
- Other verses speak of a blast and of the day of the cloud. These match their mockery of his prayer and their dare to bring down the sky.
- Ibn ʿAbbās described unbearable heat, a flight to a shaded forest, and a cloud that rained fire as the earth shook.

<<<VERSE 7:92>>>
This verse presents the fate of Shuʿayb's people as a lesson, which is the real purpose of telling the story: "Those who rejected Shu’aib were ˹wiped out˺ as if they had never lived there. Those who rejected Shu’aib were the true losers". The very people who threatened to drive the believers out were the ones who lost everything.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who rejected Shu’aib were ˹wiped out˺ as if they had never lived there."** One meaning of the Arabic word ghinā is to live somewhere in peace and comfort, and that is the meaning here. The homes where they had lived in ease became so desolate after the punishment that it was as if comfort had never been there. They were all destroyed. It looked as though they had never lived in the land from which they had wanted to expel their messenger Shuʿayb and his followers.

Grammatically, "those who rejected Shuʿayb" (alladhīna kadhdhabū Shuʿayban) is the subject. Its predicate is introduced by "as if" (ka-an), a softened form of ka-annahum, "as if they", with its subject left unstated.

**"Those who rejected Shu’aib were the true losers"**: they were the losers, punished, losing themselves and their possessions. The believers lost nothing at all, contrary to what the disbelievers had claimed. Repeating "those who rejected Shuʿayb" serves to refute what they had said before: "If you follow Shu’aib, you will surely be losers!" (7:90). They threatened to throw Shuʿayb and his believing companions out of their city, but in the end the loss fell on no one but themselves.

### When does a people lose all claim to mercy?

Allowances can be made for a person as long as the truth is not clear to him. But once the truth has been made clear and he still chooses arrogance, he loses any claim to sympathy. Punishment in this world is given on this basis. On the same basis, people's punishment in the Hereafter will be decided according to their guilt.

### In short

- Shuʿayb's people were wiped out so completely that it was as if they had never lived in comfort in their homes.
- Those who threatened to expel the believers, warning that following Shuʿayb meant loss, turned out to be the real losers.
- Once the truth is clear, persisting in arrogance forfeits all sympathy, in this world and the next.
