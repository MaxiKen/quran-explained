<<<VERSE 7:90>>>
The chiefs make one last attempt to hold their people. "The disbelieving chiefs of his people threatened, “If you follow Shu’aib, you will surely be losers!”" The word is a warning put out among themselves, and it is aimed at the people who might still be listening.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The disbelieving chiefs of his people"** names those who spoke. They are the leading men of the community who had refused the call to believe in the oneness of Allah, and the word for chiefs is the word for the leading men and the notables.

**"If you follow Shu’aib, you will surely be losers"** is what they said among themselves, and it is also taken as what they said to their followers. In one report the words were spoken by the chiefs privately to one another, and the account of it stands in Al-Bahr Al-Muhit on the authority of Ata. What they meant by being losers was that the person who followed him would be ignorant and duped.

Another reading of their words is that they were warning the people against him openly, telling them that if they took the path of Shu'ayb and left the way of life their ancestors had followed, ruin would certainly come to them through it.

Their words are a misleading statement, and the thing they were attacking was the faith they had been given every chance to see. The mockery they had made of the prayer of their prophet is recorded elsewhere, when they asked whether his salah commanded him to do the things he called them to (11:87). The verse brings their warning forward and leaves the answer to the verse that follows it, which is verse 91 of this surah, where the punishment itself is named.

### What the case of this community shows

The community of Shu'ayb was not guilty of denying God outright. Its guilt was that it represented Him falsely, since it had attributed to God a religion that God had never sent. That has been the position of the communities of the prophets generally. Such communities were not unlike those to whom God had revealed His religion earlier, but by means of changes and additions of their own making they turned it into something else. In that way they changed the religion of God into the religion of their own desires, and then went on calling it the religion of God.

The religious leaders of later times, who have the support of the people and hold power, are in the same position. They know very well that they cannot argue with the prophets and win, but they do not want anyone to take their importance from them, so they look for a way to silence those who speak. Allowance can be made for a person so long as the truth has not become clear to him. Once it has been made clear and a man still chooses to be arrogant, he has lost any claim to sympathy. The punishment in this world is given on that basis, and the punishment in the Hereafter will be decided on the same basis, according to the guilt of the people.

### In short

- The leading men of the community, who had refused the call, warned the people that following Shu'aib would end in ruin.
- One report has them saying it privately among themselves, and another reading has them warning the people openly against him.
- What they meant was that a follower of Shu'ayb would be ignorant and taken in.
- Their community's guilt was not a plain denial of God but a false representation of Him, since they had made a religion of their own desires and called it His.
- Men in power who cannot answer the prophets try instead to silence them, and the punishment of this world and the next is measured by what a person does once the truth has been made clear to him.

<<<VERSE 7:91>>>
The answer to their threat comes at once. "Then an ˹overwhelming˺ earthquake struck them and they fell lifeless in their homes".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then an ˹overwhelming˺ earthquake struck them"** names the punishment in the opening of the verse. The ground shook under them, and in one reading the loud noise that announced their torment is joined to it.

**"and they fell lifeless in their homes"** describes the state they were left in. The word means that they were brought to a standstill where they were, prostrate and motionless, keeled over on their knees. The morning found them dead in their dwellings, in the town and in the encampments around it.

### Why the earthquake came, and how it fits with the other accounts

The earth shook them as the punishment for the threat they had made to drive out Shu'ayb and those who believed with him. What they had said of the believers was the opposite of the truth, and what came to pass was that the loss fell on nobody but them.

The end of this people is told again in Surah Hud. It says there that when the command of Allah came, Shu'ayb and those who had believed with him were delivered by a mercy from their Lord. The wrongdoers were seized by the cry, and they were left lying in their own houses, dead (11:94). The cry is named there in that context because the people had mocked Shu'ayb over his prayer. It was fitting that the cry should be named as the thing that silenced them. Their challenge is recorded by the same account, since they had asked him to make a piece of the sky fall on them if he was one of the truthful (26:187). In Surah Ash-Shu'ara the answer is given with the punishment of the day of the shadow, Yawm az-Zullah. A gloomy cloud came over them and seized them, and the verse says that it was the punishment of a great day (26:189).

The reconciliation of the two accounts is given in a report from Ibn Abbas. The people of Shu'ayb were first subjected to such intense heat that it was as though the door of the fire had been thrown open at them. It choked them. Shade and water gave them no relief. Driven by the heat they went down into their cellars, and it was hotter there, and with nothing left to try they ran out of the town into the forest nearby. There Allah sent a thick cloud with a cool breeze passing underneath it, and, their senses gone under the heat, they all rushed for shelter under its shade. At that point the whole cloud turned into a cloud of fire raining down on them, and the earthquake came as well and turned them to ashes. So the punishments of the earthquake and of the shade converged on those people at the same time, and that account is recorded in Al-Bahr Al-Muhit.

Others said that it is possible the people were split into different groups. On that view one part of them met the shaking of the ground, while the other part met the fire that rained out of the cloud. Either way, the shade they had run to for relief was the thing that came down on them.

### In short

- The earthquake came on them as the answer to their threat to expel Shu'ayb and his followers.
- They were left prostrate where they stood, dead in their dwellings, and the morning found them so.
- The same end is given in Surah Hud, where the cry seizes the wrongdoers and the believers are saved by a mercy from Allah.
- The challenge they had put to Shu'ayb, that he should make a piece of the sky fall on them, is answered by the punishment of the day of the shadow.
- On the reconciliation given in the reports, intense heat drove them out of the town to the shade of a cloud, and the cloud turned to fire on them while the ground shook.
- The shade they ran to for relief was what destroyed them, and some hold that the people were split so that the earthquake took one part and the shade the other.

<<<VERSE 7:92>>>
The verse states what became of them, and then states the verdict twice. "Those who rejected Shu’aib were ˹wiped out˺ as if they had never lived there. Those who rejected Shu’aib were the true losers".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who rejected Shu’aib were wiped out as if they had never lived there"** describes the emptiness left behind. One sense of the word used here for dwelling is to live in a place in peace and comfort, and that is the sense intended. The houses where they had lived at ease were left so desolate after the punishment that it was as if that peace and comfort had never been there at all.

**"Those who rejected Shu’aib were the true losers"** gives the verdict. The words are repeated, and the repetition has a purpose of its own, since it is meant as a refutation of what the chiefs had said at the beginning. They had warned the people that anyone who followed Shu'ayb would be a loser. The answer comes back in the same words turned round, that the loss fell on the people who called him a liar. They lost themselves and what they had, while those who had faith did not fail in the least, which is the opposite of what the disbelievers had claimed.

The naming of the people as those who rejected him is also deliberate, since it is the rejection that explains the punishment and makes the sentence fit. In the Arabic of the verse the words alladhīna kadhdhabū Shu'ayban, those who denied Shu'ayb, are repeated as the subject of both sentences, and the repetition is aimed at the warning the chiefs had put out.

### What the verse is for

The fate of the people of Shu'ayb is set out here as a lesson, and the lesson is the real purpose behind the whole account. It was the people who had threatened to throw Shu'ayb and his believing companions out of their city who were themselves removed from the land, and the very place they had wanted to keep for themselves was left as though no one had ever lived in it. So the verses close the story of the community with a picture of what the threat came to, and of the end of those who made it.

### In short

- The places where they had lived at ease were left so empty that it was as if they had never been there.
- The verdict is repeated to overturn the threat the chiefs had made, since the losers were the ones who denied the prophet.
- They lost themselves and their possessions, while the believers lost nothing.
- Their rejection of Shu'ayb is named as the reason for what came on them.
- The whole account is given as a lesson, and the people who tried to drive the messenger out were the ones removed from the land.
