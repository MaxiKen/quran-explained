<<<VERSE 7:163>>>
The verse turns to a story the first hearers knew from their own history. "Ask them ˹O Prophet˺ about ˹the people of˺ the town which was by the sea, who broke the Sabbath." The next words describe the test they were put under. "During the Sabbath, ˹abundant˺ fish would come to them clearly visible, but on other days the fish were never seen." The verse closes with the reason for the test. "In this way We tested them for their rebelliousness"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ask them ˹O Prophet˺ about ˹the people of˺ the town which was by the sea, who broke the Sabbath."** addresses the Prophet and, through him, the Jews who were with him. They were to be asked about the story of their own people, the Israelites who had defied the command of Allah. The punishment that overtook their forefathers all of a sudden was to stand as a warning to them. The warning covered another matter as well. They were not to go on hiding the description of the Prophet that they found in their books, since a people who did that would meet what their fathers met.

The town is named in the reports. Ibn 'Abbas, whose words came through Muhammad ibn Ishaq, said that it was a village called Aylah, standing between Midian and At-Tur, which is in Sinai. The same identification of the place is given by Ikrimah and by Mujahid, and also by Qatadah and by As-Suddi. The village stood on the shore of the sea, and that sea is the one also called the Red Sea. Ibn Jarir reported these sayings. The Book itself refers to the same event in Al-Baqarah, where the words come that the people who broke the Sabbath are already known (2:65).

**"During the Sabbath, ˹abundant˺ fish would come to them clearly visible, but on other days the fish were never seen."** tells how the test worked. On the day when fishing was forbidden the fish came to the surface of the water in great numbers, close to the shore and plainly seen. On the days when fishing was allowed they stayed away. The trial was made by bringing the thing they wanted within reach on the one day when it was out of bounds.

**"In this way We tested them for their rebelliousness."** gives the reason. The test was a trial of a people who had already turned away from the command of their Lord. Ibn Jarir explained the words by saying that the fish were brought near the surface on the day of the prohibition and hidden on the day when taking them was lawful. The trial was meant to bring out what was in the people.

### The trick they used

The people did not simply break the rule out in the open. They turned to a trick. They set up their nets so that the fish would fall into them on the Sabbath and could then be collected and eaten on the following day. In that way they took the catch of the forbidden day while keeping the appearance of the law.

The offence of such a people is greater than a plain disobedience. The one who does a forbidden thing because his desire overcomes him knows what he is doing. The one who does the same thing while arranging a legal form to cover it adds the insult of making the prohibition look as though it had been kept. The people of the town, by the account, joined both wrongs together.

A report on the subject was carried by Ibn Battah from Abu Hurayrah. He said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ commanded the believers not to do what the Jews did, and not to make the prohibitions of Allah lawful by the smallest of tricks. The chain of that hadith is a reasonable one, and the warning in it is aimed at the method rather than at one single act.

### What the test brought out

The trial made the inner state of the people visible. They had been given a day set apart and a rule to keep it, and the rule came with a provision that made obedience easy on the other six days. They chose instead to hunt for the catch of the seventh day by a device. In doing so they showed that the command of Allah weighed less with them than the fish.

The lesson the passage draws is the one the verse before it had already given. A people who treat the prohibitions of Allah as a fence to be got around lose more than they gain. The account adds that such a fall takes a person away from the position given to him and puts him down on the level of the animals. The one who lives only by his appetite has given up the thing that made him a man.

### In short

- The Prophet was told to ask the Jews of his time about the town by the sea. The fate of their forefathers was to serve as a warning, and they were not to repeat the fault of hiding the description of him that was in their books.
- The town is named as Aylah, standing between Midian and At-Tur in Sinai. The event is the same one referred to in Al-Baqarah, where the words come that the people who broke the Sabbath are already known (2:65).
- The fish came in numbers to the surface on the Sabbath and stayed away on other days. The trial was meant to bring out a people who had already turned away from the command of their Lord.
- The people took the catch of the forbidden day by setting their nets so that the fish would fall into them on the Sabbath and be gathered on the next day. That is the trick of a people who wanted the fruit of disobedience while keeping the look of obedience.
- A report carried by Ibn Battah from Abu Hurayrah commands the believers not to do what the Jews did. It forbids them to make the prohibitions of Allah lawful by the smallest of tricks.

<<<VERSE 7:164>>>
The next verse shows a group inside the town that spoke up while the rest went on. "When some of ˹the righteous among˺ them questioned ˹their fellow Sabbath-keepers˺, “Why do you ˹bother to˺ warn those ˹Sabbath-breakers˺ who will either be destroyed or severely punished by Allah?”" The answer of the preachers comes next. "They replied, “Just to be free from your Lord’s blame, and so perhaps they may abstain.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"When some of ˹the righteous among˺ them questioned ˹their fellow Sabbath-keepers˺"** introduces the exchange. The people of the town, one of the Israelite settlements of that region, had separated into three groups. The same story is told in Al-Baqarah, and the account of it here says plainly what the earlier passage had left to be worked out. One group took the fish on the Sabbath and urged others to do the same. A second group neither took the fish nor warned anyone against it. A third group kept away from the fish and also warned the others, and it is this last group that the verse shows answering a question.

**"Why do you ˹bother to˺ warn those ˹Sabbath-breakers˺ who will either be destroyed or severely punished by Allah?"** is the question put by the group that did neither. Their argument was that a people headed for destruction or for a severe chastisement had nothing to gain from advice, and that the advice wasted the effort of those who gave it. The same question is reported in the words that Allah is about to destroy them, or to punish them with a torment that is severe.

**"They replied, “Just to be free from your Lord's blame, and so perhaps they may abstain.”"** gives the answer of the preachers. They said that the command to enjoin what is right and to forbid what is wrong had been laid on them. They discharged it so that they would have a defence before their Lord, and so that they would not be taken to account for leaving it undone. The second part of their answer was hope. If the people listened, they would stop taking the fish, and the door of repentance was still open. A person who turns back to Allah is met with acceptance and with mercy.

### The three groups and their ends

The division of the town into three is reported in more than one account. One group committed the prohibition, another forbade it and kept away from it, and a third neither committed it nor spoke against it.

The end of the first two groups is stated in the verses that follow. Those who warned were saved, and those who wronged were seized. The third group is passed over in silence. The reason given for the silence is that the recompense is measured against the deed, and that group had done nothing to earn praise and nothing to bring down blame.

The silence became a question among the early teachers. Ikrimah said that Ibn 'Abbas told him that he did not know whether the people who asked the question were saved. Ikrimah went on discussing the matter with him until he convinced him that they were, and when he had done so Ibn 'Abbas gave him a garment as a gift. The point that settled the question was that those who were seized were named as the ones who did wrong, which leaves the others out of it. The group that neither fished nor spoke had not done wrong in the matter of the fish.

### In short

- The people of the town were divided into three groups, one that took the fish on the Sabbath and urged others to it, one that kept away and warned the others, and one that did neither.
- The group that did neither asked the preachers why they wasted their effort on a people headed for destruction or for a severe punishment from Allah.
- The preachers answered that the command to forbid evil had been laid on them, and that they had discharged it so as to be free of blame before their Lord. They added that the people might yet turn back and stop.
- The verses that follow name the rescue of those who warned and the seizure of those who wronged, and they say nothing of the group that stood aside, since the recompense matches the deed.
- Ikrimah reported that Ibn 'Abbas was at first unsure about the fate of that group, and that once he was convinced they were saved he gave Ikrimah a garment in return for the discussion.

<<<VERSE 7:165>>>
The third verse tells what came after the warning had been given. "When they ignored the warning they were given, We rescued those who used to warn against evil and overtook the wrongdoers with a dreadful punishment for their rebelliousness"

### Phrase by phrase

**"When they ignored the warning they were given"** states the point at which the matter was settled. The words are read as meaning that the wrongdoers left what they had been commanded to do, and that the reminder made no difference to them. The warning had been given, and the warning had been refused.

**"We rescued those who used to warn against evil"** names the first result. The group that kept away from the fish and spoke against the practice was saved from the punishment. One account of the passage says plainly that Allah saved the group which had forbidden the disobedience.

**"and overtook the wrongdoers with a dreadful punishment for their rebelliousness"** names the second result. The people who took the fish and persisted in it were seized by a punishment that is called dreadful. The word used for the punishment carries the sense of a torment that is severe and painful, and the two meanings are given in the reports as near to each other. One account of the passage states that the punishment concerned was firm and that it came on those who had gone past the limits set for them.

### What the two ends show

The passage belongs with the telling of the same event in Al-Baqarah, and it sets the two groups side by side. On one side are the Israelites who had come to hate the evil so much that they spoke against it, and they were taken out of the punishment. On the other side are the people who went on in their rebellion, and they were taken by it. 

The public forbidding of evil is a declaration that the one who does it has carried out the duty laid on him. The passage shows that the duty does not depend on the answer it receives. The preachers were told that the people might yet abstain. When that hope failed, the duty they had performed still stood to their credit, and it stood between them and the punishment.

The word of the account on the point is worth putting plainly. A person is a creature into whom the Creator has put understanding and conscience. When a desire rises in such a person, the understanding weighs it and asks whether the wish should be carried out. The one who puts the bridle of God on himself is the man. The one who does whatever his lower self demands, without stopping to think and without shame afterwards, has left the rank of manhood. That is the sense in which the passage and the verse that follows it speak of the people becoming apes.

### In short

- The verse fixes the moment when the matter was settled, which is the moment the wrongdoers left the command they had been given and refused the reminder.
- The group that kept away from the fish and spoke against the practice was rescued, and one account says plainly that Allah saved the group which had forbidden the disobedience.
- The wrongdoers who took the fish and persisted in it were seized by a dreadful punishment. The word used for it carries the sense of a torment that is severe and painful.
- The forbidding of evil does not depend on the answer it receives, since the duty had been carried out whether or not the people turned back.
- The account draws the line between the man who puts the bridle of God on himself and the one who follows whatever his lower self asks of him. That line is what the next verse speaks of when it names the punishment of the town.

<<<VERSE 7:166>>>
The last verse of the passage gives the sentence. "But when they stubbornly persisted in violation, We said to them, “Be disgraced apes!”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"But when they stubbornly persisted in violation"** states what brought the sentence down. The words are read as meaning that the people refused to leave what had been forbidden to them, and that they held to their way out of pride. It was not the weakness of a single moment but a settled refusal to turn. It came after the warning had been given and the example of the preachers had been set before them.

**"We said to them, “Be disgraced apes!”"** is the sentence itself. The word attached to the ape in the verse carries the sense of being humiliated, disgraced and driven away. One account of the passage gives the command in the words that they were to be monkeys, brought low. It adds that what Allah willed for them came to pass as He willed it.

The reports explain the sentence in two ways. One line takes it as a change in the outward form of the people, so that they were made into animals of that kind. The other line takes it as a change in their character rather than in their shape. It holds that their hearts and their thinking became like the hearts and thinking of apes instead of those of human beings. The reference to the second reading is given to Al-Qurtubi. It has the support of the words of the verse that describe the people as disgraced, since the disgrace of a people lies in the loss of what made them a people.

### What becoming an ape means

The verse describes a state that a man can reach in this life, and it closes the account of the Israelites who took the fish of the Sabbath. The same passage is told in Al-Baqarah, and this verse adds the sentence that the earlier telling had pointed to. A man who acts again and again against what his understanding and his conscience tell him becomes insensitive to both of them. When that has happened, nothing is left in him of the finer feelings. Whatever desire takes shape in his heart goes straight into action.

The picture given of such a person is drawn in plain terms. If someone comes within his reach, he attacks the honour and the property of that person. If he has a complaint against somebody, he sets about bringing that person down. If he has a difference with somebody, he begins to speak ill of him. If he finds a man standing in his way, he fights him at once. There is no interval in him between the wish and the doing of it, and no shame after it.

The man who keeps the command of Allah over himself is the one who keeps his rank. The one who does whatever his lower self demands has fallen from it, and the verse gives that fall its own name. The sentence came on the town after the warning, after the preachers had done their work, and after the people had refused to turn.

One report belongs to the question that the earlier verse left open. Al-Hakim carried in a hadith that Ikrima referred his opinion back to Ibn 'Abbas, and that Ibn 'Abbas was delighted with it. The opinion was that the group which stood aside was not destroyed. The passage closes with that question settled among the early teachers, and with the punishment resting on the people who took the fish.

### In short

- The sentence came when the wrongdoers refused to leave what had been forbidden to them, holding to their way out of pride after the warning had been given.
- They were told to be apes that are disgraced, and the word carries the sense of humiliation, disgrace and rejection.
- One reading of the sentence takes it as a change in the outward form of the people. Another takes it as a change in their character, so that their hearts and thinking became like those of apes rather than those of human beings, and that reading is given to Al-Qurtubi.
- A man reaches that state in this life by acting against his understanding and his conscience until he becomes insensitive to both, so that nothing stands between a wish in his heart and its doing.
- Al-Hakim carried a hadith in which Ikrima referred to Ibn 'Abbas the opinion that the group which stood aside was not destroyed, and Ibn 'Abbas was delighted with it.
