<<<VERSE 7:163>>>
Allah tells His Prophet ﷺ to remind the Jews of his time of a story from their own past: "Ask them ˹O Prophet˺ about ˹the people of˺ the town which was by the sea, who broke the Sabbath." The fish behaved in a way that tested them: "During the Sabbath, ˹abundant˺ fish would come to them clearly visible, but on other days the fish were never seen. In this way We tested them for their rebelliousness". This verse explains another passage: "You are already aware of those of you who broke the Sabbath." (2:65). The events are discussed in detail in Sūrat al-Baqarah, and they are clear enough to need little explanation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ask them ˹O Prophet˺"**: ask the Jews who are with you, O Muhammad, by way of rebuke. Ask them about their fellow Jews who defied Allah's command, so that His punishment struck them suddenly for their evil deeds, their transgression and their defiance through deceit. Remind them how Allah punished some of the Israelites. Warn them, too, not to conceal how their books describe you, or they may meet the fate of their forefathers.

**"about ˹the people of˺ the town which was by the sea,"** The town was Aylah, or Eilat, on the shore of the Qulzum, the Red Sea. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq recorded, through Dāwūd ibn al-Ḥuṣayn and ʿIkrimah, that Ibn ʿAbbās placed this village, Aylah, between aṭ-Ṭūr in Sinai and Madyan. ʿIkrimah, Mujāhid, Qatādah and as-Suddī said the same.

**"who broke the Sabbath."** They overstepped Allah's limits on the Sabbath by fishing, which they had been forbidden to do that day, defying His command to keep the day sacred.

**"During the Sabbath, ˹abundant˺ fish would come to them clearly visible,"** On their Sabbath day great fish came in scores, rising from the depths up to the shore and floating openly on top of the water. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported this meaning from Ibn ʿAbbās.

**"but on other days the fish were never seen."** On the days when they did not keep the Sabbath, the fish did not come to them.

**"In this way We tested them for their rebelliousness"**: Ibn Jarīr explained that Allah tested them by making the fish swim near the surface on the day they were forbidden to fish, and hiding them on the days fishing was allowed. This trial came because of their disobedience and their rebellion against His command.

### How did they break the Sabbath?

Eventually they decided to fish by a trick. They set up their nets so that the fish would fall into them on Saturday, and then took them out and ate them on Sunday. So these were people who used a ruse to break Allah's prohibitions. On the surface their action looked lawful, but in reality it was meant to violate the prohibition.

The imam and scholar Abū ʿAbdullāh Ibn Baṭṭah reported from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: do not commit what the Jews committed, making lawful what Allah has forbidden by cunning tricks. This hadith has a reasonable chain.

### Why is a trick worse than a plain sin?

It is a sin to do anything Allah has forbidden. But to do a forbidden act while trying to make it lawful through some trick adds insult to injury. By breaking the rule of the Sabbath in this way, the Jews committed a double sin. Such people must suffer divine retribution. They lose the blessings Allah has reserved for human beings alone, and in this world they fall from the level of humanity to the level of animals.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ was told to remind the Jews of the Sabbath-breakers of Aylah, and to warn them against hiding his description.
- Allah tested that town by sending fish openly on the Sabbath and withholding them on other days.
- They used nets as a trick to catch the fish on the Sabbath, a sin made worse by pretending it was lawful.

<<<VERSE 7:164>>>
When some of the townspeople broke the Sabbath, the rest divided over how to respond: "When some of ˹the righteous among˺ them questioned ˹their fellow Sabbath-keepers˺, “Why do you ˹bother to˺ warn those ˹Sabbath-breakers˺ who will either be destroyed or severely punished by Allah?”" The warners had their answer: "They replied, “Just to be free from your Lord’s blame, and so perhaps they may abstain.”" When the fishing began, the town split into three groups.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When some of ˹the righteous among˺ them questioned ˹their fellow Sabbath-keepers˺,"** A group among them who neither fished nor forbade it spoke to those who were forbidding it.

**"“Why do you ˹bother to˺ warn those ˹Sabbath-breakers˺"**: why do you keep admonishing and cautioning these people?

**"who will either be destroyed or severely punished by Allah?”"** Allah is going to destroy them in this world for their disobedience, by turning them into animals, or punish them severely on the Day of Judgement with the Fire. Since they had already earned Allah's punishment, these people said, there was no point in forbidding them.

**"They replied, “Just to be free from your Lord’s blame,"** We admonish them to clear ourselves before your Lord and have a plea before Him. We were commanded to enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong. By doing so we keep Him from holding us to account, and from blaming us for failing to forbid them in any way.

**"and so perhaps they may abstain.”"** Perhaps they will take our advice, fear Allah, stop fishing on the Sabbath and give up their disobedience. If they repent to Allah, He will accept their repentance and grant them His mercy.

### Who were the three groups?

The people of the town were divided into three groups:

1. Those who broke the prohibition and caught fish on the Sabbath, and encouraged others to do the same.
2. Those who did not fish, but forbade the others and kept away from them.
3. Those who did not fish but did not warn others against it either.

The first group's fishing is described in Sūrat al-Baqarah. It was the third, silent group who asked the warners why they bothered.

### In short

- When the Sabbath-breaking began, the townspeople split into those who fished, those who warned against it, and those who stayed silent.
- The silent group asked why anyone should warn people already doomed to Allah's punishment.
- The warners spoke out to clear themselves before Allah, as He had commanded, and in the hope that the sinners might yet stop.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:165>>>
The warning went unheeded, and the outcome separated the groups: "When they ignored the warning they were given, We rescued those who used to warn against evil and overtook the wrongdoers with a dreadful punishment for their rebelliousness". Allah saved those who had forbidden the sin and seized those who had committed it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When they ignored the warning they were given,"** Literally, "when they forgot what they had been reminded of". The wrongdoers overlooked the admonition, turned away from it and refused the advice. They abandoned what they had been commanded and did not desist.

**"We rescued those who used to warn against evil"**: Allah saved from the punishment the group that had forbidden the sin, catching fish on the Sabbath.

**"and overtook the wrongdoers with a dreadful punishment"**: Allah struck those who transgressed by fishing on the Sabbath with a grievous punishment. The word used is baʾīs. Mujāhid said it means "severe", and Qatādah said "painful". The two meanings come to the same thing, and Allah knows best. Naming only the wrongdoers as seized suggests that the others were spared.

**"for their rebelliousness"**: because they disobeyed Allah and persisted in rebelling against His command.

### What became of the silent group?

Allah stated that those who forbade evil were saved and those who committed the transgression were destroyed. He did not mention the end of the third group, who stood by. The reward matches the deed. They did nothing that deserved praise, nor any wrong that called for blame.

Forbidding evil is, in effect, a declaration that one has done one's duty. So when Allah's scourge falls on a people, those so disgusted by evil that they actively try to stop it are spared the punishment.

### In short

- When the Sabbath-breakers ignored the warning, Allah saved those who had forbidden the sin.
- He seized the wrongdoers with a severe, painful punishment for their persistent rebellion.
- Those who actively forbid evil discharge their duty, and so are spared when punishment falls.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:166>>>
The final stage of their punishment came when they kept on sinning: "But when they stubbornly persisted in violation, We said to them, “Be disgraced apes!”" This is the detail of the "dreadful punishment" mentioned in the previous verse, and the same words are recorded of them in Sūrat al-Baqarah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But when they stubbornly persisted in violation,"** When they overstepped the limits and arrogantly refused to give up what had been forbidden to them, rebelling against Allah out of pride and stubbornness, and would not accept advice.

**"We said to them, “Be disgraced apes!”"** Allah said to them, "O you disobedient ones! Be apes, despised!", humiliated, disgraced, rejected and loathed. And so they became, for when Allah wills something, He only says to it "Be!" and it is.

### Were the silent ones saved?

Ibn ʿAbbās said he did not know what happened to the group that stood aside, those who had asked, "Why do you warn them?" ʿIkrimah said that group was not destroyed, because they had disliked what the others were doing, and had asked that question. ʿIkrimah recalled that he kept discussing it with Ibn ʿAbbās until he convinced him that they had been saved, and Ibn ʿAbbās then gave him a garment as a gift. Al-Ḥākim reported in a hadith that ʿIkrimah put this opinion to Ibn ʿAbbās, who was delighted with it.

### What does it mean to become an ape?

One view, recorded by al-Qurṭubī, holds that "Be apes" does not mean their faces became the faces of apes. It means their character became ape-like, so that their hearts and minds were those of apes instead of human beings.

A person becomes an ape when, after acting again and again against the dictates of his wisdom and conscience, he grows so callous that every fine feeling is driven out of him. Whatever desire forms in his heart, he acts on it. Whoever comes within his reach, he attacks that person's honour and property. If he has a grievance against someone, he sets about degrading him at once. If he disagrees with someone, he starts to belittle him. If he finds someone in his way, he immediately fights him. A true human being is one who puts the bridle of Allah on himself. An ape is one who does whatever his lower self demands, without restraint.

The Creator has placed intelligence and conscience within every human being. When a desire forms in a person, his wisdom and conscience come alive at once and ask whether fulfilling it is right or not. In an ape, nothing stands between the wish and its fulfilment. Whatever comes to its mind, it does at once. It need not think about its wish, nor feel any shame after following it.

### In short

- When the Sabbath-breakers persisted in arrogance, Allah commanded them to be despised apes, and they became so.
- The fate of the silent group is debated, but ʿIkrimah persuaded Ibn ʿAbbās that they were saved because they disliked the sin.
- One view sees the change as one of character, the state of a person who follows every desire without the bridle of conscience.
