The previous verses dealt with fasting, in which the use of lawful things is forbidden for a fixed period, and now the acquisition and use of unlawful wealth is forbidden altogether. "Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly, nor deliberately bribe authorities in order to devour a portion of others' property, knowing that it is a sin". The two themes belong together, since the real purpose of fasting is to train a person to abstain from what is lawful to him, after which abstaining from what is wholly unlawful should come more easily. There is a further link: one must make an effort to break the fast with what is ḥalāl, for whoever endures the rigours of the day and then breaks his fast with ḥarām will find his fast unacceptable to Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly"**. The lawful acquisition and use of things was already stressed in verse 168 of this sūrah, which calls mankind to eat of what is lawful and good on earth and not to follow the footsteps of Satan, and again in Sūrat al-Naḥl: "So eat from the good, lawful things which Allah has provided for you, and be grateful for Allah's favours" (16:114). Here the opposite is forbidden. The words are general and cover usurpation, theft and robbery, by which property is taken by force, and also interest, gambling, bribery and every defective sale or transaction that Islamic law disallows even where the parties plainly consent. Money gained by lying or a false oath, or by earnings the law forbids even if one has sweated for them, is all ḥarām and false. Although the verse speaks of eating, it means use in the absolute sense, by eating, drinking, wearing or any other mode, just as one says that so-and-so ate up another's property even where nothing edible was involved. One reading lists oppression, theft, usurpation and perjury among the forbidden means, another extortion and cheating. The Qur'an pointedly says "your wealth", amwālakum, of the wealth of others. This hints that whoever encroaches on another's property should remember that the other loves and cares for his property as the encroacher does for his own, and would be hurt in the same way. It also suggests that when one person encroaches and the practice becomes general, others will encroach on him in turn. Imagine a society in which adulteration becomes common: the dealer who earns a little extra by mixing cheaper fat into butter goes to the milk shop and is given milk watered down, his spices are adulterated, so are his medicines, and the vicious circle goes on without end. The adulterator enjoys the extra in his coffers without noticing the hole beneath, for in reality he keeps none of it. Snatching another's property by false means opens the door to the plundering of one's own.

**"nor deliberately bribe authorities in order to devour a portion of others' property"**. Do not take property cases to the authorities so that through them you eat up some of people's wealth sinfully, nor offer judges bribes or the goods themselves, so as to obtain by their arbitration what belongs to others. This includes filing false cases, taking false oaths and giving or procuring false witness. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reports Ibn ʿAbbās as explaining that the verse concerns a debtor who, when there is no evidence of the loan, denies it and takes the matter before the authorities, though he knows the money is not his and that he is a sinner consuming what is forbidden to him. Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ʿIkrimah, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah, al-Suddī, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said the same: do not go to law when you know you are in the wrong.

**"knowing that it is a sin"**. While you know the falsehood of what you claim and know that Allah has forbidden it. To sin knowingly is the worst of things and carries the greatest punishment. The words also show that someone who claims something through a genuine misunderstanding and goes to court for it is not covered by this warning.

### The occasion of revelation

The verse was revealed over a land dispute between two Companions, Imruʾ al-Qays ibn ʿĀbis al-Kindī and ʿAbdān ibn Ashwaʿ al-Ḥaḍramī, as Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān relates. Imruʾ al-Qays held the land and ʿAbdān was reclaiming it. The plaintiff had no witnesses, so the Prophet ﷺ asked the defendant to swear an oath as the law requires. As he prepared to do so the Prophet ﷺ recited to him, by way of counsel, "Indeed, those who trade Allah's covenant and their oaths for a fleeting gain will have no share in the Hereafter" (3:77). Hearing this warning to those who seize property by a false oath, he abandoned the oath and surrendered the land, so that ʿAbdān received it without a judgment being pronounced. Imruʾ al-Qays admitted taking the money when the verse was revealed. The verse therefore first forbids acquiring property by unlawful means and then warns against false suits, false oaths and false witness.

### A court ruling does not change the truth

The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from Umm Salamah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that he was only human, that people brought their disputes to him, and that one of them might be more eloquent in presenting his case so that he judged in his favour on the strength of what he heard. Whoever was thus given something of his brother's right should not take it, for he would be allotting him a piece of the Fire. Verse and hadith together prove that the ruling of an authority does not change the reality of the matter. It neither makes the forbidden lawful nor the lawful forbidden, and it holds only as far as it goes, so if the ruling matches the truth there is no harm, and if not, the judge earns his reward while the cheat carries the burden. Qatādah said: son of Ādam, know that the judge's ruling does not permit you what is prohibited or prohibit what is permitted. He rules by his best judgment and the testimony of witnesses, and being human he may err. If he wrongly rules in someone's favour, that person will still face the dispute when the parties meet Allah on the Day of Resurrection, and the wrongdoer will then be judged swiftly and precisely in a way that outweighs whatever he gained by the mistaken judgment in this world. A Muslim judge may, through a misunderstanding, give one party what belongs to the other, but such a judgment does not make it lawful for him, nor does it make unlawful what is lawful for the rightful owner. Whoever grabs another's property through a court by fraud, false witness or a false oath bears the curse of it and should give it up, mindful of the accountability of the Hereafter before the All-Knowing, All-Aware. According to Abū Ḥanīfah, however, in matters of contract and annulment where the qāḍī has authority under Islamic law, his judgment renders the contract or annulment valid even if it rested on a false oath or false testimony, and the rules of lawful and unlawful then follow from it, though the curse of lying and of procuring false witnesses still hangs round the culprit's neck.

### The criterion of lawful earning

The whole world agrees that money and goods are needed and that life depends on them, and it agrees that there are favoured and permissible ways of acquiring them and undesirable, forbidden ways. Nearly everyone looks down on theft, robbery and fraud, yet people lack a sound criterion for deciding which means are permissible, and perhaps cannot devise one, since the matter concerns the well-being of all humanity. A universal, sound and reasonable criterion can only come from the Lord and Creator of all the worlds through revelation. Had human beings been left to forge their own, those framing the law would naturally think first of their own nation or community, and even an international body representing the whole world cannot, as experience shows, satisfy everyone, so that legal injustice ends in war and chaos. The law of the permissible and forbidden enforced by the Sharīʿah of Islam comes directly from revelation or is derived from it, and being reasonable, natural and comprehensive it is the only law that can work for every nation and guarantee peace. Everything of common utility, to which all people have equal claim, such as air, water, vegetation, heat, unowned forests and the growth of uninhabited mountains, is left as the shared property of all, and no one may take it over as owner. Things which, if shared, would disturb society or breed conflict are governed by private ownership. The law of initial ownership of land and its produce differs from the law of transfer, and the whole is so framed that no one is left without the necessities of life provided he makes the effort to earn them, while no one usurps others' rights or inflicts loss on them so as to concentrate capital in a few hands. Under the law of transfer, whether by the divine rules of inheritance or by agreed sale, there must be no fraud or deception and no ambiguity or deficiency that could breed future dispute. Consent must be genuine and not extracted under pressure. Every transaction the law calls void, invalid or sinful has some inherent defect: plain fraud, a return for something unknown, the usurping of a right, gain through another's loss, or the illegal disposal of public rights. Interest, gambling and the like are forbidden chiefly because they harm the public interest, letting a few individuals grow as parasites while the community sinks into poverty, so that even mutual consent cannot make them permissible, since they are a formalised crime against the whole community. This verse covers all such cases.

### Ḥalāl brings blessing and ḥarām produces evil

The Qur'an stresses in many places abstention from the unlawful and the acquisition of the lawful, and points out that human deeds and morals are deeply affected by what one eats, for good conduct can hardly be expected from one who does not eat and drink what is ḥalāl: "O messengers! Eat from what is good and lawful, and act righteously. Indeed, I fully know what you do" (23:51). Here lawful eating is joined to the command to do good, hinting that righteous deeds flow only from lawful food. The Prophet ﷺ explained in a hadith that although the verse addresses the prophets, all Muslims are charged with the same command, and added that the prayer of one who eats the unlawful is not answered, describing people who take great pains in worship and then raise their hands crying "O Lord, O Lord" while their food, drink and clothing are all ḥarām, and asking how such prayer could be answered. A great part of the Prophet's ﷺ teaching was devoted to saving his Ummah from the unlawful. He said that whoever ate ḥalāl, followed the Sunnah and did not harm people would enter Paradise, and when the Companions said that these things were common among the Muslims of his day he replied that in every future age too there would be people who kept to them, a hadith al-Tirmidhī reports and grades ṣaḥīḥ. He told ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar that four traits would suffice a person even if he had nothing else in the world: guarding trusts, telling the truth, good character and care about eating what is ḥalāl. When Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ asked him to pray that he become one whose prayers are answered, he told Saʿd to make his food pure and lawful and he would be so, swearing by Him in whose hand was Muḥammad's life that when a servant puts a morsel of ḥarām into his stomach no deed of his is accepted for forty days, and that flesh grown from unlawful gain has the Fire as its only destination. ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd reports him swearing that no servant becomes a Muslim until his heart and tongue are Muslim and his neighbours are safe from his harm, and that when a servant acquires the unlawful and gives it in charity it is not accepted, if he spends it it is stripped of blessing, and if he leaves it to his heirs it becomes his provision for the journey to Hell, for Allah does not wash away one evil with another but does wash away an evil deed with a good one.

### The questions of the Day of Resurrection

Muʿādh ibn Jabal reports the Prophet ﷺ as saying that no servant will move a step on the Day of Judgment until he answers four questions: about his life and how he spent it, his youth and how he used it, his wealth and where he earned it and where he spent it, and his knowledge and how far he acted on it, as al-Bayhaqī records. ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar reports a sermon in which the Prophet ﷺ sought refuge with Allah for the Emigrants from five traits. The first was immodesty, for when it spreads among a people they are struck by plagues, epidemics and diseases their elders never heard of. The second was cheating in weights and measures, which brings famine, high prices, crushing labour and oppressive rulers. The third was withholding zakāh, for which rain is withheld. The fourth was breaking the covenant with Allah and His Messenger, after which Allah sets foreign enemies over them who seize what is theirs without right. The fifth was the failure of rulers to judge by the Book of Allah because His revealed injunctions do not suit their fancies, upon which Allah sends mutual hatred and strife among them. Ibn Mājah, al-Bayhaqī and others report this and al-Ḥākim grades it ṣaḥīḥ by the standard of Muslim. May Allah grant us and all Muslims the fullest ability to stay safe from such calamities.

### A life of renunciation

The life of a believer is thus one of renunciation, for throughout his life he must keep away from certain things, and Ramaḍān is the training for it. A month of restraint teaches that the true servant of Allah worships at the level of taqwā and lives in piety and fear of Him, and that Allah answers the call only of those who bring themselves close to Him by a life of sacrifice. In the devotional reading, when you turn to created beings to judge your affairs, know that Allah is watching and His knowledge encompasses you, so stand in shyness before the Real. Created beings know only externals, while the Real governs the innermost secrets of hearts.

### In short

- After the fast's abstention from the lawful comes the ban on the unlawful: usurpation, theft, interest, gambling, bribery, false oaths and every defective transaction, in every mode of use (cf. 2:168, 16:114).
- "Your wealth" reminds the encroacher that others love their property as he loves his, and that wrong invites wrong in return.
- Revealed over the land dispute of Imruʾ al-Qays and ʿAbdān, where 3:77 stopped a false oath, the verse forbids winning others' property through the courts knowingly.
- A judge's ruling cannot make the forbidden lawful (Umm Salamah, Qatādah), though Abū Ḥanīfah holds that it validates contracts and annulments outwardly.
- Only revelation gives a universal criterion of lawful earning, and ḥalāl food is the root of good deeds and answered prayer (23:51, the hadiths of Saʿd and Ibn Masʿūd), while wealth is among the four questions of the Day of Judgment (Muʿādh).
