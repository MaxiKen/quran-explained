<<<VERSE 2:142>>>
With verse 142 the surah turns to the change of the qiblah, the direction of prayer, an event that shook Madinah in the second year after the Hijrah. "The foolish among the people will ask, “Why did they turn away from the direction of prayer they used to face?” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “The east and west belong ˹only˺ to Allah. He guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path.”" The verse announces the objection before it was made, and supplies the answer. Having spent many verses on the religion of Ibrāhīm (Abraham), the Qur'an now turns the Muslims towards his House.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The foolish among the people will ask"**. The ignorant among the Jews, the Arab idolaters and the hypocrites. The Arabic verb carries a prefix that marks the future and so informs of the unseen: Allah tells the believers what will be said before it is said. When the qiblah was changed, those afflicted with hypocrisy and doubt, and the disbelieving Jews, lost their bearings and fell into confusion. The Qur'an reports their objection and adds that only stupid people could make it, just as earlier in the surah those who turned from Ibrāhīm's religion were described as making fools of themselves. In the devotional reading, the disbelievers' eyes were weak, so the correct view of the believers' affairs never became apparent to them. They looked with an eye for finding fault and loosed their tongues against everything the believers did or would do. They never saw anything new without producing a new objection to it, and the shift of the qiblah to the Kaʿbah was one more occasion.

**"“Why did they turn away from the direction of prayer they used to face?”"**. What has turned the Prophet ﷺ and the believers from Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem), the direction they used to face in prayer? What is the matter with these people, who face one direction one day and another the next? Or, as some took it: there was no reason to leave the direction they had been praying in. The Jews added that the Muslims must want to return to the religion of their ancestors. Before Islam people had prayed towards Jerusalem, and the old qiblah had come to seem sacred and beyond change. How, some asked, could any other place be the qiblah when Jerusalem had held that position from time immemorial? The opponents of Islam used the change to spread every sort of rumour. Earlier prophets always faced Jerusalem, they said, so how could this prophet go against them? It showed that the whole purpose of his mission was to spite the Jews. Some poured scorn on his claim to prophethood: he seemed to be in two minds about his own mission, facing Jerusalem one moment and Makkah the next. Others said that if the Kaʿbah was the real qiblah, then all the prayers the Muslims had offered towards Jerusalem had been wasted. If their qiblah was correct, the objectors asked, what could turn them from it?

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “The east and west belong ˹only˺ to Allah."**. All directions are His. The command, the decision and the authority belong to Allah alone, so He orders His servants to face whichever direction He wills, and no objection is possible. The prayer towards the Kaʿbah and the prayer formerly offered towards Jerusalem were both by Allah's command. As He has said, "To Allah belong the east and the west, so wherever you turn you are facing ˹towards˺ Allah" (2:115), and, "Righteousness is not in turning your faces towards the east or the west. Rather, the righteous are those who believe in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Books, and the prophets" (2:177). The best act is to keep to Allah's commands. Wherever He tells us to face, we face. Since obedience means carrying out His orders, if He commanded us to face a different place every day we would do so, for we are His servants and at His disposal. Their stupidity lies in failing to see that east and west alike belong to Allah, and that He guides whom He likes to the straight path.

**"He guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path.”"**. He establishes whom He wills on a straight religion and a straight prayer direction, the religion of Islam, free of distortion and misguidance, and you are among those He has guided. The verse fully answers the enemies of Islam. The Straight Path is the readiness to obey Allah's command without demur, and that path was granted to the Muslims by His grace. There is also a hint that where the injunctions of the Sharīʿah are concerned, rectitude lies in obeying each divine command unquestioningly, without being too curious about its rationale, since those who go hunting for a rationale usually do so because they wish to deny, belittle or disobey.

### How the qiblah was changed

Al-Bukhārī relates from al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ prayed facing Bayt al-Maqdis for sixteen or seventeen months, while wishing he could face the Kaʿbah. The first prayer he offered towards it was ʿAṣr, with a group of people. One of those who had prayed with him then went out and passed some people bowing in prayer in a mosque. He said: by Allah, I bear witness that I have prayed with the Prophet ﷺ facing Makkah. On hearing this they turned towards the House just as they were, still bowing. Some Muslims who had prayed towards the old qiblah had died or been martyred before the change, and the Companions did not know what to say about them, so Allah sent down the words, "And Allah would never discount your ˹previous acts of˺ faith" (2:143). Muslim recorded the same report by another chain. In another version from al-Barāʾ, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to pray towards Bayt al-Maqdis but kept looking up at the sky, awaiting Allah's command, until the words came down, "Indeed, We see you ˹O Prophet˺ turning your face towards heaven. Now We will make you turn towards a direction ˹of prayer˺ that will please you" (2:144). A Muslim then said: we wish we knew about those of us who died before the qiblah was changed, and about our own prayers offered towards Bayt al-Maqdis, and 2:143 was revealed. And the foolish among the people, the Jews and Christians, said, "What made them change the qiblah they used to face?", and this verse came down. Al-Bukhārī also records the report through ʿAbd Allāh ibn Rajāʾ and Abū Isḥāq from al-Barāʾ, which names the Jews as the foolish ones.

Ibn ʿAbbās, in the report of ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah, said that when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ migrated to Madinah, Allah commanded him to face Bayt al-Maqdis, and the Jews were delighted. He faced it for over ten months, but he loved the qiblah of Ibrāhīm, and he would supplicate and look up to the sky, until Allah revealed the command to turn towards the Sacred Mosque. The Jews disliked the change and asked what had turned the Muslims from their former qiblah, and Allah answered, "The east and west belong ˹only˺ to Allah."

Many other hadiths deal with the subject. In summary: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was commanded to face Bayt al-Maqdis, and in Makkah he would pray towards it standing between the two corners of the Kaʿbah, so that the Kaʿbah lay between him and Bayt al-Maqdis. After the migration to Madinah that was no longer possible, and Allah commanded him to face Bayt al-Maqdis, as Ibn ʿAbbās and most scholars hold. Some scholars say that when ṣalāh was first made obligatory, the qiblah appointed for him was the Kaʿbah, which had already been the qiblah of his ancestor Ibrāhīm, and that some time after the Hijrah, or as others maintain shortly before it, he was commanded to turn to Bayt al-Maqdis. This particular command is not reported in the Qur'an at all, which shows the hollowness of the claim that the Qur'an can be fully understood without the help of hadith. The spot in the mosque at Madinah where he prayed facing Bayt al-Maqdis is still marked, as al-Qurṭubī notes.

The Prophet ﷺ was obedience personified. He went on praying towards Bayt al-Maqdis as commanded, but he longed for the Kaʿbah, the qiblah of Ādam (Adam) and Ibrāhīm, to be made his qiblah as well. Allah's way is to grant, in His grace, the wishes of servants who have found His favour. So the Prophet ﷺ hoped this wish too would be granted. The Qur'an describes him turning his face to heaven and promises him a qiblah he would like (2:144). That verse speaks not of the "Kaʿbah" or "the House of Allah" but of al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, the Sacred Mosque. This indicates that those far from Makkah need not have the Kaʿbah itself exactly in front of them while praying, and that facing in the direction of the House is enough. Those present in the Sacred Mosque, or able to see the Kaʿbah from a distance, must have the Kaʿbah or some part of it directly in front of them, failing which the prayer is not valid.

Al-Bukhārī records that the news reached some of the Anṣār while they were praying ʿAṣr towards Bayt al-Maqdis, and they at once turned to face the Kaʿbah. The Ṣaḥīḥayn (the collections of al-Bukhārī and Muslim) record from Ibn ʿUmar that while the people in the mosque of Qubāʾ were praying Fajr, a man came and said that Qur'an had been revealed to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that night commanding him to face the Kaʿbah, so they should face it. They had been facing ash-Shām (Syria), and they turned towards the Kaʿbah. At-Tirmidhī adds that they were bowing at the time and changed direction without rising, and Muslim reports this last detail from Anas. The believers who were praying with the Prophet ﷺ when the command came turned, still in prayer, from Jerusalem to Makkah, a turn of about 160 degrees from north-west to south. All these hadiths show the Companions' perfect obedience to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ and their compliance with every command. They also prove a legal point: an abrogating text takes effect for a person only once he knows of it, even if it has already been revealed and announced. That is why these Companions were not told to repeat the ʿAṣr, Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ prayers they had offered towards Jerusalem after the qiblah had in fact been changed. Allah knows best.

### Why one qiblah at all?

Qiblah means the direction one turns one's face. In every form of worship a true Muslim turns to Allah alone, and Allah is not confined to any direction. He transcends all dimensions. Logically, then, everyone might have been left free to choose whatever orientation he liked and to change it as suited him. Divine Wisdom found it more fitting that all worshippers turn the same way, to a fixed orientation. Worship takes several forms. Some concern the individual alone, such as fasting and dhikr (remembrance of Allah), which can be performed in private. Others, such as ṣalāh and the Hajj, are performed openly and in congregation. These have a secondary function besides worship: they give Muslims a social and collective discipline. The basic principle of any social organisation is the unity and integration of its individuals. On the firmness of that unity its strength depends, whereas an improper stress on individuality encourages disintegration and splintering.

As to what should serve as the principle of unity, people have chosen differently at different times: race or colour, homeland or region, language. All such considerations are arbitrary and accidental. Instead of bringing people together they divide them, producing, as the newspapers show every day, convulsions across the world. The revealed religions and the Sharīʿahs of the prophets, whose collective name is Islam, have paid no unnecessary regard to such accidental factors. They have founded unity and integration on the only basis that can possibly be valid: unity of mind arising from unity of belief. Islam calls on people not to scatter in the worship of a thousand false gods but to join in worshipping the True God, the One without equal, the only worship that can gather people from the four corners of the earth, of the past, the present and the future, into one body of the faithful. To give this inner unity a visible form, and to reinforce it, certain outward expressions of unity were also prescribed. In both cases the governing principle is the same. Unity must not be forced by circumstances. It should spring from a free act of will and choice, and give rise to a spiritual brotherhood.

Accidental factors like race, colour and birthplace are given their proper place in social life, but none is allowed to usurp the centre. Only in the field where the human will can exercise its power of choice has Islam sought to establish unity, inner and outer. A further consideration behind the relevant rules is that whatever serves as a point of unity should be something every human being, man or woman, literate or illiterate, townsman or villager, can adopt with equal ease. That is why the Sharīʿah has not imposed a single rigid mode of dress, food or housing on all the peoples of the world. Climates, needs and even tastes differ from region to region, and such uniformity would have made life hard. Had a certain minimum of dress been prescribed, it would have been inconvenient for some, offended against moderation and amounted to rejecting Allah's bounties, while a more elaborate compulsory dress would have been impossible for the poor. So instead of a uniform, the Sharīʿah permits the modes of dress current among different peoples and lays down only necessary restrictions: the dress must cover a specified area of the body, defined separately for men and women, it must not be chosen out of extravagance or vanity, and it must not be worn in order to imitate non-Muslims. In short, Islam has fixed as points of unity only things that can be freely chosen, are easy to adopt and involve no undue hardship or expense, such as straightening the rows in congregational prayer, following the imām's movements strictly, and wearing a single form of dress during the Hajj.

Among these the qiblah is one of the most important. Allah Himself is free of all dimensions, but a single definite qiblah gives people an easy and concrete unifying principle. Had the choice been left to human beings, it would itself have become a source of dissension and conflict, so a matter of such weight had to be settled by Allah. In fact the angels had laid the Kaʿbah's foundation, the House of Allah, before Ādam was ever sent down to the earth, and it was the first qiblah of mankind: "Surely the first House ˹of worship˺ established for humanity is the one at Bakkah—a blessed sanctuary and a guide for ˹all˺ people" (3:96). As noted under verse 125, it remained the qiblah until the time of Nūḥ (Noah), when the Flood destroyed it. Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) rebuilt it by divine command and it became their qiblah. Afterwards Bayt al-Maqdis in Jerusalem was appointed the qiblah for the Hebrew prophets and their people. Even so, according to Abū al-ʿĀliyah, as al-Qurṭubī records, those prophets would pray in Bayt al-Maqdis in such a way as to face not only the Rock (the Ṣakhrah) but the Kaʿbah as well.

### What the change teaches

The qiblah has to do with the form of worship rather than its reality. A direction is appointed so that prayer may take an organised pattern, and Allah can change it as He pleases, for whichever way one turns, one faces Him. Whatever direction He lays down is the one to face, whatever it may be. Neither the Kaʿbah nor Bayt al-Maqdis possesses any exclusive merit of its own in this respect. It is the divine command alone that confers the distinction of being the qiblah, and Allah could as easily have chosen some other place. The only merit in adopting a particular qiblah lies in obeying the divine command and submitting wholly to Allah's will, which is the basic principle of the religion of Ibrāhīm, the builder of the Kaʿbah. The verses already cited, 2:177 and 2:115, define the matter clearly: the place chosen has no merit in its own right, its special merit arises from Allah's having chosen it, and turning towards it is meritorious only insofar as it shows readiness to obey. The very reason for changing the Prophet's ﷺ qiblah may well have been to show people visibly that a qiblah is no idol to be worshipped. It is a concrete expression of a divine command, and may therefore be changed whenever Allah wills. The next verse says explicitly that the earlier appointment of Bayt al-Maqdis was meant to show who was willing to obey the Prophet ﷺ and who was not. True believers, those not caught up in the externals of religion, were not disturbed by the change. They understood that what mattered was not the direction but Allah's command. He could lay down any qiblah whenever He liked, and whatever He prescribed must be followed.

Allah's care and kindness towards His servant and Messenger Muḥammad ﷺ and his Ummah is profound. He guided them to the qiblah of Ibrāhīm, His Khalīl (close friend), and commanded them to face the Kaʿbah, the most honoured house on the face of the earth, built by Ibrāhīm in the name of Allah, the One without partner. Aḥmad relates in his Musnad from ʿĀʾishah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said of the People of the Book that they envy the Muslims for nothing so much as for three things: the Friday, Jumuʿah, to which Allah guided us and from which they were led astray, for when every community was told to set aside one day of the week for worship, the Jews chose Saturday, the Christians chose Sunday, and the Muslims were given Friday, the day Allah favoured; the true qiblah, to which He directed us and from which they strayed; and our saying "Āmīn" behind the imām.

In the devotional reading, the servants worship in whatever region and direction He wills. In the same way, the companions of absence, veiled from witnessing how the Real grants free scope to His friends, seize on isolated aspects of an affair and bring their own states to bear on them. Had they seen the whole picture with a single vision, they would have been spared the pain of divided thinking, the distraction of accursed thoughts and the claims of broken conjecture. But Allah guides whom He wills to His light.

### In short

- Allah foretold that the Jews, idolaters and hypocrites would mock the Muslims for leaving Jerusalem for the Kaʿbah, and answered that every direction is His, so the only thing that matters is obeying His command (2:115, 2:177).
- The Prophet ﷺ prayed towards Bayt al-Maqdis for sixteen or seventeen months while longing for Ibrāhīm's qiblah, until 2:144 came down. Companions who heard the news mid-prayer turned at once, and those who had prayed unaware were not told to repeat, since abrogation binds only once it is known.
- A single qiblah is one of the easy, freely chosen outward points of unity Islam prescribes, founded on unity of belief rather than race, land or language. The Kaʿbah was the first House (3:96), the qiblah of Ādam and Ibrāhīm, before Jerusalem served the Hebrew prophets.
- The change showed that a qiblah is an expression of Allah's command, not an idol, and the People of the Book envy the Muslims for Friday, the qiblah and saying Āmīn.
