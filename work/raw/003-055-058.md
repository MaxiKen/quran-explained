<<<VERSE 3:55>>>
After the plot, Allah speaks directly to the intended victim: "˹Remember˺ when Allah said, “O Jesus! I will take you and raise you up to Myself. I will deliver you from those who disbelieve, and elevate your followers above the disbelievers until the Day of Judgment. Then to Me you will ˹all˺ return, and I will settle all your disputes”". Five promises are packed into one address. He will take ʿĪsā, raise him, cleanse him from the disbelievers, set his followers above them until the Resurrection, and judge their disputes at the return.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Remember˺ when Allah said, “O Jesus! I will take you"**: I will take you in full, mutawaffīka, and raise you to Myself while you are asleep. The taking is elsewhere used of sleep: "It is He Who takes your souls by night (when you are asleep)" (6:60), and "It is Allah Who takes away the souls at the time of their death, and those that die not during their sleep" (39:42). When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would awaken he used to say, "All the thanks are due to Allah Who brought us back to life after He had caused us to die (sleep), and the Return is to Him." Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded that al-Ḥasan said the taking here refers to sleep, because Allah raised ʿĪsā while he was asleep.

**"and raise you up to Myself"**: I will gather you, seize you and raise you to Me away from the world without death, taking your body and soul alive and ridding you of the filth of those who disbelieved you.

**"I will deliver you from those who disbelieve"**: I will purify and save you from those who disbelieve, by raising you to heaven, cleansing you and removing you far away from them.

**"and elevate your followers above the disbelievers until the Day of Judgment"**: Your followers will be placed over the disbelievers until Resurrection Day, prevailing by decisive proof and sword, by help, by force and by evidence.

**"Then to Me you will ˹all˺ return, and I will settle all your disputes”"**: then to Me shall be your return after death, and I will judge between you concerning the matters of religion in which you used to differ and argue.

### What "I will take you" means

The word mutawaffī takes tawaffī as its verbal noun from the root wafy, which lexically means "to take in full". Its derivations wafā, īfāʾ and istīfāʾ convey the same sense. Because a person completes his appointed time at death and the soul given by Allah is taken back fully and conclusively, the word is also used figuratively for death, and the same word covers daily sleep in the verse "Allah takes away lives of the living at the time of their death and of those that do not die, in their sleep" (39:42).

Ibn Taimiyyah says in al-Jawāb al-Ṣaḥīḥ that al-tawaffī in Arabic means to exact fully or take in full. Three forms are distinguished: taking during sleep, taking in death, and taking soul and body together. Kulliyāt Abū al-Baqāʾ describes common usage as putting to death and exacting the soul, but classical usage as taking in full and exacting due right. Most scholars therefore render mutawaffīka "taking in full": ʿĪsā would not be left in Jewish hands, but taken away into the heavens.

Some authentic explainers read the word as "giving death", but never death at enemy hands. On their account Allah comforted Jesus with two things while the Jews were bent on killing him. His death would be natural, not a killing, and to rescue him from their designs Allah would first raise him toward Himself. That is the explanation reported from Ibn ʿAbbās through al-Suyūṭī in al-Durr al-Manthur, on the authority of several narrators. It depends on precedence in wording: "I shall raise you" happens first, and "I shall give you death" later. The order itself hints at what is coming. The raising will not last forever. It will be temporary, and he will return to the mortal world, prevail over his enemies, and only then meet a natural death. His return and death after victory become a consummation of honour, and a refutation in advance of Christian divinity, since a Jesus who will die cannot be the Living and Eternal.

A second gloss makes the return smaller and inward: death will follow your descent, or alternatively, love of worldly life will die inside your heart.

Raising, rafʿ, can be figurative, as rank: "And raised some of you in ranks over others" (6:165), and "and Allah will raise up in rank those of you who believe and those who have been given knowledge" (58:11). In this verse the word ilā, "towards", removes that figure. Rafiʿuka ilayya, "I shall raise you towards Me", cannot mean rank alone. Sūrat Al-Nisāʾ settles it further: the Jews certainly did not kill Jesus. Rather, Allah raised him towards Himself (4:158), an expression used only for raising the spirit and the body alive. In his Tafsīr, Imām al-Rāzī, in al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr, observed that precedence of a later event over an earlier one is frequent in the Qur'an for exactly such expedient reasons.

### Why this prophet's emigration went to heaven

Prophets opposed to the end usually end in one of two ways. Either their people are annihilated, as with ʿĀd and Thamūd and the peoples of Lūṭ (Lot) and Ṣāliḥ, or the prophet is told to migrate and later achieves power and victory elsewhere. Ibrāhīm (Abraham) migrated from Iraq and took refuge in Syria. Mūsā (Moses) migrated from Egypt and came to Madyan. Muhammad ﷺ migrated from Makkah and came to Madinah, and from there finally attacked Makkah and conquered it. The raising of Jesus to the heavens was an emigration of its own kind, after which he would return to the world and gain total victory over the Jews.

Why should his emigration alone have heaven as destination? His case is like Adam's. Adam's birth differed from normal birth, without father and mother, and the birth of Jesus took a miraculous form different from normal human birth, without a father. His death too will take a unique and unmatched form, materialising after hundreds of years following his return to the world. His emigration fittingly follows a unique pattern. The marvels led ignorant Christians to declare him God, while reflection on those same marvels proves his servitude as a human being, his obedience to the divine will and his human traits. The Qur'an therefore refutes the godhood of Jesus at all such points. Because a raising to heaven would have strengthened that doubt, the word mutawaffīka comes first and eliminates it. The verse negates the Jewish plan, since they were set to crucify and kill Jesus and Allah made their plans come to nothing, and the precedence of the words also refutes the Christians, because Jesus is no God who never dies.

### Five promises and their fulfilment

1. **His death would not come by Jewish killing.** It will be natural, at its appointed time near the Day of Doom, when ʿĪsā descends from the heavens to earth, as detailed in sound hadith transmitted through unbroken chains.

2. **He would be raised toward the heavens in that situation.** This was fulfilled at once, reported in Surah al-Nisa: "the Jews certainly did not kill him; rather, Allah raised him towards Himself" (4:158). The wider passage runs: "And because of their disbelief and allegations against Maryam and because of their saying 'We killed Al-Masih 'Isa, son of Maryam, the Messenger of Allah', but they killed him not, nor crucified him, but it appeared that way to them... For surely they killed him not. But Allah raised him up unto Himself. And Allah is Ever All-Powerful, All-Wise. And there is none of the people of the Scripture (Jews and Christians) but must believe in him before his death. And on the Day of Resurrection, he ['Isa] will be a witness against them" (4:156-159). "Before his death" refers to ʿĪsā. The People of the Book will believe in him before he dies, when he returns before the Day of Resurrection, annuls the jizyah and accepts only Islam from people.

3. **He would be cleansed of false accusations.** This was fulfilled when the last of the prophets came and refuted them. The Jews slandered his parentage because he was born without a father. The Qur'an answers that this was Allah's power and will, and that Adam, born without both father and mother, is more surprising still. They also accused ʿĪsā of claiming to be God. Many verses report him publicly confessing instead that he is human, a servant and bondsman of Allah.

4. **His followers would stand above the disbelievers.** Once Allah raised ʿĪsā, his followers split into sects. One company accepted his actual commission: Allah's servant, His Messenger, and son of His female servant. Others reached extremes by calling him God's son, God Himself, or a Trinity member. False creeds are named and refuted in the Qur'an. Christians persisted until the third century CE, when a Greek king, Constantine, became Christian in order, the narrative says, to destroy Christianity. Whether philosopher or simply ignorant, Constantine inserted and deleted material, created rituals and the so-called Great Trust (really the Great Treachery), permitted swine, turned prayer eastward, founded churches and added ten fast days to compensate for an alleged personal sin. Christianity became Constantinian, with more than twelve thousand churches, temples and monasteries and Constantinople bearing his name. Christians then dominated Jews for a time, helped because they stood closer to truth than Jews did, though both are described as disbelievers.

Following here means belief in and confession of the prophethood of ʿĪsā. Practising all the injunctions ascribed to him is not a precondition for the worldly dominance promised. Christians and Muslims both fall in this category, since both believe in his prophethood and messengership. Salvation in the Hereafter is another matter. It depends on believing all the injunctions ʿĪsā brought, including the instruction to believe in the last of the prophets who would come after Jesus departed. Christians did not follow that instruction in faith, so they deprived themselves of salvation, while Muslims acted on it and became deserving of it. But dominance over the Jews depended on the prophethood of ʿĪsā alone, and from the time of the promise to now, Christians and Muslims have dominated the Jews, and so it shall be through the Day of Judgment. Their empires have covered the world.

5. **Their disputes would be judged at the return.** "Then, to Me is your return, whereupon I shall judge between you" (3:55). This fifth promise will be fulfilled at its appointed time.

### What happened after Muhammad ﷺ was sent

Muhammad's followers changed the map. Belief in Allah, angels, Books and messengers took its correct form, making them true followers of every earth-sent prophet. They embraced the unlettered final Prophet and master of mankind, summoning humanity to the entire truth. Hence they deserved every prophet more than nations that claim his path while altering his religion. Muhammad's Law abrogated earlier laws and will remain unchanged, dominant and victorious until the Last Hour.

Under that promise, Muslims conquered the world's east and west and earthly kingdoms. Countries submitted. Kisrā of Persia fell, the Czar was destroyed, and their treasures were taken and spent for Allah, exactly as promised: "Allah has promised those among you who believe and do righteous good deeds, that He will certainly grant them succession in the land, as He granted it to those before them, and that He will grant them the authority to practice their religion which He has chosen for them. And He will surely give them in exchange a safe security after their fear (provided) they worship Me and do not associate anything with Me" (24:55).

So Muslims became the true believers in ʿĪsā. They acquired Ash-Sham from the Christians, who evacuated to Asia Minor and their fortified city of Constantinople. Muslims will remain above them until the Day of Resurrection, and the truthful one who received the true news has told them they will conquer Constantinople in the future and seize its treasures.

### The objection of a Jewish state in the present age

The present state of Israel is treated as no counter-evidence. In the argument given, that state is a manifestation of the military might of Russia and Christians of Euro-American origin, established and maintained against Muslims. Withdraw that patronage and the state would collapse and disappear from the map, so it is not a manifestation of Jewish domination at all. Even allowing it as a Jewish state for argument's sake, Christians and Muslims together remain wielders of far greater influence, and Jews remain much subjugated with fear. A temporary Jewish domination near the Day of Judgment is itself reported in authentic hadith of the Prophet ﷺ, so even such an episode would not contradict the promise, and a short-lived occupation cannot be named a state or government, especially one said to rest on a conspiracy of Christians and communists.

### Life, death and return: the consensus described

The Jews alone say that ʿĪsā was crucified, killed and buried and never returned to life. The Christians say he was indeed killed on the cross, then brought back to life and raised into the heavens. Both are refuted by mistaken identity: "And they did not kill him and they did not crucify him, but they were deluded by resemblance" (4:157). Jews rejoiced after killing their own man. Christians made the same mistake by believing it was Jesus who died on the cross. The Islamic belief stated here and in several other verses is that Allah raised him alive onto the heavens to rescue him from the Jews. He was not killed and not crucified. He is alive in heaven, will descend close to the Day of Judgment, lead Muslims to victory over the Jews, and finally die a natural death.

Consensus of the entire Muslim community is claimed for this belief. Ḥāfiẓ ibn Ḥajr reported the consensus in Talkhīṣ al-Ḥabīr. It is said to stand on several Qur'anic verses and on mutawātir hadith transmitted through unbroken chains of reporting. On Surah Al-Zukhruf, a classical explanation adds that uninterrupted hadith transmission presents ʿĪsā as a just leader arriving before Judgment Day (43:61).

Special works on the question are named. ʿAqīdat al-Islām fī Ḥayāti ʿĪsā, by Maulānā Sayyid Muḥammad Anwar Shāh al-Kashmīrī, Ḥayāt-e-ʿĪsā in Urdu by Ḥaḍrat Maulānā Badr ʿĀlam Muhājir Madanī, and Ḥayāt-e-Masīḥ in Urdu by Maulānā Sayyid Muḥammad Idrīs are given among hundreds of published books and essays. A student executing al-Kashmīrī's orders gathered over one hundred mutawātir hadith on the raising and future descent. His al-Taṣrīḥ bimā Tawātara fī Nuzūl al-Masīḥ appeared lately in Beirut, annotated by ʿAllāma ʿAbd al-Fattāḥ Abū Ghuddah of Aleppo, Syria.

### Why so much detail is given about him

In the eleventh section of this surah, Adam, Nūḥ (Noah), Āl-Ibrāhīm and Āl-ʿImrān are covered briefly in one verse. Nearly three sections and twenty-two verses then treat ʿĪsā and his family in meticulous detail. His grandmother's pledge, his mother's birth, name and upbringing, his conception and birth, what his mother ate and drank after childbirth, her return to her family with the newborn, their blame and curses, the newborn's gift of eloquence, his growing up and call, the opposition and the disciples' help, the Jewish attempts to trap and kill him, his raising alive into the heavens, and after that his additional traits, looks, physique and dress. No prophet receives this much detail in the whole spectrum of Qur'an and hadith.

The wisdom given is identification. The final Prophet, with no apostle after him, guided his community about what it would face until the Day of Judgment. He identified by name those it should follow and marked the astray who endangered its faith. Of later mischief-makers, Dajjāl, the false Messiah, is the most notorious, so the Prophet ﷺ detailed his profile and distinguishing traits. Among later reformers and elders, ʿĪsā is the most distinguished, blessed with prophethood, kept alive in heaven to rescue the Muslim community in Dajjāl's wicked period, and appointed to kill Dajjāl near the Day of Judgment. Clear indications of his person and qualities prevent doubt when he comes a second time.

Secondly, he will not come then designated as Prophet in function. He will lead the Muslim community as its Khalīfa, vice-regent, while his personal station of prophethood remains unremoved, like a provincial governor visiting another province whose office at home is not thereby taken away. Rejecting his prophethood is infidelity still. A community already professing faith in it must not fall into the grievous error of rejection by failing to recognise him.

Thirdly, because the event falls in the world's later years, ambiguity in his marks could let another person claim to be the Messiah. In pre-partition India, Mirza Ghulam Ahmad of Qadian claimed precisely that. Scholars of the Muslim community refuted his claim using these very indicators. The detailed description therefore indicates his reappearance close to the Day of Judgment, and an Urdu treatise, Masīḥ Mawʿūd kī Pahchān, "Identifying the Promised Messiah", is offered for the full details.

### The inward gathering

The allusion inside the address runs: I will gather you away from yourself and raise you above mortal human qualities, purifying your own desire, irāda, so completely that you act as Our agent, muṣarrif, through and for Us. Free choice, ikhtiyār, will belong to you in nothing. Divine governorship, tawallī, will descend instead and stand for you. Through that quality dead people came alive by Jesus's hands, which is possible only through divine deliberative power.

His heart was purified from turning toward others, aghyār, or seeing simulacra and traces, al-amthāl wa-l-āthār, across states and phases, al-aḥwāl wa-l-aṭwār. Unchanged followers of his religion, dīn, and creed, ʿaqīda, within tawḥīd are believers, muʾminūn, right and helped until Resurrection Day. Divine judgment then assigns disbelievers to hellfire, ammā l-kuffār fa-fī l-jaḥīm, and believers to felicity, wa-ammā l-muʾminūn fa-fī l-naʿīm.

### In short

- "I will take you" means taken in full, in sleep, and raised body and soul to heaven, not killed by the Jews (4:158, 6:60, 39:42).
- Five promises follow: natural death deferred, raising fulfilled, slanders refuted, followers dominant through Christians and Muslims, and disputes judged at the return.
- Raised alive, ʿĪsā will descend near the Day of Judgment, and the unmatched detail of his story lets the community identify him and reject false claimants.

<<<VERSE 3:56>>>
The judgement announced to Jesus begins with its first half: "As for those who disbelieve, I will subject them to a severe punishment in this life and the Hereafter, and they will have no helpers". The previous verse said that Allah will be the final judge in what they used to differ about. This verse describes the decision for one side, and its form raises a question about timing, because punishment in this world is named from the standpoint of a decision delivered at the end.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who disbelieve"**: unbelief in Allah and His messengers, Muhammad and Jesus, is meant. The statement recounts God's response to Jews who rejected ʿĪsā and Christians who exceeded all bounds over him.

**"I will subject them to a severe punishment in this life and the Hereafter"**: heavy chastisement here takes the form of sword and capitation tax, jizyah, while the Hereafter throws them into Fire. Historically they were killed, captured, taxed and stripped of wealth and kingdoms, but later torment is worse and more severe.

**"and they will have no helpers"**: no helper can save them from Allah's punishment in either world, and no Waq, defender or protector, stands against Allah: "And they have no Waq (defender or protector) against Allah" (13:34).

### A judge's sentence counts from its award

The difficulty is resolved by an analogy. A judge says to a culprit, "Right now I am sending you to jail for a year. If you misbehave there, I shall make it two years." The two years are counted from the day the punishment is awarded. Given the misconduct, the whole sentence regulates itself and one more year becomes effective as an add-on. Here, punishment in the world has already been given. The punishment of the Hereafter will now be added, and the total will be executed on the Last Day. Worldly punishment will not serve as expiation for the punishment of the Hereafter.

Believers stand in the opposite condition. Struck by suffering in mortal life, they have their sins forgiven and their due punishment in the Hereafter lightened or warded off. The hint is given in the next verse, "Allah does not like the wrongdoers": believers are dear because of belief, and the beloved are treated with that mercy. Disbelievers are detested because of disbelief, and the detested do not receive such treatment.

The Jewish community's worldly decree is framed in a second way: the Jews made a false case against Jesus and took him to the Roman court in Palestine, which decreed crucifixion, but God saved him and Roman soldiers crucified another person who resembled Christ. As punishment for that crime, believers in Jesus Christ would forever, until Doomsday, hold dominion over the Jewish people. That was God's decree for this world. The Hereafter is another matter.

### In short

- Disbelievers in Muhammad and in Jesus are punished severely in this world by sword, capture and jizyah, and in the Hereafter by the Fire, with no protector (13:34).
- Worldly punishment does not expiate for them, as a judge's added year runs from sentencing. It is added to the punishment of the next world.
- For believers, worldly suffering works in reverse, forgiving sins and lightening what comes after.

<<<VERSE 3:57>>>
The other half of the decision balances the first: "And as for those who believe and do good, they will be rewarded in full. And Allah does not like the wrongdoers.”" Belief and righteous action meet complete payment in both worlds, and the closing statement returns the contrast to God's own attitude: wrongdoing is not loved, and wrongdoers will be chastised.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And as for those who believe and do good"**: faith covers Allah, Scripture and messengers Muhammad and Jesus. Good works are done sincerely between servants and their Lord. Prayer, charity, fasting and family ties exemplify them.

**"they will be rewarded in full"**: their wages are paid completely, read as yuwaffīhim, "He will pay them in full", or nuwaffīhim, "We will pay them in full". Victory and domination reward them now. Paradise and elevated grades reward them later. On Judgment Day Allah completes payment.

**"And Allah does not like the wrongdoers.”"**: Allah loves not the evildoers, meaning He will chastise them. The wrongdoers are the idolaters, with their transgressions and association of partners with Allah. The greatest wrong is to ascribe partners to Allah and disbelieve His messengers.

### The ascension pictured, and the descent in hadith

A report about the raising itself says that God sent Jesus a cloud which raised him up while his mother clutched at him in tears. He said to her, "Verily, the Resurrection shall bring us together again." This took place on Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Ordainment, in Jerusalem's Holy House, when he was thirty-three. Six further years of life remained for his mother.

Bukhārī and Muslim, the two Shaykhs, report a descent close to the Hour, with rule conducted by our Prophet Muhammad's Law. The cross will be broken, the swine slain, the false messiah killed and jizya imposed. In Muslim's hadith he will remain seven years. According to Abū Dāwūd al-Ṭayālisī, he will remain forty years, then die and have prayers performed over him. Forty may instead total all his earthly years, before ascension and after return.

The believers here are glossed as Messiah's followers prior to Prophet Muhammad's coming, whose arrival the Messiah himself confirmed. Faith in his truth led them to good actions, and their reward comes without reduction.

### In short

- Believers in Allah, Scripture, Muhammad and Jesus who do good are paid in full, here with victory and hereafter with Paradise.
- Allah does not love wrongdoers, whose greatest wrong is associating partners with Him and disbelieving His messengers.
- Reports put his raising on Laylat al-Qadr at age thirty-three, and his descent before the Hour with a rule by Muhammad's law.

The story of Jesus closes with its seal: "We recite ˹all˺ this to you ˹O Prophet˺ as one of the signs and ˹as˺ a wise reminder". What has been narrated about ʿĪsā, his birth and his life is presented as recitation from Allah, not reconstruction, and its function is dual: signs that prove, and a reminder that instructs.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We recite ˹all˺ this to you ˹O Prophet˺"**: what We narrate to Muhammad ﷺ conveys commands and prohibitions and comes through Gabriel. This account of Jesus clearly signals the truth of revelation, a perfect and falsehood-free message for God-conscious people.

**"as one of the signs"**: of the verses, the revelations of the Qur'an. Allah conveyed and revealed this to you from the Preserved Tablet, Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz, leaving it free of doubt. In the wording, "of verses" is a circumstantial element: min al-āyāt qualifies what is recited, its force carried by the demonstrative "This".

**"and ˹as˺ a wise reminder"**: a wise, clear remembrance, namely the Qur'an, enjoining what is lawful and what is unlawful. It is also said that it corroborates the Torah and the Gospel, and that it confirms what is in the Guarded Tablet.

### Knowledge not reached by effort

To Muhammad ﷺ the point is sharpened. This We recite to you in order to inform you of the meanings of what We have revealed. No exertion to attain knowledge, no parable-derived learning and no reasoning-based deduction delivered these meanings to you.

Surah Maryam seals the same account: "Such is 'Isa, son of Maryam. (It is) a statement of truth, about which they doubt (or dispute)." It then denies that Allah begets a son and repeats that decreeing a thing requires only "Be!" The account was not assembled by the Prophet. Allah conveyed the story of Jesus from the Preserved Tablet, untouched by doubt.

### In short

- The Jesus narrative ends as revelation recited to Muhammad ﷺ, not history he reconstructed.
- It is both sign and wise reminder: proof of the revelation's truth, and instruction about lawful and unlawful.
- The Preserved Tablet, Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz, is its source, leaving it untouched by doubt.
