<<<VERSE 2:214>>>
The earlier warning about hostility now recalls the suffering of past believers. "Do you think you will be admitted into Paradise without being tested like those before you?" The verse encourages patient effort for the Hereafter. It also reassures those who cry for help that Allah's promised relief is near.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do you think you will be admitted into Paradise without being tested like those before you?"** Earlier communities met trials that believers should be prepared to endure. One early report addresses ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān and his fellow believers after a trying experience. Their suffering is not a new feature of faith.

**"They were afflicted with suffering and adversity"** Al-baʾsāʾ is explained as poverty, and aḍ-ḍarrāʾ as ailments. Ibn ʿAbbās gives the latter sense, which includes illness and pain. The description also covers disasters, fear, calamities, hardship and hunger.

**"and were so ˹violently˺ shaken"** Fear of the enemy and repeated afflictions shook them as an earthquake would. Their trial reached an extreme intensity.

**"that ˹even˺ the Messenger and the believers with him cried out, “When will Allah's help come?”"** The cry asks for the promised help to arrive soon. It does not deny the promise or express doubt about Allah. The verb has the readings yaqūla and yaqūlu.

**"Indeed, Allah's help is ˹always˺ near"** Allah answers with the nearness of help, victory over the enemy and safety. Those who trust Him are reassured. The conjunction of hardship and ease also appears in (94:5-6).

### Why does faith demand sacrifice?

Clear proofs do not always overcome resistance. People may fear losing the comfortable life, interests and honour they have built. Accepting the truth requires willingness to surrender those protections when they conflict with it.

The road to Paradise can therefore appear dangerous. Its price is the self: habits, thought and action must be reshaped according to Allah's direction. Calling others to good and criticising wrongdoing also invites resistance. Teachers of truth need patience with their hearers' adverse reactions.

Earlier peoples and their fate are recalled in (43:8). The testing of those who claim belief is described in (29:1-3). These references locate the present trial within an established pattern.

### Does this exclude entry through mercy?

Other Qur'anic passages and sayings of the Prophet ﷺ show that sinners may enter Paradise through divine grace and forgiveness, without undergoing hardship. The explanation distinguishes degrees of trial rather than denying that mercy.

The lowest degree is resisting the desiring self and Satan, or defending the foundations of belief against hostile forces. Every Muslim can face that kind of effort. Beyond it are intermediate and higher degrees. The level of strenuous effort corresponds to the level of entry into Paradise. None is altogether untouched by struggle.

A saying of the Prophet ﷺ gives the prophets the hardest trials, followed by those closest to them.

### What did earlier believers endure?

The poverty reading is given by Ibn Masʿūd and Ibn ʿAbbās, along with Abū al-ʿĀliyah and Mujāhid. Other holders include Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Murrah al-Hamdānī, al-Ḥasan and Qatādah, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Rabīʿ, al-Suddī and Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān.

An authentic hadith describes Khabbāb ibn al-Aratt and others asking the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to pray for divine support. He recalled earlier believers sawn from the head downwards or torn between flesh and bone with iron combs. Even those ordeals did not make them abandon religion.

He swore that the religion would be established until someone could ride between Ṣanʿāʾ and Ḥaḍramawt without fearing anyone but Allah, apart from a wolf threatening his sheep. He reproached their haste.

The Companions experienced severe shaking at al-Aḥzāb. Their terror and conflicting thoughts are described in (33:10-12). When Heraclius asked Abū Sufyān about the fighting with the Prophet ﷺ, he heard that each side sometimes lost. He recognised the pattern of prophets being tested before their final victory.

### Why is asking for help not a failure of trust?

The prophets and their followers knew Allah had promised support. Its time and place were not specified. Their cry arose from distress and longing for deliverance, not doubt incompatible with their station.

Earnest supplication does not contradict tawakkul. Allah loves His servants' sincere prayers, and prophets and pious believers have the strongest claim to that devotion. Asking for early relief remains an expression of dependence on Him.

### What do the inward images teach?

The devotional reading contrasts wishful speech with the courage to face difficulty. The moth meets the candle through burning. The candle gives light by losing its own head. Joining, life and fulfilled desire are pictured through relinquishment.

Firdaws is a sweet garden approached through thorns. Muṣṭafā ﷺ describes the Garden as surrounded by disliked things. The comparison between those who know and those who do not appears in (39:9).

Another image places precious pearls beneath an ocean guarded by sharks and great fish. Two seekers arrive. One retreats before the dangers, exposing a wish without adequate resolve. The Sharīʿah rejects reaching religion through mere wishes and self-display. A poetic comparison makes his mismatch like a black crow seeking a white falcon.

The other seeker is so drawn by the pearl that fear no longer rules him. He plunges into the water. If felicity assists him, he wins the pearl. Otherwise the sharks take his life, and his name enters the register of "I do not care". His state is likened to that of two thousand lovers killed each month without a sigh. These images press the distinction between desire alone and costly commitment.

### In short

- Earlier believers faced severe trials, and present believers should not expect faith without effort.
- Degrees of trial differ, and the account preserves entry through Allah's mercy.
- The cry for help expresses longing for promised relief, not a denial of tawakkul.
- Allah's help is near, while the inward images warn against seeking the pearl through wishes alone.
