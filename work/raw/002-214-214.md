The previous verse showed how hostile the disbelievers have always been to prophets and believers, comforting Muslims hurt by mockery with the knowledge that there is nothing new in it. This verse goes further and recounts the enormous suffering that past prophets and believers endured at the hands of hostile disbelievers, so that the Muslims may bear hostility patiently, since real happiness is won only by preparing and working hard for the Hereafter. "Do you think you will be admitted into Paradise without being tested like those before you? They were afflicted with suffering and adversity and were so ˹violently˺ shaken that ˹even˺ the Messenger and the believers with him cried out, "When will Allah's help come?" Indeed, Allah's help is ˹always˺ near".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do you think you will be admitted into Paradise without being tested like those before you?"**. The verse was revealed after the Muslims had passed through a trying experience, and one early report addresses it to ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān and his fellow believers. Did you suppose that you would enter Paradise before being tested and tried as the nations before you were, without the like of what came upon the believers who passed away before you, that is, their way of life and its trials, so that you might endure as they did? Allah says, "So We destroyed those who were far mightier than these ˹Meccans˺. The examples of ˹their˺ predecessors have ˹already˺ been related" (43:8), and, "Do people think once they say, "We believe," that they will be left without being put to the test" (29:1-3). Truth supported by clear proofs is revealed to men and still they refuse it, because they see it as destructive of the dream world they have built, damaging to their interests and honour and threatening to the comfortable, secure lives they are used to. Yet to be faithful to Allah one must make sacrifices of exactly this kind. It is a road men are reluctant to tread because of its dangers and difficulties, but it is the only road to Paradise. The price of Paradise is one's own self, and the only way to earn it is total self-abnegation, which means changing the very foundation of one's life, thinking and acting on the pattern Allah has laid down, and giving up former habits. This cannot be done without exhorting others to do good and criticising their misdeeds, to which people in every age have been especially averse, so teachers of truth must be prepared for adverse reactions from their hearers. At first sight the verse seems to say that nobody enters Paradise without passing through hardship, whereas the Qur'an and the sayings of the Prophet ﷺ prove that many sinners will enter it purely through the grace, mercy and forgiveness of Allah, without undergoing any hardship. The answer is that hardship has different levels. The lowest is resisting one's own desiring self and Satan, or strengthening the foundations of one's belief against the forces working against the faith, and this degree is within the reach of every Muslim. Beyond it lie the middle and higher levels, and the degree of one's strenuous effort determines the degree of one's entry into Paradise. Thus nobody remains untried by effort and struggle and the hardship they bring. In a hadith the Prophet ﷺ said that the hardest trials among men are those of the prophets, and then of those nearest to them. In the devotional reading, the words of the good are not so easily spoken, and whoever is too timid to engage with fearful things will not reach his hopes. Joining is found in breaking off, life in death, and desires in not reaching one's desires: the moth reaches union with the candle when it burns, and the candle finds life when it loses its head. The high Firdaws is a sweet garden, but the road to it is hard, a rosebed full of thorns, and the Prophet ﷺ said that the Garden is surrounded by disliked things, lest the unqualified claim familiarity with it. "Are those who know equal to those who do not know?" (39:9). A simile for this rule is the ocean, made the resting place of precious pearls and night-brightening gems, with sharks and huge fish made the veil over them. Two men, drawn into the field of seeking by love of gems, come to the shore and see its dangers. One is frightened by the sharks, steps back from seeking and disowns his own words. He had a wish, but his manliness was incomplete, and he fancied that the work could be finished by wishing alone and the treasure reached without suffering. The Sharīʿah answers him that religion is not reached by wishfulness and self-adornment, and in the words of the poet, a black crow is a fine bird, but it wants to mate with a white falcon. The other man is the possessor of desire. Passion for the beauty of that pearl cleanses his mind of the ocean's terrors, and as that beauty discloses itself to him hour by hour he grows more entranced, and goes head first into the ocean. If felicity assists him the pearl falls into his grasp, and if not, the sharks take his life as booty and his name is written in the register of "I do not care", while the tongue of his state says that two thousand lovers like him are killed every month and not one lets out a sigh.

**"They were afflicted with suffering and adversity"**. Al-baʾsāʾ means poverty, according to Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Murrah al-Hamdānī, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Rabīʿ, al-Suddī and Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, and aḍ-ḍarrāʾ means ailments, according to Ibn ʿAbbās: illness, pain, disasters and hardships, fear, calamities and hunger.

**"and were so ˹violently˺ shaken"**. They were shaken as by an earthquake, for fear of the enemy, tested and put to a tremendous trial, convulsed by every kind of tribulation. An authentic hadith relates that Khabbāb ibn al-Aratt and others asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ why he did not invoke Allah's support for them and pray for them. He replied that among those before them a man would have a saw set on the parting of his head and be sawn down to his feet, or be combed with iron combs between his flesh and bone, and none of that would turn him from his religion. He swore by Allah that this religion would be completed until a rider travelled from Ṣanʿāʾ to Ḥaḍramawt fearing none but Allah, and the wolf for his sheep, and told them they were a hasty people. The Companions themselves experienced tremendous trials at the battle of al-Aḥzāb, of which Allah says, "when they came at you from east and west, when your eyes grew wild ˹in horror˺ and your hearts jumped into your throats, and you entertained ˹conflicting˺ thoughts about Allah" (33:10-12). When Heraclius asked Abū Sufyān whether he had fought the Prophet ﷺ and how the war between them had gone, and was told that sometimes they lost and sometimes he did, he observed that such is the case with prophets, who are tested but win the final victory.

**"that ˹even˺ the Messenger and the believers with him cried out, "When will Allah's help come?""**. The verb may be read yaqūla or yaqūlu. Not expecting to see any help because of the extreme hardship upon them, their Messenger and those who believed in him asked when the promised help would come, pleading for victory over their enemies and for deliverance from their hardships and trials, and wanting Allah's help to come as quickly as possible. That the prophets and their followers reached such a point was not out of doubt, which would be contrary to the dignity of their station. The cry was made against the background of Allah's promised help, whose time and place had been left undetermined, and so to use these words in distress expressed a desire for early help. Such a prayer is not against trust in Allah (tawakkul) or against the station of prophethood. On the contrary, Allah favours the earnest supplication of His servants, and who could deserve what Allah loves more than the prophets and the pious of the community?

**"Indeed, Allah's help is ˹always˺ near"**. Allah answered them that His help over their enemy was close at hand, through their safety, and it is near to those who have faith and trust in Him. As He says, "So, surely with hardship comes ease" (94:5-6): just as there is hardship, its equal measure of relief will soon arrive.

### In short

- Paradise is not entered without the trials that earlier believers endured (43:8, 29:1-3), though their degrees vary and the lowest, resisting the self and Satan, is within everyone's reach, while prophets bear the hardest.
- Baʾsāʾ is poverty and ḍarrāʾ illness (Ibn ʿAbbās and others), and earlier believers were sawn in half and combed with iron rather than leave their religion (Khabbāb), as the Companions were shaken at al-Aḥzāb (33:10-12).
- Even the Messenger and the believers cried out for Allah's help, not from doubt but from longing for the promised relief, a prayer that does not contradict tawakkul.
- Allah's help is near, and with every hardship comes ease (94:5-6), the truth Heraclius recognised when he said that prophets are tested but prevail.
- Inwardly, the Garden is surrounded by disliked things, and only the diver who braves the sharks reaches the pearl.
