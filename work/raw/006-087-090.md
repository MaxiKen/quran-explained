<<<VERSE 6:87>>>
And ˹We favoured˺ some of their forefathers, their descendants, and their brothers. We chose them and guided them to the Straight Path

The list of seventeen prophets closes with a sentence that widens it twice over.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And some of their forefathers, their descendants, and their brothers"** This clause is a supplement. It is attached either to the "all of them" that came before it, or to the word nuhan, that is, to Nuh himself. The forefathers are named in one account as Adam, Seth, Enoch, Nuh, Hud and Salih, and they were guided by being given prophethood and surrender to Allah. The descendants are the offspring of Yaqub, and the brothers are the brothers of Yusuf. Some of these prophets' ascendants and descendants were guided and chosen as well, and that is what the verse is stating.

**"some of"** The partitive is there for a reason. Not every one of them had offspring, and some of those who had offspring had children who disbelieved. The wording keeps the family tree honest at the same moment as it enlarges it.

**"We chose them and guided them to the Straight Path"** We selected them and made them firm on a straight path, that is, on the path of Allah's oneness and of obeying him. Guidance here is not a claim they made for themselves. It is something done to them, by a choosing that came first.

### Who the fathers and the brothers are

One account names the forefathers: Adam, Seth, Enoch, Nuh, Hud and Salih. Each of them was guided by being given prophethood and surrender to Allah, which is the same gift the previous verses recorded for their sons. The offspring are the children of Yaqub. The brothers are the brothers of Yusuf. None of these men is being praised for a genealogy here, and the point of naming them is that the favour of guidance ran through one household in more than one direction, forward and back, while remaining a gift in every case.

The verbs are given twice in the same breath. "We chose them" and "We guided them" are two separate acts, and the second follows the first rather than causing it. To be made firm on a straight path is the description of a state, and it is a state they were put into, not one they built. That is why the verse can end with a claim about guidance in general, which is what verse 88 then does.

### In short

- The end of the list pulls in the generations on either side of it, and it does so with a partitive that leaves room for unbelieving sons.
- The forefathers and the brothers are identified in the material, and the identification shows how loosely "progeny" is being used in these verses.
- Choice and guidance are both given, which is the same point verse 83 made about Ibrahim's argument.

<<<VERSE 6:88>>>
This is Allah’s guidance with which He guides whoever He wills of His servants. Had they associated others with Him ˹in worship˺, their ˹good˺ deeds would have been wasted

One sentence about how guidance is distributed, and one about what would undo it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is Allah's guidance with which He guides whoever He wills of His servants"** That straight path is the religion of Allah, and he guides by it whoever he wills of his servants, whoever is fit for it. What happened to these prophets happened by Allah's leave and because he directed them to guidance. It did not happen because they were gifted, or because they were descended from gifted men.

**"Had they associated others with Him, their good deeds would have been wasted"** Had these prophets set up anything beside him for worship, all that they did would have come to nothing. The sentence is about the greatest of the prophets, and it says that the one thing which would have undone them is available to them in principle. That is how seriously the danger of shirk is stated here, and how heavy the crime is made to look. It is also said of those who came after them, since the same words were revealed to the Prophet and to the messengers before him: if you join others in worship with Allah, your deeds will surely be in vain (39:65).

**"Had they"** The condition is not a rumour about them. The condition is read as a hypothesis, and the same manner of speech appears in three other verses. "Say: if the Most Merciful had a son, then I am the first of the worshippers" (43:81). "If We had intended to take a pastime, a wife or a son, We could surely have taken it from ourselves, if We were going to do that" (21:17). "If Allah had willed to take a son, he could have chosen whom he pleased out of those he creates. Glory be to him. He is Allah, the one, the compelling" (39:4). As Allah said in the second of the three, "If We had intended to take a pastime, a wife or a son, We could surely have taken it from ourselves." Nobody supposes that a pastime was ever in question there, and nobody should suppose that these prophets came close to shirk here. The point of the grammar is the size of the stake, not the likelihood of the loss.

### Why sincerity is tied to this

The other half of the verse's lesson is on the side of the one who delivers the call. The task of giving the call to the truth is taken up only by people who have become so selfless and so immersed in the work that they have no material expectation whatsoever from those they are addressing. A preacher who then stages protests, or runs a campaign of demands against the very persons to whom he is conveying the message of the Hereafter, risks having his exhortations treated as ridiculous by those same addressees, and in society at large his call can never be taken as a serious effort to convey the word of God. Deeds are wasted by joining partners with Allah, and they are wasted just as surely when the work is done for the sake of what comes back from the crowd.

### In short

- Guidance is distributed by will and fitness, which rules out inheritance as a cause.
- The hypothetical about shirk is the strongest warning in the passage, because it is aimed at prophets.
- Three other verses use the same "if" form for things that will never happen, and that is how the conditional here has to be read.
- The same verse is applied to the motives of whoever carries the call, since a paid or petitioning preacher discredits the message he brings.

<<<VERSE 6:89>>>
Those were the ones to whom We gave the Scripture, wisdom, and prophethood. But if these ˹pagans˺ disbelieve in this ˹message˺, then We have already entrusted it to a people who will never disbelieve in it

Three gifts are named, and then a refusal is answered before it is completed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those were the ones to whom We gave the Scripture, wisdom, and prophethood"** The Books of Allah, and al-Hukm, which means judgement, knowledge and understanding, and prophethood. The Scripture is what Gabriel brought down from heaven. These bounties were bestowed on them as a mercy for the servants and out of kindness to creation, and the list is not a reward for good behaviour in the ordinary sense.

**"But if these disbelieve in this message"** The ones in mind are the people of Makkah, as Ibn Abbas, Saʿid bin al-Musayyib, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah, As-Suddi and others said. What they are being offered is their own patriarchs' religion, and the disbelief contemplated is a disbelief in the prophethood, or in all three gifts at once.

**"then We have already entrusted it to a people who will never disbelieve in it"** We have kept apart for it a people who will not disbelieve in it. These are the Emigrants, the Muhājirūn, and the Helpers, the Ansār, and those who follow them in good until the Day of Reward. If the Quraysh reject these favours, and if the rest of the peoples of the earth reject them, Arab and non-Arab, illiterate and People of the Book, the trust simply passes to another people. They will not deny any of these favours. Not one letter of them will be refused. They will believe in them wholly, and that includes whatever is not plain to some of them. The prayer the comment closes with belongs to the same order: "O Allah, make us one of them and raise us in their company." The sense for the Prophet is comfort, and the commentary puts it as an address to him. If some of the people you call turn a deaf ear, and settle for denial after all they have been told about the teachings of the past prophets, then you do not have to worry. A great people has been appointed for this. They will answer your call, stand by your word, and take the mission on themselves. Disbelief and denial are beneath them.

### Where the new people came from

In Makkah a few individuals had accepted the faith on the strength of his preaching. As a community, the people rejected him. After that Allah inclined the people of Madinah to his call, and they accepted the faith as a community. It became possible for him to emigrate there and set up a centre of Islam. The aid came to him in its complete form. Nor is it confined to his own lifetime. Allah gives this aid to those who come after the Prophet and stand up to carry his call, and that is something he has always done according to the need of whoever carries the call. This is not a boast the new community is allowed to make about itself. The verse does not say: they are better than you. It says that the trust was entrusted, and that those who received it would not refuse it.

All of this is a matter of God's gift, and it reaches past the generation that met the Prophet. The people named in the verse include all the Muhajirin and all the Ansar of his blessed age. Then they include all the Muslims who keep coming until the end of time, until the Qiyamah arrives. The verse is a fond asset for exactly that class of people, since Allah named them on an occasion which called for praise from him.

### In short

- The three gifts are scripture, judgement and prophethood, and they are given as mercy rather than earned as wages.
- "These" is identified with the people of Makkah by a chain of early interpreters, and the refusal is treated as already answered.
- The trust passes to the Emigrants and the Helpers and to everyone who follows them in good, which makes the verse a promise about the future of the message rather than a threat.
- The verse is read as a comfort to the Prophet, and as a glad tiding that a community's acceptance can arrive from an unexpected direction.

<<<VERSE 6:90>>>
"These ˹prophets˺ were ˹rightly˺ guided by Allah, so follow their guidance. Say, “I ask no reward of you for this ˹Quran˺—it is a reminder to the whole world.”"

The last verse of the list, and it turns from the prophets to the man being told to imitate them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"These prophets were rightly guided by Allah"** They are the people of guidance, and they alone. The verse is addressed to the Prophet, and through him it is addressed to the Muslims, and what the people of Makkah are being told is that a people's ancestors do not become role models simply by being ancestors. They are not to be followed to the letter in every word and deed. That was the general assumption in Arabia, and in Makkah most of all. Before you follow anyone, the verse says, find out whether the person being followed is himself on the path of true guidance. So the enumeration of the prophets is what makes the command possible.

**"so follow their guidance"** Imitate them. The command to the Messenger certainly applies to his ummah, in whatever he legislates and commands them. A gloss adds that what is to be followed is their most noble character, such as patience, endurance, goodwill and contentment, and their way of affirming Allah's oneness. Recitation allows the verb to be read with a silent ending in pause or in continuation, and one variant drops it in continuous recitation.

A question follows at once, because the laws of the earlier prophets differed in their subsidiary details, and many injunctions came for this ummah that differ from theirs. So what can it mean to be told to follow them? The answer drawn from other verses and from narrations of hadith is that the command is not to follow every partial and subsidiary ruling. It is to hold to the basic principles of religion, which are tawhid, risalah and the Hereafter, and these never changed in the law of any prophet. From Adam to the last of the prophets, all of them held one belief and one way. Where a subsidiary injunction stayed unchanged, the practice is common to all of them, and where a new injunction came because of the demands of the time or the wisdom behind it, that new injunction was acted upon. For this reason it was the practice of the Prophet, until particular guidance reached him through revelation, to act in accordance with the way of the prophets before him.

### The prostration verse that proves the point

While explaining this verse al-Bukhari recorded that Mujahid asked Ibn Abbas whether there is a place of prostration in the surah of Sad. Ibn Abbas said yes. He recited from "And We bestowed upon him Ishaq and Yaqub" through to "so follow their guidance". Then he commented on that span, and his comment was that our Prophet was among them. Mujahid added, in a second narration, that the answer had been put more plainly: "Your Prophet was among those whose guidance we were commanded to follow." The argument runs on the strength of that span. The guidance of the prophets named in these verses includes the man who was commanded to follow it, and the list of the guided ends with him.

**"Say: I ask no reward of you for this, it is only a reminder to the whole world"** I do not ask you for a wage or a fee for delivering the Qur'an to you, or for Allah's oneness, or for anything else. It is an admonition to all the creatures, to jinn and human beings, so that they are reminded by it and turned from blindness into clarity, from misguidance into direction, and from disbelief into belief. Not accepting any return for teaching and preaching is the common legacy of all the prophets, and it is a powerful factor in making the conveying of the message effective. If you accept it, I gain nothing. If you refuse it, I lose nothing.

### What obedience returns

The verses before this one promised Ibrahim a return in this world as well as the next, and the commentary makes the bargain plain. He took total obedience to Allah as the sole purpose of his life and gave up everything he held dear. He left his father and his mother, his people and his country, all for the sake of Allah. To found and raise the House of Allah he abandoned the greeneries of Syria and travelled into the deserts of Makkah. When he was ordered to leave his wife and his infant son in the wilderness he obeyed at once, and when he was commanded to sacrifice his first-born and dearest son he showed his readiness to go to the limit of his choice and his will. For people obedient in that class and degree the real reward comes only after the Qiyamah, and in Jannah alone. But Allah blesses such people here too with ranks and riches that put the ranks and riches of the whole world to shame. He left his people, and became the patriarch of prophets. He left Syria and Iraq, and in return he was given Makkah, the honoured city. There he founded and raised the House of Allah. Two names of the place are explained by what it was made for: al-Balad al-Amin, meaning the trusted city, and Umm al-Qura, meaning the mother of towns. Makkah al-Mukarramah is the place itself, and its name says only that it is honoured. His own people tried to disgrace him, and he was made the supreme leader of the generations right through the Last Day, in a world where nations and religions, for all their differences, have gone on agreeing to revere him.

### In short

- The list ends in an instruction about whom to follow, and the criterion offered is guidance rather than descent.
- Following the prophets means the unchanging principles, not the details in which their laws differed.
- The refusal of a wage is put forward as the method of every prophet, and as the reason a call is taken seriously.
- Ibrahim's biography is read as a demonstration that total obedience is paid for in this world as well as the next.
