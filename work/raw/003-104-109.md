<<<VERSE 3:104>>>
Collective correction is organised: "Let there be a group among you who call ˹others˺ to goodness, encourage what is good, and forbid what is evil—it is they who will be successful". After taqwa and holding to the cord, the community must care affectionately for one another's correction through daʿwah. A dedicated segment is therefore required, although the duty itself reaches every Muslim according to ability.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Let there be a group among you who call ˹others˺ to goodness"**: Let there arise out of you a group of people inviting to all that is good, Islam, in the manner Allah commanded. This group's primary objective is its call towards good. A dedicated mission is therefore required to call people to the Qur'an and the Sunnah through word and deed, whenever people incline away from good or indulge in evils, according to ability. Ad-Dahhak said, "They are a special group of the Companions and a special group of those after them, that is those who perform Jihad and the scholars."

**"encourage what is good, and forbid what is evil"**: Al-Ma'ruf is what Islam orders and Al-Munkar what Islam forbids. Literally "recognised", Maʿruf covers all good promoted by prophets in their ages and known as good. Literally "non-recognised" or "alien," Munkar covers evils and disorders recognised as impermissible by the Holy Prophet's declaration.

**"it is they who will be successful"**: And it is they who are the successful, saved from Allah's wrath and punishment, with complete success in this world and the Afterlife.

### A collective obligation, and an individual duty

Min in minkum can be partitive. The commanded function is then fard kifaya, a collective duty, because an ignorant person cannot perform it competently. Min can also be extra, yielding the sense that the community as a whole should become a calling community. Either way, a segment of the Ummah must actually carry the task, while each member remains responsible in proportion to ability.

Muslim records Abu Hurayrah's report:

> "(Whoever among you witnesses an evil, let him change it with his hand. If he is unable, then let him change it with his tongue. If he is unable, then let him change it with his heart, and this is the weakest faith.)"

Another narration removes anything beyond that lowest point: "(There is no faith beyond that, not even the weight of a mustard seed.)" Imam Ahmad records Hudhayfah bin Al-Yaman's warning that a community will enjoin righteousness and forbid evil or face a punishment from Allah, after which supplication will not be accepted. At-Tirmidhi collected it and called it Hasan.

The mission also sits inside collective well-being. First comes self-correction through taqwa and holding Allah's cord, then comes correction of others through da'wah and positive propagation. The whole community is addressed elsewhere: "You are the best Ummah raised for mankind. You bid the Fair and forbid the Unfair" (3:110). Shaykh al-Islam Maulana Shabbir Ahmad ʿUthmani's warning was that the cord itself cannot break, but a hand can lose its hold on it. Surah al-ʿAsr joins the same duties: belief, good deeds, mutual invitation to truth and patience (103:2-3).

### Knowledge before exhortation, and ability as its limit

Competence is a precondition. A person unable to distinguish Fair from Unfair can invert the mission, forbidding good and urging evil, and can produce disorder rather than discipline. Learning Ma'ruf and Munkar as Shari'ah determines them must come first. Until prerequisites are acquired, standing up for this service is impermissible. The condemned contemporary scene is ignorant enthusiasm: sermons without Qur'an or Hadith knowledge, and common people arguing from hearsay about how things should be done. That method corrupts Muslim society and multiplies disputes.

Danger also limits obligation. The hand-tongue-heart sequence assumes change without formidable danger or unbearable harm. "Unable" with the tongue does not mean physically unable to move it. It means a strong apprehension that speaking truth will cost life or bring serious injury or loss. Such a person is not able in the relevant sense and is not sinful for abandoning verbal correction. Voluntarily staking life and property, bearing losses, and pressing on regardless is another class of action, reported from blessed Companions and Successors. It is high determination and great merit, not duty.

### What is necessary, what is commendable, and what is actually seen

The moral class of the act governs the call's class. Counseling toward obligatory action is itself necessary, counseling toward nawafil and other commendable acts remains commendable. The five salats generate necessary counsel for a non-performer, optional prayers generate commendable counsel.

Tone follows. Commendable acts always call for soft language and attitude. Even obligatory correction begins softly, with firmness reserved for outright rejection of the soft call. Modern practice often reverses that scale: commendable or indifferent matters draw vehement objection while obligatory duties are abandoned in silence.

Actual witnessing triggers the general duty. A person watching wine-drinking, stealing or rape must stop it to the best of his ability. Where sin is not before his eyes, inquiry, investigation and punishment belong to Islamic government. That is the force of the hadith's wording, "Whoever from among you sees that an evil is being committed."

### The dedicated group and its two calls

A higher level requires a dedicated group whose exclusive mandate is calling people to Faith and guidance. Word and deed carry them to Qur'an and Sunnah. Inclination away from good or indulgence in evil triggers pointed correction and prevention according to ability. Effective performance needs complete knowledge of the questions involved and conversance with methods that make the call effective in the Sunnah's light. If government does not shoulder this, Muslims must establish and operate such a group, because the Ummah's vital role depends on its existence.

Calling to good remains intrinsic even where no munkar is visible and no obligation is currently due. Between sunrise and Zawal (noon), no salah is prescribed, yet people can be exhorted toward salah when due. Ramadan may be far away, yet reminders of fasting's future obligation continue. Complacency is excluded.

The first sub-level calls non-Muslims to khayr, Islam. Every Muslim shares it generally and the dedicated group particularly, by words and acts to all peoples of the world. Authority under Islam is pictured in (22:41): establish obedience to Allah, express it outwardly in salah, build finance on zakah principles, and make bidding the Fair and forbidding the Unfair the purpose of life. Blind imitation of non-Muslim nations would then lose its ills, as a unified, outward-looking community finds disunity dissolved by its teaching objective. In a hadith, the Prophet ﷺ recited this verse and said: "This special group is the group of the noble Companions" (Ibn Jarir). Each blessed soul considered himself personally charged with calling people to good.

Muslims themselves form the second audience. All Muslims generally, and the special group particularly, owe tabligh among Muslims. Its general form gives open education in necessary injunctions and morals. Its selective form produces experts in Qur'an and Sunnah sciences, as (9:122) directs: a group from every section goes forth to acquire perfect understanding of the Faith and warn their people on return, that they may be cautious.

### Recognised, not disputed

The choice of ma'ruf and munkar rather than wajib ("what is obligatory") and ma'asi ("sins") carries a principle. Bidding and forbidding should address acts recognised by the entire Muslim Ummah as fair or unfair without interpretive difference. Rules deduced by capable jurists through ijtihad are open to different interpretations and should not become battlegrounds for exhortation. A Hanafi act judged fair should not draw Shafi'i reproach over the opposite interpretation, and vice versa. Neglect of that principle makes Muslims fight over secondary interpretive issues, calling the fight piety, while unanimously recognised sins receive less attention and often go unchecked.

The Companions are the first instance. Their objective, calling toward good and curbing bad, carried them in a very brief period across the world of their time. Byzantine and Persian power centres could not stop them from teaching morality and purity and spreading righteousness and Godliness.

Elders and public share the burden. Educated people should work strenuously at enjoining goodness and forbidding wrong, concerned with the state of the people, the public must listen, respect, follow and stop where told to stop, surrendering to religious reformers. Society reforms in an atmosphere of listening and obeying, with success in both worlds.

### Those who stand for God in God

An allusion follows to a people made to stand by the Real itself. Blame does not turn them, and created causes do not replace God as reliance. Approval of God organizes action, counsel and invitation, while shortcomings of their own souls remain visible to them. Their friendship is for the Patron, their sight is coloured by Self-disclosure, and they read artisanry backward from the Artisan. Presence brands them as elect servants, safe by the king's brand. Union burns and love kills, even to spilled blood and destroyed property, because hearts are held and spirits embraced. "When someone is destroyed in God, God takes his place."

### In short

- A group must call to goodness, enjoin Al-Ma'ruf and forbid Al-Munkar, the duty is collective, fard kifaya, and individual according to ability, hand, tongue then heart.
- Exhortation requires knowledge and stops short of serious harm: the ignorant must learn first, and terror of death or grave loss excuses the tongue but not the heart.
- The recognised, not ijtihad's disputed ground, is the field of correction, and the Companions who embodied the call are named as its first group (22:41, 9:122, 3:110).

<<<VERSE 3:105>>>
The mission carries its own warning: "And do not be like those who split ˹into sects˺ and differed after clear proofs had come to them. It is they who will suffer a tremendous punishment". This verse is a complement of verse 103. There, Muslims were asked to seek unity by attaching themselves to Allah's commands, individually and collectively, so an entire community acts like one body, one person, one entity. Then comes the perpetual mission of da'wah, which nurtures and strengthens that unity. Now past communities destroyed by dissension are held up as the lesson.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And do not be like those who split ˹into sects˺ and differed after clear proofs had come to them"**: Do not scatter in religion as Jews and Christians did after clear proofs had come to them, proofs from their Scriptures of Islam. Believers must not imitate People of the Scripture who fragmented into groups and divisions over faith and way of life after clear signs had reached them from Allah.

**"It is they who will suffer a tremendous punishment"**: It is they for whom there is an awful torment, an awful doom greater than anything one can imagine. Those there awaits a mighty chastisement. Such people will have a great punishment from Allah.

### The division this verse censures

The censured divisiveness grows out of arrogant and egotistic self-assertiveness. It can infect fundamentals or subsidiaries, provided a selfish motive supplies the force. "After clear signs had come to them" points to this: all fundamentals are dear, and even some subsidiaries are so clear that they allow no margin of disagreement, unless of course there be a selfish motive behind it.

The Ummah is forbidden to reproduce earlier nations' division and discord. They, too, abandoned enjoining righteousness and forbidding evil despite possessing proof of its necessity.

Earlier peoples are held up as a warning. The believers must not replay their splitting and quarrelling. Those communities had clear evidence, yet they still dropped the work of calling to good and restraining wrong.

### Ijtihad is not this division

Not every subsidiary issue is definite or clear. Some have no clear textual support in Qur'an and Sunnah and are deduced by analogy. Some rest on texts open to more than one interpretation. Difference there is not the division of this verse. Al-Bukhari and Muslim narrate from ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAs that a person who performs Ijtihad (competent inquiry within the framework of the Shari'ah to resolve a religious issue) and reaches the correct ruling receives a twofold reward, a mistaken Ijtihad still receives one reward.

Utmost effort can therefore make even erroneous ijtihad rewardable and unblameworthy. The Companions' and great Imams' differences have absolutely no connection with this verse. Qasim ibn Muhammad and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbdul-Aziz called Companions' difference a source of mercy and ease, as reported by al-Baihaqi and al-Mudkhal.

Ijtihad remains Shari'ah-bound, there is no ijtihad outside its framework. One view alone is true in Allah's sight, while judgment between interpretations belongs to Him alone. Each scholar may say his own choice is closest to Qur'an and Sunnah in his understanding, while admitting his possible error and the other's possible correctness. Leading Imams of Fiqh agree on that discipline. No interpretive side is therefore munkar in itself, and objecting to what is not Unfair would itself be unfair. Contemporary abuse and verbal attacks over interpretations violate that rule and produce internecine confrontation.

Handling matters more than the difference itself. When tertiary debates are nursed as if they were the basis of Muslim faith, confrontation and abuse certainly violate "and be not divided". No precedent exists among the early scholars, the best of the community, for blame on matters of interpretation in this manner. Shafi'i and other Imams make recitation of Surah al-Fatihah obligatory behind an imam, so omitting it voids the salah. Abu Hanifah prohibits a follower from reciting his own Surah al-Fatihah behind the imam, so Hanafis do not recite. Yet Shafi'i followers nowhere treated Hanafis as deserters of salah's obligation whose prayers were incomplete, nor attacked them as perpetrators of forbidden evil.

Imam ibn ʿAbd al-Barr describes the early attitude: fatwa-givers have always issued fatwas, one ruling a matter lawful, the other ruling it unlawful, without either side treating the other as doomed to perdition (Jami' Bayan al-ʿIlm, p. 80).

Qualified ijtihad also has strict entry rules. It addresses questions with no categorical decision in Qur'an and Sunnah, texts that are unclear and susceptible to more than one interpretation, or apparent contradictions among Qur'anic verses and hadith narrations. Only a person with all related Islamic sciences at the highest level, comprehensive Arabic and comprehensive knowledge of Companions' and Successors' sayings can undertake it. Dabbling in settled questions with contrary opinions disqualifies an opinion as ijtihad. So does failure to meet its conditions. Modern educated self-expression on settled matters is ugly ignorance.

### Seventy-two sects, seventy-three

Imam Ahmad records Abu 'Amir 'Abdullah bin Luhay's Hajj with Mu'awiyah bin Abi Sufyan. At Makkah, after Zuhr prayer, Mu'awiyah quoted the Messenger of Allah ﷺ:

> "(The People of the Two Scriptures divided into seventy-two sects. This Ummah will divide into seventy-three sects, all in the Fire except one, that is, the Jama'ah. Some of my Ummah will be guided by desire, like one who is infected by rabies; no vein or joint will be saved from these desires.)"

Mu'awiyah added: "By Allah, O Arabs! If you do not adhere to what came to you from your Prophet ﷺ then other people are even more prone not to adhere to it." Abu Dawud recorded similarly through Ahmad bin Hanbal and Muhammad bin Yahya.

### Dispersion and disagreement are not the same thing

Dispersion and disagreement differ. One breaks togetherness, the other agreement; one scatters the folk of the Tariqah, the other the folk of the Shariah. The core dispersion is desire split between servant and Real. Togetherness restores one concern, and God spares such a person the concerns of this world and the next. Looking only at creatures and secondary causes keeps suffering and creaturely antagonism alive; looking at the Real locates work and decree at one door.

Principles and branches carry opposite danger. Divergence over principles puts one road east and one west: the farther you go, the farther you fall behind (6:153). Branch roads can be nearer or farther and still arrive at one destination. That kind of disagreement is mercy itself: "Disagreement in my community is a mercy." It prevents the religion's work from becoming narrow and difficult, because "He placed no hardship upon you in..." (22:78) and "God desires for you ease and does..." (2:185).

### In short

- Division after clear proofs, driven by desire and ego, is what is forbidden, ijtihad within the Shari'ah, rewarded even when mistaken, is another matter.
- No school may treat the other's ijtihad as munkar: Hanafi and Shafi'i read the Fātiḥah question oppositely without attacking one another's prayers.
- Seventy-two sects became seventy-three as a warning, and branch disagreement remains mercy when the goal and the straight path are shared (6:153, 22:78, 2:185).

<<<VERSE 3:106>>>
Division has a complexion on the last Day: "On that Day some faces will be bright while others gloomy. To the gloomy-faced it will be said, “Did you disbelieve after having believed? So taste the punishment for your disbelief.”" On the Day of Resurrection, followers of the Sunnah and the Jama'ah radiate whiteness, and followers of Bid'ah, innovation, and division are darkened.

### Phrase by phrase

**"On that Day some faces will be bright while others gloomy"**: Some faces will become white and some black on Resurrection Day. The great punishment arrives then: faithful faces whiten, lighting up with joy and happiness; deniers' faces blacken, darkened with sadness and despair.

**"To the gloomy-faced it will be said, “Did you disbelieve after having believed?"**: The rebuke reaches back to the covenant day. They once affirmed faith, then broke their pledge to worship Allah alone. The question exposes that reversal before sentence is passed.

**"So taste the punishment for your disbelief.”"**: Hell's keepers announce the consequence. What is tasted now corresponds to the denial practised before.

Al-Hasan Al-Basri said, "They are the hypocrites." And this description befits every disbeliever.

### Brightness and darkness across the Qur'an

The same imagery recurs elsewhere. Liars against Allah are seen with blackened faces (39:60). Other passages set shining, laughing faces against dusty, darkness-covered ones (80:38-41), and radiant faces looking toward their Lord (75:22).

Several Arabic words carry this field of meaning, among them terms for whiteness, blackness, dust, darkness and radiance. For most commentators, whiteness is faith made visible. Believers shine, fresh and smiling under reward. Blackness is unbelief made visible, grief on the face deepened further by sin.

### Who these people are

The groups are identified in more than one way. Ibn ʿAbbas links brightness to adherence to the Sunnah and darkness to Bid'ah, religious innovation foreign to what Allah and His Messenger intended. ʿAta' sees the Muhajirin and Ansar on one side and Bani Qurayzah and Bani Nadir on the other.

Imam Tirmidhi narrates from Sayyidna Abu Umamah that the Khawarij have the dark faces and those they kill the bright. Abu ʿIsa At-Tirmidhi records Abu Ghalib's scene of Khawarij heads hanging in Damascus streets. Abu Umamah said:

> "The Dogs of the Fire and the worst dead people under the cover of the sky. The best dead men are those whom these have killed."

He recited this verse through its end. Asked whether he heard it from the Messenger, he said he would not narrate it had he heard it only once, twice, thrice, four times, or seven times. At-Tirmidhi said, "This Hadith is Hasan." Ibn Majah and Ahmad recorded similarly.

ʿIkrimah points to People of the Book who had affirmed the coming Prophet beforehand yet rejected and falsified him when he came. These readings converge rather than clash. In summary, sincere Muslims shine, while those who altered religion, apostatised or hid hypocrisy are darkened.

### Why brightness is named first

Word order carries an argument. Verse 106 names brightness (bayad) before darkness (sawad), but verse 107 begins with dark faces before bright ones, although the original statement would lead one to expect brightness again. The reversal points to creation's main purpose: blessing creation with mercy, not punishment. Bright faces deserve mercy first. Dark and anguished faces arrive later as punishment's objects. "They rest in Allah's mercy" at the close stresses the same mercy. Bright faces are identified at the beginning and again at the end, very affectionately, with the dark between them, suggesting that human beings were created to flourish under Allah's mercy rather than exhibit divine punishment.

Ibn ʿAbbas says rahmah, "mercy", in the verse means Jannah, Paradise. The naming works because worship does not create an inherent right to Paradise. Even devotion's ability is itself Allah's gift, so Paradise is enjoyed solely through His mercy.

### In short

- Bright faces are faith's light on followers of the Sunnah and community, dark faces are disbelief, innovation and hypocrisy, the Khawarij named among them (39:60, 80:38-41, 75:22).
- Brightness is mentioned before darkness because mercy, not punishment, is creation's purpose, and Paradise itself is called mercy.
- A white heart today becomes a white face then, and attachment to anything other than God darkens both.

<<<VERSE 3:107>>>
The bright faces receive their address: "As for the bright-faced, they will be in Allah’s mercy, where they will remain forever". Whitened faces are in God's mercy, Paradise, dwelling for eternity without desiring removal. They never die or leave it. These are the believers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for the bright-faced, they will be in Allah’s mercy"**: God's mercy is Jannah, Paradise. Entry comes by mercy alone, since worship itself, and the ability to worship, are His gifts and create no inherent right against Him.

**"where they will remain forever"**: the mercy believers rest in will not be temporary. It will be forever and eternal, a blessing never taken away or reduced in their case. Those whose faces are whitened remain in Paradise, eternally blessed. Eternal duration is not expressly stated for the dark-faced in this verse's counterpart.

### In short

- The bright-faced remain in Allah's mercy, Paradise, forever, with no removal or reduction.
- Paradise is entered by mercy alone, because even the ability to worship is a gift.

<<<VERSE 3:108>>>
The narrative is sealed before its ground is stated: "These are Allah’s revelations We recite to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth. And Allah desires no injustice to ˹His˺ creation". They are Allah's verses, proofs and signs, revealed to Muhammad in truth to make known this world and the Hereafter's true reality.

### Phrase by phrase

**"These are Allah’s revelations We recite to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth"**: These are the revelations of Allah, the Qur'an, sent down with Gabriel, recited to Muhammad in truth to show what is true and what is an error. These verses contain Allah's promises and threats, true in information and just in laws.

**"And Allah desires no injustice to ˹His˺ creation"**: Allah never treats creatures unjustly. He is the Just Ruler, able to do everything and knowing everything, so He has no need to treat any creature with injustice. God desires not any injustice for the worlds (punishing them for no crime), nor is He unjust to jinn or human beings. No one receives a punishment unless he earned it through his own bad actions.

### Sinning man earns his own punishment

"Taste the punishment because you have been sinning" (3:106) locates the origin. Paradise's blessings and Hell's hardships are simply deeds in changed form. Verse 108 says Allah wants no injustice for anyone in the worlds, meaning no intention to be unjust to creation. Reward and punishment express perfect divine wisdom and mercy in justice.

A continuous address of love, widād, also frames it. Injustice cannot be ascribed to Him in determining events, taqdīr, and existence, wujūd, because all created beings are His creation and what has been determined for them is His wise determination, ḥukm.

### In short

- The recited verses are true in their information and just in their laws, sent with Gabriel.
- No punishment is unmerited: hell and Paradise are deeds in changed form, and injustice cannot be ascribed to the Just Ruler.

<<<VERSE 3:109>>>
Everything returns to its owner: "To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And to Allah ˹all˺ matters will be returned ˹for judgment˺". All are His servants and property. Decision about this life and the Hereafter is His, and Supreme Authority in both is His.

### Phrase by phrase

**"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth"**: created beings and marvellous things all belong to Him. Heaven and earth are His as possessions, creatures and servants, in creation and decree, and they are all His property.

**"And to Allah ˹all˺ matters will be returned ˹for judgment˺"**: All matters go back (for decision) to Allah in the Hereafter. Everyone will be rewarded according to what they have done, and His is the Supreme Authority in this life and the Hereafter. Belonging is by possession, milk, return is by determination, ḥukm.

### In short

- Heaven and earth are Allah's possessions, creatures and servants, by creation and decree.
- All matters return to Him for decision in the Hereafter, and everyone is rewarded according to what they have done.
