<<<VERSE 6:17>>>
"If Allah touches you with harm, none can undo it except Him. And if He touches you with a blessing, He is Most Capable of everything." This is an article of faith before it is a comfort. The verse says that Allah, in reality, is the one Master and Dispenser of gain and loss. No created being can truly bring the smallest benefit to anyone or cause the smallest harm. What people see passing between one person and another, a favour here, an injury there, is appearance. Looked at in the full flash of what is real, it is no more than a curtain that is about to vanish.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If Allah touches you with harm"** means hardship and poverty, an illness, a trial, a loss. **"none can undo it except Him"** means none can lift it off you. **"And if He touches you with a blessing"** means good fortune, wealth, health, plenty, **"He is Most Capable of everything"** means that as He is able to give these things, none can take them away, and He has power over all of it, including the touching and the lifting. The address is to every son of Adam. Nobody can keep Allah's grace back, and nothing can overpower Him or stop Him from giving whatever He chooses to give.

The same claim appears in another verse, quoted here as its parallel: whatever mercy Allah opens for people, there is no one to hold it back, and whatever He holds back, there is no one to send it after that (35:2). The Ṣaḥīḥ carries this prayer, which the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would often say:

> "O Allah, there is no one to withhold what You have given, and no one to give what You have withheld, and the standing of a man of standing is of no use to him against You."

### What that belief did to people

This is one of the revolutionary beliefs of Islam. It taught Muslims to drop dependence on created things and rely on the Creator alone. Out of it came an unprecedented company of people who won hearts without trying: with meager means and constant hunger, they weighed more than a whole world, because they would not bow their heads before anyone. A Persian poet caught the same idea in a couplet about love, where the cause is obvious and the blame is still pushed onto something else:

> Musk on your hair is the work of your own tresses, yet your lovers find it convenient to blame the deer of China.

Alas, the commentary continues, in spite of this plain verse and the long teaching of the Prophet ﷺ, this ummah has gone off course in exactly this matter, handing out to created beings what belongs to Allah alone. Today a very large number of Muslims, instead of calling on Allah and asking Him in the hour of distress, cry out for help under all sorts of names, and the name of Allah does not seem to occur to them. To pray to Allah by way of the spiritual standing of prophets and of men of Allah is a different thing, and it is allowed, with evidence for it inside the Prophet's ﷺ own teaching. But to call directly on a created being, or to pray to one, to have a need met or a problem solved, is open rebellion against this command in the Qur'an.

### What the Prophet ﷺ told a young man

Under his comments on this verse, al-Baghawī reports from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās an hour on a ride. The Prophet ﷺ mounted, and asked the boy to sit behind him. After some distance he turned to him and said: young man. Here I am, he answered, at your service, O Messenger of Allah. Is there anything I can do? The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Remember Allah, and Allah will remember you. If you remember Allah you will find Him in front of you in every situation. Know Allah when you are at ease and comfortable, and He will know you when trouble comes. When you ask, ask only Allah. When you need help, seek help only from Allah. Whatever is going to happen in this world has already been written by the writer of destiny. If every created being were to combine to bring you a benefit for which Allah has kept no share, they would not be able to do it. And if they all came together to inflict a harm that is not in your lot, they would never be capable of it. If you are confident you can hold to patience, then do it by all means. If you do not have the strength for it, then still be patient, because there is great good and barakah in being patient with what goes against your temperament. And understand this clearly: the help of Allah comes with patience, ease comes with hardship, and prosperity comes with adversity."

The same hadith appears in at-Tirmidhī and in the Musnad of Aḥmad with sound chains of authority.

### Why no one else can act

The universe is not a heap of separate objects. Its parts are so connected that for anything at all to happen, the whole of it has to be coordinated together, and nobody controls that. Even the smallest event arrives only when countless factors in the universe come together to start it going, and God alone is the one who controls those factors. In the middle of all of them, a human being owns only a will. So blessings and afflictions alike happen by the direct orders of God, and a man who thinks he can rescue one person or finish another is talking like a fool. It is just as empty to live in fear of anybody except God, or to build your hopes on anyone but Him.

The inner reading goes one step further and takes that single origin seriously. Just as Allah is one and without partner in creating harm, He is one and without equal in pushing harm away. If everyone in the world gathered, jinn and mankind joining hands, to produce a pain that is not there, they would not manage it. And if they gathered to remove a pain that is there, without Allah willing it, they would find no way in. So know this: the wellspring of pain and of remedy is one. Blessing and trial come out of one place. Faith and unbelief rise from one place. Inside the circle of togetherness they all have one color, and only at the way stations of dispersion do they look multicolored. A poet of this way put the double face in verse: on your two cheeks sit both unbelief and faith, and on your two lips sit both pain and its cure.

### In short

- Real agency belongs to Allah alone: He removes harm and He gives blessing, and nobody can undo either.
- The belief is meant to wean people off dependence on created things, and it has a long history of producing unbowable heads.
- Asking Allah through the standing of His prophets and saints is one matter. Calling on a creature directly is another, and that is what the verse forbids.
- The hadith to Ibn ʿAbbās makes the same point practical: remember Allah in ease, ask Him alone, and accept what was never written for you.
- No event in the universe happens without its whole system agreeing, and that system has only one controller.

<<<VERSE 6:18>>>
"He reigns supreme over His creation. And He is the All-Wise, All-Aware." Verse 17 said that harm and blessing are in one hand. Verse 18 gives the reason: He is dominant over His servants, and wisdom and awareness go with that dominance. His mastery over them is absolute, and everyone stays under His power and control all the time.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He reigns supreme over His creation"** translates al-Qāhir, the Dominant, the Vanquisher, the Omnipotent, the All-Conquering. He is the Irresistible, above His servants, in the way that befits Him. To Him necks are bent, tyrants are humbled before Him, and He has complete control over everything. Nothing is impossible for Him, and nothing can overpower or defeat Him. The creatures bow before Allah and are small before His grace, his honor, his pride, his greatness, his highness and his ability over all things, because they all sit under his irresistible decision and power. He breaks the pleasures of His servants, and in essence and in attributes He is above all the travelers on the road.

**"And He is the All-Wise"** means wise in all He does, wise in His creation, wise in His command and decree, in His handling of matters and in the laws He sets. **"All-Aware"** means fully informed about everything: He places each thing where it belongs, and He gives favor and favors whoever deserves it. He knows people's innermost thoughts as well as their outward actions, and nothing about His creatures or their deeds is hidden from Him. He perceives what cannot be perceived, and He sees the unseen face to face.

### Why nobody gets everything they want

The two names at the end of verse 18 are doing a piece of work together. Al-Qāhir points to Allah's perfect power, and al-Ḥakīm to knowledge that leaves nothing out. Perfection in power and perfection in knowledge belong to Him and to nobody else, and that is why no human being, however great, comes out successful at everything he tries, or has every wish granted, whether he is a prophet close to Allah or the most powerful ruler on earth.

One of the great sayings of this path puts the same teaching as an exchange rate: one breath with the Real, in return for both worlds, is cheap. A hundred thousand spirits would be a fair price for one sight of Him, and it costs nothing. One moment of intimacy with Him is sweeter than the spirit itself. Anyone killed in this work is standing between fire and joy, and anyone who does not know this work is locked in the prison of his own mortal nature. And the prayer that belongs to it: Allah, seeing You is near, and at that nearness the work is too subtle for me. Allah, everyone is standing on something, and I do not know what I am standing on. I fear only the moment when what I am appears. Since the one who is in remembrance is happy with You, why should the one who is happy with You lament? When a person holds a sweetheart like You in his arms, how would he even notice the noise of the Resurrection?

### In short

- "Reigns supreme" means total dominance, with every creature's neck bent, and it arrives as the reason for what verse 17 claimed.
- "All-Wise" and "All-Aware" put power and knowledge together, and only Allah has both in full.
- Because His wisdom decides the outcome, no one, prophet or king, gets everything he tries for.
- The inner reading makes His being "above His servants" a rank rather than a place, and the traveler's only profit is one moment with Him.

<<<VERSE 6:19>>>
"Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, 'Who is the best witness?' Say, 'Allah is! He is a Witness between me and you. And this Quran has been revealed to me so that, with it, I may warn you and whoever it reaches.'" This verse answers a demand for credentials, and it answers it in a way that changes the question. Somebody had been asked to testify, and Allah offers Himself as the witness instead, then hands the same testimony to a Book that is still traveling. The clause "and whoever it reaches" extends the warning past the people standing in front of the Prophet ﷺ to everyone the Qur'an arrives at, and that single phrase is what makes the surah's argument a live case in every later generation, including this one.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, 'Who is the best witness?'"** was revealed out of an occasion reported widely. A deputation of the people of Makkah came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: you claim to be a Messenger of Allah, so who is your witness for that? We have not yet found a man to attest to its truth, and we tried hard to confirm it from the Jews and the Christians. On another telling of the demand it runs: produce for us someone to testify to the truth of your prophethood, since the People of the Scripture have denied you. The question, then, is: what thing carries the most weight in testimony, and what is fairer and more pleasing as evidence?

**"Say, 'Allah is!'"** Say it even if they will not. There is no other answer available, and no witness more truthful than the Real's own testimony to what He testified at the very first. He is the witness between us that I am His Messenger and that this Qur'an is His speech. He knows what I brought to you and how you are answering me, and the proof He gave is the miracles and signs He made appear to confirm the truth of this Prophet's ﷺ claim. "God bears witness that there is no god but He" (3:18) is the verse this testimony rests on. His is the witness that the Real is the Real, given on the first day at the covenant that has no beginning, with words that are true and speech that is pure, reporting a single existence, a self-sufficient being, majesty without end, beauty that lasts, an essence that never breaks off, and attributes that stand on their own.

**"And this Quran has been revealed to me so that, with it, I may warn you and whoever it reaches"** widens the audience to everybody, human or jinn, that the Qur'an arrives at. Gabriel brought this Book down to him, and the Qur'an is inspired to the Prophet ﷺ so that he may make people fear. Whoever it reaches has a warner, whoever it reaches has been warned. The same point comes out in another hadith: to whoever the Qur'an reaches, I am his warner. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr put it in a sentence of his own: whoever the Qur'an has reached is like someone who visited Muḥammad ﷺ in person. Because the warning travels by the Book and not by the man, the Prophet ﷺ pressed his Companions hard to pass on what they had, telling people what he told them even if it was one verse. And ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd reports him saying: may Allah keep hale and hearty a person who heard a saying of mine, kept it in memory and then delivered it to my ummah. That matters, because a man may not himself grasp the sense of what he has heard, while someone in a later time, who got it from him, understands it far better.

The obligation of this verse, then, falls on the ummah as well as on the individuals in it, and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas drew the duty from it plainly: whoever follows the Messenger of Allah ﷺ ought to call people to what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ called them to, and to warn them against what he warned them against. Since the Book reaches people everywhere, those of the sects that refuse it have the Fire as the place they are promised (11:17).

**"Do you ˹pagans˺ testify that there are other gods besides Allah? Say, 'I will never testify ˹to this˺!'"** The question is not really a question. It is a disavowal. Do you, O people of Makkah, in sober fact bear witness that there are gods besides Allah, meaning the idols, which you call the daughters of Allah? And if they do testify, do not testify along with them (6:150). I bear no such witness with you, and what you acknowledge is false. **"And say, 'There is only One God. And I totally reject whatever ˹idols˺ you associate with Him.'"** He is one God, the only one worthy of worship, with no partner, no sharer and no associate in His unspoiled divinity, and I am innocent of what you set up beside Him.

### Why the Book is the arbiter

In the standing quarrel between the people of the truth and the people of falsehood, the one factor that decides is the Book of God. Nobody knows the full facts, and nobody has real authority, except God. So God, who is the only Being able to end the dispute, gave the parties an arbiter, and that arbiter is the Qur'an. That leaves one course. A person who is not sure the Qur'an is true should investigate until he is satisfied that it is God's Book. Once he knows that, he has to accept whatever it settles, and the man who will not agree with its decision runs the risk of being forced onto its verdict in the Hereafter, after degradation and punishment. The Book was revealed so that people learn, before the time of judgement arrives, what is waiting for them, and it tells in advance how God will treat people in the world that lasts. Whoever is given the job of delivering the message is clear of the responsibility once he has conveyed it fully, and whoever receives it is clear in God's eyes only when he accepts it and changes accordingly. The preacher's part ends at preaching. The hearer's part ends at doing what the religion asks.

The same verse has been read as a lesson in how to testify at all. Abū ʿAbdallāh al-Qurashī read it as Allah teaching His servants, and as a straight lead for seekers. In His own gentleness Allah is teaching the servants how to bear witness, in their measure, to His oneness and His being alone, the way He bears witness about Himself in a way that befits Him. Leave off the Ṭarīqah of resistance, the path of pushing back, or you will fall into the pit like Iblīs, abandoned. Others said about this that Allah bears witness to His oneness, His being one and His self-sufficiency, while everyone else, the angels and the people of knowledge among them, bears witness by agreeing that what Allah testified about Himself is true. He Himself testified to His lordship, His greatness and His oneness, because nobody else was fit to testify for Him. Created beings cannot reach the inmost core of His majesty and His tremendousness, and all their witnessing amounts to is agreeing that His witnessing is true.

Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad, Abū ʿAbdallāh, said that people's witnessing stands on four pillars: following the commands, keeping away from what is prohibited, being content, and approving. It is also said that witnessing comes in three kinds. The witnessing of ordinary people is coming out of associationism, the tawḥīd of partners. The witnessing of the elect is entering into contemplation. The witnessing of the elect of the elect is a breeze of companionship blowing from the direction of nearness, for the sake of union. Someone who purifies himself sees everything as coming from Him. Someone who knows Him sees everything in Him. Someone who voices tawḥīd sees everything as He. Every being that has a name is a loan. Real existence is His, and the rest are under suspicion. Say "Allah", and then leave them (6:91). All is He, and that is the end of it, so how could anyone appear alongside Him?

### In short

- The demand for a witness was answered with the greatest possible one, and the testimony cited for it is Allah's own, in 3:18.
- "Whoever it reaches" makes the Qur'an itself the warner for every generation after, and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said that reaching the Book is like meeting the Prophet ﷺ.
- Conveying one verse counts, and the messenger's duty ends with delivery while the hearer's ends with obedience.
- To testify that other gods exist is a claim the Prophet ﷺ refuses to join, and the surah tells him not to testify with those who do.
- In a dispute nobody except God knows the whole facts, which is why the Book is the arbiter, and refusing its ruling has a cost that is paid in the next world.

<<<VERSE 6:20>>>
"Those to whom We gave the Scripture recognize him ˹to be a true prophet˺ as they recognize their own children. Those who have ruined themselves will never believe." The people who said they had asked the Jews and the Christians and found nobody to testify are answered here, and the answer is that they knew. Recognition is not the shortage. What is short is the willingness to pay for it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those to whom We gave the Scripture"** means the holders of the Torah among the Jews and the Gospel among the Christians, and the knowledge written in them. They know what he brought them, Muḥammad al-Muṣṭafā, the way a man knows his own son, because they had good news of him from the earlier Messengers and prophets: his coming, his qualities, the land he would be born in, his migration, and what his community would be like. The Torah and the Injil carry a full description of his physical features, his home city, and the place he would live in after the migration, along with his habits, his morals and what he would achieve, in such detail that a person could not fail to identify him. And the two scriptures do not stop with him: they carry a detailed account of his noble Companions as well. So nobody who recited the Torah and the Injil and believed in them could possibly miss recognizing him.

**"as they recognize their own children"** chooses the direction of the simile deliberately. It does not say: as children recognize their parents. A parent's recognition of a child is the most detailed and certain kind there is. The child is in front of them day and night, in every condition, raised in their hands and on their laps from infancy into boyhood. Children cannot know their parents to that degree.

One question about this simile was put to ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, who had belonged to the Jewish faith and then became a Muslim, and it was put to him by Farūq al-Aʿẓam: Allah says in the Qur'an that you recognize our Messenger the way you recognize your sons. Why? His answer went further than the comparison. We know the Prophet ﷺ from the qualities and the features Allah made known through the Torah, he said, so our knowledge of him is certain and absolute. Our own children are not like that, because a person could doubt whether a child is really his.

**"Those who have ruined themselves will never believe"** are the people who lost their own souls by walking into the fire of Hell, and they will not believe in him. Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and his followers are named as the ones who ruined their own souls in this way, by losing this world and the next. Theirs is the final loss: they ruin themselves at their own hands, losing this world and the next together, and they refuse to believe in a matter so clear, a matter the earlier prophets announced and that was spoken of in former times and in these.

### Why recognition did not turn into faith

Reality is not foreign to a person. It is part of his own make-up, and it speaks one silent language through the whole universe. For the Jews and the Christians recognition was even plainer, because their prophets and their books had announced the last of the prophets and this Qur'an so clearly that knowing him was like knowing their sons. So why not accept? Because of the fear of a loss right now. Accepting the truth costs a person his high standing: he has to come down from it, step outside the crowd he belongs to, and give up advantages that came ready-made. When a man will not pay that, he refuses the truth, and for a gain he can hold today he pushes himself into a loss that lasts.

### Zayd ibn Saʿnah's test

Zayd ibn Saʿnah, who had been one of the People of the Book, recognized the Prophet ﷺ from the description of his qualities and features in the Torah and the Injil. One quality alone could not be confirmed from what came earlier, so he decided to test it: his forbearance would get the better of his anger. He went to see him, put him to that test, and found him true to it, and he became a Muslim on the spot.

### In short

- Recognition was complete, and it is compared to the surest knowledge a person has, a parent's knowledge of a child.
- The Torah and the Gospel are said to carry his description, his city, his migration, his character, his community, and even his Companions.
- ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām said the knowledge they had was more certain than the knowledge of an offspring, since parentage can be doubted.
- What stops belief is not evidence but the price: status, conformity and ready-made advantage.
- Ruining yourself is losing both worlds by refusing what you already recognized.

<<<VERSE 6:21>>>
"Who does more wrong than those who fabricate lies against Allah or deny His signs?" The question is rhetorical, and the answer is: nobody. This is the worst thing a person can do, and the verse names two versions of it, inventing about Allah and rejecting what Allah sent. Then it states where either of them ends.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Who does more wrong than those who fabricate lies against Allah"** means attributing to Him partners He never gave Him, saying that He has daughters, as the people of Makkah said of their idols, or claiming that Allah sent you when He did not. No one is more daring, and no one acts with greater wrongdoing, than the person who invents something about Allah and worships alongside Him what he invented. **"or deny His signs"** means refusing the proofs, the verses and the evidences He has shown, that is, the Qur'an and Muhammad ﷺ, which is the other way to do the same wrong. **"Indeed, the wrongdoers will never succeed"** covers both of them whoever invents the lie and whoever refuses the signs. Neither will ever get success, and neither will escape or be safe from Allah's torment. Those who do wrong by setting up partners and by denying His verses will not prosper, if they do not repent.

### The excuses keep working, until they stop

A man who has taken a stand is rarely short of reasons for it. In this world of trial he keeps finding explanations and justifications that support him, and words to wave away the arguments for the truth, until he feels free to twist it deliberately and announce that the real truth is whatever he has chosen. It works because he has put something other than God at the centre of his attention, and around anything at the centre of attention a magic halo slowly forms. He feeds himself on wishful thinking until he feels he has a grip on a support that cannot move. The people who did this are named in verse 20 as those who ruined their own souls, knowing and refusing.

### In short

- Fabricating about Allah and denying His signs are two halves of one wrong, and both are named here.
- The first is claiming a rank, a partner or a son that Allah did not claim for Himself. The second is rejecting the proofs that arrived.
- The excuses that make refusal comfortable are themselves part of the trial, and they are the reason such people are described as ruining themselves.
- Neither kind succeeds, and the only exit the verse leaves open is repentance.
