<<<VERSE 7:123>>>
The magicians' public conversion was a disaster for Pharaoh, and he answered with threats and cunning: "Pharaoh threatened, “How dare you believe in him before I give you permission? This must be a conspiracy you devised in the city to drive out its people, but soon you will see". Some historical reports say that after the magicians declared their faith, six hundred thousand more people followed them. Before the contest only two people had believed in Allah. Now a great host had become Muslim. Like a shrewd politician, Pharaoh hid his state of mind from the people and turned the situation around by accusing the magicians of conspiracy and rebellion.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Pharaoh threatened, “How dare you believe in him before I give you permission?"** You have believed in the Lord of Mūsā (Moses) and Hārūn (Aaron) before I commanded you to! In the Arabic, "have you believed" (a-āmantum) may be read with both hamzahs pronounced, or with the second turned into an alif. The words were a threat to the magicians. They were also meant to convince his people that the magicians had decided in haste and fallen into the trap of Mūsā and Hārūn. Otherwise, he implied, he too would have believed if they had proved truthful.

**"This must be a conspiracy you devised in the city"**: what you have done is a plot hatched between you and Mūsā, a trick with which you have deceived the people. Pharaoh was saying that Mūsā had beaten them only because they had plotted with him and agreed to it beforehand. Elsewhere he says, "He must be your master who taught you magic" (20:71).

**"to drive out its people"**: so that you can all work together, by guile, to seize influence and power, replace the chiefs and masters of this land, and take control of the state.

**"but soon you will see"**: you will soon find out what I am going to do to you, and how I will punish you as a warning to others.

### Did Pharaoh believe his own charge?

Pharaoh knew perfectly well, as did every sensible person, that the accusation was baseless. From the moment he arrived from Madyan, Mūsā had called Pharaoh to Allah and shown him tremendous miracles and clear proofs of his truth. Pharaoh then sent agents to the cities of his kingdom and gathered the magicians scattered across Egypt. He and his people chose among them, summoned them and promised them rich rewards. The magicians were eager to beat Mūsā before Pharaoh and win his favour. Mūsā had never known, seen or met any of them before. Pharaoh knew all this, but claimed otherwise to fool the ignorant masses of his kingdom:

> "And so he fooled his people, and they obeyed him. They were truly a rebellious people" (43:54).

A people who believed Pharaoh when he said, "I am your lord, the most high!" (79:24), were among the most ignorant and misguided of Allah's creatures.

As-Suddī reported in his tafsīr, from Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās and several other Companions, why Pharaoh spoke of a plot. Mūsā had met the chief of the magicians and asked whether he would believe and testify to the truth if Mūsā beat him. The chief swore by Allah that he would, confident his magic could not be overcome. Pharaoh had seen the two of them talking, and that is why he said what he said.

### How did Pharaoh twist the event?

It was a clever scheme. Pharaoh tried to keep his people in their old ignorance and make them believe that the contest had been fixed in advance between Mūsā and the magicians. He twisted the plain fact that Mūsā's miracle and the magicians' open conversion had exposed his own ignorance and falsehood. He turned it into a political matter, telling his people that the whole affair was a plan to take over the country and drive them out.

Pharaoh treated accepting or rejecting Mūsā's call as a matter of his "permission", while the magicians treated it as a matter of a "sign" from Allah. A haughty person always lets his own opinion override argument and proof. Such people are never moved to accept the truth.

### In short

- After the magicians and many others believed, Pharaoh accused them of conspiring with Mūsā to seize power.
- He knew the charge was false, since Mūsā had never met them, but used it to fool his people.
- He turned a clear sign into a political plot, to keep his people in ignorance.
- Pharaoh saw faith as needing his permission, while the magicians saw it as answering Allah's sign.

<<<VERSE 7:124>>>
Having made his vague threat, Pharaoh now spelled it out: "I will certainly cut off your hands and feet on opposite sides, then crucify you all.”" The magicians' support for Mūsā (Moses) had stung his pride. To side with Mūsā against him was to insult him before the whole nation, so he was enraged and resolved on harsh punishment, as any arrogant man in power would.

### Phrase by phrase

**"I will certainly cut off your hands and feet on opposite sides"**: the right hand and the left foot of each of you, or the left hand and the right foot. The aim was to leave them completely crippled.

**"then crucify you all.”"** I will crucify every one of you, making an example of you to terrify all who see you. One explanation says on the river bank. Another verse says, "and crucify you on the trunks of palm trees" (20:71), where the Arabic preposition fī means "on". Ibn ʿAbbās said Pharaoh was the first to crucify people and the first to cut off hands and feet on opposite sides.

### What drove Pharaoh to such cruelty?

Both the magicians and Pharaoh had been beaten in the field of argument. The magicians admitted their defeat and so became worthy of Allah's lasting rewards. Pharaoh made it a matter of prestige. To satisfy his false pride, all he could do was persecute people who loved the truth, and in the Hereafter he himself would be given over to Allah's lasting punishment.

### In short

- Pharaoh threatened to cut off the magicians' hands and feet on opposite sides and crucify them all.
- Ibn ʿAbbās said Pharaoh was the first to use these punishments.
- Defeated in argument, he turned to brutality to save face, as the arrogant in power do.

<<<VERSE 7:125>>>
The magicians' reply showed the power of faith: "They responded, “Surely to our Lord we will ˹all˺ return". Only a few hours before, they had been Pharaoh's worshippers. Now, with faith in Allah, they showed such strength and courage that their only answer to his threats against their lives was a calm "To our Lord we will return".

### Phrase by phrase

**"They responded, “Surely to our Lord we will ˹all˺ return"**: after their death, however it came, they would be restored to their Lord and return to Him in the Hereafter. Their return was to their Lord alone, so they did not fear what Pharaoh threatened them with.

### Why were they unafraid?

They were now certain they would go back to Allah. Allah's punishment, they knew, is harsher than Pharaoh's. His punishment for what Pharaoh was calling them to that day, and for the magic Pharaoh had forced them to practise, would be greater than anything Pharaoh could do. So they would bear Pharaoh's punishment patiently that day, to be saved from Allah's.

Faith in Allah, which the Qur'an calls īmān, is a great power. When it enters a heart, the person feels strong enough to face the whole world and every force gathered against him. That was the great change in the magicians.

They knew well Pharaoh's power and authority over them. They did not say he could not kill them for their new faith. Their answer meant that the whole of this passing life had no value in their eyes. They were content because they were sure of meeting the Lord of the worlds after leaving this world, where they would have an everlasting life of peace and comfort. Another reading of their reply is this: Pharaoh had the power to end their lives, but he too would soon be brought before the Lord of the worlds and called to account for his tyranny. In another passage their reply continues: "So do whatever you want! Your authority only covers the ˹fleeting˺ life of this world" (20:72). This too shows how little they cared for this temporary life.

### What does their sacrifice prove?

To give one's life for the truth is the final testimony that the truth is true. With Allah's help, the magicians were able to make that sacrifice. By offering themselves for the worst punishment, they proved that their faith in Mūsā (Moses) was no excuse or conspiracy. It was a genuine acknowledgement of the truth.

### In short

- Facing torture and death, the magicians calmly answered that they would return to their Lord.
- Faith had turned Pharaoh's worshippers into men who valued Allah's judgement above his punishment and this world above nothing.
- Their readiness to die proved their faith was no plot but a sincere witness to the truth.

<<<VERSE 7:126>>>
The magicians named the real reason for Pharaoh's rage, and turned to Allah in prayer: "Your rage towards us is only because we believed in the signs of our Lord when they came to us. Our Lord! Shower us with perseverance, and let us die while submitting ˹to You˺.”" This great change in their thinking and action came from true faith in Allah. Their faith also opened to them a door of knowledge and wisdom, as their prayer at that moment shows.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Your rage towards us is only because we believed in the signs of our Lord when they came to us."** You are finding fault with us, punishing us and taking spiteful revenge only because we believed in our Lord's signs when they came to us through Mūsā (Moses). If that is our crime, we stand by it.

**"Our Lord! Shower us with perseverance"**: pour out patience and steadfastness on us when we are crucified and mutilated, so that we may stand firm for the truth and hold to Your religion. Then we will not slide back into disbelief when what he threatens comes to pass.

**"and let us die while submitting ˹to You˺.”"** Take us to You as Muslims, people who have sincerely surrendered to You, obeying Your commands and following Your messenger Mūsā and his religion.

### What happened to the magicians?

They told Pharaoh:

> "By the One Who created us! We will never prefer you over the clear proofs that have come to us. So do whatever you want! Your authority only covers the ˹fleeting˺ life of this world. Indeed, we have believed in our Lord so He may forgive our sins and that magic you have forced us to practice. And Allah is far superior ˹in reward˺ and more lasting ˹in punishment˺.” Whoever comes to their Lord as an evildoer will certainly have Hell, where they can neither live nor die. But whoever comes to Him as a believer, having done good, they will have the highest ranks" (20:72-75).

The magicians began the day as sorcerers and ended it as honoured martyrs. Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr, Qatādah and Ibn Jurayj all said: they began the day as sorcerers and ended it as martyrs.

### Why is patience the key to victory?

Their prayer showed knowledge and wisdom, and it was also the best way out of their trial, because steadfastness and patience are the only keys to success against an enemy. Tafsīr al-Manār records a related finding. A commission formed to study the causes and effects of the World War remarked that Muslims who believe in Allah and the Hereafter are the bravest of people on the battlefield and the most patient in hardship, because of that faith. So German military experts urged that honesty and a sense of accountability in the Hereafter be instilled in their soldiers, as a great source of strength and courage.

### Was their change itself a miracle?

The magicians' instant and complete change of heart was no smaller a miracle than Mūsā's other miracles. Men who had spent their whole lives in disbelief and ignorance became, in a moment, the most aware and truthful of Muslims, ready to give their lives for their faith. It is a pity that Muslims and Muslim states try every other means to make themselves strong, while neglecting the real source of power and strength: faith, steadfastness and patience.

### Why did their prayer carry such force?

The strength the magicians showed at that critical moment came entirely from Allah's help, and the prayer on their lips was inspired. When a servant surrenders himself to Allah body and soul, he comes so close to Him that he begins to receive special blessings and speaks words that are inspired in the fullest sense. Their prayer meant: we have surrendered to You completely, O Allah. We have done all that was in our power, and we leave the rest to You. When a servant prays like this from the bottom of his heart, Allah is surely enough for him in all his difficulties.

### In short

- The magicians told Pharaoh his only grievance was their faith in Allah's signs, and they stood by it.
- They asked Allah for steadfastness under torture and to die as Muslims, and they began the day as sorcerers and ended it as martyrs.
- Faith and patience are the true sources of strength, a lesson even military experts have noted, though Muslims often neglect it.
- A servant who surrenders wholly to Allah is given inspired words and finds Allah sufficient for him.
