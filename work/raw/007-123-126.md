The king answers the prostration with a threat, and he dresses the whole thing up as a plot. "Pharaoh threatened, “How dare you believe in him before I give you permission? This must be a conspiracy you devised in the city to drive out its people, but soon you will see"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Pharaoh threatened, “How dare you believe in him before I give you permission?"** is his opening charge. The whole of the matter stood differently in the two minds. To the king, the acceptance of the messenger was a thing that needed his leave, and the belief of the magicians was an act of disobedience to him personally. To the magicians, it was a sign that had reached them from their Lord, and it was not his to give or to withhold. It is always the way of a proud man in power, that his own opinion is put above the arguments and the proofs, and such people are never moved to accept the truth.

**"This must be a conspiracy you devised in the city to drive out its people"** is the charge he built on it. He proclaimed to the people that the defeat of the magicians had come about because they had plotted with Musa and agreed to it together. He said of Musa in another place that he was their chief who had taught them magic (20:71). The claim was a lie, and the king and every man of sense in that court knew it was a lie. Musa had come back from Madyan and was a stranger in Egypt, the magicians had been gathered from the towns of the land at the king's own command, they had been promised rewards by him, and Musa had never seen or met any of them before the day of the contest. He knew all of that, and he said what he said to deceive the ordinary people of the kingdom.

The Book describes him as a man who fooled his people and found them obedient (43:54). A people who accepted his word when he said that he was their lord, the most high (79:24), are among the most ignorant and the most misguided of the creatures of Allah.

What the words about driving out its people were meant to accomplish is plain. The charge was that the magicians and Musa had joined hands to gain influence and power, so that the chiefs and the masters of the land would be replaced by them, and the rule of the state would pass into their hands. Nothing of that had ever been said by Musa. He had asked for one thing only, which was that the Children of Israel be allowed to go with him.

**"but soon you will see"** ends the sentence with the thing he did not yet name. It is an indefinite threat, and the next verse spells it out.

### What the reports say about how it began

As-Suddi reported that Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Abbas and several other Companions said the following. Musa met the leader of the magicians and asked him whether he would believe in him and bear witness that what he had brought was the truth, if Musa defeated him. The man answered that on the next day he would produce a kind of magic that could not be beaten by any other magic, and he swore that if Musa defeated him he would believe in him and testify to his truth. Pharaoh was watching the two of them, and that is why he said what he said about a plot.

**What the declaration did to the country.** Some historical reports say that after the magicians declared their faith, six hundred thousand more people followed them and declared their belief in Allah. Before the open contest there had been only two individuals in the land who believed. So a great number of people entered the faith in a single day, and the position of the king became an awkward one from the moment it happened.

He handled it the way a clever politician handles such things. He hid what he felt from the people and turned the ground under himself into firm footing again by putting the charge of conspiracy and rebellion on the magicians. In one stroke the contest became a plot, the miracle became a scheme, and the men who had stood in front of the nation as their own were presented as traitors who had conspired with Musa and Harun to bring disorder into the country. His aim was to keep the people in the ignorance they were in, and by the claim that the whole thing had been arranged beforehand, to rob the miracle of its weight and the conversion of its plainness.

### Why the threat came out of pride

To give up one's life for the truth is the last and fullest testimony a person can bear to it, and the magicians were enabled to make that offering. By putting themselves in front of the worst punishment the king could threaten, they proved that their belief in Musa was not an arrangement and not a conspiracy, but a plain acknowledgement of the truth.

That action stung the pride of Pharaoh. To side with Musa against him was to insult him in front of the whole community, and a haughty man in his position answers an insult with force. Both sides had been defeated in the field of argument, and the difference is what each did afterwards. The magicians admitted the defeat and became entitled to the reward of God, while the king made it a matter of prestige and took it out on the people who loved the truth. What he could not do was undo the argument, so he turned to power, and for that the price was his own eternal punishment.

### In short

- The king treated the belief of the magicians as an act needing his permission, while to them it was a sign from their Lord.
- He called the contest a conspiracy between Musa and the magicians to drive the people out and take the rule.
- The magicians had been gathered by his own order and paid by his own promise, and Musa had never met them before, so the charge was false and he knew it.
- The Book describes him as a man who fooled his people, and a people who accepted the claim that he was their lord the most high (79:24) are among the most misguided.
- The reports say six hundred thousand followed the magicians into the faith, where only two individuals had believed before, and the day left the king exposed.
- He answered it by turning the matter into a plot, and his pride, once wounded, chose force over truth.

### Phrase by phrase

**"I will certainly cut off your hands and feet on opposite sides"** is the first part of the sentence he passed. The meaning of the words is the right hand and the left leg of every one of them, so that the body is left altogether disabled, and the same words are reported in another place with the crucifying named on the trunks of the date palms (20:71). The cutting from opposite sides was the part that made the punishment a lasting injury even for those who survived it.

**"then crucify you all"** is the second part, and together the two make a punishment that is meant to be seen. One reading places the crosses on the river bank, where the traffic of the people would pass them. Pharaoh ordered that they be made an example, so that whoever looked at them would be frightened away from the same step.

Ibn Abbas said that Pharaoh was the first to crucify people and to cut off hands and legs on opposite sides. The whole of the sentence was delivered to men who had just bowed to the Lord of the worlds, and it was delivered in front of the nation, which is part of what the answer in the next verse was measured against.

### In short

- Verse 124 of this surah names the punishment. It is the cutting of the right hand and the left leg, the opposite sides of the body, so that the person is left crippled for life.
- The same sentence is reported in another place with the crosses on the trunks of the date palms (20:71).
- The crucifying was to be done publicly, on one reading along the river bank where the people would pass.
- The purpose was to make an example of them, so that the sight would frighten anyone who thought of following them.
- Ibn Abbas said that Pharaoh was the first to inflict both parts of that punishment.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They responded, “Surely to our Lord we will ˹all˺ return"** is the whole of their reply to the sentence that Pharaoh had passed on them. The words are an affirmation about the end of every person, and one reading gives the sense plainly, that they would be restored to their Lord after death, however that death should come about.

**What had changed in them.** A few hours before they had been the followers of the king's religion, and Iman is a power that makes such a change possible. When it enters a heart, a person finds in himself enough to stand against the whole world and all the forces that have been gathered against him. The men who said these words had been hired to defend a throne, and they were now saying to the king's face that they were going back to their Lord. What the words also show is their whole indifference to the life of this world. The promises that had brought them to the contest were gone from their minds in that hour, and what the words say is that they had another end in view.

**What they said besides.** Another passage reports more of their answer to him, that he should decide whatever he wished to decree, since he could only decide for the life of this world, and that they had believed in their Lord so that He might forgive them their faults and the magic to which he had compelled them, and that Allah is better to reward and more lasting in punishment. The same passage names the two ends, that whoever comes to his Lord as a criminal has the Fire, where he neither dies nor lives, while whoever comes to Him as a believer who has done good deeds has the high ranks (20:72-75). So the magicians spoke to the king about what outlasts a king, and they left the sentence in his hands.

### In short

- Their answer to the threat was that every person returns to his Lord, whenever and however death comes.
- The change in them in a few hours shows what Iman does when it enters a heart, since a person then finds strength against the whole world.
- Their words show how little the life of this world weighed with them after that, since the rewards they had come for were already forgotten.
- They told the king to decree whatever he wished, since his decree reached only this life (20:72-75).
- The passage names the two ends, the Fire for the criminal and the high ranks for the believer who does good.

<<<VERSE 7:126>>>
The last thing they say is a statement of their crime and a prayer. "Your rage towards us is only because we believed in the signs of our Lord when they came to us. Our Lord! Shower us with perseverance, and let us die while submitting ˹to You˺.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Your rage towards us is only because we believed in the signs of our Lord when they came to us"** names the fault the king was punishing. They had found fault in them for nothing except the faith itself, and the words put the charge on the table plainly. The signs of their Lord had come to them, and they had believed them, and if that was the crime, then they stood by it.

**"Our Lord! Shower us with perseverance"** is the first half of the prayer, and it asks for firmness at the very point where the punishment was going to fall. The word carries the sense of being honoured with steadfastness when the crucifying and the cutting came, and the point of the asking was that they should not go back to disbelief under it. So they asked for strength at the exact place where strength would be needed, which is the way a servant asks when he knows his own limits.

**"and let us die while submitting ˹to You˺"** is the second half, and it is the sum of what a believer wants from the whole of his life. They asked to be taken while they were surrendered to Him, sincere in it, and following the religion of Musa.

**Where that prayer came from.** The change in their thought and their action was the fruit of the faith that had entered them. Beyond that, their faith opened the door of knowledge and wisdom in them, and their prayer at that hour is one of the marks of it. The prayer was also the best way out of the difficulty they were in, since perseverance and patience are the keys to victory over an enemy, and the hardest minutes a person spends are the ones where he must simply stand still and hold on.

The proof of what patience does belongs to the field as much as to the cell. The report of the commission formed to investigate the causes and effects of the World War found that the Muslims who have faith in Allah and in the Hereafter are the bravest of men in battle and the most patient in hardship, because of that faith. The lesson was clear enough to the German military officials whose speciality was the science of war, and they urged that honesty and a sense of accountability in the Hereafter be planted among their soldiers, since those are among the great sources of strength and courage.

**The change itself was a sign.** The instant and complete turning of the magicians was no less a miracle than the staff that ate the snakes. Men who had spent their whole lives in disbelief and ignorance became, in one hour, among the most knowing and the truest of people, ready to give their lives for what they had come to see. The pity of the matter is that the Muslims and the Muslim states try every other means to make themselves strong, and pay no heed to the thing from which that strength comes, which is faith, perseverance and patience. Ibn Abbas, Ubayd bin Umayr, Qatadah and Ibn Jurayj all said of these men that they began the day as sorcerers and ended it as martyrs.

The prayer itself carried a surrender that has its own answer. When a servant of God gives himself body and soul to his Lord, he comes so near that he begins to receive what is special from Him, and the words he speaks at such a moment are inspired in the full sense. What the magicians said came to this, that they had surrendered entirely, that they had done what was in their power, and that they left the rest to Him.

### In short

- They put the charge in plain words, that the king was punishing them for believing the signs of their Lord when the signs came.
- Their prayer asked for perseverance at the exact point where the punishment would fall, and that they be taken while surrendered to Him.
- Faith had opened knowledge and wisdom in them, and the prayer was both a mark of it and the best way out of their difficulty.
- Perseverance and patience are the keys to victory over an enemy, and the report on the World War found the believers strongest in battle and steadiest in hardship because of their faith in Allah and the Hereafter.
- The instant change in the magicians was itself a miracle, and what it shows is that the strength of a people is drawn from faith, perseverance and patience.
- Their prayer was a full surrender, and the account treats it as words that were placed in them at that hour.
