<<<VERSE 2:115>>>
Verse 115 answers those who made the direction of prayer a weapon, and comforts those who had been driven away from the House they loved. "To Allah belong the east and the west, so wherever you turn you are facing ˹towards˺ Allah. Surely Allah is All-Encompassing, All-Knowing".

### Phrase by phrase

**"To Allah belong the east and the west"**. That is, the whole earth, since these two directions stand for its two sides. Allah has authority over the entire world and instructs His servants as He wills. This ruling was a comfort to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his Companions, expelled from Makkah and forced to leave the precincts of al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, the Sacred Mosque. In Makkah the Messenger ﷺ used to pray towards the Bayt al-Maqdis with the Kaʿbah between him and his qiblah. After the Hijrah to al-Madinah he kept facing the Bayt al-Maqdis for some sixteen or seventeen months, until Allah directed him to the Kaʿbah. Ibn ʿAbbās, through ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah, said that the first thing in the Qur'an to be abrogated was the qiblah. When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ migrated to al-Madinah, a city of Jews, he was first commanded to face the Bayt al-Maqdis, which pleased the Jews, and he did so for some ten months. But he loved the qiblah of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and used to look up to the sky and pray, until Allah revealed: "Indeed, We see you ˹O Prophet˺ turning your face towards heaven. Now We will make you turn towards a direction ˹of prayer˺ that will please you. So turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque" (2:144). The Jews were disturbed and asked what had made the Muslims abandon the qiblah they had been facing, and Allah revealed, "Say, to Allah belong the east and the west". The new command delighted the Muslims, but the Jews in their habitual malice taunted them with having abandoned the way of the earlier prophets, and tried to plant doubts which, had they taken root, would have led to denial of the Prophet ﷺ and the abandonment of the prescribed prayer, thereby laying waste the Prophet's ﷺ own mosque. This was one of three incidents in which the three groups hostile to Islam were each guilty, in one way or another, of hindering worship in Allah's mosques and laying them waste. The second was the Romans' invasion of Jerusalem, when the ignorant among them defiled the Bayt al-Maqdis so that no one could pray there. The Christians regarded the Romans more or less as their ancestors, and the Jews' humiliation pleased them, so by refusing to condemn the Romans' deed they shared indirectly in the ruin of that mosque. The third was the Peace of al-Ḥudaybiyyah, when the idolaters refused the Prophet ﷺ entry to Makkah for the pilgrimage. Ibn ʿAbbās related the previous verse (2:114) to the second incident, and Ibn Jarīr accepted that, while others followed Ibn Zayd in preferring the third. The Holy Qur'an, however, speaks generally of "the mosques of Allah" so as to lay down a permanent law for all peoples covering every case of desecrating mosques or hindering the remembrance of Allah, calling those who do it "unjust" and threatening them with humiliation.

**"so wherever you turn you are facing ˹towards˺ Allah"**. ʿIkrimah related Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss: wherever you face, east or west, there is Allah's direction. Mujāhid said: wherever you are, you have a qiblah to face, namely the Kaʿbah. Whichever way you turn your faces in prayer, at His command, there is Allah's Face, that is, the direction of prayer He is pleased with, and the prayer is performed in His good pleasure. Wherever you turn you will find Allah, who surrounds His creation. Whether the instruction is to face Jerusalem or the Kaʿbah, and whether you mistake the direction or are unsure of it, there is no problem, since every direction belongs to Allah. One gloss reads the opening clause as the direction of prayer for someone who does not know the correct one, and another takes it to mean that east and west alike, all their peoples, have one qiblah, the Sacred Precinct, so that "the Face of Allah" means the direction of prayer towards Him.

Several occasions of revelation are recorded, and scholars disagree about them. It has been said that the verse came down before the command to face the Kaʿbah. Ibn Jarīr related the view that it was revealed to permit the one offering voluntary prayers to face wherever he wishes, east or west, while travelling, in fear, or when facing the enemy. Ibn ʿUmar used to face whichever way his mount was heading and say that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ did the same, citing this verse in explanation. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr related from Ibn ʿUmar that the verse was revealed so that one prays towards whatever direction one's mount takes when offering voluntary prayers. This ḥadīth was collected by Muslim, at-Tirmidhī, an-Nasāʾī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim and Ibn Marduwayh, and the Two Ṣaḥīḥs contain its basis from Ibn ʿUmar and ʿAmr ibn Rabīʿah, without the verse being mentioned. In his Ṣaḥīḥ, al-Bukhārī recorded from Nāfiʿ that whenever Ibn ʿUmar was asked about the prayer of fear he would describe it and then add that when the fear is worse than that, one prays standing or riding, facing the qiblah or not. Nāfiʿ added that he thought Ibn ʿUmar attributed this to the Prophet ﷺ. Others say the verse concerns those who cannot find the direction of the qiblah in the dark or under cloudy skies and so pray the wrong way by mistake. Jābir ibn ʿAbd al-Malik told of a military expedition on which it was so dark that the men could not determine the qiblah. Some said it lay to the north and prayed that way, drawing lines on the ground, and others said it lay to the south and did the same. When the sun rose they found their lines pointed away from the qiblah. On their return they asked the Prophet ﷺ, who gave no answer until Allah revealed this verse. ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿĀmir ibn Rabīʿah related from his father a similar account: they were praying with the Prophet ﷺ on a journey on a dark night, each man faced the direction he thought right, and when they told the Prophet ﷺ in the morning the verse came down. An early gloss likewise says it was revealed about a group of Companions who prayed in the wrong direction while travelling after doing their best to determine it.

In his commentary on this verse al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Marduwayh recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "What lies between the east and the west is the qiblah for the people of al-Madinah, ash-Shām and Iraq." At-Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah recorded it with the wording, "What lies between the east and the west is a qiblah."

**"Surely Allah is All-Encompassing, All-Knowing"**. Ibn Jarīr explained that Allah encompasses all His creation by providing for their needs and by His generosity and favour, and that "Knowing" means He knows their deeds, nothing escapes His watch, He is unaware of nothing, and His knowledge embraces everything. His bounty embraces all things, He knows how to manage His creation, He covers His creatures with His mercy and ease, and He knows their intentions and all that they do. He is all-embracing with regard to the direction of prayer, and all-knowing of what the worshippers intend.

### Why a fixed qiblah at all?

The verse has two facets: an answer to the Jews' objection, and comfort to the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions. Had Allah been confined to one direction, a fixed and permanent orientation would have been necessary in order to worship Him. Being infinite and beyond every limitation, He is Lord of the east and the west and of every conceivable direction, everywhere, surrounding everything. Wherever a person turns he finds Allah "facing" him, ready to accept his prayer and shower His bounty on him. It follows that neither the Bayt al-Maqdis nor the Kaʿbah has any inherent or inalienable superiority. Either acquires a privileged position only by divine appointment. What matters is to obey Allah's command, which alone makes a person worthy of His grace, and to win His pleasure one must orient oneself as He has determined. If, despite being free of all limitation, Allah has fixed a particular orientation, it is because He is all-knowing and knows what is best in a given situation and for a given people. The fixing of a definite direction for prayer has an obvious rationale even if no one can fully grasp the wisdom in every divine command. Whichever way one turns one would find Allah "facing" one, but having to choose a direction every time one prays would scatter one's attention, and when several people pray together it would be absurd for each to face a different way. A single orientation helps individual and congregation alike to concentrate and to feel a shared purpose. This also disposes of the objection of certain opponents who accuse the Muslims of worshipping the Kaʿbah. If they reply that they too keep idols before them while meditating or worshipping for the sake of concentration, the claim does nothing to strengthen their accusation, and an impartial look at the two frames of mind shows how genuine the Muslims are in worshipping none but Allah and how doubtful the others' position is. Even granting that idols or icons are only a means to an end, using them as a "support" would still require a ruling from a Sharīʿah that has not been abrogated, and today only the Muslims possess one. A modern reflection adds that one of the errors of the opponents was to liken God to man. Since a human being cannot be in two places at once, people imagine that God too is found only in a particular place. But God is everywhere. He has prescribed a direction for us to face in worship, yet this is purely an organisational requirement of prayer and does not mean He is found in one direction and not another.

A caution is in order. The verse speaks of "the Face of Allah" and of Allah being All-Encompassing. Wisdom lies in not probing needlessly into the meanings of such statements. Just as a creature cannot fully comprehend the Being (Dhāt) of Allah, it is equally impossible to grasp the essential reality of His Attributes (Ṣifāt). What is required is a general faith in the realities of the divine order, not an inquiry into particulars that lie wholly beyond human reach.

### The east and west of the heart

The devotional reading finds here an allusion to the eastern and western regions of the believers' hearts. A heart has its rising lights (shawāriq) and its night visitors (ṭawāriq). The visitors of the night are the murmurings (hawājis) of the lower self, which come in the darkness of desire and carnal passion. The lights that rise are the stars of the various sciences, the moons of presence, the suns of gnosis. While those lights are rising, the heart's qiblah stays clear and visible. Once the realities take over, the lights lose their authority and are hidden, as the stars are at sunrise. When the Real appears there is destruction and subjugation. No trace is witnessed any more, no sensation or comprehension is left, neither the power of intellect and knowledge nor the illumination of deeper knowledge, because all such qualities last only as long as human nature does, and once those who bear attributes are effaced, how can any attribute of theirs remain? Hence "wherever you turn you are facing towards Allah". Yet as long as even a sliver of sensory perception and the ability to differentiate remain, the qiblah should be aimed at, and if it is not known it should be sought. The scholars hold that when the landmarks in every direction are obscure and the qiblah is unknown, all directions are equally permissible for prayer, since none can be preferred for the intention (niyyah).

### In short

- Allah owns every direction, so the change of qiblah from the Bayt al-Maqdis to the Kaʿbah (2:144) gave the Jews no ground for their taunts and consoled the Muslims cut off from the Sacred Mosque.
- The verse is also applied to voluntary prayers on a mount facing any direction, as Ibn ʿUmar did following the Prophet ﷺ, to the prayer of fear, and to Companions who prayed the wrong way in the dark, whose prayer was accepted.
- "What lies between the east and the west is a qiblah" for the people of al-Madinah, ash-Shām and Iraq.
- A fixed qiblah is an organisational aid to concentration and unity, not a limit on Allah, who is everywhere, and the Muslims worship Him alone, not the Kaʿbah.
- The "Face of Allah" and His encompassing are to be believed in generally, not probed, and for the heart the qiblah stays visible as long as its lights are rising.

<<<VERSE 2:116>>>
Verse 116 confronts the gravest error the surrounding communities had made about Allah. "They say, “Allah has offspring.” Glory be to Him! In fact, to Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth—all are subject to His Will".

### Phrase by phrase

**"They say, “Allah has offspring.”"**. "They" are the Jews, the Christians and those who claimed that the angels are Allah's daughters. Certain Jews called ʿUzayr (Ezra), a prophet, the son of God. The Christians said the same of ʿĪsā (Jesus), and most still do, and the idolaters of Makkah held the angels to be God's daughters. The word is read either as "and they say" (wa-qālū) or simply "they say" (qālū). This verse and the next refute the Christians and their like among the Jews and the Arab idolaters, all of whom claimed that He had begotten a son.

**"Glory be to Him!"**. Allah exalts Himself above such a thing. He is holier and more perfect than their claim, far too exalted to have a son or a partner. Of His creation He has no need, and having a son is a mark of need. Only those with needs beget children, and God is above every such imperfection and limitation, complete in Himself. Such beliefs do not come from God. People invented them.

**"In fact, to Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth"**. The truth is not as the disbelievers claim. The kingdom of the heavens and the earth is Allah's, together with everything and everyone in them, on them and between them. He is the supreme authority in the heavens and the earth, the Creator, Provider and Sustainer who decides the affairs of creation as He wills. All creatures are His servants and His possessions, so how could one of them be His son? A son is born of two comparable beings, and nothing is Allah's equal or peer in His grace and greatness. Having no wife, how could He have a son? Allah said: "˹He is˺ the Originator of the heavens and earth. How could He have children when He has no mate? He created all things and has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of everything" (6:101), and "They say, “The Most Compassionate has offspring.” You have certainly made an outrageous claim, by which the heavens are about to burst, the earth to split apart, and the mountains to crumble to pieces, in protest of attributing children to the Most Compassionate. It does not befit ˹the majesty of˺ the Most Compassionate to have children. There is none in the heavens or the earth who will not return to the Most Compassionate in full submission. Indeed, He fully knows them and has counted them precisely. And each of them will return to Him on the Day of Judgment all alone" (19:88-95), and "Allah—the Sustainer ˹needed by all˺. He has never had offspring, nor was He born. And there is none comparable to Him" (112:2-4). In these verses Allah declares Himself the supreme Master without equal or rival, the Creator of everything and everyone, so how could He have a son from among them? The particle "whatever" (mā) is used so as to include all of creation, rational and non-rational, and this sovereignty is incompatible with having a child. Al-Bukhārī recorded in the commentary on this verse that Ibn ʿAbbās reported the Prophet ﷺ saying that Allah said: "The son of Ādam has denied Me, and he had no right to. He has insulted Me, and he had no right to. His denial of Me is his claim that I cannot restore him as he was. His insult to Me is his claim that I have a son. Glory be to Me, far be it from Me to take a wife or a son." The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record the Messenger of Allah ﷺ saying that no one is more patient under insult than Allah: they attribute a son to Him, and He still grants them provision and health.

Reason itself shows the impossibility of offspring for God. Were it possible, the offspring would have to share the father's genus or belong to a different one. If to a different genus, that is plainly a defect, and God must be free of all defects, as reason requires and as this verse affirms. If to the same genus, that is a contradiction in terms, since God has no equal and no existent can share His genus. God alone is the Necessary Being (adh-Dhāt al-Wājib) and therefore necessarily possesses the Attributes of Perfection peculiar to Him alone, which cannot exist in anyone else. To deny a being its necessary attribute is to deny its existence, so nothing other than God can be a necessary being, and since necessity is the very essence of the Ultimate Reality, or an inseparable quality of it, nothing other than God can share that Reality. To claim that anything belongs to His genus is a plain contradiction. Having refuted the Jews, the Christians and the idolaters, the two verses go on to show why the Attributes of Perfection belong to Allah alone. First, everything in the heavens and the earth belongs to Him. Second, everything is subservient to Him, in that nothing can interfere with His omnipotence, His power to create and to destroy, even if some people are lax in obeying His law. Third, He is the Creator and Inventor of the heavens and the earth. Fourth, His creative power is such that when He wishes to do something He needs no instruments or helpers but simply says "Be", and it becomes what He wishes. None of these four qualities is found in anyone but Allah, and even those who attributed offspring to Him acknowledged them, so their claim refutes itself.

**"all are subject to His Will"**. All are qānitūn to Him. Ibn ʿAbbās explained the related word qānitīn in "stand in true devotion to Allah" (2:238) as "they pray to Him". ʿIkrimah and Abū Mālik said qānitūn means bound to Him in servitude, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said it means sincerity, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said standing before Him on the Day of Resurrection, and as-Suddī said obedient on the Day of Resurrection. Mujāhid said it means obedient: He says "be a human" and it becomes a human, "be a donkey" and it becomes a donkey. Mujāhid also said that the disbeliever's obedience is when his shadow prostrates while he himself hates it. Ibn Jarīr preferred Mujāhid's explanation because it gathers all the meanings together: qunūt is obedience and submission to Allah, and it is of two kinds, legislated and decreed, as Allah said, "To Allah ˹alone˺ bow down ˹in submission˺ all those in the heavens and the earth—willingly or unwillingly—as do their shadows, morning and evening" (13:15). All obey His will, each submitting to what is required of it, with the emphasis here falling on rational beings, bound to Him by servitude and by the declaration of His oneness. As His servants they are dealt with as He wills.

### Two further lessons

First, if Allah has chosen to assign certain tasks to certain angels, such as sending rain or bringing creatures their sustenance, or has chosen to work through causes, materials and physical forces, He has done so in His wisdom. It is therefore neither permitted nor proper to regard those angels, causes or forces as effective agents in themselves and to turn to them for help in need. Second, al-Bayḍāwī remarked that, Allah being the First Cause of things, the earlier sharīʿahs had allowed the title "Father" to be used of Him, but the ignorant so misunderstood and distorted the sense of "fatherhood" that to hold such a belief, or to apply the title to Allah, has now been declared unbelief (kufr). Because the practice can lead to every kind of doctrinal disorder, it is no longer permissible to use this word or any similar expression of Allah.

### The one Lord

The devotional reading answers the claim with a litany. He is pure and without fault, incomparable, the one Lord: one in forbearance, so that when the servant does harm He does not cut him off, one in loyalty, so that when the servant inclines to another He does not incline away, one in love, so that when the servant breaks the covenant He does not break it. He is one in Essence and one in attributes, free of causes, praised by every expression, fitting for every allusion, who created time and the hours and determines every instant and moment. No defect mars His artisanry, no contrivance His determining, no likeness His description. He is Powerful and Knowing, Alive and Desiring, Hearing and Seeing, clothed in majesty, too holy to have an equal, beyond all opinion and talk. Lord without associate or partner, king without equal or need, He makes no false promise and bears no metaphorical name. Withholding's door is shut, and the door of His generosity stands open. He forgives sins and caresses the faulty, knows without cause and is able without artifice, alone without poverty, outside number, an artisan without toil, self-subsisting to the Endless, hallowed beyond envy. His names are the Subtle, the Self-Standing, the Self-Sufficient, who "has never had offspring, nor was He born. And there is none comparable to Him" (112:2-4). So present is He face to face in the heart and so hidden from the eyes that no describer can describe Him: He is as He is. Ears are open to His name, hearts captive to His message, the voicers of tawḥīd caught in His snare and the yearners drunk with love from His cup. No one in the universe matches His loving kindness. The disobedient and the destitute hope in Him, and the poor rejoice in the subsistence of His majesty.

### In short

- Jews who called ʿUzayr God's son, Christians who said it of ʿĪsā, and Arabs who called the angels His daughters all attributed offspring to Allah, and Allah declares Himself glorified above it.
- Everything in the heavens and the earth is His property and His servant, so none of it can be His son, and a son requires a mate and a peer, which He has not (6:101, 19:88-95, 112:2-4).
- The ḥadīth qudsī calls the claim an insult to Allah, who nonetheless keeps providing for those who make it.
- Reason rules out offspring of the same or a different genus, and the four marks of ownership, subjection, origination and creation by "Be" belong to Him alone.
- All creatures are qānitūn, obedient and submissive by law or by decree (13:15), and after the distortion of "fatherhood" the title may no longer be used of Allah.

<<<VERSE 2:117>>>
Verse 117 completes the refutation by describing how Allah actually brings things into being. "˹He is˺ the Originator of the heavens and the earth! When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, “Be!” And it is".

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹He is˺ the Originator of the heavens and the earth!"**. The Badīʿ created them when nothing resembling them existed, with no exemplary precedent and out of nothing. Mujāhid and as-Suddī said this is the linguistic sense, for every new thing is called a bidʿah. Muslim recorded the Messenger of Allah ﷺ saying that every innovation in religion is a bidʿah. Bidʿah is thus of two kinds, the religious kind named in the ḥadīth "every innovation is a bidʿah, and every bidʿah is misguidance", and the linguistic kind, as when the Commander of the Faithful ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb gathered the Muslims for the Tarāwīḥ prayer in congregation, something the Prophet ﷺ had himself done earlier, and said, "What a good bidʿah this is." The scholars define the Originator as the One who gives the entity (ʿayn) existence with no model before Him (lā ʿalā mithāl). For the people of allusion the name says that nothing is like Him, denying at once any likeness (mithl) to His essence and any model (mithāl) for His acts. He is al-Aḥad, the One, comprised by no quantity. He is aṣ-Ṣamad, the Eternal, cut off by no end-point in time. He is al-Ḥaqq, the Real, whom no imagination pictures, and al-Mawjūd, the Existent, whom no understanding grasps. Nothing in creation resembles Him. Ibn Jarīr drew the two verses together: Allah is far too glorious to have a son, for He owns everything in the heavens and the earth, all of which testifies to His oneness and its own submission to Him. He is their Creator and Maker, who shaped the creatures in their present forms without any created precedent, and He bears witness to His servants that ʿĪsā (Jesus), whom some called His son, is among those who testify to His oneness. Having created the heavens and the earth from nothing and without precedent, He likewise created ʿĪsā the Messiah by His power and without a father. This explanation is very good and correct.

**"When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, “Be!” And it is"**. This displays His perfect power and tremendous authority. Once He has decided a matter, a single order to be brings it into existence, and nothing stops His command and decree. Allah said: "All it takes, when He wills something ˹to be˺, is simply to say to it: “Be!” And it is" (36:82), and "If We ever will something ˹to exist˺, all We say is: “Be!” And it is" (16:40), and "Our command is but a single word, done in the blink of an eye" (54:50). So Allah created ʿĪsā merely by saying "Be": "Indeed, the example of Jesus in the sight of Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust, then said to him, “Be!” And he was" (3:59). When He wants to create a child without a father, like ʿĪsā, He says "be a child without a father", just as Ādam was without father or mother. The verb is read fa-yakūnu, "and it is", or in a variant fa-yakūna in the subjunctive as the response to the command. Whatever He decides, no preordained thing (maqdūr) opposes it and no created thing (mafṭūr) can break away from His decree.

### "Be" and the mystery of creation

For those drawn to Western philosophy, Christian theology or the writings of the Orientalists, a note on the command "Be" is in order, with the warning that this is a mystery in the original sense: a reality beyond human understanding, and one which even when partly grasped must not be handed to those without the aptitude to receive it, so that silence is often the better part. What is revealed, and when, is a matter of spiritual etiquette rather than of liberalism or egalitarianism. The theologians (mutakallimūn) approach it in two ways. For the Ashʿarīs, "Be, and it is" (kun fa-yakūn) is a metaphor. The phrase does not mean that Allah actually addressed an existent thing and commanded it to be. It is an allegorical picture of His omnipotence, suggesting that no interval separates His act of will from its realisation. Al-Bayḍāwī took this view, and the kalām debate turns on it. For the Māturīdīs the phrase means literally what it says, which raises a difficulty. A command is addressed only to something that exists. How can Allah address what does not exist at all, and why command what already exists to be? Two considerations resolve the problem. First, this command does not belong to the order of legislation (tashrīʿ), which requires an addressee that actually exists and understands, but to the order of creation (takwīn), which gives existence to non-existents. Second, there is the question that has muddled so much Western thought, creation "out of nothing" (ex nihilo). Existence (wujūd) is usually set against non-existence (ʿadam), yet it has also been said that non-existence does not exist. Allah is omniscient, and His knowledge comprehends all that has been, is or will be, so that what does not yet exist by our reckoning already exists in His knowledge. Everything past, present or future has its pure, subtle counterpart in divine knowledge. One may call these prototypes, numbers, essences, ideas or archetypes, provided the terms are given a higher and more refined meaning than Pythagoras or Plato ever gave them. The Sufis call them al-aʿyān ath-thābitah. When Allah wishes to create a thing, He says "Be" to its essence, which is already present in His knowledge, and it comes to be, that is, it is actualised in the world. Creation therefore does not arise out of nothingness. Before a thing exists as an actuality in the world it exists as a potentiality in divine knowledge, and it is this potentiality that the command "Be" addresses. Hence it is equally true to say that the essences do not exist, from the standpoint of created knowledge, and that they do exist, from the standpoint of divine knowledge. Once again, no good comes of meddling with such delicate questions needlessly, least of all for the sake of a new sensation.

### In short

- Allah originated the heavens and the earth without model or precedent, which is the linguistic root of bidʿah, a word that is blameworthy in religion but neutral in language, as ʿUmar's remark about Tarāwīḥ shows.
- Whatever He decrees He brings about simply by saying "Be" (36:82, 16:40, 54:50), and so He created ʿĪsā without a father as He created Ādam (3:59), which is why ʿĪsā cannot be His son.
- Ashʿarīs read "Be" as a metaphor for instant omnipotence, Māturīdīs literally as a creative command addressed to essences already present in Allah's knowledge, the Sufis' al-aʿyān ath-thābitah, so that creation is not strictly from nothing.
- Such matters are a mystery to be believed, not a puzzle to be probed for novelty.

Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, Qatādah and as-Suddī said it was in fact the saying of the Arab disbelievers, and that "those who came before" were the Jews and the Christians. That the Arab idolaters said such things is confirmed by other verses: "Whenever a sign comes to them, they say, “We will never believe until we receive what Allah’s messengers received.”" (6:124), and "They challenge ˹the Prophet˺, “We will never believe in you until you cause a spring to gush forth from the earth for us" down to "or until you have a house of gold, or you ascend into heaven—and even then we will not believe in your ascension until you bring down to us a book that we can read.” Say, “Glory be to my Lord!" (17:90-93), and "Those who do not expect to meet Us say, “If only the angels were sent down to us, or we could see our Lord!” They have certainly been carried away by their arrogance" (25:21), and "In fact, each one of them wishes to be given a ˹personal˺ letter ˹from Allah˺ for all ˹to read˺" (74:52). Many other verses attest the Arab idolaters' disbelief, transgression and obstinacy, and their needless questions asked in disbelief and arrogance. One gloss takes "those who have no knowledge" to be the Jews, who did not know Allah's oneness, demanding that Allah speak to them directly or send a sign confirming Muḥammad's ﷺ prophethood, "for then we would surely believe". Another takes them to be the disbelievers of Makkah, the Quraysh, asking that God tell them Muḥammad ﷺ was His Messenger, or send the kind of sign they had requested as proof of his sincerity. They were the ignorant among the People of the Scripture and the idolaters, speaking out of stubbornness against the truth.

The Jews, the Christians and the idolaters denied the prophethood of Muḥammad ﷺ, some out of sheer malevolence, and to feel triumphant in their obstinacy they made absurd and impossible demands, two of which are mentioned here. First came the insistence that Allah speak to them Himself, directly as He does to the angels, or through angels as He does to the prophets, and either proclaim His commands to them Himself, making a new prophet unnecessary, or at least announce that He had sent Muḥammad ﷺ, so that believing in him would be easy. Should Allah decline, they had a second demand ready: that He send them a sign or proof confirming his prophethood.

**"The same was said by those who came before"**. The demand of the Arab idolaters echoed earlier nations. Those earlier peoples included recipients of the two Scriptures and adherents of other religions. Allah said: "The People of the Book demand that you ˹O Prophet˺ bring down for them a revelation in writing from heaven. They demanded what is even greater than this from Moses, saying, “Make Allah visible to us”" (4:153), and "And ˹remember˺ when you said, “O Moses! We will never believe you until we see Allah with our own eyes,” so a thunderbolt struck you while you were looking on" (2:55). Past communities said the like to their prophets in their obstinacy and demands for signs, and so did their forefathers. Allah dismisses the demand as a foolish custom practised unthinkingly by ignorant people in every age.

**"Their hearts are all alike"**. The hearts of the Arab idolaters are just like the hearts of those before them, full of disbelief, obstinacy and injustice, as Allah said: "Similarly, no messenger came to those before them without being told: “A magician or a madman!” Have they passed this ˹cliché˺ down to one another? In fact, they have ˹all˺ been a transgressing people" (51:52-53). Their words and their hearts are the same. All disbelievers are alike even when they belong to different times and places. The verse traces the demand to a distortion of the heart in which the ignorant of every age are alike, which is why the same demand is repeated parrot-fashion down the centuries. The observation is meant as consolation for the Prophet ﷺ.

**"Indeed, We have made the signs clear for people of sure faith"**. We have made plain the arguments proving the truth of the Messengers, so that those who believe, follow the Messengers and understand what Allah sent them with need no further questions or proofs. Those who are certain know that these are God's signs and believe in them, and to ask for other signs would be sheer obstinacy. The signs include the commands and prohibitions and the Prophet's ﷺ description in the Torah. As for those whose hearts and hearing Allah has sealed and whose eyes He has covered, "those against whom Allah’s decree ˹of torment˺ is justified will not believe—even if every sign were to come to them—until they see the painful punishment" (10:96-97). The first of the two demands was silly on its face, for with all the coarseness of their minds and hearts they had the audacity to put themselves on the level of angels and prophets, so the Qur'an treats it as unworthy of reply. To the second Allah answers that He has sent not one but many clear signs and proofs establishing the prophethood of Muḥammad ﷺ, which help only those who sincerely wish to know the truth and attain certainty. For those not seeking truth but content to wallow in malice and obstinacy there is no help. One may wonder why Allah calls the Jews and Christians, People of the Book, some of them learned, "those who have no knowledge". The answer is that despite the many clear signs and definite proofs sent to establish his prophethood they persisted in denial, and that is the mentality and conduct of the ignorant.

### The ear of comprehension

The devotional reading notes that God's speech (kalām) reaches every created thing, its entity (ʿayn) and its traces (āthār), by way of the creative command "Be" (amr at-takwīn), and to those charged with the law through the prescriptive command (amr at-taklīf). Whoever lacks the ear of comprehension is deaf to the Real. He did address some of the People of the Book, and they heard but could not bear it, and after recognising the enormity of the signs they fell to distortion and alteration. Among the signs He made manifest were those that remove the defect of everything other than Him and quench the thirst with what is better. But what proofs, however plain, could suffice those whose portion is wretchedness, and whose wretchedness has already arrived?

### In short

- Rāfiʿ ibn Ḥuraymilah, and the Arab idolaters with him, demanded that Allah speak to them directly or send them a sign of their own choosing (6:124, 17:90-93, 25:21, 74:52).
- Earlier peoples made the same demands of their prophets, asking Mūsā to show them Allah (4:153, 2:55), because the hearts of the obstinate are alike in every age (51:52-53).
- The first demand, to be addressed like angels and prophets, is beneath reply. The second is answered by the many clear signs already given, which suffice people of sure faith but never the sealed heart (10:96-97).
- Learned deniers are still "without knowledge", for persisting in denial against clear proof is the conduct of the ignorant.

<<<VERSE 2:119>>>
Verse 119 turns to the Prophet ﷺ himself with reassurance about the limits of his task. "We have surely sent you with the truth ˹O Prophet˺ as a deliverer of good news and a warner. And you will not be accountable for the residents of the Hellfire".

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have surely sent you with the truth ˹O Prophet˺"**. With Islam, the Qur'an and the declaration of Allah's oneness, the guidance and the religion of truth in which there is no doubt. The previous verse (2:118) implied that those who persisted in denying the Prophet ﷺ did so from sheer malice and ignorance and could not be expected to reform. Since he was sent as a mercy to all the worlds, the thought that they were incorrigible was bound to sadden him on their account, so here Allah consoles him. He has been sent to mankind bearing the truth and the genuine faith.

**"as a deliverer of good news and a warner"**. Good news of Paradise for those who believe in what he brought and respond to the guidance, and a warning of the Fire for those who disbelieve and do not respond. His task has two sides: glad tidings for those who accept the truth, and a warning of dire punishment for those who deny it.

**"And you will not be accountable for the residents of the Hellfire"**. You will not be asked about the unbelief of those who rejected you, nor why they failed to believe, because delivering the message is the whole of your responsibility. Allah said likewise: "your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺. Judgment is for Us" (13:40), and "So, ˹continue to˺ remind ˹all, O Prophet˺, for your duty is only to remind. You are not ˹there˺ to compel them ˹to believe˺" (88:21-22), and "We know best what they say. And you ˹O Prophet˺ are not ˹there˺ to compel them ˹to believe˺. So remind with the Quran ˹only˺ those who fear My warning" (50:45), among many similar verses. A variant reading, lā tasʾal with the final vowel dropped, makes it an imperative, "do not ask about the inhabitants of the Fire", and one gloss explains that it is not fitting for him to ask about the people of Hell, or that he should not ask forgiveness for them. Ibn ʿAbbās said that one day the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, "Would that I knew what became of my parents", and the verse came down in answer, though this applies only on the imperative reading. Muqātil said the Prophet ﷺ remarked that the Jews would have believed had Allah sent His harm upon them, and Allah revealed the verse in response. Muḥammad ﷺ is assured that he will not be held responsible or called to account for those who willingly take the road to Hell. All he must do is keep performing his own function, without worrying about who accepts the truth and who does not.

### His description in the Torah

Imām Aḥmad recorded that ʿAṭāʾ ibn Yasār met ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ and asked him to describe the Messenger of Allah ﷺ as the Torah describes him. He answered: "Yes, by Allah. He is described in the Torah with some of the same qualities as in the Qur'an: O Prophet, We have sent you as a witness, a bearer of good news, a warner, and a refuge for the unlettered. You are My servant and My Messenger. I have named you al-Mutawakkil, the one who relies on Allah. He is not harsh or rough, nor loud in the markets. He does not repay evil with evil but forgives and pardons. Allah will not take him until through him He has set the crooked religion straight, so that people declare that there is no god but Allah, and through him He opens blind eyes, deaf ears and sealed hearts." Al-Bukhārī alone recorded this.

### Under his banner

The devotional reading hears Allah telling His Prophet ﷺ that He has singled him out with qualities He gave to no one else, so that all people stand under his banner. The accepted are those in conformity with him and the rejected those who oppose him (al-maqbūl man wāfaqaka wa-l-mardūd man khālafaka). He will not be questioned about the states of others, and he will not be turned away on anyone's account.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ was sent with the truth to bring good news of Paradise and warning of the Fire.
- He is not answerable for those who choose Hell. His duty is to deliver the message, not to compel belief (13:40, 88:21-22, 50:45).
- On the imperative reading, "do not ask about the people of the Fire", connected with his words about his parents or about the Jews.
- The Torah's description of him, witness, bringer of good news, warner, refuge of the unlettered, al-Mutawakkil, gentle in the markets and forgiving of wrongs, matches the Qur'an's.
