<<<VERSE 4:131>>>
The standing counsel: "To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Indeed, We have commanded those given the Scripture before you, as well as you, to be mindful of Allah" (4:131). After the rules of behaviour toward women and orphans, the passage returns to the promise for those who abide and the warning for those who contravene, the usual style of the Qur'ān.

### Phrase by phrase

**"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth"** states the ownership and the supreme authority over both. The refrain repeats through this passage with three shades in it, the first being the boundless reach of the power of creation, which lacks nothing.

**"We have commanded those given the Scripture before you, as well as you, to be mindful of Allah"** means that what was recommended to the People of the Scripture is recommended to you: taqwā of Allah, the worship of Him alone without partners. The people of the Torah were charged in the Torah, the people of the Gospel in the Gospel, and every Scripture people in its Scripture, and the community of Muḥammad ﷺ in its Book.

**"But if you disobey"** carries no threat to Allah, only to the disobedient. As Allah said in the word of Mūsā to his people: "If you disbelieve, you and all on the earth together, then verily, Allah is Rich, Owner of all praise" (14:8). And as Allah said of those who disbelieved and turned away: "But Allah was not in need of them" (64:6). He is far too Rich to need His servants, and He deserves praise for every decision and commandment. The second shade of the refrain is this need-freedom: whoever denies the good counsel given takes nothing from Him, who stands ever-praised in His pristine glory. Over every soul He keeps perfect watch, knowing what it deserves, the Watcher and Witness of all things.

### The life the counsel builds

The point of the whole teaching is the life it builds. A believer's virtuous life is possible only as a God-worshipper at heart and soul. Discovering Allah as the Lord of the universe, fearing and trusting Him alone, and turning the full attention to the Hereafter, these enable the godly life in this world, and these were the focus of prophetic teaching.

The inward reading hears the direct address. Worshipper and abstainer alike are told to come fully into the street of godwariness, make godwariness their shelter, and leave the road of uncertainty and suspicion. So He commands, giving success to one group and placing another in the road of abandonment, and to all He gives the awareness:

> I am without needs. I have no benefit from the obedience of the one given success, nor any loss from the disobedience of the one abandoned. Everything in the heavens and the earth is My possession and kingdom. All are My predetermined things and artifacts. Had I wanted, I would have created all successful, or all abandoned. No one can protest against Me, and there is no turning away from My decree.

### In short

- The counsel of taqwā is one counsel, given to the Torah people, the Gospel people and the Qur'ān people alike.
- Disbelief harms none but the disbeliever, for the Owner of the heavens and earth needs nothing.
- The repetition teaches creation's limitless scope, Allah's need-freedom, and His perfect watch over every soul.
- The godly life is built on the God-worshipping heart, and godwariness is the shelter ordered for it.

<<<VERSE 4:132>>>
The Guardian: "To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs" (4:132). The refrain returns to ground the counsel a second time.

### Phrase by phrase

**"To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth"** is repeated, in the reading of one school, to reaffirm the reason why the fear of Allah is necessary. What is contained in the heavens and the earth belongs to Him, and He witnesses that belonging.

**"And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs"** means He alone takes care of the affairs of His creation, the Guardian and Disposer of all, so that the trusting heart needs no other trustee.

### The world of trial

The wider teaching explains why the trustee must be feared as well as trusted. The present world is a world of trial, built to find out who is good and who is bad, and to serve that purpose it provides every kind of freedom. Here a person may even label his misdeeds good deeds. He may sin freely and pass himself off as pious, find beautiful words to justify himself, refute an open truth and dress the denial finely. He may build a life on honour-seeking, hoarding and self-glorification and still be hailed as a servant of truth, exploit Allah's religion for worldly purposes and flourish, or abandon the lawful for the unlawful and go scot free. Of all the aspects of a person's relationship with Allah, the most important is whether he fears Him, for the fear of Allah enables the responsible life. Without it nothing checks the path of evil, and evil here can be couched in beautiful words, injustice brings great advancement, and every tyrant finds fine words to hide his tyranny.

The inward reading addresses the sincerely truthful and the lovers. To them He says that the seven heavens, the seven earths and all within them are His. The face was not meant to turn toward them nor the heart to hang on them, held back from Him. Their purpose was to be shown and adorned for the soul, that once all is put aside and the face comes to Him, the whole of them enters service and lies in the hands. Of it the report says:

> O this world! Serve those who serve Me, and trouble those who serve you!

The story of Sahl Tustarī is well known. The caliph of the day offered him great wealth, and he accepted nothing. Someone asked him why, and Sahl called upon Allah to lift the veil from the questioner's eyes, and the man looked and saw a whole realm of gems and pearls before him. Sahl said:

> O chevalier! I have no need for the wealth of the caliph. The whole world is at my command. The storehouses of the earth have been offered to me, but I do not want them.

And the word goes further:

> Why do you stay in this low place like crows looking for carrion? Break the cage and fly at once like peacocks to the heights. All on the face of gold and gems is unbelief. If you have the religion's fervour, step beyond the gold.

### In short

- The refrain grounds the fear, and the trusteeship of Allah covers every affair of creation.
- The world of trial permits evil to dress itself finely, and only the fear of Allah keeps the soul responsible.
- The heavens and the earth were made to be shown, not clung to, and whoever turns his face to Allah finds them all in his service.
- Sahl Tustarī refused the caliph's wealth with the storehouses of the earth already open to him.

<<<VERSE 4:133>>>
The replaceable: "If it is His Will, He can remove you altogether, O humanity, and replace you with others. And Allah is Most Capable to do so" (4:133). The refrain's third shade is the mercy behind the might.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If it is His Will, He can remove you altogether, O humanity"** means the power to take you away for disobedience, to destroy you and create in your stead beings better and more obedient.

**"and replace you with others"** is spelled out elsewhere in the Book, where the turning away of a people is answered by their exchange for another, unlike them in every good.

**"And Allah is Most Capable to do so"** closes the matter.

The inward reading weighs the might on eternity's scale. Whoever can do without others in all eternity without beginning, istaghnā ʿanhu fī āzālihi, needs nothing of them in all eternity without end. He, it is said, has no need for anyone, while the servant cannot do without Him for even a single breath. The things decreed know no limit: were it not this man, it would be that one, were it not one servant, it would be servants. The only one without substitute or replacement is the One, the Unique.

### In short

- Obedience is not a favour done to Allah, for humanity itself is replaceable at a word.
- The one needed from before beginning needs nothing after end, and every substitute but Him has a substitute.

<<<VERSE 4:134>>>
The two rewards: "Whoever desires the reward of this world, then ˹let them know that˺ with Allah are the rewards of this world and the Hereafter. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Seeing" (4:134). The verse answers the one whose ultimate desire is this life.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever desires the reward of this world"** addresses the one whose ultimate desire is this life, and the answer is the ownership: Allah holds the rewards of this life and the Hereafter, and whoever asks Him for both finds enrichment, award and sufficiency. The Qur'ān has drawn the two askers already. Some say only:

> Our Lord! Give us in this world!

and for such there is no portion in the Hereafter. Others say:

> Our Lord! Give us in this world that which is good and in the Hereafter that which is good, and save us from the torment of the Fire!

and for them there is a share for what they have earned. The scale is set in the Book: "Whosoever desires the reward of the Hereafter, We give him increase in his reward" (42:20), and on the other side, "Whoever desires the quick-passing, We readily grant him what We will for whom We like" (17:18), until the words "See how We prefer one above another in this world" (17:21). One school reads the verse as a rebuke. Why demand the lower reward? The higher is there for the sincere devotion, and whatever reward one seeks is found with Him alone. He is All-Hearing of what you say, All-Seeing of your works.

The inward reading sees the two corrections of the heart. To hearts attached to the transitory of this world He held out the hereafter, its blessing standing far above the aspirations of this paltry place. Then, when the search was raised to the hereafter, He cut the seekers off from every external and created thing, saying: "And Allah is better and more enduring" (20:73).

### In short

- Both rewards are with Allah, and the asker may ask for both, the good of this world and the good of the next.
- The one who asks for this world alone buys no portion in the Hereafter.
- The heart attached to the transitory is reminded of the Hereafter, and the heart fixed on the Hereafter is freed from everything created.

<<<VERSE 4:135>>>
The justice of the witness: "O believers! Stand firm for justice as witnesses for Allah even if it is against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives" (4:135). The verse instructs all Muslims in upholding justice and true witness, and removes, in a manner most eloquent, the things that stand in their way.

### The purpose of prophets and scriptures

The real purpose of sending prophets and scriptures stands here in the open. A verse of sūrat al-Māʾidah that follows this sūrah carries the same subject, nearly the same words, and sūrat al-Ḥadīd states the purpose outright. Sayyidna Ādam was sent as the vice-regent of Allah in this world, followed by the blessed prophets one after another with Scriptures and missions, so that justice would prevail in the world with peace coming in its wake, every human being, as a distinct individual, adopting justice as a hallmark within the circle of his or her influence. As for the chronically contumacious, whom good counsel, education and communication cannot bring to the path of justice, they are the ones to be compelled to observe it through legal process, due penalization and punishment. The words of sūrat al-Ḥadīd are these:

> We have sent Our messengers with clear signs, and We have sent with them the Book and the Balance, so that people stand firm with justice, and We have sent the iron, in which there is great awe and benefits for people. (57:25)

The sending down of iron, mentioned at the end of that verse, hints at the eventuality when counsel alone fails to make people abide by justice. Then the compulsive miscreant is disciplined by the deterrent of iron, of chains and bars and other weapons, in the best interest of establishing justice.

Establishing and maintaining justice is therefore not simply the duty of governments and courts. The instruction is given to every human being, obligating each to fulfil the demands of justice personally and to make efforts that others stay equally firm about seeing justice done. A certain level of justice does fall to government and its officials, when the wicked and the rebellious flout justice personally and stop others from carrying out its dictates, and on such occasions penalization and punishment become necessary, an enforcement only government can perform. In the world today, even fairly educated people think the dispensing of justice is the sole business of governments, and this attitude is a major reason why government and people stand as two confronting parties in every country, a gulf of conflict dividing the rulers and the ruled. Masses everywhere demand justice from their government yet are not themselves ready to uphold it, and the outcome is visible everywhere: law on hold, the crime wave riding high. Law-making bodies cost millions, elections shake the earth, the elected make laws with care, the machinery of government goes about enforcing them. Compare a hundred years of it, from 1857 to 1957, and the data will confirm that with every increase in law-making and every new kind of police, crimes escalated and people remained further deprived of justice, and more disorder showed up in the world.

The only guarantee of universal peace is the belief in the Hereafter and the fear of Allah. This twosome is the channel through which the obligations of the ruler and the ruled merge on a common platform, everyone pulsating with the sense of individual responsibility, and the masses can no longer say that respecting law was the job of the officials. The verses on justice end with exactly this article of faith. This sūrah ends with the reminder that Allah is all-aware of what you do. Sūrat al-Māʾidah first gives the instruction to observe taqwā and then the awareness. Sūrat al-Ḥadīd ends with the power and might. Seen together, the three verses do not rest at instructing officials and masses to uphold justice personally and see that others do. They go further, focusing all attention on the decisive reality able to generate a great revolution in human life: the power and domain of Almighty Allah, the thought of standing present before Him, of reckoning and retribution. That, in a nutshell, was the secret of the peace that prevailed in the less educated world of a hundred years ago, and its abandonment is what has deprived the modern, progress-claiming, satellite-borne world of genuine peace. Science can climb the skies, camp on stars and visit the depths of seas, yet the end-product of all that equipment is not found on far away stars. It is in the message brought by the Prophet ﷺ who appeared in Arabia, within the belief in Allah and in the life-to-come and its reckoning: "Listen! Hearts rest at peace through the remembrance, the dhikr, of Allah" (13:28). Day by day, the discoveries of science do nothing but confirm the most perfect power of Almighty Allah and the mastery of His creation, before which every human claim to progress stands humbled. The Persian line asks it plainly: with no wisdom and no vision, what is the use of what you do?

To recapitulate, the Qur'ān has declared the establishment of justice the very purpose of a universal order, and proposed a system which, once adopted, would metamorphose this blood-thirsty and iniquitous world into a society of the virtuous. That is a Paradise now, present and immediate, well before the promised Paradise of the Hereafter. One explanation of the verse that promises two Paradises to the one who fears the high station of his Lord runs exactly so: the God-fearing have two Paradises, one in the Hereafter and one right here in this world. There is nothing utopian about it, for the feasibility was demonstrated by the harbinger of the message himself, who bequeathed it to posterity not as an unproven theory but as a practical and functioning system. After him came al-Khulafāʾ al-Rāshidūn, the rightly-guided caliphs, and many rulers who followed the Sunnah of the noble Prophet ﷺ strictly. Whenever they acted by the principles set for them, the lion and the lamb drank at the same water trough, the image of the ideal climate of justice for all, strong and weak, poor and rich, labourer and capitalist. Law was respected in bolted homes and in the darkness of nights. These are facts of authentic history, corroborated and confessed even by open-minded non-Muslims.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Stand firm for justice as witnesses for Allah"** opens with the word qisṭ, which, beginning with the vowel of i, means justice and fairness. The true sense of it is that every holder of a right be given the due in full, which includes the rights of Allah and all kinds of human rights. Upholding justice carries a wide thrust: nobody shall inflict injustice on anybody, the perpetrator of injustice shall be stopped, the victim shall be supported, appearing as witness shall not be avoided when it would restore a usurped right, the witness shall be true and factual as it really is, for or against anyone, and those who hold the reins of authority shall treat both parties equally and equitably, hearing the statements carefully, investigating every effort possible, and observing perfect justice in the verdict. Witnesses for Allah parallels the command to establish the testimony for Allah (65:2). The testimony is to be delivered precisely, for His sake, correct, truly just, and free of alteration, change or deletion.

**"even if it is against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives"** names the first impediment. Give correct testimony and say the truth when asked, even if harm follows, for with every matter of obedience Allah opens a way out and sends relief. Even against parents and kin, do not compromise, for the truth presides above everyone and is preferred to everyone. One record ties the verse to Miqyās ibn Ṣababah, who had something to testify against his own father. Another, reported through as-Suddi, tells of a poor man and a rich man who brought their dispute to the Prophet ﷺ. His inclination went toward the poor man, on the thought that a poor man cannot wrong a rich one, and Allah wished justice established over poor and rich alike, and the verse came down.

**"Be they rich or poor, Allah is best to ensure their interests"** removes the second pull. Do not favour the rich in testimony, nor pity the poor, for Allah is the caretaker of both, a better Protector than you, with better knowledge of their good, closer to the two than you are.

**"So do not let your desires cause you to deviate"** warns against the whole inner crowd, desire, lust and the hatred held against others. Stand for justice in all situations, as the command says: "And let not the enmity and hatred of others make you avoid justice. Be just: that is nearer to piety" (5:8). The hadith of Khaybar shows the command standing. ʿAbdullāh ibn Rawāḥah was sent by the Prophet ﷺ to assess the tax on the fruits and produce of the Jews of Khaybar, and a bribe was offered him, that he might go easy on them. His answer has stayed on record:

> By Allah! I have come to you from the dearest of the creation to me, and you are more hated by me than an equivalent number of apes and swine. However, my love for him and hatred for you shall not prevent me from being just with you.

On hearing it they said that this justice is the basis on which the heavens and the earth were created. The same removal of impediments appears in the other verse: malice against a people should not bid you to desert justice, for that too is nearer to taqwā (5:2).

**"If you distort the testimony or refuse to give it"** names the two corruptions of witness. The first word, Talwū, includes distortion and intentional lying, according to Mujāhid and several others among the Salaf, as the Book says of a party who distort the Scripture with their tongues (3:78). The second, Tuʿriḍū, includes hiding and withholding the testimony, as the word says: "Who hides it, surely, his heart is sinful" (2:283). And the Prophet ﷺ said that the best witness is the one who gives his testimony before anyone asks.

### The impediments and the address

Justice is normally impeded by two things. The first is love, kinship, friendship or linkage, which pushes the witness to testify in favour of someone so he stays shielded from loss or profits by it, and moves the judge to decide for the party of his linkage. The second is hostility, which may take the witness to testify against someone and bend the judge to an unfavourable ruling. Love and hate are the two passions that carry a person off the path of justice into injustice and oppression. The verse of this sūrah removes the impediment of kinship, and the verse of sūrat al-Māʾidah removes the impediment of grudge and malice. A slight difference of address runs between them. Here it is: uphold justice, witnesses for Allah. There it is: be steadfast for Allah, witnesses for justice. Most readings take the change to show that both are interpretations of the same reality. Worth noticing too is the mode of expression. Both verses prefer multiple-worded sentences, though the command could have been given in the single word of the verse of sūrat al-Ḥujurāt, Aqsiṭu, do justice (49:9). The longer sentence indicates that accidental fairness in a single case does not liquidate the responsibility, for even the most evil and tyrannical ruler may find himself having done justice in some case. The word of the verse, upholders, qawwāmīn, makes justice a constant duty, at all times, in all conditions, for every friend and foe.

### The two principles of universal justice

The principles enunciated through these verses carry two major aspects. First, officials and masses alike have been alerted to the supreme subduing power of Almighty Allah and the ultimate reckoning of the Day of Retribution, so that all could think, stop and deter, or be prepared to face the consequences. The masses themselves have to respect law, and the officials who enforce it have to keep the thought of Allah and the Hereafter before them, remaining servants rather than thoughtless masters of Allah's multitudes. Law is to be a source of service and betterment, never of increased worry, never a mill of red tape grinding the victims of injustice, never an injustice multiplied with injustices, never a thing sold for mean desires or paltry gains. By the words be steadfast for Allah, witnesses for Allah, both officials and masses are exhorted to act for Allah, with sincerity at its best. Second, the responsibility of establishing justice as a way of life rests on the shoulders of all human beings. The verses of this sūrah and of al-Māʾidah address the Muslim community by name, but the verse of al-Ḥadīd, saying that mankind should stand firm with justice, binds all human beings. And by saying even against yourselves, the instruction adds that justice is not something to be demanded from others only. It must be exacted from one's own self: to say nothing against what is true and just, even when the declaration goes against oneself and brings loss, a loss insignificant, tiny and transitory, whereas placating the self with flat lies buys the severe punishment of the Day of Retribution.

The divide runs through daily life. A person is often confronted with the path of desire and self-interest on one side and the path of justice on the other. Those unmindful of Allah, not convinced that He watches on all such occasions, follow the path of desire and count it an achievement to have ignored the call of truth. Those who fear Allah and regard Him as their guardian keep their eyes set on truth and justice and act by their demands, sincerely desiring to leave this world having done no injustice to anyone. Their desire for justice runs so high that behaviour falling short of its absolute standard becomes intolerable to them, and when wrongdoing confronts them they dare to proclaim the demands of truth, even against family interest or worldly considerations. From the standpoint of justice it is also very wrong to give the influential their due while denying the rights of the weak and without influence. A true believer deals justly with everyone, weak or powerful.

The inward reading defines the standing. Justice, qisṭ, is being fair and equitable. To be upright for Allah in fairness is to uphold His rights against yourself and to seek the same from everyone under your authority, by exhortation to kindness, by rebuke of the despicable, by direction to a legal issue, or by guidance to the right thing, the ḥaqq. The deepest realization never touches the innermost self of one who holds back what is rightly due to Allah. The foundation of religion is the preferring of the right of the Real over the right of created beings. Whoever prefers anyone, father, mother, child, kinsman or relation, to Allah, or holds back any portion due to Him, is not upright in justice.

### In short

- The purpose of prophets, scriptures and the Balance is universal justice, with iron held in reserve for the compulsive.
- The duty binds every individual, and the belief in the Hereafter with the fear of Allah is the only working guarantee of it.
- Testimony goes to Allah alone, over self, parents and kin, unbought by wealth and unsoftened by poverty, as Ibn Rawāḥah's answer at Khaybar shows.
- Distortion and withholding are the two corruptions, and the disclosed testimony stands highest.
- Love and hate are the two impediments, the constant word qawwāmīn makes justice a standing duty, and the heart that prefers any relation to Allah has left the ranks of the upright.
