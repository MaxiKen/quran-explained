<<<VERSE 2:2>>>
After the three letters, the surah makes a claim about the Qur'an itself: "This is the Book! There is no doubt about it—a guide for those mindful ˹of Allah˺". Three things are said in one breath. This is the Book. It contains no doubt. It guides a particular kind of person. Each phrase carries more than it seems to, and the Companions and the scholars after them drew out of this short verse a whole account of what the Qur'an is, what doubt means, what guidance means, and who the people of taqwā are.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is the Book!"** In Arabic the pointing word is dhālika, "that", the word used for something at a distance, although the Book is here in front of the reader. The distance is a mark of honour: the demonstrative is used to magnify the Book. There is also a quieter reason. The surah before this one ended with the prayer "Guide us along the Straight Path", and "that Book" points back to the request. The prayer has been answered, and the Qur'an is the answer, the detailed map of the Straight Path for anyone who asks for it and is willing to walk it. The Book is the Qur'an that Muhammad ﷺ was reciting to his people. It has also been said that "the Book" is the Preserved Tablet, or the Book that Allah promised to send down on the day the covenant was taken from the souls, or that the sentence means there is no doubt that the description of Muhammad ﷺ is found in the Torah and the Gospel.

**"There is no doubt about it"** translates lā rayba fīh, and rayb means doubt. Ibn ʿAbbās, in the reports of Abū Mālik and Abū Ṣāliḥ handed down through as-Suddī, and Ibn Masʿūd with other Companions, in the report of Murrah al-Hamadānī, explained it exactly so: there is nothing in it to doubt. Abū ad-Dardāʾ, Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Nāfiʿ, ʿAṭāʾ, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, Qatādah and Ismāʿīl ibn Abī Khālid said the same, and Ibn Abī Ḥātim said he knew of no disagreement about it. The meaning is that the Qur'an is beyond doubt revealed from Allah, as the opening of Sūrat as-Sajdah says in almost the same words: "The revelation of this Book is—beyond doubt—from the Lord of all worlds" (32:1-2). Some scholars read the phrase as a command in the form of a statement: do not doubt the Qur'an. There is no doubt about where it came from and no doubt about what it contains. The challenge to anyone who does doubt comes a few verses later: "if you are in doubt about what We have revealed to Our servant, then produce a sûrah like it" (2:23). A thousand people of small or twisted understanding may raise objections, and the sentence remains true.

Where a reciter pauses changes the sense slightly. Some stop after "no doubt" and continue with "in it is guidance for the mindful". It is better to stop after "no doubt about it", because then "a guide" describes the Book itself, which is a stronger statement than saying that guidance can be found inside it. Grammatically, "there is no doubt about it" is what is said about "that Book", and "a guide" is a second statement about it: the Book guides.

**"a guide for those mindful ˹of Allah˺"** translates hudan lil-muttaqīn. Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and other Companions said that it means a light for the people of taqwā. The Qur'an is itself guidance, but the guidance in it is received by the righteous in particular. The reason is given in several places: "It is a guide and a healing to the believers. As for those who disbelieve, there is deafness in their ears" (41:44), "We send down the Quran as a healing and mercy for the believers, but it only increases the wrongdoers in loss" (17:82), and "there has come to you a warning from your Lord, a cure for what is in the hearts, a guide, and a mercy for the believers" (10:57). The same light that guides one person leaves another in the dark, and the difference lies in the person.

### Two kinds of guidance

The word hudā is used in the Qur'an for two different things, and both are present in this verse. The first is the faith that lives in the heart. Only Allah can create that, and no one else can put it there, not even a prophet: "You surely cannot guide whoever you like ˹O Prophet˺, but it is Allah Who guides whoever He wills" (28:56), "You are not responsible for people's guidance" (2:272), "Whoever Allah allows to stray, none can guide" (7:186), and "Whoever Allah guides is truly guided. But whoever He leaves to stray, you will never find for them a guiding mentor" (18:17). The second is to explain the truth, point to it and lead toward it. That is what the Prophet ﷺ and the Book do: "you are truly leading ˹all˺ to the Straight Path" (42:52), "You ˹O Prophet˺ are only a warner. And every people had a guide" (13:7), and "As for Thamûd, We showed them guidance, but they preferred blindness over guidance" (41:17). Thamūd were shown the way and refused it. Allah has "shown them the two ways" (90:10), and the correct view is that the two ways are the road of good and the road of evil.

This also answers an obvious objection: surely guidance is needed most by those who are not yet mindful of Allah? The answer lies in degrees. The Qur'an provides guidance to all people, and in one sense to everything in the universe. The first degree of guidance is shared by all mankind and even by animals. The second is particular to human beings and jinn. The third is reserved for those who have drawn near to Allah and found His favour, and that degree has no upper limit. The verse speaks of the last two. For the person who accepts the guidance, there is the hope of rising to the rank of the mindful. For the person who is already mindful, there is more guidance, without end. Specifying the mindful is not a denial of guidance to anyone else.

A further reading makes the same point through human nature. The search for truth is built into a person. Whoever sincerely searches uncovers his own nature, and with Allah's help its vague longings start to receive definite answers. The urge to bow to a higher power becomes bowing to the Almighty. The wish to give oneself for something greater becomes spending one's wealth for Allah's cause. The question about what lies beyond this life finds its answer in faith in the life to come. People who find the truth in this way become free of complexes. They recognise the truth wherever it is and whoever speaks it, and no rigidity, custom or prejudice stands between them and it. Allah protects such people, His order carries them along a sure course in this world, and Paradise is their destination. Only those who seek the truth find it, and whoever seeks it does find it. On this road there is no gap between seeking and finding.

### Who the muttaqīn are

The muttaqīn are the people of taqwā. The word's root meaning is to keep away from what one dislikes, and in the language of the religion it means fearing Allah and keeping away from breaking His commands. Ibn ʿAbbās said the muttaqīn are the believers who keep clear of shirk, associating anything with Allah, and who act in obedience to Him. He also said they are those who fear Allah's punishment, which would fall on them if they left the guidance they recognise and know, and who hope for His mercy by believing in what He revealed. Qatādah said they are the people the next verses describe: those "who believe in the unseen, establish prayer, and donate from what We have provided for them" (2:3) and what follows. Ibn Jarīr held that the verse includes all of these meanings together, and that is the correct view. Another gloss says they are the people who guard themselves against disbelief, shirk and indecencies, and that the Book is a favour to the believers and a mercy to the mindful of this community. In the shortest description, they lean toward piety by keeping the commands and avoiding what is forbidden, and so shield themselves from the Fire.

ʿAṭiyah as-Saʿdī reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that a servant does not reach the rank of the muttaqīn until he leaves what is harmless for fear of falling into what is harmful. At-Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah recorded it, and at-Tirmidhī graded it ḥasan gharīb. ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb once asked Ubayy ibn Kaʿb what taqwā is. Ubayy asked him whether he had ever walked along a path full of thorns. ʿUmar said he had. "What did you do?" Ubayy asked. ʿUmar said, "I rolled up my sleeves and struggled through." Ubayy said, "That is taqwā."

The verse was also read as Allah speaking directly: this is the Book that has come from Me, and there is no doubt of it. If you believe in it I will guide you, and if you do not, I will punish you.

### The inner reading

"The writing in which there is no doubt" has been taken as an allusion to what Allah has written upon Himself for the community of Muhammad ﷺ, that His mercy outruns His anger, as in "Your Lord has taken upon Himself to be Merciful" (6:54). It has also been taken as the faith and recognition that Allah has written on the hearts of the believers, as in "Allah has instilled faith in their hearts" (58:22). On this reading the verse is a love letter: I have written the mark of faith in your heart, mixed in the perfume of friendship, lit the candle of union, sealed your heart with love and inscribed longing in your awareness. In the Tablet I wrote your description and showed it to Jibrīl. In your heart I wrote My description, and how could I have shown that to an enemy? In the Tablet I wrote your loyalty and your failures. In your heart I wrote praise and recognition, and what I have written about Myself does not change. When Mūsā (Moses) cut a stone from the mountain and the Torah was written on it, the stone turned to emerald. The heart of the one who recognises Allah was hard stone, and when His name was written on it, it became a book of glory. As for "a guide for the godwary", the Qur'an is guidance for the mindful and healing for the faithful, a source of intimacy and a help toward clarity. It is a lamp for hearts and a mirror for the eyes, a key that opens the ears, a cure for pain, light for the eyes of those who know Him, springtime for the spirits of His friends, a warning for the fearful and a mercy for the believers.

### In short

- "That Book" points from afar to honour the Qur'an and points back to the prayer for the Straight Path, which the Qur'an answers.
- There is no doubt about its origin or its contents. The Companions agreed on this meaning without exception, and it is better to pause after "no doubt about it" so that "a guide" describes the Book itself.
- Guidance is of two kinds: the faith only Allah creates in a heart, and the explaining and pointing that the Book and the Prophet ﷺ do. The Qur'an guides everyone in the second sense and the mindful in the first.
- The muttaqīn avoid shirk, obey, fear Allah's punishment and hope for His mercy. Taqwā, in Ubayy's picture, is walking a thorny path with your sleeves rolled up, and in the Prophet's ﷺ words it means leaving the harmless for fear of the harmful.
