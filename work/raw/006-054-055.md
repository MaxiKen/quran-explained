<<<VERSE 6:54>>>
When the believers in Our revelations come to you, say, “Peace be upon you! Your Lord has taken upon Himself to be Merciful. Whoever among you commits evil ignorantly ˹or recklessly˺ then repents afterwards and mends their ways, then Allah is truly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.”

The verse comes straight after a passage in which certain men were asked to be moved out of the Prophet's circle to make room for their masters. It answers that with a greeting nobody had thought to offer them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When the believers in Our revelations come to you"** The revelations here carry both meanings the word can bear: the verses of the Qur'an, and the wide signs of Allah's power set out in creation. Belief in either kind brings a person into the same room.

**"say, 'Peace be upon you'"** Two senses are given for this address. It can mean: pass on to them the greeting of peace from your Lord, which is the highest honour such a person can be given. Or it can mean: give them the good news that they are safe. Anything they fell short in stands forgiven, and no calamity will catch them. The verb matters. Allah does not tell the Prophet to excuse these people, or to defend their poverty, or to put up with them. He tells him to greet them.

**"Your Lord has taken upon Himself to be Merciful"** The word "Lord" is itself the proof offered to the hearer. A Lord is a nurturer and sustainer, and a nurturer does not let what he nurtures go to waste. Then the promise is made heavier and kinder at the same time: he has prescribed this mercy on himself. A promise is one thing. A mercy written down as an obligation he chose to take on, out of generosity and compassion, is another. An ordinary good person would not go back on his word.

**"Whoever among you commits evil ignorantly"** The wording is not that the sinner did not know the ruling. It is that he acted the way an unaware person acts, without reckoning what the act leads to. On that reading no one is excluded, because every act of disobedience has some of that blindness in it.

**"then repents afterwards and mends their ways"** Repentance is being ashamed of what was done. Mending is making the deed sound again, and that takes work of a specific kind, taken up below.

**"then Allah is truly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"** Forgiveness is not the end of the sentence. A person who is only forgiven has been spared. This pair of names promises being spared and then given something besides.

### Which event does this verse answer

Leading authorities differ, and the difference turns out to be harmless. Most of them relate the verse to what came just before it: the demand that the chiefs of Quraysh put to the Prophet through his uncle Abu Talib, that he first remove the poor people he sat with, since they could not bear to sit beside them, and that then they would come and listen. It was Umar ibn al-Khattab who advised that this would be no great matter. If the chiefs asked, the poor could step aside for a sitting, hear the word of Allah, and perhaps believe. The revelation in the previous verses refused that advice outright, and called it cruelty and injustice. Umar then understood how serious his suggestion had been, and came to apologise for it. These verses were sent down to comfort him. In their gist, the Prophet is told to assure such people that there is no retribution for a mistake, that they will be blessed besides, and that a Muslim who does evil in ignorance, then is alerted, repents and watches himself afterwards, is forgiven what went before and is not deprived of blessing in this world or the next.

Some commentators take the verse differently, as a free-standing rule for anyone who sins, then feels shame, then repents and corrects himself. A little thought shows the two readings do not fight. It is agreed that an injunction of the Qur'an revealed against the background of one particular event, but whose words and subject are general, is not chained to that event. It keeps the force of a general rule. So even if the verse came about this episode, it stands open for every sinner who wakes up after the fact.

### How wide the word "ignorantly" reaches

One might hear the promise as narrow: it covers a person who did not know, and leaves out the man who sinned with his eyes open. The wording resists that. The Arabic noun used here is not the plain opposite of knowledge. It is used in opposition to forbearance and dignity, for ignorance shown in conduct, the behaviour of someone who has not thought about the end of the act. That is exactly what a sin is whenever anyone commits one. Hence the saying of certain pious elders that whoever acts against a command given by Allah and his Messenger is ignorant, in this practical sense, whether or not he was informed. And countless clear statements of the Qur'an and sound reports establish that every sin can be covered by repentance, done in heedlessness or in knowledge, deliberately, out of self-wickedness, or following one's own desire.

### What repentance and mending require

The promise in this verse is conditioned on two things: repentance, and the correction of the deed. Repentance is remorse. There is a hadith to that effect: repentance is another name for being ashamed of what was done. As for the correction of the deed, it means that what happened will not be repeated. That takes a firm resolve to stay away from the sin, and a habit of auditing one's conduct, and it also takes settling what the sin left unsettled.

So the way is prepared for those who fear the gathering, and they are the people of taqwa, and those who live in awe of their Lord, and who dread the terrible day of reckoning (13:21, 23:57). They are the ones described in the verse of the cave, who call on their Lord morning and evening, and the same Lord who has said: "Call on Me, I will respond to your invocation" (40:60). That is what supplication is worth here. The answer to these chiefs is the answer of Nuh to his people, who said: "Shall we believe in you, when the meekest of the people follow you?" (26:111). He said their account was for Allah and not for him.

The practical side of mending is spelled out at some length. Obligations that belong to Allah and were left undone through heedlessness have to be made up. Prayers, that is, the salah, and fasts, the sawm, missed out of laziness are replaced by making them up. Zakah unpaid is paid now. If there was a shortcoming in the sacrifice, the qurbani, or in the charity at the breaking of Ramadan, that is settled. A hajj that was fard on the person and never performed is performed now, and if he cannot do it in person, he arranges for it to be done on his behalf, and if he has neither the means to arrange a proxy nor the time to make up what he owes while he lives, then he leaves a will, so that his heirs pay the ransom of what he owed or arrange the pilgrimage for him.

The rights of Allah's servants go the same way. Property taken unlawfully is returned to its owner, or he is asked to forgive it. Pain caused by word or hand is answered by asking the person who was hurt to pardon. If that is no longer possible, because he has died or gone somewhere unknown, then the way out is to keep praying for his forgiveness before Allah steadily. It may be hoped that the holder of the right will be satisfied, and that the debt will be lifted from the one who could not reach him in his lifetime.

### The mercy that was written down

The scale of this is given in a report narrated by Abu Hurayrah, that the Messenger of Allah said: when Allah finished the creation, he wrote, in a Book that is with him above the Throne, his Arsh: "My mercy prevails over my anger." It is recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim, and in the collection of Ahmad, and it is in the two sahihs besides. Salman said that he found it written in the Torah, that when Allah created the heavens and the earth and what is in them, he divided his mercy into a hundred parts. One part he spread over the whole creation. Every trace of tenderness you meet among people, animals and other created things comes from that one part: the love between parent and child, between brothers and sisters, between husbands and wives, between relatives, neighbours and friends. The ninety-nine parts he kept for himself. Other reports give the same content as a hadith of the Prophet, which shows how far this mercy was meant to reach.

Set against that, no act of worship or obedience is good enough to be presented to Allah as it stands, by an angel or by a human. Deeds looked at on their own do not bear inspection. Looked at in relation to the highest of the high, what we managed was not much better than bad. Still, a person should be thankful for what he was able to do, because nobody is free of real fault unless Allah protects him. Justice would then leave nobody safe from a general punishment. What actually happens is that every human being is showered with blessings all the time, and that is the direct outcome of the mercy which the Lord and nurturer of the universe wrote down as his own responsibility.

### The inner reading

One Sufi treatment of this verse draws a line between two kinds of people. The faithful look first at the artifacts and the signs, and reach Allah through them. The recognizers reach him first, and then return from him to the signs. The address is put on the lips of Allah: "O Muhammad", and he is told to convey the greeting of peace to those who believe by way of the signs, while those who find Allah without intermediary and without the artifacts receive it without one. That last kind is what is meant by the word: "Peace, a word from an ever-merciful Lord" (36:58). The Pir of the Tariqah said, in effect, that whoever recognises Allah through artifacts leans on secondary causes, whoever recognises him through the attributes is confined by what is reported, whoever recognises him by allusion is still looking for company, and whoever is taken hold of by him is protected from himself. Al-Wasiti said that they reached worship through his mercy, and not the other way round, and that whatever they attained with him came to them through mercy rather than through their own acts. For the Prophet said: "Not even I, unless Allah envelops me with his mercy."

It has come in a report, in the first person, that Allah says: you called on me and I answered you, you asked me and I gave you, you turned your back on me and I let you be, you ran off and I kept watch over you, you disobeyed me and I veiled you, come back to me and I welcome you. This is what the greeting of peace in the verse rests on.

The passage as a whole keeps its ear open for those who call on their Lord morning and evening, in the sense given by Said bin al-Musayyib, al-Hasan and Qatadah, that the wording refers to the obligatory prayers, sought with sincerity in acts of worship and obedience, seeking the generous face of Allah. They are occupied with remembrance, and their rank in this life is not the question. When the idolators of Quraysh said: is it these that Allah has favoured from among us, they were repeating the objection of the people of Nuh, and the same answer closes. Heraclius, emperor of Rome, asked Abu Sufyan whether the nobles of his people or the weak followed Muhammad, and Abu Sufyan said: rather the weak. Heraclius said: such is the case with the followers of the messengers.

### In short

- The poor believers who were to be cleared out of the sitting are the first addressees of a divine greeting of peace, and that is the answer to the chiefs' demand.
- "Your Lord has written mercy on himself" is a promise backed by the name Lord, and preserved in writing, so it is not a favour that can be withdrawn on a mood.
- Sin is called acting ignorantly in conduct, not in information, so the door of repentance is open to the deliberate sinner as much as to the heedless one.
- Repentance needs remorse, and mending needs restitution: missed acts of worship made up, zakah, hajj and sacrifice settled, and the rights of people returned or pardoned.
- The reports about the hundred parts of mercy, and the writing above the Throne, are the background that makes "All-Forgiving, Most Merciful" a large promise rather than a polite one.

<<<VERSE 6:55>>>
This is how We make Our signs clear, so the way of the wicked may become distinct

This verse is the hinge at the end of the passage. It steps back and names what has just been done.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This is how We make Our signs clear"** In the same way that the earlier rulings, arguments and stories were set out, the Qur'an is expounded in detail: commands, prohibitions, and what follows each of them. Whatever a responsible person needs explained, in the affairs of this life and of religion, is explained.

**"so the way of the wicked may become distinct"** The path of those who defy the prophets is made apparent and clear, so that it can be seen for what it is and avoided. Truth becomes manifest so that it can be worked out in deeds. A variant reading takes the verb as addressed to the Prophet himself, so that the sense is: so that you may discern the way of the wicked. On either reading, the exposition of the signs sorts the roads rather than forcing the traveller.

The concrete case named here is the way of the idolaters, and the folk of Uyaynah, and the question being asked about them is why they do not believe once the road is that visible.

### Why distinctness, and not compulsion

There is a deliberate design in the way guidance is offered in this world. Some people demanded miracles before they would accept the Prophet. Others believed on hearing the verses recited. That test goes on in every era. Allah does not appear in physical form now. He advances his arguments through the bearer of the truth, cast in words, so that they can be put before people. A nature that is still alive sees the glow of his Lord in those arguments and bows to it. A nature wrapped in artificial veils cannot find Allah through his words, cannot see him through reasoning, and so wants him to appear bodily, in an observable shape. That is not possible in a world of trial. Only the person who can recognise him while he is still invisible finds him, and the one who insists on seeing him here faces spiritual destitution of the most extreme kind.

That explains a habit worth noticing. People who carefully avoid the truth accuse those who accept it of every kind of wrongdoing, to prove that they themselves are better. They cannot see their own transgressions, but if a truthful person slips, the slip is magnified to show that everyone rallying to the quest for truth is unreliable. The reality is the reverse. Believers who have given up untruth and taken the truth tread the path of faith and righteousness, and in accordance with the will of Allah they become entitled to inspiration for the betterment of their present position, and share in his bounties. Those who keep clear of the truth only prove by their actions that they have no interest in that path. Even when they are deprived of guidance their bravado does not end, and that effrontery is the greatest crime in this world. Allah speaks in the language of symbols, and a symbol is useful only to somebody who wants to read it. Guidance is available only to somebody who is eager for it.

### What is not in the Prophet's hand

The same exposition is what makes the demand for punishment look absurd. Say: I have been forbidden to worship those you call on besides Allah, and I will not chase your desires the way you want me to. If I did that, I would be misguided, and not one of those who are rightly guided. Say: I am on clear proof from my Lord, and you deny it. I do not have what you are hurrying to get, and the decision belongs to Allah alone, who declares the truth and is the best of judges. If the torment they ask for were in his hand, the matter would have been settled between them at once.

A question is worth asking here, because a famous hadith seems at first to pull the other way. Aishah asked the Messenger of Allah whether he had met a day harder than the day of Uhud. He told her that his people had troubled him greatly, and that the worst of it was the day at al-Aqabah, when he had gone to Ibn Abd Yalil bin Abd Kulal and received no answer. He left in heavy sorrow and did not relax until he found himself at Qarn ath-Thaalib, where he raised his head and saw a cloud shading him unexpectedly. He looked up and saw Jibril in it, who called out that Allah had heard what his people had said to him and how they had answered him, and had sent the Angel of the Mountains, so that he could order him to do whatever he wished with them. The angel greeted him and offered the same choice: if he wished, al-Akhshabayn, the two mountains to the north and south of Makkah, would be let fall on them. The Prophet said: no. He hoped that Allah would raise from among them descendants who worship Allah alone and associate nothing with him. That is the wording of Muslim, and the hadith is in the two sahihs.

So how does the hadith sit with the verse? The answer given is that the verse says only this: if the punishment they were asking for were in the Prophet's hand at that time, he would have brought it down on them as they asked. In the hadith, the disbelievers had not asked him to bring a torment down at all. An angel with charge of the mountains came and offered him the choice, and he declined it and asked for respite, out of compassion. The two reports are about different moments and different people, and neither puts the reckoning in anyone's hand but Allah's.

### The knowledge that makes every road visible

The passage closes by handing back what the demand for a sign had assumed. The treasuries and the unseen were refused to anyone but Allah at the start of this passage, and they are refused again here: with him are the keys of the ghayb, which none knows but he. Al-Bukhari recorded a hadith in which Salim bin Abdullah reports from his father that the Prophet counted the keys of the unseen as five, and said that no one but Allah knows them. Then he recited the verse: "Verily, Allah! With him alone is the knowledge of the hour, he sends down the rain, and he knows what is in the wombs. No person knows what he will earn tomorrow, and no person knows in what land he will die. Verily, Allah is All-Knower, All-Aware" (31:34).

His knowledge covers whatever is on the land and in the sea, so that not even the weight of an atom escapes him, and not a leaf falls without his knowing its movements, which leaves the living, and leaves us. "Allah knows the fraud of the eyes, and all that the breasts conceal" (40:19). A way of conduct is not hidden from that knowledge while it is being chosen, and it will not be hidden when it is finished. That is how the signs are made clear, and that is how the way of the wicked becomes distinct.

### In short

- The verse states the purpose of the whole passage: the signs are expounded so that roads become visible, not so that anyone is driven.
- Two readings of the clause are kept side by side: that the way of the sinners be clear and avoided, and that the Prophet discern it.
- In a world of trial Allah gives arguments in words, and only the person who wants to read them reads them.
- The torment the chiefs demanded was never in the Prophet's hand, and the offer of the two mountains at Qarn ath-Thaalib shows him refusing what was given to him, so the verse and the hadith agree.
- The keys of the unseen stay with Allah, and the same knowledge that hides the hour sees the trick of the eyes, which is what makes every path distinct.
