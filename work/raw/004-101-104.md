<<<VERSE 4:101>>>
The concession of the road: "When you travel through the land, it is permissible for you to shorten the prayer" (4:101). Jihād and hijrah both run through travel, and travel in that age ran through danger, so the verse opens the concessions of the ṣalāh.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When you travel through the land"** uses the word for striking through the land, which means travelling in it, as the Qur'ān says elsewhere: "He knows that there will be some among you sick, others travelling through the land, seeking of Allah's bounty" (73:20). The Sunna fixes the sense of travel as the long-distance journey, put at roughly 50 miles.

**"it is permissible for you to shorten the prayer"** means reducing the units from four to two. The wording of no sin marks a dispensation and not a requirement, which is the opinion of al-Shāfī.

**"if you fear an attack by the disbelievers"** names the fear that prevailed when the verse came down. In the beginning of Islam and after the hijrah, Muslims experienced fear during most of their travels, restricting their movements to large or short military expeditions, for most areas were the ground of combatant enemies of Islam and its people. When the prevalent circumstances cease, a decree of this nature may no longer apply, as with the word "And force not your slave girls to prostitution, if they desire chastity" (24:33), which answers a condition of its time. The fear condition itself has passed into leniency. Yaʿlā ibn Umayyah asked ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb about the verse, saying that Allah had granted the Muslims safety, and ʿUmar answered that he had wondered about the same thing and asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, who said:

> A gift that Allah has bestowed on you, so accept His gift.

Muslim and the collectors of the Sunan recorded this hadith, and at-Tirmidhī called it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. ʿAlī ibn al-Madīnī vouched for it: preserved by no route besides this one, every narrator known. From the record of Abū Bakr Ibn Abī Shaybah comes the question of Abū Ḥanzalah al-Ḥaḏāʿ to Ibn ʿUmar about the qaṣr prayer, and he said that it consists of two rakʿahs. Asked what then of the fear mentioned in the verse, now that safety prevails, he answered that this is the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. Al-Bukhārī recorded from Anas that they went out with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ from al-Madinah to Makkah, and he prayed two rakʿahs until they returned, ten days spent in Makkah, and the Group recorded it. Imām Aḥmad recorded from Ḥārithah ibn Wahb al-Khuzāʿī the memory of praying behind the Prophet ﷺ the Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr at Minā, with the crowd huge and the safety complete, two rakʿahs. The Group recorded it with the exception of Ibn Mājah, and al-Bukhārī's narration reads that he led two rakʿahs at Minā during the peace period.

### The remembrance beneath the rules

No sin therefore attaches to the shortening, and no apprehension of sin is warranted, for the qaṣr is itself a rule of the Sharīʿah, following which brings reward. The purpose beneath the rules is the remembrance itself. All acts of religion, the prayer and the zakāh, the propagation of the word and the struggle for the cause, have the ultimate purpose of remembering Allah, creating a person who lives with the thought of Allah at every moment, who fears Him in apprehension and longs for Him in hope, whose attention turns to Him in gain and loss alike, until the whole inner existence is lost in His majesty and grace. The matter is so important that even in war one is directed to say the prayer in some form, so that face to face with death one is reminded of what is of real value, to be carried along to the Hereafter.

The inward teaching reads the concession as pure kindness. The shortening of prayers is the recommended practice in travel. Though first legislated in a time of fear, it remained in force without the fear, as a kindness to the servants. Once travel had licensed the shortening of the obligatory prayers, a further leave covered the supererogatory devotion performed on the camel mounts, with no search for the qibla, the mount turning as it pleased, and the same held for one on foot. Thus it became known that permission to engage in talk with Allah is ongoing at any time, to be taken up at will or set aside at will, the most extraordinary kindness, a protection of the practice of fulfilment and the consummation of the true meaning of friendship. Even the sujūd, the prostration appointed by the Sharīʿah of Islam as a part of the ṣalāh, stands within this open door.

### In short

- Travel shortens the four-unit prayers to two, with the distance and the fifteen-day stay setting the boundaries of the concession.
- The fear condition described the early era, and the Prophet ﷺ called the continuing concession a gift to be accepted.
- The ʿUmar, Ibn ʿUmar, Anas and Ḥārithah reports all show two rakʿahs prayed in safety.
- The shortening is no sin but reward, an open door of talk with Allah that travel merely widened.

<<<VERSE 4:102>>>
The prayer under threat: "When you ˹O Prophet˺ are ˹campaigning˺ with them and you lead them in prayer, let one group of them pray with you" (4:102). The verse teaches the fear prayer, the ṣalāt al-khawf, in which worship and war stand in one line.

### The occasion

Imām Aḥmad recorded from Abū ʿAyyāsh az-Zurraqī that they were with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in the area of ʿUsfān, near Makkah, when the idolaters met them under the command of Khālid ibn al-Walīd, positioned between them and the qiblah. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ led them in the Ẓuhr prayer, and the idolaters said among themselves that the Muslims had been busy with something during which a chance to attack had passed. They added that a prayer would come next, dearer to the Muslims than their children and themselves. Then Jibrīl came down with these verses between the Ẓuhr and the ʿAṣr. When the time for prayer came, the command went out to hold the weapons, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ made them stand in two lines behind him. At his bow they all bowed, and at the raising of his head they all raised theirs. Then came the prostration with the first line, while the rest stood on guard. Those finished and rose, the lines exchanged positions, and the others sat and made their prostration under the guard of the first. The second rakʿah ran the same course, and then the Prophet ﷺ performed the taslīm and ended the prayer. He performed this prayer twice, once at ʿUsfān and once in the land of Banū Sulaym, and the two shaykhs, Bukhārī and Muslim, report the like of it from Baṭn Nakhlah. Abu Dāwūd and an-Nasāʾī recorded this narration, which has an authentic chain and many supporting texts. The other records add their colours: in one, the idolaters say the coming prayer is more beloved to the Muslims than their own fathers, in another, than their wives and wealth, and the plot is the same, to raid them while they are performing it. Allah then revealed to His Prophet the verses to their end, informing him of the plot and teaching the prayer of fear within them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When you ˹O Prophet˺ are ˹campaigning˺ with them and you lead them in prayer"** addresses the Prophet ﷺ in person, and the restriction belongs to the situation of the time, for there can be no uncommanded imam of prayer in his presence. After him, whoever is the imam stands deemed in his place and he leads the fear prayer, and all the leading religious authorities agree that the injunction remains operative and was never abrogated. The same permission extends to being gripped with fear of hurt from a beast, a lion or a python, when little time remains for the ṣalāh.

**"let one group of them pray with you"** divides the company, and the verse continues with the rotation. Before the detail, the boundaries of the shortening that this prayer shares with all travel prayer: a journey shorter than 48 miles keeps the full prayer, a traveller intending to stay under fifteen days keeps the journey rules, and fifteen days or more makes the place a residence where the full prayer returns. The shortening applies to the fard of Ẓuhr, ʿAṣr and ʿIshāʾ only, never to Maghrib or Fajr, nor to the sunnah and witr prayers, all as the Sharīʿah fixes it. One party stands with the imam, taking their arms, and when they finish their prostrations they take their positions in the rear, and the other party, which has not yet prayed, comes up and prays with him, taking the precautions and bearing arms. The records differ in the completion. Al-Bukhārī has Ibn ʿAbbās describing the Prophet ﷺ leading the fear prayer, the people bowing in part and prostrating in part. Standing for the second rakʿah, he was joined by the second party, while those who had prayed the first left and guarded their brothers. All the people were in prayer, and all were guarding one another. Imām Aḥmad recorded from Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh the version in which the Prophet ﷺ led those behind him with one rakʿah and two prostrations, they moved to the position of those who had not prayed, and he performed one rakʿah and two prostrations with the others and said the salām, so that he prayed two rakʿahs while they prayed one, a hadith recorded by an-Nasāʾī with other wordings in Muslim, and by the collectors of the Ṣaḥīḥ, Sunan and Musnad collections. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded from Sālim, from his father, the form in which each group received one rakʿah from the Prophet ﷺ, then completed one more on its own while the other stood guard, collected by the Group with Maʿmar in the chain, with many other chains from several Companions, gathered by al-Ḥāfiẓ Abū Bakr Ibn Marduwyah, as did Ibn Jarīr. After the salām, the two groups went on to complete their one rakʿah each on their own, as the hadith says, with the detail found in the books of Islamic fiqh.

The fear prayer takes more forms than one. The enemy may stand in the direction of the qiblah or away from it, and the prayer itself may run to four rakʿahs, to three as with Maghrib, or to two as with Fajr and the travel prayer. It is prayed in congregation when that is possible, but when the battle is raging and congregation fails, each person prays by himself, facing the qiblah or otherwise, riding or on foot, walking and fighting while performing the acts of the prayer. Some scholars held that in that last case only one rakʿah is prayed. Ibn ʿAbbās narrated that by the words of the Prophet ﷺ, Allah ordained the prayer of four rakʿahs while residing, two during travel, and one during fear, recorded by Muslim, Abu Dāwūd, an-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah, and this is the view of Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal. Al-Mundhīrī counted ʿAṭāʾ, Jābir, al-Ḥasan, Mujāhid, al-Ḥakam, Qatādah and Ḥammād of the same view, with Ṭāwūs and aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk preferring it. Muḥammad ibn Naṣr al-Marwazī added that the Fajr prayer also becomes one rakʿah during fear, the opinion also of Ibn Ḥazm, and Isḥāq ibn Rāhawayh said that amid raging battle a single rakʿah with a nod of the head suffices, and failing that, one prostration, the prostration being itself remembrance of Allah.

**"The disbelievers would wish to see you neglect your weapons and belongings, so they could launch a sweeping assault on you"** states the reason for the arms. The enemy longs for the moment of heedlessness, the forgotten weapons and baggage, to descend upon the praying company all at once, and herein lies the reasoning behind keeping weapons on oneself. A group of scholars therefore said that holding the weapons during the fear prayer is obligatory according to the verse, and one of two opinions held by al-Shāfī makes it compulsory whenever no excuse exists, the other counting the precaution a sunna, and this is the more preferable opinion.

**"But there is no blame if you lay aside your weapons when overcome by heavy rain or illness"** gives the dispensation: intense rain impeding, or sickness and wounds, lift the carrying, which implies that without such excuse the carrying stands.

**"but take precaution"** keeps the guard in every case, as best as the company can.

**"Indeed, Allah has prepared a humiliating punishment for the disbelievers"** closes the passage on the enemy whose plot it answered.

The inward reading carries the law to its root: no servant, while a breath of choosing remains in him, is ever released from the obligation of prayer. Not in fear and not in security, not in the state of separation where the precepts of the law predominate, nor when overwhelmed by the authority of reality, at the wellspring of gathering.

### In short

- The fear prayer was taught at ʿUsfān through Jibrīl when Khālid ibn al-Walīd stood between the Muslims and the qibbah of their prayer.
- One group prays behind the imam while the other guards, then they exchange, and the records vary on whether the completion comes before or after the salām.
- The forms multiply with the battle: four, three, two or one rakʿah, each alone when the fighting presses, down to a single prostration, which is remembrance.
- The weapons are the point of the verse, obligatory in one reading, sunna in the more preferable one, laid aside only for rain or sickness.
- Prayer is never lifted from the servant while a breath of choice remains.

<<<VERSE 4:103>>>
The remembrance after: "When the prayers are over, remember Allah" (4:103). The verse moves from the form of the prayer under fear to the spirit that outlasts every form.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When the prayers are over, remember Allah"** commands the dhikr after the fear prayer in particular, though such remembrance is encouraged after every prayer in general. In the fear prayer it is urged even more, because the pillars of the prayer are diminished, the praying company moving about within it, unlike other prayers. The Sacred Months run the same logic: "so wrong not yourselves therein" (9:36), though injustice is prohibited all year long, it is particularly outlawed in those months for their sanctity and honour.

**"whether you are standing, sitting, or lying down"** means in all conditions, standing for the healthy, sitting for the sick, reclining for the wounded. The remembrance itself is spelled out: the tahlīl, declaring His Oneness with the words lā ilāha illallāh, the tasbīh, saying subḥān Allāh, and the praise, saying alḥamdulillāh, in whatever posture is possible.

**"But when you are secure, establish regular prayers"** restores the full form. In safety, tranquillity returning and fear subsiding, the prayer is performed as commanded, fulfilling its obligations with humbleness, completing the bowing and the prostration, four units for the resident, who may not postpone the prayer from its appointed time without valid reason, while the traveller joins and shortens.

**"Indeed, performing prayers is a duty on the believers at the appointed times"** fixes the law. The word of the verse means enjoined, as Ibn ʿAbbās stated, and he said that the prayer has a fixed time, just as the ḥajj does. The same report runs from Mujāhid, from Sālim ibn ʿAbdullāh, from ʿAlī ibn al-Ḥusayn and Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī, from al-Ḥasan and Muqātil, and from as-Suddī with ʿAṭiyyah al-ʿAwfī.

### The eminence of the prayer

The prayer is the secret whispering between Allah and the servant, and in the whispering there is both need and joy. Today the need, tomorrow the joy. Today suffering, tomorrow the treasure. Today a heavy burden, tomorrow "repose and ease" (56:89). Today the bowing and the prostration, tomorrow the finding and the seeing. Part of the eminence of the prayer is that the Exalted Lord mentioned it 102 times in the Qur'ān and named it thirteen times over: prayer and devotion, Qur'ān and glorification, book and remembrance, bowing, prostration and praise, the asking of forgiveness, the declaring of greatness, beautiful deeds, and subsistent things. Muṣṭafā said:

> The prayer is the miʿrāj of the person of faith.

He said also that the prayer is the banquet of Allah in the earth. The ʿulamāʾ of the past called the prayer the throne of the desirers, the delight of the recognizers, the sinners' means of approach, and the scented garden of the renunciants. Its performer, they said, receives seven generous bestowals: guidance, sufficiency, expiation, mercy, proximity, degree, and forgiveness. The first step in associationism is to leave the prayers, for the Exalted Lord will ask, "What brought you into Saqar?" (74:42-43), and the answer of the guilty runs: we did not pray. He placed the very name of faith on the prayer, where He says, "But Allah would never leave your faith to waste" (2:143), that is, your prayer. He promises daily provision with prayer, where He says, "And command your folk to the prayer" (20:132). Five is the number of the obligatory prayers, in conformity with the roots of the rules, for Muṣṭafā said that the submission is built on five. The servant who performs the five prayers with their stipulations and at their times receives the reward of all the roots. Four are the guises of the prayer: standing, bowing, prostrating and sitting.

The wider teaching of the passage keeps the balance of reliance and means. Though Muslims rely entirely on Allah, they are instructed to be ready with the means of defence against their enemies, for help from Allah reaches the recipient through tangible means, and to refuse preparation is to refuse the receptacle through which the help could arrive. The difficulties faced in this world are part of the scheme of trial, by which it is judged who remains steadfast and who turns reckless, and when defeat and harm come in the struggle of truth and untruth, the pessimism they breed misses the providence hidden in adversity, which comes so that repentance may grow and the turning to Allah may make a person more deserving of His benevolence.

### In short

- The dhikr after the fear prayer repairs the diminished form, as the sacred months concentrate the year-round ban on injustice.
- Tahlīl, tasbīh and praise fill every posture, standing, sitting or lying.
- Security restores the full prayer at its fixed times, a duty as fixed as the ḥajj.
- The prayer is the whispering with Allah, His banquet, the miʿrāj of the believer, and the bearer of the name of faith itself.

<<<VERSE 4:104>>>
The pursuit: "Do not falter in pursuit of the enemy" (4:104). The verse was revealed after Uḥud, when the Prophet ﷺ dispatched a group to seek out Abū Sufyān and his companions, and they complained of their wounds.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not falter in pursuit of the enemy"** means do not weaken the resolve in the chase. Rather, pursue vigorously, fight and be wary.

**"if you are suffering, they too are suffering"** sets the ledger straight. Just as you suffer injuries and death, the same happens to the enemy, who shrinks from nothing and yet bleeds alike: "If you suffer a harm, be sure a similar harm has struck the others" (3:140).

**"But you can hope to receive from Allah what they can never hope for"** gives the asymmetry. You and they are equal in the injuries and the pain, but you hope for Allah's reward, victory and aid, as He promised in His Book and by the words of His Messenger ﷺ, and His promise is true. Your enemies hope for none of this. It is you, not they, who should be eager to fight, to establish the Word of Allah and raise it high.

**"And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise"** seals it. He is most knowledgeable and wise in all He decides, decrees, wills and acts upon, in the worldly and the religious ordainments alike, and He is worthy of praise in all conditions.

### The lesson of the pursuit

The pursuit itself belongs to a pattern of trial. The difficulties faced by the Muslims in this world are part of the plan by which conditions of trial are created, so that it may be seen who remains steadfast and who turns into a reckless victimiser of his fellow men. In the struggle between truth and untruth the believers are sometimes defeated and harmed, and pessimism spreads among some of them, yet in such adversities the providence of Allah lies hidden. They come so that repentance may be the greater, and so that the turning to Allah in them makes a person more deserving of His benevolence. The wounded of Uḥud, sent out again with this verse ringing in their ears, are the proof that the plan never abandons the believers to their pain.

The inward reading sets the same charge at a steeper angle. Stand up for Allah and let the trust be in Allah in the struggle of jihād. The enemy shares the experience of suffering, but stands apart from you in what the heart witnesses. Yours is the seeing they cannot reach, and yours the inward finding that never enters their hearts. To trail behind such opponents in effort and struggle would be unseemly.

### In short

- The verse answered the wounded after Uḥud who hesitated to pursue Abū Sufyān.
- Pain is common to both sides, but reward, victory and aid are hoped for by one.
- The believer's eager fight establishes the Word of Allah, and the wisdom of the All-Knowing covers both armies.
- What the heart witnesses is the difference no wound can measure, and lagging behind the enemy in effort is unseemly.
