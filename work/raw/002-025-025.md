<<<VERSE 2:25>>>
After the Fire, the Gardens. "Give good news ˹O Prophet˺ to those who believe and do good that they will have Gardens under which rivers flow. Whenever provided with fruit, they will say, “This is what we were given before,” for they will be served fruit that looks similar ˹but tastes different˺. They will have pure spouses, and they will be there forever". Having described the punishment prepared for His enemies who deny Him and His messengers, Allah turns to the state of His happy, loyal friends who believe in Him and His messengers, hold to the faith and do good. This pairing is why the Qur'an is called mathānī, according to the sounder view of the scholars: it mentions faith and then disbelief, or disbelief and then faith, the wretched and then the happy, or the reverse. Mentioning like with like is a different thing, called tashābuh.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Give good news ˹O Prophet˺"** means: inform them of what will delight them. The warnings of the previous verses were for the disbelievers, and now the Messenger ﷺ is told to address the believers. The address to the Makkan idolaters that began at verse 21 ends here, with a verse revealed about the believers, so that their reward is mentioned right after the reward of the disbelievers.

**"to those who believe and do good"** means those who have faith in Allah, in Muhammad ﷺ and in the Qur'an, and who perform righteous deeds, the obligatory and the voluntary acts of worship, the acts of obedience that bind them to their Lord, or, more broadly, any righteous deed. The condition of good deeds is added on purpose. Faith alone does not earn these glad tidings. Faith does save a person from staying in the Fire forever, and every Muslim, however great a sinner, will in the end be brought out of Hell after a period of punishment. But no one escapes the Fire altogether without the good deeds the Sharīʿah defines.

**"that they will have Gardens under which rivers flow"** means gardens of trees and dwellings with rivers running beneath the trees and the palaces, rivers of water, milk, honey and wine. The Arabic nahr is the channel in which water flows, so named because the water carves its way through it, and calling the rivers "running" is a figure of speech. From the hadith it is known that the rivers of Paradise do not run in valleys, that the banks of al-Kawthar, the Prophet's ﷺ river in Paradise, are domes of hollow pearl, that the soil of Paradise is fragrant musk and its stones are pearls and jewels. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that these rivers have their source beneath hills, or mountains, of musk. Masrūq related the same from ʿAbdullāh: the rivers of Paradise come out from under mountains of musk. We ask Allah for Paradise, for He is the Most Beneficent, Most Gracious.

**"Whenever provided with fruit, they will say, “This is what we were given before,”"** means that whenever they are fed from these gardens, with fruit of every kind, they will say that this is what they ate before. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded from Yaḥyā ibn Abī Kathīr that the grass of Paradise is saffron and its hills are musk, and that the boys of everlasting youth will serve the believers fruit, then bring them more that looks the same, and the people of Paradise will remark that this is what was just brought, and the boys will tell them to eat, for the colour is the same but the taste is different.

**"for they will be served fruit that looks similar ˹but tastes different˺"** is explained in two ways. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, as reported through Abū Jaʿfar ar-Rāzī from ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, said the fruits resemble one another while their tastes differ. On this view "before" means earlier in Paradise itself, and the resemblance between its own fruits is the proof. ʿIkrimah said they resemble the fruits of this world but taste better. Sufyān ath-Thawrī reported through al-Aʿmash and Abū Ẓabyān that Ibn ʿAbbās said nothing in Paradise resembles anything in this world except in name, and in another narration, that only the names are shared. Fruit that carries the same name and shape as earthly fruit lets the believers recognise it and desire it, while the taste is new. Whichever view is taken, the point is that the believers will have a joy in Paradise they never knew before, and that it renews itself at every moment. The fruits of Paradise may share a name with the fruits of the earth, but their nature is different.

A note of caution belongs here. Modernists have for some time liked to claim that when the Qur'an speaks about the fruits and the other joys of Paradise, its language is figurative, hinting at a spiritual bliss that cannot by nature be touched or tasted. Symbolic or analogical interpretation of the Qur'an is not ruled out. Many genuine scholars, the Sufis especially, have practised it with illuminating results. But no real Sufi ever claimed that such interpretation, iʿtibār, is the same thing as tafsīr, or that his reading was the only valid one. Its purpose has always been to aid spiritual realisation or to elaborate doctrine, never to deny or oppose the ordinary exegesis. What the modern exegetes overlook, in their zeal and their simplicity, is that using a thing as a metaphor or symbol does not mean it has no objective existence. Since the Qur'an speaks of the fruits of Paradise and the like, they must exist objectively, though not physically in the current sense of the word, and their nature and state, which we do not know, can safely be left to Allah. That way lies safety, and that is the Straight Path.

**"They will have pure spouses"** means spouses, ḥūrīs and others, purified from everything filthy and impure. Ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported Ibn ʿAbbās's words: purified from filth and impurity. Mujāhid said: from menstruation, from relieving themselves, from urine, spittle, semen and pregnancy. Qatādah said: purified from impurity and from sin, and in another narration, from menstruation and pregnancy. ʿAṭāʾ, al-Ḥasan, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, Abū Ṣāliḥ, ʿAṭiyah and as-Suddī said the same. In short, they will be clean outwardly and pure inwardly, free of everything physically repellent, like excrement and menstruation, and of everything morally repellent, like bad temper or unfaithfulness. They will have none of the unpleasant traits people have on earth.

**"and they will be there forever"** means ultimate happiness. The believers will enjoy a delight that never ends, safe from death and from any break in their bliss. They will neither die nor be expelled from it. In this too the joys of Paradise differ from the joys of the earth: they are not short-lived, and no one will tremble at the thought of losing them. May Allah count us among these believers. He is the Most Generous, Most Kind and Most Merciful.

### A glimpse of the next world

The call of truth is unmistakably marked with a divine radiance. Its inimitable style and its unanswerable reasoning show it to be a message from Allah. Those who deny it in spite of this will find no refuge anywhere in Allah's universe. Those who find Allah in the word of Allah are already given a glimpse of the next world, and it is they who will be admitted to the gardens of Paradise.

### Fear and hope

This verse is a caress for the friends of Allah. It gives them hope of everlasting bounty and bliss and encourages the faithful to obey and to seek an increase of blessing. The previous verse warned the strangers against confusion in the heart and idolatry on the tongue and threatened them with the Fire and with the bitter severing from the Real. The person of faith is the one who, hearing the first verse and thinking of Hell, becomes frightened and loses his ease, and then, hearing the second, becomes glad, makes his heart firm and his hope strong, and brings ease back into his heart. The Lord of the Worlds praises both states. Of the frightened He says, "The ˹true˺ believers are only those whose hearts tremble at the remembrance of Allah" (8:2). Of those at ease He says, "those who believe and whose hearts find comfort in the remembrance of Allah. Surely in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find comfort" (13:28). It is the custom of the Generous Lord that whenever He sends a verse of fear to frighten His servants, He follows it with a verse of hope and mercy to put their hearts at ease, so that they do not despair.

Good news, then, for all who serve today on the field of obedience. Tomorrow their place is the assembly of "serenity, fragrance, and a Garden of Bliss" (56:89). Not everyone who reaches the paradise of approval reaches the generous gift of serenity. The paradise of approval is the furthest limit of the worshipful servants' pleasure. Serenity is the qiblah of the lovers' spirits. The High Chambers and the Abode of Peace make up the paradise of approval, while serenity in the Presence is the gift kept for the spirit of the passionate. Whoever watches over his actions will reach the paradise of approval, and whoever watches over his breaths will reach serenity. Who can explain that serenity, and how can it be put into words? When a thing does not come to the tongue, how can it be described? A wind begins to blow from the world of the unseen, and it is called the wind of bounty.

### What comes next

When the Jews asked why Allah strikes a parable about flies, in the verse where the idols cannot recover what a fly snatches from them, and about a spider, in the verse that likens false protectors to a spider's house, and what He could want with such lowly creatures, Allah revealed the verse that follows.

### In short

- The Qur'an pairs its warning with its promise. After the Fire prepared for the disbelievers comes the Garden promised to those who believe and also do good, for faith without deeds saves from eternal Hell but not from Hell altogether.
- Rivers run beneath the trees and palaces of Paradise, rising from mountains of musk. Its fruits share names and looks with what the believers knew, in this world or earlier in Paradise, but each taste is new, a joy renewed at every moment.
- The fruits of Paradise are real, not mere metaphors, even though their nature is unlike anything physical here.
- Its spouses are pure in body and character, and the whole of it lasts forever, with no fear of loss.
