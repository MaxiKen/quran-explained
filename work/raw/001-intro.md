Al-Fatihah is the surah the Qur'an opens with and the surah every prayer opens with. It has seven verses, and the scholars count twenty-five words and one hundred and thirteen letters in it. It was revealed in Makkah, as Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah and Abū al-ʿĀliyah stated. Short as it is, the Prophet ﷺ called it the greatest surah in the Qur'an and said that nothing like it had been sent down in the Torah, the Gospel, the Psalms or the Qur'an itself.

The surah moves in a single arc. It begins with the name of Allah and His mercy (verse 1), states that all praise belongs to Him as Lord of all worlds (verse 2), names His mercy again and His ownership of the Day of Judgment (verses 3 and 4), turns to Him with the declaration that He alone is worshipped and He alone is asked for help (verse 5), and ends with the request for guidance along the Straight Path, defined by the people who walk it and by the people who have left it (verses 6 and 7). Praise comes first, then the pledge, then the plea.

### What its names mean

- **Al-Fatihah**, the Opener of the Book: the Qur'an starts with it and the prayer is begun with it.
- **Umm al-Kitāb** and **Umm al-Qur'ān**, the Mother of the Book, the name used by most scholars. One explanation is that the Qur'an and the prayer both begin with it. Another is that it contains the meanings of the whole Qur'an. Ibn Jarīr explained that the Arabs call anything comprehensive that holds several particular things within it an umm: the membrane around the brain is umm ar-raʾs, the flag the army gathers around is an umm, and Makkah is Umm al-Qurā, the Mother of Towns, because it is the greatest of them and leads them, and because it was said that the earth was spread out starting from it.
- **As-Sabʿ al-Mathānī**, the seven oft-repeated verses. Abū Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said, "Al-ḥamdu lillāhi Rabbil-ʿālamīn is the Mother of the Qur'an, the Mother of the Book, and the seven repeated verses of the Glorious Qur'an." At-Tirmidhī recorded this and graded it ṣaḥīḥ, and Aḥmad and Ibn Jarīr recorded similar wordings. The Qur'an refers to the surah this way in 15:87, "We have given you the seven oft-repeated verses."
- **Al-Ḥamd**, the Praise, after its opening word.
- **Aṣ-Ṣalāh**, the Prayer, because reciting it is a condition for the prayer to be valid, and because of the hadith in which Allah says, "I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant," and then goes through its verses one by one.
- **Ash-Shifāʾ**, the Cure, and **Ar-Ruqyah**, the Remedy, after the story of the Companion who treated a poisoned chief with it.

### How many verses, and where the basmalah stands

There is no disagreement that the surah has seven verses. Where they differ is over the first line, "In the Name of Allah, the Most Compassionate, Most Merciful": according to most of the reciters of Kūfah, a group of the Companions and their Successors and many later scholars, it is a separate verse at the head of the surah, while others count the seven differently. That question is taken up under verse 1.

### Its virtues

Abū Saʿīd ibn al-Muʿallā said that he was praying when the Prophet ﷺ called him, and he did not answer until he had finished. The Prophet ﷺ asked what had stopped him from coming, reminded him of the verse "Answer Allah and the Messenger when he calls you to what gives you life," and said, "I will teach you the greatest surah in the Qur'an before you leave the mosque." He took him by the hand, and when they were about to leave, Abū Saʿīd reminded him. He said, "Yes: Al-ḥamdu lillāhi Rabbil-ʿālamīn. It is the seven oft-repeated verses and the Glorious Qur'an that I was given." Aḥmad recorded this, and so did al-Bukhārī, Abū Dāwūd, an-Nasāʾī and Ibn Mājah.

Ubayy ibn Kaʿb had the same experience. The Prophet ﷺ called him twice while he was praying, and when Ubayy came he asked why he had not answered, reminded him of the same verse, and said, "Would you like me to teach you a surah the like of which has not been revealed in the Torah, the Gospel, the Psalms or the Qur'an?" He kept hold of Ubayy's hand while they walked to the door, and Ubayy walked slowly, afraid they would reach it before he was told. At the door he asked, and the Prophet ﷺ asked him what he recited in the prayer. Ubayy recited the Mother of the Qur'an, and the Prophet ﷺ said, "By Him in whose hand is my soul, Allah has never revealed in the Torah, the Gospel, the Psalms or the Qur'an a surah like it. It is the seven oft-repeated verses that I was given." Aḥmad recorded it, and at-Tirmidhī recorded it and graded it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. Anas ibn Mālik reported something similar. In the wording of an-Nasāʾī, which at-Tirmidhī graded ḥasan gharīb, the Prophet ﷺ said, "Allah has never revealed in the Torah or the Gospel anything like the Mother of the Qur'an. It is the seven oft-repeated verses, and it is divided into two halves between Allah and His servant."

ʿAbdullāh ibn Jābir said that he greeted the Prophet ﷺ three times after he had poured out water to purify himself and received no answer, followed him home, and sat in the mosque sad and downcast. The Prophet ﷺ came out after his purification, returned his greeting three times with "Peace and the mercy of Allah be upon you," and said, "Shall I tell you the best surah in the Qur'an? Recite 'All praise is for Allah, Lord of all worlds' to the end." Aḥmad recorded this with a good chain. Some scholars took this hadith as proof that some verses and surahs have more merit than others.

Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī told of a journey on which a servant girl came to say that the chief of her people had been stung or poisoned and their men were away, and asked whether anyone could treat him. A man whose skill none of them had suspected went with her, recited over the chief, and he recovered. The chief gave him thirty sheep and some milk. When he came back his companions asked whether he had known a remedy before, and he said, "I only recited the Mother of the Book over him." They agreed to do nothing with the sheep until they had asked the Prophet ﷺ. When they told him in Madinah, he said, "How did he know that it is a remedy? Divide the sheep, and keep a share for me." Al-Bukhārī recorded it.

Ibn ʿAbbās reported, in a hadith recorded by Muslim and an-Nasāʾī, that while Jibrīl (Gabriel) was with the Prophet ﷺ he heard a sound above him, looked up and said, "This is a door in heaven that has been opened today and has never been opened before." An angel came down through it, came to the Prophet ﷺ and said, "Rejoice in two lights you have been given that no prophet before you was given: the Opening of the Book and the closing verses of Sūrat al-Baqarah. You will not recite a letter of them without being given what it holds."

### The surah and the prayer

Abū Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said three times, "Whoever prays a prayer in which he does not recite the Mother of the Qur'an, his prayer is incomplete." Muslim recorded it. When Abū Hurayrah was asked what to do when standing behind the imam, he said, "Recite it to yourself," and then related the hadith in which Allah says, "I have divided the prayer into two halves between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks for," answering each verse of the surah in turn. Muslim and an-Nasāʾī also recorded the wording "Half of it is for Me and half for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks for." The word "prayer" is used in that hadith for the recitation of the Fatihah. The Qur'an speaks the same way when it says "offer your prayer neither aloud nor in a low voice, but follow a way between," which Ibn ʿAbbās explained, in a report in the Ṣaḥīḥ, as referring to the recitation in the prayer, and when it calls the dawn prayer "the recitation of the Qur'an in the early dawn," the prayer which, as the two Ṣaḥīḥs record, the angels of the night and the angels of the day both attend. The point of the division is that reciting the Qur'an is one of the greatest pillars of the prayer.

From this the scholars drew the ruling, on which there is consensus, that reciting the Fatihah in the prayer is required, by the imam and by those praying behind him, in every prayer and in every unit of it. ʿUbādah ibn aṣ-Ṣāmit reported the Prophet ﷺ saying, "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book," recorded in the two Ṣaḥīḥs, and Abū Hurayrah reported, in the Ṣaḥīḥs of Ibn Khuzaymah and Ibn Ḥibbān, "The prayer in which the Mother of the Qur'an is not recited is invalid."

### In short

- Seven verses, revealed in Makkah, recited in every unit of every prayer.
- Its names describe it: the Opener of the Book, the Mother of the Book, the seven oft-repeated verses, the Praise, the Prayer, the Cure.
- The Prophet ﷺ called it the greatest surah in the Qur'an, unlike anything revealed in the earlier scriptures, and one of two lights given to no prophet before him.
- It is a conversation: Allah answers each of its verses as the servant recites it, and the servant is given what he asks for.
- It runs from praise of Allah, through the pledge to worship and rely on Him alone, to the request for guidance.
