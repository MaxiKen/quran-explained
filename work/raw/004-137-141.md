<<<VERSE 4:137>>>
The oscillating soul: "Indeed, those who believed then disbelieved, then believed and again disbelieved" (4:137), "Allah will neither forgive them nor guide them to the ˹Right˺ Way". The passage now details the censure of the hypocrites, and the first verse draws their curve.

### Phrase by phrase

**"believed then disbelieved, then believed and again disbelieved—˺only˺ increasing in disbelief"** describes whoever embraces the faith, reverts, embraces again, reverts again and remains on disbelief, increasing in it until death. Ibn ʿAbbās, in the report through ʿIkrimah, glossed the increase plainly: they remain on disbelief until they die, and Mujāhid said similarly. This is the characteristic of the hypocrites, believing and disbelieving by turns, and that is why their hearts become sealed.

**"Allah will neither forgive them nor guide them to the ˺Right˺ Way"** means no accepted repentance after death, and no deliverance from the plight to the path of correct guidance. Some read the verse over the Jews instead, as Rūḥ al-Maʿānī carries it. They believed, then took to the golden calf and disbelieved. They repented and believed, then rejected the prophethood of Sayyidna ʿĪsā (Jesus) and fell back into disbelief. Finally, refusing the prophethood of Sayyidna Muḥammad ﷺ, they increased the disbelief further. One school renders the curve in names: belief in Moses, disbelief after him, belief in ʿUzayr, disbelief in Christ after him, and then a firm stand of refusal toward Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'ān.

Read as a whole, the two halves teach that the repeated return to disbelief strips the very ability to receive true guidance. No door of repentance stays open, and none of believing remains. Otherwise, the definitive texts of the Qur'ān and the Sunnah lay down a general rule: the disbeliever, the kāfir, or the apostate, however hardened, has his past sins forgiven if the repentance he makes is sincere and true. Should such people change and repent, the law of amnesty and forgiveness stands open.

### The alternation as the ruin

The inward reading sees the wavering itself as the ruin. States that rise and fall, rise again and stumble, end sealed in their own unseemliness, struck by the arrows of the decree, the misery of fate their final state. The Real guides them to no purpose and leads them to no good sense.

> Give them tidings of separation without end, fa-bashshirhum bi-l-furqati al-abadiyya, and tell them of punishment uninterrupted, wa-akhbirhum bi-l-ʿuqūbati al-sarmadiyya.

The tussle runs through all of life. In every matter the mind tilts either to the demands of desires or to truth and justice. The tilt to desires denies the faith a person professes, and subservience to truth and justice makes the true Muslim. When truth comes before people, the modest acknowledge and the proud deny, the first response born of īmān, the second of faithlessness. The untrue believer loves worldly honour and prestige, and so gravitates to the like-minded who will add to his glory, even if they be deniers of the truth. He has no interest in those whose association adds nothing to his prestige, even if they be true God-worshippers.

### In short

- The alternating believer ends sealed on disbelief, and the increase is the dying state.
- Jews who cycled through calf, ʿĪsā and the refusal of Muḥammad ﷺ are one reading of the curve.
- Repetition can remove even the ability to repent, though the sincere repentance of any kāfir is otherwise always open.
- The proud tilt toward honour and the like-minded, and the modest tilt toward truth.

<<<VERSE 4:138>>>
The grim tidings: "Give good news of a painful punishment to hypocrites" (4:138). The verse names the addressees and the news in one breath.

The word basharah, good news, is deliberately chosen. Everyone looks forward to some good news to brighten the future, but for the hypocrites there is just no other news except this. The tidings are announced to ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy and his followers, and to all hypocrites until the Day of Judgement, and the command to announce them is addressed to the Prophet ﷺ himself. The doom will reach into their hearts.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Give good news"** is the word basharah, deliberately chosen, for everyone looks to some bright word about the future, and these people possess no other news than this.

**"of a painful punishment to hypocrites"** names the addressees, ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy and his followers, and every hypocrite after them until the Day of Judgement, with the command to announce it laid on the Prophet ﷺ himself, and the doom reaching into their hearts.

### In short

- The one announcement the hypocrites await is the painful punishment.
- Ibn Ubayy's company and every hypocrite after them are the addressees.

<<<VERSE 4:139>>>
The borrowed honour: "who choose disbelievers as allies instead of the believers. Do they seek honour and power through that company? Surely all honour and power belongs to Allah" (4:139). The verse exposes the motive and prices it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"who choose disbelievers as allies instead of the believers"** describes the hypocrites as the disbelievers' supporters in reality, giving them loyalty and friendship in secret. Alone with them, they say that they are with them and only mock the believers by pretending to follow their religion. One gloss names the allies as the Jews, sought for assistance and help.

**"Do they seek honour and power through that company?"** asks after the motive, and the answer dismantles it. The urge comes from the assumption that the outwardly visible influence, power and collective strength of the disbelievers may confer honour. Allah exposes the absurdity: honour is being sought from those who themselves have none. The ʿizzah, might and mastery, belongs to none but Allah, and whatever might anyone holds is given by Him. Gross is the failure of reason here: honour is courted by displeasing its owner and giver, and temporal honour is scrounged through His enemies. The Book says it twice over: "Whosoever desires honor, then to Allah belong all honor" (35:10), and "But honor belongs to Allah, and to His Messenger, and to the believers, but the hypocrites know not" (63:8). The addition of the Messenger and the believers in the verse of sūrat al-Munāfiqūn emphasizes that Allah alone owns real honour and bestows its portions on whom He wills, and since the Messenger and the believers are dear in His sight, honour and mastery are given to them, while the disbelievers and polytheists have none of it to give.

Sayyidna ʿUmar said that whoever seeks honour through human beings is disgraced by Allah, a word preserved by al-Jaṣṣāṣ. The same authority, Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ, holds in the Aḥkām al-Qur'ān that the verse forbids seeking honour through friendship with disbelievers and sinners, while seeking honour and power through Muslims is not forbidden, for the verse of sūrat al-Munāfiqūn has made clear that Allah blessed His Messenger and the believers with honour. If the honour meant is the everlasting honour of the life-to-come, its applicability to the Messenger and the believers in the present world is obvious, for that honour never becomes the lot of a disbeliever. If it is honour in the present life, then, barring transitional periods and accidents, the honour and mastery are ultimately the right of Islam and the Muslims alone, as the whole world witnessed while Muslims remained Muslims in the true sense, and as will be seen again in the later period when Muslims reassemble around true Islam under the leadership of Sayyidna ʿĪsā. The weakness of the interim, born of weak faith and sins, does not go against the rule. In the Mustadrak of al-Ḥākim stands the address of Sayyidna ʿUmar to Sayyidna Abū ʿUbaydah, the governor of Syria:

> You were the lowest in numbers and the weakest in strength among the people of the world, then Allah made you exceed in numbers and strength with the grace of Islam. And you were the meanest in status among the people of the world, then Allah raised you in honour with the grace of Islam. So understand this very clearly: if you seek honour from any source other than Allah, Allah will disgrace you.

### The inward question

The inward reading asks the sharper question. To cling to a created being is to take refuge with other than the Protector, leaning on a shelter that never shelters, fallen into a bottomless abyss. Do they desire power from those who have themselves been humbled by the command to Be? How can those with no power extend power to anyone else? Shameless either way, seeking power while humbled by the divine subjugation, or imagining that power can come from anything other than Allah. Whoever seeks something from any direction other than Him finds failure the end of his effort. Power is of two kinds, the eternal power that is an attribute of Allah, and the contingent power He apportions to whom He wills.

### In short

- The secret alliance seeks borrowed honour from those who own none.
- Honour belongs to Allah, His Messenger and the believers, and seeking it from His enemies buys disgrace.
- ʿUmar's warning to Abū ʿUbaydah in Syria states the law for every age.
- The humbled cannot lend power, and the clinger to created beings shelters in a bottomless abyss.

<<<VERSE 4:140>>>
The company to leave: "He has already revealed to you in the Book that when you hear Allah's revelations being denied or ridiculed, then do not sit in that company unless they engage in a different topic, or else you will be like them" (4:140). The verse points back to an earlier command and then draws its line in the gathering.

### The earlier command

What was already revealed in the Book is the āyah of sūrat al-Anʿām, revealed in Makkah al-Mukarramah before the hijrah. It says: "And when you see those who engage in false conversation about Our verses by mocking at them, stay away from them" (6:68). Its addition runs: "And if Satan makes you forget, do not sit with the unjust people after the recollection" (6:68). The re-emphasis lands on people who have passed beyond mere sitting into friendship, taking the disbelievers for bearers of honour and wielders of power. The two verses carry the same sense. When a group is engaged in denying and deriding the verses of Allah, sitting with them to participate or observe is ḥarām for as long as the vain exercise lasts. The āyah of al-Anʿām generalizes a little more. Its reference to disputation in the divine verses includes disbelief and mockery, and the distorting of a verse's meaning as well, meanings deduced contrary to the explanation of the Prophet ﷺ and his blessed Companions, or against the consensus of the community. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, included within the sense those who explain the Qur'ān erroneously, distort its meaning or invent what is not there. Hence the ruling. The teaching sessions of a person giving meanings contrary to the exegetical authenticity of the righteous early elders may not be attended. The authority is the Qur'ān itself, and attendance is a sin rather than a source of reward. In the Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ, Abū Ḥayyān drew the rule that the sin of saying extends to the hearing. What the tongue may not utter, the ear may not take in willingly. His poetic exhortation runs:

> Protect your ears from hearing the evil, as you protect your tongue from saying it.

### The questions of the gathering

For one who joined such company unknowingly, the rule is immediate departure once it is realised, for the point is not to sit with the unjust people when alerted. When they stop that strain of talk and digress to another topic, the scholars differ. Some hold that the prohibition was for the disparagement and distortion, and stops when they stop, so that sitting is no sin once the talk changes. Others hold the company of such disbelieving, sinning and unjust people incorrect even after the change, the position of Ḥasan al-Baṣrī. The verse of al-Anʿām says not to sit with the unjust people once you remember, and the unjust person remains unjust after the questionable conversation ends, as al-Jaṣṣāṣ argues. Qāḍī Thanāʾullāh Pānipatī found consistency in both views in his Tafsīr al-Mazharī. Once the comments of disbelief, mockery and distortion cease and another topic takes over, unnecessary sitting remains forbidden, while participation prompted by religious contingency or physical need is permissible.

Seclusion is better than bad company. Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ said in the Aḥkām al-Qur'ān that a Muslim charged with forbidding evil, on witnessing sin in a gathering, should stop it by force if he has the strength. Lacking strength, he should at the least show displeasure, its lowest degree being to rise and leave. It is for this reason that ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz had some men arrested on the charge of drinking wine. Investigation found one of them fasting, a man who had drunk nothing but sat among the drunkards. He was punished too for the unexplained sitting, a case preserved in the Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ. And the hadith reported at this point in the record runs:

> One who believes in Allah and the Last Day should not sit to dine where liquor is being served.

The rule of leaving is hemmed by one condition: leaving must not entail a sin of one's own. Joining the congregation in the mosque is necessary. Should something contrary to the Sharīʿah start happening there, the congregational prayer is not abandoned over it, simple emotional displeasure being sufficient. The same holds for any gathering whose necessity stands proved. Abandoning duty because of others' sins paves the way for sinners to come and destroy the Sunnah and the Sharīʿah, as Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said. The forms of socialization with disbelieving or falsely-believing people sum up as five. Condescending to their infidelistic assaults is kufr. Showing repugnance when confronted with open disbelief is, without valid excuse, sinfulness. Socializing for some worldly need is allowed. Socializing to disseminate the injunctions of Islam is worship. And socializing under compulsion, exigency or helplessness is excusable.

### Phrase by phrase

**"or else you will be like them"** carries the warning inside the assembly. If, aware of the prohibition, one keeps sitting where the āyahs of Allah are rejected, mocked and denied, and sanctions the conduct, one has joined in the doing and shares the burden. The likeness can be read strictly, that satisfaction with disbelief is disbelief, for liking kufr is nothing but kufr, or more broadly, that by abetting the conspiracy against Islam and Muslims the sitter becomes an accomplice like them.

**"Surely Allah will gather the hypocrites and disbelievers all together in Hell"** closes the file. Just as the hypocrites participate in the kufr of the disbelievers, Allah joins them together in the Fire for eternity, dwelling in torment and punishment, enchained and restrained, drinking boiling water. The glosses name them: the hypocrites of Madinah under ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy, the disbelievers of Makkah under Abū Jahl, and the disbelievers of Madinah under Kaʿb. Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān held that this verse abrogated the part of the al-Anʿām verse leaving the exception, while the word of the exception itself stands: "Those who fear Allah, keep their duty to Him and avoid evil, are not responsible for them in any case, but (their duty) is to remind them, that they may have Taqwa" (6:69).

The teaching beneath explains why the command runs so hard. Whenever the call of Allah is addressed to any group, its basis is too strong to be refuted by arguments, so those who refuse it attempt to show its worthlessness by ridicule, heaping contempt to convey that the message is unworthy of acceptance. Such people demonstrate that they do not regard truth as a serious matter, and when people are not serious, arguing is of no use. Silence is better, waiting for the time the hearers are in a state of mind to give attention, and withdrawal from any company where the divine call is held low is itself the sign of seriousness about truth.

### The reason of the heart

The inward reading gives the reason of the heart:

> Do not make neighbours of those alienated in feeling and affection, for the darknesses of their souls extend to your hearts as you seek to inhale the fragrances they refuse.

Whoever sits with a people takes a share of their state. The companion of the intimate becomes intimate, and the companion of the darkened is estranged.

### In short

- The command predates this sūrah, given in Makkah in al-Anʿām, and this verse re-emphasizes it against those who went further into friendship.
- Sitting with mockery of the Book is ḥarām while it lasts, the corrupted tafsīr counts as mockery, and the ears share the tongue's sin.
- The unknowing joiner leaves on realisation, the changed topic divides the scholars, and need or duty may justify staying.
- The sitter becomes the accomplice, and liking kufr is kufr.
- The hypocrites and the disbelievers share one Fire, gathered as they were gathered in unbelief.

<<<VERSE 4:141>>>
The watchers: "˹The hypocrites are˺ those who wait to see what happens to you. So if Allah grants you victory, they say ˹to you˺, “Were we not on your side?”" (4:141). The verse draws the portrait of the double party to its finish.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who wait to see what happens to you"** is the word alladhīna of the verse, substituted in the reading of one school for the same word of verse 139, and it means watching and awaiting the harm that befalls the believers, awaiting the time when the Muslim circumstances and religion dissolve and the state of kufr takes over. They lay in wait for hardship and the turn of events, hoping for misfortunes.

**"So if Allah grants you victory, they say, “Were we not on your side?”"** shows them at the moment of triumph, aid and booty, drawing close to the believers, claiming the same religion and asking a share of the gains.

**"But if the disbelievers have a share ˹of victory˺, they say, “Did we not have the advantage over you, yet we protected you from the believers?”"** shows the other face. The disbelievers gain victory over the believers sometimes, just as occurred during Uḥud, for the messengers are tested and the final victory is theirs. To them the hypocrites say that they helped in secret, divulging secrets, confusing the believers and weakening their resolve until the victory came. They claim the authority to capture and slay, exercised in sparing them, and the defence of them against the believers. The statement strengthens relations with the disbelievers, for the hypocrites pretend friendship with both parties to be safe from harm, out of weak faith and lack of certainty.

**"Allah will judge between ˹all of˺ you on the Day of Judgment"** answers the pretence. He judges by what He knows of them. The shade of Islamic law spread over them in this life, a protection existing only out of Allah's wisdom, deceives no one on that Day. The pretending brings no benefit there, the secrets of souls stand disclosed, the contents of hearts are gathered, the believers enter Paradise and the pretenders the Fire.

**"And Allah will never grant the disbelievers a way over the believers"** is the closing promise, and the reports gather around it. ʿAbdur-Razzāq recorded from Yāsiʿ al-Kindī that a man came to ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib and asked about the āyah, and he said:

> Come closer, come closer. Allah will judge between you on the Day of Resurrection, and He will not grant victory for the disbelievers over the believers.

Ibn Jurayj recorded from ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī that Ibn ʿAbbās dated it to the Day of Resurrection, and as-Suddī recorded the same from Abū Mālik al-Ashjaʿī, adding that the way means proof. It may also carry the meaning of this life, where the disbelievers are unable to exterminate the believers completely, though they sometimes gain victory over some Muslims. The final triumph belongs to the believers in this life and the Hereafter: "Verily, We will indeed make victorious Our Messengers and those who believe in the worldly life" (40:51). This rebuts the hopes of the hypocrites, who wish the believers destroyed and keep loyalty with the disbelievers, and the Book has drawn them already: "And you see those in whose hearts there is a disease of hypocrisy, they hurry to their friendship" (5:52), until the end where they stand regretful (5:52).

The portrait completes itself in the wider teaching. The hypocrite is outwardly religious and inwardly irreligious. Without principles, he watches for opportunities to turn events to his advantage, associating with any group that may help realise his worldly ambitions, believers or deniers alike. Into whichever gathering he goes, he speaks to please everybody. Worldly convenience associates him at times with the believers, yet he is no well-wisher of theirs. True believers, by their very existence, become a criterion of truth in a society, so those standing on false religiosity want such criteria demolished. Their mischief runs in this world alone, the believers being safe from it in the Hereafter. The hypocrite is bracketed with the deniers, for in Allah's judgement there is no difference between outward religiosity and blatant irreligiosity. Both states are identical in reality, and what matters to Allah is the inward state, not the outward appearance.

### The anatomy of the double

The inward reading adds the anatomy. Ikhlāṣ, sincerity in the truest sense, they lacked, and true conviction, ʿaqīda, they had never tasted, so their character differed from those who submitted within the decree, and the submitted and the disbelievers are not the same. The people of the Real must be wary of them and stay aloof. Then comes the beautiful expression of sufficiency, that Allah will never grant the disbelievers a way over the believers, an all-encompassing statement. Their deception, turned back on its authors, and their plotting, reserved for requital:

> The evil of their deception is diverted back to them, fa-inna wabāla kaydihim ilayhim maṣrūfun, and the requital for their plotting is reserved for them, wa-jazāʾa makrihim ʿalayhim mawqūfun.

And the two verdicts:

> The right and true reality, its people are aided from the power of the Real, wa-l-ḥaqqu min qibali al-ḥaqqi subḥānihi manṣūrun ahluhu, and the false, its foundation is uprooted through His help, mujtaththun aṣluhu.

### In short

- The watchers lay in wait for misfortune, claiming either side as fortune turns, safe in both camps and true to neither.
- The shade of Islamic law deceives them only in this life, for the Day of Judgement discloses the secrets of souls.
- ʿAlī, Ibn ʿAbbās and Abū Mālik al-Ashjaʿī carry the promise, and the way of the disbelievers over the believers never opens, in this life's final reckoning or the next.
- The believers are the criterion the false want demolished, and the deceived schemers find their own deceit returned upon them.
