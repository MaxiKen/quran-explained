<<<VERSE 7:46>>>
A third group now appears. "There will be a barrier between Paradise and Hell. And on the heights ˹of that barrier˺ will be people who will recognize ˹the residents of˺ both by their appearance. They will call out to the residents of Paradise, “Peace be upon you!” They will have not yet entered Paradise, but eagerly hope to". The verse names the dividing wall, names the people standing on top of it, and says how they know the two crowds below.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There will be a barrier between Paradise and Hell"** is the wall that keeps the people of the fire from reaching the garden. Ibn Jarīr said that this is the wall Allah described elsewhere, where a wall will be put up between them with a gate in it, and inside it will be mercy while outside it will be torment (57:13). As-Suddī said the same in fewer words, that the screen is the wall, and that the wall is al-Aʿrāf. Mujāhid said that al-Aʿrāf is a barrier between the garden and the fire, and that it is a wall with a gate.

**"And on the heights ˹of that barrier˺ will be people"** names where they stand. The word for the heights is a plural of a word meaning the highest part of anything, and the Arabs use it for every elevated piece of land. As-Suddī explained the name in another way, that the place is called al-Aʿrāf because the people on it recognise the people below. The top portion of the wall between the garden and the fire is what the word names, and since the top of a thing is distinct and can be made out from a distance, the word fits it.

**"who will recognize ˹the residents of˺ both by their appearance"** describes what they can do from where they stand. They know the people who enter the fire by the marks on them, and they know the people who enter the garden by theirs. The signs are given plainly: the faces of the people of the fire are darkened and their eyes are blue, while the faces of the people of the garden are bright and glowing, at once handsome and radiant.

**"They will call out to the residents of Paradise, “Peace be upon you!”"** is the greeting. It is the greeting the people of the heights give to the people of the garden, and they give it in honour of them.

**"They will have not yet entered Paradise, but eagerly hope to"** describes their own position. The people on the wall have not gone in yet, and what they have in place of it is hope.

### Who the people of the heights are

Commentators differ over who these people are and why they are made to stop there, and the narrations about them vary as well. What carries weight and is accepted by the majority is that these are the people whose good deeds and bad deeds come out equal on the scale. Because of the good they did they are delivered from the fire, and because of the sins they carry they have not yet been admitted to the garden.

This is what was said by Hudhayfah, by Ibn Masʿūd and by Ibn ʿAbbās among the Companions, and by others among the Companions and the generation after them. The narrations converge in the same direction. Hudhayfah was asked about the people of al-Aʿrāf, and his answer was that they are people whose good and evil actions balance each other. What they did wrong kept them short of the garden, and what they did right was enough to keep them out of the fire, and so they are held there on the wall until Allah judges them.

Ibn Jarīr reports from Hudhayfah that the Holy Prophet ﷺ was asked about the people of al-Aʿrāf and said that they are a people whose good deeds and bad deeds are equal. They are delivered from Jahannam, and they have not yet entered the garden. They are held back at that place until the accounting of deeds and the judgement are finished. After that their case is decided, and in the end they are forgiven and brought into the garden.

There is another narration, from Jābir ibn ʿAbdullah, reported by Ibn Marduwayh, in which the Holy Prophet ﷺ was asked who the people of al-Aʿrāf are. He said that they are people who took part in the fighting without the consent of their parents, and who gave up their lives in the way of Allah. The disobedience to their parents is what kept them out of the garden, and the shahādah in the way of Allah is what kept them out of the fire.

The two narrations do not contradict each other. The second is an example of the same equality of virtue and vice, with martyrdom in the way of Allah on one side of the scale and disobedience to parents on the other, and the two weighing the same.

### What the hope on their faces means

Maʿmar said that al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī recited this passage of the verse about them not having entered yet although they hope to, and then said: by Allah, Allah did not plant this hope in their hearts except for an honour He intends to give them. Qatādah said that those who hope are the ones among you whom Allah informed of their places on the Day of Resurrection. And al-Ḥākim reported that Hudhayfah ibn al-Yamān said that while they are standing in that state, God appears to them and says: rise up and enter the garden, for I have forgiven you.

### Three groups, and the wall that separates them

The verses of Sūrat al-Ḥadīd set out what the wall is for. They tell of three groups of people on the Day of Resurrection. The first are the declared disbelievers and those who associated partners with Allah, who never reach the bridge of Ṣirāṭ at all, because they are thrown into the fire through its doors before that stage is reached.

The second are the believers, who have the light of their faith with them as they walk. The third are the hypocrites, who followed the believers about in this world and who therefore start out behind them on the same path. When they begin walking on the bridge, a heavy darkness comes over them all. The believers keep moving ahead on the strength of their light, and the hypocrites call out to them to wait, so that they can take some of that light. Then a caller calls out for the sake of Allah: turn back, and find your light where you came from. The meaning is that the light they want is the light of faith and of good deeds, and that it could only be got at the place they have left behind. Whoever did not get that light there, through faith and through deeds, will not have the benefit of it here.

And in that state a wall is set up between the hypocrites and the believers. The wall has a gate. Beyond the gate there is nothing but punishment, and inside it are the believers with the mercy of Allah filling the air and the garden in view. The verses put it as follows. On the Day when the hypocrites, men and women alike, will turn to those who believed and ask them to hold back and spare a share of their light, the answer they get is that they should go back where they came from and look for light there. And then a wall will be put between them with a gate in it, and inside the gate will be mercy, and outside it, facing them, the punishment (57:13).

The word used for the wall in that verse is the word for a wall built round a great city. Such a wall is usually wide and fortified, with guards posted in it who keep watch against enemies. And the word used in this verse for the barrier is a different one, meaning a screen. Ibn Jarīr and other authorities hold that the screen in this verse is the same wall that the other verse calls by the city-wall word, and that the top of it is al-Aʿrāf.

### Why these meeting points exist

When the people of the garden reach the garden where they belong and the people of the fire reach the fire where they belong, the two places are far apart in every respect. Even so, many verses of the Qur'an confirm that between the two there will be points from which one side can see the other and hold a conversation with it.

Sūrat aṣ-Ṣāffāt tells of two men who knew each other in this life, one a believer and one a disbeliever. In the Hereafter the believer goes to the garden and the disbeliever to the fire, and they notice each other and speak (37:55). When the friend in the garden looks for the friend in the fire, he finds him lying in the middle of it, and he curses him, remarking that he had wanted him ruined along with himself, and that had it not been for the grace of Allah he would have been lying there too. Then he reminds him of what he used to say in the world, that there was no life after death, no accounting, no reward and no punishment. He can now see for himself what the reality is.

Conversations of that kind between the people of the garden and the people of the fire appear not only in those verses but continue through almost a whole section of the Qur'an.

And the meeting points are themselves a punishment of a kind for the people of the fire. They find themselves reproached from every side. They see the blessings being enjoyed by the people of the garden, and they burn with rage at having missed them, and they are already burning in the fire. For the people of the garden the same view is a comfort of another kind. Having seen the plight of the people in the fire, they are more grateful for what they have been given. Then they remember the people who used to mock them for believing, and who could do nothing against them, and when they see those people disgraced and punished in the end, they have the last laugh at their undoing.

Sūrat al-Muṭaffifīn says the same: on that Day those who believed will be laughing at the disbelievers, seated on raised couches watching, and the disbelievers will have been repaid for what they used to do (83:34-36). And against the record of the people of the fire, the admonition also comes from the angels, who tell them that this is the fire they used to deny, and ask whether this is magic or whether they simply do not see (52:14-15).

### The greeting itself

The greeting the people of the heights give is one we already use in this world. When people meet, the greeting is offered as a gift and as a mark of respect and goodwill for the one greeted. It is a sunnah practice, a way the Holy Prophet ﷺ lived by and recommended to his community. The same words are said to the dead when their graves are visited. The same words will serve as the greeting on the plain where all are gathered, and in the garden itself.

Looking closely at the Qur'an and the narrations, there is a distinction worth noting. The full greeting with the definite article at the front is what is prescribed while we are alive in this world. Once a person has left this world, what is prescribed is the form of the words without that article at the beginning. The greeting given to the people of the garden in one verse runs in that form, peace be upon you for what you patiently endured, and how excellent is the final home (13:24). And when the angels receive the people of the garden it appears in the same form, peace be upon you, you have done well, so enter it to live there for ever (39:73). And here, on the heights, the people on the wall greet the people of the garden with the same words.

### The inner reading

What sort of people are these, that the Exalted Lord should give them the name of men? A wind of solicitude and a breeze of kind favour passed over them suddenly from the side of nearness. Everyone knows what happens when the north wind blows across a rose, and the rose leaves its scent behind it in the garden. When the wind of His care passes over these people, it wakes their hearts with the light that comes from recognising Him. It makes their spirits sweet with the perfume of union with Him, and it brightens what is hidden in them with the polish of His care. It lets them enjoy the company of aspiration and the beauty of conduct, so that they lift their aims altogether away from creatures and busy themselves with love for the Real.

The yearning for Him has left the one who yearns bewildered in His street. Cut off from creatures, he is content with rags. His eyes are like a ring of pearls from the burning in his heart, and his sighs like a string of coral from the fire in it.

So in this world the Exalted Lord lets them look down on the inner lives and the states of the servants, and in the world to come He will let them look down on the stations and degrees of the faithful. He raises their standing above that of all creatures, so that they see everyone while nobody sees them, and know everyone while nobody knows them. Hence the words of the verse about recognising each one by his marks. Each person carries a mark, and the mark these people carry is that they carry none. Each person stays tied to some quality of his own, and the quality belonging to these people is that they have none.

What holds the people of the fire away from the Real is the chain of opposition. What contents the people of the garden is what has been apportioned to them there. But He keeps these people away from both, and gives them an overview of all. And one of the masters of the path prayed: O God, how lovely are the days Your friends spend with You, how beautiful their practice as they hope to see You, how sweet their conversation on the road as they search for You, how magnificent their time when they are at work in Your cause.

### In short

- A barrier stands between the garden and the fire, and the heights are the top of it, from which both crowds can be seen.
- The people standing there recognise both groups by their marks, the darkened faces of one and the glowing faces of the other.
- The majority reading is that they are the people whose good and bad deeds weigh equally, delivered from the fire and not yet admitted to the garden, and the narrations from Hudhayfah, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās and the Prophet ﷺ converge on that.
- A second narration gives them as those who fought without their parents' consent, so that the disobedience and the martyrdom balance each other, and the two accounts do not contradict.
- The verses of Sūrat al-Ḥadīd show what the wall does, and the greeting the people on the heights give is the form of greeting proper to the next life rather than to this one.

<<<VERSE 7:47>>>
The other direction. "When their eyes will turn towards the residents of Hell, they will pray, “Our Lord! Do not join us with the wrongdoing people.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"When their eyes will turn towards the residents of Hell"** describes the look in the other direction from the heights. What they see is the severe punishment that those people are in.

**"they will pray"** marks the turn in the verse. The same people who greeted the people of the garden with a blessing now speak about themselves.

**"“Our Lord! Do not join us with the wrongdoing people.”"** is the prayer, and it is a request to be kept out of their company. What the wrongdoing is, another reading spells out: they disbelieved and they worshipped others besides Allah.

### What the two looks do

The verse completes the picture that the one before it began, and the two verses, 46 and 47, describe the same group from the two directions. From the top of the wall the people of the heights can look both ways, and the verse gives the two looks in order. And they are people of the early generations as much as of any later time, since the sects and parties of this world are not what carries weight there. First they look at the garden, and what comes out of them is a greeting offered in honour of the people there. Then they look at the fire, and what comes out of them is a prayer about themselves.

The prayer is worth noticing for what it does not say. They do not ask to be admitted to the garden. They ask not to be placed among the wrongdoers. The request is the smallest one a person can put, and it is put by people who have already been told that their good and their bad deeds came out equal, and who have hope and nothing else to stand on. Their own situation is the reason for the prayer. The report from Ibn ʿAbbās has it that when the people of al-Aʿrāf turn their gaze to the people of the fire and make them out, this is the supplication they make.

### In short

- The verse gives the second of the two looks from the heights, and this one is directed at the fire.
- What the people there say is a prayer about themselves, and it asks to be kept out of the company of the wrongdoing.
- The wrongdoing named is disbelief and the worship of others besides Allah.
- They do not ask for the garden. They ask only not to be placed among the wrongdoers, which is the smallest request a person can make.
