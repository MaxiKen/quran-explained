<<<VERSE 7:46>>>
This is the verse that gives the surah its name, al-Aʿrāf, "the Heights": "There will be a barrier between Paradise and Hell. And on the heights ˹of that barrier˺ will be people who will recognize ˹the residents of˺ both by their appearance. They will call out to the residents of Paradise, “Peace be upon you!” They will have not yet entered Paradise, but eagerly hope to". The previous verses described the people of Paradise addressing the people of the Fire. Now Allah tells of a barrier between the two places, which keeps the people of the Fire from reaching Paradise. He also tells of a group of people who stand upon it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There will be a barrier between Paradise and Hell."** The word is ḥijāb, a screen or partition, and it means a wall. Ibn Jarīr said it is the wall Allah describes in another verse, quoted in full below (57:13), and that al-Aʿrāf is also part of it. As-Suddī said, "The screen is the wall, and it is al-Aʿrāf." Mujāhid said, "Al-Aʿrāf is a barrier between Paradise and the Fire, a wall with a gate."

**"And on the heights ˹of that barrier˺"**: al-Aʿrāf is the plural of ʿurf. Ibn Jarīr said the Arabs call any raised piece of ground an ʿurf. The word refers to the top of anything, since the top stands out and can be recognised from afar. So al-Aʿrāf is the top of the wall dividing Paradise from Hell. One explanation calls it the wall of Paradise.

**"will be people"**: literally "men". Most say they are people whose good and bad deeds are equal, as the hadith states. One explanation adds that they are men of learning and experts in law who held doubts about provision.

**"who will recognize ˹the residents of˺ both by their appearance."** They will know everyone who enters Hell and everyone who enters Paradise by their marks. The people of Hell have darkened faces and blue eyes. The people of Paradise have bright faces, handsome and radiant, with light on them. The people of the Heights can see both groups because they stand so high. As-Suddī said al-Aʿrāf is named for this, because its people recognise (yaʿrifūn) others.

**"They will call out to the residents of Paradise, “Peace be upon you!”"**: the people on the wall will greet the people of Paradise to honour them. The form of this greeting is discussed below.

**"They will have not yet entered Paradise, but eagerly hope to"**: here Allah speaks about the people of the Heights. They have not entered Paradise yet, but they hope to enter it through Allah's mercy.

### How can the two sides see each other?

Once the people of Paradise and the people of Hell have reached their places, the two will be far apart in every respect. Yet many verses confirm that there will be points between them from which each can see the other and speak. One example is the believer and his disbelieving friend from Sūrat aṣ-Ṣāffāt (37:55), quoted under 7:44. The believer finds his old friend in the middle of the Fire and blames him for trying to ruin him. He reminds him of how he used to deny any life, reckoning, reward or punishment after death, and now he can see the reality for himself. Such exchanges between the two groups run on for almost a whole section here.

These viewing points will be a further kind of punishment for the people of Hell. They will be reproached from every side. Seeing the blessings the people of Paradise enjoy, they will burn with rage at what they missed, while already burning in Hell. For the people of Paradise they will bring a further comfort. The sight of Hell's people in their misery will deepen their thankfulness for what they were given. They will remember the people who mocked them for their faith, when they could do nothing in return. Seeing those mockers disgraced and punished, they will have the last laugh. Sūrat al-Muṭaffifīn says the same:

> "But on that Day the believers will be laughing at the disbelievers as they sit on ˹canopied˺ couches, looking on. ˹The believers will be asked,˺ “Have the disbelievers ˹not˺ been paid back for what they used to do?”" (83:34-36).

The angels' rebuke, also quoted under 7:44 (52:14-15), belongs to the same series. So does the exchange in which the people of Paradise ask whether the warning came true, and a caller curses the wrongdoers.

In this world, believers and deniers alike receive both the blessings and the hardships Allah sends. The Hereafter is different, for a barrier will separate the two groups. None of Paradise's joy will cross over to Hell, and none of Hell's misery will cross over to Paradise.

### What is the wall?

Sūrat al-Ḥadīd explains it. On the Day of Resurrection people will be in three groups.

1. Open disbelievers and polytheists. They will never reach the Bridge of Ṣirāṭ, because they will be thrown into Hell through its gates before that stage.
2. The believers, who will have the light of faith with them.
3. The hypocrites. Since they trailed behind the Muslims in the world, they will follow behind them on the same path there too.

When they begin walking on the Ṣirāṭ, a thick darkness will cover them all. The believers will keep moving ahead by the light of their faith. The hypocrites will call out, asking them to stop and wait so that they can use their light. Then a caller will say, on Allah's behalf, "Go back and look for your light there." The light they want is the light of faith and righteous deeds, and it could only be gained in the place they have left behind. Whoever failed to gain it there through faith and deeds will not have its benefit here. Then a wall will be raised between the hypocrites and the believers, with a gate in it. On the outside there will be nothing but punishment. On the inside will be the believers, surrounded by Allah's mercy and the sight of Paradise. The verse says:

> "On that Day hypocrite men and women will beg the believers, “Wait for us so that we may have some of your light.” It will be said ˹mockingly˺, “Go back ˹to the world˺ and seek a light ˹there˺!” Then a ˹separating˺ wall with a gate will be erected between them. On the near side will be grace and on the far side will be torment" (57:13).

That verse calls the wall sūr. The word is mainly used for the defensive wall round a great city. Such a wall is broad and fortified, with soldiers stationed in its towers keeping watch for enemies. Ibn Jarīr and other authorities on tafsīr say that the ḥijāb in this verse is the same wall called sūr in Sūrat al-Ḥadīd. So on the Day of Resurrection some people will stand on the top of that wall. They will see what happens in Paradise and in Hell, and they will speak with people on both sides.

### Who are the people of the Heights?

Commentators differ over who these people are and why they are held there, and the hadith narrations vary. The view that is sound, weighty and accepted by most commentators is this. They are people whose good and bad deeds weigh exactly equal on the Scale. Their good deeds rescue them from Hell, but their sins keep them out of Paradise for a time. In the end they too will enter Paradise by Allah's grace and mercy. Ḥudhayfah, Ibn Masʿūd and Ibn ʿAbbās among the Companions held this view. So did other Companions and Successors and several of the Salaf and later generations, and various hadith narrations point the same way.

In a report recorded by Ibn Jarīr, someone asked Ḥudhayfah about the people of al-Aʿrāf. His answer was that their good and bad deeds are equal. Their bad deeds kept them from qualifying for Paradise, and their good deeds qualified them to escape the Fire. So they are held there on the wall until Allah judges them. Ibn Jarīr also reports from Ḥudhayfah that the Prophet ﷺ himself was asked about them and gave the same answer. They would stay at al-Aʿrāf until the reckoning and judgement were over, and then be forgiven and admitted into Jannah.

Ibn Marduwayh reports from Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh that the Prophet ﷺ was asked who the people of al-Aʿrāf are. He said they are people who went out to jihad without their parents' consent and permission, and were killed in Allah's way. Their disobedience to their parents kept them out of Paradise, and their martyrdom (shahādah) in Allah's way kept them out of Hell. The two hadiths do not contradict each other. The second is simply an example of people whose virtues and vices are equal. Martyrdom in Allah's way lies on one side and disobedience to parents on the other, and the scale is level.

### Why do they hope?

Maʿmar reported that al-Ḥasan recited this verse and said, "By Allah, Allah did not put this hope in their hearts except for an honour He means to give them." Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī is also reported as saying that Allah gives them this hope only because He wishes to be generous to them. Qatādah said, "Those who hope are those among you whom Allah has told of their places." Al-Ḥākim reported from Ḥudhayfah ibn al-Yamān that while they are in this state, Allah will appear to them. He will say, "Rise and enter Paradise, for I have forgiven you."

### What form does the greeting take?

The people of the Heights greet the people of Paradise with "Salāmun ʿalaykum", "Peace be upon you". People use this greeting in the world when they meet, as a gift and a mark of respect and goodwill. It is masnūn, a Sunnah practice that the Prophet ﷺ followed and recommended to his community. It is also said to the dead when visiting their graves. It will be the greeting on the plain of Resurrection and in Paradise too.

A closer look at the Qur'an and the hadith narrations shows a difference in form. "As-salāmu ʿalaykum", with the definite article, is the Sunnah while we live in this world. After leaving this world, the Sunnah form is "Salāmun ʿalaykum", without the alif and lām at the start of salām. The greeting at the graves follows the Qur'anic wording, which takes this form: "Peace be upon you for your perseverance. How excellent is the ultimate abode!" (13:24). The angels will use the same form when they receive the people of Paradise: "Peace be upon you! You have done well, so come in, to stay forever" (39:73). The people of the Heights, too, greet the people of Paradise in this form.

### The inner reading: the people with no mark

What kind of men are these, that the Exalted Lord should call them "men"? They are people over whom the wind of His care and the breeze of His kindness have suddenly passed from the side of nearness. A north wind that brushes a rose carries its fragrance through the whole garden. So too His care, blowing over them, revives their hearts with the light of recognition and scents their spirits with union with Him. It polishes their secret cores. It gives them focused aspiration and beautiful conduct, so that they lift their aspiration from the creatures altogether and busy themselves with love of the Real.

As the verse says: "Longing for You has left the longing one bewildered in Your street. Parted from people, he is content with rags. His tears are a ring of pearls from the burning within him, and his sighs a string of coral from the fire in his heart."

So in this world the Exalted Lord lets them look upon the inner states of His servants. In the next world He will let them look upon the stations and ranks of the faithful. He will raise their station above the creatures, so that they know everyone while no one knows them. They recognise everyone, and no one recognises them. That is why He says they "recognize ˹the residents of˺ both by their appearance".

Everyone has a mark, and their mark is having no mark. Everyone is left with some attribute of self, and their attribute is selflessness. Hell's people are chained away from the Real by their opposition. The people of Paradise are content in Paradise with their own portions. But these people He keeps apart from both, and gives a view over all.

The master of the spiritual path (ṭarīqah) said, "O Allah, how lovely are Your friends' days with You! How beautiful their conduct as they hope to see You, how sweet their talk on the road of seeking You, how splendid their time when busy with Your work!"

### In short

- A wall stands between Paradise and Hell, the same wall with a gate described in Sūrat al-Ḥadīd. Its top is called al-Aʿrāf, "the Heights".
- The viewing points between the two places add to the torment of the people of Hell and to the gratitude of the people of Paradise.
- By the majority view, the people of the Heights are those whose good and bad deeds are equal. They will finally enter Paradise by Allah's mercy.
- Their hope is a sign of the honour Allah intends for them. They greet the people of Paradise with "Salāmun ʿalaykum", the form used beyond this world.

<<<VERSE 7:47>>>
The people of the Heights look from one side to the other: "When their eyes will turn towards the residents of Hell, they will pray, “Our Lord! Do not join us with the wrongdoing people.”" They have not yet entered Paradise, though they hope to, and the sight of the Fire fills them with dread.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When their eyes will turn towards the residents of Hell"**: when the men of the Heights look toward the people of the Fire and see the severe punishment afflicting them.

**"they will pray, “Our Lord!"** This is what they say at once, calling on their Lord.

**"Do not join us with the wrongdoing people.”"** Do not place us in the Fire with the wrongdoers, the disbelievers in Hell who denied You and worshipped others besides You. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported that Ibn ʿAbbās said this is what they will pray when they look at the people of the Fire and recognise them.

### Why do they pray this way?

They stand between the two homes. They have been spared the Fire, but they have not yet been admitted to Paradise. When they look at the Fire and recognise the people in it, their fear makes them turn to Allah and ask Him not to put them among them. Their prayer, coming from people whose deeds stand balanced, shows that even those close to safety rely wholly on Allah's mercy.

### In short

- The people of the Heights, seeing the people of the Fire and their torment, will pray not to be placed with the wrongdoers.
- Standing between Paradise and Hell, they depend entirely on Allah's mercy for their final place.
