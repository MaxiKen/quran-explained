<<<VERSE 2:4>>>
The description of the mindful continues. They are the people "who believe in what has been revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ and what was revealed before you, and have sure faith in the Hereafter". The previous verse gave faith in the unseen in general. This verse names two parts of it in particular: faith in revelation, all of it, from the Torah and the Gospel to the Qur'an, and certainty about the life to come. Both are singled out for reasons, and the scholars found in the wording of the verse a proof that there is no prophet and no Book after Muhammad ﷺ.

### Phrase by phrase

**"who believe in what has been revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺"** is the Qur'an. Ibn ʿAbbās said the mindful believe in what Allah sent the Prophet ﷺ with.

**"and what was revealed before you"** is the Torah, the Gospel and the other scriptures given to all the prophets. Ibn ʿAbbās also includes what the earlier Messengers were sent with. The believers accept each Messenger without rejecting anything brought from the Lord. The faith of the mindful is one piece. Belief in the unseen, prayer and zakāh cannot be joined with rejection of what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ brought. The same holds for rejecting the earlier Messengers. Certainty about the Hereafter belongs with that acceptance. Allah commanded the believers, "Have faith in Allah, His Messenger, the Book He has revealed to His Messenger, and the Scriptures He revealed before" (4:136). He told them to say to the People of the Book, "We believe in what has been revealed to us and what was revealed to you" (29:46). He told the People of the Book, "Believe in what We have revealed—confirming your own Scriptures" (4:47), and that they would have nothing to stand on "unless you observe the Torah, the Gospel, and what has been revealed to you from your Lord" (5:68). He described the believers at the end of this surah: the Messenger believes in what was revealed to him, and so do the believers, each believing in Allah, His angels, His Books and His Messengers, making no distinction between any of His Messengers (2:285). And He promised that "those who believe in Allah and His messengers—accepting all; rejecting none—He will surely give them their rewards" (4:152). These are a few of the verses which show that true believers believe in Allah, in all His Messengers and in all His Books.

**"and have sure faith in the Hereafter"** means the Resurrection, the standing before Allah, Paradise, the Fire, the Reckoning and the Scale that weighs deeds, the mīzān. The Hereafter, al-Ākhirah, is so called because it comes after this life. They know it is real, and they believe in rising after death and in the bliss of Paradise, and in the rewards and punishments of that life.

### Who the verse describes

ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd and ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās said that in the Prophet's ﷺ time the mindful were of two kinds. Some had been idolaters and disbelievers before they accepted Islam, and some had been People of the Book, Jews and Christians, who later embraced it. The previous verse describes the first group, and this verse describes the second. That is why it names belief in the earlier Books beside belief in the Qur'an. One gloss says the verse refers specifically to ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and his followers. The hadith says that such people receive a double reward: once for believing in and following their earlier Book before the Qur'an came to replace it, and once for believing in and following the Qur'an when it came as Allah's final Book. The believers among the People of the Book have a special place here because they believe in their Books in detail, and when they embrace Islam and sincerely accept its details they earn the two rewards. Other Muslims can believe in the earlier teachings only in a general way, in line with the Prophet's ﷺ instruction that when the People of the Book relate something, one should neither confirm nor deny it but say, "We believe in what was revealed to us and what was revealed to you." Some Arabs may hold a fuller and firmer faith in the Islam revealed to Muhammad ﷺ than some converts from the People of the Book. Such firmer faith can earn a reward matching the converts' two rewards. Allah knows best.

Mujāhid said that the opening of this surah portrays three groups: the believers in four verses, then the disbelievers in two, then the hypocrites in thirteen. The four are general. They include every believer, Arab or non-Arab, a person of an earlier scripture or not, human or jinn, and the qualities they list belong together, each requiring the others.

### Believing in the earlier Books today

Every Muslim must believe in Allah's earlier Books. Everything He revealed in them is true, unlike the changes introduced by self-interested people. Their original recipients were bound to act on them. The explanation says those earlier Books and laws have now been superseded, so practice follows the Qur'an. This is a required part of the creed. A Muslim accepts every prophet and messenger Allah sent. Each divine Book named in the Qur'an is accepted in its original revealed form, not as later altered. Messengers and Books guided every people and age. Muhammad ﷺ is believed to be the last prophet, and the Qur'an the final Book replacing the earlier laws. As for whether a particular book revered by some other religion or tradition is authentic and of divine origin, a Muslim may neither affirm it outright nor needlessly deny the possibility. Where its contents agree with what the Qur'an says, that much may be accepted as true. Otherwise good manners require an ordinary Muslim to keep quiet about things he is unlikely to understand. Reading the sacred books of other traditions calls for a rare aptitude and great caution. The explanation warns that casual reading from idle curiosity can unsettle thought rather than bring the promised truth and peace. Even a person with the required aptitude and knowledge should undertake such study under a genuine spiritual master. The question has become a practical one. The lust for reading scriptures of every tradition has grown into a mania, especially among young people who feel uprooted, and Turkey's government saw a need, in 1974, to bar certain Hindu scriptures from entering the country.

### The verse as a proof that prophethood has ended

The way the verse is worded yields a basic principle: Muhammad ﷺ is the last of the prophets and the Book revealed to him is Allah's last revelation. Had Allah intended to send down another Book, or to continue revelation after the Qur'an, then this verse, which lays down belief in the earlier Books as a condition of faith, would have had to mention belief in the Book or Books to come. Such a statement would have been all the more necessary, because people were already familiar with the duty of believing in the Torah, the Gospel and the earlier Books and were practising it, whereas a future prophet and a future book would have had to be announced clearly so that no one was left in doubt. Yet in defining faith the Qur'an mentions the earlier prophets and Books and makes no reference at all to any prophet or Book to come after the last Prophet ﷺ. Nor is this verse alone. The Qur'an returns to the subject in forty or fifty verses, and in every one it speaks of the prophets, Books and revelation that came before him and never hints at a prophet or revelation to come whom one would have to believe in. "And what We have sent down before you" (16:43). "And We have certainly sent messengers before you" (40:78). "And certainly before you We have sent messengers" (20:47). "And what was revealed before you" (4:60). "And it has certainly been revealed to you and to those who have gone before you" (39:65). "Thus He reveals to you and He revealed to those who have gone before you" (42:3). "Fasting is decreed for you as it was decreed for those before you" (2:183). "Such was Our way with the messengers whom We sent before you" (17:77). Wherever the Qur'an speaks of sending down a Book or a revelation, a prophet or a messenger, it attaches the words min qabl, "before", or min qablik, "before you", and nowhere does it use or suggest anything like "after you". Even if other verses were not explicit about the finality of his prophethood and the end of revelation, the wording of this verse alone would be enough to prove them.

### Faith in the Hereafter

The second quality named here is faith in al-Ākhirah. In the language the word means "that which comes after", and here it stands in contrast to the physical world: the other world, beyond physical reality as it is known and beyond the reach of the senses and of reason. The Qur'an also calls it Dār al-Qarār, the everlasting abode, Dār al-Ḥayawān, the abode of true life, and al-ʿUqbā, the outcome, and it is full of vivid descriptions of it, of the joys of Paradise and the horrors of Hell. Faith in the Hereafter is already included in faith in the unseen, but the Qur'an mentions it separately because it may be considered the most important element of faith in one respect: it is what moves a person to turn faith into practice and to act as faith requires. Together with the oneness of Allah and prophethood it is the third doctrine common to all the prophets, on which all the revealed laws agree.

A misconception needs naming here. Some people who are not familiar with the Qur'an, or do not wish to be, and who have been touched by the rationalism, materialism and libertarianism of Western society, nurse reservations, of mind and of feeling, about Hell's horrors at least, if not about Paradise's joys, and some have gone so far as to call these the inventions of "obscurantist" scholars, borrowing the jargon of the European Reformation and Enlightenment. They forget that faith in the Qur'an means faith in every word of it, and that no one can affirm one part of the Book and deny another and remain a Muslim: "Do you believe in some of the Scripture and reject the rest?" (2:85). They have also never seriously examined the history that produced the Enlightenment or what became of ethics afterwards. Even in its heyday there were thinkers happy to dispense with ethics altogether as superstition or tyranny, and those who admitted the need for rules of conduct to keep society in order generally discarded the idea of divine sanction, despite Voltaire's own remark that man would have to invent God if He did not exist. The historical argument lists successive proposed bases for moral rules: the state, social will, custom, human nature and biological law. It says these authorities withered during the second half of the twentieth century. In that account, modern people were left without a dependable replacement. Only against that background can the significance of faith in the Hereafter for human society be seen properly.

Among the doctrines of Islam, faith in the Hereafter is the one whose role in history deserves the word revolutionary. It began by transforming the morals and manners of the Qur'an's followers and went on to give them distinction even in the political history of mankind. The reason is plain. Consider people who believe that this life is the only life, its pleasures the only pleasures and its pains the only pains, whose only aim is the satisfaction of the senses and of physical and emotional needs, and who refuse to believe in the Hereafter, the Day of Judgment, the assessment of deeds and their requital. When the distinction between truth and falsehood, lawful and forbidden, gets in the way of their desires, the distinction itself becomes intolerable to them. Then what can hold them back from crime? The penal laws of the state or of any human authority have never been a real deterrent or an agent of moral reform. Habitual criminals get used to penalties. A milder or more timid person may give up his desires for fear of punishment, but only as far as he is in danger of being caught. In private, where the law cannot reach, who can make him renounce his pleasures and accept restraint? The argument holds that faith in the Hereafter and fear of Allah unite private and public conduct. The mindful person knows that even a sealed room at night is not beyond divine watch. The smallest deed is recorded. That awareness brings inward purpose into line with outward behaviour. This is the secret of the clean society of early Islam, when the mere sight of a Muslim and his manners was enough to make non-believers fall in love with the religion.

### Certainty, not just belief

The verse does not say that the mindful "believe" in the Hereafter. It says they yūqinūn, have complete certainty of it. The opposite of belief is denial, but the opposite of certainty is doubt and hesitation. The hint is that for faith to be complete it is not enough to affirm the Hereafter with the tongue. One must have a certainty that leaves no room for doubt, the kind of certainty that comes from seeing a thing with one's own eyes. The mindful keep before their eyes the whole scene of people presenting themselves for judgment before Allah, their deeds being assessed, and reward or punishment following according to what they did in this world. A man who piles up wealth by taking what belongs to others, or who gains some petty advantage by means Allah has forbidden, may proclaim a thousand times that he believes in the Hereafter, and in worldly matters the Sharīʿah will count him a Muslim, but he does not have the certainty the Qur'an demands. It is this certainty alone that transforms a life, and it brings with it the guidance and success promised in the next verse. In the growth of a seeker, the questions about the final outcome of life beyond this world find their answer in faith in the life to come.

### The inner reading

This verse too describes the mindful, affirming their faith in the Qur'an and in every message and sign that came down from heaven on the tongues of the prophets. The Lord of the Worlds praises them for it, approves of them and accepts their faith. Every distinction and honour that earlier communities had, He gave to this community and added to it, and every burden and hardship they carried He lifted from it. Earlier communities practised for longer days, yet this community is rewarded more for its obedience. They had a set time for repentance and then the punishment fell, but this community can repent until the moment of death, and its punishment is left to Allah's will. Allah reminded His Prophet ﷺ of a favour: "And you were not at the side of Mount Ṭûr when We called out ˹to Moses˺" (28:46), that is, you were not there when We spoke to Mūsā (Moses) about you and your community. Mūsā (Moses) had read in the Torah of a community richly adorned, refined and approved, beautiful in conduct and flourishing within, and asked who they were. He was told they were the community of Muhammad ﷺ. He longed for them and asked to be shown them, and was told that it was not yet their time to appear, but that their voices could be brought to his ears. Then Allah Himself called out, "O community of Muhammad!", and everyone who would belong to it until the Hour answered, "Here I am, at Your service!" Having called them, He did not send them away without a gift: "I bestowed on you before you asked, and I forgave you before you asked for forgiveness."

### In short

- The mindful believe in the Qur'an and in every Book and Messenger before it, making no distinction, which is the faith the Qur'an demands throughout.
- The verse fits the People of the Book who entered Islam, who earn a double reward, and Muslims still believe in the earlier Books as originally revealed while acting by the Qur'an alone, since the earlier laws are abrogated.
- Because the verse names only what was revealed "before", and the Qur'an does so in dozens of verses without ever mentioning a Book or prophet "after", the wording itself proves that Muhammad ﷺ is the last prophet.
- The Hereafter is named separately because certainty about it is what turns faith into conduct, in public and in private alike. The verse asks for certainty, yaqīn, not mere belief.

<<<VERSE 2:5>>>
After the five qualities, the verdict: "It is they who are ˹truly˺ guided by their Lord, and it is they who will be successful". The sentence points twice, "it is they ... and it is they", and both pointers rest on the same people. The ones who believe in the unseen, pray, spend, accept all of revelation and are certain of the Hereafter are the ones who walk on guidance, and they are the ones who win.

### Phrase by phrase

**"It is they"** refers to the people just described: those who believe in the unseen, establish prayer, spend from what Allah has given them, believe in what He revealed to His Messenger and to the Messengers before him, are certain of the Hereafter, and prepare for it by doing good and avoiding what is forbidden.

**"who are ˹truly˺ guided by their Lord"** means that they walk in a light, with guidance and insight from Allah. It means they enjoy favour, mercy and the clear exposition that has come down to them from their Lord. The wording, "upon guidance", pictures guidance as a road they are standing on.

**"and it is they who will be successful"** means successful in this world and the next. They will have what they sought and be saved from the evil they tried to escape, so they will have their reward, eternal life in Paradise, and safety from the torment Allah has prepared for His enemies. Put simply, they succeed in entering Paradise and are saved from the Fire. One reading takes them as the people who grasped and found what they were seeking and escaped the evil they were fleeing, and says these are the Companions of Muhammad ﷺ. The guidance and triumph named here are the reward of the certainty described in the previous verse: that certainty transforms a life, and this is what it brings in its wake.

### Those whose company to keep

These two verses were set down so that whoever asks for the Straight Path knows who the guided are and whose path it is, and can join their company and take up their beliefs and their way of life. Those who seek the truth in this way find it. Allah's protection and His order carry them along a sure course in this world, and Paradise is their destination in the next. On this road, seeking and finding are not divided.

### The inner reading

Here is the great triumph and the praise worth having, good fortune without end and generosity without limit. Allah has opened for these people the door of insight and set the gaze of His care working inside their hearts. He has lit the lamp of guidance there, so that what is unseen to others is plain to them, and what is hearsay to others is seen face to face by them. Anas ibn Mālik once came before ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān after noticing a woman in the street and dwelling on her beauty. ʿUthmān said that one of those who had come in to him had the traces of adultery showing in his eyes. Anas asked whether there was revelation after Allah's Messenger ﷺ. ʿUthmān said no, but there is insight, proof and truthful discernment, and he recalled the Prophet's ﷺ words: beware the discernment of the believer, for he looks with the light of Allah. An elder, asked what this discernment is, said it is spirits moving through the Kingdom, gazing on the meanings of unseen things, then speaking of the secrets of the Real with the speech of direct witness, not the speech of opinion and calculation. A poet offered himself as a ransom for such men, whose innermost selves roam in the unseen, who crave from the Real a place of witnessing no one else reaches, who meet the holy spirit within and dwell with it in meaning, men with nearness and presence in the unseen while their lower selves have been slain by what they found.

A story shows guidance arriving from both directions at once. Sarī as-Saqaṭī, the master of al-Junayd, told him one day that it was time he spoke to the people and gave them counsel. Al-Junayd did not see himself as worthy and declined. Then one night, the night before Friday, he saw the Prophet ﷺ in a dream telling him, "Speak to the people!" He rose that same night and before morning was knocking at Sarī's door, and Sarī said to him: so you would not agree to it from me until they told you to. The next day al-Junayd sat in the congregational mosque, and word spread through the city that he was going to speak.

### In short

- The five qualities of verses 3 and 4 define the people, and this verse gives their reward: they are on guidance from their Lord, and they are the ones who succeed.
- Guidance here is a light and an insight from Allah to walk by, and success is getting what one sought and escaping what one feared, in this world and in the next, with Paradise at the end.
- The verses name these people so that anyone asking for the Straight Path knows whose company to join.
- Guidance can be seen in a believer's discernment, which looks with the light of Allah, as ʿUthmān showed Anas.
