<<<VERSE 6:12>>>
The verse asks and answers a question, then promises the gathering. "Ask them, O Prophet, 'To whom belongs everything in the heavens and the earth?' Say, 'To Allah!' He has taken upon Himself to be Merciful. He will certainly gather all of you together for the Day of Judgment, about which there is no doubt. But those who have ruined themselves will never believe." The disbelievers of Makkah already accepted that Allah is the Master of the heavens and the earth and everything they contain. The verse uses that accepted fact to press the point: if He is the Master, then He will gather everyone on the Day of Resurrection, and those who have brought loss to themselves by disbelief will not believe.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ask them, O Prophet, 'To whom belongs everything in the heavens and the earth?'"** is the question put to the disbelievers. This is the opening of the passage. The disbelievers of Makkah already accepted that Allah is the Master. Tawhid (oneness of Allah) is the theme. "Do you order me to worship other than Allah, O you fools?" (39:64). "I created not the Jinn and humans except that they should worship Me" (51:56). The number 12 marks this verse. Say to them: travel and see. The answer is not waited for, because the disbelievers of Makkah were no deniers of the fact that Allah Almighty is the Master of the heavens and the earth and everything they contain, even though they were involved in shirk and idolatry.

**"Say, 'To Allah!'"** is the answer given through the Prophet ﷺ. To Allah belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth and whatever is between them. Their total control belongs to Allah.

**"He has taken upon Himself to be Merciful"** means that Allah has prescribed mercy for Himself, out of grace for His servants. He will not rush to punish them. It is recorded in the Two Sahihs that Abu Hurayrah said that the Prophet ﷺ said: "When Allah created the creation, He wrote in a Book that He has with Him above the Throne: 'My mercy overcomes My anger.'" Abu Hurayrah narrated that a man from Al-Ansar from the area of Quba' invited the Prophet ﷺ to eat some food. When the Prophet ﷺ ate and washed his hands, he said: "All praise is due to Allah. He feeds but is never fed. He bestowed bounty unto us. He gave us guidance. He fed us. He gave us something to drink. He covered our nakedness. For every favor He has given us. All praise is due to Allah alone. Indeed, praise that should not be neglected, my Lord. So be it. All the while affirming that we will never be able to duly thank Him. Indeed. Nor be appreciative enough of Him. Nor be free of needing Him." Sayyidna Abu Hurairah reported this in the Sahih of Muslim.

**"He will certainly gather all of you together for the Day of Judgment"** means that Allah will gather everyone, from the beginning to the end, on the Day of Qiyamah. Muhammad ﷺ is the one who conveys this. You Yourself are the Master. The unseen is known to Him. The word "towards" can also mean to gather in the graves. In that case it means that human beings will be kept being gathered into their resting places. This is unto the Day of Qiyamah. Then they will be raised back to life. The Day of Resurrection is a known Day, about which there is no doubt. Allah swore by His Most Honored Self that He will gather His servants for an appointed meeting of a known Day (56:50).

**"about which there is no doubt"** is the assurance for the believing servants. As for those who deny and refuse, they are in confusion and disarray.

**"But those who have ruined themselves will never believe"** means that those who have brought loss to themselves by disbelief will not believe and rescue their souls from loss. The deprivation of the disbelievers from the universal mercy of Allah mentioned earlier was caused by their own deeds. They did not take to the way which brings mercy. That is, the way of belief and faith.

### The mystical reading

The mystical reading asks whether anyone truly dwells in the house, and answers that God in His lordship is enough. It says that all is from God to God. It says that God struck the coin of mercy before creating anything, so that His mercy takes precedence over His wrath. On the Day of Mustering, the Messenger will intercede for the disobedient, asking that they be clothed in the shirt of mercy, citing God's own words: "We sent thee only as a mercy to the worlds" (21:107) and "O My servants who have been immoderate against yourselves, despair not of God's mercy" (39:53). From the Court of Majesty will come the call that the work of the community is one of three: people of faith, recognizers, or the disobedient, and to each will be given paradise or mercy and forgiveness. "Surely I am God; there is no god but I" (20:14).

### In short

- Allah is the Master of everything in the heavens and the earth.
- He has prescribed mercy for Himself and will not rush to punish.
- He will gather everyone on the Day of Resurrection, about which there is no doubt.
- Those who ruin themselves by disbelief will not believe.
- The verse is a gentle summoning to faith, grounded in mercy.

<<<VERSE 6:13>>>
The verse extends the thought. "To Him belongs whatever exists in the day and night. And He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing." Whatever rests hidden in the night and whatever moves about openly in the day are all His servants. They are His creation. They are all under His domination and control. He hears their statements. He knows their actions. He will judge by them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"To Him belongs whatever exists in the day and night"** means that all creatures in the heavens and earth are Allah's servants and creatures. They are all under His authority, power, and will. Indeed. There is no deity worthy of worship except Him. "Do you order me to worship other than Allah, O you fools?" (39:64). "I created not the Jinn and humans except that they should worship Me" (51:56). Say: the tawhid is clear. The word "sukun" could mean the state of being still or at rest, in which case the verse means that Allah is the Master of everything present in the night and the day. Or it could mean a combined state of stillness and movement, but what was mentioned here is the state of rest only, because movement, which stands in contrast to it, can be understood as being obvious enough.

**"And He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing"** means that He hears the statements of His servants and knows their actions, secrets, and what they conceal.

### The mystical reading

The mystical reading says that the newly arriving things are God's as a kingdom, in God as manifestation, from God in appearance, and unto God in return. He is the Hearing of the moans of the yearners and the Knowing of the longing of the finders. Night is a general darkness and day a general brightness. Before the creation of the world there was neither night nor day. In paradise there is no sun, but all is day. Whatever is closer to God has a more complete light. 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud said: "Surely there is no day or night with your Lord. He illuminates the heavens with the light of His face." The Lord of the Worlds created a tremendous angel, placing night in one of his fists and day in the other. By way of allusion, God is saying: "I placed the disk of the sun in the fist of the angel, but I did not give the hearts of My friends to anyone." The heart of the lovers is in the fist of the King. Mus'tafa said: "The hearts of the servants are between two fingers of the All-Merciful."

### In short

- Everything that rests or moves in the night and the day belongs to Allah.
- He is the All-Hearing and All-Knowing.
- The mystical reading speaks of night and day as light and darkness, and of the heart as held by God alone.

<<<VERSE 6:14>>>
The verse commands the Prophet ﷺ to declare his exclusive worship of Allah. "Say, O Prophet, 'Will I take any guardian other than Allah, the Originator of the heavens and the earth, Who provides for all and is not in need of provision?' Say, 'I have been commanded to be the first to submit and not be one of the polytheists.'" The disbelievers asked the Prophet ﷺ to take a guardian other than Allah. The answer is that Allah is the Creator of the heavens and earth, who gives provision to His servants and is not in need of them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, 'Will I take any guardian other than Allah'"** is the question put to the idolaters. They worship idols and other things together with Allah. Does it make sense? Indeed. that I take any one other than Allah as a guardian and a protector from whom I seek help? "Do you order me to worship other than Allah, O you fools?" (39:64). "I created not the Jinn and humans except that they should worship Me" (51:56). The oneness of Allah is the point. Makkah was where this was said.

**"the Originator of the heavens and the earth"** means Allah is the One Who created the heavens and the earth without any precedent. There was no Creator before Him.

**"Who provides for all and is not in need of provision"** means He is the One Who gives provision to whichever of His servants He wishes. None of His servants provide for Him. He is not in any need of His servants. His servants are in need of Him. He feeds but is not fed. Indeed. To Him belongs the attribute of generosity, so He feeds. To Him belongs the rightful due of eternity, so He is not fed. Indeed.

**"Say, 'I have been commanded to be the first to submit'"** means my Lord has instructed me to be the first one from this nation to submit and be humble. I am ordered to be the first of those who submit themselves to Allah as Muslims from this Ummah.

**"and not be one of the polytheists"** means He has prohibited me from being of those who associate others as partners with Him. Do not be among those who associate others with Him.

### The mystical reading

The mystical reading asks: after He has honored me with His beautiful friendship, shall I take another as friend? Indeed. After the brightness of His solicitude has fallen upon me, shall I gaze on someone else in the two houses? The sun of solicitude and kind favor has shone upon me from the Majestic and Exalted Threshold. It took care of my business without me in the two worlds. It lit up my heart with the eternal love. It adorned it with the ornament of intimacy. Indeed. He gave me such eminence at the forefront of acceptance that sometimes He puts up with my disdain: "By thy life!" (15:72). Sometimes the grasp of the attributes explains the polish of my heart's mirror by virtue of solicitude: "Did We not expand for thee thy breast?" (94:1). Sometimes the assessor of the beginningless and endless register turns over the acceptance and rejection of the creatures to my threshold: "Whatever the Messenger gives you, take; whatever he prohibits you, forgo" (59:7). With all this good fortune, rank, solicitude, and kind favor, indeed, how would it be appropriate for my heart to request someone else or to gaze upon this world and the afterworld? Indeed. So be it. Hence he said concerning this world, "What do I have to do with this world?" And concerning the afterworld: "The eyesight did not swerve, nor did it exceed" (53:17). Neither this world nor the afterworld. Rather, seeing the Patron. That is the goal. The Originator of the heavens and earth. The God who created earth and heaven. Indeed. He is the enactor of the world and the world's folk. Indeed. The knower of the apparent and the hidden. He does not partake of daily provision. He assigns daily provision to the servants.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ will not take any guardian other than Allah.
- Allah is the Originator of the heavens and earth and provides for all.
- He has commanded the Prophet ﷺ to be the first to submit.
- The Prophet ﷺ is forbidden from being among the polytheists.
- The mystical reading speaks of God's friendship as enough for the heart.

<<<VERSE 6:15>>>
The verse adds a warning. "Say, 'I truly fear, if I were to disobey my Lord, the torment of a tremendous Day.'" In the previous verses, the command given was to shun shirk and believe in the perfect power of Allah mentioned therein. In this verse, the punishment for the contravention of this command has been mentioned in a particularly endearing manner. The Prophet ﷺ has been commanded to tell the people something. Should he too come to contravene the command of his Lord, then he too has the fear. The fear is of the punishment. It is of the Day of Qiyamah. So be it. It is obvious that the noble Messenger of Allah is divinely protected from every sin. Indeed. So the likelihood of disobedience coming from him simply does not exist. Indeed. But by mentioning this hypothetical situation, the purpose is to convey the message to the community. The contravention of the Divine command is so serious a matter. Even the greatest prophet cannot stand excused from it. If not him, who else?

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, 'I truly fear'"** means the Prophet ﷺ is to say that he knows and fears. "Do you order me to worship other than Allah, O you fools?" (39:64). "I created not the Jinn and humans except that they should worship Me" (51:56). Jalla Sha'nuhu is the One feared. Tawhid is the command.

**"if I were to disobey my Lord"** means if I go against Allah by committing that which He has prohibited. Indeed. Such as associating partners with Him, indeed. Or by leaving out what He has instructed me to do, indeed. Such as having faith and doing good actions. So be it.

**"the torment of a tremendous Day"** means the punishment on the Day of Qiyamah, the Day of Resurrection. I would face then a tremendous torment on an awful Day.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to say he fears the torment of a tremendous Day.
- The hypothetical disobedience shows the seriousness of contravening the Divine command.
- Even the greatest prophet cannot stand excused from the Divine command.

<<<VERSE 6:16>>>
The verse closes with mercy. "Whoever is spared the torment of that Day will have certainly been shown Allah's mercy. And that is the absolute triumph." The punishment of the Day of Resurrection is extremely horrendous and harsh. Whoever finds this removed from him or her should know that it was great mercy from Allah. Deliverance from punishment and admittance into Paradise are inseparable from each other. The word "success" means being admitted into Jannah (Paradise). This tells us that deliverance from punishment and admittance into Paradise are inseparable from each other.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever is spared the torment of that Day"** means the person from whom Allah keeps away and removes the punishment on the Day of Resurrection. "Do you order me to worship other than Allah, O you fools?" (39:64). "I created not the Jinn and humans except that they should worship Me" (51:56). Tawhid is the way. Al-Mahshar is the gathering place.

**"will have certainly been shown Allah's mercy"** means he has attained success through the mercy of Allah. He has been blessed with His mercy.

**"And that is the absolute triumph"** means such salvation and safety from the punishment is a clear victory which no other victory can equal. Success here indicates acquiring profit and negates loss. Whoever is moved away from the Fire and admitted to Paradise, he indeed is successful (3:185).

### In short

- Being spared the torment of that Day is Allah's mercy.
- Deliverance from punishment and admittance into Paradise go together.
- That is the absolute triumph, a clear victory which no other can equal.
