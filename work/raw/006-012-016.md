<<<VERSE 6:12>>>
The passage about demanded signs has ended, and the opening of what follows puts the demanders a question instead of answering them. "Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, 'To whom belongs everything in the heavens and the earth?' Say, 'To Allah!' He has taken upon Himself to be Merciful. He will certainly gather ˹all of˺ you together for the Day of Judgment—about which there is no doubt. But those who have ruined themselves will never believe." Two things are established by this order to ask: ownership, which they already conceded, and mercy, which Allah has written for Himself, and the second is what makes the first a hope rather than a threat.

The reason for answering the question instead of waiting is that the reply was already agreed. The disbelievers of Makkah were soaked in shirk, the association of partners with Allah, and in idolatry, yet they did not deny that Allah Almighty was the Master of the heavens and the earth and of everything in them. Allah states here that He is the King and the Owner of both and of all they contain. If they answer, well. If they do not, the questioner answers for them, because no other reply is available, and there is nothing else that could be said.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, 'To whom belongs everything in the heavens and the earth?'"** is addressed to Muḥammad ﷺ, and what he is to ask about is dominion: who owns the created beings in the heavens and the earth, and whatever lies between them.

**"Say, 'To Allah!'"** Their total control belongs to Allah. He is the Owner of the creation of the heavens and the earth.

**"He has taken upon Himself to be Merciful"** means He wrote mercy on His own noble Self, decreed it as something He binds Himself to, and made mercy His own choice rather than something owed to Him. It is recorded in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs, from Abū Hurayrah, that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> "When Allah created creation, He wrote in a Book which He has with Him above the Throne: My mercy overcomes My anger."

The same wording appears in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, in a narration from Abū Hurairah, as al-Qurṭubī cites it: when Allah made His creation, He made Himself a written promise, and what is written in it is that His mercy stays in front of His wrath. On one reading this mercy is specified for the community of Muḥammad and consists in the punishment being deferred. He establishes mercy upon Himself out of grace for His servants, and so does not rush to punish them. This is not a neutral piece of information. It is a gentle summons: come, and the invitation is being made by the One who has already committed Himself.

**"He will certainly gather ˹all of˺ you together for the Day of Judgment"** is an oath taken by Allah's own honored Self that He will assemble His servants. The Day of Resurrection will certainly occur, and about it there is no doubt, no uncertainty at all. It is the day of an appointed meeting at a known time (56:50). Allah's believing servants have no doubt in this fact, while those who deny it and refuse it are left in confusion and disorder. The verb "gather" opened a question of its own about the particle meaning "towards" in the phrase "He will surely gather you towards a day of doom". Al-Qurṭubī reports two answers. It may be used in the sense of "take in full" or "bring to a finish", so that Allah gathers everyone, first and last, on the day of Qiyamah. Or it may mean gathering into the graves, so that human beings are kept being collected into their resting places until the Day of Qiyamah, when they are raised back to life.

**"But those who have ruined themselves will never believe"** gives the reason some do not believe in the Return and therefore do not fear what that Day will do to them. They are the ones who destroy themselves on the Day of Resurrection, who forfeit their own souls by exposing them to the chastisement, and who bring them to the point of ruin by their disbelief. The loss of the universal mercy named earlier in verse 12 comes from their own deeds, because they did not take the road that leads to mercy, which is the road of belief and faith. And they will not believe, and so will not rescue their souls from that loss.

### Why they are told to ask

The wording of the second half of the sentence has been read as a single thought with its two parts reversed: "those who have forfeited their souls" is the subject, "they do not believe" is the predicate. The promise of mercy and the threat of the gathering stand in one verse on purpose. Allah swore by Himself that He will assemble everyone to requite them for their deeds, and the same verse says that His mercy was written before that Reckoning, so that a person who hears the warning also hears what the warning is for.

### The inner reading

There is a way of hearing this verse that leaves no room for the question at all. Ask them: does anyone at all dwell in the house? Does the rightful due of anything that exists carry any weight with the Real? If they cannot answer, that silence is a healing for them. So say: Allah in His lordship is enough. Allah, and nothing else, and the rest is nonsense. It does not belong to anyone to give to Him, nor to Him to receive from anyone. All is from Him to Him. A world full of things and full of people, and He overturns all of it in a single breath, and a hundred eyes would not be enough for me to look at that work.

Before He began making what comes into being, and before He originated anything that is generated, mercy was minted in the treasury of the Unseen, and it became the hard cash of what His servants do and are. He had already said: "Surely I am Allah. There is no god but Me" (20:14). His mercy comes before His anger. On the Day of Mustering the Messenger will call out in the bazaar of the resurrection: here is a handful of the disobedient. Let me be told to dress them in the shirt of Your mercy, since You said: "We sent thee only as a mercy to the worlds" (21:107). It is the day of the bazaar for these beggars, and I was catching them with the lasso of the invitation and making them many promises. Do not put Muḥammad to shame in this gathering of the crowds, and make Your promise of generosity real, since You Yourself said: "O My servants who have been immoderate against yourselves, despair not of Allah's mercy" (39:53).

And the call of generosity comes back from the court of majesty: the affair of your community lies between three things. They are either people of faith, or people who have come to know, or people who disobeyed. If they believed and hoped for Paradise, then here is My Paradise. If they disobeyed and hoped for My forgiveness, then here is My mercy and My forgiveness.

### In short

- Ownership of the heavens and the earth was already conceded by the people of Makkah, which is why Allah answers the question He tells the Prophet ﷺ to ask.
- Mercy is something Allah wrote for Himself before the Reckoning, and it is stated here as a summons rather than a doctrine.
- The gathering for the Day of Judgement is sworn to by Allah Himself, and only those who deny it are thrown into confusion by it.
- Ruining oneself means failing to take the road that brings mercy, and so not believing in the Return.

<<<VERSE 6:13>>>
"To Him belongs whatever exists in the day and night. And He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing." Verse 12 claimed ownership of everything in the heavens and the earth. This verse claims the two halves of time and the ears and the knowledge that go with them. Whatever exists in the night and whatever exists in the day is His, all of it under His authority, His power and His will, and there is no deity worth worshipping except Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"To Him belongs whatever exists in the day and night"** uses a word, sukūn, that is at the center of the sentence's meaning. It can mean being still, settled, at rest, and then the sense is that Allah owns everything present in the night and in the day. It can also carry stillness and movement together, everything that tarries and everything that moves, and only rest is named because movement, which is its opposite, was obvious enough to go unstated. All that inhabits night and day is His: He is their Lord, their Creator and their Possessor. Whatever lies hidden in the night and whatever goes about openly in the day are His servants and His creation, all of them under His domination and control.

**"And He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing"** means He hears what His servants say and knows what they do, what they keep and what they hide. He is the Hearer of what is said and the Knower of what is done, and He will judge by them.

### What they had said, and what it is worth

The hearing is particular. It came about, on one account, because of what people said to Muḥammad ﷺ: give up our religion and come back to ours, and we will make you rich, marry you, give you standing, and make you king over us. Allah's answer sets His ownership of night and day against that offer. He is the Hearer of what they say, and the Knower of the punishment they are heading into, and of the provision every created being lives on. Kingship, wealth and a wife are exactly the kind of thing that belongs to Someone else.

### Night, day, and the angel holding both

The same verse has been read as a picture of time itself. Night, on this account, is a darkness spread over the world at large, and day is a brightness reaching the whole of it. Neither night nor day existed before the world was made, or before light and darkness were made. And in the Garden there is no sun, yet the whole of it is day, because brightness in general is already there. Whatever stands nearer to Allah has the fuller light and the brighter glow.

ʿAbdallāh ibn Masʿūd said: there is no day and no night with your Lord. He lights the heavens with the radiance of His own face. And the traditions mention that the Lord of the Worlds created an angel of tremendous size and put the night in one of his fists and the day in the other. Whenever he closes one fist and opens the other, that is the day taking charge. Whenever he closes that one and opens this one, that is the night taking charge. On the strength of that image, the allusion runs: the disk of the sun sits in the angel's fist, but the hearts of My friends were never handed to anyone else. The angel has no share and no power over the hearts of the friends of Allah. The disk of the sun rests in the angel's fist, while the hearts of those who love are held in the fist of the King, and majestic and high is He. Al-Muṣṭafā said that the hearts of the servants lie between two fingers of the Most Merciful.

### In short

- Ownership extends over time as well as space: what rests and what moves, in night and in day, is all His.
- Sukūn means rest, and it can be taken to cover rest and movement together, with the stillness named because the movement needs no naming.
- Hearing and knowing are here an answer to a specific bargain offered to the Prophet ﷺ, and a warning that nothing said is unheard.
- The inner reading takes day and night as created things held in an angel's fists, with human hearts outside that arrangement and inside Allah's hand.

<<<VERSE 6:14>>>
Two orders are given here, and both are spoken by one mouth. The Prophet ﷺ is told to say: "Will I take any guardian other than Allah, the Originator of the heavens and the earth?" And then: "I have been commanded to be the first to submit and not be one of the polytheists." The question is rhetorical and it is personal, and it is addressed to the idolaters of Makkah who worship their idols alongside Allah. Allah told His servant and Messenger Muḥammad ﷺ, sent with pure tawḥīd and the straight religion, to call people to Allah's straight path, and this is how he is told to answer a demand that he find a patron somewhere else. He will not take a guardian except Allah, with no partners, and he says so out loud.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Will I take any guardian other than Allah"** asks whether it makes sense for him to hold to anyone besides Him as a lord to worship, a protector from whom help is sought. It was said of this Prophet ﷺ: after Allah honoured him with His own friendship, should he take another as a friend? After the brightness of Allah's care fell on him, should his eyes go to anyone else in either of the two worlds?

**"the Originator of the heavens and the earth"** is the One who made them without any precedent, with no creator before Him, the Originator who brought earth and heaven into being. He is the one who arranges the world and its people, and He knows the outward and the hidden.

**"Who provides for all and is not in need of provision"** means He gives sustenance to His creatures without needing them. Some read it: He feeds but does not eat, meaning that Allah does not eat. He does not take a daily portion, He assigns a daily portion to His servants. Generosity is His own attribute, so He feeds. What is owed to Him by right is everlastingness, so He is not fed. None of His servants provides for Him. He has no need of them, and they have every need of Him.

That is also why He does not need anyone's worship at all: "I did not create jinn and humans except to worship Me" (51:56).

**"Say, 'I have been commanded to be the first to submit'"** means: my Lord ordered me to be the first of this ummah, this community, to surrender to Him as a Muslim, to be humble and to accept. It is also said that the meaning is the first among the people of his own time to worship sincerely and to declare Allah's oneness. **"and not be one of the polytheists"** is the same order from the other side: do not be one of those who associate partners with Him, do not join the idolaters and their religion. And do not let anyone order you to worship other than Allah. The Qur'an puts that answer elsewhere in his mouth: "Say: Do you order me to worship other than Allah, you fools?" (39:64).

### A prayer after eating

That He feeds and is not fed was drawn out of a meal. Abū Hurayrah narrated that a man from al-Anṣār, from the district of Qubāʾ, invited the Prophet ﷺ to food, and we went along with him. When the Prophet ﷺ had eaten and washed his hands, he said:

> "All praise is due to Allah, who feeds and is never fed. He bestowed bounty on us. He guided us and fed us and gave us drink and covered our nakedness. For every favour He has given us, all praise is due to Allah, a praise that must not be left aside. My Lord, we affirm that we can never thank You enough, or appreciate You enough, or be free of need of You. All thanks and praise are due to Allah, who fed us food and gave us drink and covered our nakedness and guided us after misguidance and gave us sight after blindness and honoured us above many of His creatures. All praise is due to Allah, Lord of everything that exists."

### Why his heart could not ask for anyone else

He was given such standing at the front of what is accepted that Allah at times puts up with his disdain. "By your life!" (15:72) is the oath the Qur'an uses where this rank is in view. At other times it is the grip of created qualities that polishes the mirror of his heart, out of care for him: "Did We not expand for you your breast?" (94:1). And at other times the recorder of the register that has no beginning and no end hands the acceptance and the refusal of the creatures over to his threshold: "Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it, and whatever he forbids you, leave it" (59:7). With that much good fortune, rank, care and kindness, how could his heart go asking for somebody else, or look toward this world or the next? About this world he said plainly: what have I to do with it? About the next world the verse says of his eye that "the sight did not swerve, nor did it overreach" (53:17). Neither this world nor the next, then, but the seeing of the Patron.

### What a will is for

A human being has a will of his own, and that is why his worship has to be given freely. Those who will not use that will properly have no share in God's bounties, since they have not done what they were made for. Nothing has power here, because power belongs to God alone. Everyone depends on Him and He depends on nothing. Whatever a person's arrogance leans on has no value in this universe, and on the Day when God takes the direct control of everything into His own hands and His scale of justice is working, the ones who come forward are the ones who admitted the reality and surrendered. The rest are the losers.

### In short

- "Originator" means He made the heavens and the earth with no precedent and no predecessor, so nothing else can be the first place to go for protection.
- "He feeds and is not fed" states that He sustains creation without needing it, and one reading takes it to mean plainly that Allah does not eat.
- The Prophet ﷺ is told to be the first to submit in this ummah and never to join the polytheists, and that is an order, not a personal preference.
- The verse is explained out of a shared meal, in a prayer that thanks Allah for feeding, guiding, covering and honouring.
- Taking a patron besides Allah is a refusal to use the will a person was created with.

<<<VERSE 6:15>>>
"Say, 'I truly fear—if I were to disobey my Lord—the torment of a tremendous Day.'" This is the penalty for the contravention of the order in verse 14, and it is put in a manner meant to win the heart rather than to threaten it. The Prophet ﷺ is told to say that if he himself disobeyed, he would fear the torment of a tremendous Day, which is the Day of Resurrection. The noble Messenger is protected by Allah from every sin, so disobedience from him is not a possibility at all. What the hypothetical does is teach the ummah how serious the matter is: if contravening a divine command is not excusable even for the greatest of the prophets, then who else can claim to be excused?

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, 'I truly fear'"** means: I know, and I am afraid. **"if I were to disobey my Lord"** means to rebel against Him, to worship anything other than Him, which would be a return to the religion they are asking him to accept, or to leave out what He has ordered, faith and good deeds being the first of them. **"the torment of a tremendous Day"** is what he would face then, and the Day is the Day of Rising.

### How serious the command is

The verses just before this one told them to keep away from shirk and to believe in the perfect power of Allah, Jalla Shaʾnuhu, mighty and exalted is He, and this verse names what happens if that is contravened. The threat is stated as a fear, in the first person, and the point of that manner is gentleness rather than force.

### The Day named twice

The wording leaves two readings of the fear. I would face a tremendous torment on an awful Day. Or simply: I would face torment on an awful Day. Either way, the point of putting it this way is that the person who is the closest to Allah is the one who fears most, and the community is meant to read the sentence as being about them. The fear of the Day of Qiyamah is what keeps the shirk out of a life, and the same Day that verse 12 promised mercy for is the Day this verse trembles at.

### In short

- The verse states the consequence of going against the command in verse 14: the torment of a tremendous Day.
- The Prophet ﷺ is sinless by Allah's protection, so the "if" is a teaching device, not a confession.
- The lesson is the seriousness of disobedience itself, measured by the fact that even the greatest prophet would not be excused.
- Fear of the Resurrection and hope of the mercy promised earlier belong in the same mouth, in the same passage.

<<<VERSE 6:16>>>
"Whoever is spared the torment of that Day will have certainly been shown Allah's mercy. And that is the absolute triumph." One verse does the whole arithmetic of hope. The torment is turned away, and turning it away is mercy, and mercy is victory. The punishment of the Day of Gathering, al-Maḥshar, is described as horrendous and harsh beyond description, so whoever finds it lifted off himself should know that it was a great mercy from Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever is spared the torment of that Day"** is read two ways. It can be passive: the torment is turned away from him on the Day of Judgement. Or it can be active, with Allah as the doer: He turns the torment away. The word for the torment itself is left out of the Arabic in one of the readings, and understood. **"will have certainly been shown Allah's mercy"** means Allah will have been merciful to him, preserved him and forgiven him.

**"And that is the absolute triumph"** is that forgiveness, and it is called a plain, evident, manifest success. The word used for it, fawz, means being admitted into al-Jannah, Paradise, which tells you that deliverance from the punishment and entry into the Garden are one event and not two.

### What success is worth

The same claim is stated in another verse as its explanation: whoever is moved away from the Fire and let into Paradise has certainly succeeded (3:185). Success in this verse means profit gained and loss avoided, and no other victory can be compared with it. On the Day when God's scale of justice is working, the ones who succeed are those who went looking for the support that is real and made the true faith the religion of their whole life. The person from whom Allah keeps the punishment away has attained success through His mercy, and that safety is a clear win.

### In short

- Being spared is itself the proof of mercy, since nothing else could have turned that torment away.
- The verse can be read either as the torment being averted or as Allah averting it, and both readings say the same thing about who saves.
- Triumph means Paradise, so escape from punishment and entry into the Garden are the same gift.
- The successful on that Day are the people who relied on what actually has power, and not on backing that turns out to have no value.
