On this reading the "word of Allah" is the Torah, which the Jews had "heard" from the prophets, and the group is a party of their rabbis and scholars, who heard it recited and knew what it said.

<<<VERSE 2:78>>>
Verse 78 moves from the learned to the masses. "And among them are the illiterate who know nothing about the Scripture except lies, and ˹so˺ they ˹wishfully˺ speculate". Having no direct knowledge of the Torah, the common people lived on hearsay and comfortable fables fed to them by their leaders.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And among them are the illiterate"**. Among the People of the Book, as Mujāhid said. Ummiyyūn is the plural of ummī, someone who cannot write, as Qatādah, Ibrāhīm an-Nakhaʿī, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ar-Rabīʿ and others explained. They could neither read the Torah nor write it. The rest of the phrase confirms the meaning: they do not know the Book, they are unaware of what is in it. Ummī was also one of the descriptions of the Prophet ﷺ, because he was unlettered: he could not read any writing before this revelation, nor write at all (29:48). He said, we are an ummī nation, we neither write nor calculate, and the month is like this, this and this, meaning thirty days or twenty-nine. This hadith shows that the timing of Muslim worship does not depend on books or calculations. And Allah describes Himself as the One who raised among the unlettered a messenger from among themselves (62:2).

**"who know nothing about the Scripture"**. These people had no knowledge of the Torah and were weak in understanding besides. Their scholars did not give them correct information about their religion, so their minds were stuffed with baseless superstitions that they found pleasant and flattering. They did not know what the Torah contained or what it said.

**"except lies"**. The word is amānī. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation that it means nothing more than false statements uttered with their tongues. Others said amānī means wishes and hopes. Mujāhid said that Allah described the ummiyyūn as not understanding any of the Book He sent down to Mūsā (Moses) and yet inventing lies and falsehood. So amānī here means lying and falsehood: the lies handed down to them by their leaders, which they relied upon and took to be the Torah that Allah revealed. All they had was the unfounded things they heard.

**"and ˹so˺ they ˹wishfully˺ speculate"**. Mujāhid said: they lie. Qatādah, Abū al-ʿĀliyah and ar-Rabīʿ said: they hold evil, false ideas about Allah. Everything they say is surmise, built on what their leaders told them. In rejecting the prophethood of the Prophet ﷺ and in their fabrications about other matters they have mere conjecture and no firm knowledge.

### Tales of cheap salvation

According to Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Farrāʾ and Mujāhid, among the most reliable of the early commentators, amānī refers to the mythical tales invented by the Jewish scholars. The stories existed, above all, to prove that Paradise belonged to the Jews alone. Sayings in support of the "chosen people" theory were attributed to venerated saints of the past, and the religion was credited with magical properties, so that salvation was guaranteed by nothing more than going through the routine rites of Judaism. Recipes for cheap salvation of this kind are very attractive to the masses, because they confirm the wishful notion that there is no need for self-discipline or a responsible life, that charms and magical remedies will cure one's spiritual ills and get one into heaven. The scholars who taught these tales became highly regarded, since their theories were designed to win popular support, and the business of easing people's way to eternal salvation became a lucrative source of income. People flocked to them bearing offerings. The scholars pointed the way to a paradise nobody had to earn, and were paid with wealth nobody had to work for. In every age the People of the Book have shown this weakness. They wanted to live in a dream world, imagining that they had no obligations beyond the perfunctory performance of empty rituals and deluding themselves that God had granted them inalienable rights. Such people cannot accept the call of true religion, because truth tears down wishful thinking and forces them to confront life's hard realities.

### The three meanings of ẓann

The verse says that the illiterate Jews follow their ẓann, their conjecture. Some people ignore the context and conclude that it is never legitimate to accept or follow any view or ruling based on ẓann, which they always translate as "fanciful supposition". That is one of the word's lexical meanings, but only one. The Qur'an uses ẓann in three ways. It can mean perfect certainty, as of those who are certain that they will meet their Lord (2:46). It can mean the greatest likelihood, as when Yūsuf (Joseph) spoke to the prisoner he thought would be saved (12:42). And it can mean a mere fanciful supposition, as in the present verse. The ẓann the Qur'an forbids us to follow is the third kind, a fanciful opinion unsupported by any respectable argument or contrary to a valid one, and this is what the Jews are condemned for. The first two kinds, on the contrary, must be followed. When certain hadiths, or certain arguments and conclusions in the Sharīʿah, are described as resting on ẓann, the word is being used in the second sense. No sphere of life can do without this kind of ẓann, and the Sharīʿah requires us to accept and act on it. Some verses of the Qur'an do condemn those who follow their ẓann, but to stretch that condemnation to every possible case, and to use such verses to reject all the legitimate arguments and rulings of the Sharīʿah that rest on ẓann, is to betray ignorance of the Qur'an.

### The same word, two different meanings

The devotional reading notices that the word "unlettered" is used here as a censure of the estranged and a mark of their shortcoming, while in the verse about following the Messenger, the Prophet who is unlettered (7:157), it praises Muṣṭafā ﷺ and marks his perfection. This alludes to the fact that sharing a name does not mean being of the same kind. Names may agree while meanings do not. On this rule the Folk of the Sunnah built their position on affirming the Real's attributes. God has attributes and descriptions that befit divinity, and creatures are far from them. Created things have created attributes, and God is pure of those. "Exalted" (ʿazīz) is a name of God, and Yūsuf (Joseph) was also addressed as "exalted" (12:78), but God's exaltedness is worthy of Him and the creature's exaltedness is worthy of the creature. By the agreement of the Muslims and the admission of most unbelievers, God exists and the creatures exist, but the creatures exist because God gives them existence, while God exists by His own abidance, being and subsistence. The Muslims agree that God is alive, and many created things are alive, but the created thing lives through breath and timely nourishment, while God's life is His own life and subsistence, His firstness and His lastness, with no when, no how many and no how. Even those who oppose the Folk of the Sunnah say that God is a maker and the creature a maker, but the creature makes through artifice, instrument, striving and measure, and God makes by power and wisdom, whatever, however and whenever He wills. The Qur'an contains many such cases. The sum of it is that God knows how He is, and is exactly as He has said, while His howness lies beyond the servant's knowledge. What God said about Himself should be seized with the eyeteeth and assented to from the depths of the spirit. To fancy that sharing a meaning means being of the same kind is to take the road of the roadless, and it is sheer rebellion. To hope to grasp God through imagination and searching is absurd, and whatever is gained that way is faulty. Safety in religion lies in accepting the message, approving of the one who conveyed it, bowing the head and setting seeking aside. Whoever believes this walks the right path, and his end will be as the Exalted Lord describes, where the passage breaks off.

### In short

- The ummiyyūn are those who cannot read or write, as Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Qatādah, ar-Rabīʿ and Ibrāhīm an-Nakhaʿī explained, and the word was also a title of the unlettered Prophet ﷺ (29:48, 62:2).
- Knowing nothing of the Torah, they lived on amānī: lies, wishful tales and the fables their scholars sold them as recipes for cheap salvation.
- Ẓann has three senses in the Qur'an, certainty (2:46), strong likelihood (12:42) and mere fancy, and only the third is condemned, so rulings of the Sharīʿah based on probable evidence stand.
- The same word can censure a people and praise the Prophet ﷺ (7:157): shared names do not mean shared meanings, which is the key to affirming Allah's attributes.

They will go on through their torment until they reach Saqar, where the tree of Zaqqūm grows, and until the number of days they spoke of has run out. Then the people of Hell will say to them: you claimed you would be punished only for a certain number of days. Those days have passed, and eternity remains.

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ 'Have you taken a pledge from Allah'"**. For what you are saying. Allah refutes the claim through this question. If you had a promise from Allah to this effect, then Allah would never break His promise. But no such promise ever existed.

**"or are you ˹just˺ saying about Allah what you do not know?"**. Things you know are not in your Book. You have no knowledge of what you say about Allah, so you are uttering a lie against Him.

### The argument behind the claim

Scholars have explained the Jews' claim that Hell would not hold them for their sins, or would hold them only a few days, in several ways. One explanation runs as follows. All the Sharīʿahs share the principle that a believer who commits sins may be punished in Hell for a time, according to the degree and kind of his sins, but because he possesses faith (īmān) he will not be consigned to Hell forever and will be released once his term is served. The Jews reasoned that the Sharīʿah of Mūsā (Moses) had, in their view, never been abrogated, so they remained true believers and had not become unbelievers by denying the prophethood of ʿĪsā (Jesus) and of Muḥammad ﷺ. Hence, they concluded, if they were sent to Hell for some sin they would be brought out again after a few days. This false assertion rests on another false assertion. Nowhere does the Torah declare that the Sharīʿah of Mūsā was meant to last forever, so the claim of perpetuity is baseless. Jews who made that claim and denied the prophethood of ʿĪsā and of Muḥammad ﷺ must, on account of that denial, be counted as unbelievers, and no Book of Allah promises unbelievers release from Hell after a while. The verse calls such a promise the "pledge" of Allah. Since Allah never gave it, the claim collapses.

### The poisoned sheep at Khaybar

Al-Ḥāfiẓ Abū Bakr ibn Mardawayh reported from Abū Hurayrah that when Khaybar was conquered, a roasted sheep laced with poison was presented to the Prophet ﷺ as a gift. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ ordered all the Jews there to be assembled before him and asked them, "Who is your father?" They named a man. He said, "You have lied. Your father is so-and-so." They said, "You have spoken the truth." He asked, "Will you tell me the truth if I ask you about something?" They said, "Yes, Abū al-Qāsim, and if we lie you will know it, as you knew about our fathers." He asked, "Who are the people of the Fire?" They said, "We shall be in it for a short period, and then you will replace us in it." The Prophet ﷺ said, "May you be cursed and humiliated in it! By Allah, we shall never replace you in it." He asked again whether they would answer truthfully, and they said yes. "Have you poisoned this sheep?" "Yes." "What made you do it?" "We wanted to know whether you were a liar, in which case we would be rid of you, and if you were a Prophet the poison would not harm you." Imām Aḥmad, al-Bukhārī and an-Nasāʾī record similar accounts. The Jews' own words at Khaybar repeat the claim of this verse, a short stay in the Fire and then release.

### The heart filled with false claims

The devotional reading sees in the verse an allusion to those whose hearts are filled with false claims. Conjecture has got the better of them, for in their utter foolishness they count themselves among the people of the story (ahl al-qiṣṣah), the chosen ones of whom the sacred accounts speak. Such a person makes his home among the murmurings of his lower desires. His judgement of the unseen rests on what he himself has neglected. The ugliness of his own deeds he forgets, and only the errors of his false thinking stay with him. He is a servant of his lower self, and his good opinion of himself has got the better of him, while in reality the results of his neglect and self-deception possess him. Allah said that the assumption they entertained about their Lord brought about their doom, so they became losers (41:23).

### In short

- The Jews claimed a stay of only a few days in the Fire, forty for the calf, or seven for the world's seven thousand years, after which, in one report, Hell's inhabitants will tell them that the days are over and eternity remains.
- Allah's answer is a question: do you hold His pledge, which He never breaks, or do you speak of Him without knowledge?
- The claim rested on the false premise that the law of Mūsā (Moses) could never be abrogated, so that denying ʿĪsā (Jesus) and Muḥammad ﷺ left them believers who would only be punished for a term.
- At Khaybar the Jews repeated the same boast to the Prophet ﷺ over a poisoned sheep, a report in Imām Aḥmad, al-Bukhārī and an-Nasāʾī, and the verse warns every heart that lives on self-flattering assumptions about Allah (41:23).

<<<VERSE 2:81>>>
Verse 81 states the real law in place of the Jews' wishful one. "But no! Those who commit evil and are engrossed in sin will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever". Allah is saying that the matter is not as they hoped and wished.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But no!"**. Here Allah answers the Jews. It is not as they imagine. The Fire will touch them, and they will abide in it.

**"Those who commit evil"**. Whoever does an evil deed and persists in his error on purpose, arriving on the Day of Resurrection with no good deeds but only evil ones, will be among the people of the Fire. One early gloss reads "evil" here as ascribing partners to Allah.

**"and are engrossed in sin"**. Literally, whose sin has surrounded him. Abū Hurayrah, Abū Wāʾil, ʿAṭāʾ and al-Ḥasan said this means that his shirk, his associating of partners with Allah, has surrounded him, so that he dies in that state. Al-Aʿmash reported from Abū Razīn that ar-Rabīʿ ibn Khuthaym explained it as whoever dies before repenting of his wrongs, and as-Suddī and Abū Razīn said much the same. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said it refers to the major sins. All these explanations come to similar meanings, and Allah knows best. The transgression overcomes such a person and encircles him completely, for he has died an idolater. The word for sin is read in the singular and also, in a variant, in the plural. Those who sink to the evil of disbelief, and whom disobedience rules, are repaid by Allah with the Fire.

**"will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever"**. Those with these attributes are the dwellers of Hell, there perpetually. They will neither die nor leave it. The word for "abiding" is plural because "whoever" carries a plural sense.

### How evil can take hold completely

Having refuted the Jews' claim, the Qur'an lays down the divine law on the matter. People who commit evil knowingly and deliberately, until evil takes hold of them so completely that no trace of goodness remains, will go to Hell and stay there forever, without respite or release. How can evil possess a person so completely? This happens only with unbelievers, never with Muslims, even sinful ones. Because of his unbelief, none of an unbeliever's good deeds is acceptable to Allah, and even the good he did before his apostasy and unbelief is lost and annulled, so on the Day of Judgement he has nothing to show but evil, and for that he lives in Hell forever. A person of faith, by contrast, begins with the greatest and highest of good deeds to his credit, faith (īmān) itself, and his lesser good deeds are recorded in his account as well. He can never be wholly without goodness, so evil cannot be said to have taken hold of him completely. In short, under this law the unbeliever must remain in Hell. Since Mūsā (Moses) was not the last prophet but was followed by ʿĪsā (Jesus) and Muḥammad ﷺ, the Jews became unbelievers by denying these two, and by this law they too will be consigned to Hell forever like other unbelievers, which shows their talk of release after a few days to be baseless. The Qur'an says the same elsewhere: it is not by your wishes nor the wishes of the People of the Book, for whoever does evil will be repaid for it, while whoever does good, male or female, and believes, will enter Paradise (4:123-124).

### Small sins that gather

Here belongs the hadith that Imām Aḥmad records from ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: beware of the sins you belittle, for they gather upon a person until they destroy him. He then gave them an example. Some people camped on open ground, and their servants came, one bringing a piece of wood and another bringing a piece, until they had collected a great heap. Then they lit a fire and cooked whatever they put on it.

### A heart that has stopped calling for help

The devotional reading notes that in the language of the scholars the one encompassed by his transgression is the disbeliever, but that the allusion is to a person whose heart has ceased its constant calling for help. The possessors of realities are like grain in a frying pan in their moments of sobriety (ṣaḥw). Those among them who have become still are so because of His overwhelming majesty, not out of listlessness. Whoever leans on his obedience, seeks favour through it and imagines he can draw near by means of it should keep his distance from that idea. Whoever comes to know the truth of God's oneness knows that there is no way to Him except through Him.

### In short

- Allah rejects the Jews' hope: whoever earns evil and is encompassed by his sin dwells in the Fire forever.
- The encompassing sin is shirk, or dying unrepentant, or the major sins, according to Abū Hurayrah, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Khuthaym, as-Suddī, Mujāhid, Qatādah and others, and the meanings converge.
- Only unbelief leaves a person with no good at all, since it annuls every deed, whereas a believer always has faith to his credit, as 4:123-124 confirms.
- Ibn Masʿūd's hadith in Imām Aḥmad warns that belittled sins pile up like sticks until they make a fire.

<<<VERSE 2:82>>>
Verse 82 completes the pair with the law's other side. "And those who believe and do good will be the residents of Paradise. They will be there forever". Faith and righteous deeds together, not membership of a chosen people, are what earn eternity in the Garden.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And those who believe"**. They believe in Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, in Muḥammad and the Qur'an. Faith itself is the greatest good deed a person can bring, and no one who has it is wholly without goodness.

**"and do good"**. They perform the good deeds that conform to Islamic law, acts of obedience between them and their Lord. Since the Sharīʿah of the Prophet ﷺ has abrogated the earlier Sharīʿahs, good deeds now mean deeds done in conformity with it. Entry into the Garden is the reward of those who believe in Allah and His Prophet ﷺ and act well.

**"will be the residents of Paradise. They will be there forever"**. Perpetually, they will never die nor come out of it. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq reported Ibn ʿAbbās explaining the verse this way: whoever believes in what you Jews refused to believe in, and practises what you refrained from practising of Muḥammad's religion, will gain Paradise for eternity. Allah has stated that the recompense for good works and for evil works remains with its people forever. The same promise appears elsewhere: those who do good, whether male or female, and have faith will enter Paradise and will not be wronged even the speck on a date stone (4:123-124).

### Faith, deeds and Paradise as a single tree

It has been said that "those who believe" alludes to the tree of faith and its planting in the believer's heart, that "do good" alludes to the branches of that tree and their nurturing and growth, and that "the residents of Paradise" alludes to what the tree yields and to its fruit ripening. This is the tree the Lord of the Worlds speaks of in another place, the good word likened to a good tree whose root is firm and whose branches reach the sky, yielding its fruit in every season by the will of its Lord (14:24-25). Its fruit is unlike the fruit of other trees, which bear once a year, year after year. This tree puts out fresh fruit every hour, indeed every moment, each with another colour, another taste and another fragrance. The sweetness the worshippers taste is a fruit of this tree. The feast of the seekers' hearts is a fruit of this tree. The clarity of the recognisers' present moment is another of its fruits. Today, in the house of service and on obedience's carpet, they already possess the paradise of recognition, neither turned away from them nor veiled. Tomorrow, in the house of union and on friendship's carpet, the paradise of approval will be theirs, neither cut off nor withheld, with fruit never out of season nor forbidden, and raised couches (56:33-34).

### Why the two verses belong together

Read together with the verse before it, this verse closes the argument with the Jews. Their boast of a short stay in the Fire assumed that they were still believers under the law of Mūsā (Moses). But the law of reward is the same for everyone: the person whose sin swallows him, above all shirk against Allah's oneness, remains in the Fire, and the person who believes in Allah's final Messenger ﷺ, after Mūsā and ʿĪsā (Jesus), and does good remains in Paradise. Neither outcome has an end, and neither depends on lineage.

### In short

- Faith in Allah and His Messenger ﷺ together with good deeds under the Islamic law brings eternal Paradise.
- Ibn ʿAbbās read it as a direct answer to the Jews: whoever believes what you rejected gains what you claimed.
- Like 4:123-124, it ties reward to belief and deeds, not to a people's wishes.
- Faith is a tree in the heart, deeds its branches and Paradise its fruit (14:24-25), a fruit that ripens anew every moment and lasts without end (56:33-34).
