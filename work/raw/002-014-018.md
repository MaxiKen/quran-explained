<<<VERSE 2:14>>>
Now the hypocrites are shown with both faces at once. "When they meet the believers they say, “We believe.” But when alone with their evil associates they say, “We are definitely with you; we were only mocking.”" In the company of the Muslims they loudly professed Islam. Back among their own leaders they explained that the profession was a joke at the believers' expense. The verse quotes them in both settings, and the next verse answers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When they meet the believers"** means when they fall in with Abū Bakr and his companions. The Arabic laqū is originally laquyū, with the ḍammah dropped as too heavy to pronounce and the unvowelled yāʾ dropped before the wāw.

**"they say, “We believe.”"** They claimed to believe deep in their hearts and to be as genuine in their faith as the believers were in theirs. They did it to misdirect, mislead and deceive the believers, to pose as loyal friends, and because they expected it to pay: they wanted a share of whatever gains and benefits the believers might come by.

**"But when alone with their evil associates"** means when they went off by themselves and returned to their devils, that is, their leaders and masters among the Jewish rabbis, the hypocrites and the idolaters. Ibn Jarīr said that the devils of every kind of creature are the mischief-makers among them, and that there are human devils as well as jinn devils, as Allah said: "And so We have made for every prophet enemies—devilish humans and jinn—whispering to one another with elegant words of deception" (6:112). One report names five such leaders: Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf in Madinah, Abū Burdah al-Aslamī among Banū Aslam, Ibn as-Sawdāʾ in ash-Shām, ʿAbd ad-Dār in Juhaynah and ʿAwf ibn ʿĀmir among Banū ʿĀmir.

**"they say, “We are definitely with you"** means: we are with you in religion, we follow your religion in secret.

**"we were only mocking.”"** Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq reported Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation: we are with you, and we were only mocking those people and deceiving them. Through aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ibn ʿAbbās said it means they were mocking the Companions of Muhammad ﷺ, and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and Qatādah said the same. They were mocking the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions by feigning belief.

### The occasion of revelation

Through Abū Ṣāliḥ, Ibn ʿAbbās said that the verse was revealed about ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy and his followers. They went out one day and were met by a group of the Prophet's ﷺ Companions. ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy said to his people, "Watch how I keep these fools off you." He went forward and took the hand of Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq, and said: welcome to the truthful one, chief of Banū Tamīm, leader of Islam, the Messenger's companion in the cave, who spent himself and his wealth for him. Then he took the hand of ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb: welcome to the chief of Banū ʿAdī ibn Kaʿb, the one who separates truth from falsehood, firm in Allah's religion, who likewise spent himself and his wealth for the Messenger. Then he took the hand of ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib: welcome to the Messenger's cousin and his son-in-law, chief of Banū Hāshim after the Messenger himself. Then the two groups parted. ʿAbdullāh asked his companions, "How did I do? When you meet them, do as I did," and they praised him. When the Muslims returned to the Prophet ﷺ and told him what had happened, Allah sent down this verse.

### Where the verse stands

The surah opened by declaring the Qur'an beyond doubt, and its first twenty verses draw the features of those who believe in it and those who do not. The first five deal with the believers under the title of the mindful, the next two with the disbelievers who were open and violent in their hostility, and the following thirteen with the crafty disbelievers who claimed to be Muslims but were not, whom the Qur'an calls the hypocrites. The first two of these thirteen verses, 8 and 9, define the hypocrites' behaviour, exposing their claim to be Muslims as false and deceitful, an attempt to be clever. No one can deceive Allah, and probably they never imagined they could, but the Qur'an in a sense equates trying to deceive the Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims with trying to deceive Allah Himself. Such an attempt has only one result: they deceive no one but themselves, because Allah cannot be deceived and revelation protects His Prophet ﷺ from every trick, so that the hypocrites themselves bear the punishment for their presumption in both worlds. As for Allah making them grow in their sickness, they were jealous of the Muslims' growing strength, and it was Allah's will to make the Muslims stronger still, as the hypocrites could see for themselves, which fed their bile and kept the disease of their hearts growing. Verses 11 and 12 describe their insensibility and ignorance: they counted their defects as merits. Verse 13 shows the other side of that corruption: the merit of others, the pure faith of the Muslims, turned in their eyes into a defect, even into something contemptible. When they openly refused to believe as others believed and called the Muslims fools, they could have been so outspoken only in front of the poorer Muslims, since otherwise they were very careful to keep their disbelief hidden. The present verse shows their two-facedness and trickery.

### What faith really is

Reflection on these verses reveals the true nature of Islam and faith, and of disbelief. The Qur'an reports the hypocrites' claim, "We believe in Allah" (2:8), and refutes it at once: they are not believers. To grasp the point fully, keep in mind that the hypocrites in question were Jews. Belief in Allah and the Hereafter was an essential part of their creed too. What their creed, as their scholars defined it, did not include was belief in the prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ. So when they professed Islam they very cleverly left out faith in the Prophet ﷺ and named only belief in Allah and belief in the Hereafter. As far as those words go they could not be called liars, yet the Qur'an rejects their claim to be Muslims and calls them liars. Why?

Because to be a Muslim it is not enough to profess faith in Allah and the Hereafter in whatever form suits one's individual or collective fancy. Idolaters of every kind believe in Allah in some fashion and hold Him to be all-powerful, and even the peoples Western writers label "savages" or "primitives", for whom "degenerates" would be the truer word, hold some vague idea of a supreme deity and usually quite vivid ideas about the next world. The Qur'an lets none of this pass for faith. Faith in Allah is valid and deserves the name only when it conforms to what the Qur'an lays down about the divine names and attributes, and belief in the Hereafter is valid only when it matches what the Qur'an and the hadith specify. Books are made without end, and so are gods and names for gods, never more than in modern times. Reason, Nature, Man and Life, having served their turn and grown rusty, are being replaced by fancier names, "the ground of being", the "archetype of the collective unconscious", and lately even a godless theology. Measured by these requirements, the Jews who posed as Muslims believed in neither Allah nor the Hereafter. They took the prophet ʿUzayr (Ezra) for the son of God, and they fondly believed that the prophets' descendants, whatever they did, would stay God's chosen people and escape the reckoning of the Day of Judgment, or at worst suffer a token punishment. Holding such beliefs, their claim to faith in Allah and the Hereafter was rightly rejected.

Verse 13 defines faith: believe as other people have believed. The standard for judging any claim to faith is the faith of the Companions, and a claim that does not conform to it is not accepted by Allah or His Prophet ﷺ. If someone presumes, as is all too common nowadays, to interpret an Islamic doctrine or a verse of the Qur'an in a way that departs from the clear explanation given by the Qur'an itself or by the Prophet ﷺ, his private opinion, however much it pleases his contemporaries or feeds their fancy, has no value or validity in the Sharīʿah. For example, the Qadianis, who style themselves Ahmadis, claim that like Muslims they believe in the finality of the prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ, but they depart from what the Prophet ﷺ himself stated and the Companions believed, and twist the doctrine until it has room for Mirza Ghulam Ahmad of Qadian as a prophet, so by the Qur'an's indication the same verdict falls on them: they are not believers. To sum up, when a man reads an Islamic doctrine in a way that clashes with the faith of the Companions and still claims to be a Muslim on the strength of that doctrine, performing his religious duties exactly like Muslims, he is not counted a believer until he agrees to conform to the criterion of faith the Qur'an has laid down.

This also clears up a misunderstanding, often raised and more often raised on purpose, about the well-known rule in hadith and fiqh that the "people of the qiblah", those who face the Kaʿbah in the prescribed prayer, may not be branded disbelievers. These verses define the phrase. It applies only to those who deny none of the essential doctrines and commandments of Islam, the ḍarūriyyāt. The hypocrites mentioned in the Qur'an prayed exactly as the Muslims did, yet facing the Kaʿbah was not taken as enough to make them true Muslims, simply because they did not believe in all the essentials of Islam as the Companions did.

### Belonging to neither side

The hypocrites wanted to keep their ties with the disbelievers while mixing with the Muslims. Before the Muslims they said, "We are with you," and alone with their own kind among the disbelievers they voiced their loyalty to them. They wanted relationships with both sides, and were shut out of both: "Torn between belief and disbelief—belonging neither to these ˹believers˺ nor those ˹disbelievers˺" (4:143). The inner reading extends this to anyone who wants to combine the path of spiritual desire with the habits of ordinary people. It cannot be done, because two opposites do not combine. The slave who has contracted with his master for his freedom remains a slave until the last dirham is paid. When night draws near, day slips away. Whoever keeps a mixed crowd in every corner and a heart tied to every nook is headed for ruin. One group after another takes its turn living in his heart, so that it is forever wrecked, and he gets no pleasure from life and no real provision from his heart. In the words of a poet about such people: I see you as a leftover from the people of Mūsā (Moses), for they would not wait patiently for their food.

In a dark room black and white look the same, but turn on the light and black shows as black and white as white. So it is with the message Allah revealed to the prophets. The divine scriptures are His beacons to the world, and when their light falls on mankind, guidance and error appear for what they are, and so do good and evil deeds and their consequences. But some people do not bow to the truth. They want the truth to serve them, and Allah's light only confuses them. Hidden jealousy and pride take hold of them, and the moment they see themselves in the divine mirror their resentments are stirred and their prejudices overwhelm their senses.

### In short

- The hypocrites professed faith to the believers for safety and advantage, then told their leaders, the "devils", human and jinn, that it was all mockery. ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy's staged greeting of Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿAlī was the occasion of the verse.
- Their profession, "We believe in Allah and the Last Day", was rejected because faith counts only when it matches what the Qur'an and the Prophet ﷺ laid down and what the Companions believed. That standard excludes anyone who reshapes an essential doctrine, and it defines who the "people of the qiblah" are.
- Wanting both camps, they ended up in neither, which is the fate of anyone who tries to join the way of Allah with the way of the crowd.

<<<VERSE 2:15>>>
The answer to "we were only mocking" comes from Allah Himself. "Allah will throw their mockery back at them, leaving them to continue wandering blindly in their defiance". The hypocrites were delighted with themselves, sure they could make fools of the Muslims and get away with it. Allah gave them a long rope, and the rope was the punishment.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah will throw their mockery back at them"** means He requites them for their mockery, repaying them in equal measure. Ibn Jarīr pointed to what Allah will do with them on the Day of Resurrection: "On that Day hypocrite men and women will beg the believers, “Wait for us so that we may have some of your light.” It will be said ˹mockingly˺, “Go back ˹to the world˺ and seek a light ˹there˺!”" (57:13), and "Those who disbelieve should not think that living longer is good for them. They are only given more time to increase in sin, and they will suffer a humiliating punishment" (3:178). This, he said, and things like it, is Allah's mockery of the hypocrites and the people of shirk. One description of it: in the Hereafter a gate of Paradise is opened to them and then shut in their faces, while the believers laugh. In this world they are treated as believers, and in the next they are repaid for their lying and deception.

Allah describes the deed and its punishment with the same word, though the meanings differ, just as He said, "The reward of an evil deed is its equivalent" (42:40), and "if anyone attacks you, retaliate in the same manner" (2:194). The first act is injustice and the second is justice. The two share a name but differ in reality. This is how the scholars explain deceit, cunning and mockery whenever they are attributed to Allah in the Qur'an. He takes revenge for certain evil acts with a punishment that matches the act in kind. By consensus of the scholars He does none of this in play. It is a just punishment for particular evil deeds.

**"leaving them to continue wandering blindly in their defiance"** means that He gives them respite, as Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and several other Companions explained through as-Suddī. Mujāhid said it means He increases them in their deviation. Both are in the Qur'an: "Do they think, since We provide them with wealth and children, that We hasten to ˹honour˺ them ˹with˺ all kinds of good? No! They are not aware" (23:55-56). Ibn Jarīr took the correct meaning to be as in "We turn their hearts and eyes away ˹from the truth˺ as they refused to believe at first, leaving them to wander blindly in their defiance" (6:110). Ṭughyān, "defiance", means transgressing the limits, as the water did in "when the floodwater had overflowed, We carried you in the floating Ark" (69:11), and here it is the limits of disbelief that they overstep. Ibn Jarīr said the word for wandering, ʿamah, means deviation, and explained the phrase: in the misguidance and disbelief that have surrounded them they are confused and cannot find a way out, because Allah has stamped and sealed their hearts and blinded their sight, so they neither recognise guidance nor find the exit from their error. The final word, yaʿmahūn, describes their state: wavering in perplexity, left in this world blind in their disbelief, unable to see faith or guidance, more and more lost, doing more and more wrong, confused and doubtful about the truth.

### How the mockery worked

The hypocrites were mightily pleased with themselves for fooling the Muslims, when they were only fooling themselves. Allah, in His forbearance and mercy, gave them a long rope, and that very rope was the means of making them ridiculous. Seeing no visible sign of divine punishment coming down on them, they grew bolder in their complacency and rebellion, until the cup of their wickedness was full and one day they were caught. Allah acted this way in response to their mockery, so the Qur'an calls His action mockery too.

### The inner reading

When people put the reins in the hands of their desires they are lured into the valleys of separation. Their feet find no firm place, and they wander in the maze of absence. Just as Allah leaves the hypocrites bewildered in their defiance, He prolongs the time they spend with their muddled hopes, so that when death approaches their hopes have never been higher and their deeds never lower. That is their repayment for what they did, the poisonous fruit of their own making. The worst of their punishments is that their ugly deeds look beautiful to them, and their greatest misfortune is that they are content with their lethargy.

### In short

- Allah's "mockery" is the just repayment of their mockery, named after the crime as retaliation is named after the offence. It is never play.
- It takes the form of respite in this world, which lets them fill their cup, and of humiliation in the next, when they beg for the believers' light and are turned back.
- Their defiance is overstepping the bounds of disbelief, and their wandering is the blindness of sealed hearts that cannot find the way out.

<<<VERSE 2:16>>>
The hypocrites thought of themselves as shrewd men of the world. The Qur'an judges them as traders. "They are the ones who trade guidance for misguidance. But this trade is profitless, and they are not ˹rightly˺ guided". They gave away the most valuable thing there is for something worthless and even harmful, and in calling that a trade the verse implies that these worldly-wise men did not understand even the art of trading.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They are the ones who trade guidance for misguidance"** means, as Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd said through as-Suddī, that they took up misguidance and abandoned guidance. Mujāhid said they believed and then disbelieved. Qatādah said they preferred error to guidance, which matches what Allah said of Thamūd: "As for Thamûd, We showed them guidance, but they preferred blindness over guidance" (41:17). Taken together, these explanations show that the hypocrites turned from true guidance and chose misguidance, putting wickedness in the place of righteousness. They exchanged guidance to buy error, choosing disbelief instead of faith. The verse covers both those who first believed and later disbelieved, of whom Allah said, "This is because they believed and then abandoned faith. Therefore, their hearts have been sealed, so they do not comprehend" (63:3), and those who simply preferred deviation over guidance, for the hypocrites are of several kinds.

**"But this trade is profitless"** means their dealing did not succeed. They gained nothing from it and in fact lost, since their destination is the Fire, made everlasting for them. The exchange of faith for disbelief helped them in no way, because they lost their faith in Allah and lost the road to the truth. In this they showed themselves the real fools.

**"and they are not ˹rightly˺ guided"** means they were neither righteous nor rightly guided in any of this, and they were not guided to a way out of their error. Ibn Jarīr recorded Qatādah's comment: "By Allah, I have seen them leave guidance for error, leave the community for the sects, leave safety for fear, and leave the Sunnah for innovation." Ibn Abī Ḥātim reported similar statements.

### A failure of ordinary judgment

The verse shows the hypocrites' basic dullness. They failed to use the ordinary power of telling things apart. Raised in a pagan society, they knew perfectly well what the disbelievers' way was, and by now they were familiar enough with Islam to see the difference easily. Yet out of greed, for petty worldly profit, they still chose disbelief over Islam, bartering something as priceless as faith for something as worthless and harmful as disbelief.

### The inner reading

The verse alludes to those who have neglected what they owe by staying in the familiar territory of worldly fortunes. They have lost their bargain. Whoever is content with this world over the next is in plain loss. Whoever prefers this world, or the next, over the Real suffers the most intense loss of all. The one who loses the gardens of bliss falls short, but the one who truly suffers is the one afflicted with distance from intimate conversation with his Protector, whose heart has been cut loose from Him, who stays captive to his desires with no messenger reaching his heart, whose spirit attains nothing, with no intimate speech, no approach and no witnessing within. If even a moment slips from him, his Lord has slipped from him, and moments have no substitutes. A poet pictures the beloved as the pupil through which the eye sees. That eye now weeps at the loss. He wishes death upon any desire that survives the beloved, recalling how carefully he used to watch over the one he loved.

### In short

- The hypocrites swapped guidance for error, some by believing and then disbelieving, some by preferring blindness as Thamūd did.
- It was a losing trade: nothing gained, faith and the way to truth lost, and the Fire at the end.
- Men who prided themselves on worldly shrewdness showed they could not tell a good bargain from a ruinous one.

<<<VERSE 2:17>>>
The surah now paints the hypocrites in a picture. "Their example is that of someone who kindles a fire, but when it lights up all around them, Allah takes away their light, leaving them in complete darkness—unable to see". Two extended similes close the portrait, this one of fire and the next of water, and they describe two kinds of hypocrite. This verse is about the kind in whom disbelief had taken deep root, who had little inclination toward Islam and pretended to be Muslims for worldly motives: the man who found light and lost it again.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Their example is that of someone who kindles a fire"** describes how the hypocrites stand in their hypocrisy. A man lights a fire in the darkness, at the beginning of the night, to keep his person, family and property safe. Though the verse speaks of one man, the pronoun that follows is plural, "their light", because the Arabic alladhī here carries a plural sense.

**"but when it lights up all around them"** means that when the fire is lit and illuminates the area around him, so that he can see, feel warm and feel secure from whatever he feared, at that very moment it is put out.

**"Allah takes away their light"** means He removes what benefits them, the light, and leaves them what harms them, the darkness and the smoke. He extinguishes it.

**"leaving them in complete darkness—unable to see"** means that total darkness falls on this man and he cannot see anything around him or find his way out. He is confused about the road and afraid. The darkness is their doubts, disbelief and hypocrisy, and not seeing means being unable to find the right path or even its direction.

### What the picture means

When the hypocrites bought error with guidance they acquired utter blindness, and this is their likeness. The man benefited from his fire and felt safe, then it suddenly went out and he was left in the dark. Worse, he cannot hear or speak, and he has become so blind that even if there were light he could not see, so he can never return to the state he was in before. Such are the hypocrites who preferred misguidance to guidance and deviation to righteousness. The parable indicates that they first believed and then disbelieved, as the Qur'an says of them elsewhere. In practical terms, the hypocrites professed belief in Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an openly and so felt that their persons, families and property were safe, escaping imprisonment or death. But when they die Allah takes away the benefit of that outward faith and leaves them in the darkness of the grave's hardships, where they see no comfort. People who have bought temporary security by professing faith will meet terror and punishment at death. Something of this world's benefits appears to them outwardly, and then the pain of the punishment overtakes them in the Hereafter. Or, in another reading, a flash of affirmation appears in them, and then they remain in the darkness of their denial.

It is also said that the simile refers to the Jews. Their situation with regard to Muhammad ﷺ was that of a man who, in a lost battle, raises a flag and gathers the other defeated fighters to it, then lowers it once they have gathered, so that the benefit and safety it offered vanish at once. Before the Qur'an was revealed the Jews used to say that Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an would support them, and when it came they denied it, so Allah took away their light, their desire to believe and the benefit it would have brought. Though they wanted to believe in him they did not, and they were left in the error of their religion, unable to see guidance.

The hypocrites are also warned in these parables that they are not beyond Allah's power. He can take away their sight and hearing whenever He wills, and even destroy them.

### The objection to parables

When Allah struck these two similitudes for the hypocrites, this one and the one about the rainstorm from the sky (2:19), the hypocrites said, as Ibn ʿAbbās reported through Abū Ṣāliḥ, that Allah is too exalted and sublime to coin similitudes, and Allah sent down the verse "Surely Allah does not shy away from using the parable of a mosquito or what is even smaller" (2:26). Al-Ḥasan and Qatādah said that when gnats and spiders appeared in Allah's Book as similitudes for the disbelievers, the Jews laughed and said this did not resemble Allah's speech, and so that verse came down. Through Ibn Jurayj from ʿAṭāʾ, Ibn ʿAbbās explained that Allah had mentioned the idolaters' gods, saying, "if a fly were to snatch anything away from them, they cannot ˹even˺ retrieve it" (22:73), and had compared their scheme to a cobweb, and the idolaters said, "See how Allah mentions gnats and spiders in the Qur'an revealed to Muhammad. What is the use of this?" The verse answered them.

### The seeker who turns back

In the inner reading, the verse alludes to a person who makes a beautiful beginning on the path of spiritual desire. He toils for a time and bears one hardship after another, then returns to the world before reaching the truth and falls back into the darkness of his own nature. His stem sprouted but bore no fruit. His branch blossomed but never ripened. He hurried on the eclipse of lethargy over the moons of Allah's presence, and the hand of subjugation rejected him after the tongue of kindness had beckoned him. His heart moved away from nearness, and his lower self filled with rancour toward the seekers. As the poet says, when passion settled in and we called ourselves happy, we thought ourselves safe from growing apart, but division sent its messengers in secret and destroyed what our union had built. The verse also alludes to the one who grasps only the lowest level of understanding yet makes claims far above it: when the increase in his states is cut off he is left in the darkness of his claims. And it alludes to the one who leans on the rubble and glitter of this world. When his affairs have settled into a well-worn road, hope and rising expectation carrying him along, death springs on him as an ambush of the divine ruse. He leaves everything behind and carries the burden forward.

### In short

- The hypocrite is a man who lit a fire for safety and had it snuffed out the moment it began to serve him. His profession of faith secured him in this world, and death takes that light away.
- The simile points to those who believed and then disbelieved, and by another reading to the Jews who awaited a prophet and denied him when he came.
- The parables drew mockery, and Allah answered it in verse 26: He does not shy away from any example, however small.

<<<VERSE 2:18>>>
The parable ends with a verdict on the man left in the dark. "They are ˹wilfully˺ deaf, dumb, and blind, so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺". Three senses are named, and the loss of all three explains why there is no way back.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They are ˹wilfully˺ deaf"** means they cannot hear the guidance. They are deaf to the truth, unable to hear it and accept it, and they behave as if they were deaf.

**"dumb"** means they cannot utter the words that would benefit them. They are mute where goodness is concerned, unable to speak of it, and they act as if they were dumb.

**"and blind"** means they are in total darkness and deviation, blind to the path of guidance so that they cannot see it, and they act as if they were blind. Allah said, "it is not the eyes that are blind, but it is the hearts in the chests that grow blind" (22:46).

**"so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺"** means they cannot get back to the guidance they once had, because they sold it for misguidance. They do not turn back from their disbelief and error.

### Senses that refuse to work

People of this kind have eyes but do not see, ears but do not hear, tongues but do not speak. They can neither hear the call of truth, nor answer it in any way, nor find their way by any sign from Allah. Instead of reflecting on the call as they should, they dodge it, either by treating it as unimportant or by not listening at all.

### The inner reading

Their hearts' ears are deaf to the Real's calls. The tongue of their innermost self is dumb, unable to converse with Him in whispers. The eyes of their insight are blind to the flow of decreed events. They do not turn back from their persistent shamelessness, and nothing deters them from the error they have embraced with their whole heart. Another way of putting it: they cannot hear through the Real, cannot speak through the Real, and cannot study creation through the Real. Nothing was decreed for them that would make them desist, and no share was allotted to them that would help them be deterred.

### In short

- Deaf to guidance, dumb about anything good, blind to the way: the three channels by which truth reaches a person are all shut.
- The blindness is of the heart, not the eyes, and it is wilful, a pretence that became a condition.
- Having sold their guidance, they have nothing to return to, and so they do not return.
