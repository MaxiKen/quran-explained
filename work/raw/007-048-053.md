<<<VERSE 7:48>>>
The people of the Heights now speak to some of those in the Fire: "Those on the heights will call out to some ˹tyrants in the Fire˺, who they will recognize by their appearance, saying, “Your large numbers and arrogance are of no use ˹today˺". They rebuke certain chiefs of the idolaters, whom they recognise by their marks. The wealth and power those chiefs relied on, and which made them proud, have gone to waste. None of that strength helped them in their hour of need.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those on the heights will call out to some ˹tyrants in the Fire˺"**: men from among the disbelievers, the people of the Fire who had disbelieved. One explanation gives their names, as the people of the Heights call to them: "Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah! Abū Jahl ibn Hishām! Umayyah ibn Khalaf! Ubayy ibn Khalaf al-Jumaḥī! Aswad ibn ʿAbd ul-Muṭṭalib! You leaders!" That is what they say to them.

**"who they will recognize by their appearance"**: by their marks, their darkened faces and blue eyes. One explanation says they knew them before they entered Hell.

**"saying, “Your large numbers"**: literally "your gathering". It means your great numbers, your masses of property, your wealth and servants. Another explanation is "your gathering together to keep people from Allah's path".

**"and arrogance are of no use ˹today˺"**: nor did your pride help you, the haughtiness that made you scorn belief in Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an and turn arrogantly from the truth. Your numbers and wealth did not shield you from Allah's punishment. Instead you now live in His torment.

### Who else might the people of the Heights be?

According to al-Qurṭubī, "the people of the heights" in this verse are the shuhadāʾ, the witnesses. They are those special servants of Allah who, in serving His religion, witnessed the deeds of the nations. Some accepted their call and some rejected it. The Qur'an speaks of these bearers of the divine message under many names, such as "warner", "bearer of glad tidings" and "caller". The group first consisted of the prophets. Later it was made up of the special servants who followed the prophets' example and called the people of their own times (daʿwah).

On this view, the final judgement in the Hereafter will rest on the witness (shahādah) these people gave to the world. Their witness divides people into two groups, one that accepts the divine message and one that rejects it. On the Day of Judgement the two will be separated and judged by their deeds, and will meet two different outcomes. The judgement will be Allah's alone. Yet its announcement will be made by those special servants who took on the task of calling people to Him, and that will be a great honour for them.

High platforms will be raised for "the people of the heights" to stand on. From there they will see everyone and announce Allah's judgement. The witnesses (shuhadāʾ) and the callers to truth (duʿāt) strove very hard in the world to deliver Allah's message. They devoted their entire lives to this mission and treated it as their own personal concern. So on that Day they will be honoured by being asked to announce the final result of their call. They held a high rank in the world by their mission, and they will be called the people of high rank on the Day of Judgement by the result of their work.

### Is calling others to the truth optional?

The duty of conveying the message of truth to everyone has been placed on the Muslims. It is not an optional subject, to be taken up or dropped with some excuse. It must be carried out at any cost. Allah will not accept any excuse from the Prophet's followers. Even their other religious acts will not be enough to save the believers if they ignored the duty of conveying the truth to all people.

Every day thousands of men and women die without the message of Allah ever reaching them. Without the chance to accept it, they have missed the chance to better their life in the Hereafter. In such a situation the believers carry a grave responsibility. They must stop making excuses and take up the mission of proclaiming the divine truth in earnest.

### In short

- The people of the Heights will rebuke the leaders of disbelief in the Fire, recognising them by their marks.
- The numbers, wealth and pride those leaders relied on could not protect them from Allah's punishment.
- Al-Qurṭubī took the people of the Heights to be the witnesses who called nations to Allah, honoured with announcing His judgement.
- Conveying Allah's message to all people is a binding duty on Muslims, not an optional extra.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:49>>>
The rebuke ends with a pointed question, and then comes a sudden declaration of mercy: "Are these ˹humble believers˺ the ones you swore would never be shown Allah’s mercy?” ˹Finally, those on the heights will be told:˺ “Enter Paradise! There will be no fear for you, nor will you grieve.”" Those whom the arrogant had written off are the very ones admitted to Paradise.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Are these ˹humble believers˺ the ones you swore would never be shown Allah’s mercy?”"** Two explanations are given of who "these" are.

- ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that "these" are the people of the Heights themselves. When all the questions and answers between the people of the Heights and the people of Paradise and Hell are over, the Lord of the worlds will speak to the people of Hell about the people of the Heights. He will remind them that they had sworn these would never be forgiven or shown mercy.
- Another explanation is that the people of the Heights turn and look at the people of Paradise. Among them are Salmān al-Fārisī, Ṣuhayb al-Rūmī, ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir and all the other humble, weak believers, the Muslims who were once oppressed. Pointing to them, they say to the disbelievers: are these the weak people of whom you swore in the world that Allah would never admit them to Paradise? They have entered it in spite of you.

**"˹Finally, those on the heights will be told:˺ “Enter Paradise!"** At once comes Allah's declaration of mercy, when Allah decrees it. One reading has the command udkhulū, "enter". Another reads udkhilū, "they have been admitted", or dakhalū, "they entered".

**"There will be no fear for you, nor will you grieve.”"** You need fear nothing for what happened in the past, nor worry about the future. You will have no fear of punishment and no grief over the pleasures of the world that passed you by, because of the eternal bliss you are in. Grammatically, this clause describes their state: they enter Paradise even as these words are said to them.

### Why does this question cut so deep?

The arrogant made confident promises about who would and would not receive Allah's mercy. Now they watch those very people enter Paradise, while they remain in the Fire. Whether "these" are the people of the Heights or the humble believers, the scene shows that it is Allah alone who decides who will receive His mercy.

### In short

- The people in the Fire once swore that certain people would never receive Allah's mercy.
- By one account those people are the people of the Heights. By another they are humble believers such as Salmān, Ṣuhayb and ʿAmmār.
- Allah's mercy overturns the oath: they are told to enter Paradise with no fear and no grief.

<<<VERSE 7:50>>>
The disgrace of the people of the Fire deepens as they beg: "The residents of the Fire will then cry out to the residents of Paradise, “Aid us with some water or any provision Allah has granted you.” They will reply, “Allah has forbidden both to the disbelievers". They plead with the people of Paradise to share their food and drink, and are given nothing.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The residents of the Fire will then cry out to the residents of Paradise"**: they will call out, begging. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said one of them will call to his father or his brother: "I am burning, pour some water on me!"

**"“Aid us with some water"**: literally "pour on us some water".

**"or any provision Allah has granted you.”"** As-Suddī said, "That is food." Others say it means the fruits of Paradise, or some of the food Allah has given them.

**"They will reply, “Allah has forbidden both to the disbelievers"**: the believers will be told to answer, and they will say that Allah has prohibited both. ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said this means the food and drink of Paradise, its water and its fruits. They are forbidden to the disbelievers because of their disbelief. The people of Paradise will not help anyone in what Allah has forbidden them.

### Why can't the people of the Fire enjoy Paradise's food?

The world offers two kinds of food, one worldly and one belonging to the next world. One kind of person feeds his soul on seeing himself made prominent. He is happiest when surrounded by worldly glory, and counts himself a success when he gains and owns worldly goods. He has forgotten Allah and the Hereafter completely. If anything about Allah is brought to his notice, he thinks it trivial and ignores it. He treats it lightly, as if it were not a serious matter but a game.

Such a person will have no share in the bounties of the Hereafter. He has raised a soul in himself that can feed only on worldly things, so how could the things of the Hereafter nourish it? Someone who did not live for the Hereafter in his life today cannot deserve Allah's blessings in it.

The other kind of person is absorbed in unseen realities. His soul delights in the remembrance of the Hereafter. His daily bread is living with Allah and breathing in the air of nearness to Him. For him the Hereafter will be a place where he finds every good thing he desires. He found Allah in the unseen world, and in the same way he will find Allah in the seen world of the Hereafter.

### In short

- The people of the Fire will beg the people of Paradise for water and food, and be refused.
- Allah has forbidden the food and drink of Paradise to the disbelievers because of their disbelief.
- A soul raised only on worldly pleasures has no capacity to enjoy the bounties of the Hereafter.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:51>>>
The reply goes on to describe the disbelievers, and then Allah speaks: "those who took this faith ˹of Islam˺ as mere amusement and play and were deluded by ˹their˺ worldly life.” ˹Allah will say,˺ “Today We will ignore them just as they ignored the coming of this Day of theirs and for rejecting Our revelations.”" They are described by what they did in this life. They took religion as a game and were fooled by the world and its glitter, instead of working for the Hereafter as Allah commanded.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who took this faith ˹of Islam˺ as mere amusement and play"**: they treated their religion as sport and falsehood, as merriment, or as a joke and mockery. They used to mock and make fun of faith.

**"and were deluded by ˹their˺ worldly life.”"** The comfort and luxury of this world fooled them. They were absorbed in its pleasures.

**"˹Allah will say,˺ “Today We will ignore them"**: on the Day of Judgement. Others render it "We will forget them", meaning Allah will treat them as though He had forgotten them. Nothing escapes Allah's perfect watch, and He never forgets anything, as another verse says: "My Lord neither falters nor forgets ˹anything˺" (20:52). The word is used as a fitting repayment for what they did. Explanations of it differ.

- Al-ʿAwfī reported Ibn ʿAbbās as saying that Allah will forget the good about them, but not their evil.
- ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported Ibn ʿAbbās as saying, "We shall forsake them as they forsook the meeting of this Day of theirs."
- Mujāhid said, "We shall leave them in the Fire."
- As-Suddī said, "We shall leave them without any mercy, just as they left off doing anything for the meeting of this Day."

**"just as they ignored the coming of this Day of theirs"**: they forgot the meeting of this Day, giving up belief in it and neglecting the good deeds it called for, though they knew full well it would come.

**"and for rejecting Our revelations.”"** They denied Allah's signs, His scripture and His Messenger, even while knowing and recognising the truth of His message.

The same repayment appears in other verses: "They neglected Allah, so He neglected them" (9:67). "Just as Our revelations came to you and you neglected them, so Today you are neglected" (20:126). And: "This Day We will neglect you as you neglected the meeting of this Day of yours!" (45:34).

### A conversation on the Day of Resurrection

It is recorded in the Ṣaḥīḥ that Allah will question a servant on the Day of Resurrection. He will remind him that He gave him a spouse and honoured him. He made horses and camels serve him, and let him become a leader and a master. The servant will agree to all of it. Then Allah will ask whether he ever thought he would meet Him, and he will answer no. Allah will say, "Then today I forget you, as you forgot Me."

### Why do people forget Allah in His own world?

Allah makes His presence felt in the world through signs that a person grasps only by reflecting. Human beings lean toward what is outward and ignore the signs that point to Allah. But to do so is to give up the Hereafter for the sake of this world. A person who ignores the Hereafter while alive will find himself shut out of it after he dies.

When people show open disregard for something Allah has put before them as the truth, they are in effect treating Allah Himself as too unimportant to deserve serious attention. They may be fooled because the divine power behind the truth cannot be seen. Still, they run the risk of being ignored by Allah on the Day of Reckoning, just as they ignored Him in this life, imagining no harm could come of it.

### In short

- The disbelievers made a game of religion and were fooled by the comforts of the world.
- Allah never forgets, but on that Day He will treat them as forgotten and leave them in the Fire without mercy.
- This is a fitting repayment: they ignored the meeting with Him, so He will ignore them.
- People forget Allah because His signs need reflection while the world's glitter is easy to see.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:52>>>
After describing the loss the idolaters will suffer, Allah makes clear that they have no excuse: "We have certainly brought them a Book which We explained with knowledge—a guide and mercy for those who believe". He sent them the Book the Messenger ﷺ brought, set out in full detail. Since He has sent prophets and revealed books in this life, no one is left with an excuse.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have certainly brought them a Book"**: Allah sent the people of Makkah Muhammad ﷺ with the Qur'an. Another verse describes it: "˹This is˺ a Book whose verses are well perfected and then fully explained" (11:1).

**"which We explained"**: which We set out in detail and made clear, through tidings, the promise of reward and the threat of punishment. It explains and clarifies all things.

**"with knowledge"**: Allah has perfect knowledge of everything He explained in it. Another verse says, "He has sent it with His knowledge" (4:166). One explanation is "with knowledge from Us", and another is "We have taught it". Grammatically, "with knowledge" describes the state of the One who detailed it, knowing what He set out.

**"a guide and mercy"**: a guide away from error, to the right path and to the truth. A mercy that saves from punishment, because of the good it leads to in this world and the next. In the Arabic, "a guide" also describes the Book, through the pronoun attached to "We explained it".

**"for those who believe"**: for people who believe in it, in Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an.

### Can anyone be punished without warning?

Allah says elsewhere, "And We would never punish ˹a people˺ until We have sent a messenger ˹to warn them˺" (17:15). That is why this verse reminds the idolaters that a detailed Book has already reached them. Once the warning has come, no excuse remains.

### Why do some hear the Qur'an and others do not?

The Qur'an warns humanity of life after death and tells of the reckoning in the Hereafter, yet people do not wake up. Its warnings are not mere "news". They are the certain realities of the universe, which have not yet appeared as events and are still hidden behind the veil of the future. So the careless take them for empty talk and ignore them. But these messages come from Allah, who knows all.

People who have not spoiled their nature, whose eyes are not covered by artificial veils, find that the Qur'an speaks in tune with the voice of their own hearts. They find in it the very thing their nature was already looking for. For them the Qur'an becomes a treasure of life and faith.

### In short

- Allah sent a Book perfected and explained in detail, with full knowledge of all it contains.
- It is guidance and mercy for those who believe, leading to good in this world and the next.
- Since Allah punishes no one before sending a messenger, the idolaters who received the Qur'an are left without excuse.
- A sound nature recognises the Qur'an as the answer to what it was already seeking.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:53>>>
The verse asks what the deniers are waiting for, and pictures the Day when the warnings come true: "Do they only await the fulfilment ˹of its warning˺? The Day it will be fulfilled, those who ignored it before will say, “The messengers of our Lord certainly came with the truth. Are there any intercessors who can plead on our behalf? Or can we be sent back so we may do ˹good,˺ unlike what we used to do?” They will have certainly ruined themselves, and whatever ˹gods˺ they fabricated will fail them". After refusing to believe, they have nothing left to wait for but the outcome of what the Book promised.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do they only await the fulfilment ˹of its warning˺?"** The question means: they are waiting for nothing but this. What are the people of Makkah waiting for, after refusing to believe? Only the outcome of what Allah promised them in the Qur'an. Mujāhid and several others said this refers to what they were promised: torment, punishment and the Fire, or Paradise. The disbelievers are warned of a painful punishment in the Hereafter, and the believers were also told of that Day's reward.

**"The Day it will be fulfilled"**: Ibn ʿAbbās said this is the Day of Resurrection, the Day of Judgement.

**"those who ignored it before will say"**: those who disregarded the Qur'an in this life, did not believe in it and did not live by what it required.

**"“The messengers of our Lord certainly came with the truth."** They brought the truth, beyond doubt, explaining the resurrection, Paradise and Hell, but we denied it all.

**"Are there any intercessors who can plead on our behalf?"** If only someone could plead for us now, so that we might be excused and spared the punishment.

**"Or can we be sent back"**: back to the first life, life on earth.

**"so we may do ˹good,˺ unlike what we used to do?”"** So that we may believe and do good deeds that would save us, instead of our evil and our idolatry. We would then affirm Allah's Oneness and stop associating partners with Him. The answer they will receive is "No!" Their wish is like the one in another passage:

> "If only you could see when they will be detained before the Fire! They will cry, “Oh! If only we could be sent back, we would never deny the signs of our Lord and we would ˹surely˺ be of the believers.” But no! ˹They only say this˺ because the truth they used to hide will become all too clear to them. Even if they were to be sent back, they would certainly revert to what they were forbidden. Indeed they are liars" (6:27-28).

**"They will have certainly ruined themselves"**: they destroyed themselves by entering the Fire forever. They lost Paradise and must stay in Hell, ending in perdition because of their disbelief.

**"and whatever ˹gods˺ they fabricated will fail them"**: what they worshipped falsely instead of Allah, claiming He had a partner, will abandon them. Those false gods will not intercede for them, help them or save them from their fate.

### What happens when the warning becomes real?

Those who do not take the Qur'an's warnings seriously will stay asleep until the event they were warned of breaks over them. Then a person will suddenly find himself completely helpless. The concerns he thought important, and was so caught up in, will turn out to be baseless. The things he relied on will be gone. The rosy hopes he lived on will all prove false.

Belief in the Hereafter seems to him an abstract idea now, so he does not take it seriously. But after death, when the Hereafter in all its terror is thrust upon him, everyone will be forced to accept what he once refused. He will realise that what he was told in the language of argument was absolute fact. Because he was not serious, he could not grasp its importance. When everything he relied on in the world deserts him, he will want to be sent back to earth to live properly. But no one is given that chance twice.

### In short

- The deniers have nothing left to wait for but the coming true of the Qur'an's warnings on the Day of Resurrection.
- On that Day they will admit the messengers told the truth, and wish for intercessors or a second chance.
- No return is granted. Even if they were sent back, they would go back to what they were forbidden.
- They will have ruined themselves, and the false gods they invented will abandon them.
