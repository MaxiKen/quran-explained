<<<VERSE 7:48>>>
The people on the wall now look the other way and speak. "Those on the heights will call out to some ˹tyrants in the Fire˺, who they will recognize by their appearance, saying, “Your large numbers and arrogance are of no use ˹today˺"". The verse is an admonition delivered from above, and it is delivered to the men who led the crowds below.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those on the heights will call out"** means the people of al-Aʿrāf, who are still standing where they were in the verse before this one.

**"to some ˹tyrants in the Fire˺"** picks out particular men. They are among the disbelievers, and the people on the wall know them before they go into the fire. The word for them is the word for the chiefs and the great ones.

**"who they will recognize by their appearance"** repeats what the previous verse said about how the two groups are known. The people of the fire are known by the mark on them, which is the darkening of the face and the blueness of the eye.

**"saying, “Your large numbers and arrogance are of no use ˹today˺"** is the admonition itself. Their large numbers did not save them, and their arrogance did not save them. What the words mean is that the great numbers and the wealth did nothing for them at all against the torment of Allah. Instead of being spared, they are the ones dwelling in it.

### They are called by name

One reading of the verse fills in the names, and the names belong to the men who had been the leaders of the town. In that reading they are called out one by one, Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah first, then Abū Jahl ibn Hishām, then Umayyah ibn Khalaf, then Ubayy ibn Khalaf al-Jumahī, then Aswad ibn ʿAbd, of the clan of al-Muṭṭalib, and after them the rest of the leaders. Then the question is put to them, about the multitude of their wealth and their servants, and about the thing they took such pride in, which was what made them scorn belief in Muhammad ﷺ and in the Qur'an.

This is the shape of the admonition. It is not addressed to the crowd. It is addressed to the men the crowd followed, and the question is what all their following came to. The wealth and the power they had leant on, and the pride they had taken in both, went to waste in the one hour when they needed them, and none of that strength worked for them at their hour of need.

### Who the people of the heights are, on another reading

One view, reported from al-Qurṭubī, is that the people of the heights in this verse are the shuhadāʾ, the witnesses. What is meant is those special servants of God who were witnesses, in the service of His religion, to what the nations of the world did. Some of the people they called accepted the call and some rejected it.

People of that kind are mentioned in the Qur'an under many names: warner, bearer of glad tidings, the one who calls. The group began with the prophets and was afterwards made up of those special servants of God who followed the prophets' example and carried the call to the people of their own times. The final judgement on human beings, which will take place in the Hereafter, is based on the task the witnesses performed for the people of the world. That task is what divides the world into two groups, one accepting the divine message and the other rejecting it, and on the Day of Judgement the two groups are separated and judged by their deeds and sent to two different ends.

The judgement is God's judgement entirely, and what these servants do is announce it. That is a great honour for them. On that Day high platforms will be raised for these people to stand on, from which they can see everyone and pronounce God's judgement to the people. Those who bore witness and those who called people to the truth worked hard in the world to carry the message of God to people, and they gave their whole lives to the mission as though it were their own personal work. So the honour done to them on that Day is that they are the ones asked to announce how the call they gave turned out. They ranked high in the world by their mission, and they will be called high-ranked on that Day by the result of what they did.

The task of carrying the message, which has been laid on the Muslims, is not an optional subject that a person may take up or drop when he finds an excuse. It is the kind of duty that must be discharged whatever it costs. No excuse will be heard from the followers of the Prophet ﷺ and found acceptable by God. The other acts of worship and good deeds will not save the believers either, if the duty of carrying the message to all people has been ignored. Men and women by the thousand die every day with nobody having brought them the message of God, and never having had the chance to accept it, they have lost their opportunity to better their state in the life to come. In that situation it is the serious duty of the believers to stop making excuses and take up the mission of proclaiming the divine truth.

### In short

- The people of the heights speak to the chiefs among the people of the fire, and what they know them by is the mark on them. The witnesses, as the word shahādah is used of those who carry the call, are the ones standing there.
- What they say is that the great numbers and the arrogance did nothing for them, since neither saved them from the torment.
- One reading of the verse names the leaders by name, and the question is put to them about the wealth and the pride that made them scorn belief.
- A further view is that the people of the heights are the witnesses, the ones who carried the call to the nations, and on that Day they announce the judgement their call divided people towards.

<<<VERSE 7:49>>>
The verse turns the admonition into a question, and then into an announcement. "Are these ˹humble believers˺ the ones you swore would never be shown Allah's mercy?” ˹Finally, those on the heights will be told:˺ “Enter Paradise! There will be no fear for you, nor will you grieve.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Are these ˹humble believers˺ the ones you swore would never be shown Allah's mercy?"** is the question put to the people of the fire. What the people of the heights are doing is looking at the dwellers of the garden as they say it. The ones being pointed at are the weak and the lowly among the believers, and the names given in one reading are Salmān al-Fārisī, Ṣuhayb ar-Rūmī, ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir and every other humble and weak believer of the same kind.

**"you swore would never be shown Allah's mercy"** is what the great ones of the world had said about them. They swore in this life that Allah would not show these people mercy and would not bring them into the garden. And the point of the question is that they have gone in, in spite of everything that was said about them.

**"Enter Paradise!"** is then said to the people of the heights themselves. One variant reading has the words as a passive, that they have been admitted, and another has them as a past tense, that they entered. The negation that follows, about no fear and no grief, is a state they are in as they enter, so the sense is that they go in with those words being said over them.

**"There will be no fear for you, nor will you grieve."** means no fear of anything that awaits, and no sorrow over anything that has passed.

### When the announcement comes

Ibn ʿAbbās explained the order of the scene. When the questions and the answers between the people of al-Aʿrāf and the people of the garden and the people of the fire are all finished, the Lord of all the worlds addresses the people of the fire about the people of al-Aʿrāf. He tells them about those people, whom they had sworn would never be forgiven their sins and would never be shown mercy.

Then comes the declaration of mercy, immediately after. The people of al-Aʿrāf are told to go and enter the garden. What they are told about the two fears is exact: there is nothing to fear from what has already happened, and nothing to be anxious about in what is to come. Their past is settled and their future is safe.

### In short

- The question put to the great ones is about the humble believers they had sworn would never see mercy.
- The people being pointed at are the weak and lowly, named in one reading as Salmān al-Fārisī, Ṣuhayb ar-Rūmī and ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir.
- The announcement to the people of the heights themselves is that they should enter the garden, with no fear about the past and no grief about the future.
- The order is that the exchanges finish first, then the Lord speaks to the people of the fire about the people of al-Aʿrāf, and then the declaration of mercy is made to them.

<<<VERSE 7:50>>>
The people below now begin to speak, and what they want is small. "The residents of the Fire will then cry out to the residents of Paradise, “Aid us with some water or any provision Allah has granted you.” They will reply, “Allah has forbidden both to the disbelievers"". The verse sets the two groups against each other at the one point where the difference shows most plainly.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The residents of the Fire will then cry out to the residents of Paradise"** begins the asking. The word for crying out is the word for calling at the top of the voice, which is what people do when they have nothing else left.

**"“Aid us with some water or any provision Allah has granted you.”"** is the request. What they ask for is water and food from what the people of the garden have been given. The provisions named are the fruits of the garden.

**"They will reply, “Allah has forbidden both to the disbelievers""** is the answer, and it is final. Both the water and the food of the garden have been made forbidden to the disbelievers.

### How the answer is given

As-Suddī identified the provision as the food of the garden. ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said that what is forbidden is the food and the drink of the garden, both of them.

Saʿīd ibn Jubayr described the moment in the plainest terms. One of the people of the fire will call to his own father or to his brother and say that he has been burned, and ask him to pour a little water over him. The believers are asked for an answer, and the answer they give is that Allah has forbidden both to the disbelievers.

That is the whole of what the verse does. It puts a father and a son or two brothers on opposite sides of a wall, with the one on the near side unable to help the one on the far side, and it makes the words of the refusal a statement about what was already decided. The believers are not the ones refusing. The matter was settled long before, and the verse says so plainly, because of their disbelief.

The point of the scene is the disgrace of the people of the fire. They ask the people of the garden for some of their drink and some of their food, and they are given none of it, and the reason given is not a wish of the people of the garden but a decree of Allah.

### In short

- The people of the fire beg for water and for food from the people of the garden, and they are refused.
- The prohibition covers both the water and the food of the garden, and the reason given is the disbelief of the ones asking.
- Saʿīd ibn Jubayr described a man calling to his father or his brother, saying he has been burned, and the answer that comes back.
- The refusal is not the wish of the people of the garden but a decree already settled.

<<<VERSE 7:51>>>
The verse says who these people are. "those who took this faith ˹of Islam˺ as mere amusement and play and were deluded by ˹their˺ worldly life.” ˹Allah will say,˺ “Today We will ignore them just as they ignored the coming of this Day of theirs and for rejecting Our revelations.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who took this faith ˹of Islam˺ as mere amusement and play"** names what they did with religion itself. The adjectives used mean falsehood, merriment, and also a joke and mockery. Religion was not something they took seriously or lived by. It was something they treated as a pastime.

**"and were deluded by ˹their˺ worldly life"** is the second charge. What deceived them was the comfort and luxury of this world, and it kept them from working for the Hereafter as they had been commanded.

**"Today We will ignore them"** is the recompense. The word does not mean that Allah forgets, since nothing escapes His perfect watch and He never forgets anything. The Qur'an says as much elsewhere, that the record is with my Lord, who neither errs nor forgets (20:52). What the word means is that He treats them as if He had forgotten them, and leaves them where they are.

**"just as they ignored the coming of this Day of theirs"** gives the measure of it. The recompense matches the offence exactly. They forgot Allah, so He forgets them (9:67). Their verses came to them and they disregarded them, and so on that Day they are neglected (20:126). And it will be said to them that on this Day He forgets them as they forgot the meeting of this Day of theirs (45:34).

**"and for rejecting Our revelations"** names the second offence that the same response answers. They used to deny the Scripture and the messenger.

### What the forgetting means

The explanations given for the verse come to the same point from different sides. Al-ʿAwfī reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that Allah will put out of mind the good there was about them, and will not put their evil out of mind. ʿAlī bin Abī Ṭalḥah reported from him that the meaning is that they are forsaken as they forsook the meeting of this Day of theirs. Mujāhid said that they are left in the fire. As-Suddī said that they are left outside any mercy, just as they left aside any action on behalf of the meeting of that Day.

And the two sahih collections record what Allah will say to a servant on the Day of Resurrection. He will ask him whether He had not given him a wife, honoured him, made horses and camels serve him, and allowed him to become a leader and a master, and the servant will agree that He had. Then He will ask whether the servant thought he would meet Him, and the servant will say no. And Allah will say that on this Day He forgets him just as he forgot Him.

### The two foods

There is a way of seeing the whole passage that puts this verse beside the one before it. The world offers a spread with two kinds of food on it, one worldly and one belonging to the life to come.

One kind of person takes his nourishment from seeing himself hold the first place. He is happy when he is surrounded by worldly glories, and he counts himself a success when he acquires and owns the goods of the world. This man is entirely forgetful of God and of the Hereafter. If something about God is brought to his attention, he counts it unimportant and passes over it. He deals with it as though it were nothing serious, a trivial entertainment. And there is no share for such a person in the good things of the Hereafter. He has grown in himself a soul that feeds only on worldly material, and the things of the next life cannot nourish a soul like that.

The other kind of person has been lost in things unseen. His soul relishes the remembrance of the Hereafter, and his bread and his drink are living in God and breathing the air of God. For him the Hereafter is the place where he finds every good thing he wanted. And just as he found God in the unseen world, so he finds God in the seen world of the Hereafter.

Why does a person forget God at all in a world God made? The reason is that God makes Himself felt in the world through signs that a person grasps only by reflecting. People lean towards outward things and ignore the signs that point to God, and doing that is nothing other than giving up the Hereafter for the sake of this world. And whoever takes no account of the Hereafter in this life is kept out of it in the next.

There is one more layer in it. When people show open disregard for something God has presented to them as the truth, they are in effect treating God Himself as too unimportant to be given serious thought. The divine power behind the truth is invisible, and they may be deceived by that. But they risk exactly the same treatment being handed back to them on the Day of Reckoning, being passed over as they passed over God, imagining all the while that no harm would come of it.

### In short

- The verse names what the people were: they turned religion into a pastime, and the comfort of this life deluded them.
- "Today We will ignore them" does not mean that Allah forgets, since He neither errs nor forgets (20:52). It means He treats them as though He had.
- The response matches the offence, as the other verses put it: they forgot Allah, so He forgets them (9:67), and they are neglected as they neglected His signs (20:126 and 45:34).
- Ibn ʿAbbās said the good about them is put out of mind and the evil is not, and the other readings say they are left in the fire and left outside mercy.
- The two kinds of food are the world's and the Hereafter's, and a soul fed only on the first cannot be nourished by the second.

<<<VERSE 7:52>>>
The passage turns back to what was given in this life. "We have certainly brought them a Book which We explained with knowledge—a guide and mercy for those who believe". After the loss the idolaters end up with is set out, the verse states that they were left no excuse. The Book came to them, and it is set out in detail, so that the warning had reached them before the punishment.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We have certainly brought them a Book"** identifies what was sent: the Qur'an, sent with Muhammad ﷺ to the people of Makkah.

**"which We explained with knowledge"** says what the Book is like. It is set out in detail, and the detail comes with perfect knowledge of what has been explained in it. Another verse describes the same Book as one whose verses are perfected and then explained in detail (11:1). And the knowledge is Allah's own, as another verse says of it that He has sent it down with His knowledge (4:166).

**"a guide and mercy for those who believe"** names what the Book does for the people who accept it. It guides them out of error and it is a mercy that saves them from the torment. One reading makes both words describe the Book itself, so that the Book is the guidance and the Book is the mercy.

### Why the Book is mentioned here

The connection with the verses before and after is exact. Having set out what the idolaters lose in the Hereafter, the Qur'an now says that the messengers and the books were sent in this life, so that the excuse was taken away from them. And the principle is stated elsewhere: no one is punished until a messenger has been sent (17:15). So the mention of the Book here is not a change of subject. It is the reason the punishment that has just been described is just.

What the Book is, and what it does for the people who take it, are set out in the same sentence. It is explained in detail, which is the opposite of being vague. It is guidance, which is what a person needs. It is mercy, which is what a person hopes for. And it works for those who believe, since the guidance and the mercy are received by the people who accept them.

One reading of the verse draws out what the mercy consists of. The Book points the believers towards the right road and towards the truth, and the mercy in it lies in what it brings them to, of good in this world and good in the next.

### In short

- The Book is the Qur'an, sent to the people of Makkah, and it is set out in detail with perfect knowledge.
- The same Book is described elsewhere as having verses perfected and then explained in detail, and as having been sent down with His knowledge.
- It is a guide out of error and a mercy that saves from the torment, and both are for the people who believe.
- It is mentioned here because the sending of the Book is what removes the excuse, and no one is punished before a messenger has come (17:15).

<<<VERSE 7:53>>>
The passage closes on the question. "Do they only await the fulfilment ˹of its warning˺? The Day it will be fulfilled, those who ignored it before will say, “The messengers of our Lord certainly came with the truth. Are there any intercessors who can plead on our behalf? Or can we be sent back so we may do ˹good,˺ unlike what we used to do?” They will have certainly ruined themselves, and whatever ˹gods˺ they fabricated will fail them". The verse follows them to the moment they finally believe, and it shows what that belief is worth by then.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do they only await the fulfilment ˹of its warning˺?"** asks what the people of Makkah are waiting for, now that they have refused to believe. What is meant is the outcome of what Allah has promised them in the Book, and one view is that the promise in question is the torment and the punishment and the fire, while another is that it takes in the garden as well, which is how Mujāhid and several others read it.

**"The Day it will be fulfilled"** is the Day of Resurrection, which is how Ibn ʿAbbās identified it.

**"those who ignored it before will say"** describes the people who passed over the Book in this life and did not act on what it required of them.

**"“The messengers of our Lord certainly came with the truth."** is the first thing they say. What the messengers brought was the truth about the resurrection, about the garden and about the fire. Another reading adds what follows in their own minds, that they denied all of it at the time.

**"Are there any intercessors who can plead on our behalf?"** is the first request. They are asking for someone to intercede for them, so that the torment might be kept away.

**"Or can we be sent back so we may do ˹good,˺ unlike what we used to do?”"** is the second request, and it is the larger one. They want to be returned to the life of this world so that they can act differently from the way they acted, believing and doing good instead of the idolatry they practised. And what follows in the same passage of the Qur'an answers the request before it is fully made. If you could see them when they are held over the fire, they will say that they wish they were sent back, that they would not deny the signs of their Lord and would be among the believers. But no, what they had been concealing has become clear to them, and if they were sent back they would go straight back to what they had been forbidden, since they are liars (6:27-28).

**"They will have certainly ruined themselves"** is the verdict. They have destroyed themselves by entering the fire to stay there.

**"and whatever ˹gods˺ they fabricated will fail them"** is the last line. What they used to worship instead of Allah has abandoned them. Those things will not intercede for them, will not help them and will not save them from what is coming.

### Why the warning did not wake them

The Qur'an warns human beings about the life to come and tells them about the reckoning, and still people are not awakened by it. The reason is what those intimations are in themselves. They are not simply pieces of news. They are the inevitable realities of the universe, and they have not yet appeared as events. They are still hidden behind the veil of the future. So a person, paying no attention, takes the whole thing for meaningless talk and counts it unimportant.

But these messages come on behalf of God, who knows all things. Anyone whose nature has not been spoiled, and whose eyes are not covered by veils of his own making, will find that the tone of the Qur'an matches the voice of his own heart. He will find that this is the very thing his nature was searching for, and the Qur'an becomes for him a treasure of life and of faith.

The position of those who do not take the warnings seriously is completely different. They linger in their slumber until the event they were warned about breaks on them. At that point a person suddenly understands that he is completely helpless. The problems he had thought important, and the ones he had entangled himself in, turn out to have had no basis. The things he had relied on have ceased to be with him. The rosy hopes he had lived on all prove false.

Belief in the Hereafter looks like an abstract idea, and that is the reason a person never gets serious about it. Then the life after death arrives, and the Hereafter with all its terror is thrust upon him. At that point everyone is compelled to accept what he had refused to accept before. And when he leaves the world behind and turns to the oneness of God at last, it is too late for the admission to count. He understands then that what had been told to him in the language of argument was plain fact, and that not being serious about it is what stopped him from grasping its meaning. When everything he had leant on leaves him in the lurch, he wants to be sent back to the earth to live his life properly. And no one is given that chance twice.

### In short

- The verse asks what the people of Makkah are waiting for, and the answer is the fulfilment of what the Book promised and warned about, on the Day of Resurrection.
- On that Day those who ignored the Book admit that the messengers came with the truth, and then ask for intercessors and for a return to the world.
- The request for a return is answered before it is made, since the Qur'an says that if they were sent back they would go straight back to what was forbidden them (6:27-28).
- They ruin themselves by entering the fire to stay, and everything they set up beside Allah abandons them and cannot help.
- The warning does not wake people because it speaks of realities that have not yet appeared as events, and no one is given the chance to live his life over again.
