The verse completes the previous one and then takes up another of the Companions' questions, this time about sharing the maintenance of orphans. "upon this world and the Hereafter. And they ask you ˹O Prophet˺ concerning orphans. Say, "Improving their condition is best. And if you partner with them, they are bonded with you ˹in faith˺. And Allah knows who intends harm and who intends good. Had Allah willed, He could have made it difficult for you. Surely Allah is Almighty, All-Wise."".

### Phrase by phrase

**"upon this world and the Hereafter."**. The reflection commanded at the end of the previous verse, where Allah said He makes His signs clear so that you may give thought, is reflection on this world and the Hereafter, that the one is fleeting and the other lasting, so that you follow what is best for you in both. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah relates from Ibn ʿAbbās that it means thinking of the imminent end and brevity of this life and the imminent beginning and permanence of the next. The rules Allah lays down are for you to consider what benefits you in both worlds. The previous verse answered the questions about wine and gambling and about how much to spend. Imam Aḥmad records from Abū Maysarah that ʿUmar prayed for a clear ruling on khamr. The account also states that after the verse of al-Baqarah, "They ask you about Khamr and gambling" (2:219), and the verse of al-Nisāʾ, "Do not approach prayer while intoxicated" (4:43), with its herald forbidding the drunk to attend prayer, he went on praying until the verses of al-Māʾidah came, "Intoxicants, gambling, idols, and drawing lots for decisions are all evil of Satan's handiwork" (5:90-91), and when the reciter reached "Will you not then abstain" (5:91) he said that they abstained. Abū Dāwūd, together with al-Tirmidhī and al-Nasāʾī record the same, with a chain ʿAlī ibn al-Madīnī and al-Tirmidhī called sound. ʿUmar defined khamr as whatever clouds the mind. Ibn ʿUmar, together with al-Shaʿbī, together with Mujāhid, together with Qatādah, together with al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Aslam gave the order of the three revelations, al-Baqarah, together with al-Nisāʾ and al-Māʾidah. The spending asked about there is the surplus beyond the family's needs, according to Ibn ʿAbbās through al-Ḥakam and Miqsam, and to Ibn ʿUmar, together with Mujāhid, together with ʿAṭāʾ, together with ʿIkrimah, together with Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, together with Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb, together with al-Ḥasan, together with Qatādah, together with al-Qāsim, together with Sālim, together with ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī and al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, in the order the Prophet ﷺ gave to the man with the dinars, himself, his wife, his children and then as he saw best (Abū Hurayrah in Ibn Jarīr and Muslim), and to the man told to begin with himself, then his family, then his relatives, then charity in every direction (Jābir in Muslim). In the devotional reading, the surplus is the rule for the elect, who give from the excess of their wealth according to their capacity, while the elect of the elect practise preference (īthār), putting others before themselves even when they themselves need what they give and even though the One for whose sake they prefer is unseen. And just as khamr is forbidden. Therefore, is drunkenness from any drink, by the Prophet's ﷺ words. Allusively whoever is drunk on the drink of heedlessness deserves the same as the drinker of khamr: as the drunk is barred from prayer, the one possessed by the drunkenness of heedlessness is veiled from intimate communication and from the clearest evidence of ecstatic finding. Whoever doubts this should test it. The notion of gambling is found in most of the transactions of the heedless, who follow the way of stratagem and deception and lie in what they say, while wholehearted commitment to truthfulness and fair dealing is rare and precious.

### How to protect an orphan’s interests

**"And they ask you ˹O Prophet˺ concerning orphans."**. In pagan Arabia, as elsewhere, there was a general carelessness about the rights of orphans, and so the warning came that consuming what belongs to them is like filling one's belly with the embers of Hell. Ibn Jarīr relates from Ibn ʿAbbās that when the verses "do not come near the wealth of the orphan—unless intending to enhance it" (6:152) and "those who unjustly consume orphans' wealth ˹in fact˺ consume nothing but fire into their bellies" (4:10) were revealed, those who had charge of orphans separated their own food and drink from the orphans'. Whatever remained of the orphans' food was kept for them until they ate it or it went bad. This caused them great distress. They mentioned it to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, whereupon this verse came down and they mixed their food and drink with the orphans' again. Abū Dāwūd, together with al-Nasāʾī and al-Ḥākim in his Mustadrak also record this, and Mujāhid, together with ʿAṭāʾ, together with al-Shaʿbī, together with Ibn Abī Laylā, together with Qatādah and others of the Salaf and after them describe the occasion in the same way. Sufyān al-Thawrī relates through Sālim al-Afṭas that Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said people rushed to separate their wealth from the orphans' when the verse about swallowing fire was revealed, and mixed it again when this verse came. One early report names ʿAbdullāh ibn Rawāḥah as the one who asked about the permissibility of sharing shelter, food and drink with orphans. The guardians were in genuine difficulty: if they became intimate with the orphans they might slip into error, but if they set aside the orphans' money and cooked their meals separately it distressed them. This is because they could neither use food that belonged to the orphans nor give it away in charity, and the orphans suffered loss as well. Ibn Jarīr relates that ʿĀʾishah said she disliked having an orphan's money in her care unless she mixed her food with his food and her drink with his drink.

**"Say, "Improving their condition is best."**. Working honestly in their property, looking after it and setting their affairs aright, and mingling with them, is better than holding aloof from them. This is required in any case. If you are kind to them by administering their affairs freely and leaving their wealth untouched, that is better in Allah's sight and carries greater reward. It also means that you keep your own wealth intact. In the devotional reading, setting their state aright by teaching them discipline and manners is more perfect than setting their wealth aright, and patient counsel and guidance without weariness is better than a permissiveness that could be called a failure in one's duty to them.

**"And if you partner with them, they are bonded with you ˹in faith˺."**. There is no harm in mixing your food and drink with theirs, your funds with theirs for food, drink and shelter. This is because they are your brothers in religion. It is natural for a man to share his affairs with his brother. Therefore, you should safeguard their shares as a brother would. The purpose is never to compromise the welfare of the orphans. Since their welfare is served better by a joint system of expenses there is nothing to fear, for brothers in faith share. Who can truly call himself a believer? One who makes the next world his goal and continually seeks his Lord’s favour. Worldly possessions are not that person’s goal, though he does not renounce the world either. This is because a certain number of material things are plainly necessary. When he earns a living he does so only so far as it relates to the necessities of life, never to amass wealth. Out of what he has he keeps what he really needs and spends the rest for the cause of Allah. If one tried to lay down in explicit legal terms how men should deal with one another and conduct their business, it would be too complicated for daily life. Therefore, it is more useful to set out basic guidelines: to wish others well, and to keep in mind not only one's own interests but those of the people one deals with, regarding them as brothers.

**"And Allah knows who intends harm and who intends good."**. He knows the guardian who, in mixing the orphans' wealth with his own, means to swallow their property and the one who means to preserve and improve it. He will requite both. The permission is thus hemmed in by a warning that Allah watches over the conduct of guardians in this matter. He deals with everyone according to the intentions that dwell in the heart, not according to the external things one acquires in various ways. Allah will never take us to task for acting in a way that promotes harmony and justice.

**"Had Allah willed, He could have made it difficult for you."**. Had He wished, He could have made this matter hard for you by forbidding such sharing and setting a harsher code of conduct, for He is All-Powerful. Instead He made it easy and allowed you to mix your affairs with the orphans' in the way that is better. This is because the sacred law is founded on ease and He does not burden people with what they cannot do. Allah has thus allowed the executor of an orphan's estate to spend from it in reasonable proportions, on condition that he intends to compensate the orphan later when he can afford it, a matter treated in detail in Sūrat al-Nisāʾ.

**"Surely Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.""**. He is Mighty, victorious in His affair. Nothing can overpower Him. Therefore, He takes vengeance on whoever spoils the wealth of orphans. He is Wise in His actions, His planning and His legislation, ruling that the wealth of orphans should be improved.

### In short

- The believer is to reflect on the fleeting world and the lasting Hereafter, the lesson of the gradual rulings on wine (2:219, 4:43, 5:90-91, ʿUmar's prayers) and on spending the surplus, which the elect of the elect exceed by preferring others to themselves.
- After 4:10 and 6:152 frightened guardians into cooking separately for orphans, to the loss of both sides, this verse was revealed (Ibn ʿAbbās, together with Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, the question of ʿAbdullāh ibn Rawāḥah). ʿĀʾishah would keep an orphan's money only if she shared his food.
- Improving the orphans' condition, including their upbringing, is best. Pooling expenses with them is lawful because they are brothers in faith.
- Allah judges the guardian by his intention to harm or to benefit. Could have imposed hardship but chose ease, allowing reasonable use of the estate with the intention to repay.
- Allah is Mighty against those who spoil orphans' wealth and Wise in ruling that it be improved.
