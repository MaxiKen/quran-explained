<<<VERSE 7:19>>>
With Iblīs expelled, Allah turns to Ādam and his wife: "˹Allah said,˺ “O Adam! Live with your wife in Paradise and eat from wherever you please, but do not approach this tree, or else you will be wrongdoers.”" Allah let Ādam and his wife live in Paradise and eat from all of its fruits, except for one tree. The story was told in detail in Sūrat al-Baqarah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O Adam! Live with your wife in Paradise"**: in the Arabic, the word "you" (anta) is repeated after the verb uskun, "dwell". This stresses who is being addressed and lets "your wife" be joined to it. His wife is Ḥawwāʾ (Eve). The sense is: go, and dwell in Paradise, you and your wife.

**"and eat from wherever you please"**: eat of its good things, from wherever you wish and whenever you wish.

**"but do not approach this tree"**: do not come near it to eat from it. One explanation says the tree was wheat. Another calls it the tree of knowledge.

**"or else you will be wrongdoers.”"** If you eat from it after I have forbidden it, you will be among those who harm themselves and overstep My limits.

### What does Ādam's clay teach?

Four names are given to Ādam: man, mortal, vicegerent, and Ādam itself. The last comes from adīm, the earth's surface, because his clay was gathered from every region of the earth. As Allah says, "from an extract of clay" (23:12). He was taken from every kind of ground, sweet and salty, soft and hard. Since Ādam's clay held the salty and the sweet, the coarse and the soft, the natures of his children came out different. Some are sweet-tempered and some bad-tempered, some open and some closed. There are generous and stingy, easy-going and difficult, black and white.

Elsewhere Allah says, "He created humankind from ˹sounding˺ clay like pottery" (55:14). Pottery is dried clay that rings and is full of noise. In the same way the human being is noisy, his head full of tumult and turmoil, and fond of talk. Elsewhere He says, "We created them from a sticky clay" (37:11). That is clay which sticks to everything and mixes with everyone. And elsewhere, "from sounding clay moulded from black mud" (15:26), a clay that is dark and black.

In this way Allah taught Ādam his measure, so that he would not step beyond his station. He showed him his root so that, if he ever saw generosity in himself, he would not credit it to himself. True rank comes from nurture (tarbiya), not from soil (turba). What comes from soil? Wrongdoing, ignorance and harshness: "So Adam disobeyed his Lord" (20:121). What comes from nurture? The generosity of guidance, the acceptance of repentance, and tender care: "Indeed, Allah chose Adam" (3:33). The fruit of nurture is in His words, "He loves them, and they love Him" (5:54).

### The patched cloak of Ayāz

Maḥmūd once went to the house of Ayāz. There he saw all the wealth and blessings he had given him: gold, silver, jewels, coloured silks and robes of honour. In a corner, hanging from a nail, he saw an old cloak made of patches. "What is this?" he asked. Ayāz answered, "This is me, in all my helplessness and lowliness. All that beauty and adornment, all that rank and delight, is you. When I look at this, I see my own weakness and know my worth. When I look at that, I see you and know it is yours, so I rejoice and lift my head."

### In short

- Allah let Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ live in Paradise and enjoy all of it, except one tree.
- Eating from that tree would make them wrongdoers who harm themselves and overstep Allah's limits.
- Ādam was made from every kind of earth, which is why human natures differ so widely.
- His honour came from Allah's nurture, not from his clay. Remembering one's lowly root keeps a person humble.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:20>>>
Shayṭān now sets to work: "Then Satan tempted them in order to expose what was hidden of their nakedness. He said, “Your Lord has forbidden this tree to you only to prevent you from becoming angels or immortals.”" He envied Ādam and his wife and plotted against them, whispering and suggesting treachery. He wanted to strip them of the favours and the fine clothing they enjoyed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then Satan tempted them"**: Satan, that is Iblīs, whispered to them to eat from the tree.

**"in order to expose what was hidden of their nakedness"**: to bring into view what had been covered from them. The Arabic for "hidden", wūriya, is on the pattern fūʿila and comes from al-muwārā, "covering". One explanation says they had been covered by a garment of light.

**"He said, “Your Lord has forbidden this tree to you only"**: he spoke lies and falsehood to Ādam and Eve.

**"to prevent you from becoming angels or immortals.”"** The meaning is, so that you do not become angels or live here forever. If you eat from this tree, he claimed, you will gain both. One explanation describes these angels as those who teach good and evil in Paradise. The word for "angels", malakayn, may also be read malikayn, "kings". He made immortality sound like the natural result of eating, as another verse shows: "But Satan whispered to him, saying, “O Adam! Shall I show you the Tree of Immortality and a kingdom that does not fade away?”" (20:120).

The construction here means "so that you do not". The same form appears in "Allah makes ˹this˺ clear to you so you do not go astray" (4:176). It appears too in "He has placed into the earth firm mountains, so it does not shake with you" (16:15).

### Shayṭān's oldest trick

Paradise in all its breadth lay open to Ādam and his wife. It held countless good things, and they were free to enjoy them as they liked. Among all those permitted things, only one was forbidden. Shayṭān made that single ban his point of attack. He told them that the thing kept from them was the most important thing in Paradise, holding the whole secret of holiness and eternity. Worn down by his constant urging, they finally ate the forbidden fruit. The result was the reverse of what they had hoped. Their disobedience stripped the cloak of safety from their bodies. They became helpless in a world where they had enjoyed every comfort and complete safety.

This shows the special weapon Shayṭān uses to draw people away from Allah's grace and help. He makes the wide world of lawful gifts look small, and blows up the importance of the few forbidden things. He dresses them up attractively and keeps assuring people of the great benefits hidden in them. He also suits his approach to each person's tastes and circumstances.

- He leads some astray by making them dislike wholesome food and telling them that wine is the way to fine health.
- A man may see that he should strengthen his own position to overcome a rival. Shayṭān whispers that the surest way to win is to launch destructive attacks on him.
- Another has every chance to improve himself. Shayṭān teaches him that protests and demands are the shortest road to success.
- Yet another has countless religious tasks he could carry out without any clash with the government of the day. Shayṭān plants the idea that Islam's complete system can prevail across the country only once un-Islamic rulers are toppled.

### Allah's care, even in the fall

Even this is a mark of Allah's care and generosity. Ādam and his wife sinned, yet He laid it at the door of Shayṭān's whispering. Then He increased His care, for their nakedness was exposed to them and not to others.

It is said that Ādam and Iblīs met afterwards. Ādam said, "You wretch! Do you know what you did to me, and what dust you stirred up on my road?" Iblīs answered, "Ādam, suppose I led you off the road. Then tell me, who led me off the road?"

### Appetite and pride

Both turned away from the command, but there is a difference between them. Ādam slipped through appetite, and Iblīs slipped through pride. Pride is worse than giving in to an appetite. When a sin comes from appetite there is room for pardon. When a sin comes from pride, faith is lost in it. A report says, "Magnificence is My cloak and tremendousness My shawl. Whoever contends with Me over either, I will smash him."

### In short

- Out of envy, Shayṭān whispered to Ādam and his wife, to strip them of the covering and comforts they enjoyed.
- He lied that the tree would make them angels or immortals.
- His oldest trick is to make the lawful look small and the few forbidden things look grand, shaped to each person's tastes.
- Ādam slipped through appetite, which leaves room for pardon. Iblīs slipped through pride, which destroys faith.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:21>>>
Shayṭān backed his lie with an oath: "And he swore to them, “I am truly your sincere advisor.”" He swore to Ādam and his wife by Allah that he wanted nothing but their good.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And he swore to them"**: the word is qāsamahumā, "he swore to the two of them", and he swore by Allah.

**"“I am truly your sincere advisor.”"** In one paraphrase his words are: "By Allah, I am a sincere adviser to you both, Ādam and Eve, in what I direct you to." His sincerity was claimed "in this matter", and what he swore to was that this was the tree of immortality. He added, in effect, "I was here before you, so I know this place better." Qatādah said that Shayṭān swore by Allah, saying, "I was created before you, and I know more than you do. So follow me, and I will guide you."

### Can a believer be deceived?

It is a fact that a believer in Allah may sometimes fall victim to deceit. Ādam and his wife believed in Allah, and Shayṭān swore by Allah to win them over.

### In short

- Shayṭān swore by Allah that he was a sincere adviser to Ādam and his wife.
- He claimed the authority of seniority: he was created first and knew the place better.
- A believer in Allah can sometimes be taken in by deceit.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:22>>>
The deception worked: "So he brought about their fall through deception. And when they tasted of the tree, their nakedness was exposed to them, prompting them to cover themselves with leaves from Paradise. Then their Lord called out to them, “Did I not forbid you from that tree and ˹did I not˺ tell you that Satan is your sworn enemy?”" Shayṭān lured them with falsehood and lies until they ate. At once they lost their covering and heard their Lord's reproach.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So he brought about their fall through deception."** He led them on by guile. He lowered their rank through his delusion, bringing them down from their high position by trickery, until they ate from the tree.

**"And when they tasted of the tree"** means when they ate of it.

**"their nakedness was exposed to them"**: the front and back private parts of each became visible to the other. Each is called sawʾah, "shameful", because its exposure shames (yasūʾu) the person. Wahb ibn Munabbih commented on the later phrase "stripping them of their clothing" (7:27). He said a light once covered the private parts of Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ (Eve), so neither could see the other's. Eating from the tree removed it, and their private parts became visible to them. Ibn Jarīr reported this with an authentic chain.

**"prompting them to cover themselves with leaves from Paradise"**: out of shame they began to stick leaves onto themselves to cover up. Ibn ʿAbbās said these were fig leaves, and this has an authentic chain to him. Mujāhid said they made the leaves into a garment.

**"Then their Lord called out to them"**: "O Ādam and Eve".

**"“Did I not forbid you from that tree and ˹did I not˺ tell you that Satan is your sworn enemy?”"** The question is really a statement: I did forbid you to eat from that tree, and I did warn you. Satan, that is Iblīs, is an enemy whose hostility is plain.

### Why did Ādam run?

According to Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, Ādam was as tall as a palm tree and had a thick head of hair. When he committed his error his private part appeared to him, though he had never seen it before. He ran through Paradise in fright until a tree caught him by the head. He said to it, "Let me go," but it answered, "No, I will not let you go." Then his Lord called him: "Ādam, are you running away from Me?" He said, "My Lord, I felt ashamed before You."

Ibn Jarīr and Ibn Marduwyah collected this through several chains, from al-Ḥasan from Ubayy, who traced it back to the Prophet ﷺ. Even so, it is more correct to treat it as Ubayy's own statement.

### The inner reading: one taste, and a kingdom lost

Whoever follows the soul's appetite against the command of the Real is held back from the Real, and does not reach the appetite either. Ādam the Chosen had taken no more than a taste of the forbidden tree when the whip of rebuke fell on his head and his state changed. He neither fully satisfied the appetite nor kept Allah's approval. When he looked again there was no crown on his head and no robe on his body. Before, he had seen himself seated on the throne of chosenness, leaning on the cushion of vicegerency, dressed in the robes and ornaments of Paradise. Now he saw himself cut off from all of it, naked and hungry, in need of a single leaf.

Blessed are the noble ones who set out early like kings and took their rest like the poor! As the verse says, "Wonder not at my abasement. I am he with whose lifeblood time sported, then abased."

Then the command came: "Ādam, you could not enjoy all those blessings without hardship and toil. Now go to the house of trial and adversity. Work, sow seeds, take pains and be patient." Ādam said, "All of this is a small thing, if one day I am let back to this Threshold." He grieved over the pain in his heart and laid his need and weakness on the palms of remorse. Then he wept and made the plea of the next verse.

### In short

- Shayṭān brought Ādam and his wife down from their high rank by lies and trickery.
- When they ate, the light that covered them was gone. They saw their nakedness and covered themselves with leaves, fig leaves in one report.
- Allah reminded them that He had forbidden the tree and warned them of Shayṭān's open enmity.
- Following an appetite against Allah's command loses both the appetite and Allah's approval.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:23>>>
Unlike Iblīs, Ādam and his wife turned straight back to their Lord: "They replied, “Our Lord! We have wronged ourselves. If You do not forgive us and have mercy on us, we will certainly be losers.”" They admitted their fault without excuses and asked for forgiveness and mercy.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They replied, “Our Lord! We have wronged ourselves."** We have harmed ourselves by our disobedience, by doing what You forbade us. Ādam and Eve both said it.

**"If You do not forgive us"** means if You do not overlook our wrongdoing.

**"and have mercy on us"**: by sparing us punishment.

**"we will certainly be losers.”"** We will be among the lost, those tricked into earning punishment, who lose their share of both this world and the Hereafter. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk ibn Muzāḥim also commented on this plea.

### Two requests, two answers

ʿAbd ar-Razzāq reported from Qatādah that Ādam said, "My Lord, what if I repent and seek forgiveness?" Allah said, "Then I will admit you into Paradise." Shayṭān, for his part, asked not for forgiveness but for respite. Each of them was given what he asked for.

### The inner reading: the plea of the dust

Ādam's plea is drawn out in the language of intimate prayer.

"O Allah, if we weep, weeping for You is sweet. If we lament before You, our lament is fitting. O Allah, what comes from dust but error? What is born of a flaw but disloyalty? And what comes from the Generous but faithfulness? O Allah, we have come back with both hands empty. What would it cost You to lay balm on the wounded?"

"O Allah, You are the treasure of the poor and the provision of the distressed. You are the refuge of those who flee and the One who takes the hand of the helpless. You created us and saw the flawed jewel, yet You chose it and bought it with all its faults. You raised us up when no one asked You to. Now that You have raised us, do not cast us down. Keep us in the shade of Your gentleness, and leave us to nothing but Your bounty."

"If You water, it was You who planted. If You level the foundation, it was You who raised it. I, the servant, am only what You made of me. Do not throw me down, for You lifted me up."

### In short

- Ādam and his wife admitted their wrong at once, without excuses, and asked for forgiveness and mercy.
- They knew that without Allah's forgiveness they would lose both this world and the Hereafter.
- Ādam asked for forgiveness and Shayṭān asked for respite, and each was given what he asked.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:24>>>
The sentence of descent follows: "Allah said, “Descend as enemies to each other. You will find in the earth a residence and provision for your appointed stay.”" Everyone in this story was sent down from Paradise to the earth. There the enmity begun in Paradise would play out, and there each person would have a home and provision until a fixed term.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah said, “Descend"** means go down from Paradise to the earth. One report says the command was given to four: Ādam, Ḥawwāʾ (Eve), Iblīs and the snake. Other scholars leave the snake out. Allah knows best. One explanation names Ādam, Eve, the serpent and the peacock. Another says it means Ādam and Eve, together with all the descendants they carried within them. Another verse says, "Descend, both of you, from here together ˹with Satan˺ as enemies to each other" (20:123).

**"as enemies to each other."** The enmity is chiefly between Ādam and Iblīs, and Ḥawwāʾ follows Ādam in it. If the story of the snake is true, the snake is one of Iblīs's followers. Another explanation is that Ādam's descendants will be enemies to one another, because of the wrongs they do each other.

**"You will find in the earth a residence"**: a dwelling, a shelter and a place to settle.

**"and provision for your appointed stay.”"** You will enjoy what is in the earth for a while, until death, when your terms of life are complete. These terms are known and fixed. They were recorded by the Pen, counted out by Allah's decree and written in the First Record.

### Where did they land?

Some scholars named the places on earth where they came down. These accounts come from the Israelite tales, and only Allah knows whether they are true. If knowing these places were of any use to people in their religion or their worldly life, Allah would have mentioned them in His Book. His Messenger ﷺ would have mentioned them too.

### An enmity that lasts until the Last Day

Ādam and Shayṭān were both sent to earth as enemies, and the battle between them will go on until Doomsday. Shayṭān strives without rest to bring people onto his path and to rob them of Allah's grace, as he himself was robbed of it. A person should stand against him and wreck his plans. He should ignore Shayṭān's call and hurry to answer the call of Allah.

This struggle shows itself in humanity splitting into two groups. Some give in to Shayṭān's lures and fall in line behind him. Those who answer Allah's call run the risk that all of Shayṭān's companions will plot to slander and defeat them. In every age the true devotees of truth, always few in number, have suffered the bitterest hostility from those who deny the truth. The reason is Shayṭān's enmity. He turns people against the true believers and fills their hearts with a burning hatred for them. Some people become his tools and set about harassing those devoted to the truth.

Shayṭān's real sin was his refusal to acknowledge the favour Allah had given Ādam. He tries to create the same temperament in every human being.

- He stirs up the young to deny their elders the regard they are due.
- In social or business dealings, when one party is bound by honour to give the other his due, Shayṭān persuades him to break his word.
- When a servant of Allah brings the message of truth, Shayṭān plants all kinds of doubts in people's hearts so that they refuse it.
- In a dispute where one side is ready to pay what fits its means, Shayṭān quietly urges the other side to refuse. He prompts demands so huge that they cannot be met, so the quarrel goes on.

In this way, through Shayṭān's misguidance, enmity spreads everywhere. Two opposing camps form among human beings, and Shayṭān makes sure their confrontation never ends.

### In short

- Ādam, Ḥawwāʾ and Iblīs, with the snake in some reports, were sent down to earth, with enmity between humankind and Shayṭān.
- The earth gives each person a home and provision until a term that Allah has fixed and recorded.
- Accounts of where they landed come from Israelite tales. Such knowledge has no use, or Allah and His Messenger ﷺ would have given it.
- The struggle with Shayṭān runs until the Last Day and splits people into two camps.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:25>>>
The verse completes the sentence on the earth: "He added, “There you will live, there you will die, and from there you will be resurrected.”" Allah addressed Ādam, Eve and their descendants. He made the earth the home of the children of Ādam for the rest of this worldly life, and the place of their return.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He added, “There you will live"**: on the earth, for the length of time Allah decrees for you.

**"there you will die"**: on the earth too, and there you will be buried in your graves.

**"and from there you will be resurrected.”"** From the earth you will be brought out of your graves on the Day of Judgement and returned to life. The verb can be read actively, takhrujūn, "you will come forth", or passively, tukhrajūn, "you will be brought forth". The verse is like another: "From the earth We created you, and into it We will return you, and from it We will bring you back again" (20:55).

### What comes after the resurrection?

On the Day of Resurrection Allah will gather the first and the last of all creatures. He will then reward or punish each person according to what he or she did.

### In short

- The earth is humanity's home in life, its grave in death, and the place from which it will be raised.
- At the resurrection Allah will gather all creation and repay each person for his or her deeds.
