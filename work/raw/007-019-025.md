<<<VERSE 7:19>>>
The scene moves into the garden itself. "˹Allah said,˺ “O Adam! Live with your wife in Paradise and eat from wherever you please, but do not approach this tree, or else you will be wrongdoers.”" The permission is wide and the prohibition is one point wide. Everything in the garden is open to the two of them except a single tree, and the verse names the consequence of going near it. The next three verses show what happened to that one narrow prohibition when the enemy went to work on it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O Adam! Live with your wife in Paradise"** is the instruction to settle there. In the Arabic the pronoun "you" is repeated before the command to dwell, and the repetition is there to emphasise the subject of the verb and to attach what follows to it. The wife is Hawwāʾ (Eve), and the name is spelled that way in the reading given.

**"and eat from wherever you please"** sets out how wide the permission is. It covers all the fruits of the garden and any part of it the two of them might want.

**"but do not approach this tree"** is the single limit. The prohibition is not on eating only, since approaching is what is forbidden, and nearness to a forbidden thing is itself the danger. The tree has been described as wheat. In another place the warning is worded as eating from it, and the sense comes out the same.

**"or else you will be wrongdoers"** names the result. If they eat from that tree after the prohibition has reached them, they belong among those who transgress the limits and harm themselves.

### Why the story is told again

The story of Adam and Iblīs has already appeared in the fourth section of Surah Al-Baqarah, and detailed comments were given there on the same material. The retelling here is not a repeat for its own sake. Each place the story appears it is asked to carry a different weight, and this surah is using it as the frame for everything that follows about the enemy and about the children of Adam.

### What Adam was made of

Adam is called by four names: Adam, vicegerent, mortal, and man. He was named Adam because he was made from the surface of the earth, drawn from every region of it. That is why the Qur'an says: "And indeed, We created humankind from an extract of clay" (23:12). Every part of the earth was drawn on in the making of him. What went into him came from the briny places and from the sweet ones, from the hard ground and from the soft.

The point of that variety is what came out of it. Salt and sweetness were in the clay of Adam, and coarseness as well as softness, so the natures of his children came out differing. Among them are the sweet-tempered and the bad-tempered, the open and the closed, the generous and the stingy, the easy-going and the difficult, the black and the white. The extraction drew on every part of the earth, from the sweet and the briny to the soft and the hard.

Other verses describe the same clay from other angles. "He created humankind from ˹sounding˺ clay like pottery" (55:14). Pottery is dried clay that gives off a sound, and it is full of noise. The child of Adam, on this picture, is a noisy being. His head is full of commotion, and talk is what he clings to. "Indeed, We created them from a sticky clay" (37:11) points to a clay that clings to everything and mixes with everyone. "Indeed, We created man from sounding clay moulded from black mud" (15:26) points to a clay that is dark and black.

All of these were taught to Adam so that he would know his own measure and not step outside his rank. He was shown his own root, so that if he ever saw generosity he would not think it came from himself. He would know that eminence lies in nurture and not in soil.

And the two are not the same thing. Out of soil come wrongdoing, ignorance and harshness, and out of soil came the disobedience of Adam to his Lord (20:121). Out of nurture comes guidance freely given, repentance accepted and tenderness, and it was by His choice that Allah took Adam (3:33). The fruit that nurture bears is what the Qur'an says of a people: "He loves them, and they love Him" (5:54).

There is a story told to make this plain. Maḥmūd visited the house of Ayāz, and there he saw the property and the blessings and the gold and silver and jewels and silks and robes of honour that had been given to the man. In one corner hung an old patched cloak on a nail, and Maḥmūd asked what that was doing there. Ayāz said: that thing is what I am, in my helplessness and my lowliness. Everything beautiful, everything adorned, everything exalted and delightful in this house is yours. Looking at the cloak, I see how little I can do and I learn what I am worth. Looking at the rest, I see you, and knowing that it belongs to you makes me glad, and I lift up my head.

### In short

- The whole garden was permitted and one tree was not, and what was forbidden was even to go near it.
- The story has been told before, in the fourth section of Surah Al-Baqarah, and this surah retells it for a different purpose.
- Adam was named from the surface of the earth, and he was drawn from every region of it, sweet and briny, soft and hard.
- That mixture is why human natures differ so widely, and the lesson drawn is that eminence lies in how a person is nurtured and not in what he is made of.

<<<VERSE 7:20>>>
Then Satan tempted them in order to expose what was hidden of their nakedness. He said, "Your Lord has forbidden this tree to you only to prevent you from becoming angels or immortals."

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then Satan tempted them"** means he whispered to them and suggested to them what he wanted. The Arabic word for what he did carries the sense of a quiet speech that is not heard by others. What he wanted was to strip them of the favours and the fine clothing they were enjoying.

**"in order to expose what was hidden of their nakedness"** is the aim. The word for their private parts is built on a form that comes from the idea of covering, and the thing hidden was hidden by a covering of light. Before this, the two of them could not see each other's private parts.

**"He said, "Your Lord has forbidden this tree to you only to prevent you from becoming angels or immortals.""** is the lie itself. The sense he was working on is that if they ate from the tree they would gain both things at once, becoming angels and living for ever. The words are the words of a being uttering falsehood.

### The grammar of the lie

In the Arabic there is a construction at work in that sentence, and it is the same construction found in "Allah makes ˹this˺ clear to you so you do not go astray" (4:176), and in "He has placed into the earth firm mountains, so it does not shake with you" (16:15). In those two, the last clause gives the result that is being prevented. So the sentence of Iblīs means: so that you do not become angels or dwell here for ever. That is how the negation is to be read, and it is the opposite of what he wanted them to hear.

The same temptation is reported in another place, and the wording there is a question rather than a statement: "But Satan whispered to him, saying, "O Adam! Shall I show you the Tree of Immortality and a kingdom that does not fade away?"" (20:120). The offer there is immortality and a kingdom that never decays, and here it is angelhood and permanence. Read side by side, both are the same coin, and the coin is the promise of passing beyond the limits of a made thing.

### Where the tree stood

Paradise lay open to Adam and his spouse across all its vast expanses, and the good things in it were beyond counting. The two of them could use those things however they wished. Then, in the middle of more permitted things than anyone could list, one thing alone was forbidden. That narrow prohibition was the ground the enemy picked to attack from.

What he did was to belittle the wide world of legitimate gifts in their eyes and to inflate the importance of the one forbidden thing, presenting it in an attractive light and assuring them of the immense advantages hidden inside it. Adam and his spouse were worn down by his continuous urging, and they finally ate the fruit of the forbidden tree. What they got was the exact opposite of what they had been promised.

### How the same trick is worked on everyone

The strategy is not altered for anyone else, and it is worked for each person according to his own tastes and his own circumstances. One man is taught to hate health-giving food, and told that if he wants wonderful health he should take to drinking wines. Another has a position to defend, and is told that the most effective way of defeating his opponent is to begin destructive activities against him. Another has every opportunity to work on himself, and is told that the shortest road to success is protest and demands. Another has countless religious duties he could perform without any clash with the government of the day, and is made to believe that the system of Islam can only prevail if those in power are first unseated. The particulars change and the shape of the offer does not.

### The mark of care in the telling

The way the verse reports all of this is itself a mark of solicitude and a proof of generosity. The two of them sinned, and the verse turns the matter over to the whispering of Satan, and then adds the detail that the nakedness revealed was hidden from them. One reading spells out that their private parts were made to appear to them and not to others.

### In short

- The temptation was a quiet suggestion aimed at stripping them of what they had, and the aim was to expose what the covering of light had hidden.
- The lie was that the prohibition existed only to keep them from becoming angels or from living for ever, and the promise was immortality and a kingdom that does not fade.
- The attack was made on the one narrow point in a very wide permission, by belittling everything allowed and inflating the one thing forbidden.
- The same method is used on everyone, tailored to their tastes, and the shape of the offer never changes.

<<<VERSE 7:21>>>
And he swore to them, "I am truly your sincere advisor."

### Phrase by phrase

**"And he swore to them"** is the verse's whole weight. The Arabic verb behind the words is qāsamahumā, and it says that he swore to the two of them, and what he swore by was Allah. This is the act that makes the next verses intelligible. A lie had been told, and now the lie is put under an oath.

**"I am truly your sincere advisor"** is the content of the oath. The word for a sincere advisor is the word for one who gives good counsel out of goodwill, and he attaches it to himself in an unqualified way. Another rendering gives the sense as: I am a sincere adviser to you in this matter, and the oath is that the tree is the tree of immortality.

### Why the oath was needed

The claim in the previous verse was not a small one. What he was asking them to believe was that their Lord had forbidden the tree for a reason that had nothing to do with their good. Against that, the reputation he needed was the reputation of a friend.

So he swore. He swore by Allah, and he told them that he was created before them and therefore had better knowledge of the place. That is the whole argument: an older being, with longer experience of the garden, offering a piece of advice out of concern. The oath does not add any evidence to the claim. It adds only the appearance of a man who would not lie.

Qatādah commented on this verse and gave the same reading of it. The oath, as he described the matter, ran like this. I was made before you were made. My knowledge of this place is therefore better than yours. So follow me, and I will set you on the right road.

### The fact worth remembering

It is a fact that a believer in Allah can sometimes fall victim to deceit. That is the reason the story is set out in this much detail, verse by verse, with the whisper, the false claim and the oath laid one after another. The reader is not being told about two people in a garden. He is being shown the exact sequence of steps by which a being who believes in Allah is talked into doing what Allah forbade.

The two of them met afterwards, and that meeting is also reported. Adam asked him what had been done to himself, and what dust had been thrown up on the road he was walking. Iblīs gave back a question of his own. He said that he accepted having taken Adam off the road, and then asked who it was that had taken him off the road.

### In short

- The verse is the oath, and the oath was sworn by Allah, which is what gives the lie its weight.
- What the oath was asked to do was to establish him as a friend, since an older being offering help is harder to refuse than a stranger.
- He added the argument that he was created first and knew the place better, which is an argument from age rather than from evidence.
- The sequence is preserved in detail because a believer can be deceived, and the steps are what the reader needs to see.

<<<VERSE 7:22>>>
So he brought about their fall through deception. And when they tasted of the tree, their nakedness was exposed to them, prompting them to cover themselves with leaves from Paradise. Then their Lord called out to them, "Did I not forbid you from that tree and ˹did I not˺ tell you that Satan is your sworn enemy?"

### Phrase by phrase

**"So he brought about their fall through deception"** means he led them on to eat from the tree by guile, with falsehood and lies, until they ate from it. In another reading the phrase carries the sense that he brought them down, meaning that he lowered their station by his deception.

**"And when they tasted of the tree, their nakedness was exposed to them"** is the first result. The private part of each of them was revealed to the other, the front and the back alike, and every one of these parts is called shameful because exposing it puts a person to shame.

**"prompting them to cover themselves with leaves from Paradise"** is the second result. They began to piece the leaves together, that is, to stick them onto themselves, in order to cover up. Ibn ʿAbbās said the leaves they used were fig leaves. This statement has an authentic chain of narration reaching him. Mujāhid said they used the leaves of the garden, making them into a garment.

**"Then their Lord called out to them"** begins the question, and the question is addressed to Adam and to Hawwāʾ.

**"Did I not forbid you from that tree and ˹did I not˺ tell you that Satan is your sworn enemy?"** The interrogative is meant as an affirmation, so the sentence is a statement in the form of a question, and the answer to it is yes on both counts.

### What the change looked like

A report going back to Ubayy bin Kaʿb describes the moment. Adam stood as tall as a palm tree, and his hair was thick. Only when he committed the error did his private part become visible to him, and he had never seen it before. He took to running through the garden in his fright, and a tree caught hold of his head. He asked the tree to let him go, and the tree refused. Then his Lord called to him and asked whether he was running away from Him. He said that he was not. He said that he felt shame in front of his Lord.

The report reached Ibn Jarīr and Ibn Marduwyah through more than one chain, passing by way of Al-Ḥasan to Ubayy bin Kaʿb, and Ubayy related it as coming from the Prophet ﷺ. Making it Ubayy's own statement is the sounder of the two ways.

Another account of the covering comes from Wahb bin Munabbih. On his account a light lay over the two of them, and while it was there neither could see the other uncovered. Eating from the tree ended that, and the parts became visible. Ibn Jarīr reported the statement with a chain that is authentic. The same thought lies behind the words "stripping them of their raiment" (7:27).

### The rebuke and what it did

Whoever tracks his own appetite against the command of the Real is held back from the Real, and he does not reach the appetite either. That is what this verse shows. Adam the chosen had barely tasted the forbidden tree before the rebuke fell on him and his condition changed. The appetite was never satisfied, and the approval he had enjoyed was gone with it.

He looked again, and the crown was off his head and the robe was off his body. Before this he had seen himself seated on a throne of chosenness, resting back on the cushion of the vicegerency, dressed in the robes and ornaments of the garden. Now he saw himself shut out from all of it, uncovered and hungry, reduced to needing a single leaf from a tree. And the word came to him:

> O Adam, you were not able to enjoy all those blessings without suffering and toil. Now go to the house of tribulation and adversity and work, plant seeds, take pains, and be patient.

### Where the two of them differed

Afterwards the two of them met, and Adam put the question to Iblīs about what had been done to him. The answer given back was a question of its own. Both of them had turned away from the command, and there is a difference between the two. Adam slipped because of appetite. Iblīs slipped because of pride. Pride is the worse of the two. A sin that comes out of appetite leaves room for pardon. A sin that comes out of pride takes the faith down with it. And a report has come down on the point:

> Magnificence is My cloak and tremendousness My shawl. If anyone contends with Me in either of them, I will smash him.

The distinction is the answer to the whole passage. The two of them did the same thing in the sense that both disobeyed, and they did not do the same thing at all in the sense that matters, which is what the disobedience was made of.

Adam said of all of it: this is trifling if one day I am given access to this threshold again. He lamented the pain in his heart and placed his need and his incapacity on the palms of remorse, and he wept.

### In short

- The deception worked, and the first result was that the covering of light was taken away, so that each saw the other's nakedness.
- The second result was the leaves, which Ibn ʿAbbās named as fig leaves, and which they stuck on themselves to cover up.
- The question from their Lord is a statement in the form of a question, and it affirms both the prohibition and the warning about the enemy.
- Both of them disobeyed, and they differed in what the disobedience was made of: Adam slipped through appetite, which leaves room for pardon, and Iblīs slipped through pride, which loses the faith.

<<<VERSE 7:23>>>
They replied, "Our Lord! We have wronged ourselves. If You do not forgive us and have mercy on us, we will certainly be losers."

### Phrase by phrase

**"They replied"** is already the whole difference between the two of them. Iblīs, faced with a question, answered with an excuse. Adam and Eve, faced with the same kind of question, answered with a confession.

**"Our Lord! We have wronged ourselves"** is the confession, and the wording is exactly right. They did not say that they had wronged Allah. They said that they had harmed themselves by transgressing, which is what the wrong against Allah amounts to for a creature who can gain nothing for Him and lose everything for himself.

**"If You do not forgive us and have mercy on us"** asks for two things and treats both as necessary. To forgive is to overlook the transgression. To have mercy is to refrain from punishing it.

**"we will certainly be losers"** gives the consequence. The word is the word for being duped into receiving punishment, and one gloss adds what the loss covers, which is their share of the life of this world and of the Hereafter.

### What they did not do

They did not blame the tree and they did not blame each other. They did not blame the enemy, although the enemy was the one who had sworn a false oath to them. Every one of those answers was available, and not one of them was given. The confession is complete and unshared.

The contrast with the previous speaker in this surah is put directly in a report. ʿAbdur-Razzāq reported from Qatādah that Adam asked his Lord what would happen if he turned back and asked to be forgiven, and that the answer was that he would then be brought into Paradise. Shaytan, on the other hand, asked for time and not for pardon:

> As for Shaytan, he did not ask for forgiveness, but for respite. Each one of them was given what he asked for.

That sentence is the two stories laid side by side. One asked for time and got it, and the time went on the same work. One asked for pardon and got it, and the pardon was the start of a road back. Both requests were granted, and what each of them asked for is the measure of what he had learned.

### What was said about the confession

Ad-Dahhak bin Muzāḥim commented on these same words. The substance of his comment is what the verse itself says, that the two of them owned the wrong as their own doing and asked their Lord to cover it and to show them mercy.

### A prayer under the prayer

The words themselves are used as a prayer, and they have been read as the model of every sincere turning back. In full it runs like this:

> O God, if we weep, weeping for You is sweet. If we lament over You, our lamenting is appropriate. O God, what comes from dust other than error? What is born from defect other than disloyalty? And what comes from the generous other than loyalty? O God, we have come back with two empty hands. What would happen if You placed balm on the wounded? O God, You are the treasure of the poor, the travelling supplies of the distressed, the resource of the fleeing, the hand holder of the helpless. You created, so You saw the faulty jewel, but You selected it and bought it with all its faults. You lifted us up and no one said, "Lift up". Now that You have lifted up, do not put down. Keep us in the shadow of gentleness and entrust us to none but Your bounty. If You water, You Yourself planted. If You flatten the foundation, You Yourself raised it. I the servant am just what You fancied. Do not throw me down, for You lifted me up.

### In short

- The answer to the question is a confession and not an excuse, which is the difference between Adam and Iblīs.
- The wrong is stated as a wrong done to themselves, and both forgiveness and mercy are asked for, since either without the other leaves the matter unfinished.
- The loss they named covers their share of this life and of the next, so nothing is left outside the request.
- Adam asked for pardon and was given it, while Iblīs asked for time and was given that, and each received what he asked for.

<<<VERSE 7:24>>>
Allah said, "Descend as enemies to each other. You will find in the earth a residence and provision for your appointed stay."

### Phrase by phrase

**"Descend"** names Adam and his wife, and Iblīs, and the snake with them. Not every scholar lists the snake, and Allah knows best. The enmity is chiefly the enmity between Adam and Iblīs. Hawwāʾ (Eve) is with Adam in the matter. One reading takes the plural as covering Adam and Hawwāʾ together with all the offspring the two of them comprise, and another reading names the group as Adam, Eve, the serpent and the peacock.

**"as enemies to each other"** states the relation that the descent creates. Each of them is an enemy to the others on account of the wrong each does to the other.

**"You will find in the earth a residence"** means there will be for you a dwelling and a shelter down there. In another place the same descent is commanded in these words: "Descend, both of you, from here together ˹with Satan˺ as enemies to each other" (20:123).

**"and provision for your appointed stay"** means provision until death, that is, until the terms of their lives are fulfilled. Those terms are known and fixed. They are written in the first record, already set down by the Pen, and counted out by predestination.

### What the earth is for them

The story about the snake, if it is true, has the snake as a follower of Iblīs. Some scholars named the places on earth that the two of them were sent down to. These accounts are taken from Israelite tales, and only Allah knows whether they are true. And there is a rule that follows from it. If knowing those places were useful to people in their religion or in their lives, His Book would have named them, and His Messenger ﷺ would have named them as well.

What is given instead is the thing that actually matters. The earth is a dwelling place for the children of Adam for what remains of this earthly life. On it they live. On it they die and are buried. And out of it they will be raised for the Day of Resurrection, on which the first of creation and the last of it are gathered together and each is given what his deeds earned, whether that be reward or punishment.

### The war that began here

Both Adam and Satan were sent to the earth as each other's enemies, and the battle between them goes on until the Day of Doom. Satan never stops working to bring human beings onto his path and to strip them of God's grace, just as he himself was stripped of it. And man should set himself against him and spoil his plans. He should ignore his call and run to answer the call of God.

The fight takes visible shape in the way human beings divide into two groups. Some give in to the inducements of Satan and fall in with him. The others answer the call of God, and they take on the risk that all of Satan's companions will work to vilify them and defeat them. In every age the true devotees of the truth, who are always few, have been the ones who suffered the bitterest enmity of those who deny the truth. The reason lies in the enmity of Satan. He turns people against the believers, filling their hearts in one way or another with a burning hatred for them, until some of those people become his tools and begin to make life hard for the devotees of the truth.

### How the enmity shows itself

The original sin of Satan was the refusal to acknowledge the favour that had been given to Adam, and his effort now is to breed a temperament like his own in every man. He prods the younger ones to give no proper regard to their elders. In a deal between two parties, where one of them is bound to pay the other what is due, he persuades the first not to honour the pledge at all. When a servant of God brings the message of truth, he plants doubts of several kinds in people's hearts, so that they will not accept it. In a dispute between two parties, where one is ready to pay an amount that matches his circumstances, he slyly persuades the other party to refuse the offer and to demand more than the other can ever meet, so that the quarrel goes on and never dies.

That is how enmity between people comes to prevail everywhere. Two opposing groups form among human beings, and Satan takes care to see that the confrontation between them will never end.

### In short

- The descent is commanded for Adam, Hawwāʾ, Iblīs and those with them, and the enmity is chiefly between Adam and Iblīs.
- The earth is given as a dwelling and a provision for a term that has already been recorded and fixed.
- The reports that name the places of the descent come from Israelite tales, and what is useful for religion and life is what the Book and the Prophet ﷺ gave.
- The enmity began here and runs to the Day of Doom, dividing human beings into two groups and working through ordinary things like debts, quarrels and the message itself.

<<<VERSE 7:25>>>
He added, "There you will live, there you will die, and from there you will be resurrected."

### Phrase by phrase

**"There you will live"** means life on the earth, and the address is to Adam and Hawwāʾ and their descendants.

**"there you will die"** means that death too belongs to the earth, and the burial that follows it.

**"and from there you will be resurrected"** means that the earth is not only the place of dying. It is the place from which they will be brought out. In the Arabic the verb can be read in the active, "you shall come forth", or in the passive, "you shall be brought forth". Another verse gives the sense of the whole of it: "From the earth We created you, and into it We will return you, and from it We will bring you back again" (20:55).

### The three stages

The verse puts the whole of a human life into three clauses, and it gives each of them the same location. The earth is where people are made, where they live, where they die, where they are buried, and where they are raised. Nothing in that list happens anywhere else.

That is also what makes the sentence a comfort rather than a sentence. The descent in the previous verse is a departure, and this verse says what the departure is for. The one who leaves a garden is not sent nowhere. He is sent to a place laid out for him, with a term set for his stay, and with a last stage in which he comes out of it to give an account.

### In short

- Life, death and burial all belong to the earth, and so does the resurrection out of it.
- The sentence reads as a promise as much as a sentence, since the place of exile is the same place the return begins from.
- The verb can be read either as "you shall come forth" or as "you shall be brought forth", and the sense is the same either way.
- The whole of a human life is contained in three clauses, and all three of them name the earth.
