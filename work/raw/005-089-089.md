<<<VERSE 5:89>>>
"Allah will not call you to account for your thoughtless oaths, but He will hold you accountable for deliberate oaths. The penalty for a broken oath is to feed ten poor people from what you normally feed your own family, or to clothe them, or to free a bondsperson. But if none of this is affordable, then you must fast three days. This is the penalty for breaking your oaths. So be mindful of your oaths. This is how Allah makes things clear to you, so perhaps you will be grateful"

This verse sets out the rule for oaths. Allah does not hold the believers to account for oaths made without intention, but He does hold them to account for deliberate oaths. When a deliberate oath is broken, the expiation is to feed ten poor people, or clothe them, or free a slave, and if none of this can be done, to fast three days.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah will not call you to account for your thoughtless oaths, but He will hold you accountable for deliberate oaths."** The material explains that the oath without intention is the word that slips from the tongue, such as saying "no, by Allah" or "yes, by Allah" without meaning it. The material says the same rule was explained in Surah Al-Baqarah, and it cites 2:225, where the accountability for what the hearts have intended is the sin of the Hereafter. The material notes that the accountability in this verse is in this life, through the expiation.

**"The penalty for a broken oath is to feed ten poor people from what you normally feed your own family."** The material says the food should be of the average quality of the household's own meals, given to each poor person at full satisfaction, or in the other reading, one measure of food for each of the ten. Ibn Abbas, Sa'id bin Jubayr and Ikrimah are reported to have read the scale as the average of what one feeds one's family, and Ata' al-Khurasani said it means the best of what one feeds one's family. The material says Ibn Jarir gave the same meaning. The fitrah is the measure used in some of the readings, which is about one and three quarter kilograms of wheat or its value.

**"Or to clothe them."** The material says the clothing must be enough to cover the body, such as a garment for the upper part and another for the lower part. Ibn Abbas and Mujahid are reported to have said that the least is a single garment. The material reports from Abu Malik, Al-Hasan, Ata', Tawus and Ibrahim An-Nakha'i that each of the ten receives one garment.

**"Or to free a bondsperson."** The material gives the report of Umar ibn al-Hakam al-Sulami, who freed a slave girl as an expiation. The Prophet ﷺ asked her where Allah is, and she said above the heavens. He asked who he was, and she said he was the Messenger of Allah. He said to free her, for she is a believer. The report is recorded in Malik's Muwatta, in the Musnad of al-Shafi'i and in the Sahih of Muslim.

**"But if none of this is affordable, then you must fast three days."** The material says that the three expiations are an order of ease, with feeding the easiest and freeing a slave the hardest. It says the choice among them is open, and that the halal and haram of the earlier verses are not made unlawful by an oath. The material reports that Ubayy bin Ka'b and Ibn Mas'ud read the verse as "three consecutive days," and says that this reading has the force of an explanation from the Companions. Abu Hanifah and some other imams held that the fasts must be consecutive.

**"This is the penalty for breaking your oaths. So be mindful of your oaths."** The material says that the expiation is due only after the oath is broken. An expiation given before the oath is broken is not valid, as there is no prayer before its time and no fast of Ramadan before the month. The verse also tells the believer to guard his oaths, and not to swear casually.

**"This is how Allah makes things clear to you, so perhaps you will be grateful."** The material says that Allah explains His rules so that the believers may give thanks.

### The kinds of oath

The material divides oaths into three kinds. The first is the oath of falsehood, sworn knowingly about a past event that is false. This is a grave sin, and it has no expiation, but it requires repentance and seeking forgiveness. The second is the ineffectual oath, which is sworn by mistake or in the belief that a past event was true when it was not. This brings neither sin nor expiation. The third is the binding oath, sworn about a future act, and it requires expiation when it is broken.

The material explains that the binding oath, which the material calls al-Yamin al-Mun'aqidah, requires the expiation in this verse. The oath of falsehood, Yamin Ghamus, is judged in the Hereafter, and the ineffectual oath, Yamin Laghw, carries no sin. Istighfar, seeking forgiveness, is the remedy for a false oath. It adds that the oath about something lawful should not be made into a vow of abstention, as the earlier verses teach.

### The obligation to keep an oath

The material says that the believer should keep his oaths, and break them only when there is a legal reason or a true need. It says that some scholars held that a person should not swear hastily, and should keep his oath as a thing held in reserve.

### In short

- An oath made without intention is not counted against the believer, but a deliberate oath is.
- The expiation for a broken deliberate oath is feeding ten poor people, clothing them, or freeing a slave, and if none is possible, three days of fasting.
- The expiation is due only after the oath is broken, and the believer should guard his oaths and not swear casually.
