<<<VERSE 4:84>>>
The command and its setting: "So fight in the cause of Allah ˹O Prophet˺. You are accountable for none but yourself. And motivate the believers ˹to fight˺, so perhaps Allah will curb the disbelievers' might" (4:84). The battle of Uḥud had passed in the month of Shawwāl, and in keeping with the date the disbelievers had set, the Prophet ﷺ prepared to meet them at Badr in the month of Dhū al-Qaʿdah, the engagement the historians call the minor Badr. Some people betrayed a little hesitation about going along, some from recent wounds and some because of rumours, so the verse came down telling him to ignore the infirm and the scared among the Muslims and march to jihād without hesitation, even alone, for Allah is his helper. With that guidance he went ahead to the lesser Badr with seventy Companions, as promised to Abū Sufyān after Uḥud. Allah put awe and fear into the hearts of Abū Sufyān and his accomplices of the tribe of Quraysh, and not one of them showed up, so they proved false to their solemn promise. Allah prevented the mischief of the disbelievers, and the Prophet ﷺ returned safely with his companions, as al-Qurṭubī and al-Mazharī relate the event.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So fight in the cause of Allah ˹O Prophet˺"** addresses the Prophet ﷺ alone, and the implication is that he should fight irrespective of whether anyone is ready to go with him. The word for perhaps, when used in relation to Allah, means that He has taken the thing upon Himself. He has been promised victory, so the fight goes on even if he stands single, and the report has him saying:

> By Him in Whose Hand is my soul, I shall sally forth to fight even if I go alone.

**"You are accountable for none but yourself"** cuts the responsibility down to one man. He is not tasked with the staying behind of others. The verse about self-destruction was once read against such a fighter. Abu Isḥāq asked al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib whether a man who meets a hundred enemies and still fights them belongs among those meant by the words "And do not throw yourselves into destruction (by not spending your wealth in the cause of Allah)" (2:195). He said no, because Allah said this to His Prophet, and the destruction verse is about spending in His cause. Imām Aḥmad recorded the parallel question, whether a man who attacks the lines of the idolaters is throwing himself to destruction, and received the same answer for the same reason.

**"And motivate the believers ˹to fight˺"** keeps the mission of persuasion alive. He is to incite them by encouraging them and strengthening their resolve, and if they stay unresponsive even after the effort, his prophetic work is done, and from that point he is not responsible for what they do. At the battle of Badr, while organising the lines, he said to the believers:

> Stand up and march forth to a Paradise, as wide as the heavens and Earth.

Many hadiths encourage jihād. Al-Bukhārī recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> Whoever believes in Allah and His Messenger, offers prayer, pays the zakāh and fasts the month of Ramaḍān, will rightfully be granted Paradise by Allah, no matter whether he migrates in Allah's cause or remains in the land where he is born.

Asked whether the people should acquaint others with this good news, he spoke instead of the hundred grades:

> Paradise has one hundred grades which Allah has reserved for the mujāhidīn who fight in His cause, the distance between each two grades being like the distance between heaven and Earth. So, when you ask Allah, ask for al-Firdaws, the best and highest part of Paradise, above which is the Throne of the Most Beneficent, and from which originate the rivers of Paradise.

The hadith also has narrations from ʿUbādah, from Muʿādh and from Abū ad-Dardāʾ. Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī heard his own version addressed to him by name:

> O Abū Saʿīd, whoever accepts Allah as his Lord, Islam as his religion and Muḥammad as the Prophet, he would rightfully acquire Paradise.

He liked the words so much that he asked for them to be repeated, and the Prophet ﷺ repeated them, then mentioned another deed, one by which Allah raises a servant a hundred grades in Paradise, the distance between two grades being as between heaven and Earth. Asked what it was, he said: jihād in Allah's cause. Muslim collected it.

**"so perhaps Allah will curb the disbelievers' might"** explains what the motivating achieves. Their resolve is strengthened to meet the enemy, to defend Islam and its people, and to endure the enemy patiently. Perhaps awe enters their hearts and they fold, and the lone fighter wins through alone.

**"And Allah is far superior in might and in punishment"** gives the reason such success is certain. He is able over them in this life and the Hereafter: "But if it had been Allah's will" (47:4), He could have had His way with them outright. The punishment may fall on the Day of Judgement, or right here in this mortal world, as some hold. Whichever it is, He is the strongest in war and the mightiest in punishing.

### The two kinds of religion

One form religiosity takes is the public observance of outward rites, with no change made in one's life, and such religion is resented by no one, since nobody needs to oppose it. Another form calls for sacrifice and disturbs the pattern of a life. When that kind is invoked, people divide. One group of opponents is made of people whose leadership in the religious field stands on rites that cost their worldly interests nothing. They work by the maxim that speaking in defence of truth damages those interests, while silence or opposition brings benefit, so they oppose the religion of sacrifice to protect their positions. The other group is made of people with a live conscience who judge above gain and expediency, accepting without hesitation a reality that argument has proved. True Muslims are told not to let such differences constrain their social relationships and not to adopt a discourteous attitude, for a Muslim's manner is free of reactionary impulse. It is for Allah to judge and requite as He wishes.

### In short

- After Uḥud the appointment was kept at the lesser Badr with seventy men, and Allah filled Abū Sufyān and the Quraysh with awe, so the enemy never showed and the promise of the disbelievers proved false.
- The Prophet ﷺ is charged with his own fight and with urging the rest, no more. The lone charge answered a question about whether fighting against odds is self-destruction, which it is not, for that verse concerns spending.
- The urging rests on the hundred grades and al-Firdaws, the highest of them, and on the deed that raises a servant a hundred degrees, which is jihād in Allah's cause.
- Success belongs to Allah, whose might and punishment exceed the disbelievers', in this world and the next.

<<<VERSE 4:85>>>
The law of the helping word: "Whoever intercedes for a good cause will have a share in the reward, and whoever intercedes for an evil cause will have a share in the burden. And Allah is Watchful over all things" (4:85). The verse takes the common human act of putting in a word for someone and sorts it by what the word serves.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever intercedes for a good cause will have a share in the reward"** means that whoever intercedes in a matter producing good results acquires a share in that good. The word for intercession, shafāʿah, means literally to meet or to join, or to cause a meeting or joining, which is why shaf, its root word, names an even number, a pair, the opposite of witr, the odd. Taken literally, it is the yoking of your strength to a weak seeker of justice so the appeal grows stronger, or the standing beside a helpless solitary person so that he has the strength of a pair. In the reading of one school it names whoever helps to unite or reconcile two parties, and the reward of that good deed reaches him, the person of the good intercession being Abū Bakr. Mujāhid ibn Jabr read the verse as sent down about people recommending on one another's behalf.

**"and whoever intercedes for an evil cause will have a share in the burden"** means he carries a burden from what resulted and from the intention. In the same school reading, the evil intercession covers the ascribing of partners to Allah and the work of the tale-bearer, and its face among the opponents of the faith was Abū Jahl. Directing another person to some good in a manner containing no sin or oppression earns a share of the reward of the outcome, while a bad intercession, one containing sin or oppression, earns a share of the sin. Become a means of goodness and a share of the good is yours. Become a means of evil and you are guilty of a part of it.

**"And Allah is Watchful over all things"** turns on the word muqīṭ. Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAṭāʾ, ʿAṭiyyah, Qatādah and Matar al-Warrāq said it means Watcher. Mujāhid said it means Witness, and in another narration, Able to do. Lexically the word carries power, observation and the dispensation of sustenance, and all three meanings stand here. Taken as power, rewarding and punishing the maker of a recommendation and its executor is nothing difficult for Him. Taken as observation, He is ever present, knowing best who recommends with what intention, whether sincerely to help a brother-in-faith for Allah's pleasure, or to make him a target of self-interest and extract an advantage as a bribe. Taken as sustenance, what He has written for someone reaches that one without fail. No recommendation corners Him into giving. He bestows as He wills and on whom He wills, while the good recommender is rewarded for free, since a helping hand went out from him to the helpless.

### The rules of a fair recommendation

For a recommendation to be fair and permissible, the claim of the person recommended must be true and permissible, and in the second place the person may be unable to carry his claim to those in authority because of weakness or lack of resource, which is what another may do for him. Recommending what is not true and rightful, or forcibly influencing its acceptance, falls under bad recommendation, and so does pressure exerted through one's connections and influence, for that is an act of injustice. Whoever recommends in the permissible way for a permissible claim or job gets a share in its reward, and whoever recommends something impermissible, or uses an impermissible method, gets a share in its punishment. The share does not depend on the recommendation succeeding. Should the official do the needful for the one wronged, the recommender is rewarded much like the official who removed the injustice. Should he recommend the impermissible, he becomes a sinner either way.

The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever talks another into a good deed earns a reward like the doer's own, and the reverse holds with matching weight. A hadith from Abū Hurairah appearing in Ibn Mājah reports the warning:

> Whoever helps in the killing of a Muslim, even by part of a word, will face Allah with a sign written between his eyes: this man is deprived of the mercy of Allah.

The limit of the act is set in the hadith of Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī:

> Intercede and you will gain a reward of it. Yet Allah shall decide whatever He wills by the words of His Prophet.

Where a weak person cannot carry his problem up to a higher authority, or cannot set out correctly what he needs, carry it for him. Past that point the affair leaves your hands. Acceptance or rejection is not yours to manage, no interference in the deciding is allowed, and disgust at an outcome that goes against you has no place. Read as a word of cause, the verse carries the same hint, and al-Mazharī reports from the great exegete Mujāhid that the maker of a recommendation earns its reward even when nobody accepts it. This is not special to the Prophet ﷺ. Any recommendation made to any human being is bound by the principle: make it and be done with it. He illustrated it himself. He recommended to Barīrah, a bondwoman freed by ʿĀʾishah, that she consider remarrying her husband Mughīth, from whom she had dissolved her marriage, for the man was emotionally disturbed out of his love for her. She answered that if it was a command she was all for it, but if it was a recommendation, she did not feel like accepting it at all. He said that it was a recommendation and not a command, and she, knowing he would take nothing amiss that went against the set rule, said in all frankness that she did not accept it. He took it gracefully and let her stay as she was.

In the present day the whole thing has been mutilated. What passes for recommendation is a pushy exercise of cashing in on connections, acquaintance and VIP influence, with relentless pressure, which is why people grow angry when their recommendation is rejected, and some stoop to open hostility. Yet compelling a person to do something against his conscience and good discretion is compulsion and coercion, a grave sin, like depriving a free person of his freedom of action, or like stealing from someone in order to give to a destitute. And a recommendation against which anything is taken in return is a bribe, declared by hadith to be ill-gotten and ḥarām, whether the bribe is money or a job-related favour, such as harnessing someone to a personal chore in return. Al-Kashshāf draws the full outline. The good recommendation aims at fulfilling the right of a Muslim, bringing him a permissible benefit, or shielding him from harm and loss. It is made for no worldly expediency, but to help a weak person with the pleasure of Allah as the only seek. No bribe is taken against it, financial or physical. It concerns nothing impermissible, and it does not seek pardon for a proven crime whose punishment stands fixed in the Qur'ān. Praying to Allah that the need of some Muslim be fulfilled also counts, as al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ and al-Mazharī note, as good recommendation, its reward reaching the one who prays. When someone prays for the good of his brother-in-faith, the angel answers: may Allah fulfil your need as well.

### The inner reading

Intercession sets free the state of the one interceded for, and by it the intercessor wins a weighty rank from Allah. Whoever rushes instead to corrupt what concerns the command carries the responsibility and the weight of the sin on his own back.

### In short

- Intercession couples strength with weakness. Its claim must be true, its method free of force, and its seeker unable to carry the matter alone.
- The share in reward or burden is earned by the act itself, not by its success, so the recommender neither pressures the decision nor rages at a refusal.
- Barīrah's refusal of the Prophet's ﷺ own recommendation, accepted gracefully, is the standing pattern of the limit.
- Payment for a recommendation is a bribe and ḥarām, and a prayer for a brother's need is itself a good intercession.

<<<VERSE 4:86>>>
The etiquette of the greeting: "And when you are greeted, respond with a better greeting or at least similarly. Surely Allah is a ˹vigilant˺ Reckoner of all things" (4:86). The verse opens the Islamic teaching on the salām, the greeting Muslims exchange, and it was revealed, one report holds, about a group of people who refrained from returning greetings.

### The greeting Islam gave

Lexically, taḥiyyah belongs to the sayings of ḥayyākallāh, may Allah keep you alive. In pre-Islam Arabia people greeted each other with ḥayyākallāh, or with asks for blessing on the morning, or other expressions of that nature. Islam changed the style and fixed a standard form, As-Salāmu ʿAlaykum, commonly though incompletely translated as peace be on you, for the words mean: may you remain safe from every pain, sorrow and distress. Ibn al-ʿArabī notes in the Aḥkām al-Qur'ān that Salam is one of the good names of Allah, so that the greeting declares Allah the guardian and caretaker of the one greeted.

The Islamic greeting stands alone among the customs of the world. All civilised people say something to express familiarity or affection at meeting, but compared broadly, this form stands out for comprehensiveness. It does not stop at affection. It joins to it the fulfilment of the demands of love, a prayer that the other be kept safe from all calamities and sorrows. It is no bland prayer for long life, as with the pre-Islam Arabs, but a prayer for a good life. It expresses the reality of the relation with Allah, that greeter and greeted are both dependent on Him, needing Him all the time, with no one bringing benefit to anyone without His will. Taken that way, the greeting is an act of worship in its own right and a medium for the remembrance of Allah, reminding a brother-in-faith of the object of his obedience and love. It also carries a promise, that the person greeted is safe from the greeter's own hands and tongue, so that the greeter stands as guardian of the other's life, property and honour. Ibn ʿUyaynah, in the report quoted by Ibn al-ʿArabī, asked whether one knows what the salām is, and answered that the greeter is saying: you are safe from me. The hadith of at-Tirmidhī, in the Book of Faith, runs:

> A Muslim is the one from whom all Muslims remain safe, safe from his tongue and safe from his hands.

One may wish Muslims would not utter these words as a habitual custom like the greetings of other peoples. Offered out of a full understanding of its reality, the greeting might be enough for the reform of a whole community, which is why the Prophet ﷺ laid such emphasis on popularising it.

### The merits of the greeting

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim carries from Abū Hurairah the report that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> You cannot enter Paradise until you are a believer, and your belief, your īmān, cannot be complete until you love each other. I tell you something which, if you put it in practice, will establish bonds of love among you all: make the salām a common practice among you, including every Muslim, whether an acquaintance or a stranger.

ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar relates that someone asked the Prophet ﷺ which practice of Islam is the worthiest, and he said to feed people and spread the practice of the salām, known face or stranger, a report carried by al-Bukhārī and Muslim. The Musnad of Aḥmad, at-Tirmidhī and Abū Dāwūd report from Abū Umāmah that the person nearest to Allah is the one who greets first. A hadith of ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd, in the Musnad of al-Bazzār and the al-Mu'jim al-Kabīr of aṭ-Ṭabarānī, teaches that the salām is one of the names of Allah with which He has blessed the people of the earth, so it must be made common. A Muslim who offers his salām in a gathering is blessed with a station of distinction, having reminded everyone of Allah. Should the gathering not return it, others better than they will respond, the angels of Allah. And in another hadith from Abū Hurairah, the big miser is the man who scrimps on the greeting, as aṭ-Ṭabarānī records in the al-Mu'jim al-Kabīr. The effect on the Companions can be gauged from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar, who would go to the bazaar for the single purpose of meeting any Muslim there in the hope of offering the salām and earning the reward of an act of worship, never intending to buy or sell, a narration of Tufayl ibn ʿUbayy ibn Kaʿb that appears in the Muʾatta of Imām Mālik.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And when you are greeted, respond with a better greeting"** means that if a Muslim greets with the salām, the greeting is returned with what is better, and the better return is recommended. The Prophet ﷺ taught it by action. ʿImrān ibn Ḥuṣayn, in the record of Imām Aḥmad, watched three men come in turn. The first said As-Salāmu ʿAlaykum, the Prophet ﷺ returned it, and after the man sat he said: ten. The second added the mercy of Allah, and heard: twenty. The third added the blessings of Allah, and heard: thirty. Abu Dāwūd recorded this narration, and at-Tirmidhī, an-Nasāʾī and al-Bazzār recorded it also, at-Tirmidhī calling it ḥasan gharīb, with further hadiths on the subject from Abū Saʿīd, ʿAlī and Sahl ibn Ḥanīf. The fuller teaching report has its own detail. One man greeted, and the return added a word of mercy. A second greeted with the mercy included, and the return added the blessings. The third combined all three in his initial greeting, and the Prophet ﷺ answered with a single word, and upon you. Asked why the prayers had shrunk, he said that the man had left nothing to add, for he had used up all the words in his salām, so the return on the principle of like for like was sufficient, in accordance with the teaching of the Qur'ān. Ibn Jarīr and Ibn Abī Ḥātim reported it with different chains.

Three rules emerge. The salām offered is returned by adding words to it, mercy upon peace, blessings upon mercy. The addition is restricted to three words as a sunnah-conforming act, for Ibn ʿAbbās, in the report al-Mazharī cites from al-Baghawī, explained that the salām ends at the word barakah, and saying anything beyond that was not the practice of the Prophet ﷺ. The logic is plain, since the occasion requires brevity, and excess that interferes with ongoing business or weighs on the listener is not appropriate. And if someone packs all three words into his initial salām, returning it with one word is correct, like for like, sufficient in obedience to the command of returning the same.

**"or at least similarly"** states the obligation. Returning the equal salām is a duty on every Muslim greeted with the full form, and failing it without an excuse the Sharīʿah admits makes a person a sinner, though the mode is his to choose, better words or identical ones. The passive voice of the verse, when you are greeted, without naming the greeter, hints that the salām was already the habitual common practice of the Muslims. The initial salām itself is a sunnah mu'akkadah, an emphasised practice, as the majority of the ʿulamā hold in the reading of al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ, and Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said that the initial salām is voluntary while returning it is an obligation.

**"Surely Allah is a ˹vigilant˺ Reckoner of all things"** closes the account. Everything is reckoned with Allah, and the reckoning includes all human and Islamic rights. The salām and its answer stand among them, and they too will be accounted for before Him.

### The wider rules of the greeting

The riding person greets the walking person, the walking person greets the one sitting, and a small group walking near a larger group is first to greet, as a hadith in al-Bukhārī and Muslim directs. On entering his house, a person should offer the salām to his family, an act of grace bringing blessings for him and them, as at-Tirmidhī has it. Meeting a Muslim more than once, the salām is offered every time, and it belongs to the practice at leave-taking as well, a rule that appears in at-Tirmidhī and Abū Dāwūd on the narration of Qatādah and Abū Hurairah. With the people of the covenant, the ahl adh-dhimmah, the salām is not initiated, nor is anything added when their greeting is returned, for the Two Ṣaḥīḥs direct that their greeting is returned equally. Ibn ʿUmar narrated the reason in the words of the Prophet ﷺ:

> When the Jews greet you, one of them would say: death be unto you. Therefore say: and the same to you.

In his Ṣaḥīḥ, Muslim recorded from Abū Hurayrah the further instruction not to initiate the greeting with the Jews and the Christians, and when meeting them on a road, to force them to its narrowest path. The schools add further notes. The Sunnah specifies leaving unanswered the greeting of the open disbeliever, the innovator and the wicked person. The same holds for one relieving himself, one in the bath and one eating, the disapproval being relaxed only in that last case. And to the disbeliever who says peace upon you, the answer is simply: and upon you. The exceptions to the obligation run the other way too. No answer is owed to a salām said to a person performing ṣalāh, for it spoils the prayer, and the same holds for one delivering a sermon, reciting the Qur'ān, calling the adhān or the iqāmah, teaching religious texts, or busy with the compulsions of human need, where even offering the initial salām is not permissible.

### The inner reading

In this verse the lovingly kind Beautiful-doer teaches His servants the courteous acts of companionship. Whoever wears no courtesy is unfit for company. Companionship has three sorts to it: courtesy in conforming to the Real, courtesy of sincerity with people, and courtesy in opposing the soul. A person not nurtured on these three has no part in the path of Muṣṭafā, and counts for nothing in the world of no god but God. The Lord first adorned Muṣṭafā himself with this quality, in his own words:

> My Lord taught me courtesy, so how beautifully I have been made courteous.

On the night of the miʿrāj, standing in the most tremendous station, he kept the courtesy of the Presence, of whom the verse says: "The eyesight did not swerve, nor did it transgress" (53:17). With people he kept the courtesy of company, of whom the verse says: "Surely you have a tremendous character" (68:4).

The courteous acts toward the Real run by principles. Work knowledge into every dealing. Reverence the Sharīʿah, and refuse to enact what your own wishes command. Honour the Sunnah and its folk, and keep clear of innovation and its folk. Lay suspicion and supposition aside. Stay far from disquieting thoughts, from eye-service, from ignorance and from laziness in worship. Do not dress yourself as the worshipful servant of anything outside the Sunnah, and keep the supererogatory acts hidden. Do not take the name of Allah in heedlessness, nor mix levity with seriousness, nor play with the Sharīʿah and the religion. Carry scrupulousness into speech, action, sight, food, sleep, movement and rest.

Even the one whose days pass in truthfulness and limpidity never approves of himself. Displeasure with self stays, and repentance binds him in every state, on the word of the Messenger:

> My heart becomes clouded, so I ask forgiveness from Allah one hundred times a day.

Abū Yazīd Basṭāmī carried displeasure with his own limpidness and self-purification so far that his glorification at times was a finger pointed at himself and the words: you are the miserable man of the time.

### In short

- Islam replaced the greetings of the age with one standard prayer for safety. That prayer carries a name of Allah, a reminder of Him, and a pledge of safety from the greeter's own hand and tongue.
- The merits are many: love and īmān, the worthiest deed after feeding people, nearness to Allah for greeting first, and a station of distinction in every gathering.
- The return is obligatory, and the better return is recommended. The addition is capped at barakah, and like for like suffices when the greeter spends all the words at once.
- The salām is not initiated with the people of the covenant, whose twisted greeting is answered with a plain and the same to you, and obligations pause where prayer, sermon, recitation, adhān, iqāmah, teaching and need hold a person.
- The greeting is a school of courtesy whose courtyard runs from the miʿrāj to the market, and its graduates never approve of themselves.

<<<VERSE 4:87>>>
The seal of the passage: "Allah, there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him. He will certainly gather ˹all of˺ you together on the Day of Judgment" (4:87). After the commands about fighting, intercession and greeting, the verses close with the foundation everything rests on.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah, there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him"** declares His Oneness, informing that He alone is God of all creation. The address works by negation and affirmation together. Everything other than Allah falls under the negation, and divinity is impossible for them. The affirmation belongs to Him through His divinity, and what He has affirmed for Himself cannot be denied. The exhortation follows: believe in Him as the only object of worship, and let whatever you do be done as an act of worship before Him.

**"He will certainly gather ˺all of˺ you together on the Day of Judgment, about which there is no doubt"** is an oath that He will gather the earlier and the latter generations in one area, raising them from their graves, to reward or punish each person according to his or her actions. The Day of Qiyāmah, the fateful Day of Doom, is certain, and with it the promise of reward and punishment.

**"And whose word is more truthful than Allah's"** needs no answer, for no one utters more truthful statements than Allah, in His promise and His warning, in the stories of the past and the information of what is to come. The news of what will happen is true because Allah is its giver, and whose word can be more true than that of Allah.

### In short

- The verse declares the Oneness of Allah, negating every other object of worship and affirming what He has affirmed of Himself.
- The gathering on the Day of Qiyāmah is sworn and certain, and every soul meets its own return there.
- His word is the measure of truth, in promise, warning, history and news of the unseen alike.
