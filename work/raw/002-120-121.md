<<<VERSE 2:120>>>
Verse 120 tells the Prophet ﷺ plainly what lies at the bottom of the opposition he faced from the People of the Book. "Never will the Jews or Christians be pleased with you, until you follow their faith. Say, “Allah’s guidance is the only ˹true˺ guidance.” And if you were to follow their desires after ˹all˺ the knowledge that has come to you, there would be none to protect or help you against Allah".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Never will the Jews or Christians be pleased with you, until you follow their faith"**. Ibn Jarīr explained: the Jews and the Christians will never be happy with you, Muḥammad, so do not seek what pleases or appeases them, but hold to what pleases Allah by calling them to the truth He sent you with. The Jews of al-Madinah and the Christians of Najrān are meant, and "their faith" is their religion and their direction of prayer. Two occasions are reported. According to one, they used to ask the Prophet ﷺ for a truce, giving the impression that if he granted them respite they would follow him and believe in him, and Allah revealed the verse. Ibn ʿAbbās, however, said it was revealed about the qiblah. The Jews of al-Madinah and the Christians of Najrān had hoped that the Prophet ﷺ would go on praying towards their qiblah, and when Allah directed him to the Kaʿbah they were disappointed and despaired of his ever following their religion. Anxious to save as many people as possible from misguidance and damnation, the Prophet ﷺ took great pains to convince the deniers and was especially gentle and lenient with the People of the Book. Allah explains their denial through pride and self-satisfaction, not a lack of convincing proof. Each group regarded its religion alone as genuine. Neither could therefore be pleased without his accepting its religion. Both faiths had once been genuine, laid down by Allah Himself, yet each community went on to twist its own religion out of shape. In any case, once Islam came as the final Sharīʿah, Allah abrogated every earlier code, so that Islam alone is now acceptable to Him and the only real guidance in this last of the ages. A modern reflection adds that when the Jews and Christians declined, their religion shrank into a set of ethnic customs. Piety lay in belonging to the community and impiety in standing apart from it, and the one test of truth and falsehood became a person's relation to his own group. This sectarian attitude kept them from accepting genuine, unadulterated religion when it was offered. Every preacher of truth has met the same reaction. People ask why God's envoy is not blessed with worldly treasures, because they think only in worldly terms and cannot conceive of a messenger of God without worldly grandeur. How, they wonder, could the great God whose kingdom spans the earth and the furthest heavens choose so ordinary a man? Yet the lives and teachings of the prophets were exactly where God's signs showed themselves: because their words and deeds rested on truth, those words and deeds were His signs in human form. Materialistic minds recognised greatness only through tangible, visible signs. They could not discern these signs in the prophets. Their negative outlook also kept them from grasping the preacher's forceful arguments. True religion can be accepted only by those alive to their own true human nature, not by those who have suppressed it and traded natural religion for artificial dogmas.

**"Say, “Allah’s guidance is the only ˹true˺ guidance.”"**. Say, Muḥammad, that the guidance Allah sent me with is the true guidance, the straight, perfect and comprehensive religion. Islam is the religion Allah has chosen, the Kaʿbah is the direction He has fixed for prayer, and whatever lies outside that guidance is error. Qatādah described this reply as a true argument: Allah taught it to Muḥammad ﷺ and to his Companions, and with it they answered the people of misguidance. Qatādah also related that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to say: "A group of my Ummah will never cease fighting upon the truth, prevailing, unharmed by those who oppose them, until the decree of Allah comes." This ḥadīth is in the Ṣaḥīḥ, narrated from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr. Because of the distorted state of the earlier religions, and above all because they have been abrogated by divine command, the verse equates them with ahwāʾ, the plural of hawā, personal desires, private opinions and baseless conjectures. Since the deniers will not free themselves from their desires and fancies, there is no pleasing them short of accepting their opinions, which a Messenger of Allah can never do. Even a friendlier approach must be answered plainly. The only true guidance comes from Allah. He has already made clear that Islam is now the guidance He accepts.

**"And if you were to follow their desires after ˹all˺ the knowledge that has come to you"**. The particle is that of an oath, and the case is hypothetical: were you to follow the whims to which they are calling you, their religion and their qiblah, after the divine revelation that has come to you, after knowing that Islam is Allah's religion and the Kaʿbah His qiblah. For the Muslim Ummah the verse is a stern warning: once people have knowledge of the Qur'an and the Sunnah, they must not copy the ways and methods of the Jews and Christians, may Allah protect us from such conduct. Though the words are addressed to the Messenger ﷺ, the ruling applies to his whole Ummah. Were he, or anyone who follows him, to act in that way after the clear truth had come, Allah would give them neither protection nor help, which shows the gravity of abandoning the truth and following the people of falsehood.

**"there would be none to protect or help you against Allah"**. No friend (walī) to benefit you or shield you from His punishment, and no helper to defend you against Him. Supposing, purely for the sake of argument, that he were to accept their fancies despite having received the truth by revelation, he would find no one to save him from divine wrath. Other verses establish beyond doubt that Allah is pleased with the Prophet ﷺ and will remain so, and that he can never be an object of divine wrath. Since wrath necessarily follows the acceptance of baseless fancies, and no one can be the object of divine pleasure and divine wrath at once, it follows that he could never, in logic, adopt the opinions of the Jews and Christians. They for their part will never be pleased with him unless he follows their wishes, so no change of heart can be expected from them, and the purport of the verse is to advise the Prophet ﷺ not to worry too much about them. The warning is addressed to him on the surface but is really aimed at the deniers, to make them realise the dire consequences of their vanity. Indeed divine wrath is already visible in the fact that the warning reaches them obliquely rather than directly, a mark of the contempt in which Allah holds them.

### Free of them, through Him

The devotional reading paraphrases the verse as Allah's counsel: do not concern yourself with pleasing the enemies once you have received Our good pleasure (riḍāʾ), for nothing short of following their religions will please them, and that is impossible. So declare yourself quit of them, make your difference plain and show them your opposition. Know, too, that when people lean on whatever they find pleasing, that leaning is what brings endless wretchedness. Do not trouble yourself. Call your community to free themselves from them and from their path, and be for Us and through Us, free of everyone but Us, trusting that Our help is enough, for through Us and from Us is all that you are.

### In short

- Neither the Jews nor the Christians will ever be satisfied with the Prophet ﷺ short of his adopting their religion and qiblah, so he is told to stop seeking their approval.
- Allah's guidance, Islam, is the only guidance now that the earlier religions are distorted and abrogated, which is why they are called mere "desires".
- Following their whims after revelation would leave even a prophet without protector or helper against Allah, a warning aimed through him at his whole Ummah and at the deniers themselves.
- A group of this Ummah will always stand victorious upon the truth until Allah's decree comes.

<<<VERSE 2:121>>>
Verse 121 turns from the hopeless opponents among the People of the Book to those of them who were honest and just. "Those We have given the Book follow it as it should be followed. It is they who ˹truly˺ believe in it. As for those who reject it, it is they who are the losers".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those We have given the Book"**. Scholars differ about who they are. Qatādah, in the narration of Maʿmar through ʿAbd ar-Razzāq, said they are the Jews and Christians, which was also the view of ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam and the choice of Ibn Jarīr. In the narration of Saʿīd, Qatādah said they are the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, in which case the Book is the Qur'an. Ibn ʿAbbās reported that the verse was revealed when forty Christians arrived from Abyssinia and accepted Islam, and it is likewise said to concern a group of Ethiopians who presented themselves to the Prophet ﷺ and embraced Islam. One early gloss identifies them as those among the People of the Book who believed, naming ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and his companions, Baḥīrā the monk, and the Negus with his followers, and it takes the Book to be the Torah. On the first reading, the verse describes those Jews and Christians who, having recognised the truth, affirmed the Prophet ﷺ and accepted Islam, and explains how they were able to change themselves so radically. The Christians, no less than the Jews, had received a Book from Allah, and unlike most of their co-religionists these people gave that Book the reading it deserved.

**"follow it as it should be followed"**. Literally, they recite it with its true recitation (ḥaqqa tilāwatihi), as it was revealed. Abū al-ʿĀliyah relates Ibn Masʿūd's explanation, given under an oath by the One who holds his soul. True recitation permits what the Book permits and forbids what it forbids. It recites the Book as Allah revealed it, without moving its words from their places or giving it an improper interpretation. Ibn ʿAbbās, through Abū Mālik and as-Suddī, said: they permit what it permits, forbid what it forbids, and do not alter its wording. ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb said they are those who, when they recite a verse of mercy, ask Allah for it, and when they recite a verse of punishment, seek refuge with Allah from it. The same is attributed to the Prophet ﷺ, who used to ask for mercy when he recited a verse of mercy and seek refuge when he recited a verse of punishment. ʿUmar is also reported, by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, to have said that reading the Book with the right of its recitation requires that on reaching a description of Paradise one pray to be granted it, and on reaching a description of Hell one seek Allah's protection from it. Reading it rightly also means pronouncing each word correctly and clearly, keeping the fear and love of Allah present in the heart while reading, and resolving to follow divine guidance and obey divine commands. One gloss explains that they describe the Book as it is and do not alter it, setting out its lawful and unlawful, its commands and prohibitions, to whoever asks, acting on what is clear and unambiguous and believing in what is ambiguous. These people distorted neither the words nor the meanings, and neither misinterpreted nor concealed the prophecies of the Prophet's ﷺ coming. They used their intellect to understand the meanings and their will to accept the truth and follow it.

**"It is they who ˹truly˺ believe in it"**. This clause explains the one before. Those among the People of the Book who adhered perfectly to the Books revealed to the earlier prophets will believe in what Allah sent Muḥammad ﷺ with, and in Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'an. Acknowledging the Qur'an affirms their own Books and acts on them. Those Books explicitly foretell the Last Prophet and Allah's final Book. Finding the signs of his coming in their scriptures, they believe in him at once. Allah said: "And had they observed the Torah, the Gospel, and what has been revealed to them from their Lord, they would have been overwhelmed with provisions from above and below" (5:66), and "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O People of the Book! You have nothing to stand on unless you observe the Torah, the Gospel, and what has been revealed to you from your Lord.”" (5:68), which means that if they adhere to the Torah and the Gospel properly, believe in them as they should, and accept what those Books report about Muḥammad ﷺ, that is, his prophethood, the description given of him and the order to follow him, help him and stand by him, then they will be led to truth and righteousness in this life and the next. Allah also said: "˹They are˺ the ones who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, whose description they find in their Torah and the Gospel" (7:157), and "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Believe in this ˹Quran˺, or do not. Indeed, when it is recited to those who were gifted with knowledge before it ˹was revealed˺, they fall upon their faces in prostration, and say, ‘Glory be to our Lord! Surely the promise of our Lord has been fulfilled.’" (17:107-108). These verses show that what Allah promised concerning Muḥammad ﷺ was bound to come about. He said further: "˹As for˺ those ˹faithful˺ to whom We had given the Scripture before this ˹Quran˺, they do believe in it. When it is recited to them, they declare, “We believe in it. This is definitely the truth from our Lord. We had already submitted ˹even˺ before this.” These ˹believers˺ will be given a double reward for their perseverance" (28:52-54), and "And ask those who were given the Scripture and the illiterate ˹people˺, “Have you submitted yourselves ˹to Allah˺?”" (3:20).

**"As for those who reject it, it is they who are the losers"**. Whoever disbelieves in the revealed Book by distorting it, or disbelieves in Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, is among the losers, duped out of both this world and the next and destined for a Fire made everlasting for them. As Allah said: "˹Can these people be compared to˺ those ˹believers˺ who stand on clear proof from their Lord, backed by ˹the Quran as˺ a witness from Him, and preceded by the Book of Moses ˹which was revealed˺ as a guide" (11:17), where whoever of the factions disbelieves in it is promised the Fire. The Ṣaḥīḥ records that the Prophet ﷺ said: "By Him in whose hand is my soul, no one of this community, Jew or Christian, hears of me and then dies without believing in what I was sent with, but will be among the people of the Fire." Those who persist in denial suffer the greatest loss, for they have refused the last revelation and have shown little real belief in their own Books either, having ignored the guidance those Books give on this very point.

### The rightful due of recitation

For the devotional reading, the rightful due of recitation is to recite the Qur'an with burning and need. The heart must be limpid and the belief pure. The tongue must remember, the heart must believe, the spirit must be limpid. The tongue is in remembrance, the heart in grief, the spirit in love. The tongue is in loyalty, the heart in limpidity, the spirit in shame. The tongue is at the work, the heart in the mystery, the spirit in joy. The Pīr of the Ṭarīqah said that in remembrance the servant reaches a point where the tongue arrives at the heart, the heart at the spirit, the spirit at the secret core, and the secret core at the Light. The heart tells the tongue to be silent, the spirit tells the heart to be silent, the secret core tells the spirit to be silent, and then God says to the traveller: "My servant, you have been speaking for a while. Now I will speak, and you listen."

### In short

- "Those given the Book" are either the honest Jews and Christians who read their scriptures rightly and so believed in the Prophet ﷺ, such as the forty converts from Abyssinia, ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and the Negus, or the Companions reading the Qur'an.
- True recitation means permitting what the Book permits, forbidding what it forbids, keeping its words in place, interpreting it rightly, and responding to verses of mercy and punishment with prayer and refuge, as ʿUmar and the Prophet ﷺ did.
- Faithful adherence to the Torah and Gospel leads straight to faith in Muḥammad ﷺ (5:66, 5:68, 7:157, 17:107-108, 28:52-54), while rejecting him after hearing of him is loss in both worlds (11:17).
- Inwardly, the due of recitation is reached when tongue, heart and spirit fall silent and God Himself speaks.
