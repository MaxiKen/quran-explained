<<<VERSE 4:171>>>
The great correction: "O People of the Book! Do not go to extremes regarding your faith; say nothing about Allah except the truth. The Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, was no more than a messenger of Allah and the fulfilment of His Word through Mary and a spirit ˹created by a command˺ from Him. So believe in Allah and His messengers and do not say, “Trinity.” Stop!—for your own good. Allah is only One God. Glory be to Him! He is far above having a son! To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs" (4:171). Having dealt with the Jews, the passage turns to the Christians and corrects, in one verse, the largest doctrinal error ever charged to a prophet's name.

### Phrase by phrase

The opening words of the verse, **"O People of the Book! Do not go to extremes regarding your faith"**, address the Christians, the people of the Gospel, and the Jews with them, for excess is the common factor between them. The Arabic word al-Ghuluww means to cross the limits or transgress, and Imām al-Jaṣṣāṣ said in his Aḥkām al-Qur'ān that excess in faith is crossing the limit set therein. The verse was revealed about certain Christian denominations who claimed that Jesus is the son of Allah, and about the Nestorian Christians of Najrān who claimed that Jesus and the Lord are partners. Over ʿĪsā the Christians went so far past the grade Allah gave him that prophethood was left behind and a god emerged, worshipped as Allah is worshipped.

**"say nothing about Allah except the truth"** means: no lying claim that Allah has a wife or a son. Glorified, praised and honored is He in might, grandeur and greatness, and no deity deserves worship, no Lord exists, but Him.

**"The Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, was no more than a messenger of Allah"** — one of Allah's servants, one of His creatures. To him Allah said Be, and he was, and as a messenger he was sent. Before him passed many messengers, and his mother was a Siddīqah, and the two of them ate food, as the Book states: "They both ate food" (5:75).

**"and the fulfilment of His Word through Mary"** names a word from Allah bestowed on Maryam: He created him with the word Be, carried by Jibrīl to Maryam. By Allah's leave Jibrīl breathed ʿĪsā's life into her, and so he came to be, in place of the ordinary conception of man and woman.

**"and a spirit ˹created by a command˺ from Him"** means created by Allah's command, for he had no father to conceive him. He came to existence through the word that Allah uttered and the life that Allah sent with Jibrīl.

**"So believe in Allah and His messengers"** asks belief that Allah is One and Alone, without son or wife, and know with certainty that ʿĪsā is the servant and messenger of Allah, believing in all the messengers without making a distinction between them.

**"and do not say, “Trinity.”"** forbids elevating ʿĪsā and his mother to be gods with Allah. The three in their saying were a son, a father and a wife, God, Jesus and his mother.

**"Stop!—for your own good"** orders cessation and repentance, for it is better in this world and the Afterlife, and what is better to say is the profession of His Oneness.

**"Allah is only One God. Glory be to Him! He is far above having a son!"** — He is holier than such a claim.

**"To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth"** — all are creatures, property and servants under His control and disposal.

**"And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs"** names the Disposer of affairs, the Guardian and Witness over what is said about ʿĪsā, sufficient as Defender and Lord of all created beings.

### The excess forbidden

Human weakness it is: spot an outstanding aspect in a thing, and exaggerated views of it follow. Polytheism and personality cults alike are the fruit of that excess. The Christians committed excess in believing and honoring ʿĪsā when they went to the extreme of taking him to be God, son of God, or the third of three. The Jews committed the excess of the opposite direction: they did not accept him even as a prophet, and they were audacious enough to impute a false accusation to his revered mother and cast a slur on her parentage. The truth between the two: Masīḥ is a human being born to Maryam and a true messenger of God, and whatever is said beyond that is false and ineffectual, whether loaded with under-estimation or over-estimation.

The excess did not stop at the prophets. Once used to it, both groups extended the attitude to the apostles, followers and deputies of their prophets. Having assigned godhood to a prophet, the Christians invested the prophet's followers with immunity from sin, without even investigating whether such followers genuinely adhered to his teachings or were no more than hereditary religious guides. The result was a leadership astray in itself, adding to the strayings of others. The Qur'ān described this condition exactly: "They took their rabbis and their monks to be their lords besides Allah" (9:31). Having made a prophet a god, they proceeded to worship later-day religious leaders in the name of following the prophet.

Because the deviation was a common scene of the time, the Holy Prophet ﷺ built defences for his own community. In the Musnad of Imām Aḥmad, from a report of Sayyidnā ʿUmar, he said:

> Do not exaggerate in my praise as was done by Christians in the case of ʿĪsā, son of Maryam. Beware, I am only a servant. So call me a servant of Allah and His messenger.

Al-Bukhārī recorded it through Ibn ʿAbbās from ʿUmar, and Ibn al-Madīnī rated it sound and authentically reported. Anas bin Mālik supplies the companion scene: a man hailed the Prophet ﷺ as our master, son of our master, the most righteous of us and son of our most righteous. The reply came:

> O people! Say what you have to say, but do not allow Shayṭān to trick you. I am Muḥammad bin ʿAbdullāh, Allah's servant and Messenger. By Allah, I do not like that you elevate me above the rank that Allah has granted me.

The highest rank he claimed was messengerhood, and raising it higher, to partnership in the attributes of Almighty Allah, is precisely the excess he forbade.

The Christians, having no limit to their disbelief for their ignorance, multiplied creeds that contradict each other: some believe ʿĪsā is Allah, some that he is one in a trinity, some that he is the son of Allah, prompting the wry saying that ten Christians meeting will part as eleven sects. Saʿīd bin Baṭrīq, Patriarch of Alexandria and a noted Christian scholar, recorded around the year four hundred after the Hijrah how a Christian council was convoked under Constantine, the founder of the city bearing his name. There the assembled Christians produced what they named the Great Trust, in reality the Great Treachery. Patriarchs attended by the thousand, two thousand and more, in such disarray that sects multiplied among them, some numbering twenty members, some fifty, some a hundred. Finding upward of three hundred patriarchs agreed on a single notion, the king lent it his backing and made it his creed. Deviant philosopher that he was, Constantine threw his weight behind that party alone: churches rose in its honor, its doctrines were drilled into children at baptism, books multiplied around it, and rival sects felt the king's oppression. Out of another council came the Jacobites, out of a third the Nestorians. All three agreed that ʿĪsā was divine, disputing only how his divinity related to his humanity: unity, or incarnation. Each accuses the others of heresy, while all three stand in disbelief.

### His Word

Three meanings are given for calling ʿĪsā the word of Allah. Imām al-Ghazzālī noted that two factors operate in the birth of a child: the sperm, and the saying of the word Be by Almighty Allah. Since the first factor was out of the question for ʿĪsā, he was called the word of Allah by attribution to the second, coming into existence solely through the word, without material means, the word delivered to Maryam. Others said the expression means the glad tiding of Allah, as when the angels said: "O Maryam, Allah gives you the good news of a word" (3:45). Still others read kalimah in the sense of sign, as elsewhere: "She testified to the word of her Lord as true" (66:12). The likeness is Adam's: "He created him from dust, then said to him: Be, and he was" (3:59). ʿAbdur-Razzāq narrated that Maʿmar said that Qatādah said the verse means He created him with the word Be. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded that Aḥmad bin Sinān al-Wāsiṭī heard Shādh bin Yaḥyā say that the word itself ʿĪsā was not. His existence arose from the word, not as the word. Al-Bukhārī recorded that ʿUbādah bin aṣ-Ṣāmit said that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> If anyone testifies that none has the right to be worshipped but Allah alone, who has no partners, and that Muḥammad is His servant and Messenger, and that ʿĪsā is Allah's servant and Messenger and His Word which He bestowed on Maryam and a spirit created by Him, and that Paradise is true and Hell is true, then Allah will admit him into Paradise with the deeds he performed.

Another narration has the entry made through whichever of the eight doors of Paradise he wishes, and Muslim records it too.

### A spirit from Him

Why is ʿĪsā called a spirit, and what does from Him mean? Several readings are reported. Some explain by lexical usage: in recognized usage spirit carries the sense of essence, lending a thing the effect of pristine purity. Born of no father, the outcome of nothing but the will of Allah and the word Be, ʿĪsā was blessed with the most perfect degree of purity, and the attribution to Allah is there to hold him in esteem. It is like attributing mosques to Allah as the mosques of Allah, or the Holy Kaʿbah as the House of Allah, or a worshipfully obedient person as ʿAbd Allāh, the servant of Allah. In the same way the Holy Prophet ﷺ is referred to in sūrat Banī Isrāʾīl: "Glory to Him who carried His servant" (17:1), the attribution reflecting honor. Others said his purpose was to infuse spiritual life into dead hearts, as the spirit is the cause of physical life, and in this sense the word is used of the Qur'ān as well: "thus We have revealed to you a spirit of Our command" (42:52). Others said spirit means secret: being, through his unusual birth, a sign and secret of Allah, he was called the spirit of Allah. Others supply the omitted word: the possessor of a spirit from Him, the distinction being that Allah turned his attribution toward Himself though all rational beings possess a spirit. And yet others read the word as the blowing of breath: Jibrīl, as commanded by Allah, blew upon the collar of Sayyidah Maryam, and that became the conception, as the verse points: "then We blew Our spirit in her" (21:91). None of these readings makes ʿĪsā a part of Allah or a divine person. One that possesses a spirit is compound, and God transcends being compound.

The classic repartee makes the logic plain. Rūḥ al-Maʿānī preserves a scene from the court of the Caliph Hārūn ar-Rashīd. A Christian physician there took on the scholar ʿAlī ibn al-Ḥusayn al-Wāqidī in debate, claiming the Qur'ān itself makes ʿĪsā a part of Allah, and reading this very verse with its words "a spirit from Him". The reply came from the same Book: "He has subjected to you all that is in the heavens and all that is in the earth; it is all from Him" (45:13), where everything is attributed to Allah by the same from Him. If a spirit from Him means part of Him, then everything in the heavens and the earth is a part of Allah. Silenced, the Christian physician chose to become a Muslim. So it is with the she-camel of Allah and the command to "sanctify My House for those who circumambulate it" (22:26): the attribution honors the thing, and an authentic hadith says "I will enter on my Lord in His Home". The honor is real, the partness is not.

### One God, no son

The verse closes the argument with ownership. Everything was created by Him, everything lies under His mastery, everyone is His servant: who then could be His partner, His associate, His son? Almighty Allah is the dispenser of all needs, the sole caretaker of everyone's wants, universally and perennially sufficient, needing nobody. "He is the originator of the heavens and the earth. How can He have children" (6:101), and of those who say the Most Gracious has begotten a son, the Book says they have brought forth a terrible thing, up to His word "Alone" (19:95). Sūrat al-Māʾidah says it at both ends: "Surely, in disbelief are they who say that Allah is the Messiah, son of Maryam" (5:17), and at the Day of Resurrection Allah will ask: "O ʿĪsā, son of Maryam! Did you say unto men: worship me?" (5:116). The Christians of the trinity held three separate principles: one sect that Masīḥ is God himself appearing in the world, one that he is the son of God, one a unity in trinity of father, son and Mary, with a further group substituting the Holy Spirit, the rūḥ al-Quds, for Maryam. All are addressed in the Qur'ān, separately and jointly, and told the one truth. The inward reading adds: they followed their own opinion, describing what they worshipped in the likeness of created beings, and contradicted themselves with one that is three and three that is one. Persistence in the false only deepens the falseness.

### The fences against excess

The Prophet ﷺ set the fences in practice. It appears in hadith that at the ḥajj he asked Sayyidnā ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās to go and collect pebbles to throw at the Jamarāt. He returned with average-sized pebbles, which the Prophet ﷺ liked very much, saying twice: like these, like these. Then he said:

> It is your duty to avoid excess in faith, for communities before you were destroyed because of being excessive in their faith.

Three rules come out of this. The sunnah limit for pebbles thrown at the Jamarāt is average size, for big rocks are excess. The legal limit of everything is what the Holy Prophet ﷺ determined by word and deed, and going beyond it is ghulūw. Precisely defined, excess in faith is the crossing of the limit set for doing something.

The same principle governs the material world. Greed for luxury beyond need is blameworthy, but the Prophet ﷺ, while prohibiting worldly greed, set the proper limits by word and deed: he declared marriage his way, explained the blessings of children, prescribed living well with the family and fulfilling everyone's rights, called working for one's family an obligation after the obligation, and stressed business, agriculture, industry and labor. The establishment of an Islamic state and the promotion of a system governed by Islam he declared part of the mandate of prophethood. Engagement in these pursuits within the limits of need is not gross love of the material. The Jews and Christians, not realizing this, involved themselves in monasticism, which the Qur'ān refuted: they took to ways not prescribed for them, seeking Allah's pleasure, "then they failed to fulfill the conditions of what they had themselves imposed" (57:27).

The same principle guards the sunnah against invention. Every bidʿah, every self-promoted innovation in established religion, departs from the limits of moderation he demarcated in worship and in dealings, and he said:

> Every bidʿah is straying, and every straying ends in Jahannam.

Bidʿah is everything assumed to be part of religion that is not in his word and deed, clearly or by hint. Shah Waliyyullāh said Islam condemns it as a serious offense because it opens the doors to alteration in religion, and in his famous book, Hujjatullāh al-Balīghah, he detailed the circumstances under which attempts have been made to inject alterations into Islam, and the concern of the Sharīʿah, which installed defensive mechanisms on every such door. That is what happened to the earlier communities: they added things to what their Book said and their prophet taught, generation after generation, until the true religion could no longer be told apart from the additions.

The disease has two faces among Muslims. One group holds there is no such thing as a religious teacher or shaykh: the Book of Allah is sufficient for us, they are men, so are we. Every ambitious pseudo-intellectual, unlettered in Arabic and unfamiliar with the exegesis given by the Prophet ﷺ and the early scholars, then looks at a translation and is hoisted as a scholar of the Qur'ān. Had a book without a teacher been enough, Allah could have made written copies appear in every home, and no prophet needed teaching. No one becomes a physician by reading translations of medical books, no engineer by browsing engineering texts, no master tailor or chef from books on sewing and cooking. The system needs teaching and learning under a teacher. The other face takes just about anyone as a guide, almost blindly, without checking intellectual achievement, personal behavior, concern for people, or whether the teaching is against the Qur'ān and sunnah. The Sharīʿah marks out the middle course: learn the Book of Allah from the men of Allah, and know the men of Allah by the Book of Allah.

### In short

- Excess in faith destroyed the communities before: the Christians raised a messenger to godhood, the Jews dropped him below his rank, and both extended the error to the followers of their prophets.
- ʿĪsā is a messenger, a word bestowed on Maryam by Be, and a spirit created by command, an honor of attribution and not a part of Allah, as the court of Hārūn ar-Rashīd heard.
- The trinity, in all its sects and councils, is told to cease, for Allah is one God, far above a son, the owner of the heavens and the earth and the sufficient Trustee of Affairs.
- The fences stand: average pebbles at the Jamarāt, limits set by word and deed, no monasticism, no bidʿah, and teachers recognized by the Book they carry.
