<<<VERSE 2:236>>>
Divorce before consummation does not remove every obligation of kindness: "There is no blame if you divorce women before the marriage is consummated or the dowry is settled." In the case where neither intercourse nor a fixed marriage payment has occurred, the husband gives a suitable parting provision instead. The amount follows his means. The wife's disappointment is not treated as something that can simply be ignored.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There is no blame if you divorce women before the marriage is consummated or the dowry is settled."** A marriage contract may end before intercourse. The expression about touching is understood as intercourse by Ibn 'Abbas, Tawus, Ibrahim and Al-Hasan Al-Basri. Two readings, tamassuhunna and tumassuhunna, express this meaning. The sentence removes blame for ending the marriage in this situation, but the following command preserves a financial duty.

**"But give them a ˹suitable˺ compensation—the rich according to his means and the poor according to his."** The provision is called mut'ah or mata', something given for the woman's benefit and comfort. It is not a fixed mahr, the agreed marriage payment, in the case where none was specified. Both affluent and poor husbands must give, but they are not required to give identical amounts.

**"A reasonable compensation is an obligation on the good-doers"** The provision must accord with Islamic law and recognised decency. The wording makes it a duty, not merely a gift that a husband may choose to omit. Those who do good show their obedience in dealing fairly with someone whose marriage has ended.

### How do the different payment cases fit together?

There are four situations in the legal discussion. Where no payment was fixed and intercourse did not occur, this verse requires the parting provision rather than a dowry. Where a payment was fixed but intercourse did not occur, the following verse requires half that payment. Where the payment was fixed and intercourse occurred, the full amount is due. Where intercourse occurred without a previously fixed amount, mahr al-mithl is due: a full payment comparable to that customarily received by women in her immediate family.

These distinctions prevent the rule of compensation from being mistaken for the rule of half a specified payment. They also show that a marriage without a prearranged amount is not therefore a marriage without any possible financial claims.

The discussion mentions permission to divorce before consummation or before payment of a deferred dowry. Deferral is not an excuse to erase whatever claim applies. The particular gift rule here concerns the situation without consummation and without a fixed sum.

### What might a suitable gift be?

The verse sets no single sum. Ibn 'Abbas describes a set of clothes as the minimum form of provision. Another description lists a shirt, head covering and outer wrap. The amount should be appropriate, with the wealthy urged not to behave as though they have only a poor person's resources.

Examples illustrate generosity rather than impose a uniform tariff. Hasan gave twenty thousand dirhams in such a case, while Qadi Shurayh gave five hundred. Their gifts show how means and liberality can vary. A man should not turn these illustrations into a demand that every poorer husband imitate the larger sum.

Al-Bukhari records from Sahl bin Sa'd and Abu Usayd that the Prophet ﷺ married Umaymah bint Sharahil. When she was brought to him, she did not welcome his extending his hand. He directed Abu Usayd to provide for her and give two garments. The ending of the relationship was accompanied by material kindness.

One gloss describes this compensation as standing in place of an unspecified dowry and connects its duty with belief in Allah's oneness. It also uses a comparison with payment for prostitution, calling the lawful provision something beyond that degraded exchange. The comparison is part of the supplied gloss. It does not turn a marriage or its ending into the hiring of sexual services.

### Why does separation still call for comfort?

An inward reading observes that a human relationship may end without the separation itself being forbidden. It contrasts this with the servant's relationship to Allah, from whom one must not choose estrangement. Permitted human companionship is not always required to continue in every circumstance.

Yet the marriage has linked the spouses' names and reputations. Separation is painful even before consummation. Where a payment has been fixed, half belongs to the woman under the next verse. Where none has been fixed, the contract still calls for the compensatory gift. Leaving her to bear the bitterness of separation with no comfort is rejected.

The practical and inward meanings meet here. Permission to end a marriage is not permission to act coldly about its consequences. A suitable gift acknowledges that the woman is a person affected by the decision, not merely a contract whose file has been closed.

### In short

- A marriage may end before intercourse and before a payment has been fixed.
- In that situation, a suitable parting provision is obligatory, with its amount following the husband's means.
- The gift rule differs from half a fixed dowry or the full payment due after consummation.
- Lawful separation should still recognise disappointment and offer comfort.

<<<VERSE 2:237>>>
Where the marriage payment was fixed but intercourse has not occurred, the financial rule is clear: "pay half of the dowry, unless the wife graciously waives it or the husband graciously pays in full." The verse then calls both spouses beyond insisting on the last measure of their claims. They should remember kindness even while the marriage is ending. Allah sees the generosity as well as the legal calculation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And if you divorce them before consummating the marriage but after deciding on a dowry,"** The mahr is the agreed marriage payment. This case differs from the preceding verse, where no amount had been fixed. It concerns the actual specified payment, not another parting gift substituted for it.

**"pay half of the dowry,"** Half of the fixed amount belongs to the wife. The discussion describes this as the agreed rule among scholars. The other half is not owed to her under the ordinary calculation for this case.

**"unless the wife graciously waives it or the husband graciously pays in full."** The wife may freely release the claim that is hers. The husband may instead leave her with the whole payment. Neither form of generosity is to be manufactured through pressure. A legal disagreement over the person described as holding the marriage bond is considered below.

**"Graciousness is closer to righteousness."** At-taqwa means mindfulness of Allah expressed in responsible conduct. Giving up a personal claim can be nearer to that quality than insisting on its full recovery. Ibn 'Abbas and Ash-Sha'bi explain the merit as belonging to the one who pardons.

**"And do not forget kindness among yourselves."** The ending of the marriage does not cancel the call to liberality. A wife may give up her half, or a husband may leave her the whole. The instruction addresses the way both treat one another, not only the side with the stronger bargaining position.

**"Surely Allah is All-Seeing of what you do"** Allah sees the deed and will recompense it. Generosity that another person fails to recognise is not lost before Him. Conversely, an outwardly compliant payment does not conceal a purpose of humiliating the recipient.

### Why is paying the whole described as waiving a right?

The explanation considers the custom of paying the mahr when the marriage takes place. If the full sum has already been handed over, a preconsummation divorce gives the husband a claim to recover half. Leaving that half with the woman is therefore literally relinquishing something he may take back.

If it has not yet been paid, the same generosity appears as paying the full sum rather than only the required half. The financial result is the same. The word for forgiving does not mean the husband has forgiven a sin committed by the wife. It describes his releasing his own claim.

The wife's waiver concerns her own entitlement to half. As-Suddi reports this explanation through Abu Salih from Ibn 'Abbas. Numerous other authorities are given with it, including Shurayh, Sa'id bin Musayyib, 'Ikrimah, Mujahid, Ash-Sha'bi, Al-Hasan, Nafi', Qatadah and Jabir bin Zayd. Also named are 'Ata' Al-Khurasani, Ad-Dahhak, Az-Zuhri, Muqatil bin Hayyan, Ibn Sirin and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas. The rule preserves the difference between a freely waived right and a right someone else simply refuses to pay.

### Who holds the marriage bond?

The main explanation identifies the husband. Ibn Abu Hatim records through 'Amr bin Shu'ayb from his grandfather a saying of the Prophet ﷺ identifying him as the holder of the marriage tie. Ibn Marduwyah also transmits it. Ibn Jarir selects this interpretation. Another account of the report appears in Darqutni and is also connected with 'Ali and Ibn 'Abbas.

The reasoning is that the husband's talaq, pronouncement of divorce, determines whether this marriage continues or ends under the arrangement discussed here. The wife's guardian, or wali, therefore cannot simply dispose of her payment without her permission. Her property claim does not become his to give away.

A different interpretation, attributed to Ibn 'Abbas, identifies the legal guardian where the bride is a minor. That is a distinct reading of the expression. It should remain visible alongside the stronger emphasis on the husband, rather than be silently treated as though no alternative was given.

The discussion's statement that a woman does not pronounce talaq upon her husband concerns this particular allocation of the direct divorce power. It does not erase the other means of lawful release already addressed in the surrounding passage.

### How does kindness extend beyond the minimum?

Ibn Jarir reports the view that the invitation to remit addresses both men and women. Mujahid, An-Nakha'i, Ad-Dahhak, Muqatil bin Hayyan, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas and Thawri explain the liberality in terms of the woman's releasing her half or the man's giving the whole. Sa'id describes it as kindness and generosity.

The legal rule supplies a fair settlement, while goodwill invites each side to consider giving rather than taking. The wife need not turn separation into a struggle for every possible payment, and the husband need not make the permitted deduction his highest aim. This is encouragement to voluntary generosity, not a new rule cancelling the half-payment obligation.

The broader lesson concerns social conduct in friendship and estrangement alike. A community is strengthened when people are interested in what they can give one another rather than only what they can extract. The difficult moment of divorce is one place where that principle is tested.

### What inward stages of righteousness are described?

An inward reading joins pardon to mindfulness of Allah and mindfulness to Paradise, recalling (43:35), which assigns the next world to the mindful. It describes a beginning and an end of this generosity.

At the beginning, a person recognises what another owes him, then freely lets the claim go. This is described as a station of submission and the conduct of worshippers. At the further stage, he recognises what he owes the other person and gives that person preference over himself. Even when hurt by disloyalty, he answers without making retaliation his aim.

This further stage is associated with tawhid, the oneness of Allah, and the sincerity of the truthful. A poetic image reverses the usual expectations: when ill, the lover goes to tend the beloved, and when wronged, goes to offer an apology. Its purpose is to picture selfless generosity, not to replace the legal rights that the verse has just defined.

A saying of the Prophet ﷺ expresses the same moral direction: maintain ties with someone who cuts them, pardon someone who wrongs, and give to someone who withholds. No collector or grading is added where this account does not provide one. The instruction makes kindness more than a reward for being treated kindly first.

### In short

- Divorce before intercourse requires half the fixed marriage payment.
- The wife may waive her claim, or the husband may voluntarily leave her the full sum.
- The main explanation identifies the husband as the holder of the marriage bond, with an alternative concerning a minor's guardian also recorded.
- Kindness and voluntary generosity remain valuable even when the relationship is ending.
