<<<VERSE 7:152>>>
Allah warns the calf-worshippers who persisted in their worship of a grim end in the Hereafter and a disgraced life in this world: "Those who worshipped the calf will certainly be afflicted with Allah’s wrath as well as disgrace in the life of this world. This is how We reward those who invent falsehood". The verse covers them and whoever follows their example. They displeased their Lord and did not act on His command.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who worshipped the calf"**: those who took the calf as a god.

**"will certainly be afflicted with Allah’s wrath"**: the wrath that struck the children of Israel for worshipping the calf. Allah did not accept their repentance until some of them, those who had not worshipped the calf, killed others who had. Sūrat al-Baqarah tells this story (2:54). Their punishment was the command to slay themselves.

**"as well as disgrace in the life of this world."** This is the disgrace and humiliation the Jews suffered in this world. One explanation names the poll tax imposed on them. Another says abasement was stamped on them until the Day of Resurrection.

**"This is how We reward those who invent falsehood"**: those who invent lies against Allah, through idolatry and in other ways. As Allah repaid them, so He repays every such inventor.

### Who are "those who invent falsehood"?

The phrase covers everyone who invents an innovation in religion. The disgrace of inventing an innovation and defying Allah's message settles in the heart and from there spreads to the shoulders. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said that the disgrace of innovation weighs on their shoulders even as they gallop on their mules or trot on their workhorses.

Ayyūb as-Sakhtiyānī narrated that Abū Qilābah al-Jarmī said of this verse: "By Allah! It applies to everyone who invents a lie, until the Day of Resurrection." Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah said that every inventor of an innovation in religion will taste disgrace. Those who bring new things into the Sharīʿah commit the sin of fabrication and deserve this punishment. Imām Mālik also concluded from this verse that such people deserve Allah's wrath in the Hereafter and disgrace in this life.

### Why is the calf called a falsehood?

The Israelites' deed is called iftirāʾ, a false attribution. They did this wrong in the name of the truth. Theirs was not a rejection of the truth but an exploitation of it for worldly gain, justified in religious terms. Like the idolaters, they claimed that Allah had taken bodily form in the idol they had made with their own hands, so worshipping it was as good as worshipping Him. Sāmirī also invented a dream. In it, he claimed, he had seen Gabriel and taken a handful of earth from his horse's footprint, which he put into the calf, making it give out a sound. Sāmirī and his companions made up falsehoods and attributed them to Allah. When the People of the Book commit such irreligious acts in the name of religion, they bring Allah's wrath upon themselves, and they are punished in this life as well as the Hereafter.

### Are sins punished in this world?

Part of the punishment for some sins comes in this life. That is what happened to Sāmirī. Mūsā (Moses) ordered him to live apart from the people, touching no one and touched by no one. For the rest of his life he lived in desolate seclusion, wandering among animals, and no human came near him. Al-Qurṭubī reports from Qatādah that his punishment from Allah was a fever that struck both him and anyone he touched or who touched him. The author of Rūḥ al-Bayān observed that his descendants still inherit signs of this illness.

### In short

- The calf-worshippers met Allah's wrath, the command to slay themselves, and lasting disgrace in this world.
- Inventing falsehood includes every innovation in religion, and its disgrace weighs on the innovator wherever he goes.
- The calf was a false attribution to Allah, a wrong done in the name of truth.
- Sāmirī's lonely life, untouched by anyone, shows that some sins are punished in this world.

<<<VERSE 7:153>>>
Allah opens the door of repentance even after the worst of sins: "But those who commit evil, then repent and become ˹true˺ believers, your Lord will certainly be All-Forgiving, Most-Merciful". Allah tells His servants that He accepts repentance for any sin, even shirk, kufr, hypocrisy and disobedience.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But those who commit evil"**: those who do wrong by joining partners with Allah and committing sins.

**"then repent"**: then turn back after their idolatry, or after their transgressions, and give them up.

**"and become ˹true˺ believers"**: and believe in Allah, affirming His Oneness. They repent from shirk to faith in Him alone and from disobedience to good deeds, leaving every kind of sin while seeking His forgiveness.

**"your Lord"**: addressed to Mūsā (Moses), or, by another view, to Muhammad ﷺ, the Messenger of Repentance and the Prophet of Mercy.

**"will certainly be All-Forgiving, Most-Merciful"**: after they have repented and believed, after that evil deed, He overlooks and erases what they have done and shows them mercy. Sūrat al-Baqarah ends the story the same way: "That is best for you in the sight of your Creator.” Then He accepted your repentance. Surely He is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful" (2:54).

### How were the Israelites forgiven?

The verse speaks of those who repented after their prophet's warnings and met the hard condition set for their forgiveness, that they kill one another. They did so as Allah had commanded. Mūsā then called them and told them that Allah had accepted their repentance. Those killed were granted martyrdom, and those who survived were forgiven.

Another account says Mūsā told the leaders of each tribe to find those who had first started the wrongdoing. Once found, the guilty were executed by men of their own tribe. Only those who were deeply ashamed, admitted their guilt and sincerely repented were spared. The Israelites were punished by their own people. Such punishment is sometimes carried out by other peoples, especially when they can also inflict humiliation and disgrace.

### What is true repentance?

Whoever falls into sin and then repents and sets his belief right, Allah forgives in His mercy, however grave the deed. So it is wise to turn to Allah at once after every sin.

Real shame for a sin is the essence of tawbah, repentance. That deep shame guarantees that, in the depths of his heart, a person resolves never to do the deed again. When a sinner shows this shame and proves his resolve, it is as if he had embraced faith anew. After a time of backsliding, he comes back into the fold of Allah's religion.

### Ibn Masʿūd and the verse

Someone once asked ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd about a man who had committed fornication with a woman and afterwards married her, as Ibn Abī Ḥātim reported. Ibn Masʿūd recited this verse ten times, neither permitting nor forbidding it.

### In short

- Allah accepts repentance for any sin, even shirk and kufr, from those who turn back and truly believe.
- The Israelites' repentance was accepted after the hard command was carried out, and the slain were counted as martyrs.
- Sincere shame and a firm resolve never to return are the heart of repentance, and they restore a person to faith.
