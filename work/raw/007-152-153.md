<<<VERSE 7:152>>>
The sentence on the calf and its makers comes down hard. "Those who worshipped the calf will certainly be afflicted with Allah’s wrath as well as disgrace in the life of this world." Then the wider rule is stated. "This is how We reward those who invent falsehood"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those who worshipped the calf will certainly be afflicted with Allah’s wrath"** names the punishment that followed the sin. The wrath that came on the Children of Israel for the worship of the calf was not lifted at once. It stayed until some of them put others of them to death, and the ones who did the killing were those who had kept away from the calf. That story is told in full in Al-Baqarah. They were told to turn in repentance to the One who had made them. They were told also to put the guilty among them to death, since that would be better for them with their Maker. Their turning was accepted after that (2:54).

**"as well as disgrace in the life of this world"** adds to the wrath something that was carried in this world. The words speak of the humiliation and the shame that fell on the people in their own lifetime. One reading takes it as the abasement that was stamped upon them until the Day of Resurrection. Another names the capitation tax by which the humiliation was imposed on them.

**"This is how We reward those who invent falsehood"** turns the matter from one nation to a rule. The sentence is taken to cover everyone who invents a new thing in the religion. A person who does that has added to the law of Allah what Allah did not send. The disgrace that follows such an invention is described as being placed in the heart of the man first, and from the heart it moves to his shoulders. Al-Hasan Al-Basri said of this that the shame of an invented practice sits on a man's shoulders. It stays there whether he rides out on a mule or gallops along on a horse kept for work.

Ayyub As-Sakhtiyani carried from Abu Qilabah Al-Jarmi the statement that, by Allah, this verse covers every person who invents a lie. It will go on doing so until the Day of Resurrection. Sufyan bin 'Uyaynah said that every person who invents an innovation in the religion will taste disgrace.

### Where the punishment was seen in this life

A part of the punishment of certain sins is given in this very life, and the case of As-Samiri is given as the example of it. Musa commanded him to live apart from the people, so that he would not touch anyone and no one would touch him. For the rest of his life he lived a deserted life, walking about among the animals, and no human being came near him. Al-Qurtubi carried from the companion Qatadah a report about the punishment of the man. If he touched anyone, or anyone touched him, fever came on both of them. The author of Ruh al-Bayan observed that the marks of that illness are still found in his descendants.

The rule drawn from the verse was applied by the scholars in the widest way. Sufyan bin 'Uyaynah said that those who invent new things in the shariah have committed the sin of fabrication and deserve that punishment. Imam Malik took from this verse that whoever invents new practices in the shariah deserves the wrath of Allah in the world to come and disgrace in this one.

### Why the act is called a fabrication

The act of the calf is named as a fabrication because of the way it was done, and not only because of what it was. The people had done a wrongful thing in the name of the truth. Their case was not a plain rejection of the truth, but the use of the truth in order to gain benefit. They used to justify such acts in religious language. Like the idol worshippers they said that God had taken form in the idol they had made with their own hands. On their own account, worshipping it was the same as worshipping God.

There is more of the same in the account of As-Samiri. He made up a dream, saying that he had seen the angel Gabriel in it. In the dream he had taken up a handful of earth from the footprint of the horse and placed it in the statue, and that, he said, was what caused the sound to come out of its mouth. So the maker and the people with him invented falsehoods and attributed them to God, and that is what fabricating against God means.

### The shame that is at the heart of repentance

How the punishment fell on the people themselves is also recorded. Musa told the leaders of each tribe to search out the ones who had been responsible for the act. The guilty were found and put to death by their own tribesmen. Only those who were greatly ashamed of what they had done, admitted their guilt and repented sincerely were not punished. So the Israelites were punished for their wrongdoing by their own people. Punishment of that kind is sometimes given through other people or other communities, above all where the shame of it can be made plain to them.

Being truly ashamed of sin is the essence of repentance, and that shame is what makes the verse stand. The strong feeling of shame is the guarantee that the one who repents resolves in the deepest part of his heart never to go back to the act. When a person who has sinned shows that shame and proves his determination not to repeat the sin, it is as if he has taken up the faith afresh. After a time of slipping back, he returns into the fold of the religion of God, and the door stays open while the shame is real.

### In short

- The worshippers of the calf were struck with the wrath of Allah and with disgrace. Their repentance was not accepted until some of their own people put the guilty to death, as the account in Al-Baqarah records.
- The wrath is followed by humiliation in this life, and the words are read as the abasement carried by the people, or as the capitation tax imposed on them.
- The rule at the end covers everyone who invents a lie about the religion. Al-Hasan Al-Basri said the shame of it would weigh on the shoulders of such a person however he travelled.
- Ayyub As-Sakhtiyani carried from Abu Qilabah Al-Jarmi that the verse holds for every inventor of a lie until the Day of Resurrection. Sufyan bin 'Uyaynah said every inventor of an innovation will taste disgrace.
- As-Samiri was punished in this life by separation from people and by fever on contact. Al-Qurtubi reported it from Qatadah, while the author of Ruh al-Bayan said the marks of the illness remained in his line.
- The calf is called a fabrication because the people dressed their act in the name of the truth, and the shame which they felt afterwards is the essence of their repentance.

<<<VERSE 7:153>>>
The door is held open in the same breath. "But those who commit evil, then repent and become ˹true˺ believers, your Lord will certainly be All-Forgiving, Most-Merciful"

### Phrase by phrase

**"But those who commit evil, then repent and become ˹true˺ believers"** describes the change the verse is speaking of, and verse 153 names it in two parts. The first part is tawbah, the turning back. The second is the entry into belief. The people meant are the ones who turned back from their sin after their prophet had warned them. They met the hard condition that had been laid on them for forgiveness. It is also taken to cover everyone who does wrong. One reading names the wrong as the joining of partners with Allah, and the repentance as the turning away from that to the oneness of Allah and the belief in Him alone. Repentance in that reading carries a second turn with it, away from disobedience and towards good deeds, with the giving up of sin and the seeking of forgiveness from the Lord.

The words say that turning back is enough, whatever the sin has been. Allah tells His servants that He accepts the repentance of the one who turns to Him. That holds for any sin at all, whether it is shirk or kufr or hypocrisy or plain disobedience. That includes the worship of the calf, which the verse before this one had punished, and it includes the sins that come after it.

**"your Lord will certainly be All-Forgiving, Most-Merciful"** is the promise that closes the verse. The two names are placed after the description of the sin on purpose. So no one reads the promise as though the sin were small, and no one reads the sin as though the door were shut. The Prophet ﷺ is called in this connection the Messenger of Repentance and the Prophet of Mercy, since that invitation stands at the front of his message.

### What the two verses do together

The two verses put a punishment and a promise side by side, and the pairing is what the passage is teaching. The first says that the people who made a god out of their ornaments were struck with wrath and with shame. Their repentance passed through a hard command before it was accepted. The second says that those who sin and afterwards turn back and believe find their Lord forgiving and merciful. The seriousness of the deed is not softened, and the mercy that follows a real turning is not withdrawn.

**What the words say** about the order of the two things is worth keeping in view. The turning comes before the promise, and the belief comes with the turning. Nothing in the sentence lets a man hold on to the sin and take the mercy as a cover for it. What is named is the one who did the evil and then left it, and the Book measures the sincerity of that leaving by the shame and the resolve it produces.

### The report of Ibn Mas'ud

Abdullah bin Mas'ud was asked about a man who commits fornication with a woman and afterwards marries her. He recited this verse in answer, and he recited it ten times, neither permitting the marriage in that case nor forbidding it. The report shows how the companions handled a question when they did not wish to give a ruling on it. It also shows the place this verse held in their minds as the answer to the question of a sin followed by a change of life.

### After the killing, a people forgiven

What happened among the Israelites after the command is given in the account of the verse that comes before this one. The people met the condition that had been laid on them, and Musa called them together and told them that Allah had accepted their repentance. Those of them who were killed in the affair were given the rank of martyrs, and those who survived were forgiven. The matter ends there, and it teaches what the Book says plainly in this verse. A person who becomes caught up in a sinful act and then turns to Allah and puts his belief right is forgiven by the mercy of his Lord. The weight of the deed does not shut the door on him. It is therefore wise to turn to Allah at once after every sinful act, and not to let the delay build a wall where the verse has left a door.

### In short

- The verse promises forgiveness and mercy to those who committed evil, then repented and believed, and it follows the punishment of the calf in the verse before it.
- Repentance here is read as turning from joining partners with Allah to His oneness, and from disobedience to good deeds, with the sin given up and forgiveness sought.
- The two names of forgiveness and mercy are placed after the description of the sin, so that neither the sin is treated as small nor the door as shut.
- Abdullah bin Mas'ud recited this verse ten times when asked about a man who fornicated with a woman and then married her, neither permitting nor forbidding the marriage.
- The Israelites who met the hard condition had their repentance accepted. Those killed were counted as martyrs, and those who lived were forgiven. The verse holds out the same door for every sin afterwards.
