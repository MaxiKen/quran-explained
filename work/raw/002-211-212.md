<<<VERSE 2:211>>>
The Children of Israel provide an example of rejecting guidance after receiving clear signs. "Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We have given them." The question rebukes ingratitude. It also introduces a general warning about exchanging Allah's favour for disbelief.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We have given them."** The Prophet ﷺ is instructed to question the children of Jacob about the manifest proofs they received. Allah honoured them with religion and gave commands and prohibitions in the time of Moses. The question requires acknowledgment, not fresh proof of something doubtful.

**"And whoever trades Allah's favour—after receiving it—˹for disbelief˺"** The favour includes signs that lead towards guidance, Allah's religion and His Scripture. Rejecting these after they arrive reverses their intended use. The explanation also includes refusing the guidance brought by Muhammad ﷺ.

**"should know that Allah is indeed severe in punishment"** Allah has power to punish denial. The warning is not restricted to one people or one incident. Losing a blessing after its value has become clear is itself a severe consequence.

### Which signs had they received?

Moses brought signs that included his shining hand, the divided sea and water flowing from the rock he struck. Clouds sheltered the people from intense heat. Manna and quails supplied their food. Such gifts testified to the Creator and to the truth of His messenger.

Yet many preferred disbelief to faith and turned from these blessings. The grammatical discussion treats "how many" as an interrogative expression. It links the second object of the command to ask with the object and specification of what Allah gave.

The inward reading emphasises the purpose of asking. The Messenger ﷺ did not need to resolve doubt about an established proof. Those addressed needed to admit what the proof had already shown.

### What happened when blessings were rejected?

The account links particular favours with particular acts of ingratitude. They received the Torah but denied what deserved acceptance, and were threatened by the mountain above them. They heard Allah's word but entertained doubts, and lightning struck them.

The parted sea delivered them from Pharaoh. Instead of gratitude, they turned to the calf and suffered killing. Disobedience spoiled the food supplied as manna and quails. Their further distaste ended that provision and left them to grow food through their own labour.

Prophets raised among them should have brought satisfaction and guidance. Killing those prophets was followed by loss of power. Several of these episodes appear earlier in the surah. The sequence shows repeated rejection, not a shortage of signs.

### Why does the warning extend beyond them?

The same principle is applied to the disbelievers of Quraysh in (14:28-29). There, ingratitude for Allah's gifts brings a people and their followers to ruin. Receiving favour does not protect anyone who deliberately exchanges it for denial.

The devotional reading dwells on withdrawal of grace. Its worth may become apparent only after it is taken away, when those who rejected it mourn what they cannot recover. The poetic image is of an abandoned beloved who can no longer be found when sought again.

### In short

- The Children of Israel received many signs through Moses but repeatedly rejected their meaning.
- The account connects rejected favours with punishment and loss.
- The warning applies to anyone who exchanges received guidance for disbelief.
- Losing grace can reveal its worth too late.

<<<VERSE 2:212>>>
Love of wealth and comfort can turn into contempt for people who put faith first. "The life of this world has been made appealing to the disbelievers, and they mock the believers." The verse reverses that apparent superiority: the mindful will stand above their mockers at judgment, and provision belongs to Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The life of this world has been made appealing to the disbelievers,"** The comforts of worldly life attract those satisfied with them. They accumulate wealth but refuse the spending that would earn Allah's pleasure. The explanation identifies the disbelievers of Makkah, including Abū Jahl and his circle.

**"and they mock the believers."** Poor believers are ridiculed for not placing worldly gain first. Those mentioned include Salmān al-Fārisī, Bilāl ibn Rabāḥ al-Ḥabashī, ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir and Ṣuhayb al-Rūmī. Their spending seeks their Lord's pleasure rather than the approval of wealthy mockers.

**"Those who are mindful ˹of Allah˺ will rank above them on the Day of Judgment."** Those who reject kufr and shirk will have the better state at resurrection. The believers enter Jannah, while the disbelievers enter Jahannam. Their final ranks overturn the judgments made from wealth alone.

**"And Allah provides for whoever He wills without limit"** Provision may mean abundant wealth here or ample gifts in Paradise. Another explanation includes the mocked believers eventually gaining control of their mockers' property and lives. None of these readings makes possessions a proof of honour before Allah.

### How does worldly attachment produce mockery?

When material gain rules a person's outlook, faith may be abandoned whenever it interferes with that gain. The same outlook treats practising believers as foolish. Chiefs among the Israelites and ignorant polytheists are included in this pattern of ridicule.

The inward reading describes people deceived without recognising their deception. Their vision grows dark, and they deride Allah's friends. Their attitude reveals what they value, not the real worth of those they mock.

Those who put personal interests before Islam often possess the visible splendours of life. Those who accept Islam without conditions may lack them. This difference tempts the former to assume superiority. But the present order is temporary. The next order lowers those once thought great and raises those once considered insignificant.

The believers' spending for Allah's Face secures happiness at the return to Him. They are exalted at the gathering, resurrection and final abode. The account contrasts their high ranks with the depths of the Fire. One early report also gives them superiority here through a stronger argument and the standing they attain.

When people testify on that Day and ignorance clears from their hearts, the true losers will become evident.

### Why does wealth not establish honour?

Provision depends on Allah's decree, not merely on personal ability or influence. Respect based on possessions is not necessarily valid in His sight. To claim honour from wealth and despise the poor confuses temporary resources with lasting worth.

A report from ʿAlī warns against despising a Muslim man or woman for hunger. Allah will disgrace the offender before the gathering of all people. It also warns against falsely alleging a weakness in a Muslim: the accuser will be set on a high mound until the words are withdrawn.

### What remains of a person's possessions?

A hadith qudsī promises divine provision to the son of Adam who spends. The Prophet ﷺ likewise encouraged Bilāl to give without fearing deprivation from the Owner of the Throne. The promise of replacement also appears in (34:39), which calls Allah the Best Provider.

The Ṣaḥīḥ records two angels descending each day. One asks Allah to replace the gifts of those who spend in His cause. The other asks for destruction of the miser's wealth.

Another recorded saying challenges the repeated claim, "my money, my money". A person's effective possession is what is eaten and consumed, worn and worn out, or given in charity and preserved in the record of deeds. What remains passes to the heirs.

Imam Aḥmad records a further warning from the Prophet ﷺ. It pictures this world as a home and wealth for those who lack a true home and wealth, gathered by people without sound understanding. The point is not to mistake temporary accumulation for lasting security.

### In short

- Attachment to worldly gain can lead people to ridicule devoted believers.
- The final ranks of believers and disbelievers reverse appearances based on wealth.
- Provision is Allah's gift, not a certificate of honour.
- Spending for Allah brings replacement and preserves what accumulation alone cannot keep.
