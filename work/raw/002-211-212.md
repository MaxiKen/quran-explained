<<<VERSE 2:211>>>
The previous verse said that opposing the truth after clear signs have come deserves punishment. This verse cites the case of the Children of Israel, punished for their hostility to the truth despite the clear signs they were given. "Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We have given them. And whoever trades Allah's favour—after receiving it—˹for disbelief˺ should know that Allah is indeed severe in punishment".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We have given them."**. The Prophet ﷺ is told to ask the children of Jacob, by way of rebuke, how many manifest proofs Allah gave them, how often He addressed them with commands and prohibitions and honoured them with religion in the time of Moses. They witnessed many clear signs attesting to the truth of what Moses brought: his hand shining with light, the parting of the sea, the water flowing from the rock he struck, the clouds that shaded them in the intense heat, the manna and the quails, and more. These signs attested to the existence of the Creator and the truthfulness of the one through whose hand they appeared, yet so many of them exchanged Allah's favour, preferring disbelief to faith and ignoring His blessings, and turned away. Grammatically, "how many" is the interrogative particle that links the second object of "ask" and also serves as the object of "We gave" and its specifier. In the devotional reading, the point of questioning them was to make them acknowledge the matter of the proof, not to resolve any doubt on the part of the Messenger ﷺ about what the proof had plainly established. Rather than follow the guidance given them, they chose to stray and suffered punishment after punishment. They received the Torah, which deserved acceptance, and denied it, and were threatened with the mountain coming down on them. They heard the word of Allah, which they should have loved and revered, toyed with doubts about it and were struck by lightning. They were delivered from Pharaoh by a passage made through the parted sea, showed no gratitude, and took to the calf, for which they were killed. They should have been thankful for the manna and quails, but disobeyed and the food rotted, and when they showed distaste for it, it stopped coming and left them to the toil of growing their own food. Finally, the chain of prophets raised among them should have been a source of deep satisfaction, yet they began to kill them, and were punished by being stripped of power. Several of these misdeeds are mentioned earlier in this surah.

**"And whoever trades Allah's favour—after receiving it—˹for disbelief˺"**. Whoever changes what Allah has blessed him with in the way of signs, which are the causes of guidance, exchanging the religion and Scripture of Allah for disbelief after it has reached him, and after what Muhammad ﷺ has brought, when he should have followed its guidance instead of turning back, falls under the divine law stated here.

**"should know that Allah is indeed severe in punishment"**. He is severe in retribution against whoever disbelieves in Him and strong in punishing the deniers, as He says of the disbelievers of Quraysh, "Have you not seen those ˹disbelievers˺ who meet Allah's favours with ingratitude and lead their own people to their doom" (14:28-29). In the devotional reading the severity lies in taking the grace away: at that moment they will recognise its worth and mourn it, since they will never attain it again, as the poet speaking for them said that you will leave and abandon me, and then look for me and not find me.

### In short

- Israel saw sign after sign through Moses, the shining hand, the parted sea, the rock, the clouds, the manna and quails, and still exchanged Allah's favour for disbelief.
- Each rejected blessing brought a punishment: the mountain raised over them, the lightning, the slaughter after the calf, the loss of the manna, the loss of power after killing the prophets.
- The law is general: whoever changes Allah's favour after receiving it, like the Quraysh of 14:28-29, meets severe punishment, not least in the withdrawal of the grace itself.

<<<VERSE 2:212>>>
This verse identifies the real cause of opposition to the truth, the love of this world and its material gains, one effect of which is contempt for those who love their faith. "The life of this world has been made appealing to the disbelievers, and they mock the believers. Those who are mindful ˹of Allah˺ will rank above them on the Day of Judgment. And Allah provides for whoever He wills without limit".

### Phrase by phrase

**"The life of this world has been made appealing to the disbelievers,"**. The comforts of this world have been decked out in disguise for the disbelievers of Makkah, Abū Jahl and his entourage, and they have fallen in love with them. Allah has made this life beautiful for those who are satisfied with it, who amass wealth but refuse to spend it as they have been commanded on what would earn His pleasure. When materialism takes over, the urge for faith evaporates, and people will go so far as to abandon their faith when it works against their worldly interests, and even have the audacity to laugh at those who love and practise it. In the devotional reading, they were deceived without realising it, and the deepening darkness of their vision led them to slander and deride the friends of Allah.

**"and they mock the believers."**. They ridiculed the believers who paid no heed to this life and spent whatever they earned on what pleased their Lord, men like Salmān al-Fārisī, Bilāl ibn Rabāḥ al-Ḥabashī, ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir and Ṣuhayb al-Rūmī, mocked and treated condescendingly for their abject poverty by people proud of their wealth. It is no surprise, then, that many chiefs among the Israelites and the ignorant among the polytheists made fun of poor Muslims. Mockery is the derogatory stance that the love of this world takes towards those who love their faith.

**"Those who are mindful ˹of Allah˺ will rank above them on the Day of Judgment."**. Those who have said no to kufr and shirk, Salmān and his fellow believers, will be in a better state than the disbelievers on the Day of Resurrection, the disbelievers in Jahannam and the believers in Jannah. Because they spent seeking Allah's Face they gained the ultimate happiness and the best share on the Day of Return, exalted above the disbelievers at the gathering place, at the resurrection and in their final abode, the believers in the highest grades and the disbelievers in the lowest depths of the Fire. One early report adds that they are above them in this world too, by having the stronger argument and by the standing they attain. When people bear witness on that Day and the error of ignorance is dispelled from their hearts, they will know who was in loss and who had gone far astray. Those who put their own interests first and those who enter Islam unconditionally are of very different mettle: the former usually gather the splendours of this life while the latter go without, and mere possession of resources makes the former assume an air of superiority and look down on the true believers. But this state of affairs is short-lived. The world will end and give way to a new and finer order in which the great will be cut down to size and those once considered lowly will occupy the high places of the heavenly world.

**"And Allah provides for whoever He wills without limit"**. He gives sustenance to whomever He wills of His servants without count or limit, in this world and the next, abundant wealth given without faltering, or ample provision in Paradise, or, in this world, when He gives those who were mocked possession of the property and lives of those who mocked them. The sentence warns those who pride themselves on brute economic strength, for everything depends on divinely ordained destiny, not on personal excellence or influence. It does not follow that one respected for his possessions is honoured with Allah, for real honour is only what proves valid in His sight, and to stand on the crutches of worldly riches, claim honour for oneself and despise the less fortunate as mean is a plain absence of sense, the consequence of which will be revealed on the Last Day. ʿAlī is reported to have said that whoever holds a Muslim man or woman in low esteem because of their hunger, Allah will disgrace before the assembly of all people from first to last, and whoever falsely accuses a Muslim man or woman of a weakness, Allah will set on a high mound until he retracts his own words. A hadith qudsī has Allah say to the son of Adam that if he spends, He will spend on him, and the Prophet ﷺ told Bilāl to spend and not to fear deprivation from the Owner of the Throne. Allah says, "whatever you spend in charity, He will compensate ˹you˺ for it. For He is the Best Provider" (34:39). The Ṣaḥīḥ records that every day two angels come down, one praying that Allah compensate everyone who spends in His cause and the other that He destroy every miser, and that the son of Adam cries "my money, my money", when his money is only what he eats and uses up, wears and wears out, and gives in charity and so keeps in his record, while the rest passes to his heirs. Imam Aḥmad records that the Prophet ﷺ said this world is the home of those who have no home and the wealth of those who have no wealth, harvested by those who have no sense.

### In short

- Love of this world is the root of opposition to truth, and the rich disbelievers of Makkah, Abū Jahl and his circle, mocked the poor believers, Bilāl, ʿAmmār, Ṣuhayb and Salmān.
- The God-fearing will stand above the mockers on the Day of Resurrection, the believers in Paradise and the disbelievers in Hell, and already here they hold the stronger argument.
- Provision is Allah's gift without measure, in this world and in Paradise, so wealth is no proof of honour with Him (ʿAlī on despising the hungry).
- Spending is rewarded and miserliness cursed (the two angels, "spend and I will spend on you", Bilāl, 34:39), and only what one gives truly remains.
