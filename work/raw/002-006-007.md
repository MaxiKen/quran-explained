<<<VERSE 2:6>>>
The surah now turns from the people who benefit from the Book to the people who refuse it. "As for those who persist in disbelief, it is the same whether you warn them or not—they will never believe". The first five verses described the believers, whom the Qur'an also calls the mindful, and the next fifteen describe those who reject its guidance and fight it out of spite. Two of those fifteen verses, this one and the next, deal with open disbelievers. The other thirteen deal with the hypocrites, who hid their disbelief behind a show of faith. Read together, the first twenty verses of the surah point to the Book as where guidance comes from, and divide mankind according to how people respond to it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"As for those who persist in disbelief"** means those who covered the truth and hid it, and who remain firm in their denial. The ones meant are people like Abū Jahl and Abū Lahab, who reject what Allah revealed to His Messenger and stubbornly stay on the wrong road. The root sense of kufr is to hide or conceal. Ingratitude is also called kufr, because it covers up a kindness someone has shown. In the terminology of the Sharīʿah, kufr is the denial of anything in which belief is obligatory. The heart of faith and the basis of the Islamic creed is to affirm with the heart, with certainty, everything the Prophet ﷺ brought from Allah that is established by definite and conclusive proof, so a person who dares to question or dismiss even one teaching of that kind is called a kāfir.

**"it is the same whether you warn them or not"** means that whether the Prophet ﷺ instils fear into them by means of the Qur'an or leaves them alone, nothing changes for them. The Arabic word for warning here, indhār, means bringing news that ought to alarm, as opposed to ibshār, bringing good news that makes people rejoice. One reading describes indhār as giving knowledge of something and instilling fear at the same time. It is not the ordinary frightening of people. It is a warning that springs from compassion and love, the way one warns one's children about fire, snakes or wild animals. That is why a thief, a bandit or an aggressor who threatens people is never called a nadhīr, a warner. The title is kept for the prophets, who warn people about the pains and punishments of the next world out of love for their fellow human beings. In choosing this title the Qur'an also hints that anyone who sets out to reform others must do more than deliver a message: he must speak to his listeners with sympathy, understanding and a real concern for their good.

**"they will never believe"** means that they do not want to believe, and also that, as is in Allah's knowledge, they will not. There is a point of grammar in the Arabic, a-andhartahum, which may be read with both hamzahs pronounced, or with the second softened to an alif, with or without a lengthening between them.

### Why the warning makes no difference to them

Allah has written that these people would disbelieve, so whether the Prophet ﷺ warned them or not they were going to reject what he was sent with. The same is said elsewhere: "Indeed, those against whom Allah's decree ˹of torment˺ is justified will not believe—even if every sign were to come to them—until they see the painful punishment" (10:96-97). Of the rebellious among the People of the Book Allah said, "Even if you were to bring every proof to the People of the Book, they would not accept your direction ˹of prayer˺" (2:145). These verses show that whoever Allah has written down as wretched will find no one to lead him to happiness, and whoever Allah sends astray will find no one to guide him. So the Prophet ﷺ was told not to grieve over them but to deliver the message. Whoever accepts it gains the best reward, and whoever turns away is not to be mourned or fretted over, because "your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺. Judgment is for Us" (13:40), and "You are only a warner, and Allah is the Trustee of All Affairs" (11:12). ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported that Ibn ʿAbbās explained the verse this way: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ longed for all people to believe and follow the guidance he brought, and Allah informed him that only those for whom He had decreed happiness from the first would believe, and only those for whom He had decreed it would go astray.

The verse is a comfort to the Prophet ﷺ. Some disbelievers were so vain, arrogant and set in their opinions that even after recognising the truth they refused it and would not listen to it or look at the plainest proofs, so his efforts to reform and win them would bear no fruit, and for them it made no difference whether he tried or not. The next verse gives the reason: their hearts and ears were sealed and their eyes covered, so every avenue of knowing and understanding was shut, and no change could be expected from them.

Two limits must be kept in mind. First, "they will never believe" is said specifically about those disbelievers who refused to listen to the Prophet ﷺ and who, as Allah knew, would die in disbelief. It is not a statement about disbelievers in general, for many of them later accepted Islam. Second, the verse says it is the same "for them". It is not the same for the Prophet ﷺ. He earns his reward in any case for bringing Allah's message to people and for his effort to teach and reform them, which is why not one verse in the Qur'an tells him to stop calling even such people to Islam. From this it follows that whoever strives to spread the word of Allah and improve his fellow human beings is always rewarded for the work, even when it has no visible effect.

### The two camps

In the Prophet's ﷺ time there were two distinct groups among those who rejected the Qur'an. One came out in open hostility and denial, and the Qur'an calls them kāfirūn. The other, out of moral weakness and greed, lacked even the courage to speak its mind. They tried to convince the Muslims that they believed in the Qur'an and its teachings, that they were as good Muslims as anyone and would stand with the Muslims against the disbelievers, while nursing denial in their hearts and assuring the disbelievers, when alone with them, that they had nothing to do with Islam and mixed with the Muslims only to deceive them and spy on them. The Qur'an names them munāfiqūn, hypocrites. Seen whole, the opening of the surah sets out guidance and then sorts people into two kinds by their response to it: those who choose to follow it, the believers or the mindful, and those who reject it or swerve from it, the disbelievers or the hypocrites. The first kind are the people whose path is asked for at the end of al-Fātiḥah, the path of those Allah has favoured, and the second kind are those whose path refuge is sought from, those who earned anger and those who went astray.

A basic principle follows from this teaching. Any division of mankind into groups, if it is to mean anything, must rest on differences of principle, not on birth, race, colour, geography or language. The Qur'an gives a clear verdict: "He is the One Who created you, yet some of you are disbelievers while some are believers" (64:2).

### Why a clear call can fail

If a person closes his eyes he cannot see, and if he blocks his ears he cannot hear. However clear the call of truth, it can be understood and accepted only by someone who opens his mind to it. The silent call present in the universe, and its spoken form on the tongue of the preacher of Allah's word, cannot touch closed minds. The call of truth in its pure form rests on reality and agrees with human nature, and no one who hears it with an open mind can fail to recognise it. But where a society is rigidly bound by old conventions, people care less about opening their minds to divine realities than about protecting the vested interests of the existing system. The preacher, as Allah's envoy, neither represents nor supports the conventional structures of society, and his call, being new and unfamiliar, looks like a threat to people's material security. However much he urges them to submit to Allah's will, they baulk, because submitting would mean demolishing the world they have built for themselves. It is also hard for him to reach highly placed people, who fear that following an "insignificant" man will diminish their own glory. The two greatest obstacles to his success are worldliness and arrogance, and it is these states of mind, which stop people from accepting the truth, that the Qur'an calls seals on hearts.

### Bounty and justice

From the start of the surah up to this point the verses allude to the Lord's bounty and gentleness with His intimates and friends. This verse alludes to His severity and justice toward strangers and enemies. Allah has both bounty and justice. When He acts with justice it is fitting, and when He is bounteous it suits Him. Not everything that fits justice suits bounty, but everything that suits bounty fits justice. One person He calls toward bounty, by His decree. Another He drives toward justice, by His will. The good news is that bounty rules over justice: justice falls silent before bounty, and the fruit of bounty is felicity and triumph while the outcome of justice is wretchedness and estrangement. Both are done deeds. The Prophet ﷺ said that the Pen has already dried over everything that is to happen until the Resurrection. The decree is beginningless, and the work already complete. Eagerness cannot lift a person beyond the portion appointed to him. The master of the path prayed in this spirit: O Allah, what comes of what You do not want, and when does he come whom You have not called? What good is water to ground that was never sown, or an answer to one who is unworthy? What does bitter water gain from sweet water beside it, or the thorn from the rose's scent next to it? The apportioning has gone before and nothing can be added or removed. Iblīs dwelt on the highest horizon, with thousands of acts of worship to his name, and gained nothing by them, because he was not worthy. Approval and wrath are beginningless attributes, so rolled-up sleeves and determined steps bring no profit on their own. One day ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb came upon Iblīs, seized him by the collar and told him he had been looking for him for a long time.

### In short

- Verses 6 and 7 describe the open disbelievers, and the thirteen verses after them describe the hypocrites. Together with the first five verses they divide mankind by its response to guidance, the only division that matters.
- Kufr means covering the truth. In the Sharīʿah it is denying anything that must be believed, even a single established teaching.
- Indhār is a warning born of compassion, which is why "warner" is a title reserved for the prophets.
- Warning and silence are the same for those who Allah knew would die in disbelief, like Abū Jahl and Abū Lahab, not for disbelievers in general, many of whom later believed. And it is never the same for the one who calls, who is rewarded for his effort whatever the result.

<<<VERSE 2:7>>>
The reason the warning changes nothing for them follows at once. "Allah has sealed their hearts and their hearing, and their sight is covered. They will suffer a tremendous punishment". A seal keeps anything from entering. A covering keeps anything from being seen. When the heart, the hearing and the sight are all shut, every path by which truth might reach a person is closed, and what remains is the punishment of the next world.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah has sealed their hearts and their hearing"** means He has stamped them. As-Suddī said that khatama means "He sealed". Qatādah said that Shayṭān gained control of them when they obeyed him, so Allah sealed their hearts, their ears and their eyes, leaving them unable to see guidance, to hear it, or to grasp and understand it. Ibn Jurayj reported Mujāhid's explanation: the seal is a stamp, and it comes about when sin settles in the heart and surrounds it from every side, so that the heart's drowning in sin becomes a stamp, that is, a seal. Ibn Jurayj added that the seal is set on both the heart and the hearing. The seal on the heart makes certain that no good enters it. The seal on the hearing is something deposited in the ears so that the person cannot profit from the truth he hears. Allah has locked their falsehood inside their hearts, and sealed their hearing so that they neither listen to the truth nor accept it. In as-Suddī's commentary, Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd said it means that they neither understand nor hear.

**"and their sight is covered"** means a veil or shield lies over their eyes, and the truth in its clarity is hidden from them. In reciting, it is correct to pause after "their hearing" and then continue with "and their sight is covered", because the stamp belongs to the heart and the hearing while the covering, ghishāwah, belongs to the eyes. The distinction is deliberate. An idea can enter the heart from any direction, and a sound can enter the ears from any direction, so the only way to block them is to seal them. The eyes work in one direction only and see only what lies in front of them, so a covering is enough to stop them.

**"They will suffer a tremendous punishment"** means a dreadful torment in the Hereafter, intense and everlasting.

### Whom the verse was about

The verse is said to have been about particular Jews of Madinah: Ḥuyayy ibn Akhṭab, his kinsman Judayy ibn Akhṭab, and Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf. Another view names three of the Makkan idolaters instead: al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah and the two brothers ʿUtbah and Shaybah, sons of Rabīʿah.

### How a heart gets sealed

Mujāhid taught that the stain is less serious than the stamp, and the stamp less serious than the lock, which is the worst. Al-Aʿmash described Mujāhid showing it with his hand: the heart, they used to say, is like an open palm. When the servant commits a sin, part of it rolls up, and he closed his index finger. Another sin, and another part rolls up, and he closed another finger, until all his fingers were closed. Then, he said, the heart is sealed. Mujāhid said this is what the Qur'an calls the rān, the stain: "But no! In fact, their hearts have been stained by all ˹the evil˺ they used to commit" (83:14). Ibn Jarīr held that this stain is the truth of the matter.

A hadith reported from Abū Hurayrah says the same. When a man sins, a black dot appears on his heart. The first dot troubles him, the way a smudge on white cloth is always displeasing, but if instead of asking Allah's pardon he sins again, another dot appears, and with every new sin the dots multiply until the whole heart is dark, and then he can no longer see good as good or evil as evil, and loses the power to tell them apart. The Prophet ﷺ added that this darkness is what the Qur'an calls rayn, rust, and recited the verse above. This is recorded in Mishkāt from the Musnad of Aḥmad and from at-Tirmidhī. At-Tirmidhī, an-Nasāʾī and Ibn Mājah recorded the hadith, and at-Tirmidhī graded it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. In another sound hadith from Abū Hurayrah, recorded by at-Tirmidhī, the Prophet ﷺ said that when a man commits a sin his heart darkens, and if he seeks Allah's forgiveness it becomes clear again.

Ḥudhayfah's hadith, recorded in the Ṣaḥīḥ, describes the process on a larger scale. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that trials are presented to hearts one after another, like the straws woven into a reed mat. Any heart that absorbs a trial gets a black spot marked on it, and any heart that rejects it gets a white spot, until hearts fall into two kinds: one white like smooth rock, which no trial will harm as long as the heavens and the earth remain, and one black and dull like an overturned cup, which no longer recognises good or rejects evil.

### Punishment in this world as well as the next

These two verses show that the real punishment for disbelief, and for some sins, comes in the next world. But some punishment can come for certain sins in this world too, and sometimes it takes a very grave form: the divine favour that helps a person reform is withdrawn, so that he stops thinking about how his deeds will be judged on the Day of Judgment, grows in disobedience, and finally loses even the awareness of evil. Some of the early scholars put it this way: one punishment for a bad deed is another bad deed that follows it, and one reward for a good deed is another good deed that follows it. Allah's way with people who become so stubborn in denial that they will not hear the truth or weigh a clear argument is to give them this kind of punishment already in this world. Their hearts get sealed and their ears and eyes shut against the truth, so that, as far as truth is concerned, they are left with no mind for thinking, no eyes for seeing and no ears for hearing. The last phrase of the verse adds the grievous punishment kept for them in the next world.

### A doubt removed

A question is sometimes raised. If Allah Himself sealed their hearts and blocked their senses, are they not helpless, and how can they be blamed and punished for disbelief they did not choose? The answer lies in reading this verse together with the verse on rust, 83:14, which makes plain that it was their own arrogance and evil deeds that settled on their hearts as rust. It is that same rust which this verse calls a seal and a covering. By choosing arrogance and pride they wilfully destroyed their own capacity to accept the truth, and so they are the authors of their own ruin. Allah, as the Creator of all the actions of His creatures, attributes the sealing to Himself, and in doing so points out that when these people insisted, by their own choice, on wrecking their aptitude for truth, He produced in their hearts and senses the insensitivity that is His way in such cases. Al-Qurṭubī said that the ummah agrees on this: Allah describes Himself as the One who seals and closes the disbelievers' hearts, and does so to punish their disbelief, as in the verse about the People of the Book: "it is Allah Who has sealed their hearts for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few" (4:155). He also cited the Prophet's ﷺ supplication, "O Turner of hearts, make our hearts firm on Your religion."

Put in modern terms, the minds of people who do not take the call of truth seriously, who are proud and worldly, are wrapped in an invisible veil that makes them impervious to it. A person prejudiced against something cannot see its reasonableness even when the signs in its favour are clear.

### Two kinds of seal

On one heart the seal of estrangement was placed, and its owner stayed in disbelief. On another the seal of perplexity was placed, and its owner stayed in lethargy. The first is a stranger, driven off, the road lost. The second is helpless, stuck on the road, kept back from the Friend by something other than the Friend. Anything that holds you back from the road is a hindrance, whether it is called disbelief or faith, and any image that keeps you from the Friend is a hindrance, whether it is ugly or beautiful. Not everyone who escapes disbelief reaches the Real. He reaches that only when he escapes from himself. Escaping disbelief brings a person to familiarity, and escaping from himself brings him to friendship, and between familiarity and friendship lie a thousand stations, and between love and the Friend a thousand valleys.

### What comes next

Four verses at the start of the surah described the believers, and these two described the disbelievers, who are at fault inside and out. Next Allah describes the hypocrites, who look good on the outside but are not so within. Because hypocrisy is hard to see and many people do not grasp its reality, Allah describes it in detail, and each of the traits He lists is itself a kind of hypocrisy. Two whole surahs, Barāʾah (chapter 9) and al-Munāfiqūn (chapter 63), were revealed about the hypocrites, and they are mentioned in an-Nūr (chapter 24) and other surahs, so that people would know what they look like and steer clear of their ways and errors.

### In short

- The heart and the hearing are "sealed" because ideas and sounds come from every direction. The eyes are "covered" because they look one way only.
- The seal is not arbitrary. Sin stains the heart dot by dot, finger by finger, until it is dark all over. That is the rust of 83:14, and this verse calls the same rust a seal and a covering.
- Allah attributes the sealing to Himself as the Creator of all acts, but the disbelievers brought it on themselves by their arrogance, so they are answerable for it, and the ummah agrees that the seal is a punishment for disbelief.
- Losing the sense of right and wrong is itself a punishment in this life. The "tremendous punishment" waits in the next.
