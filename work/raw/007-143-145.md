<<<VERSE 7:143>>>
At the appointed time Mūsā (Moses) came for his meeting, and Allah spoke to him directly: "When Moses came at the appointed time and his Lord spoke to him, he asked, “My Lord! Reveal Yourself to me so I may see You.”" The answer came with a demonstration: "Allah answered, “You cannot see Me! But look at the mountain. If it remains firm in its place, only then will you see Me.”" Then: "When his Lord appeared to the mountain, He levelled it to dust and Moses collapsed unconscious." And when he came to, "he cried, “Glory be to You! I turn to You in repentance and I am the first of the believers.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"When Moses came at the appointed time"**: at the time Allah had promised to speak to him, after the full forty nights. One explanation places the meeting in Midian.

**"and his Lord spoke to him"**: without any intermediary, in speech that Mūsā heard from every direction, giving him commands, prohibitions and other instructions.

**"he asked, “My Lord! Reveal Yourself to me so I may see You.”"** Mūsā longed to see his Lord, and asked for it.

**"Allah answered, “You cannot see Me!"** The Arabic is lan tarānī, "you will not see Me". In this worldly life, Mūsā, seeing Allah is beyond your strength. The word lan, "never", by no means shows that seeing Allah will never happen, as the misguided sect of the Muʿtazilah claimed. The believers' vision of Allah in the Hereafter is established by hadiths of the Messenger ﷺ that come through so many chains that they are mutawātir: "On that Day ˹some˺ faces will be bright looking at their Lord" (75:22-23).

The wording also points to this. Allah did not say lan urā, "I cannot be seen", but lan tarānī, "you cannot see Me". That implies that seeing Allah is not impossible, only that Mūsā could not bear it in his present physical state. Had it been impossible, the verse would have said "I cannot be seen". So seeing Allah is logically possible even in this world, but this verse rules out its happening in this world. That is also the unanimous view of most scholars. Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim contains a hadith that none of you will see his Lord until he dies. Earlier scriptures also report that Allah said to Mūsā, "O Mūsā, no living soul sees Me without perishing, and no solid thing without crumbling."

**"But look at the mountain."** Look at the mountain, which is stronger than you. One explanation says it was the highest mountain in Midian. This was a physical demonstration that, in his present state, Mūsā could not endure such an experience.

**"If it remains firm in its place, only then will you see Me.”"** If the mountain stays standing and is unaffected when I reveal Myself to it, then you will be able to see Me, that is, you will be able to stand firm and see Me. If it is flattened, the sight of Allah in this world is beyond you.

**"When his Lord appeared to the mountain"**: the word is tajallī, disclosure or manifestation. According to the spiritual masters (Sufis), it means seeing something indirectly, through some other means, as one sees a thing reflected in a mirror. So tajallī cannot mean "seeing". The verse itself shows this, since it denies seeing yet speaks of Allah's disclosure to the mountain.

Imam Aḥmad recorded in his Musnad from Anas ibn Mālik that the Prophet ﷺ, explaining these words, held out the tip of his little finger and said, "Like this." At-Tirmidhī recorded it in his chapter of tafsīr on this verse and called it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ gharīb. Al-Ḥākim recorded it too in his Mustadrak, through Ḥammād ibn Salamah. He said it is ṣaḥīḥ by the criteria of Muslim, though they did not record it. Another report from Anas says the Prophet ﷺ recited the verse, put his thumb on the tip of his little finger, and said that only this much of Allah's light was disclosed to the mountain, and it burst apart. One explanation speaks of the equivalent of half the nail of a little finger, in a hadith al-Ḥākim verified. As-Suddī reported, through ʿIkrimah, that Ibn ʿAbbās said only the measure of the little finger appeared. This does not have to mean that the whole mountain was shattered. Perhaps only the part directly exposed to the light was affected.

**"He levelled it to dust"**: He made it collapse into dust. The word can be read dakkan or dakkāʾa, meaning flattened.

**"and Moses collapsed unconscious."** He fainted, losing consciousness at the awe of what he had seen. Ibn Jarīr recorded these reports because of their bearing on the word al-ghashī, fainting.

**"When he recovered, he cried, “Glory be to You!"** Once he came round, he praised, glorified and honoured Allah, declaring Him far above any flaw, for no living soul could see Him in this life and live.

**"I turn to You in repentance"**: Mujāhid said this means from having asked to see You. Others explain it as: I repent for asking what I was not commanded to ask, and I ask Your forgiveness for asking to see You in this world.

**"and I am the first of the believers.”"** Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid said it means the first of the children of Israel to believe, and Ibn Jarīr preferred this view. Others say the first believer of his time, or the first of his people. Another narration from Ibn ʿAbbās says it means: the first to believe that no one will see You in this life.

### Did the Prophet ﷺ place himself above Mūsā?

Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī and Abū Hurayrah each narrated a fitting hadith here. Al-Bukhārī recorded Abū Saʿīd's version in his Ṣaḥīḥ. A Jewish man came to the Prophet ﷺ, having been slapped on the face, and complained that one of his Companions from the Anṣār had struck him. The Prophet ﷺ had the man summoned and asked why he had done it. He explained that he had heard the Jew swear by the One who chose Mūsā over all humanity. When he asked, "Over Muhammad too?", he grew angry and slapped him.

The Prophet ﷺ told them not to prefer him over the prophets. On the Day of Resurrection, he said, people will fall unconscious, and he will be the first to come round. Then he will find Mūsā holding one of the pillars of the Throne (ʿArsh). He will not know whether Mūsā came round before him, or whether he was spared because of his fainting at aṭ-Ṭūr. Al-Bukhārī recorded this hadith in many places in his Ṣaḥīḥ, and so did Muslim and Abū Dāwūd. Abū Hurayrah's version was collected by Imam Aḥmad and by the two shaykhs, al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

### Why can Allah not be seen in this world?

Mūsā wanted to see Allah. When he learnt that it was not possible, he turned to Allah in repentance and placed his faith in the unseen Allah. So human beings are tested by believing in Allah without seeing Him. Seeing Allah is a reward kept for the Hereafter, so how could it happen in this world?

Mūsā received his first call to prophethood on a mountainside, and he was called to the mountain again to receive the commands of the Torah. This hints that the best place to receive inspiration from Allah is the setting of nature rather than human society. When a person leaves the din of the crowded human world for the quiet world of trees, mountains and rivers, he begins to feel closer to Allah. His mind is free of worldly problems and not taken up with worldly thoughts. This is the best moment to think without bias or complexes clouding his thinking, and then he can truly commune with Allah.

### The inner reading: the journey of longing

Mūsā made two journeys: a journey of seeking and a journey of rapture. The journey of seeking was the night of the fire, of which Allah says, "he spotted a fire on the side of Mount Ṭûr" (28:29). The journey of rapture was this one.

Mūsā came lost to himself, absorbed in his inmost secret. Love's wine, poured from the cup of holiness, had gone to his head. The pain of longing pressed on him, and the wave of "Show me" rose from the ocean of his passion. He had gone round the neighbourhoods of the children of Israel, gathering their messages and requests, so that he could lengthen his words when he reached the Presence. As the verse says, "I count it forbidden to talk with others, and whenever I talk with You, I draw out my words." When he reached the place of intimate prayer, he was drunk with the wine of longing and burnt by hearing the Real's speech. He forgot everything, and the plain truth of his state came out: "Show me, that I may gaze upon You!"

The angels cast stones of blame at his wish. Did he hope to see the Exalted Lord? What has dust to do with the Lord of lords? How can a being of dust and water speak to the Eternal? How can someone who once was not, and then came to be, seek union with the One who always was and always will be? In his rapture and selflessness, Mūsā answered in the language of solitude: "Accept my excuse, for I did not fall here of my own accord. He wanted me first, before I wanted Him. When I woke from sleep, I found the Friend at my pillow."

### In short

- Mūsā asked to see Allah, and Allah told him he could not see Him in this life, then showed him by disclosing Himself to the mountain, which crumbled to dust.
- "You will not see Me" does not deny that believers will see Allah in the Hereafter, as many hadiths affirm. It denies only that a person can bear it in this world.
- Only a fingertip's measure of Allah's light was disclosed, and Mūsā fainted, then glorified Allah and repented of his request.
- The Prophet ﷺ forbade preferring him over the prophets in a way that belittles them, and told of finding Mūsā at the Throne on the Day of Resurrection.

<<<VERSE 7:144>>>
After the vision was refused, Allah reminded Mūsā (Moses) of the honours he had been given: "Allah said, “O Moses! I have ˹already˺ elevated you above all others by My messages and speech. So hold firmly to what I have given you and be grateful.”" Allah was telling Mūsā that, through His message and His speaking to him, He had raised him above everyone else of his era.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah said, “O Moses! I have ˹already˺ elevated you above all others"**: I have chosen you and preferred you above other people, the people of your time, above the children of Israel.

**"by My messages and speech."** By the message I sent you with, and by My speaking to you directly. The word can be read in the plural, bi-risālātī, "by My messages", or the singular, bi-risālatī, "by My message".

**"So hold firmly to what I have given you"**: take what I have given you of My speech and My conversation with you, the great honour and bounty I have granted, and act on it.

**"and be grateful.”"** Be among those who thank Allah for My favours, and that I chose to speak to you out of all people. Thank Him for it, and stop asking for what you cannot bear.

### Where does Mūsā stand among the prophets?

Without doubt, the master of all Ādam's (Adam's) descendants, from the first to the last, is Muhammad ﷺ. That is why Allah chose him to be the Final Prophet and Messenger, whose law will remain dominant and in force until the Last Hour. His followers outnumber the followers of all the other prophets and messengers. After Muhammad ﷺ, the next in honour and virtue is Ibrāhīm (Abraham), and then Mūsā son of ʿImrān, who was spoken to directly by the Most Merciful.

A prophet is a human being like any other, not a supernatural creature. His one special quality is that he keeps his inborn abilities intact. So Allah chooses him to carry His message, and he can be trusted to represent Allah among the people. In that sense Mūsā was the best person of his community, so Allah chose him as His prophet and revealed His message to him.

### How did Allah speak to Mūsā?

The Qur'an confirms in clear terms that Allah spoke directly to Mūsā. The first time was when he was given prophethood. This was the second time, when he was given the Torah. The wording of this verse suggests that this later speech had features the first did not.

What the speech was like, and how it took place, no one can know but Allah. Reasoned suppositions about it may be allowed, so long as they go against no rule of the Sharīʿah. But none of them can be held as certain without sound proof. The best course is the one the Companions, their students and the elders after them followed. They left such matters to Allah and never tried to settle them by groundless guesswork.

### The inner reading: one thing withheld, much given

It is as if Allah said: "O Mūsā, I have held back one thing from you, the vision, but I have singled you out for many virtues. I have chosen you for messengership and honoured you with an exalted rank. Give thanks for all this and recognise these blessings. Be among the thankful, and do not put yourself in the place of complaint." A poet sang in this sense: "If they turn away, it was they who first came with tenderness. How faithful they have been, so bear with them when they keep their distance."

### In short

- Allah reminded Mūsā that His message and His direct speech had set him above everyone of his era.
- He told him to hold firmly to what he had been given, be grateful, and not ask for what he could not bear.
- After Muhammad ﷺ and Ibrāhīm, Mūsā is the next in honour, the prophet to whom the Most Merciful spoke.
- How Allah's speech took place is beyond human knowledge, and the early Muslims left it to Allah.

<<<VERSE 7:145>>>
Allah then gave Mūsā (Moses) the Tablets: "We wrote for him on the Tablets ˹the fundamentals˺ of everything; commandments and explanations of all things." With them came a command and a promise: "˹We commanded,˺ “Hold to this firmly and ask your people to take the best of it. I will soon show ˹all of˺ you the home of the rebellious". The Torah was given to Mūsā inscribed on Tablets.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We wrote for him on the Tablets ˹the fundamentals˺ of everything"**: Allah wrote on the Tablets lessons and admonition about everything people need in their religion and their worldly affairs. According to one explanation, the Tablets were cut from the lote tree of Paradise, or else from chrysolite or emerald, and numbered either seven or ten. It is said the Tablets held advice and the detailed rules of what is lawful and forbidden. They contained the Torah, of which Allah says:

> "Indeed, We gave Moses the Scripture—after destroying earlier nations—as an insight for the people, a guide, and mercy so perhaps they would be mindful" (28:43).

It is also said that Allah gave Mūsā the Tablets before the Torah, and that they were not part of it. Allah knows best.

**"commandments and explanations of all things."** Admonition for those who will take it, and a detailed explanation of the laws that needed explaining: the lawful and the unlawful, commands and prohibitions. One explanation reads "the lesson" as the prohibitions. In the Arabic, "of all things" (li-kulli shayʾin) restates the earlier "about all things".

**"˹We commanded,˺ “Hold to this firmly"**: before "hold to this" the words "We said" are understood. Take it with strength, seriously and diligently, and stand firm in obedience.

**"and ask your people to take the best of it."** Command your people to hold to its finest precepts. Act on what is clear in it, and believe in what is ambiguous. Hold to its most excellent instructions in the most complete way, with patience and forgiveness, for the greatest reward. Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah reported, through ʿIkrimah, that Ibn ʿAbbās said Mūsā was commanded to hold himself to the hardest of what was laid on his people.

**"I will soon show ˹all of˺ you the home of the rebellious"**: you will see the reward of those who defy My command and turn from obeying Me, and the destruction, ruin and utter loss they suffer. One explanation says it is the home of the transgressors, Hell. Others say it is Iraq, or Egypt, the home of Pharaoh and his followers, shown to them so that they might learn from it.

### Was "the home of the rebellious" Egypt or Syria?

This is a kind of promise from Allah that the Israelites would soon take over Egypt, or perhaps Syria. There are two views about which land is meant. The difference rests on whether the Israelites went back to Egypt after Pharaoh and his people were destroyed. If they did return and rule there, as the earlier verse about their inheriting the land suggests (7:137), then this verse must mean Syria. In that case they had taken Egypt before Allah's light was disclosed to the mountain. If they did not go back to Egypt after Pharaoh's destruction, the verse may mean both Egypt and Syria.

### How should Allah's words be read?

Allah's revelation gives the guidance a person needs for life. But divine messages come in the form of words, and in this world of testing it is always possible to twist those words to suit one's own desires. Someone who is serious and sincere about guidance, and fears Allah's punishment and displeasure, will draw from the words the meaning Allah intended. Only those who long for the true path receive inspiration from Allah's words.

"I will soon show you the home of the rebellious" means: on your journey you will pass the ruins of peoples who were destroyed. They had been given Allah's guidance but went astray. They could not stand firm in their faith, because they gave in to the pressure of circumstances and to their own desires and ambitions. So they earned Allah's displeasure and were destroyed. The children of Israel would meet the same fate if they disobeyed Allah. In the scales of divine justice Allah favours no community over another. In this world a person can interpret Allah's words by his own whims and even distort them. But that kind of insolence puts even those who claim to obey on Allah's list of the disobedient.

### The inner reading: the admonitions of the Tablets

The traditions say that among the admonitions Allah wrote on the Tablets for Mūsā was this: "O Mūsā, if you want to be honoured at the threshold of My majesty and singled out for My nearness, be good to orphans and do not humiliate the poor. O Mūsā, I am the One who is tender to orphans and wishes them well, the Loving One who is generous to the poor. Be tender to those I am tender to, and do not drive away those I call."

Al-Muṣṭafā said of the poor that those who stay patient will sit in Allah's company on the Day of Resurrection. Of orphans he said that the weeping of an orphan makes the All-Merciful's Throne shake, and Allah says that whoever makes him content, He will make content.

"O Mūsā, if you want Me to speak proudly of you among the angels, harm no one, and keep stones and thorns off the path of those who have submitted." Al-Muṣṭafā said that faith has seventy-odd branches, the highest being "There is no god but Allah" and the lowest the removal of harm from the road.

"O Mūsā, if you want Me to answer your supplications, deal well with people, learn knowledge and teach it to others, for I have honoured those who know by giving them knowledge. For them I make the dust sweet and light and widen their graves, and tomorrow I will gather them among the ranks of the prophets." Al-Muṣṭafā told of what Jibrīl (Gabriel) said to him: "O Muḥammad, do not look down on a servant to whom Allah has given knowledge, for Allah did not look down on him when He gave it to him." He added that Allah will gather the people of knowledge in one place and tell them that He placed His knowledge in them only because He wished them good, and that He forgives them what came from them.

### In short

- Allah gave Mūsā the Torah written on Tablets, holding admonition and detailed rulings for every need of faith and life.
- Mūsā was to hold it firmly and command his people to keep its finest teachings, holding himself to its hardest demands.
- "The home of the rebellious" promised them the lands of the destroyed, Egypt or Syria, as a lesson about the fate of the disobedient.
- Allah's words guide only those who read them sincerely. Twisting them to suit one's desires counts as disobedience.
