<<<VERSE 7:143>>>
The promise of the forty nights comes to its meeting, and Musa asks for something no one in this life can bear. "When Moses came at the appointed time and his Lord spoke to him, he asked, “My Lord! Reveal Yourself to me so I may see You.”" The answer came at once. "You cannot see Me! But look at the mountain. If it remains firm in its place, only then will you see Me.”" What followed was the appearance itself. "When his Lord appeared to the mountain, He levelled it to dust and Moses collapsed unconscious." Then, when his senses came back to him, he said, "Glory be to You! I turn to You in repentance and I am the first of the believers.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"When Moses came at the appointed time"** brings the forty nights to their end. He came to the place that had been fixed for him. One explanation of the account says the tryst was in Midian, and that the mountain he was told to look at was the highest mountain in that country.

**"and his Lord spoke to him"** is the thing for which the whole appointment had been made. The speech came without any intermediary, and one report says Musa heard it from every direction, so that the words were not arriving from one side or one quarter.

**"he asked, “My Lord! Reveal Yourself to me so I may see You.”"** records what Musa said once the speech had been given. He asked to look at his Lord. What the words say is plain, and it is the greatest request that a human being can put into words.

**"Allah answered, “You cannot see Me!"** is the answer given in verse 143, and the wording of it carries more than a refusal. The Book says, in the form it uses, that he would not see Him, rather than that He cannot be seen. The difference is deliberate. It shows that seeing Allah is not impossible in itself, and that what stood in the way was the condition Musa was in. Had the matter been impossible of its own nature, the answer would have been given in the other form, and nothing more would have been said about it. The answer therefore says that he had not the capacity to endure it in this life.

This is the argument against the claim of Al-Mu'tazilah, who held that no one would ever see Allah at all. Their reading of this verse took it as a permanent negation. The reports of transmitted grade from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, related by so many chains that their truth cannot be doubted, affirm that the believers will see Allah in the Hereafter. The Book points to it in the words about the faces on that day that will be radiant, looking at their Lord (75:22-23). And a report in the collection of Muslim has it that none among people will see his Lord unless he dies, which is the same division of the matter. Seeing in this world is closed by agreement of the majority of the learned, and seeing in the life to come is established by the reports.

**"But look at the mountain. If it remains firm in its place, only then will you see Me."** turns the request into a demonstration that the man himself can watch. The mountain is stronger than he is, and larger, and older. If it stays fixed when the light appears to it, then there is a capacity in the created thing for that encounter. If the mountain breaks, the answer to the request is not a word but a scene. One report renders this as gazing at the mountain, and asks whether it will stand in its place when the matter comes to it.

**"When his Lord appeared to the mountain, He levelled it to dust"** gives the demonstration itself. The word used here for the appearing carries the sense of exposure and disclosure. The spiritual masters read it in their own way, saying that a disclosure of this kind is the seeing of one thing through something else, in the manner of seeing a thing reflected in a mirror. On that reading the word cannot mean seeing at all, and the verse itself confirms it, since the same passage has just denied that seeing would take place and then reported the appearing on the mount.

**"and Moses collapsed unconscious"** tells what happened to the man standing there. He fell senseless, and one report names the mountain in this place as At-Tur, the mountain on which the whole affair took place.

**"When he recovered, he cried, “Glory be to You!"** is what he said when his senses came back to him, and it is praise, glorification and honour given to Allah. No living soul is able to look at Him in this world and keep its life, and it was the impossibility of that which put the words of praise on his tongue.

**"I turn to You in repentance"** is the repentance of the request itself. One report from Mujahid says plainly that it was a turning away from the asking to look at his Lord, and another says he turned in repentance for having asked something he had not been commanded to ask.

**"and I am the first of the believers"** is the last of his words. Ibn 'Abbas and Mujahid are reported to say that it means the first of the believers among the Children of Israel, and Ibn Jarir preferred that view. Another narration from Ibn 'Abbas gives the sense that no one shall see You in this life, so that Musa was the first to believe that. Another report carries the sense of the first of the believers of his own time. All of these readings meet in the same place, that the first among his people in faith was the messenger himself, at the very moment when his request had been refused.

### What the appearing on the mountain was

The reports of what exactly appeared that day are given in some detail. Anas said that the Prophet ﷺ recited this verse and then placed his thumb on the tip of his little finger. Only that much of the light was exposed to the mount, he said, and the mountain burst into pieces. Ahmad recorded it in his collection. At-Tirmidhi recorded it in the chapter of explanation of this verse, and then said of it that the report is hasan sahih gharib, which is a high grade of soundness. Al-Hakim also recorded it in his Mustadrak through the route of Hamad bin Salamah. He said that it is sound by the criteria of Muslim, although they did not record it themselves.

Ibn 'Abbas is reported to have said on the same words that only the extent of a little finger appeared from Him. As-Suddi reported from 'Ikrimah from Ibn 'Abbas that when He made the mountain collapse into dust and Musa fell down unconscious, the falling was from what he saw. No blow had touched him. Ibn Jarir recorded these reports because of the relation of the whole scene to the word Al-Ghashi. That word carries the sense of a thing that comes over a person and overwhelms him, so that he loses his senses.

It is also said that the mention of the little finger does not mean the whole mountain was left standing except for one part. What the words describe is the part of the mountain directly exposed to the light, and the mountain as a whole was shattered. And in the earlier scriptures it was reported that Allah said to Musa that no living soul sees Him but perishes. No solid thing survives it either, and that is the same lesson in another form.

### The two reports from the companions

Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri and Abu Hurayrah each carried a hadith from the Prophet ﷺ that belongs in this place.

Al-Bukhari recorded in his Sahih from Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri that a Jew came to the Prophet ﷺ after his face had been smacked. He said that one of the companions from Al-Ansar had smacked him on the face. The Prophet ﷺ told that the man be summoned, and he was summoned, and then he asked him why he had smacked the man's face. The man said that he had passed by that Jew and heard him swearing that no one was chosen over mankind above Musa. So he had said to him, over Muhammad too, and then anger took him and he struck his face. The Prophet ﷺ said then that people should not prefer him above the prophets. On the Day of Resurrection people will be struck unconscious, he said, and he himself would be the first to wake. He would then find Musa holding onto a pillar of the Throne, the arsh of Allah, and he would not know whether Musa had woken before him, or had already received his due because of what had happened to him on Mount At-Tur.

Al-Bukhari recorded this hadith in many places of his Sahih, and Muslim and Abu Dawud recorded it as well. As for the hadith of Abu Hurayrah, Imam Ahmad and the two collections of Al-Bukhari and Muslim carried his narration.

### The two journeys of Musa

The account of the two meetings is set out in a way that shows how the same messenger stood in two very different positions. Musa had two journeys. One was the journey of seeking, and it is the night of the fire, when he saw the fire on the side of the Mount (28:29). The other was this journey of standing in the presence of his Lord at the appointed time.

What was said of this second journey is that Musa came to it with no self of his own left to consult. He had drunk from the cup of the love of his Lord, and the longing for the meeting was pressing on him. The cry to see carried him beyond the measure of what a man can ask. He had gathered up the words and the aims of his people as he went, so that he could bring them with him when he reached the Presence. He said that he counted it unlawful to speak with others whenever he was speaking with his Lord. When he came to the place of whispered prayer he was overtaken by the wine of yearning and by the listening to the Speech, and he forgot everything. What had been hidden in him came out in the words of his request.

The angels threw the stones of blame at the desire that had risen in him. They asked how dust could hope for the Lord of lords, and what a being made of dust and water had to do with Eternity. They asked how one who was not, and then was, could be worthy of union with the One who always was and always will be. In that state, and with no pride in him at all, Musa answered with the tongue of one who stands alone. He asked that his excuses be accepted, since he had not come to that place by his own doing. His Lord had willed it first, and he had not willed it. He had seen the Friend at his pillow when he woke from sleep.

### What the passage settles about the unseen

The account of the request is also a lesson about faith in the One who is not seen. Musa asked to see, and when he learned that seeing was not for this world he turned to his Lord and put his faith in God, the unseen. That is the test on which a human being is placed. He is asked to believe in God without looking at Him, and the reward of looking is kept back for the life to come, so it cannot be given in a world that is itself passing away.

There is a further point in the place where all of this happened. The first call of Musa came on a mountainside, and the second time he was called to a mountain again to receive the commands of the Torah. That is an indication that the best place to receive what comes from God is the open world of nature rather than the crowded world of human society. When a man steps out of the noise of people and comes into the quiet of trees and mountains and rivers, he begins to feel himself nearer to his Lord. His mind is free of the daily press of affairs and is not held by worldly thoughts. That is the best moment for him to think without the bias that comes of wanting to please others, and to be in true communion with his Lord.

### In short

- Musa came to the appointment at the end of the forty nights, and his Lord spoke to him directly, in a speech he heard from every direction.
- He asked to look at his Lord, and the answer was given in a form that leaves the door of seeing open in itself while closing it for this world.
- The mountain was placed in front of him as the demonstration, and when the light appeared to it the mountain was levelled to the dust and Musa fell unconscious.
- The claim of Al-Mu'tazilah that no one will ever see Allah is answered by this wording and by the reports of transmitted grade, and the seeing of the believers in the life to come is established in the Book and in the hadith, with the reports of Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri and Abu Hurayrah carrying the place of Musa on that day.
- Musa turned in repentance from the request itself and named himself the first of the believers, and the whole scene leaves the servant with the lesson of faith in the unseen and the worth of withdrawing to quiet places for the sake of his Lord.

<<<VERSE 7:144>>>
The answer comes back to the man who asked, and this time it is a statement of what was given to him. "Allah said, “O Moses! I have ˹already˺ elevated you above all others by My messages and speech. So hold firmly to what I have given you and be grateful.”"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah said, “O Moses! I have ˹already˺ elevated you above all others by My messages and speech."** He, God, said to him that He had chosen him and preferred him over the people of his own time. The means by which the preference was given are named, and they are two. The first is the messages, that is the revelation sent down to him. The word is read in the plural as bi-risālātī, or in the singular as bi-risālatī, meaning My message. The second is the speech, that is the direct conversation with his Lord that no other messenger of his age was given. So the man who had just been refused the sight of his Lord was being told what he had been given instead, and the honour was not small.

**"So hold firmly to what I have given you"** is the command that follows the statement of the gift. What he was to hold to was the speech he had been granted and the conversation with his Lord. He was to act upon what he had been given rather than to keep asking for more.

**"and be grateful"** is the second command. The sense of it is that he was to be of those who thank Allah for the gift, and that he was not to ask for what was beyond his capacity to bear. The request of the previous verse had come out of a heart that wanted more than it could hold. The answer to it closes with thanks, and with holding to what was already in his hand.

### The rank of Musa among the messengers

The preference spoken of here is a preference over the people of that age, the Children of Israel and their neighbours, and not over every man of every age.

Beyond that, the ranking of the messengers is set out in a way that keeps the matter in its place. Muhammad ﷺ stands at the head of all the children of Adam, those who came before him and those who came after. That is why Allah chose him to be the last of the prophets and messengers, carrying a law that remains in force until the last hour begins. With him came a following larger than the following of every prophet and messenger before him put together. After him, the next in rank of honour and virtue is Ibrahim, and after Ibrahim comes Musa son of Imran, the one who spoke with the Most Beneficent directly. So the man who spoke with his Lord is honoured above the rest of creation and still stands below the chief of the children of Adam in rank.

### The two speeches and what is left unsaid

The first time Allah spoke with Musa was when he was given prophethood, at the fire by the side of the Mount. The second time was here, at the occasion of the giving of the Torah. The wording of this verse indicates that the later speech had some additional characteristics over the first one, since the message and the speech are both named together in the statement of preference.

What the nature of that speech was, and how the hearing of it took place, cannot be settled by anyone but Allah. The explanations that the learned have given are suppositions of the mind, and only those of them can be entertained that do not go against any rule of the sacred law. None of them can be taken as certain unless it is carried by a valid argument. The safest practice in a matter like this is the one followed by the companions of the Prophet ﷺ, by their students, and by the elders who came after them. They left such questions to Allah, and they did not set out ungrounded suppositions in order to settle them.

### What the honoured position carries

The inner reading of the answer is put as a word spoken to Musa in his own situation. It is said to him that he had been held back from one thing, which is the sight. But he had been marked out for many virtues. He had been chosen for the carrying of the message, and honoured with standing and nearness. He was told to give thanks for all of it and to recognise the gifts as gifts, and he was told not to open the door of complaint about what had been withheld. What was sung of this meaning is that the ones who turn away are the ones who came in tenderness. They were loyal, so he should be patient with them if they should break away.

There is a lesson in the whole exchange for the one who is asked to believe in the unseen. The gift given to Musa was the speech and not the sight, and the sight belongs to the life to come. So the servant is asked to hold to what he has been given in this world, and to thank his Lord for it. He is to wait for the reward of looking until the place where looking is possible.

### In short

- Allah told Musa that He had preferred him over the people of his time by two things. They are the messages sent down to him and the speech given to him directly.
- The word for the messages is read in the plural as bi-risālātī or in the singular as bi-risālatī.
- The commands that follow are to hold firmly to what he was given and to be grateful. The thanks includes not asking for what is beyond his capacity to bear.
- The preference was over the people of his own age. Musa stands in rank after Muhammad ﷺ and Ibrahim among the children of Adam, honoured as the one who spoke with his Lord.
- The first speech came when he was given prophethood and the second when the Torah was given. The nature of that speech is left to Allah, following the practice of the companions and the elders. They left such questions alone.

<<<VERSE 7:145>>>
The writing of the law is placed in his hands, and with it the instruction to hold on and the promise of what would be shown. "We wrote for him on the Tablets ˹the fundamentals˺ of everything; commandments and explanations of all things. ˹We commanded,˺ “Hold to this firmly and ask your people to take the best of it. I will soon show ˹all of˺ you the home of the rebellious""

### Phrase by phrase

**"We wrote for him on the Tablets ˹the fundamentals˺ of everything"** records the giving of the law in written form. What was written on the Tablets was instruction for all things and an explanation of all things. That is the lessons to be drawn, and the detailing of what was commanded and what was forbidden. One explanation says the Tablets carried advice for the people and the details of the rulings for the lawful and the unlawful. Another says that what was on them was the Torah itself. The Book describes that book elsewhere, as the Scripture given to Musa once the generations of old had been destroyed, a light for mankind (28:43). And a further view holds that the Tablets were given to him before the Torah came down and were not a part of it.

Of the Tablets themselves the reports say that the wood of them is given as the Lote-tree of Paradise, and the stone of them as chrysolite or emerald. The number of them is put at either seven or ten.

**"commandments and explanations of all things"** is the description of the content. It was a laying out of what was required and a spelling out of what it all meant. The people would have both the rule and the reason for it, the command and the guidance that goes with the command.

**"Hold to this firmly"** is the instruction to Musa himself. The sense is that he was to take hold of it seriously, earnestly and diligently, and to be firm in the obedience it laid down.

**"and ask your people to take the best of it"** hands the same thing on to the community. The command was that he should enjoin his people to take the better of what was in it, and to hold to the fairest of its precepts. One reading of that instruction is that they were to act upon what was clear and unambiguous. What was ambiguous they were to believe in without forcing a meaning upon it. Ibn 'Abbas, in a report carried from 'Ikrimah, said that Musa was commanded to hold to the toughest of what had been laid on his people. In that way the effort of the following would be real. And it was promised that the greatest reward would be for those who carried out what they were instructed in the most complete way. The carrying out was to be done with patience and with a forgiving spirit.

**"I will soon show ˹all of˺ you the home of the rebellious"** is the promise that follows the command. What would be shown is the recompense of those who set aside the command of Allah and turn away from obedience to Him. The words name the destruction and the loss and the ruin they would meet. One reading takes the abode of the transgressors to be Hell itself. Another takes it as the abode of the wicked among the people of Pharaoh together with their followers, which is Egypt. The purpose of showing it would be that the Israelites take their example from it.

### The two views about that home

The sentence is a promise, and it says that the Israelites would soon take over the land of the sinners. What land is meant has been taken in two ways, and the disagreement rests on a question of history. Had the Israelites gone back to Egypt after the destruction of the king and his people, or had they not?

If they did go back at that time and rule the land, then this promise refers to Syria. Verse 137 indicates as much when it speaks of Allah making them inherit the land. The land of Egypt had already passed into their hands before the light appeared on the mount. If they did not go back to Egypt after the king was destroyed, then the reference may be to Egypt and to Syria together. And one report names Iraq, just as others name Egypt.

### What was written among the admonitions

Among the traditions about the Tablets, some carry the admonitions written for Musa as words of counsel. One of them says that if he wanted to be respected at the threshold of his Lord's exaltation and to be marked out for nearness to Him, then he should act beautifully toward orphans. He was not to abase the poor. It says that the Lord is the caresser and the well-wisher of orphans, and the kindly bestower of bounty on the poor. So the command was to caress those whom He caresses and not to drive away those whom He calls.

On the poor, Al-Mustafa, the chosen Prophet ﷺ, said that the poor who hold out in patience are the ones who will sit in the company of God on the Day of Resurrection. On the orphan he said that the Throne of the All-Merciful is set trembling by the crying of an orphan. God then says that whoever brings the orphan to contentment will himself be brought to contentment. Another of the admonitions says that if Musa wanted his Lord to speak of him with pride among the angels, then he should be without harm. He should keep stones and thorns away from the road of those who submit. Of that same meaning the Prophet ﷺ said that faith has more than seventy branches. The greatest of them is the declaration that there is no god but God, and the smallest of them is the taking of a harmful thing off the road. And another says that if he wanted his supplications to be answered, then he should deal with people beautifully, learn knowledge, and teach knowledge to others. The knowers have been honoured by being given knowledge, and there is more in store for them. Their dust is made sweet, their graves are lit and made wide, and on the day of rising they will be gathered among the companies of the prophets.

What the Prophet ﷺ said on this last point came with a report from Gabriel. Gabriel said to him that he should not count as lowly a servant whom God has given knowledge, since God did not count him as lowly when He gave it to him. The word from God on that day will be that He had placed His knowledge with the knowers only for a good that He desired for them. He forgives them for what has come out of them.

### Why the examples are shown to a people on the way

The promise that they would be shown the home of the rebellious was to be fulfilled during the journey itself. They would pass through the ruins of communities that had been destroyed before them. Those communities had been given guidance and had turned aside from it. They could not stay firm in the belief they had been given, and they could not withstand the pressure of the circumstances around them. They gave way to their own desires and ambitions, and so they earned the displeasure of God and were destroyed. The children of Israel were told to look at what was left of them and to take the lesson. The same end waited for them if they went the same way. God is not partial to any community in the balance of His justice, and there is no difference between one people and another.

There is a further point in how the guidance was given to Musa at all. A prophet is a man like other men. He is not a creature of another kind, and his only special quality is that he is able to keep intact the nature he was born with. Because of that he is chosen to carry the message of God and may be trusted to represent Him among the people. In that sense Musa was the best person of his community, and that is why he was chosen and given the message.

The messages themselves come down in the form of words. In this world of trial there is always the possibility that a person will put a wrong construction on the words, bending them to suit his own desires. But one who is serious and sincere in the matter of guidance, and who stands in awe of the censure of his Lord, will be able to draw from the words the same meaning that God intended by them. It is only the one who is genuinely looking for the true path who receives true guidance from the words of God.

### The honour and the warning together

The two verses before this one had placed Musa above the people of his time. This verse puts the law in his hands, and warns the same people about the home of the rebellious in the same breath. The honour was not a shield. Musa was the most honoured among the children of Adam after Muhammad ﷺ and Ibrahim, and his people were still told that they would be shown where the disobedient ended. No rank and no book in the hand exempts a community from the rule that God deals justly with all of them.

### In short

- The law was written for Musa on the Tablets, carrying instruction for all things and an explanation of all things. The Tablets are described in the reports as made of the wood of Paradise, or of chrysolite, or of emerald, and as seven or ten in number.
- One view holds that what was on the Tablets was the Torah itself, and another that they were given before the Torah. The Book describes the Scripture given to Musa after the earlier generations had been destroyed as an enlightenment for mankind.
- Musa was told to hold to it firmly and to enjoin his people to take the best of it. That best is read as acting on what is clear and believing in what is ambiguous, and the reward is promised for carrying it out in the best way.
- The home of the rebellious is read as Hell, or as the land of the people of Pharaoh, which is Egypt. The two views about whether the promise refers to Egypt or Syria, or both, rest on whether the Israelites returned to Egypt after the king was destroyed.
- The traditions of the Tablets carry admonitions about orphans and the poor and the removal of harm from the road. They join the knowledge of God to the kind treatment of people. The promise to show the ruined homes of the destroyed communities warns that no honour exempts a people from the justice of God.
