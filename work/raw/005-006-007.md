<<<VERSE 5:6>>>
This verse sets out the washing that a believer performs before prayer. It names the washing of the face, the washing of the hands up to the elbows, the wiping of the head and the washing of the feet up to the ankles. It then sets out the full bath after major impurity, and the substitute of clean earth for a person who cannot find water because he is ill, travelling, has relieved himself, or has been intimate with his wife. It ends by saying that Allah does not want to put the believers in difficulty, but wants to purify them and to complete His favour upon them, so that they may give thanks. The material places the revelation of this verse in the story of a necklace of 'A'ishah that was lost on the road back to Madinah, after which the Prophet ﷺ stopped and there was no water at dawn. The verse is the first place in the chapter where the rules of worship are set down, after the earlier verses dealt with food and marriage.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! When you rise up for prayer, wash your faces and your hands up to the elbows, wipe your heads, and wash your feet to the ankles."** The command is tied to the moment of standing for prayer. The material explains that wudu is obligatory when a person is in a state of impurity and recommended when he is already pure. It reports that early in Islam a believer had to perform wudu for every prayer, and that this was later eased. Sulayman bin Buraydah reported from his father that the Prophet ﷺ performed wudu before every prayer. On the day of the conquest of Makkah he performed it once, wiped over his leather socks, and prayed the five prayers with that one wudu. 'Umar asked him about the change, and the Prophet ﷺ replied that he had done it on purpose. Al-Fadl bin al-Mubashshir reported that he saw Jabir bin 'Abdullah pray several prayers with one wudu. Ubaydullah bin 'Abdullah bin 'Umar reported that Ibn 'Umar renewed his wudu for every prayer. The material cites a report from Asma' bint Zayd, through 'Abdullah bin Hanzalah, that the Prophet ﷺ was first commanded to renew his wudu for every prayer, and that when this became hard on him he was told to use the siwak for each prayer and to renew his wudu only when he became impure. The material concludes, as most scholars do, that renewing wudu for every prayer is encouraged and not obligatory.

The material gives several other sayings of the Prophet ﷺ on the same subject. When he came back from relieving himself and food was brought to him, he was asked whether he wanted to perform wudu, and he replied that he was not about to pray. He is also reported to have said that he was commanded to perform wudu when he stood up for prayer. These reports are recorded by Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi and Muslim.

**The intention and the Name of Allah.** The material argues that intention is required before wudu, because the verse says "when you stand to pray." It takes the phrase to mean "when you intend to pray," as in the idiom where someone is told to stand when the leader comes, meaning stand for him. It cites the saying recorded by Al-Bukhari and Muslim that actions are judged by their intentions, and that each person gets what he intended. It recommends saying the Name of Allah before wudu begins, and it cites the saying that there is no wudu for the person who does not mention the Name of Allah over it. It also recommends washing the hands three times before they are put into the water, especially after sleep, because one does not know where the hand has been during the night. Abu Hurayrah is reported to have said this in the Two Sahihs.

**The face and the beard.** The material says that the scholars of fiqh define the face as the area from the point where the hair on the head begins to the end of the chin, and from one ear to the other, whatever the amount of hair a person has. Abu Wa'il reported that he saw 'Uthman perform wudu and pass his fingers through his beard three times while washing his face, and that 'Uthman said he had seen the Prophet ﷺ do the same. At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah recorded this, and Al-Bukhari graded it as acceptable.

**The arms to the elbows.** The material says that the word translated "up to" means that the elbows are included, and it cites a usage elsewhere in the Qur'an where the same word means "together with." It recommends washing a little of the upper arm beyond the elbow. The material records the saying that the Prophet's community will be called the people with radiant limbs on the Day of Rising because of the traces of wudu, and that whoever can increase the area of that radiance should do so. Muslim recorded a related report from Abu Hurayrah that the radiance of the believer reaches as far as the water of his wudu reaches.

**The head.** Here the material gives the fullest account of the wudu of the Prophet ﷺ. Malik bin 'Amr bin Yahya al-Mazini reported from his father that a man asked 'Abdullah bin Zayd bin 'Asim to show him how the Prophet ﷺ performed wudu. He asked for water, poured it over his hands and washed them twice, rinsed his mouth and nose three times, washed his face three times, washed his forearms to the elbows twice, and then passed his wet hands over his head from the front to the back and back to the front, ending at the nape of the neck. The material says these reports show that it is necessary to wipe the whole head. Abu Dawud recorded similar descriptions from Mu'awiyah and al-Miqdad bin Ma'dikarib. Humran bin Aban reported that he saw 'Uthman perform wudu in the same way, washing the hands three times, the face three times, the forearms three times, wiping the head, and then washing the feet three times. 'Uthman then said that the Prophet ﷺ had done the same and had said that whoever performs wudu like this and prays two units without thinking of anything else will have his past sins forgiven. Abu Dawud also recorded a version of 'Uthman's report in which the head was wiped once.

**The feet.** The verse orders washing the feet up to the ankles. The material reports that Ibn 'Abbas said the verse means washing, and that A long list of early authorities held the same position, among them Ibn Mas'ud, 'Urwah and Mujahid, and the material names several more. The material says this clearly shows that the feet must be washed, and that wiping over the top of the bare foot is not enough. It cites reports from 'Uthman, 'Ali, Ibn 'Abbas, Mu'awiyah and al-Miqdad, showing the Prophet ﷺ washing his feet once, twice or three times. In a report recorded in the Two Sahihs, 'Abdullah bin 'Amr said that during a journey the Prophet ﷺ caught up with a group who were hurrying their wudu and only wiping their feet, and he called out in a loud voice that they should perform wudu thoroughly and save their heels from the Fire. Abu Hurayrah and 'A'ishah are reported to have said the same. 'Abdullah bin al-Harith bin Jaz' reported that the Prophet ﷺ said to save the heels and the soles from the Fire. Al-Bayhaqi and Al-Hakim recorded this with a sound chain. Muslim recorded that Umar bin al-Khattab saw a man leave a dry spot the size of a fingernail on his foot, and that the Prophet ﷺ told him to go back and perform wudu properly. Anas bin Malik reported a similar case. Ahmad recorded that some of the wives of the Prophet ﷺ reported a man praying with a dry spot the size of a dirham on his foot, and that he was told to repeat his wudu and his prayer. Abu Dawud collected this report from Baqiyyah, and the material says its chain is strong and reasonably good.

**The fingers.** Humran's report says that 'Uthman washed between his fingers. The collectors of the Sunan recorded Laqit bin Sabrah's question about wudu. The Prophet ﷺ replied that wudu should be performed thoroughly, that the fingers should be washed between each other, and that the nose should be rinsed well unless the person is fasting.

**The socks.** The material records that wiping over the leather socks is an established part of the Sunnah. Ahmad and Abu Dawud recorded Aws bin Abi Aws, who saw the Prophet ﷺ perform wudu, wipe over his socks, and then stand for prayer. Ahmad also recorded Jarir bin 'Abdullah al-Bajali, who embraced Islam after this chapter was revealed and then saw the Prophet ﷺ wipe over his socks. Hammam reported that Jarir wiped over his socks after relieving himself and said that he had seen the Prophet ﷺ do the same. Al-A'mash reported that Ibrahim said the scholars liked this report because Jarir embraced Islam after the chapter was revealed. The material says the practice reaches the level of widely transmitted reports, and that the Prophet ﷺ is described by both word and deed as wiping over his socks.

**"And if you are in a state of ˹full˺ impurity, then take a full bath."** The full bath is required after major impurity. The material says the rule is clear and needs little explanation. It is the same rule that is set out in the verse of the chapter on women, and the material points the reader there.

**"But if you are ill, on a journey, or have relieved yourselves, or have been intimate with your wives and cannot find water, then purify yourselves with clean earth by wiping your faces and hands."** The material says that the permission covers four cases: illness, travel, the call of nature, and sexual relations, and that a person who cannot find water after searching for it may use clean earth. The material says the earth is struck once with the hands and the face and hands are wiped with it. The material says the earth must be clean, as the Qur'an says, and it notes that the striking is to be done with two blows according to some readings, one for the face and one for the hands. The Prophet ﷺ is described as confirming that the whole of the two parts must be covered. It adds that the verse was revealed in connection with 'A'ishah's necklace. On the road back to Madinah the necklace was lost at the place called al-Bayda. The Prophet ﷺ stopped there and slept with his head in her lap. Abu Bakr came and struck her in the side and said she had delayed the people for a necklace. She wished she could not move, because the Prophet ﷺ was asleep and because of the pain. The Prophet ﷺ woke at dawn, there was no water, and the verse came down. Usayd bin al-Hudayr said to Abu Bakr's family that Allah had made the people blessed through them. The material records this as part of the story. Ibn Jarir and others also recorded it.

**"It is not Allah's Will to burden you, but to purify you and complete His favour upon you, so perhaps you will be grateful."** The material says that Allah made the rules easy for the believers, and that dry ablution is a mercy for the sick and for those without water. It explains that dry ablution is a substitute for wudu and is valid for the same things as wudu, except for certain matters. The purpose is purity, and the favour is the easy and gentle law. The verse ends by asking for gratitude, which the material says is the right response to the ease of the law.

### The virtue of wudu and what follows it

The material says that the Sunnah encourages a supplication after wudu, and that the person who does so is among those who seek purity, as the verse says. 'Uqbah bin 'Amir reported that he heard the Prophet ﷺ say that any Muslim who performs wudu well, and then prays two units with his heart and face turned to Allah, will enter Paradise. 'Umar was nearby and added that when a person completes wudu and says the testimony that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger, the eight gates of Paradise open for him, so that he may enter by whichever he wishes. Abu Hurayrah reported in Malik's collection that when a believer washes his face, every sin his eyes committed leaves his face with the water, and the same is true of his hands and his feet, until he is free of sin. Abu Malik al-Ash'ari reported that the Prophet ﷺ said purity is half of faith, that the praise of Allah fills the scale, that fasting is a shield, that patience is a light, that charity is evidence of faith, and that the Qur'an is proof for or against a person. Ibn 'Umar reported that Allah does not accept charity from someone who has taken what is not his, or prayer without purity. The material says these reports link wudu to the whole of the believer's conduct.

### In short

- The verse sets out wudu in order, with the full bath after major impurity and the dry substitute of clean earth for illness, travel, the call of nature and sexual relations.
- The verse presents these rules as a mercy and as a completion of Allah's favour, so that the believer may give thanks.
- The reports on wudu are many and well known, and they show that the feet must be washed, that the whole head must be wiped, and that wiping over socks is part of the Sunnah.

<<<VERSE 5:7>>>
"Remember Allah’s favour upon you and the covenant He made with you when you said, “We hear and obey.” And be mindful of Allah. Surely Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart"

This verse turns from the rules of worship to the covenant that the believers made with Allah. It reminds them of the favour of guidance to Islam and of the pledge they gave to hear and obey, and it ends by calling them to taqwa, since Allah knows what the hearts hide. The material places the pledge in the time of allegiance to the Prophet ﷺ, and it links the verse to the call to justice in the verses that follow.

### Phrase by phrase

#### The favour and the pledge

**"Remember Allah's favour upon you and the covenant He made with you when you said, “We hear and obey.”"** The favour is the guidance to Islam. The covenant is the pledge that the believers gave when they accepted Islam, to hear and obey the Prophet ﷺ in ease and in difficulty, and not to dispute leadership with those who hold it by right. The material says this is the pledge the Companions gave when they embraced Islam, and it says the pledge bound them to accept the law they had taken on. The material cites 57:8, which asks what is wrong with the believers that they do not believe in Allah, when the Messenger calls them to believe in their Lord and has taken their covenant. Another reading takes the verse as a reminder to the Jews of the covenant they made to follow the Prophet ﷺ and to keep his law.

#### Taqwa

**"And be mindful of Allah."** The material says taqwa means being mindful of Allah in every time and situation, and it explains that it means keeping the commands and avoiding the prohibitions. It makes the point that the pledge is only kept by a person who fears Allah, because the law by itself does not guard the one who has no fear.

#### The knowledge of the heart

**"Surely Allah knows best what is hidden in the heart."** The verse closes by stating that Allah knows the secrets and thoughts that the hearts conceal. The material says this makes obedience a matter of the heart as well as the limbs, and that nothing is hidden from Allah. It links the verse to the next, which calls the believers to justice even when they have grievances against others.

### In short

- The believers are reminded of the favour of Islam and of the pledge they gave to hear and obey.
- The verse calls for taqwa in every situation, and it reminds the reader that Allah knows the secrets of the heart.
