<<<VERSE 5:6>>>
Allah turns from rules about food and marriage to preparation for worship: "O believers! When you rise up for prayer, wash your faces and your hands up to the elbows". The verse gives the principal actions of wudu, ritual ablution, and requires a full bath for major ritual impurity. When water is unavailable or its use would cause harm, clean earth provides the permitted substitute.

The purpose is stated openly. Allah does not seek to impose needless difficulty. He seeks purification and the completion of His favour, so that believers respond with gratitude. Bodily preparation for prayer thus belongs to a larger movement away from impurity and sin and toward obedient remembrance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! When you rise up for prayer"** means when believers intend to offer salah, the prescribed prayer. If they are in a state of minor ritual impurity, wudu is obligatory. If their previous purification remains valid, renewing it is recommended rather than required. The command concerns readiness for prayer, not merely the physical act of standing.

**"wash your faces and your hands up to the elbows"** requires washing the face and both forearms, including the elbows. The face extends from the ordinary hairline to the chin and the ends of the cheeks, and across from ear to ear. A person's unusually abundant or sparse hair does not change those normal boundaries. The Prophet's ﷺ practice clarifies that the elbows are included, not excluded by the words "up to".

**"wipe your heads"** calls for passing wet hands over the head, not pouring water over it as in washing. The form bi-ru'usikum expresses contact or adherence. The explanations differ over how much of the head must be wiped. One requires the whole head on the evidence of the Prophet's ﷺ demonstrations. Al-Shafi'i holds that wiping some of its hair fulfils the minimum expressed by the wording.

**"and wash your feet to the ankles."** The feet are washed together with the ankles, the protruding bones at the junction of foot and leg. The explanation distinguishes washing bare feet from the separately established practice of wiping over khuffs, leather footwear. A light wipe over an otherwise bare foot is not treated here as the prescribed washing.

**"And if you are in a state of ˹full˺ impurity, then take a full bath."** Janabah is major ritual impurity, including the state following sexual intercourse. It requires ghusl, washing the whole body, rather than only the parts washed in wudu. The greater condition of impurity has its corresponding form of purification.

**"But if you are ill, on a journey"** introduces circumstances in which ordinary use of water may be impossible or harmful. Illness includes a condition likely to worsen through water, or whose healing would be delayed. A gloss gives wounds and smallpox as examples and associates its notice with 'Abdullah Ibn 'Awf. Travel is also mentioned, even where the traveller is not ill.

**"or have relieved yourselves, or have been intimate with your wives and cannot find water"** includes both minor impurity following the toilet and major impurity following intercourse. The inability to find water is understood after making an effort to look for it. An inability to use water safely is also included in the explanation of the concession.

**"then purify yourselves with clean earth by wiping your faces and hands."** Tayammum is purification using clean earth when the permitted conditions apply. It substitutes for purification with water in both minor and major impurity. The explanations differ about one or two contacts with the earth and about the extent of the hands and arms to be wiped, as described below.

**"It is not Allah’s Will to burden you, but to purify you and complete His favour upon you, so perhaps you will be grateful"** gives the reason for the accessible law and its concessions. Purification concerns bodily uncleanness and sins. Allah makes the duties clear and provides a substitute when water would bring harm or cannot be found. Gratitude means recognising that mercy and using the instructions obediently.

### Is fresh wudu required for every prayer?

One account says that wudu was initially required before every prayer, whether or not the previous state of purity had ended, and that the requirement was later relaxed. Ibn 'Umar continued renewing it because he believed he could manage it. His regular practice shows its recommendation, not a duty imposed on everyone.

Ahmad records the account through 'Abdullah bin Hanzalah bin Abi 'Amir Al-Ghasil, related to Ibn 'Umar through Asma' bint Zayd bin Al-Khattab. It describes the earlier command becoming difficult, followed by an instruction to use the siwak, a tooth-cleaning stick, before prayer and to renew wudu when impurity actually occurred. Abu Dawud also records it.

Sulayman bin Buraydah reports from his father that the Prophet ﷺ ordinarily made wudu for each prayer. On the day of the conquest, however, he wiped over his khuffs and offered the five prayers with a single wudu. 'Umar noticed the difference. The Prophet ﷺ told him that it was deliberate. Ahmad bin Hanbal, Muslim and the compilers of the Sunan record the report. At-Tirmidhi grades it hasan sahih, good and authentic.

Al-Fadl bin Al-Mubashshir saw Jabir bin 'Abdullah offer several prayers with one wudu, renewing it after relieving himself and wiping the tops of his khuffs. Asked whether this was personal opinion, Jabir answered that he was following what he had seen the Prophet ﷺ do. Ibn Jarir and Ibn Majah record the account.

Wudu is also not made a prerequisite for eating. Ibn 'Abbas reports that food was brought after the Prophet ﷺ returned from relieving himself. Asked whether water for wudu should be brought, he explained that he had been commanded to make wudu when preparing for prayer. Abu Dawud, An-Nasa'i and At-Tirmidhi record the report, which At-Tirmidhi grades hasan. Muslim records a similar answer asking whether he was about to pray and therefore needed wudu.

### What place do intention and preparation have?

The wording connects washing with the intention to pray. One explanation draws the requirement of intention from that connection, just as an instruction to stand on seeing a leader means to stand for him. The hadith in the Two Sahihs that deeds are judged by intentions supplies the broader principle. Another explanation states the requirement of intention through the Sunnah, alongside its place in other acts of worship.

Mentioning Allah's name before beginning is recommended in the discussion. A report transmitted by several Companions warns that there is no wudu for someone who does not name Allah over it. The explanation preserves that report while describing the invocation as recommended.

The hands should be washed before being put into the vessel. This is especially stressed after sleep. Abu Hurayrah reports in the Two Sahihs that the Prophet ﷺ instructed the person waking to wash his hands three times before putting them into the container, since he does not know where they rested during the night.

Thoroughness also includes the beard and the spaces between fingers. Abu Wa'il saw 'Uthman pass his fingers through his beard three times while washing the face. 'Uthman said that he had seen the Messenger ﷺ do it. Ahmad, Ibn Majah and At-Tirmidhi record this. At-Tirmidhi calls it hasan sahih, while Al-Bukhari grades it hasan.

Laqit bin Sabrah asked the Prophet ﷺ about wudu. The reply told him to perform it fully, wash between the fingers and rinse the nose thoroughly, except when fasting. The Sunan collections record it. The qualification protects the fasting person from carrying that particular action too far.

### How did the Companions demonstrate the washing?

Ibn 'Abbas demonstrated wudu with separate handfuls of water. He rinsed his mouth and nose, washed his face with both hands, then washed the right and left arms. He wiped the head and washed each foot with another handful. He explained that this was what he had seen the Messenger ﷺ do. Ahmad and Al-Bukhari record the demonstration.

'Abdullah bin Zayd bin 'Asim was asked to show the Prophet's ﷺ wudu. He washed his hands twice, rinsed his mouth and nose three times, washed the face three times, and washed the forearms and elbows twice. For the head, his wet hands travelled from the front to the nape and back to the starting place. He then washed the feet. The Two Sahihs record the account.

A similar demonstration is reported from 'Ali through 'Abdu Khayr. Abu Dawud records related descriptions from Mu'awiyah and Al-Miqdad bin Ma'dikarib. The explanation requiring the entire head to be wiped relies on these reports. The alternative minimum stated by Al-Shafi'i should still be recognised as a different juristic reading, not silently removed.

Humran bin Aban watched 'Uthman wash the hands three times, rinse the mouth and nose, and wash the face and each forearm three times. He then wiped the head and washed each foot three times. Abdur-Razzaq records it, as do Al-Bukhari and Muslim. Abu Dawud's narration specifies one wiping of the head.

Afterward 'Uthman said that the Prophet ﷺ had performed wudu in this manner and promised forgiveness of past sins to the person who followed it with two attentive units of prayer. The attention belongs to the promise too. The outward washing is followed by prayer without allowing the mind to wander elsewhere.

### Why are the elbows, order and ankles discussed?

The preposition ila, rendered "up to", can include the endpoint. The explanation compares another Qur'anic use concerning adding an orphan's property to one's own. Here the Sunnah establishes inclusion of the elbows. Washing a little of the upper arm beyond them is also recommended.

Abu Hurayrah relates in Al-Bukhari and Muslim that the community will be recognised on the Resurrection by radiance left by wudu. The report encourages extending that radiance. Muslim also records the saying that the believer's adornment reaches where the water of wudu reaches. These reports connect careful washing with a future honour.

The feet are read as arjulakum, grammatically connected to the arms that are washed. Another reading is arjulikum, explained here through its proximity to the grammatical form used for the head. Neither is used in this discussion to cancel the reports of washing bare feet.

Al-Shafi'i also draws a requirement of order from the placement of the head between arms and feet. The two washed parts are separated by a wiped part, which he understands as indicating the intended sequence. The explanation thus distinguishes the different actions rather than treating the list as an interchangeable set.

### Why insist on washing the feet thoroughly?

Ibn Abi Hatim records Ibn 'Abbas explaining the verse as washing the feet. The same understanding is attributed to Ibn Mas'ud, 'Urwah, 'Ata' and 'Ikrimah. Other named early authorities include Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Ibrahim, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi, Muqatil bin Hayyan, Az-Zuhri and Ibrahim At-Taymi. The demonstrations of 'Uthman, 'Ali and other Companions likewise describe washing, whether once, twice or three times.

The Two Sahihs record 'Abdullah bin 'Amr describing a journey when the Prophet ﷺ caught up with people hurrying to prepare for the afternoon prayer. They were merely wiping their feet. He called out loudly that they should complete wudu properly, warning their heels of the Fire. Abu Hurayrah also relates the warning in the Two Sahihs, and Muslim records it from 'A'ishah.

'Abdullah bin Al-Harith bin Jaz' reports a warning that names both the heels and the soles. Al-Hakim and Al-Bayhaqi record it, with its chain described as sahih, authentic. Neglect of the underside is therefore no less significant than neglect of the top.

Muslim records 'Umar bin Al-Khattab describing a man who left a dry patch the size of a fingernail on his foot. The Prophet ﷺ instructed him to return and improve his wudu. Al-Bayhaqi records a similar event from Anas bin Malik.

Ahmad records from one of the Prophet's ﷺ wives that a man was seen praying with a dry area on his foot the size of a dirham. He was told to repeat wudu. Abu Dawud's narration also includes repeating the prayer. The chain is described as strong and reasonably good. These reports explain why haste must not leave part of the required area unwashed.

### Is wiping over khuffs still allowed?

Yes. The washing of bare feet and the wiping of footwear under its conditions are different rulings. Ahmad records Aws bin Abi Aws seeing the Prophet ﷺ perform wudu, wipe over his khuffs and pray. Abu Dawud's version places this after relieving himself and mentions wiping over his khuffs and feet.

Jarir bin 'Abdullah Al-Bajali said that he accepted Islam after Surah Al-Ma'idah had been revealed and then saw the Prophet ﷺ wipe over his khuffs. Ahmad records his statement. In the Two Sahihs, Hammam describes Jarir doing the same after relieving himself and explaining that he was following the Prophet ﷺ.

Al-A'mash reports Ibrahim's observation that this narration was especially valued because Jarir's Islam came after the surah. It shows that the permission was not cancelled by the verse's requirement of washing. Muslim records that wording.

The reports of the Prophet's ﷺ statements and actions on wiping are described as mutawatir, transmitted through numerous routes. The condition stated here is that the footwear was put on while the person had wudu. The account does not turn this into permission to omit purification altogether.

### When is tayammum the proper substitute?

The absence of usable water, after seeking it, and illness in which water would harm or delay recovery provide the stated circumstances. Tayammum stands in place of wudu or ghusl for the person entitled to it. The fact that it is easier does not make it an inferior act of disobedience. Its authority comes from Allah's permission.

The descriptions differ in their details. One describes a single contact with the earth, followed by wiping the face and hands. Other glosses describe two contacts, the first for the face and the second for the hands. The latter legal explanation includes the elbows and requires the wiping to cover the whole of those areas. These are recorded as different explanations rather than combined into one supposedly undisputed procedure.

Clean earth is required. A gloss speaks of clean, raised ground and dust from it. The purpose is not to coat the whole body with dirt. Even when the original obligation is a full bath, the substitute uses the specified wiping actions.

Al-Bukhari records the occasion through 'A'ishah. On a return journey toward Madinah, her necklace broke and was lost near Al-Bayda. The party stopped. The Prophet ﷺ slept with his head on her lap. Abu Bakr reproached her for holding everyone up and struck her side. She could not move because of the Prophet ﷺ resting there and was distressed by the pain.

At dawn no water was available. The verse was then revealed. Usayd bin Al-Hudayr spoke of the blessing that had reached the people through Abu Bakr's family. A difficulty that seemed only to delay the travellers became the occasion for a lasting concession.

### What should follow wudu?

'Uqbah bin 'Amir tells of returning from his turn guarding camels and arriving during a speech by the Prophet ﷺ. He heard the promise of Paradise for a Muslim who performed wudu properly and then offered two units of prayer with full attention of heart and face.

'Umar, standing nearby, told him that an earlier statement was also especially good. Whoever made wudu properly and testified that Allah alone is worthy of worship and Muhammad ﷺ is His servant and Messenger would have the eight gates of Paradise opened for him. He could enter through whichever he wished. Ahmad, Muslim and the Sunan compilers record the report, with the cited wording belonging to Muslim.

Supplication after wudu thus fits the verse's purpose. The believer does not merely finish washing and forget why he washed. Testimony and prayer join bodily preparation to faith and remembrance.

### How does purification remove sins?

Malik and Muslim record Abu Hurayrah describing the Prophet's ﷺ teaching about the washing. As the face is washed, sins committed through the eyes depart with the water or its final drop. With the hands, their sinful actions depart. With the feet, the sins toward which they walked depart. The believer emerges cleansed.

Muslim records Abu Malik Al-Ash'ari relating that purity is half of faith. Al-hamdu lillah, praise belonging to Allah, fills the mizan, the scale. Remembrance through glorifying Allah and declaring His greatness is described as filling the space between earth and heaven. The report names fasting as a shield, sabr or patience as light, and sadaqah or charity as evidence. The Qur'an can testify for a person or against him. Each morning's actions amount to a transaction in which a person either frees his soul or ruins it.

Muslim also records Ibn 'Umar reporting that prayer is not accepted without purification, nor charity from ghulul, the wrongful taking of entrusted spoils. An outwardly religious act does not cancel the conditions of integrity attached to it.

### What inward cleansing accompanies the outward washing?

Water prepares the body for prayer, while prayer is pictured as a spring cleansing harmful emotions and thoughts. The actions of wudu can awaken an unspoken plea for reform. Washing the hands recalls the need to abandon wrongdoing done through them. Rinsing the mouth and washing the face brings to mind unlawful food, harmful speech and improper looking.

Wiping the head suggests turning away from corrupt plans and thoughts. Washing the feet becomes a wish not to walk toward wrongdoing again. These are reflections on the act, not a prescribed spoken formula required for each limb. The whole preparation becomes an embodied request to turn from evil.

A further allusive account distinguishes outward and inward purity. Outward purity concerns impurity itself, cleansing after bodily discharge or sexual activity, and care concerning hair, nails and dirt. It introduces three inward duties, but the available passage only develops the first two.

The first is cleansing the limbs from sins such as lies, backbiting, betrayal, unlawful consumption and illicit looking. Obedience and reverence then replace those habits. Its signs include remembrance on the tongue, awareness of Allah's promise in the heart and gratitude for His favours. Visiting the sick and cemeteries, praying for good people and seeking Paradise belong to the portrait.

The second concerns the heart's character. Self-admiration damages friendship, envy diminishes regard for others, and pride darkens the heart. Hypocrisy dries up obedience. Avarice undermines respect, hostility blocks familiarity, and frivolity weakens companionship. Removing these traits is another kind of washing.

The ascetic portrait that follows is of someone who avoids easy excuses and doubtful indulgences, fears the final judgment and is content with little food and poor clothing. His supplies are taqwa, his foundation is faith, his stopping place is the grave and his destination is the Hereafter. His reluctance to rely on concessions describes this inward discipline, not a cancellation of the verse's lawful tayammum.

Even such a person does not congratulate himself on guaranteed success. He fears what will be disclosed about his real condition and pleads with Allah. The account breaks off during that plea, without presenting the third inward duty. Its unfinished part should not be supplied from imagination.

### In short

- Wudu is required for prayer after minor impurity. Ghusl is required for major impurity, while renewing an already valid wudu is recommended rather than obligatory.
- The face, arms with elbows and feet with ankles are washed, and the head is wiped. The explanations preserve disagreement about the minimum head wiping and details of tayammum.
- Thorough washing matters, including heels and spaces between fingers. Wiping over khuffs remains established under its conditions.
- When water is unavailable or harmful, clean earth provides the permitted substitute. Allah intends purification and ease, not needless hardship.
- The outward actions prepare for attentive prayer and call the heart and limbs away from sin. Gratitude is the proper response to this complete favour.

<<<VERSE 5:7>>>
Allah calls believers to remember the gift of guidance and the commitment it brings: "Remember Allah’s favour upon you and the covenant He made with you". Their answer was not merely that they had heard a message. They declared, "We hear and obey." The verse asks that this pledge govern life, including the intentions no other person can see.

After explaining practical duties and the mercy within the rules of purification, the reminder returns to their foundation. Islam and the sending of the Messenger ﷺ are favours. Following the guidance is the responsibility accepted in response.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Remember Allah’s favour upon you"** means recalling His guidance to Islam and the religion He has given through His Messenger ﷺ. Remembrance is more than remembering an event. It involves recognising that faith, the law and the opportunity to obey are gifts rather than achievements independent of Allah.

**"and the covenant He made with you"** refers, in the principal explanation, to the pledge given to the Prophet ﷺ on accepting Islam. It includes following him, supporting him, practising the law he conveys and passing it on while accepting it oneself. Another gloss connects it with the pledge on the Day of the Covenant.

**"when you said, “We hear and obey.”"** states the believer's response to Allah's commands and prohibitions. The pledge applies when obedience is welcome and when it is difficult, in ease and hardship, not only when it agrees with personal preference.

**"And be mindful of Allah."** Taqwa means guarding against disobedience out of awareness of Him. Here it especially requires fidelity to the covenant, carrying out what is commanded and avoiding what is forbidden. There is no condition in which betrayal becomes hidden from the One who took the pledge.

**"Surely Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart"** includes loyalty and treachery before they are visible in action. He knows concealed thoughts and intentions. Outward words of obedience cannot conceal inward refusal from Him.

### What did the pledge require?

The pledge to the Messenger ﷺ included hearing and obeying in energetic and reluctant moments, even when others were preferred in the distribution of rights. It also included not contesting leadership with those entitled to it. The reminder presents obedience as a commitment extending beyond moments of personal satisfaction.

Another verse joins the Messenger's call to belief with the covenant already taken (57:8). The connection makes the present command a summons to fulfil an accepted responsibility, not merely to admire the idea of faith.

A further interpretation, reported from Ibn 'Abbas, takes this as a reminder to the Jews of their undertaking to follow Muhammad ﷺ and adhere to his law. That reading is preserved alongside the explanation concerning Muslims' pledge. Both give the covenant practical consequences.

### How does faith shape the inward response?

Faith is described as a bond between Allah and His servant. The servant undertakes to live in awareness of Him, and Allah provides guardianship in this life and the next. The first expression of fidelity is steadfastness toward the Lord in the whole pattern of life.

Looking at the universe should awaken recognition of His greatness and power. Looking at oneself should awaken awareness of His grace and mercy. Love, fear and concern should take their direction from Him. Prayer, remembrance, obedience and spending for His cause express that orientation, and devotion should become a source of willing joy rather than mere outward compliance.

The covenant also requires justice toward other people. The following verse develops that duty, especially when resentment makes fairness difficult. Here the emphasis remains on the faithful heart from which both worship and just dealings should proceed.

### In short

- Guidance to Islam and the sending of the Messenger ﷺ are favours to remember with gratitude.
- The covenant requires genuine hearing and obedience, including when personal preference or advantage points elsewhere.
- Fidelity includes inward loyalty as well as outward conduct, because Allah knows concealed intentions.
- A life centred on Allah should bear fruit in worship, remembrance and just treatment of others.
