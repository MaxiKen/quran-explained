<<<VERSE 4:23>>>
"Also forbidden to you for marriage are your mothers, your daughters, your sisters, your paternal and maternal aunts, your brother's daughters, your sister's daughters, your foster-mothers, your foster-sisters, your mothers-in-law, your stepdaughters under your guardianship if you have consummated marriage with their mothers—but if you have not, then you can marry them—nor the wives of your own sons, nor two sisters together at the same time—except what was done previously. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful." This verse lists the women forbidden for marriage: mothers, daughters, sisters, aunts, nieces, foster-mothers, foster-sisters, mothers-in-law, stepdaughters (if marriage consummated), wives of sons, and two sisters together. It excepts what was done previously, and says Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

### Phrase by phrase

"Your mothers, your daughters, your sisters" are forbidden by blood relation.

**"Your paternal and maternal aunts"** are forbidden by blood relation.

**"Your brother's daughters, your sister's daughters"** are forbidden by blood relation.

**"Your foster-mothers, your foster-sisters"** are forbidden by suckling, which prohibits what birth prohibits.

**"Your mothers-in-law"** are forbidden by marriage.

**"Your stepdaughters under your guardianship if you have consummated marriage with their mothers"** are forbidden if the marriage was consummated. Otherwise they may be married.

**"Nor the wives of your own sons"** are forbidden by marriage.

**"Nor two sisters together at the same time"** is forbidden, except what was done previously.

**"Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"** affirms Allah's forgiveness and mercy.

### Degrees of prohibition

Ibn Abi Hatim recorded that Ibn Abbas said Allah prohibited seven types of relatives by blood and seven by marriage, then recited this verse. At-Tabari recorded Ibn Abbas saying seven degrees of blood relation and seven degrees of marriage relation are prohibited. Suckling establishes prohibited degrees just as blood does: "Suckling prohibits what birth prohibits." Less than five incidents of suckling do not establish prohibition. Aishah reported that among the parts of the Qur'an revealed was "Ten incidents of suckling establishes the prohibition," later abrogated with five, and the Prophet died while this was still recited. Sahlah bint Suhayl narrated that the Prophet ordered her to suckle Salim, the freed slave of Abu Hudhayfah, with five. The verse cites 2:233, 3:40, 5:1, 33:4, 33:37, 33:40. It was commented on by Ibn Abbas, Ad-Dahhak, Az-Zuhri, and others. Collectors include Al-Bukhari and At-Tabari. The verse involves iddah, nikah, shariah, sunnah, halal, haram, and related terms. The scholars and terms mentioned include Abu Burdah ibn Niyar, Abu Hanifah, Abu Hudhayfah, Abu Salamah, Abu Sufyan, Abu Yusuf, Ahmad, A'ishah, Al-Baqarah, al-Bukhri, Ad-Dahhak bin Fayruz, hadith, halal, haram, Al-Hasan bin Muhammad, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Firoz, Ibn Jurayj, ibrahim, iddah, makkah, Muslim, nikah, shariah, Suhayl, sunnah, Two Sahih, Umm Habibah, Umm Salamah, Az-Zuhri.

### In short

- The verse lists women forbidden for marriage by blood, suckling and marriage.
- Suckling prohibits what birth prohibits. Five incidents suffice.
- Past cases are excepted.
- Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.
- The discussion adds the views of Ibn Abbas and others.
