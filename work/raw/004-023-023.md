<<<VERSE 4:23>>>
This verse completes the list of women who are forbidden in marriage: "˹Also˺ forbidden to you for marriage are your mothers, your daughters, your sisters, your paternal and maternal aunts, your brother’s daughters, your sister’s daughters, your foster-mothers, your foster-sisters, your mothers-in-law, your stepdaughters under your guardianship if you have consummated marriage with their mothers—but if you have not, then you can marry them—nor the wives of your own sons, nor two sisters together at the same time—except what was done previously. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful" (4:23).

It sets out the degrees of women who can never be married, because of blood, because of suckling or because of marriage. Ibn ʿAbbās reported that Allah said, "I have forbidden you seven kinds of relatives by blood and seven by marriage," and then recited this verse. Aṭ-Ṭabarī recorded the same words from him about the seven degrees of blood relation and the seven of marriage.

Many natural desires make themselves felt in human beings, and sexual desire is one of them. Islamic law sets a limit for every human feeling, and it has set limits for sexual relations as well. Only the union of a man and a woman that is made through a solemn and recognised social agreement, which is marriage, is valid. Along with satisfying a natural desire, the law requires an atmosphere of chastity in family life. So certain close relatives are declared forbidden, to keep them beyond and above sexual desire. Some of the women in this verse are forbidden forever and can never become lawful under any condition. Others are forbidden only for a time.

### Phrase by phrase

**"your mothers, your daughters, your sisters"**. Marrying one's own mother is unlawful, and the word "mothers" takes in all grandmothers, on the father's side and the mother's. "Daughters" means one's real daughter, the daughter of one's daughter and the daughter of one's son. In short, a daughter, granddaughter or great-granddaughter, whether through a son or a daughter, is unlawful. Adopted children are different. Marrying an adopted son or daughter, or their children, is permissible, as long as it is not unlawful for some other reason. A girl born from adultery is treated as a daughter, and marrying her is not valid. Marrying a stepdaughter, meaning a daughter the wife brought from another husband, has rules of its own that come later.

Sisters are forbidden whether they are real sisters, half-sisters on the father's side only (ʿallātī) or half-sisters on the mother's side only (akhyāfī).

**"your paternal and maternal aunts, your brother’s daughters, your sister’s daughters"**. A paternal aunt is the real sister of one's father, or his half-sister from the father's side or from the mother's side. All three kinds are forbidden. A maternal aunt is the sister of one's mother, in the same three kinds. Nieces are also forbidden, whether the brother or sister is a real sibling or a half-sibling of either type. Aunts include the sisters of one's grandfathers and grandmothers, and nieces include their children, as one explanation spells out.

**"your foster-mothers, your foster-sisters"**. A woman who nursed you is forbidden to you, just as the mother who bore you is. A woman who nursed one of your siblings is, in the same way, a mother to the whole bond, and her daughters become your sisters through suckling. The details are given in the section below.

**"your mothers-in-law"**. The mother of one's wife is forbidden to him as soon as the marriage contract is made, whether or not he ever had relations with his wife. The word "mothers" includes the wife's grandmothers, whether on her mother's or father's side, by blood or by suckling. The same applies to the mother of a woman he slept with thinking she was his wife, or with whom he committed adultery, or whom he touched with desire. Short explanations of the verse say the same: the mother-in-law is forbidden whether or not the marriage with her daughter was consummated.

**"your stepdaughters under your guardianship if you have consummated marriage with their mothers—but if you have not, then you can marry them"**. When a man marries a woman and has relations with her after the marriage, her daughter from another husband becomes forbidden to him, and so do her granddaughters, on either side. If he divorced the mother before ever being with her, her daughter and granddaughter are not forbidden to him. But if, after the marriage contract, he touches his wife with desire, or looks at her private parts with desire, that too counts as having relations with her, and it makes her daughter forbidden. "Having gone in unto them" means sexual relations, as Ibn ʿAbbās and several others said.

The words "your women" are general. They cover not only a lawfully wedded wife. If a man had relations with a woman who was not truly his wife, either because he wrongly believed that she was, or through adultery, the daughter and granddaughter of that woman also become forbidden to him.

The words "under your guardianship" describe the usual situation, a girl brought up in the stepfather's house. They do not limit the ruling. The Arabic term rabāʾib, the plural of rabībah, means a wife's daughter from another husband who is being raised in one's care. The majority of scholars say that the stepdaughter is forbidden to her stepfather, if he has consummated his marriage to her mother, whether or not she was in his care.

**"nor the wives of your own sons"**. The wife of one's own son is forbidden. The word "son" also covers grandsons, on the father's or mother's side, so their wives are forbidden too. The words "from your own loins" are in the verse to exclude the adopted son. Marrying his wife is lawful. A foster son, though, is treated like a son by birth, and his wife is forbidden.

**"nor two sisters together at the same time"**. It is also forbidden to have two sisters together as wives. They may be real sisters, half-sisters on the father's side or half-sisters on the mother's side. They may be sisters by blood or by suckling. Allah prohibits taking two sisters as rival wives, or as rival female servants. If one sister has been divorced, a man may marry the other, but only after the first one's waiting period, the ʿiddah, has ended. A marriage during the ʿiddah is not permitted.

**"except what was done previously"**. Whatever happened in the days of ignorance is not held against anyone. The same words appear in 4:22 and carry the same sense. Now that people have accepted Islam, their past deeds are not counted, but they must keep away from those practices from now on.

**"Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"**. Anything people did wrongly before Islam is overlooked by Allah once they embrace Islam, and they can be sure He will turn to them with the full reach of His mercy. Another explanation says that He forgives what happened in the days of ignorance and shows mercy for what happens in Islam, if they repent.

### How suckling creates a prohibition

Ibn ʿAbbās's words show that the verse treats milk kinship like blood kinship. Al-Bukhārī and Muslim recorded that ʿĀʾishah said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "Suckling makes unlawful what birth makes unlawful."

In another wording recorded by Muslim: "Suckling establishes the same prohibitions that blood does." Another report in Mishkāt (page 273), from Muslim, says that Allah has made unlawful through suckling what He has made unlawful through kinship. The principle gives a whole network. When a woman nurses a boy or girl in the nursing period, she is their foster-mother and her husband is their foster-father. Her children become their brothers and sisters, her sisters become their maternal aunts, her husband's brothers become their uncles on the father's side, and his sisters become their paternal aunts. Every marriage that is forbidden between blood relatives becomes equally forbidden between milk relatives.

Scholars differ on how much nursing is needed. Less than five separate feedings do not create the prohibition, according to one view. Muslim recorded that ʿĀʾishah said that among the verses revealed, there was "Ten feedings make marriage unlawful", and that it was later replaced by five, and that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ died while this wording was still recited as part of the Qur'an. A hadith from Sahlah bint Suhayl says that the Messenger ﷺ told her to nurse Sālim, the freed slave of Abū Ḥudhayfah, five times. Another explanation also names five feedings within the first two years, and adds that the prohibition covers the foster-sisters' daughters. Among the Ḥanafī jurists, the quantity and the number of feedings make no difference, and the prohibition stands in every case. This is the view stated in the explanation of the word for foster-mothers.

Scholars also differ on the age. The nursing must take place at the usual time in early childhood. The Prophet ﷺ said that fosterage is only from hunger, meaning that the prohibition applies when the child has no way to grow except by nursing. Al-Bukhārī and Muslim recorded it. It must happen before the age of two, as in the verse in Sūrat al-Baqarah that says mothers breastfeed their children for two whole years (2:233). According to Imām Abū Ḥanīfah the period runs from birth until two and a half years. Other jurists, including his own students Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad, say two years only. If a boy or girl is nursed after that, no prohibition arises.

The rulings that follow from this are as follows:

1. If a boy and a girl were nursed by the same woman, they cannot marry each other. Marrying the daughter of a foster-brother or a foster-sister is also not allowed.
2. It is lawful to marry the birth mother of a foster-brother or foster-sister. It is also lawful to marry the foster-mother of one's own sister's foster-sister, or the foster-sister of one's own sister.
3. The prohibition arises if the child takes the milk during the nursing period through the mouth or the nose. If the milk is given in any other way, or injected, it does not arise.
4. No milk except that of the woman nursing the child creates fosterage. Milk of animals or of men does not.
5. If the milk is mixed with medicine or with the milk of a goat, cow or buffalo, the prohibition arises only if the woman's milk is the larger part or at least equal. If it is less, it does not arise.
6. If a man's breasts happen to produce milk, it does not create a prohibition.
7. If a woman lets a child hold her nipple in its mouth and it is not certain that the child swallowed any milk, no prohibition arises, because a prohibition is not established when the actual nursing is doubtful.
8. If a man marries a woman and another woman claims she nursed them both, and the couple both confirm it, the marriage is ruled invalid. If they both deny it, they are not required to separate. If the woman who made the claim appears God-fearing and practising, it is better for them to separate by divorce.
9. The testimony of two practising Muslims is needed to prove a prohibition through suckling. One man or one woman is not enough. But as it is a serious matter, on which the lawfulness of a whole married life depends, caution is always wise. Some jurists rule that if someone intends to marry a woman and a single practising Muslim testifies that they are foster brother and sister, they may not marry. If one witness comes forward after they have married, it is safe and prudent to separate.
10. By the usual rules of evidence, one man and two women count as two male witnesses, so a foster relationship is proved if one man and two women testify to the nursing.

### Why the Prophet ﷺ could not marry Umm Salamah's daughter

The two Ṣaḥīḥs record that Umm Ḥabībah said, "O Messenger of Allah, marry my sister, the daughter of Abū Sufyān." In one wording the sister is named ʿAzzah bint Abī Sufyān. He asked, "Would you like me to do that?" She said, "I would not give you up for anything. But the person I would most like to share the good with is my sister." He said, "That is not lawful for me." She said, "We were told that you want to marry the daughter of Abū Salamah." He asked, "The daughter of Umm Salamah?" She said, "Yes." He said:

> "Even if she were not my stepdaughter in my care, she would still not be lawful for me. She is the daughter of my brother through suckling, since Thuwaybah nursed me and Abū Salamah. So do not offer me your daughters or your sisters."

In another wording recorded by al-Bukhārī he said, "Had I not married Umm Salamah, her daughter would not have been lawful for me anyway." So he said that his marriage to Umm Salamah was the real reason for the prohibition.

### Is the wife of an adopted son lawful?

The verse forbids the wives of "your sons who are of your own loins". That excludes adopted sons, since adoption was common in the days of ignorance. Allah says: "So when Zaid totally lost interest in ˹keeping˺ his wife, We gave her to you in marriage, so that there would be no blame on the believers for marrying the ex-wives of their adopted sons after their divorce" (33:37).

Ibn Jurayj said he asked ʿAṭāʾ about the words "the wives of your sons who are of your own loins". ʿAṭāʾ said they were told that when the Prophet ﷺ married the former wife of Zayd, who had been his adopted son before Islam stopped that practice, the idolaters in Makkah criticised him. Allah revealed these words, and also: "Nor does He regard your adopted children as your real children" (33:4), and: "Muḥammad is not the father of any of your men" (33:40).

Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded that al-Ḥasan ibn Muḥammad said the two phrases, "the wives of your sons" and "your wives' mothers", are wide in their reach. Ṭāwūs, Ibrāhīm, az-Zuhrī and Makḥūl explained them the same way. The two phrases take in these women whether or not the marriage was consummated, and there is a consensus on the ruling.

There is a doubt here. Why is the former wife of one's foster son forbidden to him, as most scholars hold, though they are not related by blood? The answer is a saying of the Prophet ﷺ: "Suckling makes unlawful what blood relations make unlawful."

### Two sisters, and a woman with her aunt

No one may take or keep two sisters as rival wives. This is the consensus of the scholars among the Companions, their followers and the Imams, past and present. Whoever accepts Islam while married to two sisters is given the choice to keep one and divorce the other. Aḥmad recorded a statement of aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk ibn Fayruz, who quoted his father: "I became Muslim while married to two sisters, and the Prophet ﷺ ordered me to divorce one of them." Ibn Firoz ad-Daylamī related in the same way from his father that when he became Muslim, he had two sisters as wives, and the Prophet ﷺ told him to part with one by divorce and keep the other. This is in Mishkāt (page 274).

These reports show that, just as it is not lawful after accepting Islam to marry one's father's wife or to join two sisters in marriage, it is also not lawful to keep up such marriages if someone made them before becoming Muslim. Al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib said that the Prophet ﷺ sent Abū Burdah ibn Niyar to put to death a man who had married his father's wife.

The Sunnah adds that a man may not marry a woman together with her paternal aunt or her maternal aunt. The Prophet ﷺ said, as al-Bukhārī and Muslim recorded, "Do not combine a woman with her paternal aunt, nor a woman with her maternal aunt." Jurists state a general rule from this. If any two women are such that, were one of them a male, their marriage would be invalid, then those two cannot be combined as wives of one man. According to one explanation, it is permissible to marry each of them separately, or to own both as female slaves, but a man may have relations with only one of them.

### Surrender, not reasons

The allusion in this verse is that the Sharīʿah rests on being a servant, not on one's own effort and reasoning. The law of the religion is handed down, not worked out by reason, and the Sunnah is built on submission, not on looking for causes. Surrender is an easy road. Its home thrives, and its aim is the approval of the All-Merciful. The way of self-exertion and self-determination is hard. Their home is in ruins, and their goal is unwholesome. So leap away from the road of self-exertion and hold to surrender. Avoid self-determination and the hunt for reasons.

Whatever the Sharīʿah has declared forbidden, regard it as forbidden, a decree of Allah's Desire, founded on His Will. If the same thing had been allowed in place of forbidden, it would be just the same, since it would be current in the holy Sharīʿah, with no cause or ambiguity. For He is the Real, and majestic is His majesty. "Allah does what He wills" (3:40), and "Allah commands what He wills" (5:1). He forbids what He wills to whom He wills, and He allows what He wills to whom He wills. His work has no cause, and His decree has no one to object.

### In short

- The verse names the women forbidden in marriage: mothers and grandmothers, daughters and granddaughters, sisters, aunts, nieces, foster-mothers and foster-sisters, mothers-in-law, stepdaughters whose mothers were consummated with, sons' wives, and two sisters together.
- Suckling creates the same prohibitions as blood. Scholars differ on how many feedings and on the age limit, with five feedings within two years in one view and no minimum in the Ḥanafī view, and an upper age of two or two and a half years.
- A mother-in-law is forbidden from the marriage contract onward, while a stepdaughter becomes forbidden only after relations with her mother. The wife of an adopted son is not forbidden, but the wife of a foster son is.
- Two sisters, or a woman and her aunt, may not be married together, and anyone who becomes Muslim with two sisters must part from one.
- What was done before is forgiven, but such marriages must not be kept up. Inwardly, the Sharīʿah calls for surrender and not for hunting for reasons.
