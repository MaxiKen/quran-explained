<<<VERSE 4:29>>>
From marriage law to the two foundations of all social law, property and life: "O believers! Do not devour one another's wealth illegally, but rather trade by mutual consent. And do not kill ˹each other or˺ yourselves. Surely Allah is ever Merciful to you". The surah has guarded the orphan, the woman and the heir. Now it guards everyone.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not devour one another's wealth illegally"** forbids every false means of taking property. The word is literally "do not eat", but in common usage it means do not usurp another's property by any false means at all, for the unauthorized use of what someone else owns is called eating, whether or not it is eatable. The word bāṭil, false means, covers in the view of ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd and the majority of the Companions every impermissible form: theft, robbery, usurpation, breach of trust, bribery, interest and gambling, and all false transactions, as Abū Ḥayyān notes in his al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ. The same reading includes a man's own property spent on what is religiously sinful, or in straight extravagance, for that too is devouring in the verse's sense. The methods forbidden are often dressed as lawful, ribā and gambling and other wicked ways that appear legal, while Allah knows that those involved seek only the interest.

**"but rather trade by mutual consent"** is the one door left open, and the whole of the verse's economics stands in it, as the next section shows.

**"And do not kill ˹each other or˺ yourselves"** covers both the taking of another's life without right and suicide, and beyond both, the committing of Allah's prohibitions generally, falling into sin and eating up property unjustly, for that too is a killing of the self.

**"Surely Allah is ever Merciful to you"** closes the command. The injunctions come as mercy, so that you may guard against the punishment of the life to come and stay safe from punishments that could afflict you right here in the present life.

### The garment and the extra dirham

The earliest readers pressed the verse into corners. There is the buyer of a garment who declares that he will keep it if it pleases him, and otherwise return it with an extra dirham added. The comment on Ibn ʿAbbās's authority, in Ibn Jarīr's record, is that this is exactly what Allah meant by: "Eat not up your property among yourselves unjustly" (2:188). ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that when this verse came down, some Muslims said that Allah had forbidden them to eat each other's property unjustly, and food is the best property, so none of them might eat anyone else's food. Then Allah sent down: "There is no restriction on the blind" (24:61), to the end of the verse, and Qatādah said similarly.

### What counts as trade

Two conditions make a transaction lawful under this verse. It must be trade, an exchange of property for property, and it must be by mutual consent. Where there is no commodity on one side, there is no trade but deception. An interest-based loan pays the interest for the time allowed, and time is not a commodity. In speculation and gambling the commodity exists on one side but its counterpart is doubtful. In forward trading, the commodity does not exist and a promise stands in its place. Muslim jurists call these void sales, al-baiʿ al-bāṭil. And where both commodities exist but consent is missing, the sale is corrupt, al-baiʿ al-fāṣid, and equally forbidden. The second condition reaches further than it looks. Consent squeezed out by compulsion is not consent. The trader who corners the market's daily needs, stocks up, raises the price and leaves the customer no choice has taken a consent that is null and void. The husband who spins the terms of married life into thorns until his wife yields up her dower has taken nothing. The man ready to offer a bribe because his valid work will not move without one has not consented of his own free will either.

Trade itself is read generously. According to at-Tafsīr al-Mazharī, dealings of service, labour and tenancy are included, for in a sale one acquires property for property, and in hiring, property for service, and trade covers both. Mujāhid read the exception as selling and buying, or giving someone a gift. In the two Ṣaḥīḥs, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "The seller and the buyer retain the right to change their mind as long as they have not parted." Al-Bukhārī's wording reads: "When two men conduct a transaction, they retain their right to change their mind as long as they have not parted." The option before parting is part of the mutual consent the verse requires, and this one word of the Qur'an, trade by consent, is the key to the whole treasure of Muslim jurisprudence on sales and hiring. Every form the shariah forbids stands outside the approved core.

### The cleanest earning

The verse names trade for a reason: trade and labour are the best means of earning among all open to man. The Prophet ﷺ, asked by Rāfiʿ ibn Khadīj which earning was the purest, said: "Man's work with his own hands, and all honest sale transactions." Al-Mundhirī carries it in his at-Targhīb. Abū Saʿīd al-Khudhrī narrates that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The truthful and trustworthy trader shall be with the prophets and the truthful and the martyrs", recorded by at-Tirmidhī. Anas narrates: "The truthful trader shall be under the shade of the Throne on the Day of Doom", reported by al-Iṣfahānī in his at-Targhīb. From Muʿādh ibn Jabal comes the price of cleanness: the trader who does not lie when he talks, commits no breach when trusted, does not pick faults in what he buys, does not over-praise what he sells, does not evade a debt he owes and does not harass a debtor who owes him. Otherwise the trade turns: "Traders will be raised on the Day of Doom as the sinners, except him who fears Allah, acts righteously and says what is true", as al-Ḥākim records from Rifāʿah ibn Rāfiʿ. Trading and the provision of services are the right way of earning. Theft, deceit, lies, bribery, usury and gambling are only different ways of plundering.

### The right to life

The verse places property before life, probably because injustice and negligence are far more common in matters of property, though unjust killing is graver. The right of life is guarded for all, related or unrelated, Muslim or non-Muslim bound by a pact of peace, as at-Tafsīr al-Mazharī notes. Whoever deprives a person of life outside the law of God commits an unlawful act, and the right to execute belongs to the established authority alone, after guilt is proved.

The prohibition of self-killing has its own reports. The story behind the mercy is ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ's. Sent by the Prophet ﷺ to the battle of Dhāt as-Salāsil, he had a wet dream on a night of severe cold, feared that a bath would end him, and performed tayammum with pure earth instead, leading his company in the Dawn prayer. Told of it, the Prophet ﷺ asked: "O ʿAmr, have you led your people in prayer while you were in a state of sexual impurity?" ʿAmr answered that he had recalled Allah's statement "And do not kill yourselves. Surely, Allah is Most Merciful to you", and feared to perish. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ smiled and said nothing. Abū Dāwūd carries the report. The mercy of the verse covered his case. Ibn Marduwyah then reported from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> Whoever kills himself with an iron tool, his tool will be in his hand and he will keep stabbing himself with it in the Fire of Jahannam, forever and ever. Whoever kills himself with poison, his poison will be in his hand and he will keep taking it in the Fire of Jahannam forever and ever. Whoever kills himself by throwing himself from a mountain will keep falling in the Fire of Jahannam forever and ever.

The two Ṣaḥīḥs collected it, and the Group collected from Thābit ibn ad-Daḥḥāk the word of the Prophet ﷺ: "Whoever kills himself with an instrument will be punished with it on the Day of Resurrection." And the Ṣaḥīḥ records the Messenger of Allah ﷺ saying: "Cursing a Muslim is a sin and fighting him is kufr."

### Where the verse leads

Verse 29 closes one road and the next two open the accounting. Whoever takes or kills sinfully and unjustly, knowingly, will find the Fire, and that is easy for Allah. But the major sins named around these verses have their own catalogues, which the following verse recites: the seven destructive sins from Abū Hurayrah, the false testimony from Anas ibn Mālik, the greatest sins from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr, from Abū Bakrah and from ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd, down to Salmān al-Farīsī's Friday, the day Allah brought together the creation of Ādam (Adam), on which the bath and the prayer and the quiet erase what lies between two Fridays.

### In short

- Every false means of taking property is devouring, including ribā, gambling, bribery and false contracts, and including the sinful or extravagant waste of one's own.
- The lawful door is trade by real mutual consent, with the option to withdraw before parting, and service and labour stand inside the same door.
- The truthful, trustworthy trader ranks with the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs, and the cleanest earning is the work of one's own hands.
- Life is guarded like property and above it: no self-killing, even to escape a cold bath, and no killing outside the proved judgement of law.
- The commands are mercy, closing the roads whose ends are fire.

<<<VERSE 4:30>>>
The sentence that follows the two prohibitions: "And whoever does this sinfully and unjustly, We will burn them in the Fire. That is easy for Allah". Verse 29 forbade the devouring and the killing. Verse 30 prices them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"whoever does this sinfully and unjustly"** carries two conditions, aggression and injustice, doing the forbidden thing deliberately, knowing it is forbidden and daring it still. The restriction matters: should the wrong come about through forgetfulness or mistake, it does not enter this warning. That is the reading of Islamic law on the passage.

**"We will burn them in the Fire. That is easy for Allah"** is a stern warning and a truthful promise, and every sane person who hears the speech with full comprehension should beware of it. The casting and the burning are an easy matter for Him, for He is powerful over everything.

### The place of the verse

The Fire here receives the deliberate taker and the deliberate killer, the ones who consumed the ribā and the false trade of verse 29, or destroyed a life or a self in the manner the reports of Abū Hurayrah and Thābit ibn ad-Daḥḥāk described. Against them stands the mercy the same verse promised, the mercy ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ leaned on at Dhāt as-Salāsil when he chose tayammum over death and the Prophet ﷺ smiled, a story Abū Dāwūd carries. Mujāhid and Qatādah read the warning as reaching every forbidden act committed knowingly, and Ibn ʿAbbās's garment bought with an extra dirham falling due shows how fine the line of injustice runs. The verse after this one opens the exit, the avoiding of the major sins, from shirk to false testimony to the killing of the child, which the hadith of ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd and ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr catalogued, and ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr's own report, carried by al-Bukhārī, at-Tirmidhī and an-Nasāʾī, closes the catalogue with the false oath.

### In short

- The deliberate wrong, in property or in life, is met with the Fire, and that is easy for Allah.
- Forgetfulness and mistake are excluded by the verse's own conditions.
- Between the mercy of verse 29 and the pardon of verse 31, this verse stands as the price list of persistence.

<<<VERSE 4:31>>>
After the warning, the offer: "If you avoid the major sins forbidden to you, We will absolve you of your ˹lesser˺ misdeeds and admit you into a place of honour". The Qur'an's habit is to follow a warning with a persuasion, and this verse is one of its greatest offers. Avoid the great sins, and the small ones are wiped out on arrival at a noble entrance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you avoid the major sins forbidden to you"** names the kabāʾir, the sins whose punishment has been threatened, murder, fornication, theft and their like. Avoiding them includes fulfilling every obligation, fard and wājib, for neglecting an obligation is itself a major sin.

**"We will absolve you of your ˹lesser˺ misdeeds"** is the promise of pardon for the ṣaghāʾir, written off and replaced with reward instead of punishment and Paradise instead of Hell.

**"and admit you into a place of honour"** is Paradise, entered through a noble gate.

### How the pardon works

Righteous deeds operate as kaffārah, expiation, for minor sins. In the authentic hadith, when a person makes wudu for ṣalāh, the washing of each limb expiates the sins of that limb: the face carries away the sins of the eye, the ear and the nose, the gargling the sins of the tongue, the washing of the feet the sins of the feet, and every step toward the masjid brings its own expiation. The intervals are named: the minor sins between one congregational prayer and the next, between one Friday and the next, between one Ramadan and the next.

Salmān al-Farīsī is the narrator, in Aḥmad's Musnad, of the hadith on the day of al-Jumuʿah:

> The Prophet ﷺ said to me, "Do you know what the day of al-Jumuʿah is?" I said, "It is the day during which Allah brought together the creation of your father, Adam." He said, "I know what the day of Jumuʿah is. Anyone who takes a bath and cleans himself as much as he can, then proceeds for the Friday prayer and remains quiet until the imam finishes the prayer, all his sins in between the present and the next Friday will be forgiven, as long as major sins were avoided."

Al-Bukhārī recorded similar wording from Salmān al-Farīsī.

The condition is strict. The expiation through wudu, ṣalāh and the rest concerns minor sins only, and only for the one who has stayed away from the major ones. Major sins are forgiven by taubah, repentance, alone. Whoever stays involved in the major sins while performing his wudu and ṣalāh will find that neither the minor nor the major is expiated, and when he appears on the Day of Resurrection carrying the heavy burden of both, he will find no helping hand to lighten it.

### Two kinds, and the truth about the smaller

The verse teaches that sins are of two kinds, kabīrah and ṣaghīrah, and the promise binds them together. But the technical "minor" sin is not minor in itself. In the absolute sense, sin is any act against the command and will of Allah, and to disobey Him is invariably a grave crime. A large number of scholars therefore ruled that every disobedience is a major sin, and the distinction is made only when the sins are compared with each other. Ibn ʿAbbās said it plainly: any act forbidden by the sharīʿah is a major sin.

A minor sin done with nerve or heedlessness does not remain minor. It becomes major. A sage compared the two to a small scorpion and a big one, or a large ember and a tiny spark, for a person cannot bear the pain of any of them. Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī said no act of worship offered to Allah is greater than giving up sins, and that those who keep up prayers and the remembrance of Allah while still living in sins find no acceptance. Fudayl ibn ʿIyāḍ, the famous mystic, said: "The lighter you take a sin to be, the greater it will become with Allah as a crime." The most righteous elders used to say that every sin is a courier of kufr, inviting people to the manners of disbelievers. In the Musnad of Aḥmad, ʿĀʾishah wrote to Muʿāwiyah that a servant who disobeys Allah finds his admirers become fault-finders and his friends turn into enemies, and that heedlessness toward sins is the cause of permanent ruination.

The Qur'an gives the process its anatomy. In an authentic hadith, the Prophet ﷺ said that when a true believer falls into sin, a black dot appears on his heart. If he repents and seeks forgiveness, the dot disappears. If he does not, it keeps increasing until it covers the whole heart. The Qur'an names this dot rayn: "Their evil deeds have rusted their hearts" (83:14).

### What makes a sin major

The definition drawn from the Qur'an and the sunnah is: a sin on which a ḥadd punishment is prescribed for this present life, or on which words of curse have appeared, or on which a stern warning of Hell has been mentioned. Every sin whose evil outcome equals or exceeds a major sin counts as one, and so does a sin done in rebellious attitude or as a settled habit.

The number is not fixed. Someone said before Ibn ʿAbbās that the major sins were seven. He answered: "Not seven. Say seven hundred, which is better." In his book al-Zawājir, Imam Ibn Ḥajr al-Makkī listed them with full explanations, and the count reached four hundred and sixty seven. The Prophet ﷺ named them in threes and sixes and sevens as circumstances demanded, and the scholars concluded that no single number was meant as a universal count.

The lists themselves run long. In al-Bukhārī and Muslim: "I warn you against the top ones among the major sins. They are three: to associate a created being as a partner in the divinity of Allah, to disobey parents, and to give false witness or to lie." ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ what the greatest sin was. He said: "To make a rival for Allah while He alone created you." Then? "To kill your offspring for fear that he might share your food with you." Then? "To commit adultery with your neighbour's wife." He then recited from "And those who invoke not any other god along with Allah" (25:68) to "Except those who repent" (25:70). Abū Hurayrah's report in the two Ṣaḥīḥs counts the seven destructive sins: joining others in worship with Allah, killing the life Allah has forbidden except for a just cause, magic, consuming ribā, consuming an orphan's wealth, turning away from the enemy in battle, and accusing chaste women of unchastity. Anas ibn Mālik reported the mention of associating others with Allah, killing, and being undutiful to parents, then the words: "Should I tell you about the biggest of the major sins? The false statement, or the false testimony." ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn Abī Bakrah reported from his father the question "Should I inform you about the greatest of the great sins?", the answer naming the joining of partners and undutifulness to parents, and then the Prophet ﷺ, reclining, sat up and said: "And I warn you against false testimony and false speech", repeating it until they wished he would stop. ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr reported the greatest sins as joining others with Allah, being undutiful to parents or taking a life, and the false oath, recorded by al-Bukhārī, at-Tirmidhī and an-Nasāʾī. Muslim carries his report on parents: one of the ugliest major sins is a man's cursing of his own parents, which comes about when he curses another man's father and the man retaliates upon his.

Al-Bukhārī's narration counts also the eating up of an orphan's property by false means, the devouring of interest, the deserting of the battlefield of jihād, the false accusation against chaste women, and the desecration of the Holy Kaʿbah. To migrate from the land of Islam back to the land of kufr is ruled a major sin in some narrations. So are the false oath, the withholding of surplus water from those who need it, and the learning and practising of magic. The Prophet ﷺ said drinking is the greatest of major sins and the mother of all shameful deeds, and that the gravest major sin is to impute to a Muslim brother a fault that stains his character. Bracketing two ṣalāhs into one time without an excuse the sharīʿah approves is a major sin, as are losing hope in the mercy of Allah and growing careless of His punishment, and making a will that injures an inheritor. From Abū Dharr, whom Abū Dāwūd's collection also serves on these catalogues, comes the triple cry, "Destitutes, losers, they are ruined", over six: the one who lets his garment hang below his ankles, the one who spends in the way of Allah then publicizes the favour, the old man who indulges in shameful deeds, the office-holder who tells lies, the family man who waxes proud, and the one who gives allegiance to a worthy imam for material gain. The backbiter will not be admitted into Paradise, as al-Bukhārī and Muslim proclaim, and an-Nasāʾī and the Musnad of Aḥmad add the drinker, the disobeyer of parents, the unjustified severer of kinsmen, the publicizer of favours, the diviner of the unseen through jinns and satans, and the dayyuth, so at ease with his shame that neither wife nor family is ever turned back from immodesty. From the Ṣaḥīḥ of Muslim: the curse of Allah is on whoever sacrifices an animal for anyone other than Allah.

### The great sins of the proximate

Read inwardly, the verse has a second register. The great sins of the folk of service on the path of the sharīʿah are the ones catalogued above. For the folk of companionship on the Ṭarīqah's street, the great sins are of another sort and carry another tasting, for the servers are hirelings of the wage, while the companions are the near ones. In the traditions: "The beautiful deeds of the pious are the ugly deeds of the proximate." To this belongs what the Pir of the Ṭarīqah said: "The hypocrisy of the recognizers is better than the self-purification of the desirers." The basis is the subtle point Muṣṭafā ﷺ reported about cloudiness: "My heart becomes clouded, so I ask forgiveness from God seventy times a day." Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq said: "Would that I could witness that for which God's Messenger asked forgiveness." The great sins of the recognizers are the lapses of lassitude in their travelling, when the innate disposition is overpowered by human attributes, and the realities of faith grow mixed with the contaminants of personal motive. Unless a messenger of sound discipline and truthful poverty comes out to seize them by the hand, they stay in the pit of selfhood. The great ones of the religion have said that a man must first reach that place of danger and leave the station of lassitude behind before he becomes a pir of the Ṭarīqah fit to take disciples. He must have lost the road a thousand times and returned to it, if he is to bring back someone who has lost the road. First comes the road to the road, and only then the road. Someone forever on the road knows the road, yet not the road to the road. This is the secret behind the slips of the prophets and their falls into lassitude, and God knows best.

### In short

- Avoid the major sins, fulfil the obligations, and the minor sins are expiated by the wudu, the ṣalāh, the steps to the masjid and the Fridays, as Salmān al-Farīsī was told on the day of Ādam's assembling.
- Major sins are forgiven by repentance alone, and no righteous deed expiates for the one who stays in them.
- In the absolute sense every disobedience is grave, the minor sin done heedlessly turns major, and the heart collects the rust the Qur'an names rayn.
- The major sins number not seven but seven hundred by Ibn ʿAbbās's count, four hundred and sixty seven by Ibn Ḥajr's book, and the hadiths name them from shirk to the sacrifice offered to other than Allah.
- Read inwardly, the proximate have their own great sins, the lassitude of the road, and only the one who has been lost can guide the lost home.
