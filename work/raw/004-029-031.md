<<<VERSE 4:29>>>
From the first verse of the surah, the Qur'an has pointed to the creation of all people from one father and mother and the great bond of brotherhood that follows from it, and so to the need to guard everyone's rights. It then spoke at length about orphans and women, then about inheritance, and then about marriage, which is a contract that lets one person into another's life and property. This verse (4:29) now protects human life and property in general: "O believers! Do not devour one another’s wealth illegally, but rather trade by mutual consent. And do not kill ˹each other or˺ yourselves. Surely Allah is ever Merciful to you." It forbids any unjust intrusion into that area, whether the people concerned are men or women, relatives or strangers, Muslims or non-Muslims with whom there is a peace agreement.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Do not devour one another’s wealth illegally"**. The Arabic literally says "do not eat your wealth between you". The scholars agree that it means nobody should eat up another's property by false means. Abū Ḥayyān notes in al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ that the words also mean a person should not squander his own property in that way, for instance by spending it on sin or on plain extravagance. That too is forbidden here.

"Eat" in everyday speech means to use up in any way, whether by eating, drinking or using. Taking another's goods without right is called "eating up", even if the goods cannot be eaten. The word for "false means", bāṭil, takes in, according to Ibn Masʿūd and most of the Companions, every form that the Sharīʿah forbids: theft, robbery, usurpation, breach of trust, bribery, interest, gambling and every false transaction. Allah forbids His believing servants to acquire each other's property by dishonest methods such as ribā, gambling and other wicked ways that look legal, though Allah knows that those who use them are really dealing in interest. Other explanations list transgression, usurpation, false testimony and false oaths, or force, stealing and bribery.

Ibn Jarīr recorded an example from Ibn ʿAbbās. A man buys a garment and says that if he likes it he will keep it, and if not he will return it with an extra dirham. Ibn ʿAbbās said that this is what Allah meant by "do not consume one another's wealth unjustly" (2:188). ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported that, when this verse came down, some Muslims said, "Allah has forbidden us to eat up each other's property unjustly, and food is the best property we have. So none of us may eat from another's food." After that Allah revealed the verse that says there is no restriction on the blind, the disabled and the sick, and no blame on you if you eat from your homes or your relatives' homes or those of your friends (24:61). Qatādah said the same.

**"but rather trade by mutual consent"**. This means that there is no harm in commercial dealings between buyer and seller by mutual agreement, so that money is earned lawfully. Mujāhid said it means by selling and buying, or by giving someone a gift. Another explanation includes agreeing to lower a price in a sale, called muḥābāt. A variant reading, tijāratan or tijāratun, makes the sense that the goods come from trade effected through mutual goodwill, and such goods you may consume.

A trade is not the only lawful way for property to pass from one person to another. Borrowing, gifts, charity and inheritance are lawful too. But trade is the best known and most common way. The word covers more than buying and selling. Dealings in service, labour and tenancy are also included, since in a sale what one gets is property for property, and in a rental one gets property for service and labour. Trade has been singled out here because trade and labour are the best of the ways of earning a living.

**"And do not kill ˹each other or˺ yourselves"**. By the agreement of the scholars, this covers suicide as well as killing one another unjustly. The first part of the verse spoke of property, and this part covers the right to life. Property is named first, probably because injustice and carelessness are far commoner in matters of property. Unjust killing is far graver, but it happens less often. Allah forbids killing without a just right. Another explanation says that the words also mean committing what Allah forbids, falling into sin and eating up each other's property unjustly, for these destroy a person. Others add that you should not throw yourselves into ruin, and that one should not do what leads to destruction in this world or the Hereafter.

**"Surely Allah is ever Merciful to you"**. The rules in this verse come from divine mercy. They help you guard against these misdeeds, and so against punishment in the life to come and against what could strike you even here, in this life. Allah is merciful in what He commands and what He forbids.

### How the Prophet ﷺ explained "false means"

The Qur'an uses a single word, "falsely" or "illegally", to declare that all wealth acquired by false means is unlawful. The Prophet ﷺ was given the duty of explaining the details, and he explained every kind of impermissible transaction. The many details of forbidden buying and selling in the hadiths are therefore an elaboration of this verse's ruling, and those rulings are in a sense the Qur'an's own. Every ruling of the Sharīʿah in the hadiths explains some Qur'anic ruling, whether or not we know which verse it explains.

### The traders the Prophet ﷺ praised

Rāfiʿ ibn Khadīj said that the Prophet ﷺ was asked which way of earning is the purest and most lawful. He said, "A man's work with his own hands, and every honest sale that is not false or fraudulent." Al-Mundhirī records this in at-Targhīb, and al-Maẓharī cites it. Abū Saʿīd al-Khudhrī reported that the Prophet ﷺ said, "The truthful and trustworthy trader will be with the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs." This is in at-Tirmidhī. Anas reported that he said, "The truthful trader will be in the shade of the Throne on the Day of Judgment." Al-Iṣfahānī gave it in at-Targhīb.

There are conditions attached. Muʿādh ibn Jabal reported that the Prophet ﷺ said the cleanest earning is that of traders who do not lie when they speak, who do not betray what is entrusted to them, who do not look for faults in what they buy, who do not praise unrealistically what they sell, who do not delay in paying a debt, and who do not pester those who owe them. Al-Iṣfahānī has it, as cited by al-Maẓharī. Al-Ḥākim recorded, from Rifāʿah ibn Rāfiʿ, that traders will be raised on the Day of Judgment as sinners, except the one who fears Allah, does right and speaks the truth.

### What makes a trade valid

The words "trade by mutual consent" set two conditions for a transaction by which another person's property may lawfully be acquired. The first is that it must really be a trade, which means an exchange of property. Gambling, speculation, interest, and sales of goods that do not exist are forbidden, because they cannot be called trade, even if they carry the name. The second is that both parties must really agree. If the goods exist but the buyer or seller did not consent, the sale is invalid. Jurists call the first kind al-bayʿ al-bāṭil, a void sale, and the second al-bayʿ al-fāsid, a defective sale. Both are included in eating up property by false means.

For the first condition, trade means exchanging one commodity for another. A commodity on one side and none on the other is deception. Interest is the same, since the extra amount is a return for the time allowed in a loan, and time is not a commodity. In speculation and gambling, there is a commodity on one side, but whether there is one on the other side is doubtful. In forward dealing in goods that do not exist, there is a commodity on one side and only a promise on the other. These are fraud, not trade, so jurists rule them void. This explanation removes all the impermissible forms of trade.

The second condition covers cases where both goods exist but the exchange did not take place by agreement. A trade of that sort is wrong and invalid. A third kind looks as if there is a commodity on both sides and mutual consent, but one party's consent was obtained by compulsion, not free choice. This is counted with the second. Take a person or company that gathers up the everyday goods from across a market, builds a stock, raises prices and sells. The goods are not available elsewhere, so the customer has no choice but to buy at whatever price is asked. He walks into the shop and buys with apparent consent, but that consent results from compulsion and is void.

In the same way, if a husband makes life with him so difficult that his wife is forced to give up her dower, her "consent" is not real consent. Or take a man who finds that he cannot get his rightful job done without a bribe, so he agrees to pay one. His willingness is not free choice, and the payment is void in law.

So the words "trade by mutual consent" allow only those forms of buying, selling and trading that the hadiths prove to be lawful. The jurists simply put them in order. All forms of trade that the Sharīʿah forbids are excluded. This one phrase of the Qur'an is the key to the whole treasury of Muslim law on sale and hire.

### The right to cancel before parting

Mutual agreement in transactions is complete when each party has the right to confirm or cancel the deal before they part. The two Ṣaḥīḥs record that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, "The seller and the buyer have the right to change their minds as long as they have not parted." In al-Bukhārī's wording, when two men make a deal, each keeps that right as long as they have not separated.

### Suicide

Aḥmad recorded ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ's account of the expedition of Dhāt as-Salāsil, on which the Prophet ﷺ had sent him. One bitterly cold night ʿAmr became impure in his sleep and was afraid that washing would kill him. So he performed tayammum, dry ablution with clean earth, and led his companions in the dawn prayer. When he returned and told the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, the Prophet ﷺ asked, "O ʿAmr, did you lead your people in prayer while you were in a state of impurity?" ʿAmr explained his fear of dying from the cold and said that he had remembered Allah's words "And do not kill yourselves. Surely Allah is ever Merciful to you," and so he made tayammum and prayed. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ only smiled and said nothing. This is the account in Abū Dāwūd.

Ibn Marduwyah mentioned this verse and then reported that Abū Hurayrah said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "Whoever kills himself with an iron tool will have that tool in his hand, stabbing himself with it in the Fire of Jahannam forever. Whoever kills himself with poison will have his poison in his hand, drinking it in the Fire of Jahannam forever. Whoever kills himself by throwing himself off a mountain will keep falling in the Fire of Jahannam forever."

The two Ṣaḥīḥs also record this. Abū Qilābah reported from Thābit ibn aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk a saying of the Messenger ﷺ that whoever kills himself with some tool will be punished with it on the Day of Resurrection. The group of compilers collected it.

### A just order, not private vengeance

Property changes hands usefully through supply and demand and through payment for services. Under Islamic law, trade and services are the right way to earn a living. Income by other means, such as theft, deceit, lies, bribery, usury and gambling, is unlawful in Allah's sight. These are only different ways of plundering. Those who use them in place of honest means may succeed in this world, but all that awaits them in the Hereafter is fire and brimstone.

Human life must be respected in the same way, and human beings deserve justice. The right to carry out an execution lies only with an established government, which may punish an accused person after he has been proved guilty under Allah's law. Apart from that, anyone who tries to deprive a person of his life commits an unlawful act, and Allah will set a severe punishment for it. Going beyond all limits and torturing without justification are among the worst crimes in Allah's sight. Those who keep away from these unlawful acts will receive His special favour. They will enter the eternal world of the Hereafter after being forgiven for small slips and weaknesses in this world.

### The inner reading

Everything spent on something other than Allah is capital spent for nothing. The words are also taken to refer to holding back in a heedless way, or spending from a place other than that of witnessing the real truth, the ḥaqīqah. All of that comes to nothing. "And do not kill yourselves" means by committing sins. It is also said to point indirectly to whatever would bring His anger. Another reading is that you kill yourselves by looking to yourselves and paying attention to your lower selves, or by being pleased with them or preferring them to the good pleasure of the Real.

### In short

- Do not take each other's property by any false means, such as theft, bribery, interest, gambling or fraud. Do not squander your own either. The only lawful route named is trade by mutual consent.
- A valid trade needs a real exchange of property and real consent. Deals under compulsion, void sales and defective sales are forbidden, and each party may cancel before they part.
- Truthful and trustworthy traders are praised, with the conditions the Prophet ﷺ listed.
- Do not kill each other or yourselves. Suicide is warned of in the hadiths, and ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ prayed after tayammum because of this verse and the Prophet ﷺ smiled.
- These rules are mercy, to keep people safe from punishment in this world and the next.

<<<VERSE 4:30>>>
The verse that follows warns of the punishment: "And whoever does this sinfully and unjustly, We will burn them in the Fire. That is easy for Allah." It is a stern warning and a true promise, and every sane person who hears these words and understands them should take care.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And whoever does this sinfully and unjustly"**. The "this" is killing another person or wrongly taking someone's property. The words "sinfully" and "unjustly", aggression and injustice, are named together, and the second repeats the first for emphasis. They show that the person knows it is forbidden and yet dares to do it. If it happens through forgetfulness or by mistake, the warning does not cover it. One explanation says the offender must be doing wrong knowingly and not from ignorance or forgetting.

**"We will burn them in the Fire"**. In the Hereafter, Allah will cast such a person into a great fire, where he will feel its burning heat and receive his punishment. In the inner reading, Allah will not spare him from a fierce punishment. He will hand the fire over to its rightful inhabitant and give it free rein.

**"That is easy for Allah"**. Casting such a person into the Fire and tormenting him is easy for Allah. He has power over everything.

### In short

- Whoever knowingly takes another's property or life by injustice and aggression will be thrown into the Fire. This is easy for Allah.
- The warning concerns knowing wrongdoing. A mistake or forgetfulness is not covered.

<<<VERSE 4:31>>>
The verse that follows gives hope to those who avoid the gravest sins: "If you avoid the major sins forbidden to you, We will absolve you of your ˹lesser˺ misdeeds and admit you into a place of honour." It is the Qur'an's usual way. After warnings of punishment, it adds encouragement to obey.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If you avoid the major sins forbidden to you"**. The Arabic word is kabāʾir, the plural of kabīrah, a major sin. Short explanations name the ones for which a punishment is threatened, such as murder, fornication or theft. According to Ibn ʿAbbās, these number as many as seven hundred. Other explanations give the main examples as worshipping others besides Allah, disrespecting parents, killing and taking interest, or the grave things forbidden in this surah. Avoiding major sins also includes carrying out every obligation, farḍ and wājib, since neglecting an obligation is itself a major sin.

**"We will absolve you of your ˹lesser˺ misdeeds"**. If you avoid the major sins, Allah will forgive the small ones and admit you to Paradise. This is why righteous deeds are said to wipe out minor sins. When someone makes wuḍūʾ, the ablution, for prayer, each part he washes expiates sins. Washing the face removes the sins of the eyes, ears and nose, rinsing the mouth removes the sins of the tongue, and washing the feet removes the sins of the feet. Every step to the mosque brings expiation too. These sayings are about minor sins.

Several hadiths explain the verse. Aḥmad recorded that Salmān al-Fārisī said that the Prophet ﷺ asked him, "Do you know what the day of Jumuʿah is?" Salmān answered, "It is the day on which Allah gathered the creation of your father Ādam." The Prophet ﷺ said, "I know what the day of Jumuʿah is. Anyone who bathes, cleans himself as well as he can, then goes to the Friday prayer and keeps silent until the imām has finished, will have all his sins forgiven between now and the next Friday, as long as major sins were avoided." Al-Bukhārī has similar wording from Salmān al-Fārisī. Another explanation says that the forgiven sins are those committed between one congregational prayer and the next, between one Friday prayer and the next, and between one Ramaḍān and the next.

Major sins, in contrast, are not forgiven without tawbah, repentance. For minor sins the condition is that one has made the effort to avoid major sins. This brings a warning. If someone who keeps committing major sins goes on making wuḍūʾ and praying, those deeds will not wipe out even his minor sins, let alone the major ones. On the Day of Resurrection he will carry a heavy load of major and minor sins and find no hand to lighten it.

**"and admit you into a place of honour"**. This is Paradise. The Arabic can be read mudkhalan or madkhalan, an honourable entering or an honourable place. Another explanation calls it a noble gate. A person who is cleansed of all sins, major and minor, can look forward to entering the home of honour and peace.

### What counts as major, and what as minor

Sin in the absolute sense is any act against Allah's command and will. So a "minor" sin is not really minor, since disobeying Allah and opposing His will is always a grave matter. Many scholars have ruled that every act of disobedience is a major sin, and that the distinction between major and minor is drawn only by comparison. Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to have said that any act the Sharīʿah forbids is a major sin.

The definition of kabāʾir that the Qur'an and the hadith point to, as the elders explained, is this. A major sin is one for which the Qur'an or the Sunnah prescribes a ḥadd, a punishment to be carried out in this life. Or it is one on which a curse appears. Or it is one for which a stern warning of Hell is given. Any sin whose evil result equals or exceeds that of a major sin also counts. So does a sin done in a rebellious spirit or as a standing habit.

### Why a "minor" sin is never light

A minor sin does not mean something to be done carelessly, lazily or brushed off. A minor sin done with boldness or heedlessness stops being minor and becomes major. A sage compared it to a small scorpion and a large one, or a big ember and a tiny spark. No one can bear the pain of either. So Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Qurazī said that the greatest act of worship for Allah is to give up sin. The worship of people who pray and remember Allah and yet do not give up sins is not accepted. The mystic Fuḍayl ibn ʿIyāḍ said, "The lighter you take a sin, the greater it becomes with Allah." The most righteous elders used to say that every sin is a courier of kufr, calling people to the manners and morals of disbelievers.

In the Musnad of Aḥmad, ʿĀʾishah wrote a letter to Muʿāwiyah in which she said that a servant of Allah who disobeys Him finds that his admirers become fault-finders and his friends become enemies. Heedlessness toward sin is the cause of man's lasting ruin. An authentic hadith says that when a true believer sins, a black dot appears on his heart. If he repents and seeks forgiveness, it disappears. If he does not, the dot keeps growing until it covers his whole heart. The Qur'an calls this rayn, rust: "Their hearts have been stained by all ˹the evil˺ they used to commit" (83:14).

### How many major sins are there?

Someone told Ibn ʿAbbās that the major sins are seven. He said, "Not seven. Say seven hundred, which is better." In his book al-Zawājir, Imām Ibn Ḥajar al-Makkī lists the sins that fall under the definition above, and the number reaches four hundred and sixty-seven. Some scholars counted only the most prominent sins and so reached a smaller number. Others who went into detail and listed all the branches reached a higher one. The Prophet ﷺ himself pointed to many sins as major, and in different places he named them in threes, sixes and sevens, or more. The scholars concluded that no fixed total is meant. Each number in a hadith fits the occasion on which it was stated.

### The greatest sins in the reports

The two Ṣaḥīḥs record that Abū Hurayrah said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, "Avoid the seven great destructive sins." The people asked what they were, and he said:

> "Joining others in worship with Allah, killing a life that Allah has made sacred except by right, magic, taking ribā, taking an orphan's wealth, turning away from the enemy on the battlefield when the fighting starts, and accusing chaste believing women who never think of anything to tarnish their chastity."

Aḥmad recorded from Anas ibn Mālik that the Prophet ﷺ spoke of the major sins, or was questioned about them, and named associating others with Allah, killing, and being undutiful to parents. Then he asked, "Shall I tell you the greatest of the major sins? The false statement, or the false testimony." Shuʿbah, one of the narrators, said he thought it was "false testimony". The two Ṣaḥīḥs have this from Shuʿbah, from Anas. In another report in the two Ṣaḥīḥs, ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Abī Bakrah said that his father said the Prophet ﷺ asked, "Shall I tell you the greatest of the great sins?" They said, "Yes, O Messenger of Allah." He said, "Joining others with Allah and being undutiful to one's parents." He had been reclining, and he sat up and said, "And I warn you against false testimony and false speech." He kept repeating it until they wished he would stop. In another report, he said the top three are shirk, disobeying parents and false witness or lying.

In the two Ṣaḥīḥs, ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd said, "I asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, 'What is the greatest sin?' He said, 'To make a rival for Allah when He alone created you.' I said, 'Then what?' He said, 'To kill your child out of fear that he will share your food.' I said, 'Then what?' He said, 'To commit adultery with your neighbour's wife.'" Because a neighbour's household is everyone's duty to protect, as one's own household is, the gravity of this is doubled. The Prophet ﷺ then recited the verses about those who do not call on another god with Allah, up to the words "except those who repent" (25:68 to 25:70).

Aḥmad recorded a saying that ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr heard from the Prophet ﷺ: the greatest sins are to join others with Allah, to be undutiful to parents or to take a life, and the false oath. Shuʿbah was unsure which of these two it was. Al-Bukhārī, at-Tirmidhī and an-Nasāʾī recorded it. ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr also said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, "Among the worst of the major sins is for a man to curse his parents." They asked, "How could anyone curse his parents?" He said, "He insults another man's father, and that man insults his father in return, or he insults someone's mother and the man insults his mother." This is Muslim's wording, and at-Tirmidhī called it ṣaḥīḥ. It is also recorded in the Ṣaḥīḥ that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ called cursing a Muslim a sin, and fighting him kufr.

### Other sins the reports name

A narration in al-Bukhārī lists shirk, unjust killing, eating an orphan's property by false means, taking interest, deserting the battlefield of jihād, falsely accusing chaste women, disobeying parents and violating the Kaʿbah as major sins. In some hadiths, a man who lived in the land of disbelief, emigrated to the land of Islam and then went back to live among disbelievers has also committed a major sin.

Other reports include false oaths, holding back water beyond one's need and refusing to share it, learning magic and practising it. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Drinking is the greatest of the major sins," and, "Drinking is the mother of all shameful deeds," because a person who is drunk can fall into any evil. He also said, "The gravest of the major sins is for one to accuse his Muslim brother of a fault that stains his character." A person who joins two prayers at one time without a reason the Sharīʿah accepts, praying one late as a make-up with the next, has committed a major sin. Some hadiths count losing hope in Allah's mercy as a major sin, and so is becoming careless or bold enough to forget His punishment. Another report says that making a will to harm an heir or cut his share is among the major sins.

In a narration of Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ once said three times, "The destitute, the losers, they are ruined." Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī asked, "Who are these unfortunate ones, O Messenger of Allah?" He answered: one who lets his trousers or wrap or shirt hang below his ankles, one who spends something in the way of Allah and then publicises his favour, one who is old and indulges in shameful deeds, one who has authority and tells lies, one who has a family and yet is proud, and one who pledges allegiance to a worthy leader only for worldly gain.

Closing in the same vein, al-Bukhārī and Muslim record that the backbiter will not enter Paradise. A hadith in an-Nasāʾī and in the Musnad of Aḥmad adds that some people will not enter Paradise: the drinker of wine, the undutiful child, the one who cuts ties of kinship without justification, the one who publicises his favours, the one who claims to know the unseen through jinn, devils and other go-betweens, and the dayyūth, a man so content with shamelessness that he never stops his wife and household from immodesty. A hadith in Muslim says that Allah's curse is on one who sacrifices an animal to anyone other than Allah.

### The inner reading

The great sins of the people of service on the path of the Sharīʿah are the ones you have heard of. The great sins of the people of companionship, on the street of the Ṭarīqah, the way of the inner journey, are of another sort and another taste. The people of service are one thing and the people of companionship another. Those who serve are wage-earners, and the companions are the near ones. A saying in the traditions is, "The good deeds of the pious are the bad deeds of the near." Of the same kind is the saying of the elder of the Ṭarīqah, "The hypocrisy of the knowers is better than the self-purification of the seekers."

The basis of this rule is a subtle point about what Muṣṭafā, the Prophet ﷺ, said of clouding of the heart. He said, "My heart is clouded, and I seek Allah's forgiveness seventy times a day." Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq said, "I wish I could see that for which Allah's Messenger sought forgiveness." The mark of the great sins of the knowers is that, in their own journey, a heaviness sometimes comes over them, and their natural disposition is overpowered by human traits. Their lives become open to habits and customs, and the truths of their faith get mixed with the impurities of personal motives and signs of their own shares. In that state, unless a guide of true discipline, honest poverty and ecstatic joy comes forward to greet them and take their hands, they will not climb out of the pit of their own selfhood. If you want to climb out of the pit of rank like a man, take hold of the pearl-studded, musk-scented chain.

The great ones of the religion have said that a man will not become an elder of the Ṭarīqah, fit to take disciples, until he has reached this dangerous place and left behind the station of heaviness. Only a man who has lost the way a thousand times and found it again can lead a lost person back. First one needs the way to the way, and after that the way itself. A person who has never left the way knows the way, but not how to get back to it. This is the secret of the prophets' slips and their falling into heaviness. Allah knows best. This is a knock at a magnificent gate, and blessed is whoever finds it opened and is guided to it.

### In short

- If a person avoids the major sins, Allah wipes out his minor sins and admits him to a place of honour. Righteous deeds such as wuḍūʾ, prayers and the Friday prayer wipe out minor sins, while major sins need tawbah.
- Many scholars hold that no sin is truly small. A minor sin done boldly or as a habit becomes a major one, and each sin leaves a black dot on the heart until repentance clears it.
- A major sin is one that carries a prescribed punishment, a curse or a warning of Hell. Ibn ʿAbbās said seven hundred is nearer the truth than seven, and Ibn Ḥajar al-Makkī listed 467.
- The Prophet ﷺ named the greatest sins as shirk, killing, undutifulness to parents, false testimony and lying, interest, taking an orphan's property, fleeing from battle, accusing chaste women, and adultery with a neighbour's wife.
- Inwardly, the great sins of the near ones are different from those of the servants, and the way out is to follow a guide who has lost and found the road.
