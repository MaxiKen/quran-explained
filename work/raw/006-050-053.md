<<<VERSE 6:50>>>
"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ 'I do not say to you that I possess Allah's treasuries or know the unseen, nor do I claim to be an angel. I only follow what is revealed to me.'" The verse is the answer to a demand, and the answer consists of four denials and one claim.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺"** is addressed to Muhammad ﷺ and, through him, to the people of Makkah. It came down about Abū Jahl, al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith, ʿUyaynah and their companions. Then, it is said, it came down about the clients of the tribe as well. **"I do not say to you that I possess Allah's treasuries"** means that he does not own the treasures of Allah or hold any power over them. He does not have the keys of provision, vegetation, fruit, rain or punishment. **"or know the unseen"** denies knowledge of what is hidden from him and has not been revealed to him. The knowledge of the unseen sits with Allah, and he only knows what Allah conveys to him of it. **"nor do I claim to be an angel"** means he does not say that he is one of the angels from heaven. He is a human being to whom Allah sends revelation, and that revelation is the honor He gave him and the favor He singled him out with. **"I only follow what is revealed to me"** is the one positive claim in the verse. Its sense is: I say and do only what I am commanded with in the Qur'an, and I never disobey the revelation in the least. **"Say, 'Are those blind ˹to the truth˺ equal to those who can see?'"** asks whether the misled man and the man who is guided and follows the truth are the same. It asks whether the disbeliever and the believer are equal in deeds of obedience and in reward. **"Will you not then reflect?"** asks them to think about the comparisons the Qur'an sets out. Another verse puts the same contrast in these words: "Is the one who knows that what has been revealed to you from your Lord is the truth like the one who is blind? Only people of understanding pay heed" (13:19).

### The demands behind the answer

The disbelievers of Makkah put three demands to the Prophet ﷺ on different occasions. Bring us the treasures of the whole world by the power of miracle, if you really are Allah's messenger. Tell us what is going to happen to us, good and bad, so that we can take hold of it or keep clear of it in advance. And explain how a human being like us, born of a mother and a father, who eats, drinks and walks about in the streets and markets as we do, can suddenly become a messenger of Allah. If he were an angel, whose make and qualities are worthier than ours, they said, they would have accepted him. This verse answers all three at once, by refusing the terms of every one of them.

### What the denials are for

The denials are not modesty for its own sake. They define the office. Nothing in the claim to prophethood requires that a messenger own the treasures of Allah, or know everything in the domain of the unseen, or be a being with attributes other than human. What his office is, is to follow the revelation sent to him, to act on it himself, and to invite others to it. The denials remove the picture the demanders were working with. On it, a true prophet would have to be a supplier of wealth, a foreteller and a superhuman being all at once. Once the office is described correctly, only the closing question is left: is the man who sees the same as the man who has eyes and will not use them?

### In short

- The verse denies four things and keeps one: he is not the owner of Allah's treasures, not the knower of the unseen, not an angel, and he follows only what is revealed.
- Knowledge of the unseen is limited to what Allah conveys, and the treasures are not in his hands.
- The claim to be a human messenger is not a downgrade, since the revelation itself is the honor.
- The blind and the seeing are not equal, and 13:19 says the same about the man who knows and the man who is blind.
- The three demands made of the Prophet ﷺ are answered by refusing the picture of prophethood behind them.

<<<VERSE 6:51>>>
"Warn with this ˹Quran˺ those who are awed by the prospect of being gathered before their Lord—when they will have no protector or intercessor besides Him—so perhaps they will be mindful ˹of Him˺." The verse tells the Prophet ﷺ where to take the message next, and it says what the message is for. The debate with the demanders has been answered, and what follows is not another round of it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Warn with this ˹Quran˺"** means warn by means of the Qur'an, and it is also taken to mean warn by Allah. **"those who are awed by the prospect of being gathered before their Lord"** describes people who know this and are certain of it, and who live in awe, fearing their Lord and dreading the terrible reckoning (23:57, 13:21). The gathering is the Day of Resurrection, and the sinning believers are among those meant here. **"when they will have no protector or intercessor besides Him"** is what they fear. On that Day no relative, no patron and no intercessor will keep His torment off them if He decides to punish. The clause states the circumstance of the gathering rather than a separate hope. **"so perhaps they will be mindful ˹of Him˺"** is the aim, and it is double: they will ward off transgression, and that will help them perform deeds of obedience. Warn of the day when there is no judge except Allah. Then people will work good deeds in this life, and their works will save them on the Day of Qiyāmah from His torment and earn them reward in more than one form. The men named in this verse as people who know and are certain are Salmān al-Fārīsī, Khabbāb ibn al-Aratt, ʿĀmir ibn Fuḥayrah, Mihjāʿ ibn Sāliḥ, ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir, Bilāl ibn Rabāḥ, Ṣuhayb ibn Sinān and Sālim the client of Abū Hudhayfah. These are the people the Qur'an benefits, and whoever believes in the messengers and does right has no fear of what is ahead.

### Why the demanded signs were not sent

Many signs had already appeared before the disbelievers of Makkah. The Prophet ﷺ came into the world an orphan, unschooled and untutored, and lived his whole life unlettered in a land where there was no scholar and no institution of learning near or far. For forty years he lived under the eyes of his people in that state. Then, all of a sudden, there poured from his speech a stream of words whose eloquence challenged the masters of Arab diction and silenced them. Its wisdom and meaning reached to pragmatic human needs right down to the Day of Qiyāmah. With it he brought a practical system for the nurture of the complete human being. No human ingenuity could produce that, and he demonstrated it and proved it workable. The crowd of his time, whose life was eating, drinking, sleeping and waking, was made to look up at the purpose it was created for. Every period of that life, and every great event in it, was a miracle in its place and a sign of Allah, and against that background no just and reasonable person had room left to demand a sign.

Still, the Quraysh demanded miracles of a kind of their own choosing, and some of them Allah showed them openly. They asked to see the moon split in two. The parting of the moon was witnessed not only by Quraysh but by a great number of people anywhere in the world at that time. Their answer to it was that this was magic of a continuing sort, magic that had always been there. They saw, they understood, and they went on asking for something new, as verse 37 records. In reply the Qur'an tells the Prophet ﷺ to say that Allah has everything in His power. He has already manifested signs without their asking, and He is quite able to produce the ones they want. But there is a settled way Allah follows here. Once a people are shown the miracle they asked for and then refuse to believe in it, a sudden punishment seizes them. It was therefore in their own interest that the signs of their choosing should not be made to appear, and many people still do not understand the wisdom in that and keep insisting.

### The treasures of Allah

Commentators have named many things as the treasures meant in the first sentence of verse 50, but the Qur'an itself settles the reach of the phrase: there is not a thing whose treasures are not with Us (15:21). So the treasures of Allah cover everything, and cannot be pinned to a set of particular items. The scholars who listed particular things were giving examples, and there is no contradiction between the two. Then comes the point the commentary draws from this, and it draws it hard. The treasures of Allah were not in the hands of the foremost of the prophets and messengers, Muḥammad al-Muṣṭafā ﷺ. So how can anyone suppose that a pious elder or a wali in this community can do as he likes and grant whatever he chooses to anyone? That is plain ignorance.

### Why the wording changes in the middle

The first denial and the third are both framed as a claim he does not make, while the middle one says something stronger. It does not say, I do not tell you that I know the unseen. It says, nor do I have the knowledge of the unseen. Abū al-Ḥayyān pointed out the subtlety in his Tafsīr al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ. Owning the divine treasures, and being or not being an angel, are matters of observation, and his addressees knew them well. They knew that the treasures of Allah were not in his hands, and that he was not an angel. Their demands were built on malice and hostility, so it would have been enough to say: I have never claimed to own the treasures of Allah, or to be an angel. The knowledge of the unseen was different. These people already believed their astrologers and soothsayers knew the unseen, so attributing that knowledge to the Messenger of Allah was not unlikely for them. They had also heard news of the unseen from his mouth and watched it come true. So a bare denial of the claim was not sufficient, and the thing denied was the fact itself. The wording also clears up a misunderstanding. A share of knowledge about unseen things, given to an angel, a messenger or a wali through revelation or inspiration, cannot be called ʿilm al-ghayb in the terminology of the Qur'an. Nor can its recipient be called the knower of the unseen.

From the same place comes the balance the commentary insists on. No Muslim can doubt that Allah gave the Prophet ﷺ knowledge of many things of the unseen, more than angels and human beings put together from the first to the last. That is the belief of the whole Ummah. Alongside it, on the strength of countless statements of the Qur'an and the Sunnah, all the early Imams and the later ones hold that all-encompassing knowledge of the whole universe, al-ʿilm al-muḥīṭ, is the exclusive attribute of Allah. An angel or a messenger is not His equal as Creator or as Provider, and is not equal to Him as the Absolutely Powerful, al-Qādir al-Muṭlaq. So no prophet or angel, however much of the unseen he is given, is the Knower of the unseen. On the excellence of the Prophet ﷺ the commentary permits itself a line from a Persian poet: after Allah, you are the great one, and that is the whole short story. His rank in knowledge is ahead of angels, prophets and messengers, and it is not the equal of Allah's knowledge, and claiming equality there is the path of excess that Christianity took.

### Who the warning is aimed at first

There are three kinds of people about the hereafter: those who believe in it as certain, those who doubt and waver, and those who reject it outright. The prophets were commanded to convey the call and the warning to all three, and the Qur'an says so in many places. But the call is likelier to work on the first two, so this verse singles them out. It tells the Prophet ﷺ to turn the focus of his warning to people who believe they will be brought out before Allah to give an account, whether they are believers already or people who are not deniers, even if their holding is only probable. It also instructs him, in the same move, to draw a line and stop the argument with the obstinate and get on with the work of conveying. At the end of verse 50 the blind man and the seeing man were pronounced unequal. The sense is that they should drop their selfish concerns, abandon obstinacy and hostility, and look at reality as it is, so that they stop being counted among the blind. Their need was to start seeing, and a little thought and a little care about what is right would have returned to them the sight they were missing.

### Why the reckless cannot be warned

Admonition only reaches those who live in fear of God, because only a person worried about danger can be warned of an approaching calamity. People who live recklessly are never serious about being admonished, and that is why they never heed advice. Recklessness comes from two things: worship of this world, and worship of ancestors. Whoever is lost in worldly things, or satisfied with worldly success, does not remember that he will have to appear before his Creator and Lord the day he dies. The hereafter carries no weight for him, and the remembrance of it finds no place in his thinking. There is a second kind of false security as well: people who treat recommendation as the key to Paradise. They assume that the great ones they have attached themselves to will stand up for them in the next world, and that no matter how bad the conditions, that will be enough to save them. They live in the fond hope that nothing can go wrong with them, since they hold the hands of holy personalities and belong to the group that is ostensibly loved and favored. That is the temper which refuses to be afraid of the hereafter and will not consider anything that might throw doubt on its position.

### In short

- The verse directs the warning to those already softened by the thought of being gathered before their Lord, and the gathering has no protector or intercessor in it.
- Mindfulness is the goal, and it works in both directions: holding back from transgression and doing works of obedience.
- Signs already stood in front of Makkah, and a demanded sign that is refused only hastens destruction, which is why they are withheld.
- The treasures of Allah cover everything, and 15:21 is the verse that proves it, so no claim can be built on controlling them.
- The middle denial is harsher in wording because the claim it denies was the one they were actually prone to believe.
- Three kinds of people exist about the hereafter, and the warning is aimed first at the certain and the wavering.

<<<VERSE 6:52>>>
"˹O Prophet!˺ Do not dismiss those ˹poor believers˺ who invoke their Lord morning and evening, seeking His pleasure." The verse is a prohibition addressed to the Prophet ﷺ, and it is also the answer to a proposal put to him by people who considered themselves doing him a favor.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not dismiss those"** means: do not turn away from you the people with these qualities, and keep them close as your companions. **"who invoke their Lord morning and evening"** describes those who worship Him and call on Him, and one reading ties the two times to the obligatory prayers, which is how Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib, Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan and Qatādah took it. By the usage of the language the phrase covers all the times of day and night, and the calling means worship in general. **"seeking His pleasure"** is the restriction attached to it. They are seeking the Most Generous Face of Allah and nothing else in the world, and they do it by being sincere in the acts of worship and obedience they perform. Worship without sincerity is not trustworthy, and the worshipper is not seeking the fleeting things of this world when he does it. **"You are not accountable for them whatsoever, nor are they accountable for you"** is read by Ibn ʿAtiyyah, al-Zamakhsharī and others as being about the chiefs who had insisted on the removal of the poor believers. Its sense is that you are not responsible for anything in their account, and they are not responsible for anything in yours. There is therefore no reason to buy their entry into faith at the price of driving these men out. **"So do not dismiss them, or you will be one of the wrongdoers"** closes the door on the arrangement. The same ruling is spelled out again in these words: "And keep yourself patiently with those who call on their Lord morning and evening, seeking His Face, and let not your eyes pass beyond them toward the pomp and glitter of this life, and obey not him whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance, who follows his own desires and whose affair has been lost" (18:28).

### The proposal

Some of the chiefs of the disbelievers of Quraysh came to the Prophet's uncle Abū Ṭālib: ʿUtbah ibn Rabīʿah, Shaybah, Muṭʿim ibn ʿAdiyy, Ḥārith ibn Nawfal and others with them. Their complaint, reported through Ibn Jarīr, was that a real obstacle stood in the way of listening to his nephew: the people who kept company with him. They were either freed slaves of theirs or people living at their mercy, and with such men in the sitting they could not attend it. Their proposal was that these should be asked to leave when it was time for the chiefs to come. The nobles also went to ʿUmar and asked him to request that one day be set aside for them and another for the weak believers. Allah rejected the arrangement and warned them against it. One report has the Prophet ﷺ sending ʿUmar to the poor men with the message that they should come less often for a few days, so that perhaps those others would believe. ʿUmar had not taken three steps when Gabriel came down with the verse. Do not drive them away, since He has not driven them away, and do not caress those whom He has not called. The Prophet ﷺ called ʿUmar back. When the unbelievers despaired of getting days divided between them, they offered a compromise. They would sit along with the poor men for a while, they said, if the Prophet ﷺ looked at them and not at them, and showed them respect. Then ʿUmar was sent again to the poor men, to gladden their hearts and to ask their approval for it. ʿUmar himself had first advised accepting the arrangement for a few days. These men loved the Prophet ﷺ, he pointed out, and were not stiff with formality, and they would move out of the sitting when the chiefs arrived. After the verse came down he apologized and admitted the advice had been wrong. The poor men in question were Bilāl al-Ḥabashī, Ṣuhayb ar-Rūmī, ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir, Sālim the client of Abī Hudhayfah, Ṣābiṭ the client of Usayd, ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd, Miqdād ibn ʿAmr, Masʿūd ibn al-Qārī and Dhū al-Shimātayn. Other companions of the same standing are named with them, and their claim to nobility was delivered from heaven. The reason behind the whole episode, in another wording, is what ʿUyaynah ibn Ḥisn al-Fazārī said. Drive these away from you, he told the Prophet ﷺ, so that the nobles of Makkah will come to you, listen to you and believe in you.

### What the eagerness was

The Prophet ﷺ wanted those men to come to faith, and that wish is what the episode is built on. The unbelievers came to Muṣṭafā and told him that they wanted to believe in him, Muḥammad, but that sitting with these beggars, and the smell of their shabby clothes, was beneath their dignity. Their desire came first, and the Prophet ﷺ was so eager for their belief that another verse addresses the wish directly: perhaps you will consume yourself with grief over their footsteps, turning away (18:6). One Sufi reading takes the exchange as a lesson about who decides the company of the chosen. Those accepted by the Presence are one thing, and those turned away by severance another. The poor men were the ones called by the verse in which Allah invites to the abode of peace (10:25). The estranged ones were the ones driven away by the verse that says: slink into it, and do not speak to Me (23:108). The instruction that came down with the prohibition was: be with them, since I am with them, and want them, since I want them.

### The four lessons drawn from the verse

First, nobody has the right to look down on a man in tattered clothes or in a broken-down condition. People who look like that may be among those honored and accepted by Allah, and a hadith says that many a dust-covered, broken-down person is held dear by Allah, so that if he swears by Allah about something, Allah lets what he swore come about. Second, taking material affluence as the measure of nobility and lowliness is an insult to humanity, and what it actually depends on is good morals and deeds. Third, a reformer needs a call that reaches everyone, believers and dissenters alike. But the first claim on him belongs to the people who already own his teaching, and pushing them behind others in order to carry the call to outsiders is not permitted. In the case of this community, the education of unaware Muslims is not to be put off in favor of the call to non-Muslims. Fourth, the rewards and blessings of Allah rise in proportion to gratitude. Anyone who wants an increase in them must make gratitude, in word and in deed, a permanent part of his character.

### In short

- The verse forbids dismissing the people whose worship is sincere and whose only aim is the countenance of Allah.
- The proposal to trade their removal for the chiefs' attendance was rejected outright, and 18:28 repeats the ruling.
- Accountability runs each way on its own account, which is what makes the bargain unnecessary and unjust.
- The occasion names the men involved on both sides, from ʿUyaynah ibn Ḥisn al-Fazārī down to Bilāl and Ṣuhayb.
- The four lessons concern looking down on people, the real measure of nobility, the preacher's priorities and gratitude.

<<<VERSE 6:53>>>
"In this way We have tested some by means of others, so those ˹disbelievers˺ may say, 'Has Allah favoured these ˹poor believers˺ out of all of us?'" The verse explains why the arrangement of the community was left as it was, with the weak in front. The testing is not an accident of history.

### Phrase by phrase

**"In this way We have tested some by means of others"** means that He tried them against one another and checked them with each other. The noble was tested by the common man, the Arab by the client, and the rich man by the poor man. The preference was given to the last of these, by bringing him forward into faith first. **"so those ˹disbelievers˺ may say"** introduces what the test is meant to produce out of them, and their saying is: has Allah favoured these poor men, whom we look down on, with guidance from among the rest of us? They were tested by the clients. The men named here are Umayyah ibn Khalaf al-Jumahī, al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah al-Makhzumī, Suhayl ibn ʿAmr, Abū Jahl ibn Hishām, ʿUyaynah ibn Ḥisn al-Fazārī, ʿUtbah and Shaybah the sons of Rabīʿah, and their lot among the leaders of Quraysh. **"Does Allah not best recognize the grateful"** is the answer. Is not Allah more knowledgeable about the people who thank and appreciate Him, in what they say, in what they do and in their hearts, and is He not the one who guides them? He directs these believers along the ways of peace, takes them out of darkness into light by His leave, and brings them to the path that is straight.

### The logic of their objection

At first most of the people who followed the Messenger of Allah ﷺ were the weak: men and women with no standing, and slaves. Only a few chiefs or noted men followed him. Quraysh used to mock the weak among them who believed, and some of them they tortured. Their argument was that Allah would not guide these people to good instead of them, if the thing they had embraced were good at all. The Qur'an reports the same reasoning from other disbelievers: had it been a good thing, they would not have preceded us to it (46:11). And: when Our clear verses are recited to them, the disbelievers say to the believers, which of the two groups is better in position and station (19:73). The reply to that claim comes with the next verse: how many a generation before them have We destroyed that was better in wealth, goods and outward appearance (19:74). The saying in this verse is thus the outcome of a test they were given through poor and weak believers, and they failed it. Instead of looking at the demonstration of Allah's absolute power, and concluding that nobility turns on morals and deeds rather than on wealth or standing, they took it as a grievance against Allah. They spoke as though the honor had been taken from them and given where it was not due.

### Nūḥ's answer, and a question in a foreign court

The same objection had been put to Nūḥ by his people: "Shall we believe in you when the meekest of the people follow you" (26:111), and they followed you without thinking, without a moment's consideration (11:27). His answer is the answer this verse follows: "And what knowledge have I of what they used to do? Their account is only with my Lord, if you could but know" (26:112-113). Their account is not mine, just as my account is not theirs. Nūḥ did not answer by saying that these poor men were nobler than them by the standard of deeds and morals. His method of correction did not allow a reply that would only provoke his hearers further. It was enough for him to say that lowliness is a matter of deeds, and since their deeds were not fully known to him, he could not settle who was noble and who was not. This became a known test for later scholars of communities as well. The early followers of a prophet are the poor of his people, and that fact counts as evidence for the truth of his prophethood. When the letter of the Prophet ﷺ reached Heracles, the Byzantine ruler, inviting him to Islam, he wanted to investigate the claim, so he put questions to people who knew him. One of them was whether the noblemen or the weak followed him, and he was told that they were the weak. Such, he said, is the case with the followers of messengers. Another account of the same exchange has Heraclius ask Abū Sufyān the question, and Abū Sufyān answer that it is the weak among them who follow him.

### What the answer turns on

The answer to their complaint is about gratitude, not about birth. A noble and honorable person, in the real sense, is the one who recognizes the right of the Benefactor over him and is grateful. He is the one who deserves honor and reward. It is not the man who spends his days soaked in the blessings of his Provider and goes on disobeying Him. Those who have reached wealth and popularity prefer the weight of their worldly interests to supporting a call of truth stripped of every advantage. Supporting it would pull their whole structure of vested interest down. And when they see the truth being upheld by people of ordinary status, their way of thinking is only confirmed, since they feel that association with it would lower them. Instead of testing the truth by the standard of truth, they judge it by a private touchstone of their own, and when it does not measure up to that, they set it aside. Allah does not look at shapes or colors. He looks at hearts and deeds, and that is the criterion this verse is arguing for. Whoever strives, the verse says elsewhere, will be guided, and Allah is with the doers of good (29:69).

### In short

- The arrangement of who comes first is itself the test, and it runs both ways through the community.
- Their question, whether Allah favoured these poor men out of all of them, is answered by 19:74, where better-off nations were destroyed.
- Nūḥ's reply to the same arrogance, in 26:111-113, is the pattern this verse follows.
- Heracles used the same test, that a prophet's followers are the poor, as evidence for the truth of prophethood.
- Gratitude, not lineage, is what Allah recognizes, and He looks at hearts and deeds rather than at shapes and colors.
