<<<VERSE 6:98>>>
And He is the One Who originated you all from a single soul, then assigned you a place to live and another to ˹be laid to˺ rest. We have already made the signs clear for people who comprehend

The signs have been in the sky and in the soil. Now the mirror is turned on the person looking.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He originated you all from a single soul"** He created you from one being, and that is Adam. Another verse puts it the same way: "O mankind, have taqwa of your Lord, who created you from a single person, and from him he created his mate, and from them both he created many men and women" (4:1). The account is that he began the creation by shaping their father from clay, and then brought them out of him, so that the whole of the human race is one family with one start.

**"then assigned you a place to live and another to be laid to rest"** The two words are built on two roots. The first comes from a word for settling, and it names a place of rest and peace where something stays. The second comes from a word for leaving a thing with someone for a few days, so it names a place where a thing is deposited temporarily. The explanations differ, and the difference is worth setting out.

The words of the Qur'an stand as they have been stated, and there are many probabilities in their interpretation, which is why the early interpreters differ here. Mujahid, Ata, Qatadah, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi and Ata al-Khurasani took the first word for the wombs of the mothers and the second for the loins of the fathers, and most of them read it so that people are settled in the wombs and deposited in the loins. Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud, Abu Abdur-Rahman al-Sulami and Qays bin Abu Hazim are named along with them. Some say that the place of deposit is the womb and the place of living is this world. Ibn Mas'ud and several others took the pair in another way still, making the settled place this life and the place of storage the grave after death. Others say the deposit is the grave and the settled place is the abode of the Akhirah, and there are several other sayings besides. One reading of the first word runs "established", and a variant recitation has it as a noun, a resting place for you.

Qadi Thanullah Panipati, in his Tafsir Mazhari, chose the widest of the readings. The place of settling is the abode of the Hereafter, Jannah or Jahannam. Everything a human being passes through from first to last, the womb, the house on earth, the grave, and the state of barzakh between death and the Resurrection, is the place of deposit, a stay of a few days. He finds support for it in the verse that says: "you shall surely travel from stage to stage" (84:19). So before a man reaches the abode he is making for, he is a traveller all his life, and in the middle of whatever peace and rest he enjoys he is going through the stages of a long journey into the night of his own being. The comment quotes a couplet to that effect: "A traveller I am. My destination I know not. Winding and wandering from eternity, only up to the grave I have reached."

### What the verse is doing with this

The verse is held up as a mirror to people who have sold themselves to the surface glamour of what they can see and to the craze for what their neighbours envy. They have forgotten that their place is elsewhere, that they have to come to their Creator, and that there is a journey ahead of them to the last abode. The message is a single sentence: open your eyes, look at what is real, come out of the web of deception, and walk into salvation.

One of the reports on how Adam was made gives the moment a still shorter form. The Exalted Lord said: "I loved something, so I created it solitary for the Solitary." The allusion in the verse, on this reading, runs: I made you from Adam, that single soul, and among all my creatures I gave no one the fortune I gave him, or the rank and the level I set him at. On the verse where he calls himself the most beautiful of creators (23:14), Adam is set in the most beautiful stature (95:4).

**"We have already made the signs clear for people who comprehend"** We have distinguished the signs and explained them in detail, for people who understand Allah's command and his oneness, who take in his words and their meanings, and who understand what they are being told.

### One origin, and no end to it

A workshop built by human beings cannot make a machine that then goes on producing countless machines of the same sort. Factories have to turn them out one after another. In the workshop of God this happens every day. A seed is sown, and generations of trees multiply out of it. Human beings are the same. From one man and one woman, billions upon billions have come into existence, and the process has no end in sight. Whoever notices this has seen that the power of the one who made the universe has no limit, since this rare feat, one thing brought into existence first and then endlessly repeating itself in countless numbers, is his work. And the same power can raise a great and perfect world out of this world. Belief in the Hereafter is not a strained theory. It is the acceptance of future events that may well occur, on the strength of similar events that are being observed every day.

### In short

- The two words for where a person is put are argued from their roots, and the entry lists the readings rather than choosing between them.
- The widest reading makes this whole life a deposit, and the abode of the next world the only settled place.
- The remark about Adam's creation is reported as a divine saying in a report, not as a verse.
- The point of the verse is the mirror it holds up, since a being that arrives in one way and goes on multiplying cannot be an accident.

<<<VERSE 6:99>>>
"And He is the One Who sends down rain from the sky—causing all kinds of plants to grow—producing green stalks from which We bring forth clustered grain. And from palm trees come clusters of dates hanging within reach. ˹There are˺ also gardens of grapevines, olives, and pomegranates, similar ˹in shape˺ but dissimilar ˹in taste˺. Look at their fruit as it yields and ripens! Indeed, in these are signs for people who believe"

Rain, and then a list of what comes out of it, and then a command to look.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He sends down rain from the sky"** He sends the water down in a set measure, as a blessing and a provision for his servants, as relief and the means of keeping alive for the creatures, and as mercy from Allah to his creation. "And we made every living thing from water" (21:30).

**"causing all kinds of plants to grow"** With that water he brings out the growth of everything, green produce and trees on which seeds and fruits are raised.

**"producing green stalks from which We bring forth clustered grain"** From the green thing comes grain packed one above another in clusters, like the ear or the spike of wheat and whatever grows in that fashion.

**"And from palm trees come clusters of dates hanging within reach"** Out of the palm and its spathe, the first thing it puts out, come the bunches of dates, with the clusters bunched close together, one near another. They hang low, within reach and easy to pick, so that anyone sitting or standing can get at them. Ibn Abbas said that the phrase describes the short date trees whose branches hang down close to the ground, and Ibn Jarir recorded that from him through ʿAli bin Abi Talhah al-Walibi.

**"gardens of grapevines, olives, and pomegranates, similar in shape but dissimilar in taste"** Grapes and dates are the dearest fruits to the people of the Hijaz, and perhaps the two are the best fruits in this world, and Allah reminds his servants of the favour of making them. "And from the fruits of the date-palms and the grapes you derive from it strong drink and goodly provision" (16:67), which was said before intoxicating drinks were forbidden. "And we made therein gardens of date-palms and grapes" (36:34). And: "in the earth are neighbouring tracts, and gardens of vines, and green crops, and date-palms, two or three from a single root, and not so, watered with one water, yet we make some of them better than others for eating" (13:4). The leaves of the olive and the pomegranate are alike in shape and look, while the fruit they carry differs, in the explanation given by Qatadah and several others. One note adds that the likeness is in the leaves and the unlikeness in the taste.

**"Look at their fruit as it yields and ripens"** Look, you who are being addressed, at the fruit when it first begins to be borne, what it looks like, and at its ripening after it has reached full growth and come to the state it returns to. Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi and Ata al-Khurasani took the word for ripening to mean the moment the fruit becomes ripe, and Ibn Abbas and Al-Bara' bin 'Azib are named with them. The sense is: look at the power of the one who made these fruits, who brought them out of dry wood and turned them into grapes and dates. What he did here is what he does everywhere, in the colours, the shapes, the tastes and the fragrance of everything he made.

**"In these are signs for people who believe"** In the difference between them there are portents and proofs that testify to the perfect power, the wisdom and the mercy of the one who created these things, and to his ability to raise the dead. People who believe in Allah and obey his messengers are named specifically, because they are the ones who will profit from the signs by their belief in them, and the disbelievers will not.

### Why the list is in this order

The passage has a remarkable care about sequence. Three ranges are being covered: what is below, what is above, and the air between them, that is, what comes to be in the living world. The description starts with the lower level, because it is nearest to us. It then splits in two. One part takes the vegetation, the plants and trees and gardens that grow on the land, and the other takes the living things with breath in them, beasts and human beings and animals. Vegetation comes first because it plainer. Then the other part follows, and it is different, because it has a ruh, a soul or spirit. It is deep. It runs from the sperm through its stages and the conditions attached to them, which is close to what physicians do when they diagnose and judge. Vegetation, by contrast, can be watched from outside as it grows, flowers and ripens. After that comes the air and what keeps life going in it, morning and evening. Then the higher things, the sun, the moon and the stars. Then the lower level is taken up again and used to close the passage, because it is the one people see most often.

One point of order remains. The lower world was mentioned briefly at first and at length afterwards, but in the long version the earlier sequence is reversed, with living things put before vegetation. The reason may be that the detailed statement is about gratitude for blessings, and a beneficiary deserves to be named before the thing through which the blessing reached him. Vegetation keeps its earlier place, because seeds and stones are the recipients rather than the owners. The rain mentioned in the middle is subordinate to the vegetation it produces, and rain has a further refinement about it: by its origin it comes from above, by its destination it comes down below, and by the distance it covers it belongs to the air.

### What rain proves

Earth and mud look dead and inert. Rain falls on them, and as soon as the water has been absorbed a green world springs up, with crops of many kinds and fruit trees of many sorts. That event is itself a sample of the world that is coming after this one. The carpet of colour that rises out of soaked ground shows what potential the maker of the world had hidden in it, and righteous deeds stand in the same relation to God's grace. When the grace comes down, the potential turns into fact, and the crops of the Hereafter come up. A man is reared first in his mother's womb, then he enters this world, and the grave in its turn is a sort of womb to which he is finally handed over. After that he opens his eyes in another world, to be sent to Heaven or to Hell for what he did.

Arguments for the unseen world that a person has to accept are already present in this visible one. But the only person who accepts them is the one ready to. A call to faith enters a mind freely when the man has already travelled half the distance along the path toward iman. Warning a man who is walking in the opposite direction does him no good at all.

### The gazing the verse asks for

The inner reading takes the sentence as an allusion in three directions at once. The unity of the essence, the attributes of lordship, and the craftsmanship of divinity are all in it. He is a Lord who exists by his essence, who is described by attributes, and who is recognised through what he has made and through the signs. Some say the wording points to what is beyond existence, so that a seeker can find a way to it, and some say it points to existence, so that a listener gets used to it and a person wanting knowledge comes to know. One note counts something curious: the pronoun "who" comes in the Qur'an thirty-nine times, twenty of them with "and" and nineteen without. The whole verse, from the sending down of water to its end, is a mark that the creator is one and has no peer in godhood, without contrivance in his power, without change of state in his self-sufficiency, safe from any end to his kingship, transcendent in his essence and in his description. The Lord of the worlds is calling his servants to this tawhid. Does the end of the verse not say: look? Look carefully, so that you know, and know so that you perceive. Elsewhere he says that the reminder is accepted only by a heart that is sound with the Real, and such a person is looking toward him before his eyes ever do (40:13). So the closing words, "in these are signs for a people who believe", mean this. Those who reach the signs of power are the people who already credit the signs of the attributes. Those who feel shame before God are the ones aware that he is watching them. And those who fear God are the ones who know he has power over them.

### In short

- The list is read as a graded argument, from what is closest to what is highest, and then back again.
- The date-palm's hanging clusters are explained by a report about short trees, and the two fruits named are the favourites of the Hijaz.
- Look and gaze are treated as the verse's real commands, since the proof is in watching something happen.
- Rain, soil and ripening fruit are made into an argument for the Resurrection, and for the unseen world in general.
- The verse ends by restricting the benefit of the signs to believers, and the entry says so without softening it.

<<<VERSE 6:100>>>
Yet they associate the jinn with Allah ˹in worship˺, even though He created them, and they falsely attribute to Him sons and daughters out of ignorance. Glorified and Exalted is He above what they claim

The creation of the fruits and of the human being leads to a charge, and the charge is about who gets worshipped.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Yet they associate the jinn with Allah, even though He created them"** This is a refutation of the idolaters who worshipped other than Allah and joined the jinn to him in worship, and glory be to Allah above that shirk and that kufr. A question arises at once, since these people were worshippers of statues rather than of jinn. The answer is that they worshipped the idols by obeying the jinn who ordered them to do it. That is why the angels will say on the Day of Rising: "glory be to you! You are our protector instead of them. Nay, but they used to worship the jinn, and most of them were believers in them" (34:41). The same is stated in the words Allah addressed to all of them: "did I not charge you, O children of Adam, not to worship Shaytan, for he is a plain enemy to you, and that you should worship me? This is a straight path" (36:60-61).

What they were told about shaytan is put at length in another place: they call on nothing but female deities besides him, and they call on a rebellious devil whom Allah cursed, and he said: I will take an appointed portion of your servants, I will mislead them and arouse false desires in them, and I will order them to slit the ears of cattle and to change what Allah created. "And whoever takes Shaytan as a protector instead of Allah has suffered a manifest loss. He makes promises to them and arouses false desires in them, and Shaytan's promises are nothing but deceptions" (4:117-120). And: "will you take him and his offspring as protectors and helpers instead of me?" (18:50). As Ibrahim said to his father: "O my father, do not worship Shaytan. Shaytan has been a rebel against the Most Merciful" (19:44). And Ibrahim said to his people: "do you worship what you yourselves carve, while Allah created you and what you make?" (37:95-96). Allah alone is the creator without partners, and so he alone deserves to be worshipped without partners.

One report explains the first half of the verse with a specific claim. Al-Kalbi said that it came down about the unbelievers who held that Allah and Satan are two brothers and partners, Allah being the creator of human beings, beasts and cattle, and Satan the creator of vipers, scorpions and predatory animals. Such are the assertions of the Magians. The verse's own answer is inside it: he created them, and he commanded them to declare his oneness, so how can a creature be his associate?

**"and they falsely attribute to Him sons and daughters out of ignorance"** They invented for him children without knowledge, without argument and without proof. The verb here means to invent, to claim falsely, to fabricate and to lie, as the scholars of the salaf explained it. The three accusations are named in order. The Jews said ʿUzayr is the son of Allah. The Christians said ʿIsa is. The pagans of Arabia said the angels are Allah's daughters.

**"Glorified and Exalted is He above what they claim"** He has cleared himself of having children or partners, and he absolves himself of what these ignorant, misled people describe him as. Glory be to him, and exalted be he above the sons, the daughters, the rivals, the equals and the partners that are attributed to him.

### How the mistake begins

The weakness being described here is an old one. A man finds some distinction or some mystery in a thing, and then takes that thing as a partner of God and starts worshipping it, to get its help or to be safe from the harm it might do him. That is how people came to worship angels, stars and jinn. The plain proof that they are not gods is that they have no power to create. They did not make themselves, and they cannot make anything else. They were made by another being. The question left in front of them is a plain one. Will the creator be accepted as God? Or will the things he made take the position of his godhead for themselves?

The other half of the proof runs up from below. A tree is given exactly what it needs to live, in the right proportions, and this is the state of everything in the universe. If things receive what they need because a giver gives it to them, then the giver has to be aware of everything at every moment. Otherwise the needs of each thing could not be met in exactly the measure that thing asks for. And for what purpose should a god with attributes like these take anyone as a partner at all?

### In short

- Worshipping jinn is explained as obeying them, which is how the verse connects idolatry to a power behind it.
- The claim that Allah and Satan are partners is reported as the assertion of the Magians, through one named transmitter.
- Sons and daughters are two separate charges, and each is attached to the people who made it.
- The inability to create is set out as the decisive test for anything a man is tempted to worship.
- The argument from provision runs the other way: exact provision implies exact knowledge, and knowledge of that kind belongs to one being alone.

<<<VERSE 6:101>>>

One verse, and it asks a question that is meant to end the discussion rather than open it.

How can a child be ascribed to such a being, and how can one of his creatures be suitable for his majesty? Allah is glorified above having a son.

### In short

- The proof is a question with a structure: a child needs two like parents, and there is nothing like him.
- The name Originator is glossed from the root for innovation, which is the sharpest form of the point that he had no precedent.
- The verse that answers the claim ends by leaving every person alone before God, which is the same solitude the verse began with.
- Creation and knowledge are put together as the two attributes that make the claim of offspring impossible.

<<<VERSE 6:102>>>
"That is Allah—your Lord! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him. ˹He is˺ the Creator of all things, so worship Him ˹alone˺. And He is the Maintainer of everything"

The conclusion of the argument, in the form of a sentence that could be written on its own.

### Phrase by phrase

**"That is Allah, your Lord"** The one who does all these things, who created everything and has neither son nor wife, is your Lord. He who is described this way, and not any other, is the one who has the claim on you.

**"There is no god worthy of worship except Him"** He is alone, without any partner, and he is the creator of all things, so the worship he is owed is worship of him alone, as the only one deserving of it. Declare his oneness and do not ascribe partners to him. He has no descendants and no ancestors, no wife, no equal and no rival.

**"And He is the Maintainer of everything"** He is the trustee of all things, their watcher and the one who disposes their affairs, giving them their provision and their protection by day and by night. He is their witness, and it is also said that the word means the guarantor of the provision of all created beings, and their keeper.

### What the sentence is answering

It is the being with those attributes who is your Lord, and you have no lord besides him. The clause comes with a negation and then an exception, there is no god except him, that is, alone, without any partner, and then he is named the creator of all things, so that the command to worship him follows from the same sentence. He takes care of everything, and that is what makes him the only one whose worship is due. The charges made in the verses just before this one are answered clause by clause in that way. A partner was joined to him from among the jinn, and he is the creator of them. Sons and daughters were invented for him, and he has no mate, no descendants and no ancestors. The verse closes by leaving nothing out of his care, so that no created thing stands outside what he disposes.

### In short

- The verse moves from what he is to what he is owed, and it does not argue the second from the first so much as announce it.
- To worship him is spelled out as declaring his oneness, so that the command and the creed are one act.
- Maintainer is glossed four ways, trustee, watcher, disposer of affairs and guarantor of provision, and all four are reported.
- Negation comes before affirmation, which is why "no god except him" stands between the two positive sentences.

<<<VERSE 6:103>>>
"No vision can encompass Him, but He encompasses all vision. For He is the Most Subtle, All-Aware"

The verse that settles what can and cannot be done with the eyes, and it is read carefully on both sides.

### Phrase by phrase

**"No vision can encompass Him"** Sight does not reach him in this life, and no created being sees what he sees. One gloss adds that sight will not take in the manner of seeing him even in the Hereafter, since nothing can hold him in knowledge. It is a denial, and it applies in particular circumstances. It is settled that the believer will see his Lord on the Day of Rising, in the manner he decides. Numerous reports from the Prophet affirm it, through sound chains, in the two Sahihs, in the Musnad collections and in the collections of Sunan. As for A'ishah, she affirmed the vision in the next life and refused it in this one, and she argued from this very verse. Her denial was a denial of encompassing. It was about seeing his grace and his majesty exactly as he is, and that is not open to any human being, any angel, or anything created. Whoever says that Muhammad saw his Lord, she held, has spoken a lie against Allah, since Allah declares that no vision encompasses him while he encompasses all vision. The report of this comes through Masruq.

**"but He encompasses all vision"** That is to say, he perceives them while they cannot perceive him. He embraces all sight in this world and in the next, seeing what human beings do not see, and nothing of it escapes him or is hidden from him, because he made it. It is also said that "all vision" means all the people who have sight. As-Suddi took the pair of clauses in this form: nothing sees him in this life, but he sees the whole of creation. Should not the one who created know, and he is the most subtle, the well informed (67:14)?

**"For He is the Most Subtle, All-Aware"** He is subtle in his dealings, and his knowledge surrounds his creatures. Abu Al-'Aliyah said the two names mean that he is subtle in bringing things forth and well informed about their position and their place. Luqman's advice to his son carries the same pair: "O my son, if a thing were the weight of a mustard seed, and were in a rock, or in the heavens or in the earth, Allah would bring it forth. Allah is subtle and well informed" (31:16).

### What was said to Musa

A hadith recorded in the Sahih of Muslim comes from Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari, from the Prophet: "Allah does not sleep, and it does not become him to sleep." He it is who "lowers the scale of everything and raises it." "The deeds of the day are raised to him before the night, and those of the night before the day." "His veil is light, or it is fire, and if he were to lift it, the brilliance of his face would burn everything his sight reaches of his creation." In the books revealed before this Qur'an there is a sentence of the same tenor. When Musa asked to see Allah, Allah said to him that no living thing sees him and lives, and that nothing dried up sees him and stays stretched out. The Qur'an tells the rest: "when his Lord appeared to the mountain, he made it crumble, and Musa fell down senseless. When he came to his senses he said: glory be to you, I turn to you in repentance, and I am the first of the believers" (7:143). None of these texts, the verse, the hadiths and the sayings, cancels the fact that Allah will be seen by his believing servants on the Day of Resurrection, while his might and his grace stay as they are. And in the hadith of Bukhari and Muslim the believers are given the plainest image of that vision: "Verily you shall see your Lord as clearly as you see the full moon at night".

### What looking for means

Man wants to see God in a shape he can touch. When God does not appear that way, a man takes some other visible thing for a god, and so feeds his craving for appearances. This shows how little he has understood about God's being. How could the one who made this vast universe with its regulated systems be so ordinary that he presents himself to a weak creature in a form his eyes can take in and his hands can hold? A human being is meant to discover God through his heart, and to see him with the eye of his faith. Whoever is content with insight is the one who will find him. The one who insists that he must be visible to the naked eye will never find him at all, like a man who will not know a flower by its fragrance and demands a chemical test instead.

### In short

- The verse denies encompassing rather than seeing, and the distinction is what lets A'ishah affirm the vision in the next life while refusing it in this one.
- The reports about seeing Allah are reported as widely transmitted and sound, with the collections named as the material names them.
- Musa's request and the mountain's fate are the proof that no created thing can look at him and survive the look.
- The two names at the end, Most Subtle and All-Aware, are shown at work in Luqman's mustard seed, which is the smallest weight and the widest search.
- The verse closes the passage about sight by turning the wish to see into a question about what kind of eye a man is using.
