<<<VERSE 2:8>>>
A third group now enters. "And there are some who say, “We believe in Allah and the Last Day,” yet they are not ˹true˺ believers". The believers were described in four verses and the open disbelievers in two. The next thirteen verses describe the hypocrites, people whose tongues say one thing while their hearts hold another. Because hypocrisy is hard to see, the Qur'an takes its time over it, and this verse opens the portrait with the hypocrite's defining act: a claim of faith that is not true.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And there are some who say"** uses the Arabic man yaqūl, which is singular in form but plural in sense, and the verse goes on to speak of them with a plural pronoun, "they". The verses that follow were revealed about the hypocrites.

**"We believe in Allah and the Last Day"** is what they said openly, claiming to be genuine in it. The Last Day is the Day of Resurrection, so called because it is the very last day. They professed belief in rising after death and in being repaid for their deeds.

**"yet they are not ˹true˺ believers"** means they did not believe in secret and were not genuine. They uttered the words with their tongues only. The same is said of them elsewhere: "When the hypocrites come to you ˹O Prophet˺, they say, “We bear witness that you are certainly the Messenger of Allah”—and surely Allah knows that you are His Messenger—but Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are truly liars" (63:1). They said such things only when they met the believers, not because they believed them, and they stressed their faith in Allah and the Last Day with their words when the reality was otherwise. So Allah declared that they lie in their very testimony of faith.

### What hypocrisy is

Nifāq means to show agreement on the outside and to hide evil within. It is of more than one kind. There is hypocrisy in belief, which keeps its people in Hell forever, and there is hypocrisy in conduct, which is among the major sins. Ibn Jurayj described the hypocrite: what he actually does differs from what he announces, what he hides differs from what he says, and the way he comes in and sits among people is not the way he goes out and behaves in their absence.

Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq reported Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation that the verse refers to the hypocrites among the Aws and the Khazraj and to those who behaved as they did. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah and as-Suddī explained it the same way. Allah revealed the traits of the hypocrites so that the believers would not be fooled by their outward appearance and would be spared a great harm. Otherwise the believers might have taken them for believers when they were in fact disbelievers, and to count sinners among the righteous is extremely dangerous.

### How hypocrisy began

The verses on the hypocrites were revealed in Madinah, because there were no hypocrites in Makkah. The situation there was the reverse: some people were forced to pretend disbelief while their hearts held faith. Then the Messenger of Allah ﷺ migrated to Madinah, where the Anṣār of the Aws and the Khazraj lived. They had worshipped idols in the days of ignorance like the other Arabs. Three Jewish tribes lived there too: Banū Qaynuqāʿ, allies of the Khazraj, and Banū an-Naḍīr and Banū Qurayẓah, allies of the Aws. Many of the Aws and Khazraj embraced Islam, but only a few of the Jews did, among them ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām. In the early period there were still no hypocrites, because the Muslims were not yet strong enough to be feared. On the contrary, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ made peace treaties with the Jews and with several Arab tribes around the city. Then the battle of Badr took place and Allah gave Islam and its people the victory. ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl was a leading man of Madinah, chief of the Khazraj, and in the days of ignorance he had been the master of both tribes. The people were about to crown him king when the message reached Madinah and many embraced Islam, and his heart filled with hatred for Islam and its people. When Badr was fought he said that Allah's religion had now become established, so he pretended to be a Muslim, and so did many like him, and many from among the People of the Book. That is when hypocrisy began, in Madinah and among the nomad tribes around it. There were no hypocrites among the Emigrants, because they had emigrated of their own free will, seeking Allah's pleasure. A Muslim who emigrated from Makkah had to leave behind all his wealth, his children and his land, and he did it for Allah's reward in the Hereafter.

### Even they avoided an outright lie

The wording of their claim shows how ugly lying is. Even the hypocrites, with all their hostility to Islam, tried as far as possible to avoid it. When they claimed to be Muslims they mentioned only their faith in Allah and in the Day of Judgment and left out faith in the Prophet ﷺ, for fear of stating a plain lie.

### The mind of a hypocrite

People who put worldly interests and expediency first find total, unconditional devotion to truth unwise, because their real loyalty is to their interests, and they can attach themselves to the truth only on the surface. They think themselves clever: safe in this world, and at the same time counted among the pious. It is wishful thinking, a product of imagination rather than a fact of life.

### Why Allah let there be hypocrites

The inner reading traces the hypocrites' hypocrisy back to the eminence of the Prophet ﷺ, in two ways, one of jealousy and one of mercy. He was the beloved of the Real, and his beauty and perfection passed beyond what understanding and imagination can hold, so Allah, out of jealousy, kept him veiled behind His protection and used the hypocrisy of the hypocrites as a mask over that beauty. He was veiled from the people of the world so that no one recognised him as he really was: "you ˹O Prophet˺ may see them facing towards you, but they cannot see" (7:198). Had their hypocrisy not covered that shining face, all creatures would have cast dust over the unseen light. A sun so bright, a light so brilliant, needed a hypocrisy of the kind ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl and his sort supplied. Without it, that radiant beauty would have affected the children of Ādam more than the beauty of ʿĪsā (Jesus) affected his people, who said, "The Messiah is the son of Allah" (9:30). A simile is given. The disk of the sun, whose rays shine from the fourth heaven, has its face turned toward the fifth. Allah created angels and entrusted the disk to them, and created deserts full of snow before them, from which they lift mountain after mountain of snow and dash it against the sun's disk to break its heat, since otherwise the world would burn from its shine and heat. In the same way the hypocrites' hypocrisy was thrown against the presence of that sun of good fortune, or else all the creatures would have tied on the belt of associating partners with Allah. And the mercy: that paragon of the world was all gentleness, as he said of himself, "I am a guided mercy", and as Allah said, "We have sent you ˹O Prophet˺ only as a mercy for the whole world" (21:107).

### In short

- Nifāq is agreement on the outside with evil hidden inside. In belief it is eternal ruin, in conduct a major sin.
- The hypocrites professed Allah and the Last Day only with their tongues, and Allah exposed their traits so that the believers would not be taken in.
- There were no hypocrites in Makkah and none among the Emigrants. Hypocrisy began in Madinah after Badr, led by ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl, when Islam became too strong to oppose openly.
- Even the hypocrites avoided stating a bare lie about the Prophet ﷺ, which shows how contemptible lying is.

<<<VERSE 2:9>>>
The next verse says what the false profession was for. "They seek to deceive Allah and the believers, yet they only deceive themselves, but they fail to perceive it". They thought their words bought them safety and standing. In fact the whole scheme turned back on them, and they could not see it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They seek to deceive Allah and the believers"** means they show faith outwardly while hiding disbelief, so as to escape the rulings that would apply to them in this world. Secretly they went against Allah and denied Him. It is also said that they were so bold with Allah that they thought they had actually deceived Him. They imagined that this behaviour would mislead Allah, or that the words they uttered would count for them with Him, which shows how total their ignorance was. They expected to fool Allah the way they might fool some of the believers, and on the Day of Judgment they will try the same thing: "they will ˹falsely˺ swear to Him as they swear to you, thinking they have something to stand on. Indeed, it is they who are the ˹total˺ liars" (58:18). The believers they tried to deceive were Abū Bakr and all the Companions of Muhammad ﷺ. The mention of Allah here, when the practical target was the Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims, carries a lesson. Probably none of them intended to deceive Allah or even imagined it possible. What they were doing was trying to deceive His Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims, and Allah equated that with an attempt to deceive Him. Whoever is insolent toward a prophet or a man of Allah is in the end insolent toward Allah Himself, and above all when it is the Prophet ﷺ, who stands at the head of all created beings. Some also read the mention of Allah in this verse as there for rhetorical force.

**"yet they only deceive themselves"** because the evil consequences of their deception rebound on them. They are disgraced in this world when Allah informs His Prophet ﷺ of what they hide, and they are punished in the Hereafter. Allah refuted their scheme: "Surely the hypocrites seek to deceive Allah, but He outwits them" (4:142). Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded Ibn Jurayj's comment on that verse: the hypocrites pronounce the testimony that there is no god but Allah in order to make their blood and property inviolable, while hiding disbelief. The verb used, yukhādiʿūn, is the form that normally denotes a mutual action, though here it denotes a one-way act, as when someone says ʿāqabtu al-liṣṣa, "I punished the thief", using the same form. A variant reading has wa-mā yakhdaʿūn.

**"but they fail to perceive it"** means they do not realise that they are deceiving themselves, and they do not know that Allah informs His Prophet ﷺ of what lies hidden in their hearts. Allah knows all that is secret and has told the believers what these people are like.

### A portrait of the hypocrite

Saʿīd reported Qatādah's description. The hypocrite is slippery. His tongue speaks the truth while his heart and his deeds fight against it. He wakes up in one condition and goes to sleep in another, and goes to sleep in one and wakes up in another. He changes his mind like a ship that swings about whichever way the wind blows.

### The inner reading

The harm of their deception and its punishment come back to them, so it is as if they really did deceive themselves, since they cheapened and belittled themselves. No one suffered from what they did but themselves. They cut their own lifeblood. When someone knows the true nature of things, whoever sets out to deceive him deceives only himself. The allusion in the verse is to the person who has forgotten Allah's prior kindness, who says "mine, through me, from me, and I" when he should say "Yours, through You, from You, and You". That delusion is the hardest punishment of all. He sees a mirage, sarāb, and thinks it is drink, sharāb, "but when they approach it, they find it to be nothing. Instead, they find Allah there ˹in the Hereafter, ready˺ to settle their account" (24:39).

### In short

- The hypocrites hid disbelief behind a show of faith to secure their lives and property under Muslim rule, and imagined this would work even with Allah.
- Deceiving the Prophet ﷺ and the believers is treated by Allah as an attempt to deceive Him, a warning about how insolence toward His messengers is judged.
- Every such scheme rebounds: exposure in this world, since Allah tells His Prophet ﷺ what they hide, and punishment in the next. They alone are the losers, and they do not sense it.

<<<VERSE 2:10>>>
Why do they behave this way, and why can they not see the folly of it? "There is sickness in their hearts, and Allah ˹only˺ lets their sickness increase. They will suffer a painful punishment for their lies". The verse names a disease, states that it is getting worse, and then ties the punishment to one habit in particular: lying.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There is sickness in their hearts"** means doubt. That is how Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd explained it, as reported through as-Suddī from Abū Mālik, Abū Ṣāliḥ and Murrah al-Hamdānī, along with other Companions, and Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and Qatādah said the same. ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam said it is a disease in religion, not a bodily disease: they are the hypocrites, and the disease is the doubt they brought into Islam. Others gloss it as doubt, hypocrisy, conflict and darkness, a sickness that weakens the heart. The inner reading calls it the sickness of shirk.

**"and Allah ˹only˺ lets their sickness increase"** means He increases their doubt, or increases them in shameful conduct, as ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn Zayd put it, citing the verse about the hypocrites and each new surah: "as for those with sickness in their hearts, it has increased them only in wickedness upon their wickedness, and they die as disbelievers" (9:124-125), that is, evil added to their evil and deviation to their deviation. This is correct, and it is a punishment matched to the sin, as the early scholars said. The opposite is also true: "As for those who are ˹rightly˺ guided, He increases them in guidance and blesses them with righteousness" (47:17). The increase comes through what Allah reveals in the Qur'an, since they disbelieve each new revelation. Their doubt gains hesitation on top of uncertainty. They reap what they sowed, and in the inner reading they grow sicker as they imagine that they have saved themselves by deceiving the Muslims.

**"They will suffer a painful punishment for their lies"** means a painful torment in the Hereafter that reaches into their very hearts, in the lowest reaches of Hell, the price of lying against Allah and against people and of rejecting what Muhammad ﷺ brought. The hypocrites have two marks: they lie, and they deny the unseen. The word for their lying can be read yukadhdhibūn, that they called the Prophet of Allah ﷺ a liar, or yakdhibūn, that they lied when they said "we believe". The verse points in particular to ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy, Jadd ibn Qays and Muʿattib ibn Qushayr.

### Disease of the heart

In medicine, disease is a state in which the balance of elements that keep a person healthy is lost, so the body stops working properly and may be destroyed. In the language of the Qur'an and the hadith the word is also used of mental and spiritual states that keep a person from any degree of spiritual perfection, gradually stripping him of the ability to do good and even of ordinary human decency until he reaches spiritual death. Junayd of Baghdad said that bodily diseases come from the four humours falling out of balance, and diseases of the heart come from giving in to physical desires. The disease hidden in the hypocrites' hearts was disbelief and rejection of the truth, and it was as much a physical sickness as a spiritual one. Ingratitude to one's Creator and Sustainer and defiance of His commands is spiritual sickness, plainly. Hiding that disbelief for petty worldly gain, lacking the courage to speak one's mind, is another disease of the soul. And hypocrisy is a physical disease too, because the hypocrite is forever shuddering at the thought of exposure. Envy is a necessary ingredient of hypocrisy, so he cannot bear to see the Muslims grow stronger, and yet he cannot even relieve his heart of its venom. All that tension naturally comes out in bodily illness. Hypocrisy is a cancer that keeps growing. Every test in life pushes such people further from true religion and closer to their own world-centred "religion".

### The curse of lying

There is a subtle point in the last clause. The hypocrites' worst crime was their disbelief and their hypocrisy in faith itself, and they had other crimes besides, nursing envy and malice against the Muslims and actually conspiring against them. Yet the painful punishment is tied to their habit of lying. This hints that lying was at bottom their real crime, the one that led them step by step into hypocrisy and disbelief. Hypocrisy and disbelief are far greater crimes, but they grew out of the habit of lying. That is why the Qur'an puts lying beside idol worship in a single breath: "So shun the impurity of idolatry, and shun words of falsehood" (22:30).

### Did the Prophet ﷺ know who they were?

Those who say the Prophet ﷺ knew the hypocrites of his time have as their only evidence the hadith of Ḥudhayfah ibn al-Yamān, in which the Prophet ﷺ gave him the names of fourteen hypocrites during the expedition to Tabūk. They had plotted to kill him at night on a hill there by startling his camel so that she would throw him down the slope. Allah informed him of the plot, and he told Ḥudhayfah their names. About the rest, Allah said: "Some of the nomads around you ˹believers˺ are hypocrites, as are some of the people of Medina. They have mastered hypocrisy. They are not known to you ˹O Prophet˺; they are known to Us" (9:101), and "If the hypocrites, and those with sickness in their hearts, and rumour-mongers in Medina do not desist, We will certainly incite you ˹O Prophet˺ against them" (33:60-61). These verses prove that he was not told the identity of every hypocrite. He was told their traits, and he suspected certain people of having them: "Had We willed, We could have truly shown them to you ˹O Prophet˺, and you would have certainly recognized them by their appearance. But you will surely recognize them by their tone of speech" (47:30).

The most notorious hypocrite of the time was ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl, and the Companion Zayd ibn Arqam gave truthful testimony to that effect. When ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb once raised the matter of Ibn Salūl with the Prophet ﷺ, he answered that he would not have the Arabs saying to one another that Muhammad kills his Companions. Yet when Ibn Salūl died the Prophet ﷺ prayed the funeral prayer over him and attended his burial as he did for other Muslims. It is recorded in the Ṣaḥīḥ that he said, "I was given the choice, and I chose," and in another narration, "If I knew that asking forgiveness for him more than seventy times would bring him forgiveness, I would do it."

### The half-hearted seeker

The inner reading applies the verse to anyone who has confused his ultimate aim with his worldly share and mixed his spiritual desire, irādah, with his whim, taking one step forward in desire for Allah and one step back toward the world and the lower self. He is neither a sincere seeker, a murīd, nor wholly heedless. Had the hypocrites been sincere in belief they would have been as safe from punishment in the Hereafter as they were in this world from the jizyah and the like, which polytheists and protected non-Muslims paid. Likewise, had the seeker been sincere in his desire, his heart would have reached toward the realities of communion, sincerity's blessings would have come to him, and he would have gained what he sought. Instead his state is the one in the saying: we were not firm, so justice will be firm with us and will not bend, and had we been sincere we would have been saved from trial. A barrier stands between the person whose worship has weakened and the degrees of the Gardens, and a barrier between the person whose spiritual desire has weakened and the intimate confidences of nearness. For those who lean on this world and follow their whims, reliance on the abode of illusion is itself a disease of the heart, and the disease grows as their greed grows. The more they get of the world, the more they crave what they have not got, and that hastens their punishment. Among the punishments that arrive at once are the scattering of their aims and the loss of all pleasure in life. These keep them from their Protector, and there is no enjoyment or rest for them in what they preferred when they followed their whims.

### In short

- The sickness is doubt, hypocrisy and the darkness that comes with them, a disease of religion rather than of the body, though hypocrisy's tension sickens the body too.
- Allah increases it as a punishment matched to the sin: each new revelation they reject deepens their doubt, just as each one deepens the believers' faith.
- The punishment is tied to lying because lying was the root habit from which their hypocrisy and disbelief grew. The Qur'an ranks falsehood beside idolatry.
- The Prophet ﷺ was told the hypocrites' traits, and fourteen names at Tabūk, but not the identity of every hypocrite. He still prayed over Ibn Salūl, the worst of them, out of hope for his forgiveness.

<<<VERSE 2:11>>>
The hypocrites did not see themselves as wreckers. "When they are told, “Do not spread corruption in the land,” they reply, “We are only peace-makers!”". Their activities threatened general disorder, yet they presented themselves as men of goodwill who were serving peace and order. This verse records the claim, and the next refutes it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When they are told"** means when the believers, or the Prophet ﷺ, say this to them. One reading takes the people addressed to be the Jews, warned against turning people away from the religion of Muhammad ﷺ.

**"Do not spread corruption in the land"** means, according to Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd in as-Suddī's commentary, do not commit disbelief and acts of disobedience. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, as reported by Abū Jaʿfar from ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, said it means do not disobey Allah on the earth, because the hypocrites' corruption is disobedience to Allah. Whoever disobeys Allah on the earth, or orders that He be disobeyed, has spread corruption on it, and peace in the earth and in the heavens is secured by obedience. Ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and Qatādah said the same. The corruption includes disbelief, hindering people from faith, and ruining things through rejection of the truth and disobedience.

**"We are only peace-makers!"** The Arabic innamā, "only", conveys not just emphasis but exclusivity: they claimed to be nothing but reformers, servants of order, whose activities could have nothing to do with disorder. They meant that they were not engaging in corruption at all, and that they were putting things right through obedience. In practice they meant something more specific. They wanted friendship with both sides, believers and disbelievers alike, and peace with each. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq reported Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation: they claimed to be making amends between the believers and the People of the Book.

### How the hypocrites corrupt the land

Ibn Jarīr spelled out their corruption. They disobey their Lord on His earth and persist in what He forbade. They abandon what He made obligatory and doubt His religion, although He accepts no deed from anyone without faith in His religion and certainty of its truth. They lie to the believers, saying the opposite of the doubt and hesitation in their hearts. They help, as far as they are able, those who work against Allah's loyal friends, and they back the deniers of Allah, of His Books and of His Messengers. That is how the hypocrites corrupt the earth while thinking they are doing good on it. This is right, and taking the disbelievers as allies is itself one of the kinds of corruption in the land. Allah said, "As for the disbelievers, they are guardians of one another. And unless you ˹believers˺ act likewise, there will be great oppression and corruption in the land" (8:73), so cutting the bond of loyalty between believers and disbelievers, and "O believers! Do not take disbelievers as allies instead of the believers. Would you like to give Allah solid proof against yourselves" (4:144), followed by "Surely the hypocrites will be in the lowest depths of the Fire" (4:145). Because the hypocrite looks like a believer on the outside, he confuses the true believers. His deceit is corruption twice over: he deceives the believers by professing what he does not believe, and he backs the disbelievers against them.

### Claims do not decide the question

Words alone do not settle whether someone is working for order or disorder. What thief calls himself a thief? The test is what a person does, not what he says. If his activities produce mischief he is a mischief-maker even if he never intended it. Among the things that spread disorder, some are recognised by everyone as corruption, such as theft, robbery, murder and rape, and every sensible, conscientious person keeps away from them. Others do not look like corruption on the outside but work unseen, destroying people's morals, which in turn opens the door to every kind of disorder. That is exactly what the hypocrites were doing. They did keep away from theft and robbery, and on that basis they denied being corrupters and insisted that they served order. But all the while their malice and envy had free rein as they plotted with the Muslims' enemies, and these are the things that finally bring a man down to the level of beasts. They called their own policy reform and prized it because it let them steer a safe course without clashing with anyone, which only shows how twisted their reasoning was.

### The seeker who turns back

In the inner reading the verse alludes to what happens when thoughts of warning come quietly to a heart, calling it to right conduct, and the person answers with self-indulgent interpretations, deceiving himself about what in fact testifies to the hardness of his heart. When the Real's proof came to them in those visiting thoughts and they denied it, Allah took the blessing out of their states and gave them in exchange an ear deaf to Him. Their affliction lies in resisting the path and losing faith in it. Just as an apostate is a fiercer enemy to the Muslims than others, so the one who turns back from spiritual desire to the world and ordinary habits is the worst of people in rejecting this path and the furthest from its followers. As the proverb says, whoever loses his stored grain to fire wishes the same on others. The sincere people of the path held that no help should be taken from those who have deserted the way, on the pattern of the Messenger ﷺ, who refused the zakāt of Thaʿlabah.

### In short

- Corruption in the land, in the Companions' explanation, is disbelief and disobedience, and peace on earth is secured by obedience to Allah.
- The hypocrites' particular corruption was doubting the religion, lying to the believers, and quietly allying with the disbelievers against them while posing as peacemakers between the two sides.
- "Only" in their reply claims exclusivity: nothing but reformers. But it is deeds, not claims, that make a reformer or a corrupter.
- Hidden moral corruption is worse than open crime because it destroys the people on whom all order depends.

<<<VERSE 2:12>>>
Allah answers their claim directly. "Indeed, it is they who are the corruptors, but they fail to perceive it". The verse opens with an alerting particle meant to make the hearer sit up. The people who called themselves the servants of peace are the very agents of disorder, and the second half of the verse adds something worse: they do not even know it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed"** translates a-lā, a word used to alert the listener and give emphasis to what follows. Allah, exalted is He, refutes them with it.

**"it is they who are the corruptors"** means that the hypocrites' behaviour, and their claim that it was all for peace, was itself corruption. Their hindering of others from the religion was corruption. Their actions were the cause of the ruin they denied.

**"but they fail to perceive it"** means that in their ignorance they did not see it as corruption. It is also said that it means the followers do not know that their leaders are misleading them. Two things follow from Allah's comment. First, the hypocrites' activities really did produce disorder in the land. Second, they did not engage in them with the express intention of creating disorder. They were not even aware that their actions could cause it.

### Why the hidden corrupter is more dangerous

Once a person has lost his feeling for ethical values and ordinary decency, even an average man turns into an agent of social disorder, and of a disorder far greater than thieves, robbers or even wild beasts can produce. Robbers and beasts can be held in check by the physical force of law and government. But men make the laws and men enforce them, and what becomes of law when man stops being man is on view across the world today. It is assumed on every side that humanity is marching forward and that modern man is civilisation's summit. Educational institutions cover every hamlet on earth, legislatures buzz night and day, bodies for enforcing the law spend billions, and bureaucratic offices multiply. Yet crime and disorder keep step with the march of civilisation. The reason is simple. Law is not an automatic machine. It needs men to make it work, and when man ceases to be man, neither laws nor bureaucratic agencies can cure the all-pervading disorder. That is why the greatest benefactor of mankind, the Prophet ﷺ, put all his attention into making men real men, in the fullest sense of the word. Once that is achieved, crime and disorder end by themselves, without huge police forces and vast systems of courts. As long as people in certain parts of the world acted on his teachings, they saw a peace and order never witnessed before, and not likely to be seen again where his teachings are dropped or ignored.

In practical terms the core of his teaching is fear of Allah and concern for the assessment of one's deeds on the Day of Judgment. Where these are missing, no constitution or legal code, no administrative body or university can force or persuade a person to keep away from crime. Those who run the world today keep inventing new administrative measures against crime while neglecting the very soul of administration, the fear of Allah, and even deploying the means of destroying it, with the necessary result that the remedy feeds the disease.

### Reformers in name

Thieves, robbers and everyone who makes trouble in the open are easy enough to deal with. The troublemakers described in these verses always appear dressed as reformers, waving colourful schemes of social improvement that are a mask for private interests, and raising the slogan, "We are only peace-makers!" That is why Allah, having told people not to spread corruption on the earth, also said elsewhere, "And Allah knows who intends harm and who intends good" (2:220). Only Allah knows what is in people's hearts and what they intend, and only He knows the real nature of each human act and where it leads, whether it serves order or disorder. So to serve the cause of order it is not enough to intend it. Far more essential is to bring one's thinking and action into line with the Sharīʿah, because an action, however well meant, can end in mischief and disorder if the Sharīʿah does not guide it. If such people thought deeply they would realise that reform on earth can come only from man's giving himself up to Allah alone, and that obstructing any movement meant to strengthen man's bond with his Lord's will is creating disorder on earth. Their seemingly profitable business runs at a loss, because they trade pure truth for an adulterated version that benefits no one.

### A smaller evil, and a way out

If the hypocrite had stayed an open disbeliever instead of pretending to be a Muslim, the harm from him would have been less. Better still, if he became sincere with Allah and made his deeds match his words, he would succeed.

### The inner reading

It is said that exposing liars needs nothing more than saying to their face, "You have lied." When they said, "We are only peace-makers!", the Real showed them to be liars: "Indeed, it is they who are the corruptors, but they fail to perceive it," meaning, We are aware of them and have exposed them.

### In short

- The emphatic opening turns their claim on its head: the self-styled peacemakers are the corrupters, and they really did spread disorder.
- They did not perceive it, either because they had no intention of corruption or because followers could not see where their leaders were taking them.
- Law cannot replace conscience. The Prophet ﷺ cured disorder by making men whole, through fear of Allah and mindfulness of the Judgment.
- Good intentions do not make a reformer. Only action guided by the Sharīʿah does, and Allah alone knows who intends harm and who intends good.

<<<VERSE 2:13>>>
The hypocrites were also offered a measure of true faith and sneered at it. "And when they are told, “Believe as others believe,” they reply, “Will we believe as the fools believe?” Indeed, it is they who are fools, but they do not know". The "others" are the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ. The hypocrites looked down on them as simpletons, and Allah returned the word to its rightful owners.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And when they are told, “Believe as others believe,”"** means: believe the way the believers do, in Allah and His angels, in His Books and His Messengers, in the Resurrection, in Paradise and in Hell, and obey Allah and His Messenger by keeping His commands and avoiding His prohibitions. The Arabic word for "others" is an-nās, the people, and by agreement of the scholars it refers here to the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ, because they were the people who had embraced faith and accepted the Qur'an as the word of Allah while it was being revealed. One reading addresses the verse to the Jews, telling them to believe in Muhammad ﷺ as ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and his companions had.

**"they reply, “Will we believe as the fools believe?”"** By "the fools", may Allah curse the hypocrites, they meant the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. That is the explanation of Abū al-ʿĀliyah, of as-Suddī with a chain to Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and other Companions, and of ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and ʿAbdur-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam. What they mean to say is: are we to be of the same rank as them, on the same road, when they are fools? Should we believe in Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an as the stupid and ignorant do? No, we will not follow their way. They spoke looking down on people and mocking them. A fool, safīh, is the ignorant, simple-minded person with little knowledge of what benefits and what harms. That is why, according to most scholars, the word covers children in the verse, "Do not entrust the incapable ˹among your dependants˺ with your wealth which Allah has made a means of support for you" (4:5).

**"Indeed, it is they who are fools, but they do not know"** is Allah's answer. He affirms that the hypocrites themselves are the fools, the stupid and ignorant ones, and adds that they do not know it. Being so thoroughly ignorant, they are unaware of their own deviation and ignorance, and that is more dangerous, a worse blindness, and further from the truth than the state of someone who knows.

### The Companions as the measure of faith

The verse sets a criterion of true faith before the hypocrites: believe as the people have believed. It shows that the only faith acceptable to Allah is faith like that of the Companions, and that anyone else's faith deserves the name only when he believes in the same things in the same way they did. Their faith is the touchstone for testing the faith of all other Muslims. Any belief or practice that departs from their faith and practice, however attractive it looks or however good the intention behind it, is invalid in the Sharīʿah. Scholars are agreed on this position. It is also worth noticing that the hypocrites called the Companions fools. Those who go astray have always been like this: whoever tries to show them the right road is, in their eyes, ignorant and stupid. But who, the Qur'an asks, is more stupid than the man who refuses to see clear signs? To people whose loyalty is to worldly interest, the believers who act purely for the truth seem to be ruining themselves for nothing.

The verse after this one shows the hypocrites' two faces. Among the Muslims they declared their faith loudly, and back among their own people they gave assurances that their ancestors' way had never been abandoned and that they sat with the Muslims only to mock them.

### Does the category of hypocrite still exist?

It has been debated whether the distinction between kufr and nifāq still applies after the Prophet's ﷺ time. The correct position is this. While he lived, a hypocrite could be identified and declared one in two ways. Either Allah informed the Prophet ﷺ by revelation that a particular man was not a Muslim at heart, or the man exposed himself by a word or deed openly repugnant to the Islamic creed or practice, so providing clear evidence against himself. Revelation ended when the Prophet ﷺ left this world, so the first way is no longer available. The second remains. If a person is found, on evidence, guilty in word or deed of rejecting, opposing, distorting or scorning the basic doctrines of Islam established beyond denial by the Qur'an, the hadith and ijmāʿ, consensus, he is regarded as a munāfiq despite his claim to be a true Muslim. The Qur'an gives such a person the name mulḥid, heretic, as in "those who abuse Our revelations" (41:40), and the hadith calls him a zindīq. Since his kufr is proved by clear and definite evidence, the Sharīʿah does not place him in a separate category but treats him as it treats any other kāfir. That is why the authoritative scholars agree that after the Prophet ﷺ the question of hypocrites ceased to be a live one: anyone who is not a genuine Muslim is counted a kāfir. Al-ʿAynī, commenting on al-Bukhārī, relates from Imām Mālik that since the Prophet's ﷺ days this is the one remaining mark of hypocrisy, and that a man who carries it may be called a hypocrite.

### Worthy and unworthy

The inner reading turns the verse into praise of the Lord: generous, renowned, wise in what He enacts, true in promise, pure in justice, complete in bounty and eternal in love, who shows what He wants and adorns as He wants. Each person has a name, and in each heart lies His mark. On some is the stamp of worthiness and on others the brand of unworthiness. The worthy are brought along the road of bounty, mounted on approval, escorted by gentleness, at the hour of generous giving, at the turning toward nearness. The unworthy are driven into the street of justice, mounted on wrath, escorted by abandonment, at the turning toward deprivation. Neither that nearness nor this deprivation came from water or clay, since on the day the two were written out there was neither water nor clay, only beginningless bounty and gentleness and everlasting severity and justice. The first was the portion of those who purify themselves and the second the portion of the hypocrites. The master of the path lamented the apportioning that went before him and the words spoken by the One who sees Himself, asking what good it does him to be happy or distraught when what frightens him is what the Powerful decreed in the Beginningless. The hypocrites had fallen under justice's garment, yet they approved of themselves and believed their name was good, and called the purifiers, the truthful and the Messenger's Companions "fools". The Lord of the Worlds, in His generosity, answered on their behalf: the fools are not those men but the ones who call them fools. When someone no longer belongs to himself, Allah belongs to him. When someone tightens his belt to obey Allah's commands, Allah joins him. The disbelievers told Muṣṭafā ﷺ he was possessed, "O you to whom the Reminder is revealed! You must be insane!" (15:6), and Allah answered, "By the grace of your Lord, you ˹O Prophet˺ are not insane" (68:2). You are My friend, approved by Me. What harm is it to you if they do not approve of you? What you need is My approval. A friend must be approved by the Friend, not by the city.

### In short

- "Others" means the Companions, by consensus, and their faith is the standard by which every later Muslim's faith is measured. Whatever departs from it is invalid, however good it looks.
- The hypocrites called the Companions fools. Allah ruled that the hypocrites are the fools, and not knowing it makes their state worse.
- After the Prophet ﷺ there is no revelation to unmask hypocrites. Anyone exposed by his own words or deeds as denying the established basics of Islam is simply a kāfir, whatever he calls himself.
- Those who mock the believers are answered by Allah Himself, as He answered those who called His Prophet ﷺ mad.
