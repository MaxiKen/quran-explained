<<<VERSE 6:95>>>
Indeed, Allah is the One Who causes seeds and fruit stones to sprout. He brings forth the living from the dead and the dead from the living. That is Allah! How can you then be deluded ˹from the truth˺

Four verses about what Allah does in the sky and in the soil, set down straight after the scene of the Hereafter. They are the medicine for the heedlessness just described.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Allah is the One Who causes seeds and fruit stones to sprout"** He splits open the grain of the seed and the stone of the date, and out of them comes whatever grows, in all the kinds, colours, shapes and tastes of grain and produce. The marvel being pointed at is a dry seed and a hard pit opening up, and a living green thing coming out of them. That is the doing of the being who created the world, and a human being has no share in it. A farmer can clear away what would block the shoot, and that is the height of his contribution. There is ploughing, and levelling, and softening, and manuring, and watering. The most all of it can achieve is that the newcomer rising inside the seed has nothing in its path to struggle against. Everything after that is the real job, and it belongs to someone else. That the split should happen, that a bud should rise, that leaves of wonderful variety should follow, and then flowers and fruit in colours, smells and tastes that boggle the mind, is taken care of by another hand. No human ingenuity comes near it, and it cannot manufacture one leaf or one petal in that manner. So another verse asks: "Do you see the seed you sow? Is it you who make it grow, or are we the one who makes it grow?"

Another commentary sets the same point beside human manufacture. A man who has to build a motor car makes every part separately, then assembles the parts and gives them their finish. God's method with a tree or a human being is different. He produces the whole of the thing at one time, out of the one seed or the one drop, and it goes on growing. In his workshop the entire tree comes out of the seed. That is a process no human being can start, and it proves that there is a being greater than man, whose plan is above all other plans.

**"He brings forth the living from the dead"** He brings the living plant out of the seed and the stone, which are lifeless things without sense. The same clause is explained in several other ways. He brings living creatures and beasts out of drops of sperm. He makes birds out of eggs. He makes the ear of grain and the fruit out of the grain and the stone. And a sign for them is the dead land: he gave it life and brought out of it grains, so that they eat from it (36:33), until the end of the passage, which adds what they know not (36:36).

**"and the dead from the living"** He brings the egg from the hen, and the sperm and the egg out of living bodies, and grain out of what stands in the field. Others take the pair in a moral sense: the wicked child comes from the righteous parent, and the righteous child from the wicked one. The clause is left open, and more meanings than these are possible for it.

**"That is Allah! How can you then be deluded from the truth?"** Whoever does all this is Allah, one, without partners, and not any other god. So look at the direction you have been pushed in. You have been turned from the truth to the falsehood of worshipping other than him, with full knowledge, taking statues you carved yourselves to be the ones who remove difficulties and grant needs. Why do you lie, when the proof has been established and the ground is being seen under your feet?

### The heart's seed split open

The inner reading takes the splitting as two acts at once. As Allah splits open the seeds of what people eat, so that plants come out and are fitted for nourishment, he splits open the seed of the heart too, so that the jewel of self-purification shows its face, and inside it is the servant's deliverance. The first of these is what keeps the soul in being. The second is what makes the faith stand firm. He himself rears both of them and gives each its daily provision. He rears the heart by letting it witness him, and he rears the soul with his blessings. Then he makes the soul the mount of the heart, so that the heart rides it in the arena of worship and drives it over the stations of the acts of worship, until it reaches the goal: "surely to your Lord is the final end" (53:42). That is the day of triumph, of endless felicity and boundless good fortune, given to the servant for nothing, a fragrant herb sprung from the thorn of poverty, and a dawn of happiness risen from the horizon of being set free.

### In short

- The verse argues from an act every farmer watches and none of them performs.
- Living from dead and dead from living is read botanically, zoologically and morally, and the entry keeps the readings side by side.
- The rhetorical question at the end is not about ignorance but about being turned away, since the evidence is in the ground in front of them.
- The inner reading moves the split from the soil to the heart, and makes the growing plant a picture of worship reaching its end.

<<<VERSE 6:96>>>
He causes the dawn to break, and has made the night for rest and ˹made˺ the sun and the moon ˹to travel˺ with precision. That is the design of the Almighty, All-Knowing

The same argument is now run on a scale nobody can stand in, and it moves in three steps: light, rest, and measure.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He causes the dawn to break"** The word means one who splits, and what is split here is the morning. The phrase in the verse is faliq al-isbah, and al-Isbah is the time of morning. He tears the dark layer of the night open and brings the morning out of it. This is the creator of light and of darkness, and the beginning of this surah already said that he originated the darkness and the light (6:1). He makes the night's darkness vanish, brings the day forth, and so gives brightness to the world and light along the horizon, ending the night with its depth and starting the day with its clearness. It is a reminder of his power to create opposites, and it testifies to his greatness and to the reach of his authority: "he brings the night as a cover over the day, seeking it rapidly" (7:54). Three short surahs swear by the same pair of opposites. "By the morning brightness, and by the night when it is still" (93:1-2). "By the night as it envelopes, and by the day as it appears in brightness" (92:1-2). "And by the day as it shows the sun's brightness, and by the night as it conceals it" (91:3-4).

**"and has made the night for rest"** The word for rest comes from a root for being at peace, and anything, a time or a place, in which a creature reaches peace and comfort is called by that name. A person's home is called his place of rest in the Qur'an, because a home is a settled source of peace and comfort even when it is only a hut (16:80). So the sentence means that the night has been made a time of peace and comfort for every living thing. The first half of the verse named the blessings that a person uses in the daylight, which are not equally available in the dark. Then the second half says that the dark is not a bad thing either, since it lets a man who is finished with a hard day get the rest he needs to start again. Human nature cannot survive work without a break.

Consider what it would be like if everyone fixed his own hour of sleep by his own choice. Somebody would be getting into bed at eight in the morning, somebody at noon, and others at all sorts of odd hours through the day and the night. Not one hour of the twenty-four would be free of somebody at his job, or his business, or his work, with the plants and the factories running as usual. The noise of traffic and factories and workers would interfere with the sleep of the sleeping, and the absence of the sleepers would disadvantage those still working. And the sleepers would miss what can only be done when it is their time to lie down. Instead, the weight of sleep is put on every creature, so that stopping is forced on him. With the fall of dusk, birds and beasts and cattle make for their nests, their lairs and their homes. A man has to leave off and has to turn toward where he sleeps. Silence holds. The dark is sleep's ally, because light does not suit sleep. Now imagine the governments and peoples of the world agreeing on one universal hour for sleep. The agreement would raise impossible problems first, and even if human beings kept to it, who would make the animals keep to it? The majesty of Allah is high, and his power to subdue is great. He brings sleep down on every person and every living creature at an appointed time, and that is how he has spared them the need for any agreement at all: "blessed be Allah, the best of creators" (23:14).

**"and the sun and the moon with precision"** The word here is used for counting, measuring and computing. He has fixed their rising, their setting and their movement in a measure by which people can work out years, months, days, hours, and even minutes and seconds. "It is he who made the sun a shining thing and the moon a light, and measured out stages for it" (10:5). "It is not for the sun to overtake the moon, nor does the night outstrip the day. They all float, each in an orbit" (36:40). "The sun and the moon and the stars are all subjected to his command" (16:12). The sun and the moon have specific orbits on an appointed term, with a precision that does not change or alter, and each has its own positions in summer and in winter, which is what lengthens and shortens the night and the day. The grammar here is open in one respect. The words for the sun and the moon can be read as joined to the night, so that he made the night for stillness and made the two lights for the same purpose. Or a preposition can be understood, giving bi-husbān, with a reckoning, so that the sense is that they follow courses precisely calculated. That is how the surah of al-Rahman states it.

**"That is the design of the Almighty, All-Knowing"** All of this happens by the decree and the measuring of the mighty one, who is never resisted and never contradicted, and who knows all things. Nothing escapes his knowledge, not even the weight of an atom in the earth or in the heavens. He is mighty in retribution against whoever does not believe in him, and wise in his providence, knowing who believes and who does not. Allah very often names the creation of night and day and the two lights, and then closes with his attributes of power and knowledge, as here, and as in the verse: "and a sign for them is the night. We withdraw from it the day, and at once they are in darkness. And the sun runs on to its fixed term. That is the decree of the mighty, the all-knowing" (36:37-38). At the opening of the surah called Ha-Mim As-Sajdah, after the creation of the heavens and the earth, comes this: "and we adorned the nearest heaven with lamps, as an ornament and as a guard. Such is the decree of the mighty, the all-knowing" (41:12).

### The dawn split in the heart as well

The inner reading follows the same line between the two halves of the verse. He is the one who splits the dawn, and he made the night a stillness. So if the regions of the world are lit up with the dawn of existence, then it is no marvel at all that the hidden parts of the heart are lit up with the dawn of recognition. One of the masters of the Tariqah said of the phrase: "Splitter apart of the dawn means that he opens the hearts with the explanation of the lights of unseen things." And, in the same sentence: "he lights up the secret cores with the remembrance of good things, and with the repose that reports bring." Verse 95 was about the seed of the heart being split open. This verse is about the dawn of the heart, and the two belong together.

### What the system was meant to teach

Huge glowing bodies of awesome size, moving on a programme that does not vary by a second through thousands of years, are the work of a power that cannot be faced. Whatever machinery runs them needs no workshop, and no repair and no replacement of parts whose time is up. Everything in the system performs exactly as it was ordered. But it was because of this same unbroken order that a man went wrong, because he took these movements as things existing in their own right and came in the end to worship them. Had the system been open to flaws, had it broken down a little here and there or needed time off for repairs, people would have noticed that the mechanism is not self-running, that somebody made it and somebody keeps it going. The brilliance and the permanence of these bodies is what blinded them to the reality behind it. It was to tell them what that reality is that books and prophets and messengers came.

The verse also shows that the reckoning of years and months may be solar or lunar, and both are favours from Allah. The lunar calendar is used in Islamic rulings because it spares ordinary people the trouble of complicated calculation, and since Islamic law and Islamic history run on it, it is an obligation on this ummah to keep and protect it. If another method, a solar or Gregorian reckoning, is adopted out of some necessity, there is no sin in that. But letting the lunar calendar be dropped altogether and forgotten is a grave sin, and the practice is already causing a situation in which a Muslim does not know when Ramadan, Dhul-Hijjah or Muharram will come.

The one who closes the verse with the words "the Almighty, the All-Knowing" is saying that a system this precise and this perpetual, at this scale, admitting no difference of even a second, can only be the act of the one who is mighty, the subduer of everything there is, fully able to carry out his will as he sees fit. And besides that he is knowing, with the knowledge of everything and the mastery of whatever he chooses to do. The measurements behind the claim are worth a look. The volume of the sun is about 1.2 million times that of the earth, and the earth itself is about four times the size of the moon. The moon lies roughly 250,000 miles away and goes round the earth, and the earth sits about 95 million miles from the sun, moving in two ways at once, spinning on its axis while it circles the sun. The other stars turn likewise, at distances vast from us and from each other, and every one of them moves with extreme regularity. Because of that arrangement day and night come into being, systems of reckoning can be built on it, and a person can order his life on land and at sea. That no discrepancy has entered the system across millions of years proves a being whose power is unlimited in greatness. These large signs show a maker who is knowledgeable, since an ignorant being could not put up so complicated a structure. He is all-powerful, or the running of this enormous workshop would not be possible. And his planning is perfect, or there would be no such meaning and harmony in a universe this colossal.

### In short

- Dawn, night and the two lights are set out as three separate proofs, and the middle one is a proof by comfort rather than by power.
- The night is argued for at length, since a blessing that arrives without being asked for is the one nobody thinks to thank.
- The precision of the orbits is what makes the heavens a reckoning rather than a mystery, and the verse closes on knowledge to match the measurement.
- The passage is also a lesson in why these bodies came to be worshipped, and why the regularity that proved their maker blinded people to him instead.
- The keeping of the lunar calendar is drawn out of the verse as a duty, with a permission for other reckonings when they are needed.

<<<VERSE 6:97>>>
And He is the One Who has made the stars as your guide through the darkness of land and sea. We have already made the signs clear for people who know

One more item in the list, and it is the one a traveller actually uses.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And He is the One Who has made the stars as your guide through the darkness of land and sea"** There are stars besides the sun and the moon. They are no lesser sign of the perfect power of Allah. He made them for you, O children of Adam, for many reasons of his wisdom. The benefit named in the verse is the immediate one. People travelling on the high seas or over open land can chart their course by them in the dark of night, when it is not easy to do otherwise. The darknesses are the dark tracts of the land and of the sea, and the fear of crossing them when the route is unclear. Experience shows that even in an age of sophisticated guidance there are situations in which the position of the stars cannot be set aside.

**"We have already made the signs clear for people who know"** We have explained the verses in detail. We have expounded the Qur'an and the signs of Allah's oneness, and set out the proofs of our power, and made them plain. This is for people who have knowledge, who are sound in mind, who reflect and take benefit from what they are shown. Such people can tell truth from falsehood and keep clear of the second. The clause states something blunt. Anyone who does not recognise the presence and the power of Allah from signs this open is unaware, however sharp he may seem.

### What may be believed about the stars

Some of the salaf set a limit here. Whoever holds about these stars anything other than three things has gone wrong and has lied against Allah. Allah made them an ornament for the nearest heaven, and something to drive at the evil ones who eavesdrop, and signs for direction in the dark recesses of the land and the sea. Nothing else. Those who fix their eyes on the stars and never look at the one who made them and moves them have misjudged the distance: the stars are not there on their own, and they are not there to stay. They are observing the command of their maker, and a man who reads them as more than that has been deceived in an unlucky way.

### In short

- The stars are listed as a mercy for travellers, which keeps the argument practical rather than spectacular.
- Three uses are permitted as belief, and anything added to them is described as a lie about Allah.
- The closing clause about people who know makes the clarity of the signs a standing fact, and inattention the only missing element.
