<<<VERSE 2:144>>>
Verse 144 is the command itself, the moment the qiblah changed. "Indeed, We see you ˹O Prophet˺ turning your face towards heaven. Now We will make you turn towards a direction ˹of prayer˺ that will please you. So turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque ˹in Mecca˺—wherever you are, turn your faces towards it. Those who were given the Scripture certainly know this to be the truth from their Lord. And Allah is never unaware of what they do". It opens by describing how much the Prophet ﷺ wanted the Kaʿbah as the qiblah of the Muslims, and then grants the wish in the same breath.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We see you ˹O Prophet˺ turning your face towards heaven."**. The Arabic opens with a particle of affirmation: We have indeed seen you. The Prophet ﷺ kept looking up at the sky, searching for revelation and longing for the command to face the Kaʿbah, waiting for Jibrīl (Gabriel) to come down and announce the change. Ibn ʿAbbās, in the report of ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah, said that the first thing abrogated in the Qur'an concerned the qiblah. When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ migrated to Madinah, the Jews were the majority there, and Allah commanded him to face Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem). This delighted the Jews. He faced it for ten months and more, but he loved the qiblah of Ibrāhīm (Abraham), and he would supplicate to Allah and look up to the sky, until this verse was revealed. The Jews disliked the ruling and asked what had turned the Muslims from their former qiblah (2:142). Allah had already said that east and west belong to Him (2:115), and He explained that the first qiblah had been a test (2:143).

His inclination has been explained in several ways, and the explanations do not conflict. Before prophethood he already followed, by the prompting of his own nature, the way of Ibrāhīm, and once revelation began the Qur'an itself described his Sharīʿah as fully in accord with the Abrahamic way. The qiblah of Ibrāhīm and of Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) had been the Kaʿbah, so it was natural for him to want it as the qiblah of the Muslims. A further factor was that the Arab tribes, idolaters though they were, at least claimed to follow the way of Ibrāhīm and acknowledged the Kaʿbah as their qiblah, in contrast to the Jews. Once the Kaʿbah became the Muslims' qiblah, the Arabs could be expected to find Islam more acceptable. The hope that adopting Bayt al-Maqdis would draw the Jews closer to Islam had meanwhile been dashed by the events of the preceding sixteen or seventeen months, for their hostility, fed by vanity, had only grown. Whatever the motive, the Prophet ﷺ was very keen on the change. Prophets stand as close to Allah as a human being can, and that station teaches them a strict spiritual etiquette: they never put a request before Allah until they have been given leave to do so. This suggests that he had already been permitted to pray for his wish and hoped the prayer would be granted. So he turned his face again and again to the sky, anxiously awaiting the angel who would bring the ruling he longed for. Allah describes this state of His Prophet ﷺ with appreciation.

**"Now We will make you turn towards a direction ˹of prayer˺ that will please you."**. A qiblah that you will love, the qiblah of Ibrāhīm, the Sacred House of Allah instead of the direction you now face. The promise is given and then immediately fulfilled by the command that follows. Here is a subtle working of divine grace: the Prophet ﷺ was first granted the joy of hearing a promise made, and straight after it the greater joy of seeing the promise kept, as al-Qurṭubī, al-Jaṣṣāṣ and al-Maẓharī note.

**"So turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque ˹in Mecca˺"**. Face the Kaʿbah when you pray. Al-Ḥākim related from ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib that the phrase means its direction, and he judged the chain authentic though al-Bukhārī and Muslim did not include it. Abū al-ʿĀliyah and Mujāhid hold this view, as do ʿIkrimah and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr. Qatādah, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and others also give it.

**"wherever you are, turn your faces towards it."**. This addresses the whole Muslim community: wherever you are, on land or at sea, east, west, north or south, face the Kaʿbah in your prayers. There are exceptions. A voluntary prayer (nafl) offered while travelling may be prayed in whatever direction one's body is facing, with the heart intending the Kaʿbah. When battle is raging, one prays however one can. And a person unsure of the direction who prays the wrong way, believing it to be the qiblah, is excused, for Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity.

**"Those who were given the Scripture certainly know this to be the truth from their Lord."**. The Jews and Christians know well that the change of direction is the truth from their Lord, because their Scripture describes how the Prophet ﷺ would re-orient himself to the Kaʿbah, and that the Sacred Precinct is the qiblah of Ibrāhīm. They conceal this. The Jews who resented the change from Bayt al-Maqdis already knew that Allah would command him to face the Kaʿbah. They read in their Books their prophets' description of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his Ummah, and that Allah had honoured him with the complete and noble legislation. Yet the People of the Book deny these facts out of envy, disbelief and rebellion. The prophets who arose among the Jews had been told that Allah would one day alter the qiblah, and they passed that knowledge on, so the Jewish scholars should have expected it. Only a few, such as ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and Mukhayrīq, confirmed the command and acknowledged that Allah had revealed the truth through the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ.

**"And Allah is never unaware of what they do"**. He is not heedless of what they conceal. This is a threat. He will reward those who turn from the truth according to their deeds. On another reading the verb is "what you do", addressed to the believers when they obey His command.

### Why "the Sacred Mosque" and not "the Kaʿbah"

Allah is not confined to any direction (2:115). To create a visible unity among Muslims scattered across the world, Divine Wisdom assigned them one definite orientation, and Bayt al-Maqdis could have served that purpose as well as any other place. The Kaʿbah was chosen in accordance with the Prophet's ﷺ wish, and the ruling announced here. One would have expected the words "turn your face towards the Kaʿbah" or "towards the House of Allah". Instead the Qur'an says "towards the Sacred Mosque", al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, and this choice of words settles several important questions.

Strictly speaking the qiblah is the House of Allah, the Kaʿbah. But one can face it exactly only while one can see it. For people at a distance, facing it exactly would be an impossibly rigorous obligation, since in distant towns an exact bearing is hard and uncertain even with instruments and calculation. The Sharīʿah always aims at ease. So the Qur'an names as the qiblah not the Kaʿbah but the Sacred Mosque, which covers a far wider area and is easy to face even from far away. A further facility lies in the word shaṭr. It means "the half of a thing" or "the direction in which a thing lies", and there is agreement that the second sense is meant here. The word itself therefore establishes that for places far from Makkah it is not even necessary, for the prayer to be valid, to face the Sacred Mosque exactly. Facing in its direction is enough, as al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ explains.

An example makes the rule clear. For countries east of Makkah, such as Pakistan and India, the direction of the Sacred Mosque is the west, and anyone who faces west has prayed validly. Because the sun sets at one point in summer and another in winter, the jurists have ruled that in such countries, for ṣalāh, the west, which is the qiblah direction, lies between those two points. In mathematical terms an arc of 48 degrees between them counts as the direction of the qiblah. Even someone who inclines 24 degrees to the right or left of the actual bearing of the Sacred Mosque is still facing the right way and his prayer is valid, as the commentary on al-Chaghmīnī sets out in detail.

This should expose the ignorance and muddle of those who, on finding some mosques in Pakistan and India two or three degrees off true, have declared the prayers offered in them null and void. Such baseless opinions only betray a wish to sow confusion and bickering among Muslims. The Sharīʿah is for all people and all countries until the Day of Judgement, so its injunctions in every sphere of life have been made easy to practise. Muslims in remote hamlets, mountains, forests or islands must be able to act on them from their own observation and experience, without scientific instruments or mathematical calculation. People east of Makkah may therefore take an arc of 48 degrees as their qiblah, and a deviation of five or ten degrees does not affect the validity of their prayers at all. A hadith al-Tirmidhī reports from Abū Hurayrah makes this plain: "The qiblah lies between the east and the west." It was addressed to the people of Madinah, whose exact qiblah is to the south, somewhere between east and west, but in effect it explains the phrase "towards the Sacred Mosque".

That is the general principle. Still, when laying the foundations of a mosque one should try to make the orientation towards the House of Allah as exact as possible. The Successors of the Companions, and the generations after them, used a simple method. If a town had a mosque built by the Companions, the neighbouring mosques were given the same orientation, and these in turn served as models for the mosques of the surrounding villages and towns, in a chain that kept extending itself. The method for fixing the qiblah far from Makkah has therefore always been this: if an old mosque stands nearby, new mosques should follow its orientation, because in so many towns it was the Companions themselves or their Successors who built the mosques and fixed the direction that later generations followed. The mosques already built are sufficient to determine the orientation, and it is improper to raise needless objections and doubts. The Sharīʿah disapproves of attempts to throw Muslims into perplexity, because such perplexity can lead people to suspect that since the Companions, their Successors and Muslims in general failed to fix a mathematically exact bearing, their prayers were invalid. That thought is not only false but insolent.

For this reason Ibn Rajab, the famous Ḥanbalī scholar of the eighth century after the Hijrah, disapproved of using astronomical instruments and complex calculations to fix the orientation. He wrote that the scholars in general permit learning astronomy insofar as it helps in determining the qiblah or in finding one's way on a journey and the directions of roads. More knowledge than that is not necessary under the Sharīʿah, since it may lead a person to neglect more important matters, and indulgence in complex calculation sometimes breeds vile doubts about the mosques of the Muslims in their towns, a weakness especially common among amateurs of such sciences. It may even lead a person to think that the prayers of the Companions and their Successors in certain towns were invalid, which is wholly false. For this reason, he added, Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal forbade reliance on the Pole Star in fixing the orientation, since the hadith states only that the qiblah is between east and west.

As for deserted regions, forests, new settlements and the like, where no earlier mosque exists, the Sharīʿah lays down a rule based on the practice of the Companions and their Successors. One should make an approximate determination of the qiblah by the sun, the moon and the Pole Star, phenomena everyone knows well enough, and if one still suspects a slight deviation, ignore it. According to al-Badāʾiʿ, the authoritative work of jurisprudence, in places far from Makkah an approximately correct orientation chosen on such general indications stands in place of the Kaʿbah, and every ruling about the qiblah applies to the direction so chosen. The Sharīʿah offers many illustrations of the principle at work. Sleep is taken to stand for the passing of wind and so invalidates wuḍūʾ (ablution). A journey is taken to represent hardship even when it involves none, and anyone on any kind of journey receives all the concessions the Sharīʿah grants a traveller. The same principle requires that an orientation fixed by general and familiar indications be accepted as the qiblah. The scholar known as Baḥr al-ʿUlūm states the rule in his Rasāʾil al-Arkān: the only condition for facing the qiblah is that the person praying be duly convinced that his face is turned towards the Kaʿbah. The Sharīʿah does not compel us to adopt the exact bearing obtainable only with astronomical instruments, so the scholars in general conclude that a deviation invalidates the prayer only when it is as great as the difference between east and west.

### The courtesy of not asking

In the devotional reading, Allah had let His Prophet ﷺ know that the Real's eye was on him, so that his courtesy would be modelled on the Real's own. Practising that courtesy, he did not ask the Real for his heart's desire. He only gazed at heaven. Then He gave him more than He gives those who do ask. A generous Lord with an exalted servant keeps that servant within courtesy's bounds, shows him the road of deeds and grants him success, then rewards him for the deeds and praises him for his reverence: "How excellent is the reward for those who work ˹righteousness˺" (3:136), and of Sulaymān (Solomon), "what an excellent servant ˹he was˺!" (38:30). So He told Muṣṭafā ﷺ: you are in My vision and My exalted witnessing, so take care to recognise what respect the Presence is owed, and let your asking keep within courtesy. When his heart was set on the qiblah he did not show it, out of courtesy. He kept the wish in his heart, and then the Exalted Presence addressed him: We have come to know the wish in your heart and have admired your beautiful courtesy in not asking. We will bestow on you what you approve in the matter of the qiblah. O Muḥammad, all the servants in the world seek My approval, but I seek yours. Everyone searches for Me, but I call for you. Everyone wishes for My caresses, but it is you We caress: "your Lord will give so much to you that you will be pleased" (93:5). Now take the Kaʿbah as the qiblah of your soul and Me as the qiblah of your spirit. When those caresses came from the Presence of Unity, the tongue of his state said in its yearning that when the Beloved's heart reaches out once in love, the suffering of separation becomes easy to bear. Abū Bakr al-Shiblī said that the qiblahs are three: the qiblah of the common people, the qiblah of the elect, and the qiblah of the elect of the elect.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ longed for the qiblah of Ibrāhīm and kept looking to the sky without asking. Allah praised that longing, promised a qiblah he would love, and granted it in the same verse (2:142, 2:115, 2:143).
- Wherever Muslims are, they face the Kaʿbah, with exceptions for voluntary prayer on a journey, for battle, and for honest error.
- The People of the Book knew from their own Scripture that this change would come, and most denied it out of envy, though a few like ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām accepted it.
- Saying "the Sacred Mosque" and "its direction" rather than "the Kaʿbah" makes the obligation easy: distant worshippers face the general direction, an arc of about 48 degrees suffices, old mosques set the bearing for new ones, and instruments are not required.
- Inwardly, the Prophet's ﷺ courtesy in not asking won him more than asking could (3:136, 38:30, 93:5).

<<<VERSE 2:145>>>
Verse 145 closes the door on any hope that the People of the Book would come round over the qiblah. "Even if you were to bring every proof to the People of the Book, they would not accept your direction ˹of prayer˺, nor would you accept theirs; nor would any of them accept the direction ˹of prayer˺ of another. And if you were to follow their desires after ˹all˺ the knowledge that has come to you, then you would certainly be one of the wrongdoers". Continuing the subject of the divinely ordained orientation, the verse gives yet another instance of their malice.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Even if you were to bring every proof to the People of the Book, they would not accept your direction ˹of prayer˺"**. The Arabic carries the force of an oath: by Allah, even if you brought the Jews and Christians every sign and proof that the change of direction is true, every sign they asked of you, they would not face your qiblah or embrace your religion. Allah describes their disbelief, stubbornness and defiance of what they know to be true about His Messenger ﷺ. Were he to bring forward every proof of what he was sent with, they would never obey him or give up following their desires, as Allah says elsewhere: "Indeed, those against whom Allah’s decree ˹of torment˺ is justified will not believe—even if every sign were to come to them—until they see the painful punishment" (10:96-97). It is not that they demand solid and convincing arguments before accepting the new ruling. Sheer stubbornness keeps them from assenting, and no proof in the world will ever satisfy them. Those who give free rein to their desires never follow the path of reason. In their perversity they seek from denying Allah's signs the kind of satisfaction He wished them to draw from accepting them. Their refusal came from being used to behaving as they pleased, and from romantic notions about the special position of their own people, which they had made the bedrock of their life and creed.

**"nor would you accept theirs"**. You will not face their qiblah after Allah has turned you from it. This is a categorical negation both of the Prophet's ﷺ hope that they would become Muslims and of their hope that he would return to their direction. It shows the vigour with which the Messenger of Allah ﷺ carried out what Allah commanded. As firmly as the Jews cling to their opinions and desires, so firmly does the Prophet ﷺ cling to Allah's commands, obeying Him and following what pleases Him, and he would never adopt their desires under any circumstances. Praying towards Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem) had therefore never been because it was the Jews' qiblah, but because Allah had commanded it. The Prophet ﷺ never departed from the pattern of earlier prophets until he received revelation on the matter. True to that principle, he first made Jerusalem his qiblah, since the prophets from the time of Sulaymān (Solomon) had prayed towards it. Islam's coming was the signal that the Jews were being removed from their post as torch-bearers of the true faith, and the faith itself had to be set apart from Jewish tradition so that it could emerge in a new and unmistakably pure form. That is why he eagerly awaited the instruction to change, which came in the second year after the Hijrah. The phrase also declares that the House of Allah will remain the qiblah until the end of the world. The new qiblah was instituted by divine command and will never be abrogated, which refutes the scoffing allegation that Islamic rulings had no stability and that the Muslims might go back to Bayt al-Maqdis, as al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ observes.

**"nor would any of them accept the direction ˹of prayer˺ of another."**. The Jews and the Christians do not follow one another's qiblah either. The Jews took Bayt al-Maqdis, the Christians the east, and each rejects the other's choice. Each group calls the other disbelievers, and each follows its own desires in the matter of the qiblah as in other sacred laws and rulings. The two groups show the same malice towards each other that they show towards the Muslims. Since the Prophet ﷺ can accept neither orientation, there is no prospect of agreement between the People of the Book and the Muslims on this point.

**"And if you were to follow their desires after ˹all˺ the knowledge that has come to you, then you would certainly be one of the wrongdoers"**. If, after being warned, you prayed towards their qiblah, following the whims they call you to, after the revelation that has come to you and the knowledge that the Sacred Precinct is the qiblah of Ibrāhīm (Abraham), then, hypothetically, you would be among the evildoers who harm themselves. Allah warns those who defy the truth knowingly, since the case against a person who knows is stronger than against anyone else. Bayt al-Maqdis had indeed once been instituted by divine command, but that command is now abrogated. Whoever follows an abrogated ruling and ignores the new one that replaced it is disobeying Allah and acting on his own opinion and desire. For the Prophet ﷺ to follow the desires of the People of the Book is naturally impossible. But supposing, purely for argument's sake, that he did so after receiving a definite ruling by revelation, he would be counted among the unjust, those who disobey divine commands. Such a situation can never arise. As a prophet he is sinless and so cannot be among the unjust, and it follows that it is impossible for him to favour their desires and adopt their qiblah as his own. The warning is outwardly addressed to the Prophet ﷺ, whom Allah has protected from ever doing such a thing, but it is really meant for his Ummah, which is being made to realise the gravity of ignoring or disobeying the ruling that has finally fixed the House of Allah as the qiblah of the Muslims.

### A barrier between the two

In the devotional reading, devotion to the path of the Real was already the Prophet's ﷺ by the eternal decree, just as it was decreed that his enemies would fall by the wayside. Between the two sides stands a barrier, a barzakh, like the one between the two seas: "He merges the two bodies of ˹fresh and salt˺ water, yet between them is a barrier that they never cross" (55:19-20). They will not follow his qiblah even if he showed them evidence clearer than suns and moons, and he will not follow theirs whatever stratagems they bring. In that lies a decree from Allah from pre-eternity.

### In short

- No proof would ever bring the People of the Book to accept the new qiblah, for their refusal comes from obstinacy and desire, not lack of evidence (10:96-97).
- The Prophet ﷺ will never return to their direction. His earlier facing of Jerusalem was obedience to Allah, not deference to the Jews, and the Kaʿbah is now the qiblah until the end of time.
- Jews and Christians reject each other's qiblah too, so no agreement is possible.
- Following their desires after revealed knowledge would be wrongdoing. The Prophet ﷺ is protected from it, and the warning is really for his Ummah.
- Inwardly, an unbridgeable barrier was decreed between the two sides (55:19-20).
