<<<VERSE 3:59>>>
The verse states, “Indeed, the example of Jesus in the sight of Allah is like that of Adam” (3:59). Both were created by Allah’s command, but Adam had neither father nor mother. ʿĪsā (Jesus) had a mother and no father. His unusual birth is a sign of Allah’s power, not evidence that he is divine.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Indeed, the example of Jesus in the sight of Allah”** compares ʿĪsā’s creation with Adam’s. A delegation from Najrān, a Christian center in Yemen, argued that Jesus must be divine because he had no father. The answer is that an extraordinary birth does not make the person God. Adam’s creation was even more unusual.

**“is like that of Adam”** means both births occurred without a father. Adam had neither father nor mother, while ʿĪsā had Maryam (Mary) but no father. If the lack of a father made Jesus the son of God, the same claim would apply even more strongly to Adam. But Christians also accept that Adam is human, so the same reasoning cannot make Jesus divine.

Allah created Adam without a mother or father, created Ḥawwāʾ (Eve) from a man without a woman, and created ʿĪsā from a woman without a man. The rest of humanity is created through a man and a woman. These different forms demonstrate that Allah’s power is not limited to the usual means of birth.

**“He created him from dust”** refers to Adam’s origin. His body was formed from dust before Allah gave him life. An inward reading distinguishes the spirit, rūḥ, from the physical body and says that both Adam and ʿĪsā remained mortal beings despite their extraordinary creation. ʿĪsā was specially honored, but he was still a created servant.

**“then said to him, ‘Be!’ And he was”** describes the power of Allah’s command. He does not need parents, tools, or an existing cause in order to create. The command brings into being what He wills.

The same point is made in the account of Maryam’s pregnancy: ʿĪsā was made a sign for people (19:21). His birth displays Allah’s power, while his own speech as an infant identifies him as Allah’s servant. An extraordinary sign does not erase the difference between Creator and creation.

### The delegation’s question

One report places the dispute in a visit by Christians from Najrān during the tenth year after the Hijrah. Najrān was described as about 150 miles north of Ṣanʿāʾ, toward Yemen. Their delegation came to al-Madīnah to debate the status of ʿĪsā. They argued that his birth without a father made him divine. The Prophet ﷺ answered that he was Allah’s servant and messenger, and that he was the word Allah cast to Maryam.

A report attributed to Ishaq bin Yasar, also known as Ibn Ishaq, says that the delegation included sixty horsemen and fourteen chiefs. Three leaders were especially prominent: al-ʿĀqib, also called ʿAbd al-Masīḥ, who advised the group; As-Sayyid, also known as al-Ayham, their scholar and leader on journeys; and Abū Ḥārithah bin ʿAlqamah, their priest and religious leader. Shurahbil and other members of the party also took part in the discussion.

The account says that Abū Ḥārithah knew descriptions of Muhammad ﷺ in earlier scriptures. The Romans had honored him, built churches in his name, and provided him with money and servants because of his Christian commitment. His position made him reluctant to leave the religion and status he already held. The report therefore presents him as a learned man who recognized signs of the Prophet but did not follow through on that knowledge.

The Christians differed among themselves about Jesus. Some called him God, some the son of God, and some one of a trinity. The verse answers those claims by comparing his creation to Adam’s. The proof does not depend on denying the miracle. It accepts the miracle and explains it as an act of Allah.

### In short

- Adam was created without a father or mother, while ʿĪsā was created without a father.
- An unusual birth is a sign of Allah’s power, not evidence of divinity.
- The comparison answers the claim made by the delegation from Najrān.

<<<VERSE 3:60>>>
The verse declares, “This is the truth from your Lord” (3:60). The account of ʿĪsā’s creation is true, and the claims that he is divine or Allah’s son are false. The Prophet is told not to doubt the revelation he has received.

### Phrase by phrase

**“This is the truth”** refers to the account that ʿĪsā is Allah’s created servant and messenger, not Allah or His son. His birth without a father does not change his human nature. Adam’s creation makes the point clear: he had neither father nor mother and was still a human being.

**“from your Lord”** means the account comes from Allah. It is not an argument invented to answer the delegation of Najrān. Allah is the Creator, and He knows the truth about ʿĪsā, Maryam, and the events of the past.

**“so do not be one of those who doubt”** commands firmness in the truth. The Prophet is not uncertain, but the instruction confirms that he should stand by the revelation and not yield to repeated disputation. One explanation describes this as a call to remain firm in the account that Allah has revealed.

The dispute involved people who had knowledge of the extraordinary birth and miracles but interpreted them incorrectly. The delegation’s leaders included Abū Ḥārithah, al-ʿĀqib, and As-Sayyid. The truth is not decided by a person’s rank, learning, or religious office. Allah’s revelation is the measure by which the claims are judged.

The verse gives the answer before the challenge of mutual supplication in the next verse. The Prophet is to remain certain that Allah’s account is true, whether his opponents accept it or not.

### In short

- The revelation about ʿĪsā is the truth from Allah.
- Jesus’s extraordinary birth does not make him divine.
- The Prophet is told to remain firm and not doubt the revealed account.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:61>>>
The Prophet is instructed to challenge those who continue disputing: “Let us gather our children and your children, our women and your women, ourselves and yourselves” (3:61). Both sides are to assemble their families and sincerely ask Allah to curse the liars. This formal appeal is called mubāhalah.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Now, whoever disputes with you ˹O Prophet˺ concerning Jesus”** refers to the Christians of Najrān who continued to argue that ʿĪsā was divine after the truth about his creation had been explained. The Prophet ﷺ had answered their questions, but they did not accept the proof. The verse gives him a final challenge when ordinary debate has not settled the matter.

**“after full knowledge has come to you”** means after Allah has revealed the truth and the evidence has been made clear. The disagreement is no longer caused by a lack of information. The delegation has heard that Jesus is Allah’s servant and messenger, created by His command.

**“say, ‘Come!’”** invites the disputants to meet for mubāhalah. In its legal and religious use, mubāhalah is a mutual prayer asking Allah to place His curse on whichever side is lying. It is not a casual argument or a threat. It is a solemn appeal to Allah after the evidence has failed to convince the other side.

The invitation is made to the Christians and their families. The disputants themselves could pray without bringing their relatives, but the participation of children and women would make the appeal more serious. Their presence shows that each side is willing to risk the welfare of those closest to them if they are knowingly lying.

**“Let us gather our children and your children”** includes sons and grandsons. The word “children” is not restricted to sons from one’s own body. In common usage it can include grandchildren. The Prophet ﷺ brought his grandsons al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥusayn. ʿAlī was also included because he was the Prophet’s son-in-law and had grown up under his care, like a son.

**“our women and your women”** calls for the women of each side to join. The Prophet brought his daughter Fāṭimah. The delegation did not bring their families to complete the mutual appeal.

**“ourselves and yourselves”** means that the principal people on both sides also come forward. The Prophet came with ʿAlī, Fāṭimah, al-Ḥasan, and al-Ḥusayn. One explanation notes that this shows their closeness to him, but it does not make ʿAlī his biological son or prove that the phrase gives him succession. He was included as family through his upbringing and relationship, while the grandsons were included as children.

**“then let us sincerely invoke Allah’s curse upon the liars”** means that each side asks Allah to distance the false party from His mercy. The one who knowingly lies about ʿĪsā and Allah’s message will face the consequence. The challenge is meant to make the truth clear, not to create a spectacle.

### The delegation of Najrān

The delegation came to al-Madīnah in the tenth year of the Hijrah. It was said to have sixty horsemen and fourteen chiefs, with three leading figures: al-ʿĀqib, As-Sayyid, and Abū Ḥārithah bin ʿAlqamah. They arrived in robes with a caravan associated with Banū al-Ḥārith bin Kaʿb. The companions had not seen a delegation like them. They discussed ʿĪsā with the Prophet ﷺ and disagreed over whether he was God, God’s son, or one of a trinity.

When the Prophet called them to mubāhalah, they withdrew to consult al-ʿĀqib, whose advice they trusted. He told them that Muhammad was a prophet and that a people who challenged a prophet to mutual imprecation would be destroyed. He advised them to make a treaty rather than proceed. They returned and declined the challenge, asking instead for a trusted companion to settle their financial disputes. The Prophet sent Abū ʿUbaydah bin al-Jarrāḥ, whom he described as trustworthy. Al-Bukhārī records this report. Anas also reports the Prophet’s statement that every ummah has a trustee and that Abū ʿUbaydah was the trustee of this ummah.

A report from al-Ḥasan describes two monks who came from Najrān. They claimed to have submitted to Allah before the Prophet, but he pointed out that worshipping the cross, claiming Allah has a son, and consuming wine contradicted submission. When they asked about ʿĪsā, revelation came with the comparison to Adam and the invitation to mubāhalah. The report is transmitted with a ḥasan grade.

Other reports recount the leaders’ reluctance to take up the challenge. Al-Bukhārī records that al-ʿĀqib and As-Sayyid said that if Muhammad were truly a prophet and they invoked a curse against him, they and their children would not prosper. They chose a treaty and requested a trustworthy representative. Al-Bukhārī also records the account of Anas about Abū ʿUbaydah as trustee. A report from Ibn ʿAbbās says that if the delegation had proceeded with the mutual curse, they would have returned to find neither property nor family.

A separate hadith reports that Abū Jahl threatened to step on the Prophet’s neck while he prayed beside the Kaʿbah. The Prophet said that if Abū Jahl had attempted it, angels would have seized him openly. The same report says that those who demanded death would have seen their places in the Fire, and that those who took up mubāhalah would have returned without families or possessions. Aḥmad recorded it, and al-Bukhārī, At-Tirmidhī, and An-Nasāʾī also transmitted it. At-Tirmidhī graded it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ.

The delegation ultimately agreed to a treaty and to pay the jizyah rather than invoke Allah’s curse. Their decision did not change the truth of the Prophet’s message. It ended the dispute without the destruction they feared.

### What does the event show about family and leadership?

The Prophet’s willingness to bring his closest family shows the seriousness of the appeal. The word “sons” includes his grandsons, and ʿAlī’s presence reflects his family relationship and upbringing. It does not, by itself, establish the claim that he was the Prophet’s successor. The delegation, meanwhile, was led by religious figures such as Abū Ḥārithah and al-ʿĀqib, but their learning and rank did not remove their responsibility to accept the truth.

### In short

- Mubāhalah is a solemn mutual prayer asking Allah to curse the party that lies.
- The Prophet brought ʿAlī, Fāṭimah, al-Ḥasan, and al-Ḥusayn.
- The delegation declined the challenge and chose a treaty instead.

<<<VERSE 3:62>>>
The verse affirms, “Certainly, this is the true narrative” (3:62). The account of ʿĪsā is true, and Allah alone is worthy of worship. He is Almighty and All-Wise. The story rejects both denial of Jesus and the claims that he is divine or Allah’s son.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Certainly, this is the true narrative”** refers to what Allah has revealed about ʿĪsā and the delegation from Najrān. The truth is that Jesus is Allah’s servant and messenger, created by His command. He is not God, God’s son, or one part of a trinity. The revelation gives the correct account of his birth and status.

**“and there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Allah”** states the central principle of tawḥīd. Allah has no son or partner, and no created being deserves worship. The miracles of ʿĪsā do not change this. They are signs Allah gave to His messenger.

**“And indeed, Allah ˹alone˺ is the Almighty, All-Wise”** means that Allah has full power and acts with wisdom. He is able to create ʿĪsā without a father and to guide people through revelation. His wisdom is shown in giving proofs and allowing people to choose whether to accept them.

The leaders of Najrān knew the signs and descriptions of the coming Prophet in their scriptures, according to the report, but still disputed. When invited to mubāhalah, they chose a treaty and the payment of jizyah instead. Their decision did not make their claims true. The account reminds the ummah that religious disputes must be judged by revelation and that every messenger is a servant of Allah.

### In short

- The account of Jesus is true: he is Allah’s servant and messenger.
- Allah alone is worthy of worship and has no son or partner.
- Allah is Almighty and All-Wise, and His signs call people to tawḥīd.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 3:63>>>
The verse concludes, “If they turn away, then surely Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of the corruptors” (3:63). If the disputants reject the truth and refuse the appeal, Allah knows what they are doing and will judge them. Their decision does not obscure the truth from Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**“If they turn away”** means that the Christians of Najrān may refuse the invitation to accept the truth or to proceed with mubāhalah. They declined the mutual curse and chose to make a treaty. Their refusal did not change the message about ʿĪsā.

**“then surely Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge”** means that Allah knows their beliefs, intentions, and actions. They may conceal their reasons or present their position as sincere, but Allah knows whether they are seeking truth or defending a false claim.

**“of the corruptors”** describes those who turn away from the truth and spread falsehood. One explanation identifies the Christians who persist in their claims about Jesus as the people meant here. Another says Allah will either remove the corruptors or allow them to continue until their false claims become established, then seize them when they do not expect it. Their actions will not escape His knowledge or judgment.

The decision to pay the jizyah and abstain from mubāhalah ended the immediate dispute. It did not remove the responsibility to follow the truth. Allah knows the corrupting effects of false belief and will recompense those who persist in it.

### In short

- The delegation may turn away, but Allah knows their intentions and actions.
- Refusing the challenge does not make a false claim true.
- Allah will judge those who persist in corruption.
