The comparison that ends the argument is short: "Indeed, the example of Jesus in the sight of Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust, then said to him, “Be!” And he was". ʿĪsā was created without a father. Adam was created without father or mother. If fatherless creation made a son of God, Adam would have the stronger claim, and since that claim is obviously false for Adam, it is even more false for ʿĪsā.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, the example of Jesus in the sight of Allah is like that of Adam"**: regarding Allah's ability, the likeness of ʿĪsā is the likeness of Adam. His remarkable case in God's sight is as Adam's case. The comparison sets one remarkable thing beside another more remarkable, so that it convinces the disputer and settles in the mind more effectively.

**"He created him from dust"**: He created Adam, his form, of dust, without father or mother.

**"then said to him, “Be!” And he was"**: He said to Adam, "Be", a human being, and he was. Similarly He said to Jesus, "Be", without a father, and he was. Jesus became as Allah willed: "Become a man. And he became."

### The question that drew this answer

The delegation from Najran asked the Prophet ﷺ for proof from the Qur'an that Jesus was not the son of Allah. In one setting, they accused him directly: "Why do you insult Jesus?" He asked what he had said. They answered, "You say that he is a servant." He said, "Indeed, he is the servant and messenger of Allah, as well as His word which He cast into the Virgin Mary." They grew angry: "Have you ever seen a human being born without a father? If you are truthful, show us such a person." Then Allah revealed this verse.

In another report, two monks from Najran came and were invited to surrender to Allah. One claimed that surrender had preceded the Prophet. The answer named three preventions: worship of the cross, eating pork, and claiming Allah has a son. When they asked for Jesus's father, the Prophet ﷺ did not answer hastily. He waited for Allah, and revelation supplied this verse.

Adam supplies common ground for Christians too. Everyone believes he was the first man, brought directly into existence by God's command rather than born from a father and a mother. If that mode of arrival leaves Adam human and not Allah's son, fatherlessness cannot by itself make Jesus Allah's son.

### The delegation and its three claims about ʿĪsā

The reason for the surah's opening sequence is this Najran delegation. It came to Al-Madinah to argue about ʿĪsā, claiming he was divine and the son of Allah, and Allah sent down the beginning of this surah to refute them, as Muhammad bin Ishaq bin Yasar and other scholars stated.

Ibn Ishaq's famous Sīrah counts sixty horsemen, including fourteen chiefs who made decisions. Their leading three were Al-ʿAqib, also known as ʿAbdul-Masih, their leader and reference for advice and decision; As-Sayyid, also known as Al-Ayham, their scholar and leader in journeys and gatherings; and Abu Harithah bin ʿAlqamah, of Bakr bin Waʾil, their patriarch, priest and religious leader. The others were named Uways bin Al-Harith, Zayd, Qays, Yazid, Nabih, Khuwaylid, ʿAmr, Khalid, ʿAbdullah and Yuhannas. Abu Harithah was an Arab who, on embracing Christianity, was honoured by the Romans and their kings, who built churches for him or in his honour, supported him financially and gave him servants because of his firm faith. He knew the description of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ from earlier divine Books. His position and honour kept him Christian, all the same.

They entered the Prophet's mosque after ʿAsr prayer wearing robes and garments, accompanying a caravan of camels led by Banū al-Ḥārith bin Kaʿb. Companions who saw them said they had never seen a delegation like them. Abu Harithah and Al-ʿAqib spoke, as Christians of the king's community, but they disagreed about ʿĪsā among themselves. Some said, "He is Allah." Others said, "He is the son of Allah." Others said, "He is one of a trinity." Resurrection of the dead, healing of blindness, leprosy and illness, foretelling the future, and shaping birds and breathing life into them feed the first claim. Every miracle happened by Allah's leave so that ʿĪsā would be a sign for people.

The Qur'an refutes all three. As for ability, its pattern is now complete: Adam made without male or female, Eve from a male without a female, ʿĪsā from a mother without a father, and the rest of creation from male and female. In Surah Maryam the conclusion stands: "And We made him a sign for mankind" (19:21).

### Two creations, one mortality

Inwardly, the two are paired through the spirit, rūḥ. He chose to purify the spirit in them from transmigration, tanāsukh, passing through loins. Adam is singled out as the first of men, while Jesus is singled out for the distinction of the spirit breathed into him in the most exalted way. Yet both were weighty beings for whom being in time and being mortal were unavoidable: "He created him of dust then He said to him Be and he was" (3:59).

### In short

- Fatherless creation cannot make Jesus God's son, because Adam had neither father nor mother and nobody calls him God's son.
- The Najran delegation's own cases, miracles, fatherlessness and the plural "We", are answered by Allah's leave, by Adam, and by the one command "Be" (19:21).
- Both Adam and Jesus carry the mark of mortality, which separates them from God.

### What Mubāhalah is

Mubāhalah literally means mutual prayer. In the terminology of Islamic sharīʿah it is a particular form of resolving religious disputes. When argument from both sides fails to resolve a religious issue, the parties jointly pray for Allah to cast His curse on whichever party is false. A curse means moving someone far away from divine mercy. Moving far from mercy is being close to divine wrath, so its essence is: wrath be on the liar. The liar then faces the evil consequences, and the truth becomes evident even to the disbelievers.

Originally only the disputants are needed. They simply get together and pray. Assembling all the family members is not required, but it makes a Mubāhalah more effective, which is why the Prophet ﷺ was instructed to invite the families of both sides.

"Sons" carries its general sense and includes grandsons, not only sons of the loins, in common usage. So Ḥasan and Ḥusayn, the Prophet's grandsons, fall inside "our sons", and ʿAlī, his son-in-law, is included too. ʿAlī has another reason for inclusion: he grew up under the Prophet's patronage, treated like his own son. Some have claimed that because ʿAlī was not the Prophet's son he must belong instead to "ourselves", making him the Prophet's immediate successor. The answer given is that this argument fails once "sons" is read in its normal breadth.

### The day Najran declined

An ultimatum frames the meeting: accept Islam, pay jizya (the levy imposed on free non-Muslims living under Muslim rule), or prepare for war. The shorter delegation named Shurahbil, ʿAbdullah ibn Shurahbil and Jabbar ibn Fayd. Debate escalated around proving Jesus's godhood until the ordeal was announced. The Prophet ﷺ came with Fāṭimah, ʿAlī, Ḥasan and Ḥusayn. Shurahbil warned his companions that Mubāhalah against a prophet meant certain death and destruction and advised the Prophet's proposed truce. Agreement produced peace through an accepted levy.

Najran, a great Christian centre some 150 miles north of Sanaa toward Yemen, sent a deputation in the tenth Hijra year. Arguments were presented, including the contrast between God, who never dies, and Jesus, who would eventually meet death. They remained unmoved. The following day the Prophet emerged with grandsons Hasan and Husayn, daughter Fāṭimah and son-in-law ʿAlī. Consultation turned on the promised prophet among the Ishmaelites: if Muhammad was he, mutual imprecation would destroy the people and reach later generations. "By God," one scholar said, "I am seeing such faces as could move mountains with their prayers." Peace treaties and departure looked better.

The fuller account ends the same way, with a judge requested in place of the ordeal. Verses judged between the Prophet ﷺ and People of the Book and commanded the call if truth continued to be refused. Najran asked for time to decide and consulted Al-ʿAqib, who warned:

> "By Allah, O Christian fellows! You know that Muhammad is a Messenger and that he brought you the final word regarding your fellow ('Isa). You also know that no Prophet conducted Mubahalah with any people without destruction falling on them. Indeed, it will be the end of you if you do it. If you have decided to remain in your religion and your creed regarding your fellow ('Isa), then conduct a treaty with the man (Muhammad) and go back to your land."

They told Abu Al-Qasim that dual adherence should continue, each community remaining on its religion, and requested a trusted Companion to judge their monetary disputes, a role they found acceptable in him.

Al-Bukhari's report from Hudhayfah has Al-ʿAqib and As-Sayyid come seeking imprecation. One warned the other against proceeding: if Muhammad was truly a Prophet, invoking curses would ruin them and their offspring forever. They asked for "a trusted man, just a trusted man" to go with them. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ promised a truly trustworthy man, and every Companion hoped to be chosen. He called Abu ʿUbaydah bin Al-Jarrah to stand and said, "This is the trustee of this Ummah." Al-Bukhari also records Anas's version of the saying: "Every Ummah has a trustee, and the trustee of this Ummah is Abu 'Ubaydah bin Al-Jarrah." 

When Najran met the Prophet ﷺ already accompanied by al-Ḥasan, al-Ḥusayn, Fāṭima and ʿAlī, he instructed them, "When I supplicate you say 'Amen'." No imprecation followed; peace was exchanged for jizya, as Abū Nuʿaym reports. Ibn ʿAbbās adds the Prophet's warning that going through with it would mean returning home to neither possessions nor family. Another version has fire consuming those who set out with that intention.

### Why the curse was never spoken

Inward explanation gives the respite a purpose. Once truth imparted to the heart has been sincerely proclaimed, challenge should not be withheld. Force, qahr, and help, nuṣra, protect the challenger within nearness's wing. Had Najran pursued the cursing, fire would have rained down on the valley. Respite was granted because future believers would come from their progeny. The verse also evokes people no longer counted among the sincere, ṣiddīqūn: when the sincere lights appear, their remnants fade completely.

- Najran declined after its own leaders warned that no people survives a prophet's imprecation, paid jizya instead, and asked for a trustee-judge, receiving Abu ʿUbaydah bin Al-Jarrah.
- Abu Nuʿaym and Ibn ʿAbbās describe the road not taken: had they gone out, they would have found neither possessions nor family at home.
