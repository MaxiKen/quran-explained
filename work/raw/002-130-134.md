<<<VERSE 2:130>>>
After telling the story of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and the House, the surah draws the conclusion. "And who would reject the faith of Abraham except a fool! We certainly chose him in this life, and in the Hereafter he will surely be among the righteous". The earlier verses set out the basic principles of Ibrāhīm's religion, called people to it, and refuted the claim of the Jews and Christians to be its followers, showing that Islam alone is now faithful to the Abrahamic tradition and that this tradition has in essence been the religion of every prophet. Here Allah rejects the innovations of the disbelievers, who set up partners beside Him and so defied the religion of Ibrāhīm, the imām of the upright. Ibrāhīm worshipped Allah alone, with sincerity, and never called on anyone else. He did not commit shirk (associating partners with Allah) even for an instant. He disowned every deity worshipped instead of Allah and defied his whole people over it: "O my people! I totally reject whatever you associate ˹with Allah in worship˺. I have turned my face towards the One Who has originated the heavens and the earth—being upright—and I am not one of the polytheists" (6:78-79). He told his father and his people that he was free of whatever they worshipped, except the One who originated him and would guide him (43:26-27). When it became clear that his father was an enemy of Allah he broke with him, tender-hearted and forbearing though he was (9:114). And Allah describes him as a model of excellence, devoted and upright, not a polytheist, grateful for Allah's favours, chosen and guided to a straight path (16:120-122).

### Phrase by phrase

**"And who would reject the faith of Abraham"**. Who turns away from it, abandoning his path, his way and his method, and the practices he taught. The form of the question means "no one". The verse was revealed, according to Abū al-ʿĀliyah and Qatādah, about the Jews. They had invented a practice which Allah never sent down and which went against Ibrāhīm's religion, and the words "Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian" (3:67-68) bear this out. The message of Muḥammad ﷺ was exactly the message of Ibrāhīm, yet the People of the Book, who prided themselves on following Ibrāhīm, led the opposition to him. The reason is that the religion Ibrāhīm handed down to his offspring, and that the Prophet ﷺ taught, was Islam, total submission to Allah, while what the People of the Book practised as religion had nothing to do with submitting totally. Having lapsed into permissiveness and being unwilling to change, they had let their religion sink into a set of hollow rituals which they fondly believed would get them into Paradise. In the religion the Prophet ﷺ taught, salvation comes only through one's own actions. The People of the Book thought that belonging to a nation of saints and prophets would be enough to redeem them. True religion means accepting the guidance Allah reveals through His prophets. Theirs rested on a national legacy, a pile of traditions accumulated over generations.

**"except a fool!"**. Literally, one who makes a fool of himself. The Arabic can be rendered three ways: he is stupid in himself, he has besotted himself and allowed himself to become stupid, or he is ignorant of his own self. So he is the one who wrongs himself by turning from the truth to wickedness, the one who ignores that his soul was created for Allah and is bound to worship Him, or who treats his soul with frivolity and degrades it. He does not know his own worth and is content with humiliation. He has ruined himself and lost his mind. Only someone with no understanding, or who has wholly lost it, can turn from this religion, because it alone is the religion of human nature, and no one can deny it while his nature in the essential sense remains intact. Such a person defies the path of the man who was chosen in this life as a true imām from his youth until Allah made him His Khalīl (close friend), and who will be among the successful in the next life. Is there anything more insane than leaving this path for the path of misguidance? Is there a greater injustice? Luqmān told his son that associating partners with Allah "is truly the worst of all wrongs" (31:13).

**"We certainly chose him in this life"**. Allah elected him in this world for prophethood and for friendship, taking him as His Khalīl, or, as it is also explained, by giving him prophethood, Islam and goodly offspring. The superiority of his religion is shown by the fact that Allah honoured him in both worlds on account of this very religion, and his own spiritual station and glory arise from it. His greatness in this world is known to all: Namrūd (Nimrod) with all his might could not impress him, he gladly accepted being thrown into the fire rather than abandon the worship of Allah alone, and the Lord of the worlds made the fire a garden for him, so that believers and unbelievers alike came to recognise his uprightness and pure faith. The idolaters of Arabia were his descendants and, idol-worship notwithstanding, always held him in the highest esteem and even claimed to follow him. Remnants of his religion survived among them, distorted by their ignorance: the Hajj, the yearly sacrifice of animals, hospitality and the like. All of this shows the special grace that made the Friend of Allah an imām for people (2:124).

**"and in the Hereafter he will surely be among the righteous"**. With his forefathers, the messengers, in Paradise, among those of the highest stations, the righteous who fulfilled what Allah required of them and so reached the highest levels. Having spoken of his greatness in this world, the verse announces the exalted rank Allah has given him in the next.

### The Friend and the Beloved

In the devotional reading, one of these two verses praises the Bosom Friend, Ibrāhīm, and the other praises the Beloved, Muḥammad ﷺ. Both are cherished and worthy prophets adorned by the Lord's generosity, yet there is a difference between a friend and a beloved. The Friend is the desirer and the Beloved is the desired. The desirer is the one who wants, the desired is the one who is wanted. The desirer is a traveller, the desired is one snatched away. The desirer stands in the station of service as he travels, while the desired stands on the carpet of companionship, pulled along by the Real. Whoever travels by his own travelling finds that his road is not free of deception. That is why the Friend's road, for all the greatness of his state, was not free of it: when the star of deception rose on his path he said "This is my Lord" (6:76), and Lordship went on opening ambushes of deception by means of the moon and the sun, until protection took the reins of his friendship and pulled him out of the world of deception, and he said that he had turned his face to the Originator of the heavens and the earth, upright (6:79). Muṣṭafā ﷺ, by contrast, was in the pull of the Real. No ambush of deception could make his road steep. On that night, everything that had once not been and then came to be sought his help against deception and took refuge in the lights of his Sharīʿah. He was so confirmed by the Real's pull that he did not glance at any of it from the corner of his eye: "The ˹Prophet’s˺ sight never wandered, nor did it overreach" (53:17). The difference between the Friend and the Beloved is the difference between the traveller and the one snatched away. The Friend had the attribute of servants at the threshold of Lordship, standing on his own feet.

### In short

- Ibrāhīm never associated anything with Allah for a moment (6:78-79, 43:26-27, 9:114, 16:120-122), so turning from his religion, as the Jews did with their invented practices, is to make a fool of oneself and to commit the worst of wrongs (31:13).
- The "fool" is one who does not know what his soul was made for, or who degrades it, abandoning the religion of human nature itself.
- Allah chose Ibrāhīm in this world for prophethood and friendship, as Nimrod's fire and the Arabs' lasting reverence for him both show, and gave him the highest station among the righteous in the next.
- Inwardly, Ibrāhīm is the Friend who travels and must pass through deception, while Muḥammad ﷺ is the Beloved who is drawn, and whose sight never wandered.

<<<VERSE 2:131>>>
Verse 131 gives the foundation of Ibrāhīm's (Abraham's) religion in a single exchange. "When his Lord ordered him, “Submit ˹to My Will˺,” he responded, “I submit to the Lord of all worlds.”" Allah commanded Ibrāhīm to be sincere to Him, to obey Him and to surrender to Him, and Ibrāhīm complied perfectly. His Lord asked him to submit himself, and he agreed willingly and gladly to submit himself to the Lord of the worlds.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When his Lord ordered him, “Submit ˹to My Will˺,”"**. Obey Allah and devote your religion purely to Him. The word of command is aslim, from the same root as Islam. No single English word matches it exactly, for it means to obey, to submit oneself, to surrender one's will. Several settings are reported for the command. One says it came when Ibrāhīm came out of the pit of fire, in the sense: say specifically "there is no god but Allah". Another says that when he called his people to accept that Allah is One without partner, his Lord told him to make his religion and his works sincere to Allah. A third says the words came as he was thrown into the fire. Allah chose him because of the swiftness with which he surrendered, telling him to be faithful to Him in worship and to carry out humbly whatever He instructed.

**"he responded, “I submit to the Lord of all worlds.”"**. I submit to the Lord of all the worlds, and I declare that there is no god but Allah, who has no partner. Or: I comply, and make my religion and my works sincere to Allah, Lord of all the worlds, the One who created His servants, provides for them and takes care of their affairs. One would have expected him to answer "I submit myself to You". Instead he said, more fully, "to the Lord of all worlds". That form of reply expresses the respect and awe the occasion called for. It also contains the praise that the honour of being addressed by Allah demanded. And it acknowledges something else: in submitting to the Lord of the worlds he was doing no more than the basic duty a servant owes the Master of all, and he was doing it for his own good. The reply shows that the whole principle and essence of Ibrāhīm's religion is contained in one word, Islam, total obedience and willing self-surrender to Allah. He was made to pass through all his trials before reaching his exalted station precisely so that the world could see how perfectly he adhered to this principle. Islam, submission to Allah, is what the world was created for, and the end that every prophet and every divine Book was sent to serve.

### One religion of all the prophets

This verse teaches that the religion common to all the prophets, the point at which they all meet, is Islam. From Ādam (Adam) to the Last Prophet ﷺ every messenger of Allah called people to Islam alone and told his followers to keep to this Straight Path. The Qur'an is explicit: "Certainly, Allah’s only Way is Islam" (3:19), and "Whoever seeks a way other than Islam, it will never be accepted from them, and in the Hereafter they will be among the losers" (3:85). To put this in perspective: every religion that any prophet brought had divine sanction behind it, was instituted by Allah Himself and was, in its own time, accepted by Him. Each of them, whether one calls it Judaism, Christianity or anything else, must therefore in its essence have been Islam in the general sense of total submission to Allah.

The religion of Ibrāhīm has a distinguishing feature: he gave his religion the name Islam and his followers the name Muslims. In his prayer under verse 128 he asked that he and his son be kept submissive (muslimayn) and that his descendants be a submissive community (ummah muslimah), and in the next verse he will advise his descendants not to die without being sure that they are Muslims. After him this distinction, of being called specifically Muslims and the Islamic Ummah, passed by his own instruction to the community of the Prophet ﷺ, whom the Qur'an addresses as following "the way of your forefather Abraham. ˹It is Allah˺ Who named you ‘the ones who submit’ ˹in the˺ earlier ˹Scriptures˺ and in this ˹Quran˺" (22:78). When the Qur'an was revealed, the Jews, the Christians and even the idolaters of Arabia each claimed to follow the religion of Ibrāhīm. The Qur'an and its followers have made plain that, in this final phase of human history, only the religion of the Prophet ﷺ is the religion of Ibrāhīm, the religion of pure human nature (al-fiṭrah).

### What submission demands

Islam is the essence of every divine Book, every Sharīʿah and the teaching of every prophet: turning away from one's own desires in order to obey Allah's commands, and giving up private opinion in order to submit to His guidance. It is painful that so many Muslims today have forgotten this basic truth. They want to chase their own desires and call it Islam. The interpretation of the Qur'an and hadith that attracts them, or rather the misinterpretation, is whatever flatters those desires. In effect they are twisting the Sharīʿah to fit their fancies, and doing it with enough cleverness that the idols they actually serve come out dressed as religion. People like this are trying to outwit Allah Himself. He knows every particle of the universe and sees into the deepest corners of the heart. Before the Almighty nothing is of any use except complete surrender.

What Islam asks of a person is that he put every desire and inclination aside and, in everything he does, seek his Lord's pleasure. He can find that pleasure only when he knows his Lord's commands and carries them out exactly as prescribed. That is what ʿibādah, worship, really means. The perfection of this obedience, submission and love is the final stage of spiritual development, the Station (maqām) of ʿAbdiyyah, servanthood. At this station Ibrāhīm received from Allah the title Khalīlullāh, the Friend of Allah, and the Last of the Prophets ﷺ received the title ʿAbdunā, Our Servant. On the lower levels of the same station stand the abdāl, the aqṭāb and the awliyāʾ, the men of Allah, the saints of this Ummah, each in his degree. This is the essence of tawḥīd, the realisation of Allah's oneness, on reaching which all one's fears and hopes become bound to Allah and to no one else. Islam, then, means total obedience to Allah, and that obedience is possible only by following the Sunnah, the way of the Prophet ﷺ, as the Qur'an states in the plainest terms: "By your Lord, they will never be ˹true˺ believers until they accept you ˹O Prophet˺ as the judge in their disputes, and find no resistance within themselves against your decision and submit wholeheartedly" (4:65).

### The Friend's three tests

The devotional reading tells the story behind the verse. When the Bosom Friend set out on his journey, the command came from the Exalted Presence: Ibrāhīm, whoever wants Me must belong to Me completely. As long as a sliver of mortal desire and resistance of the soul is left in you, you will not move from the pain of striving to the ease of being drawn. The ransomed slave remains a slave so long as a single dirham is still owed. If you want Me, you must want what I desire and leave yourself aside entirely. The Friend answered: Lord, Ibrāhīm no longer has any self-governance or free choice. On the feet of poverty, in a state of brokenness, I have come to see what You command. I submit. I have overthrown myself, handed my affair to You and returned to You entirely. The command came: that is a very great claim, Ibrāhīm, and every claim must have a meaning and every right a reality. Now be on guard for the testing.

The tests came in three forms: through what was other than himself, by a part of himself, and by his whole self. The test by what was other than himself concerned his abundant possessions. They say he had seventy thousand sheep in seven thousand herds, and each herd had a dog with a gold collar round its neck. He was commanded to cut his heart loose from all of it and throw it away in Allah's path, and he did, keeping nothing. The traditions relate that when the call "And God took Abraham as a bosom friend" (4:125) went out in the world of the Dominion, the angels said: Lord, ever since that call our spirits have been spinning in a whirlpool and our gall-bladders melting at this special favour. How is the Friend worthy of such generosity? The call came: Jibrīl (Gabriel), spread your peacock feathers, go down from the Lote Tree's top to that mountain's summit and put him to the test. Jibrīl came down in the form of a human being, stood beside the mountain and cried out, "O Holy One!" The Friend fainted from the pleasure of hearing it. When he came round he said: servant of Allah, say that name again and this herd is yours. Jibrīl cried it out again, and the Friend rolled in the dust like a half-slaughtered bird, saying: say it again, and another herd is yours. Like the lover who said, you spoke of Him to me and increased my madness, so speak of Him more, he kept begging, and each time gave away a herd with its dog and its gold collar, until he had given everything and lost everything. Then the knots tightened, passion and destitution joined, and he said: servant of Allah, recite the name of the Friend once more, and my spirit is yours. Wealth and gold one gambles away for nothing, and when the matter reaches the spirit, gamble that away too. Jibrīl rejoiced and spread his feathers. He said: He was right to take you as His bosom friend. If anything falls short, it is our eyes. Your passion is perfect. When he showed himself, he said he had no use for the sheep, and the Friend replied that even so, chivalry does not allow taking back a gift. So they agreed to scatter them in the deserts and wildernesses to graze as they pleased, for the benefit of the world's people until the Resurrection. The mountain sheep now scattered across the world all come from that lineage, and whoever hunts and eats one until the Resurrection is a guest of the Friend, eating at the table of that majestic angel's beautiful deed. The test by a part of himself was that he was shown in a dream the sacrifice of his child and instructed to carry it out, a story told in its own place. The test by his whole self was that the rebellious Nimrod was persuaded to light a fire and build a mangonel to hurl him into it, and the divine address came to the fire: "O fire! Be cool and safe for Abraham!" (21:69).

### In short

- Allah commanded Ibrāhīm to submit and he answered at once, "I submit to the Lord of all worlds", a reply full of awe, praise and the recognition that submission is a servant's duty and his own good.
- Islam, total surrender to Allah, is the religion of every prophet (3:19, 3:85). Ibrāhīm named it Islam and its people Muslims, and that name passed to the Ummah of Muḥammad ﷺ (22:78).
- Submission means putting aside one's desires to obey Allah's commands exactly as the Sunnah shows (4:65), the station of servanthood at which Ibrāhīm became the Friend and Muḥammad ﷺ "Our Servant".
- Inwardly, the Friend was tested through his wealth, his son and his own life, and gave each away for the sake of the Friend's name (4:125, 21:69).

<<<VERSE 2:132>>>
Verse 132 shows what Ibrāhīm (Abraham) cared most to leave behind him. "This was the advice of Abraham—as well as Jacob—to his children, ˹saying˺, “Indeed, Allah has chosen for you this faith; so do not die except in ˹a state of full˺ submission.”" These verses show how anxious the prophets were to give their own descendants religious and spiritual guidance.

### Phrase by phrase

**"This was the advice of Abraham—as well as Jacob—to his children"**. The verb is read waṣṣā and also awṣā, both meaning to enjoin. Ibrāhīm commanded his offspring to follow this religion, Islam, for the sake of Allah. Yaʿqūb (Jacob) enjoined the same creed on his sons. "This" may also refer to the words of the previous verse, "I submit to the Lord of all worlds". These prophets loved those words so much that they kept them until the moment of death. They charged their children to hold to them afterwards, as Allah says elsewhere of Ibrāhīm, "And he left this enduring declaration among his descendants, so they may ˹always˺ turn back ˹to Allah˺" (43:28). One gloss says that what he enjoined, when he was dying, was that there is no god but Allah. Yaʿqūb, the son of Isḥāq (Isaac), may well have been among the children present when Ibrāhīm gave this counsel. It appears, and Allah knows best, that Isḥāq was granted Yaʿqūb while Ibrāhīm and Sārah were still alive. The good news given to her includes them both: "We gave her good news of ˹the birth of˺ Isaac, and, after him, Jacob" (11:71). If Yaʿqūb had not been alive then, there would be no point in naming him specifically among Isḥāq's children. In Sūrah al-ʿAnkabūt Allah says that He blessed Ibrāhīm with Isḥāq and Yaʿqūb (29:27), and again, "We blessed him with Isaac ˹as a son˺ and Jacob ˹as a grandson˺, as an additional favour" (21:72), which indicates that this happened in Ibrāhīm's lifetime. Yaʿqūb also built Bayt al-Maqdis, as the earlier books testify. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record that Abū Dharr asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ which mosque was built first. He said al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, the Kaʿbah. Abū Dharr asked which came next, and he said Bayt al-Maqdis. Asked how many years lay between them, he said forty. The counsel Yaʿqūb gave his own children, related in the next verse, confirms that he was among those who received this advice.

**"“Indeed, Allah has chosen for you this faith"**. The religion of Islam, the true religion of submission to Allah. These words were good news. They strengthened the sons' desire to meet the demands of submission. Once they knew that Allah had chosen them for it, they were certain He would help them and make it easy to give submission its due. Though the laws of these prophets differed in details, they were of one origin, and the spring of tawḥīd (Allah's oneness) is not divided in two.

**"so do not die except in ˹a state of full˺ submission.”"**. Hold fast to Islam until you die as Muslims, sincere to Allah in your worship and in your declaration of His oneness, surrendering inside and out. The command forbids them ever to abandon submission. It tells them to cling to it until death overtakes them. Ibrāhīm advised his sons also to say, "I have surrendered to the Lord of the worlds", and Yaʿqūb told his sons the same. In effect he made his descendants promise that before they died they would make sure they had been Muslims. That means following the teaching of Islam steadfastly all through life, so that by Allah's grace one remains a Muslim to the last breath.

### Dying on the path one lived on

The instruction means: do righteous deeds while you live and stay on this path, so that Allah will grant you the favour of dying on it. As a rule a person dies on the path he lived on, and is raised according to what he died on. A number of hadiths say the same: one dies in the state one kept up in life, and rises from the grave on the Day of Resurrection in that state. This is Allah's usual way with people. When His servant resolves to do good and strives as best he can, Allah, the Most Generous, helps him and makes it easy for him to stay on the righteous path.

This does not contradict the authentic hadith about the last stretch of a life. A man may do the works of the people of Paradise until only an outstretched arm's length or a cubit separates him from it. Then the Book, his destiny, overtakes him, and he does the works of the people of the Fire and enters it. Another may do the works of the people of the Fire until only an arm's length or a cubit separates him from it. Then the Book overtakes him, and he does the works of the people of Paradise and enters it. Some versions of this hadith add the proviso "as it appeared to people". The first man seemed to onlookers to be doing good while in reality he was doing the opposite. The second had been doing from the start what makes a person worthy of Paradise, although people took him for a sinner. Allah says: "As for the one who is charitable, mindful ˹of Allah˺" and believes in the best reward, "We will facilitate for them the Way of Ease", while for the one who is stingy, thinks himself self-sufficient and denies the best reward, "We will facilitate for them the path of hardship" (92:5-10). The person who has been steadfast in good deeds should therefore trust the divine promise. He should rely on Allah's usual way with His creatures, and hope that by His grace he will leave this world in that blessed state.

### In short

- Ibrāhīm, and after him Yaʿqūb, made Islam the one legacy they pressed on their children, enjoining the declaration of Allah's oneness to the end of their lives (43:28).
- Yaʿqūb was probably present at Ibrāhīm's counsel, since Sārah's good news named him (11:71, 29:27, 21:72), and he later built Bayt al-Maqdis forty years after the Kaʿbah.
- "Do not die except as Muslims" means living on the path so as to die on it. One dies as one lived, and the hadith about a last-minute reversal speaks of how things appeared to people (92:5-10).

<<<VERSE 2:133>>>
Verse 133 lets Yaʿqūb (Jacob) and his sons speak for themselves. "Or did you witness when death came to Jacob? He asked his children, “Who will you worship after my passing?” They replied, “We will ˹continue to˺ worship your God, the God of your forefathers—Abraham, Ishmael, and Isaac—the One God. And to Him we ˹all˺ submit.”" The verse was revealed about the Jews, when they said to the Prophet ﷺ: do you not know that when Yaʿqūb was about to die he charged his sons to follow Judaism? Allah thus settles the dispute among the Jews about what religion Yaʿqūb left to his children, and the verse carries His criticism both of the Arab idolaters descended from Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) and of the disbelievers among the Children of Israel, the descendants of Yaʿqūb son of Isḥāq (Isaac) son of Ibrāhīm (Abraham).

### Phrase by phrase

**"Or did you witness when death came to Jacob?"**. Were you present, assembly of Jews, to hear what he enjoined on his children, whether Judaism or Islam? The opening "or" works like a question that denies: you were not there at his death, so how can you ascribe to him what does not befit him? When death approached, Yaʿqūb called his sons together and charged them to worship Allah alone.

**"He asked his children, “Who will you worship after my passing?”"**. What will you worship after I die? The question is the test of what he had taught them.

**"They replied, “We will ˹continue to˺ worship your God, the God of your forefathers—Abraham, Ishmael, and Isaac"**. Ismāʿīl was Yaʿqūb's uncle, not his father, so calling him a father is a figure of speech. The father figures are mentioned together, and the standing of an uncle is like that of a father. An-Naḥḥās said that the Arabs call the uncle a father, as al-Qurṭubī records. This phrase shows that the word "father" includes the grandfather, and Ibn ʿAbbās deduced from it that in inheritance the grandfather is treated like the father. The verse serves as evidence that the grandfather is called a father, and that when his son dies he inherits in place of the brothers. This was the view of Abū Bakr, as al-Bukhārī relates on the authority of Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn az-Zubayr, adding that there was no opposing opinion. ʿĀʾishah the Mother of the Believers, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Ṭāwūs and ʿAṭāʾ held the same. Mālik, ash-Shāfiʿī and Aḥmad held that grandfather and brothers divide the inheritance between them, which is also reported from ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, ʿAlī, Ibn Masʿūd, Zayd ibn Thābit and a number of scholars among the Salaf (the early generations) and after them. In the devotional reading, the sons did not say "our God" out of respect for Yaʿqūb's special status, accepting his superiority and seeing themselves as attached to his station, and by adding "to Him we submit" they declared themselves followers of Ibrāhīm, Ismāʿīl and Isḥāq.

**"the One God"**. "One God" stands in place of "your God". We single Him out in divinity and associate nothing and no one with Him.

**"And to Him we ˹all˺ submit.”"**. In obedience and submission, bound to Him alone, declaring that there is only one true God. Allah says likewise that all in the heavens and the earth submit to Him, willingly or unwillingly (3:83). Islam is the religion of all the prophets, even though their laws differed: "We never sent a messenger before you ˹O Prophet˺ without revealing to him: “There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Me, so worship Me ˹alone˺.”" (21:25). Many verses and hadiths say the same, among them the Prophet's ﷺ words that the prophets are brothers from different mothers, with one religion. All of them followed one way in declaring Allah's oneness and in submission, inheriting it one from another, and they were the people of the house of intimacy, whom Allah made deserving of nearness and purified in truth.

### Why the prophets took such care over their children

Whether one calls it the religion of Ibrāhīm or Islam, it is meant for the whole world. Why then does the passage single out the descendants of Ibrāhīm and Yaʿqūb, and why were these two prophets so particular in counselling their sons? Because love for one's children and concern for their welfare is in no way incompatible with prophethood, or even with being the Friend of Allah. Ibrāhīm, who on one occasion was not only willing but glad to sacrifice his son at Allah's command, on another occasion prayed for his descendants' good in both worlds, and as he left this world wanted to give them what was in his eyes the greatest blessing of all, Islam. Prophets love their children too. The difference is that ordinary people think only the good things of this world worth having and try to leave their children as much of them as they can, while for the prophets and their true followers nothing counts except īmān (faith) and good deeds, in a word Islam, and it is this eternal wealth they strive to pass on whole to their descendants.

This gives parents a specific guidance. As keen as they are to secure their children's worldly comfort and happiness, they should give equal if not greater attention to disciplining their outward and inward conduct according to the Sharīʿah. Is it reasonable to strain every nerve to shield one's children from the heat of the sun and leave them exposed to the fire of Hell? The prophets' example also teaches that it is the parents' duty and the children's right that one should care first for the spiritual health of one's own children and only then worry about others. Three considerations support this. First, because of their special relationship, children are likely to accept counsel more readily and completely than others, and may later be a great help in one's efforts for Islam. Second, the easiest and most effective way to bring the truth to a whole people is for the head of each family to take on the teaching and training of its members. In current terms this localised, decentralised method spreads the responsibility over many individuals, and teaching the families one by one ends up teaching the whole society. The Qur'an lays down the principle: "O believers! Protect yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is people and stones" (66:6). The Prophet ﷺ himself, Allah's Messenger to all humanity, whose guidance holds until the last day, was commanded to carry the truth first to his own family: "And warn ˹all, starting with˺ your closest relatives" (26:214), and "Bid your people to pray, and be diligent in ˹observing˺ it" (20:132), and he always did so. Third, if a man's close relatives and household do not back what he teaches, or do not seem to act on it, his teaching does not succeed so well with others. When the Prophet ﷺ began his mission, his hearers usually answered that he ought first to convince the Quraysh, his own clan, and only then turn to others. When his own clan had accepted Islam, a process completed at the conquest of Makkah, the world saw, as the Qur'an reports, "the people embracing Allah’s Way in crowds" (110:2).

The main cause of the ignorance of Islam and indifference to it so widespread among Muslims today is that even parents who are themselves good Muslims leave their children to themselves and let the social environment shape them as it will. Their one worry is to see the children prosper in this world, and they never think of what will become of them in the next. May Allah in His mercy grant everyone concern for the other world, and help in making a real effort to acquire the only wealth that lasts: faith and rectitude.

### In short

- The Jews claimed Yaʿqūb died commending Judaism. The verse answers that they were not there, and records his sons' pledge to worship the One God of Ibrāhīm, Ismāʿīl and Isḥāq in submission.
- "Father" here covers an uncle and a grandfather, from which the jurists drew the rule on a grandfather's inheritance, with Abū Bakr, ʿĀʾishah and others giving him the father's place and Mālik, ash-Shāfiʿī and Aḥmad dividing with the brothers.
- All the prophets shared one religion of submission (3:83, 21:25), brothers of one faith from different mothers.
- The prophets' care for their children teaches parents to put their children's faith before their comfort, to begin teaching at home (66:6, 26:214, 20:132), and shows why the Prophet's ﷺ success with the Quraysh preceded the crowds of 110:2.

<<<VERSE 2:134>>>
Verse 134 closes the account of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and his family with a principle that cuts the ground from under inherited pride. "That was a community that had already gone before. For them is what they earned and for you is what you have earned. And you will not be accountable for what they have done". No one's link to prophets or righteous ancestors will help him unless he does good deeds of his own that bring him real religious benefit.

### Phrase by phrase

**"That was a community that had already gone before"**. "That" refers to Ibrāhīm, Yaʿqūb (Jacob) and his sons, and is feminine in the Arabic to agree with "community". They were a group of people who lived before your time and have passed away.

**"For them is what they earned and for you is what you have earned"**. They have the reward of what they did, good or evil, and you, the Jews who are being addressed, have what you have earned. This begins a new sentence in the Arabic. They have their deeds and you have yours.

**"And you will not be accountable for what they have done"**. On the Day of Judgement you will not be asked about what they did or said, just as they will not be asked about what you do, the second statement confirming the first. No one is held to account for another's disobedience, and everyone is rewarded for his own actions. One should not be distracted from attending to one's own conduct by looking at what those before did. Nothing will benefit a person except his own good actions. As a hadith puts it, whoever is held back by his deeds will not be moved forward by his lineage.

### What the principle rules out

The good deeds of one's forefathers will not suffice if one has not done good oneself, and in the same way no one suffers for his forefathers' misdeeds if his own account is clean. It follows that the children of mushrikīn (idolaters) and kāfirīn (unbelievers) who die before coming of age will not be punished in the next world for their parents' unbelief. The verse also refutes the Jews' claim that whatever they did they would enter Paradise on the strength of their forefathers' good deeds. Attaching oneself to saints and prophets, living or dead, lulls a person into a false sense of security. He imagines that as he was attached to them in this life he will stay attached in the next, and that their surplus of good deeds will cover his shortfall. The People of the Book were so immersed in this wishful thinking that they invented a dogma of ancestral salvation and pinned all their hopes on the holiness of their elders. To think that one person will be given the reward of another's actions is sheer self-deception. Everyone reaps the reward and bears the punishment of his own deeds, and no one shares in what is awarded to another for what he did on earth.

Let this be a warning to Muslims who, being descendants of the Prophet ﷺ or of a saint, delude themselves that their sins will go unpunished because of that privileged position. The Qur'an is explicit and insistent: "No one will reap except what they sow. No soul burdened with sin will bear the burden of another" (6:164). Addressing his own clan, the Prophet ﷺ said: beware, Banū Hāshim, lest on the Day of Judgement others come bringing their good deeds while you, having neglected good deeds, bring only your trust in being my relatives, for on that day I will not be able to save you from Allah's wrath. Another hadith says that a man whose deeds pull him back will not be pushed forward by his ancestry.

Whoever submits to Allah finds that he must face all kinds of difficulties in this life, and his worldly hopes will not necessarily be fulfilled. Yet one who is firm in faith is not put off by adversity. He perseveres and makes sure he leaves this world a believer, so that he can look forward to Allah's blessings in the next. In the devotional reading, the Real assigned each community its place and singled out for each its status by His decree. No news remains of their outward forms, and no trace of what distinguished one group from another. Each one is a king in his own region, and each has his own celestial sphere turning in felicity.

### In short

- Ibrāhīm, Yaʿqūb and his sons have passed on with their own deeds, and the Jews, like everyone else, will answer only for theirs (6:164).
- Descent from prophets or saints saves no one, as the Prophet ﷺ warned his own Banū Hāshim, and deeds that hold a person back cannot be offset by lineage.
- The same justice means that children of unbelievers who die young are not punished for their parents' unbelief.
