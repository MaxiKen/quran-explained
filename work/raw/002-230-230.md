<<<VERSE 2:230>>>
The third divorce has a consequence that the first two do not: "So if a husband divorces his wife ˹three times˺, then it is not lawful for him to remarry her". His right of return has ended. A later reunion is possible only after a genuine, consummated marriage to another husband has independently ended and the required waiting period has passed. The verse does not prescribe a temporary marriage arranged to manufacture that permission. It calls the couple back to Allah's limits, not to a device for avoiding them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So if a husband divorces his wife ˹three times˺,"** This concerns the third divorce after the two revocable occasions described in (2:229). The conditional wording is significant. The passage does not simply instruct a husband to complete three pronouncements. It explains what happens if he goes that far, after opportunities to stop and repair the marriage.

**"then it is not lawful for him to remarry her until after she has married another man and then is divorced."** The later marriage must be valid and real, with marital intercourse. A contract without consummation does not fulfil the condition. Nor does intercourse outside a valid marriage. If the second husband later divorces her, the possibility of a new marriage to the first husband can arise. His death is also included in the legal explanation.

**"Then it is permissible for them to reunite, as long as they feel they are able to maintain the limits of Allah."** The woman and her first husband may marry again after the relevant 'iddah, the waiting period. This requires a new nikah, marriage contract, and mahr, marriage payment. They should expect to fulfil their mutual duties, not repeat the same deliberate harm. Mujahid explains the condition as confidence that the purpose of the renewed marriage is honourable.

**"These are the limits set by Allah, which He makes clear for people of knowledge"** These are commands and legal boundaries. Knowledge means recognising them as Allah's rules and reflecting on what they require. Understanding is meant to produce obedience, not merely skill in finding a route around a restriction.

### Why should a husband stop before the third divorce?

A first divorce can already bring a marriage to an end if the waiting period finishes without revocation. A second or third pronouncement is not needed to make that separation complete. The three stages are limits on the power to return, not a programme that must be followed to the last stage.

The best method described here is al-talaq al-ahsan, the most favourable form of divorce: one pronouncement during a tuhr, an interval of purity in which intercourse has not occurred, followed by allowing the waiting period to run. Ibrahim al-Nakha'i reports, in an account collected by Ibn Abi Shaybah, that the Companions favoured giving one divorce and then doing nothing further until the three menstrual periods had passed.

This preserves the greatest room for reconciliation. Before the waiting period ends, revocation restores the existing marriage without a fresh contract. After it ends, the marriage has ended, but the former spouses can still marry again by mutual agreement. A third divorce removes that straightforward possibility.

The word translated as twice in (2:229) also indicates separate occasions. The discussion understands the first two pronouncements as spaced across separate intervals of purity rather than delivered in one utterance. It connects this point with Ruh al-Ma'ani. The husband must not mistake permission to reach a limit for a recommendation to use every stage available.

### Did scholars regard a third pronouncement as permitted?

The discussion gives different legal classifications while retaining agreement that the third pronouncement is undesirable. Malik and several other jurists describe it as talaq al-bid'ah, an innovated form of divorce lacking the approved procedure. Others allow three pronouncements when separated across three intervals of purity and call that talaq al-sunnah, divorce within the recognised procedure.

That second label does not mean three pronouncements are desirable or that the Prophet's ﷺ way calls for them. It distinguishes the procedure from the innovated form. The preferred course remains stopping with one pronouncement where separation cannot be avoided.

Once a second pronouncement has occurred, another link in the husband's options has been lost. Return during the waiting period remains possible, as does a fresh marriage after it. But the couple now stand at the boundary where one more divorce removes both those uncomplicated choices.

The word tasrih means freeing, untying or releasing. It helps explain why allowing the waiting period to finish is enough to release a wife. A further pronouncement is not needed merely to achieve that release. Abu Dawud records from Abu Razin al-Asadi that a man asked why the verse said divorce was twice without explicitly mentioning the third. The Prophet ﷺ pointed to release in fairness. The explanation given here is that this release accomplishes the complete separation otherwise produced by a third pronouncement.

Both retaining and releasing carry conditions of good conduct. Revocation is for restoring peaceful married life, not harassing the woman. Release should likewise avoid anger and quarrelling and include kindness, such as a parting gift of clothing. The discussion connects this with the instruction in (2:236) to provide according to one's means, rich or poor.

### Does an unlawful triple pronouncement still take effect?

The extended legal argument here says that it does. It distinguishes whether an act is wrong from whether it has consequences. An unlawful killing still causes death. Stolen goods still leave the owner's possession. In the same way, disapproval of simultaneous divorce does not itself prove that the pronouncements have no effect.

An-Nasa'i records from Mahmud ibn Labid that the Prophet ﷺ became angry on hearing of a man who pronounced three divorces together. He objected to Allah's Book being made a plaything while he was still present. Someone asked whether the offender should be killed. The account gives that question as part of the scene, not as a command to do it. The point already discussed under the previous verse is the condemnation of hurried triple divorce.

Several assessments of its transmission are also supplied. Ibn al-Qaiyyim, in Zad al-Ma'ad, regards the chain as meeting Muslim's conditions. Al-Mardini calls it authentic in al-Jauhar al-Naqi. Ibn Hajar describes its transmitters as reliable. Another assessment calls the chain excellent. The legal discussion argues that the report does not say the Prophet ﷺ reduced the man's three pronouncements to one revocable divorce.

Abu Bakr ibn al-Arabi goes further, relating that the pronouncements were enforced, as in the case of 'Uwaymir al-'Ajlani. The argument also refers to Umdah al-Athath by Abu al-Zahid Muhammad Sarfaraz as a collection of discussions on the issue. These references identify the legal argument being made. They do not turn disapproval of the act into a recommendation to perform it.

### What evidence is given for counting all three?

A report from 'A'ishah in Al-Bukhari describes a woman divorced three times who married another man and was then divorced without consummation. The Prophet ﷺ did not permit her return to the first husband until intercourse had occurred in the second marriage. The argument understands the three pronouncements as simultaneous. It names Fath al-Bari, Umdah al-Qari and al-Qastalani's discussion in support of that reading. On this understanding, the reply treated the three as effective rather than as one revocable divorce.

A further case concerns 'Uwaymir al-'Ajlani after li'an, the solemn exchange of oaths concerning an allegation of adultery. When the exchange had ended, he said that retaining his wife would make him a liar. He pronounced three divorces before the Prophet ﷺ had given his decision. Al-Bukhari and Muslim record the incident.

Abu Dharr also relates the incident on the authority of Sahl ibn Sa'd. In the wording recorded by Abu Dawud, the Prophet ﷺ enforced the separation, and the practice became established that the two would not be reunited after such a proceeding. Sahl states that he was present. The legal argument uses this as evidence for the effect of simultaneous pronouncements.

These are the reasons presented for the majority ruling. They must be read alongside the disagreement given later, rather than used to conceal that a minority took a different view.

### What did 'Umar do, and why does it raise a question?

Muslim records from Ibn 'Abbas that three divorces were treated as one during the Prophet's ﷺ time, Abu Bakr's caliphate and the first two years of 'Umar's caliphate. 'Umar then observed that people had become hasty in a matter in which they had been given room to wait. He decided to enforce the pronouncements upon them.

The discussion says that this action was taken publicly after consultation with knowledgeable Companions. No objection is reported there. Al-Tahawi, in Sharh Ma'ani al-Athar, emphasises that Companions who knew the earlier practice were present without rejecting the decision.

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr al-Maliki reports ijma', consensus, for the enforcement of simultaneous triple divorce, as cited in al-Zurgani's explanation of al-Mu'atta. The majority named here includes al-Shafi'i, Malik, Abu Hanifah and Ahmad, together with many earlier and later jurists. Taw'us and some followers of the Zahiri approach held that only one divorce takes effect. Thus the discussion both reports a claim of consensus and records a contrary minority view. Those two features must both remain visible.

Two questions follow. If earlier reports show three pronouncements being enforced, what does the report of counting three as one mean? And if the Prophet ﷺ had counted them as one, how could 'Umar alter that rule with the agreement of the Companions?

### How is the apparent conflict explained?

The answer preferred in this discussion is given from Nawawi's explanation of Muslim. It concerns ambiguous repetition rather than an explicit statement of the number three. Someone might say the sentence of divorce three times. That could mean three separate divorces, or it could mean repeating one declaration for emphasis. The speaker's intention makes a difference where the words genuinely admit both meanings.

The account says that truthfulness was widespread in the earliest period. A speaker who swore that he had intended only emphasis could be believed, and the pronouncement treated as one. This is distinguished from clearly saying three or admitting that three divorces were intended. Under the explanation, such explicit statements were treated as three even in the Prophet's ﷺ time.

The case of Rukana illustrates the distinction. He used the word albattah, associated in ordinary speech with final divorce but not necessarily explicit about the number. He said that he had meant one divorce, not three. The Prophet ﷺ asked him to swear to that intention and then treated it as one.

At-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, Abu Dawud and al-Darimi transmit the report in differing wordings and chains. Some forms say Rukana gave three divorces. Abu Dawud prefers the account in which he used albattah, explaining that a narrator interpreted its customary meaning as three. The need to ask about intention is itself part of the argument: an entirely explicit statement of three would not have left the same ambiguity.

According to the reconciliation offered here, this acceptance of a sworn claim continued under Abu Bakr, also called Siddiq al-Akbar, and early under 'Umar al-Faruq. Later, claims that repetition had meant only emphasis became frequent, while the standard of honesty declined. 'Umar feared that people would repeatedly utter divorce and then use an unverifiable claim about intention to reclaim their wives.

His order is therefore explained as closing the abuse of a concession in public adjudication. Repeating the words three times would be enforced as three, and the claim of intended emphasis would no longer be accepted in court. On this reading, he did not reverse the Prophet's ﷺ rule about an explicitly intended triple divorce. He changed the treatment of claims about ambiguous wording in a changed setting. The Companions approved because they understood the danger of allowing dishonesty to exploit the earlier trust.

This is the argument's preferred resolution, not a reason to erase the minority opinion already stated. It addresses both questions by distinguishing explicit triple divorce from repetition capable of meaning emphasis, and by distinguishing a substantive rule from acceptance of evidence about intention.

### Why must the later marriage be consummated?

The condition is not satisfied by sexual contact with someone who is not a lawful husband. The discussion explicitly includes the historical case of an enslaved woman: intercourse with her owner does not count as marrying another husband for this purpose. A valid marriage is required, and a valid marriage without intercourse is still insufficient for return to the first husband.

Muslim records from 'A'ishah a question about a woman whose second husband divorced her before intercourse. The Prophet ﷺ did not permit her return to the first husband. Al-Bukhari also records the report. The expression 'usaylah, literally an image of sweetness, is explained as sexual intercourse. Ahmad and An-Nasa'i record 'A'ishah reporting that explanation directly.

Another report gives the question in a vivid household setting. Rifa'ah Al-Qurazi's former wife came to the Prophet ﷺ while 'A'ishah and Abu Bakr were present. After her irrevocable divorce she had married 'Abdur-Rahman bin Az-Zubayr, but she complained that intercourse had not occurred. She illustrated her complaint with a small thread from her clothing. Khalid bin Sa'id bin Al-'As, waiting at the door, objected to her frank speech and asked Abu Bakr why he did not stop it. The Prophet ﷺ smiled rather than silencing the practical question.

He asked whether she wanted to return to Rifa'ah and explained that she could not until marital intercourse had occurred in the later marriage. Ahmad records the account. Al-Bukhari, Muslim and An-Nasa'i also transmit it. Muslim specifies that Rifa'ah's divorce was the third and final one. A further explanation connects the verse with 'Abd al-Rahman Ibn al-Zubayr. The identifying names are kept as given in their respective reports.

### Why is an arranged tahlil condemned?

Tahlil, also called halalah here, means arranging a later marriage merely to make the woman eligible for her former husband. The required later marriage is meant to be a real marriage, founded on desire for the woman and the intention of continuing married life. It is not a temporary instrument hired or arranged to undo the consequence of the first husband's third divorce.

The majority of scholars described here regard a contract that explicitly makes this contrived purpose its condition as invalid. The reports also condemn the arrangement itself, not only the way it is written into a contract.

Ahmad records from 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud that the Prophet ﷺ cursed the person who performs tahlil and the person for whose benefit it is performed. The same report includes condemnation of those who take and pay riba, usury. An-Nasa'i and At-Tirmidhi also transmit it, and At-Tirmidhi grades it Hasan, good. He connects the ruling with the practice of knowledgeable Companions, naming 'Umar, 'Uthman and Ibn 'Umar, and with the jurists of the following generation. It is also reported from 'Ali, Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn 'Abbas.

Al-Hakim records in his Mustadrak that Nafi' described a man asking Ibn 'Umar about a brother who married a triply divorced woman to make her lawful for the first husband without that husband's knowing. Ibn 'Umar rejected it and required a marriage involving genuine desire. He said that they had treated the arrangement as adultery in the Prophet's ﷺ time. Al-Hakim calls the chain Sahih, authentic, while noting that Al-Bukhari and Muslim did not record it. The wording is understood as carrying a ruling from the Prophet ﷺ.

A further report through Qabisah bin Jabir gives 'Umar's severe warning that participants brought to him would face stoning. Among those named in transmitting it are Harb Al-Kirmani and Al-Jawzjani. Abu Bakr Al-Athram and Abu Bakr bin Abu Shaybah are also listed. The severity reinforces the condemnation of contrived marriage. It must not be turned into advice to arrange such a marriage as the expected next step after divorce.

### When could the first couple lawfully marry again?

The later marriage must first have been real and consummated. If it subsequently ends by divorce or the second husband's death, the woman's waiting period must be completed. Only then may she and the first husband undertake a new marriage with a new payment and contract.

The condition about maintaining Allah's limits is central. Experience of separation should lead them to recognise their duties and the value of companionship. A renewed marriage undertaken without any purpose of living honourably would miss the stated condition. The lawful possibility is not a demand that the woman return, nor a right for the first husband to command the outcome of her later marriage.

### What warning and comfort lie within the restriction?

An inward reading sees a protection for the woman in a consequence the husband himself would find hard to bear: her genuine marriage to another man. He cannot choose separation while assuming that she will remain permanently available to him. Knowing that a third divorce opens a life beyond his control should make him cautious about using it.

The same reading finds mercy in the possibility of reunion when a later marriage independently ends. Love can make an otherwise painful history bearable. If the former spouses regret their lost companionship and come to understand their need for one another, returning can be without blame under the stated conditions. Its poetic image is a promise never again to separate if another meeting becomes possible.

The broader lesson concerns every serious disagreement. This carefully considered procedure in an emotional matter calls for patient, dispassionate decisions rather than impulsive retaliation. The discussion describes a process extending across months. Its precise duration follows the applicable waiting-period rules, but its lesson is clear: irreversible consequences should not be rushed through in anger.

### In short

- A third divorce ends ordinary revocation and prevents an immediate new marriage between the same spouses.
- Stopping at one properly timed divorce preserves more possibilities than using every pronouncement available.
- The majority argument counts simultaneous triple divorce as effective, while a minority view counting it as one is also recorded.
- A later marriage must be genuine and consummated. Arranged tahlil is condemned, not prescribed.
- Any eventual reunion requires the end of the later marriage, completion of the waiting period and a sincere expectation of honouring Allah's limits.
