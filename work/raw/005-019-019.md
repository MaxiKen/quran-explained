<<<VERSE 5:19>>>
"O People of the Book! Our Messenger has indeed come to you, making things clear to you after an interval between the messengers so you do not say, “There has never come to us a deliverer of good news or a warner.” Now there has come to you a deliverer of good news and a warner. And Allah is Most Capable of everything"

This verse speaks to the People of the Book again. It says that Allah's Messenger has come to them, after a gap between the messengers, to make things clear. It gives the reason: so that they cannot say that no bringer of good news and no warner ever came to them. Then it confirms that a bringer of good news and a warner has now come, and it ends by saying that Allah is able to do all things. The material places the verse in the long gap between 'Isa and Muhammad ﷺ, and it draws from that gap the honour of the Prophet's mission and the responsibility of those who received it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O People of the Book! Our Messenger has indeed come to you, making things clear to you."** The material explains that the address is to the Jews and the Christians, to the people of the Torah and the Gospel. The Messenger's task is to make clear the laws of religion, which had been obscured by changes to the Scriptures and by the passing of time.

**"After an interval between the messengers."** The material explains that the Arabic word fatrah means a period in which the sending of messengers has stopped. It identifies the interval as the time between 'Isa and the final Prophet. The material reports several figures for the length of this gap. Abu 'Uthman an-Nahdi and Qatadah are reported to have said it was six hundred years, and al-Bukhari records the same figure from Salman al-Farisi. Qatadah gave five hundred and sixty years and Ma'mar gave five hundred and forty. Others said six hundred and twenty. The material explains that the differences arise from counting in solar years or lunar years, since a hundred lunar years are about three years fewer than a hundred solar years. The gap is given as 569 years in the explanation of the translation, and the material notes that figure. It cites 18:25, where the People of the Cave are said to have stayed three hundred years and nine more, which the material reads as a lunar correction to the solar count that the People of the Book already knew.

The material says that the period was the time between the Children of Israel and the final Prophet, a time when prophets were absent from the world and the original religions had been altered. Idol worship, fire worship and the worship of the cross had spread, and evil and ignorance had covered the earth. The material notes that only a few remained loyal to the true teaching, among them some Jewish rabbis, Christian priests and Sabian monks. It presents the sending of the Prophet ﷺ as a blessing at the time when the world most needed it.

**"So you do not say, ‘There has never come to us a deliverer of good news or a warner.’"** The material explains that this is a warning against using the gap as an excuse. A people cannot say that no one came to warn them, once the Messenger has come. Ibn Jarir reads the part of the verse about Allah's power as meaning that Allah is able to punish those who disobey Him and to reward those who obey Him.

**"Now there has come to you a deliverer of good news and a warner."** The material says that the bringer of good news and the warner is Muhammad ﷺ. It explains that the Prophet is the last messenger after whom there will be no other.

**"And Allah is Most Capable of everything."** The material says that this phrase reminds the reader that Allah has the power to reward and to punish, so the warning is a serious one.

### The Prophet's place in the chain of messengers

The material cites the saying recorded by al-Bukhari, through Abu Hurayrah, that the Prophet ﷺ said he had the greatest claim among all people on the son of Maryam, because there was no prophet between them. The material says this saying refutes the view of al-Qudai and others that a prophet named Khalid bin Sinan came after 'Isa. The material notes, however, that some reports on Khalid bin Sinan place him before 'Isa, and it leaves the matter at that.

The material also cites the long address that Iyad bin Himar al-Mujash'i reported from the Prophet ﷺ. In it, Allah says that He created all His servants as people who submit to the one God, that the devils led them away from their religion, and that Allah disliked the people of the earth except a few. The address says that Allah sent the Prophet ﷺ to test and to be tested, and that He sent him a Book that water cannot wash away. The material records this report and the mention of the Quraysh in it. It also says that Ahmad recorded the report, and that Muslim recorded part of it. Al-Mustafa, the chosen one, is one of the titles that the material uses for the Prophet ﷺ.

### Why the gap matters

The material draws a lesson from the gap. The Messenger came to a world that had lost its link with God, and the people he was sent to were proud, self-centred and resistant. Yet within a short time, these same people became teachers to the whole world in knowledge, conduct and morals. The material calls this proof that the Prophet ﷺ was truly the messenger of Allah, and it says that his teaching surpassed the teachings of all the earlier prophets. The comparison given is with a physician who heals a hopeless patient in a place that lacks medicine, and then turns the patient into a healer himself.

### The question of those who lived in the gap

The material discusses the fate of those who lived in the gap and never received a messenger or the law of an earlier prophet. Such people are excused and not punished, provided they did not commit shirk. The material says that most scholars hope that such people will be forgiven if they kept to what survived of the religion of Musa or 'Isa, and if they did not oppose the oneness of Allah. The reason is that the oneness of Allah can be known by reason, without a reported proof.

The material also raises a question. The People of the Book had their Torah and Gospel, so how could they plead ignorance? The answer given is that by the time of the Prophet ﷺ the original Scriptures had been lost, and the texts they had were altered. The material notes that Ibn Taymiyyah and others held that some original copies might have survived in places, but that this does not change the conclusion.

### A reminder that no community is favoured by name

The material reads the verse with a principle that one reading makes plain: no community is favoured in the eyes of Allah merely because of its name. Reckoning is individual. Each person is judged by his deeds in this world of trial, and Paradise and Hell are not the property of any group. Allah raises up warners and bringers of good news, and it is by supporting them and cooperating with them that a person finds Allah.

### In short

- The Messenger came after a gap in the sending of messengers, so that no one could plead that no warner had ever come.
- The gap lasted several hundred years by the reports the material gives, and the Prophet ﷺ came at a time of great need.
- The sending of the final messenger is a sign of Allah's mercy, and the reckoning is individual, not by community.
