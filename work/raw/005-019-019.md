<<<VERSE 5:19>>>
The People of the Book are told that the long absence of a messenger has ended: "Our Messenger has indeed come to you, making things clear to you after an interval between the messengers". Muhammad ﷺ brings the explanation, good news and warning that the people need. They can no longer meet his message with the excuse that no one came to guide them.

His coming is both a favour and a reason for responsibility. Allah sends guidance before calling people to account, and He has the power to reward those who follow it and punish those who reject it. Belonging to a community associated with an earlier scripture does not replace a person's response to the messenger who has now arrived.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O People of the Book!"** addresses Jews and Christians, the people of the Torah and Gospel. Their scriptures and religious inheritance do not make the new explanation unnecessary.

**"Our Messenger has indeed come to you"** identifies Muhammad ﷺ as Allah's Messenger and the final Prophet. No prophet or messenger will come after him. His mission reaches people at a time of urgent need for renewed clarity.

**"making things clear to you after an interval between the messengers"** describes a fatrah, a period in which the sending of messengers was suspended. The word carries the idea of an activity slackening or ceasing. Here it refers to the interval between 'Isa (Jesus) and Muhammad ﷺ. He clarifies the religious commands and prohibitions that people need to follow.

**"so you do not say, “There has never come to us a deliverer of good news or a warner.”"** removes the proposed excuse of having received no message. The good news concerns Allah's reward and Paradise. The warning concerns punishment and Hell. The wording emphatically denies the absence of such a messenger as an available excuse after his arrival.

**"Now there has come to you a deliverer of good news and a warner."** answers that claim directly. The same Messenger ﷺ brings both encouragement and accountability. A person must respond to the message rather than choose only its comforting or only its frightening part.

**"And Allah is Most Capable of everything"** includes sending His messengers and bringing about the consequences they announce. Ibn Jarir explains it through His power to punish disobedience and reward obedience. Nothing in either task lies beyond Him.

### How long was the interval?

The reports give different figures. Abu 'Uthman An-Nahdi and Qatadah are reported as giving six hundred years, and Al-Bukhari records that figure from Salman Al-Farisi. A separate report from Qatadah gives five hundred and sixty years. Ma'mar gives five hundred and forty. Other accounts give five hundred, 569, or six hundred and twenty.

One proposed reconciliation distinguishes solar from lunar reckoning. The discussion compares six hundred solar years with the longer lunar count, noting a difference of about three years in every hundred. It illustrates that distinction through the people of the Cave, whose stay is given as three hundred years with nine added (18:25).

That explanation concerns the particular difference between the corresponding solar and lunar totals. It need not be turned into a claim that every reported figure has thereby become identical. The main point does not depend on selecting one precise number: a substantial interval separated the two messengers.

Ibn 'Abbas is also reported as describing about seventeen hundred years between Musa (Moses) and 'Isa. In that account, prophets continued to come throughout the earlier period, with a thousand sent among the Children of Israel, besides those sent elsewhere. The later interval is distinguished by the suspension of that succession. The figures are reported estimates, not a new chronological calculation.

### Was there a prophet between 'Isa and Muhammad ﷺ?

Abu Hurayrah reports in Al-Bukhari's Sahih that the Prophet ﷺ declared his special closeness to the son of Maryam because no prophet stood between them. The teaching is also cited from Al-Bukhari and Muslim. It identifies 'Isa as the last prophet to the Children of Israel and Muhammad ﷺ as the final prophet and messenger among Adam's descendants.

The three messengers mentioned in Surah Ya Sin are explained here as envoys sent by 'Isa. In that explanation they are messengers in the ordinary sense of people carrying a message, not additional prophets who interrupt the interval.

There are also different treatments of Khalid bin Sinan. Al-Quda'i and others are reported as placing a prophet of that name after 'Isa. The explicit hadith of no prophet between 'Isa and Muhammad ﷺ is used to reject that chronology. Another explanation, cited through Shihab, accepts Khalid ibn Sinan al-'Arabi as a prophet but places him before 'Isa, not within the interval. These positions should remain distinct.

### Why was the arrival such a great favour?

The period is described as one of widespread religious alteration, idolatry, fire worship and devotion to the cross. Injustice and ignorance had spread, while only a small number remained faithful to the teachings received through earlier prophets. Examples mentioned include some Jewish teachers, Christian religious figures and Sabian monks.

The few remaining faithful people prevent the description from becoming a claim that every individual was identical. Nevertheless, the general need for clear guidance was great. The message brought people from confusion toward a clear law and a known direction of worship.

'Iyad bin Himar Al-Mujash'i relates a speech recorded by Ahmad in which the Prophet ﷺ conveyed what Allah had taught him. Allah had created His servants inclined toward worship of Him alone. Devils then diverted them, made them forbid what He had permitted and commanded them to associate others with Him without authority.

The report describes Allah looking upon the people of the earth, Arabs and non-Arabs, with displeasure except for a few among the Children of Israel. Muslim's wording describes the exception as a few among the People of the Book. It then presents the Messenger's ﷺ sending as a test for him and for those reached through him.

### What support and instruction does that report describe?

The Book given to the Prophet ﷺ is described as one that water cannot wash away, enduring rather than dependent on a vulnerable written surface. He is described as reciting it asleep and awake. The emphasis is on the lasting gift and his relation to it.

The report also relates a command to confront and destroy the hostile power of Quraysh. The Prophet ﷺ feared that they would crush his head. Allah promised to drive them out as they had driven him out, support him in battle and provide for him as he spent upon his companions. It describes one force being supported by five like it and commands him to fight disobedient opponents with those who obeyed him. This belongs to the particular conflict narrated, not an independent command for a reader to attack others.

The speech then gives portraits of the people of Paradise and the Fire. Among the former are a just ruler who is prosperous and charitable, a person merciful to relatives and Muslims, and a poor person with dependants who is forgiving and charitable. Justice, compassion and generosity belong to the moral world the message builds.

The report announces five kinds associated with the Fire. Its translated wording groups the details unevenly, but it describes a weak person without religious restraint, unprincipled followers, a person treacherous even in small matters, and someone who continually schemes against another's family or wealth. It also mentions the miser or liar and the foul-mouthed person. The warning is preserved without inventing a tidier list than the supplied wording provides.

### What is said about people unreached during the interval?

The discussion asks about people who had no prophet, no deputy of a prophet and no preserved law available to them. It sees room for excuse and hope of forgiveness, while attaching the condition that they did not practise shirk, associating partners with Allah.

Jurists differ about the people of the fatrah and whether they are forgiven. The majority view described here inclines toward hope for those who adhered to whatever remained for them of the religion of Musa or 'Isa, even where its transmission was imperfect, provided they did not reject Allah's oneness or embrace associationism.

The reason given for the condition is that oneness can be recognised through reflection, rather than depending entirely on a transmitted legal detail. This is the position and reasoning supplied in the discussion, not a claim that the complex question admits no scholarly disagreement.

### Did possession of the Torah and Gospel already remove every excuse?

The question arises because the addressed communities had books and teachers. Why speak of the absence of guidance if those remained? The answer offered is that alteration and inserted narratives had obscured the original instruction so severely that the available forms did not serve as uncorrupted guidance.

The discussion does not make the survival of an original manuscript somewhere logically impossible. It notes that Ibn Taymiyyah and others wrote of original copies surviving in particular places. A possible copy unknown or inaccessible to most people does not, on this argument, settle what guidance they actually received.

The new Messenger ﷺ removes that uncertainty by making the message clear to those he addresses. Whatever excuse the earlier interval might have allowed cannot simply be carried over unchanged after his explanation arrives.

### How is the transformation of his hearers evidence of his mission?

The explanation points to a people immersed in the ignorance of the period, with stubborn habits and little access to sound knowledge. Through the Prophet's ﷺ teaching and company, they became examples and teachers in conduct, learning, worship and social dealings within a short time.

It illustrates the change through a physician treating a nearly hopeless patient without ordinary instruments or medicines. The patient not only recovers but becomes a capable healer of others. Such a result points to the extraordinary ability of the physician. In the same way, the reform of the Prophet's ﷺ hearers is offered as a public sign of the strength of his message.

The argument does not reduce his mission to a single outward achievement. It draws attention to the breadth of the change in knowledge, actions, character and relationships. The benefit of revelation becomes visible in people who then carry its guidance to others.

### Who is promised Paradise?

No community possesses Paradise as a native homeland, nor is Hell assigned merely by racial identity. The final reckoning concerns persons and their deeds. Allah sends people who reveal the realities of the test, announce reward and warn of punishment.

The response that matters is to support and follow that guidance. A claim to belong to a favoured group cannot replace it. This is why the direct address to the People of the Book ends not in reassurance based on ancestry, but in the removal of an excuse and the reminder of Allah's power.

### In short

- Muhammad ﷺ came after a substantial interval in the sending of prophets, bringing clear instruction, good news and warning.
- The reports differ over the interval's length, but the explicit teaching states that no prophet came between him and 'Isa.
- The message was a great favour in a time of altered guidance and widespread ignorance. Its arrival also removes the excuse that no messenger has come.
- The fate of unreached people is discussed with qualifications and disagreement, not reduced to a blanket claim about every individual.
- Final reward rests on the response to Allah's guidance, not on inherited communal privilege.
