<<<VERSE 2:34>>>
The angels had learned that Ādam (Adam) surpassed them in the knowledge needed for vicegerency on the earth. Now Allah made that superiority visible. "And remember when We said to the angels, 'Prostrate before Adam,' so they all did, but not Iblīs, who refused and acted arrogantly, becoming unfaithful." The verse records a great honour granted to Ādam, an honour that Allah reminds his descendants of, and at the same time it records the first sin ever committed: the refusal of Iblīs, rooted in pride. Both the honour and the refusal set the pattern for everything that follows in the story of humanity.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And remember when We said to the angels, 'Prostrate before Adam'"** describes the honour. Having shown the angels that Ādam possessed what they lacked, Allah commanded them to prostrate before him as a mark of his rank, out of recognition and respect, and they obeyed eagerly. Many hadiths testify to this command, including the hadith of the intercession, in which the believers remind Ādam that Allah created him with His own Hand, made the angels prostrate to him and taught him the names of everything. There is also the hadith of the prayer of Mūsā (Moses), who asked his Lord to show him Ādam, the one who had caused himself and his descendants to leave Paradise, and who said to him when they met, "Are you Ādam, whom Allah created with His own Hands, breathed life into, and commanded the angels to prostrate before?"

Qatādah drew the essential distinction: the obedience was to Allah, and the prostration was before Ādam. Allah honoured Ādam and commanded the angels to bow to him, so the angels in bowing were worshipping Allah, not Ādam. Many scholars describe the prostration as a bow of salutation, greeting and honour rather than an act of worship, and compare it to the verse in which the parents and brothers of Yūsuf (Joseph), arriving in Egypt, "all fell down in prostration to Joseph" (12:100).

**"so they all did"** means that every angel complied. The wording of the Qur'an addresses the command to the angels alone, but by excepting Iblīs from those who obeyed, the text implies that the command was given to every being with understanding that existed at the time, the jinn included. The angels are named because when superior beings like the angels were required to show respect for Ādam, it goes without saying that lesser creatures like the jinn were required to do the same.

**"but not Iblīs"** raises the question of who Iblīs was. He was not an angel. The Qur'an says elsewhere that he "was one of the jinn, but he rebelled against the command of his Lord" (18:50), and calls him the father of the jinn. He was included in the command given to the angels because he was living among them and imitating their conduct and their deeds, and so he was blamed for defying a command that covered him. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq reported that Ibn ʿAbbās said that Iblīs, before he turned to sin, lived among the angels under the name ʿAzāzīl. He was one of the inhabitants of the earth, among the most devoted worshippers and most knowledgeable of those who stood with the angels, and this very distinction made him arrogant. His origin was the species called jinn. Because of the excellence of his worship, Allah had admitted him to the company of the angels, but when the test came he returned to his lowly nature. His name is explained as meaning one who was far from the truth and far from the mercy of God.

**"who refused and acted arrogantly"** gives the two faces of his sin: an outward refusal and an inward pride. He would not follow his Lord's command, and he disdained to bow, saying that he was better than Ādam because he had been created from fire and Ādam from clay. Qatādah said that Iblīs, the enemy of Allah, envied Ādam the honour Allah had given him, and that arrogance was therefore the very first sin, since pride is what kept the enemy of Allah from prostrating before Ādam. The Ṣaḥīḥ records the Prophet's ﷺ warning that no one with a mustard seed's weight of arrogance in his heart will enter Paradise. Disbelief, arrogance and rebellion together drove Iblīs out of Allah's holy presence and out of His mercy.

**"becoming unfaithful"** states the verdict. One view is that he became a disbeliever by this very act, because he refused to comply with Allah's command. Another is that in Allah's foreknowledge he had always been destined to be a disbeliever, and the verse describes what Allah knew of him. He is also said to be the first unbeliever ever. The reason his refusal amounted to disbelief rather than mere sin deserves attention. In the Sharīʿah, failing to carry out an obligation in practice is a sin and a transgression, but it does not make a person an unbeliever. Iblīs became an unbeliever because he defied and challenged a divine command outright, and in refusing to bow he in effect declared that in his judgment Ādam was not worthy of it. He set his own opinion against Allah's decree.

### Why prostration to a creature was once allowed and is now forbidden

Since the Qur'an itself speaks of prostration as a mark of honour, both here and in the story of Yūsuf, it is important to understand what such a prostration was and why Muslims are forbidden it today.

The prostration before Ādam and before Yūsuf cannot have been worship, because worshipping anyone other than Allah is shirk and kufr and could never be permitted by any revealed law. In the time of the earlier prophets, prostrating before someone must therefore have been simply an act of courtesy and respect, with the same value that a greeting, a handshake, the kissing of a hand or standing up in someone's honour has today. Imām al-Jaṣṣāṣ wrote in his Aḥkām al-Qurʾān that the laws of the earlier prophets permitted prostration in honour of one's elders, but that the Sharīʿah of the Prophet ﷺ has forbidden gestures such as prostrating before a person, bowing very low, or standing before someone with the hands folded as in ṣalāh, because all of these may suggest worship, and has allowed only the greeting of salām and the handshake as gestures of courtesy and respect. Ar-Rāzī also held that the prostration was one of greeting and honour.

The reason behind the prohibition is easy to see. Shirk, disbelief and the worship of anything besides Allah contradict the very principle of īmān, so no revealed law could ever tolerate them. But there are acts and gestures which are not themselves shirk or disbelief and yet, through people's ignorance or carelessness, can become a prelude to them. The earlier laws did not forbid such acts absolutely. They only prevented them from being used as instruments of shirk. Making pictures of living things, for instance, is not in itself shirk and was permitted in earlier laws. The Qur'an says of the jinn who served Sulaymān (Solomon) that "they made for him whatever he desired of sanctuaries, statues, basins as large as reservoirs, and cooking pots fixed into the ground" (34:13). In the same way, prostration as a gesture of respect was permitted. But over time the practice opened the way to shirk through people's ignorance and thoughtlessness, and even produced grave distortions in the laws of various prophets, which later prophets and later laws had to correct.

The Prophet ﷺ is the last of the prophets and messengers, and his Sharīʿah is the last of the revealed laws, valid until the end of time. To protect it from every distortion, Allah closed every gap through which shirk or idolatry could possibly enter, and this law therefore strictly forbids all the practices that had at one time or another served as a path to shirk or idol worship. Making pictures of living things has been banned completely. Prostrating before anyone is forbidden, respect being no excuse. It is not permitted to offer ṣalāh at the hours of the day that the idolaters had set apart for worshipping their gods, since even this slight external resemblance might lead toward shirk. And according to a hadith reported by Muslim, a master may not call his slave "my ʿabd" nor may a slave call his master "my rabb," words that merely mean "servant" and "one who nurtures" and are harmless in themselves, but that could be misread and could tempt ignorant slaves or subjugated people into worshipping their masters.

Some reliable scholars add a further explanation concerning prostration in particular. Ṣalāh, the basic form of Islamic worship, consists of four kinds of action: standing, bowing, sitting and prostrating. Standing and sitting are things people do all day in the course of ordinary life, and they are also performed as acts of worship within the prayer. Bowing and prostrating, on the other hand, are not ordinary habitual movements. They are characteristically associated with ṣalāh and with worship, so the Islamic Sharīʿah has identified them as acts of worship and forbidden Muslims to bow or prostrate before anyone but Allah.

On what grounds is it affirmed that the Islamic law has forbidden the practice, given that the Qur'an mentions it with approval in earlier times? Several well-known reports from the Prophet ﷺ, transmitted through a large number of his Companions, establish that prostrating before someone as a mark of respect is unlawful (ḥarām). The best known is the hadith in which Muʿādh told the Prophet ﷺ that he had visited ash-Shām and found the people there prostrating before their priests and scholars, and said that the Messenger of Allah was more deserving of prostration than they. The Prophet ﷺ answered no, and said that if he were to command any human being to prostrate before another, it would be the wife whom he commanded to prostrate to her husband, so great is his right over her. This settles that prostrating out of respect is absolutely forbidden, with no exception for any created being whatsoever. The hadith has come down through twenty Companions, while according to Tadrīb ar-Rāwī, the famous work on the principles of hadith, a report transmitted by as few as ten Companions is already classed as mutawātir and carries the same authority in matters of law as the Qur'an itself.

### How could the Peacock of the Angels fall?

Iblīs had reached such a height of knowledge and learning that he was called Ṭāʾūs al-Malāʾikah, the Peacock of the Angels. How, then, did he come to commit so ruinous an error? Some scholars say that because of his pride and vanity Allah took back from him the wealth of knowledge and understanding he had been given, so that he behaved like an ignorant fool. Others trace his error to self-love and ambition. A line of Arabic verse has been quoted to settle the matter: once Allah's help is withdrawn from a person, he can no longer save himself from sin, and every effort he makes only drives him deeper into misguidance. May Allah in His mercy save us all from such a fate. The lesson drawn is that no one should be vain about his learning, his deeds or even his faith, for faith counts only if it lasts to the final breath and into the first stage of the journey to the next world.

### A reading for the inner life

Majestic and all-compelling is the God of the world and of its people: the Enactor, the Knower of the hidden, eternally beautiful in His doing, tremendous in rank. He is never ignorant of anything He knows, never regrets anything He bestows, is never at a loss over anything He has done. To some He makes attractive what He disapproves, and for others He makes ugly what He approves. Out of fire He created the despairing Iblīs, gave him a place at the Lote Tree of the Furthest Boundary, and sent the angels nearest the Presence to seek knowledge from him. Yet in spite of such distinction and rank, He wrote out for him the inscription of wretchedness and bound his waist with the sash of the curse. Ādam He pulled up out of dark dust, and then the Higher Assembly were made to carry his throne, the cape of exaltedness was laid on his shoulders, set the crown of generosity on his head, and said to the angels of the Presence, "Prostrate before Adam!"

It has come down in the traditions that Ādam was seated on a throne with seven hundred legs, each a journey of seven hundred years from the next, and the command went out to Jibrīl (Gabriel), Mīkāʾīl (Michael) and the chiefs of the angels. The throne was set before the Majestic Throne, and the angels were told to go to it and prostrate before Ādam. When they came and looked at him, they were all intoxicated by his beauty. A face adorned by the God of heaven needs no hairdresser's touch. They saw a beauty without end: the crown of "He created Ādam in His form" upon his head, the robe of "had a spirit of My Own creation breathed into him" (15:29) upon his body, and the embroidery of the loving care of "who love Him and are loved by Him" (5:54) upon the sleeve of his sinlessness. Strangers though we are, with hearts in disarray, we are serving-boys of that world-adorning face.

Describing Ādam, Wahb ibn Munabbih said that God gave him the most beautiful of forms at his creation and dressed him in the Garden's ornaments: rings on all ten fingers, anklets on his legs, bracelets on his arms, a crown and diadem on his head and brow, and gave him as a title the name most beloved to Him, calling him Abū Muḥammad. Then He told him to walk around the Garden and see whether anyone resembled him or whether any creature had been created more beautiful than he. Ādam wandered through the Garden and grew conceited, striding proudly through it. God found that beautiful in him, and from beyond the Throne He called: go on being conceited, Ādam, since the likes of you may be. I loved something, so I made it solitary for the Solitary. That conceit God afterwards passed on to Ādam's offspring. In the ignorant it appears as arrogance, in kings as pridefulness, and in the friends of Allah as ecstasy.

Good fortune in the spirit and in the world is no game, and felicity cannot be bought. Iblīs saw only the suffering of passing days and the toil of the work, while Ādam went to Paradise. Iblīs had obedience without end, yet it was Ādam who received the address of honour. The difference between them was never the quantity of worship. It was the acceptance of Allah's choice.

### In short

- Allah commanded the angels, and with them every being of understanding, to prostrate before Ādam as a visible sign of his superiority. The prostration was a bow of honour and greeting, like that of Yūsuf's family (12:100). The obedience was to Allah.
- Iblīs, a jinn living among the angels and once called ʿAzāzīl, refused out of pride, claiming that fire is better than clay. Arrogance was the first sin, and because he challenged Allah's command outright his refusal was disbelief, not merely disobedience.
- Prostration out of respect was permitted in earlier laws but is forbidden in the final Sharīʿah, which closed every door to shirk. The Prophet ﷺ said that if anyone were to prostrate to a human being it would be a wife to her husband, and he permitted no one.
- Even the Peacock of the Angels fell when Allah's help was withdrawn, so no one should be vain about knowledge, deeds or faith.
