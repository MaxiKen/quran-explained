<<<VERSE 3:144>>>
> “Muḥammad is no more than a messenger; other messengers have gone before him. If he were to die or to be killed, would you regress into disbelief? Those who do so will not harm Allah whatsoever. And Allah will reward those who are grateful” (3:144)

This verse was revealed in the confusion at Uhud, when a rumor spread that the Prophet ﷺ had been killed. It reminds believers that Muhammad is Allah’s Messenger, not an immortal being, and that their faith is in Allah rather than dependent on any person’s physical presence.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Muḥammad is no more than a messenger”** affirms the Prophet’s extraordinary role while making clear that he is still human and mortal. He conveyed Allah’s message and guided the believers, but worship belongs to Allah alone. Loving the Prophet ﷺ is part of faith. Treating him as divine or making one person the foundation of religion would contradict the message he brought.

At Uhud, after the Muslims suffered losses, a call spread that the Prophet had been killed. Ibn Qamiʿah claimed to have killed him, and some believers became confused and withdrew. The verse addressed the shock directly: even if the Prophet were to die, the truth of Islam would remain. The obligations of faith, worship, and jihad belong to Allah, who never dies.

A report transmitted by Ibn Abi Najih describes a man from the Muhajirun telling an injured Ansari that Muhammad had been killed. The Ansari answered that the Prophet had delivered the message and urged him to defend his religion. Al-Hafiz Abu Bakr al-Bayhaqi collected this account in his work on the signs of An-Nubuwwah, prophethood. Its lesson is not that the companions did not love the Prophet. They loved him deeply, but their devotion had to remain grounded in worship of Allah.

**“other messengers have gone before him”** reminds the community that earlier prophets also died or were killed. The message does not end with the death of its messenger. The Qur’an describes the prophet ʿIsa as a messenger and servant of Allah, not as a partner in divinity. This verse likewise protects the distinction between the honor of Muhammad ﷺ and the exclusive worship due to Allah.

**“If he were to die or to be killed, would you regress into disbelief?”** is a rebuke of turning away from faith because of grief or confusion. The question expects the answer no. A prophet’s death can be painful, but it does not make the truth false or release people from their commitments. Returning to kufr, disbelief, would harm the person who abandons faith, not Allah.

When the Prophet ﷺ did die years later, Abu Bakr came from his residence at As-Sunh, uncovered the Prophet’s face, kissed him, and wept. He then addressed the people while Umar was overcome with shock. Abu Bakr reminded them that whoever worshipped Muhammad should know that Muhammad had died, while whoever worshipped Allah should know that Allah is ever-living and never dies. He recited this verse, and the companions recognized its truth. Al-Bukhari records the account. Ibn Abbas narrates that Umar later said the verse made his legs give way when he heard Abu Bakr recite it.

**“Those who do so will not harm Allah whatsoever”** means that abandoning faith cannot diminish Allah’s power or kingdom. Allah is independent of creation. The devotional reflection in the material emphasizes this point: prophets and people may pass away, while Allah remains the keeper of all things. No one’s death reduces His majesty.

**“And Allah will reward those who are grateful”** praises those who remain faithful to Allah’s guidance in both hardship and ease. Gratitude here includes obeying Allah, defending the message without worshipping its bearer, and holding to faith whether the Prophet is present or has passed away. Ibn Jarir and early scholars including Al-Hasan explain that the grateful continue in obedience and are rewarded by Allah.

### Love without deification

The companions’ grief at the rumor shows the intensity of their love. The verse does not condemn that love. It corrects the possibility that shock may turn into despair or a retreat from faith. A community must value the Prophet’s teaching and follow his example, while understanding that Allah is the giver of life and guidance.

The Prophet’s death was not a collapse of Islam. Abu Bakr’s words helped the companions distinguish between loyalty to a messenger and worship of Allah. Umar’s initial shock was human, and his return to steadiness showed the power of the Qur’anic reminder. The verse prepared the community to continue after the Prophet’s death, preserving the message and carrying out its obligations.

### In short

- Muhammad ﷺ is Allah’s Messenger and is mortal, while Allah is ever-living.
- The rumor at Uhud tested the believers, but the message did not depend on the Prophet’s physical presence.
- Gratitude means remaining faithful to Allah through grief and change.

<<<VERSE 3:145>>>
> “No soul can ever die without Allah’s Will at the destined time. Those who desire worldly gain, We will let them have it, and those who desire heavenly reward, We will grant it to them. And We will reward those who are grateful” (3:145)

The verse teaches that every person’s death occurs by Allah’s permission and at an appointed time. It also asks believers to consider what they seek through their actions: a passing worldly benefit, the lasting reward of the Hereafter, or both in a way that remains grateful to Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**“No soul can ever die without Allah’s Will”** means that life and death remain under Allah’s authority. No one can bring death forward or hold it off by mere wish. The verse was relevant to the fear at Uhud, when some believed that the Prophet ﷺ had been killed and others feared they too would die. Their appointed time was not changed by fleeing the battlefield.

The Qur’an says that Allah created people and set an appointed term (6:2), and that no life is extended or shortened except in a record (35:11). These verses do not teach recklessness. Believers must take reasonable precautions and protect life. The point is that fear of death should not drive a person to betray a duty or imagine that disobedience can secure a longer life.

**“at the destined time”** means each person has a term known to Allah. A Muslim does not know when that time is, so the verse is not permission to expose oneself or others to needless danger. It gives courage when obedience carries risk and comfort when death comes despite care and preparation.

**“Those who desire worldly gain, We will let them have it”** means a person who works only for an immediate worldly return may receive some portion of what Allah has decreed in this life. The verse does not say that every worldly intention will result in wealth or that worldly benefits are forbidden. It warns against making temporary gain the sole purpose of one’s life and forgetting the Hereafter.

This part of the passage also speaks to Uhud. Some archers left their assigned position after seeing the Quraysh withdraw and the Muslims collect spoils. The collection of lawful spoils was part of the campaign, not inherently greed. The Prophet’s command to hold the hill remained binding, however, and leaving it to pursue a share was a mistake. Their good intention or lawful entitlement did not cancel the importance of obedience.

**“and those who desire heavenly reward, We will grant it to them”** means those who act for the Hereafter will receive what Allah wills of its reward. The Qur’an elsewhere contrasts a person who seeks the quick-passing world with one who strives for the Hereafter as a believer (17:18-19), and says that people’s shares in this life and the next differ according to their aims and deeds (42:20). The lesson is to orient one’s intention toward Allah’s lasting reward, while still fulfilling responsibilities in this world.

**“And We will reward those who are grateful”** means that Allah rewards those who recognize His gifts and use them in obedience. Gratitude is not simply a verbal thanks. It includes remaining faithful when afraid, fulfilling commands, and not letting worldly gains displace the purpose of life.

### Intention and responsibility

The verse does not require believers to reject every worldly benefit. A person may earn a living, care for family, and take a lawful share of spoils under Islamic law. Striving in jihad includes serving the community and carrying out assigned duties, not simply fighting or collecting material gain. A person’s ʿāqiba, their final outcome, depends on Allah’s judgment and the intention behind their deeds. The question is whether those pursuits are governed by Allah’s limits or whether they lead a person to abandon a duty. The companions at Uhud were not condemned as people who had become unbelievers. Their error was serious because they left a position entrusted to them.

Death being decreed also does not make effort pointless. A person remains responsible for choices. The verse gives courage to act rightly without pretending to know the unseen. It teaches both trust in Allah’s decree and care in using the means available.

### In short

- Death comes at the term Allah has decreed, but this is not permission for recklessness.
- Worldly gain is temporary, while the believer seeks the reward of the Hereafter.
- Gratitude and obedience should guide a person’s intentions and actions.

<<<VERSE 3:146>>>
> “˹Imagine˺ how many devotees fought along with their prophets and never faltered despite whatever ˹losses˺ they suffered in the cause of Allah, nor did they weaken or give in! Allah loves those who persevere” (3:146)

The verse offers the believers examples from earlier communities. Many followers stood with their prophets through death, wounds, and defeat without losing heart, abandoning the struggle, or surrendering to the enemy.

### Phrase by phrase

**“how many devotees fought along with their prophets”** refers to groups of committed followers who supported their prophets. The term ribbiyyun has been explained in different ways: numerous bands of followers, devoted people, or learned and God-conscious people. Ibn Abbas and Al-Hasan al-Basri are among those reported to have understood it as scholars and jurists. Ibn Masʿud described them as thousands, while other early interpreters, including Ar-Rabiʿ bin Anas and Ataʾ al-Khurasani, explained the term as large groups. These meanings can overlap: communities may include many devoted people, among them scholars and teachers.

Some interpreters read the verse as saying that many prophets fought with their followers. Others understand it as saying that many prophets were killed while their followers remained steadfast. Ibn Ishaq discusses the latter reading in his account, and As-Suhayli supported it. The difference does not change the central lesson: the death or injury of a prophet did not make the faithful abandon the cause.

**“and never faltered despite whatever losses they suffered in the cause of Allah”** describes their patience when companions were killed or injured. Their grief did not make them deny the truth or give up what Allah had commanded. Jihad in this passage refers to striving under the guidance of a prophet. It is not a general instruction to seek violence or to fight without lawful authority.

**“nor did they weaken”** means they did not lose their resolve when the struggle became difficult. Their bodies and resources could be strained, but they continued with patience and trust in Allah. They understood that a setback did not mean their effort had no value.

**“or give in”** means they did not submit to the enemy’s demands or abandon their faith. The phrase also warns against humiliation born of despair. It is not a command to refuse peace in every circumstance. The verse praises steadfastness when the community is being attacked and called to abandon its faith.

**“Allah loves those who persevere”** means sabr is beloved to Allah. Patience includes maintaining obedience, correcting mistakes, and asking for help. It is not a refusal to grieve or a denial of suffering. Earlier followers remained faithful despite loss, and the Muslims at Uhud were called to learn from their example.

### A model for the believers at Uhud

The Muslims had just seen how a rumor about the Prophet ﷺ could shake the community. This verse reminds them that earlier believers continued after their prophets died. The message belongs to Allah and does not end with the life of one person. The Muslim community was to preserve its worship, prayer, zakah, and other obligations, while following the path taught by the Messenger.

The past examples do not suggest that every community’s situation is identical. They offer a pattern of patience, not a guarantee that all trials will have the same form or outcome. The believer asks Allah for steadfastness and serves the truth with wisdom and restraint.

### In short

- Earlier followers stood with their prophets through loss and hardship.
- Ribbiyyun can refer to many devoted followers, including learned people.
- Allah loves patience that remains faithful without seeking conflict.

<<<VERSE 3:147>>>
> “And all they said was, “Our Lord! Forgive our sins and excesses, make our steps firm, and grant us victory over the disbelieving people.”” (3:147)

The verse gives the prayer of those steadfast followers. They did not boast about their efforts. They admitted their shortcomings, asked Allah to strengthen them, and sought His help against those who opposed them.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Our Lord!”** is a call of dependence. They address Allah as the One who sustains them, knowing that their strength does not come from themselves alone.

**“Forgive our sins and excesses”** means they ask pardon for their wrongdoing and for crossing the limits. Even while striving for Allah, they do not assume that their efforts are flawless. They ask for forgiveness before asking for victory. This keeps their struggle from becoming a source of pride or a claim that Allah owes them success.

Their prayer also recognizes that hardship may expose mistakes. A person should not assume that every defeat is punishment, but the believer can use a trial to examine their conduct and repent where needed. Seeking forgiveness accompanies good action rather than replacing it.

**“make our steps firm”** asks Allah for steadiness at the moment of danger. The phrase evokes remaining in one’s position, continuing in obedience, and not being overcome by fear. The prayer seeks inward firmness and practical courage.

**“and grant us victory over the disbelieving people”** asks Allah to help them against those who reject the message and fight them. Their request is made to Allah, not as a boast of their own superiority. Victory is sought within the cause of their prophets and their community, not as permission for aggression against those who are peaceful.

### A prayer for every stage of striving

The order of the requests matters. First comes forgiveness, then steadfastness, then victory. The believer knows that the ability to perform good deeds comes from Allah. They ask pardon for shortcomings, ask for help to continue, and entrust the result to Him. This is the opposite of arrogance.

A person can apply this prayer to many forms of struggle: resisting temptation, staying honest under pressure, helping a community through hardship, or defending people from harm. The context is earlier communities who fought alongside their prophets, but the qualities of humility and perseverance remain relevant beyond the battlefield.

### In short

- The steadfast ask Allah for forgiveness before asking for victory.
- They seek firmness in obedience and action.
- They acknowledge that success comes from Allah.

<<<VERSE 3:148>>>
> “So Allah gave them the reward of this world and the excellent reward of the Hereafter. For Allah loves the good-doers” (3:148)

Allah rewarded those steadfast followers in this life and in the Hereafter. Their good conduct, prayer, and perseverance brought them the best outcome, while the lasting reward is greater than any temporary gain.

### Phrase by phrase

**“So Allah gave them the reward of this world”** may include victory, a good end, security, or the success of the mission. It does not mean that every believer will become wealthy or avoid suffering. The worldly reward is what Allah grants according to His wisdom. A person’s faithful effort remains valuable even when its result is not immediately visible.

**“and the excellent reward of the Hereafter”** refers to Paradise and lasting favor from Allah. The word “excellent” distinguishes this enduring reward from the changing benefits of worldly life. A spiritual reflection notes that the reward of the next life is lasting and free of the interruptions that affect worldly success.

**“For Allah loves the good-doers”** means that Allah loves those who act with ihsan, excellence. The preceding verses show what that looks like: they endure trials, seek forgiveness, ask for firmness, and avoid boasting about their deeds. They do their best while knowing that guidance and the ability to act come from Allah.

The famous hadith of Gabriel describes ihsan as worshipping Allah as though one sees Him, while knowing that He sees the worshipper. This awareness shapes both prayer and dealings with people. The earlier followers’ jihad was likewise joined to patience, prayer, and good conduct, not a pursuit of worldly power. It encourages sincerity, care, and fairness, rather than a performance designed to win praise.

The prayer in the previous verse asks for pardon, steadfastness, and victory. The reward here answers those requests, but it also teaches that the believer’s deepest hope is Allah’s love. A person who receives worldly success should be grateful rather than arrogant. A person who suffers should not assume that Allah has abandoned them.

### Gratitude in success and hardship

The verse connects good action with reward, while the surrounding passage reminds believers that outcomes alternate. The faithful person accepts worldly gains as a gift and does not treat them as proof of superiority. If relief is delayed, they continue in the right path and hope for the excellent reward of the Hereafter.

The word “say” in the prayer of the previous verse is also a lesson in how believers speak under pressure: they ask for forgiveness and help instead of boasting. Allah’s love is the lasting aim, and ihsan is the manner of seeking it.

### In short

- Allah may grant good outcomes in this life and the lasting reward of the Hereafter.
- Worldly success is not a guarantee that every believer will avoid hardship.
- Ihsan means sincere worship and excellent conduct, seeking Allah’s love.
