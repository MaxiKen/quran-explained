<<<VERSE 2:248>>>
Talut's appointment receives a visible confirmation: "The sign of Saul’s kingship is that the Ark will come to you". The revered chest taken from the Israelites would return, carrying reassurance and relics of earlier prophets. Its restoration confirms the prophet's announcement that Allah, not family prestige or wealth, had chosen Talut (Saul).

### Phrase by phrase

**"Their prophet further told them, “The sign of Saul’s kingship is that the Ark will come to you"** The people had asked for a sign. At-tabut means the chest or Ark, an inherited possession associated with their earlier religious life. Its return was beyond the ordinary resources of their newly appointed king.

**"—containing reassurance from your Lord"** Sakinah means tranquillity and reassurance. Qatadah explains it as grace, while Ar-Rabi' and Ibn 'Abbas, through Al-'Awfi, give mercy. Another reported description pictures the wind of victory as golden and bearing a human face. That description remains a reported interpretation alongside the simpler explanation of inward peace.

**"and relics of the family of Moses and the family of Aaron,"** The contents were connected with Musa (Moses) and Harun (Aaron). The descriptions include Musa's staff, tablets or their fragments, sandals, a measure or pot of manna, and Harun's cloak and turban. The accounts differ over the list rather than supplying one single inventory.

**"which will be carried by the angels."** One account describes angels visibly bearing the chest between earth and sky. Another describes their directing the animals transporting it. In either telling, its return is not attributed simply to Talut's political skill.

**"Surely in this is a sign for you, if you ˹truly˺ believe.”"** The event confirms the prophet's truthfulness and the command to accept Talut. It benefits people who recognise Allah's action and the final accountability to Him. A sign is not given merely to satisfy curiosity without obedience.

### What had the Ark meant to the Israelites?

One account says it had been with the Bani Isra'il, the Children of Israel, since their departure from Egypt. It contained the Ten Commandments on stone and relics of Musa and Harun. It served as a visible symbol of Allah's gracious care, guiding their journey and accompanying victories.

Another account traces the chest back to Adam and describes images of prophets within it, handed down until enemies seized it in battle. These are differing transmitted descriptions of its history. Its barakah, blessing, and the reassurance associated with it are the common emphasis.

Musa and later prophets are described as placing it at the front of the army. Losing it therefore intensified the people's sense of defeat. Jalut (Goliath) is named in one account of its capture. the Amalekites and Philistines appear in other descriptions of the opposing people. The return mattered as a restoration of a sacred inheritance, not merely recovery of a useful wooden box.

### How was it returned?

The account of its enemies describes calamity following the chest wherever they carried it. Five cities became desolate. Alarmed, they put it on a cart drawn by two bullocks and sent it away. Angels directed the animals until it reached Talut's doorway. The people then accepted his kingship, and the account says he moved against Jalut in very hot weather.

Another telling gives two cows drawing a cart without a driver to Bayt ash-Shamsh in Judah. The Israelites rejoiced at its extraordinary return. This version describes plague and pestilence in the place where the Philistines had kept it.

Ibn 'Abbas gives a further account, through Ibn Jurayj, of angels lowering it from between heaven and earth while people watched, then placing it before Talut. As-Suddi says it reached Talut's house and confirmed the prophethood of Sham'un (Simeon) as well as the duty to obey the king. The differing accounts of transport and location are retained without inventing a route that reconciles every detail.

One telling adds that the people hurried to enlist and that Talut selected seventy thousand young men. That is a reported stage of mobilisation, not the number of the final faithful group at later tests.

### What relics are specifically mentioned?

Ibn 'Abbas, as related by Ibn Jarir, names Musa's staff and tablet fragments. Qatadah, As-Suddi and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas give that explanation, while 'Ikrimah adds the Torah. 'Abdur-Razzaq asked Ath-Thawri, who reported alternatives: manna and tablet fragments, or the staff and two shoes. The discussion connects the shoes with (20:12).

Another description lists a qafiz, a measure, of manna, broken tablets, sandals, the staff and Harun's turban. The family wording connects the relics with what the prophets and their people left behind. The lesson is continuity with their sacred past rather than certainty about every object named in differing reports.

### How is the chest contrasted with tranquillity in the heart?

An inward reading takes the heart as the dwelling place of tranquillity. It connects this with (48:4), where Allah sends tranquillity into believing hearts, and (8:24), where He comes between a person and the heart. A saying describes hearts as between two fingers of the All-Merciful.

The contrast is between peace associated with a movable chest and peace held by Allah within the heart. The chest could be moved by human hands. The inward gift cannot simply be controlled by people or reached by angels. Its security rests with the Lord, not with possession of an object.

Shibli describes an unveiling of unseen realities in which the Fire appeared like a roaring dragon or ravenous lion. He offered his outward form to its burning without fear because another inward burning occupied him. When the Fire sought the heart, he was warned that the heart was not his to dispose of.

The pir, spiritual elder, of the tariqah, the spiritual path, contrasts fire burning the body with love burning the spirit. The accompanying poetry urges acceptance whether the Beloved burns or comforts. These are allusive images of surrendered love and the heart's belonging to Allah, not a replacement for the historical sign announced to the Israelites.

### In short

- The Ark's return confirmed that Allah had appointed Talut despite objections about wealth and descent.
- It brought reassurance and relics connecting the people with earlier prophets.
- Accounts differ about its contents and transport, while agreeing on its extraordinary restoration.
- The inward reflection contrasts possession of the chest with tranquillity that Allah places directly in the heart.
