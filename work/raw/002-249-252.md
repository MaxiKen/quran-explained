<<<VERSE 2:249>>>
Talut's army is tested before it faces its enemy: "Allah will test you with a river." Most cannot restrain themselves at the water. The faithful remnant then faces another test, the apparent mismatch between its size and the enemy's strength. Those certain of meeting Allah remind the others that victory depends on His permission, not simply on numbers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When Saul marched forth with his army, he cautioned: “Allah will test you with a river."** Talut (Saul) led the Children of Israel after his appointment was confirmed. In intense heat, the army became thirsty. One account describes departure from Jerusalem through a wasteland. The river is identified as the Jordan, between Jordan and Palestine, also called the Shari'ah river in the report from Ibn 'Abbas.

**"So whoever drinks ˹his fill˺ from it is not with me, and whoever does not taste it—except a sip from the hollow of his hands—is definitely with me.”"** Drinking without restraint meant exclusion from the force continuing to battle. Complete abstinence, or the specifically permitted handful, showed obedience. Ghurfa and gharfa are readings of the word for a scoop. The test concerned response to an instruction when a desired thing was immediately available.

**"They all drank ˹their fill˺ except for a few!"** Most failed. Ibn 'Abbas, as related through Ibn Jurayj, says the handful quenched the obedient person's thirst, while those drinking freely remained thirsty. Another account says the small portion sufficed for the person and his horse. The reported effect reveals the failure of trying to obtain security through excess.

**"When he and the ˹remaining˺ faithful with him crossed the river, they said, “Now we are no match for Goliath and his warriors.”"** Jalut (Goliath) had a much larger force. Some who passed the first test still worried at their small numbers. Another explanation assigns the frightened statement to those who had drunk excessively and did not cross. These readings differ over the precise speakers while preserving the contrast between fear and firm trust.

**"But those ˹believers˺ who were certain they would meet Allah reasoned, “How many times has a small force vanquished a mighty army by the Will of Allah!"** Certainty of the Resurrection gave a different measure of the situation. The people of knowledge among them strengthened resolve by recalling that the outcome rested with Allah. The wording about how many emphasises that this was not an unheard-of possibility.

**"And Allah is ˹always˺ with the steadfast.”"** As-sabirin are the patient and persevering. Allah's being with them here means assistance, strength and support. Their small number does not exclude His help.

### How many remained, and why was the test needed?

As-Suddi gives the original army as eighty thousand, with the reservation that Allah knows best. Other accounts of mobilisation have differing numbers. One explanation gives the obedient few here as 313. Al-Bara' bin 'Azib says the Companions compared those who crossed with Talut to the believers at Badr, more than three hundred and ten. Ibn Jarir records the report, and Al-Bukhari also transmits it. Only believers crossed in that account.

The army's initial excitement could conceal weakness. In a hard encounter, panic spreads and can infect people who might otherwise stand firm. Resisting the river while thirsty tested the endurance needed later. The discussion presents this as a perceived wisdom of the trial, not as another explicit statement within the verse.

Ibn Abi Haim's report from Ibn 'Abbas, cited in Ruh al-Ma'ani, describes excessive drinkers becoming more incapacitated. The trial therefore revealed rather than repaired their lack of discipline. Its strange physical result is retained as a reported account, not expanded into an invented explanation.

Three degrees of response are distinguished. Some failed when tested. Others passed but worried about the enemy's numerical superiority. The most firmly resolved were not shaken by that disparity. Obedience to one difficult instruction does not necessarily remove every later fear.

### What historical setting is supplied?

One account places the Philistine attack about three hundred years after Musa (Moses) and roughly a thousand years before Christ. Much of Palestine was annexed. Samuel, based in Rama in Syria, managed the community's affairs. A delegation asked the ageing prophet to appoint a king, and he appointed the brave young Talut from Benjamin.

The account links the journey, the river and the eventual victory. Dawud (David), a young soldier with full trust in Allah, later played the decisive role. His killing of the enemy commander belongs to the following outcome, rather than changing the river test into a test of physical strength alone.

### How is the river read inwardly?

The allusion treats association with the world, other people and the lower self as another trial of limits. Some take what is needed for livelihood, safety and salvation. Others go beyond need to satisfy desire and preference. The handful pictures restraint, not a prohibition of every necessary worldly activity.

Doing what is forbidden has no place in obedience. Holding to excess is also criticised within the tariqa, the spiritual path. The faithful few are pictured as small in number but great in worth. Worth is not settled by the size of the crowd taking the easier course.

Those frightened by the external appearance need their hearts fortified. Success is not theirs independently, but comes through Allah's will and help. The spiritual application therefore returns to the verse's central instruction: restraint and trust must survive the pressure of what the eye sees.

### In short

- The river tested obedience and endurance before battle.
- A permitted handful differed from unrestricted drinking, and most failed the instruction.
- Passing one trial did not remove every later fear, but certainty of meeting Allah strengthened the steadfast.
- Allah's assistance, not numbers alone, determines the outcome.

<<<VERSE 2:250>>>
At the moment of encounter, the faithful remnant asks for the qualities it needs: "Our Lord! Shower us with perseverance, make our steps firm, and give us victory over the disbelieving people." Patience comes before victory in their prayer. They seek not merely a favourable result, but steadiness in meeting the duty before them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When they advanced to face Goliath and his warriors, they prayed,"** Talut's force faced Jalut (Goliath) and his troops in battle formation. The smaller believing group did not rely solely on its own strength. It turned to Allah for aid.

**"Our Lord! Shower us with perseverance,"** The image is of patience poured generously upon them. They ask for endurance in the heart to meet suffering without losing obedience. It is not only an appeal for the battle to end quickly.

**"make our steps firm,"** Firmness includes courage and a refusal to flee through weakness. The prayer asks Allah to strengthen hearts and bodies in the encounter. Their feet are to remain steady because their resolve remains steady.

**"and give us victory over the disbelieving people.”"** They ask for Allah's assistance against Jalut's people. The request comes after patience and steadfastness, keeping the hoped-for outcome joined to the qualities required for faithful action.

### Why does the order of the prayer matter?

The inward explanation says they disclaim independent power and seek Allah's support. Their earlier sufferings make patience an immediate need. They place the right of Allah, haqq, before their own share of the outcome: fulfil the duty first, then ask for victory.

This also distinguishes their purpose from revenge for homes and property taken away. The reflection understands their prayer as opposing enemies of Allah for His sake, rather than simply settling a private grievance. They stand for Allah through His help, not through confidence that they can control the result themselves.

The trial at the river had prepared that conduct. Endurance and obedience to leadership were necessary before the forces met. One historical comparison gives a biblical account of six hundred men who refrained from drinking. That figure differs from the earlier reports of 313 or a number above three hundred and ten and is not presented as the same report with an altered numeral.

The accompanying biblical words say that the Lord does not save through sword and spear alone, since the battle is His. The reference is 1 Samuel 17:47, not a Qur'anic citation. Its use reinforces the emphasis on Allah's help without denying the actual weapons and effort of the encounter.

### What does the prayer ask the believer to value?

The group seeks a steadfast heart before it seeks an enemy's defeat. Losing patience and obedience would already be a failure even if worldly advantage seemed possible. Their prayer therefore asks for inward and outward support together.

The later result in the following verse comes by Allah's permission, with Dawud's act playing its part. The wider principle recalled in (22:40) concerns Allah checking harmful power through others. It does not replace this particular prayer with an abstract claim that no human effort is needed.

### In short

- The faithful ask Allah for patience, firm resolve and victory in that order.
- Steadfastness concerns heart and conduct, not only standing in a physical position.
- The inward reading places obedience to Allah before the group's personal share of success.

<<<VERSE 2:251>>>
The prayer is followed by victory: "So they defeated them by Allah’s Will, and David killed Goliath." Dawud (David) becomes the decisive human instrument, while the result is attributed to Allah. The verse then widens the lesson: checking one group's harmful power through another is a mercy that prevents corruption from overwhelming the earth.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So they defeated them by Allah’s Will, and David killed Goliath."** Talut's force overcame Jalut (Goliath) and his army through Allah's support. Dawud was among the soldiers. The victory was not explained as the inevitable triumph of the larger body or force.

**"And Allah blessed David with kingship and wisdom and taught him what He willed."** Dawud later received the kingdom and al-hikmah, wisdom understood here as prophethood and understanding. The account places this after Talut and Shamwil. His teaching included useful knowledge for this world and the next, specifically armour-making and the speech of birds in the explanations given.

**"Had Allah not repelled a group of people by ˹the might of˺ another, corruption would have dominated the earth,"** Unchecked harmful power would destroy the weak. Allah restrains some people through others. The example is protection of the Children of Israel through Talut and Dawud against Jalut. Other explanations extend the principle to prophets protecting believers and fighters protecting those who did not participate.

**"but Allah is Gracious to all"** His bounty reaches the worlds, explained as humanity, jinn and the whole creation. The restraint of wrongdoing is part of that mercy. His wisdom governs the different means through which it happens.

### How did Dawud overcome Jalut?

Israelite accounts describe a sling whose stone killed the enemy commander. They portray Dawud as compact and of middling stature, with no weapon besides the sling. Jalut's size and drawn sword made such a victory seem unlikely. The contrast is used to emphasise help from Allah rather than physical bulk alone.

Talut's imposing body had helped intimidate the enemy, yet the decisive act came through another person. The inward lesson prevents honouring one outward means so exclusively that Allah's ability to act through another is forgotten.

Talut had promised his daughter in marriage and a share in royal authority to the person who killed Jalut. He fulfilled the promise. Later the kingship passed to Dawud, with prophethood also granted to him. One explanation calls this combination unprecedented before Dawud. These are the reported details accompanying the verse, not an added independent history.

### Why is the checking of power called mercy?

If the strong all joined together in oppression, the weak would be destroyed. Keeping some occupied by others limits their ability to harm everyone else. The allusion recognises restraint even where the agents themselves are not acting from identical intentions.

The related passage in (22:40) speaks of places of worship being destroyed if Allah did not repel people through others. Here the explanations mention the danger of idolaters overwhelming and killing believers and destroying mosques. The protection extends beyond one successful battle to the continued possibility of worship and communal life.

The verse does not portray every conflict as good in itself. Its stated good is preventing corruption through restraint. Human bravery has a place, but the preserving arrangement and its wisdom belong to Allah.

### In short

- Dawud killed Jalut, while the victory is expressly attributed to Allah's will.
- Dawud later received kingship, prophethood and useful knowledge.
- Allah's checking harmful power through others protects the weak and restrains corruption.

<<<VERSE 2:252>>>
Verse 252 closes the account by affirming its revealed character: "These are Allah’s revelations which We recite to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth." Muhammad ﷺ did not invent the past narrative or learn it by witnessing those events. Its truthful disclosure is presented as confirmation that he is one of Allah's messengers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"These are Allah’s revelations which We recite to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth."** The verses contain true accounts and just laws. Gabriel brings the revelation. The story of Talut, Jalut and Dawud (David) is presented as corresponding to what happened and to the truth remaining in earlier divine books known among the Children of Israel.

**"And you are truly one of the messengers"** The emphatic wording answers the denial that Muhammad ﷺ is a messenger. The ability to convey these earlier events through revelation supports his prophethood. One explanation specifies his mission to human beings and jinn.

### What makes the account a confirmation?

The Prophet ﷺ had not seen these long-past people or devised the narrative. The discussion also says he had not learnt or heard the story from someone. Its disclosure therefore belongs to knowledge given by Allah, not a claim of ordinary personal observation.

The closing statement gathers the earlier signs, tests, prayer and victory into an affirmation of revelation. The wisdom granted to Dawud and the preserving principle recalled in (22:40) remain parts of the preceding account rather than needing to be told again here. Verse 253 continues the discussion of messengers, but this verse already makes the present affirmation complete.

### In short

- The narrative is presented as truthful revelation, not invention or eyewitness history by the Prophet ﷺ.
- Its disclosure confirms Muhammad ﷺ among Allah's messengers.
- The account includes guidance to understand and act upon, not merely information about the past.
