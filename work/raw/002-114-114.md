<<<VERSE 2:114>>>
Verse 114 turns from the quarrels of the People of the Book to a wrong that idolaters and others had committed against the houses of Allah. "Who does more wrong than those who prevent Allah’s Name from being mentioned in His places of worship and strive to destroy them? Such people have no right to enter these places except with fear. For them is disgrace in this world, and they will suffer a tremendous punishment in the Hereafter". It is at once a condemnation, a prophecy and the basis of several rulings about mosques.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Who does more wrong than those who prevent Allah’s Name from being mentioned in His places of worship"**. No one does greater wrong. The places of worship are where Allah's Name is invoked in prayer and praise, and barring them means stopping the prayer, the remembrance of Allah and the recitation of the Qur'an within them. Ibn Jarīr reported from Ibn Zayd that the verse concerns the idolaters of Quraysh, who stopped the Prophet ﷺ entering Makkah from al-Ḥudaybiyyah, so that he slaughtered his sacrificial animals at Dhū Ṭuwā. He then made a peace treaty with them and said to them that no one before had ever barred people from the House: a man might see the killer of his own father or brother there and still not keep him from the House of Allah. They answered that whoever had killed their fathers at Badr would never enter it while one of them was alive. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās that the verse was revealed when Quraysh stopped the Prophet ﷺ praying at the Kaʿbah inside al-Masjid al-Ḥarām. Having rebuked the Jews and the Christians, Allah now rebukes the idolaters who drove the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his Companions out of Makkah and kept them from praying in the Sacred Mosque, which they reserved for their idols and their polytheism. Allah said of them: "And why should Allah not punish them while they hinder pilgrims from the Sacred Mosque, claiming to be its rightful guardians? None has the right to guardianship except those mindful ˹of Allah˺, but most pagans do not know" (8:34), its rightful guardians being the God-fearing (al-muttaqūn), and "It is not for the polytheists to maintain the mosques of Allah while they openly profess disbelief. Their deeds are void, and they will be in the Fire forever" (9:17), and "They are the ones who persisted in disbelief and hindered you from the Sacred Mosque, preventing the sacrificial animals from reaching their destination" (48:25).

A second view relates the verse to earlier history. It was revealed, some say, to tell of the Byzantines' destruction of the Holy House in Jerusalem. One early gloss names Ṭīṭūs son of Asipanos the Roman (ar-Rūmī), king of the Christians, who destroyed Jerusalem, barred Allah's sanctuary there to the declaration of His oneness and the call to prayer, and strove to ruin it by having carrion thrown into it, a state of affairs that lasted until the reign of ʿUmar. On this reading "such people" are the Romans, who could enter Jerusalem only in hiding from the believers for fear of being killed, their worldly disgrace being the ruin of their cities, Constantinople, Amorium and Rome, and their punishment in the Hereafter harsher than anything they suffered here. A modern reflection widens the charge to every community of the time. All of them, though sworn to the divine scriptures, had split into sects, and each seized every chance to deny others the right to enter places of worship, claiming to be protecting their sanctity. In truth they were ruining them, for a place of worship falls derelict when people are refused the right to worship there. A worshipper with the right attitude never denies that right to those who come to pray and never persecutes those who come to serve the Lord. Whoever is truly conscious of God's greatness and his own helplessness before Him shows his humility in his dealings with others, and will not harm his fellow men in any way, let alone deny them the basic right to worship God.

**"and strive to destroy them?"**. Through demolition, and through keeping people away. The idolaters were preventing those who kept the mosques alive with Allah's remembrance and who came to His House for ḥajj and ʿumrah. Allah said: "The mosques of Allah should only be maintained by those who believe in Allah and the Last Day, establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and fear none but Allah. It is right to hope that they will be among the ˹truly˺ guided" (9:18). If believers of this description are barred from the mosque, what ruin could be worse? Maintaining mosques does not mean beautifying them. It means remembering Allah in them, establishing His Sharīʿah in them, and purifying them of the filth of shirk. If the verse was revealed at the Peace of al-Ḥudaybiyyah, when the idolaters of Makkah barred the Muslims from the Sacred Mosque, then it is obvious that laying a mosque waste does not only mean pulling it down. It also means not letting it be used for the purpose it was built for, the prayer (ṣalāh) and the remembrance (dhikr) of Allah.

**"Such people have no right to enter these places except with fear"**. The phrase is a statement that also works as a command: frighten them with the threat of war so that not one of them enters feeling secure. Do not let the disbelievers into the mosques except under the terms of a truce or treaty. When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had conquered Makkah, he had it proclaimed at Minā in the ninth year that after that year no idolater would perform ḥajj and no naked person would circle the House in ṭawāf, except that whoever had a treaty would be allowed to see it out to its term. This agrees with the verse: "O believers! Indeed, the polytheists are ˹spiritually˺ impure, so they should not approach the Sacred Mosque after this year" (9:28). Those who tried to destroy Allah's houses should enter them only fearfully, with trembling hearts, after their disbelief and their obstruction of worship. It has also been said that the verse brings the Muslims good news from Allah that He will give them al-Masjid al-Ḥarām and all the mosques and disgrace the idolaters, so that soon no idolater would enter the House except in fear of being seized or killed, unless he accepted Islam. Allah fulfilled the promise and later decreed that idolaters be kept out of the Sacred Mosque. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ declared that two religions should not remain in the Arabian Peninsula. He also directed the expulsion of the Jews and Christians. These rulings are presented as protecting the Sacred Mosque's honour and purifying the land of his mission. Allah sent him there to warn and bring good news to all mankind.

**"For them is disgrace in this world"**. Debasement through being killed, taken captive and made to pay the jizyah, shame and humiliation on earth at the hands of the believers. The punishment matched the deed. As they had kept the believers out of the Sacred Mosque, they were kept out of it in turn, and as they had expelled the believers from Makkah, they were expelled from Makkah themselves.

**"and they will suffer a tremendous punishment in the Hereafter"**. The Fire, because they violated the sanctity of the House and defiled it, setting up idols around it, calling on others besides Allah and circling it naked. Here belongs the ḥadīth on seeking refuge from disgrace in this world and torment in the next. Imām Aḥmad recorded from Busr ibn Arṭāh that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to pray: "O Allah, make the end of all our affairs good, and save us from disgrace in this world and the punishment of the Hereafter." This ḥadīth is ḥasan.

### Rulings drawn from the verse

The verse lays down, or allows us to infer, several important rulings. First, all the mosques in the world deserve respect alike. Just as it is a grave sin to desecrate the Bayt al-Maqdis, the mosque around the Kaʿbah or the mosque of the Prophet ﷺ in any way, the same prohibition protects every other mosque. The three do enjoy a higher rank and a special reverence. One prayer in al-Masjid al-Ḥarām equals a hundred thousand prayers elsewhere, and a prayer in the Prophet's ﷺ mosque or in the Bayt al-Maqdis equals fifty thousand. Travelling far to pray in one of these three is a meritorious act that earns a special blessing (barakah), whereas the Prophet ﷺ forbade setting out on a long journey to pray in any other mosque in the belief that doing so is meritorious.

Second, it is forbidden to hinder people in any way from praying or remembering Allah in a mosque. The obvious form is to refuse someone entry, or to stop him praying or reading the Qur'an there. A subtler form is to make noise inside the mosque or play music nearby, disturbing people at prayer or in remembrance. Likewise, reciting the Qur'an or making dhikr aloud so as to disturb those offering voluntary prayers (nawāfil), reading the Qur'an or remembering Allah silently is the same sin, and the jurists (fuqahāʾ) have forbidden it, though when no one else is present one may recite or make dhikr aloud. By the same principle it is forbidden to beg or collect donations, even for a religious purpose, while people are busy praying or making dhikr.

Third, every form of laying a mosque waste is forbidden, not only demolishing it but also creating conditions that leave it deserted, for a mosque is laid waste when few come to pray in it. A mosque flourishes not through the beauty of its architecture or decoration but only when it is full of people who come to pray and remember Allah, as 9:18 indicates. The Prophet ﷺ foretold that as the Day of Judgement draws near the mosques of the Muslims will be beautifully built and decorated and apparently full, yet in reality deserted, because few will go there to pray. ʿAlī, the fourth Caliph, said that six things befit a man, three at home and three on a journey. At home: to read the Qur'an, to populate the mosques, and to gather friends who wish to serve Allah and His religion. On a journey: to spend on needy companions of the road, to be courteous to everyone, and to be cheerful with fellow travellers within the limits the Sharīʿah allows. By "populating" the mosques he meant entering them humbly with the fear of Allah in one's heart and then busying oneself with prayer, recitation or dhikr. The opposite, laying them waste, is when few pray in them or when circumstances are allowed to arise that make it hard for those present to attain the proper humility.

### A note on the verse that follows

The verse that comes next (2:115) says that wherever one turns there is the Face of Allah. Since Allah is not confined to any direction or place, the Muslims who face the Kaʿbah do not worship it. The orientation was fixed for other reasons. For sixteen or seventeen months after the Hijrah the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions were commanded to face the Bayt al-Maqdis in prayer, a practical demonstration that Allah can be found in every direction and that His attention embraces all directions and dimensions at once. The ruling on voluntary prayers (nawāfil) gives permanent expression to the same truth. One who wishes to pray them while riding a horse or a camel need not face the qiblah at all. He may face the direction in which his mount is moving and pray by gestures of the head and arms, and some scholars hold that 2:115 lays down exactly this rule. The concession applies only to travel on animals, where turning to the qiblah is difficult. In a train, ship or aeroplane, facing the qiblah is not difficult. The proper orientation must therefore be adopted even for voluntary prayers. If the train or aeroplane changes direction and there is no room to adjust, the prayer may be completed as it stands. Similarly, if a person cannot tell the direction of the qiblah, because of the darkness of night or some other valid reason, and can find no one to inform him, he may follow his best estimate and face the direction that seems most likely, and that direction serves as his qiblah. If after the prayer he discovers he was wrong, the prayer remains valid and need not be repeated.

### The three homelands

The devotional reading asks, by way of allusion: who does greater wrong than one who ruins the homeland of worship with appetites, the homeland of recognition with attachments, and the homeland of contemplation by glancing at others? The first homeland, that of worship, is the renunciant's soul. The second, that of recognition, is the heart of one who knows God. The third, that of contemplation, is the secret core of His friends. Whoever keeps his soul from appetites makes the homeland of his worship flourish, and his name is entered in the register of the renunciants. Mālik ibn Dīnār lived forty years in Basra and died without ever eating or tasting its dates, fresh or dried, and when asked about it said that the possessor of appetite is veiled from his Lord. Whoever keeps his heart clear of attachments makes the homeland of his recognition flourish and joins the ranks of the recognisers. A companion of Ibrāhīm ibn Adham told how, on a journey, hunger overtook them. After they had settled in a mosque Ibrāhīm brought out some sections of the Qur'an he carried and told him to go and pawn them for something to eat. On his way out he was met by a man leading a fully laden mule and asking for a fair-skinned man named Ibrāhīm ibn Adham. Asked what he wanted, the man said he was the slave of Ibrāhīm's father and that the goods belonged to Ibrāhīm. Brought into the mosque, he placed his head and hands on the ground and kissed Ibrāhīm. He announced the death of Ibrāhīm's father. He had brought forty thousand dinars as the inheritance. He added, "I am your slave, command me as you wish." Ibrāhīm replied: "If you are telling the truth, then you are free for the sake of God, and everything you have with you is a gift to you. Leave us." The man who had just sent his Qur'an to be pawned for bread gave away a fortune on the spot rather than let an attachment settle in his heart, and the mosque in which he sat remained, in the allusive sense, a flourishing house of God.

### In short

- The verse condemns those who bar Allah's houses to His remembrance and work to ruin them, above all the Quraysh who kept the Prophet ﷺ from the Sacred Mosque at al-Ḥudaybiyyah (8:34, 9:17, 48:25), and by another view the Romans who devastated Jerusalem.
- Ruining a mosque includes keeping it from its purpose, and only believers who pray, pay zakāh and fear Allah truly maintain it (9:18).
- The idolaters were to enter only in fear, and after the conquest were excluded from the Sacred Mosque altogether (9:28). Their disgrace mirrored their deed, and a tremendous punishment awaits them in the Hereafter.
- All mosques are inviolable, though the three great mosques rank highest. Hindering worship, even by noise or collecting money during prayer, is forbidden, and a mosque lives by its worshippers, not its decoration.
- In allusion, appetites, attachments and glancing at others lay waste the inner sanctuaries of worship, recognition and contemplation, as the examples of Mālik ibn Dīnār and Ibrāhīm ibn Adham show.
