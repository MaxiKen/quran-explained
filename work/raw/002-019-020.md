<<<VERSE 2:19>>>
A second picture follows the fire. "Or ˹those caught in˺ a rainstorm from the sky with darkness, thunder, and lightning. They press their fingers into their ears at the sound of every thunder-clap for fear of death. And Allah encompasses the disbelievers ˹by His might˺". The first simile was fire. This one is water: travellers caught at night in a downpour, with darkness around them, thunder overhead and lightning flashing. It describes the hypocrites who sometimes know the truth and at other times doubt it, the ones who recognised Islam and even wished at times to be real Muslims, but whose worldly interests would not let them, so that they lived in a permanent state of hesitation and doubt.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Or ˹those caught in˺ a rainstorm from the sky"** means that their likeness is that of people out in heavy rain. The Arabic ṣayyib is rain, as Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās and several other Companions confirmed. Among the next generations the same was said by Qatādah, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Mujāhid, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, as-Suddī, ʿAṭāʾ, ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī and ʿAṭiyah al-ʿAwfī. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk said it is the clouds, but the accepted view is the rain. The word comes from a verb meaning "to come down", and "from the sky" means from the clouds. The rain stands for the Qur'an, which came down from Allah, pouring at night onto open country.

**"with darkness, thunder, and lightning"** describes the storm: darkness layer upon layer, thunder, and lightning. In the parable, the darkness is the hypocrites' doubts, disbelief and hypocrisy, the thunder is what shocks their hearts with fear, and the lightning is the light of faith that sometimes glimmers in them. Read as a picture of the Qur'an, the darkness is its account of disbelief and of trials, the thunder is its rebukes and threats of punishment, and the lightning is its clear arguments, its exposition, instruction and promise. One opinion holds that the thunder is the angel in charge of the clouds, or the sound of his voice, and the lightning the flash of that voice as he drives the clouds along.

**"They press their fingers into their ears at the sound of every thunder-clap for fear of death"** means that people caught in such a storm stop their ears with their fingertips against the violent crash of thunder, afraid that hearing it might kill them. So it was with the Jews and the hypocrites when the Qur'an was recited with its promises and threats falling like thunder-claps. They stopped their ears so as not to hear it and be drawn toward faith and away from their own religion, which to them would have been death. The hypocrites are generally full of fear and anxiety, as Allah described them: "They think every cry is against them" (63:4), and "They swear by Allah that they are part of you, but they are not. They only say so out of fear. If only they could find a refuge, or a cave, or any hiding-place, they would rush headlong towards it" (9:56-57).

**"And Allah encompasses the disbelievers ˹by His might˺"** means that their caution does them no good, because they are held within Allah's all-embracing will and decree. He encompasses them in knowledge and in power, so they cannot escape Him. He knows them and will gather them in Hell. The same was said of earlier tyrants: "Has the story of the ˹destroyed˺ forces reached you ˹O Prophet˺—" of Firʿawn (Pharaoh) and Thamūd, "But Allah encompasses them from all sides" (85:17-20).

### Fear as a barrier

Fear is another thing that keeps people from acknowledging the truth. Rain is a wonderful blessing, but thunder and lightning come with it and terrify the faint-hearted. The word of Allah revealed to His prophets works on them in the same way. It holds out the promise of great success, and at the same time accepting it puts a person temporarily at risk: the fear of losing one's worldly position, of clashing with the established order. Fear of these things sometimes stops a person dead and sometimes makes him move forward hesitantly. Such caution cannot help him. Those who do not answer Allah's call open-heartedly are deprived of His blessings in this world and the next. The hypocrites resemble the people in both parables because they get nothing of value from either: from the fire only darkness and smouldering embers, from the storm-cloud only fear and alarm at the thunder and lightning. In the same way they saw nothing in Islam but difficulty and harshness.

### The inner reading

The "or" means that either of the two similitudes may be applied to them. The Qur'an is compared to rain falling from the sky, and its promises and threats to the thunder and lightning in the rain. Their taking refuge, their flight from hearing the Qur'an, their avoidance of attention and their refusal to seek insight, is compared to people stopping their ears against the thunder. The allusion extends to the heedless, whenever a warner's admonition reaches their ears or a light of felicity falls on their hearts. If they would stop their blind heedlessness they would be happy. Instead they keep one another distracted with false hopes and are set on their corrupt road. They make weak excuses, "And they will swear by Allah, “Had we been able, we would have certainly joined you.” They are ruining themselves" (9:42), and bring about their ruin with their own hands. When a noble person gives you his love he veils what is ugly and brings out what is beautiful. In the same way, when someone grows tired and wants to break things off he wearies of being together and says, "That was that."

### In short

- The hypocrites of this verse are not the hardened kind but the wavering kind, who see the truth in flashes and lose it again.
- The rain is the Qur'an, the darkness is doubt and disbelief, the thunder is its warnings and the lightning its light. Stopping the ears is refusing to listen for fear of where listening leads.
- Fear of worldly loss is a real barrier to faith, but the caution it produces is useless, because Allah encompasses the disbelievers on every side.

<<<VERSE 2:20>>>
The storm picture continues, and now the travellers move. "It is as if the lightning were about to snatch away their sight. Whenever lightning strikes, they walk in its light, but when darkness covers them, they stand still. Had Allah willed, He could have taken away their hearing and sight. Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything". A step or two in each flash, then a halt in the dark. That is the hesitant hypocrite, and the verse ends by reminding him that his remaining senses are his only by Allah's leave.

### Phrase by phrase

**"It is as if the lightning were about to snatch away their sight"** means that it all but takes their sight away in an instant, both because the lightning is so strong and because their understanding is so weak that it cannot bear the faith. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported Ibn ʿAbbās's comment that the Qur'an laid bare almost all the secrets of the hypocrites. In the allegorical reading, the exposition of the Qur'an all but snatched away their error.

**"Whenever lightning strikes, they walk in its light"** means that whenever it flashes they walk by it. Through ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah, Ibn ʿAbbās explained: whenever the hypocrites get a share in the victories of Islam they are content with it, and whenever Islam suffers a setback they are ready to go back to disbelief, as Allah said, "there are some who worship Allah on the verge ˹of faith˺: if they are blessed with something good, they are content with it; but if they are afflicted with a trial, they relapse ˹into disbelief˺, losing this world and the Hereafter" (22:11). Through Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq, Ibn ʿAbbās said that they recognise the truth and speak of it, so that their speech is upright, and then they go back to disbelief and fall into confusion again. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Qatādah, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and as-Suddī said the same, reporting it from the Companions, and it is the clearest and most correct view. Allah knows best. The simile pictures the turmoil the Qur'an's arguments stir in their hearts: they acknowledge the truths they like hearing and recoil from what they detest. In worldly terms, when the hypocrites declared themselves believers they went along with whatever the believers went along with, because their outward faith was accepted.

**"but when darkness covers them, they stand still"** means they stop and stay in the dark. When they die they remain in the darkness of the grave.

**"Had Allah willed, He could have taken away their hearing and sight"** means their outward hearing and sight, as He had already taken away their inner ones. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq reported Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation: because they abandoned the truth after knowing it. In the allegorical reading, He could take away the thunder and the lightning, that is, their hearing of the Qur'an's rebukes and threats and their sight of its exposition, and not give them back, since they turn from the truth.

**"Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything"** means that He is able to punish or pardon His servants as He wills, as Ibn ʿAbbās said. Ibn Jarīr commented that Allah describes Himself here as able to do everything in order to warn the hypocrites of His control over all things and to tell them that His power completely encompasses them and that He can take away their hearing and their sight. The parables thus carry a warning: the hypocrites are not beyond Allah's power, and He can remove their sight and hearing, or destroy them, whenever He wills.

### Light on the Day of Judgment

On the Day of Judgment the believers will be given light in proportion to their faith. Some will have light that shines for miles, some more and some less. Some people's light will glow at times and go out at times, and they will walk on the Ṣirāṭ, the bridge over the Fire, while it shines and stop when it goes out. Some will have no light at all, and these are the hypocrites: "On that Day hypocrite men and women will beg the believers, “Wait for us so that we may have some of your light.” It will be said ˹mockingly˺, “Go back ˹to the world˺ and seek a light ˹there˺!”" (57:13). Of the believers Allah said, "On that Day you will see believing men and women with their light shining ahead of them and on their right" (57:12), and "on the Day Allah will not disgrace the Prophet or the believers with him. Their light will shine ahead of them and on their right. They will say, “Our Lord! Perfect our light for us”" (66:8). Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd's comment on the light running ahead of them: they will cross the Ṣirāṭ according to their deeds, some with a light the size of a mountain, some with a light the size of a date palm, and the people with the least light will have it on their index fingers, lit one moment and out the next. Ibn Abī Ḥātim also recorded Ibn ʿAbbās saying that every person of tawḥīd will receive a light on the Day of Resurrection, and the hypocrite's light will go out, and when the believers see that they will be anxious and pray, "Our Lord! Perfect our light for us." Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk ibn Muzāḥim said the same: everyone who embraced faith will be given a light, and when they reach the Ṣirāṭ the hypocrites' light will be extinguished, and the believers, seeing it, will make that prayer.

### The kinds of people the surah has described

The opening of the surah has now sorted people into kinds. There are the believers of verses 2 to 5. There are the disbelievers of the two verses after them. And there are two categories of hypocrite: the complete hypocrites of the fire parable, and the hesitant hypocrites, in whom the light of faith flickers on and off, for whom the rain parable was revealed and who are less evil than the first. This resembles the parables of Sūrat an-Nūr, where the believer and the faith Allah placed in his heart are likened to a brightly lit lamp like a rising star, a heart built on faith and nourished by the revealed law without impurity or flaw. Then the disbelievers who think they have something when they have nothing, the people of compound ignorance, are given their parable: "As for the disbelievers, their deeds are like a mirage in a desert, which the thirsty perceive as water" (24:39). Then the simple, ignorant disbelievers: "Or ˹their deeds are˺ like the darkness in a deep sea, covered by waves upon waves, topped by ˹dark˺ clouds" (24:40). So Allah divided the disbelievers into leaders and followers, and He named both at the start of Sūrat al-Ḥajj, "there are some who dispute about Allah without knowledge, and follow every rebellious devil" (22:3), and "there are some who dispute about Allah without knowledge, guidance, or an enlightening scripture" (22:8). He divided the believers too, at the beginning and end of Sūrat al-Wāqiʿah and in Sūrat al-Insān, into the foremost, who are the near ones, and the companions of the right, who are the righteous.

In sum, the believers are of two kinds, the near ones and the righteous. The disbelievers are of two kinds, leaders and followers. And the hypocrites are of two kinds, the pure hypocrites and those with some hypocrisy in them. The two Ṣaḥīḥs record from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr that the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever has three traits is a pure hypocrite, and whoever has one of them has one trait of hypocrisy until he gives it up: when he speaks he lies, when he makes a pact he betrays it, and when he is trusted he breaks the trust. So a person may have a portion of faith and a portion of hypocrisy at once, in conduct, as this hadith states, or in belief, as this verse indicates.

Imām Aḥmad recorded from Abū Saʿīd that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said hearts are of four kinds: a polished heart shining like a radiant lamp, a sealed heart with a knot tied over its seal, a heart turned upside down, and a wrapped heart. The polished heart is the believer's, and its lamp is the light of faith. The sealed heart is the disbeliever's. The upside-down heart is the pure hypocrite's, because he knew and then denied. The wrapped heart holds both faith and hypocrisy. Faith in it is like a herb fed by pure water, and hypocrisy in it is like an ulcer fed by pus and blood, and whichever of the two gets the upper hand takes the heart. The chain of this hadith is good, jayyid ḥasan.

### One parable or two?

Ibn Jarīr and several other scholars held that both parables are about the same kind of hypocrite, so that the "or" of "Or ˹those caught in˺ a rainstorm" means "and", as in the verse telling the Prophet ﷺ to obey neither a sinner nor a disbeliever among them, and the "or" offers a choice of either example. Al-Qurṭubī said the "or" shows that the two choices are equivalent, as when one says, "Sit with al-Ḥasan or Ibn Sīrīn," which in az-Zamakhsharī's explanation means that each is as good as the other, so you may sit with either, and the "or" comes to mean "either". On that view Allah gave two examples because both describe the hypocrites perfectly. Another view, however, is that the two descriptions belong to two types of hypocrite, since there is a difference between them. Allah distinguished their types in Sūrat Barāʾah, chapter 9, by repeating "and among them" three times to describe their kinds, traits, sayings and deeds. So the two examples here describe two types whose characteristics are similar, just as Sūrat an-Nūr gives two examples of disbelief, the mirage for its leaders, who have compound ignorance, and the darkness of the deep sea for its followers, who have simple ignorance. Allah knows best.

### The inner reading

The similitude also fits the heedless. When they sit at public sermons, or their hearts soften, or some terror overwhelms them, their states move toward repentance and their longing to turn to Allah grows strong. Then they go back to their own planning and consult those close to them, and their family and children urge them back to their world, frightening them with weakness and dependence, until their resolve weakens and their spiritual desire declines. As the saying goes, whenever he repents he returns to his ignorance, as a feeble man relapses. "Had Allah willed, He could have taken away their hearing and sight" refers to the hypocrites' outward hearing and sight, in addition to their inner deafness and blindness. The same holds for the heedless and for those content with the outward side of Islam: Allah has the power to cancel the fruit of their outward acts of obedience, just as He has the power to cancel the realisation of whatever they have tasted of the most felicitous states.

### In short

- The wavering hypocrite walks when faith flashes and stops when it fades, content when Islam prospers and ready to turn back when it is tested.
- This hesitation has an exact counterpart on the Day of Judgment, when light is given by faith and the hypocrites' light goes out on the bridge.
- The surah has now described six kinds of people: two kinds of believer, two kinds of disbeliever and two kinds of hypocrite. A person may carry a part of faith and a part of hypocrisy at once.
- Whether the two parables describe one type or two, their last word is the same: Allah encompasses the hypocrites and could take their hearing and sight at any moment.
