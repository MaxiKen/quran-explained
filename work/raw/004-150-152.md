<<<VERSE 4:150>>>
The divided faith: "Surely those who deny Allah and His messengers and wish to make a distinction between Allah and His messengers, saying, “We believe in some and disbelieve in others,” desiring to forge a compromise" (4:150). The verse gives the verdict on the pickers and choosers among the believers of old.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who deny Allah and His messengers"** names the Jews and the Christians, who differentiate between Allah and His messengers regarding faith, believing in some prophets and rejecting others. The Jews believe in the prophets except ʿĪsā (Jesus) and Muḥammad ﷺ. The Christians accept the line of prophets yet reject its Final and Seal, the most honoured among them. The Samirah, the Samaritans, believe in no prophet after Yūshaʿ (Joshua), the successor of Mūsā (Moses) ibn ʿImrān. The Majūs are said to believe only in a prophet called Zoroaster, though they do not believe in the law he brought, casting it behind them. One gloss names Kaʿb and his followers for the words. The Makkans too belong in the pattern, for they believed in the prophethood of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) yet would not acknowledge the Prophet of their own time..

**"and wish to make a distinction between Allah and His messengers"** means in faith, in prophethood and in surrender to Allah. The division believes in Him but not in them.

**"saying, “We believe in some and disbelieve in others”"** exposes the method: what they say is a mask for what they choose. They follow their desires, lusts and the practices of their forefathers, for they have no proof for the distinction, and there is none. Whoever rejects a single prophet out of envy, bias and personal whim has disbelieved in all of them. Mankind is required to believe in every prophet Allah sent to the people of the earth. The rejection of one only demonstrates that the professed faith in the others is not valid, but an act of following desire and whim..

**"desiring to forge a compromise"** means seeking a way in between, a religion between disbelief and faith. All such attempts amount to a middle course between truth-worship and self-worship, so that Paradise is secured without any sacrifice of self-interest. The prophets of the past were all faced in their times with the same hostile reactions now faced by the Arab prophet. Yet the past prophethood, once time has done its work, is absorbed into the thinking of a person from birth, while the prophet of the day appears an ordinary human being and a controversial figure.. Believing in him requires a complete change of mindset, a new intellectual journey, a finding of Allah again at the level of awareness. Belief in a past prophet continues the forefathers' ways, a faith by birth and not by choice, and what matters in the eyes of Allah is the faith adopted by choice.

### Salvation depends on Islam

The verdict exposes the rudderless and crooked ways of those who, to appear tolerant in their dealings with other faiths, would present their religion and religious beliefs as gifts on a platter. They tell people of other faiths, contrary to the clear injunctions of the Qur'ān and Sunnah, that Muslims do not think salvation depends on Islam alone, that a Jew could keep his creed and a Christian hers and both look forward to salvation.. Islam stands unmatched in justice, fairness, sympathy, goodwill and tolerance toward non-Muslims. But favours are made within the limits of one's rights and possessions, and religious principles and articles of faith are not property to donate. Islam is generous in tolerance and equally firm in the defence of its frontiers, absolving itself decisively from all forms of disbelief while providing full protection to the national hallmarks of others. Had Islam believed in salvation through every religion, it would have had no right to stress the spreading of its message to the limit of staking property and life. The coming of the noble Prophet ﷺ with the glorious Qur'ān would be rendered redundant. The entire jihād carried out by him and the rightly-guided caliphs would become nothing but the lust for more land..

Some have fallen into doubt by misreading verse 62 of sūrat al-Baqarah, which says that those who believed, and those who were Jews and Christians and Sabeans, whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day and does good deeds, has a reward with the Lord.. That verse gives no full detail of what constitutes faith, and its incomplete study misled readers into thinking belief in the prophets no condition of salvation. The Qur'ān has its own terminology, in which belief in Allah, to be acceptable, must stand in conjunction with belief in the Prophet. The Satan himself concedes that a God exists and that He is One. The clarification is the Book's own: "So, if they believe in the like of what you believe in, they have certainly found the right path" (2:137). The faith is valid only as the faith of the Muslims, the belief in the Prophet indispensably joined to the belief in Allah. Whoever turns away is in antagonism, with Allah sufficient against them. The verse under discussion has already declared that whoever turns against a single one of Allah's prophets is an avowed disbeliever, a kāfir, and a recipient of the punishment of Hell.

The inward reading hears the watch behind the sentence. To the shamefulness of their disbelief they added the blameworthy actions enumerated here. The chastisement is doubled as requital for the sin, "that you will know that He is ever on the watch" (89:14) over those who act in corrupt and harmful ways..

### In short

- Jews, Christians, Samaritans and Majūs each hold a list of prophets and cut it short, and the rejection of one prophet is the disbelief of them all.
- The compromise sought is a way between faith and disbelief, securing Paradise without the sacrifice of self-interest.
- Salvation depends on Islam, and the faith of the forefathers by birth does not equal the faith of choice.
- Verse 62 of al-Baqarah speaks in the Book's own terminology, where belief in Allah includes the Prophet, and the doubters were answered by 2:137.

<<<VERSE 4:151>>>
The verdict: "they are indeed the true disbelievers. And We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment" (4:151). The verse closes the case of the divided faith with its name and its sentence.

### Phrase by phrase

**"they are indeed the true disbelievers"** means that their disbelief in the very prophet they claim to believe in is clear, for the claimed faith in a certain messenger is not true. Had they truly believed in him, they would have believed in the other messengers, especially when the other messenger has a stronger proof of his truthfulness, or at least they would have striven hard to acquire knowledge of his truth. Disbelieving in some of the messengers is like disbelief in Allah and all His messengers, and the verdict names the whole company, the Jews and the other disbelievers, each an avowed kāfir.

**"And We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment"** is the just requital for belittling the prophets they disbelieved in, ignoring what the prophets brought to them from Allah, out of interest in the insignificant possessions of this world. Or their behaviour followed their disbelief in the Prophet after full awareness of his truth, as the Jewish rabbis did in the time of Muḥammad, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. Envy of the great prophethood Allah gave him drove them to denial, defiance, enmity and war. Allah sent humiliation upon them in this life, to be followed by disgrace in the Hereafter: "And they were covered with humiliation and misery, and they drew on themselves the wrath of Allah" (2:61), in this life and the next. The punishment abases its bearers, and one reading adds that it is severe.

### The pattern of the rabbis

Their behaviour, in one reading, followed the disbelief of the rabbis who knew his truth. That is the gravest form of the pattern, for the belittling was not of ignorance but against knowledge, and the humiliation sent upon them in this life answers it measure for measure.

### In short

- The divided faith is kufr outright, and the claimed belief in a listed prophet is no belief at all.
- The humiliation answers the belittling, and the rabbis who envied and fought the Prophet ﷺ carry it in both worlds, as sūrat al-Baqarah has said.

<<<VERSE 4:152>>>
The undivided faith: "As for those who believe in Allah and His messengers—accepting all; rejecting none—He will surely give them their rewards. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful" (4:152). The verdict on the dividers is matched by the verdict on the whole-hearted.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who believe in Allah and His messengers—accepting all; rejecting none"** refers to the ummah of Muḥammad ﷺ, who believe in every Book Allah has revealed and every prophet Allah has sent, declaring His Oneness, associating no partner with Him, and making no distinction between any of the messengers as the disbelievers do. One gloss names ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and his followers. The Book draws their portrait: "The Messenger believes in what has been revealed to him from his Lord, and so do the believers. Each believes in Allah, His angels, His Books and His Messengers. We make no distinction between any of His Messengers" (2:285).

**"He will surely give them their rewards"** means the great rewards, the tremendous favour and the handsome bounty prepared for them, because of their faith in Allah and His messengers.

**"And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"** extends to their sin, if they have any, Forgiving toward His friends, Merciful toward those who obey Him, forgiving of anyone of them who dies, Merciful toward whoever dies in a state of repentance.

The reading of the whole passage is sealed by the rule of the Book itself. The concluding verse, printed with the reference 15:22 in the record, reaffirms that the Hereafter belongs to those who believe in Him and in all His prophets, and the Prophet ﷺ said that one part of the Qur'ān explains the other, so no one may explain the Qur'ān in any way contrary to the explanation the Qur'ān itself gives.

### The covenant at full width

The inward reading sets the condition at full width. Belief in all the messengers, with confirmation of everything commanded, earned the acceptance and the beautiful recompense. Letting go of one's faith in some individuals is letting go of the faith of whole time periods. Allah accepts no faith that omits the messages that came before, and none that omits what the faith demands, for He made that the condition of its actualization and its perfection. The allusion is that whoever does not wholeheartedly enter the covenant holds not so much as a sliver of the reality of communion. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> The pilgrimage is the day of ʿArafa. Anyone who traverses the distance, even if he comes from a deep ravine, but then holds back even a little from ʿArafāt, has not experienced the pilgrimage.

And he said:

> The servant who has contracted with his master to be freed is still a servant to the last dirham.

### In short

- The ummah of the Prophet ﷺ believes in every Book and every prophet, and the reward prepared is great, the bounty handsome.
- One part of the Qur'ān explains the other, and the concluding verse, as the record numbers it, seals the rule.
- The covenant must be entered whole, for the pilgrim who holds back a little of ʿArafāt has no pilgrimage, and the faith that omits a message or a duty has no acceptance.
