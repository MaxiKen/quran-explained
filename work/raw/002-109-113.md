<<<VERSE 2:109>>>
Verse 109 names the motive behind the Jews' campaign against the young Muslim community, and tells the believers how to respond for the time being. "Many among the People of the Book wish they could turn you ˹believers˺ back to disbelief because of their envy, after the truth has been made clear to them. Pardon and bear with them until Allah delivers His decision. Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Many among the People of the Book wish they could turn you ˹believers˺ back to disbelief"**. According to Ibn ʿAbbās, the verse came down concerning some Jews who, after the Battle of Uḥud, told the Muslims: "Can you not see what has happened to you? If you were really following the truth you would not have been defeated. Come back to our religion, it is better for you." ʿAbdullāh ibn Kaʿb ibn Mālik related from his father, in a report Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded, that Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf, a Jew and a poet, used his verses to satirise the Prophet ﷺ and to incite the disbelievers of Quraysh against him, and that the idolaters and Jews of al-Madinah used to harm the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his Companions grievously when they first arrived. Allah commanded His Prophet ﷺ to endure it all and to forgive them, and this passage was revealed about them. One early gloss names Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and his companions and Finḥāṣ ibn ʿĀzūrāʾ and his companions as the ones who longed to make the believers abandon their religion, and ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir, Ḥudhayfah ibn al-Yamān and Muʿādh ibn Jabal as those they worked on. "If only", the wording implies, they could make you unbelievers after your faith in Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qur'an, back to worshipping idols as you did before. Allah warns His believing servants against following the ways of the People of the Book, who harbour enmity and hatred for the believers openly and in secret and envy them, even while recognising the merit of the believers and their Prophet ﷺ. Some of the Jews, posing as well-wishers of the Muslims, were forever devising new schemes to turn them from Islam, and did not give up despite repeated failure. Having set their minds against Islam as worthless, they were alarmed by the heartfelt welcome many were giving it. They regarded themselves as the only ones entitled to rule on truth and falsehood, so how could others be allowed to believe what they had chosen to reject? Their first step was to incite the polytheists against the Muslims, and their second was to unsettle the converts with doubts about their new religion in the hope that they would go back to the faith of their fathers.

**"because of their envy"**. The envy sprang from their own souls, which is to say that their wicked souls prompted them to it. Ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas glossed "from their own selves" as "of their own making". It was resentful envy, arising not from anything the Muslims did but spontaneously from within. Aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk related Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation: an unlettered Messenger came to the People of the Scripture confirming what their own Books say about the messengers and the signs of Allah, and believing in all of it as they did, yet they rejected him out of disbelief, envy and transgression. After Allah had made the truth plain to them, so that none of it was hidden from them, their envy still drove them to deny the Prophet ﷺ, and for this Allah censured, rebuked and condemned them. Against this He laid down what His Prophet ﷺ and the believers should hold to: belief, faith, and acceptance of the revelation sent to them and of what was sent to those before them, a gift of His generosity and great kindness.

**"after the truth has been made clear to them"**. Abū al-ʿĀliyah explained it this way. Once it was plain to them that Muḥammad ﷺ was Allah's Messenger, the one described in their Torah and Injīl, they still rejected him, in disbelief and transgression, for the sole reason that he did not belong to them. Qatādah and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said the same. The truth about the Prophet ﷺ, his description and his traits, stood in their own Book. A modern writer adds that accepting the truth means negating the self, something people of high standing always find hard, and hardest of all for the People of the Book, who thought prophethood their exclusive right.

**"Pardon and bear with them until Allah delivers His decision"**. Leave them be, keep away from them and make no move against them, pardoning what they do and overlooking their ignorance and whatever is bad in them, until Allah's command about them comes. The command meant fighting them, and it came in the form of Islam, the jizyah or the sword. One gloss has it that the decision was the punishment Allah brought on Banū Qurayẓah and Banū an-Naḍīr by killing, captivity and expulsion. The instruction resembles Allah's words, "you will certainly hear many hurtful words from those who were given the Scripture before you and ˹from˺ the polytheists. But if you are patient and mindful" (3:186). Ibn ʿAbbās, through ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah, said this pardon was abrogated by "kill the polytheists ˹who violated their treaties˺ wherever you find them" (9:5) and by "Fight those who do not believe in Allah and the Last Day, nor comply with what Allah and His Messenger have forbidden, nor embrace the religion of truth from among those who were given the Scripture, until they pay the tax, willingly submitting" (9:29). Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, Qatādah and as-Suddī likewise said it was abrogated by the verse of the sword, and the words "until Allah delivers His decision" themselves point that way. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded from Usāmah ibn Zayd that forgiveness towards the disbelievers and the People of the Book was the settled practice of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his Companions, as Allah had commanded, and that he was patient with them as he was ordered until Allah permitted fighting them, whereupon Allah destroyed at the hands of the Prophet's ﷺ forces those of the chiefs of Quraysh whom He had decreed should be killed. The chain of this text is ṣaḥīḥ, and though its wording is not found in the six collections, its basis is in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs from Usāmah ibn Zayd.

The Muslims were asked not to give way to a justified anger at such misconduct but to forgive and wait for a fresh command on the matter. The verse thus hints that Allah would soon lay down a law for preserving peace and order on earth that would guide the Muslims in dealing with mischief-makers, namely the permission to make war on the enemies of Islam. The command of forbearance suited the Muslims' situation at that time. Later Allah fulfilled the promise of this verse and sent down the ruling on jihād, which was then applied to the Jews and to other troublemakers alike, so that to prevent disorder and establish peace the Muslims fought them, and the mischief-makers were killed, driven into exile or made to pay the jizyah, the levy on non-Muslims living under the protection of the Islamic state which exempts them from military service. The provocation was natural, but God told the believers it was not the moment for a violent reaction. Patience keeps a person from negative retaliation in the heat of the moment.

**"Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything"**. The Muslims knew their own weakness and the strength of their enemy and might have wondered how they could ever act on such a law, so the verse reminds them that Allah's power covers everything, small or great, ordinary or extraordinary, including the death and expulsion of their enemies. He is able to do all things, and their plotting does not touch Him.

### Envy and the traveller

The devotional reading says that the heedless who have lost all understanding wish that not a single star would rise over those who live in peace and safety (salāmah), and that the envious would like the sun to stop shining on those they envy. These are the attributes of disbelievers, and God abases and humbles them. The allusion is to the state of those with spiritual desire (irādah) when they first set out on their travels. If they find no help and success in good companionship, and live among people who follow externals, those people will stop them travelling and never cease "advising" them, frightening them with weakness and threatening them with poverty until they turn them aside into heedlessness and cut them off from the path of desire. Such people are truly God's enemies, overcome by the enmity of the moment, and their punishment is to be denied the least scent of the fragrance of sincerity (ṣidq). "Pardon and bear with them", because the aspirant's way is to guard his innermost self from everything other than God, to show his best nature to everyone, and to spare no effort in kindness, for the Real will soon open the way before him.

### In short

- After Uḥud, and from the day the Prophet ﷺ arrived, Jews such as Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and Finḥāṣ ibn ʿĀzūrāʾ worked to turn the believers back to unbelief, incited Quraysh and sowed doubts among converts.
- Their motive was envy from within themselves, not any wrong done to them, and it operated after the truth about the Prophet ﷺ stood plain in the Torah and the Injīl.
- The command to pardon and bear with them was for that stage only. Ibn ʿAbbās and others say it was abrogated by the verses of fighting (9:5, 9:29), when Allah's decision came as Islam, jizyah or war.
- Allah's power over all things answered the Muslims' sense of weakness, and for the spiritual traveller the verse teaches patience with those who would frighten him off the path.

<<<VERSE 2:110>>>
Verse 110 tells the believers what to do while they wait. "Establish prayer, and pay alms-tax. Whatever good you send forth for yourselves, you will ˹certainly˺ find ˹its reward˺ with Allah. Surely Allah is All-Seeing of what you do".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Establish prayer, and pay alms-tax"**. Having commanded them to forgive and be patient until His help and victory came, Allah commanded the believers to perform the prayer perfectly and to pay the zakāh, and urged them to keep up these righteous deeds. Establishing prayer means perfecting the five daily prayers with all their parts, obligatory and recommended. Paying the alms-tax means giving the poor their due from one's wealth, zakāh to those entitled to it. Until the law of jihād came down, the Muslims were to go on praying and paying zakāh, and when it came they could add fighting in Allah's cause to the good deeds they were already doing. They were not to imagine that until they could take part in jihād mere prayer and fasting would fail to earn the spiritual merit they longed for. Steadfastness in prayer brings a person closer to God, and never failing in the prescribed alms builds a society in which the rich share willingly with the poor, a society founded on compassion with a real sense of togetherness among its members.

**"Whatever good you send forth for yourselves, you will ˹certainly˺ find ˹its reward˺ with Allah"**. Whatever righteous deeds, alms and charitable works you lay up for yourselves, and whatever acts of obedience such as maintaining kinship ties and giving charity, you will find their reward with Allah. Every good act done in life before death becomes a treasure whose reward will be found with the Lord on the Day of Judgement. Allah encourages the believers to busy themselves with deeds that will profit them and earn reward on the Day of Resurrection, prayer and zakāh above all, so that they gain His help in this life and on "the Day the wrongdoers’ excuses will be of no benefit to them. They will be condemned, and will have the worst outcome" (40:52). Not a particle of anyone's good deeds will be lost.

**"Surely Allah is All-Seeing of what you do"**. He knows your intentions and sees what you spend in charity and zakāh. He is never unaware of anyone's deeds and never lets them go to waste. Righteous or evil, every deed will be requited exactly as it deserves.

### The aspirant's obligation

For the devotional reading, the aspirant's duty is to perform the acts of intimate communion (muwāṣalāt) and to keep seeking access to God through every kind of act that draws near (qurubāt), trusting that what he offers in sincere, strenuous effort (mujāhadāt) will bear fruit in the final states (ḥālāt). What is sent ahead in this world is found again in the next.

### In short

- While pardoning their enemies, the believers were to perfect their prayers and pay zakāh, the deeds that would later be joined by jihād.
- Whatever good a person sends ahead is stored up with Allah and found again on the Day when excuses will not help (40:52).
- Allah sees every deed and intention and lets none of it be lost.

<<<VERSE 2:111>>>
Verse 111 confronts a claim that both communities of the People of the Book made against everyone else. "The Jews and Christians each claim that none will enter Paradise except those of their own faith. These are their desires. Reply, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Show ˹me˺ your proof if what you say is true.”".

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Jews and Christians each claim that none will enter Paradise except those of their own faith"**. The Jews said no one would enter Paradise unless he died a Jew, and the Christians made the same claim for themselves. The Jews of al-Madinah and the Christians of Najrān both said this when they argued before the Prophet ﷺ, each party separately reserving Paradise for its own members. The Arabic word for Jews here, hūd, is the plural of hāʾid. Allah lays bare the confusion of the two communities, who likewise claimed, as the Qur'an reports in Sūrat al-Māʾidah, "“We are the children of Allah and His most beloved!” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Why then does He punish you for your sins?" (5:18). Allah refuted that claim and told them they would be punished for their sins, just as He rebuked their earlier claim that the Fire would touch them only for a few days before they were admitted to Paradise. The Jews used to tell new converts that if they must leave their ancestral religion they should become Jews or Christians, since those descended from saints and prophets through history were the ones bound for heaven. Hostile not only to the Muslims but to each other, both groups had forgotten the essence of religion, which is holding true and sound beliefs and performing the good deeds the Sharīʿah prescribes, and had reduced it to membership of a racial or social community. Each claimed an exclusive right to Paradise without a single argument to support it.

**"These are their desires"**. Abū al-ʿĀliyah said these were wishes they hoped Allah would grant them, with no foundation. Qatādah and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said the same. They are false passions, vain wishes and imaginings, things they want from Allah even though nothing in their Books supports them. The Qur'an dismisses such pretensions as fancy and self-delusion. No one earns entry into heaven by belonging to a particular community. Everyone will be judged by his own actions, and national standing counts for nothing in the sight of God.

**"Reply, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Show ˹me˺ your proof if what you say is true.”"**. Say to both parties: bring your burhān, your proof. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Mujāhid, as-Suddī and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas explained burhān as proof, and Qatādah as the evidence that supports your statement. The proof was to come from their own respective Books, if they were truthful in their claim. The Qur'an does not simply contradict them. It asks for evidence, and the demand is itself the refutation, since none was forthcoming.

### Guessing is not proof

The devotional reading remarks that every party (ḥizb) smooths things over for itself, convinced that salvation belongs to it and claiming Paradise as its portion. But mere supposition (ḥusbān) without the verification of proof (burhān) yields no result and is of no use. The next verse supplies the standard that applies to everyone alike.

### In short

- The Jews of al-Madinah and the Christians of Najrān each claimed Paradise for their own community exclusively, as they also claimed to be Allah's children and beloved (5:18).
- Such claims are wishes with no basis in their Books, the product of reducing religion to communal membership.
- Allah's answer is a demand for proof, and supposition without proof settles nothing.

<<<VERSE 2:112>>>
Verse 112 replaces the communal claims of the previous verse with the only standard that counts. "But no! Whoever submits themselves to Allah and does good will have their reward with their Lord. And there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve".

### Phrase by phrase

**"But no!"**. It is certainly not as they claim. Others too will enter Paradise, and the door is defined by two conditions rather than by lineage or label.

**"Whoever submits themselves to Allah"**. Literally, whoever submits his face to Allah. The face is mentioned because it is the noblest part of the body, so that once it has submitted the other members have all the more reason to follow. Abū al-ʿĀliyah and ar-Rabīʿ said it means whoever is sincere with Allah, and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said it means being sincere in one's religion. It is to perform one's deeds purely for Allah without partner, to make one's religion and works sincere for Him, and to adhere to His commands. The same expression appears when Allah tells His Prophet ﷺ: "So if they argue with you ˹O Prophet˺, say, “I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers.”" (3:20).

**"and does good"**. He is a muḥsin, one who does what is beautiful in word and deed, who affirms Allah's oneness and follows the Messenger ﷺ. Two conditions must be met for a deed to be accepted: it must be done for Allah's sake alone, and it must conform to the Sharīʿah. A deed that is sincere but does not conform to the Sharīʿah is not accepted. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, in a ḥadīth recorded by Muslim, that whoever does a deed that is not in accordance with our religion will have it rejected. For this reason the good deeds of the priests and rabbis are not accepted even when done sincerely for Allah alone, because they do not follow the way of the Messenger ﷺ, who was sent to all mankind. Of such cases Allah said: "Then We will turn to whatever ˹good˺ deeds they did, reducing them to scattered dust" (25:23), and "As for the disbelievers, their deeds are like a mirage in a desert, which the thirsty perceive as water, but when they approach it, they find it to be nothing" (24:39), and "On that Day ˹some˺ faces will be downcast, ˹totally˺ overburdened, exhausted, burning in a scorching Fire, left to drink from a scalding spring" (88:2-5). Conversely, a deed that conforms to the Sharīʿah outwardly but is not done sincerely for Allah is also rejected, as with the hypocrites and those who act to be seen: "Surely the hypocrites seek to deceive Allah, but He outwits them. When they stand up for prayer, they do it half-heartedly only to be seen by people" (4:142), and, in Sūrat al-Māʿūn, "So woe to those ˹hypocrites˺ who pray, yet are unmindful of their prayers, those who ˹only˺ show off, and refuse to give ˹even the simplest˺ aid" (107:4-7). Hence Allah's words: "So whoever hopes for the meeting with their Lord, let them do good deeds and associate none in the worship of their Lord" (18:110).

This is the general principle, accepted by the followers of every revealed Book. The essence of religion, whether Islam, Christianity or Judaism, lies in two things. One should obey Allah in belief and in action, and this obedience should spring not from expediency but from a sincere surrender to divine commands. Nor is it enough, to merit Paradise, to have a sincere intention to obey and then to invent the forms of obedience according to one's own fancy. The forms of worship and the modes of obedience must be exactly those Allah Himself appointed through His prophets, which since the revelation of the Qur'an can only mean accepting and following the Islamic Sharīʿah. For the first principle the Qur'an uses the word aslama, total submission to Allah, and for the second muḥsin, one who does good deeds in accordance with the Sharīʿah. Paradise is therefore open to others besides the Jews and Christians, to all who in their own time followed the Sharīʿah of their own prophet and who, now that the Qur'an has abrogated the earlier Books, follow the Islamic Sharīʿah. Whoever sincerely turns to Allah, does good, serves Him and follows what the Prophet ﷺ brought will enter Paradise whatever group he comes from. And whatever a person's original religious community, Jew, Christian, idolater or other, submission to Allah is now valid before Him only by becoming Muslim, after the coming of Muḥammad ﷺ. Having laid down the two principles, the Qur'an invites the disputants to see who is actually acting on them. One who goes on following a ruling after it has been abrogated cannot be called obedient, so the Jews and Christians no longer qualify. After abrogation, obedience means acting on the new ruling that replaced the old, and only the Muslims, who accepted the last Prophet and his Sharīʿah, fulfil this condition. The condition of sincerity excludes the hypocrites as well, whom the Sharīʿah counts among the unbelievers and assigns to Hell. To be sincere in faith is to become so devoted to God that everything else becomes secondary, so that prejudice, personal loyalties and worldly interests never stand in the way of answering the call of truth.

**"will have their reward with their Lord"**. The reward of their deeds, which is Paradise. Allah guarantees them their recompense and safety from what they fear.

**"And there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve"**. No fear of what lies ahead in the Hereafter, no fear of staying for ever in Hell, and no grief over what they left behind, no grief at the prospect of missing Paradise or over what passed them by in life. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said: no fear in the Hereafter, and no grief at the approach of death. On the Day of Judgement they will have nothing to fear and nothing to mourn, for the angels will bring them good news.

### A warning for Muslims too

These verses warn the Muslims as well, lest they delude themselves like the Jews and Christians and suppose that merely belonging to the Muslim community, and being able to call themselves Muslims, excuses them from obeying Allah and following the Sharīʿah while still entitling them to the rewards promised to true Muslims. Even Muslims have no right to hope for those rewards until they submit totally, in thought and deed, to the commands of Allah and His Prophet ﷺ.

### Purified, not merely purifying

The devotional reading calls the work described here the work of the purifiers, the good fortune of the truthful, the conduct of the pure, and the hard cash in their purse. Today their seat is the carpet of service, lit by recognition, and tomorrow it will be the carpet of companionship, with the joy of union. Allah says of such people, "We truly chose them for the honour of proclaiming the Hereafter" (38:46), that is, He made them pure and brought them out pure from the crucible of testing so that they would be fit for the Presence, for the Pure Presence admits only the pure, and Allah is good and accepts only what is good. Nothing avails there but pure action and pure speech, and then one must become so purified even of that pure action that one no longer seeks it in this world or the next, and so reach God pure: "And he will indeed have ˹a status of˺ closeness to Us and an honourable destination" (38:40). Abū Bakr az-Zaqqāq put the secret of it this way: the defect in every purifier's purity is that he sees his own purity, and if God wishes to purify a man's self-purification He removes from it his seeing of it, so that he becomes "purified" (mukhlaṣ) rather than "purifying" (mukhliṣ). Your sincerity is pure when it is pure of your watching it. This sincerity, moreover, is not in your hands or within your power. It is a secret of the Lord deposited by the Glorified, which no one knows and no one else can reach. The One says: it is one of My secrets that I place in the hearts of those of My servants whom I love. I choose a servant, approve him for My love and lay My deposit in the core of his heart, where Satan cannot reach to destroy it, nor the soul's caprice see it to change it, nor the angel reach it to write it down.

### In short

- Paradise belongs not to a community but to whoever submits himself wholly to Allah and does good, the two conditions being sincerity and conformity to the Sharīʿah of the final Prophet ﷺ.
- A sincere deed outside the Prophet's ﷺ way is rejected (25:23, 24:39, 88:2-5), and a correct deed done for show is rejected too (4:142, 107:4-7). Both conditions are joined in 18:110.
- After the Qur'an abrogated the earlier Books, only the Muslims meet the test of obedience, but Muslims who rely on the name alone are warned just as sternly.
- Such people have their reward with their Lord, with no fear ahead and no grief behind, and the highest sincerity is to be purified even of seeing one's own purity.

<<<VERSE 2:113>>>
Verse 113 shows that the two communities who each claimed Paradise for themselves could not even agree with each other. "The Jews say, “The Christians have nothing to stand on” and the Christians say, “The Jews have nothing to stand on,” although both recite the Scriptures. And those ˹pagans˺ who have no knowledge say the same ˹about people of faith˺. Surely Allah will judge between them on the Day of Judgment regarding their dispute".

### Phrase by phrase

**"The Jews say, “The Christians have nothing to stand on” and the Christians say, “The Jews have nothing to stand on”"**. Allah exposes the disputes, hatred and obstinacy the People of the Book direct at one another. Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that when the Christian delegation of Najrān arrived to see the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, the Jewish rabbis turned up and started arguing with them in his presence. The debate grew so heated that they shouted at each other. Rāfiʿ ibn Ḥuraymilah said, "You follow nothing", and declared his disbelief in ʿĪsā (Jesus) and the Injīl, and a Christian of the Najrān delegation answered, "No, it is you who follow nothing", and declared his rejection of Mūsā's (Moses') prophethood and the Torah. Then Allah revealed this verse. The Jews of al-Madinah said the Christians had no religion from God and that there was no true religion but Judaism, and the Christians of Najrān said the Jews had no religion from God and that there was no true religion but Christianity. Each party asserted out of malice that the other's religion had nothing to support it.

**"although both recite the Scriptures"**. Each party could read in its own Book the confirmation of the very thing it denied. The Jews reject ʿĪsā although they have the Torah, in which Allah took their covenant through Mūsā to believe in ʿĪsā, and the Christians reject Mūsā although the Gospel contains ʿĪsā's affirmation that Mūsā's prophethood and the Torah came from Allah. Yet each disbelieved in what the other had. The Jews read the Torah and the Christians read the Gospel, and they could easily have seen that the two Books confirm each other and that both command faith in all the prophets without distinction. Both parties read the Book, failed to believe in it, and then claimed things it never says. The clause describes their state: they say this while reciting the Scripture.

**"And those ˹pagans˺ who have no knowledge say the same ˹about people of faith˺"**. Allah thus exposes the ignorance of the Jews and the Christians in what they said, by likening them to those without knowledge. Who these are is disputed. Ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and Qatādah said the Christians said the like of what the Jews said. Ibn Jurayj asked ʿAṭāʾ who "those who have no knowledge" were, and he answered that they were the nations of old, earlier than the Jews and the Christians and earlier than the Torah and the Gospel. As-Suddī said they are the Arabs, who said that Muḥammad ﷺ followed nothing. Abū Jaʿfar ibn Jarīr preferred to take the verse generally, since no evidence singles out any one of these explanations, and the general reading is better, and Allah knows best. The ignorant among the Arabs and others said to every person of religion, "You have no basis." The polytheists, for all their ignorance, seized the excuse the two communities gave them and said the same of both religions. One gloss explains "those who have no knowledge" as people who knew Allah's oneness only through their forefathers, or who knew Allah's Book only at second hand. In this they resemble people who know nothing at all, who worship others beside Allah and reject every prophet and scripture. For the Israelites, truth was measured by their link with the prophets and the saints, and so their own community was right and every other wrong. The Christians thought themselves unique because God's "beloved son" had been sent to them. Even the polytheists of Makkah thought themselves superior as guardians of God's Sacred House. Every nation had set up its own self-made religious standard by which it inevitably came out right and everyone else wrong, while their actions did nothing to bear out their claims.

**"Surely Allah will judge between them on the Day of Judgment regarding their dispute"**. Allah will gather them all on the Day of Return and judge between them justly, for He wrongs no one by so much as an atom's weight. He will admit the one who confirmed the truth to Paradise and the one who denied it to the Fire. The verse resembles His words in Sūrat al-Ḥajj: "Indeed, the believers, Jews, Sabians, Christians, Magi, and the polytheists—Allah will judge between them ˹all˺ on Judgment Day. Surely Allah is a Witness over all things" (22:17), and "Say, “Our Lord will gather us together, then He will judge between us with the truth. For He is the All-Knowing Judge.”" (34:26). In truth the question has already been settled, by what Allah revealed in His Books and by rational argument. The judgement of the Last Day will be the visible kind, when the followers of truth are sent to Paradise and the followers of falsehood cast into Hell. Through His perfect and just judgements Allah teaches His servants that no one succeeds without believing in all that He revealed.

### The friends who quarrel

The devotional reading turns the verse's apparent sense on its head. Today God's enemies disown one another as they always have, but the friends of God do something of the kind as well. The saying goes that the Sufis stay in a good state only so long as they quarrel and decline to accept one another, since acceptance would make them settle down with each other instead of turning to God.

### In short

- When the Najrān Christians met the rabbis before the Prophet ﷺ, each side declared the other's religion baseless, the Jews denying ʿĪsā and the Gospel and the Christians denying Mūsā and the Torah.
- Each denied what its own Scripture confirms, since the Torah pledges faith in ʿĪsā and the Gospel affirms Mūsā.
- The ignorant pagans, Arabs or earlier nations, said the same of everyone, and Ibn Jarīr took the phrase generally.
- Allah will judge all parties on the Day of Judgement (22:17, 34:26), a judgement already settled in substance by revelation and reason.
