The verse lays the ground for everything the chapter says after it. "Indeed your Lord is Allah Who created the heavens and the earth in six Days, then established Himself on the Throne." Then the second half. "He makes the day and night overlap in rapid succession. He created the sun, the moon, and the stars, all subjected by His command." And the close. "The creation and the command belong to Him ˹alone˺. Blessed is Allah, Lord of all worlds". This verse 54 rests its argument on things a person can watch happening, and from them it draws a conclusion about the One who made them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed your Lord is Allah"** opens with the name of nurturing care, and then gives the name of the One who is worshipped. The heavens and the earth and the planets and the stars all perform their functions inside a system that is firmly established, and the maker of such a system must hold a power than which nothing more perfect can be conceived. That is where the verse invites every reasonable person to stop and think. Here is the Being who brought this universe out of nothing and keeps it running on the wisest of arrangements. Why should it be difficult for Him to undo the whole of it and bring it back on the Day of Qiyamah? The best course for a human being is to give up denying the rising and turn to this same Lord as master and nurturer, asking of Him what is needed and worshipping Him alone. He should come out of the swamp of worshipping his own kind, and he should recognise the truth.

**"Who created the heavens and the earth in six Days"** states a fact the rest of the Book repeats in several places, and the verse does not leave the number unexplained. The days are taken up below, together with the question of what kind of days they were.

**"then established Himself on the Throne"** is the clause that has drawn the most discussion, and what the early generations said about it is set out further down as well.

**"He makes the day and night overlap in rapid succession"** describes something the reader sees twice every day. What the eye watches is the light driving the dark out of the sky and then the dark coming back over the light. Neither of the two lags behind its partner. One has barely gone before the other is in place. So the alternation carries the whole world from light into darkness and back again, and it answers to the subduing power of its Lord so smoothly that not the least delay enters into a change of that size.

**"He created the sun, the moon, and the stars, all subjected by His command"** means that every one of them stands under His command, His will and His dominion. They move in obedience to Him and by His leave. In the Arabic of this sentence the three names may be read in one case as a continuation of the mention of the heavens. In another case they are the subject of a sentence whose predicate follows. The sense in either reading is that they run subject to His command and His power.

**"The creation and the command belong to Him ˹alone˺"** is put in the form of a general rule. The word for creation means bringing things into being, and the word for command means ordering and deciding. Being the creator and being the master belong to Him exclusively. No one besides Him can create the smallest thing, and no one has the right to subject another person to his command, except where Allah Himself gives someone a particular task, and even then the command at work is really His. So the line says that bringing all these things into being was the work of none but Him, and that putting them into service was a burden no one else could carry either. One line of teaching reads the two words as two domains, and that reading also belongs to this verse.

**"Blessed is Allah, Lord of all worlds"** closes the verse with a word built on blessing, which carries the senses of increase, growth, remaining and sustaining. In this place it means that He is high and supreme, or high and sustaining, since He is both. The reading that He is the Possessor of grace, and the reading that He is magnified and far exalted above every fault, both belong here. He is the master and disposer of all the worlds, the Lord of everything and the keeper of every being.

### Why six days, when one word would do

A question arises at once from the first line. The power of Allah is great enough to create the whole universe in a single moment, and the Book says so in more than one place. In Surah Al-Qamar the command is described as a single word, like the twinkling of an eye (54:50). In Surah Ya Sin the word is that when He intends a thing He says to it, "Be", and it comes to be (36:82). So why were six days spent on the work?

One answer given by Saʿīd ibn Jubayr is that Allah is certainly able to create everything in an instant, but divine wisdom asked for a measured span instead. Six days were chosen so that men and women might learn from the pattern what graduality means, and how a great work is perfected by being carried out step by step. A hadith supports the same point, that working with deliberation and dignity and at a steady pace comes from Allah, and that hurrying comes from Shaytan. That report is carried in the collection of traditions known as the Shuʿab al-Iman of Al-Baihaqi, and it stands behind a general lesson. Rushing into things is not good for a human being, because a person driven by haste cannot give due thought to all sides of the question in front of him. What is done in haste spoils the thing done and ends in regret. Whatever is done after proper thinking and with room to work brings barakah, the increase that Allah puts into an effort so that it works better and goes further than its means suggest.

A second question follows. Day and night are known by the movement of the sun, so before the earth and the heavens existed, and with no sun and no moon in place, on what basis were six days counted? Some say the six days were a stretch of time equal to six days and nights as the world counts them now. A clearer approach is that day and night in our sense are terms that belong to this world. Before the universe existed Allah had other signs by which a day and a night could be marked, much as the day and night of Paradise will not depend on the movement of the sun. And it does not follow that the six days of the creation were equal to six of ours. They may have been longer, since the Book speaks of a day with the Lord that is like a thousand years.

Imam Ahmad and Mujahid said the six days here mean the six days of the Hereafter, and the same appears in a narration of ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAbbas reported by Ad-Dahhak. Against the thought that such spans are hard to imagine, one authority has a remark about the speed of the outermost firmament. In the time it takes a running man to bring his raised foot back down to the ground, that firmament covers three thousand miles. That observation is recorded in Al-Bahr Al-Muhit.

### The days themselves, and the day of rest

The six days on which the creation was made began, according to the sound reports, on Friday and ended on Friday. On Yawm as-Sabt, the day of the Sabbath, which is Saturday, no work of creation was done. Some say the word sabt means to discontinue, and the day took its name because the work of creation had come to an end by then.

The order of the work is given in a hadith of Abu Hurayrah. He reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told him that Allah created the dust on the Saturday, the mountains on the Sunday, the trees on the Monday, the unpleasant things on the Tuesday, the light on the Wednesday, and spread the creatures through the earth on the Thursday. Adam came after that, in the afternoon of the Friday. He was the last of the creation, made in the final hour of that day, between the afternoon and the night.

In another accounting the six days are given as Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday. On the Friday the whole creation was assembled, and on that day Adam was created. The question whether these days were the same as the days we count, or whether each of them is a thousand years, is one on which there is a difference of opinion. The longer reading is reported from Mujahid, from Imam Ahmad bin Hanbal, and from Ibn ʿAbbas as Ad-Dahhak's narration from him gives it. Saturday is left outside the count of creation because it is the seventh day of the week.

### The four days of the earth and the two days of the heavens

The details of the six days are set out in Surah Ha Mim As-Sajdah, that is Surah Fussilat. The earth was created in two days, and then the measures of its sustenance were determined in four days, and then the seven heavens were completed in two days. The first two days, in which the earth was made, are Sunday and Monday. The four days in which the mountains, the rivers, the tributaries, the trees, the vegetation and the things human beings and animals eat were created run from the same starting point and are Tuesday and Wednesday. Then the two days in which the seven heavens were finished are Thursday and Friday. By Friday the total comes to six days, and the count agrees with what the earlier reports give. The wording runs: the earth in two days, and its sustenance measured in four days (41:9 and 10), and then the seven heavens completed in two days (41:12).

### What is said about the rising over the Throne

The clause about establishing Himself on the Throne has drawn many opinions. The safest creed to hold is the one that is clear and correct and fairly free of doubt, and it is the creed of the most righteous elders, the Companions and those who followed them, and later of many of the thinkers of the inner life as well. Their creed is that human reason cannot comprehend the reality of the Being of Allah and of His attributes, and that pursuing the exact reality of these things is an exercise in futility and is even harmful. A person should hold, as a general principle, that the meaning Allah intended is the one correct and true meaning, whatever it is, and he should not try to fix a meaning on his own.

The word Al-Istawa, which is translated as established, carries in the language the sense of taking position on something, or settling on it, or being established, and the word arsh means throne. The arsh, in the classical usage, is the elevated seat on which a king sits. What that throne is, and how it is shaped, and what the establishing on it means, are matters beyond the reach of human reasoning.

Someone asked Imam Malik about the meaning of the phrase. He paused for a while and then said that everyone knows the meaning of the word for establishing. Its particular nature and reality are such that human reason cannot grasp them. Having faith in it is obligatory, and asking about its nature and reality is an innovation in the established religion. The reason he gave that last part is that the noble Companions never put such questions to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

The ancient teachers took the same line about all the verses that speak of the Being and the attributes. The names in that list are Sufyan Al-Thawriy, the Imam Al-Awza'i, Layth ibn Saʿd, Sufyan ibn ʿUyaynah and ʿAbdullah ibn Mubarak. Their word was that these verses are to be taken as they have come and exactly as they stand, with nothing added by way of explanation or interpretation. That is the way of guidance here, and it is the way the early generations took. Among them are Malik, Al-Awza'i, Ath-Thawri, Al-Layth bin Saʿd, Ash-Shafiʿi, Ahmad and Ishaq bin Rahwayh, and the rest of the scholars of the community in the past and in later times. They accept the plain statement of the establishing without asking how its reality is, without equating it with anything in the creation, and without altering or denying it.

One of that company, Nuʿaym bin Hammad Al-Khuzaʿi, the teacher of Al-Bukhari, put the two errors plainly. Whoever likens Allah to His creation has committed kufr, and whoever denies what Allah has described Himself with has committed kufr. There is no resemblance to the creation in anything Allah and His Messenger ﷺ have described Him by. Anyone who affirms for Allah the attributes that the plain verses and the sound hadiths mention, in the manner that suits His majesty, and who at the same time rejects from Him every shortcoming, has taken the path of guidance. The Book gives the same measure in another place: nothing is like Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing (42:11), so any meaning that lets someone equate the Creator with what He made has to be put away from Him.

It can be said, in short, that the meaning of the phrase is known, its reality is unknown, faith in it is wajib, and asking about its how and its why is what the elders refused to do.

### The night, the day, the sun, the moon and the stars

The verse puts the day and the night beside the sun, the moon and the stars, and they are all signs of the same order. Of the night it is said elsewhere that it is a sign. The day is withdrawn from it, and the people are left in darkness. The sun runs to a term fixed for it, which is the decree of the All-Mighty, the All-Knowing. The moon is measured in mansions until it comes back like an old curved stalk. The sun cannot overtake the moon, and the night cannot outstrip the day. All of them float, each in its own orbit (36:37-40). The line that the night does not outstrip the day (36:40) means that the night follows the day in its order, never coming before or after its proper time. Another verse gives the same praise to the One who set big stars in the heaven (25:61), and the praise in this verse too rests on the same works.

A person looking at all this is invited to think about the machines he sees made by hands. Engineers make machines of every kind. With some of them the problems begin from the first day. Others run well enough at the start, but a machine remains a machine, however strong its material and however careful its design. Its parts wear, or need adjusting, or need repairs and servicing, and while they are being fixed the machine stands idle, perhaps for weeks. Now look at the machines made by God. The efficiency and the perfection they had on their first day are still in them. They work without stopping, their movement is as exact as it ever was, not a second fast and not a second slow, and there is no wear in their parts and no workshop they need to visit. The reason is in the verse itself. They are subject to His command, and so they need no fuel to move them and no engine to drive them. They work under the divine command alone, and a defect appearing in them is impossible. When the Absolute Master decides to remove them at a time He has appointed, the whole system will come apart, and that day is the day of Qiyamah.

### Creation and command as two words

The two words at the heart of the verse, creation and command, have been read as two domains in one line of teaching. Creation relates to matter, and command relates to the refined and the abstract. The Book points in that direction where it has the Lord say of the soul that it is a command from Him (17:85), since the ruh is named there as a command of the Lord. On this reading, everything between the heavens and the earth is matter, and its bringing into being is called creation, while what lies beyond these things, free of matter, has its bringing into being called command. On the plainer reading, the verse means that the making of the creatures and the ruling of them are both His, and that no one else shares either.

### The blessing in the last line

The last word of praise is built on barakah, and barakah carries the senses of increase, of growth, of becoming more, of remaining and of sustaining, among many other shades. In this place it means that He is high and supreme, and it can also be taken as high and sustaining, since He is both. The sense of height is confirmed by a hadith in which the servant says to his Lord that He is blessed and high, O Master of Glory and Honour. In that same line of praise the word for being blessed is explained by the word for being high.

The praise at the end of this verse is like the praise given to the One who set big stars in the heaven (25:61), and it belongs to Him as the Lord of all the worlds. One of the Companions, Abu Ad-Darda, is reported to have used a supplication that has also been attributed to the Prophet ﷺ. In it the servant says that all the kingdom is Allah's, that all praise is His, and that the ownership of all affairs is His. He then asks Him for every kind of good and seeks refuge with Him from every kind of evil.

### A reading of the two names

One strand of teaching gives the two names in the opening of the verse a work of their own. The Lord is the one whose name is light and whose message is light, and who is joined to love. He nurtures the people of this world and keeps every creature safe. He requites what people do, and His loving is kind. Nothing unworthy comes near Him, for He is pure and without peer, and He is the judge of how and why. He is clear to Himself in rightness, clear to Himself in being, and clear to the heart in friendship. He is one, and one is enough. He looks after what each has, He is able to keep hearts on the straight way, He is Lord over everything, the keeper of every being, and the nurturer of whatever is able to rise higher.

First He said Lord, which is the portion of ordinary people, and then He said God, which is the portion of those who recognise Him and of the sincerely truthful. The name Lord puts the hearts of good men at ease, while the name God carries away the spirits of those who recognise Him. The name Lord pours blessings on those who ask, and the name God throws love into the hearts of the friends. The name Lord pours the blessings of vision on the faithful, and the name God lights the lamp of love in the hearts of those who know Him.

In that line of teaching, love and vision are two gifts, and a story is told of them. They met, and love said to vision that it is like light, because it brightens the world, and vision answered that love is like fire, because it burns the world. Then vision said that when it discloses itself it pulls suffering out of the heart, and love replied that when it settles in the heart it plunders it. Vision called itself a gift for those who are tested, and love said that it throws tumult into the world.

Vision, on this reading, is the share of those who come to know Him through what He has made. They reach Him by way of the engendered, the determined and the newly arrived, which are the earth and the heavens, the sun, the moon and the stars held in subjection. Love is the share of those who come to know Him through Him, arriving at the made things from His side rather than climbing up to Him from them. And on that same reading, three kinds of people have missed the higher road. They are the one who knows Him only by the things He made, the one who loves Him for the sake of His blessings, and the one who searches for Him by his own effort. To know Him by the made things is to worship Him in fear and want.

### What the verse asks of a person

The creation could have been left haphazard, with things made and then abandoned in confusion. It was not left that way. All things were linked in a system that is perfectly ordered and wisely regulated, and each of them was set working in the way that serves the interest of the whole. Man is a small part of this world, and his role in an environment so well formed should be the same as the role of everything else. He should devote himself to the plan of his Creator, in the way the whole universe has given itself over to that plan in obedience. Every other thing in the universe takes part in the plan of God with the utmost ihsan, doing what it was made for and doing it well. Man is asked to do the same, and not to step past the limits of justice and of truth that God has prescribed for him. Man has something more than the rest of creation, which is speech and consciousness, and so his surrender has to be expressed at that level too. The recognition of God should sit so deep in him that it keeps coming out in words, and he should call on God as a servant calls on his Creator and Lord. His awareness of God's godhead should be such that nothing but God is left at the centre of his hopes or his fears. He should fear God alone and fasten his hopes on Him alone, and to bring one's fears and one's hopes to God alone is the last stage of obedience to Him.

### In short

- The verse builds its argument on the created order, and it invites the reader to see the power of the One who made it, so that the raising of the dead is no difficulty for Him.
- The creation took six days although a single word would have been enough, and the wisdom in the measured pace is that human beings should learn to work deliberately rather than in haste.
- The days and their order are given in the reports, with the work of creation finished on the sixth day and no creative work done on the day of the Sabbath.
- The establishing on the throne is affirmed as the early generations affirmed it, without asking how its reality is, without likening Him to anything, without denying it and without turning it into something else.
- The day and the night, the sun, the moon and the stars run subject to His command with a precision no made machine can keep, and the whole of them will end when He decides.
- Creation and command belong to Him alone, and the last word of the verse is praise of the Lord of all the worlds.
