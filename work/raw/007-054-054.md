<<<VERSE 7:54>>>
After the scenes of the Hereafter, the surah turns to the One who will bring it about: "Indeed your Lord is Allah Who created the heavens and the earth in six Days, then established Himself on the Throne." The verse goes on: "He makes the day and night overlap in rapid succession. He created the sun, the moon, and the stars—all subjected by His command." It ends: "The creation and the command belong to Him ˹alone˺. Blessed is Allah—Lord of all worlds". The heavens, the earth, the planets and the stars all do their work within a firmly set order, and that points to a Maker of perfect power. Any reasonable person should then ask a question. If He could bring this vast universe out of nothing and keep it running by the wisest of systems, why would it be hard for Him to undo it all and create it again on the Day of Qiyāmah? The best course for people is to stop denying the Resurrection and to turn to that same Being as their Lord, their Master and Nurturer. They should ask Him for what they need and worship Him alone, and leave the swamp of worshipping beings like themselves.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed your Lord is Allah"**: O people, your Master and Nurturer is Allah.

**"Who created the heavens and the earth in six Days"**: He created the universe, the heavens and the earth and all that is in them, on them and between them, in six days, as several verses of the Qur'an state. He did it without any precedent. They ran from Sunday through to Friday. Friday is the day the whole of creation was gathered together, and the day Ādam was made.

Opinions differ on whether these days were like our ordinary days, as first comes to mind, or whether each day was a thousand years. The second view is reported from Mujāhid and Imam Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal, and from Ibn ʿAbbās in aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk's narration. They said the six days are six days of the Hereafter. One explanation says they were days from the beginning of this world's life, each equal to a thousand years. Another says they were days of this world in length, since there was no sun yet.

No creation took place on Saturday, the seventh day of the week. Its name, as-Sabt, means stopping or a break. Some scholars say the day was so named because the work of creation was complete by then. According to authentic narrations, the work of creation ended on Friday.

Imam Aḥmad recorded from Abū Hurayrah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ described the order of creation. Allah created the soil on Saturday, the mountains on Sunday, and the trees on Monday. He created unpleasant things on Tuesday and light on Wednesday, and on Thursday He spread living creatures across the earth. He created Ādam after ʿAṣr on Friday. Ādam was the last thing created, in the last hour of Friday, between ʿAṣr and nightfall.

**"then established Himself on the Throne."** Istawā followed by ʿalā literally means to take one's position on something, to settle or be established. ʿArsh means throne. In the classical language it is the raised seat on which a king sits. One explanation says, "He seated Himself on the Throne", and another, "then He proceeded to create the Throne". Others say He presided over the Throne in a way that befits Him, or established Himself on it in a manner suited to His majesty. The way to understand this phrase is set out below.

**"He makes the day and night overlap in rapid succession."** He covers the day with the night and the night with the day. The verb can be read yughshī or yughashshī. Darkness departs with the coming of light, and light departs with the coming of darkness. Each pursues the other swiftly and is never late. When one vanishes the other arrives, and so on in turn. Another passage says:

> "There is also a sign for them in the night: We strip from it daylight, then—behold!—they are in darkness. The sun travels for its fixed term. That is the design of the Almighty, All-Knowing. As for the moon, We have ordained ˹precise˺ phases for it, until it ends up like an old, curved palm stalk. It is not for the sun to catch up with the moon, nor does the night outrun the day. Each is travelling in an orbit of their own" (36:37-40).

"Nor does the night outrun the day" (36:40) means the night follows the day in turn, arriving neither later nor earlier than it should. This alternation of night and day is a change on a revolutionary scale. It carries the whole world from light into darkness and from darkness into light. Yet it yields so swiftly, smoothly and obediently to Allah's overwhelming power that there is not the slightest delay.

**"He created the sun, the moon, and the stars—all subjected by His command."** All of them move in obedience to His will and command, under His dominion and by His leave and power. If the words for sun, moon and stars are read in the accusative, they are joined to "the heavens" as things He created. If read in the nominative, they begin a new sentence whose predicate is "subjected".

**"The creation and the command belong to Him ˹alone˺."** To Him belong dominion and decision. Khalq means to create, and amr means to command. Being Creator and Master belongs to Him alone. No one else can create even the most insignificant thing. Nor does anyone have the right to put another under his command, unless Allah Himself entrusts someone with some area of authority, and then that too is really Allah's command. So the verse means that creating all these things was His work alone, and so was putting them into service. That too displays His perfect power. Is not the whole creation His? Who besides Him creates? One explanation takes "the creation" as the creation of the heavens and the earth, and "the command" as the judgement of people on the Day of Judgement.

**"Blessed is Allah—Lord of all worlds"**: this is like the verse "Blessed is the One Who has placed constellations in the sky" (25:61). Tabāraka comes from barakah, which means to increase, grow, abound, last and endure, besides many other shades of meaning. Here it means to be high and supreme. It can also be taken as high and everlasting, for Allah is both. The sense of "high" is confirmed by a phrase from a hadith: "You are blessed and exalted, O Possessor of Majesty and Honour." There, "You are blessed" (tabārakta) is explained by the word that follows, "exalted" (taʿālayta). Others explain it as "the Possessor of grace", "far exalted is Allah", or "magnified be Allah". He is high above every flaw and has every attribute of splendour and perfection. He is the Lord of the worlds, their Master and Disposer.

Abū ad-Dardāʾ used to make a supplication that is also attributed to the Prophet ﷺ. In it he declared that all dominion and all praise belong to Allah, and that every affair is His. Then he asked Him for every kind of good and sought refuge with Him from every kind of evil.

### Why six days, when He could create in an instant?

Allah has immense power and could create the whole universe in a single moment. The Qur'an says so again and again. Sūrat al-Qamar says, "Our command is but a single word, done in the blink of an eye" (54:50). Sūrah Yā Sīn says, "All it takes, when He wills something ˹to be˺, is simply to say to it: “Be!” And it is" (36:82). Why, then, did the creation of the universe take six days?

Saʿīd ibn Jubayr answered that Allah is certainly able to create everything in a moment. But His wisdom required that the creation of our universe take six days, to teach human beings the lesson of working step by step and with care in running affairs. Another explanation agrees: an instant would have sufficed for Him, but He chose to teach His creatures caution and measure. A hadith supports this. The Prophet ﷺ said that acting with deliberation, dignity and patience is from Allah, and haste is from Shayṭān. Al-Baihaqī recorded it in Shuʿab al-Īmān.

The lesson is that rushing into things is not good for people. Someone driven by haste cannot think a matter through or weigh all its sides. A hasty act spoils the work and ends in regret. Whatever is done with forethought and at an easy pace brings barakah, the blessing from Allah that improves the effort and its result.

### How could there be days before the sun?

Day and night are known by the movement of the sun. Before the earth and the heavens existed, there was no sun or moon. So on what basis are six days counted?

For this reason some commentators said that six days means a span of time equal to six of our days and nights. A clearer approach is that our terms for day and night belong to this world. Day is what runs from sunrise to sunset, and night from sunset to the next sunrise. Before the universe existed, Allah may have set other signs for day and night. So it will be in Paradise, where day and night will not depend on the sun's movement.

This also shows that the six days in which the earth and heavens were created need not equal six of our days. They may have been longer, as the Qur'an says of a day of the Hereafter that it equals a thousand years. Abū ʿAbdullāh Rāzī said, as recorded in Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ, that the farthest sphere moves so fast compared with our earth that a running man's raised foot has not yet touched the ground before that sphere has travelled three thousand miles.

### How were the six days divided?

The details of the six days come in Sūrah Ḥā Mīm as-Sajdah (Fuṣṣilat):

> "Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, “How can you disbelieve in the One Who created the earth in two Days? And how can you set up equals with Him? That is the Lord of all worlds. He placed on the earth firm mountains, standing high, showered His blessings upon it, and ordained ˹all˺ its means of sustenance—totaling four Days exactly—for all who ask" (41:9-10).

The earth was created in two days. Then, in two more days, came the mountains, rivers and streams, trees and plants, and everything that humans and animals eat, making four days in all. The first two days, when the earth was created, were Sunday and Monday. The next two, when the mountains, rivers and the earth's means of life were created, were Tuesday and Wednesday. Then: "So He formed the heaven into seven heavens in two Days" (41:12). Those two days were Thursday and Friday. So by Friday the total came to six days.

### How should "established Himself on the Throne" be understood?

People have held many conflicting opinions about this phrase. The way followed here is that of the righteous predecessors. Among them are Mālik, al-Awzāʿī, ath-Thawrī, al-Layth ibn Saʿd, ash-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad and Isḥāq ibn Rāhwayh, and the rest of the scholars of Islam, past and present. They accept al-istawā as it appears. They do not discuss its inner nature, liken it to the attributes of creatures, distort it or deny it in any way. Whatever meaning occurs to those who liken Allah to His creation is rejected, for nothing resembles Allah: "There is nothing like Him, for He ˹alone˺ is the All-Hearing, All-Seeing" (42:11).

The imams said the same. Nuʿaym ibn Ḥammād al-Khuzāʿī, teacher of Imam al-Bukhārī, said that whoever likens Allah to His creation has committed kufr. Whoever denies what Allah has described Himself with has also committed kufr. Nothing that Allah and His Messenger ﷺ have said in describing Him involves any likeness to creation. Whoever affirms Allah's attributes as the clear verses and authentic hadiths state them, in a way that befits His majesty and denies Him every flaw, has taken the path of guidance.

The safest creed here, clear, correct and free of doubt, is that of the Companions and the Successors, and later of many Sufi thinkers too. Human reason cannot grasp the reality of Allah's Being and attributes. Chasing after its exact reality is futile, and even harmful. As a general principle one should believe that whatever meaning Allah intended is the true and correct one, and not try to fix a meaning of one's own.

Someone asked Imam Mālik exactly this question: what does "established Himself on the Throne" mean? He paused for a while and then answered. Everyone knows what istawā means, he said, but its particular nature and reality are beyond the reach of human reason. Believing in it is obligatory (wājib), and asking about its nature and reality is bidʿah, an innovation, because the noble Companions never asked the Prophet ﷺ such questions. The great early scholars (Salaf) said the same. They include Sufyān ath-Thawrī, Imam al-Awzāʿī, al-Layth ibn Saʿd, Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah and ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak. Verses about Allah's Being and attributes, they said, should be believed in just as they came, without explanation, elaboration or interpretation.

### What do machines that never break down teach?

This verse invites a thinking person to reflect on the made things around him. Experts build machines of every kind. Some have design faults from the start. Others do not, but machines are machines, however strong the material and flawless the design. Their parts wear, and they need adjustment, repairs and servicing. They stand idle until fixed, sometimes for weeks or months.

Now look at the machines Allah has made. The efficiency and perfection with which they began working on the first day are still there. They run wonderfully without stopping. Their motion is as precise as ever, not a second fast or slow. Their parts never wear out, and they never need a workshop. The reason is that they work "subjected by His command". They need no fuel to drive them and no engine to run them. They work under Allah's command alone, subject to nothing else, so even the slightest breakdown in them is impossible. Yet when the Absolute Master decides to end them, at a time He has fixed, this whole system will fall apart. That day will be the Day of Qiyāmah.

### What do "creation" and "command" mean to the Sufis?

In Sufi thought khalq and amr are two realms. Khalq concerns matter, and amr the subtle realm beyond matter. The verse about the spirit points in this direction. "They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about the spirit" (17:85), and the answer ties the rūḥ, the spirit, to the command (amr) of the Lord. In this light, "the creation and the command belong to Him" means two things. Everything between the heavens and the earth is made of matter, and its making is called khalq. What lies beyond them, free of matter, has its making called amr.

### What is a human being's place in this order?

Allah is the Creator of the earth, the sky and everything else. He could have created things haphazardly and left them in confusion, but He did not. He linked everything into a perfect, well-ordered and wisely governed system. He set it working so that each thing works exactly as it should to serve the common good.

The human being is a small part of this world. What should his role be in so well-formed a setting? It should be the same as that of everything else. He should give himself to the Creator's plan, as the whole universe has surrendered to it in full obedience. Everything in the universe plays its part in Allah's plan with the utmost excellence (iḥsān), and a person should give himself to it with the same excellence. Nothing in the universe oversteps its set limits, so a person too must not overstep the limits of justice and truth that Allah has laid down.

A human being also has speech and awareness, so his surrender to his Lord must show at that level as well. Knowing and recognising Allah should be so deeply rooted in him that it comes out again and again. He should call on Allah as a subject calls on his Creator and Lord. He should be so aware of Allah's lordship that nothing but Allah stays at the centre of his hopes and fears. He should fear Allah alone and pin his hopes on Him alone. Tying one's fears and hopes to Allah is the highest stage of obedience to Him.

### The inner reading: "Lord" and "Allah", vision and love

The name "Lord" is light itself, and His message is light bound together with love. He is the nurturer of the world's people, the keeper of creatures, the One who repays, the Loving. He is pure and without equal, the judge of every how and why, untouched by anything unworthy. He is evident to Himself in rightness and in being, and evident to the heart in friendship. He is One and sufficient, providing for what everyone has, able to keep hearts upright. He is Lord of everything, keeper of every being, and nurturer of everything able to rise higher.

First He says "Lord", the share of ordinary people. Then He says "Allah", the share of the knowers and the sincerely truthful. "Lord" puts the hearts of good people at ease, and "Allah" carries off the spirits of the knowers. "Lord" gives blessings to those who ask, and "Allah" casts love into the hearts of His friends. "Lord" pours the blessing of vision on the faithful, and "Allah" lights the lamp of love in the knowers through vision.

The master of the spiritual path (ṭarīqah) told of a meeting between Love and Vision. Love compared Vision to light, since it brightens the world. Vision compared Love to fire, since it burns the world. Vision said that whenever it appears, it pulls suffering out of the heart. Love answered that whenever it takes up residence in a heart, it ransacks it. Vision called itself a gift for those who are tried, and Love said that it throws the whole world into uproar.

Vision is the share of those who come to know Him through His works. Their road to Him runs through created, measured and newly arisen things, such as the earth and sky, and the sun, moon and stars held to their courses. Love is the share of those who know Him through Himself. They come from Him to His works, not from His works to Him. The master of the spiritual path said: "Poor is the one who knows Him only through His works! Wretched is the one who loves Him for the sake of His blessings! Foolish is the one who seeks Him by his own effort! Whoever knows Him only through His works worships Him in fear and need."

### In short

- Allah created the heavens and the earth in six days, two for the earth, two for its provisions and two for the seven heavens, ending on Friday.
- He could have created all in an instant. The six days teach people to act with care, for haste is from Shayṭān.
- "Established Himself on the Throne" is affirmed as it came, without likening, distorting, denying or asking how, as the Salaf and Imam Mālik taught.
- Night and day, sun, moon and stars run without fault because they are subject to His command alone, until He ends them on the Day of Qiyāmah.
- Creation and command belong to Him alone. A human being should take his place in this order with excellence, keeping Allah's limits and hoping in and fearing Him alone.
