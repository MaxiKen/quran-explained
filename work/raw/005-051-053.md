<<<VERSE 5:51>>>
"O believers! Take neither Jews nor Christians as guardians—they are guardians of each other. Whoever does so will be counted as one of them. Surely Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people"

This verse forbids the believers from taking the Jews and Christians as close friends and protectors. The material explains that the verse does not forbid kindness and fair dealing with them. It forbids a deep and exclusive friendship that would blur the marks of Islam and place a believer's loyalty with those who oppose the community.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers!"** The address is to the community of faith. The material explains that the command to keep distance in friendship is a matter for the Muslims as a community, which must keep its own bonds and its own protection.

**"Take neither Jews nor Christians as guardians."** The Arabic word is awliya, which means close friends, allies and protectors. The material calls the forbidden friendship al-Muwalat, a deep and intimate bond. It notes that Muslims may deal with non-Muslims in justice, kindness and goodwill, and may trade and live alongside them. What is not allowed is the kind of intimacy that makes a person lean on them for support against the community.

**"They are guardians of each other."** The material explains that Jews and Christians tend to reserve their deep loyalty for their own people, so an alliance with a Muslim would be against their own customs. The verse says they stand together against the believers, and a Muslim who joins them stands on their side.

**"Whoever does so will be counted as one of them."** The material says that a Muslim who makes such an alliance is counted with them in the sight of Islam, and is not a true Muslim in that relationship. It cites Ibn Jarir for the reading that this is about a particular case of alliance.

**"Surely Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people."** The material says that those who take the enemies of the community as protectors are doing wrong, and that the one who does wrong is not guided to the path of faith in that matter.

### The background

The material gives the reason for the verse as the treaty the Prophet ﷺ made with the Jews and Christians near Madinah. They agreed not to fight the Muslims or help their enemies, and to join the Muslims in defence of Madinah. The Jews of Banu Qurayzah broke the treaty. They secretly agreed with the pagans of Makkah against the Muslims and sent them a letter inviting them to their fortress. When the Prophet ﷺ learned of this plot, he sent a group of fighters to confront them. Some of the Jews had also made friendships with Muslims, and used these bonds to pass information to Makkah.

The material says that this verse stopped the Muslims from such friendships, so that the enemy would not learn their secrets. Ubadah bin al-Samit openly cancelled his treaty obligations to the Jews and announced that he would have no close friendship with them. Abdullah bin Ubayy, the hypocrite, said he feared the danger of cutting the bond, and so he refused to do so.

### Commentary on the principle

The material gives three principles from the verse. The first is that Muslims may treat non-Muslims with tolerance, justice and kindness, but not with deep friendship. The second is that if some Muslims drift into such friendship, the religion is not harmed by it, since Allah has taken responsibility for protecting Islam. If a people turns away and leaves the faith, Allah will raise another people to uphold its law. The third is that the true and deep friendship of a Muslim is with Allah, His Messenger and those who believe.

The material also notes the position of the Muslims in Arabia at the time. The Jews and Christians held much of the economic power, and many weaker Muslims lived in dual loyalty out of fear of them. The material says that worshipping the truth only in unimportant matters does not help a person when danger comes, since he will meet the fate of those he supported.

### Umar and the Christian scribe

The material gives an example of the rule in this verse from the time of Umar. Umar asked Abu Musa al-Ash'ari to send him a written account of the money received and spent. Abu Musa had a Christian scribe prepare it, and Umar was pleased with the work. He asked whether the scribe could read a letter that had come from Ash-Sham to the mosque. Abu Musa said he could not, and that the scribe was a Christian. Umar rebuked him and told him to send the man out of Madinah. Umar then recited the verse. The material records a report from Abdullah bin Utbah that a person should be careful, since he may be a Jew or a Christian without knowing it, and that this was understood as a reference to this verse.

### The greater enemy

The material says that the greatest enemy of a believer is his own self. It cites the story of Joseph, who was thrown into a well, sold into slavery and kept in prison for years, and who never complained as he did about the soul that commands to evil, as stated in 12:53. The material also records the Prophet ﷺ describing the self that lies between a person's two sides as the worst enemy. The verse on friendship is therefore also a warning to guard against the desires of the self.

### In short

- Muslims may deal with Jews and Christians in justice and kindness, but they must not take them as deep allies against the community.
- The verse was revealed after the Jews of Banu Qurayzah broke their treaty and passed secrets to the enemy.
- The true friendship of a believer is with Allah, His Messenger and the believers.

<<<VERSE 5:52>>>
"You see those with sickness in their hearts racing for their guardianship, saying ˹in justification˺, “We fear a turn of fortune will strike us.” But perhaps Allah will bring about ˹your˺ victory or another favour by His command, and they will regret what they have hidden in their hearts"

This verse describes those with sickness in their hearts, the hypocrites, who rushed to make friends with the Jews and Christians. It records their excuse, and it promises that Allah may bring victory or His command, which will expose what they hid and bring their regret.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You see those with sickness in their hearts."** The material explains that the sickness is doubt, hesitation and hypocrisy. The verse refers to people like Abdullah bin Ubayy and his followers, who professed Islam outwardly and kept their true loyalty hidden.

**"Racing for their guardianship."** The material says that they hurried to offer their friendship and alliance to the Jews and Christians, in public and in secret. The verse uses the word race to show how eager they were.

**"Saying, 'We fear a turn of fortune will strike us.'"** The material says that they offered this as an excuse. They feared that the enemies of Islam might win, and so they wanted to be on good terms with the Jews and Christians, so that they could use that favour for their own benefit if the Muslims lost. The material notes that this fear is a sign of weak faith, since it puts worldly safety above obedience to Allah.

**"But perhaps Allah will bring about your victory or another favour by His command."** The material says that the victory in view is the conquest of Makkah, according to As-Suddi. The other favour is the command to make the Jews and Christians pay the jizyah, which is also reported from As-Suddi. The word perhaps, when spoken of Allah, expresses a certainty that the outcome will come about.

**"And they will regret what they have hidden in their hearts."** The material explains that the hypocrites will regret their friendship with the Jews and Christians, which did not protect them. It says Allah exposed their true state to the believers in this life, though they tried to hide it.

### In short

- The people with sickness in their hearts rushed to befriend the Jews and Christians out of fear for their own safety.
- Their excuse was that they feared a turn of fortune, which shows their weak faith.
- Allah will bring victory or His command, and their hidden hypocrisy will be exposed and they will regret it.

<<<VERSE 5:53>>>
"Only then, the believers will ask one another, “Are these the ones who swore solemn oaths by Allah that they were with you?” Their deeds have been in vain, so they have become losers"

This verse describes the moment when the hypocrites are exposed. The believers ask one another in amazement whether these are the people who swore great oaths of loyalty to them. The verse ends by saying their deeds were lost.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Only then, the believers will ask one another."** The material explains that the question is asked when the hypocrisy has come out into the open. The believers are amazed at people who had sworn faithfulness and now stand exposed.

**"Are these the ones who swore solemn oaths by Allah that they were with you?"** The material says that the hypocrites swore by Allah, which is the most binding oath, that they were with the believers in faith and support. Their oaths were false, and the believers see the falsehood plainly.

**"Their deeds have been in vain."** The material says that their works were thwarted in this life, since their hypocrisy did them no good. It says their loss is also in the next life, through the punishment prepared for them.

**"So they have become losers."** The material says that they lost their true goal and the reward of the Hereafter. The verse is read as a warning that the outward claim of loyalty means nothing without true faith.

### The lesson of the trial

The material draws a lesson from the verse. Every test in the world is a test of intention. A believer proves his intention by refusing to fear the dangers of the path. The help of Allah comes after that first step. A person who does not take the first step is a transgressor in the sight of Allah, and cannot expect His help. The material says that the Islamic life is one of struggle, and that the believer's mission is to guide others away from the path of hell and towards paradise.

### The hypocrites of Madinah

The material explains that the hypocrites of Madinah, led by Abdullah bin Ubayy and his followers, were the ones who swore loyalty. They had hidden their doubts. When the victory came, first with the conquest of Makkah, and then when the Jews were punished, their claims were exposed. The material says that everyone saw these events within a few days, and that the verse foretold them.

### In short

- The believers wonder at the hypocrites who had sworn their loyalty, once their hypocrisy is exposed.
- Their deeds were wasted in this life and the next, and so they have become losers.
- The lesson is that a believer must prove his intention by standing firm rather than seeking safety in false alliances.
