<<<VERSE 5:51>>>
Believers are warned against a particular kind of relationship: "Take neither Jews nor Christians as guardians". The verse concerns alliance and intimate loyalty, not ordinary dealings. It explains that such people are guardians of one another, and that whoever joins them in that way is counted with them.

The warning comes with a statement about guidance. Allah does not guide a people who wrong themselves by placing their trust where it cannot protect them. The historical setting shows the danger being addressed was real and practical, not merely a matter of feeling.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Take neither Jews nor Christians as guardians"** forbids muwalah, deep alliance and indiscriminate intimacy. The legal discussion insists that this is not a prohibition of tolerance, sympathy, goodwill, equity, justice, favour or kindness toward non-Muslims. Muslims are taught to deal with them in those ways.

What is excluded is a closeness that garbles the distinctive marks of Islam. The term used in the legal discussion for refraining from such depth of friendship is tark al-muwalah. The prohibition therefore concerns the character of loyalty, not a command to be unjust or hostile in ordinary dealings.

**"they are guardians of each other."** explains the reason. Each group reserves that depth of loyalty for its own people. One gloss says some follow the religion of others in secret and openly, and another describes them as united in disbelief. They do not deal with Muslims on the same basis.

**"Whoever does so will be counted as one of them."** states the consequence of that alliance. One explanation says such a person stands in their alliance and is not included in Allah's protection and safety. He is counted with them rather than with the believers.

**"Surely Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people"** warns that this choice damages guidance itself. The wrongdoing described is affiliation with those hostile to the faith. A brief gloss identifies those not guided in this context, while the warning reaches anyone who makes that choice.

### What happened at Al-Madinah to prompt this?

Ibn Jarir records from 'Ikrimah that after the Prophet ﷺ settled in Al-Madinah he made a treaty with nearby Jewish and Christian groups. Neither side would fight the other or help an enemy against the other, and Muslims would defend them against aggressors while they joined Muslims against those who attacked them.

For a time both parties kept the agreement. The account then describes Banu Qurayzah making a secret arrangement with the pagans of Makkah and writing to invite them to their fortress. When the Prophet ﷺ learned of the plot, he sent fighters to confront them.

At the same time, members of that tribe had formed friendships inside the Muslim community. The explanation describes this as a front for gathering information against Muslims. The verse was revealed so that sensitive knowledge of the community's affairs would not reach people working secretly with its enemies.

Some Companions, including 'Ubadah ibn As-Samit, openly announced the cancellation of their treaty obligations and the end of such close friendships. That response treated loyalty to the community as something that could not be maintained on two sides at once.

Others were not so willing. People whose hearts had not tasted genuine faith, and those whose relationship with Muslims was chiefly pragmatic, feared that breaking these ties would expose them if the conspiracy succeeded. 'Abdullah Ibn Ubayy ibn Salul is reported as saying he saw danger in cutting off those relationships and could not do it. The next verse addresses that excuse.

### What does the account of 'Umar and Abu Musa show?

Ibn Abi Hatim records that 'Umar asked Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari to send him, on a single sheet, the balance of what he had received and spent. Abu Musa had a Christian scribe who was able to produce it, and 'Umar was pleased with the work.

'Umar then asked whether the scribe could read a letter that had come from Ash-Sham in the mosque. Abu Musa said he could not. Asked whether the man was not pure, Abu Musa answered that he was not, because he was Christian.

'Umar admonished him, poking his thigh with his finger, told him to drive the man out of Al-Madinah and recited this verse. The incident concerns entrusting a position inside the community's affairs, not a statement that a Christian may never be employed anywhere in any capacity. The concern in the account is the placement of trust where the community's business is handled.

'Abdullah bin 'Utbah is reported as warning that a person should beware of becoming a Jew or a Christian without being aware of it. The narrator understood him to be referring to this verse. The warning describes how gradually adopted loyalties can reshape a person's commitments without his noticing.

### Why cannot such conduct harm Islam itself?

The discussion states a second principle. If Muslims of any time and place abandon this instruction, they should not imagine that their conduct can damage Islam. Allah has taken responsibility for its protection, and it cannot be eliminated by anyone.

If a people leave it, Allah brings another people who uphold its principles and laws. The harm of forbidden alliance therefore falls on those who choose it. They lose their own standing rather than weakening the religion.

### Where does a believer's real loyalty belong?

The third principle answers that question. Deep, profound and reliable friendship belongs with Allah, His Messenger ﷺ and those who believe in them. Nothing else can carry that weight.

An inward reading develops the same point. The call is described as generous and gentle, turning the faces of hearts away from others toward Allah. A person should not take a stranger as a friend or approve an enemy for companionship.

Friendship should be approved for Allah's sake, taken up in His work and confirmed in His religion. The realities of faith are sought through loving Allah's friends and holding enmity toward the enemies of the religion. Muhammad al-Mustafa ﷺ is quoted describing the firmest handle of faith as love for Allah's sake and dislike for Allah's sake.

### Which enemies does that inward reading name first?

It identifies two whose enmity is obligatory: Satan first, and the commanding soul second. The soul is described as harder than Satan, because Satan pursues a believer's disobedience while the soul pulls him toward unbelief and wants it.

Satan is said to flee from the formula "There is no power and no strength but in God", while the soul does not flee from it. Yusuf (Joseph), the sincerely truthful, endured being thrown into a well, sold into slavery and years of prison, yet never lamented as he did the commanding soul, saying that the soul commands ugliness (12:53).

The Prophet ﷺ is quoted calling the soul between a person's two sides his worst enemy. The inward application therefore does not replace the outward warning. It explains that the same misplaced loyalty can operate inside a person, where the self is treated as an ally against Allah's command.

### What danger did the weaker believers misjudge?

In early Arabia, Jews and Christians held much of the region's economic resources and worked to uproot the newly emerging Muslim community. Centuries of history impressed people with their apparent strength, so that many thought they could never be overthrown.

Weaker Muslims therefore avoided participating in Islamic effort in ways that would displease them. If the struggle ended in defeat, they expected to face retaliation. To protect themselves they lived in dual loyalty, which carried its own hazards.

The account says they lost sight of something important. Honouring truth in unimportant matters does not help a person who, when danger appears, lends support to the upholders of falsehood. His fate then becomes no different from that of those he cooperated with when he felt threatened.

### In short

- The prohibition concerns deep alliance and intimate loyalty, not justice, kindness or ordinary fair dealing with non-Muslims.
- The reported setting at Al-Madinah involved a treaty, a secret plot and friendships used to gather information.
- Entrusting community affairs to such an alliance is what the reports of 'Umar and Abu Musa illustrate.
- Islam itself is protected by Allah. The harm of this choice falls on the person who makes it.
- Real loyalty belongs to Allah, His Messenger ﷺ and the believers, and the inward struggle against the commanding soul is part of the same lesson.

<<<VERSE 5:52>>>
The excuse is stated and answered: "You see those with sickness in their hearts racing for their guardianship, saying ˹in justification˺, “We fear a turn of fortune will strike us.”" Their fear is not modest caution. It drives them to hurry toward an alliance the preceding verse has just forbidden.

Allah's reply removes the ground of that fear. What they dread may not happen, and what they secretly rely on may be exposed. Their calculation is answered by events they do not control.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You see those with sickness in their hearts"** describes a disease of doubt, hesitation and hypocrisy, or weakness of faith. The glosses name 'Abdullah Ibn Ubayy and his followers. The sickness is inward, and it shows itself in the alliances a person seeks.

**"racing for their guardianship"** means hurrying to offer friendship and allegiance, in secret and in public. The competition is to be first in securing a relationship the believers have been told not to make.

**"saying ˹in justification˺, “We fear a turn of fortune will strike us.”"** gives their excuse. They fear hardship, defeat or a reversal in which the Muslims are overcome and they need those friendships. Another explanation adds the fear of drought, of defeat, and of losing supplies if the Prophet's ﷺ affair came to nothing.

**"But perhaps Allah will bring about ˹your˺ victory"** promises the opposite outcome. As-Suddi identifies this victory with the conquest of Makkah. Other explanations describe assistance to the Prophet ﷺ and the prevailing of his religion, or the crushing of the force of those tribes and their allies.

**"or another favour by His command"** is explained in several ways. As-Suddi describes a decision requiring the Jews and Christians to pay jizyah, the tax paid by protected non-Muslim subjects. Another explanation describes punishment upon Banu Qurayzah and Banu An-Nadir through killing and expulsion. A further reading describes a command exposing the hypocrites' secrets and disgracing them.

**"and they will regret what they have hidden in their hearts"** concludes the answer. What they concealed of doubt and alliance will be revealed, and they will repent or regret it after exposure. The connection they preserved will prove worthless.

### How strong is the promise behind the word perhaps?

The explanation notes that when the word for perhaps is used in connection with Allah, it denotes something required rather than merely possible. The promise of victory or a decisive command is therefore not a doubtful speculation.

The hypocrites assumed that the unbelievers and the Jews would overcome the Muslims. The account states that Allah had decided otherwise. The conquest of Makkah was near, and even before it the exposure and disgrace of the hypocrites could come.

### What is the difference between caution and this excuse?

Prudence about real danger is not condemned here. What is condemned is allowing that fear to justify the forbidden alliance and to weaken commitment to the believers. The excuse turns a possible future hardship into a present act of divided loyalty.

The treatment of this passage describes every test in this world as a test of intention. What a person must do is prove his intention by disregarding dangers, and in doing so he takes the first step toward Allah. Divine help then comes to him.

Someone who does not prove his intention and does not take that first step is described as a transgressor in Allah's sight. He cannot then expect help given without his own part. Fear of a reversal is therefore not a substitute for the commitment the test requires.

### Why does regret come after exposure rather than before?

While their hypocrisy remained hidden, they could still imagine the alliance as useful. Exposure removes that imagination. The believers see what was concealed, and the hypocrites see that their precaution protected nothing.

Their regret concerns what they kept secret in themselves, not merely the outcome of a battle. The inward concealment was itself the failure, and its disclosure is what makes the loss complete.

### In short

- The sickness of doubt and hypocrisy produces eagerness for forbidden alliance.
- The stated fear of reversal is answered by the promise of victory or a decisive command from Allah.
- Explanations identify that outcome with the conquest of Makkah, the jizyah decision, punishment of the tribes or the exposure of hypocrites.
- Every test concerns intention. Proving it by disregarding danger is the first step toward Allah's help.
- Regret arrives when the hidden loyalty is disclosed and shown to have protected nothing.

<<<VERSE 5:53>>>
Exposure produces astonishment among the believers: "Are these the ones who swore solemn oaths by Allah that they were with you?" The question is not a request for information. It expresses amazement that people so emphatic in their oaths turned out to be lying.

The verse then states the outcome. Their deeds have been in vain, and they have become losers. What looked like careful preparation for every eventuality produced nothing.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Only then,˺ the believers will ask ˹one another˺"** describes sincere believers speaking in amazement when the secrets are revealed. The wording is read as a new statement or as continuing from those who believe, and the sense is the same.

**"“Are these the ones who swore solemn oaths by Allah that they were with you?”"** recalls their strongest oaths of loyalty. The explanation notes that when a person swears by Allah, he has sworn his most binding oath. Their claim was to be with the sincere believers in religion, support and alliance.

**"Their deeds have been in vain"** states that those works are thwarted in this world and invalid. Deeds performed for pretence do not survive exposure, and they carry no reward.

**"so they have become losers"** describes their end. They lose in this world through ignominy and in the Hereafter through punishment. One gloss adds that they brought punishment upon themselves, and another that they lost their true goal.

### Why were their claims exposed?

The hypocrites pretended to belief and swore loyalty, but their claims were lies and deceit. The explanation describes the signs exposing their hypocrisy being gathered against them until their true condition became clear to the believers.

Allah exposed their reality to His faithful servants in this life, although they tried to conceal it. Their friendship with the Jews and Christians did not benefit them or protect them from harm. It brought them nothing but harm.

The account adds that the events announced in these verses, including the conquest of Makkah and the disgrace of the hypocrites, were seen with people's own eyes only a few days later. The promise was not a distant prediction. Its fulfilment was near enough for those who heard it to witness.

### What does the loss consist of?

The loss is twofold. Their efforts achieved nothing, and they incurred the consequence of what they did. A person who spends his life managing appearances loses both the reward of sincere action and the safety he was trying to purchase.

The irony is that their precaution was aimed at avoiding loss. They kept connections open in case the Muslims were defeated. When the Muslims were victorious and their own hypocrisy exposed, the precaution itself became the reason for their ruin.

### What is the believer's task in contrast?

The discussion of this passage describes Islamic life as purposeful and therefore a life of struggle. A believer's mission is to convey Allah's religion to His servants, guiding people away from the path leading to the Fire and toward the path leading to Paradise.

That task requires intention proved in practice rather than loyalty hedged in every direction. The hypocrites wanted the safety of both sides and ended with neither. The believer is asked to commit and strive, leaving the outcome to Allah.

### In short

- The believers' question expresses amazement at oaths that turned out to be false.
- Exposure came through gathered signs, and the promised events were witnessed soon afterward.
- Deeds done for pretence are invalid and bring no reward.
- The hypocrites lost in this world through disgrace and in the Hereafter through punishment.
- The contrasting task of a believer is sincere struggle to convey the religion, not divided loyalty.
