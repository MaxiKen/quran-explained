<<<VERSE 7:189>>>
Verse 189 opens the passage that turns from the long argument over the Hour to the first teaching about the One who creates the human being. "He is the One Who created you from a single soul, then from it made its spouse so he may find comfort in her. After he had been united with her, she carried a light burden that developed gradually. When it grew heavy, they prayed to Allah, their Lord, “If you grant us good offspring, we will certainly be grateful.”" The verse carries the line of descent from the first pair down to all their descendants, and it sets the ground for the charge of joining partners with Allah that comes in the next verse.

### Phrase by phrase

**"from a single soul"** means the soul of Adam alone. From him Allah created his wife Hawwa', and from the two of them the human race spread out over the earth. A later passage states the same truth in the form of a command, that Allah created you from a single soul and from it created its mate (4:1). Another passage places the pairing among the signs of Allah, that He created for you spouses from among yourselves so that you may find repose in them, and that He has put affection and mercy between you (30:21). A third passage joins the two truths, that humanity was made into nations and tribes, and that the most honoured of them before Allah is the one who holds taqwa, the careful awareness of Him (49:13).

**"so he may find comfort in her"** gives the purpose of the pairing. The phrase means that the man becomes intimate with his wife and finds compassion with her. The teachers have noted that there is no closeness between two souls like the closeness between a husband and his wife. The strength of that bond is shown by the fact that the sorcerer aims at separating a man from his wife, which tells us how hard that separation is to bring about by ordinary means.

**"After he had been united with her"** is the covering that brings the pregnancy about. The teachers read it as the sexual relation between the husband and his wife.

**"she carried a light burden that developed gradually"** describes the first part of the pregnancy. The burden is light because the woman does not feel pain in that stage. At first the fetus is a drop of fluid, then a clinging clot, then a small lump of flesh. Mujahid read the phrase as meaning that she carried it about, and Al-Hasan, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i and As-Suddi said the same. Maymun bin Mahran reported that his father said that she did not notice the pregnancy. Ayyub asked Al-Hasan about the phrase, and Al-Hasan told him that he would know what it means if he had been an Arab, since it means that she went on with the pregnancy through its stages. Qatadah said that the phrase means the pregnancy became clear. Ibn Jarir said that the fluid remained in place whether the woman stood up or sat down. Al-'Awfi reported from Ibn 'Abbas that the fluid stayed in her, and that she was unsure whether she had conceived. As-Suddi said that the fetus grew inside her womb.

**"When it grew heavy"** marks the later stage. The teachers say that the child grew in her belly at that point, and the woman felt the weight of it.

**"they prayed to Allah, their Lord"** describes the turn of the couple to supplication when the burden grew heavy. Al-Hasan Al-Basri said that the words are a request for a boy. Ad-Dahhak reported from Ibn 'Abbas that they feared their child might be born in the shape of an animal. Abu Al-Bakhtri and Abu Malik said that they feared their newborn might turn out to be something less than human. One report says that the whispering of Satan made them think the child was a beast. The prayer in the verse asks for a sound child, complete in every part.

### What the reports hold about the first pair

The surah treats the creation of Adam and Hawwa' as a display of the perfect power of Allah. The report of Mujahid says that at first all the children of the two died in their infancy. Satan came to them and told them that if a boy were born to them they should name him 'Abd al-Harith, the servant of al-Harith, and that he would live. The name of Satan before that was al-Harith. The two did as he urged them, and this is the background of the charge of shirk made in the next verse.

### The wisdom in the pairing

One commentary draws out the meaning of the pairing in the human race. The vast universe speaks of its Maker. Beings are found in it as separate parts, and all of the parts are joined into a single harmonious whole, with no clash among them. Such harmony would not be possible if the Creator and Lord of the universe were not one and in control of its working. Man and woman are a clear example. Each of them is born as an independent being, and each is a complement to the other. The harmony between them is so complete that each feels that he or she was created for the other. If more than one being had a hand in the universe, such a fit between two opposite entities could not have been formed.

### The forming of the child

One of the spiritual commentaries dwells on the forming of the child as a sign of the loving kindness of Allah. He created the creatures in pairs and bound like to like and kind to kind, which is what the words about the spouse mean. When He wills to show His power in creation, He brings a hundred thousand subtleties out of a drop of water. The stages are dust, then water, then a clot, then tissue, then bones and skin, and then a living being. At four months the child comes to life in the settled place (77:21) within three-fold darknesses (39:6). In this small creature three pools are set, the brain, the liver and the heart. From the brain, nerves open out into the whole body so that sensation and movement reach every part. From the liver, veins open out so that nourishment reaches every part. From the heart, arteries open out so that the spirit reaches every part. He made the brain in three layers, placing understanding in the first, intellect in the second and memory in the third. He made the eye in seven layers and placed brightness and sight within them. The pupil is more wondrous still, measured like a single lentil, and within it the form of heaven and earth appears with all their vastness. The forehead was made hard so that no hair would grow on it and spoil the beauty, and the skin of the eyebrow was set above it so that the hair would grow a little and not too long.

### In short

- Verse 189 states that Allah created all people from a single soul, the soul of Adam, and created from him his wife Hawwa', so that the human race spread out from the two of them.
- The pairing of husband and wife carries the purpose of comfort and intimacy, and the bond between them is deeper than any other between two souls, which is why the passage about the creation of the two is followed by a warning against joining partners with Allah.
- The pregnancy begins with a light burden that the woman hardly notices, and the teachers describe the stages of the drop, the clot and the lump of flesh, with Ibn Jarir, Al-'Awfi, Ibn 'Abbas, As-Suddi, Al-Hasan, Ayyub, Qatadah and Al-Basri all giving their readings.
- When the burden grew heavy the couple turned to Allah with the prayer for a sound child, complete in its human form.
- One report of Mujahid holds that all the children of the first pair died in infancy, and that Satan, whose name was al-Harith, told them to name a son 'Abd al-Harith so that he would live.
- The signs of the creation show the power of Allah and the wisdom in the pairing of man and woman, and the harmony of the universe stands as proof that its Maker is one.

<<<VERSE 7:190>>>
Verse 190 states what followed the answer to that prayer. "But when He granted their descendants good offspring, they associated false gods in what He has given them. Exalted is Allah above what they associate ˹with Him˺" The verse is the counterpart of the one before it, and the early teachers read it in more than one way.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But when He granted their descendants good offspring"** moves from the first pair to the people who came after them. The verse mentions the person first and then the species, a pattern that occurs elsewhere in the Book. It names Adam and Hawwa' at the start, then passes to those among their descendants who slipped into shirk.

**"they associated false gods in what He has given them"** names the ingratitude that the surah is exposing. The gift that they turned into a means of joining partners with Allah was the child itself, the very answer to their own prayer for a sound son.

**"Exalted is Allah above what they associate with Him"** declares the distance between Allah and every partner that is imagined for Him. A short commentary on the verse says that Allah is far above having any partner, and that He is alone in His divinity.

### How the verse is understood

The strongest line of teaching on this verse comes from Al-Hasan Al-Basri. Ibn Jarir recorded from him that this happened among the followers of some religion and not from Adam himself. In his reading the charge falls on the children of Adam who entered into shirk. His student Qatadah reported his view that the words are about the Jews and the Christians, whom Allah gave children and who then raised them as Jews and Christians. The chains reaching Al-Hasan are judged authentic, and this is counted one of the best explanations, since the apparent sense points away from Adam and Hawwa' and toward the idolaters among their children. The same pattern of speech is found in the Book where the lamps of the nearest heaven are mentioned and the shooting flames that drive off the devils are mentioned in the same passage, even though the two are not the same thing (67:5).

The other line of teaching takes the ascription to lie in the naming of the child. The couple were told to name a son 'Abd al-Harith, the servant of al-Harith, while servitude belongs to God alone, as in the name 'Abd Allah. One reading has the word as shirkan, meaning an associate, in place of the plural shuraka'. The teachers explain that this namesake form of 'Abd does not charge Adam with the sin of associating others in worship, since Adam was kept free of such a sin.

The report behind the naming is given from Samura b. Jundub. He reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that every child born to Eve had died in infancy, and that Satan came to her with the promise that a child called 'Abd al-Harith would live. She named him so and he lived. The Prophet ﷺ said that the affair was the work of Satan's inspiration. Al-Hakim deemed the report sound, and Al-Tirmidhi counted it fair but uncommon. Another reading of the matter has the pair making Iblis a partner in the naming of what Allah had given them of offspring, so that the child was called 'Abdullah and 'Abd al-Harth.

One commentary states the interpretation that it adopted. The chain for that reading is Ibn 'Abbas, and it comes down by way of Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim in Tafsir al-Durr al-Manthur. That reading holds that the verse speaks of the generations after Adam who were ungrateful to Allah and entered into shirk, and that Adam and Hawwa' have no part in the charge. The story that al-Tirmidhi and al-Hakim relate about the two being deceived by Satan is taken by some scholars as an unauthentic Israelite report that is to be rejected, while many of the scholars of hadith confirmed it. Either way the interpretation of the verse stands clear.

### The forms of this shirk

The same discussion lists the ways in which this failing appears. Some are misled by the belief that a newborn is the gift of some holy or godly person. Some devote the child to a living or a dead person and begin to make offerings in that name. Some take the children to such a figure and bow their foreheads before it. Some join partners with Allah by naming their children after false gods, as in names that assign the child to al-'Uzza or to the sun. All of these are forms of shirk and the worst sort of ingratitude toward Allah, their Lord.

The passage carries lessons that the teachers gather. Men and women were created in one species so that they have a natural affinity and understanding with each other and can carry out their duties toward the building of a good society. The rights and obligations given to the married couple aim at a peaceful home. Naming a child matters, and it is forbidden to give a child a name that assigns him to anything other than Allah, even if the parents do not intend that meaning. The best way to show gratitude to Allah is to give children the good names of Allah and His Messenger, and the Prophet ﷺ recommended names that begin with servitude to Allah, such as 'Abdullah. The earlier passage that names nations and tribes sets taqwa as the measure of honour before Allah (49:13), and that measure matches the lesson here.

### In short

- Verse 190 states that when the descendants of the first pair were granted a good child, they joined partners with Allah in the gift He had given them, and it declares that Allah is far above every partner they set up.
- Ibn Jarir recorded from Al-Hasan Al-Basri that the charge falls on the offspring of Adam who entered into shirk, and not on Adam and Hawwa' themselves.
- Qatadah reported from Al-Hasan that the words refer to the Jews and Christians, who were given children and then turned them into followers of their own ways.
- The other line of teaching locates the ascription in the naming of the child, as in 'Abd al-Harith and 'Abd al-Harth, or 'Abdullah, and the report of Samura b. Jundub carries the account of Satan's prompting, which Al-Hakim deemed sound and Al-Tirmidhi counted fair but uncommon.
- The adopted reading rests on Ibn 'Abbas, carried by Ibn al-Mundhir and by Ibn Abi Hatim in Tafsir al-Durr al-Manthur, and it clears Adam and Hawwa' of the charge. The story of their being deceived is treated by some as an unauthentic Israelite report, while many scholars of hadith confirmed it.
- The passage yields the lesson that a child is a trust, that names assigning a child to anything other than Allah are forbidden, and that the good names of Allah and His Messenger are the fitting way to give thanks. The readings of Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Ayyub, Al-'Awfi, As-Suddi and Al-Basri on the light burden lead into this discussion of the gift, and the people of Makkah are the first audience addressed.

<<<VERSE 7:191>>>
Verse 191 puts the question to the idolaters in a form that exposes the weakness of what they worship. "Do they associate ˹with Allah˺ those ˹idols˺ which cannot create anything, but are in fact created"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do they associate with Allah"** lays the question before those who set up rivals beside Him, whether those rivals are idols, images or any other created thing.

**"those which cannot create anything, but are in fact created"** gives the reason the ascription is absurd. The objects of their worship were made by Allah, and they own nothing. They can bring neither harm nor benefit, and they neither see nor give aid to those who worship them. They are lifeless things that do not move, hear or see. The worshippers are better than the worshipped, since the worshippers hear and see and can exert some strength of their own.

### The proof from the creation of the fly

The Book sets out the weakness of these objects in the parable of the fly (22:73-74). The verse says that those on whom they call besides Allah cannot create even a fly, even if they all combine their strength for the purpose. If the fly snatches something away from them, they have no power to take it back from it. So the seeker and the sought are both weak. The passage then says that they have not measured Allah as He deserves to be measured, since He is strong and mighty. The argument follows from this. If a thing is that weak, how can it be worshipped, and how can it be called upon for provision and help?

### The idols of the people of Ibrahim

The same theme runs through the story of Ibrahim Al-Khalil. He said to his people, "Do you worship that which you carve?" (37:95). He then turned on their idols and struck them with his right hand (37:93), and he broke them into pieces, all but the biggest of them, so that the people might come back to it (21:58).

### The idols of the people of Hud

The people of Hud had said to their prophet that all they could say was that some of their gods had seized him with madness. Hud answered that he called Allah to witness, and that he called them to witness, that he was free of what they ascribed as partners with Him. He told them to plot against him, all of them together, and to give him no respite. He said that he put his trust in Allah, his Lord and their Lord, and that there is no living creature whose forelock is not in His grasp, and that his Lord is on a straight path (11:54-56). The words carry the confidence of a man who knows that the objects which his people set beside Allah can do nothing at all.

### The worship that ends

The reports also show what becomes of an idol when its worshipper is honest. Two young men came to Al-Madinah after the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had come there and embraced Islam. They were Mu'adh bin 'Amr ibn Al-Jamuh and Mu'adh bin Jabal. They used to attack the idols of the idolaters at night, breaking them and disfiguring them, and using the pieces as fuel for needy widows. They wanted their people to see the error they were in. 'Amr bin Al-Jamuh was one of the chiefs of his people, and he had an idol that he used to worship and to perfume with incense. The two young men would turn that idol on its head and smear it with animal waste. When 'Amr saw what had been done to it, he would clean it, perfume it again and leave a sword beside it, saying to it, "Defend yourself." The two would repeat what they had done, and he would do the same in return. In the end the idol was tied to a dead dog and let down into a well on a rope. When 'Amr saw it hanging there, he knew that his religion was false, and he said that a god with any might would never have been left in that state beside a dead dog. 'Amr bin Al-Jamuh embraced Islam and became strong in it, and he was later killed at the battle of Uhud.

### In short

- Verse 191 asks the idolaters how they can set up beside Allah things that create nothing and are themselves created.
- The objects of such worship are made things that own nothing, bring neither harm nor benefit, and neither see nor help those who serve them, while the worshippers themselves hear and see and possess some strength.
- The parable of the fly (22:73-74) shows how little such objects can do, since they could not create a fly even together, and could not recover what a fly might snatch from them.
- The words of Ibrahim Al-Khalil stand against them, that he asked his people how they could worship what they carve (37:95), and he struck the idols with his right hand (37:93) and broke them to pieces except the largest of them (21:58).
- The words of Hud carry the same lesson, that he was free of what his people ascribed to Allah and told them to plot against him all together without any respite (11:54-56).
- The account of Mu'adh bin 'Amr ibn Al-Jamuh and Mu'adh bin Jabal in Al-Madinah, who broke the idols of their people, and of 'Amr bin Al-Jamuh, who saw his idol tied to a dead dog and knew that his religion was false and embraced Islam, which he held to until his death at Uhud.

Verse 193 addresses the idolaters directly. "And if you ˹idolaters˺ call upon them for guidance, they cannot respond to you. It is all the same whether you call them or remain silent"

### Phrase by phrase

**"if you call upon them for guidance"** means the call to the right path and to the oneness of Allah. The teachers record the readings of the verb in this verse. It is read as yatba'ūkum, and it is also read as yattabi'ūkum, with the same meaning of following. The pronoun ūkum, which means you, closes both forms, and both are carried in the reports of the reciters.

He accepted Islam and held to it firmly, and he was killed at the battle of Uhud.

The story answers the verse from the other side. The idol was called to defend itself and gave no answer. It was called to bring victory to its people and gave no answer. Its silence at the moment of its own disgrace is the plainest commentary on the words, that it makes no difference whether it is called upon or left in peace.

If the idols could not answer Hud when he defied them, they cannot answer the call of guidance.

- The teachers give two readings of the address. The Prophet ﷺ is told that the disbelievers will not follow him whether he calls them or holds his peace, and the disbelievers are told that their idols will not answer them whether they call or not.

<<<VERSE 7:194>>>
Verse 194 closes the passage with a challenge that leaves the idolaters nowhere to stand. "Those ˹idols˺ you invoke besides Allah are created beings like yourselves. So call upon them and see if they will answer you, if your claims are true"

### Phrase by phrase

**"those you invoke besides Allah are created beings like yourselves"** states the reason the claim collapses. The idols that the idolaters call upon were created by Allah, and they belong to Him just as the worshippers do. Allah made them, as He made the people who set them up as gods.

The teachers say that the people who worship these objects are better than the objects themselves, since the people are alive and can speak and walk and hear and see, while the idols are not like that at all. The difference lies wholly on the side of the human being, and yet the human being is the one who bows before the thing he made. The contrast with the call to Allah is the whole force of the passage. The words of Hud stand as a witness. The words of Ibrahim Al-Khalil stand beside it. The idols were called upon and gave no answer. The One who created them answered the trust of His prophets.

### In short

- Verse 194 states that the objects called upon besides Allah are created beings like the people who call on them.
- It challenges the idolaters to call upon those objects and see whether they answer, if the claim that they are gods is true.
