<<<VERSE 7:189>>>
Verse 189 turns to the most basic belief of Islam, tawḥīd, and at the same time shows the falsehood of believing in more than one god. It begins with the creation of Adam and Ḥawwāʾ (Eve), a display of Allah's perfect power: "He is the One Who created you from a single soul, then from it made its spouse so he may find comfort in her." It goes on to the birth of a child: "After he had been united with her, she carried a light burden that developed gradually." Then comes the anxious prayer: "When it grew heavy, they prayed to Allah, their Lord, “If you grant us good offspring, we will certainly be grateful.”" Such a display of Allah's power should have made people more grateful to Him and kept them from setting up partners with Him. Being heedless, they did otherwise, as this verse and the next describe.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He is the One Who created you from a single soul,"** Allah created all humankind from Adam alone, and from Adam He created his wife Ḥawwāʾ. From the two of them people spread. Allah says elsewhere: "O humanity! Indeed, We created you from a male and a female, and made you into peoples and tribes so that you may ˹get to˺ know one another. Surely the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous among you." (49:13). The most noble is the one with the most taqwā. And: "O humanity! Be mindful of your Lord Who created you from a single soul, and from it He created its mate" (4:1).

**"then from it made its spouse so he may find comfort in her."** Allah created Adam's wife, Eve, from his rib, so that he would be close to her, find rest and peace with her, and be intimate and tender with her. Allah says in another verse: "And one of His signs is that He created for you spouses from among yourselves so that you may find comfort in them. And He has placed between you compassion and mercy." (30:21). No two souls are as intimate as husband and wife. That is why Allah mentions that a sorcerer may use his tricks to separate a man from his wife, which shows how hard they are to part in normal circumstances.

**"After he had been united with her,"** Literally, "when he covered her", meaning when he had marital relations with her.

**"she carried a light burden"** This is the first stage of pregnancy, when the woman feels no pain and may not even notice it. The foetus is then only a nuṭfah, a mixed drop, then an ʿalaqah, a clinging clot, then a muḍghah, a small lump of flesh. Maymūn ibn Mihrān reported that his father said she found the pregnancy unnoticeable.

**"that developed gradually."** Literally, "and she went about with it". Mujāhid said it means she continued with the pregnancy, and the same is reported from al-Ḥasan, Ibrāhīm an-Nakhaʿī and as-Suddī. Ayyūb asked al-Ḥasan about this phrase. Al-Ḥasan replied that had Ayyūb been an Arab he would know: it means she carried the pregnancy on through its stages. Qatādah said it means it became clear that she was pregnant. Ibn Jarīr explained that the fluid stayed in her whether she stood or sat. Al-ʿAwfī recorded that Ibn ʿAbbās said the seed remained, but she was unsure whether she had conceived. Others say she came and went easily because the burden was light, going about her affairs without finding it heavy.

**"When it grew heavy,"** As the child grew in her womb, she became heavier with it, as as-Suddī explained.

**"they prayed to Allah, their Lord,"** They grew anxious about the child. Ibn ʿAbbās, as aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk reported, said they feared it might be born in the shape of an animal. Abū al-Bakhtarī and Abū Mālik said they feared it might not be human. One explanation says Satan's whispering made them think it was a beast. Another says they feared it would be born unable to speak.

**"“If you grant us good offspring, we will certainly be grateful.”"** They asked for a child sound and whole, a human being in every respect, and promised to be grateful for it. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said it means: if You give us a boy.

### How do parents fall into shirk?

At the start of a pregnancy the woman moves about freely. Later, as the foetus grows heavy in her womb, the parents worry about their unborn child. They cannot be sure the child will be whole, since sometimes a newborn is not normal, or is deaf, dumb or blind. In their worry they pray for a healthy child and promise Allah they will be grateful if He grants one. Then, when Allah answers their prayer with a normal, healthy baby, they set up partners with Him. So their own children become the cause of their going astray.

This happens in many ways. Some are misled by the false belief that their newborn is the gift of some holy or godly person. Some dedicate the child to a living or dead person and begin making offerings in that person's name. Some take their children to such people and bow their foreheads before them. Some fall into shirk by naming their children ʿAbd al-Lāt, ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, ʿAbd al-Shams or Bandah ʿAlī, and the like. Such names assign the children to false gods, as though those gods or persons had created or given them.

### Man and woman, a sign of the One Creator

This vast universe itself introduces its Creator, and the way it does so leaves no room for polytheism. The universe holds countless separate things, yet all of them combine into one harmonious whole, with no contradiction or clash among them. Such perfect harmony would be impossible unless the Creator and Lord of the universe were One, controlling all its workings.

Consider man and woman. Each is born an independent being, yet the two complete each other in every sense. The harmony is so perfect that each feels created for the other. This is one of the most wonderful examples of harmony in the world. It is clear proof that male and female were created by one and the same Lord. Had more than one being been at work in the universe, such perfect harmony between two different and opposite beings could not exist.

### The inner reading: wonders from a single drop

Great and magnificent is the lovingly kind Lord, beautiful in name, Keeper of His servants, Creator of all people, Caretaker of all! He is pure and faultless in name and sign. He has no offspring and was not born, and He has no partner or helper, no spouse and no likeness. When He created the creatures, He made them in pairs, linked to one another. He joined male and female, matched like to like and kind to kind, as He says: "and made of it its spouse, so that it may rest in her". He is Allah, the One, peerless in His attributes and free of every fault, the Creator, Keeper and Nurturer.

When He wishes to show His power in creation, He brings a hundred thousand subtleties and wonders out of one drop of feeble water. First there is dust, then water, then a clot, then tissue, then bones and skin, and then a living creature. At four months it comes to life, held "in a secure place" (77:21), within "three layers of darkness" (39:6). In each person He made three pools: the brain, the liver and the heart. From the brain, the streams of the nerves branch through the whole body, carrying the power of sensation and movement. From the liver the veins spread throughout the body to carry nourishment. From the heart the arteries spread throughout it to carry the spirit.

He made the brain in three layers, placing understanding in the first, intellect in the second and memory in the third. He made the eye in seven layers and set brightness and sight within them. More wondrous still is the pupil. It is the size of a single lentil, yet the whole vast form of heaven and earth appears within it. More surprising yet is the forehead, made firm and hard so that it grows no hair to spoil its beauty. Within it He made the skin of the eyebrow, so that a little hair grows there, but never too long.

### In short

- Allah created all humankind from Adam, and from him his wife, so that spouses would find comfort, love and mercy in each other.
- The verse traces a pregnancy from its light beginning to the parents' anxious prayer for a sound child, with their promise of gratitude.
- Many parents, once granted a healthy child, fall into shirk by crediting holy persons or naming the child after false gods.
- The perfect harmony of man and woman, and the wonders formed from one drop, point to the One Creator.

<<<VERSE 7:190>>>
The promise of gratitude was broken: "But when He granted their descendants good offspring, they associated false gods in what He has given them. Exalted is Allah above what they associate ˹with Him˺". All these acts and beliefs are forms of shirk and the worst kind of ingratitude to Allah, their Lord. The verse's last sentence confirms that such people have gone astray.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But when He granted their descendants good offspring,"** When Allah answered their prayer and gave them a healthy child, a sound human being, as they had asked.

**"they associated false gods in what He has given them."** A variant reading has "a partner" in place of "partners". One account says they made Iblīs a partner in naming the child, calling him ʿAbdullāh and ʿAbd al-Ḥārith. The name ʿAbd al-Ḥārith means "servant of al-Ḥārith", though no one should be called the servant of anyone but Allah, as in ʿAbd Allāh.

**"Exalted is Allah above what they associate ˹with Him˺"** Allah is far above having any partner, and He alone is divine. He is free of the idols the people of Makkah associate with Him. Grammatically, this sentence follows on from "He is the One Who created you", so what lies between the two is a parenthesis.

### Who set up the partners?

Ibn Jarīr recorded that al-Ḥasan said this was done by the followers of some religion, not by Adam. Al-Ḥasan also said the verse refers to those of Adam's offspring who fell into shirk. Qatādah said al-Ḥasan used to say it refers to the Jews and the Christians. Allah gave them children, and they made them Jews and Christians.

These explanations go back to al-Ḥasan through authentic chains, and this is one of the best interpretations. The verse should be understood this way. It plainly does not refer to Adam and Ḥawwāʾ but to the idolaters among their offspring. Allah mentioned the individuals first, Adam and Ḥawwāʾ, and then moved on to the species, humankind, many of whom fell into shirk. The Qur'an has similar cases. For example: "And indeed, We adorned the lowest heaven with ˹stars like˺ lamps, and made them ˹as missiles˺ for stoning ˹eavesdropping˺ devils" (67:5). The stars that serve as lamps in the sky are well known not to be the same as the missiles hurled at the devils. Allah knows best.

On this reading, Adam and Ḥawwāʾ are mentioned at the start so that their children may follow them and be grateful to Allah. The rest of the passage speaks of the misguidance of their descendants, who set up partners with their Lord instead of thanking Him. So Adam and Ḥawwāʾ have nothing to do with those who committed shirk. This interpretation rests on Ibn ʿAbbās, as Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abī Ḥātim reported, and as recorded in al-Durr al-Manthūr.

### The report about the name ʿAbd al-Ḥārith

Mujāhid said that at first all the children of Adam and Eve died in infancy. Satan whispered to them that if a boy was born they should name him ʿAbd al-Ḥārith, al-Ḥārith having been Satan's name before. They did so, and this is the meaning of "they associated false gods in what He has given them". Samurah ibn Jundub reported a hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ said that when Eve gave birth, none of her children survived. Satan came to her and told her to name the child ʿAbd al-Ḥārith so that it would live. She did, and it lived. This came from Satan's prompting and his doing. Al-Ḥākim reported this hadith and graded it ṣaḥīḥ, and al-Tirmidhī reported it and graded it ḥasan gharīb.

Some scholars regard this story, related by at-Tirmidhī and al-Ḥākim, of Adam and Ḥawwāʾ being deceived by Satan, as an unauthentic Israelite report to be rejected. Many scholars of hadith, on the other hand, have confirmed it. Even if the story is taken as authentic, it does not mean Adam associated a partner with Allah in worship, since Adam was protected from such a sin. The interpretation above removes any possible doubt in either case.

### What do these two verses teach?

1. Men and women were created as one species so that they would share a natural affinity and full understanding, and fulfil their duties in building a good society.
2. The rights and duties given to husband and wife are meant above all to make the home peaceful. Many social habits of the modern age run directly against this aim. What is called the freedom of women, in the sense of free mixing between men and women, has wrought havoc on social peace. The alarming number of divorces and bitter quarrels mostly stems from it. Experience shows that as immodest practices grow in a society, its social values decay in proportion, robbing people of peace at home and in society.
3. It is forbidden to give children names such as ʿAbd al-Shams, "slave of the Sun", or ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, "slave of al-ʿUzzā", which assign them to anyone other than Allah. Such names are forbidden even if the parents do not really mean it, and giving them is a great sin in Islam.
4. The best way to thank Allah is to name one's children after the beautiful Names of Allah and the names of His Messenger. The Holy Prophet ﷺ recommended names like ʿAbd al-Raḥmān and ʿAbdullāh.

Sadly, Muslims are giving up their Islamic manners and values one by one. Many give their children non-Islamic names simply to follow the fashion of other cultures. Many parents also shorten their children's names to English initials, which makes them even harder to recognise as Muslims, especially once Muslims have taken on the appearance of others entirely. May Allah grant Muslims a true understanding of Islam and its values.

### In short

- Granted a healthy child, people broke their promise of gratitude and set up partners with Allah in what He had given them.
- Al-Ḥasan's interpretation, with authentic chains and also supported by Ibn ʿAbbās, applies this to Adam's idolatrous descendants, not to Adam and Ḥawwāʾ.
- A report that Satan had them name a child ʿAbd al-Ḥārith is graded sound by some and rejected by others. Even if true, it was no shirk in worship.
- Children should not be given names that make them servants of anyone but Allah.

<<<VERSE 7:191>>>
Allah now rebukes the idolaters directly: "Do they associate ˹with Allah˺ those ˹idols˺ which cannot create anything, but are in fact created". Allah reproaches those who worshipped idols, rivals and images besides Him. Allah created these objects, and they own nothing and can bring neither harm nor benefit. They cannot see, and they cannot help those who worship them. They are lifeless things that neither move, hear nor see. Their worshippers are better than they are, since the worshippers can hear and see and have strength of their own.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do they associate ˹with Allah˺ those ˹idols˺ which cannot create anything,"** Do you set up as partners with Allah things that neither create nor have any power to create, nor to give life? They know that these idols create nothing, so how can they count them as Allah's partners in divinity, worthy of worship?

**"but are in fact created"** These worshipped objects were themselves created and made. They are created in the sense that they were carved. As Ibrāhīm al-Khalīl, the Friend of Allah, said to his people: "How can you worship what you carve ˹with your own hands˺" (37:95).

### Could they create even a fly?

Allah says in another verse: "O humanity! A lesson is set forth, so listen to it ˹carefully˺: those ˹idols˺ you invoke besides Allah can never create ˹so much as˺ a fly, even if they ˹all˺ were to come together for that. And if a fly were to snatch anything away from them, they cannot ˹even˺ retrieve it from the fly. How powerless are those who invoke and those invoked. They have not shown Allah the reverence He deserves. Surely Allah is All-Powerful, Almighty" (22:73-74).

Even if all the disbelievers' false gods joined their strength, they could not create a fly. If a fly stole the smallest thing from them and flew off, they could not get it back. If a thing is that weak, how can it be worshipped and called on for provision and help?

### In short

- Allah rebukes those who give Him partners that cannot create anything and were themselves carved and made.
- All their false gods together could not create a fly, or recover what a fly took from them.
- Their worshippers, who can hear, see and act, are better than the idols they worship.

<<<VERSE 7:192>>>
The idols are as helpless as they are lifeless: "which cannot help them, or even help themselves". They cannot help their worshippers, and they cannot even defend themselves.

### Phrase by phrase

**"which cannot help them,"** They cannot give victory to those who worship them or save them from defeat.

**"or even help themselves"** They cannot protect themselves against anyone who means to harm them, whether by breaking them or in any other way. They cannot stop what is done to them. The question in the previous verse is a rebuke. Since such things cannot help those who worship them, or even themselves, worshipping them is plainly foolish and ignorant.

### Idols that could not defend themselves

Ibrāhīm al-Khalīl, the Friend of Allah, broke and disgraced the idols of his people, as Allah describes: "Then he swiftly turned on them, striking ˹them˺ with his right hand" (37:93). And: "So he smashed them into pieces, except the biggest of them, so they might turn to it ˹for answers˺" (21:58).

When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ came to al-Madinah, Muʿādh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-Jamūḥ and Muʿādh ibn Jabal accepted Islam while they were still young. At night they used to raid the idolaters' idols. They broke and defaced them and used them as firewood for needy widows, hoping to teach their people a lesson and show them their error.

Among his people's chiefs was ʿAmr ibn al-Jamūḥ, who kept an idol that he worshipped and anointed with perfume. The two Muʿādhs used to go to it, turn it upside down and smear it with animal dung. When ʿAmr saw what had happened, he would clean his idol, perfume it and leave a sword beside it, telling it to defend itself. The young men repeated their prank, and he repeated his care. In the end they bound the idol to a dead dog with a rope and dropped it into a well.

When ʿAmr ibn al-Jamūḥ saw this, he knew his religion was false. He swore by Allah that if it had been a god with any power, it would not have ended up tied to a dog on a rope. ʿAmr accepted Islam and was firm in it. He was later martyred at the battle of Uḥud. May Allah be pleased with him and make Paradise his home.

### In short

- The idols cannot help their worshippers, nor even defend themselves.
- Ibrāhīm smashed his people's idols, and none of them could resist.
- When two young Companions disgraced ʿAmr ibn al-Jamūḥ's idol, he saw it was powerless, accepted Islam and died a martyr at Uḥud.

<<<VERSE 7:193>>>
The idols cannot even hear a call: "And if you ˹idolaters˺ call upon them for guidance, they cannot respond to you. It is all the same whether you call them or remain silent". They are mere objects without awareness. They do not hear and cannot speak.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And if you ˹idolaters˺ call upon them for guidance, they cannot respond to you."** If you call these idols, which you have taken as gods, to guidance, they will not follow you or answer what you call them to. The verb can be read yatbaʿūkum or yattabiʿūkum. Allah says elsewhere: "If you call upon them, they cannot hear your calls. And if they were to hear, they could not respond to you." (35:14).

One explanation offers a second reading. The address is to the Prophet ﷺ: if you call the disbelievers to guidance, to Allah's oneness, they will not follow you. On the first reading, the disbelievers are addressed: the idols will give no answer when called to the truth, since they are dead things, not living.

**"It is all the same whether you call them or remain silent"** The idols do not hear those who worship them, so the result is the same whether they call on them or shun them. The idols will not follow, because they cannot hear. On the second reading, the disbelievers will not follow the Prophet ﷺ in Allah's oneness, whether he calls them or keeps silent.

### What did Ibrāhīm ask his father?

Ibrāhīm said: "O dear father! Why do you worship what can neither hear nor see, nor benefit you at all" (19:42). The idols hear no call and see no one, so they can give no guidance and bring no benefit. A thing that cannot respond to a call is no more use when called than when left alone.

### In short

- The idols cannot answer any call, for they are lifeless objects that neither hear nor speak.
- Calling them or keeping silent makes no difference, as Ibrāhīm told his father.
- On another reading, the stubborn disbelievers will not follow the Prophet's call to Allah's oneness, whether he calls them or not.

<<<VERSE 7:194>>>
The Prophet ﷺ is told to say to the idolaters what their gods really are: "Those ˹idols˺ you invoke besides Allah are created beings like yourselves." Then comes a challenge: "So call upon them and see if they will answer you, if your claims are true". The idols, like those who worship them, were created.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Those ˹idols˺ you invoke besides Allah"**: the idols you call on and worship instead of Allah.

**"are created beings like yourselves."** Literally, "servants like you". Allah created them and they belong to Him, so in that respect they are the same as their worshippers, servants He owns. Yet the people are better than the idols, since they are alive, can speak and walk, hear and see, and can do harm. The idols have no such powers. The false gods are mere servants like their worshippers. Lacking the basic faculties of moving, seeing, hearing and speaking, they cannot help themselves, let alone help others in need.

**"So call upon them and see if they will answer you, if your claims are true"** Call on the idols, then, and let them hear your call and answer it, if you are telling the truth when you claim they are gods who can benefit you. Allah then goes on to show how utterly helpless they are, and how far their worshippers surpass them.

### In short

- The idols are created and owned by Allah, just like the people who worship them.
- Their worshippers can move, see, hear and speak, so they are better than their idols.
- The idolaters are challenged to call on their gods and see whether they answer.
