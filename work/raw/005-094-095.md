<<<VERSE 5:94>>>
A test is announced before the rule is given: "Allah will surely test you with game within the reach of your hands and spears to distinguish those who fear Him in secret." The trial is not a distant possibility. It is game so close that it can be taken by hand.

The purpose is stated plainly. Allah wants to know who fears Him when nobody is watching.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Allah will surely test you with game"** concerns land game pursued while a person is in ihram. One explanation says the unlawfulness of game was revealed in the year of al-Hudaybiyyah.

**"within the reach of your hands and spears"** is explained by Ibn 'Abbas, in the reports given by 'Ali bin Abi Talhah and Al-Walibi, as the weak and young game. Allah tests His servants with such game during ihram, which they could catch with their hands if they wished, and He has commanded them to avoid catching it.

Mujahid says the wording about the hands refers to young game and chicks, while the wording about lances refers to mature game. Another gloss describes taking their eggs and their little ones with the hands and catching wild animals with spears.

A further account says Allah tests them with game He releases toward them, the smaller of it caught by hand and the larger by lance.

**"to distinguish those who fear Him in secret."** Allah tests His servants with game coming near their camp, which they could take with hands and spears in public and in private. That is how the obedience of those who obey Allah openly and secretly becomes apparent and is tested.

One gloss describes Allah seeing whoever fears Him in secret and so leaves the game alone. Another explains that Allah may know, through knowledge outwardly manifested, who fears Him in the unseen, that is while He is absent from eyes, so that a person who does not see Him nevertheless avoids hunting.

A verse elsewhere gives the same promise, that those who fear their Lord in the unseen will have forgiveness and a great reward (67:12). Reward and punishment in the Hereafter are based on people's actions, and nothing is hidden from Allah.

**"Whoever transgresses from now on will suffer a painful punishment"** follows the warning. As-Suddi explains transgression thereafter as transgression after this warning and threat, and the punishment as the consequence of defying Allah's command and what He has decreed.

One gloss describes the transgressor as hunting intentionally after Allah decreed the requital and ruling, with a painful beating reaching his back and belly. Another describes the painful punishment on the Day of Rising for violating Allah's prohibition while in ihram for Hajj or 'Umrah.

### What was the setting of the test?

Muqatil bin Hayyan says the verse was revealed during the 'Umrah of al-Hudaybiyyah, when wild game and birds came to the Muslim camp in numbers the like of which they had never seen. Allah prohibited them from hunting while in ihram.

Another account describes the plain of al-Hudaybiyya, where beasts and birds flocked to their caravans while they were in the state of pilgrimage inviolability. A third describes many birds and animals roaming so close that they could easily have been taken with arrows or spears.

The Muslims wanted to hunt as they normally would to meet their needs, but they stopped at once on receiving the order. That immediate stopping is part of what the verse records.

### How does this test relate to the surrounding rulings?

An earlier verse forbade believers to declare good things unlawful. This one shows that Allah holds the absolute power to declare particular things unlawful in particular circumstances. The difference between the two is the difference between human restriction and divine command.

The order that applies in ihram is also described as something an ordinary person must follow in day to day life. The state of consecration makes a visible test of a discipline that belongs to every day.

### What kind of game is meant?

The legal discussion explains that game prohibited in the sacred precincts and in ihram is general in sense. It may be an eatable, lawful animal or an uneatable, unlawful one.

Game means animals that are wild and habitually do not live close to human beings. Naturally domesticated animals such as sheep, goats, cows and camels may therefore be slaughtered and eaten in ihram.

Certain animals are exempted by a specific rule and may be trapped and killed. Water game is permitted, as Allah allows the game of the sea. The exemption also covers some land animals, crawlers and birds, such as the wolf, the biting dog, the crow, the kite, the snake and the scorpion, and killing an attacking beast is permissible. Their exemption is stated in hadith.

The definite article in the word for game is therefore explained as restricting the sense, so that not every kind of game is prohibited. The rulings of the prohibition itself, and of its compensation, are given in the next verse.

### In short

- The test is game close enough to be taken by hand or lance while a person is in ihram.
- Its purpose is to distinguish those who fear Allah in secret from those who obey only when observed.
- The setting is al-Hudaybiyyah, where birds and beasts crowded the camp and the Muslims stopped at once.
- Allah alone declares particular things unlawful in particular circumstances, unlike a person who forbids what is lawful.
- Game means wild animals, with domesticated animals and certain exempted creatures treated differently.

<<<VERSE 5:95>>>
The rule is now stated: "Do not kill game while on pilgrimage. Whoever kills game intentionally must compensate by offering its equivalence". The verse sets out the compensation, the judges who assess it, the alternatives of feeding and fasting, and the treatment of the past and of repetition.

It ends by naming Allah as Almighty and able to exact retribution. The detail is legal, and the motive behind it is inward.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Do not kill game while on pilgrimage."** prohibits killing game in the state of ihram for Hajj or 'Umrah, and one gloss adds the sacred precinct. The wording says do not kill rather than do not eat, and that choice is used below in a ruling.

**"Whoever kills game intentionally must compensate by offering its equivalence"** requires an equivalent animal from what people own. The explanations describe compensation from camels, cattle or sheep, judged as equivalent to what was killed.

One account gives the occasion as Abu'l-Yusr ibn 'Amr, who killed game intentionally while forgetting that he was in a state of consecration.

**"as judged by two just men among you"** requires two just Muslim men to determine the equivalent animal or the amount of its price. The two are described as possessing the astuteness to identify the nearest animal in equivalence to the one slain.

**"to be offered at the Sacred House"** means the equivalent animal is brought to the Ka'bah, that is to the sacred area, slaughtered there and its meat divided among the poor of that area. There is consensus on this ruling, and one account stresses that it cannot be sacrificed anywhere else.

**"or by feeding the needy, or by fasting"** gives alternatives where there is no equivalent, or where the animal hunted is not comparable to anything. One gloss describes giving money for it by feeding some of the poor of Makkah, and another describes feeding from the principal food of the town at one mudd for each poor person.

For the fasting, one account describes a day of fasting for each half sa' of food that should have been given. Another describes the fast as equivalent to the number of poor people the food would have reached.

**"so that they may taste the consequences of their violations."** The expiation is required so that the offender tastes the weight of his error. The compensation is not merely a transfer of value.

**"Allah has forgiven what has been done."** covers what happened in the period of ignorance, provided a person becomes good in Islam, follows Allah's law and avoids the sin. Another gloss describes it as whatever happened before the prohibition, including game killed in the sanctuary in the past.

**"But those who persist will be punished by Allah."** concerns whoever does this after it has been prohibited in Islam and with knowledge of the prohibition. One gloss adds that a person who relapses after being condemned and severely beaten in this world should be left until Allah takes retribution from him.

**"And Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment"** describes Allah as invincible in His control. Nobody can resist Him, prevent Him from exacting retribution or stop Him from punishing, because all creation is His and the decision is His. He punishes those who disobey Him for their disobedience, and nothing can prevent Him.

### Which animals may be killed in ihram?

The prohibition has stated exemptions. 'A'ishah reports in the Two Sahihs that the Messenger ﷺ named five harmful creatures that may be killed whether a person is in ihram or not: the crow, the kite, the scorpion, the mouse and the rabid dog.

Ibn 'Umar reports the same five as not harmful to kill in ihram, also in the Two Sahihs, and Ayyub reports similar wording from Nafi' on Ibn 'Umar's authority. Ayyub says he asked Nafi' about the snake and was told there is no doubt that killing it is allowed.

The ruling on the rabid dog is extended to the wolf, lion, leopard, tiger and their like, either because they are more dangerous or because the word for dog covers them.

Abu Sa'id reports that the Prophet ﷺ was asked what a person in ihram may kill and named the snake, the scorpion, the mouse, the crow, which is shot at but not killed, the rabid dog, the kite and wild beasts of prey. Abu Dawud records the hadith, as do At-Tirmidhi, who calls it hasan, good, and Ibn Majah.

The legal discussion adds water game, which is permitted, and the killing of an attacking beast. The animals naturally domesticated are outside the definition of game altogether.

### What does intentionally mean here?

Mujahid bin Jabr explains that intending to kill the game while forgetting one is in ihram is what the word covers here. Whoever intentionally kills game while aware of being in ihram has committed an offence graver than one requiring expiation, and he also loses his ihram.

That statement is described as odd, and the view of the majority is that expiation is due for killing game whether the person forgot he was in ihram or not.

Az-Zuhri explains the arrangement by saying that the Book asserts expiation for intentional killing while the Sunnah included those who forget. The meaning given is that the Qur'an states the expiation and the sin of intentional killing, in the wording about tasting the weight of the deed, while the Sunnah, which includes the rulings of the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions, indicates that expiation is also required for unintentional killing.

Killing game is a form of waste requiring expiation in both cases, although the one who intends it has sinned while the one who made an honest error has not.

### How is equivalence determined?

The Companions gave rulings that a camel is the equivalent of an ostrich, a cow of wild cattle and a goat of a deer. Ibn 'Abbas, 'Umar and 'Ali are described as adjudging a sacrificial beast as redemption for a slain ostrich.

Ibn 'Abbas and Abu 'Ubayda are described as adjudging a cow for a wildebeest or wild ass, and 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar and 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf a sheep for a gazelle. A sheep is also given as the redemption for killing pigeons, on the reasoning that pigeons resemble sheep in taking scoops of water when drinking.

Where an animal has no equivalent, Ibn 'Abbas says its amount should be spent in Makkah in charity, as Al-Bayhaqi records. Another account describes the obligation to compensate with equivalent value where there is no comparable animal of flock, as with a small bird or locusts.

Ibn 'Abbas also gives a scale in the report from 'Ali bin Abi Talhah. For an antelope, a sheep is slaughtered in Makkah, and if he cannot, he feeds six poor people, or else fasts three days. For a deer, a cow, and if unable, feeding twenty poor people or fasting twenty days. For an ostrich or zebra, a camel, or feeding thirty poor people, or fasting thirty days. Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Jarir record the statement, and in Ibn Jarir's narration the measure is a mudd, four handfuls, for each poor person.

### How did 'Umar apply the two judges?

Ibn Jarir records Abu Jarir al-Bajali saying that he killed a deer while in ihram and mentioned it to 'Umar, who told him to bring two of his brothers and let them judge him. He went to 'Abdur-Rahman and Sa'd, and they said he should offer a male sheep.

Ibn Jarir also records Tariq saying that Arbad killed a deer in ihram and went to 'Umar for judgment. 'Umar said that the two of them would judge together, and they judged that Arbad should offer a goat fed on abundant water and grass. 'Umar then quoted the wording about two just men among you.

### What further legal detail is given?

The discussion sets out eleven points. A person in ihram may eat lawful game hunted by someone else outside ihram and outside the sacred precincts, provided he was not an assistant, adviser or guide in the hunt. That is what the hadith states, and the wording do not kill rather than do not eat points the same way.

Compensation is obligatory for killing land game in the sacred precincts intentionally, and, as deduced in Ruh al-Ma'ani, also when done by mistake or forgetfulness. The compensation obligatory the first time remains equally obligatory on a second or third killing.

The substance of the compensation is that, after determining the time and place of the killing, the person obtains an estimate of the animal's cost from two just people, which is better, though one just person suffices. If the animal killed is inedible, the obligatory cost is not more than the cost of a she goat. If it is edible, the whole of the estimate is obligatory.

Three options then follow. He may buy an animal at that price, meeting the conditions governing a sacrificial animal, slaughter it properly within the sacred precincts and distribute it to the poor and needy. Or he may give grain equivalent to the price, according to the conditions of sadaqat al-fitr, at half a sa' for each poor and needy person. Or he may keep as many fasts as the number of poor people the grain would have reached at that rate.

Distribution of the grain and the keeping of fasts are not restricted to the sacred precincts. If the obligatory amount is less than half a sa', he may give it to one poor person or keep a fast, and the same choice applies to any remainder after distributing at that rate. Half a sa' is given as about one and three quarter seers, roughly one and six tenths kilograms, usually rounded up as a precaution.

Instead of giving the shares of grain arrived at in the estimate, feeding that number of poor people two meals of the day to their fill is also permissible. If an animal matching the estimate is slaughtered and an amount is left over, he may buy another animal with it, give grain against it or fast in proportion to what the grain would have distributed.

Where an animal is wounded rather than killed, an estimate determines how much its price has fallen because of the wound, and the same three options apply to that amount. An animal whose hunting is unlawful for a person in ihram is also unlawful to slaughter, and if he slaughters it, its legal status is that of carrion. The wording do not kill hints that slaughter is like killing.

If the animal was killed in a forest, the estimate is made by reference to the nearest habitation. Pointing at game, guiding someone to it or assisting in the hunt is as unlawful as hunting itself.

### What is the difference between the past and repetition?

Ibn Jurayj records asking 'Ata' about the meaning of Allah having forgiven what is past, and being told it means the period of ignorance. Asked about whoever commits it again, 'Ata' said that whoever commits the offence again in Islam, Allah will take retribution from him and he must also pay the expiation.

Asked whether he knew of any punishment for repeating the offence, 'Ata' said no. Asked whether the authorities should punish such a person, he said no, because it is a sin between him and Allah and he should pay the expiation. Ibn Jarir records the exchange.

Another explanation says the taking of retribution refers to the expiation itself. This is attributed to Sa'id bin Jubayr, to 'Ata' and to the majority of earlier and later generations, who state that when a person in ihram kills game, expiation becomes necessary whether it is the first, second or third offence and whether intentional or in error.

### Why is the test set in this form?

The real purpose of the order is that Allah may know those who fear Him even though they cannot see Him. Allah has placed human beings in this world while concealing Himself from their eyes, and He wants to see who, without seeing Him, is so conscious of reality that he behaves righteously as though he saw Him with all His powers.

The contrast is with those who, not finding Allah before them, become fearless and behave as they please. This test is experienced not only during the short period of pilgrimage but daily in human relations.

A person may gain such control over another that he could threaten his life, ruin him financially or falsely defame and degrade him. Another, holding the same control and opportunity, keeps silent and stays his hand out of fear of Allah.

Of the two, the second has proved that he fears Allah without seeing Him, and the first has proved the opposite. Countless divine blessings are stored for the one, and terrible punishment for the other.

### What does the inward reading add?

Hunting is forbidden to the ritually consecrated because such a person intends to visit the Ka'bah. The allusion drawn is that when someone aims for Allah's house and turns his face toward the eminent and holy Ka'bah, hoping to be in His neighbourhood, at least the game of the desert should be safe from him.

By his undertaking he has joined the crowd of the pious and the good, and the attribute of the pious is that they do not harm an ant and do not harbour hidden thoughts of evil.

Ritual consecration is then described as being of two kinds: the consecration of the pilgrim in the body and the consecration of one who recognises Allah in the heart. While the pilgrim is consecrated in the body, game is forbidden to him. While the recognizer is consecrated in the heart, seeking, wanting and free choice are forbidden to him.

Three marks of the heart's consecration are given: to be on loan to the creatures, to be a stranger to oneself and to be at ease from attachment. Its fruit is three things today and three tomorrow. Today it is the sweetness of whispered prayer, the birth of wisdom and the soundness of insight. Tomorrow it will be the light of contemplation, the call of gentleness and the cup of wine.

The inward reading does not cancel the outward ruling. It explains why a state of consecration restricts even the taking of harmless game, since the person in it has undertaken to be harmless.

### In short

- Game may not be killed in ihram or in the sacred precinct, with stated exemptions for harmful creatures and water game.
- Intentional killing requires an equivalent animal judged by two just men, brought to the Ka'bah and distributed to the poor there.
- Feeding the needy or fasting replaces the animal where there is no equivalent, and the detailed rulings cover estimation, measures, wounding, assisting and the status of slaughtered game.
- Expiation is required whether the killing was intentional or in error, and the past is forgiven while repetition brings retribution.
- The rule trains a person to fear Allah unseen, which is the same discipline required in daily relations of power.
