<<<VERSE 5:94>>>
"O believers! Allah will surely test you with game within the reach of your hands and spears to distinguish those who fear Him in secret. Whoever transgresses from now on will suffer a painful punishment"

This verse tells the believers that Allah will test them with game that comes within reach of their hands and their spears, so that He may know who fears Him in secret. It warns that whoever transgresses after this will suffer a painful punishment.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Allah will surely test you with game within the reach of your hands and spears."** The material explains that the test came during the pilgrimage to Hudaybiyyah, when the Muslims were in the state of ihram. Wild game and birds came close to their camp in numbers they had never seen. The material says the young and weak game could be caught with the hands, and the mature game with the spears. Ibn Abbas and Mujahid are reported to have said so.

**"To distinguish those who fear Him in secret."** The material says the purpose of the test is to see who fears Allah when He is not seen. A believer who holds back from the game when he could easily take it shows a fear of Allah that is real. The material cites 67:12, which speaks of those who fear their Lord in the unseen, for whom there is forgiveness and a great reward.

**"Whoever transgresses from now on will suffer a painful punishment."** The material says that after the prohibition has been set, whoever disobeys it will receive a painful punishment. The material reports from As-Suddi that the warning is for disobedience of the command.

### The inner test

The material explains that the test is not only in the pilgrimage. A person may have power over another, to harm his life, his wealth or his honour, and it is the one who holds back out of fear of Allah who has truly passed the test. The material says that the former will have a painful punishment, and the latter the blessings of Allah.

### The creatures that may be killed in ihram

The material gives the hadith that A'ishah reported, that the Prophet ﷺ said five creatures are fawasiq and may be killed in ihram or outside it: the crow, the kite, the scorpion, the mouse and the rabid dog. Ibn Umar reported the same five, and Ayyub reported from Nafi' that the snake is also allowed, since its killing is permitted. The material says that the rule for the rabid dog extends to the wolf, the lion, the leopard and the tiger, since they are more dangerous. Abu Sa'id reported a similar list, and Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah recorded these reports. The material says that these exceptions show that the prohibition in verse 95 covers the game that is not excepted by the Prophet ﷺ.

### In short

- The test at Hudaybiyyah was game that came close to the believers in the state of ihram.
- The purpose is to see who fears Allah when He is unseen.
- Transgression after the prohibition brings a painful punishment.

<<<VERSE 5:95>>>
"O believers! Do not kill game while on pilgrimage. Whoever kills game intentionally must compensate by offering its equivalence, as judged by two just men among you, to be offered at the Sacred House, or by feeding the needy, or by fasting so that they may taste the consequences of their violations. Allah has forgiven what has been done. But those who persist will be punished by Allah. And Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment"

This verse forbids the believers to kill game while they are on pilgrimage. Whoever kills game deliberately must make compensation, by an animal equal to what he killed as judged by two just men, to be offered at the Sacred House, or by feeding the needy, or by fasting. Allah forgave what was done before, but whoever persists will be punished.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Do not kill game while on pilgrimage."** The material explains that the prohibition covers the state of ihram and the sacred area around the Haram. The material says the word for killing, taqtulu, is used here and not the word for eating, so the prohibition is on killing, and eating from game killed by others is allowed for a pilgrim who did not help in the hunt. The material says the same rule is in the hadith.

**"Whoever kills game intentionally must compensate by offering its equivalence, as judged by two just men among you."** The material says that the expiation is due for a deliberate killing, and according to the majority also for a killing by mistake or by forgetting. The equivalent is set by two just Muslims. The material gives reports of the Companions: Umar told a man who had killed a deer to bring two of his brethren to judge him, and the judgement was a goat. The material says Az-Zuhri said the Qur'an mentions the expiation for deliberate killing and the sunnah extends it to the forgetful. Camels are the equivalent of ostriches, cows of wild cattle, and goats of deer. Ibn Abbas is reported to have said that where there is no equivalent, its value should be given in Makkah as charity.

**"To be offered at the Sacred House."** The material says the animal must be brought to the Ka'bah in the sacred area, slaughtered there, and its meat divided among the poor of the sacred area. The material says there is consensus on this.

**"Or by feeding the needy, or by fasting."** The material says that if the pilgrim cannot offer an equivalent, he may feed the poor. Ibn Abbas is reported to have given a scale: For an antelope, a sheep or six poor people fed, or three days of fasting. For a deer, a cow or twenty poor fed or twenty days of fasting. For an ostrich, a camel or thirty poor fed or thirty days of fasting. The material says the measure of food for each poor person is a mudd. The material says the weight of half a sa' is about 1.62 kilograms and that the expiation may be paid in grain at that rate.

**"So that they may taste the consequences of their violations."** The material says the expiation is so that the person feels the weight of his error.

**"Allah has forgiven what has been done. But those who persist will be punished by Allah."** The material explains that what was done before the prohibition, in the time of ignorance, is forgiven. Whoever repeats the offence after the prohibition is punished by Allah, and must pay the expiation again. The material reports that Ata' said there is no punishment from the authorities for it, since it is a sin between the person and Allah, and he must pay the expiation.

**"And Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment."** The material says that Allah is the one who requites the wrongdoer.

### The reading of the verse

The material says that the verse was revealed about a man from the Companions who killed game and forgot that he was in ihram. The reports say that the Companions in the time of the Prophet ﷺ took the same rule for those who forgot. The material says the expiation is due in every case, whether first, second or third, deliberate or accidental.

### In short

- The pilgrim in ihram must not kill game, and whoever kills it deliberately must make compensation.
- The compensation is an equal animal judged by two just men, brought to the Ka'bah, or the feeding of the poor, or fasting.
- Allah forgave what was done in the time of ignorance, and whoever repeats the offence will be punished.
