<<<VERSE 7:96>>>
The surah names the condition that would have changed everything for those towns. "Had the people of those societies been faithful and mindful ˹of Allah˺, We would have overwhelmed them with blessings from heaven and earth. But they disbelieved, so We seized them for what they used to commit".

### Phrase by phrase

**"Had the people of those societies been faithful and mindful ˹of Allah˺"** sets the condition on two things, iman and taqwa. The first is that the hearts accept what the messenger brought, believe him and obey him, and the second is that they carry out what was commanded and keep away from what was forbidden.

**"We would have overwhelmed them with blessings from heaven and earth"** gives what the two would have brought. The blessing spoken of is barakah, and the word means increase and growth. The blessing of the heaven is the rain that comes down from it, and the blessing of the earth is the vegetation and the fruit that come up out of it. Beyond the rain and the produce, the expression covers every means of prosperity, and above all a carefree enjoyment of what a person owns, with no anxiety spoiling the pleasure of it.

Barakah shows itself in two ways, and the reports of the Prophet ﷺ carry an instance of the first of them. Jabir ibn Abdallah reported that on the day of the trench a thousand of the companions were at work and had no food with them. He told the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that there was a small measure of barley and one sheep, and asked what he was to do. The instructions were to grind the barley and make it into dough, to slaughter the sheep and clean it, and to set a pot on the fire. Mustafa ﷺ then placed his blessed hands on the dough, moistened a finger with his mouth and passed it over the top of the pot. The companions were then called group by group, and bread was cooked with the dough. They ate from the pot until a thousand men had eaten, and there was still some left over. The point of the report is that barakah gets the work done and not quantity. In one of them the thing itself grows in quantity, as is reported of the Prophet ﷺ, that a large number of people drank their fill from a small pot of water, and a whole army ate to satisfaction from a small amount of food. In the other the quantity stays as it is and the usefulness of the thing is multiplied many times over. A single morsel of food can become a source of great strength and health, while on another occasion a large amount of food does nothing. A person can do a great deal of work in one short hour, and at another time the same work cannot be finished in four or five hours. In such cases the food and the time have not increased, and what has increased is their effect.

What the words of the verse say is this. Barakah in the things of heaven and of earth comes by faith in Allah and by taqwa, and where the two are absent the barakah is withheld. Faith is set here against kufr, and a town that believed was given good in this world on account of what it believed.

There is a case for that in the stories of the messengers. No town believed as a whole after seeing the punishment, except the town of Yunus. When his people believed, the torment of disgrace was lifted from them in the life of this world, and they were allowed to enjoy themselves for a while (10:98). The Book says of that messenger that he was sent to a hundred thousand or more, and that they believed, and so they were given their enjoyment for a time (37:147-148). Every other township that was sent a warner took the same road, and the warners were denied (34:34). So the opening of this verse is not an empty supposition. The people of one town did what the others did not, and the difference in their end is on record. Take the world as it stands. The produce of the earth is greater than it has ever been, and inventions serve our comfort in ways past generations could not have imagined. Yet a man in the middle of all that abundance is worried, low and dissatisfied, and further from peace and comfort than those who came before him. Nothing explains that except that barakah has gone out of these things.

**"But they disbelieved, so We seized them for what they used to commit"** states the outcome. They gave the lie to the messengers and the Books, and they were seized on account of what they used to earn, with drought, with a lack of fruit and with punishment.

### Wealth as favour and wealth as anger

Prosperity, good health and possessions are not always a sign that Allah is pleased with a person. They are sometimes given out of anger, which is what verse 44 of Surah Al-An'am says, that when the people forgot what they had been reminded of, the doors of everything good were opened to them, and then they were caught suddenly by the punishment of Allah (6:44). So wealth and ease are not reliable signs of favour, and they can be a sign of wrath.

What this verse adds is that barakah in the things of heaven and earth is a sign of favour. Wealth given against a person's sins is usually short-lived and marks displeasure, while wealth given to a reward of iman and taqwa carries lasting benefit. The two look so much alike that telling them apart is difficult. The men of knowledge have given signs for it. When prosperity and good health make a person more grateful to Allah and more given to worship than before, that is a mark of favour. When they push him deeper into sin, that is a mark of wrath, and shelter is sought from that state.

### In short

- The verse makes the coming of good things conditional, and the two conditions are faith and taqwa.
- Barakah means increase and growth, and it works either by growing the thing itself or by multiplying the good it does.
- Blessings from heaven and earth cover the rain from above, the produce from below, and a settled freedom from anxiety over what one owns.
- The report of the Prophet ﷺ shows a whole army fed from a little food, which is the first kind of barakah.
- Where faith and taqwa are absent, abundance can still be given, and that is the wealth that comes as anger rather than as favour.
- The sign that separates the two is what the wealth does to a person, since gratitude marks favour and deeper sin marks wrath.

<<<VERSE 7:97>>>
The next two verses take up the false confidence of the same people. "Did the people of those societies feel secure that Our punishment would not come upon them by night while they were asleep"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Did the people of those societies feel secure"** addresses the people of the towns, and in the plainest reading it is the people of Makkah who are meant, since they were the ones being warned by these stories. The question carries a rebuke, and the mention of the towns recalls the peoples whose end had just been told, of Nuh, of Ad, of Thamud and of the rest.

**"that Our punishment would not come upon them by night while they were asleep"** names one of the two times a man is most helpless. The word used here for the coming by night is bayatan, which is the arrival of a thing on a people in the darkness while they are unaware of it. Verse 97 of this surah puts that half of the question, and the verse that follows it, verse 99, gathers both halves into one and answers them. The word used for coming by night carries the sense of a raid in the dark. The picture is of people lying asleep and completely unaware of anything approaching.

**Where the surah is going with the question.** The verse is a warning put to the world. The words say the same to every generation that follows, and to the people of the Prophet's own time first of all. The residents of those towns lived unmindful of one fact. The punishment of Allah can take a people at any hour, when they are sleeping at night or when they are taken up with their affairs. The peoples described in the verses before were destroyed by night and by day. What happened to them should have been enough to teach those who came after them to avoid the things that brought death and disaster on those who went before.

### In short

- The question is put to the people of Makkah and to the people of the world, since the warning belongs to all who live after those towns.
- One of the two hours named is the night, and the word used for it carries the sense of a raid in the dark.
- The picture is of people asleep and unaware of anything coming towards them.
- The verse rebukes a false confidence, since what the earlier peoples suffered shows that the punishment of Allah comes at any hour.
- The lesson is that a person should take warning from what happened to others and keep away from what destroyed them.

<<<VERSE 7:98>>>
The second half of the question follows. "Or did they feel secure that Our punishment would not come upon them by day while they were at play"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Or did they feel secure"** puts the same rebuke a second time, and the two questions together close off every hour of the day.

**"that Our punishment would not come upon them by day"** names the daytime, and the word here is the forenoon, the part of the morning when a person is at his work.

**"while they were at play"** describes the state they were caught in. It covers the people busy with their own affairs and unaware of anything else, and it covers the part of it that is play, since the verse says of some that they were amused forgetfully, taken up with the life of this world and its falsehood.

So the two verses cover sleeping and waking, the night and the day, and no hour is left in which a person may consider himself beyond reach. The question is put as a rebuke of the feeling, and the answer is implied in the fact that whole nations had been taken in exactly those hours.

### In short

- The second question completes the first, so that the two together cover the whole of the day.
- The daytime named here is the forenoon, the hours of work and busyness.
- The state described is one of being at play, whether in the sense of being absorbed in one's own affairs or in the sense of being taken up with amusement and falsehood.
- No hour is left unmentioned, and the earlier nations were taken in just such hours.
- The rebuke falls on the false confidence itself rather than on the hours.

<<<VERSE 7:99>>>
The two questions are now drawn into one and answered. "Did they feel secure against Allah’s planning? None would feel secure from Allah’s planning except the losers"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Did they feel secure against Allah’s planning?"** is the summary of both. The word for planning is used of His drawing people on by degrees, through favours and ease, and then seizing them suddenly. That is why the increase of a people's wealth and strength was never safe ground for confidence. The favours arrive as a test and not as a warrant.

**"None would feel secure from Allah’s planning except the losers"** is the answer, and it turns the confidence back on the person who holds it. Al-Hasan Al-Basri said that the believer performs acts of worship while he is in fear, in fright and anxiety, and that the wicked one commits acts of disobedience while feeling safe from the punishment of Allah.

The point was put even more sharply in another place. Nasrabadi said that a sinner has no ground for feeling secure from the drawing on by degrees, and asked which sin is greater than the sin of the man who takes note of something of his own acts. He meant that such a person is laying claim to a share in what belongs to the Lord alone, and that is a contention with His oneness.

So fear is the mark of the one who believes, and the sense of safety is the mark of the one who has lost. What the words of the verse say is plain enough on its face. The people who were given strength and abundance in this world are the ones who should look at those gifts as a test, and the ones who succeed are those who are not made proud by what they are given, but see the generosity of Allah in it and give thanks.

### In short

- The word for planning here means drawing a people on by degrees with favours and then taking them suddenly.
- The two earlier questions are gathered into one and the false confidence itself is answered.
- No one is safe from that drawing on except the people who are lost, since safety from it is itself the mark of the losing side.
- The believer worships in fear and anxiety, while the wicked one disobeys while feeling safe.
- The man who feels secure is claiming something that belongs to the Lord alone, which is the sharpest form of the sin.
- The blessings given to a people are a test, and the successful ones are those who see the generosity behind them and give thanks.

<<<VERSE 7:100>>>
The lesson is put to the people who now stand where the destroyed peoples stood. "Is it not clear to those who take over the land after ˹the destruction of˺ its former residents that—if We will—We can punish them ˹too˺ for their sins and seal their hearts so they will not hear ˹the truth˺"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Is it not clear to those who take over the land after ˹the destruction of˺ its former residents"** addresses the inheritors of the land, and the plainest instance of such inheritors in the time of the Prophet ﷺ were the people of the land of Makkah. Verse 100 of this surah puts the question to them. The words say that the events just told should have carried the lesson home. The word used here carries the sense of guiding or informing, and the events that have just been told are made the subject of it. The verse means that those events should serve as a lesson and as a means of guidance for the generations who took the land over from those who held it before.

**"that—if We will—We can punish them ˹too˺ for their sins"** is the teaching itself. The people who came after can be caught by the punishment of Allah just as their ancestors were caught, and for the same reason. Ibn Abbas explained the words as asking whether it had not been made clear to them that if Allah willed, He would punish them because of their sins, and Mujahid and several others said the same.

Abu Ja'far bin Jarir At-Tabari set out the sense at length. Allah asks whether He has not made it clear to those who succeeded the earlier nations on the earth. Those successors followed their own ways, and they behaved as those before them had behaved. If He willed, He would bring on them the same end that was decreed for those who preceded them.

**"and seal their hearts so they will not hear ˹the truth˺"** names the further punishment. To seal is to place a cover over the heart. The ones who take no lesson from what happened to those before them are made unable to listen to the truth, so that words of advice and reminder do not reach them.

### What the inheritors of the land should see

The pattern repeats itself, and it should be read as a sign. A community reaches a high level of honour and prosperity, and then it declines, is removed from the scene, and another community takes the same place it had. That rise and fall is a sign of God. It should remind a person of His supremacy, since all the strings of gain and loss are in the hands of a supreme being. He may give good fortune to one and take it away from another.

The powers of observation and of thought that a person has been given are enough to draw the conclusion. If the real source of power were in any other hands, the group that had once reached ascendancy would never have allowed others to replace it. A person who learns this finds nourishment in the rise and fall of nations.

What happens instead is that the new community takes its own rise as proof about itself. When one community falls and another rises in its place, the members of the rising one assume that what happened to the previous community was a fate particular to that community, and that it will never happen to them. Those are the people the verse addresses, since they are not learning from the ruin they have walked past, and in some cases they are copying the very behaviour that brought it about.

The contrast between the two groups runs through the whole passage. On one side are those who walk through the dwellings of the destroyed peoples without understanding, and on the other are those who take the lesson. The Book makes the point in another place, that there is guidance in the many generations destroyed before them, in whose dwellings they walk about, and it asks whether they will not then listen (20:128 and 32:26). The same point is carried in the words addressed to a people who lived in the houses of men who had wronged themselves (14:44-45). It is carried again in the question of whether any of those generations can still be seen, or whether the least whisper of them can be heard (19:98). So the ruins the people passed each day were the plainest sign, and the warning came to them in the very ground they walked on.

### In short

- The verse turns the lesson on the people who inherited the land from those who were destroyed.
- If Allah wills, He can punish the later people for their sins just as He punished the earlier ones.
- The sealing of the heart comes after a people refuse the lesson, and it leaves them unable to hear the truth.
- The rise of one community as another falls is a sign that all gain and loss is in the hand of God.
- The rising community tends to take the fate of its predecessor as a fate peculiar to them, and the Book points out the error.
- Ruins the people walked past every day were the plainest warning they had, and the Book asks whether any of those generations can be seen or heard.
