<<<VERSE 7:96>>>
After the stories of destroyed peoples, the surah states the rule behind them: "Had the people of those societies been faithful and mindful ˹of Allah˺, We would have overwhelmed them with blessings from heaven and earth. But they disbelieved, so We seized them for what they used to commit". Blessings come with faith, and disbelief brings punishment. Allah points here to how little faith there was among the towns to which He sent messengers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Had the people of those societies been faithful"**: had the people of the towns that were destroyed believed in the scriptures and messengers sent to them. Had their hearts accepted what the messenger brought, believed him and obeyed him.

**"and mindful ˹of Allah˺"**: had they had taqwā, doing acts of obedience and keeping away from what is forbidden. Had they guarded against disbelief, idolatry and indecency, repented, followed His commands, and given up their disbelief and disobedience.

**"We would have overwhelmed them with blessings from heaven and earth."** Literally, "We would have opened blessings upon them". The verb can be read la-fataḥnā or la-fattaḥnā. The blessings from heaven are the rain that falls from the sky, and those from the earth are its plants and fruits. Allah would have opened the doors of good to them in every way.

**"But they disbelieved"**: they denied His messengers and scriptures and rejected what their prophets brought.

**"so We seized them for what they used to commit"**: Allah punished them and destroyed them for the sins and wickedness they earned. He struck them with drought, failed fruits and punishment, overtaking them suddenly.

### Did any town believe as a whole?

Elsewhere Allah says:

> "If only there had been a society which believed ˹before seeing the torment˺ and, therefore, benefited from its belief, like the people of Jonah. When they believed, We lifted from them the torment of disgrace in this world and allowed them enjoyment for a while" (10:98).

This shows that no town believed as a whole except the town of the Prophet Yūnus (Jonah), whose people believed once the punishment was upon them. Of Yūnus, Allah says, "We ˹later˺ sent him ˹back˺ to ˹his city of˺ at least one hundred thousand people who then believed ˹in him˺, so We allowed them enjoyment for a while" (37:147-148). Another verse says, "Whenever We sent a warner to a society, its elite would say, “We truly reject what you have been sent with.”" (34:34).

### What is barakah, and how does it show itself?

The Arabic word barakah, translated "blessing", means increase or growth. "Blessings from heaven and earth" covers every means of prosperity: proper and timely rain from the clouds, rich and healthy crops, and above all the untroubled enjoyment of one's possessions, with no worry to spoil the pleasure. Everything would have been touched with barakah.

Barakah shows itself in two ways. Sometimes the thing itself grows in quantity. So it is reported of the Prophet ﷺ that many people drank from a small pot of water and were satisfied, or that a whole army ate its fill from a small amount of food. On the Day of the Trench (Khandaq), a thousand of the Messenger's Companions were working and grew hungry, but there was no food. Jābir ibn ʿAbdallāh told the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that they had half a bushel of oats and one sheep, and asked what he should do. The Prophet ﷺ told them to grind the oats, make dough, slaughter and clean the sheep, and put a pot on the fire. Al-Muṣṭafā came and placed his blessed hands on the dough. He touched the pot's rim with a finger moistened from his mouth. Then the Companions were called, group after group. Bread was baked from the dough and they ate from the pot until a thousand men had eaten, and there was still food left over.

At other times the quantity does not grow, but its usefulness is multiplied. Often one thing in a household lasts as long, or serves as many people, as three or four others of the same kind would. Some things bring lasting benefit, while others hardly serve at all, because they are spoilt by some accident or are not at hand when needed. A single mouthful of food may give great strength and health, while at another time a large meal does no good. In one short hour we may get a great deal done, while at another time the same work can hardly be done in four or five hours. In these cases the food and the time did not increase, but their effect and benefit were multiplied many times over.

### Why does a world of plenty feel so poor?

The verse implies that barakah in all things of heaven and earth comes through faith in Allah and through taqwā, and that without these two a person is deprived of it. Look at the world today. The earth's produce is far greater than ever before. Modern inventions serve us in ways past generations could not have imagined. Yet despite this abundance of means and resources, people are more worried, depressed and dissatisfied than ever, and more deprived of peace and comfort. What has robbed them of peace? There is no explanation except that barakah has gone out of these things.

### Is prosperity always a sign of Allah's favour?

Prosperity, health and worldly possessions are not necessarily signs of Allah's favour. Sometimes they are given in anger, as Sūrat al-Anʿām makes clear:

> "When they became oblivious to warnings, We showered them with everything they desired. But just as they became prideful of what they were given, We seized them by surprise, then they instantly fell into despair" (6:44).

So wealth and plenty are not sure signs of Allah's favour. Sometimes they are signs of His anger. But this verse shows that barakah in the things of heaven and earth is a sign of His favour. The difference is this. Prosperity and health are sometimes given to people despite their sins and transgressions. Then they are usually short-lived and a sign of Allah's displeasure. At other times they are given with lasting benefit, as a reward for īmān and taqwā. Since the two look alike, it is hard to tell which is which.

The friends of Allah have suggested clear signs to tell them apart. When prosperity and health make a person more grateful to Allah and more devoted in worship, it is a sign of His favour. When they draw a person further into sin, it must be a sign of His anger. We seek refuge with Allah from such a state.

### The inner reading: the blessing of heaven and the blessing of earth

Had the people of the towns believed, that is, had they accepted the truth of My promise, and been mindful, wary of opposing Me, I would have lit up their hearts with contemplation of Me. That is the blessing of heaven. And I would have adorned their limbs with service to Me. That is the blessing of earth. He calls the heart's contemplation the blessing of heaven because the heart belongs to the heavenly world, whose root is light. He calls the service of the limbs the blessing of earth because the body belongs to the earthly world, whose root is dust.

"We would have opened blessings upon them" hints that what counts is not quantity but blessing. He did not say He would multiply their favours. He said He would put blessing into the favours they receive. The meal at the Trench shows this: it is blessing that gets the work done, not quantity.

### In short

- Had the towns believed and been mindful of Allah, He would have opened to them blessings of rain and harvest.
- Only the people of Yūnus believed as a whole and were spared. The rest denied and were seized for their sins.
- Barakah may multiply a thing itself or multiply its benefit, and it comes with faith and taqwā.
- Prosperity that makes a person more grateful is a favour. Prosperity that leads deeper into sin is a sign of Allah's anger.

<<<VERSE 7:97>>>
Having stated the rule, Allah warns against defying His commands and daring to break His prohibitions: "Did the people of those societies feel secure that Our punishment would not come upon them by night while they were asleep". The next three verses (7:97-99) warn the people of the world.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Did the people of those societies feel secure"**: the disbelievers among them, those who reject the truth in these cities. One explanation says it means the people of Makkah.

**"that Our punishment would not come upon them"**: Our torment and Our exemplary punishment.

**"by night while they were asleep"**: the word is bayātan, by night, a night raid, while they sleep, completely unaware of it.

### Who is being warned?

The people of the towns in the Prophet's ﷺ own time seemed to forget that Allah's punishment could seize them at any time while they slept at night. The fate of the earlier peoples described in the preceding verses should have been a lesson to them. A wise person learns from what happened to others, and avoids the deeds that led them to death and ruin.

### In short

- Allah asks whether the deniers felt safe from a punishment that could come by night as they slept.
- The warning is aimed at the Prophet's ﷺ contemporaries and all who follow, who should learn from the destroyed peoples.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:98>>>
The warning continues with the other half of the day: "Or did they feel secure that Our punishment would not come upon them by day while they were at play". Night or day, asleep or busy, there is no hour when they are beyond Allah's reach.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Or did they feel secure"**: or did the people of the towns feel safe from Allah's punishment overtaking them? In one reading the word is a-wa-amina, "and did they feel secure", and in another aw amina, "or did they feel secure".

**"that Our punishment would not come upon them by day"**: literally in the forenoon, the middle of the morning.

**"while they were at play"**: while busy with their affairs and unaware, amusing themselves forgetfully, wrapped up in the life of this world. One explanation says it means engaged in falsehood.

### Why mention sleep and play?

They should not feel safe from Allah's punishment, which may come suddenly on any day while they are busy with their worldly pursuits. Sleep and play are the two states in which people feel most at ease and least on guard. Naming them shows that the punishment can come precisely when no one expects it.

### In short

- The deniers are asked whether they felt safe from punishment in broad daylight, while busy with play and worldly affairs.
- Punishment can come in the very hours when people feel most at ease, so no time is safe for those who defy Allah.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:99>>>
The questions end with the deepest one: "Did they feel secure against Allah’s planning? None would feel secure from Allah’s planning except the losers". What made them so fearless of Allah's plan?

### Phrase by phrase

**"Did they feel secure against Allah’s planning?"** Allah's planning (makr) here is His punishment and retribution, His ability to bring ruin on them in their moments of carelessness. One explanation says it is His drawing them on by degrees through His favours and then seizing them suddenly. Another calls it His flawless ordering of things. Look at the blessings of strength and plenty Allah has given them, and how He gave them as a test.

**"None would feel secure from Allah’s planning except the losers"**: only those who are doomed to perish, the disbelievers, feel safe from it. The successful fear His planning. They are not dazzled or made proud by what Allah has given them. They see His generosity toward them and are thankful to Him.

### How do the believer and the sinner differ?

Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said that the believer does his acts of worship with fear, alarm and anxiety. The wicked person (fājir) commits acts of disobedience while feeling quite safe.

### The inner reading: feeling secure from His deception

Naṣrābādī said: how can a sinner feel safe from deception? And what sin is greater than that of someone who sees something of his own in his deeds? Is that anything but grabbing at Lordship and contending with Allah's Oneness?

### In short

- Allah's "planning" means His punishment, which can come when people are heedless, often after He has drawn them on with favours.
- Only the losers feel secure from it. The successful stay thankful and afraid.
- The believer worships in fear, while the sinner sins at ease. Seeing one's own hand in one's good deeds is itself a grave fault.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 7:100>>>
After telling of the earlier peoples, the surah uses this verse (7:100) to invite the people of Arabia and of the whole world to learn from them: "Is it not clear to those who take over the land after ˹the destruction of˺ its former residents that—if We will—We can punish them ˹too˺ for their sins and seal their hearts so they will not hear ˹the truth˺". They should avoid the deeds that brought Allah's anger, and follow the ways that led the prophets and believers to lasting success.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Is it not clear to those who take over the land after ˹the destruction of˺ its former residents"**: the verb yahdi here means to guide or to inform, and the events just told are its subject. These events should guide and teach later generations, who have inherited the land from its former owners. Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as: did We not make it clear to them? Mujāhid and several others said the same. One explanation says the land is the land of Makkah.

Abū Jaʿfar ibn Jarīr aṭ-Ṭabarī explained: Allah says, did We not make it clear to those who succeeded on the earth after We destroyed the earlier nations who lived there? Those later people then followed the same ways, behaved as their predecessors did and were unruly toward their Lord. Did We not make clear to them what follows?

**"that—if We will—We can punish them ˹too˺ for their sins"**: by bringing on them the same end decreed for those before them, as Allah punished earlier nations. They too can incur Allah's punishment for their disbelief, as their forerunners met ruin for their disobedience. In the Arabic, "that" (an) is a softened form of annahu. The question-words in these verses are rebukes.

**"and seal their hearts"**: the word ṭabʿ means to print or stamp. Allah places a cover over their hearts. They learnt no lesson from past events, and so they brought Allah's anger on themselves and their hearts were sealed.

**"so they will not hear ˹the truth˺"**: they do not hear words of advice and reminder in a way that makes them reflect. They do not hear guidance, and so they do not believe in Muhammad ﷺ or the Qur'an. They do not learn from the destroyed peoples. Instead they copy their behaviour.

### How does a heart become sealed?

The Prophet ﷺ said that when a person commits a sin for the first time, a black spot is placed on his heart. If he sins a second and third time, a second and third spot are added. If he keeps adding sins without repenting to Allah, the black spots spread until the whole heart is blackened. In the end this robs a person of his natural ability to tell right from wrong. He comes to see evil as good and good as evil, the harmful as useful and the useful as harmful.

The Qur'an calls this corruption of understanding rayn, the rust of the heart. In this verse, as in many others, the final stage is called ṭabʿ, sealing.

### Why "hear" and not "understand"?

The result of the sealing is given as "so they will not hear". One might say that "so they will not understand" would fit better, since sealing the heart affects understanding rather than hearing. But the Qur'an says "hear" to show that understanding usually comes from listening to the truth. Once their hearts are sealed, they can no longer listen to it. Another explanation is that the heart governs all the human faculties and limbs. When it goes wrong, every other part works badly too. When a person loves someone or something, he likes everything in it, good or bad.

### Can anyone find a trace of the destroyed peoples?

Allah says elsewhere:

> "Is it not yet clear to them how many peoples We destroyed before them, whose ruins they still pass by? Surely in this are signs for people of sound judgment" (20:128).

Another verse ends, "Surely in this are signs. Will they not then listen" (32:26). Allah also says to the wrongdoers, "Did you not swear before that you would never be removed ˹to the next life˺? You passed by the ruins of those ˹destroyed peoples˺ who had wronged themselves" (14:44-45). And: "˹Imagine˺ how many peoples We have destroyed before them! Do you ˹still˺ see any of them, or ˹even˺ hear from them the slightest sound" (19:98). That is, do you see any of them or hear their voices? Many other verses testify that Allah's punishment strikes His enemies while His bounty reaches His faithful servants.

### What do the rise and fall of nations teach?

Again and again a community rises to great honour and prosperity, then declines and is removed from the scene. Another community takes its place, enjoying the same honour and prosperity. This is a sign from Allah, meant to remind people of His supremacy. It shows that all the strings of gain and loss are in the hands of a Supreme Being, who may give good fortune to one and take it from another.

With the powers of observation and thought Allah has given them, people can easily see this. If the real source of power had not lain elsewhere, the group that once rose to the top would never have let others replace it. Those who learn this lesson find divine nourishment in the rise and fall of nations. Yet whenever one community falls and another rises in its place, the rising community imagines that what happened to its predecessor was a fate peculiar to that people. It assumes the same could never happen to itself.

### In short

- Those who inherit the land from destroyed peoples should see that Allah can punish them for their sins in the same way.
- Unrepented sins blacken the heart spot by spot until it is sealed and can no longer hear the truth.
- The ruins of past peoples, of whom not a whisper remains, are signs for those who will listen.
- Every rising nation should know that the fall of its predecessors could be its own fate too.
