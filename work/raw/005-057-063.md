<<<VERSE 5:57>>>
"O believers! Do not seek the guardianship of those given the Scripture before you and the disbelievers who have made your faith a mockery and amusement. And be mindful of Allah if you are ˹truly˺ believers"

This verse forbids the believers from taking as close friends the People of the Book and the disbelievers who mock the religion of Islam. It ends with a call to taqwa, the fear of Allah, as the sign of true faith.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers!"** The address opens the section with the same warning as 5:51. The material explains that the believers are commanded to keep their own loyalty, and not to seek friendship with those who ridicule their faith.

**"Do not seek the guardianship of those given the Scripture before you."** The material says the People of the Book are named first. Abu Hayyan, in al-Bahr al-Muhit, notes that they were already included among the disbelievers, but they are named separately because they are nearer to Islam than other disbelievers. Few of them embraced Islam, since their pride in their Scripture made them refuse the truth. The mockery of the Muslims was the thing they showed most.

**"And the disbelievers who have made your faith a mockery and amusement."** The material explains that the word for disbelievers here refers to idolaters. Ibn Mas'ud's recitation read it as those who commit shirk. The verse names their conduct as mockery, because they regard the acts of Islam, which bring good in this life and the next, as something to laugh at.

**"And be mindful of Allah if you are truly believers."** The material says that taqwa is the fear of Allah that keeps one from taking enemies as friends. The believer shows that his faith is true by keeping this fear.

### The wider principle

The material cites 22:30, which tells the believers to shun the evil of idols, and it reads the word disbelievers in 5:57 as a clarification of that category. It cites the verse that says believers should not take the disbelievers as friends in place of the believers, and that whoever does so will not be helped by Allah, except when there is a real danger.

The material records that Rifa'ah ibn Zayd and Suwayd ibn al-Harith were among those who showed faith outwardly but were hypocrites, and that Ibn 'Abbas said the verse was revealed about them. Some Muslims used to show them affection, and the verse forbade it. The material also notes that a people who mock sacred things lose the sense of what is right, and the friendship of believers with them is possible only in the course of calling them to the truth.

### In short

- The believers must not take as close friends the People of the Book or the disbelievers who mock Islam.
- The mockery of the Muslims is the thing that marks such people out, and the verse was applied to hypocrites who showed false affection.
- True faith is shown by the fear of Allah that keeps the believer from such friendship.

<<<VERSE 5:58>>>
"When you call to prayer, they mock it in amusement. This is because they are a people without understanding"

This verse describes how the disbelievers mock the call to prayer. It says the reason is that they are a people without understanding.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When you call to prayer."** The call is the adhan, which is the greatest of the acts of worship in public. The material says that the adhan is announced to call people to salah, and that for those with sound minds it is a noble act. The iqamah is the second call, made just before the prayer begins. Az-Zuhri said that Allah mentioned the adhan in His Book, in the verse on the call to prayer.

**"They mock it in amusement."** The material explains that the mockery was made in play and jest. The verse describes the people of the Book and the disbelievers, and the material gives the following reports.

The first report is from al-Kalbi, who said that when the Prophet ﷺ appointed a caller to prayer and the Muslims stood to pray, the Jews mocked them, saying that they had stood up and might never stand up, that they had prayed and might never pray. This verse was revealed in reply.

The second report is from al-Suddi, about a man of the helpers in Madinah. Whenever he heard the caller say that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, he said, may the liar be burnt. One night his servant brought a fire into the house, and a spark set the house alight while the family slept, and all of them died in the fire.

The third report concerns the disbelievers who envied the Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims for the call to prayer. They asked why the Prophet ﷺ had invented a cry like the cry of camels, which the earlier prophets did not use. The material says that Allah answered this in 41:33, which says that no one is better in speech than the one who calls to his Lord, does good, and says he is of those who submit.

### The lack of understanding

The material explains that the reason for their mockery was that they do not understand. Qadi Thanaullah of Panipat, in Tafsir Mazhari, is said to have observed that Allah calls them without understanding even though their intelligence in worldly matters is well known. The material cites 30:7, which says that they know the outward matters of the present life and are heedless of the Hereafter. It concludes that a person may be clever in one field and blind in another.

### In short

- The disbelievers mocked the call to prayer, which is an act of worship.
- The verse was revealed in response to Jews who mocked the Muslims, and to disbelievers who envied the adhan.
- The reason for the mockery is that they lack understanding of what matters most, as 30:7 says.

<<<VERSE 5:59>>>
"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O People of the Book! Do you resent us only because we believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us and what was revealed before—while most of you are rebellious?”"

This verse commands the Prophet ﷺ to ask the People of the Book a question. Do they resent the believers for anything but their faith in Allah, the Qur'an and the earlier books? It says that most of them are rebellious.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, O Prophet."** The command is to speak directly to the People of the Book. The material explains that the address is a challenge to them to find fault with the believers, and that they cannot find any fault except faith.

**"Do you resent us only because we believe in Allah."** The material says that the believers accept Allah and His books, and they do not reject the earlier prophets. The verse brings out the sense that their resentment is baseless.

**"And what has been revealed to us and what was revealed before."** The material records a report from Ibn 'Abbas that a group of Jews asked the Prophet ﷺ about the prophets he believed in. He said that he believed in Allah and what was revealed to him, and in what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob and the tribes, and to Moses and Jesus. He made no distinction between them. When he mentioned Jesus, they rejected his prophethood and said that they knew of no people less fortunate in this world and the next than the Muslims. Allah then revealed this verse. The material cites 2:136 for the statement of the belief of the Prophet ﷺ.

**"While most of you are rebellious."** The material explains that the word most is not all. Some of the People of the Book kept faith and followed the Torah and Injil, and then believed in the Qur'an when it came. The material says that the word most is used to show that many have turned away, and it is connected to the earlier claim that they resent the believers.

### The cause of the resentment

The material cites 85:8 and 9:74 for the same point. In 85:8 the believers are described as those who had no fault but that they believed in Allah, the Almighty, the Praiseworthy. In 9:74, Allah and His Messenger had enriched them by His bounty, and yet some turned against them. The material gives an agreed hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ asked what caused Ibn Jamil to refuse zakah, when Allah had made him rich after he was poor. The material says the refusal of charity is a sign of the rebellious state.

### In short

- The verse commands the Prophet ﷺ to ask the People of the Book what fault they find in the believers.
- The only thing they can name is faith in Allah and His books, and this is no fault.
- The verse says that most of them are rebellious, and that the believers' faith is a reason for their resentment only in their own hearts.

<<<VERSE 5:60>>>
"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Shall I inform you of those who deserve a worse punishment from Allah ˹than the rebellious˺? It is those who earned Allah's condemnation and displeasure—some being reduced to apes and pigs and worshippers of false gods. These are far worse in rank and farther astray from the Right Way.”"

This verse continues the speech to the People of the Book. It asks whether they want to be told of those who deserve a worse punishment. It describes those who earned Allah's condemnation, some of whom were transformed into apes and pigs, and those who worshipped false gods.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Shall I inform you of those who deserve a worse punishment from Allah?"** The material explains that the question is put in the form of a comparison. The Qur'an speaks in this gentle way to avoid direct blame, and the material describes it as a principle of calling people to the truth, that one should not make addressees angry.

**"Those who earned Allah's condemnation and displeasure."** The material says that the curse of Allah means being cast out of His mercy, and His wrath means the anger after which He will not be pleased with them. The material notes that this describes the people who rejected the call of the prophets.

**"Some being reduced to apes and pigs."** The material notes that this transformation is mentioned in Surat Al-Baqarah and again in Surat Al-A'raf. Ibn Mas'ud reported that the Prophet ﷺ was asked whether the monkeys and pigs of the present day were those who had been transformed. He said that Allah never destroyed a people by transforming them and giving them offspring, and that the monkeys and pigs existed before. Muslim recorded it.

**"And worshippers of false gods."** The Arabic word taghut refers to anything that is worshipped besides Allah with its consent. The material says those who worshipped such beings and served them became their servants.

**"These are far worse in rank and farther astray from the Right Way."** The material explains that the word far worse does not mean that the believers are less astray, but that these people are very far astray. The material ends with a description of the true believers, who enjoy the best abode in Paradise.

### The rebuke of the People of the Book

The material reads the verse as a rebuke to the People of the Book, who mocked the religion of Allah, which is the oneness of Allah and worship of Him alone. How could they mock the believers while they had these same characteristics? The verse is a reminder that their own history includes curse and transformation, and that the worship of false gods is a deeper error than anything the believers do.

### In short

- The verse asks the People of the Book whether they want to be told of those who deserve a worse punishment than the rebellious.
- It names those who earned the curse of Allah, some of whom were transformed into apes and pigs, and those who worshipped taghut.
- These people are farther astray than the believers, and the People of the Book who mock the believers share this condition.

<<<VERSE 5:61>>>
"When they come to you ˹believers˺ they say, “We believe.” But they are committed to disbelief when they enter and when they leave. And Allah knows what they hide"

This verse describes hypocrites who come to the believers and say they believe, while they enter and leave in disbelief. Allah knows what they hide in their hearts.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When they come to you they say, 'We believe.'"** The material explains that these people claimed faith in words, when they came to the believers. The verse describes the hypocrites of Madinah, who were impressed by Islam and did not want to face its open enmity.

**"But they are committed to disbelief when they enter and when they leave."** The material says their hearts were steady in the religion of their fathers, and they only made a show of belief. They entered with disbelief and left with it. The material says they did not benefit from the knowledge they heard from the Prophet ﷺ, and that his advice did not move them.

**"And Allah knows what they hide."** The material explains that Allah knows the secrets of the heart, even when the hypocrites pretend otherwise to His creatures. The material says that they forget that their dealings are with God, and that God deals with them based on the reality and not on their words.

### The rabbis and the scholars

The material records that some Jews of Madinah were impressed by Islam, and that the leading rabbis and the scholars of the Torah were busy with the work of religion, living on what their position brought them. The verse concerns those who showed belief while their hearts remained with their old religion, for the sake of their worldly position. The material says that Allah knows this, and that it is folly to think that a show of faith can deceive Him.

### In short

- The hypocrites claim belief when they come to the believers, but they enter and leave with disbelief in their hearts.
- They do not benefit from the knowledge they hear, because their hearts are closed.
- Allah knows what they hide, and they cannot deceive Him by a show of faith.

<<<VERSE 5:62>>>
"You see many of them racing towards sin, transgression, and consumption of forbidden gain. Evil indeed are their actions"

This verse describes the eagerness of many of the People of the Book for sin, transgression and forbidden gain. It calls their conduct evil.

### Phrase by phrase

**"You see many of them racing towards sin."** The material explains that the word many is used to exclude the good ones among them. The word racing shows how habitual their sin had become. The material says a bad deed repeated becomes a fixed habit, so that it is done almost without thought. The same expression is used of the believers in 3:114, who race towards good deeds.

**"Transgression, and consumption of forbidden gain."** The material explains that aggression and the eating of the unlawful are sins, but they are named separately to stress the ruin they bring. The material says the Jews were guilty of unjustly taking the wealth of people through bribes and riba.

**"Evil indeed are their actions."** The material says this is a judgement on their conduct. It calls their deeds evil and horrible, because of the wrong they committed.

### In short

- Many of the People of the Book were eager for sin, transgression and forbidden gain.
- The word racing shows that these evils had become a fixed habit.
- Their deeds are judged evil, and the verse names bribery and riba as examples of the forbidden gain.

<<<VERSE 5:63>>>
"Why do their rabbis and scholars not forbid them from saying what is sinful and consuming what is unlawful? Evil indeed is their inaction"

This verse rebukes the rabbis and scholars for failing to forbid the people from sin and from unlawful gain. It calls their silence evil.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Why do their rabbis and scholars not forbid them?"** The material explains the two names. The Rabbaniyyun are the people of Allah, the scholars in positions of authority. The Ahbar are the scholars of the Torah. The question is one of rebuke, for the leaders were expected to stop the people from wrongdoing.

**"From saying what is sinful and consuming what is unlawful."** The material says that the sin is the lying, false testimony, and the wrongful consumption of wealth. The leaders were guilty because they did not forbid these acts.

**"Evil indeed is their inaction."** The material says the verse ties the duty of forbidding evil to the leaders, as Al-Bahr al-Muhit and Ibn Jarir both note. Ali ibn Abi Talib said in a speech that those before them the earlier nations fell into ruin after their sins went unopposed by their scholars. When they persisted, they were overtaken by punishment. Ibn 'Abbas said that no verse of the Qur'an contains a more severe warning than this one.

### The duty of forbidding evil

The material gives a hadith from Jarir, which Ahmad recorded. The Prophet ﷺ warned that when a people has among them those who commit sins, and others who have the strength to stop them but do not, Allah will send a punishment that reaches them all. Abu Dawud recorded a version in which the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever lives among people committing evil, and does not stop them when he is able to, will be punished by Allah before he dies. Ibn Majah also recorded it.

The material says the duty of enjoining the right and forbidding the wrong is a unique distinction of the Muslim community, and that the Muslims kept their excellence while they held to this duty. When they left it to the state alone, their families and children turned away from the Shari'ah. Ibn Abi Hatim recorded this. The material gives an account from Malik ibn Dinar, that Allah asked the angels to destroy a town where a devout servant lived, because he had never shown anger at sins. The material also gives the example of Yusha ibn Nun, whose story is told in Ibn Jarir's collection, to whom Allah revealed that a hundred thousand of his people would be destroyed, forty thousand of them righteous, because the righteous had eaten and drunk with the wicked and never frowned on their sins.

### In short

- The rabbis and scholars were rebuked for not forbidding the people from lying, false testimony and wrongful gain.
- Ali and Ibn 'Abbas both cited this verse as a severe warning, and Ibn Jarir recorded Ibn 'Abbas's words, and the Prophet ﷺ taught that sins not stopped bring a punishment on all.
- The duty of forbidding wrong is a mark of the Muslim community, and its neglect brings the same ruin the verse describes.
