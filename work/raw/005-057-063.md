<<<VERSE 5:57>>>
The earlier prohibition of intimate alliance is restated with its reason made explicit: "Do not seek the guardianship of those given the Scripture before you and the disbelievers who have made your faith a mockery and amusement." The identifying mark is not another person's religion as such. It is treating the faith of the believers as a joke.

The verse closes by tying this instruction to taqwa. Reverence for Allah is what makes a person unwilling to place his closest loyalty where his religion is ridiculed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"O believers! Do not seek the guardianship of those given the Scripture before you"** addresses those who believe in Allah and follow His Messenger ﷺ. The guardianship meant here is close friendship, seeking their assistance and support, not ordinary fair dealing with them.

**"and the disbelievers who have made your faith a mockery and amusement."** adds the idolaters and other unbelievers. The wording is read in two ways in the recitation, one treating the word for disbelievers as governed by the preceding phrase and the other as a predicate. Both convey the same prohibition.

The meaning of the word here is explained as idolaters. Ibn Jarir records that in the recitation of Ibn Mas'ud the phrase reads as those who associate partners with Allah. Another verse commands shunning the evil of idols (22:30), which shows how the category is identified.

**"And be mindful of Allah if you are ˹truly˺ believers"** joins the command to faith itself. Fear Allah and do not take the enemies of your religion as intimate friends, if you truly believe in the law and religion they mocked.

### Why are two groups named?

Abu Hayyan observes in al-Bahr al-Muhit that the People of the Book were already covered by the general word for disbelievers. Their separate mention is explained by something else. Although they were nearer to Islam than other unbelievers, experience showed that very few of them embraced it.

A survey of those who accepted Islam during and after the age of prophethood is said to show that most came from the common polytheists rather than from the People of the Book. The number from the latter group was much smaller.

The reason given is pride in belonging to a divine faith and scripture. That proud partisanship made acceptance of the truth harder for them, and mockery of Muslims was the attitude they displayed most. When mockery hardens into wickedness, worse things can follow, as the next verse illustrates.

This is an explanation of a historical pattern, not a claim about every individual in those communities. The verse's own test is conduct toward the faith, not ancestry.

### What was the reported occasion?

Ibn 'Abbas says that Rifa'ah ibn Zayd and Suwayd ibn al-Harith displayed faith in Islam while being hypocrites, and that some Muslim men showed affection toward them. This verse was revealed about that situation.

The concern is therefore practical. Close affection for people who privately opposed the faith gave them access to the community's confidence. The instruction protects the believers rather than expressing contempt for anyone's person.

### How does this relate to the earlier ruling?

The prohibition restates the instruction given earlier in the chapter about intimate friendship. That ruling distinguished deep alliance from justice, kindness and fair dealing, and this verse repeats it with the additional reason of mockery.

A related verse forbids believers taking disbelievers as intimate allies in preference to believers, and warns that whoever does so will not be helped by Allah in any way, except where danger from them is genuinely feared. That exception concerns protecting oneself from harm, not adopting their loyalties or approving their ridicule.

The closing command to be mindful of Allah keeps the matter where it belongs. The issue is a person's reverence for his Lord, not merely social strategy.

### In short

- Intimate guardianship is forbidden with those who turn the faith into mockery, whether People of the Book or idolaters.
- The separate mention of the People of the Book is explained by their closeness to Islam and their historical reluctance to accept it.
- The reported occasion concerned hypocrites who received affection from some Muslims.
- Fear of genuine danger is recognised elsewhere, but it does not make ridicule of the faith a basis for loyalty.
- The command is grounded in taqwa rather than in hostility toward people as such.

<<<VERSE 5:58>>>
The mockery is given a concrete example: "When you call to prayer, they mock it in amusement." The adhan announces the greatest act of worship, and it is treated as a joke. The verse explains why: "This is because they are a people without understanding".

### Phrase by phrase

**"When you call to prayer"** refers to the adhan, the call to prayer, and the iqamah, the announcement that prayer is beginning. One explanation says the call is made through both.

**"they mock it in amusement."** They treat it as a joke and a falsehood. The prayer itself is also described as the object of their mockery, with laughing and jesting among themselves.

**"This is because they are a people without understanding"** gives the cause. They understand neither Allah's command nor His oneness nor His religion, and they do not grasp the meaning of worshipping Him or the sacred laws He has made.

### What was said about the call to prayer?

Al-Kalbi relates that when the caller appointed by the Messenger ﷺ made the adhan and the Muslims stood to pray, some Jews mocked them with words wishing that they would never stand, never pray and never bow. That mockery is given as the occasion of the verse.

As-Suddi reports a man among the helpers of Madinah who, on hearing the caller testify that Muhammad ﷺ is the Messenger of Allah, said that the liar should be burnt. One night his servant brought fire into the house while the family slept. A spark set the house alight and it burned down with the man and his family inside.

Tafsir Mazhari, referring to Ibn Abi Hatim, reports a similar account of a Christian in Madinah who used the same words whenever he heard that testimony, until the remark became the cause of his whole family burning to ashes. Another brief account describes a Jewish man poking fun at Bilal's call to prayer, and says Allah burned him.

These reports differ about the man's identity and community. They are preserved as differing accounts of the same warning rather than merged into one version. Their point is that contempt for the call to prayer is a serious matter, not that any individual may take punishment into his own hands.

Other explanations describe a different objection. Some heard the call, resented it and said that the Prophet ﷺ had innovated something unheard of among earlier nations. They argued that if there had been good in it, previous prophets would have had a better right to it, and asked where he had obtained this cry resembling the cry of camels, calling it an ugly sound.

The Qur'an answers that kind of contempt by asking who is better in speech than one who calls to his Lord, does right and says that he is among those who submit (41:33). The call to prayer is thus placed among the best of speech rather than among inventions to be scorned.

### Why is Satan connected with the call?

An agreed hadith describes Satan fleeing when the call to prayer is made, passing wind so that he will not hear it, returning when it ends, fleeing again at the iqamah and returning afterward to interfere with the worshipper's thoughts.

He is described as making a person remember this and that, things not previously on his mind, until the worshipper loses track of how much he has prayed. Whoever cannot remember is told to make two prostrations before the greeting of peace.

Az-Zuhri notes that Allah mentioned the adhan in His book. The hadith and the verse together show that the call is a real event in the spiritual life, resisted by those who hate it and disrupted even for the sincere.

### What kind of understanding is missing?

Qadi Thanaullah of Panipat explains in Tafsir Mazhari that Allah calls them devoid of understanding although their intelligence in worldly matters was well known. A person can be sharp in certain work while his mind does not operate at all in another direction.

The Qur'an describes people who know the outward matters of the present life while being heedless of the Hereafter (30:7). That is the deficiency named here. Technical cleverness and comprehension of worship are different capacities, and the second can be entirely absent in someone admired for the first.

### In short

- The adhan and iqamah announce the greatest act of worship, and mocking them exposes a lack of understanding.
- Reports differ over the identity of the man who cursed the caller, and the differing accounts are preserved.
- The objection that the call was an innovation is answered by the praise of one who calls to his Lord and does right.
- Satan is described as fleeing the call and returning to disturb the worshipper's attention.
- Worldly intelligence does not compensate for heedlessness of the Hereafter.

<<<VERSE 5:59>>>
The believers are told how to answer the contempt: "Do you resent us only because we believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us and what was revealed before". The question exposes the emptiness of the objection. Nothing blameworthy is being alleged except faith itself.

The verse adds a second clause about most of the addressees being rebellious. Its grammatical connection is explained below, and the difference it makes is important.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O People of the Book!"** directs the Messenger ﷺ to address those who mocked the religion from among the People of the Scripture. The reply is not left to impulse. It is given to him.

**"Do you resent us only because we believe in Allah"** asks whether they find any fault beyond belief in Allah alone, without partners. The wording implies that this is no ground for blame at all.

**"and what has been revealed to us and what was revealed before"** includes the Qur'an and the earlier scriptures and messengers. The believers do not deny the previous revelations. They accept them all.

**"while most of you are rebellious?”"** is connected in one explanation to the statement of belief. The meaning is that the believers also hold that most of the addressees have rebelled and strayed from the straight path. Another reading treats it as completing the description of what is resented: their refusal of the faith is itself described as wickedness.

### What prompted this reply?

Ibn 'Abbas reports that a group of Jews asked the Messenger ﷺ which messengers he believed in. He answered that he believed in Allah, in what was revealed to them, in what was revealed to Ibrahim (Abraham), Isma'il (Ishmael), Ishaq (Isaac) and Ya'qub (Jacob) and the tribes, in what Musa (Moses) and 'Isa (Jesus) received, and in what the prophets received from their Lord, making no distinction between any of them and submitting to Him (2:136).

When he mentioned 'Isa, they denied his prophethood. They then said that they knew of no adherents of any religion less fortunate in this world and the next, and no religion more evil than his. This verse was revealed in reply.

Another account describes the same exchange and adds that when 'Isa was mentioned they said they knew of no religion worse than his. The response turns their own words back on them, as the next verse develops.

### Is belief really a fault?

The Qur'an elsewhere describes people whose only fault was that they believed in Allah, the Almighty, worthy of all praise (85:8). Another passage says that people could find no cause for resentment except that Allah and His Messenger ﷺ had enriched them from His bounty (9:74).

An agreed hadith asks what made Ibn Jamil refuse zakah, although he had been poor and Allah made him rich. The examples share one pattern: the objection is not based on any real defect in the faith but on resentment of a blessing.

That is what makes the question here so pointed. When the only alleged crime is belief, the accuser has nothing to accuse.

### Why does the verse say most rather than all?

The explanation notes that some among them remained believers in every condition. Before the coming of the Prophet ﷺ they followed the Torah and the Injil and believed in them. When he came and the Qur'an was revealed, they believed in him and followed its injunctions.

Saying most therefore excludes those people. It does not lump every member of a community into one moral verdict. The verse's charge is directed at rebelliousness, not at ancestry or at a whole people without exception.

### What method of invitation does this show?

The treatment of this passage notes a principle of da'wah, the call to the religion. When a charge had to be made against the very people being addressed, the wording shifted from direct accusation to a description of a comparable case.

That is explained as consideration for the addressee. Prophets did not speak in a way calculated to make people angry before they had heard the point. The reply here follows the same method: it asks a question rather than hurling an insult, and lets the answer convict the objection itself.

### In short

- The believers' only alleged fault is faith in Allah and in every revelation He sent.
- The reported occasion was a question about which messengers the Prophet ﷺ believed in, and the denial of 'Isa's prophethood.
- Similar passages show resentment of blessing rather than any real defect in the faith.
- Most, not all, are described as rebellious, which leaves room for those who believed.
- The reply is framed as a question, following a method of invitation that avoids needless provocation.

<<<VERSE 5:60>>>
The answer continues with a comparison: "Shall I inform you of those who deserve a worse punishment from Allah". The people described are those who earned Allah's curse and wrath, some of whom were transformed, and who worshipped false objects of devotion. Their rank is worse and their distance from the straight path greater.

The verse is a reply to a claim made against the believers, and its form is deliberate. The charge is presented as a description rather than a direct accusation.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Shall I inform you of those who deserve a worse punishment from Allah"** answers their statement that they knew of no religious community more disfavoured than Muhammad ﷺ and his Companions. The question asks whether they want to know who really stands worse with Allah in recompense.

**"It is those who earned Allah’s condemnation and displeasure"** describes people expelled from His mercy and subjected to His anger, after which He is not pleased with them. One gloss explains the curse historically through the imposition of the capitation tax.

**"some being reduced to apes and pigs"** recalls the transformations mentioned in Al-Baqarah and later in Al-A'raf. One account places the transformation into apes in the time of Dawud (David) and that into swine in the time of 'Isa, after people ate from the table sent down from heaven.

**"and worshippers of false gods."** describes those who served taghut and became its servants. The term is explained as any being worshipped besides Allah with its own consent, and the glosses name Satan, idols and soothsayers.

**"These are far worse in rank and farther astray from the Right Way.”"** concludes the comparison. Their abode will be the Fire, and they are further from the path of truth. The wording of worse and further astray answers their claim that no religion was worse than the believers'.

### Does comparison imply that the believers were astray?

No. The explanation notes that the comparative does not mean the other party was less astray. It means that the People of the Scripture described here were far astray. A similar use of comparison appears where the dwellers of Paradise are said to have the best abode and the fairest place of repose on that Day.

The comparative is therefore a rhetorical answer to an insult, not a concession that the believers share the same road at a slightly better position.

### What did the Prophet ﷺ say about the transformed people?

Sufyan Ath-Thawri narrates that Ibn Mas'ud reported the Messenger ﷺ being asked whether the monkeys and pigs then existing were descendants of those transformed. He answered that Allah never destroyed a people by transformation and then gave them offspring or descendants, and that monkeys and pigs existed before that.

Muslim also records this. The answer closes a question about the present animals without denying the historical transformation described in the Qur'an. The two statements are kept distinct rather than merged.

The moral point of the transformation is explained elsewhere in this discussion. Delicate inner feelings such as modesty, breadth of mind and the desire for cleanliness act as guards within a person and restrain him from evil. When these are smothered, the last stage of decline follows.

A group that reaches that stage and is distanced from Allah's grace becomes a curse to itself and to others. Having drifted far from the right path of nature, it begins to live like animals. What restrains a person from his desires is his moral fibre, and when obstinacy and enmity dominate him, his capacity to think is suppressed and nothing remains to resist desire.

Such a person may look human while being inwardly no better than an animal. Someone of deep insight, it is said, can tell what animal is hidden inside the human exterior. This is the moral reading of the comparison, not a description of every individual in any community.

### Why is the charge put indirectly?

The explanation notes that the condition described actually applied to the addressees themselves, and the occasion called for charging them directly. The Qur'an instead turned direct address into a comparison.

That is presented as a distinctive method used by prophets in calling people to the religion: do not put things in a manner that makes the hearers angry before the point can reach them. The indirect form keeps the door open for reflection rather than provoking immediate defiance.

It also makes the argument harder to dodge. The listener is asked to judge a described case, and in judging it he judges himself.

### What does the reply establish about mockery?

Those who claim a monopoly of devotion on the basis of a self-made religion can develop such aversion to a call for pure faith that they lose all sense of proportion. They ridicule things treated as sacred by everyone.

Some people of Madinah did not hesitate to ridicule the adhan. Any relation with such people can be one of conveying the message, never of intimate friendship. Their lack of fear of Allah leads them to regard pure Muslims as criminals while insisting, despite their own crimes, that their dealings are entirely fair.

That insensitivity blunts the conscience where truth and falsehood are distinguished and prevents the state of affairs from being corrected. The verse's answer therefore does more than defend the believers. It identifies the real location of disgrace.

### In short

- The comparison answers the claim that no community was worse than the believers.
- The described people earned Allah's curse and wrath, some were transformed, and some worshipped false objects of devotion.
- The Prophet ﷺ denied that present monkeys and pigs descend from those transformed, while the historical transformation stands.
- The comparative does not imply the believers were astray to a lesser degree.
- The charge is put indirectly, following a method of invitation that avoids provoking anger before the point is heard.

<<<VERSE 5:61>>>
The verse exposes a pretence: "they say, “We believe.” But they are committed to disbelief when they enter and when they leave." What is said at the door is not what is carried in the heart. Allah knows what they hide, and He will deal with them according to reality rather than according to words.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When they come to you ˹believers˺ they say, “We believe.”"** describes people who present faith outwardly. One gloss identifies them as the lowly among the Jews, and another as the hypocrites among them. A further explanation says they professed belief in the Messenger's ﷺ traits and description as they found them in their own scripture.

**"But they are committed to disbelief when they enter and when they leave."** They come in with disbelief in their hearts and depart with the same. The profession changes nothing inward, and they leave as they arrived.

**"And Allah knows what they hide"** states that their concealment is not successful. Allah knows their secrets and what their hearts keep, even when they pretend otherwise before people. He will recompense them accordingly.

### Why does their hearing benefit them nothing?

Because they enter and leave in the same state, they do not benefit from the knowledge they hear, and advice and reminder do not move them. Listening becomes a formality rather than an opening toward change.

That is the practical danger of pretence. It is not only a lie told to others. It deadens the liar's own capacity to receive guidance, since the words he hears are never allowed to reach the place where decisions are made.

### What led some of them to this posture?

Certain Jews of Madinah were impressed by Islam and, seeing its increasing strength, did not want to incur its open enmity. In their hearts they remained steadfast in the religion of their forefathers, while their speech made a show of belief.

Such people forget that their dealing is not really with a human being but with Allah, who knows what is inside the heart. When He deals with anyone, it is on the basis of reality, not on the basis of words chosen to protect an interest.

The verse therefore answers a calculation. Managing appearances may work on people, and it may even appear prudent. It does not work on the One who knows the unseen and the seen, and who has more knowledge of the hypocrites than any of His creatures.

### In short

- The profession of belief is contradicted by the state in which they enter and leave.
- Pretence prevents the hearing of guidance from benefiting the person who makes it.
- The reported motive was impression of Islam combined with unwillingness to accept it.
- Allah knows what is hidden and deals with people according to reality, not words.

<<<VERSE 5:62>>>
The conduct behind the pretence is then described: "You see many of them racing towards sin, transgression, and consumption of forbidden gain." The verse does not speak of occasional lapses. It speaks of speed and eagerness, of people moving toward these things as though competing.

The conclusion is severe: "Evil indeed are their actions".

### Phrase by phrase

**"You see many of them"** addresses the Messenger ﷺ about what is visible. The word many is important, since it excludes the good people among them. The description is of a widespread condition, not of every individual.

**"racing towards sin"** describes competing in contraventions. The glosses include lying, calumny, enmity and idolatry. The racing image suggests that these are not reluctant acts but eagerly pursued ones.

**"transgression"** adds wrongdoing and violation against other people. Aggression and unlawful consumption are included within sin generally, but they are named separately to emphasise the ruin they bring.

**"and consumption of forbidden gain."** This is explained as devouring what is prohibited, including bribes, riba, interest, and unlawful payment received in return for changing legal rulings. People's property is consumed unjustly.

**"Evil indeed are their actions"** condemns what they used to do and the transgression they committed. The evil is horrible in itself, not merely unsuccessful.

### What does racing imply about habit?

Ruh al-Ma'ani is cited for the observation that describing these people as racing toward sin indicates a chronic condition. The evil deeds have become fixed habits, so ingrained that a person moves in that direction automatically, even without forming an intention.

A deed repeated many times becomes part of a person's inner make up, almost a skill or drive. After that there is no exertion or hesitation in doing it. That is the limit these people reached.

The same expression is used of good. Another passage describes people who race toward good deeds (3:114), including prophets and men of Allah. Habit therefore works in both directions. The question is which direction a person's repeated acts have trained him.

### What lesson is drawn for others?

The moral decline of a group is described so that people who know of it may take a lesson and keep away from such deeds and from the causes leading to them. The passage is not merely a description of other people.

The religion favoured by Allah is described as taqwa. A person should live in society uttering no sinful words, avoiding forbidden ways in his activities and behaving with complete fairness toward those with whom he deals.

Selfishness pulls a person toward worship of the world. He wants a life in which he is not troubled by distinguishing right from wrong and needs to consider only his own interests. The general run of the people described trod that path of self-centredness mindlessly.

### What made the condition worse?

It was the duty of the foremost among them to alert people to their wrongdoing and turn them away from it. Instead they reached a tacit understanding with the common people.

They disseminated a version of religion that guaranteed salvation and promised high status without requiring any change in actual living. They left current lives undisturbed and recited false stories of the community's supremacy, gave a religious colour to communal events and told people that repeating a number of ritual actions would build palaces for them in Paradise.

Before Allah, spreading such a religion is described as the most evil deed, because it deludes people into thinking that showy rites guarantee Paradise while no change of life is required. This failure of leadership is taken up in the next verse.

### In short

- Many, not all, are described, which leaves the good people among them outside the charge.
- Racing toward sin indicates habits so fixed that the person moves toward evil without deliberate intention.
- The same expression describes those who race toward good, showing that habit forms in either direction.
- Forbidden gain includes bribes, interest and payment for altering rulings.
- A religion that promises salvation without changing how a person lives is the most serious form of this corruption.

<<<VERSE 5:63>>>
The failure moves upward to those who should have stopped it: "Why do their rabbis and scholars not forbid them from saying what is sinful and consuming what is unlawful?" The question is not neutral. It is a rebuke, and the verse ends by calling their inaction evil.

Ibn Jarir records Ibn 'Abbas saying that no verse in the Qur'an carries a more severe admonition than this one. Ad-Dahhak described it as, in his view, the most frightening verse for religious leaders.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Why do their rabbis and scholars not forbid them"** names two groups. Rabbaniyyun are explained as men of Allah intensely devoted to worship and abstaining from worldly temptations, commonly called dervish, pir or shaykh, or as scholars holding positions of authority. Ahbar are the religious scholars and interpreters.

Another reading distinguishes them as scholars appointed and authorised by the government on one side and scholars generally on the other. On that reading the duty of stopping people from crime falls on officials and scholars together.

**"from saying what is sinful"** concerns utterance. The glosses describe sinful speech as calumny and lying, and one identifies it with idolatrous speech.

**"and consuming what is unlawful?"** concerns taking what is forbidden, explained as bribes and unlawful payment, including payment for changing legal rulings. The two named offences match those of the preceding verse.

**"Evil indeed is their inaction"** condemns the leaders' abstention from forbidding. The wording refers to the rabbaniyyun, as 'Ali bin Abi Talhah reported from Ibn 'Abbas, because they abandoned forbidding evil.

### Why is the leaders' failing rated more gravely?

The two verses use different words for what is done. One term covers everything done, whether or not it is intended. Another applies specifically to what is done with intention and volition. A third adds that the act is repeated as a settled habit and considered purpose.

For the common people's evil, the verse uses the word indicating intentional action. For the leaders' failing, it uses the word indicating deliberate, repeated, purposeful conduct. The choice of terms marks a difference in gravity.

The hint drawn from this is that the leaders' attitude was false. They knew that their people would listen if they spoke and would probably abstain from evil. Yet greed for the offerings they received, or fear of losing their clients' confidence, kept their hearts from standing up for the truth.

The conclusion is stark. If a people fall into sin and their religious leaders know that an appeal would be heard, but do not act because of temptation, fear or apathy, their crime is graver than that of the actual criminals.

### When does the obligation apply?

The stern warning operates where the leaders are reasonably certain that their appeal will be heard and accepted. Where prevailing conditions or experience make it strongly likely that nobody will listen, or that speaking will bring harm, the obligation is described as falling away.

Even then, the better and higher conduct remains to continue the duty whether heard or opposed, without regard for blame or pain. That is the quality described earlier in this chapter, of people striving in Allah's way who fear no blame from any blamer.

The wider rule is stated for all Muslims, not only leaders. Once a person knows something to be sinful or unlawful, he is bound to check it as far as he is able: with his hand, with his word, or at the least by dislike in his heart. That is subject to the same condition about the likelihood of being heard.

These details of the well known Qur'anic maxim of commanding good and forbidding wrong are said to be drawn from authentic hadith. By placing this responsibility on Muslims generally and on scholars and spiritual guides particularly, Islam is described as offering a priceless principle of peace, which could cleanse nation after nation of the evils afflicting them.

### How did the spiritual masters apply this insight?

Those most concerned with correcting deeds are described as the Sufis and men of Allah. From such Qur'anic statements they drew the principle that human deeds, good and bad, come from ingrained habits and traits that become a second nature. To change the deeds, they work on the habits.

Consider a person whose heart is filled with greed for worldly acquisition. From that greed he takes bribes, devours income from interest, and may go on to theft and robbery if given the opportunity. Rather than correcting each crime separately, the prescription given is the realisation that this world is mortal and that its luxuries are poisoned.

Similarly, someone suffering from excessive pride, arrogance or anger belittles and insults others and quarrels with friends and neighbours. The same prescription applies: keep the concern for the life to come and the fear of accountability before Allah actively in view, and those patterns die out.

The general principle is that ingrained traits tilted toward good produce good deeds on their own, and traits tilted toward evil produce evil deeds automatically. Total correction of behaviour therefore requires correction of the traits.

### What happens when a community abandons this duty?

As long as Muslims upheld this principle through the early and later centuries, they held a position of distinction in knowledge, deed, morals and character. When they neglected it and began treating the prevention of crime as the sole responsibility of government and its law enforcement agencies, the result became visible.

The description given is of a father, a mother and a whole family religiously observing and adhering to the Shariah, while their children and family circle are almost their reverse, thinking, feeling and acting differently. Personal observance alone did not carry the next generation.

That is why commanding good and forbidding wrong is stressed in the Qur'an and hadith as the surest method of collective reform in the Muslim ummah. It is identified as a unique distinction of this community, and contravening it is declared a grave sin and a certain cause of punishment.

A hadith says that when sins are committed among a people and someone living among them does not stop them, it is likely that Allah sends a punishment falling on all of them. This is cited in al-Bahr al-Muhit.

### Which reports illustrate the danger of silence?

Malik ibn Dinar relates that Allah commanded His angels to destroy a town. The angels noted that a devoted servant of His lived there, busy praying. The command came that he too should taste the punishment, because he never showed anger against people who openly disobeyed Allah and sinned, not even a sign of it on his face.

Another report concerns Yusha' ibn Nun, the Israelite prophet Joshua. Allah revealed to him that a hundred thousand of his people would be destroyed by punishment, forty thousand of them righteous and sixty thousand evil. He asked why the good would perish along with the evil.

The answer was that the good had friendly relations with the wicked, ate and drank with them and enjoyed life together, and never frowned upon people sinning and disobeying their Creator. Details of these narrations are given in al-Bahr al-Muhit.

These reports describe shared punishment reaching those who were personally upright but socially silent. They are warnings about the cost of indifference, not a claim that mere acquaintance with a sinner is itself a sin.

### What did 'Ali say about earlier communities?

Ibn Abi Hatim records Yahya bin Ya'mar reporting that 'Ali bin Abi Talib once gave a speech beginning with praise and thanks to Allah. He told the people that those before them were destroyed because they committed sins while the rabbaniyyun and ahbar did not forbid them from evil.

When those people persisted in sin, they were overcome by punishment. He therefore urged commanding good and forbidding evil before what struck them struck the listeners too.

He added that commanding good and forbidding evil does not reduce provision or shorten life. That statement answers a common fear, namely that speaking up costs a person his livelihood or safety.

Ahmad records Jarir reporting the Messenger ﷺ as saying that no people among whom sins are committed, while the rest are more powerful and mightier than the sinners yet do not stop them, escape Allah sending punishment upon them. Ahmad alone has this wording.

Abu Dawud records Jarir's account with a different formulation: whoever lives among people committing evil, and they do not stop him although able to do so, will find that Allah punishes them all before they die. Ibn Majah also records the hadith.

### In short

- The verse rebukes religious leaders and scholars for not forbidding sinful speech and unlawful gain.
- Ibn 'Abbas and Ad-Dahhak describe it as the severest admonition and the most frightening verse for such leaders.
- The differing words for action mark the leaders' failing as deliberate and habitual, and therefore graver.
- The duty binds all Muslims according to ability, and remains praiseworthy even when it is no longer obligatory because no one will listen.
- Reports of earlier communities warn that silence can bring shared punishment, while correction of ingrained habits is the way to change deeds.
