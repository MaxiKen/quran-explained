The verse is a command to the Prophet to address the whole of mankind. "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O humanity! I am Allah’s Messenger to you all." The right of the One who sent him follows at once, since He owns all that exists. "To Him ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth." His sole right to worship is stated next. "There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him." His power over life and death comes after that. "He gives life and causes death.”" The verse then names what the hearers owe the Messenger. "So believe in Allah and His Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, who believes in Allah and his revelations." The last words promise the result of following him. "And follow him, so you may be ˹rightly˺ guided"

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say"** is addressed to the Prophet himself. The instruction that follows it, **"O humanity! I am Allah's Messenger to you all,"** is the declaration he was told to make. The word for mankind here is taken to cover people of every colour, and the Arabs and the non-Arabs alike. It leaves out no one.

The verse names the honour and the greatness of the Prophet in that declaration. He is the final prophet, and he was sent to all mankind, and to the jinn as well. The Holy Qur'an says it in other places. One passage has the words that the Book has been revealed to him so that he may warn whoever it reaches (6:19). Another says that the sects which reject it have the Fire as their promised meeting place (11:17). Another has the command to say to those who were given the Scripture, and to the unlettered, whether they submit themselves. The words that follow say that the duty of the Messenger is only to convey the message (3:20). Reports on the subject are more than can be counted, and the universality of his mission is well known in the religion itself.

**"To Him ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him. He gives life and causes death."** describes the One who sent him. The words point to the Creator, the Lord, the King of all things, in whose hand lies the control of everything, and life, and death, and the decision.

**"So believe in Allah and His Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, who believes in Allah and his revelations."** is the command that follows the declaration. Belief in Allah is joined here with belief in His Messenger, and the Messenger is named as the one who could neither read nor write. The description adds that he believes in Allah and in His words. This is read to mean that his actions agree with what he says, and that he believes in what he was given from his Lord. The words of Allah that he believes in are the Books revealed to the prophets. They include the Torah, the Gospel revealed to Jesus, and the Book given to him.

**"And follow him, so you may be ˹rightly˺ guided"** names the second demand. Belief in him is asked for first, and the following of him is asked for after it. The order of the two tells a reader that a bare belief, or the saying of the words of belief with the tongue, is not enough by itself for guidance. Putting the shariah into practice is what the verse is aiming at.

### The messenger sent to all mankind

The verse is one of the basic matters of prophethood. The belief that the Prophet was sent to the whole of mankind, and to the jinn as well, is one of the fundamental tenets of the religion.

The mission he was given was not limited to one people or one place or one period, which was the case with the earlier prophets. Each of them came to a particular people, in a particular place, for a limited time. He was sent at the end of the prophets. His coming at the end, for the guidance of all people and all times to come, leaves no room for another prophet after him.

One of the marks of this ummah follows from the same fact. A report of the Prophet says that there will always be a group among the Muslims who stand against the forces working against the religion. They resist sacrilegious thought and practice, and they correct false interpretations of the Book and the sunnah. That group is helped by Allah and is promised the final victory, since such people are the real inheritors of the prophetic mission and carry its duty after him.

Al-Razi drew a further point out of the same fact. Commenting on the command to be in the company of the truthful, he said that the command carries an assurance that a group of the truthful will always be found among the people. Otherwise the command to seek their company would mean nothing. From that he took the principle of consensus, that the presence of such truthful people prevents the ummah from agreeing together on an error.

Another account drew from the verse a proof that he is the last of the prophets, since his message covers all the people of the world and all the ages to come. Some reports add that Jesus will follow the law of the Prophet after his descent from the heavens in the last age. The Book speaks of the finality of prophethood in many places. One of them has the words that the Book has been revealed to him so that he may warn whoever it reaches (6:19). That lays the following of the Book on all the people who come after him, and it lays on them the duty of learning it.

### The two sides of the mission

The words that he is the Messenger to all people are not a comparison drawn between him and the prophets before him. They are the plain statement of a fact. His prophethood has two sides to it. One is direct and the other works through his ummah.

He was sent to convey the message directly to the people living in Arabia (6:92), and the message was carried to the whole world through his community (22:78). This is, in principle, the nature of the task given to every prophet of God. What happened to the religions brought by the other prophets is that they did not stay in their original and pristine condition. It was not possible, then, for those prophets to go on giving good tidings or warning people of what comes after death, which is the main duty of a prophet.

The Bible carries a prediction about the Arab Prophet, that blessing will come to every tribe on the earth through him. It has been possible for his blessings to reach every nation because the religion he brought has been preserved as it was. The religion brought by Moses and by Jesus was distorted. Everyone may hear its call, yet the blessing of it does not travel with the call.

A number of Jewish tribes were settled in Arabia, and they were proud of having the holy Book. Such people resist accepting a truth that comes from outside them, since the feeling that they possess the greatest truth keeps them from accepting any other. The great majority of the Jews were blinded in this way by obstinacy and prejudice. A few were not, among them 'Abdullah ibn Salam, who could look at Islam with an open mind, vouched for its truth without caring for his worldly honour, and surrendered himself to it wholeheartedly.

### The distinctions given to this prophet

The report of the distinctions came at the battle of Tabuk. Imam Ahmad recorded it on the authority of Ibn 'Abbas. The Prophet was engaged in the late night prayer, the tahajjud, and the companions had gathered round him in a circle to keep him safe from any attack by the enemy. When he had finished the prayer he said that he had been given five distinctions on that night which had not been given to any prophet before him. He said it, as the report has it, and he did not say it out of pride.

The first is that his prophethood was extended to all mankind, while the message of the prophets before him was limited to their own people. The second is that his presence put dread into the heart of his enemy and that the dread overtook the enemy from a distance of a month's journey. The third is that the spoils taken from the enemy were made lawful for his people, while they had been forbidden to the peoples before them. The fourth is that the whole earth was made a place of prayer for them, and it was made a means of purification in tayammum. The prayers of the earlier people were confined to their churches and synagogues, and they were not allowed to pray at home or out in the open land. In the absence of water, his people were allowed to use clean soil in place of water. That was another concession the earlier people did not have. The fifth, which he called the most promising and the most helpful of them, is the supplication that every prophet was asked to make and that was certain to be granted. Each prophet used his for some particular aim of his own. He was asked to make such a supplication too, and he chose to keep his for the Day of Judgement. There it will bring great benefit to the people of the declaration that there is no god but Allah.

Another Tradition, reported on the authority of the companion Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, has the Prophet saying that anyone who hears of his appearance must believe in him, be he Muslim, Christian or Jew. Otherwise, he said, that person will be placed in Hell.

The chain of narration of the report of the five distinctions is suitable, and the two Sahihs did not record it. The distinction of the reserved supplication is the one that matters most for the community, since it is kept until a day when all people stand in need.

### The two companions and the word of Abu Bakr

The other well-known report belongs to this verse directly, and it was recorded by Al-Bukhari. Abu al-Darda said that Abu Bakr and Umar had an argument, and that in the course of it Abu Bakr made Umar angry. Umar went off in his anger, and Abu Bakr followed him asking to be forgiven. Umar refused and shut his door in his face. Abu Bakr then came to the Messenger of Allah while the companions were sitting with him, and the Messenger of Allah said that this companion of theirs had made someone angry.

Umar regretted what he had done, and he came to the Prophet, greeted him with the greeting of peace and sat beside him. He told him what had passed, and the Messenger of Allah became angry with him. When Abu Bakr saw what was happening, he said that it was he who had been in the wrong. The Messenger of Allah answered with the words that they should leave his companion alone. He said that when he had declared to the people that he was the Messenger of Allah to them all, they had called him a liar. Abu Bakr alone had declared that he had spoken the truth.

The report is placed with this verse because it turns on the same declaration. The man who stood by the declaration at the moment when it was contested is the one whose standing the Prophet defended, and the anger of the Prophet was for the sake of a companion who had been wronged rather than for himself.

### What the verse asks of a believer

The verse closes with belief and with following. The Prophet is named in it again as the unlettered one, the man who could neither read nor write, whom the earlier Books had promised and given good news of. His description was carried in those books at length, and the promise of him was the reason the men of the Book were told to watch for his coming.

The phrase that he believes in Allah and his words carries the sense of a life in which the man and the message agree. He believed in what he was given from his Lord, and the words of Allah that he believed in cover every Book revealed before him and the Book given to him.

Following him means taking his path and his guidance. The command of following comes immediately after the command of believing, which shows that a person is not excused from acting on what he holds. The law he brought is the road, and the safe arrival is on it. A saying of a spiritual leader of Baghdad has it that no road to Allah stays open to a man except the path the Prophet marked out.

The distinction between the God of the prophet and the God of the philosopher is drawn from this verse by one of the accounts. The God of the philosopher is an abstract spirit, and faith in Him resembles faith in the pull of gravity. Gravity neither speaks nor commands anything. The God of the prophet is living and aware. He addresses human beings, He lays commands on His servants, and He settles reward and punishment on the basis of obedience or disobedience. It is this God who sent the Messenger, and it is this God the verse commands people to believe in.

### In short

- The command to the Prophet is to declare that he has been sent to the whole of mankind. The verse names every colour and every nation, and the jinn as well, and it follows the declaration with the right of the One who sent him over the kingdom of the heavens and the earth.
- The universality of the mission is the ground of the finality of prophethood, since a message for all people and all ages leaves no room for another prophet after him. It is also the ground of the promise that a group within the ummah will always stand for the truth and keep correcting false interpretations of the Book and the sunnah.
- The report of the distinctions came at Tabuk after the tahajjud prayer. The five named in it are the universal mission, the dread cast at a month's distance, and the spoils made lawful. The rest are the earth made a place of prayer with tayammum in place of water, and the supplication kept for the Day of Judgement.
- Abu Bakr alone declared that the Prophet had spoken the truth when he announced his mission. The Al-Bukhari report of the argument between him and Umar ends with the Prophet defending him and asking the people to leave his companion alone.
- Belief in him and following him are both asked for. The order of the two shows that a faith without the practice of the shariah is not enough. The account adds that the God of the prophet is a living God who speaks and commands, unlike the abstract force of the philosopher.

<<<VERSE 7:159>>>
Verse 159 turns from the address to all mankind to a group within an earlier community. "There are some among the people of Moses who guide with the truth and establish justice accordingly"

### Phrase by phrase

**"There are some among the people of Moses who guide with the truth and establish justice accordingly"** states that of the Children of Israel there are people who follow the truth and judge by it. They guide people by the truth, and when they pass judgement they act justly according to it.

The Book says the same of such people in other places. One passage speaks of a group among the people of the Book who take their stand for what is right. They recite the verses of Allah in the night hours and prostrate themselves in prayer (3:113). Another says that among the people of the Scripture there are those who believe in Allah, in what has been revealed to the Prophet and in what was revealed to them. They humble themselves before Allah and do not sell the verses of Allah for a small price. Their reward is with their Lord (3:199). Another speaks of those to whom the Scripture was given before this. They believe in the Book when it is recited to them, and they say it is the truth from their Lord. They had been among those who submitted themselves even before it, and they will be given their reward twice over for their patience (28:52-54). Another passage describes those who were given knowledge before it. They fall on their faces in humble prostration when the Book is recited to them, and they say that glory be to their Lord and that the promise of their Lord must be fulfilled. They fall down weeping, and it increases their humility (17:107-109).

### The group that guides by the truth

The question is which of the Israelites the verse means. It means those who followed the commandments of the Torah and the Injil faithfully at the time when those Books were revealed. It also means those who accepted the faith of Islam after the appearance of the Prophet. They did so on the strength of the predictions about him that were carried in the Torah and the Injil. The Book has spoken of this group in a number of passages in terms of praise.

The Book also says that those to whom the Scripture was given before this believe in this Book (28:52). The community of Moses was not one body with a single conduct. Alongside those who were blinded by obstinacy there were people who held to what had been given to them. The verse names them so that the difference is clear.

### The community beyond the river of sand

A narrative is carried by Ibn Jarir and by other reporters about a group of the Israelites who greatly despised the corruption that had spread among their own people. They prayed to Allah for a land of their own, a place cut off from the other tribes. Their aim was to live by the original faith preached by Moses. Allah answered them and settled them in a distant land in the far east, where they lived by that faith with care.

Another favour was granted to those people. In the time of the Prophet they were given the chance to accept Islam at his own hands. On the night of the ascension, the Archangel Gabriel took the Prophet to them on the way to the heavens. They accepted the faith, and the Prophet taught them some surahs of the Book.

The Prophet spoke with them, and the account gives the questions he put to them. He asked them whether they had any means of weighing and measuring, and what other means of subsistence they had. They answered that they tilled the soil and heaped the whole harvest in one place. Each person then took what he needed from the heap. They said that they therefore needed no weights and measures. He asked them whether anyone among them ever told a lie. They said that no one among them lied. It was the established practice among them that any person who spoke a lie was burnt up by a fire from heaven. He asked them why all their houses were of the same kind. They answered that it was to remove the possibility of showing off one's wealth against the wealth of others. He asked them why they had built their graves in front of their houses. They said that it kept death before their eyes at all times.

After the Prophet returned from the heavens to Makkah, this verse was revealed to him. Al-Qurtubi narrated the event along with other suggestions and remarked that the narrative was not authentic. The other account called it an unusual event, but did not reject it. One of the other works gives a plainer statement of the same identification. The community who lead with truth and act upon it are the people who live beyond the river al-Raml.

### What the people of this description are given

The verse draws a picture of a people whose whole concern is the truth. The Lord of the Worlds showed them the road of felicity. He singled them out for nearness to Him and honoured them out of His generosity. Godwariness lives through such people. The path of truthfulness stands firm under their feet, and the fortune of the religion is joined to the blessing that comes with their breathing.

The description of them is the description of the friends of Allah, and of those who travel the road to Him. God's Messenger is reported to have said that the light of one of them, if it were shared out, would take in all the people of the earth. Suppose the light of their hearts were given an open road. Suppose the glitter of that radiance fell on the world and its people. Then every stubborn man would turn into a voicer of the oneness of Allah. Each would trade the sash of unbelief for a belt of love for the religion.

Such people are rare and precious. He does not show them to everyone, and He does not hand them over to this world or to the world to come. He keeps them in His own protection, as a man might keep fugitives, and He nurtures them with love under the domes of jealousy.

The revelation that came to Moses carries the same care. He was told, as the account has it, that he was the speaking companion of the empire. He was told to be careful not to open the oyster of his pain's pearl before those without eyes. He was told not to recite the verses of the passion for His majesty to those who are not privy to them. The same warning covered those who have been dismissed from hearing the reality. He was told that if he wanted to expose the mystery to someone, he should do it to those who are the locus of the covenant of the secrets. Such people are busy night and day serving His threshold, with the tent of their passion pitched in the contemplation of His majesty. Those are the people branded with the mark of most-worthiness by the threshold of Lordhood, the community that guides by the truth and does justice thereby.

That mark belongs among the divine secrets. It is a subtlety of the Lord that came out of the World of the Unseen. It came to rest in one place only, behind the curtain that veils the stages of the clay of the poor. The one who wants to catch a whiff of it is told to go into the curtains of the soul until he reaches the heart. He is told to pass from the curtains of the heart to the spirit, and from the curtains of the spirit to union with the Beloved. He will not see the mark placed anywhere except in the spirit of the friends. The language of the passage is the language of inward states, and the outward meaning of the verse stays what it was. The verse makes plain that among the people of Moses there has always been a group who held to the truth. That group is either the people described in the narrative or those who entered Islam when the Prophet appeared.

### In short

- The verse states that there are people among the Children of Israel who guide by the truth and judge by it. The Book praises such people in other places, among them the party who stand for the right and recite the verses of Allah at night (3:113).
- Those who believe in what was revealed to the Prophet and in what was revealed to them, and who do not sell the verses of Allah for a small price, have their reward with their Lord (3:199). Those who believed before the Book came to them will be given their reward twice over (28:52-54).
- The group meant by the verse are those who followed the Torah and the Injil faithfully when they were revealed. They also include those who accepted Islam when the Prophet appeared, on the strength of the predictions about him in those Books.
- A narrative carried by Ibn Jarir and other reporters tells of Israelites who asked for a land apart from the corruption of their people. The Prophet visited them on the night of the ascension, when Gabriel took him to them. They accepted the faith and were taught some surahs of the Book.
- Al-Qurtubi narrated that account with other suggestions and called it unauthentic. The other account called it unusual without rejecting it. One of the other works identifies the community of the verse as the people beyond the river al-Raml.
- The inner reading of the passage describes such people as the friends of Allah, whose light would embrace the folk of the earth if it were divided among them. It places the mark of that worthiness in the spirit of the friends rather than in any outward show.
