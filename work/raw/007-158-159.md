<<<VERSE 7:158>>>
Allah commands His Prophet ﷺ to make a declaration to the whole of humanity: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O humanity! I am Allah’s Messenger to you all." The One who sent him is the Lord of everything: "To Him ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him. He gives life and causes death.”" The call that follows is addressed to everyone: "So believe in Allah and His Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, who believes in Allah and His revelations." And then: "And follow him, so you may be ˹rightly˺ guided". This verse sets out one of the fundamental beliefs of Islam about prophethood. The Prophet ﷺ was sent as Allah's Messenger to all mankind and to the jinn, to everyone who will exist until the Day of Judgement.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O humanity!"** Say, O Muhammad, to all people, red and black, Arabs and non-Arabs alike.

**"I am Allah’s Messenger to you all."** This shows the Prophet's honour and greatness, for he is the Final Prophet, sent to all mankind and to the jinn. He was told to declare openly that his prophethood was not limited to one people or place.

**"To Him ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth."** The One who sent him is the Creator, Lord and King of all things. All the treasures of the heavens and the earth are His, and Allah alone controls them.

**"There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him."** Nothing besides Him deserves worship, glory be to Him. One explanation renders it: there is no provider except Him.

**"He gives life and causes death.”"** He brings the dead to life at the resurrection and causes the living to die in this world. Control, life, death and judgement are all in His hand, so He alone is Lord of the universe.

**"So believe in Allah and His Messenger, the unlettered Prophet,"** Allah proclaims that Muhammad ﷺ is His Messenger and confirms it by commanding people to believe in him and follow him. He is the unlettered Prophet whom they were promised, and whose coming was announced, in the earlier scriptures. Those books described him fully, including as the unlettered Prophet. He cannot read or write, and he brought only what his Lord revealed to him, to guide people to what is good for them in this world and the next.

**"who believes in Allah and His revelations."** His deeds match his words, and he believes in what his Lord gave him. His Lord's words here are the Qur'an. Another reading, in the singular, takes "His word" as ʿĪsā (Jesus), who came into being through a word, "Be", and he was.

**"And follow him, so you may be ˹rightly˺ guided"**: take up his path and guidance and his religion, so that you may be led out of error into faith and onto the Straight Path.

### Was the Prophet ﷺ sent to everyone?

Allah says elsewhere: "And this Quran has been revealed to me so that, with it, I may warn you and whoever it reaches." (6:19). And: "But whoever from the ˹disbelieving˺ groups rejects it, the Fire will be their destiny." (11:17). And: "And ask those who were given the Scripture and the illiterate ˹people˺, “Have you submitted yourselves ˹to Allah˺?” If they submit, they will be ˹rightly˺ guided. But if they turn away, then your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺." (3:20). There are many other verses on this subject, and more hadiths than can be counted. Every Muslim knows that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ came for all mankind and for the jinn too.

The earlier prophets came to a particular people in a particular place, for a limited time. Another tradition, which Imām Aḥmad recorded from Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī, has the Prophet ﷺ say that anyone who hears of his coming, Jew or Christian or otherwise, must believe in him, or he will be put in Hell. So this verse is clear proof that the Prophet ﷺ was sent to all of humanity. His message is for every generation, every age to come and every place. No one can reach salvation without believing in him, even if he devotedly practises some other faith or follows some other book.

### "Abū Bakr said: You speak the truth"

Al-Bukhārī recorded from Abū al-Dardāʾ that Abū Bakr and ʿUmar once had a sharp argument in which Abū Bakr angered ʿUmar. ʿUmar walked off in anger. Abū Bakr followed him, asking his forgiveness, but ʿUmar refused and shut his door in Abū Bakr's face. Abū Bakr went to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, where Abū al-Dardāʾ and others were sitting. The Prophet ﷺ remarked that their companion had been in a quarrel. Later ʿUmar regretted what he had done. He came to the Prophet ﷺ, greeted him with salām, sat beside him and told him what had happened.

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ grew angry with ʿUmar. Seeing this, Abū Bakr said, "O Messenger of Allah, I was the one in the wrong," and that his own fault was greater. The Prophet ﷺ said: will you not leave my Companion alone for my sake? When I said, "O people, I am the Messenger of Allah to you all," you all said, "You lie." Abū Bakr alone said, "You speak the truth."

### Five things given to no prophet before

Imām Aḥmad recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said he had been given five things that no prophet before him was given, and that he did not say this out of pride. The chain of this hadith is acceptable, though the two Ṣaḥīḥs did not record it. Imām Aḥmad's Musnad also reports, through reliable narrators, that this was said during the campaign of Tabūk. The Prophet ﷺ was praying Tahajjud late at night, and his Companions formed a circle around him to guard him from any attack. When he finished, he told them that five distinctions had been given to him that night:

1. He was sent to all mankind, black and white alike, while each earlier prophet's message was limited to his own people.
2. Allah gave him victory through fear, which seized his enemies from as far as a month's journey away.
3. The spoils of war were made lawful for him and his people, though they had been unlawful for everyone before.
4. The whole earth was made a place of prayer for him and his followers, and its soil a means of purification. The earlier peoples could pray only in their churches and synagogues, not at home or out in the open. When water is lacking, his people may use clean earth instead, in tayammum, a concession no one before was given.
5. The fifth, he stressed, was the greatest and most helpful. Every prophet was given one supplication that was certain to be answered, and each used it for his own purpose. He kept his supplication back as intercession for his Ummah on the Day of Resurrection. It will reach everyone who bears witness that there is no god but Allah and associates no partner with Him.

### Why does this verse show the finality of prophethood?

Because the Prophet ﷺ came at the end of all the prophets, the belief in the finality of prophethood follows naturally. Since he is the last of them and came to guide all people of all times to come, there is no room for any other prophet. The verse is thus taken as proof that he is the seal of the prophets, since his message is for all the world and all the ages to come. Some authentic traditions say that ʿĪsā (Jesus), when he descends from heaven in the last age, will also follow the law of the Prophet ﷺ. Many other verses state the finality of prophethood just as plainly. The verse of the Holy Qur'an quoted above (6:19) makes following the Qur'an binding on all who come after the Prophet ﷺ, and with it the duty of learning it.

The same fact explains a special quality of the Muslim Ummah. According to a tradition of the Prophet ﷺ, there will always be a group among the Muslims who stand up to the forces against Islam and resist irreligious ideas and practices. They will also correct false interpretations of the Qur'an and the Sunnah. Allah will favour this group with His special help, and in the end it will prevail over its opponents. These people are the true heirs of the prophetic mission, faithfully carrying out its duty after the Prophet ﷺ.

Imām al-Rāzī, explaining the Qur'anic command "Be with the truthful", remarked that it guarantees there will always be a group of truthful people available. Otherwise the command to keep their company would make no sense. Al-Rāzī drew the principle of consensus from this phrase. The presence of the truthful is enough to prevent the whole Ummah from agreeing on an error.

### Another view: directly and through his Ummah

Another view holds that "I am Allah’s Messenger to you all" is no claim that every other prophet went only to his own people while Muhammad ﷺ went to all nations. The statement does not compare one prophet with another. It is simply a statement of fact. His prophethood works in two ways, directly, and by way of his community, the Ummah. His direct task was to bring the divine message to the people of Arabia: "so you may warn the Mother of Cities and everyone around it" (6:92). And he was to convey it to the whole world indirectly: "so that the Messenger may be a witness over you, and that you may be witnesses over humanity" (22:78).

In principle, this was the task given to every prophet of Allah. But the religion the other prophets brought did not stay in its original, pure condition. So it could no longer give good news, or warn people of the results of their deeds in the life to come, which is a prophet's main duty. The Bible foretells that through the Arab Prophet every tribe on earth will receive blessing. His blessings have been able to reach all nations because the religion he brought has been preserved in its original state. The religion brought by Mūsā (Moses) and ʿĪsā has been distorted. Its voice may reach everyone, but its blessings will not.

### Why "believe" and then "follow"?

Having established that the Prophet ﷺ was sent to all the people of the world and all times to come, and that no one has any choice but to follow his law, Islam, the verse calls for belief in Allah and His unlettered Messenger. He himself believes in Allah and His words, so people should follow him to stay on the right path. Allah's words here are the scriptures revealed to His prophets, such as the Torah, the Gospel revealed to ʿĪsā, and the Qur'an.

The command to believe in him is followed by a command to follow him. This shows that belief alone, or saying the words of faith, is not enough for guidance or salvation. Living by the Sharīʿah of Islam is necessary for salvation in the Hereafter. The great saint and spiritual guide Shaykh Junayd of Baghdad said that every road to Allah is closed except the path the Prophet ﷺ marked out.

"The unlettered Prophet, who believes in Allah and His revelations" also shows the difference between the philosopher's idea of the Divine and the prophet's. The philosopher's deity is an abstract spirit. Accepting it is like accepting the force of gravity in the universe. But gravity neither speaks nor gives commands. The Lord of the prophets is living and conscious. He speaks to human beings, commands His servants and decides reward and punishment by their obedience or disobedience to His commands.

### In short

- The Prophet ﷺ was told to declare that he is Allah's Messenger to all mankind, unlike earlier prophets sent to one people.
- No one can reach salvation without believing in him, and Abū Bakr was the first to say, "You speak the truth."
- He was given five unique gifts, among them the whole earth as a place of prayer and his intercession kept for his Ummah.
- As the last Prophet sent to all ages, he seals prophethood, and a truthful group of his Ummah will always uphold his message.
- Belief must be joined with following him, for every road to Allah is closed except his.

<<<VERSE 7:159>>>
After so much about the failings of the Israelites, Allah does justice to the righteous among them: "There are some among the people of Moses who guide with the truth and establish justice accordingly". Among the children of Israel there is a group who stand for the truth, guide people to it and judge fairly between people by it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"There are some among the people of Moses"**: a group, a community, from among the Israelites, the people of Mūsā (Moses). One explanation says they are the people who live beyond the river al-Raml, the river of sand.

**"who guide with the truth"**: who call others to the truth, follow it and act on it themselves.

**"and establish justice accordingly"**: who act justly by that truth when they pass judgement, settling their disputes by what the truth demands, which is Islam.

### Who are these people?

Allah says the same of some of the People of the Book in other verses: "there are some among the People of the Book who are upright, who recite Allah’s revelations throughout the night, prostrating ˹in prayer˺" (3:113). And: "Indeed, there are some among the People of the Book who truly believe in Allah and what has been revealed to you ˹believers˺ and what was revealed to them. They humble themselves before Allah—never trading Allah’s revelations for a fleeting gain. Their reward is with their Lord." (3:199).

Allah also says: "˹As for˺ those ˹faithful˺ to whom We had given the Scripture before this ˹Quran˺, they do believe in it. When it is recited to them, they declare, “We believe in it. This is definitely the truth from our Lord. We had already submitted ˹even˺ before this.” These ˹believers˺ will be given a double reward for their perseverance" (28:52-54). And: "when it is recited to those who were gifted with knowledge before it ˹was revealed˺, they fall upon their faces in prostration and say, ‘Glory be to our Lord! Surely the promise of our Lord has been fulfilled.’ And they fall down upon their faces weeping, and it increases them in humility.”" (17:107-109).

These are the people who faithfully followed the commands of the Torah and the Injīl in the times when those books were revealed. They also include those who readily accepted Islam once the Prophet ﷺ appeared, as the Torah and the Injīl had foretold. The Qur'an praises this group in a number of verses.

A number of Jewish tribes were settled in Arabia, proud of possessing Allah's holy Book. Such people resist accepting any truth from outside. The feeling that they already hold the greatest truth keeps them from accepting a truth brought by anyone else. Most of the Jews were blinded by stubbornness and prejudice. Only a few, such as ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, could look at Islam with an open mind. They affirmed that it was true, without caring for their worldly standing, and gave themselves to it wholeheartedly.

### The people beyond the river of sand

Ibn Jarīr records a narrative under this verse. A group of Israelites, disgusted by the corrupt practices of the rest, prayed to Allah for a land of their own, apart from the other tribes, where they could practise the original faith that Mūsā had taught. Allah took them away to a distant land in the far east, and there they lived by their faith without fail.

Allah favoured them further by letting them accept Islam directly at the hands of the Prophet ﷺ. On the night of the Ascension, Jibrīl (Gabriel) took the Prophet ﷺ to them on the way to the heavens. They accepted the faith, and he taught them some surahs of the Qur'an.

The Prophet ﷺ asked whether they had weights and measures, and how they made their living. They said they farmed the land and gathered the whole harvest into one heap, and people came and took from it what they needed. So they had no need of weights and measures. He asked whether any of them ever lied. They said no one did, because anyone who told a lie was burned up by fire from heaven. He asked why all their houses were alike. They said it was so that no one could show off his wealth over others. He asked why they had built their graves in front of their houses. They said it kept them mindful of death at all times. When the Prophet ﷺ returned from the heavens to Makkah, this verse was revealed to him.

Al-Qurṭubī related this story under the verse along with other suggestions, and remarked that the narrative is not authentic. Others called it an unusual report but did not reject it. Either way, the verse teaches that the Israelites have never been without some who followed the truth. They may be the people described in this story, or those who embraced Islam after the Prophet ﷺ appeared.

### The inner reading: the hidden friends of Allah

This is the story of Allah's friends, a description of the chivalrous and of how the travellers on the path conduct themselves. The Lord of the worlds has shown them the road of happiness, singled them out for the special favours of nearness, and honoured them with the pull of His generosity. Awareness of Allah lives on through them. The well-trodden path of truthfulness rests on their firmly planted feet, and the good fortune of the religion is joined to the blessing of their breaths. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that if the light of one of them were divided among the people of the earth, it would be enough for them all.

If the light of their hearts were given free rein, and its radiance fell on the world and its people, every stubborn soul would become a voice of tawḥīd. They would trade the sashes of unbelief for belts of passion for the religion. Yet such people are rare and precious. Allah shows them to no one, and gives them over neither to this world nor to the next. He keeps them like refugees under His own protection and nurtures them with love beneath the domes of His jealousy.

Revelation came to Mūsā: O companion in conversation, do not open the shell that holds the pearl of your pain before those who have no eyes. Do not recite the verses of passion for My majesty to those not admitted to the secret, or to those shut out from true hearing. If you wish to reveal My mystery to anyone, reveal it to those in whom the covenant of My secrets is kept. They serve at My threshold night and day, the tent of their passion pitched in contemplation of My majesty. The threshold of Lordship has branded them with the mark of worthiness: "a community that guides by the truth and does justice thereby".

This brand of worthiness is one of the divine secrets, a subtle gift of the Lord that came down from the world of the unseen and settled only behind the veils of the clay of the poor. To catch its scent, go behind the veils of the soul until you reach the heart. Then go behind the veils of the heart until you reach the spirit, and behind the veils of the spirit until you reach union with the Beloved. You will find it nowhere but in the spirits of His friends.

### In short

- Among the people of Mūsā has always been a group that guides by the truth and judges justly by it.
- They include those who kept faithfully to the Torah and the Injīl in their time, and those, like ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, who accepted Islam.
- A report about a hidden people beyond a river of sand who met the Prophet ﷺ on the night of the Ascension is judged not authentic by al-Qurṭubī.
- Allah keeps His true friends hidden and protected, and the light of their hearts could guide the whole world.
