<<<VERSE 2:91>>>
Verse 91 records the Jews' standard reply to the call of Islam and turns it against them. "When it is said to them: “Believe in what Allah has revealed,” they reply, “We only believe in what was sent down to us,”" and so they deny what came afterwards, "though it is the truth confirming their own Scriptures". The Prophet ﷺ is told to ask them a question that exposes the claim: "Why then did you kill Allah’s prophets before, if you are ˹truly˺ believers?"

### Phrase by phrase

**"When it is said to them: 'Believe in what Allah has revealed'"**. When the Jews and the People of the Book are told to believe in the Qur'an and the other Books, to believe in what Allah sent down to Muḥammad ﷺ and to follow him.

**"they reply, 'We only believe in what was sent down to us'"**. It is enough for us, they say, to believe in what was revealed to us in the Torah and the Injīl (Gospel), and this is the path we choose. They flatly refused to believe in any Book of Allah except the Torah, and they denied the Gospel and the Qur'an. That denial is itself clear proof of their unbelief (kufr). They added that they believed in the Torah because it had been sent down to them, which means that they rejected the other Books merely because those Books had not been sent down to them, an argument that betrays envy and malice. They also said to the Prophet ﷺ, "Our forefathers were believers."

**"and they deny what came afterwards"**. Whatever lies beyond the Torah, such as the Qur'an. The Arabic marks this clause as describing their state while they make their claim.

**"though it is the truth confirming their own Scriptures"**. The Qur'an is the truth, affirming the oneness of Allah and agreeing with what they possess of the Book. The phrases "it is the truth" and "confirming" are two circumstantial qualifiers piled up for emphasis. Since the revelation given to Muḥammad ﷺ matches the revelation the People of the Book already hold, that very match is a proof against them. Allah says that those given the Scripture recognise this Prophet as they recognise their own children, yet a group of them knowingly hides the truth (2:146). Had they truly believed in what was revealed to them, they would have believed in the Qur'an.

**"Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, 'Why then did you kill Allah’s prophets before, if you are ˹truly˺ believers?'"**. If your claim to believe in what was revealed to you were true, why did you kill the Prophets who came to you upholding the law of the Torah, knowing them to be true Prophets? You killed them out of nothing but transgression, stubbornness and injustice toward Allah's Messengers, so you follow only your lusts, opinions and desires. The Torah you profess forbade killing them. The question concerns what their forefathers did, but it is addressed to the Jews of the Prophet's ﷺ time because they approved of what their forefathers had done. As-Suddī said that in this verse Allah chastised the People of the Book.

### Three answers to "we believe only in what was sent to us"

The Qur'an refutes the Jews' position in three ways. First, once the truth and authenticity of the other Books have been established by irrefutable arguments, what can a flat denial mean? Had they doubted those arguments they could have raised their doubts and satisfied themselves, but a plain refusal to believe is something else. Second, the other Books, the Qur'an among them, confirm the Torah, so to deny them is to deny the Torah itself. Third, killing a prophet counts as unbelief in every divine Book, and the prophets the Jews killed were themselves Israelites whose teaching rested on the injunctions of the Torah. The Jews not only killed these prophets but accepted the killers as their chiefs, a direct denial of the Torah that falsifies their claim to believe in it. Nothing the Jews say or do, in short, holds up from any point of view, and the verses that follow refute them on further counts.

### Group loyalty mistaken for love of truth

The Jews were not ready to accept the Qur'an because they considered themselves already rightly guided. They were sure that belonging to the largest body of followers of the truth, the Israelites, would be enough to earn them salvation. In reality it was ethnic superiority dressed up as love of truth. The communal values of their group had taken the place of pure truth, and so, when truth appeared in its original form, they could not accept it. Had their aim been to find pure truth, it would not have been hard, for predictions in their own sacred book, the Torah, had announced that the Book of truth to be followed was now the Qur'an and not the religion of their group.

The devotional reading sees the same selectivity on the inner path. When such people are told to recognise the truth of what has been shown them from the decree of divine harmony, by acknowledging the situation and the proof established, their lower selves admit whatever ambiguous part agrees with their self-serving desires, and then disbelieve in whatever lies beyond their worldly concerns. They remain far from the elite and are not counted among the masters with a special claim (arbāb al-ikhtiṣāṣ).

### In short

- Told to believe in the Qur'an, the Jews claimed to believe only in what was sent down to them, rejecting the Gospel and the Qur'an although both confirm the Torah (2:146).
- A Book is not denied because it was sent to someone else, and to deny the Books that confirm the Torah is to deny the Torah.
- The question "why did you kill Allah's prophets?" exposes the claim, since the murdered prophets were Israelites upholding the Torah and their killers were made chiefs.
- Behind the refusal lay ethnic pride mistaken for love of truth, and a faith that accepts only what suits the lower self.

<<<VERSE 2:92>>>
Verse 92 brings the second count against the claim of faith. "Indeed, Moses came to you with clear proofs, then you worshipped the calf in his absence, acting wrongfully". A people who turned to an idol while their own prophet was away can hardly claim that their forefathers were steadfast believers.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, Moses came to you with clear proofs"**. Mūsā (Moses) came with commands, prohibitions and miracles, clear signs proving that Allah had sent him and that no deity but Allah deserves worship. The signs named in this connection are the staff and the hand, the blood, the frogs, the lice, the locusts and the flood. His miracles also include the sea parting, the clouds shading the Israelites, the manna and quails, and the stone gushing water. Scholars note that these signs include ones that appeared even before the Torah was given, such as the staff that became a serpent, the shining hand and the sea split open as a road for the Israelites. Their purpose was to affirm Allah's oneness and omnipotence and to prove that Mūsā was a prophet.

**"then you worshipped the calf in his absence"**. You took the calf as a god instead of Allah, in Mūsā's own lifetime, after he had gone to Mount Ṭūr for his appointment with his Lord. In his absence his people made from their jewellery the idol of a calf that lowed, which could neither speak to them nor guide them, and took it as a god (7:148). The devotional reading hears in this the failure of tawḥīd: Mūsā had called them to declare God's unity and to separate the true object of worship (maʿbūd) from every other object and every limited thing (maḥdūd), but they inclined to worship only what attracted them, the calf they adopted and the idol they desired. The calf was taken away from them, yet its traces stayed in their hearts, and in the hearts of their descendants. That, on this view, is why most Jews believe in anthropomorphism (tashbīh).

**"acting wrongfully"**. You were unjust in worshipping the calf when you knew that there is no deity worthy of worship but Allah, and you were unbelievers in taking it as a god. Later they were filled with remorse, realised they had gone astray, and cried that unless their Lord had mercy on them and forgave them they would be lost (7:149). Worshipping others alongside Allah is wrongdoing, since He alone deserves worship.

### Why this answers the Jews of Madinah

The verse refutes the Jews by pointing out that for all their claim to faith (īmān) they had fallen into the sin of shirk, association, which involves denying not only Mūsā but Allah Himself. The Jews who were the Prophet's ﷺ contemporaries had not themselves worshipped the golden calf, but they respected the forefathers who had and took their side, so for all practical purposes they shared the guilt. The verse also hints that no one should be surprised if people whose forefathers denied Mūsā now deny the Prophet ﷺ.

### In short

- Mūsā (Moses) brought overwhelming proofs, the plagues, the staff, the hand, the sea and more, all pointing to Allah's oneness.
- While he was at Mount Ṭūr, his people worshipped the calf (7:148), an act of shirk that they later repented of (7:149).
- The Jews of Madinah inherited the guilt by approving their forefathers, and the calf's trace lingered in their hearts.

<<<VERSE 2:93>>>
Verse 93 returns to the covenant at the mountain and lets the Israelites condemn themselves in their own words. "And when We took your covenant and raised the mountain above you ˹saying˺, “Hold firmly to that ˹Scripture˺ which We have given you and obey,” they answered, “We hear and disobey.”" The verse then explains why: "The love of the calf was rooted in their hearts because of their disbelief." And it ends with a challenge: "How evil is what your ˹so-called˺ belief prompts you to do, if you ˹actually˺ believe ˹in the Torah˺!"

### Phrase by phrase

**"And when We took your covenant"**. When We took your consent, your binding promise to act according to what is in the Torah, to follow Mūsā (Moses) and to accept what he brought from Allah. Allah reminds the Jews of their errors, their breaking of His covenant, their transgression and their defiance.

**"and raised the mountain above you"**. He pulled it up, lifted it and fixed it over their heads, to drop it on them because they had refused to accept the covenant, and they were awestruck. Scholars trace the need for such a threat back to Egypt. When the Israelites had escaped from Egypt with Allah's help and crossed the sea, they came upon a people who worshipped idols, found the cult so attractive that they asked Mūsā to make them a visible, concrete god too, and repented when he reprimanded them. But repentance has many degrees, and theirs was not of a high order, so the darkness of that error never wholly left their hearts. It grew until it broke out in the worship of the golden calf. As penance, some of them had to be slain, while others, according to some reports, were forgiven and spared, and possibly their repentance too was of a low order. Those who had not joined in worshipping the calf did not hate the deed as much as they should have, so their hearts also carried a trace of shirk. Whether through feeble repentance or through a want of proper hatred for unbelief, their hearts grew indolent in religious matters, to the point that when Allah asked them to pledge to follow the Torah's injunctions steadfastly, Mount Ṭūr (Sinai) had to be suspended over their heads to threaten them. The story was told earlier in the sūrah in the first account of the raised mountain.

**"Hold firmly to that ˹Scripture˺ which We have given you and obey"**. Take forcefully, seriously and with effort what We have given you of the Book, be diligent and earnest with it, and listen to what you have been commanded, ready to accept it. Otherwise the mountain would fall on you.

**"they answered, 'We hear and disobey'"**. We hear your words and disobey your command. It is as if they said, were it not for the mountain we would have heard what you say and defied it. Out of sheer fright they said with their tongues that they had heard, that is, accepted, Allah's command, but their hearts gave no real consent, and their whole posture said that they would not, or could not, obey. They heard with their ears and rebelled in their actions, and they broke the covenant soon afterwards.

**"The love of the calf was rooted in their hearts because of their disbelief"**. Literally, they were made to drink the calf in their hearts. ʿAbd ar-Razzāq related from Maʿmar that Qatādah explained it as absorbing its love until that love lodged in their hearts, and Abū al-ʿĀliyah and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas said the same. The love of the golden calf intoxicated their hearts the way wine does. Their love of worshipping it sank into their hearts as a punishment for their disbelief, their denial and ingratitude.

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ 'How evil is what your ˹so-called˺ belief prompts you to do, if you ˹actually˺ believe ˹in the Torah˺!'"**. Worse yet is the way you behaved in the past and behave even now, disbelieving in Allah's signs and defying the Prophets. If love of worshipping the calf weighs as much with you as love of your Creator, then evil indeed is what your belief commands, if you are truthful in claiming that your forefathers were believers. Faith does not command anyone to worship a calf, so the claim to faith is false. Their forefathers are meant, and the same logic applies to the present: you do not believe in the Torah either, because you have denied the prophethood of Muḥammad ﷺ, and faith in the Torah does not command you to reject him. Your disbelief in Muḥammad ﷺ is the worst of your deeds and the harshest of your sins, for you have disbelieved in the Final Messenger, the master of all Prophets and Messengers, the one sent to all mankind. How can you claim to believe while committing the evil of breaking Allah's covenant, disbelieving in His signs and worshipping the calf instead of Him?

### A faith judged by its fruits

Having set out these examples of perversity and rebellion, the Qur'an asks the Jews to look at themselves and at their claim to genuine faith. Could true faith ever inspire people to such deeds? If it is their brand of faith that leads them into such grave sins, it cannot be a true faith, and their claim to be true believers is plainly false. If they had any faith at all they would not disbelieve and turn away from what Allah instructs them to do. The devotional reading adds that the verse repeats the tale of their excessive love for the calf and their aversion to accepting the Real, and of their being warned of the punishment that would overtake them for their evil. No counsel helped them and no punishment made them give up their disobedience. They paid no attention to censure and did not act (ʿamilū) as the situation required.

### In short

- At the mountain the Israelites pledged to hold fast to the Torah and answered, in effect, "we hear and disobey", consent on the tongue with none in the heart.
- The calf's love had soaked into their hearts like wine, as Qatādah, Abū al-ʿĀliyah and ar-Rabīʿ explained, the residue of a shallow repentance that began with the request for a visible god after leaving Egypt.
- A faith that commands calf-worship, or the rejection of Allah's final Messenger ﷺ, is no faith, so the Jews' claim to believe in the Torah collapses.
- Neither counsel nor punishment moved them, a warning that belief is judged by what it prompts one to do.
