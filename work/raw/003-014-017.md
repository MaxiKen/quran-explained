<<<VERSE 3:14>>>
After earlier warnings about opposition to Islam and jihad, striving in Allah’s cause, the verse turns to the worldly attractions that can drive people to hostility toward the truth. It lists “the enjoyment of ˹worldly˺ desires—women, children” and other attractions that draw people toward this life. These are not described as evil in themselves. Their appeal is part of the test: will people use them with moderation and remember their return to Allah, or let them take over the heart and make the Hereafter seem unimportant? The verse calls these things temporary enjoyment and points to the better return with Allah.

### Phrase by phrase

**“The enjoyment of ˹worldly˺ desires—women, children”** names family among the attractions people love. The explanations say women are mentioned first because desire for them can be a powerful test for men. Marriage can be good when it serves chastity and the raising of children. A report in a Ṣaḥīḥ collection says that the Prophet ﷺ left no temptation more powerful for men than women. Other reports say that the best members of his ummah, the Muslim community, are those with the most wives, and describe a righteous wife as one of the best joys of this life. The Prophet ﷺ is also reported to have said that women and perfume were made beloved to him, while prayer was the comfort of his eye. ʿĀʾishah is reported to have said that nothing was dearer to him than women, except horses, with another version reversing the order.

The desire for children can become a temptation when it is driven by pride or boasting. It is praised when parents hope to raise worshippers who increase the community of Muhammad ﷺ in worshipping Allah alone. A report encourages marrying a loving and fertile spouse so that the Prophet ﷺ may take pride in the size of his community on the Day of Resurrection.

**“treasures of gold and silver, fine horses, cattle, and fertile land”** names material wealth and the means of livelihood. The term qanaṭīr al-muqanṭarah means amassed or heaped-up wealth and points to a large amount. Scholars, including al-Ḍaḥḥāk, differed about the precise measure of a qintar. Abū Hurayrah is reported to have described it as twelve thousand uqiyahs, each of which he considered greater than what lies between the heavens and earth. Ibn Jarīr recorded that report. The point of the different definitions is the abundance represented by the word.

The attraction of wealth can lead to arrogance and the desire to control or dominate others. It can also be used to care for family and relatives, help the poor, and carry out acts of worship. In the latter case, it is praised. The same distinction applies to horses. One person keeps them for service in Allah’s cause and may use them in battle when needed. Another collects them for pride and hostility toward Islam and the Muslim community, which brings blame. A third keeps horses to meet ordinary needs and raise offspring, while paying what is due to Allah. In that case they can be a means of sufficiency. The verse about preparing mounts for defense is cited in this connection (8:60).

Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Abzā, as-Suddī, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, and Abū Sinān explain fine, marked horses as branded or beautiful. Makhūl says the description can refer to a horse with white markings on its face or feet. A report from Abū Dharr, recorded by Aḥmad, says that an Arabian horse makes two supplications at dawn, asking Allah to make it among the owner’s most valued possessions. “Cattle” includes camels, cows, and sheep. “Fertile land” means cultivated ground used to grow plants.

**“has been made appealing to people”** points to a desire placed in human nature. An explanation says that Allah made lawful goods attractive as a test. Without a desire for food, shelter, family, work, and useful possessions, people might not farm, take a job, trade, build, or earn. Their natural attraction helps sustain human life and society. It can also give people some sense of the blessings of the unseen next life. The test is whether a person becomes absorbed in present enjoyment and forgets the return to Allah, or uses these means while keeping the final account in view.

The same attraction has a further purpose: people are tested over whether they will take the glitter of this life as their final goal or recognize it as temporary. The Holy Qur’an describes the world’s adornments as a test of who acts best (18:7). Where harmful deeds are made attractive, the material also speaks of Satan’s role (8:48, 16:63, 27:24, 29:38). Lawful pleasures are not condemned merely because they are attractive. The danger is crossing the limits set by Allah or letting them displace concern for the life to come.

**“These are the pleasures of this worldly life”** says that the listed things are temporary. Their comfort lasts only for a time, then fades. They can support a person’s life and work, but they are not the lasting destination. One explanation compares worldly possessions to water around a boat. Water beneath the boat helps it travel. If water enters and fills the boat, it sinks. In the same way, possessions can support a person’s role in this world and the next when kept in their proper place. When love of them fills the heart, it can destroy the person’s spiritual life. The saying is attributed to Rūmī.

Another explanation notes that love of these things can keep society moving. Workers earn, employers hire, merchants sell, and customers obtain what they need because people want goods and livelihood. The same desires can be used for good or can become obstacles. A person who enjoys lawful blessings and uses their means to prepare for the Hereafter can benefit in both lives. A person who gives them the heart’s full attention may lose the lasting life and turn those very blessings into punishment.

**“but with Allah is the finest destination”** directs desire toward the lasting return. The best outcome is not the accumulation of worldly goods but the final home with Allah. The verse calls for moderation: people may seek good things and keep what meets genuine needs, but they must not use them unlawfully or become so engrossed that they forget life after death.

### Why are ordinary desires part of a test?

The explanations say these attractions are not pointless. They help people live, support families, and maintain the work of society. They may also awaken a desire for lasting blessings. Their danger lies in mistaking the means for the final goal. A person can use wealth, family, and work to serve Allah and prepare for the Hereafter. Or the person can become so attached to them that friends, family, and social standing matter more than the truth.

A person who treats worldly means as a path can enjoy what is lawful without losing sight of the lasting return. Another person may spend wealth and attention on worldly security until the next life no longer enters the plan. A verse warns that wealth and children can become a punishment in this life (9:55). The central distinction is moderation and purpose, not a rejection of every worldly blessing.

### What does the inward reading of the test add?

An allusive reading, in a report from Muṣṭafā (the Prophet ﷺ), portrays Paradise as a destination reached through hardship. After Allah created Paradise, He sent Gabriel to see it. Gabriel said that whoever heard of its blessings would aim to enter it. Allah then surrounded the road to Paradise with hardship, disappointment, and desires that cannot all be fulfilled. The traveler must cross that trial to reach the destination. Shaykh al-Islām Anṣārī reflected that suffering can conceal happiness and that disappointment may lie above a thousand treasures. He asks how he could have known that the way to companionship with Allah passes through bewilderment and difficulty. Another image calls the Kaaba a resting place for Allah’s friends, with a vast desert and many stations to cross before reaching it. The seeker must keep travelling through the desert of love to reach Allah’s acceptance. The imagery presents difficulty as part of the test, not as proof that the destination is absent.

### In short

- Family, wealth, horses, livestock, and farmland are attractive parts of worldly life, but they are also a test.
- These blessings may be used for worship and care of others, or they may lead to pride, excess, and neglect of the Hereafter.
- The world’s comfort is temporary. The best return is with Allah, so the believer keeps worldly means within proper limits.
- The inward reading presents hardship as part of the road to lasting reward.

<<<VERSE 3:15>>>
The Prophet ﷺ is told to ask, “Shall I inform you of what is better than ˹all of˺ this?” The answer contrasts the temporary attractions of verse 14 with lasting blessings for those mindful of Allah: Gardens, purified spouses, and Allah’s pleasure. The greatest promise is not simply a place of comfort. It is Allah’s approval, with no fear that it will be withdrawn.

### Phrase by phrase

**“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ ‘Shall I inform you of what is better than ˹all of˺ this?’”** directs Muhammad ﷺ to call people away from being absorbed in perishable pleasures by telling them about what lasts. The question is meant as an invitation and assurance, not a request for information.

**“Those mindful ˹of Allah˺ will have Gardens with their Lord”** promises Paradise to those who keep away from disbelief, idolatry, indecency, and what Allah has forbidden, and who obey Him. Taqwa, being mindful of Allah, is shown through obedience and restraint. The material says Gardens have degrees corresponding to degrees of taqwa. It mentions the Garden of Refuge and the Garden of Eden, with Allah’s greatest approval as higher still. An explanation applies the promise to Abū Bakr and the believers with him. A passage about the one who fears standing before his Lord and restrains desire is cited (79:40-41). Another promises good dwellings in the Gardens (9:72). The highest goal is Allah’s pleasure.

**“under which rivers flow, to stay there forever”** describes a permanent home rather than a passing pleasure. The rivers are described as carrying water, milk, honey, and wine unlike anything people have known. The people of Paradise do not die or leave. Their blessings do not diminish or disappear.

**“and pure spouses”** means companions free of the impurities and defects associated with worldly life. The word also carries the sense of purity in character and form. The verse mentions spouses but does not list every possible blessing. Other reports say that if a person in Paradise wishes for a child, the process of pregnancy, birth, and growth will occur at once. Another says that someone who wishes to farm will be given what is needed, and ploughing, sowing, ripening, and harvest will all be completed immediately. These reports are attributed to at-Tirmidhī and al-Ṭabarānī respectively.

Gold and silver are not listed as central rewards because there will be no buying, selling, or need to use money. Whatever people need will be provided. Reports still describe some parts of Paradise as built with bricks of gold and silver. Horses, cattle, farmland, and produce are also not needed there in the way they are in this life, because their benefits will already be provided. Sound hadith reports describe fine horses presented to people in Paradise on Fridays for riding to visit friends and relatives.

**“along with Allah’s pleasure”** names the greatest blessing. One report describes Allah asking the people of Paradise whether they are content and need anything. They answer that they have received everything. Allah then gives them His approval and promises never to be displeased with them. With that approval, there is no fear that their blessings will end or be taken away.

A report from the Prophet ﷺ says that worldly life and what it contains are cursed except what is used to seek Allah’s pleasure. Another version excepts remembrance of Allah, what Allah loves, a religious scholar, and a student of religious knowledge. Ibn Mājah and al-Ṭabarānī report it from Abū Hurayrah.

**“And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants”** means that Allah knows His servants’ deeds and conditions and gives them what is fitting. The reward is not hidden from Him, and He sees how people use the attractions of this life.

### Why are only some blessings named?

The previous verse lists six attractions of earthly life. This verse names three corresponding blessings of the next life: Gardens, purified spouses, and Allah’s approval. Children are not listed because people in Paradise will not need heirs or support, and everyone who had children will have them there. Those who had none will not begin with a desire for children, though reports say their wishes can be granted. Gold and silver are not needed for trade in a place where every need is supplied. Other earthly goods are likewise unnecessary because their benefits are already provided. The list therefore points to the lasting nature of Paradise rather than setting a limit on what its people may enjoy.

### What is greater than the Gardens?

The verse identifies Allah’s pleasure as greater than the other blessings. The people of Paradise will have every need met, but the assurance that Allah is pleased with them and will never be displeased is the highest good. An allusive interpretation also distinguishes levels of taqwa and the Gardens that correspond to them. Its greatest degree is the approval of Allah, greater than the Garden of Eden (9:72). Ibrāhīm (Abraham) is quoted as saying that created things are his enemies except the Lord of the Worlds (26:77). The reading calls the seeker to loosen the heart from everything created and turn it wholly toward Allah.

### In short

- Those mindful of Allah are promised lasting Gardens and purified spouses.
- Paradise includes what its people desire, so worldly things such as money and farmland are not needed there as they are here.
- Allah’s approval is the greatest blessing, and it will never be withdrawn.
- Allah sees His servants and knows their deeds and conditions.

<<<VERSE 3:16>>>
The verse describes the al-Muttaqin, those mindful of Allah who receive the promise of Paradise, as people who pray, “Our Lord! We have believed, so forgive our sins.” They acknowledge faith in Allah and His Messenger and ask Him to cover their wrongs and protect them from the Fire. Their request rests on Allah’s mercy, not on a claim that their belief or deeds entitle them to a reward.

### Phrase by phrase

**“who pray, ‘Our Lord! We have believed’”** describes people who affirm faith in Allah, His Book, and His Messenger. The word alladhīna, “those who,” can be read as a further description of the people mindful of Allah, or as a continuation describing those who make the prayer. Another explanation says they follow the sacred law brought by the messengers. Their words and actions are joined: they do not treat faith as a claim with no practice behind it.

**“so forgive our sins”** asks Allah to pardon errors and shortcomings, including sins committed before and after Islam. The prayer covers sins committed before Islam and those committed after embracing it. It is made with reliance on Allah’s bounty and mercy. An inward reading says these servants turn wholly to Allah and seek nearness and intimate friendship, especially when they remember severe trials and afflictions. They ask for help and for a pleasing end.

**“and protect us from the torment of the Fire”** asks Allah to keep punishment away. The prayer recognizes that salvation is in His hands and that people need His protection.

### In short

- The people of faith believe in Allah, His Book, and His Messenger, and seek to live by the sacred law.
- They ask forgiveness for past and present sins and depend on Allah’s mercy.
- They seek protection from the Fire and turn to Allah in times of hardship.

<<<VERSE 3:17>>>
The verse expands on the character of those who pray in verse 16. They are “patient, sincere, obedient, and charitable,” and they seek forgiveness before dawn. Their faith appears in steady conduct: they persist in obedience, tell the truth, submit to Allah, give from their wealth, and turn to Him when others are asleep.

### Phrase by phrase

**“˹It is they˺ who are patient”** includes patience in acts of obedience, in avoiding what Allah forbids, and when hardship arrives. An inward reading adds patience through trials without complaint until the seeker reaches Allah.

**“sincere, obedient”** describes truthfulness in faith and submission to Allah. Their words agree with their deeds. They meet difficulty while staying firm on Allah’s path and following His sacred law rather than abandoning it for worldly approval.

**“and charitable”** means they spend their wealth in obedience to Allah, care for relatives, help those in need, and comfort the poor. An inward reading says generosity extends beyond property to the effort of the body and sincerity of the heart.

**“and who pray for forgiveness before dawn”** describes seeking Allah’s pardon in the late part of the night. It is a time when many people are unaware or enjoying sleep, so turning to Allah then shows attention and devotion. The verse about Yaʿqūb (Jacob) says he would ask forgiveness for his children (12:98). One explanation says he waited until the late night to make that prayer.

The Prophet ﷺ described Allah descending to the lowest heaven in the last third of each night and calling: whoever asks will be given, whoever calls will be answered, and whoever seeks forgiveness will be forgiven. Several Companions reported this hadith. It is recorded in the Two Ṣaḥīḥ collections, the Musnad, and Sunan collections. ʿĀʾishah said that the Prophet ﷺ prayed witr in the early, middle, and later parts of the night, then later in his life restricted it to the later part. ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar would ask Nāfiʿ whether the late part of the night had begun, then pray and seek forgiveness until dawn. Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded this report.

### What do these qualities look like together?

The qualities describe the whole person. Patience governs the heart, truthfulness guides the spirit, obedience shapes the body, and charity directs wealth toward Allah’s path. The heart is filled with dhikr, remembrance of Allah, while the tongue asks forgiveness. The person is patient in hardship, truthful in seeking, constant in service, and generous with what is available. The inward reading describes such a person as not held back by either worldly reward or fear of punishment, but seeking Allah Himself and the “seat of truthfulness” with an all-powerful King (54:55).

### In short

- The righteous persist in obedience, avoid wrongdoing, and remain patient in hardship.
- They are truthful, obedient, and generous with their wealth and effort.
- They seek forgiveness in the quiet hours before dawn, following the example of the prophets and the Prophet ﷺ.
