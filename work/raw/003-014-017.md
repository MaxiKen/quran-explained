<<<VERSE 3:14>>>
Desire for worldly things, the verse says, "has been made appealing to people". The list runs "The enjoyment of ˹worldly˺ desires—women, children, treasures of gold and silver, fine horses, cattle, and fertile land—has been made appealing to people. These are the pleasures of this worldly life, but with Allah is the finest destination." The verse does not call these things evil. It calls them enjoyable, temporary, and dangerous when they occupy the heart, because hostility to truth usually grows out of love for them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The enjoyment of ˹worldly˺ desires—women, children, treasures of gold and silver, fine horses, cattle, and fertile land—has been made appealing to people"**: what the self wants and calls for has been beautified. Women are named first because the test through them is more tempting, then children, because most earning is done for a wife and children to share. After them come stored and piled wealth in gold and silver, the qanāṭir al-muqanṭarah, then fine marked horses, livestock and tilled land. For unbelievers these things are made attractive in a special way, while for everyone they form the working material of life.

**"These are the pleasures of this worldly life"**: they give benefit for a while and then vanish. One explanation likens their duration to the useful life of household things, a bowl or a plate, after which the object is gone.

**"but with Allah is the finest destination"**: the more excellent abode and best return is with Him, Paradise in the Hereafter. A believer should desire no other final home, and should not become attached to gifts whose best use is to lead there.

### Women, children, wealth and horses: how each desire can turn

It is recorded in the Ṣaḥīḥ that the Prophet ﷺ said: "I did not leave behind me a test more tempting to men than women." Yet attraction is not condemned in itself. When a man enjoys marriage in order to preserve chastity and have children, he is encouraged. Marriage is urged in many hadiths, including the words that the best members of this ummah are those with the most wives. Another hadith calls this life a delight and its best delight a righteous wife. The Prophet ﷺ also said that women and perfume were made dear to him, while the comfort of his eye was prayer. ʿĀʾishah reported his dearest worldly love as women, with horses next in one version and before them in another.

Children likewise divide by intention. Desire for children can become pride and boasting, and then it tempts. When the aim is to increase the ummah of Muhammad ﷺ with worshippers of Allah alone, it is praised. A hadith says, "Marry the Wadud (kind) and Walud (fertile) woman, for I will compare your numbers to the rest of the nations on the Day of Resurrection."

Wealth divides in the same way. Wanting money can grow from arrogance, the wish to dominate the weak and control the poor, and that is forbidden. It can also be sought for worship, kindness to family and relatives, and spending in righteousness and obedience, and then it is praised. Scholars disagreed over the amount of a qinṭār, though Ad-Daḥḥāk and others all make it a large sum of money. Abū Hurayrah put it as twelve thousand ūqiyah, adding that each ūqiyah is better than what lies between heaven and earth. Ibn Jarīr recorded that report.

Horses fall into three classes. Some are collected for the cause of Allah and used in battle when needed, and their owner is rewarded. Some are kept for boasting and hostility toward the people of Islam, and their owner earns a burden. Some are kept for need and breeding while Allah's right over them is not forgotten, and they become a shield of sufficiency for their owner. The same threefold division stands behind the command, "And make ready against them all you can of power, including steeds of war" (8:60). The musawwamah horses drew two explanations. Makḥūl pointed to a horse marked by a white-spotted face and white feet. Ibn ʿAbbās called them branded, beautiful animals, and the same account came from Abū Sinān, as-Suddī, ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn ʿAbdullāh ibn Abzā, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ʿIkrimah and Mujāhid. Imām Aḥmad recorded that Abū Dharr heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say that every Arabian horse is allowed two supplications each dawn, asking Allah, who made it subservient to the son of Adam, to make it among the dearest of his wealth and household, or the dearest of his household and wealth, to him.

Cattle means camels, cows and sheep. Fertile land means land used to farm and grow plants. None of these is dismissed. Each is measured by the use to which it is put.

### Why attraction was built into human beings

Jihad against hostile disbelievers occupied the verses before this, and now the text explains why people make themselves hostile. Love of the world is called the head of error, and the people ranged against truth are driven by wealth, power, lust or defence of ancestral custom. All are reaching for a share of present enjoyment.

There is wisdom in the attraction itself. If people had no natural inclination toward family, food, trade and land, worldly business would collapse. Nobody would sweat over farms and fields, work for wages, risk capital in industry, stock a shop, or go out to buy what is needed to live. Love of desirable things draws workers, employers, traders and customers out of their houses and silently keeps a social system running. There is a second wisdom. A person who had no taste for blessings here would have no appetite for blessings promised in the next world, and no motive to do good and avoid evil. And there is a third, more searching wisdom. Attraction sets the test. One person becomes engrossed in present enjoyment and forgets the life to come. Another sees these things as real but temporary, takes what he needs, and directs his effort toward making the next life good and safe. Allah states the testing purpose in His words: "We made whatever there is on the earth its adornment so that We can test as to who among them acts best" (18:7).

Where other verses say that Satan made deeds look attractive (8:48) (16:63) (27:24) (29:38), the attraction belongs to things evil in religion and reason, or to enjoyment pushed past Allah's limits. Lawful adornment is not bad in itself. That is why the placing of attraction in things is elsewhere attributed to Allah.

### The boat, and the water around it

The practical rule is moderation. Good things may be sought and stored according to genuine need. Using them in forbidden ways, or indulging until the reality after death is forgotten, is self-destruction. The poet-sage Rūmī pictured the heart as a boat and worldly things as water. Water beneath and around a boat supports it and serves its purpose. Water inside the boat capsizes it and destroys everything in it. Wealth and possessions are meant to stay underneath a person, carrying him in this world and toward the next, not to enter the heart and kill it.

That distinction changes the value of the same possessions. One person enjoys present blessings and uses them as milestones toward a prosperous Hereafter, so they never become a roadblock. Another squanders his chance at the next life through them, and they begin to punish him already here: "So, their wealth and children should not attract you. Allah wants to punish them with these in this lowly life" (9:55). This world is a place of trial. Worldly acquisitions bring social honour, and family, wealth and property become the centre of interest until no future beyond this life is remembered, no other home is considered, and no other wealth seems worth a life's devotion. Only a person whose focus rests on the permanent life sees how small these glittering things are in the scale of the unseen.

### Paradise ringed by hardship

Muṣṭafā ﷺ, the world's paragon and master of Adam's children, told that the Eternal Enactor first created Paradise and sent Gabriel to inspect what awaited His servants and friends. Gabriel returned from gardens of endless bliss near Allah's Presence and swore that nobody hearing their description would fail to strive and obey. Only then did God ring Paradise with hardship, disappointment, unworthy distractions and desires left unmet, placing a bridge of trial across its road for every seeker of the Patron.

Shaykh al-Islām Anṣārī wondered at hidden reversals: disappointment can conceal a thousand treasures, suffering can mother happiness, union's exaltation can hide inside bewilderment's abasement, and companionship can amount to the greatest Resurrection. The Kaʿbah remains sweet as God's friends nest there truthfully, yet a man-eating desert stretches before it through mile and station after station. Who finally seeks hard enough to cross? A world wanders through that desert of love, hoping to enter the Kaʿbah of acceptance. With the road thus filled by unmet longing and disappointment, heaven commanded again: "O Gabriel, look again." 

### In short

- Women, children, piled gold and silver, marked horses, cattle and land are made appealing as a test, not condemned as things in themselves.
- Each desire turns on intention: marriage, children, wealth and horses can serve Allah or feed pride, domination and forgetfulness.
- The safe measure is need without captivity, the boat kept on the water rather than filled with it, because with Allah is the final and perishing-free return.

<<<VERSE 3:15>>>
Verse 14 calls present blessings temporary, and verse 15 asks: "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Shall I inform you of what is better than ˹all of˺ this? Those mindful ˹of Allah˺ will have Gardens with their Lord under which rivers flow, to stay there forever, and pure spouses, along with Allah’s pleasure.” And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants". The question expects a yes. Against six beloved things of this world, it sets gardens, purified spouses and the approval of Allah, and approval is the greatest of the three.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Shall I inform you of what is better than ˹all of˺ this?"**: the interrogative is meant as an affirmation. It asks whether people obsessed with imperfect, perishable blessings want to be told of blessings far better, prepared for those who fear Allah and obey Him.

**"Those mindful ˹of Allah˺ will have Gardens with their Lord under which rivers flow, to stay there forever"**: for those who have taqwa there are Gardens with their Lord, underneath which rivers flow, and they remain there forever and do not want removal. The rivers run throughout Paradise carrying honey, milk, wine and water, moving beneath trees and habitations, of a kind no eye has seen, no ear has heard and no heart has imagined. They will neither die nor leave.

**"and pure spouses, along with Allah’s pleasure"**: purified mates, free from filth, dirt, harm, menstruation, post-natal bleeding and every defect of form or character that belongs to this world. The pleasure of Allah is added after the gardens, and it is greater than all of them: "But the pleasure of Allah is greater" (9:72). Once it descends, Allah will never be angry with them after it.

**"And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants"**: Allah sees His servants, their places in Paradise and their works in the life of this world. Nothing is hidden from Him, and He gives and requites each according to what he deserves and what he did.

### Who first hears the promise

The fearful are described as those who keep away from disbelief, from ascribing partners to Allah and from indecencies, and Abū Bakr and his fellow believers are named as the first case of them. Their gardens stand with their Lord. Their pure companions are spared menses and filth. Their Lord's contentment is far greater than the bliss they enjoy, and He sees the believers both in their stations in Paradise and in their deeds here.

### Why the next world names only three blessings

Verse 14's infatuations numbered six: children and women, cattle and tillage, fine horses, and heaps of silver and gold. Verse 15 names three blessings of the Hereafter: gardens, purified wives and Allah's approval. The others are not denied, and each absence has a reason.

Children were not named because in this world a man loves children for their help and strength and because his name lives on through them. In the Hereafter he will not need such help, and he will not die and need an heir. Those who have children will have them in Paradise, and whoever has no children here will not desire them there. Should someone wish for a child, Allah will grant him one. A hadith in the Jāmiʿ of at-Tirmidhī says that if a dweller of Paradise wishes to have a child, pregnancy, birth and growth will be completed in no time, and he will have what he wished for.

Gold and silver were not named because here they buy and procure. In the next life there is nothing to buy, sell or pay for, and whatever a dweller needs is instantly provided. Paradise is not without gold and silver, since some reports describe places there built with bricks of gold and silver laid alternately, but metal is not the blessing worth naming.

Horses cover distances here, and there is no travel there of that kind, though sound hadith reports that fine horses will be brought before the dwellers of Paradise on Fridays for them to ride out to meet friends and relatives. Cattle and farms serve needs already supplied in Paradise without intermediaries. One report in aṭ-Ṭabarānī says that even a person who wishes, without need, to try farming will have everything assembled instantly, and ploughing, seeding, ripening and cutting will be done in no time.

The one named blessing of companionship then covers the rest, because Paradise holds a comprehensive promise: for its people there is whatever they wish. What is mentioned is already present without asking. What is not mentioned arrives on wishing.

### The approval that cannot be withdrawn

The last and greatest blessing is hard to picture. When the people of Paradise have settled, happy and content with no desire unfulfilled, Allah will ask whether they are happy and whether they need anything. They will answer that He has given them everything, so what else could they need. He will say that He now gives them the greatest of all blessings: His approval, with the promise that He will never be displeased with them, and nearness forever. Since displeasure cannot return, the blessings of Paradise cannot be taken back or reduced either.

The Prophet ﷺ summed up the whole subject in a hadith reported by Ibn Mājah and aṭ-Ṭabarānī from Abū Hurairah: "Worldly life is cursed and cursed is what is in there, except what is used to seek the pleasure of Allah." Another narration gives the exceptions as the remembrance of Allah, dhikr, and what Allah likes, and the religious scholar, ʿālim, and the student of religion, ṭālib ʿilm.

### The degrees of godwariness and of Paradise

The text leaves enemies at their furthest goal and returns to friends. Godwariness rises by levels, so Paradise rises by degrees. Its first degree, the Garden of the Shelter, belongs to the first godwariness, shunning what is forbidden and refusing the soul's caprice. Scripture binds them: "As for him who fears the standing..." (79:40-41). Higher stands Eden Garden, and beyond Eden stands approval at its greatest: "And goodly dwellings in the gardens of..." (9:72).

The Greatest Approval belongs to those who reach the utmost godwariness. That utmost is reached when a person treats everything carrying the scar of new arrival and the mark of creation as an enemy, as Abraham said: "Surely they are an enemy to me, ..." (26:77). Then he turns away from all things, keeps his heart detached, and busies himself with the suffering of passion for the ultimate Reality. The intrusion of others finds no room there. The heart becomes a garden with room either for the intruder or for the image of the Beloved, so it is given wholly to Him, and a whiff of union does what a hundred spirits cannot. Tomorrow all people are carried to the furthest limit of their goals. Whoever hopes for the Garden of Refuge is told to flee the unlawful and deal justly, and it will not be held back from him.

### In short

- Better than all worldly loves are Gardens with rivers, purified spouses and Allah's pleasure, and His pleasure is the greatest because it never turns to anger.
- Children, gold, silver, horses, cattle and farms are not separately promised because Paradise supplies their purposes, and whatever is wished for is given there.
- Allah sees His servants and their works, and Paradise itself is graded with godwariness, up to the Greatest Approval, which outranks even the Garden of Eden.

<<<VERSE 3:16>>>
The sixteenth verse gives the words of the people entitled to that promise: "who pray, “Our Lord! We have believed, so forgive our sins and protect us from the torment of the Fire.”" They are described first by a prayer, not by an achievement. Faith is confessed, sin is admitted, and protection is begged, and the two verses around this one explain why such people ask in exactly this way.

### Phrase by phrase

**"who pray"**: the verse introduces the people of Paradise by what they say in prayer. The word "those", alladhīna, here qualifies the mindful who were promised the gardens in the verse before, or stands in substitution for them, so that the prayer and the promise belong to one people.

**"Our Lord! We have believed"**: we believe in You, in Your Book and in Your Messenger, and in what You revealed to Your messengers. We have followed Your sacred law.

**"so forgive our sins"**: forgive our errors and shortcomings on the ground of faith in You and in the law You gave, out of Your bounty and mercy. The sins named cover those committed before Islam and those committed after its advent.

**"and protect us from the torment of the Fire"**: guard us and drive away from us the punishment of Hell.

### Why this prayer follows belief so closely

A person convinced that the matters of the next world are entirely in his Lord's hands finds worldly glitter meaningless. God becomes the being he fears most, and the next life the thing he wants most. He stops following mundane desire and settles his course by looking toward the court of the Almighty. Then his prayer grows natural. In solitude God is his companion. Before divine greatness and perfection his own being shrinks to insignificance, and he has hardly any words left except, "O my God, forgive me."

The prayer therefore joins two confessions. We have believed, so the relationship is real, and we have sinned, so forgiveness cannot be assumed. Forgive us our sins because of our faith in You is the order of the sentence: faith is what a servant brings, while forgiveness comes from the bounty and mercy of the Lord addressed.

### Wholly occupied, and the nearness it wins

Read inwardly, the saying of these people means that they occupy themselves with Allah entirely, bi-l-kulliyya, and plead for help before Him at the mention of severe trials and afflictions, al-raziyya. Those who pray this way obtain nearness and intimate friendship with Him, al-qurba wa-l-khuṣūṣiyya, together with high degrees, al-darajāt al-ʿuliyya, and pleasing shares, al-qisam al-murḍiyya. Their own summary is simple. In the presence of His greatness they call on Him spontaneously, and He becomes the companion of their solitude.

### In short

- The people of the promise are introduced by prayer: belief confessed, sins laid before Allah, and protection from the Fire begged.
- Forgiveness is asked for on the ground of faith, from Allah's bounty and mercy, for sins before Islam and after it.
- Read inwardly, the prayer is the mark of people wholly occupied with Allah, who receive nearness, high degrees and pleasing shares.

<<<VERSE 3:17>>>
The seventeenth verse completes the portrait: "˹It is they˺ who are patient, sincere, obedient, and charitable, and who pray for forgiveness before dawn". Five qualities are named, and the last one is timed. Prayer for forgiveness is placed in the final part of the night, when sleep is sweetest and no audience is present, and the whole verse looks back to the prayer of verse 16 and explains who can honestly say it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"who are patient"**: patient while performing acts of obedience and abandoning what is forbidden. The steadfast are also understood as those steady in carrying out the obligations Allah enjoined and in shunning His contraventions, and as the steadfast when misfortunes strike.

**"sincere"**: truthful in their proclamation of faith, proving it by difficult deeds. They are truthful in faith, and truthful in their words and actions too.

**"obedient"**: they submit to Allah and obey Him and His Messenger, fulfilling His sacred law completely. They are compliant before God.

**"and charitable"**: they spend from their wealth on the acts of obedience they were commanded, in kindness to kith and kin, help for the needy and comfort for the destitute. They spend in the way of Allah.

**"and who pray for forgiveness before dawn"**: they beg Allah's pardon in the last hours of the night, saying, "Lord, forgive us." That time is singled out because it is the hour of unawareness and of the joy of sleep, when hearts are least distracted and prayers are most likely to be accepted.

### What al-Muttaqīn add to the prayer

Allah calls these people al-Muttaqīn, the mindful who fear Him, and He promised them tremendous rewards. Their patience covers obedience performed and prohibitions left. Their truthfulness covers faith declared and faith acted on. Their obedience is plain submission. Their spending runs from commanded worship to the care of relatives and the poor. Their seeking of forgiveness is then set at night because that practice testifies to the virtue of asking pardon in its latter part.

A family precedent is even given. When Yaʿqūb (Jacob) told his sons, "I will ask my Lord for forgiveness for you" (12:98), he waited until the latter part of the night to make the supplication.

### The last third of the night

Reports in the Two Sahihs, the Musnad and the Sunan collections agree on the Prophet's words:

> "Every night, when the last third of it remains, our Lord, the Blessed, the Superior, descends to the lowest heaven saying, 'Is there anyone to ask Me, so that I may grant him his request? Is there anyone to invoke Me, so that I may respond to his invocation? Is there anyone seeking My forgiveness, so that I may forgive him?'" 

His practice fits that hour. ʿĀʾishah, in the Two Sahihs, remembered witr offered across the night (early, middle and late), but toward the end of his life he confined it to the late part. ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar prayed by night while repeatedly asking Nāfiʿ whether the last part had arrived. Once Nāfiʿ confirmed it, supplication and seeking forgiveness continued until dawn. Ibn Abī Ḥātim collected that report.

### The five qualities, limb by limb

The five are also distributed across the person. The patient are patient with their hearts, the truthful truthful with their spirits, the devoted devoted with their souls, the expenders spend what they are able, and those who seek forgiveness at dawn do so with their tongues. Such people are the chevaliers, whose speaking and doing agree. In the heart they remain patient under the Real's command. In the spirit they walk straight in His covenant. In the body they observe the rightful due of His command. With wealth they spend on His road. With the tongue they ask forgiveness and seek from His generosity.

Neither world can detain the patient. Trial after trial is borne without complaint until the Patron is reached, with faces turned from worldly and otherworldly distractions. Truthfulness begins in speech aimed straight at the goal, continues in travel and thought, and finally drops even its visible marks. Such a person plunges onward toward security and "the seat of truthfulness, at an Omnipotent King" (54:55). The devoted grip the door, swallow sorrow, set loved ones and companions aside, and seek proximity. Poverty becomes clothing and empty hands rise at generosity's doorway: "Until You open, we will not leave, and until You caress, we will not go away." Standing alternates with prostration, hope with fear. Then comes the caress from the Exalted Presence: friendship's road is solitary, its wine's drinker is promised vision, and the truthful eventually reach desire. A longing poem waits for one glance from Allah, sorrow's end, fortune entering the door and elation's sun rising. Expenders give property as far as ability reaches, then spend selves through deeds and hearts through truthful states.

The portrait of verse 16's prayer can now be seen from the inside. God's people fear Him most, desire the next life most, and keep words and actions in agreement. Their wealth no longer belongs to them but is devoted to His cause. Hardship on His path does not turn them, because no one else can rescue a person who turns away from God. Their hearts are full of His remembrance, dhikr, and they call on Him spontaneously. The path, the remembrance and the pardon-seeking are one life, not three.

### In short

- The mindful are patient, truthful, obedient, generous, and devoted to seeking forgiveness in the last part of the night.
- That hour is honoured by the Prophet's own practice and by the promise that Allah calls out then for anyone who asks, invokes or seeks forgiveness.
- Inwardly the five qualities cover heart, spirit, body, wealth and tongue, and they make the prayer of the verse before a description of a whole life.
