<<<VERSE 4:60>>>
The claim measured against the courtroom: "Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who claim they believe in what has been revealed to you and what was revealed before you? They seek the judgment of false judges, which they were commanded to reject. And Satan ˹only˺ desires to lead them farther away". The verses before commanded the reverting of all matters to Allah and the Messenger. These ones censure the reverting to anything else.

### Phrase by phrase

**"those who claim they believe in what has been revealed to you and what was revealed before you"** catches the pretenders. Their claim runs: the Torah before, the Book revealed to Muḥammad ﷺ now. Some had been of the people of the Book and then entered Islam, or claimed to. The claim is a verbal camouflage for hypocrisy, with hearts stuffed with the old disbelief.

**"They seek the judgment of false judges, which they were commanded to reject"** is the exposure. In their disputes, judgment is sought from something other than Allah's Book and the sunnah of His Messenger. The word left untranslated is Ṭāghūt, one who exceeds the limits, and in usage the Satan, so that carrying a dispute to Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf is equated with carrying it to Satan: he was a satan personified, or bypassing a God-given decision can only come from Satan's teaching, and the follower of that course has taken his case straight to Satan's court.

**"And Satan ˹only˺ desires to lead them farther away"** warns that whoever follows Satan will be misled onto a remote wrong way.

### The two courts of Madinah

In the early period of Madinah, judgement on controversial issues could be sought at two courts. One pre-dated Islam, run by the leaders of the Jews. The other was set up by the Prophet ﷺ after the Migration. Muslims with flimsy cases, aware they could not win in the Prophet's court, used to approach the court of Kaʿb ibn Ashraf, a Jewish tribal chief and a sworn enemy of the Prophet and his followers.

The special incident behind the verses identifies a hypocrite by the name of Bishr, who got into a dispute with a Jew. The Jew proposed going to Muḥammad ﷺ, and Bishr countered with Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf. The secret on both sides was the same: both were certain the Prophet's ﷺ judgment would be true and just, free of any tilt or favour, so the Jew, being in the right, trusted him over his own chief, and Bishr, being in the wrong, knew the judgment would go against him. After cross-talk they did go to the Prophet ﷺ, whose investigation proved the Jew right, and he decided for him. Bishr then hit on a new plan: persuade the Jew to take the case to ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, expecting that ʿUmar, usually hard on disbelievers, would decide for the Muslim claimant against the Jew. The Jew agreed, and before ʿUmar told the whole story, including the Prophet's ﷺ decision. ʿUmar asked Bishr whether this was really how it happened, and Bishr confessed. ʿUmar told him to wait, went into the house, and came out with a sword and finished the hypocrite off. The word that came with the stroke: this is the decision for him dissatisfied with the decision of the Holy Prophet ﷺ. The incident appears in the Rūḥ al-Maʿānī from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās, as narrated by Thaʿlabī and Ibn Abī Ḥātim. The heirs of the killed man then sued ʿUmar for killing a Muslim without legal authority, submitting interpretations of his kufr in words and deeds to prove he had been a believer, and the verse unfolded the reality: the person killed was a hypocrite, and ʿUmar stood absolved.

Other reports give other faces of the same disease. A man from the Anṣār had a dispute with a Jew, who urged going to the Prophet ﷺ because he knew he would not be wronged, while the Helper insisted on a soothsayer in Medina, and Allah rebuked them both. At-Ṭabarānī recorded from Ibn ʿAbbās that Abū Barzah al-Aslamī was a soothsayer who judged between the Jews in their disputes, and when some Muslims came to him for judgment, the verses came down. The occasion reports come through several chains, naming Abū Burdah al-Aslamī the soothsayer, a property dispute involving one Qays, and, through Dāwūd from ash-Shaʿbī with Isḥāq in the chain, a hypocrite who steered a Jewish disputant to arbiters he knew took bribes, away from the Prophet who did not, with Abū Ṣāliḥ and Abū Ḥātim aṭ-Ṭamīmī among the transmitters. The verse's meaning is general regardless: it censures all who refrain from the Qur'an and sunnah and prefer whatever judgment of falsehood they choose. The contrast is drawn on both sides: the polytheists say, when told to follow what Allah has sent down, "No, we shall follow what we found our fathers following" (31:21), while the only saying of the faithful believers, called to Allah and His Messenger to judge between them, is: "We hear and we obey" (24:51).

### The inner reading

Outwardly they proclaimed sincerity while acting hypocritically in secret, He exposed them through the tongue of Gabriel, yakfurū bihi, reject him, the command they ignored. Whoever abandons his path and returns to anyone other than his master, his ustādh, earns exclusion and condemnation.

### In short

- The claim to believe both revelations is refuted by the choice of judge, and the Ṭāghūt of the verse is the false judge, the Satan, and Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf in one.
- Bishr's route from the Prophet's true judgment to ʿUmar's sword is the occasion, and the verse absolved ʿUmar by naming the killed man a hypocrite.
- The soothsayer's court, the bribetaking arbiters and the flimsy case are all faces of the same refusal.
- The believers' answer to the call of judgment is "we hear and we obey", the polytheists' answer is the path of the fathers, and the pretenders stand between, claiming the first while walking the second.

<<<VERSE 4:61>>>
The flinch that gives them away: "When it is said to them, 'Come to Allah's revelations and to the Messenger,' you see the hypocrites turn away from you stubbornly". Called to the court of the Book, the munāfiq turns his back.

### Phrase by phrase

**"When it is said to them, 'Come to Allah's revelations and to the Messenger'"** is the call to the ruling Allah has revealed and to the judgment of the Messenger ﷺ in matters of mutual quarrels and disputes.

**"you see the hypocrites turn away from you stubbornly"** means they turn away in arrogance and aversion, to others. One report of the first occasion names Ḥāṭib ibn Abī Baltaʿah, the pretender, in a dispute with az-Zubayr ibn al-ʿAwwām, the Prophet's cousin, turning away from the judgment in complete rejection.

### What the flinch proves

No Muslim turns away from a decision given under the dictates of the sacred law. Whoever does it is a munāfiq by definition. When this hypocrite's disbelief was practically exposed through his dissatisfaction with the Prophet's ﷺ decision, his being killed by ʿUmar became all the more justified, for he no longer remained a concealing hypocrite and became an open kāfir, an apostate. The occasion stands with all the reports of the passage: the Anṣārī with the soothsayer, Abū Barzah al-Aslamī the soothsayer of the Jews, the property dispute, Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf's court, and the two courts of Madinah after the Migration, the one of the Jewish leaders and the one of the Prophet ﷺ.

Read inwardly, everything other than the word of the Real is easy listening for hypocrites, while God's unity is heard only by the sincere. The listless and the hostile miss what is true, since opposing personal whims is hard for the insincere. The bat's eye cannot face the sun, and the hypocrites could not stand firm before him ﷺ, which is why they turned away so vehemently.

### In short

- The call to the Book and the Messenger meets the hypocrite's back, and the flinch is itself the diagnosis.
- Dissatisfaction with the Prophet's judgment turned the concealer into an open apostate, justifying what ʿUmar did.
- Everything but the Real's word is easy listening for hypocrites, and the sun that the bat cannot face is the sincerity of the call.

<<<VERSE 4:62>>>
The oaths that arrive too late: "How ˹horrible˺ will it be if a disaster strikes them because of what their hands have done, then they come to you swearing by Allah, 'We intended nothing but goodwill and reconciliation.'". The disaster strikes, the accomplice is killed, and then the swearing begins.

### Phrase by phrase

**"if a disaster strikes them because of what their hands have done"** is the catastrophe befalling them for the unbelief and disobedience their hands sent before them. The question carries one answer: there is no turning away and escaping it.

**"then they come to you swearing by Allah"** shows them compelled by the disaster, in need of the Prophet ﷺ, apologizing and swearing.

**"'We intended nothing but goodwill and reconciliation'"** was their plea in gist: not any lack of belief in the truth of his station and mission, not a preference of others' decisions over his as the ultimate truth, but expedient considerations. A case going to the Messenger of Allah would have meant a decision strictly legal, true and just, devoid of options for mutual patch-up, so, they said deceptively, they took it elsewhere so that something good would show up for both parties through a mutual conciliation, an approximate judgment without regard for the painful truth.

### The timing of the oaths

They came up with these interpretations only when their secret was revealed, their wickedness and hypocrisy came into the open, and their man was killed at the hands of ʿUmar. The verse makes clear that they are liars in their oaths and interpretations, and that everything they did, they did because of their disbelief and hypocrisy. Their pattern of behaviour stands exposed. Called to the Book of Allah and His Messenger, they turn away, all averse, and that is no hallmark of a true Muslim. But once their own evil deeds bring trouble, the exposure of their breach or the killing of their man, they become all alert and come swearing by Allah that they meant nothing but good. The like of them appears elsewhere in the Book, those in whose hearts is a disease, hurrying to the friendship of disbelievers and saying: we fear a turn of fortune, until they become regretful for what they kept secret in themselves (5:52).

Read inwardly, the insincere beseech at misfortune's attacks without foundation, and their plea deserves no consideration, for it lasts only until the tribulation passes. Greater than the affliction is indifference to the affliction brought on by one's own hands. And part of the affliction is losing the blessing of the present moment to what is of no use.

### In short

- The disaster is homemade, and the oaths come only after the secret is out and the accomplice dead.
- Goodwill and reconciliation were the camouflage for preferring an approximate judgment to the painful truth, when doing good involves following the rulings of Allah's sacred law.
- The insincere plea expires with the tribulation, and the worst affliction is not to feel the affliction.

<<<VERSE 4:63>>>
The sentence on the whole affair: "˹Only˺ Allah knows what is in their hearts. So turn away from them, caution them, and give them advice that will shake their very souls". After the exposure and the oaths, the instruction to the Prophet ﷺ.

### Phrase by phrase

**"˹Only˺ Allah knows what is in their hearts"** names what is rooted there: disbelief and hypocrisy, with all the mendacity of the excuses. Nothing escapes Allah's watch, and He will punish accordingly. Let Him be sufficient for His Messenger, whose knowledge covers their apparent and hidden affairs.

**"So turn away from them"** means do not punish them because of what is in their hearts, turning aside from them with forgiveness, and, in the case at hand, rejecting their charge of excess against ʿUmar, since the killed man's disbelief had become manifest.

**"caution them"** means advising them against the hypocrisy and evil residing in their hearts, making them fear God.

**"give them advice that will shake their very souls"** means speaking, between the Prophet ﷺ and them, effective words that might benefit them, penetrating words affecting the issue of their souls, a reprimand in plain terms, so that they repent.

### The counsel that remains

The end of the verse asks the Prophet ﷺ to give good counsel to the rest of the hypocrites in a way that touches their hearts: tell them to fear what lies beyond the present life, invite them to follow the path of Islam sincerely, and remind them of the likely worldly punishment if they cling to their hypocrisy, which might be exposed some day, and they might meet the same fate as Bishr. Another reading of the passage applies the verses to the builders of the counter mosque, Thaʿlabah and Ḥāṭib, who came swearing they intended nothing by the building except kindness to the believers and religious harmony, asking only that a man of knowledge be sent to teach them, while Allah knew the hypocrisy and antagonism in their hearts. The reports of the soothsayer's court in Madinah, the Anṣārī's insistence, Abū Barzah al-Aslamī and Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf stand behind the whole passage, in the records Ibn Abī Ḥātim shares, and the sunnah it defends.

Read inwardly, the instruction balances generosity with reserve: be generous with them in admonition as sympathy dictates, but hold the heart back from concern and from trust. None can help those whom God does not support, in any way.

### In short

- Allah's knowledge of the hearts settles the case, and ʿUmar's absolution comes with it.
- The pretenders are turned from, not punished for their hidden state, but cautioned and shaken with plain words that reach the soul.
- The counsel aims at repentance, from the fear of the beyond to the reminder of Bishr's fate.
- Generosity in admonition goes with reserve of trust, for none can help whom God does not support.
