The verses before this one showed the perfect power of the Lord and the great blessings that come from it. From that the next step follows by itself. If He holds that power, sends those blessings and is the Lord of all the worlds, then He is the One to be called on in every condition, in want and in distress. To pass Him by and turn somewhere else is ignorance and loss. "Call upon your Lord humbly and secretly. Surely He does not like the transgressors". With the command come the rules of conduct that belong to it, and when those rules are kept, the hope that the prayer is answered grows.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Call upon your Lord"** rests on a word that means asking someone to remove a need, and the same word is also used for remembering in the plain sense. Both meanings can be taken here. On the first, the verse says to ask Allah alone for what you need. On the second, it says to keep your remembrance and your worship for Him alone. Both explanations have been reported from the early authorities.

The act itself is given a high rank. Muṣṭafā said that supplication is worship. Allah commands His servants to call on Him, since that is what secures their welfare in this life and in the next. In one place the Book says, "and remember your Lord within yourself" (7:205). The Two Sahihs carry a report from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, who said that the people raised their voices in supplication, and that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told them: O people, take it easy on yourselves, for you are not calling on one who is deaf or absent. The One you are calling is All-Hearing and Near. If it is taken as calling out, then it is the same as praise. If it is taken as asking, then it is the proper business of a servant. Either way it is worship and a means of deliverance.

**"humbly"** translates a word that carries the sense of knowing one's own inability, of humility, and of submission with a refined lowliness of spirit. The one who prays is to stand before his Lord as weak, helpless and unable to hold his own, modest and low, with the pride and the self-view put away, and then ask. Supplication belongs to the soul. It requires that its words match the weakness of the one who speaks them, that the manner of it mirrors that modesty, and that the humility show in the very way he comes to the act.

That is why a great deal of what passes for supplication today cannot really be called by that name. It is recitation rather than asking. People do not know what they are saying, and the words roll on as a routine. An imam reads out words in Arabic that he has memorised at the end of the prayer, and often he does not know what they mean, while the people behind him repeat Amen after each line with no idea of what has been said. What comes out of all of it is the sound of some words, while supplication has a reality of its own that is not there. That Allah in His mercy may accept even such lifeless words and give them the effect of an answered prayer is another matter. But it has to be understood that supplication is not read out. It is asked. And so it should be asked in the way that asking deserves.

There is a further point. If a person does know the meaning of the words he uses, and understands what he is saying, but comes without the right approach and manner, the whole thing is reduced to a claim that no created servant is entitled to make.

**"and secretly"** gives the second rule. The asking is to be done privately and in a lowered voice, which is better in merit and more likely to be answered. Three drawbacks attach to a raised voice. It is hard to keep modesty and humility while doing it. It opens the door to showing off and to wanting to be noticed. And the manner of it suggests that the one praying hardly knows that Allah is All-Hearing and All-Knowing, who hears what is said quietly and knows what is hidden inside. When the voices of the Companions rose loud in supplication on the day of Khaybar, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told them that they were not calling on one who is deaf or absent. The One they were calling is Hearing and Near. Allah Himself has described the supplication of one of the righteous in the words "when he called his Lord with a call kept quiet" (19:3). The state that He likes is the one in which the asking is kept lowered and subdued.

**"Surely He does not like the transgressors"** names the fault that spoils all of this. The word comes from going past a limit, and the sense is that Allah does not love those who cross the lines. Crossing the line in supplication takes several forms. The first is when the prayer is dressed up with rhyming and ornamental devices that damage the humility and submission which are its real substance. The second is when unnecessary restrictions are laid on what is asked. Abdullah ibn Mughaffal heard his son praying for the white palace on the right side of Paradise, and stopped him, saying that fixing such conditions is crossing the limit that the Book and the hadith have forbidden. The third is when a person prays for harm to Muslims in general, or asks for something that will hurt them. And it is also a form of the same fault that a prayer be made in a raised voice where there is no need for it.

Ibn Abbas said of this line that it covers supplication and everything else as well. Abu Mijlaz said that an example of it is a person asking to reach the rank of the prophets. Imam Ahmad reported that Abu Ni'amah said that Abdullah bin Mughaffal heard his son making a supplication in which he asked for the white castle on the right side of Paradise if he entered it. He told his son to ask Allah for Paradise and to seek refuge with Him from the fire, and then said that he had heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say that there would come people who go past the limit in supplication and in purification. Ibn Majah and Abu Dawud recorded that report.

### Keeping the voice where it belongs

One of the early teachers said that a prayer made quietly stands seventy degrees above one made loudly. The habit of the righteous elders was to exert themselves to the full in remembrance and prayer, so that these kept them busy most of the time, and yet nobody heard their voices. Their supplications stayed between them and their Lord. Many of them had the whole Qur'an by heart and recited it while nobody knew. Others were deep in religious study and never told anyone about it. Others would come home after long sessions of prayer and no one would know anything of it. He said that he had seen such people who never performed a private act of worship out in the open where others could see, and whose voices in supplication stayed very low.

Ibn Jurayj said that raising the voice in supplication and making a noise of it is makruh. In his book on the rulings of the Qur'an, Imam Abu Bakr al-Jassas said that this verse shows a lowered voice to be more deserving than a raised one, and that the same has been reported from Hasan al-Basri and from Ibn Abbas. The same verse shows that the Amin said at the end of the al-Fatihah should also be said quietly, since that Amin is itself a supplication.

The same teaching bears on the imams of the mosques, who in our time seem to have forgotten what the Book and the Sunnah and the elders said on this subject. What follows the prayer as a collective supplication has become an artificial performance. Words are read out loud, which is against the etiquette of supplication, and which disturbs the prayer of those who joined the congregation late and are still finishing what they missed. Custom has such a hold on people that they do not notice the harm. There is no objection where a group has to make one supplication together and a single person says the words in a reasonably audible voice while the rest say Amen, provided that it disturbs nobody and does not turn into a habit that ordinary people take as the standard way of praying.

In the second sense of the verse, where the command is taken as remembrance and worship, the position of the early scholars is that quiet remembrance is better than loud. Those who advise loud remembrance for beginners in the Chishtiah order do it with an eye on the condition of the seeker, as a treatment, so that the voicing of it drives away sluggishness and the heart learns to settle into the remembrance of Allah. Even with them, raising the voice in remembrance is not desirable in itself. It is permitted, and its justification is established from the hadith, so long as no showing off or desire to be seen enters into it.

The weight of the matter is settled in a report that Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Ibn Hibban and Al-Baihaqi all carry from Saʿd ibn Abi Waqqas, that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that the best remembrance is the hidden one and the best provision is what suffices.

There are times and places, however, where a voice is wanted. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ taught those by his words and by his practice. The Adhan and the Iqamah are given aloud, the recitation in the prayers that are said aloud is raised, and so are the takbirs of the Salah, the takbirs of the days of sacrifice, and the Talbiyah of the Hajj. On that basis the jurists concluded that where the Prophet ﷺ taught by word or by deed that the voice should be raised, it is raised, and that in every other place quiet remembrance is better and more useful. The same measure holds for every act of worship. Take Salah, Sawm, Hajj and Zakah, and all dealings and transactions. When the limits set by the sacred law are crossed in them, they do not remain acts of worship, and they become sin instead.

### What the verse asks of the one who calls

The words humble and secret have been explained in more than one way. Ibn Jarir said that the first means obeying Him in humility and lowliness, and that the second means humbleness in the heart together with certainty of His oneness and His lordship, and that a person should not pray loudly in order to be seen. Others read the two words as openly and in secret, and others again as humble and out of fear. The transgressors, on that reading, are those who call for what is not allowed for them against the righteous. Another reading of the same line says that the transgressors here are the braggarts and the loud-mouthed.

One short commentary puts the whole command in a few words. Believers are to call on their Lord with complete humility, silently and in private, sincere in their prayer, not showing off and not worshipping others along with Him in their prayers. Those who pass beyond the limits in their prayers are not loved by Him. One of the worst ways of doing that is calling on others alongside Him, which is what the idolaters do.

There is a wider frame around all of this. The greatest thing a human being can reach is the mercy of God, and that grace falls to the lot of those who are attached to God so completely that every emotion they have is turned towards Him. They call out to Him only, they are humble before Him alone, they hope to receive from Him, and they fear Him alone lest what they have be taken away. Those who live like that are seeking nearness to God, and God gives them a place near to Him.

### What the earliest teachers said of the call

The inner preconditions of supplication are given a full treatment in one strand of teaching. The servant is told to plead, to weep, and to throw himself down in lowliness at the Exalted Threshold, and it is to that state that the words "in pleading and secret" point. Yaḥyā Muʿādh said that worship is a storehouse, that supplication is the key to it, and that the teeth of the key are lawful morsels, which is to say that a prayer fed on unlawful food does not turn the lock.

A report tells of Adam, who mourned and pleaded over his slip for a hundred years. Finally Gabriel asked how long the pleading and the weeping were to go on, and whether there was any way for the apology to be accepted and the wound to be healed. The answer came that Adam was to be left alone, for if that pleading and weeping had not been foreseen from him, the slip would not have been decreed for him in the first place. It was decreed because it was known that helplessness would bring him to supplication and pleading, and because the lament of a servant and his weeping for his Lord are loved. The sobs of the sinner are dearer than the murmur of the one who glorifies.

The same teaching is in another verse of the Book, where the Lord says, "supplicate Me and I will respond to you" (40:60). The sense is that a person should call on Him so that He answers, know Him so that He forgives, and ask of Him so that He gives. And in another place the Book praises the One "who responds to the distressed when he calls on Him" (27:62). The distressed person is the one whose capacity to bear the trial has run out. Who else would answer his call, or hear his supplication, or come to the aid of his helplessness? He has no handhold left, and he looks at his own days as so many days given to the wind, his hands empty of every means of approach and of every act of obedience. The supplication of such a person is like an arrow that flies straight to its target.

### In short

- Supplication is worship, and the word carries both the sense of asking for a need and the sense of remembering, so the verse commands both kinds of turning to Him.
- The prayer is to be made with the humility of a person who knows his own helplessness, and in a lowered voice, since the loud voice is hard to keep modest, invites showing off, and forgets that Allah hears everything.
- The line about the transgressors covers every kind of excess in prayer, from ornamental wording and invented restrictions to asking for what is forbidden or praying for harm.
- Quiet remembrance is the better way, and it is the way the righteous elders kept, while a raised voice belongs to the few places where the Prophet ﷺ himself taught that it should be raised.
- Even the Amen at the end of the al-Fatihah is a supplication, and so it too is said in a low voice.
- The ones who reach the mercy of God are those whose hopes and fears have been gathered onto Him alone.

<<<VERSE 7:56>>>
From the manner of calling, the passage turns to the state of the world in which the calling is done. "Do not spread corruption in the land after it has been set in order. And call upon Him with hope and fear. Indeed, Allah's mercy is always close to the good-doers". The three parts of the verse hold together. The earth was put right, the one who lives on it is not to spoil it, and the call that goes up from it is to carry both fear and hope.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not spread corruption in the land after it has been set in order"** is built on two words set against each other. The first word means the state of being good, correct and properly arranged, and the second means the state of evil, corruption, mischief and disorder. Imam Raghib al-Isfahan said in his study of the words of the Qur'an that this second word describes a thing going past the limits of moderation, whether the crossing is small or enormous. The amount of corruption in any case is measured by how far those limits were crossed. Since the word for corruption means making things bad while its opposite means correcting, reforming and putting into order, the verse as it stands means that they should not make things bad on the earth after Allah had made them good and proper. The same command is given elsewhere in the Book: "and do not do mischief on the earth after it has been set in order" (5:56).

**"And call upon Him with hope and fear"** gives the inner state of the one who prays. The fear is of the severe punishment He holds, and the hope is in the tremendous reward He holds.

**"Indeed, Allah's mercy is always close to the good-doers"** closes the verse with a promise. His mercy is for those who do good, which is to say those who obey His commands and keep away from what He has forbidden. The Book says in another place, "and My mercy encompasses all things, and I will ordain it for those who are mindful of Me" (7:156). Matar Al-Warraq said that a person should earn the promise of Allah by obeying Him, since He has decreed that His mercy is near to those who do good. Ibn Abi Hatim collected that saying.

### The three ways a thing is set right

The same authority set out three ways in which Allah puts a thing right. He may begin by creating it right and proper, as when the Book says that He will improve the condition of a people (47:2). He may take out a corruption that has entered into something, as when the Book says that He may make deeds good and right (33:71). Or a command may be given to put something in order. This verse carries a command to abstain from corruption on the earth after it has been set in order, and the ordering of the earth spoken of here can be taken in two senses.

In the first sense the ordering is outward. The earth was made arable and able to grow trees, rain was made to come from the clouds, and flowers and fruits come from plants and trees as gifts. The earth was made the means by which human beings and animals are kept alive.

In the second sense the ordering is inward and spiritual. Allah set things right on the earth by sending His messengers and His Books, with instructions, and by clearing it of error, of shirk and of kufr. The verse may well mean the outward ordering and the inward ordering together. On that reading it says that God has put the earth in order, physically and spiritually, and that people are not to create disorder in it or engineer evil there through their sins and their disobedience.

### The order that is inward and the order that is outward

The outward ordering can be seen in the way the earth itself was made. It was given a middle state, neither liquid like water, on which it would be hard to settle, nor hard like rock or iron, which could not be dug. It can be worked, broken up and smoothed, so that it lends itself to farming and to growing plants and trees, flowers and fruits. It can be dug for wells, cut for moats and canals, and built upon with strong foundations. Inside the earth and on it, things and conditions were created that help people live there and that bring vegetation, trees, flowers and fruits out of it. From outside it, wind, light, heat and cold were created, and water was made to rain down from the clouds so that trees could grow. The stars and the planets were brought into service, casting warm and cold rays on the earth, and those rays fill flowers and fruits with colour and juice. Human beings were given intelligence, and with it they dig raw materials out of the earth, iron, copper, wood and many other metals and rocks, and work them into a whole new world of industry and craft. That is the outward ordering of the earth, and it was brought about by the perfect power of Allah.

The inward ordering of life rests on three things, on the remembrance of Allah, on a person's tie with his Lord, and on obedience to Him. To begin with, a basic substance and an urge to remember Him and to obey Him have been put into every human heart, as the Book says of the soul that He inspired it with its wrong and its right (91:8). Beyond that, there is no particle anywhere around a human being in which Allah has not placed wonders of His power and His creativity, and a person of ordinary understanding is moved by them to say, "blessed be Allah, the best of creators" (23:14). And beyond those signs, He sent the messengers and revealed the Books, and through them the essential link between the Creator and the created was established. The command that follows is that the earth, having been put in order outwardly and inwardly, should not be allowed to go bad through human misdeeds.

### How the two kinds of disorder feed each other

Disorder has two aspects as well, the outward and the inward, and the verse forbids both. The recurring insistence of the Book and of the Prophet ﷺ is on the inward order, since that is the central duty, and the aim of that insistence is to stop any inward disorder from rising. But the link between the two runs in both directions in the world we live in, and disorder in one becomes a source of disorder in the other. So when the sacred law closed the doors of inward disorder, it also closed the doors of outward disorder. Theft, robbery, murder and every kind of immodesty and indecency create disorder of both kinds, and firm restrictions were laid down and penalties prescribed against such acts in particular. The ordinary sins and offences were forbidden too, because every sin sooner or later becomes a source of outward disorder, and at another time a source of inward disorder. Looked at closely, every outward disorder turns into an inward one, and every inward disorder ends by producing an outward one.

That outward disorder leads to inward disorder is plain enough, since it is a violation of the command to obey what has been laid down, and disobedience to Allah is another name for inward disorder. How inward disorder turns into outward disorder takes a little reflection to see. The whole world and everything in it was created by the Master of creation, and all of it stands subject to His command. As long as human beings obey Allah, everything stands harnessed into their service as is proper. But once they begin to disobey Him, everything around them begins to disobey them, working from behind a curtain that their eyes cannot see through. The proof of that hostility shows up when a person studies the effects and outcomes of things.

On the surface, people go on using these things as usual. Water goes down the throat and does not refuse to quench the thirst. Food does not stop removing hunger. Clothing and housing do not fail to defend against heat and cold. But if the final outcome is considered, it turns out that none of these things is doing its assigned job, because the real function and use of them is to give peace and comfort, to take away anxiety and to cure sickness. Look at the condition of the world now. Comforts and the means of curing disease have multiplied far beyond anything once imagined, and at the same time most human beings are caught in extreme distress and illness. Diseases that were never known and disasters that follow one after another have become ordinary. Nobody, not even the greatest among people, is content and at peace in his station. The more the output of these comforts and skills rises, the more the diseases, anxieties, disasters and hardships rise alongside them, very like the saying, "the more they treat you, the more sick they make you".

If a person caught up in the modern world could lift his head a little above his fascination with machines and gadgets, and above his enchantment with what energy can do and with the material gains that come with it, he would see something. The whole effort to invent and to build, and to raise a society on top of those inventions, has failed to deliver the peace and comfort that was its real purpose. Nothing explains that failure except the inward disorder described above, the disorder in which people have turned away from their Lord, their Creator and Master. What He made has turned away from them on the inward front, and it refuses to give them real rest, however fast they run. The famous Maulana Rum put his finger on this when he observed that dust, water, air and fire, four elements that look senseless and inert, in truth carry a measure of intelligence and so serve the command of their Master. The same Maulana Rumi says that the cloud holds back its rain when people stop paying Zakah, and that an epidemic follows from Zina and spreads in every direction. That is not poetic imagining. It is a reality supported by the Book and the hadith. What shows itself here, in illness, epidemics, storms, hurricanes and floods, is only a small sample of the punishment that is still to come.

So the command not to spread corruption includes the crimes and sins that bring outward disorder into the world, and it also includes sin, disobedience and heedlessness towards Allah. And so this same verse goes straight on to say that He should be called upon in fear and hope. The calling is to be done in a way that keeps a person afraid that his prayer may go unanswered, and at the same time full of hope tied to the mercy of his Lord.

### The two wings of fear and hope

The two together are the wings on which the human soul flies, and the journey they carry it on is the journey with the truth. By them it rises and reaches the ranks prepared for it. The text shows that the two should be kept in balance. Some scholars said that fear should dominate through life while a person is healthy, so that no shortcoming creeps into his obedience. When death comes, they said, hope should dominate, since the strength to do what should have been done is gone and there is nothing left but to hope for the mercy of Allah. That observation stands in Al-Bahr Al-Muhit. Others among the researchers said that the purpose of the two is to keep a person on the straight path of the religion and constant in obedience. Since temperaments differ, some hold to that constancy through fear while others hold to it through love and hope, and each should take the way that helps him.

So the first verse carries two rules of the etiquette of supplication that belong to the outward person, that the supplication be made with humility and submission and that it be kept quiet, and this verse carries two rules that belong to the heart. The one praying should feel the danger that his prayer may not be answered, and he should hope at the same time that it will be. To grow careless about one's errors and sins is contrary to iman, and to lose hope in the boundless mercy of Allah is kufr. Both are extremes that are not allowed. The hope of being answered can only be held in the space between fear and hope.

The hint at the end of the verse is that of the two, hope carries more weight. The one who prays is addressing the Lord of all the worlds, whose mercy cannot be measured and whose generosity has no limit and no reluctance. He can answer the prayer of the worst of offenders. If a prayer stands in danger of going unanswered, that can only be because of the person's own misdeeds or because of a curse of sins hanging over him. Good deeds are needed for a person to come near the mercy of Allah. The Prophet ﷺ said that there are people who travel long distances, who look like the poor, who raise their hands to Allah, and yet their food is haram and their drink is haram and their clothing is haram. How is the prayer of such a person to be answered? That report comes from Abi Hurairah and is carried by Muslim and Tirmidhi. In another hadith the Prophet ﷺ said that the prayer of a servant keeps finding acceptance so long as he does not pray for a sin or for the cutting of a relationship, and so long as he does not make haste. The Companions asked what making haste meant, and he said that it is when a person thinks that he has been praying for so long and has not been answered, until he loses hope and gives up. And in a further report he said that when a person prays to Allah, he should do it in a state in which he has no doubt that it will be answered. The meaning is that he should keep his eye on the widest mercy of Allah and let his heart believe that the prayer will be answered, and that this does not contradict the fear that his sins may stand in the way of acceptance.

### The beautiful doing that brings mercy near

The last words of the verse speak of mercy coming close to those who do good. Muṣṭafā defined the beautiful doing when he said that it is to worship God as though you see Him, for if you do not see Him, He surely sees you. That report points to the meeting of the heart with the Real, to the secret core coming together with the Unseen, and to the spirit contemplating God. The verse urges the servant to purify himself in his deeds, to cut back his wishes, and to be loyal to what he accepted on the day of the compact and the covenant of Yes indeed (7:172). Since a person knows that He sees him, he should keep his heart on Him and pull it away from everything else. He should be a purifier of himself in his deeds and truthful in his states. In that line of teaching the Pir of the Tariqah said that an eye which has seen Him will not busy itself glancing at anything else. A spirit that has found companionship with Him will not long be content with water and dust. One accustomed to the presence of contemplation will not put up with the humiliation of the veil, and the ruler of his own city will not spend his life in exile. The words "as though you see Him" point to the Real being seen, and the words "for He sees you" are the seeing of the Real. And it was said in the same line that when the awe of seeing the Real is present, the blame of those who deny it does not matter. A person should strive in a service worthy of the One he worships rather than settle for the portion of water and dust, since awe at the sight of the Real is a flood, while the approval of people is only debris.

### In short

- The earth was set right outwardly and inwardly, and the command forbids spoiling that order through corruption, sin and disobedience.
- Corruption is any crossing of the limits of moderation, and setting right is the work of putting things back into order.
- Keep the call to Him balanced between fear of the punishment and hope in the reward, and the mercy He promises is near to those who do good.
- Outward disorder and inward disorder feed each other, and all the comfort and technology in the world cannot give real peace while people are disobeying their Lord.
- To lose hope in the mercy of Allah is as wrong as to grow careless about one's sins, and the prayer of a person whose food, drink and clothing are haram does not rise.
- Doing good in the sense the Prophet ﷺ gave it, worshipping as though seeing Him, is what brings a person close to that mercy.
