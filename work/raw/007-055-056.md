<<<VERSE 7:55>>>
The previous verse displayed Allah's perfect power and His great blessings. It follows that He alone should be called on, in distress and in need alike: "Call upon your Lord humbly and secretly. Surely He does not like the transgressors". He is the Master of all power, the giver of every blessing and the Lord of all the worlds. To pass Him by and turn elsewhere is ignorance and failure. Allah commands His servants to call on Him, because that secures their good in this life and the next. The verse also sets out rules of manners for supplication, and keeping them raises the hope that a prayer will be answered.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Call upon your Lord"**: in Arabic, duʿāʾ means calling on someone to remove a need. It is also used for remembrance in general. Both senses fit here. In the first, the verse means: ask Allah alone for what you need. In the second it means: make your remembrance (dhikr) and worship for Him alone. Both explanations are reported from early authorities on tafsīr. One explanation addresses the verse to all mankind. Another addresses it to the believers: call on your Lord sincerely, without showing off and without joining anyone with Allah in your prayers.

**"humbly"**: the word is taḍarruʿ. It means feeling one's own helplessness, humility and submission, with a refined lowering of the self. Ibn Jarīr explained it as obeying Him in humility and lowliness. Grammatically it describes the state of the one praying: call on Him in submission. One explanation pairs it with "openly". Another reads the pair as "humbly and out of fear".

**"and secretly"**: the word is khufyah, meaning in private, quietly, with a lowered voice. Ibn Jarīr said it means with humility in your hearts and certainty of His Oneness and lordship, not calling out loudly to show off. A later verse of this surah says the same: "Remember your Lord inwardly with humility and reverence" (7:205).

**"Surely He does not like the transgressors"**: the word al-muʿtadīn comes from iʿtidāʾ, crossing the limits. Allah does not like those who overstep limits, as the translation says. ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī reported Ibn ʿAbbās as saying this applies in supplication and in other matters too. Abū Mijlaz gave as an example asking to reach the rank of the prophets. Other explanations name those who pray for what is not permitted to them against the righteous, and the boastful and loud-mouthed in their prayers. One of the worst ways to overstep is to call on others besides Him, as the idolaters do.

### Why must supplication be humble?

The first word lays down the first rule. For a prayer to be answered, a person should come before Allah weak, helpless and unable to stand on his own, modest and humble. He should lay aside every trace of pride, dignity, ego and self-regard, and then ask. Supplication is a matter of the soul. Its words should match the weakness and lowliness of the one who asks. Its manner should mirror modesty, and that humility should show even in his outward bearing.

By this standard, much of what passes for supplication today is not supplication at all. It is a reading. Very often people do not know what they are saying, and it has become a routine, as can be seen in many mosques. At the end of the prayer the imam recites some memorised Arabic words of supplication. In some places the imams themselves do not know what the words mean. Even when they do, the less learned in the congregation have no idea. They keep saying "Āmīn, Āmīn" mechanically after the imam without any clue what is being asked. The result of all this performance is the sounding of some words. Supplication has a reality of its own, and here that reality is missing.

It is another matter that Allah, in His boundless mercy, may accept even these lifeless words and give them the effect of answered prayers. Still, everyone should understand that supplication is not read, it is asked. So it matters greatly to ask properly, as one should. And even someone who knows and understands the words of his prayer reduces it to a bland claim if he asks without the right attitude and bearing. No created servant has any right to make such a claim on Allah.

### Why should it be quiet?

The second word teaches that one should ask secretly and in a low voice. That is more meritorious and more likely to be answered. Praying aloud carries three risks. First, it is hard to keep modesty and humility. Second, hypocrisy and the wish for recognition can creep in. Third, the very manner suggests that the one praying hardly knows Allah is All-Hearing and All-Knowing. He knows what we show and what we hide, and He hears every word, spoken softly or loudly.

The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record from Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī that people once raised their voices in supplication. One account places this during the expedition to Khaybar. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ told them to go easy on themselves, for they were not calling on someone deaf or absent. The One they were calling, he said, is All-Hearing and Near. Allah Himself describes a righteous man's prayer in the words "when he cried out to his Lord privately" (19:3). This shows that the supplication Allah loves is made in a low, subdued voice.

Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said there is a difference of seventy degrees between a supplication made openly and aloud and one made in a low voice. The early righteous elders, he said, strove to the utmost in remembrance and supplication and spent most of their time in it, yet no one heard their voices. Their supplications stayed between them and their Lord. Many had memorised the whole Qur'an and kept reciting it, and others never knew. Some were busy with advanced study of religion and never told anyone. Others came out of their houses after long hours of prayer, and no one learnt of it. He said he had seen people who never did in public any worship they could do in private, and whose voices in supplication were very low.

Ibn Jurayj said that raising the voice and making a noise in supplication is makrūh, disliked. Imam Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ said in his Aḥkām al-Qurʾān that this verse shows a low-voiced supplication to be more meritorious than a loud one. The same is reported from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and Ibn ʿAbbās. He added that the verse also shows that "Āmīn" after Sūrat al-Fātiḥah is better said quietly, because Āmīn, "so be it", is itself a supplication.

### What about the loud prayer after congregational ṣalāh?

In many mosques today, the imams appear to have lost sight of what the Qur'an, the Sunnah and the early elders taught on this point. The supplication after each prayer has become an artificial ritual. Words are recited loudly, which goes against the manners of supplication. It also disturbs the prayer of latecomers who are still completing the part they missed after the imam has finished. The weight of custom has made people blind to these drawbacks.

On a particular occasion, when the aim is for a whole group to make a particular supplication, one person may say the words in a reasonably audible voice while the others say Āmīn. There is no harm in that. The conditions are that it does not disturb anyone else's prayer or worship, and that it does not become a fixed custom. Otherwise ordinary people come to think of it as the standard way of supplicating, as so often happens now.

### Is remembrance better quiet or aloud?

All of the above concerns supplication for one's needs. If duʿāʾ here is taken to mean remembrance and worship, the settled view of the early scholars is that quiet remembrance is better than loud. Shaykhs of the Chishtī order recommend loud remembrance for beginners. They do so as a remedy suited to the seeker's spiritual state, so that voicing it may drive away heedlessness and tune the heart to the remembrance of Allah. Otherwise raising the voice in remembrance is not desirable in itself, even for them. It is permissible, and hadiths support it, on condition that there is no hypocrisy or showing off (riyāʾ) in it.

Imam Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal, Ibn Ḥibbān, al-Baihaqī and others report from Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ that the Prophet ﷺ said the best remembrance is the hidden kind, and the best provision is what is enough.

Yet at certain times and in certain conditions, voiced remembrance is more desirable and meritorious. The Prophet ﷺ explained these by word and deed. Examples are the adhān and iqāmah, reciting the Qur'an aloud in the prayers that are recited aloud, the takbīrs of prayer and the takbīrs of tashrīq, and the talbiyah in Hajj. So all the Muslim jurists agree that voices must be raised wherever the Prophet ﷺ taught by word or deed to raise them. Everywhere else, silent remembrance is the most preferable and beneficial.

### What counts as overstepping in supplication?

Overstepping, in supplication or anything else, has the same result: Allah does not like it. Looked at closely, the religion of Islam is itself the keeping of limits and the choice to submit and obey. Ṣalāh, ṣawm, Hajj, zakāh and all dealings and transactions stop being worship and become sin once the limits set by the Sharīʿah are crossed in them.

Overstepping in supplication can take several forms.

- Using rhyme and other stylistic flourishes that spoil the humility and submission at its heart.
- Adding needless details. Imam Aḥmad reported, through Abū Niʿāmah, that ʿAbdullāh ibn Mughaffal heard his son ask Allah for the white palace on the right side of Paradise once he entered it. He told his son to ask Allah simply for Paradise and to seek refuge with Him from the Fire. He had heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say that people would come who overstep in supplication and in purification. Ibn Mājah and Abū Dāwūd also recorded this hadith, with a good chain that has no fault, and Allah knows best.
- Praying for harm to Muslims in general, or asking Allah for something harmful to them.
- Raising the voice in supplication without need.

### The inner reading: the sinner's sobs

Al-Muṣṭafā said, "Supplication is worship." Supplication is either calling or asking. As calling, it is praise. As asking, it suits the servant. Both are worship and a means of salvation. Yaḥyā Muʿādh said, "Worship of Allah is a storehouse. Its key is supplication, and the teeth of the key are lawful mouthfuls." The condition of supplication is pleading, weeping, and throwing oneself down in lowliness at the Exalted Threshold. That is why He says "humbly and secretly".

A report says that Ādam mourned and pleaded over his slip for a hundred years. At last Jibrīl (Gabriel) said, "Lord, You see Ādam's pleading Yourself, and You hear his weeping. Is there no way for You to accept his apology and put balm on his wound?" The command came: "Jibrīl, leave Ādam to Me. Had I not known this pleading and weeping in him, I would not have decreed the slip for him. I decreed it because I knew that when he became helpless he would loosen his tongue in supplication and pleading. I love My servant to lament and weep before Me. The sobbing of sinners is dearer to Me than the murmuring of those who glorify Me."

A verse like this one is "Call upon Me, I will respond to you" (40:60). It is as if He says: call on Me so that I may answer you, know Me so that I may forgive you, ask of Me so that I may give to you. Elsewhere He speaks of the One "Who responds to the distressed when they cry to Him" (27:62). It is as if He asks: when that poor, helpless man can bear his trial no longer, who but Me will answer him? Who else will hear his plea and come to his aid?

The distressed person has nothing left to hold on to. He looks at his days and sees them scattered to the wind. He sees his hands empty of every means of approach and every act of obedience. His supplication flies like an arrow straight to the target. And one of the conditions of supplication is that one's food be lawful.

### In short

- Since Allah alone has power and gives every blessing, He alone should be called on, for needs and in worship.
- Supplication should be humble, a real asking with understanding, not a mechanical reading of words.
- It is better made quietly, which guards humility and sincerity. Loud remembrance is for occasions the Prophet ﷺ taught, such as the adhān and talbiyah.
- Overstepping in supplication includes artful rhyme, needless details, praying for harm and needless loudness, and Allah does not like those who overstep.

<<<VERSE 7:56>>>
The next command joins the manners of supplication to a warning: "Do not spread corruption in the land after it has been set in order. And call upon Him with hope and fear. Indeed, Allah’s mercy is always close to the good-doers". Causing corruption after things have been put right does the greatest harm, so Allah forbids it. Instead He commands worship of Him, supplication to Him, earnest asking and humility before Him.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Do not spread corruption in the land"**: two opposites meet here, ṣalāḥ and fasād. Ṣalāḥ is the state of being good, right, sound and in order. Fasād is the state of evil, corruption and disorder. Imam Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī says in his Mufradāt al-Qurʾān that fasād means something going beyond the bounds of moderation, slightly or greatly. Every corruption grows or shrinks with how far those bounds are crossed, and the further they are crossed, the greater the corruption. Fasād is making things bad, and iṣlāḥ is putting them right. Others explain the corruption as sins and calling people to other than Allah, or as idolatry and acts of disobedience.

**"after it has been set in order"**: after Allah made the earth sound and good, through obedience and the call to Allah. He set it right by sending messengers to it and by establishing things according to His laws. When affairs are in good order and corruption then breaks out, it does the greatest harm to people.

Rāghib also says Allah puts things right in several ways. First, He creates them sound from the start, as in "and improve their condition" (47:2). Second, He removes corruption that has crept into something, as in "He will bless your deeds for you" (33:71). Third, He commands that things be kept in order, and this verse is such a command.

**"And call upon Him with hope and fear."** Worship Him and call on Him, fearing His severe punishment and hoping for His immense reward. Hope that you will enter His Paradise. Call on Allah alone. The manner meant is to fear that the prayer may go unanswered, while placing full hope in His mercy.

**"Indeed, Allah’s mercy is always close to the good-doers"**: Those who receive it are the ones who carry out His commands and keep away from His prohibitions, believers whose words and deeds are good. So be one of them. One explanation takes "mercy" here as Allah's Paradise. In the Arabic, "close" (qarīb) is masculine though "mercy" (raḥmah) is feminine, because mercy is joined to the name of Allah. Another verse says, "But My mercy encompasses everything. I will ordain mercy for those who shun evil" (7:156), that is, for those who have taqwā. Maṭar al-Warrāq said, "Earn Allah's promise by obeying Him, for He has decreed that His mercy is close to the good-doers." Ibn Abī Ḥātim collected this saying.

### What order is meant: outward or inward?

The setting in order of the earth can be taken two ways. The first is outward. Allah made the earth fit to farm and to grow trees. He sent rain from the clouds, so that plants and trees bring forth flowers and fruit, and He made the earth the support of life for humans and animals. The second is inward and spiritual. Allah set the earth right by sending His messengers, His Books and His guidance, cleansing it of kufr, shirk and error. Very possibly the verse means both. Allah has put the earth in order in body and spirit, so do not wreck it with sin and disobedience.

Consider the outward order first. Allah made the earth neither liquid like water, which would be hard to settle on, nor hard like rock or iron, which could not be dug. He made it in between, so that people could soften and level it and make it bear crops, trees, flowers and fruit. They could dig wells, cut moats and canals, and lay firm foundations for houses. Within it and upon it He placed what makes it habitable and lets plants, flowers and fruit grow. From outside it He sent wind, light, heat and cold, and rain from the clouds so that trees could grow. He put the stars and planets to work, sending warm and cool rays that fill flowers and fruit with colour and juice. He gave human beings the intelligence to draw raw materials from the earth, iron and copper, wood and many other metals and stones, and shape them into a new world of industry and craft. That is the outward order, brought about by Allah's perfect power.

The inward, spiritual order rests on remembrance of Allah (dhikr), a living bond with Him, and obedience (iṭāʿah). For this, Allah has placed in every human heart a basic urge to remember and obey Him: "then with ˹the knowledge of˺ right and wrong inspired it" (91:8). Around human beings there is not a particle in which He has not placed wonders of His power and astonishing creativity. Seeing them, even a person of ordinary understanding is moved to say, "So Blessed is Allah, the Best of Creators" (23:14). Beyond these signs, He sent His messengers and revealed His Books, which fully established the bond between Creator and creature. So the command is that, now the earth has been set in order outwardly and inwardly, human misdeeds must not ruin it.

### How do sins cause disorder in the physical world?

Just as order has an outward and an inward side, so does disorder, and this verse forbids both. The Qur'an and the Prophet ﷺ insist above all on spiritual order, which shuts the door on spiritual disorder. But in this world the two are so linked that disorder in one breeds disorder in the other. So the Sharīʿah of the Qur'an, having closed the doors of spiritual disorder, has also forbidden physical disorder. Theft, robbery, murder and every kind of shamelessness and indecency spread both kinds of disorder. Hence stern restrictions and punishments were laid down for these evils in particular, and ordinary sins were forbidden too. Every sin causes physical disorder at one time and spiritual disorder at another.

That outward disorder leads to inward disorder is obvious. It goes against the command to obey Allah, and disobedience to Allah is simply another name for spiritual disorder. How inward disorder turns into outward disorder takes a little serious thought to see. The whole world and everything in it, great and small, was made by the Master of creation and is subject to His command. As long as human beings obey Allah, everything is harnessed to serve them as it should. When they start disobeying Allah, everything in the world starts disobeying them, from behind a curtain that mortal eyes cannot see through. Careful thought about the effects and outcomes of things gives clear proof of this hostility.

On the surface, people go on using things as normal. Water still quenches thirst, food still removes hunger, and clothes and homes still keep out heat and cold. But judged by their final purpose, none of these things is doing its real job. Their true function is to give peace and comfort, to remove anxiety and to cure illness.

Look at the state of the world today. Comforts and cures have multiplied far beyond anyone's expectations, yet most people suffer severe distress and disease. Diseases no one had heard of appear, and fresh calamities arrive one after another. No one, not even the highest, is content and at peace in his station. In fact, the more these conveniences and products of skill increase, the more disease, anxiety, disaster and hardship increase alongside them. An Urdu saying puts it: the more they treat you, the sicker they make you.

Modern man is spellbound by power and its material gains. If he rose a little above his infatuation with things and technology, he would see that all his inventions, and the society built on them, have failed to give the peace and comfort that were their real purpose. The only reason is the inward disorder described above. We have begun to disobey our Lord, our Creator and Master, and so His creation has begun to disobey us inwardly. It refuses to give us real peace and comfort, however frantically we strive. The famous Maulānā Rūm pointed to this. Earth, air, water and fire, he said, look lifeless and senseless, yet they have a kind of awareness by which they work under their Master's command.

In sum, sin, heedlessness of Allah and disobedience to Him do not only cause spiritual disorder. They lead inevitably to physical disorder as well. The same Maulānā Rūmī says that when people stop paying zakāh the clouds stop bringing rain, and that adultery brings an epidemic that spreads in every direction. This is not poetic fancy. It is a reality firmly supported by the Qur'an and Hadith. And what appears in this world, as disease, epidemics, storms, hurricanes and floods, is only a light foretaste of the final punishment.

So the verse's ban on corruption in the land covers the crimes that disorder the world outwardly. It also covers sin, disobedience and heedlessness of Allah. That is why it goes straight on to say, "call upon Him with hope and fear".

### Why both hope and fear?

Fear and hope together are the two wings of the human soul on its journey of truth and steadfastness. On them it flies high, and by them it reaches the ranks meant for it. As the wording shows, the two should be equal.

Some scholars said that while alive and well, it is fitting to let fear prevail, so that there is no slackness in obedience. When death comes, it is better to let hope prevail. By then the strength to do what should have been done is gone, and nothing remains but to hope for Allah's mercy. This view is recorded in Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ.

Some careful scholars said the real aim is to hold to the straight path of religion and stay constant in obedience to Allah. People's temperaments and tastes differ. Some reach that steadfastness by letting fear lead. Others reach it by letting love and hope lead. Whichever of the two helps a person reach the goal, he should take that route.

### What are the manners of supplication, in sum?

The previous verse gave two rules for supplication, humility and quietness. Both concern the outward person. Taḍarruʿ means that while supplicating one should take on the look and bearing of someone weak, helpless and needy, never proud, arrogant or seemingly self-sufficient. Quietness concerns the mouth and tongue.

This verse gives two inner rules, which concern the heart. The one supplicating should feel in his heart the danger that his prayer may not be answered. At the same time he should hope that it will be. Growing careless about one's faults and sins is contrary to īmān, faith. Losing hope in Allah's boundless mercy is kufr, disbelief. Both are forbidden extremes. Hope of an answer is only rightly held by someone who stays between fear and hope.

### Why does the verse end with mercy?

The closing words, "Allah's mercy is always close to the good-doers", hint that of the two states, hope weighs more. The prayer is made to the Lord of all the worlds. The reach of His mercy cannot be imagined, and His generosity knows no shortage or reluctance. He can answer the prayer of the worst offender, even the prayer of Shayṭān himself. If there is any danger of a prayer going unanswered, it can only come from one's own misdeeds or the weight of one's sins. Being good in deeds is what brings a person close to Allah's mercy.

That is why the Prophet ﷺ spoke of a man who travels far, dishevelled and dusty, and raises his hands to Allah in supplication. But his food is ḥarām, his drink is ḥarām and his clothing is ḥarām. How, he asked, can such a man's prayer be answered? Muslim and at-Tirmidhī recorded this from Abū Hurairah.

In another hadith, recorded by Muslim and at-Tirmidhī, the Prophet ﷺ said a servant's prayer keeps being answered as long as he does not pray for a sin or for cutting family ties, and does not grow impatient. A Companion asked what growing impatient meant. He explained it as a person thinking he has prayed for so long without an answer, until he loses heart and gives up praying.

In yet another hadith the Prophet ﷺ said that whenever you call on Allah, do so with no doubt that you will be answered. That means keeping one's eyes on Allah's vast mercy and letting the heart trust that the prayer will be answered. This does not conflict with the fear that one's sins may stand in the way.

### Who comes close to Allah's mercy?

The greatest thing a person can achieve is to win Allah's mercy. But Allah's grace falls to the lot of those who bind themselves to Him so fully that all their feelings, without exception, are turned toward Him. They call on Him alone and humble themselves before Him alone. They hope to receive from Him, and they fear Him alone, lest what they have be taken away. People like this seek closeness to Allah, and Allah gives them a place near Him.

### The inner reading: worship as though you see Him

Al-Muṣṭafā said that excellence (iḥsān) is to worship Allah as though you see Him, for even if you do not see Him, He certainly sees you. This report points to the heart meeting the Real, the inmost self drawing near the Unseen, and the spirit beholding Allah. The verse urges the servant to purify his deeds, cut short his worldly hopes, and stay loyal to what he accepted on the day of the covenant, when he said, "Yes indeed" (7:172). Knowing that He watches you, fix your heart on Him and withdraw it from all else. Purify your deeds and be truthful in your states.

The master of the spiritual path (ṭarīqah) said: "Once an eye has seen Him, how can it busy itself with looking at anything else? Once a spirit has found His company, how long will it settle for water and clay? Once someone is used to the presence of contemplation, how can he bear the humiliation of the veil? Would a man who rules his own city choose to live out his days in exile?" "As though you see Him" hints that the Real is to be seen, and "He sees you" is the Real's own seeing.

He also said that once the awe of seeing the Real takes hold, the blame of deniers holds no terror. Serve in a way worthy of the One you worship, he said, not for a share of water and clay, for the awe of beholding the Real is a flood and the approval of people is mere debris.

### In short

- Do not corrupt the earth after Allah has set it in order, outwardly by its design and inwardly by His messengers and Books.
- Sin breeds disorder in the physical world as well as the spiritual, which is why comforts multiply while peace declines.
- Call on Allah with fear and hope together, the two wings of the soul. Carelessness about sin and despair of mercy are both forbidden.
- Hope weighs more, since Allah's mercy is vast and close to those who do good. Unlawful earnings and impatience can block a prayer.
- The highest goodness is to worship Allah as though seeing Him, knowing that He always sees you.
