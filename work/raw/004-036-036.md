<<<VERSE 4:36>>>
The great roster of rights, opened by the greatest right of all: "Worship Allah ˹alone˺ and associate none with Him. And be kind to parents, relatives, orphans, the poor, near and distant neighbours, close friends, ˹needy˺ travellers, and those ˹bondspeople˺ in your possession. Surely Allah does not like whoever is arrogant, boastful—". One sentence holds the whole of Islamic social teaching: eight classes of people, and the one disposition that withholds from all eight.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Worship Allah ˹alone˺ and associate none with Him"** commands worship of Him with no partner joined to it, for He alone is the Creator and Sustainer, sending favours and bounties on His creation in every situation. To Muʿādh the Prophet ﷺ said: "Do you know what Allah's right on His servants is?" Muʿādh replied that Allah and His Messenger know better. He said: "That they should worship Him and should not worship any others with Him." Then he said: "Do you know what the right of the servants on Allah is if they do this? That He should not punish them."

**"And be kind to parents"** comes immediately after the worship of Allah. The word is iḥsān, doing beautiful good, far more comprehensive than mere fulfilment of rights. It includes spending to support the parents as needed, serving them physically when required, not talking in a voice too sharp or loud, saying nothing that breaks their heart, and treating their friends and well-wishers in a way that pleases them. Even where the parents fell short in their own duty to the children, the children may not take that as an excuse for ill-treatment.

**"relatives"** covers kind treatment of relatives after the parents, males and females. For the poor, a gift is ṣadaqah. For a relative, it is ṣadaqah and ṣilah together, charity and the keeping of the tie.

**"orphans"** names those who lost the caretakers who would spend on them, so they are to be treated with kindness and compassion.

**"the poor"**, al-masākīn, who have various needs and cannot find what sustains them, so they are to be helped in a sufficient manner that ends their inadequacy. The matter of the destitute is elaborated where the shares of ṣadaqah are set out (9:60).

**"near and distant neighbours"** names the two kinds of the Arabic al-jār, the neighbour. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah said that Ibn ʿAbbās read the near neighbour as the one who is also a relative, and the distant neighbour as the one who is not, with Qatādah, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, Zayd ibn Aslam, Ad-Daḥḥāk, Maymūn ibn Mihrān, Mujāhid and ʿIkrimah saying similarly. Mujāhid was also reported to read the distant neighbour as the companion during travel. Generally the near neighbour is the one next to the house and the distant one lives at some remove, and some read the near as the Muslim neighbour and the distant as the non-Muslim. The words of the Qur'an lend themselves to all of these, and it is agreed that every neighbour, whatever the distance, kinship or faith, holds the essential right of help and care to the best of one's ability, while an extra right gives its holder precedence.

**"close friends"**, the companion at your side, is the person sitting next to one in a meeting or a journey, and by extension everyone who works beside a person in some job or vocation, in industry or wage-earning or office work, as the Rūḥ al-Maʿānī notes.

**"˹needy˺ travellers"**, the son of the road, is the wayfarer who, having no relative or friend at hand, becomes a guest. Muslim or non-Muslim, his right of good treatment is obligatory as much as possible and convenient.

**"those ˹bondspeople˺ in your possession"** is an order of kindness to the owned, because they are weak, being held as captives by others, and by analogy and by the sayings of the Prophet ﷺ the same rules cover servants and employees.

**"Surely Allah does not like whoever is arrogant, boastful—"** closes the roster with the disposition that empties it: the one who thinks himself better than others, thinks high of himself while insignificant to Allah and hated by people.

### Why the worship of Allah opens the roster

The opening of the rights roster with the exhortation to worship Allah alone carries many wise considerations, and the basic one is fear. A person neglectful of Allah's right can hardly be expected to remain particular about anyone else's. He will find an excuse to satisfy his immediate circle, to keep his prestige in society, or to escape the law of the government. What forces a person to honour human rights, watched or unwatched, is the fear of Allah and the sense of responsibility before Him, and that taqwā is achieved through nothing but tawḥīd, the firm belief in the Oneness of Allah to the total exclusion of everything else. Muʿādh ibn Jabal said that of the ten things the Prophet ﷺ asked him to hold to, the first was to not associate anyone with Allah even if he were killed or burnt alive, and the second was to not disobey or hurt his parents even if they asked him to leave his children and family, in the Musnad of Aḥmad.

### The parents after tawḥīd

All blessings are really from Allah, yet in worldly means the favours closest after His are those from the parents. They are the fountain-head of one's being, and they carry a child through every stage of trial from birth to youth. This is why the command to worship Allah is supplemented with the parents' rights across the Qur'an: "Give thanks to Me and to your parents" (31:14), and "Your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him, and that you be dutiful to your parents" (17:23), and the covenant of the Children of Israel to worship none but Allah and do good to parents (2:83). The rewards are countless. In al-Bukhārī and Muslim: one who wishes his earnings and life increased by Allah's blessing should fulfil the rights of his relatives. In at-Tirmidhī: Allah's pleasure rests in the father's pleasure, and His displeasure in the father's displeasure. Al-Baihaqī reports in the Shuʿab al-ʾĪmān that when an obedient son looks at his parents with love and regard, every glance brings him the reward of an accepted Ḥajj, and by another narration that Allah forgives most sins, but the one who disobeys and hurts his parents is condemned to unforeseen troubles right here in this world, long before the reckoning of the Hereafter.

### Relatives and the verse recited every Friday

The relative's right goes beyond the general command. The verse still recited in the khuṭbah during Friday prayers all over the world reads: "Allah commands you to do justice and be good to all and to fulfil the rights of the relatives" (16:90). That includes serving relatives to the best of one's ability and keeping in touch with them. Recalling the orphans and the needy under the general head of relatives hints that helping orphaned children and the helpless should be felt as necessary as helping one's own kin.

### The neighbour's right

Jibrīl kept reminding the Prophet ﷺ of the neighbour's right, until he thought he was going to give him a share of the inheritance, as ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar narrated in a hadith the two Ṣaḥīḥs carry. Imam Aḥmad recorded the like from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr, in a report Abu Dāwūd and at-Tirmidhī also carry, which at-Tirmidhī called fair though odd through its route. The degrees of the right were set out by the Prophet ﷺ himself: neighbours hold one, two or three rights by rank. A non-Muslim neighbour with no kinship holds one. A neighbour who is also a Muslim holds two. A neighbour who is a Muslim and a relative holds all three. From ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ comes the ranking: the best companions in Allah's sight are the best to their friends, and the best neighbours are the best to their neighbours, carried by at-Tirmidhī. And the Musnad of Aḥmad carries the rule that no neighbour may eat his fill while the next door goes hungry.

The weight of the wronged neighbour is heavier in the scale. Al-Miqdād ibn al-Aswad narrated that the Prophet ﷺ asked his Companions what they said of adultery, and they said it was forbidden until the Day of Resurrection. He said that for a man to commit adultery with his neighbour's wife is worse than committing it with ten women. Asked the like about theft, he said that stealing from a neighbour is worse than stealing from ten homes, and only Aḥmad recorded this hadith. The two Ṣaḥīḥs carry the companion report from Ibn Masʿūd, who asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ what the greatest sin was. He said: "To make a rival for Allah while He alone created you." He said: "Then?" He said: "To kill your offspring for fear that he might share your food with you." He said: "Then?" He said: "To commit adultery with your neighbour's wife." On the practical side, ʿĀʾishah asked the Messenger of Allah which of her two neighbours should receive her gift, and he said: "The one whose door is closest to you", in a narration al-Bukhārī carries.

### The companion, the wayfarer, the owned

The companion by one's side, even for a single meeting or ride, holds a right made as obligatory as the regular neighbour's. Its lowest degree is to cause no pain by word or deed: nothing that hurts, embarrasses, annoys or troubles. Not a puff of cigarette smoke blown toward a face. Not things chewed spat at someone's side. Not a style of sitting that leaves the other person no room. If people followed this, the common disputes between passengers on trains, especially in overcrowded countries, would end, for each person's right is about enough space to seat one person, and no more.

To the wayfarer belongs honour as a guest. Honouring a guest for three days is the guest's right, and what is bestowed after the third day counts as charity from the host. One reading of the companion at your side even includes one's wife at home, with kindness to her commanded in the same breath.

For those in one's possession the instruction turns tender. In the illness before his death, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ kept advising his ummah about the prayer, the prayer, and those slaves whom the hands possess, repeating it until his tongue was still. From al-Miqdam ibn Maʿdykarib: what you feed yourself is ṣadaqah for you, what you feed your children is ṣadaqah, what you feed your wife is ṣadaqah, and what you feed your servant is ṣadaqah, carried by an-Nasāʾī with an authentic chain. Ibn ʿAmr said to a caretaker who had not yet fed his slaves to go and feed them, for the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had said: "It is enough sin for someone to prevent whomever he is responsible for from getting their food", as Muslim records. Abū Hurayrah narrated that the slave has the right to food, clothing, and work only within what he can bear, which Muslim also carries. And in al-Bukhārī's wording: when a servant brings a meal, let him sit and share it, or at least receive a mouthful or two of it, for he prepared it. For hired servants and employees the same rules run by analogy. There is to be no niggardliness or delay in the due salary and meals, and no burdening beyond ability.

### The arrogance that withholds

The verse's last sentence is the unifying complement of the whole roster. Whoever falls short in the rights of these eight kinds must be none other than the arrogant and the proud, whose sense of superiority is offensively heightened and who love lording over others to exact esteem. Mujāhid said the proud one is the arrogant, and the boastful one boasts of what he has while giving no thanks to Allah, boasting before people of a bounty he is actually ungrateful for. Ibn Jarīr recorded from ʿAbdullāh ibn Wāqid Abū Rajāʾ al-Harawī that the mean are also proud and boasting, and that he who is undutiful to parents is also arrogant, deprived, reciting "dutiful to my mother, and made me not arrogant, deprived" (19:32). A man asked the Prophet ﷺ for advice and was told: avoid lengthening the dress below the ankles, for that practice is from arrogance, and Allah does not like arrogance. Ibn Masʿūd narrated the two-sided warning: faith the weight of a mustard seed keeps a heart out of the Fire, and arrogance the weight of a mustard seed keeps a heart out of Paradise. Asked about people who like good clothes and good shoes, he said: "Allah is beautiful, He likes beauty. Arrogance is wanton disregard of the other person's right and the holding of people in contempt." May Allah keep all Muslims safe from it.

### The inner reading

The verse begins with tawḥīd, root of the sciences and secret of the recognitions, the religion's basis and the Muslim's foundation, the partition between enemy and friend. Obedience without tawḥīd carries no value or weight, and its outcome is darkness and captivity. Disobedience along with tawḥīd yields familiarity and brightness. Tawḥīd is twofold: to say that God is one, and to be single for Him. Saying one is the submitter's tawḥīd, which drives away the devil, washes away sin and opens the heart. Being one is the recognizer's tawḥīd, which cuts away attachments, washes away the creatures and brings forth the realities, effacing the customs of human nature and burning the veil of mortality so that the breeze of familiarity blows and the friend gazes upon the Friend. Shaykh al-Islām Anṣārī said:

> The tawḥīd of the Muslims amounts to three words: affirmation of attributes without excess, negation of similarity without declaring ineffectuality, and going forward according to the outwardness without mixing.

Its reality is to acknowledge God's own explanation and what Muṣṭafā stated plainly about Him, surrendering to it without likenesses and without interpretation, for God comes into knowledge but not imagination. His are the creation and the command, and blessed is God, the Lord of the Worlds (7:54). Whoever is held back from this transmitted tawḥīd stays in the great associationism, far from forgiveness. The other tawḥīd belongs to the recognizers and the sincerely truthful. Water and clay cannot do this work, nor can tongue and heart serve as its place. With what tongue shall the tawḥīd-voicer speak it, when his state is itself a tongue? The unity of the One remains unvoiced, for every voicing is a denial, and every description a deviation. The Pir of the Ṭarīqah prayed:

> O God, the recognizer knows You through Your light and cannot express the radiance of finding. The tawḥīd-voicer recognizes You through the light of proximity and burns in the fire of love without turning away from joy.

That tawḥīd arrives amid heedlessness, unasked, and busies the servant with itself. Lit by the contemplation of the Near, the servant reaps beginningless love and forfeits the two worlds, saying: if seeing You brings loss to the spirit, I will buy the spirit's loss with the spirit.

### In short

- The worship of Allah alone opens the roster because the fear of Him is the only enforcer of rights that works watched or unwatched.
- The parents stand first after Allah, and iḥsān to them is a comprehensive beauty of word, service and support, repaid in lengthened earnings, accepted-Ḥajj glances and forgiveness.
- The neighbour's right was pressed by Jibrīl until inheritance seemed next, and its betrayal weighs ten times the ordinary wrong.
- Every companion at the side, every wayfarer, and every bondsman, servant and employee holds a right of kindness, food, clothing and fit work, with the deathbed advice still repeating it.
- The withholding disposition is arrogance, and a mustard seed of it keeps a person out of Paradise, for arrogance is contempt of people and disregard of their rights.
- Within, tawḥīd is both the saying that God is one and the being one for Him, and the recognizer loses the two worlds in exchange for the Friend's gaze.
