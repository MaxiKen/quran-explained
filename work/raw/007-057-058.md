<<<VERSE 7:57>>>
After Allah had stated that He created the heavens and the earth, that He owns and disposes of all affairs, and that He has made things subservient, He laid down that He be called upon in supplication, since He is able to do all things. The chapter has been speaking of the Lord's power in the heavens and the earth. It now turns to a mercy that falls from above and works below. "He is the One Who sends the winds ushering in His mercy." Then the next stage. "When they bear heavy clouds, We drive them to a lifeless land and then cause rain to fall, producing every type of fruit." And the argument is drawn. "Similarly, We will bring the dead to life, so perhaps you will be mindful". The rain is put forward as proof of the rising, and nothing in it is hard to watch.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He is the One Who sends the winds ushering in His mercy"** announces good news before a blessing. The winds are sent ahead of the rain, and the Book says in another place that among His signs is that He sends the winds with glad tidings (30:46). The mercy spoken of here is the rain itself, and the sending of it belongs to Allah alone.

The practice of the Lord in this matter is a standing one. Cool winds come before the rains arrive. They are a blessing in themselves, since they brace and refresh the creation, and they also tell people that rain is on its way well before it comes. Human beings are a delicate kind of creation, and rain can stop them from doing what they need to do. If they know in advance, they can arrange their affairs and find cover for themselves and for what belongs to them. So the winds carry two blessings at once.

**"When they bear heavy clouds"** describes the next stage. The clouds are heavy because they are loaded with water. They travel as if riding on the shoulders of the wind, and the water that weighs thousands of tons is carried high on those wings without a machine and without anyone labouring over it. When the Lord wills, vapour rises from the waters, turns into cloud, and what results is a vessel of the sky carrying hundreds of thousands of gallons in its hold, moving on the cushion of the winds towards the higher air.

**"We drive them to a lifeless land"** explains where the rain is taken. The word for driving carries the sense of herding something towards a chosen destination, and the word for the land means a town or a settled place, while the lifelessness means that the place has died. So the verse says that the clouds are herded to a dead town, which is a settlement left desolate by the lack of water. That a settled place is named, rather than open wilderness, fits the purpose of the rain, since the rain is sent to feed a thirsty land and to bring out what human beings need, and the place for that is where people live.

Two things are settled by this point. The first is that rain comes through the clouds, so that wherever the Book speaks of rain coming down from the sky, the sky there means the clouds. Whether the clouds form directly in the upper air, rather than rising from the waters as the monsoons do, is not impossible either. The second is that the direction a cloud takes, and the particular stretch of land it goes over, are matters of the divine command. He gives the order for rain when He wills, where He wills and in the measure He wills, and the clouds carry out that order.

Anyone can observe the proof of it. A town may lie under heavy clouds and be in great need of rain, and still not receive a drop. The clouds move on to a town for which the share of water has been determined, and it rains there and nowhere else. Nobody has the power or the boldness to pull water out of those clouds at any other place. The weather scientists have worked out rules about the movement of the monsoon from the waters of the sea, and there are offices in many countries that publish forecasts. Experience shows that the news from those offices is often the opposite of what happens. When the divine command goes another way, their rules do not hold, and the winds and the laden clouds turn somewhere else, and the experts can do nothing about it. None of that denies the rules the meteorologists propose, since the practice of the Lord in the affairs of this world is that His command shows itself from behind the curtain of physical causes. People pick those causes up and build rules on them, while the reality lies elsewhere.

**"and then cause rain to fall, producing every type of fruit"** is the third stage. Water comes down on the dead land, and by that water the land brings out fruits of every kind.

**"Similarly, We will bring the dead to life, so perhaps you will be mindful"** draws the conclusion. The way dead land was given new life, and trees, flowers and fruit were brought out of it, is the way the dead will be raised on the day of Qiyamah after they have died. The examples are set out so that people may reflect on them. In one comparison, the dead land is given as a sign, which is the same sign that appears elsewhere in the Book where the dead earth is given life, and the raising of the dead is mentioned in the same breath (36:33).

The Book says of the rain in another place that it is sent down after people have given up hope, and that the Lord spreads His mercy, and that He is Al-Wali, the Guardian, and Al-Hamid, the Praiseworthy (42:28). And it invites the reader to look at the results of the mercy of Allah, how He revives the earth after its death, and it says that the One who does that will surely raise the dead, and that He is able to do all things (30:50).

### What the clouds are, and how they are loaded

The clouds that carry rain are heavy, and their heaviness is the water inside them. That is why they hang low near the earth and their colour is dark. The land they are driven to is dry and has no vegetation of any kind. Rain falls on it, and the surface that had died comes back to life, and every kind of fruit is produced from it.

On the day of the raising, the Lord will send rain down from the sky, and it will pour on the earth for forty days. The bodies will then be brought up out of their graves in the same way that seeds come up out of the ground when the rain reaches them. This likeness between the greening of the earth and the raising of the dead is one the Book uses often when it sets out what will happen on that day.

The reports give more of the order of that day. In a narration from Abu Hurairah, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that the Sur, the horn, will be blown twice on the day of Qiyamah. At the first blowing the whole world perishes and nothing that lives remains. At the second, a new world comes into being and the dead become alive. The same report mentions an interval of forty years between the two blowings, with rain falling throughout those years. In that span, the parts of every dead human being and animal are gathered back into them, so that each has a complete skeleton. Then, when the second blowing comes, the ruh enters those bodies and they rise alive. A large part of this narration is found in Al-Bukhari and Muslim, and some portions of it are taken from the book on the raising of the dead by Ibn Abi Dawud.

### The rain that falls on the heart

There is a reading of this passage that turns the rain inward. When the hearts catch the breeze of nearness, they are carried away into the dominion of majesty and stripped of everything they had traced out and grown used to. Once that breeze of the Beginningless comes from the side of nearness, and the wind of generosity blows out of solitariness, servanthood turns into freedom and every suffering turns into happiness. Fear carries the fearful one to safety. Want carries the hopeful one to the shore of the gift. Regret takes the disobedient person to the shore of repentance. And tawhid takes the one who declares the oneness of God to the shore of solitariness.

Rain comes from the sky, and the dead earth comes alive, and plants and flowers and blossoms appear. In the same way, the rain of mercy comes out of the storehouse of power, and withered hearts come alive. One heart received the seed of regret, and when the water of success reached it, its owner turned away from the world. Another received the seed of concern, and the water of kind favour produced a repenter. A third received the seed of awe, and the water of reverence produced a knower of God. In that line of teaching the Pir of the Tariqah spoke of the water of solicitude reaching a stone, so that the stone itself became pregnant, and fruit grew from the stone and gained flavour and became food. He spoke of the remembrance of the Lord bringing the heart to life, throwing down the seed of love, making the tree of happiness grow, and the tree giving the fruit of freedom. And he said that a seed comes up as a goodly tree (14:24) and gives nothing but the jasmine of the Covenant when the clay has become receptive, the soil sweet and the earth soft.

That reading is carried by the same material that the earlier verses used. Before this passage the Lord had shown His particular blessings in the upper domain, and the blessings in this passage belong to the lower one, which is to say the rain and the trees and the farmland and its produce. On that arrangement the needs of the creation are in the hand of no power but the sacred Being, and so the turning to Him is the key to success, and no one else should be asked for anything. That is the observation recorded in Al-Bahr Al-Muhit.

### The land that drinks and the land that does not

Something particular is marked out in the second of the two verses. The blessing of rain is common to every part of the earth, falling on rivers and mountains, on good land and on bad alike. What grows from the earth, however, grows only from land that has the quality to make it grow. Rocky and sandy ground takes nothing from the rain.

The same figure explains how guidance works. The message of the Book, the prophets who brought it, and the teaching and training carried on by their successors, the scholars and the teachers of the community, are like the rain, and they are open to every human being. But just as not every land takes benefit from the rain, so not every person takes benefit from that spiritual rain. It is taken by those who have the capacity to absorb it. Hearts that are like rocky, sandy ground, without that capacity, hold to their error even with clear guidance, clear signs and scriptures in front of them.

This is why the verse ends with the words about the verses being varied for a people who give thanks. The statement was made for everyone, and in the end it profited those who had the capacity for it and saw what it was worth.

### In short

- The winds are sent ahead of the rain as good news, and they are a blessing in themselves, since they refresh the creation and warn people that rain is coming.
- The clouds are heavy because they carry water, and they are herded by the divine command to a dead and settled land, where the rain revives the ground and brings out fruit of every kind.
- Which land receives the rain and which stays dry is settled by the command of Allah, and the forecasts of the experts fail whenever that command goes another way.
- The revival of dead land is the likeness of the raising of the dead, with the rain of that day falling on the earth and the bodies coming up out of their graves as seeds come up.
- The reports of the two blowings of the Sur and the entry of the ruh into the gathered bodies belong to the same subject, and they are carried by the sound collections.
- The rain is also read as the mercy that falls on hearts, which come alive or stay dry according to what they can receive.

<<<VERSE 7:58>>>
The lesson is now put in one line about two kinds of ground. "The fertile land produces abundantly by the Will of its Lord, whereas the infertile land hardly produces anything. This is how We vary ˹Our˺ lessons to those who are thankful". The rain has fallen on both, and the difference is in what the ground does with it.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The fertile land produces abundantly by the Will of its Lord"** describes ground with something good in it. Its vegetation comes out quickly and well, and its flowers and fruits and its many other benefits are brought out by the permission of its Lord. The Book uses the same image elsewhere of a good upbringing, where it says of Maryam, the mother of ʿIsa, that her Lord made her grow in a good manner (3:37).

**"whereas the infertile land hardly produces anything"** describes the other kind. The word carries the sense of what is useless and scanty at the same time. This is hard, barren or saline ground that has no ability to grow plants. Very little comes up on it, and what does come up is small in quantity and worthless in quality. Mujahid and others, among them As-Sibakh, said the same.

**"This is how We vary ˹Our˺ lessons to those who are thankful"** closes the verse. The verses are turned this way and that, and put in different forms, for people who are grateful, since gratitude is what opens a person to what is being said.

### The two lands and the two hearts

The comparison that runs through the material is between the two kinds of ground and the two kinds of hearers. Good land is ground that is not a swamp, and its plants come out by the will of its Lord without toil and without effort. That is the sincere believer, who does what Allah commands him to do willingly and with a good disposition of his soul. The other ground is bad and swampy, and nothing but what is useless comes out of it, and it comes out only with labour and great effort. That is the hypocrite, who does not do what Allah commands except when he is compelled, and without any good disposition in his soul.

Another reading of the same comparison says that the good land is the one with rich soil, whose plants come up wholesome by the permission of its Lord, and that this is the likeness of the believer who hears an admonition, takes it in and benefits from it. The bad soil brings its vegetation out miserably and laboriously, and that is the likeness of the disbeliever. Even so the Book sets out what it has to say, and makes the signs plain for a people who give thanks to God and so believe.

A third reading of the same lines says that good land brings its vegetation out in abundance by the permission of Allah, and that the believer is like it, understanding the teaching he is given, benefiting from it and acting on it with righteousness. The rotting, salty land produces only a little, and there is little good in what it produces. The disbeliever is like that land, since he does not benefit from the true teaching that reaches him, and he does not perform the good acts that would help him. By varying the examples in these ways, Allah reveals His signs and His proofs, confirming the truth to a people who are thankful for His blessings, who do not deny them, and who follow their Lord.

One of the early teachers said of the goodly land that its goodliness comes about through the continuity of security, through the justice of the ruler, and through the obedience of those who obey. And Abū ʿUthmān said that the good land is the heart of the person of faith, on whose limbs the lights of obedient acts become visible, while the vile land that yields so little is the heart of the disbeliever, on whose limbs nothing but acts of opposition appears.

The Prophet ﷺ gave the whole matter in a parable of his own. Al-Bukhari recorded from Abu Musa that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that the guidance and knowledge with which Allah sent him are like a heavy rain falling on a stretch of land. One part of that land was good soil, which took in the water and brought out vegetation and grass in abundance. Another part was hard ground, which held the water, and Allah made it useful to people, who drank from it, gave it to their animals and used it to irrigate their fields. A third part was barren and could neither hold the water nor bring out any vegetation. The first is the likeness of the one who understands the religion of Allah and takes benefit from what Allah sent him with, by learning it and teaching it to others. The last is the likeness of the one who pays no attention to it and does not accept the guidance with which Allah sent him.

### In short

- The rain is the same for everyone, and what the ground returns depends on the kind of ground it is.
- The good land yields its fruit easily by the permission of its Lord, and the barren land yields only a little that is worth nothing.
- The two lands stand for the believer who hears and takes benefit and for the disbeliever or the hypocrite who gains nothing from the teaching.
- Gratitude is the quality that opens a person to the message, and the verses are varied in their forms for such people.
- The Prophet ﷺ gave the same picture in the parable of the rain that fell on good soil, on hard ground that held the water, and on barren ground that held nothing at all.
