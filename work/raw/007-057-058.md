<<<VERSE 7:57>>>
Allah has shown that He created the heavens and the earth, owns every affair and makes things serve humankind. He has told people to call on Him in duʿāʾ, since He has power over all things. Now He shows that He is also the Provider, and the One who will raise the dead on the Day of Resurrection: "He is the One Who sends the winds ushering in His mercy. When they bear heavy clouds, We drive them to a lifeless land and then cause rain to fall, producing every type of fruit. Similarly, We will bring the dead to life, so perhaps you will be mindful". The previous verses spoke of blessings from above: the heavens, day and night, sun, moon and stars. This verse turns to blessings below, on which the life of humans and of all earthly creatures depends: rain, trees, fields and crops. So Al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ explains it. Since no one but Allah meets these needs, people should pray to Him alone for everything and see turning to Him as the key to success.

### Phrase by phrase

**"He is the One Who sends the winds"**: ar-riyāḥ is the plural of rīḥ, wind. He sends the winds that spread the rain-laden clouds. Another verse says, "And one of His signs is that He sends the winds, ushering in good news ˹of rain˺" (30:46).

**"ushering in His mercy."** The word bushran means good news, and "mercy" here is the rain. Allah Himself sends the winds to announce the coming rain of mercy. There are several readings: nushuran, "spreading out", also read nushran and nashran, and bushran, "bearing good tidings". The singular of the first is nashūr, on the pattern of rasūl, and of the last bashīr. Another verse says, "He is the One Who sends down rain after people have given up hope, spreading out His mercy. He is the Guardian, the Praiseworthy" (42:28). Here the Guardian is al-Walī and the Praiseworthy al-Ḥamīd. Another says, "See then the impact of Allah’s mercy: how He gives life to the earth after its death! Surely That ˹same God˺ can raise the dead" (30:50).

**"When they bear heavy clouds"**: saḥāb means cloud, and thiqāl is the plural of thaqīl, heavy. When the winds lift the clouds heavy with water, those clouds hang low and dark.

**"We drive them to a lifeless land"**: sawq means to drive or urge on toward a chosen destination. Balad means a town or settlement, and mayyit means dead. Here the speaker shifts from "He" to "We". A dead land is a dry land without vegetation. Allah drives the clouds there to bring it back to life. Another verse says, "There is a sign for them in the dead earth: We give it life" (36:33).

**"and then cause rain to fall, producing every type of fruit."** We send down water on that land, and by it bring out fruits and crops of every kind.

**"Similarly, We will bring the dead to life"**: just as We revive the earth with plants, We will bring the dead out of their graves and restore them to life on the Day of Resurrection, after they have crumbled away. Allah often draws this comparison in the Qur'an between reviving dead land and the Day of Resurrection.

**"so perhaps you will be mindful"**: so that you may take heed, reflect on Allah's power and the wonders of His creation, and believe that He can raise the dead.

### Why do the winds come before the rain?

It is Allah's way to send cool winds ahead of the rain. They please people, and they also give warning that rain is coming, long before it arrives. So these winds join two blessings. They are good for people and refreshing for all creatures, and they announce the rain in advance. Human beings are delicate creatures. Rain can keep them from their work, and if they know it is coming they can plan for it. Nor are they and their belongings always sheltered from rain. Early signs let most of them take cover for themselves and their goods.

The heavy clouds seem to travel riding on the wind's shoulders. Thousands of tons of water drift along, carried lightly by the wind. What a marvel of divine design, needing no machines and making no human slave over it. It is simplicity itself. When Allah wills, vapour rises from the waters and becomes clouds. Then a sky-filling aircraft, carrying hundreds of thousands of gallons of water in its hold, flies on a cushion of wind to greater heights.

### Why does it say a dead town?

The word balad, a town or settlement, is used here rather than open land. A dead town is a settlement left desolate by lack of water. This suits the real purpose of the rain, which is to water thirsty land so that it grows what people need. The place for that is a settled area, not an uninhabited wilderness where growth is less likely to serve that first purpose.

### Who directs the clouds?

The verse shows, first, that rain comes through the clouds, as everyone can see. So in verses that speak of rain coming down from the sky (samāʾ), the word refers to the clouds. It is also not impossible that some clouds come directly from the sky, rather than rising from the waters like monsoons.

Second, the direction a cloud takes, and the land it goes to, depend directly on Allah's command. He decides the time, the place and the amount of the rain, and the clouds carry out His command. This can be seen everywhere. Sometimes heavy clouds hang over a town in desperate need of rain, and not a drop falls. The clouds move on to the town whose share of water Allah has fixed, and rain falls only there. No one has the power or the boldness to take water from those clouds anywhere else.

Weather scientists have worked out rules for the movement of monsoons. With them they predict where a monsoon rising from the sea will go, and where and how much it will rain. Many countries have weather bureaus for this. Yet experience shows that their forecasts often turn out wrong. When Allah's command runs the other way, their rules fail. Winds and rain clouds turn elsewhere, against the forecast, and the experts can do little about it. Nor do any of their rules contradict the fact that the clouds obey Allah's command. Allah's usual way in this world is for His command to work from behind the curtain of physical causes. Human beings notice those causes and build rules from them, but the reality lies elsewhere.

### How will the dead be raised?

When the dead are raised, Allah will send down rain from the sky, and it will pour on the earth for forty days. The bodies will then grow in their graves as seeds grow in the ground after rain.

Abū Hurairah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said the Horn (ṣūr) will be blown twice on the Day of Qiyāmah. At the first blowing the whole world will perish, and nothing will remain alive. At the second a new world will come into being, and the dead will come to life. There will be forty years between the two blasts, and rain will fall all through them. In that time the scattered parts of every dead human and animal will be gathered, until each has a complete frame. Then, at the second blowing, the rūḥ, the soul, will enter these bodies and they will rise alive. Most of this narration is in al-Bukhārī and Muslim. Some parts come from the Kitāb al-Baʿth of Ibn Abī Dāwūd.

So the first verse draws a clear conclusion. The One who brings dead land back to life will have no difficulty bringing back to life human beings who once lived and then died.

### The inner reading: ships to the shore

When hearts breathe in the breeze of nearness, they are carried away into the realm of majesty and stripped of everything outlined and habitual. When the breeze from before time blows from the side of nearness, and the wind of generosity blows from His oneness, servitude turns into freedom and every suffering turns into joy. The fearful reach the shore of safety in the ship of fear. The hopeful reach the shore of His giving in the ship of need. The disobedient reach the shore of repentance in the ship of regret. The one who speaks tawḥīd, Allah's oneness, reaches the shore of being alone with Him in the ship of tawḥīd.

Rain comes from the sky, and the dead earth comes to life with plants, flowers and blossoms. In the same way the rain of mercy comes from the storehouse of His power, and withered hearts come to life. In one heart the seed of regret was planted and watered with success, and he became a renunciant. In another the seed of His care was planted and watered with His kindness, and he became a penitent. In another the seed of awe was planted and watered with reverence, and he became one who knows Allah.

The master of the spiritual path (ṭarīqah) said, "O King, the water of Your care reached a stone, and the stone conceived. Fruit grew from the stone, and the fruit ripened into food. O King, Your remembrance brought the heart to life and cast into it the seed of love. It grew the tree of happiness, and the tree bore the fruit of freedom." Where the ground is soft, the soil sweet and the clay ready to receive, the seed can only become "a good tree" (14:24). It yields nothing but the jasmine of the Covenant.

### In short

- Allah sends winds as heralds of rain, drives the heavy clouds to dead land and brings forth every kind of fruit.
- The winds are a double blessing, refreshing in themselves and a warning that lets people prepare for rain.
- Clouds go where Allah commands. Weather rules describe the causes He works through, but the decision is His.
- Reviving dead land is a picture of the Resurrection, when rain will fall for forty years and the dead will rise at the second blast of the Horn.

<<<VERSE 7:58>>>
The rain falls on all land alike, but not all land responds in the same way: "The fertile land produces abundantly by the Will of its Lord, whereas the infertile land hardly produces anything. This is how We vary ˹Our˺ lessons to those who are thankful". When rain comes it falls on rivers and mountains, on good land and bad. Yet plants grow only where the land is able to grow them. Rocky and sandy ground gains nothing from the rain.

### Phrase by phrase

**"The fertile land"**: good land with rich soil, and one explanation adds land that is not a swamp.

**"produces abundantly by the Will of its Lord"**: its plants come up quickly and well, with Allah's permission, without toil or strain. A similar expression is used of Maryam (Mary), mother of ʿĪsā (Jesus): "blessed her with a pleasant upbringing" (3:37). One explanation compares the good land to the sincere believer, who does what Allah commands willingly and gladly. Another compares it to the believer who heeds a reminder when he hears it, benefits from it and acts rightly.

**"whereas the infertile land hardly produces anything."** The word nakid means something both worthless and scanty. On hard, barren, salty or swampy land, little grows at all, and only with toil and great difficulty. Whatever does grow is small in quantity and poor in quality. Mujāhid and others, among them as-Sibākh, said the same. One explanation compares this land to the hypocrite, who does what Allah commands only when forced and without any goodwill. Another compares it to the disbeliever, who gains nothing from the true teachings he receives and does no deed that benefits him.

**"This is how We vary ˹Our˺ lessons to those who are thankful"**: in this way Allah sets out His signs and proofs in different forms, explaining the Qur'an through the parable of the believer and the disbeliever. He does it to confirm the truth for people who are thankful for His blessings, who believe and follow their Lord. The message was for everyone. Yet in the end it benefited only those who had the capacity for it and recognised its true worth.

### What is the parable of the rain?

Al-Bukhārī recorded from Abū Mūsā that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ compared the guidance and knowledge Allah sent him with to abundant rain falling on land of three kinds.

1. One part was fertile. It soaked up the water and grew grass and plants in plenty.
2. Another part was hard ground that held the water. Allah let people benefit from it: they drank from it, watered their animals and irrigated their crops.
3. A third part was barren. It neither held the water nor grew anything.

The first is like the person who understands Allah's religion and benefits from what the Prophet ﷺ brought, learning it and teaching it to others. The last is like the person who pays it no attention and does not accept the guidance he was sent with.

### Why does guidance take root in some hearts and not others?

Allah's guidance, His revealed Books, His blessed prophets, and the teaching and training of their heirs, the scholars and spiritual guides, are like the rain. They reach everyone. But just as not every piece of land benefits from the rain, only those able to absorb this spiritual rain benefit from it. Some hearts are like rocky or sandy ground, with no capacity to absorb it and grow. They cling to their errors despite clear guidance, signs and scriptures. The closing words hint at this. Allah's guidance and clear signs are open to all, like the rain, but only the grateful benefit, those who value what they receive.

Allah has made the world so that its material events stand for spiritual truths. When rain falls, the water reaches every part of a place, but different lands use it differently. One area turns into lush green fields. Another, after the same rain, stays as it was and grows nothing but scrub. So it is with the spiritual rain that comes from Allah as His guidance. Its message reaches every ear, but each person benefits according to how receptive he is.

The one who is ready to accept the truth draws its full benefit. He gains a new life from it, and his nature suddenly comes awake. His bond with his Almighty Lord is formed, and his parched soul blossoms with divine inspiration. The one who has lost his natural receptivity is the opposite. For all its rich promise, the rain of guidance does him no good at all, and he stays as dry as before. If anything grows, it is a thin crop of scrub. In him the rain of guidance breeds jealousy, arrogance, hollow sophistry and opposition to the truth, so he fails to accept it and stand by it.

For land to soak up rain, it must be dry. If it is not, the water runs off the surface without sinking in. In the same way, Allah's guidance takes root only in someone who longs for it, who has emptied his soul of the dross of this world. Another person is careless of Allah's guidance, with a heart absorbed in other interests and other glories. He may meet the guidance, but it will not soak into him. It will not feed his soul, water its ground or help the garden of Allah grow there.

### The inner reading: the good heart

One of them said, "Its goodness comes from lasting security, the justice of the ruler and the obedience of the obedient." Abū ʿUthmān said, "This is the heart of the faithful, on whose limbs the lights of obedience appear. 'Whereas the infertile land hardly produces anything' is the heart of the disbeliever, on whose limbs only acts of opposition appear."

### In short

- Rain falls on every land, but only good land yields a rich harvest. Bad land yields little, and with difficulty.
- In the same way, Allah's guidance reaches everyone, but only receptive and grateful hearts benefit from it.
- The Prophet ﷺ likened his guidance to rain falling on fertile, water-holding and barren ground.
- A heart must long for guidance and be emptied of worldly dross for guidance to take root in it.
