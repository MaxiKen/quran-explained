<<<VERSE 5:45>>>
"We ordained for them in the Torah, “A life for a life, an eye for an eye, a nose for a nose, an ear for an ear, a tooth for a tooth—and for wounds equal retaliation.” But whoever waives it charitably, it will be atonement for them. And those who do not judge by what Allah has revealed are ˹truly˺ the wrongdoers"

This verse states the law of equal retaliation, qisas, as it was given in the Torah. It applies that law to killing and to bodily injury. It then praises the one who forgives, and calls those who do not rule by Allah's revelation wrongdoers. The material reads the verse as a rebuke to the Jews, who applied the Torah's law selectively.

### Phrase by phrase

**"We ordained for them in the Torah."** The material explains that the Torah contained this law from the start, and that the Jews had it in their own book. The verse is a reminder that they could not claim to be ignorant of it.

**"A life for a life, an eye for an eye, a nose for a nose, an ear for an ear, a tooth for a tooth."** The material explains that each phrase is a rule of equal retaliation for a deliberate injury. Ibn 'Abbas read the phrases as they were applied in the Torah, so that a life taken is answered by a life, and each limb by the same limb.

**"And for wounds equal retaliation."** The material says that a wound is answered by an equal wound, where that is possible. Where it is not possible, the matter is left to the judge and to arbitration. The material says the rule for wounds is the same for free Muslims and for their slaves, men and women alike.

**"But whoever waives it charitably, it will be atonement for them."** The material explains that the waiver is an act of charity. It is an expiation for the one who forgives and a reward for the victim. Ibn 'Abbas, Jabir bin 'Abdullah, Al-Hasan al-Basri, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i and Abu Ishaq al-Hamdani all held this view. The material records a hadith from Ubadah bin As-Samit that whoever suffers a wound and forgoes his right of retaliation will be pardoned for a similar sin. An-Nasa'i and Ibn Jarir recorded it.

**"And those who do not judge by what Allah has revealed are truly the wrongdoers."** The material says the Jews rejected the law of the Torah when it did not suit them. They applied retaliation only when a man of Banu Nadir was killed by a man of Banu Qurayzah, and paid blood money in the reverse case. The verse calls this injustice.

### The case of Banu Nadir and Banu Qurayzah

The material explains that the two tribes had a harsh pact. If a man of Banu Qurayzah killed a man of Banu Nadir, the killer was put to death, and blood money was also taken. If a man of Banu Nadir killed a man of Banu Qurayzah, only blood money was due, and it was half the amount. The verse exposes this unfairness by showing that the same rule of equality was in their own Torah.

### Equality of the killer and the victim

The material records that the scholars agree that this verse is to be enforced. The imams agree that a man is killed for a woman he kills, based on the general sense of the verse. An-Nasa'i records a letter that the Prophet ﷺ gave to Amr bin Hazm, which says that a man is killed for a woman. Another hadith says that Muslims are equal in the sanctity of their blood. The majority of the scholars hold this view.

The material gives a hadith from Anas bin Malik, which Ahmad recorded. Ar-Rabi, the aunt of Anas, broke the tooth of a girl. The family of the girl refused to forgive, and went to the Prophet ﷺ, who ordered retaliation. Anas bin An-Nadr, the brother of the girl, asked whether the tooth of Ar-Rabi would be broken, and the Prophet ﷺ said the Book of Allah prescribes retaliation. The girl's family then forgave her. The Prophet ﷺ said that there are some servants of Allah who, if they swear by Allah, will have their oath fulfilled. The hadith is recorded in the two sahihs.

### Wounds and the waiting period

The material says that retaliation for a wound must not be carried out until the wound has healed. A report from Amr bin Shu'ayb, from his father and grandfather, which Ahmad recorded, tells of a man who stabbed another in the leg. The Prophet ﷺ told the victim to wait until he healed. He did not wait, and the retaliation went ahead. He then limped, and the Prophet ﷺ said that the limp had no compensation because he had disobeyed. The Prophet ﷺ then forbade retaliation for a wound before it heals. The material says that if the victim takes retaliation and the aggressor dies from it, there is no compensation, according to the majority of the Companions and their followers.

### The reasons for the revelation

The material says that the verse is linked to the case of the Jewish tribes in the previous verses. The Torah's law of equal retaliation was ignored by the Jews, and so the verse reminds them of it. The material also says that the law of wounds and retaliation is a matter of justice, and that the law applies to all people without regard to their rank. Sometimes a person causes harm without any evil intent, under emotional pressure. In such cases the forgiveness of the victim is a form of magnanimity, and it builds a society that is broad-minded.

### In short

- The Torah ordained equal retaliation, and the verse uses this to expose the selective justice of the Jews.
- Forgiveness of a wound is an expiation for the one who forgives, and a reward for the victim.
- Retaliation for a wound must wait until it heals, and the one who rejects the law of Allah is a wrongdoer.

<<<VERSE 5:46>>>
"Then in the footsteps of the prophets, We sent Jesus, son of Mary, confirming the Torah revealed before him. And We gave him the Gospel containing guidance and light and confirming what was revealed in the Torah—a guide and a lesson to the God-fearing"

This verse moves from the Torah to the Gospel. It presents Jesus as a prophet who confirmed the Torah, and it gives the Gospel as a book of guidance and light. The verse prepares the ground for the call to judge by the Gospel in 5:47.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Then in the footsteps of the prophets, We sent Jesus, son of Mary."** The material explains that Isa came after the prophets of the Children of Israel. He is named as the son of Maryam, and the verse marks his place in the line of the prophets.

**"Confirming the Torah revealed before him."** The material says that he believed in the Torah and judged by it. The verse does not say he replaced it entirely. The material notes that in another verse Isa told the Children of Israel that he came to make lawful some of what had been forbidden to them, and the scholars say that the Gospel abrogated some rules of the Torah.

**"And We gave him the Gospel containing guidance and light."** The material explains that the Gospel is guidance to the truth and light that removes doubt and settles disputes. The Gospel also confirms the Torah, and in most of its rules it agrees with it.

**"A guide and a lesson to the God-fearing."** The material says that the Gospel warns against sin and error for those who have taqwa, and who fear the warning and punishment of Allah.

### The Gospel and the coming of Muhammad

The material says that the people of the Gospel were commanded to believe in all that it contains, including the good news of the coming of Muhammad ﷺ, and to follow him when he was sent. The verse about the Prophet in 7:157 is cited in the material to show that the Torah and the Gospel both described him.

### In short

- Isa came after the prophets of the Children of Israel, confirmed the Torah, and was given the Gospel.
- The Gospel was guidance and light, and it warned the God-fearing against sin.
- The people of the Gospel were commanded to believe in all of it, including the good news of the Prophet ﷺ.

<<<VERSE 5:47>>>
"So let the people of the Gospel judge by what Allah has revealed in it. And those who do not judge by what Allah has revealed are ˹truly˺ the rebellious"

This verse commands the people of the Gospel to judge by the revelation it contains. It names those who refuse as rebellious.

### Phrase by phrase

**"So let the people of the Gospel judge by what Allah has revealed in it."** The material explains that the command is for the people of the Gospel to follow its rules, and to believe in the good news it gives of Muhammad ﷺ. The material notes a reading that makes the verb a continuation of the earlier command to give the Gospel to Isa.

**"And those who do not judge by what Allah has revealed are truly the rebellious."** The material says the rebellious are those who reject the truth and prefer falsehood. The material says that this verse was revealed about the Christians, and that the context shows it.

### In short

- The people of the Gospel are commanded to judge by what Allah revealed in it.
- Those who refuse this command are rebellious, because they prefer falsehood to the truth.
- The verse is read with 5:45 and 5:46 as a judgement on the two groups of the People of the Book.
