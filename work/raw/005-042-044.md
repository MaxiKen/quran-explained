<<<VERSE 5:42>>>
The corrupt hearing described earlier is joined to corrupt gain: "They eagerly listen to falsehood and consume forbidden gain." The Messenger ﷺ is given a choice about hearing these parties, but no choice to rule unfairly. If he judges, hostility toward the litigants must not displace justice.

The verse therefore addresses both the conduct that corrupts a court and the standard that must govern it. Lies, bribes and pressure cannot become reasons to distort a ruling, even when the people seeking judgment are themselves dishonest.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They eagerly listen to falsehood and consume forbidden gain."** Their willingness to receive lies accompanies unlawful earnings. Suht, forbidden gain, is particularly explained as bribes by Ibn Mas'ud and others. Another gloss includes usury. Both concern profit obtained in violation of Allah's command.

**"So if they come to you ˹O Prophet˺, either judge between them or turn away from them."** grants a choice concerning the parties described. They are not approaching sincerely to obey the truth. The accounts connect them with the blood-money dispute between Banu Qurayzah and An-Nadir or the adultery case sent from Khaybar.

**"If you turn away from them, they cannot harm you whatsoever."** reassures the Prophet ﷺ that declining the case will not put him at their mercy. Their pressure cannot defeat Allah's protection.

**"But if you judge between them, then do so with justice."** makes fairness obligatory once judgment is undertaken. Their own wrongdoing or enmity does not permit a biased ruling against them.

**"Surely Allah loves those who are just"** gives the judge a higher concern than the litigants' approval. Fair dealing earns Allah's love and reward, including when justice is owed to an enemy.

### Was the choice to decline judgment later removed?

One explanation says the later command to judge by Allah's revelation abrogated this choice. Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, 'Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Zayd bin Aslam and 'Ata' Al-Khurasani are named among those holding that position.

The stronger of two views attributed to Al-Shafi'i consequently requires judgment when these parties seek arbitration. If a Muslim is involved, the same account states that hearing the case is required by scholarly consensus, not merely by his individual view.

Another explanation, developed in the preceding legal discussion, distinguishes protected citizens from outsiders under a more limited agreement. On that reading, different duties correspond to different legal relationships rather than every difference being removed by abrogation. Both explanations preserve the requirement of justice wherever the court acts.

### What responsibility does a listener bear for false religious teaching?

The warning does not fall only upon leaders who alter the law. People who knowingly follow convenient falsehood also bear responsibility. Depending on learned guidance is necessary for those without the training to derive rulings themselves, but that dependence does not excuse choosing a guide without any care.

The practical comparison is with finding a physician. A patient asks knowledgeable people about competence, education, experience and conduct. If he takes reasonable care but is still deceived, he is not in the same position as someone who entrusts his health to an obvious impostor without inquiry.

A person seeking religious guidance should similarly investigate training, trustworthiness and integrity as far as he reasonably can. The aim is not to make every layperson a jurist before asking a question. It is to distinguish responsible reliance from indifference about who holds one's religious trust.

The discussion warns against judging competence by appearance or fluent speech alone. Religious clothing, a persuasive manner and popular stories do not establish knowledge of the Qur'an and Sunnah or the character needed to teach them faithfully.

### Who bears responsibility for a mistaken ruling?

A prophetic report says that, in the situation described, responsibility for an incorrect ruling rests with the person issuing it rather than the layperson who properly relied on him. The explanation qualifies this blame: it concerns someone knowingly giving a wrong answer, failing to deliberate as required or falsely occupying an office for which he is unqualified.

It does not treat every sincere scholarly mistake after proper effort as the same offence. Nor does it absolve a listener who carelessly chooses an unfit guide because the answer is pleasant.

The warning is especially relevant when people show great care in choosing doctors, lawyers, architects or engineers but little care over guidance concerning iman, faith. They may then mistake comfortable stories and outward observances for reform while their desires remain untouched.

Sound reliance calls for knowledge joined with taqwa, reverence for Allah, and honest conduct. The listener's responsibility is proportionate to what he can investigate, not an impossible demand to know everything himself.

### Why is bribery called suht?

The word carries the sense of uprooting or complete destruction. The related wording in the warning against fabricating lies describes being destroyed by punishment (20:61). Here Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Mujahid, Qatadah and Ad-Dahhak explain suht through bribery.

The description reaches beyond the private exchange. Once bribes govern decisions, the law ceases to protect people impartially. Honest ways of obtaining a right shrink, and access goes to whoever can pay. Life, property and honour all become less secure.

Bribery is therefore called haram, forbidden, not merely discourteous or inefficient. Its damage can spread through a department, institution or whole community. The corruption of judgment undermines the means by which other wrongs should be corrected.

### Which payments are treated as bribes?

The legal definition given is compensation taken for something for which such compensation is not lawful. A salaried official must perform the duty already owed. Money demanded from a person whose case he handles is not made lawful merely because he performs that duty afterward.

The discussion applies this to senior officials and ordinary clerks alike. It also warns against gifts associated with public office. An account described as authentic treats such gifts as forbidden gain, closing a route by which a bribe can be renamed as courtesy.

A further prophetic warning includes the giver, receiver and broker in the curse attached to bribery. The intermediary is not morally detached from the exchange simply because he does not issue the final decision.

Other examples concern compensation demanded for duties already binding. The account includes parents charging a prospective husband for arranging their daughter's marriage. It also includes taking compensation for personally obligatory worship such as salah, prayer, sawm, fasting, Hajj and Qur'an recitation.

It then records an exception made by later jurists for teaching the Qur'an and serving as the prayer leader. The exception must remain visible rather than turning the preceding examples into a blanket claim that every religious service may never receive support.

### Is the payment harmless when the decision is correct?

No. Taking a bribe remains a sin even if the official finally performs the proper duty. If he also gives a wrongful decision, he adds another offence: depriving someone of a right or altering the command that should govern the case.

The apparent success of the payer does not cleanse the transaction. Lawful entitlement should not have to be bought from an official already obliged to recognise it. Both the transaction and the resulting judgment need their own moral examination.

### Can religious popularity become another form of corrupt gain?

Yes. The discussion describes an indirect exchange in which no explicit bribe changes hands. A speaker reshapes religion to suit popular desires, and receives prestige, donations and followers in return.

A message promising Paradise through a few easy formalities while leaving an unjust life unchanged can become very attractive. So can a message that sanctifies every national or communal ambition and lets people pursue glory under religious language.

The problem is not popularity by itself. It is knowingly altering the demand of truth to obtain that popularity. Such a trade resembles direct bribery because the teaching has become a means of buying worldly advantage instead of faithfully conveying Allah's command.

### In short

- Justice remains obligatory even toward dishonest or hostile litigants.
- The reported legal views differ over the continuing choice to decline a case, but not over the duty of fairness when judging.
- Laypeople should seek trustworthy, trained guidance rather than follow attractive claims without reasonable inquiry.
- Bribery corrupts both individual conduct and the public protection provided by law.
- Distorting religion for prestige or donations can reproduce the same corruption without an explicit cash bargain.

<<<VERSE 5:43>>>
The contradiction in the parties' conduct is exposed by a question: "But why do they come to you for judgment when they ˹already˺ have the Torah containing Allah’s judgment"? They claim allegiance to a scripture that already contains the ruling, yet seek another judge in hope of a lighter answer. When his judgment confirms the rule they wished to avoid, they turn away again.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But why do they come to you for judgment"** expresses amazement at their inconsistency. They do not accept the Prophet's ﷺ authority sincerely, but ask him to decide when they think doing so may serve their desires.

**"when they ˹already˺ have the Torah containing Allah’s judgment"** points particularly to the known ruling on adultery in the narrated case. They do not need another court merely to discover what their own book says.

**"then they turn away after all?"** describes rejection after asking for arbitration and after the rule has been made clear. The judgment they refuse accords with the instruction in the scripture they profess to honour.

**"They are not ˹true˺ believers"** identifies the failure behind the inconsistency. Their conduct does not express faith in their claimed scripture or in the message brought by Muhammad ﷺ. Desire, rather than revelation, determines their acceptance.

### What did they actually want from the hearing?

They hoped the Prophet ﷺ would replace the punishment they already knew with something easier. His decision would then supply a religious justification for their preferred practice. They were not simply choosing between judges while remaining ready to accept a lawful outcome.

The account of the concealed passage, including the intervention of 'Abdullah bin Salam, is narrated with the preceding verse's background (5:41). Its relevance here is the acknowledgement that the rule existed and had been hidden. Bringing the book and exposing the passage removed the pretence of ignorance (3:93).

The wider disputes over unequal blood money reveal a similar motive. Privilege was to be preserved if possible, and an unwelcome equal judgment avoided. The defect lay in making the result the condition for recognising the judge.

### How can a sincere question differ from seeking a convenient answer?

A person requesting a fatwa, a religious ruling, should seek the command of Allah and His Messenger ﷺ in order to act upon it. Asking more than one qualified person is not described here as wrong in itself. The warning concerns continuing to shop for an answer until personal desire is satisfied.

The earlier delegation listened on behalf of people who stayed away. Their purpose was to discover whether the result would suit those absent leaders. A Muslim can reproduce the same fault without belonging to that historical delegation if he treats religious inquiry as a device for confirming a decision already made.

### What do vested interests do to the hearing of truth?

Leaders who gain honour and support by offering a comfortable religion may see genuine correction as a threat to the whole arrangement. In the account, this hostility made them eager to hear and spread bad reports about the Prophet ﷺ while refusing to welcome what was good.

The conflict was therefore not only intellectual. Status, financial support and control of followers depended on preserving a particular message. A true ruling threatened those interests and was rejected for that reason.

The verse turns that conduct back upon the claim of belief. Honouring a book in speech while refusing its unwelcome command is not faithful submission. The point reaches anyone who would invoke revelation for advantage while denying its authority over desire.

### In short

- The parties seek a new judgment despite already knowing the rule in their own scripture.
- Their rejection of the matching judgment exposes the preference governing their request.
- Religious inquiry should seek truth to follow, not a succession of opinions from which to select the most convenient.
- Status and financial interests can make a person resist the very guidance he claims to represent.

<<<VERSE 5:44>>>
The Torah is honoured as revelation from Allah: "Indeed, We revealed the Torah, containing guidance and light". Prophets and learned guardians judged by it, entrusted with preserving its instruction rather than reshaping it for advantage. The verse then warns against fear of people, the sale of revelation and refusal to judge by what Allah has sent down.

Its praise of the book sharpens the criticism of those who betray it. The failure is not in the original revelation, but in the conduct of people who conceal, distort or disregard what they were entrusted to uphold.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We revealed the Torah, containing guidance and light"** identifies Allah as its giver. It guides away from error and makes right conduct clear. The explanations also describe a light that moves the heart toward obedience, while a specific gloss connects its clarity with the ruling on stoning.

**"by which the prophets, who submitted themselves to Allah, made judgments for Jews."** recalls the obedient prophets among the Children of Israel. They followed the revealed law rather than altering it to suit circumstances of wealth or status. The account extends from Musa (Moses) to 'Isa (Jesus), with one report giving a thousand prophets in that interval.

**"So too did the rabbis and scholars judge according to Allah’s Book"** includes rabbaniyyun and ahbar. The first term is explained through learned, devoted servants of Allah, and the second through scholars or jurists. Knowledge and devotion are both necessary to their responsibility.

**"with which they were entrusted and of which they were made keepers."** They had to preserve the book, bear witness to its truth and enforce its guidance without concealment. Their position was a trust, not ownership of the revelation.

**"So do not fear the people; fear Me!"** warns against hiding a command because followers may object or withdraw their support. The duty owed to Allah is greater than fear of losing religious leadership.

**"Nor trade my revelations for a fleeting gain."** forbids selling truth for money, recognition or influence. Whatever is gained by concealment is small beside what is forfeited.

**"And those who do not judge by what Allah has revealed are ˹truly˺ the disbelievers"** gives a grave warning. Its application is explained with a distinction between rejecting revelation and sinfully failing to apply a command one still acknowledges. The reported explanations below preserve that distinction.

### Does a later law imply that the earlier revelation was defective?

No. The discussion says that the Torah's law served its appointed time with wisdom. Its replacement by a later revealed law is not attributed to a fault in Allah's original book.

The prophets share faith in Allah and the duty of obedience. Particular practical rules can differ according to the wisdom appropriate to their communities and periods. When a later law replaces an earlier one, following the later command becomes the duty, just as following the earlier command had been obligatory in its time.

The honour given to the Torah therefore stands alongside the authority of the final revelation. It does not permit people to invent a preferred law and then call their alteration a new dispensation.

### Are devotion and scholarship separate paths?

Rabbani is connected with Rabb, Lord, and describes a person devoted to Allah. Such devotion requires enough knowledge to obey Him correctly. Deeds without necessary understanding cannot reliably fulfil the command.

Ahbar is the plural of hibr, a learned person in the terminology being explained. Knowledge likewise requires action. Someone who understands binding duties but neglects them without concern does not gain Allah's approval merely by knowing more than others.

The separate terms identify predominant occupations rather than mutually exclusive religions. One person gives much time to worship, good deeds and dhikr, remembrance, while acquiring the knowledge needed for that life. Another develops expertise in religious sciences and teaches others while fulfilling the required duties.

The account names fard and wajib, obligatory acts, and sunnah al-mu'akkadah, strongly emphasised prophetic practice, among those duties. A scholar whose teaching leaves less time for additional voluntary worship is not therefore outside devotion to Allah.

### How does this relate to scholars and spiritual guides?

The explanation rejects a necessary division between Shariah, revealed law, and tariqah, the path of spiritual discipline. Learned teachers and guides devoted to worship should share the same aim: obedience to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ.

Names such as shaykh, murshid or pir may identify a person's dominant activity, but do not remove the need for knowledge and upright conduct. Likewise, a scholarly title does not excuse neglect of worship and character.

The verse joins these responsibilities through the common trust. Both forms of service are accountable for preserving and following revelation, not for establishing rival authorities independent of it.

### What does guarding the book require?

It requires more than keeping a written copy safe. Those entrusted with it must not hide its rulings, alter its meaning or use it to provide paid exemptions for influential people.

The historical application includes concealing the description of Muhammad ﷺ and the ruling on adultery. The explanation attributes that concealment to fear of losing followers and to profits obtained by bending the law for clients.

The earlier warning also extends to distortion of meaning among Muslims. Allah's protection of the Qur'an is described through its written transmission and memorisation, such that even a small recitation error is noticed. Yet people can still attempt false interpretations.

The account says a group within the ummah, the Muslim community, will continue to uphold the correct meaning of the Qur'an and Sunnah until Qiyamah, the Resurrection, exposing those distortions. Preservation is therefore no excuse for an individual to be careless about the meaning he teaches.

### Who was first addressed by the final warning?

Al-Bara' bin 'Azib, Hudhayfah bin Al-Yaman, Ibn 'Abbas and others identify the original setting with the People of the Book. Al-Hasan Al-Basri adds that the warning also applies to Muslims. Ibrahim similarly describes the verses as revealed concerning the Children of Israel and accepted as instruction for this community, in an account recorded by Ibn Jarir.

The sequence goes on to address Christians and then the Muslim community. Its moral claim cannot be dismissed as merely a criticism of someone else's past. Being entrusted with revelation brings responsibility wherever that trust is held.

The reported blood-money dispute and the concealed adultery ruling explain the concrete abuses behind the warning. The detailed narratives have already been allocated to their historical setting, but their central issue remains present: replacing Allah's command with a rule shaped by power or desire.

### Does every failure to apply a ruling expel a person from faith?

The supplied explanations do not treat every case identically. Ibn 'Abbas, in a report through 'Ali bin Abi Talhah recorded by Ibn Jarir, distinguishes rejecting what Allah revealed from acknowledging it while failing to judge by it. Rejection is kufr, disbelief. The latter conduct is described as injustice, rebellion and sin.

A further explanation identifies disbelief where a person regards contrary judgment as lawful, better than Allah's judgment or equal to it. This qualification concerns the person's rejection of divine authority, not simply the observation that a wrong decision occurred.

Tawus reports Ibn 'Abbas calling the act kufr, while Ibn Tawus explains that it is not like disbelief in Allah, His angels, His books and His messengers. Another report from Tawus explicitly says it is not the disbelief that removes a person from his religion.

'Ata' likewise distinguishes a lesser form of kufr, a lesser injustice and a lesser rebellion from their greater forms. These reported distinctions prevent the verse from being used as an automatic declaration of unbelief against every sinful judge or individual.

They do not make sinful judgment harmless. Someone who recognises the command and deliberately acts against it remains accountable for injustice. The distinction concerns the nature of that accountability, not permission to disobey.

### Why are fear and profit named together?

One temptation is to bend under pressure. Another is to profit from it. A person may conceal a ruling to preserve his office, avoid hostility, receive payment or remain admired by a crowd.

The verse answers both motives by restoring the scale of value. Allah is the One to fear, and worldly gain is fleeting. A revealed book is a covenant governing desires, not an instrument for sanctifying them.

A person who pays lip service to that book while making self-interest supreme cannot claim Allah's approval. The entrusted task is to convey and judge faithfully, even when that costs popularity or advantage.

### In short

- The Torah was revealed as guidance and light, and its guardians were entrusted to preserve and apply it.
- Devotion requires knowledge, and scholarship requires obedient conduct. Neither title removes the common responsibility.
- Fear of people and desire for profit must not lead to concealment or distortion.
- The warning extends beyond its original audience to anyone entrusted with revelation.
- The reported explanations distinguish rejection of divine authority from sinful failure to follow an acknowledged command, without excusing either.
