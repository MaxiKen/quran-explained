<<<VERSE 5:42>>>
"They eagerly listen to falsehood and consume forbidden gain. So if they come to you ˹O Prophet˺, either judge between them or turn away from them. If you turn away from them, they cannot harm you whatsoever. But if you judge between them, then do so with justice. Surely Allah loves those who are just"

This verse continues the description of the Jewish listeners who came to the Prophet ﷺ. It names two faults, listening to falsehood and taking forbidden gain. It then gives the Prophet ﷺ the choice to judge between them or to turn away, and sets the condition for judging, which is justice.

### Phrase by phrase

**"They eagerly listen to falsehood."** The material explains that these are people who are used to listening to what is false and going along with it. They hear lies from their leaders, and they keep following them even when those leaders openly deny the Torah. The material says this is the first trait the verse names.

**"And consume forbidden gain."** The Arabic word is suht, which the material explains as bribery. The root sense is destruction, but here it means money taken unlawfully. The material cites the hadith that Allah curses the giver and the taker of a bribe and the broker between them. The material gives a legal definition: taking payment for a duty that one is already bound to perform. A judge, a clerk or a father who is paid for a marriage that is his duty all fall under it. The Qur'an treats such gain as forbidden.

**"So if they come to you, either judge between them or turn away from them."** The material says the choice was given first. Some scholars held that this choice was later replaced by the command in 5:49 to judge between them. Al-Shafi'i held one of his two opinions on this point. The material says that if the parties ask for arbitration from a Muslim judge, he is bound to judge between them. If a Muslim is one of the parties, the judge is bound by the consensus of the jurists, not by al-Shafi'i alone.

**"If you turn away from them, they cannot harm you whatsoever."** The material says that the Prophet ﷺ was not harmed by their refusal, since their hearts were already closed to him and their aim was to find a verdict that suited them.

**"But if you judge between them, then do so with justice."** The material notes that justice is required even if the Jews were unfair and were enemies of the Muslims. The verse says that Allah loves those who are just in their judgement, even when the matter is between their enemies. The material links this to the reward that Allah gives to those who judge fairly.

### The background of the judgement

The material explains the case of the blood money between Banu Nadir and Banu Qurayzah in more detail. Banu Nadir took more blood money than Banu Qurayzah, and the people of Banu Qurayzah refused to accept the difference any longer. The report says that Ibn 'Abbas linked the verse to this dispute. In another report, Abu Dawud and al-Bukhari record that a Jewish case reached the Prophet ﷺ, and the Torah was opened to show that its punishment for adultery was stoning. The material says that the man who hid the verse with his hand was exposed by Abdullah bin Salam, and the Prophet ﷺ ordered the stoning.

The material gives the report that Ibn 'Umar was among those who stoned the Jewish adulterers and saw the man shielding the woman with his body. It also gives the report of al-Bara' bin Azib, which describes the Jewish man flogged and blackened with coal, and the Prophet ﷺ saying that he was the first to bring back the command that had been set aside.

### The two options and the abrogation

The material says that the choice in this verse was later replaced by the command to judge with what Allah has revealed. Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, 'Ikrimah, al-Hasan, Qatadah and As-Suddi are reported as holding that the choice was abrogated. The material notes that the subject of judgement under a pact was discussed in the previous entry, and that the Prophet ﷺ decided the cases of non-Muslims by the law revealed to him.

### In short

- The verse names two faults, listening to falsehood and taking forbidden gain.
- The Prophet ﷺ was given a choice to judge or to turn away, and the material records that this choice was later replaced by the command to judge.
- Where he judges, he must judge with justice, which Allah loves.

<<<VERSE 5:43>>>
"But why do they come to you for judgment when they ˹already˺ have the Torah containing Allah's judgment, then they turn away after all? They are not ˹true˺ believers"

This verse is a rebuke. It asks why people who have the Torah would come to the Prophet ﷺ for a judgement and then reject it. The material reads the question as an expression of wonder at their behaviour.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But why do they come to you for judgment?"** The material explains that this question is asked in amazement, and that the purpose of asking was not to find the truth. They wanted a lighter punishment, and they hoped the Prophet ﷺ would agree with their desires.

**"When they already have the Torah containing Allah's judgment."** The material says the Torah is in their hands, and the judgement of stoning for adultery is written in it. They could have followed it, and the verse shows that they had the answer already.

**"Then they turn away after all."** The material says that they turned away from the ruling of the Prophet ﷺ when it did not suit them. They came with a desire to be judged in their favour, and they abandoned his judgement when it went against them. The material notes that this is the act of people who follow their desires.

**"They are not true believers."** The material says that the rejection of the judgement is a sign that their faith is not true. The verse does not say they are unbelievers in every sense. It says they are not believers in the way they claim to be, since their conduct shows that they do not follow what they hold to be true.

### The lesson for the believers

The material draws a lesson for every Muslim who asks for a religious ruling. The one who seeks a fatwa must seek the command of Allah and His Messenger and follow it. A person who goes from one scholar to another until he finds an answer that suits him is following his own desires. The material says the verse warns against this, and that it is a way of following Shaytan.

### In short

- The verse asks in wonder why people with the Torah go to the Prophet ﷺ and then reject what he rules.
- They came seeking a lighter verdict, not the truth, and the material says that this shows their faith is not true.
- Anyone who seeks a ruling must seek the command of Allah and follow it, and not choose the verdict that suits his desire.

<<<VERSE 5:44>>>
"Indeed, We revealed the Torah, containing guidance and light, by which the prophets, who submitted themselves to Allah, made judgments for Jews. So too did the rabbis and scholars judge according to Allah's Book, with which they were entrusted and of which they were made keepers. So do not fear the people; fear Me! Nor trade my revelations for a fleeting gain. And those who do not judge by what Allah has revealed are ˹truly˺ the disbelievers"

This verse describes the Torah as guidance and light, and it names the people who were entrusted with its judgement. It then warns against fear of people and against trading the revelations for a small gain. It ends with a judgement on those who do not rule by what Allah has revealed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Indeed, We revealed the Torah, containing guidance and light."** The material explains that the Torah was guidance, that it showed the path away from error, and that it was light that moved the heart. The material says the abrogation of the Torah's law at a later time was not a fault in the Torah. It was done because the rules were to change with the times, and the same Revealer sent both books.

**"By which the prophets, who submitted themselves to Allah, made judgments for Jews."** The material says the prophets who came after Musa, up to the time of Jesus, judged the Jews by the Torah. The material mentions that this period had many prophets.

**"So too did the rabbis and scholars judge according to Allah's Book."** The material explains the two groups. The Rabbaniyyun are the people of Allah who are devoted to worship and remembrance and who have the knowledge they need. The Ahbar are scholars who know the rules and teach them. The material says the two names are different because a person is named after his main activity, but that a true man of Allah must be a scholar, and a true scholar must act on his knowledge. The material links the Ahbar to the Ulama and the Rabbaniyyun to the Mashaikh, and it says that there is no real split between the two.

**"With which they were entrusted and of which they were made keepers."** The material says the scholars were given the protection of the Book of Allah and were made its guardians, so they were to enforce it and not change it.

**"So do not fear the people; fear Me."** The material explains that the Jews were told not to fear the loss of their followers or the anger of their people. Their fear of Allah should be greater than their fear of people, and they should not change the law for worldly gain. The material says the warning is also given to the Muslims, to keep them from altering the law out of greed for money or position.

**"Nor trade My revelations for a fleeting gain."** The material links this to the rule against taking a paltry price in exchange for the truth. The scholars had made it a habit to bend the rules for influential people in return for payment, and the verse condemns it.

**"And those who do not judge by what Allah has revealed are truly the disbelievers."** The material says that the verse distinguishes between different kinds of disbelief. Ibn 'Abbas said that rejecting what Allah has revealed is kufr, while rejecting it without rejecting Allah is a lesser kind. Ibn Tawus added that it is not the kind that puts a person outside the religion, and 'Ata' said there are degrees of kufr. Abu Hanifah and other jurists read the verse as applying to the Jews. The material records that Al-Hasan al-Basri added that it applies to the Muslims too.

### The trade of the revelations

The material explains that the scholars of the Jews traded the revelation for worldly gain in more than one way. Some took direct bribes for false rulings. Others changed the rules to suit popular tastes, because a religion that asks for nothing of the believer is popular and draws crowds. The material says the greater danger is the second kind, because it is hidden behind respect and public admiration. The material says a religion that gives sanctity to national or communal interests without any demand for true obedience is one of the traps the verse warns about.

### In short

- The Torah was guidance and light, and the prophets and scholars judged by it because they were entrusted with its protection.
- The believers must not fear people more than Allah, and must not trade His revelations for worldly gain.
- Failing to judge by what Allah has revealed is kufr, though the scholars differ on whether it is the kind that takes a person out of the religion.
