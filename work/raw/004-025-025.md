<<<VERSE 4:25>>>
This verse continues the rules of marriage with a dispensation for those who cannot afford a free woman, and it adds the punishment for bondwomen who commit zinā: "But if any of you cannot afford to marry a free believing woman, then ˹let him marry˺ a believing bondwoman possessed by one of you. Allah knows best ˹the state of˺ your faith ˹and theirs˺. You are from one another. So marry them with the permission of their owners, giving them their dowry in fairness, if they are chaste, neither promiscuous nor having secret affairs. If they commit indecency after marriage, they receive half the punishment of free women. This is for those of you who fear falling into sin. But if you are patient, it is better for you. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful."

The status of a bondman or bondwoman differs from that of a free man or woman, which is why the verse deals with the punishment as well as with the marriage.

### Who the "bondwomen" are

The bondmen and bondwomen in this discussion are those of the Sharīʿah, in its legal sense. Disbelieving men and women taken prisoner in jihād were given to the fighters as part of the spoils by the Amīr al-Muʾminīn, the head of the Muslims. Those prisoners became bondmen and bondwomen, and their children also stayed in bondage, with a few exceptions that the books of fiqh set out. Today Muslims have left the Sharīʿah's way of carrying on jihād, and they have put the decisions of war and peace into the hands of the enemies of their religion. The text says that for this reason they have been deprived of what used to be a bondman or bondwoman. Domestic servants in our time are free and are not bondmen or bondwomen. In some parts of the world, children are sold or enslaved. This is entirely forbidden, ḥarām, and it does not make them bondmen or bondwomen.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But if any of you cannot afford to marry a free believing woman"**. The Arabic word ṭawl means power, capability and means. The sense is that a man who lacks the means to marry free women, faithful and chaste, may marry believing bondwomen who belong to believers. This shows that a man should, as far as he can, marry a free woman and not a bondwoman. If he must marry a bondwoman, he should look for a believing one.

Scholars differ on how strong the discouragement is. Imām Abū Ḥanīfah held that marrying a bondwoman, especially one from the People of the Book, when one has the means to marry a free woman, is makrūh, not desirable. Imām ash-Shāfiʿī and other Imams held that it is unlawful, ḥarām, to marry a bondwoman if one can afford a free woman, and that a bondwoman from the People of the Book is never permitted.

In short, avoiding marriage with a bondwoman is better for a free man in every case. If he has no choice, a Muslim bondwoman is better. The reason is that the child of a bondwoman automatically becomes bound to her owner. There is also the risk that the child of a non-Muslim bondwoman will follow his mother's religion. To free the children from bondage and keep them Muslim, their mother should be free, or at least a Muslim. For this reason scholars hold that a marriage with a free woman of the People of the Book is valid, yet avoiding it is better. In our times the point is obvious, since Jewish and Christian women often marry Muslim men in order to draw the husband and his children into their religion.

**"then ˹let him marry˺ a believing bondwoman possessed by one of you"**. One explanation says that the word "believing" with free women is in line with the usual practice and does not restrict the ruling. But with the bondwomen, the word rules out unbelieving women. It is unlawful to marry them, even if a man cannot find a believing woman and fears falling into fornication.

**"Allah knows best ˹the state of˺ your faith ˹and theirs˺. You are from one another"**. Allah knows the true reality and the secrets of all things, while people know only what is on the surface. So a man should settle for the outward show of faith and leave what is inside to Allah, who alone knows a person's real worth. Many a bondwoman may be better in faith than a free woman. This is said to encourage marriage with them. Faith is the measure of precedence, and a bondman or bondwoman may be far ahead of free men and women in it. So the choice of a believing bondwoman should not be treated as something contemptible. Her faith should be appreciated.

The words "you are from one another" mean that human beings, free or bonded, come from one kind, all children of Ādam (Adam), born of a single soul. One is better than another only through faith and taqwā, awareness of Allah. Other explanations say that it means some of you follow the religion of others, or that you are strengthened by one another. Some of the scholars say that these two sentences are there to get people used to the idea of marrying bondwomen and rid them of scorn for it.

This links to a wider principle. Outward glamour is not the scale by which to judge a person's honour and importance. The real measure of greatness is the unseen faith in a person's heart, known to Allah alone. So who is great and who is not lies beyond human understanding. That unknown side will be judged and made known in Allah's court. Realising this removes a person's sense of superiority, which is usually the root of most social evils.

**"So marry them with the permission of their owners, giving them their dowry in fairness"**. The owner is responsible for his bondwoman, so she cannot marry without his permission. The same rule holds for a male slave. A hadith says:

> "Any male slave who marries without his master's permission is a fornicator."

If the owner is herself a woman, those who may give a free woman in marriage with her consent become responsible for giving away her bondwoman as well. A hadith says that a woman does not give another woman in marriage, or herself, for only an adulteress gives herself in marriage. If an owner refuses permission, the marriage is not valid, because a bondwoman does not control her own person.

The dowry must be paid with a good heart, promptly, honestly and in full, without hedging or delay. Nothing may be kept back, nor should the matter be belittled because she is owned. No trouble is to be caused to her over it because she is a bondwoman. Imām Mālik held that the dowry is the bondwoman's own right. Other Imams say that her owner becomes the owner of what is paid as her dowry.

**"if they are chaste, neither promiscuous nor having secret affairs"**. Marriage with believing bondwomen is to be made with women who are chaste, honourable women who do not commit adultery. "Promiscuous" describes women who are not honourable and do not refuse illicit relations with anyone who asks. Ibn ʿAbbās said that these are the prostitutes, who do not object to having relations with whoever wants them. "Having secret affairs" means taking boyfriends. As-Suddī, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, Yaḥyā ibn Abī Kathīr, ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, ash-Shaʿbī, Mujāhid and Abū Hurayrah gave similar explanations. Others describe the open fornicators and those who keep lovers in secret. Although the verse speaks of seeking chaste bondwomen, it is also much better to stay away from marrying a free woman who commits fornication.

**"If they commit indecency after marriage, they receive half the punishment of free women"**. This refers to bondwomen who have married, as Ibn ʿAbbās and others said. The punishment that can be halved is the one that consists of lashes, and stoning to death cannot be halved. The "free women" meant are unmarried free women, whose punishment for zinā is one hundred lashes, as Sūrat an-Nūr says (24:2). The punishment for a married free person is stoning. All four Imams agree that, since stoning cannot be halved, the punishment for a bondman or bondwoman who commits zinā, married or unmarried, is fifty lashes. The verse speaks of bondwomen, and the bondman follows by clear analogy. One explanation adds that the fifty lashes come with banishment for half a year. Another says that Allah did not make marriage a condition for the punishment, to show that stoning does not apply to bondwomen.

**"This is for those of you who fear falling into sin"**. The dispensation to marry a bondwoman is for a man who fears he will fall into fornication. The Arabic word ʿanat originally means distress. It is used for zinā because of the distress that it brings, in the punishment in this world and in the Hereafter. So marrying bondwomen is for those who fear this, if they meet the conditions, and who find it hard to be patient and abstain. It is not for those who have no such fear about free women. Imām ash-Shāfiʿī's opinion is that it is unlawful for such a person to marry a bondwoman, and the same holds for a man who has enough to marry a free woman.

**"But if you are patient, it is better for you"**. If a man, even though he fears falling into zinā, holds back and stays morally clean, rising above the pull of his desire, that is better than marrying bondwomen. It is better to refrain and be patient, since otherwise the children become slaves of the girl's owner. In another way of saying it, patience is better because the children will then be free.

**"And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"**. Marrying bondwomen is makrūh, but if someone goes ahead with it, Allah will forgive him. He is also Most Merciful, because He allowed marriage with them and did not forbid it completely. One explanation says that He forgives the adultery you might commit, and that His mercy is in giving a legal dispensation, in case of need, to marry the captives. The same words are explained as Allah making room in these matters.

### What this verse shows about temporary marriage

The verse says that if a man cannot afford a free woman, his second choice is a bondwoman. This also shows that mutʿah, temporary marriage, is not permissible. If it were permissible, a man who could not afford a free woman could take the easiest course and enter a mutʿah, which would satisfy his desire at a much lower cost than a regular marriage.

The verse also names the mark of a bondwoman: being chaste while in wedlock, and not a fornicator. Mutʿah, by contrast, is spillage and plain whoredom. One woman is used by several men over short periods, and any child cannot be credibly traced to anyone. The benefit of having children is lost, and the unique store of human strength all those people hold is wasted, only to indulge a wanton pursuit of desire. The details are given in the commentary on the previous verse.

### The inner reading

Concessions are granted for the weak. The strong are asked to make serious efforts and to take up what is most complete and strict in the law, since they have no work except to uphold the right of the Real, ḥaqq al-ḥaqq. When something external distracts them from their attention to the heart, so that they take what is easier and lesser, it moves them farther from their attention to the innermost self. It is a desertion of the most important and noble matters. When a person comes down from the strongest and most complete way to what is merely permitted to him, he is really sliding into a state of indulgence.

Then the verse ends with "But if you are patient, it is better for you". This means to be patient in bearing what is hard in one's situation. It is a gentle way of speaking to the servant, because Allah did not say "Be patient". He said that patience is better for you.

### In short

- A man who cannot afford a free believing woman may marry a believing bondwoman, with her owner's permission and with her full dowry, and she must be chaste, neither promiscuous nor taking secret lovers.
- Abū Ḥanīfah called it makrūh to marry a bondwoman if one can afford a free woman, and ash-Shāfiʿī called it ḥarām. The best course is patience, since the children of a bondwoman would belong to her owner.
- Faith, and not status, is what counts. Allah knows each person's true state and all are children of one father.
- A bondwoman who commits zinā after marriage gets half the free woman's punishment, which is fifty lashes, since stoning cannot be halved.
- The verse shows that temporary marriage is not permitted, as there would have been no need for the bondwoman option if it were.
- Inwardly, concessions are for the weak, and the strong are asked to hold to the most demanding way.
