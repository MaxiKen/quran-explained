<<<VERSE 4:25>>>
The marriage laws close with a concession for the man who has nothing: "But if any of you cannot afford to marry a free believing woman, then ˹let him marry˺ a believing bondwoman possessed by one of you. Allah knows best ˹the state of˺ your faith ˹and theirs˺. You are from one another. So marry them with the permission of their owners, giving them their dowry in fairness, if they are chaste, neither promiscuous nor having secret affairs. If they commit indecency after marriage, they receive half the punishment of free women. This is for those of you who fear falling into sin. But if you are patient, it is better for you. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful". The verse opens a door, sets its conditions, halves its penalty, and then, at the end, quietly suggests the door be left closed.

### Phrase by phrase

**"But if any of you cannot afford to marry a free believing woman"** sets the threshold. The word behind "afford" is ṭaul, power, capability and means. The sense is that one who lacks the capability or means to marry a free believing woman may marry a believing bondwoman, and this alone already indicates that, as far as possible, a man should marry a free woman and simply not marry a bondwoman.

**"then ˹let him marry˺ a believing bondwoman possessed by one of you"** is the permission, narrow by design: a bondwoman who believes, owned by a believer.

**"Allah knows best ˹the state of˺ your faith ˹and theirs˺"** removes the sneer from the permission. Allah knows the true reality and secrets of all things, while people know only the apparent. Many a bondwoman stands far ahead of free women on the scale of faith.

**"You are from one another"** makes the kinship plain. Human beings, free or in bondage, all come from one species, the children of Ādam (Adam), all born of one soul. One reading takes it as some of you following the religion of others, another as you being strengthened by one another. Whichever way it is read, the betterness of one over another hangs on ʾīmān (faith) and taqwā (righteous conduct in fear of Allah), nothing else.

**"So marry them with the permission of their owners, giving them their dowry in fairness"** gives the two conditions: the owner's consent and the dower paid properly.

**"if they are chaste, neither promiscuous nor having secret affairs"** requires the woman herself to be muḥṣanah, guarded in decency, not a musāfiḥah, an open fornicator, and not the keeper of secret lovers.

**"If they commit indecency after marriage, they receive half the punishment of free women"** halves the ḥadd for the married bondwoman, as unfolded below.

**"This is for those of you who fear falling into sin"** ties the permission to need, and **"But if you are patient, it is better for you"** ties it further, for patience, and the children born free, outweigh it.

**"And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"** closes the door gently. Marrying a bondwoman is makrūh, not desirable, yet should a man do it, Allah will forgive him, and He is Merciful in that He allowed it at all rather than prohibiting it outright.

### Free first, bondwoman by need

The jurists graded the permission. According to Imam Abū Ḥanīfah, marrying a bondwoman while having the means to marry a free woman is makrūh, not desirable, especially a bondwoman of the people of the Book. According to al-Shāfīʿī and other Imams it is unlawful outright for the man who can afford a free woman, and a bondwoman of the people of the Book is absolutely impermissible. Under all conditions, avoiding the bondwoman is better for a free man. Two reasons press it. The offspring of a bondwoman becomes automatically bonded to her owner, and where the mother is not a Muslim, the child may follow her religion rather than Islam. To rescue the children from bondage and secure their faith, the mother should be free, and if she cannot be free, let her at least be a Muslim so the child's faith remains secure for future flowering. On the same principle, marrying a free woman of the Book is correct in itself, but staying away is better, and in an age when such women often marry Muslim men precisely to draw the husband and the children to their own religion, the point needs no emphasis.

The verse also closes the mutʿah argument a second time. Had temporary marriage been permissible, the man who could not afford a free wife held the cheapest exit already, gratifying desire at a fraction of the cost of a regular marriage. By offering him the bondwoman instead, and requiring of her chastity within wedlock, the verse proves that mutʿah is not on the table, for mutʿah is all ṣafḥ, spillage and rank whoredom, one woman used by several men in short spans, the offspring unattributable to anyone, and the unique reservoir of human strength sent down the drain to oblige the wanton pursuit of sex.

### Faith is the unseen scale

Matters of external glamour are not the scale of a person's honour. The real standard is the unseen faith in the heart, which lies in the knowledge of God alone, and whether someone is great or otherwise is beyond human comprehension. That unknown aspect will be judged and made known in the court of God. Realizing it takes away the superiority complex that breeds most social evils. The verse plants exactly that realization. Faith is the criterion of precedence, so the marriage with a believing bondwoman is not to be held contemptible, and, as at-Tafsīr al-Mazharī puts it, the two sentences about Allah knowing your faith and your being one from another are there so that people become accepting of such marriage and shed any attitude of contempt for it.

### Permission and dower

The owner is responsible for the bondwoman, and she cannot marry without his permission. The same holds for the male slave, who cannot wed without his master's leave. A hadith states: "Any male slave who marries without permission from his master, is a fornicator." Where the owner is herself a woman, those allowed to give away a free woman in marriage, with her permission, become responsible for giving away her bondwoman as well, and another hadith states: "The woman does not give away another woman, or herself in marriage, for only the adulteress gives herself away in marriage."

The dower is owed to the bondwoman as to any wife, paid with a good heart and in full, with nothing withheld in belittlement of her status. One who marries her should pay the mahr promptly, honestly and gracefully, without hedging or delaying, and without causing her trouble in the matter just because she is a bondwoman. Imam Mālik holds the mahr to be her own right, while other Imams hold that the master becomes owner of what is received as her dower.

### Chaste, not promiscuous, no lovers

The two closing descriptions of the permitted woman were drawn sharply. Ibn ʿAbbās said the promiscuous are the whores, who do not object to relations with whoever seeks them, while "having secret affairs" refers to taking boyfriends. A similar explanation is reported from Abū Hurayrah, from Mujāhid and ash-Shaʿbī, from ad-Daḥḥāk and as-Suddī, from ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī, from Yaḥyā ibn Abī Kathīr, and from Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān: not the open fornicator, and not the keeper of clandestine paramours. The verse addresses the choice of a bondwoman, but it is much better, on the same logic, to stay away from marrying a fornicating free woman as well.

### Half the punishment

The indecency in question is zinā after marriage. The free unmarried woman's punishment is one hundred lashes, as Sūrat an-Nūr gives it: "The fornicator, woman or man, administer each one of them a hundred lashes" (24:2). The married free woman's punishment is rajm, death by stoning, and since stoning cannot be reduced to half, the punishment the verse halves must be the lashes. All four Imams agree: the punishment of zinā by a bondsman or bondwoman, married or unmarried, is fifty lashes, and the bondman's case follows by the obvious analogy of the text. One reading adds banishment for half a year. In not making wedlock the precondition of the prescribed punishment here, the verse quietly shows that stoning does not apply to the bondwoman at all.

### For whom the door stands open

The permission is addressed to the man who fears being harmed in his religion or his body, the one who apprehends falling into the sin of fornication and finds patience hard. For the man who does not fear that distress, and for the one with means enough to marry a free woman, the door is shut, and on al-Shāfīʿī's reading, unlawfully opened. For the eligible man, the verse still ends by holding up patience: to keep himself morally pure above the pull of desire is a better state than marrying the bondwoman, and the practical reason stands beside the spiritual one, for the child of the bondwoman becomes enslaved to her master.

### Who the bondmen and bondwomen are

The bondmen of this verse are the bondmen of the sharīʿah. Disbelievers taken prisoner in jihād were distributed to the mujāhidīn by the Amīr al-Muʾminīn, the Chief Executive of the Muslims, as part of the spoils, and they became the bondmen and bondwomen, whose offspring remained in bondage except for cases detailed in the books of fiqh. In later times Muslims forsook jihād in the manner the sharīʿah prescribes, entrusted the options of peace and war to the enemies of their religion, and chained themselves to principles and policies that have nothing to do with the sharīʿah, and from that point they were deprived of what was known as the bondman and bondwoman. Common domestic servants of the present day are not bondmen and bondwomen, because they are free. And in parts of the world where children are sold or enslaved, the act is absolutely and totally ḥarām (forbidden), and those children do not become bondmen by it.

### The inner reading

Read inwardly, the concession has a rank and the abstention has a higher one. Concessions have been granted for the weak. As for the strong, He bids them to serious endeavour and the adoption of what is most comprehensive and restrictive in the law, since their work is to uphold the right of the Real, ḥaqq al-ḥaqq. When an external matter distracts from attention to the heart, so that a person takes what is easier and less, it draws him away from the innermost self, for it abandons the most important and sublime matters, and whoever descends from the strongest and most comprehensive to what is merely permissible descends into the state of indulgence. Notice, then, the softness of the verse's close. He did not say "Be patient." He said "it is better for you to be patient", a soothing way of speaking to the servant, leaving him the choice while showing him the height.

### In short

- A man without the means to marry a free believing woman may marry a believing bondwoman with her owner's permission and a fair, promptly paid dower. With means, the marriage is makrūh on the Ḥanafī reading and unlawful on al-Shāfīʿī's.
- Faith, not status, is the real scale of worth, for all are children of Ādam of one soul, and Allah alone sees the unseen faith in any heart.
- The woman must be chaste, not an open fornicator, not a keeper of secret lovers, and her punishment after marriage is half the free woman's, fifty lashes, since stoning cannot be halved.
- The concession serves the man who fears sin, and the verse proves mutʿah unlawful a second time, since the poor man's lawful exit would otherwise have been the cheapest one.
- Patience remains better, freeing the children from bondage and the heart for the most sublime matters, and the verse's last words forgive what it merely permitted.
