<<<VERSE 4:110>>>
The open door: "Whoever commits evil or wrongs themselves then seeks Allah's forgiveness will certainly find Allah All-Forgiving, Most Merciful" (4:110). The verse keeps the sinner from despair after the passage has finished with the deceitful.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Whoever commits evil or wrongs themselves"** covers both kinds, the sin by which another is harmed, as when Tuʿmah falsely accused the Jew, and the sin whose consequences stay with the doer alone. The wise way of the Qur'ān is at work here, saving sinners from total disappointment by saying that a sin is a sin, big or small, and that both stand at the same open door.

**"then seeks Allah's forgiveness"** means repenting, with regret admitted and the sin left. The persuasion is deliberate: those who have committed sin still have the time and the hope to desist and turn back sincerely, and nothing is lost.

**"will certainly find Allah All-Forgiving, Most Merciful"** is the promise. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah reported from Ibn ʿAbbās that Allah informs His servants of His forgiveness, His forbearing generosity and His expansive mercy. Whoever commits a sin, minor or major, and then seeks His forgiveness, finds Him ready to pardon, whatever the pile of it, even a heap greater than the heavens, the earth and the mountains.

### The practice of the door

Imām Aḥmad recorded from ʿAlī the practice of that door:

> Abu Bakr told me, and Abu Bakr has said the truth, that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: No Muslim commits a sin and then performs ablution, prays two rakʿahs and begs Allah for forgiveness for that sin, but He forgives him.

He then recited this verse together with the word "And those who, when they have committed Fahishah or wronged themselves with evil" (3:135).

The inward reading finds a generosity past all deserving. The word then indicates extended time: lives pass in useless activities and opposition, and at the end comes the prayer for forgiveness. Finding is the ultimate event. The disobedient seek only forgiveness, but Allah brings a person to the utmost by His grace if He wills, for His way is to bring about more than what one hopes for.

### In short

- Evil done to others and wrong done to oneself meet at one door, and repentance opens it.
- The forgiveness outlasts any measure, even sins greater than the heavens, the earth and the mountains.
- Ablution, two rakʿahs and the begging of forgiveness seal the promise, as ʿAlī learned from Abū Bakr.
- The way of Allah is to give beyond what is hoped for.

<<<VERSE 4:111>>>
The private ledger: "And whoever commits a sin" (4:111), "it is only to their own loss. Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise". The verse closes the account for those who refuse the open door.

### Phrase by phrase

**"it is only to their own loss"** means the sin is earned against the self alone, like the word of the Book that no bearer of burdens shall bear the burden of another. No one avails anyone else, and every soul, and none besides it, carries its own burden. The choice not to repent brings no harm to Allah, or to His Messenger, or to the Muslims, for the sad nemesis of a person's evil deed is faced by the person himself. The gloss keeps the case in view: the one who steals and falsely swears by Allah earns only the punishment of that sin, and Allah is Knower of the one who stole the armour, Wise in commanding that his hand be cut.

**"Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise"** adds the governance: this occurs by His knowledge, wisdom, fairness and mercy.

The inward reading frees the whole affair of any need on Allah's side. The Real has no need for the obedience of the obedient or for the avoidance of error by the disobedient. The one who obeys acquires his good fortune, fa-ḥaẓẓuhu, and the one who disobeys has abandoned his own.

### In short

- A sin unrepented harms only the sinner, for no soul carries another's burden.
- The theft and the false oath of the episode stand in the ledger under one name only.
- Allah needs neither obedience nor its absence, and the fortune lost by sin was the sinner's own.

<<<VERSE 4:112>>>
The double sin: "And whoever commits an evil or sinful deed then blames it on an innocent person, they will definitely bear the guilt of slander and blatant sin" (4:112). The verse takes the trick of the episode and makes it a standing rule of conduct.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And whoever commits an evil or sinful deed"** covers the mistake and the minor sin, the grave sin, the theft and the false oath sworn afterwards.

**"then blames it on an innocent person"** names the second crime, the casting of the deed upon one innocent of it, as the theft was thrown upon Zayd ibn al-Samin. The pattern repeats wherever people deliberate: a man steals something and, facing discovery, hides it in someone else's house, then raises the cry that another man is the thief. A man intends to outrage the modesty of a woman, and when the pious woman does not relent, defames her with fabricated stories. Two partners run a business, and one, feeling his interests harmed, manages its closure while putting the blame on the other. All such activities are deliberate efforts to implicate others and lay the blame at their door.

**"they will definitely bear the guilt of slander and blatant sin"** totals the account: calumny by the false accusation, and a manifest sin evident on account of what was committed. Whoever commits a sin and then ascribes it to an innocent man has doubled and hardened it. He earns the punishment of his own sin, then adds the punishment of the lie and the false accusation. The misdemeanours only increase the guilt and never absolve the doer, and the wrongdoers are advised, through the immense bounty of Allah, to accept their faults without argument, to shun arrogance built on the strength of supporters, to remain humble out of fear of Allah, and to pray, if retaliation ever comes their way, for deliverance from becoming tyrants.

The inward reading watches the tables turn. Whoever attributes his own disgraceful acts to an innocent person finds that Allah clothes the innocent one with the robe of the accuser's good deeds and pulls the garment of pardon over the innocent one's bad deeds, while the situation of the aggressor is reversed by the exposure of his behaviours to his own peers.

### In short

- Blaming the innocent doubles the sin: the deed plus the calumny.
- The thief, the defamer and the cheating partner all follow the pattern of Banū ʿUbayriq.
- The guilt only grows, never transfers, and the supporters' strength buys nothing.
- The innocent comes out robed in the accuser's good deeds, and the accuser stands exposed before his own.
<<<END>>>

<<<VERSE 4:113>>>
The rescue of the judge: "Had it not been for Allah's grace and mercy, a group of them would have sought to deceive you ˹O Prophet˺" (4:113). The verse closes the episode by telling the Prophet ﷺ what saved him, and then opens outward into the widest statement of what he was given.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Had it not been for Allah's grace and mercy"** reads the rescue. The bounty is the bestowing of prophethood, and the mercy the sending of Gabriel with the Qur'ān. The party concerned is the clan of Tuʿmah, resolved and planning to mislead him in the issuing of the right judgement. It was Allah's grace and mercy that revealed to him the reality behind the event of the theft, otherwise these people would have misled him into error. Since he is not alone, and the grace and mercy are with him, they can never do so.

**"Yet they would deceive none but themselves, nor can they harm you in the least"** turns the plot on its plotters. They themselves fall into the error they dug, and the hurt befalls those who committed the perjury.

**"Allah has revealed to you the Book and wisdom and taught you what you never knew"** moves from the rescue to the endowment. Before this revelation came down, he knew not, as the Book itself says: "And thus We have sent to you a Ruh, a revelation and a mercy, of Our command. You knew not what is the Book" (42:52). And again: "And you were not expecting that the Book would be sent down to you, but it is a mercy from your Lord" (28:86). What he was taught runs from the lawful and the unlawful through judicial decision to the legal rulings and punishments, and matters of the Unseen with them.

**"Great ˹indeed˺ is Allah's favour upon you"** sums the ledger of the gifts, infinite in the receiving.

### The Prophet ﷺ and ijtihād

From verse 105, which opens this passage, five rulings are established. First, situations not covered by any explicit authority of the Qur'ān lay open to his judgement. Many a decision of major importance he took by his own ijtihād. Second, an ijtihād counts as trustworthy in the sight of Allah only when based on and deduced from Qur'ānic principles and the clear authority of the text. Simple opinions and views are not trustworthy, nor can they be termed ijtihād in the sense recognised in the Sharīʿah. Third, his ijtihād was not like that of the mujtahid imams, in whom the probability of error always remains. Any possible error in his judgement stood corrected by Allah Almighty. When he decided by ijtihād and nothing against it came from Allah, the decision was thereby indicated as finding favour and being correct in His sight. Fourth, what he understood from the Qur'ān was nothing but what Allah Himself made him understand, and the possibility of misunderstanding simply did not exist. Other scholars and mujtahids are unlike him here, for their understanding cannot be attributed to Allah in that sense. This is the force of the words about judging by what Allah has shown you. When someone said to ʿUmar, decide as Allah shows you to, he scolded him, saying that this is special to the Prophet of Allah ﷺ. Fifth, pursuing or advocating a false case or a false claim, or seconding and supporting one, is totally ḥarām.

### The reality of repentance

From verse 112 the passage had already shown that a sin ascribed to an innocent man doubles and hardens itself. What remains is the teaching on the way back. All sins, whether they violate the rights of human beings or the rights of Allah, can be forgiven by repenting and praying for forgiveness, but the reality of the act must be known. All sins, whether they violate the rights of human beings or the rights of Allah, can be forgiven by repenting and praying for forgiveness, but the reality of the act must be known. Mere verbal declarations of seeking forgiveness and turning back are not repentance as such. The scholars by consensus hold that the person involved in a sin, unashamed of it, not leaving it and not resolving to leave, makes of his declaration an open mockery of the act. For repentance to be genuine, three things are necessary. One is being ashamed of past sins. The second is immediately leaving the sin one is in. The third is resolving to stay away from sin in the future. Forgiveness for sins touching the rights of Allah's servants has, in addition, to be sought from the wronged party. What was due must first be paid, and what was denied fulfilled. These are binding conditions.

### The Book and the Wisdom

By pairing al-Kitāb, the Book, with al-Ḥikmah, the Wisdom, the verse points out that the wisdom, which is the name of the Sunnah and the teachings of the Prophet ﷺ, was also sent down by none but Allah. The difference is that its words are not from Allah, which is why they are not part of the Qur'ān, while its meanings, like the meanings of the Qur'ān, are certainly from Allah, and it is therefore obligatory to act in accordance with both. This is what some jurists mean by revelation being of two kinds. The revelation which is recited, matlūw, is the Qur'ān, its words and meanings both from Allah. The revelation which is not recited, ghayr-matlūw, is the hadith of the Messenger, its words from the Prophet ﷺ and its meanings from Allah. The words that He taught him what he did not know also settle a dispute about his knowledge. His knowledge of the entire universe was never the all-comprehending knowledge equal to Almighty Allah that some ignorant people insist upon. What is true is that his knowledge ran to the measure of what Allah bestowed, and the knowledge bestowed upon him is beyond doubt greater than all the knowledge of created beings combined.

### The inward reading

Bounty is beneficence, iḥsān, beyond what is due, and it alludes to the protection, ʿiṣma, of Muḥammad, singled out for him alone. The Real shielded him from abandoning His right, and shielded him likewise from the deception of His creatures. No one has any way of leading him astray, for the grip of the divine power holds him fast. What is guarded is secure from everything else. Allah conferred distinction upon him with the revelation of the Book and made him a special intimate in the ways He preferred and approved, and there is nothing like the knowledge granted to him alone. It may be the knowledge of Allah and His majesty, or the knowledge of his own servanthood, and of what his state is worth in strength and in beauty. It is said that He taught him the proprieties of service, with a deep knowledge of reality unambiguous to him. It is said that He left him in no need of instruction from any others, so that no one would hold a light except what is borrowed from his. Those who do not walk under his banner will not reach the entirety of the beneficence, nor receive the good fortune of nearness and communion. The bounty is ever great in all eternity without end, and he was recognised in the most exalted degree and honourable rank in all eternity without beginning. It is said that he was taught the sublimity of his rank over all others, and it is said, finally, that one can only value the measure of Allah to the degree one conforms to His command.

### In short

- The grace and mercy revealed the truth of the theft and turned the clan's plot back on their own heads.
- The Book, the wisdom and the unknown knowledge together outweigh every deception.
- Ijtihād belonged to the Prophet ﷺ in a corrected and protected form, false advocacy is ḥarām, and repentance means shame, departure from the sin, and resolve.
- The Sunnah is revelation unrecited, words his and meanings Allah's, and his knowledge, given by measure, exceeds all creation's combined.
- The bounty toward him runs from eternity without beginning to eternity without end.
