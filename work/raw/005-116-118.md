<<<VERSE 5:116>>>
A question is put on the Day of Rising: “Did you ever ask the people to worship you and your mother as gods besides Allah?” The answer begins with glorifying Allah and ends by assigning all hidden knowledge to Him.

The exchange is not asked for information. It is asked in the presence of the people who made that claim, and its purpose is plain.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And ˹on Judgment Day˺ Allah will say"** places the address on the Day of Resurrection. Allah will speak to His servant and messenger 'Isa son of Maryam in the presence of those who worshipped him and his mother as gods besides Allah.

One gloss describes the question as put to him about what he said to people in the life of this world. Another describes it as a rebuke of his followers.

**"“O Jesus, son of Mary! Did you ever ask the people to worship you and your mother as gods besides Allah?”"** is the question. It is described as a threat and a warning to the Christians, chastising them in public, as Qatadah and others said. Qatadah cited the following statement about the day on which the truthful will benefit from their truth as evidence for this (5:119).

**"He will answer, “Glory be to You!"** is described as spoken while shuddering. It exalts Allah above everything that does not befit Him, such as having a partner. Another gloss renders it as be glorified.

**"How could I ever say what I had no right to say?"** contains Allah's direction to 'Isa to utter the perfect answer. One gloss says it was not proper and not permissible for him to say such a thing, and another that it is unjustified for him to say what he has no right to.

Ibn Abi Hatim records Abu Hurayrah saying that 'Isa will be taught his argument in reply to what Allah asks him. Abu Hurayrah then narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said Allah taught 'Isa this answer. Ath-Thawri narrates the hadith from Ma'mar, from Ibn Tawus, from Tawus.

**"If I had said such a thing, you would have certainly known it."** means that had he said it, his Lord would have known it, since nothing escapes His knowledge. Rather, he did not say those words, and the thought never crossed his mind.

**"You know what is ˹hidden˺ within me, but I do not know what is within You."** One gloss explains that Allah knows what he conveyed to them of commands and prohibitions, while he does not know what proceeded from Allah toward them in the way of being forsaken or granted success. Another describes Allah as knowing what he hides within himself while he does not know what Allah keeps hidden of His knowledge.

**"Indeed, You ˹alone˺ are the Knower of all unseen”** describes Him as the Knower of things hidden from the servants, and as the only one who knows everything hidden and everything apparent.

### Why is the question asked at all?

The question and the answer establish that Allah alone has full knowledge of everything. He is therefore not asking 'Isa because He does not know.

The purpose is to admonish his people, who are called Christians, that the one they take to be a god is himself confessing his servitude and disowning shirk, quite contrary to what they believe, and that he is free of all their accusations.

When the Day of Judgment arrives, a person will know what is right and what is wrong without anyone telling him. People will see with their own eyes that all power belongs to the one and only Allah, that He has no partner in being their Creator and Lord, that nobody but He holds any power, and that none except He deserves worship and obedience.

In those circumstances, asking the prophets what message they were sent with amounts to asking about something the people already know. The answer will be so clear at that time that, without anyone speaking, the whole atmosphere of the Day will have been proclaiming it.

The question and answer therefore serve only to increase the abasement of those being judged. Their purpose is to make clear, in the presence of the prophets, that the religion contrived in their names had no connection at all with their teaching.

### How is the scene described in another report?

A narration from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari reports the Prophet ﷺ as describing the Day of Resurrection, when the prophets and their communities will be summoned. 'Isa will then be called.

Allah will remind him of His blessings and draw him near, telling him to remember the favour upon him and upon his mother. At the end He will ask whether he told people to take him and his mother as gods beside Allah.

'Isa will deny it and say that he did not. The question will then be put to the Christians, and they will say that this is what he ordered them to do. After that they will be driven toward the Fire.

### In short

- The question is put publicly to rebuke those who worshipped 'Isa and his mother, not to obtain information Allah lacks.
- 'Isa is taught the perfect answer, glorifying Allah and disclaiming any right to such a statement.
- He affirms that Allah knows what is in him while he does not know what is in Allah, and that Allah alone knows the unseen.
- The exchange increases the abasement of those judged and shows that the religion contrived in the prophets' names was not theirs.
- Another report describes the Christians affirming the claim after 'Isa denies it, and then being driven to the Fire.

<<<VERSE 5:117>>>
The answer continues with what he actually said: "I never told them anything except what You ordered me to say". The verse then describes his witnessing while he was among them, and Allah's witnessing after he was taken.

### Phrase by phrase

**"“Worship Allah—my Lord and your Lord!”"** is the content of his message. He called them only to what Allah sent him with and commanded him to convey, which is to declare Allah's oneness against shirk and obey Him, since He is his Lord and theirs.

**"And I was witness over them as long as I remained among them."** He was a witness over what they did while he was among them. One gloss describes him as a watcher preventing them from saying what they used to say while he was with them, and another as witnessing by conveying the message.

**"But when You took me, You were the Witness over them"** describes the change. When Allah raised him to Himself, or raised him from among them, Allah was the watcher over them, their protector and witness, and the observer of their deeds.

One account describes his term ending and his being raised to the sky alive, after which Allah was watching their actions.

**"and You are a Witness over all things”** describes a knowledge that nothing escapes. Allah is witness to what he said and what they said, including what they said after him and whatever else.

### Does this wording settle how he was taken?

A detailed discussion of his death or of his being raised toward Allah appears in the commentary on Surah Āl-'Imrān at the verse where Allah says He will take him in full and lift him toward Himself. That treatment is referred to rather than repeated here.

Using the wording when You took me to deny his ascension to the heavens and to establish a natural death is described as an unsound inference. The time of this conversation will be the Day of Resurrection, and by then, after he has descended from heaven, he will have had his natural and real death.

The statement therefore describes a past witnessing that ended when he was taken, and it does not by itself decide the manner of his taking.

### How does the Prophet ﷺ use these words?

Abu Dawud At-Tayalisi records Ibn 'Abbas saying that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ once stood and addressed the people. He told them that they would be gathered to Allah barefoot, naked and uncircumcised, and that as the first creation was begun it will be repeated (21:104).

The first of creation to be clothed will be Ibrahim. Then some men of his ummah will be brought and taken to the left, toward the Fire, and he will call out that they are his followers. He will be told that he does not know what they innovated in religion after him.

He will then say what the righteous servant 'Isa said, that he was a witness over them while he dwelt among them, and that when Allah took him, Allah was the watcher over them. It will further be said that those people kept turning back on their heels after he left them. Al-Bukhari also records this hadith in explanation of this verse.

The use is exact. The Prophet ﷺ borrows the words of 'Isa for a situation of the same shape, in which a messenger has conveyed and then been separated from people who altered things after him.

### In short

- His message was limited to what he was commanded, which is worship of Allah as his Lord and theirs.
- He witnessed over them while among them, and Allah witnessed over them after he was taken.
- The wording does not decide the manner of his taking, and the inference that it proves a natural death is called unsound.
- The Prophet ﷺ used these very words about people of his own community who innovate after him, in a hadith recorded by At-Tayalisi and Al-Bukhari.

<<<VERSE 5:118>>>
The answer closes by leaving the matter with Allah: "If You punish them, they belong to You after all." The second half names two attributes rather than the ones a pleader might have chosen, and the choice is significant.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If You punish them, they belong to You after all."** They are Allah's servants, and He is their Master, disposing of them as He wills, with no objection possible to what He does. One gloss specifies those among them fixed upon disbelief.

**"But if You forgive them, You are surely the Almighty, All-Wise.”"** names might and wisdom. One gloss specifies those among them who are believers. Another describes Allah as the Mighty who cannot be overpowered and the Wise in His handling of matters, so that nothing can prevent Him from graciously forgiving whoever had faith.

Every matter returns to Allah. He acts as He wills. No one may ask Him what He does, though He will ask them.

The verse also lays bare the offence of those Christians who contrived a falsehood against Allah and His Messenger, assigning to Allah a rival, a wife and a son. Allah is glorified, and far above what they attribute to Him. This verse is described as having tremendous value and delivering singular news.

### Why might and wisdom rather than forgiveness and mercy?

Allah does not bring undue hardship on His servants. If punishment comes to them it will be only just, right and wise. Should He forgive them, that forgiveness will not arise from any inability to act differently. He is mighty, wholly able and prevailing, and no evildoer slips beyond His reach or His control.

Since He is also wise, it is not possible that He would let a wrongdoer go for no reason. The divine verdict concerning wrongdoers will therefore be wholly wise and masterly.

This saying of 'Isa will take place in the place of gathering, where no intercession on behalf of disbelievers and no appeal for mercy for them will be entertained. That is why he does not use the attributes of the Most Forgiving and the Very Merciful in place of the Mighty and the Wise of the text.

The contrast given is with what Ibrahim said to his Lord during his life in this world, that the idols had led many people astray, that whoever followed him is of him, and that whoever disobeyed him, Allah is Most Forgiving and Very Merciful (14:36). In that setting the likelihood still existed that Allah might, in His mercy, give them the ability later to repent and return to the path of truth, and so forgive their sins.

The two wordings therefore belong to two different situations, and neither is a correction of the other.

### What is reported about the Prophet ﷺ and this verse?

A narration from Abu Dharr reports that the Prophet ﷺ once spent a whole night reciting a single verse, and that verse was this one about punishing and forgiving.

When morning came Abu Dharr said that he had kept reciting just this verse, making his ruku, his bowing, with it and his sujud, his prostration, with it until the break of dawn. The Prophet ﷺ said that he had prayed to his Lord to grant him the station of intercession, and that He had granted it, and that, Allah willing, it would be for whoever never associated anyone with Allah.

Another narration says that after reciting the verse he raised his hands toward the sky and asked Allah for mercy on his people, and then wept. Allah sent Jibra'il to ask why he wept, and he told the angel what he had said. Allah then told Jibra'il to go back and tell Muhammad ﷺ that He would soon please him concerning his people and would not let him be unhappy.

### What does the night of recitation show?

The report joins this verse to intercession. The words leave the fate of the people wholly with Allah, and the Prophet ﷺ prayed through the night with them for the station by which he may intercede.

The condition attached to that hope is stated plainly. It is for whoever never associated a partner with Allah, and so never fell into shirk. The verse's naming of might and wisdom, rather than an appeal for mercy on that Day, and the report about intercession for the people of tawhid, belong to the same teaching.

### In short

- Punishment and forgiveness both belong to Allah, and no objection lies against what He does with His servants.
- Might and wisdom are named because the scene admits no intercession or appeal for mercy on behalf of disbelievers.
- Ibrahim's wording in this world differs because repentance remained possible there, and the two sayings suit their situations.
- The verse exposes the crime of inventing a rival, a wife and a son for Allah.
- The Prophet ﷺ is reported to have spent a night reciting it while asking for the station of intercession, which is for whoever associated nothing with Allah.
