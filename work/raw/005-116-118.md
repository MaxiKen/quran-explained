<<<VERSE 5:116>>>
"And ˹on Judgment Day˺ Allah will say, “O Jesus, son of Mary! Did you ever ask the people to worship you and your mother as gods besides Allah?” He will answer, “Glory be to You! How could I ever say what I had no right to say? If I had said such a thing, you would have certainly known it. You know what is ˹hidden˺ within me, but I do not know what is within You. Indeed, You ˹alone˺ are the Knower of all unseen"

This verse describes a question that Allah will put to Isa, son of Mary, on the Day of Judgement: whether he told the people to worship him and his mother as gods besides Allah. Isa will deny it, saying that he had no right to say what he did not say, and that Allah alone knows the unseen.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Did you ever ask the people to worship you and your mother as gods besides Allah?"** The material explains that the question is a rebuke to the Christians, who took Isa and Mary as gods. The material says that Allah does not ask because He does not know, but to show the people that the one they took as a god is himself confessing to servitude, and that he is free from what they say about him.

**"Glory be to You! How could I ever say what I had no right to say?"** The material says that Isa glorifies Allah, and that Allah taught him this perfect answer. Abu Hurayrah is reported in the hadith to have said that Isa will be taught his argument in reply, and the Prophet ﷺ said that Allah taught Isa to say this. Ibn Abbas and Ibn Tawus are named in the chain of reports on this.

**"If I had said such a thing, you would have certainly known it."** The material explains that Isa is saying that if he had said it, Allah would have known, since nothing escapes His knowledge. He is saying that he did not say these words, nor did the thought cross his mind.

**"You know what is hidden within me, but I do not know what is within You."** The material says that Isa acknowledges the complete knowledge of Allah, and that his own knowledge is limited to what is apparent.

**"Indeed, You alone are the Knower of all unseen."** The material says that the knowledge of the unseen belongs to Allah alone.

### In short

- Allah will ask Isa whether he told the people to worship him and his mother.
- Isa will deny it and glorify Allah, and say that Allah knows what is hidden.
- The question rebukes those who took Isa and Mary as gods, and shows that Isa is free of their claim.

<<<VERSE 5:117>>>
"I never told them anything except what You ordered me to say: “Worship Allah—my Lord and your Lord!” And I was witness over them as long as I remained among them. But when You took me, You were the Witness over them, and You are a Witness over all things"

This verse has Isa saying that he never told the people anything except what Allah commanded him: to worship Allah, his Lord and their Lord. He was a witness over them while he was among them, and after Allah took him, Allah was the Witness over them, since He is a witness over all things.

### Phrase by phrase

**"I never told them anything except what You ordered me to say: Worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord!"** The material explains that Isa called the people only to what Allah sent him with, and that the message was tawhid, the worship of Allah alone. The material says that the same words are repeated in the report, and that Isa conveyed them to his people.

**"And I was witness over them as long as I remained among them."** The material explains that Isa watched over what they did while he was among them, and that he bore witness to their conduct in that time.

**"But when You took me, You were the Witness over them, and You are a Witness over all things."** The material says that after Allah took him, Allah was the one who watched over them. The material cites 5:117 on the point that Allah is the witness over all things, and notes that a detailed discussion of the taking up of Isa appears in the commentary on Surah Al Imran.

### The reports on the Day of Gathering

The material reports that Ibn Abbas said the Prophet ﷺ stood up and said that people will be gathered barefoot, naked and uncircumcised, as the first creation was made, citing 21:104. The first to be clothed will be Ibrahim. Some men from the Ummah will be taken to the left, and the Prophet ﷺ will say that they are his followers. He will be told that they introduced innovations after him, and he will say what the righteous servant said. The material gives the report recorded by Al-Bukhari, which says that they kept turning back on their heels after he left them. Abu Dawud At-Tayalisi and Al-Bukhari are named for these reports.

The material also reports from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari a saying of the Prophet ﷺ that on the Day of Qiyamah Isa will be called, and Allah will remind him of His favours, and will ask whether he told the people to take him and his mother as gods. Isa will deny it, and the Christians will say that he had ordered them to do so. Then they will be driven to Hell.

### In short

- Isa says he told the people only what Allah commanded: to worship Allah alone.
- He was a witness over them while he was with them, and Allah is the witness after that.
- The reports on the Day of Gathering name Ibrahim, the Ummah, and the innovators, and the Prophet ﷺ's words that they turned back on their heels.

<<<VERSE 5:118>>>
"If You punish them, they belong to You after all. But if You forgive them, You are surely the Almighty, All-Wise."

This verse has Isa say that if Allah punishes the people, they are His servants and He may do with them as He wills. If He forgives them, He is the Almighty and the All-Wise.

### Phrase by phrase

**"If You punish them, they belong to You after all."** The material explains that the people are the servants of Allah, and that Allah does not bring undue hardship on His servants. If punishment comes, it is just, right and wise.

**"But if You forgive them, You are surely the Almighty, All-Wise."** The material says that forgiveness, too, is not a matter of Allah being unable to do otherwise. He is the Almighty, from whose reach no wrongdoer can escape, and He is the Wise, who does not let a wrongdoer go for no reason. The material says that Isa speaks at a place where intercession for the disbelievers will not be accepted, so he does not name the attributes of the Most Forgiving and the Most Merciful. The material contrasts this with the words of Ibrahim, who said to his Lord that whoever follows him is his, and that his Lord is the Most Forgiving, the Most Merciful, citing 14:36, which leaves open the hope of repentance.

### The night of the one verse

The material reports from Abu Dharr that the Prophet ﷺ spent a whole night reciting this verse in his prayer, bowing and prostrating with it until dawn. He said that he had prayed to his Lord for the station of intercession, and that it would be granted to one who had not associated anything with Allah. In another report, he raised his hands and said, O Allah, my Ummah, my Ummah, and wept. Allah then sent Jibril to ask why he wept, and told him that his Lord would please him in his concern for his people.

### In short

- If Allah punishes them, they are His servants, and His judgement is just.
- If He forgives, it is because He is the Almighty and the All-Wise.
- The Prophet ﷺ wept for his Ummah and was given the promise that Allah would please him.
