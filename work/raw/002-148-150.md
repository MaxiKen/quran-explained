<<<VERSE 2:148>>>
Religious orientation matters enormously to any community, so verse 148 continues the theme of the qiblah and adds a further reason for the change. "Everyone turns to their own direction ˹of prayer˺. So compete with one another in doing good. Wherever you are, Allah will bring you all together ˹for judgment˺. Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything". The verse introduces the subject with the word wijhah, which literally means the thing one turns one's face towards. Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as the qiblah, and the reading of Ubayy ibn Kaʿb actually has the word qiblah in its place, which removes any ambiguity.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Everyone turns to their own direction ˹of prayer˺."**. Every person of every community has a direction he faces in prayer. The verb is also read in the passive, "he is made to turn to it". Ibn ʿAbbās, in al-ʿAwfī's report, said this speaks of the followers of the various religions: every nation and tribe has a qiblah it has chosen, while the qiblah Allah appointed is the one the believers face. Abū al-ʿĀliyah said that the Jew has a direction he faces and the Christian has a direction he faces, and Allah has guided this Ummah to the true qiblah. Mujāhid, ʿAṭāʾ, aḍ-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, as-Suddī and others said the same. Another gloss takes it to mean that every prophet had a qiblah, the Kaʿbah, which he was ordered to face. One reading has each adherent of a religion facing a direction according to his own whim. Nations differ in their directions of prayer as in their sacred laws, and there is nothing wrong in that when it is by Allah's command, just as He says: "To each of you We have ordained a code of law and a way of life. If Allah had willed, He would have made you one community, but His Will is to test you with what He has given ˹each of˺ you. So compete with one another in doing good" (5:48). It is an observable fact that every religious community has had an orientation of its own, whether appointed by Allah or chosen by itself. Why then should anyone object, or wonder, that Allah has given the Islamic Ummah a qiblah peculiar to it? It is a distinct religious community in its own right, in fact the last of them all, which makes a distinct qiblah all the more necessary. The People of the Book have no right to object to the Kaʿbah being divinely chosen for the Muslims.

**"So compete with one another in doing good."**. Hasten, O community of Muḥammad ﷺ, to perform acts of obedience, outdoing every other nation and striving in the good deeds you have been commanded. Once the divine command has gone out, the Muslims need not trouble themselves about the objections or ridicule of others. Their concern should be the doing of good deeds as Allah and His Prophet ﷺ define them. They should abandon fruitless controversies and strive to excel in good, since they must appear before Allah on the Day of Judgement to be rewarded or punished for their deeds. Since the Muslims can be sure of their right to a qiblah of their own, and the People of the Book cannot be expected to listen to reason, Allah tells the Muslims not to waste themselves on futile disputes. The Qur'an discourages unnecessary argument, because it distracts a person from his real task, which is to prepare for death and the next world.

**"Wherever you are, Allah will bring you all together ˹for judgment˺."**. On land or at sea, wherever you may be, He will gather you on the Day of Resurrection and reward you for the good you did. The verse ends with this reminder, suggesting that the wish to be safe from others' criticism, and the anxiety to win arguments, only betray attachment to this passing world. Wisdom lies in caring more for what lasts for ever.

**"Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything"**. He can gather you from the earth even after your bodies and flesh have decayed and scattered. Bringing you all together and rewarding you is not hard for Him.

### Hastening to good deeds, including prayer on time

The command to compete in good also means that one should hurry to do a good deed, whether ṣalāh, ṣawm (fasting), the Hajj or zakāh, as soon as the opportunity arises. The ability to do good is a favour from Allah, and neglecting it is ingratitude and disrespect towards Him. There is reason to fear that putting it off may be punished by the withdrawal of that favour, so that the person loses the ability to do good altogether. May Allah protect us from such a fate. Another verse makes the point explicit: "O believers! Respond to Allah and His Messenger when he calls you to that which gives you life. And know that Allah stands between a person and their heart" (8:24).

From the same expression some jurists have concluded that it is more meritorious to offer each of the five daily prayers as soon as its time begins, or as early as possible, citing hadiths in support. Ash-Shāfiʿī shares this view. Abū Ḥanīfah and Mālik hold that certain prayers are better offered somewhat late, as the Prophet ﷺ indicated by word or deed, while the rest should be offered as early as possible within their time. Al-Bukhārī reports from Anas the superior merit of praying ʿIshāʾ rather late in the night, and Abū Hurayrah too reports this preference on the Prophet's ﷺ part, as al-Qurṭubī notes. Al-Bukhārī and al-Tirmidhī report from Abū Dharr that on a journey Bilāl wanted to call the adhān as soon as the time of Ẓuhr began, but the Prophet ﷺ told him to wait until it was cooler, remarking that the heat of midday is part of the fire of Hell. Evidently he preferred Ẓuhr to be offered late in summer. On the basis of such hadiths Abū Ḥanīfah and Mālik concluded that where no instruction to delay exists, as with Maghrib, it is better to pray as soon as the time begins, but where a specific indication exists one should pray somewhat later within the prescribed time. They add that whoever wishes to act on "compete in doing good" even in the latter case can do so only by not delaying once the recommended (mustaḥabb) time has arrived. In short, by the consensus of the jurists verse 148 establishes the principle that when the time of a prescribed prayer has come, one should not delay it without a valid excuse, which may be a clear specification of the Sharīʿah of the kind just described, or a physical disability such as illness.

### Five qiblahs and one Friend

In the devotional reading, created beings have five qiblahs: the Throne, the Footstool, the Inhabited House, the Holy House in Jerusalem, and the Kaʿbah. The Throne is the qiblah of the angels who bear it, the Footstool that of the cherubim, the Inhabited House that of the spiritual beings, the Holy House that of the prophets, and the Kaʿbah that of the faithful. Light makes up the Throne, gold the Footstool, carnelian the Inhabited House, marble the Holy House, and plain stone the Kaʿbah. The allusion for the faithful servant is this: if you cannot reach the Throne to circumambulate it, the Footstool to visit it, the Inhabited House to worship there or the Holy House to serve there, you can at least turn your face five times a day to this stone, the qiblah of the faithful, and receive the reward of all of them. One of the masters said the allusion is that every person has been distracted from Allah by something that came between them, so, O faithful, belong to Me and be through Me. All the people have turned away from Me and grown familiar with others, making their sweetheart someone other than Me. You who are the chevaliers of the Path and claim friendship with Me, lift your eyes from everything beneath Me, even if it be the highest paradise. Then you will go straight in following the Sunnah and conduct of Muṣṭafā ﷺ and give that paragon of the world his due of emulation, for as the greatest of the prophets his conduct was to turn his eyes from all beings and to see no refuge and approve no resting place other than the shelter of Unity. When a man wears his spirit down on passion's road, he had best incline to none but the Friend, and think of neither hell nor paradise. Whoever sets himself right in following Muṣṭafā ﷺ will find the candle of friendship with the Real lit on his road, and will never fall away from friendship's avenue.

### In short

- Every community has its own direction of prayer, so the Islamic Ummah, the last of them, is entitled to its own (5:48). Objections should be ignored, not debated.
- The real task is to outdo one another in good deeds, for Allah will gather everyone, wherever they died, for judgement.
- "Compete in good" also means hastening to good works as soon as they are possible (8:24), including praying when the time comes, with the schools differing only over the prayers the Prophet ﷺ himself delayed.
- Inwardly, the Kaʿbah of stone earns the faithful the reward of the four higher qiblahs, and the believer is called to turn from everything but Allah in the footsteps of Muṣṭafā ﷺ.

<<<VERSE 2:149>>>
Verse 149 repeats the command of verse 144 with a new phrase at its head. "Wherever you are ˹O Prophet˺, turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque. This is certainly the truth from your Lord. And Allah is never unaware of what you ˹all˺ do". The Arabic says "from whatever place you set out", which shows that the ruling now covers the state of a journey. The repetition points out that the stipulation applies equally whether one is travelling or not.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Wherever you are ˹O Prophet˺, turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque."**. From whatever place you set out on a journey, O Muḥammad ﷺ, and wherever you are, you and your followers face the Sacred Mosque, the Kaʿbah at Makkah, when you pray. The reasoning given in this passage requires the Muslims, at home or on the road, to turn towards the Sacred Mosque, for that is beyond doubt the qiblah appointed for them by divine command. Obeying it, like every other command, is obligatory.

**"This is certainly the truth from your Lord."**. The Sacred Mosque is the qiblah of Ibrāhīm (Abraham), and the truth revealed to you from your Lord. In the first command (2:144) Allah recorded the fulfilment of His Prophet's ﷺ wish and ordered him to face the qiblah he loved. In this second command He adds that the change is also the truth from Him, raising the matter a step above the first verse. There Allah granted what His Prophet ﷺ wished for. Here He declares that this is the truth He Himself loves and approves.

**"And Allah is never unaware of what you ˹all˺ do"**. The verb is read "you do" and also "they do", as in 2:144. He is not heedless of what the People of the Book conceal about the qiblah of Ibrāhīm, nor of anything else. He is aware of all you do and will reward you accordingly. Muslims should obey, bearing in mind that Allah is not unaware of what people do.

### Why the command is given three times

Allah commands the facing of the Sacred Mosque three times in verses 144, 149 and 150. One explanation is that the ruling is tied to what comes before it and what comes after it. In the first instance Allah fulfilled the Prophet's ﷺ wish. In the second He stated that the change is the truth from Him. In the third He refuted the Jews' claim that the Prophet ﷺ had followed their qiblah, since they knew from their Books that he would be commanded to face the qiblah of Ibrāhīm, the Kaʿbah. The Arab disbelievers, for their part, had no argument left once he was commanded to face the qiblah of Ibrāhīm, which is more honoured than the qiblah of the Jews. The Arabs revered the Kaʿbah and were pleased that the Messenger ﷺ was told to face it.

In announcing the change, verses 144 to 150 address the listeners three times in the singular and twice in the plural. In a general way the repetition is for emphasis. The new qiblah not only gave the opponents of Islam an occasion for glee, it was also a sudden and conspicuous change in the Muslims' own observances, and without such emphatic repetition their hearts would have stayed unsettled. The reiteration also signals that this is the final and definite decision, and that no further change is to be expected. Al-Qurṭubī, however, explains the repetition so that each phrase acquires a new implication. The commands in the singular are addressed to the Prophet ﷺ himself and those in the plural to the Companions and Muslims in general. Verse 144 concerns those in Madinah or in their own home town, whatever it may be, making clear that the ruling is not peculiar to the Prophet's ﷺ mosque but applies to every town, village and quarter. Verse 149 repeats the command with "from wherever you set out", which refers to the state of travel. Because a journey involves different situations, unbroken travel for days at a time, or a short or long stay somewhere along the way, verse 150 repeats the injunction once more to cover them all.

Many people today, especially those with a Western education, approach the Qur'an as they would a book by a human author who keeps to what commonly passes for logic and sequential argument. They feel embarrassed or confused by the close repetition of words and phrases in the Book of Allah, unable to explain what seems redundant, and this embarrassment can open the way to doubts even among those who wish to serve Islam in the modern world. They overlook an elementary fact: the Word of Allah is not bound by the rules of philosophical or literary composition, and the repetition of words, phrases and even whole sequences of sentences is a regular mode of expression in all the Sacred Books of the world. The great orthodox commentators, those who adhere unfailingly to the Qur'an and Sunnah, have each tried to suggest the reasons for this device and to explain the implications of each instance. Maulānā Muḥammad Idrīs summarised the explanations for these verses in five points. First, the first declaration addresses the residents of Makkah, the second the people of the Arabian peninsula, and the third all people anywhere in the world. Second, the first covers all situations and states, the second all places, and the third all periods of time. Third, the passage gives three reasons for the change of orientation, and the command is affirmed afresh alongside each reason. Fourth, this was the first occasion in the Islamic Sharīʿah on which a new command came to abrogate an earlier one, so repetition was needed to impress the manifold significance of the occasion on people's minds. Fifth, the abrogation of any command tends to raise all kinds of doubts and to produce internal or external disorder, and the naive cannot grasp the why and how of a divine command being abrogated, which makes emphatic reiteration all the more essential.

In the devotional reading, the verse says: just as you face the qiblah from wherever you are, near or far, so draw near to Us in your heart whatever your state, whether you are enjoying good fortune from Us or being afflicted.

### In short

- The command of 2:144 is repeated for the traveller: from wherever one sets out, the Sacred Mosque is the qiblah.
- Here Allah adds that the change is the truth from Him, not merely a wish granted.
- The threefold command (144, 149, 150) answers three objections and covers home, journey and every state, and the repetition itself seals the ruling as final. Repetition is a normal mode of divine speech, not redundancy.
- Inwardly, as the body turns to the qiblah from any distance, the heart should turn to Allah in every state.

<<<VERSE 2:150>>>
Verse 150 gives the command for the third time and states the third reason for the change. "Wherever you are ˹O Prophet˺, turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque. And wherever you ˹believers˺ are, face towards it, so that people will have no argument against you, except the wrongdoers among them. Do not fear them; fear Me, so that I may ˹continue to˺ perfect My favour upon you and so you may be ˹rightly˺ guided". This is the third command from Allah to face the Sacred Mosque from every part of the world in prayer.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Wherever you are ˹O Prophet˺, turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque. And wherever you ˹believers˺ are, face towards it"**. From wherever you set out, O Prophet ﷺ, turn towards the direction of the Sacred Mosque when you pray, and wherever you may be, O believers, on land or at sea, turn your faces towards it in prayer. All of this is repeated for emphasis.

**"so that people will have no argument against you"**. So that neither the Jews nor the idolaters have any ground for contention over the change of direction. The People of the Book knew from the description of the Muslim Ummah that it would be ordered to face the Kaʿbah. Had the Muslims not matched this description, the Jews would have used the fact against them. Had they stayed on the qiblah of Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem), which was also the Jews' qiblah, that too could have served the Jews as an argument against others. The Torah and the Injīl had indicated that the promised Last Prophet ﷺ would have the Kaʿbah as his qiblah. Had the Muslims gone on facing Bayt al-Maqdis, the opponents of Islam would have had an excuse for denying the Prophet ﷺ. The new command cuts the ground from under their feet, and at least the fair-minded among them can no longer raise this objection. The change silences two arguments at once: the Jews' claim that he rejected their religion yet followed their qiblah, and the idolaters' claim that he professed the creed of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) yet contravened Ibrāhīm's qiblah. "People" here, on one reading, means ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and his companions, who knew from their Scripture that the Sacred Mosque was the qiblah of Ibrāhīm. Once you pray towards it, they have no argument against you.

**"except the wrongdoers among them."**. The stubborn and malicious will still carp. Those who transgress with their words, such as Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and the Arab idolaters, retain their contention. The phrase indicates the mushrikīn of Quraysh, whose unsound reasoning ran: this man claims to follow the religion of Ibrāhīm, so if facing Bayt al-Maqdis was part of that religion, why did he change it? The answer is that Allah chose, for a wisdom of His own, to have His Prophet ﷺ face Bayt al-Maqdis first, and he obeyed. Then Allah changed the qiblah to that of Ibrāhīm, the Kaʿbah, and he obeyed again. He obeys Allah in every case and never defies Him for an instant, and his Ummah follows him in this. The obstinate also said that he changed direction only out of a natural inclination towards the idolatrous religion of his forefathers, or that Bayt al-Maqdis rather than the Kaʿbah had been the qiblah of the earlier prophets, so that adopting the Kaʿbah broke with established prophetic tradition. The exception is a continuous one: no one will have anything to say against you except what these people say, and they will go on arguing with the weakest of arguments.

**"Do not fear them; fear Me"**. Do not fear their arguing with you over the change, nor the doubts raised by unjust and stubborn people. Fear Allah alone, by complying with His command, following His instructions and keeping away from what He has forbidden, for He alone deserves to be feared. The Muslims need not worry about defending Islam against baseless objections. The only thing that can harm them is not the hostility of people but disobedience to, or disregard of, divine commands. Fearing Allah alone is the only way to remain true to the guidance they have received, which is Islam.

**"so that I may ˹continue to˺ perfect My favour upon you"**. This connects with "so that people will have no argument against you". I will perfect My bounty on you by legislating that you face the Kaʿbah, so that the Sharīʿah is complete in every respect, just as I have completed your religion for you. Allah instructed the believers to face the Kaʿbah to perfect His blessings on them, to distinguish them from all other communities and to guide them to the noblest direction of prayer, the principal rituals of their religion. Steadfastness in this is itself a blessing from Allah, and the blessing will appear in its full glory in the next world, when the Muslims are admitted to Paradise as the reward of their faithfulness.

**"and so you may be ˹rightly˺ guided"**. Guided to the truth and to the qiblah of Ibrāhīm, directed to what the other nations were led astray from. Allah guided this Ummah to it and favoured it with it, which is why this Ummah is the best and most honoured of nations.

### The change of qiblah as the completion of guidance

When the Kaʿbah was appointed as the qiblah, the People of the Book began to debate whether the east was Allah's direction or the west. They saw the matter as one of direction alone, and that was sheer ignorance on their part. Choosing the House of Allah as the qiblah was not merely the specification of a direction for prayer. It was a sign that the time had come for Allah to reveal His greatest favour to humanity. It had been decided long before, in answer to the prayer of Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) for a messenger to be raised from their descendants (2:129), that Allah would send a final Prophet to the world. The path to everlasting life was now being thrown open to all. Bringing religion to its final perfection in Islam does not mean the earlier religions were incomplete. They were complete in themselves, but none was preserved in its original and authentic form. Allah had revealed true religion again and again, and each time humanity rebelled against it and either distorted it or let it fall into oblivion. Now it was revealed in its ultimate and indelible form. Divine religion that had degenerated into a set of mythological tales was now incorporated into history as solid fact. Until then no pattern of true religion had been preserved for posterity, and now it was placed before humanity in a permanent, living and practical form. The change of qiblah, rather than marking a move from one direction of worship to another, more sacred one, showed the completion of Allah's guidance to humanity.

In the devotional reading: if you want no one to have a pathway against you, no shadow from any created thing to fall on you and no hand to reach you with harm, then from whatever place you set out, wherever you are and whatever your state, be for Us and be from Us, for nothing can reach those who occupy themselves exclusively with Us. "Do not fear them": when they have been effaced from their engendered existence as so many mortal remains, and Our decrees flow over them, why should there be any fear of them? The perfection of grace is to add unveiling to kindness. The one whom He suffices through the demands of His generosity stands below the one whom He enriches by the rightful due of His existence. As the lovers recite, we are in the most perfect happiness, yet only through you is the happiness complete, and the only imperfection in our state, O people of my love, is that you are absent while we are present.

### In short

- The third command adds the third reason: facing the Kaʿbah fulfils the description of the Last Prophet ﷺ in the earlier Scriptures, leaving fair-minded people no argument, and refuting both the Jews' and the idolaters' objections.
- Only the obstinate, like Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and the Quraysh idolaters, go on carping. Their objection is answered by the Prophet's ﷺ obedience in both directions.
- Muslims are to fear Allah, not critics, for only disobedience can harm them. The qiblah perfects Allah's favour and completes the Sharīʿah.
- The change marks the completion of divine guidance promised in answer to Ibrāhīm's prayer (2:129), not a mere swap of directions.
- Inwardly, whoever is wholly for Allah, wherever he is, is beyond the reach of any created harm.
