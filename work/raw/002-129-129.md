Verse 129 is the last part of the prayer Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and Ismāʿīl (Ishmael) made while building the House, and it is a prayer for the Prophet ﷺ himself. "Our Lord! Raise from among them a messenger who will recite to them Your revelations, teach them the Book and wisdom, and purify them. Indeed, You ˹alone˺ are the Almighty, All-Wise.”" Ibrāhīm had asked security and provision for the people of the sacred land, and he perfected the prayer by asking Allah to send a Messenger from his own offspring. The prayer was accepted, in keeping with what Allah had already decreed: that Muḥammad ﷺ be sent among the unlettered Arabs and to all non-Arabs, to jinn and to mankind. Ibrāhīm was thus the first to speak of the Prophet ﷺ to people, and from then on he was known, until the last prophet sent to the Children of Israel, ʿĪsā (Jesus) son of Maryam, mentioned him by name: "O children of Israel! I am truly Allah’s messenger to you, confirming the Torah which came before me, and giving good news of a messenger after me whose name will be Aḥmad" (61:6). This is why the Prophet ﷺ said that he was the supplication of his father Ibrāhīm and the good news of ʿĪsā son of Maryam, a hadith Imam Aḥmad reports in his Musnad. He also said that his mother saw a light go out from her which lit up the palaces of ash-Shām (Syria). It is said that she saw this while she was pregnant with him and told her people, and the story became well known among them.

The Prophet ﷺ is the messenger Ibrāhīm prayed for. The words of this prayer are repeated in two other places in the Qur'an, once in Sūrah Āl ʿImrān and once in Sūrah al-Jumuʿah, and both passages speak of the prophethood of Muḥammad ﷺ. All three say the same thing about him in the same words: they define why he was sent and what his functions as a prophet are. According to a hadith, in accepting the prayer Allah promised that this prophet would be sent in the last of all ages, as Ibn Jarīr and others relate.

### Phrase by phrase

**"Our Lord! Raise from among them a messenger"**. Send among the people of this House, the offspring of Ismāʿīl, a messenger of their own lineage. Allah granted this petition by sending Muḥammad ﷺ. Why did Ibrāhīm, praying for the good of his descendants in both worlds, ask for a prophet from among them? For two reasons. The appearance of a prophet from among them would itself be an honour and a blessing. And since the prophet would be one of their own, they would know his past, his present and his ways thoroughly, and so find it easy to trust him and benefit from his guidance. One gloss adds that these are the very words with which Allah tried Ibrāhīm, and he fulfilled them by asking Allah for them in his prayer.

**"who will recite to them Your revelations"**. The verses of the Qur'an. The verb yatlū comes from tilāwah, which in the dictionary means to follow and obey, but in the usage of the Qur'an and hadith means reciting the Qur'an or a divine Book, since whoever reads a divine Book is also required to obey it fully. The word also implies that the Qur'an must be read exactly as Allah revealed it, without adding or omitting a word or even altering the pronunciation of a word, which in Arabic can change its meaning. In his "Mufradāt al-Qurʾān" ar-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī says that in current idiom tilāwah cannot be applied to reading any book other than the Word of Allah. The first task of Allah's messenger is to set out His signs, the proofs of His greatness and power. Allah has placed countless signs in human nature and in the outside world so that people may know their Creator, but these signs have to be unveiled by His messenger, who shares his divinely given insight so that people may glimpse Allah's glory.

**"teach them the Book and wisdom"**. The Book is the Qur'an. "Wisdom", ḥikmah, is the Sunnah, the Prophet's ﷺ way, as al-Ḥasan, Qatādah, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān and Abū Mālik affirmed. It has also been explained as comprehension in religion, and both meanings are correct. In Arabic the word carries several senses, such as arriving at the truth, justice and exact knowledge, as al-Qāmūs records. Ar-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī says that when used of Allah it means total and perfect knowledge of all that exists together with flawless creation, and when used of anyone else, sound knowledge of things together with good deeds. Maulānā Maḥmūd al-Ḥasan rendered it here as profound truths or subtle realities, and Maulānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānavī as the art of understanding properly. The Companions and their successors, whose explanations come directly from the Prophet's ﷺ teaching, gave various accounts: the commentary and exegesis of the Qur'an, the proper understanding of the religion, the injunctions of the Sharīʿah, or the commands of Allah received through the Prophet's ﷺ words. Despite the variety of expression these all come to the same thing, the Sunnah and the hadith, which is the explanation reported from Qatādah by Ibn Jarīr. Other glosses take wisdom as the judgments the Book contains, or as the lawful and the prohibited.

**"and purify them"**. Cleanse them of idolatry, of worshipping others alongside Allah, and of every evil. ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥah related that Ibn ʿAbbās explained it as purifying them through obedience to Allah. One gloss says: from sins, through the declaration of Allah's oneness.

**"Indeed, You ˹alone˺ are the Almighty, All-Wise"**. Allah is able to do everything and nothing escapes His power. He is the Victor, and He is wise in His decisions and actions, putting everything in its rightful place through His perfect knowledge, wisdom and justice. He is mighty in His essence and wise in what He does and decrees. One gloss reads the Mighty as the One who takes vengeance on whoever does not answer the Messenger He sent, and the Wise as wise in sending him.

### Why reciting and teaching are named separately

The verse treats reciting the Qur'an and teaching it as two things. Reciting concerns the words and teaching concerns the meanings, so the distinction shows that the words of the Qur'an matter in themselves no less than their meanings, and that reciting and preserving the words is obligatory and an act of worship. The point is sharper when one remembers who the first listeners were. The Prophet's ﷺ immediate disciples knew Arabic perfectly and were eloquent speakers, some of them poets. For such an audience a recitation without commentary should have been teaching enough, and reciting and teaching should in practice have been one and the same. Why then are they named as two distinct functions?

Two conclusions follow. First, the Qur'an is not like other books, in which the meaning is the real object and the words are only a vehicle that can tolerate small changes as long as the sense survives. With a man-made book it would be frivolous to go on reading the words without attending to the meaning. With the Qur'an the words are as essential a part of the intention as the meanings, and the Sharīʿah lays down rulings specifically about them. That is why the science of the principles of jurisprudence (uṣūl al-fiqh) defines the Qur'an as comprising both words and meaning. If its meanings are expressed in another language, or even if other Arabic words are substituted for the revealed ones, the result is not entitled to the name "Qur'an" however intact the meaning may be. Reciting such a version in ṣalāh would not make the prayer valid, reading it would not earn the reward promised in hadith for reciting the Qur'an, and none of the rulings attached to the Qur'an would apply to it. The jurists have therefore forbidden printing a translation of the Qur'an without the Arabic text, and it is quite wrong to speak of an "Urdu Qur'an" or an "English Qur'an", since no translation into any language can properly be called the Qur'an.

The word yatlū therefore leaves no doubt that reciting the verses is an end in itself, for one recites words, not meanings. It goes without saying that Allah sent the Qur'an to be understood and followed, and to be content with memorising the words while ignoring the meanings shows ignorance of the nature of Allah's Book and ingratitude. But many people today suppose the Qur'an is like other books and think it a waste of time to read or memorise words whose meaning they do not know. Against this widespread error it cannot be stressed enough that reciting the words of the Qur'an is in itself a regular act of worship that brings great reward. The practice of the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions proves it. They understood the Qur'an as no one else can, yet they never thought that once they had understood and acted on it nothing more was required. They went on reciting it all their lives. Some Companions recited the whole Book in a single day, some in two days, some in three, and reciting it in a week has always been a common practice among Muslims, as the division of the Qur'an into seven stages (manāzil) shows. By making recitation an act of worship with its own reward, and a separate prophetic function, Allah showed great mercy to Muslims who cannot yet understand the meanings, saving them from indifference to the words and so from being wholly cut off from the blessings of His Book. Such Muslims should still keep trying to understand the meanings too, so that they receive the blessings in full and the ultimate purpose of the revelation is realised.

### Why the Book needs a teacher

Second, since teaching the Book is a prophetic function distinct from reciting it, knowing Arabic is not enough to understand the Qur'an. It also requires the Prophet's ﷺ teaching. To learn any science or art, medicine or engineering or something as ordinary as cookery, it is not enough to read a book or know a language. If it were, anyone could master every subject on which books exist in a language he knows. Even the humblest craft needs the regular guidance of a teacher. How then could anyone hope to understand unaided the Qur'an, which speaks on the hardest subjects imaginable, from theology to philosophy and physics? Had competence in Arabic been sufficient, scores of Jewish and Christian scholars and men of letters in Arab countries today would rank among the greatest exegetes, and so would Abū Jahl and Abū Lahab in the Prophet's ﷺ own day. By separating the teaching of the Book from the recitation of the verses, the Qur'an underlines that even those who know Arabic very well cannot understand the Book of Allah properly just by hearing it recited. That understanding comes only through the Prophet's ﷺ teaching, and to divide the one from the other and attempt interpretation on one's own is self-delusion. If it had not really been necessary to explain and teach the meanings, why would Allah have sent a Messenger at all, when there were many other ways to convey His Book? But Allah knows all things and is All-Wise. He knows that understanding His Book depends on a teacher's guidance far more than the human sciences do, and not an ordinary teacher but one who himself receives guidance directly from Allah through revelation (waḥy), the one called in Islamic terms a nabī (prophet) and a rasūl (messenger). The Qur'an says that Allah sent him precisely to explain the Book: "And We have sent down to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Reminder, so that you may explain to people what has been revealed for them" (16:44).

Teaching the Book includes teaching wisdom, and since the Companions and their successors understood wisdom here as the Sunnah, the prophetic function includes teaching the principles and methods of spiritual discipline. The Prophet ﷺ said that he had been sent only as a teacher. It follows that his followers are required to be disciples, and that every Muslim man and woman should be a lifelong student eager to learn what the Prophet ﷺ taught. Whoever cannot master the sciences of the Qur'an and the Sunnah must at least acquire a sound knowledge of the basic doctrines of Islam and the fundamental rulings of the Sharīʿah that no Muslim can do without.

### Purification: knowing is not enough

Purifying the people is also an essential prophetic function. Tazkiyah means cleansing a person or thing of every kind of filth, inward as well as outward. The outward kinds are plain, and the Sharīʿah has defined them. The inward kinds include, on one side, false beliefs such as kufr (unbelief), shirk (associating partners with Allah) and total reliance on anyone other than Allah, and on the other side pride, vanity, malice, jealousy and love of worldly things. The Qur'an and the Sunnah have fully explained how evil these beliefs and tendencies are. Yet by making purification a separate prophetic function the verse indicates that, just as knowing words and technical terms does not make someone master of a science or art, so knowing and even properly understanding its principles does not by itself make him perfect in it. To reach any degree of perfection one must also learn to put the principles into practice and realise them in oneself, and that again requires the supervision of an authentic teacher and guide. In the Ṣūfī path (ṭarīqah) the role of the spiritual guide, the shaykh, is to help the disciple obey the Qur'an and the Sunnah so thoroughly that obedience becomes not an effort but a habit, almost his nature.

A warning is added here about the modern habit of calling Islam "the religion of action", with the implication that action is the opposite of thought and practice the opposite of theory, as if the two could hardly be reconciled and a self-respecting person must choose action over thought. Such formulations are no older than four centuries and grew out of dilemmas particular to the Christian West. They cannot belong to a religious or metaphysical way of seeing things. Any activity that is specifically human is not automatic but preceded by thought, so all practice is governed by some theory and all action by some principle, good or bad, including the actions of those who promote the dichotomy. In Islam no action, however good, has the slightest merit or spiritual efficacy unless it carries divine sanction, so the first duty of a Muslim is to learn the divine commands and then to obey them. One may call the first theory and the second practice, but there is no dichotomy and no choice between them. No practice is valid unless informed by theory, and no theory is of much use unless put into practice. Theory gives practice its meaning, and only through practice does one truly come to know theory. They are not two things but two ways of looking at one reality, and what finally matters is realisation, making the essential truths real to oneself. Even in the West, as late as the end of the Middle Ages, people knew that theōria and prāxis went together in the language of spiritual discipline. The Greek word itself says it: although "theory" in modern European languages has come to mean speculation, supposition or fancy, the Greek verb theōreō meant to see and theōros meant the man who sees, so theōria was not a fancy but a truth that could be seen and actively realised.

### A Book and a man: the two means of guidance

From the days of the first prophet to the days of the last, it has been Allah's way to guide people and show them the Straight Path by sending them not only His Books but His prophets as well. This points to a general principle: for their guidance people need both a divine teaching revealed as a Book and a human teacher in the form of a prophet who trains and disciplines them until they absorb that guidance fully. Not one or the other, but both, for only a man can be the teacher of another man, and a book serves only as an aid. That is why Islam began with a Book and a Prophet, and the two together produced a society of people unparalleled in history for their rectitude. For later generations the same two principles continue to work in the form of the Sharīʿah and the men of Allah. The Qur'an stresses this again and again: "O believers! Be mindful of Allah and be with the truthful" (9:119), and the verse that sums up the qualities of the truthful (aṣ-ṣādiqūn) ends, "It is they who are true ˹in faith˺, and it is they who are mindful ˹of Allah˺" (2:177). Al-Fātiḥah is the essence of the Qur'an, and the essence of al-Fātiḥah is guidance to the Straight Path (aṣ-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm). Yet to indicate that path the Qur'an does not call it the path of the Qur'an, or of the Prophet ﷺ, or of the Sunnah, but speaks of the men of Allah who can show it to the seeker: "the Path of those You have blessed—not those You are displeased with, or those who are astray" (1:7). Another verse specifies who they are: those blessed by Allah are "the prophets, the people of truth, the martyrs, and the righteous" (4:69). The Prophet ﷺ likewise named for all later generations the people to be followed in religion. He said he was leaving two things behind, and whoever held fast to them would never go astray: the Book of Allah, and his descendants and family, as at-Tirmidhī reports. A hadith in al-Bukhārī says to follow Abū Bakr and ʿUmar after him. A third says to hold to his Sunnah and the way of al-Khulafāʾ ar-Rāshidūn, the four rightly guided caliphs.

In religion as in the sciences and arts, real knowledge comes from authentic books and authentic teachers together. In religion, however, people are liable to lean excessively or exclusively on one of the two, which does them more harm than good. On one side are those who neglect the Book of Allah and come to adore their scholars and spiritual masters without troubling to find out whether those masters obey the Sharīʿah. This has been the characteristic sickness of the Jews and Christians, of whom the Qur'an says, "They have taken their rabbis and monks as well as the Messiah, son of Mary, as lords besides Allah" (9:31). It is the royal road to shirk and kufr, on which millions have perished and go on perishing. On the other side are those who claim that the Book of Allah is enough for them by itself and that they need no teacher, scholar or guide to understand it. This too is misguidance, for interpreting the Book of Allah on one's own without reliable specialists draws a person into every kind of error, makes him a slave of his own desires, and can in some cases lead him right out of Islam. What is required is to give each of the two means its proper place and benefit from both. The basic principle is clear: the authority to command belongs to Allah alone, and it is Allah alone whom people are called to obey. The Prophet ﷺ is the means by which they learn how Allah is to be obeyed, and he is obeyed because obeying him is obeying Allah. Beyond that, when one meets difficulty in understanding or acting on the Qur'an and hadith, one should turn willingly and respectfully to the words and deeds of the masters of these subjects, and treat that as the key to the door of salvation.

### The Sunnah is preserved along with the Book

A second conclusion follows from the fact that teaching the Book is a prophetic function. Allah has promised to protect the Qur'an Himself: "It is certainly We Who have revealed the Reminder, and it is certainly We Who will preserve it" (15:9). So every word, every consonant and every vowel of the Qur'an has remained intact to this day and will remain so as long as the world lasts. Since the verse makes the Prophet's ﷺ teaching indispensable for understanding the Qur'an and acting on it in any real sense, it follows that his teaching must also enjoy divine protection in its own degree and remain intact as a whole until the end of the world, for otherwise preserving the words alone would not fully serve the purpose for which Allah revealed them. His teaching is what is called the Sunnah or the hadith. Allah has not promised the hadith the same degree of protection as the Qur'an, and the words of the Sunnah have not been preserved in exactly the same way, yet by this verse the prophetic explanations too must remain intact, and taken as a whole they have. Whenever anyone has tried to distort a hadith or invent a spurious one, the specialists of the science have exposed the fraud. In fulfilment of the prediction implicit in this verse, Allah has preserved the Prophet's ﷺ teaching from the days of the Companions to the present through fully authentic collections of hadith and through the masters of the subject, and this protection will continue to the last day. The Prophet ﷺ himself assured his Ummah that until the end of the world there would always be a group of authentic scholars guarding the Qur'an and the hadith against distortion and misrepresentation. The Two Ṣaḥīḥs record his words that a group of his community will always remain on the truth, undeterred by those who fail them or oppose them, until the command of Allah comes while they are so, and al-Bukhārī adds in his Ṣaḥīḥ that they will be in ash-Shām. The light that the Prophet's ﷺ mother saw shining over ash-Shām pointed to the firm establishment of his religion there, which is why at the end of time ash-Shām will be a refuge for Islam and its people, and ʿĪsā son of Maryam will descend there, by the eastern white minaret in Damascus. This hadith also gives the lie to some contemporary writers who, in order to discredit the rulings of the Sharīʿah, have tried to spread the notion that the whole body of hadith is inauthentic and unreliable. Anyone with eyes can see the stratagem: if the hadith cannot be trusted, the text of the Qur'an can no longer be trusted either, and that is exactly what the Westerners and their local allies want, to turn the Muslims away from the Qur'an.

### The prayer fulfilled

The three prophetic functions that Ibrāhīm named in his prayer, and that the Prophet ﷺ was sent to perform, were accomplished in his own lifetime. To grasp the transformation that the recitation of the Qur'an, the Prophet's ﷺ teaching and his purifying influence worked in people, it is enough to read what the Qur'an says in praise of his Companions: "And those with him are firm with the disbelievers and compassionate with one another. You see them bowing and prostrating ˹in prayer˺, seeking Allah’s bounty and pleasure" (48:29).

### In short

- Ibrāhīm's prayer for a messenger from his own offspring was answered in Muḥammad ﷺ, whom ʿĪsā later announced by name (61:6), and who called himself the prayer of his father Ibrāhīm and the good news of ʿĪsā.
- The messenger's three functions are to recite the verses, to teach the Book and wisdom, which is the Sunnah, and to purify people of shirk, sin and inward vices.
- Recitation is named separately from teaching because the words of the Qur'an are themselves an object of worship and reward, and no translation is the Qur'an.
- Teaching is named separately because knowing Arabic cannot replace the Prophet's ﷺ explanation (16:44), which implies that the Sunnah, like the Qur'an (15:9), is preserved, guarded by a group of his community that will never cease.
- Guidance needs both a Book and men of Allah (9:119, 2:177, 1:7, 4:69), and the Companions described in 48:29 show what the three functions achieved.
