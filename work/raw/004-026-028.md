<<<VERSE 4:26>>>
After the laws, the reason for them: "It is Allah's Will to make things clear to you, guide you to the ˹noble˺ ways of those before you, and turn to you in mercy. For Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise". The verses that follow a long run of injunctions now step back and say what the whole legislation is for.

### Phrase by phrase

**"It is Allah's Will to make things clear to you"** means He sets out for the believers what He has allowed and what He has forbidden, in this surah and in others, the laws of the religion together with what lies in their interest. He makes His sacred law explicit, and one should not think the obligation is a burden without purpose, even where the purpose cannot be traced in every detail.

**"guide you to the ˹noble˺ ways of those before you"** means their righteous ways, and how to adhere to the commandments He likes and is pleased with. The communities before were charged with injunctions like these, and those who acted on them were rewarded with Allah's favour. One reading keeps the focus narrow: the people of the Book before you were forbidden the captives, and patience is better for you too.

**"and turn to you in mercy"** is His acceptance of repentance from sin and error, bringing you back from the disobedience you practised to obedience to Him, and forgiving what ensued in pre-Islamic times.

**"For Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise"** means He knows His creatures, knows your dire need for marriage, and is Wise in what He has ordained for you, making the laws according to His servants' interests.

### Nothing new in the standards

There is nothing new about the moral standards the Qur'an lays down. In every age God proclaimed them through His messengers, and believing people followed them. The ancient heavenly books could not remain untampered with, so the divine modes of living were lost or obscured, and God revealed them again, in Arabic, through His final Messenger. Whoever follows them today joins the eternal caravan of righteous humanity that walked the divine path under grace. The verse puts the believers in the line of the prophets and the righteous who came earlier, and the guidance itself is described as the path of those prophets and friends.

### The inner reading

When the Prophet ﷺ and his community were told the stories of past communities, what those people did and how they were dealt with, the believers looked to what would be done with them, for among them were some who had also done the impermissible. Their question was: if only we knew how God will deal with us, whether by abasement, by metamorphosis, or by punishment. So God most high said: "And to guide you in the ways of those before you", informing them of what was done with them, "and to turn toward you." To you, I turn. To those who came before you, destruction was already given in full. Other readings unfold the verse further: He desires to make clear to you by unveiling His secrets, so that what was hidden from others is manifest to you. He desires to make clear His singularity in bringing things into existence, in which no one else has any part. That guidance is self-entrustment to God, contentment of heart, and complete surrender before the decree and the judgement. And He turns toward you by accepting your repentance after having created it, then repaying you according to what He created for you in it.

### In short

- The laws were made clear so the believer knows the halāl from the ḥarām and walks the path of the righteous who came before.
- The moral standard is not new, only restored, and following it joins the company of the old caravan of the righteous.
- Read inwardly, the stories of the past are a mercy: their end was destruction, and yours is accepted repentance.

<<<VERSE 4:27>>>
The mercy named in the previous verse now meets its obstacle: "And it is Allah's Will to turn to you in grace, but those who follow their desires wish to see you deviate entirely ˹from Allah's Way˺". Two wills pull at the believer, the will of God to forgive, and the wish of the followers of desire to drag everyone down with them.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And it is Allah's Will to turn to you in grace"** repeats the turning of the previous verse on purpose, expanding it. When God purposes to turn toward a servant, no enemy is allowed to gloat over him, and nothing bad reaches him in either abode.

**"but those who follow their desires wish to see you deviate entirely"** names the pull from the other side. The followers of Shayṭān among the Jews, the Christians and the adulterous wish that the believers would take the horrendous path of falsehood instead of the truth, transgressing what is right by committing what was forbidden them, so that they might become like them.

### The company that drags

The followers of desire include the fornicators and adulterers, and the nations and peoples of false creeds. For them the restrictions of halāl and ḥarām simply do not register, and no line is drawn between lawful and unlawful. There are creeds in which marrying women is permitted where marriage with them is not proper. There are many atheists who favour eliminating the institution of marriage itself as out of tune with the times. Not content with what they call living together, there are places where people talk of women as a shared item of enjoyment. One report of the verse's first audience names the Jews, who claimed that marrying the sisters of the father's side was lawful in their Scripture, and the Magians are named with the people of the Book in the same breath.

The verse is a guard for the weak. Some Muslims, subscribing to Islam yet weak in faith, keep company with disbelievers and atheists of such inclinations, fall prey to their lustful claims, and begin to think their own religion outdated and the enemy's position the very epitome of human progress. They have been framed into believing that people of such ideas are modern and forward-looking, and they start wishing their own religion had permitted what the enemy permits. Refuge with Allah from such spineless surrender. The best policy is the one the verse itself gives: take Allah's warning and stay away from owning and practising the ideas and ways of people with dispositions so wicked.

### In short

- God's will is to turn to you in grace, and no enemy gloats over the one He turns toward.
- The followers of desire, the adulterous and the creedless, wish the believers a tremendous deviation, so that they may be like them.
- Weak Muslims in their company begin to see the crooked way as progress. The verse's warning is to keep out of that company and those ideas.
- Read inwardly, the desire of the followers of passion is weak and held back before the desire of the Real, whose wish to forgive is the stronger pull.

### The inner reading

The followers of passion are measured against the Real, and the measure is desire itself. Their desire, their irāda, becomes weak, enfeebled and held back before the desire of the Real. And the closing word of the passage, that man was created weak, describes the servants' need and affliction, their ḍurr, but does not accept it as their ʿudhr, their excuse. The will that turns toward them is stronger than the wish that pulls them away, and the lightening the Real desires for His servants runs through the whole of the law.

<<<VERSE 4:28>>>
The passage's last word on the law is mercy: "And it is Allah's Will to lighten your burdens, for humankind was created weak". The legislation itself is shaped to the creature it addresses.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And it is Allah's Will to lighten your burdens"**, the first part of verse 28, means His legislation, His orders, His prohibitions and His decrees are made easy, prescribed so that everyone obligated with them can carry them. He does not burden a soul with more than it can do, for the sacred law is fitted to the strength of the creature. This is why the free man was allowed to marry a bondwoman under certain conditions, as Mujāhid and others stated, why the parties to a marriage were given the right to settle the dower by mutual consent, and why marrying more than one woman was allowed under circumstantial necessity, on condition that justice is not compromised.

**"for humankind was created weak"** is the reason. Man is weak by nature and constituted desire-prone. Had the demand been made of him to turn from women completely and forever, he would have proved a hopeless performer at keeping Allah's injunctions. In view of his weakness he was not only permitted to marry but induced to. Marriage is a smart mutual contract that removes the chances of the weakness afflicting men and women, for the life of the couple brings mutual self-realization, a sight untainted and a character unalloyed, and that purity makes both strong, self-reliant and happy.

### The weakness itself

Ibn Abī Ḥātim recorded that Ṭāwūs said the weakness is concerning women. Wakīʿ said: man's mind leaves when women are involved. One reading keeps it plain: man cannot do without women. Wherever the name of man appears in the Qur'an, a displeasing attribute is linked to it: "Surely man is a great wrongdoer, ungrateful" (14:34), "Surely man was created anxious" (70:19), "Surely man is rebellious" (96:6), "Surely man is ungrateful to his Lord" (100:6), "Surely man is in loss" (103:2). The reason lies in his origin: man comes of dust, and dust is the ground of density and the root of opacity.

The servant's hope rests on the day of his making: He saw the faults, and He bought along with the faults. The Pir of the Ṭarīqah prayed:

> O Lord, You called me ignorant. What comes from the ignorant other than disloyalty? You called me weak. What comes from the weak other than error? Take our inability to overcome ourselves as stemming from that weakness. Take our boldness and insolence as stemming from that ignorance. You lifted us up, and no one said "Lift up." Now that You have lifted up, don't put down. Keep us in the shadow of Your bounty. If You water, You Yourself planted. If You flatten the foundation, You Yourself raised it. I, the servant, am just what You fancied. Don't throw me down. You lifted me up.

Read inwardly, the verse gives both the diagnosis and its cure, and it does not let the weakness serve as a licence. By "man was created weak", He describes the servants' need and affliction, but He does not accept it as their excuse. The lightening is real and manifold: He lightens the weight of litanies through the intermittent incomings that visit the heart, the struggles of the seeker with the lights of witnessings that enter deeply, the burdens of service with the sweetness of acts of obedience, the difficulties of the trust He carries for you, and the hardships of seeking with the refreshment of arriving.

### In short

- The law is built light: bondwomen permitted under conditions, dowers settled by consent, plural marriage under necessity with justice.
- Man's weakness, Ṭāwūs's "concerning women" and Wakīʿ's departing mind, is the reason the law bends, and marriage is the smart contract that cures what it bends for.
- Wherever the Qur'an names man, it links a displeasing attribute, for he is dust upon dust, and his hope is that his Maker bought him with his faults.
- The weakness explains the law's ease but excuses nothing, and the lightening reaches from litanies to the refreshment of arrival.
