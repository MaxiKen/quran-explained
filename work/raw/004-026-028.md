<<<VERSE 4:26>>>
After the long run of marriage rules, three short verses step back to show the purpose behind them. Allah is pointing to His favours: the rules are for your own good, even where you cannot see how in every detail. The first says: "It is Allah’s Will to make things clear to you, guide you to the ˹noble˺ ways of those before you, and turn to you in mercy. For Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise." The verses also encourage people to follow the rules, and they warn against those who mean harm and want you to stray from the straight path.

### Phrase by phrase

**"It is Allah’s Will to make things clear to you"**. Allah makes plain to the believers what is lawful, ḥalāl, and what is unlawful, ḥarām, in this surah and in others. He is setting out His commands clearly and openly. One explanation reads it as explaining what is lawful for you. Another says it means explaining that patience, and refraining from marrying captive women, is better for you than marrying them. Others say it covers the rules of your faith and whatever serves your good, in this world and the next, and they describe the sacred law as His way of life for you.

**"guide you to the ˹noble˺ ways of those before you"**. The ways meant are the righteous ways of those who came earlier, and how they kept the commands that Allah loves and is pleased with. Allah is placing you on the road of the noble prophets and the righteous before you. Their ways include their rulings on what is lawful and unlawful. Do not think that these details of the lawful and unlawful are for you alone. Earlier communities were also charged with such rules, and those who acted on them were rewarded with Allah's favour. One explanation points to the People of the Book, who were forbidden to marry captive women. The aim in guiding you to those ways is that you may follow them, and be returned, as Allah says, from disobedience to obeying Him. Allah also wants you to take up the noble qualities and the praiseworthy conduct of the earlier prophets.

**"and turn to you in mercy"**. Allah accepts repentance from sin and error. One explanation says that He will forgive what happened in the days before Islam.

**"For Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise"**. He is the Knower and the Wise in His commands, His decrees, His actions and His words. He knows what serves His servants' good, and He shapes His laws to that. He also knows how great their need for marriage is, and He is wise in having forbidden marriage with captive women except when there is need.

### Not a new law

There is nothing new about the moral standards the Qur'an lays down. In every age Allah announced them through His messengers, and the believers in every age followed them. But the earlier heavenly books could not stay free of tampering, so the divine way of life was lost or covered over. Allah revealed it again, in Arabic, through His last Messenger. Today any group that follows these standards joins the long caravan of righteous people who were blessed with Allah's grace and held firmly to His path.

In every community, certain customs take root through centuries of habit, and people grow too used to them to look at them critically. When an original thinker tries to reform society, traditionalists oppose him bitterly. They find it hard to leave familiar ways for unfamiliar ones, and they are hostile to any reform that would take them away from their forefathers' ways. In these situations the strongest reaction comes from the religious class. Once the heart of a religion has gone weak, hair-splitting argument spoils it, and a system of detailed rules built on outward form grows up. Without the real spirit, people go on with the old rituals and think they are following the religion of Allah. This religion of human making is gradually identified with their forefathers and becomes sacred. In the end, the simple and natural religion of Allah is so heavily veiled that it is hard to recognise as the original. So any movement to revive the simple and natural religion meets bitter opposition, because people find their ritual dogma threatened by it. Those whose dogma rests on a self-made faith attack any attempt to revive Allah's plain and straightforward religion, since they expect it to end their dominance and leadership.

### The inner reading

When Allah told the Prophet ﷺ and his community the stories of earlier communities, what they did and how they were dealt with, the believers looked to their own case, since there were among them some who also did what is not allowed. They said, "If only we knew how Allah will deal with us. Will it be disgrace, a change of form, punishment, or what?" So Allah said, "and guide you to the ways of those before you". It is as if He said, We will tell you what We did with them. And "turn to you in mercy" means: as for you, I turn to you, while those before you I have already destroyed completely.

It is also said that Allah wants to make things clear to you by lifting the veil from His secrets, so that what was hidden from others is made plain to you. Or He wants to make clear His singleness in bringing things into existence and creating new things, in which no one else has any part. "The ways of those before you" is the path of the prophets and the friends of Allah. This guidance is entrusting everything to Allah, being content with Him and surrendering completely to His decree and judgment. And "turn to you in mercy" means that He accepts your repentance after having created it in you, and then rewards you according to what He created for you in your repentance.

### In short

- Allah's rules in this passage are meant to make things clear and to put you on the path of the prophets and the righteous before you, and to turn to you in mercy.
- Earlier communities were charged with the same kinds of rules, and Allah rewarded those who acted on them.
- The moral standards are old, and they get covered over by tradition and ritual, so that reform is bitterly resisted.
- Inwardly, the guidance is trust in Allah, contentment and surrender, and His acceptance of repentance is something He creates in the servant first.

<<<VERSE 4:27>>>
This verse repeats the offer of grace and sets the people who want the opposite beside it: "And it is Allah’s Will to turn to you in grace, but those who follow their desires wish to see you deviate entirely ˹from Allah’s Way˺." The repetition is deliberate, to bring out the point more fully.

### Phrase by phrase

**"And it is Allah’s Will to turn to you in grace"**. Allah wants to turn to you and forgive your disobedience. One explanation says that He wants to overlook your transgressions, since He made adultery and marrying one's half-sisters on the father's side forbidden for you.

**"but those who follow their desires"**. The scholars name them in different ways. One explanation says that these are the followers of Shayṭān among the Jews, Christians and adulterers. Another names the Jews and Christians, or the Magians and adulterers. Another names the Jews alone, who claimed that a man's marrying his half-sisters on the father's side is lawful in their Scripture. The people meant include fornicators, adulterers, and nations and peoples who hold false creeds. They do not recognise any limit between the lawful, ḥalāl, and the unlawful, ḥarām, and make no distinction between them.

**"wish to see you deviate entirely ˹from Allah’s Way˺"**. They want you to take the terrible road of falsehood instead of the truth, and to give way to their false intentions and crooked ways. They would like you to transgress what is right by doing what is forbidden to you, so that you become like them. Allah tells you to be on guard against them.

### Who wants you to deviate

There are creeds in which it is acceptable to marry women whom it is not proper to marry. There are many atheists who want to do away with the institution of marriage altogether, as something out of step with modern times. Some are not content with what they call "living together". In some countries people go so far as to speak of women as a shared object of enjoyment. All of this comes from those who take orders from their own desires.

Some Muslims are weak in faith, though they say they follow Islam. When they keep company with disbelievers and atheists of this kind, they fall for their claims of desire, and they begin to think that their own religion is out of date and that the position of the enemy is the height of progress. They do not see that they have been made to believe that people with such ideas are modern and forward-looking, and they even begin to wish that their own religion had allowed these things. We seek refuge with Allah from such spineless surrender. The best policy is to heed Allah's warning and stay away from adopting or practising the ideas and ways of people with such disordered dispositions.

### The inner reading

The verse separates the story of the earlier group from that of the later one. Whenever Allah wants to turn toward someone, He does not let an enemy gloat over him, and He does not give him anything bad in the two abodes, this world and the next. As for those who follow their passions, their will, irāda, becomes weak and feeble and is held back before the will of the Real.

The words that follow, about lightening things, are explained in several ways. Allah lightens the weight of the litanies, through intermittent visitations that arrive in your hearts, the wāridāt. Or He lightens what you bear in your struggles, mujāhadāt, with the lights of witnessing, mushāhadāt, that enter deeply into your hearts. Or He lightens the burdens of service with the sweetness of acts of obedience. Or He lightens the difficulties of the trust, the amānah, by carrying its burden for you. Or He eases the hardships of the search with the relief of arrival. About human weakness, He describes by it their need and affliction, ḍurr, but He does not accept it as an excuse, ʿudhr.

### In short

- Allah wishes to turn to you in grace and forgive, and those who follow their desires wish to turn you far from the right path.
- The people meant include fornicators and adulterers, followers of false creeds, and, in some explanations, Jews, Christians and Magians who made lawful what is forbidden.
- A believer must be careful with company that mocks marriage and the lawful and unlawful, because weak Muslims can end up thinking their own religion outdated.
- Inwardly, whoever Allah wishes to turn toward is not left to an enemy's gloating, and He lightens the weight of service with the sweetness of obedience.

<<<VERSE 4:28>>>
The third verse (4:28) names a further wish of Allah's, which is to make things easier: "And it is Allah’s Will to lighten your burdens, for humankind was created weak."

### Phrase by phrase

**"And it is Allah’s Will to lighten your burdens"**. Allah wants to lighten the burden of His legislation, the sacred law, His orders, what He prohibits and what He decrees for you. He gives rulings that everyone obliged to follow them can carry out. He does not burden you with more than you can do. The marriage rules show this. If a man cannot afford to marry free women, he is allowed to marry bondwomen. The parties to a marriage may settle the dowry by mutual consent. A man may marry more than one woman when there is a real need, on condition that justice is not compromised. Mujāhid and others said that this is why Allah allowed free men to marry bondwomen under certain conditions. Another explanation says that Allah wants to make it easy for you to marry captives when there is a need.

**"for humankind was created weak"**. People are weak, and their make-up is full of desire. This is why it suits them to have commands made easy for them. Short explanations say that man cannot do without women, or is unable to hold back from women and from his passions. Ṭāwūs said, in a report recorded by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, that the words are about women. Wakīʿ added, "A man's mind leaves him when women are involved." The point is that Allah knows how weak human beings are by creation and by nature, and He makes His law fit that.

### Why marriage suits human weakness

If man were told to stay away from women completely and absolutely, he would be hopeless at obeying Allah's commands. Because of his weakness and limits, he was not only allowed to marry but also encouraged to. Life after marriage brings great benefits of mutual self-realisation to a couple, and above all the blessing of an untainted gaze and a clean character. Their purity toward each other raises the moral quality of life for both, and it makes them strong, self-reliant and happy. Marriage is therefore a shrewd mutual contract that removes the chances of weakness that may affect men and women. It is a method without equal.

### The inner reading: why "man" is described this way

In the Qur'an, wherever the name "man" appears, a displeasing attribute is attached to it. Allah says, "Indeed humankind is truly unfair, ˹totally˺ ungrateful" (14:34). He says, "Indeed, humankind was created impatient" (70:19). He says, "Most certainly, one exceeds all bounds" (96:6). He says, "Surely humankind is ungrateful to their Lord" (100:6), and "Surely humanity is in ˹grave˺ loss" (103:2). The reason is that man comes from dust, and heaviness begins in dust, and darkness has its root there.

The servant takes hope from this. On the day Allah created him, He saw his faults and bought him together with them. As the saying goes, "You bought me with my faults on the first day." The elder of the Ṭarīqah, the path, prayed, "O Lord, You named me ignorant, so what can an ignorant one bring but unfaithfulness?" He went on, "You named me weak, so what can a weak one bring but error?" He asked Allah to take our failure to master ourselves as coming from that weakness, and our boldness and insolence as coming from that ignorance. "You lifted us up when no one asked You to," he said. "Now that You have lifted us, do not let us fall. Keep us in the shadow of Your bounty."

He added that if Allah waters the plant, Allah planted it. If He levels the foundation, Allah raised it. The servant is only what Allah wanted him to be, and having lifted him up, Allah should not throw him down.

### In short

- Allah wishes to lighten the burden of His law, as He does by allowing marriage with bondwomen when a man cannot afford a free woman, by letting couples agree on the dowry, and by allowing a second wife in need on condition of justice.
- Humankind was created weak and desire-prone, so the law is made to fit human nature, and marriage is the lawful answer to it.
- Inwardly, the Qur'an links a fault to the word "man" because he is made of dust, and the servant hopes that Allah bought him with his faults.
