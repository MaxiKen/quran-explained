## Verse 6

Verse 6 says: *He is the One Who shapes you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise.*

In simple terms, the verse speaks of the womb's shaping: Allah forms each of you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills; and there is no god except Him — the Almighty, the All-Wise.

"He is the One Who shapes you in the wombs of your mothers as He wills" — the verse's shaping: the yusawwirukum — He shapes you — the fi al-arham — in the wombs — the kayfa yasha' — as He wills. Ibn Kathir (and the classical notes): Allah forms the creation in the wombs of the mothers as He wills — the male and the female, the beautiful and the otherwise, the long and the short — the shaping's variety is the Will's work — as in the hadith of the creation's stages (the nutfah, the 'alaqah, the mudghah; the angel records the provision, the deeds, the term, and the misery or the happiness; the soul is breathed). The verse's frame: the womb's art: the shaping is the divine form: the mother's womb is the workshop of the Will — the unseen knowledge of the Not-Hidden (3:5) becomes the visible form.

"There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise" — the verse's close: the la ilaha illa Huwa — the 'Aziz — the Almighty — al-Hakim — the All-Wise. The verse's frame: the Shaper is the Only — the Almighty's power in the womb's formation, the All-Wise's purpose in every shape: the formed and the forming.

The verse's lesson: every shape in every womb is the Will's signature — the One who forms is the Only — the Almighty, the All-Wise. And the reader's part: the shaping of your own form is the proof of the Shaper: the Only One, the Almighty, the All-Wise.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 7

Verse 7 says: *He is the One Who has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book, of which some verses are precise—they are the foundation of the Book—while others are elusive. Those with deviant hearts follow the elusive verses seeking ˹to spread˺ doubt through their ˹false˺ interpretations—but none grasps their ˹full˺ meaning except Allah. As for those well-grounded in knowledge, they say, “We believe in this ˹Quran˺—it is all from our Lord.” But none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason.*

In simple terms, the verse divides the Book: some verses are precise — the foundation; others are elusive; the deviant-hearted follow the elusive seeking doubt, but none knows their full meaning except Allah; the well-grounded in knowledge say: we believe — it is all from our Lord.

"of which some verses are precise—they are the foundation of the Book" — the verse's muhkamat: the ayat muhkamat — the precise, the entirely clear — the umm al-Kitab — the mother of the Book, the foundation. Ibn Kathir: the muhkamat — entirely clear and plain — the foundations of the Book, plain for everyone; the ayat that explain the abrogating rulings, the allowed, the prohibited, the laws, the limits, the obligations and the rulings that should be believed in and implemented. The verse's frame: the foundation verses: the clear commandments, the decisive rulings: the mother of the Book — the reference point of the understanding.

"while others are elusive" — the verse's mutashabihat: the ukhari mutashabihat — the others, the elusive, the ambiguous. Ibn Kathir: the mutashabihat — not entirely clear for many people — they include the abrogated ayat, the parables, the oaths, and what should be believed but not implemented — the wording encompasses a wide area of meanings. The verse's frame: the elusive: the verses of the multiple senses — the ones whose apparent meaning can be turned by the interpreters: the test of the faith.

"Those with deviant hearts follow the elusive verses seeking ˹to spread˺ doubt through their ˹false˺ interpretations" — the verse's deviants: the alladhina fi qulubihim zaygh — those in whose hearts there is deviation — the yattabi'una ma tashabaha — they follow the elusive — the ibtigha' al-fitnah — seeking the doubt — the ibtigha' ta'wilih — seeking its interpretation. Ibn Kathir: those who are misguided and deviate from truth to falsehood — they follow the mutashabih because they are able to alter its meanings to conform with their false interpretation — as for the muhkam they cannot be altered because they are clear. The hadith (al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, from 'A'ishah): the Prophet recited this ayah and said — "When you see those who seek the ambiguous verses in it, they are those whom Allah has named. Beware of them!" The verse's frame: the deviance's method: the elusive is the instrument of the fitnah — the false interpretation rides the multiple meanings: the deviants cite the ambiguous to fight the clear.

"but none grasps their ˹full˺ meaning except Allah" — the verse's knowledge: the wa ma ya'lamu ta'wilahu illa Allah — none knows its interpretation except Allah. Ibn Kathir: only Allah knows the true ta'wil of the mutashabihat — the tafsir's four types (Ibn 'Abbas): the tafsir the Arabs know in their language; the tafsir no one is excused of being ignorant of; the tafsir the scholars know; and the tafsir that only Allah knows. The verse's frame: the full meaning's reserve: the realities of the unseen (the Hour, the worlds' realities) are reserved: the pause at the divine Name is the school of Ibn 'Abbas and Ibn Kathir — the interpretation of the unseeable is Allah's alone.

"As for those well-grounded in knowledge, they say, "We believe in this ˹Quran˺—it is all from our Lord."" — the verse's firmly rooted: the al-rasikhuna fi al-'ilm — those well-grounded in knowledge — they believe in the whole — the muhkam and the mutashabih — the kullun min 'indi Rabbina — it is all from our Lord. Ibn Kathir: "all of it is from our Lord" — both the muhkam and the mutashabih are true and authentic, and each one testifies to the truth of the other — because they both are from Allah, and nothing that comes from Allah is ever met by contradiction or discrepancy. The verse's frame: the rooted believers' stance: the whole is the Lord's: the clear is obeyed, the elusive is believed — the muhkam is the key of the mutashabih.

"But none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason" — the verse's close: the ma yadhdhakkaru illa ulu al-albab — none will be mindful except the people of reason. Ibn Kathir: those who have good minds and sound comprehension — understand, contemplate and comprehend the meaning in the correct manner. The verse's frame: the reason's receivers: the mindful are the ones who refer the unclear to the clear and believe the whole.

The verse's lesson: the Book is the clear foundation and the elusive test; the deviants ride the elusive; the rooted believe the whole; and the mindful are the receivers. And the reader's part: take the muhkam as the key; believe the mutashabih; beware of the arguers of the ambiguous — and say with the rooted: we believe, it is all from our Lord.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 8

Verse 8 says: *˹They say,˺ “Our Lord! Do not let our hearts deviate after you have guided us. Grant us Your mercy. You are indeed the Giver ˹of all bounties˺.*

In simple terms, the verse is the prayer of the well-grounded: Our Lord, do not let our hearts deviate after You have guided us; grant us Your mercy — You are the Giver of all bounties.

"Our Lord! Do not let our hearts deviate after you have guided us" — the verse's plea: the la tuzigh qulubana — do not make our hearts deviate — the ba'da idh hadaytana — after You have guided us. Ibn Kathir: "Do not deviate our hearts from the guidance after You allowed them to acquire it. Do not make us like those who have wickedness in their hearts, those who follow the mutashabih in the Qur'an." The verse's frame: the rooted ones' fear: the guidance's stability is the request: the deviance after the guidance is the ruin — the well-grounded do not boast of the knowledge; they pray for the heart's steadiness.

"Grant us Your mercy. You are indeed the Giver ˹of all bounties˺" — the verse's mercy: the hab lana min ladunka rahmah — grant us mercy from You — the innaka anta al-Wahhab — You are the Bestower, the Giver. Ibn Kathir: "and grant us mercy from You" — the mercy of the guidance's keeping — the Wahhab: the Giver without measure. The verse's frame: the mercy before the gathering (3:9): the steadfastness is the mercy's grant.

The verse's lesson: the guidance's keeper is the prayer: after the guidance, the request of the steadfastness and the mercy. And the reader's part: pray it with the rooted — do not let the hearts deviate after the guidance, and grant the mercy, O Giver of all bounties.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 9

Verse 9 says: *Our Lord! You will certainly gather all humanity for the ˹promised˺ Day—about which there is no doubt. Surely Allah does not break His promise.”*

In simple terms, the verse closes the prayer: You will gather all humanity for the Day about which there is no doubt — Allah does not break His promise.

"Our Lord! You will certainly gather all humanity for the ˹promised˺ Day" — the verse's gathering: the innaka jami' al-nas — You will gather the people — the li-yawmin la rayba fihi — for a Day about which there is no doubt. Ibn Kathir: "Our Lord! Verily, it is You Who will gather mankind together on the Day about which there is no doubt" — the rooted believers' confession of the resurrection: the same gathering of the first and the last: the Day of the standing before the Lord. The verse's frame: the prayer's horizon: after the guidance's plea (3:8), the Day's confession: the gathering is the Lord's promise.

"—about which there is no doubt. Surely Allah does not break His promise" — the verse's certainty: the la rayba fihi — no doubt in it — the la yukhlif Allah al-mi'ad — Allah does not break His promise. Ibn Kathir: the promise of the gathering — the Word Who does not fail: the believers' certainty is the promise's stability. The verse's frame: the promise's keeper: the Day is sure because the Promiser does not fail: the gathered after the guided.

The verse's lesson: the gathering is certain; the promise is unbreakable — the well-grounded believe the Day as they believe the Book. And the reader's part: stand in the Day's certainty — the One who guided promises the gathering, and the promise is the unbroken.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 10

Verse 10 says: *Indeed, neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah—and they will be the fuel for the Fire.*

In simple terms, the verse warns: the wealth and children of the disbelievers will not benefit them against Allah — they will be the firewood of the Fire.

"Indeed, neither the wealth nor children of the disbelievers will be of any benefit to them against Allah" — the verse's uselessness: the lan tughniya 'anhum amwaluhum — their wealth will not avail — the wa la awladuhum — nor their children — the min Allah — against Allah. Ibn Kathir (with 3:116 and the classical notes): the wealth and the children — the measures of this world's pride — do not avail against Allah: the Day's currency is the deed, not the counted. The verse's frame: the world's powerlessness: the treasures and the offspring are the world's strengths — and they are the strengths that fail at the meeting with Allah.

"—and they will be the fuel for the Fire" — the verse's fuel: the wa hum waqud al-nar — they are the fuel of the Fire. Ibn Kathir (cf. 2:24): the disbelievers are the firewood of the Fire — as the fire is fed by the wood, the Fire is fed by the ones who made the wealth and the children their refuge: the fuel of the place whose food is the denial. The verse's frame: the reversal: the pride's measure becomes the Fire's food.

The verse's lesson: the wealth and the children are the trusts, not the shields — the Day's benefit is the deed of the One who owns them. And the reader's part: do not lean on the counted — the wealth and the children avail nothing against Allah — and the fuel of the Fire is the denial.

===VERSE-END===
