## References

- Ma'arif al-Qur'an (Mufti Muhammad Shafi), tafsir of 2:81–82 — "engrossed in sin" explained as evil taking hold completely with no trace of goodness; the verdict stated to apply to the disbeliever whose disbelief nullifies his deeds; the refutation of the Jews' claim of a few days' punishment. Quran.com: https://quran.com/2:81/tafsirs/en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir*, commentary on 2:81 — whoever commits evil and abides purposefully in his error, coming on the Day of Resurrection with no good deeds, will be among the people of the Fire. Quran.com: https://quran.com/al-baqarah/81/tafsirs
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir*, commentary on 2:84–85 — the covenant not to shed blood or expel one another; the three Jewish tribes of Madinah (Banu Qaynuqa' allied to the Khazraj; Banu Nadir and Banu Qurayzah allied to the Aws) fighting in the tribal wars, shedding each other's blood, then ransoming captives per the Torah; "Do you believe in a part of the Scripture and reject the rest?" Quran.com: https://quran.com/al-baqarah/85/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir; also https://quranx.com/Tafsirs/2.84
- Al-Jalalayn, tafsir of 2:85 — the fulfillment of "disgrace in this worldly life": Qurayzah were slain and the Nadir were expelled to Syria; Ibn Abbas (Tanwir al-Miqbas) on the covenant referring to Banu Qurayzah and Banu Nadir. https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-85-sora-2.html
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir*, commentary on 2:87–88 — the holy spirit (Ruh al-Qudus) identified as Gabriel: Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Muhammad ibn Ka'b, Isma'il ibn Khalid, al-Suddi, al-Rabi' ibn Anas, 'Atiyah al-'Awfi and Qatadah; *ghulf* as "screened" (Ibn Abbas via Muhammad ibn Ishaq), "covered" (Mujahid), "stamped" ('Ikrimah), "do not comprehend" (Abu al-'Aliyah); the alternate reading that their hearts contain all knowledge; cf. 41:5 and 4:155. https://www.alim.org/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/surah/2/88/ and https://quranx.com/tafsirs/2.88
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir*, commentary on 2:89 — the Jews used to invoke Allah (for the coming of the Prophet) for victory over the polytheists: Ibn Abbas via Muhammad ibn Ishaq (against the Aws and Khazraj); Ibn Abbas via al-'Awfi and al-Dahhak (the people of the Book sought victory by Muhammad's coming); Abu al-'Aliyah's report; Salam ibn Mushkim's reply "he did not bring anything we recognize"; envying the Arabs. https://quranx.com/Tafsir/Kathir/2.89 and https://quran.com/al-baqarah/89/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir
- Al-Mizan (Sayyid Muhammad Husayn al-Tabataba'i), *Tafsir al-Mizan*, commentary on 2:90 — *baghyan* as envy, the reason for their disbelief in the Prophet after recognizing him; "they returned with wrath upon wrath" as accumulated wrath; the commentary also covers 2:91 and 2:93 (the question about killing prophets; the imbibing of the calf's love into the hearts). https://al-islam.org/al-mizan-exegesis-quran-volume-1-sayyid-muhammad-husayn-tabatabai/surah-al-baqarah-verses-89-93
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir*, commentary on 2:93 — Qatadah (via Ma'mar from 'Abdur-Razzaq): the hearts "absorbed its love until its love resided in their hearts"; same view attributed to Abu al-'Aliyah and al-Rabi' ibn Anas; "We hear and disobey" against the believers' "We hear and obey" (2:285); Ibn Kathir's comment that their disbelief included rejecting the Final Messenger. Quran.com: https://quran.com/al-baqarah/93/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir*, commentary on 2:94–96 — "wish for death" as invoking Allah to destroy the lying party (mubahalah-like challenge); Ibn Abbas via al-Dahhak and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: had they invoked death they would have perished; Ibn Jarir's report of the Prophet's statement that they would have died and seen their seats in the Fire; parallel in 62:6–8; the world as a prison for the believer and a paradise for the disbeliever; the Jews greediest for life because they know their evil end; the thousand-year wish. https://www.alim.org/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/surah/2/94/
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir*, commentary on 2:97–98 — al-Tabari: the scholars of tafsir agree these verses were revealed in response to the Jews claiming Gabriel is their enemy and Michael their friend; Ibn Abbas (Tanwir al-Miqbas) naming 'Abdullah ibn Suriyyah; Muqatil and al-Qushairi on the rabbis' exchange — Gabriel brings war and hardship, Michael rain and mercy; the mention of both angels specifically; "Allah is the enemy of the disbelievers" as punishment in the Hereafter. https://quranx.com/Tafsir/Kathir/2.97 and https://quranx.com/tafsirs/2.98
