## Verse 168

## Verse 169

Verse 169 says: *Never think of those martyred in the cause of Allah as dead. In fact, they are alive with their Lord, well provided for—*

In simple terms, the verse corrects the view of the martyrs: do not think of those killed in Allah's cause as dead — they are alive with their Lord, receiving provision.

The verse is the doctrine of the martyrs' life. Ibn Kathir: "Think not of those as dead who are killed in the way of Allah. Nay, they are alive, with their Lord, and they have provision." The verse's *wa la tahsabanna alladhina qutilu fi sabil Allahi amwata* — and do not think of those killed in the way of Allah as dead — is the negation; the *bal ahya'un 'inda rabbihim yurzaqun* — rather, they are alive with their Lord, provided for — is the life; and the *yurzaqun* — they are provided for — is the provision.

"Never think of those martyred in the cause of Allah as dead" — *wa la tahsabanna alladhina qutilu fi sabil Allahi amwata* — and never think of those who were killed in the way of Allah as dead. The *hasb* — the thinking, the supposing — is the negation: the *la tahsabanna* — do not think — the *amwat* — as dead — the negation of the death's view. Ibn Kathir: "Allah states that even though the martyrs were killed in this life, their souls are alive and receiving provisions in the Dwelling of Everlasting Life." The verse's *qutilu* — they were killed — is the cause's killing: the *fi sabil Allah* — in the way of Allah — the *qatl* — the killing — the apparent death — the *amwat* — the dead — the view of the world — negated.

"In fact, they are alive with their Lord, well provided for" — *bal ahya'un 'inda rabbihim yurzaqun* — rather, they are alive with their Lord, being provided for. The *bal* — rather — is the correction: the *ahya'* — the alive — the *'inda rabbihim* — with their Lord — the *yurzaqun* — provided for — the life and the provision. Ibn Kathir: "their souls are alive and receiving provisions in the Dwelling of Everlasting Life." The verse's *ahya'* — alive — is the life: not the dead but the alive; the *'inda rabbihim* — with their Lord — the nearness — the *yurzaqun* — provided for — the *rizq* — the provision — of the divine.

The hadiths of the martyrs' life, per Ibn Kathir: Muslim/Masruq from 'Abdullah from the Prophet — "Their souls are inside green birds that have lamps, which are hanging below the Throne (of Allah), and they wander about in Paradise wherever they wish. Then they return to those lamps. Allah looks at them and says, 'Do you wish for anything?' They say, 'What more could we wish for, while we go wherever we wish in Paradise?' Allah asked them this question thrice, and when they realize that He will keep asking them until they give an answer, they say, 'O Lord! We wish that our souls be returned to our bodies so that we are killed in Your cause again.' Allah knew that they did not have any other wish, so they were left." (Similar narrations from Anas and Abu Sa'id.) Imam Ahmad/Anas — "No soul that has a good standing with Allah and dies would wish to go back to the life of this world, except for the martyr. He would like to be returned to this life so that he could be martyred again, for he tastes the honor achieved from martyrdom" (Muslim). Imam Ahmad/Ibn 'Abbas — "When your brothers were killed at Uhud, Allah placed their souls in the bodies of green birds that drink from the rivers of Paradise and eat from its fruits, dwelling at golden lamps in the shadow of the Throne. When they found the goodness of their drink and food and the beauty of their resting place, they said, 'Would that our brothers knew what Allah has done for us, lest they abstain from Jihad and shrink from war.' Allah said, 'I will convey to them for you.'"

The verse's context: the martyrs of Uhud — the seventy dead — and the doctrine of their life: the *qutilu fi sabil Allah* — the killed in the way — the *ahya'* — the alive — with the Lord — the *yurzaqun* — provided — the *ahya'* — the life — of the martyrs. Ibn Kathir: "the martyrs were killed in this life... their souls are alive."

For the reader, the verse is the life of the martyrs and the correction of the view. The believer is taught: the *tahsabanna amwat* — the thinking of the dead — negated: the *qutilu fi sabil Allah* — the killed in the way — are not the dead; the *bal ahya'* — rather alive — the *'inda rabbihim* — with their Lord — the life of the nearness; and the *yurzaqun* — provided for — the *rizq* — the provision — of the divine. The verse's *bal* — rather — is the correction: the world's view of the death — the *hasb* — the thinking — negated; the martyrs' life — the *ahya'* — the alive — the *yurzaqun* — the provided. The believer's confidence: the death of the martyr is the life with the Lord — the green birds, the lamps, the Throne — the *ahya'* — the alive — the *yurzaqun* — the provided — the doctrine of the martyrs' life.

## Verse 170
