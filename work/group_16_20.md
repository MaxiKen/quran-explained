## Verse 16

Verse 16 says: *who pray, “Our Lord! We have believed, so forgive our sins and protect us from the torment of the Fire.”*

In simple terms, the verse describes the mindful: they pray — Our Lord, we have believed, so forgive our sins and protect us from the Fire's torment.

"who pray, "Our Lord! We have believed"" — the verse's prayer: the alladhina yaquluna — who say — the rabbana innana amanna — Our Lord, we have believed. Ibn Kathir: the description of the mindful — the ones who say: we have believed — the belief's confession as the prayer's opening. The verse's frame: the believers' address: the faith's declaration before the requests: the believing is the prayer's ground.

"so forgive our sins" — the verse's forgiveness: the fa-ighfir lana dhunubana — forgive us our sins. Ibn Kathir: the plea of the forgiveness — the believers' request: the sins' pardon after the confession: the faith's first request. The verse's frame: the mercy's request: the believed — then the forgiveness: the sins of the believers are the prayer's subject.

"and protect us from the torment of the Fire" — the verse's protection: the wa qina 'adhab al-nar — and protect us from the punishment of the Fire. Ibn Kathir: the protection from the Fire's torment — the second request: the saved from the Fire: the believers' prayer of the two: the forgiveness and the protection. The verse's frame: the believers' two asks: the past's pardon and the future's protection — the Fire's distance.

The verse's lesson: the mindful pray the two: forgive and protect — the faith's confession is the prayer's opening. And the reader's part: pray with them — we have believed, forgive, and protect from the Fire.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 17

Verse 17 says: *˹It is they˺ who are patient, sincere, obedient, and charitable, and who pray for forgiveness before dawn.*

In simple terms, the verse gives the mindful's five marks: the patient, the truthful, the obedient, the charitable, and the ones seeking forgiveness at the dawn's approach.

"˹It is they˺ who are patient" — the verse's first mark: the al-sabirin — the patient, the steadfast. Ibn Kathir (with the classical notes of 3:15–17): the description of the mindful: the patient — the ones who endure the obedience's burdens and the trials' weights. The verse's frame: the patience: the first of the character's list: the steadiness under the command and the trial.

"sincere" — the verse's second: the al-sadiqin — the truthful, the sincere. Ibn Kathir: the truthful — the words and the hearts' truth: the ones whose speech is the heart's report. The verse's frame: the sincerity: the second mark: the true in the claim of the belief: the believe as the truth is the say.

"obedient" — the verse's third: the al-qanitin — the devoutly obedient, the devoted. Ibn Kathir: the qanit — the obedient, the devoted to the obedience. The verse's frame: the submission: the third mark: the devoted standing: the obedience of the one who surrendered.

"and charitable" — the verse's fourth: the al-munfiqin — those who spend. Ibn Kathir: the spenders — the ones who give of the wealth in the cause and the good. The verse's frame: the giving: the fourth mark: the wealth's service: the spenders of the provision.

"and who pray for forgiveness before dawn" — the verse's fifth: the wa al-mustaghfirin bi al-as-har — the ones seeking forgiveness at the dawns. Ibn Kathir: the seekers of forgiveness before the dawn — the late-night prayer's people — the istighfar of the last part of the night: the ones who wake for the forgiveness before the light. The verse's frame: the dawn's mercy: the fifth mark: the forgiveness-seeking of the night's end: the hour of the descent's mercy.

The verse's lesson: the mindful are the five-marked: the patient, the truthful, the obedient, the spenders, the seekers of the dawn's forgiveness. And the reader's part: clothe yourself in the marks — the patience, the truth, the obedience, the giving, and the night's forgiveness.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 18

Verse 18 says: *Allah ˹Himself˺ is a Witness that there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—and so are the angels and people of knowledge. He is the Maintainer of justice. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise.*

In simple terms, the verse declares the threefold witness: Allah Himself, the angels, and the people of knowledge witness that there is no god except Him — the Maintainer of justice — the Almighty, the All-Wise.

"Allah ˹Himself˺ is a Witness that there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him" — the verse's first witness: the shahida Allah — Allah bears witness — the annahu la ilaha illa Huwa. Ibn Kathir: Allah bears witness that none has the right to be worshipped but He — His testimony: the proof and the signs: the declaration of the Oneness by the One. Al-Jalalayn: Allah has made it clear to His creation through proofs and signs that there is no god except Him. The verse's frame: the divine testimony: the highest witness: the One Himself declares the Oneness: the proofs of the creation are His witness.

"—and so are the angels and people of knowledge" — the verse's second and third: the wa al-mala'ikat — and the angels — the wa ulu al-'ilm — and those having knowledge. Ibn Kathir: Allah then mentioned the testimony of His angels and those who have knowledge after He mentioned His own testimony — the angels affirm it — and those having knowledge (the prophets and the believers) through their conviction and words — this ayah emphasizes the great virtue of those who have knowledge. The verse's frame: the threefold: the Lord's testimony, the angels' testimony, the learned's testimony: the witnesses of the Oneness: the highest, the unseen's hosts, the knowledge's holders.

"He is the Maintainer of justice" — the verse's justice: the qa'iman bi al-qist — standing, maintaining the justice. Ibn Kathir: (He always) maintains His creation in justice — in all that He does — the upright with equity: the justice of the law and the judgment. The verse's frame: the just Maintainer: the witness's content: the One who is witnessed is the One who maintains the justice.

"There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—the Almighty, All-Wise" — the verse's close: the la ilaha illa Huwa — the 'Aziz — the Almighty — al-Hakim — the All-Wise. The verse's frame: the repeated declaration: the Oneness with the power and the wisdom: the witness's seal.

The verse's lesson: the Oneness is the witnessed of the Lord, the angels, and the learned — the justice's Maintainer is the Almighty, the All-Wise. And the reader's part: bear the witness with them — the Lord, the angels, and the people of knowledge testify — and be among the knowledge's holders of the testimony.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 19

Verse 19 says: *Certainly, Allah’s only Way is Islam. Those who were given the Scripture did not dispute ˹among themselves˺ out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them. Whoever denies Allah’s signs, then surely Allah is swift in reckoning.*

In simple terms, the verse declares: the religion with Allah is Islam; the People of the Scripture did not dispute out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them; and whoever denies the signs — Allah is swift in reckoning.

"Certainly, Allah's only Way is Islam" — the verse's religion: the inna al-din 'inda Allah al-Islam — the religion with Allah is Islam. Ibn Kathir: Allah said, asserting that the only religion accepted with Him is Islam — (and the cross-reference 3:85: whoever seeks a religion other than Islam, it will never be accepted of him). Ma'arif (and Tanwir): the religion that is accepted with Allah is the surrender — the Islam — the submission to His will and guidance. The verse's frame: the accepted religion: the Islam — the submission: the way of the prophets before: the surrender to the One: the religion of the truth.

"Those who were given the Scripture did not dispute ˹among themselves˺ out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them" — the verse's dispute: the ma ikhtalafa alladhina utu al-Kitab — those given the Scripture did not differ — the illa min ba'di ma ja'ahum al-'ilm — except after knowledge came to them — the baghyan baynahum — out of mutual envy, the transgression among them. Ibn Kathir: some of them wronged others — the differences came after the knowledge: the envy's dispute: the division of the People of the Book was not the ignorance's — it was the knowledge's and the envy's. The verse's frame: the division's cause: knowledge came and the envy followed: the dispute of the informed against the truth: the enmity made the sects.

"Whoever denies Allah's signs, then surely Allah is swift in reckoning" — the verse's close: the man yakfur bi ayat Allah — whoever denies the signs of Allah — the fa inna Allah sari' al-hisab — Allah is swift in reckoning. Ibn Kathir: the denial of the signs — the reckoning's swiftness: the One who gave the knowledge accounts the denial: the swift is the immediate's judgment. The verse's frame: the denier's account: the signs' denial is the swift reckoning's subject.

The verse's lesson: the religion with Allah is the surrender — the disputes after the knowledge are the envy's — and the denial is the swift reckoning's. And the reader's part: the submission is the way; the dispute is the envy's; and the signs' denial is the swift account.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 20

Verse 20 says: *So if they argue with you ˹O Prophet˺, say, “I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers.” And ask those who were given the Scripture and the illiterate ˹people˺, “Have you submitted yourselves ˹to Allah˺?” If they submit, they will be ˹rightly˺ guided. But if they turn away, then your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺. And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants.*

In simple terms, the verse gives the answer to the arguers: I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers; ask the People of the Scripture and the illiterate — have you submitted? If they submit, they are guided; if they turn away, your duty is only the delivery — and Allah sees the servants.

"So if they argue with you ˹O Prophet˺, say, "I have submitted myself to Allah, and so have my followers."" — the verse's answer: the fa in hajjuka — so if they dispute with you — the qul aslamtu wajhi li Allah — say: I have submitted my face to Allah — the wa mani ittaba'ani — and those who follow me. Ibn Kathir: "So if they dispute with you say: 'I have submitted myself to Allah, and (so have) those who follow me'" — the answer to the disputers: the submission's declaration: the face surrendered to the Lord: the Prophet's answer is the surrender, not the debate's prolongation. The verse's frame: the submission's proof: the argument is answered by the deed: I and my followers have submitted: the answer is the life, not the rhetoric.

"And ask those who were given the Scripture and the illiterate ˹people˺, "Have you submitted yourselves ˹to Allah˺?"" — the verse's question: the wa qul li alladhina utu al-Kitab — say to those given the Scripture — the wa al-ummiyyin — and the illiterate, the unlettered (the Arab pagans) — the a-aslamtum — have you submitted? Ibn Kathir: say to those given the Scripture (Jews and Christians) and to those who are illiterates (Arab pagans): "Do you (also) submit yourselves?" — the question to both: the People of the Book and the pagans: the one call: the submission.

"If they submit, they will be ˹rightly˺ guided" — the verse's guidance: the fa in aslamu fa qadi ihtadaw — if they submit, they have been guided. Ibn Kathir: if they do, they are rightly guided. The verse's frame: the submission's result: the surrender is the guidance's attainment: the submitter is the guided.

"But if they turn away, then your duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺" — the verse's delivery: the fa in tawallaw fa innama 'alayka al-balagh — but if they turn away, your duty is only the conveying. Ibn Kathir: their reckoning is with Allah and their return and final destination is to Him — the messenger's duty is the message; the reckoning is the Lord's. The verse's frame: the duty's limit: the delivery, not the compulsion: the message's bearer is not the hearts' turner.

"And Allah is All-Seeing of ˹His˺ servants" — the verse's close: the wa Allah basir bi al-'ibad — Allah is All-Seeing of the servants. The verse's frame: the seeing of the submitters and the turners: the One who sees the servants' responses.

The verse's lesson: the answer to the arguers is the submission; the call to the People of the Book and the pagans is the same; the submitter is guided, the turner is the message's limit. And the reader's part: answer with the submission — have you submitted? — and deliver and leave the reckoning to the All-Seeing.

===VERSE-END===
