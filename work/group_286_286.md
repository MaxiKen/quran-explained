## Verse 286

Verse 286 says: *"Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford. All good will be for its own benefit, and all evil will be to its own loss. ˹The believers pray,˺ "Our Lord! Do not punish us if we forget or make a mistake. Our Lord! Do not place a burden on us like the one you placed on those before us. Our Lord! Do not burden us with what we cannot bear. Pardon us, forgive us, and have mercy on us. You are our ˹only˺ Guardian. So grant us victory over the disbelieving people.""*

In simple terms, the verse is the surah's close: Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity — the good is for it and the evil against it — and the believers' prayer: no punishment for forgetfulness or error; no burden like those before us; no burden we cannot bear; pardon, forgive, have mercy; You are our Guardian; grant us victory over the disbelieving people.

"Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford" — the verse's capacity: the la yukallifu — the does not require — the nafsan — any soul — the illa wus'aha — more than its capacity. Ibn Kathir: "Allah burdens not a person beyond his scope" — the verse's mercy: the requirement is the capacity's measure: the burden is the strength's limit. The verse's frame: the divine mercy's law: the command is the possible: the obligation is the affordable: the Lord's requirements are the soul's measure.

"All good will be for its own benefit, and all evil will be to its own loss" — the verse's earning: the laha ma kasabat — the good for it — the 'alayha ma iktasabat — the evil against it. Ibn Kathir: "He gets reward for that (good) which he has earned, and he is punished for that (evil) which he has earned" — the earning's personal accounting: the good's benefit and the evil's loss: the justice's personal. The verse's frame: the two accounts: the good's gain and the evil's burden: the earned: the personal responsibility.

"˹The believers pray,˺ "Our Lord! Do not punish us if we forget or make a mistake"" — the verse's first plea: the la tu'akhidhna — do not punish us — the in nasina — if we forget — the aw akhta'na — or made a mistake. Ibn Kathir (the hadith in Muslim): the believers said — "Our Lord! Do not punish us if we forget or err" — and Allah replied — "I have done so" — the answered prayer: the forgiveness of the forgetfulness and the error. The verse's frame: the mercy for the lapse: the forgetfulness and the mistake are not the rebellion: the Lord's answer: the pardon of the unintentional.

"Our Lord! Do not place a burden on us like the one you placed on those before us" — the verse's second plea: the la tahmil 'alayna — do not place a burden on us — the kama hamaltahu 'ala alladhina min qablina — like the one You placed on those before us. Ibn Kathir: the burdens of the Children of Israel — the heavy obligations the previous communities bore — the believers ask for the lighter path. The verse's frame: the previous burdens: the communities before had the heavier loads: the believers' plea: the mercy of the ummah: the easy of this legislation.

"Our Lord! Do not burden us with what we cannot bear" — the verse's third plea: the la tuhammilna — do not burden us — the ma la taqata lana bihi — what we cannot bear. Ibn Kathir: the believers asked — and Allah answered — "I have done so" — the mercy: the not-burdening beyond the capacity: the answer of the Lord. The verse's frame: the answered capacity: the verse of the capacity is the answer to the plea: the recitation of the prayer is the answer's reception.

"Pardon us, forgive us, and have mercy on us" — the verse's triad: the u'fu — the pardon — the ighfir — the forgive — the irham — the have mercy. Ibn Kathir: the three pleas answered — the pardon, the forgiveness, the mercy — the believers' triple request and the Lord's triple grant. The verse's frame: the mercy's completeness: the pardon of the errors, the forgiveness of the sins, the mercy of the hearts: the three mercies.

"You are our ˹only˺ Guardian" — the verse's guardianship: the anta mawlana — You are our Guardian. The verse's frame: the singular reliance: the Guardian of the believers (2:257): the one Protector: the helper and the watcher: the believers' wali.

"So grant us victory over the disbelieving people" — the verse's close: the fansurna — the grant us victory — the 'ala al-qawm al-kafirin — over the disbelieving people. The verse's frame: the surah's end: the believers' prayer of the victory: the same people of the earlier stories (2:249–251: the victory of the few) — the prayer of the final verse: the grant of the aid.

The verse's place: the surah's final verse. Ibn Kathir (the virtue of the last two verses): the hadith (al-Bukhari, Muslim, from Abu Mas'ud al-Ansari): whoever recites the last two verses of Surah Al-Baqarah at night, they will suffice him — the two verses (2:285–286) are the surah's seal: the creed and the prayer. The verse's frame: the night's sufficiency: the two verses of the faith and the mercy: the protection of the recitation.

The verse's lesson: the capacity's mercy; the personal earning; the answered prayers; the believers' reliance on the Guardian and the victory's request. And the reader's part: Allah does not burden beyond the capacity — the good is earned and the evil is borne — pray as the believers prayed: the pardon, the forgiveness, the mercy — and the Guardian's victory is the final request.
