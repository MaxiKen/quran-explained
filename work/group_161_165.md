## Verse 161

Verse 161 says: *It is not appropriate for a prophet to illegally withhold spoils of war. And whoever does so, it will be held against them on the Day of Judgment. Then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged.*

In simple terms, the verse clears the prophets of the violation of the trust: a prophet does not unlawfully take from the spoils of war; whoever does so will bear it on the Day of Judgment — then every soul will be repaid in full for what it did, and none will be wronged.

The verse turns from the shura and the trust to the doctrine of the *ghulul* — the misappropriation of the spoils. Ibn Kathir: "It is not for any Prophet to illegally take a part of the booty, and whosoever deceives his companions over the booty, he shall bring forth on the Day of Resurrection that which he took." The verse's *wa ma kana li-nabiyyin an yaghulla* — and it is not for a Prophet to misappropriate — is the exoneration; the *wa man yaghlul ya'ti bima ghalla yawma al-qiyamah* — and whoever misappropriates will come with what he misappropriated on the Day of Resurrection — is the recompense; and the *thumma tuwaffa kullu nafsin ma kasabat wa hum la yuzlamun* — then every soul will be paid in full for what it earned and they will not be wronged — is the justice.

"It is not appropriate for a prophet to illegally withhold spoils of war" — *wa ma kana li-nabiyyin an yaghulla* — and it is not for a Prophet to commit ghulul, to breach the trust. The *ghulul* — the misappropriation, the treachery with the spoils — is the violation: the *yaghulla* — he misappropriates — the *ghulul* — the taking of the trust. Ibn Kathir: Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid and Al-Hasan — "It is not for a Prophet to breach the trust." The verse's *ma kana li-nabiyyin* — it is not for a Prophet — is the categorical: the prophecy is incompatible with the *ghulul*; the *li-nabiyyin* — for a Prophet — the absolute of the office — the *ghulul* is the breach of the prophecy's character. Ibn Jarir/Ibn 'Abbas: the verse was revealed in connection with a red robe missing from the spoils of Badr — some said the Messenger might have taken it; "This Ayah exonerates the Messenger of Allah of all types of deceit and treachery, be it returning what was entrusted with him, dividing the spoils of war, etc." (Ibn Kathir).

"And whoever does so, it will be held against them on the Day of Judgment" — *wa man yaghlul ya'ti bima ghalla yawma al-qiyamah* — and whoever misappropriates will come with what he misappropriated on the Day of Resurrection. The *yaghlul* — misappropriates — and the *ya'ti bima ghalla* — will come with what he took — the *ghulul* carried: the misappropriated thing produced on the Day — the *ya'ti'* — the coming — with the *ghalla* — the taken — the burden of the Day. Ibn Kathir: the verse "contains a stern warning and threat against Ghulul" — and the hadiths that prohibit such practice. The verse's *yawma al-qiyamah* — the Day of Resurrection — is the disclosure: the *ghulul* is brought to the Day — the misappropriated wealth, the breached trust — the *ya'ti* — the coming with — the burden of the account.

"Then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged" — *thumma tuwaffa kullu nafsin ma kasabat wa hum la yuzlamun* — then every soul will be paid in full for what it earned, and they will not be wronged. The *tawfi'* — the full payment — the *kull nafs* — every soul — the *ma kasabat* — what it earned — the *waf* — the complete; the *la yuzlamun* — they will not be wronged — the justice. Ibn Kathir: "Then every person shall be paid in full what he has earned, and they shall not be dealt with unjustly." The verse's *tuwaffa* — is paid in full — is the exactness: the account is the full — the *kasb* — the earning — the *tawfi'* — the complete payment; and the *la yuzlamun* — not wronged — is the justice of the accounting: no soul is wronged — the *yaghull* — the misappropriator — brought with the *ghalla* — and the *tawfi'* — the full — the justice of the Day.

The hadiths of the warning, per Ibn Kathir: Imam Ahmad/Abu Malik Al-Ashja'i — "The worst Ghulul with Allah is a yard of land... when you find two neighbors in a land or home and one of them illegally acquires a yard of his neighbor's land. When he does, he will be tied with it from the seven earths until the Day of Resurrection." Imam Ahmad/Abu Humayd As-Sa'idi — the Zakah collector Ibn Al-Lutbiyyah returned and said, "This (portion) is for you and this has been given to me as a gift" — the Prophet stood on the Minbar: "What is the matter with a man whom we appoint to collect Zakah... Why hadn't he stayed in his father's or mother's house to see whether he would be given presents or not? By Him in Whose Hand my life is, whoever takes anything from the resources of the Zakah (unlawfully), he will carry it on his neck on the Day of Resurrection; if it be a camel, it will be grunting; if a cow, it will be mooing; and if a sheep, it will be bleating." — and raised his hands till the whiteness of his armpits, saying three times, "O Allah! Haven't I conveyed Your Message."

For the reader, the verse is the exoneration of the prophets and the warning of the trust. The believer is taught: the *ghulul* — the misappropriation — is the breach of the prophecy's character: the *ma kana li-nabiyyin* — not for a Prophet — the categorical of the office; and the *yaghlul* — whoever misappropriates — the *ya'ti bima ghalla* — the coming with the taken — the burden of the Day. The verse's *tuwaffa kull nafs* — the full payment of every soul — is the justice: the *kasb* — the earned — the *tawfi'* — the complete — and the *la yuzlamun* — the not-wronged — the justice of the account. And the *ghulal* — the misappropriated — the *ya'ti* — the coming with — the recompense of the breach. The believer's program: the *ghulul* of the *ghunaim* — the misappropriation of the spoils — avoided; the *wafa'* — the fulfillment — of the trust — the *amanah* — the trust — of the wealth — the *tawfi'* — the full payment of every soul — the justice of the Day — the *la yuzlamun* — the not-wronged.

## Verse 162

Verse 162 says: *Are those who seek Allah’s pleasure like those who deserve Allah’s wrath? Hell is their home. What an evil destination!*

In simple terms, the verse poses the rhetorical contrast: are those who seek Allah's pleasure like those who earn His wrath? Hell is their home — what an evil destination.

The verse is the contrast of the two groups. Ibn Kathir: "Is then one who follows (seeks) the pleasure of Allah like the one who draws on himself the wrath of Allah? His abode is Hell, and worse indeed is that destination." The verse's *afamani ittaba'a ridwana Allahi kaman ba'a bi sakhatin mina Allahi* — is he who follows the pleasure of Allah like he who drew the anger of Allah — is the question; the *wa ma'wahu Jahannam* — and his abode is Hell — is the recompense; and the *wa bi'sa al-masir* — and evil is the destination — is the condemnation.

"Are those who seek Allah's pleasure like those who deserve Allah's wrath?" — *afamani ittaba'a ridwana Allahi kaman ba'a bi sakhatin mina Allahi* — then is he who follows the pleasure of Allah like he who drew (bought) the wrath of Allah? The *rida* — the pleasure — is the divine: the *ittaba'a ridwan Allah* — who follows the pleasure of Allah — the seeker of the divine pleasure; the *sakhat* — the wrath — is the divine: the *ba'a bi sakhatin* — who drew the wrath — the *ba'* — the selling, the purchasing — the *sakhat* — the anger — earned. The verse's *afaman... kaman* — is he who... like he who — is the contrast: the seeker of the pleasure and the earner of the wrath — are they equal? The *am* — the question — the denial of the equality: the two paths, the two ends — the *rida* and the *sakhat* — the pleasure and the wrath — not equal. The verse's *man* — he who — is the individual: the pursuer of the divine pleasure, the one of the *ridwan*; the *ba'a* — he drew — the one of the *sakhat* — the anger — the buyer of the wrath — the trade — the *ba'* — the exchange — of the pleasure for the wrath.

"Hell is their home" — *wa ma'wahu Jahannam* — and his abode is Jahannam (Hell). The *ma'wa* — the abode, the home — is the recompense: the *Jahannam* — the Hell — the abode of the one of the wrath — the *ma'wah* — the home — the *masir* — the destination. Ibn Kathir: "His abode is Hell." The verse's *ma'wahu* — his abode — is the dwelling: the *sakhat* — the wrath — the *ma'wa* — the Hell — the home of the earner; the *Jahannam* — the Hell — the destination of the *ba'a* — the purchaser of the wrath.

"What an evil destination!" — *wa bi'sa al-masir* — and evil is the destination. The *bi'sa* — how evil — is the condemnation: the *masir* — the destination, the journey's end — the evil — of the wrath's dweller. Ibn Kathir: "and worse indeed is that destination." The verse's *bi'sa* — how evil — is the verdict: the *masir* — the ending — of the one of the anger — the Hell — the evil destination.

The verse's place: the contrast of the two paths after the *ghulul* (161) — the *ridwan* of the faithful and the *sakhat* of the faithless — the two, not equal. Ibn Kathir's context: the section of the *ghulul* and the grades (161–163) — the Prophet's exoneration, the contrast of the two ends, and the degrees of the people. The verse's *afaman* — the question — is the denial: the seeker of the pleasure is not like the earner of the wrath; the two are the contrast — the *ridwan* and the *sakhat* — the Pleasure and the Wrath — the two destinations: the Grace and the Hell.

For the reader, the verse is the contrast of the two objectives. The believer is taught: the *ittaba'a ridwan Allah* — the follower of Allah's pleasure — and the *ba'a bi sakhat* — the one who drew the wrath — are not equal: the *am* — the question — the denial of the equality; the *ridwan* — the pleasure — and the *sakhat* — the wrath — the two objectives of the life: the pursuit of the divine pleasure and the earning of the divine anger. And the *ma'wa* — the abode — the Hell of the wrath — and the *bi'sa al-masir* — the evil destination — the recompense of the earner. The believer's program: the *ridwan* — the pleasure — the objective of the faithful: the *ittaba'* — the pursuit — of the divine pleasure — the path of the pleasure over the path of the wrath; and the *masir* — the destination — the avoidance of the evil ending — the *Jahannam* — the abode of the wrath — the *bi'sa* — the evil — of the destiny.

## Verse 163

Verse 163 says: *They ˹each˺ have varying degrees in the sight of Allah. And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do.*

In simple terms, the verse states the degrees: they have varying ranks with Allah — and He is All-Seeing of what they do.

The verse is the grading of the two paths. Ibn Kathir: "They are in varying grades with Allah, and Allah is All-Seer of what they do." The verse's *hum darajatun 'inda Allahi* — they are degrees with Allah — is the grading; and the *wa Allahu basirun bima ya'malun* — and Allah is All-Seer of what they do — is the seeing.

"They ˹each˺ have varying degrees in the sight of Allah" — *hum darajatun 'inda Allahi* — they are (various) degrees with Allah. The *darajat* — the degrees, the ranks — are the grading: the *hum darajatun* — they are degrees — the varying — the *'inda Allah* — with Allah — the divine grading. Ibn Kathir: the degrees of the reward and the punishment according to the deeds — 162's contrast and 163's grading: the *ridwan* and the *sakhat* — the pleasure and the wrath — and the *darajat* — the degrees — of the two paths. The verse's *darajatun* — degrees — is the multiple: not the single verdict but the graded — the *darajat* — the levels — the *'inda Allah* — the divine scale — the different ranks of the doers. The verse's *hum* — they — is the two groups: the seekers of the pleasure and the earners of the wrath — each with the varying degrees — the *darajat* of the two paths.

"And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do" — *wa Allahu basirun bima ya'malun* — and Allah is All-Seer of what they do. The *basir* — the All-Seeing — is the vision: the *bima ya'malun* — of what they do — the deeds — seen; the *basirun* — the All-Seer — the divine vision of the degrees' deeds. Ibn Kathir: "Allah is All-Seer of what they do." The verse's *ya'malun* — they do — is the deeds: the *ridwan* and the *sakhat* — the pleasures and the angers — the *darajat* — the degrees — the *basir* — the seeing — the divine vision of the works — the basis of the grading.

The verse's place: the contrast (162) and the grading (163) — the two paths' ends and the two paths' degrees. Ibn Kathir's frame: the section of the *ghulul* and the warning (161), the contrast of the two (162), and the degrees of the doers (163) — the *darajat* — the ranks — with Allah — the *basir* — the seeing — the justice of the grading.

For the reader, the verse is the grading of the deeds and the divine vision. The believer is taught: the *darajat* — the degrees — with Allah — the varying ranks: not the single verdict but the graded — the *darajat* — the levels — of the two paths; the *'inda Allah* — with Allah — the divine grading; and the *basir* — the All-Seeing — the vision of the deeds — the basis of the degrees. The verse's *ya'malun* — they do — is the works: each deed seen, each degree assigned — the *ridwan* and the *sakhat* — the pleasures and the angers — graded. ## Verse 164

Verse 164 says: *Indeed, Allah has done the believers a ˹great˺ favour by raising a messenger from among them—reciting to them His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom. For indeed they had previously been clearly astray.*

In simple terms, the verse states the great favor: Allah favored the believers by sending a messenger from among them — reciting His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom — for they had been in clear error before.

The verse is the favor of the messengership. Ibn Kathir: "Indeed, Allah conferred a great favor on the believers when He sent among them a Messenger from among themselves, reciting unto them His verses (the Qur'an), and purifying them, and instructing them (in) the Book (the Qur'an) and Al-Hikmah (the wisdom and the Sunnah), while before that they had been in manifest error." The verse's *laqad manna Allahu 'ala al-mu'minina idh ba'atha fihim rasulan min anfusihim* — and Allah has certainly conferred favor on the believers when He sent among them a Messenger from among themselves — is the favor; the *yatlu 'alayhim ayatihi* — reciting to them His verses — is the tilawah; the *wa yuzakkihim* — and purifying them — is the tazkiyah; the *wa yu'allimuhumu al-kitaba wa al-hikmah* — and teaching them the Book and the wisdom — is the teaching; and the *wa in kanu min qablu lafi dalalin mubin* — and before that they were in manifest error — is the prior state.

"Allah has done the believers a ˹great˺ favour by raising a messenger from among them" — *laqad manna Allahu 'ala al-mu'minina idh ba'atha fihim rasulan min anfusihim* — and Allah has certainly been gracious to the believers when He sent among them a Messenger from among themselves. The *mann* — the favor, the grace — is the divine: the *ba'atha* — He sent — the *min anfusihim* — from among themselves — the *rasul* — the Messenger — from the believers' own: the Arab from the Arabs — the familiar, the kin. Al-Jalalayn: "a messenger from among their own, that is to say an Arab like them, not an angel or a non-Arab, so that they can understand what he says and feel honoured thereby." The verse's *manna* — He favored — is the grace: the *min anfusihim* — from themselves — the intimacy — the *rasul* — the messenger — the favor of the familiar — the *mann* — the favor of Allah — the greatest.

"reciting to them His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom" — *yatlu 'alayhim ayatihi wa yuzakkihim wa yu'allimuhumu al-kitaba wa al-hikmah* — reciting to them His verses, purifying them, teaching them the Book and wisdom. The *tilawah* — the recitation — is the first: the *ayat* — the verses — recited; the *tazkiyah* — the purification — is the second: Al-Jalalayn — "to cleanse them of sins"; the *ta'lim* — the teaching — is the third: the *kitab* — the Book — the Qur'an — and the *hikmah* — the wisdom — the Sunnah (Ibn Kathir). The verse's three functions — the *yatlu*, the *yuzakki*, the *yu'allim* — the recitation, the purification, the teaching — are the message's work: the verses recited, the souls purified, the Book and the wisdom taught — the *mann* — the favor — of the messenger's functions.

"For indeed they had previously been clearly astray" — *wa in kanu min qablu lafi dalalin mubin* — and before that they had certainly been in manifest error. The *dalal* — the error, the straying — is the prior: the *mubin* — the manifest, the clear — the *dalal* — the error — of the before — the *min qablu* — before — the prior state. Al-Jalalayn: "though they... before that — before he was sent — they were in clear error." The verse's *kanu* — they were — is the prior: the *dalal mubin* — the manifest error — the pre-message state — the *mann* — the favor — the rescue from the *dalal* — the *mubin* — the clear — the error of the before. Ibn Kathir's parallel: 9:128 — "Verily, there has come unto you a Messenger from among yourselves. It grieves him that you should receive any injury or difficulty. He is anxious over you..." — the *mann* — the favor — the *rasul* — the messenger — the *dalal* — the error — rescued.

The verse's place: the favor of the messengership after the mercy of the consultation (159) — the *rahmat* of 159 and the *mann* of 164 — the mercy of the prophet's conduct and the favor of the prophet's mission. Ibn Kathir's section: "Among the Qualities of Our Prophet Muhammad are Mercy and Kindness" — the *lin* and the *rahmah* (159) — the favor of the *ba'ath* (164) — the qualities of the Prophet — the mercy and the function.

For the reader, the verse is the favor of the messengership and the functions of the message. The believer is taught: the *mann* — the favor — the *rasul min anfusihim* — the messenger from themselves — the intimacy of the mission: the familiar, the kin — the Arab from the Arabs — the understanding and the honor (Al-Jalalayn); and the functions — the *yatlu* — the recitation of the verses; the *yuzakki* — the purification of the sins; the *yu'allim* — the teaching of the Book and the wisdom — the Qur'an and the Sunnah. And the *dalal mubin* — the manifest error — the prior — the favor's rescue: the *kanu min qablu* — they were before — in the error — and the *ba'ath* — the sending — the rescue. The believer's response: the *mann* of Allah — the favor of the messenger from themselves — the *tilawah* and the *tazkiyah* and the *ta'lim* — the recitation, the purification, the teaching — the message's three functions — the gratitude of the favor and the adherence of the message.

## Verse 165

Verse 165 says: *Why is it when you suffered casualties ˹at Uḥud˺—although you had made your enemy suffer twice as much ˹at Badr˺—you protested, “How could this be?”? Say, ˹O Prophet˺, “It is because of your disobedience.” Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything.*

In simple terms, the verse rebukes the believers' protest: when you suffered at Uhud — although you had made the enemy suffer twice as much at Badr — you asked, "how could this be?" Say: it is from yourselves, your disobedience — and Allah is Most Capable of everything.

The verse is the rebuke of the protest and the doctrine of the cause. Ibn Kathir: "When a single disaster smites you, although you smote (your enemies) with one twice as great, you say: 'From where does this come to us?' Say, 'It is from yourselves.' And Allah has power over all things." The verse's *awalama asabatkum musibatun qad asabtum mithlayha* — and when a disaster struck you, having struck them with twice the like — is the proportional; the *qultum anna hadha* — you said, from where is this — is the protest; the *qul huwa min 'indi anfusikum* — say, it is from yourselves — is the cause; and the *inna Allaha 'ala kulli shay'in qadir* — and Allah is over all things capable — is the power.

"Why is it when you suffered casualties ˹at Uḥud˺—although you had made your enemy suffer twice as much ˹at Badr˺—you protested, 'How could this be?'?" — *awalama asabatkum musibatun qad asabtum mithlayha qultum anna hadha* — and when a disaster struck you, having struck the like of it twice, you said: from where is this? The *musibah* — the disaster — is the Uhud loss: the seventy fatalities; the *mithlayha* — twice the like — is the Badr blow: the seventy killed and the seventy captured. Al-Jalalayn: "when distress befell you at Uhud when seventy of you were slain and you had afflicted twice the like of it at Badr, slaying seventy of them and taking another seventy captive — did you say in amazement, 'How is this?' — that is, how did this defeat happen to us when we are Muslims and God's Messenger is among us?" The verse's *anna hadha* — from where is this — is the protest: the amazement of the loss — the *anna* — the whence — the question of the defeat's cause. Ibn Kathir: "Why did this defeat happen to us?" The verse's *mithlayha* — twice the like — is the proportion: the loss of Uhud and the twice of Badr — the scale of the comparison — the believers' question at the scale.

"Say, ˹O Prophet˺, 'It is because of your disobedience.'" — *qul huwa min 'indi anfusikum* — say: it is from yourselves, from your own. The *min 'indi anfusikum* — from yourselves, from your own selves — is the cause: the *'indi* — from — the self — the source of the loss — the *anfusikum* — your selves. Ibn Kathir: Ibn Abi Hatim recorded 'Umar bin Al-Khattab — "When Uhud occurred, a year after Badr, Muslims were punished for taking ransom from the disbelievers at Badr in return for releasing the Mushriks whom they captured in that battle. Thus, they suffered the loss of seventy fatalities and the Companions of the Messenger of Allah gave flight and abandoned him. The Messenger suffered a broken tooth, the helmet was smashed on his head and blood flowed onto his face." And Muhammad bin Ishaq, Ibn Jurayj, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas and As-Suddi: the Ayah means "because you, the archers, disobeyed the Messenger's command to not abandon your positions." The verse's *min 'indi anfusikum* — from yourselves — is the answer: the loss was the self — the ransom of Badr, the disobedience of the archers — the *anfus* — the selves — the cause. Al-Jalalayn: "because you abandoned your battle stations and were thus defeated... He requited you for your disputing the Prophet's command."

"Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything" — *inna Allaha 'ala kulli shay'in qadir* — and Allah is over all things capable. The *qadir* — the capable, the powerful — is the divine: the *'ala kulli shay'* — over every thing — the power — the *qadir* — the capable. Ibn Kathir: "He does what He wills and decides what He wills, and there is none who can resist His decision." Al-Jalalayn: "including the giving of assistance and the withholding of it." The verse's *qadir* — the capable — is the close: the power — the assistance and the withholding — the *qadir* — the divine capability — the requital of the dispute.

The verse's context: the *musibah* of Uhud — the *mithlayha* of Badr — the question of the cause — and the answer: the *anfusikum* — the selves. Ibn Kathir's frame: "The Reason and Wisdom Behind the Defeat at Uhud" — the seventy of Uhud, the twice of Badr, the *'Umar* report of the ransom, and the archers' disobedience — the *min 'indi anfusikum* — the self's cause.

For the reader, the verse is the rebuke of the protest and the doctrine of the self's cause. The believer is taught: the *musibah* of Uhud — the seventy — and the *mithlayha* of Badr — the twice — the proportion: the loss and the double — the scale; and the *anna hadha* — from where is this — the protest — the amazement of the believers at their loss — and the answer: the *min 'indi anfusikum* — from yourselves — the self's cause: the ransom of Badr (''Umar), the archers' disobedience (the scholars) — the *anfus* — the selves — the source. And the *qadir* — the capable — the divine power: the assistance and the withholding — the *qadir* — over all things. The believer's lesson: the loss was the self's — not the divine's injustice but the human's disobedience; the *anna* — the whence — the protest — answered by the *min 'indi anfusikum* — the self's cause; and the *qadir* — the capable — the divine power — the justice of the requital. The believer's program: the *ta'ah* — the obedience — the avoidance of the *fashal* and the *'isyan* — the courage and the command — the self not the cause of the loss.
