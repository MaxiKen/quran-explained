## Verse 236

Verse 236 says: *"There is no blame if you divorce women before the marriage is consummated or the dowry is settled. But give them a ˹suitable˺ compensation—the rich according to his means and the poor according to his. A reasonable compensation is an obligation on the good-doers."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the divorce before consummation or dowry: there is no blame if you divorce women before the marriage is consummated or the dowry is settled. But give them a suitable compensation — the rich according to his means and the poor according to his. A reasonable compensation is an obligation on the good-doers.

"There is no blame if you divorce women before the marriage is consummated or the dowry is settled" — the verse's case: the divorce before the consummation and before the dowry's settlement: the marriage was contracted, the dowry was not fixed, and the marriage was not consummated — and the divorce: no sin, no half-dowry (there is none to give). Ibn Kathir: the verse's no blame — the divorce in this case is permitted and the dowry's half is not due because no dowry was appointed. Al-Jalalayn: the verse's no sin — if you divorce them before you touch them or appoint a dowry. The verse's frame: the marriage's end before the consummation — the fault is not automatically the sin; the divorce in the early stage is the permitted.

"But give them a suitable compensation — the rich according to his means and the poor according to his" — the verse's *matti'unna* — the compensation: the *mut'ah* — the gift of the farewell: the husband gives the divorced woman a suitable gift — the rich by his measure, the poor by his measure. Ibn Kathir: the compensation — the suitable gift of the parting — the rich according to his means, the poor according to his — the standard is the capacity: not the fixed amount but the fair measure of the able. The verse's frame: the divorce is the end, but the parting is the gift: the woman is not dismissed without the token of the honor.

"A reasonable compensation is an obligation on the good-doers" — the verse's *haqqan 'ala al-muhsinin* — an obligation upon the good-doers: the compensation is the duty of the good — the *muhsinin* — the ones who do the ihsan (the excellence, the kindness). Ibn Kathir: the verse's obligation — the gift is a right due on the good-doers; the one who does the kindness in the parting — the compensation — is the one who is the muhsin. The verse's frame: the law's minimum (the compensation is the duty) and the excellence's standard (the good-doers' honor).

In modern life, the verse is the law of the honorable parting. The modern breakup is often the cold: the dismissal, the silent end, the "goodbye" without the dignity. The verse says: the end can be the honor: the compensation — the gift of the farewell — the rich by the ability, the poor by the ability; and the obligation is on the good-doers — the kindness of the parting is the mark of the good. The verse's message: the divorce's honor is not only the letter of the law but the gift of the grace: the woman is not left with nothing and no word; the ending has the dignity — the compensation, the kindness, the good.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 237

Verse 237 says: *"And if you divorce them before consummating the marriage but after deciding on a dowry, pay half of the dowry, unless the wife graciously waives it or the husband graciously pays in full. Graciousness is closer to righteousness. And do not forget kindness among yourselves. Surely Allah is All-Seeing of what you do."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the half-dowry: when the divorce comes before the consummation but after the dowry was fixed, half is due, unless the wife waives or the husband pays the whole — the forgiveness is nearer to the righteousness; the kindness is not forgotten.

"And if you divorce them before consummating the marriage but after deciding on a dowry, pay half of the dowry" — the verse's case: the dowry was fixed and the marriage was not consummated — and the divorce: the half of the fixed dowry. Ibn Kathir: this honorable ayah requires the husband to relinquish half of the appointed mahr if he divorces his wife before the marriage is consummated — the giving away of half of the bridal-money in this case is the agreed practice according to the scholars. The verse's *nisf ma faradtum* — the half of what you appointed: the fixed dowry and the half — the divorce's cost: the husband pays the half, the wife's right of the half.

"Unless the wife graciously waives it or the husband graciously pays in full" — the verse's two releases: the wife's forgiveness (the waiver — she forgives the half, or the whole) or the husband's forgiveness (the payment in full — the one in whose hand is the marriage knot pays the whole). Ibn Kathir: the wife may remit, and the husband may pay the full. The verse's phrase — the one in whose hand is the marriage tie, per the hadith (Ibn Marduwyah, chosen by Ibn Jarir): the husband is the one who holds the marriage tie; the wife's wali is not allowed to give away any of her rightful dues without her permission, especially the dowry.

"Graciousness is closer to righteousness" — the verse's *an ta'fuq aqrab li al-taqwa* — that you forgive (the waiver, the full payment) is nearer to the taqwa: the forgiveness — the wife's waiver, the husband's full payment — is nearer to the righteousness. Ibn Kathir (ash-Sha'bi and several scholars): the one who forgives is nearer to the taqwa. Ma'arif: the forgiveness symbolically indicates that the severance of the bond of the marriage was also done with magnanimity and good grace — the objective of the Shari'ah — the forgiveness could come from the woman or the man. The verse's scale: the law's minimum (the half) and the excellence (the waiver/full) — the forgiveness is nearer to the righteousness.

"And do not forget kindness among yourselves" — the verse's *la tansaw al-fadl baynakum* — do not forget the favor, the kindness, the generosity among yourselves: the marriage's end is not the end of the decency; the couple's parting keeps the memory of the kindness. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *fadl* — the generosity, the kindness — do not forget it between you; the marriage's kindness remains in the parting.

"Surely Allah is All-Seeing of what you do" — the verse's close: the *Basar* — the All-Seeing — the Lord sees the parting's generosity, the waiver, the full payment, the kindness, the greed. The verse's frame: the divorce's settlement is before the All-Seeing — the one who sees the forgiveness and the one who sees the withholding.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the fair ending. The modern separation often fights over the money: the dowry's battle, the "take back", the greed's ledger. The verse's structure: the half — the law's fair minimum; the waiver — the wife's grace; the full — the husband's grace; the forgiveness nearer to the righteousness; and the kindness not forgotten. The verse's message: the divorce's money is settled by the law's fairness and the couple's grace — the woman's right is the half, and the grace above the half is the righteousness; the man's right is the half's return, and the full payment is the grace. The parting's kindness is not forgotten, and the All-Seeing sees the generosity and the greed of the settlement.

## Verse 238

Verse 238 says: *"Observe the ˹five obligatory˺ prayers—especially the middle prayer—and stand in true devotion to Allah."*

In simple terms, the verse commands the prayers: the five prayers are to be guarded, especially the middle prayer, and the standing before Allah is with the true devotion.

"Observe the five obligatory prayers" — the verse's *hafizu 'ala al-salawat* — guard, preserve, observe the prayers: the command of the preservation — the prayers' times, the prayers' conditions, the prayers' constancy. Ibn Kathir: the ayah commands the preservation of the prayers — the five daily prayers, the times, the perfection. The verse's *hifz* — the guarding — the prayer is not only the performance but the preservation: the watch of the time, the conditions, the quality.

"Especially the middle prayer" — the verse's *al-salat al-wusta* — the middle prayer. Ibn Kathir: at-Tirmidhi reported from Ibn Mas'ud — the Prophet said, "The 'Asr prayer is the middle prayer" (at-Tirmidhi: hasan sahih); Muslim reported from the hadith of the battle of the ditch (al-Khandaq): "They (disbelievers) busied us from performing the middle prayer, the 'Asr prayer"; and the authentic hadith (Ibn 'Umar): "Whoever misses the 'Asr prayer, it is as if he lost his family and his wealth." The verse's *wusta* — the middle — the commentators: the 'Asr (the majority, per the hadiths) — the middle of the five, and the emphasized. The verse's note: the middle prayer's emphasis — the prayer of the middle — the guarding of it is the sign of the constancy.

"And stand in true devotion to Allah" — the verse's *qum li Allah qanitin* — stand for Allah in obedience, in devotion, in humble obedience — the *qunut* — the standing of the devotion, the obedience, the humility. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *qanitin* — the obedient, the devoted, and (in some readings) the standing in the prayer. The verse's frame: the prayer's outward (the standing, the times) and the prayer's inward (the qunut — the devotion, the humility, the obedience) — the prayer is the standing before the Lord with the true devotion.

In modern life, the verse is the standard of the prayer's priority. The modern life is the schedule of the busy: the work, the meetings, the tasks — and the prayer is the first to be dropped. The verse says: guard the prayers — the watch of the times, the constancy of the schedule; especially the middle — the prayer of the middle's emphasis; and the stand with the true devotion — the quality of the presence, not only the motion of the body. The believer's life: the prayer is the anchor of the day — the five, and the middle, and the standing with the heart — the true devotion before the Lord. The verse's message: the prayer is the preserved; the middle is the emphasized; and the stand is the devotion — the believer's day is ordered by the prayer, not the prayer by the day.

## Verse 239

Verse 239 says: *"If you are in danger, pray on foot or while riding. But when you are safe, ˹take time to˺ remember Allah for teaching you what you did not know."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the prayer in the danger: the prayer is offered on foot or riding when the believer fears; when safe, the believer remembers Allah in the manner He taught.

"If you are in danger, pray on foot or while riding" — the verse's *fa-in khiftum fa-rijalan aw rukbanan* — if you fear, then on foot or riding: the prayer in the danger — the prayer is not abandoned in the fear. Ibn Kathir: after Allah commanded His servants to perform the prayer perfectly and emphasized this commandment, He mentioned the situation where the person might not be able to perform the prayer perfectly, during battle and combat — pray in the appropriate manner under these circumstances, whether on foot or riding and whether facing the qiblah or otherwise. Ibn Kathir (Imam Malik from Nafi' from Ibn 'Umar): if there is intense fear, pray on foot, riding, facing the qiblah and otherwise — the description of the fear prayer — al-Bukhari and Muslim. Al-Jalalayn (Ibn Abbas, Tanwir): the fear — the enemy, the torrent, the predatory animal — the prayer on foot or riding. The verse's point: the prayer is not suspended in the danger; the prayer is adapted: walking, riding, the direction, the posture — the prayer's core (the remembrance) continues.

"But when you are safe, take time to remember Allah for teaching you what you did not know" — the verse's *fa-idha amintum fa-udhkuru Allah kama 'allamakum ma lam takunu ta'lamun* — when you are secure, then remember Allah as He has taught you what you did not know: the return to the full prayer — the normal, the complete, the taught — at the safety. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *kama 'allamakum* — the manner He has taught you — the full prayer, the complete rites; the safety's return to the perfect prayer. The verse's frame: the danger's adaptation, the safety's completion — the prayer is the constant: adapted in the danger, perfected in the safety.

The verse's close — the gratitude: *kama 'allamakum ma lam takunu ta'lamun* — as He has taught you what you did not know: the prayer's knowledge, the manner, the rites — the teaching of the Lord — and the remembrance is the gratitude: the believer prays because the Lord taught; the believer remembers because the Lord taught the remembrance. The verse's frame: the prayer's knowledge is the divine gift: the one who did not know the prayer, the one who was astray — now knows — and the prayer is the gratitude of the teaching.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the faith in the emergency. The modern person says: "in the emergency I cannot pray, I cannot worship, I cannot think of the faith." The verse says: the danger is not the suspension — the prayer in the fear, on foot, riding; the faith's core survives the emergency. And the modern lesson: the safety is the completion: the return to the full worship when safe — the danger's reduced prayer is not the replacement but the adaptation; the safety's full prayer is the taught. The verse's message: the Lord taught you what you did not know — the prayer's knowledge is the gift — and the believer remembers: in the danger, the adapted prayer; in the safety, the complete prayer — the remembrance in both.

## Verse 240

Verse 240 says: *"Those of you who die leaving widows should bequeath for them a year's maintenance without forcing them out. But if they choose to leave, you are not accountable for what they reasonably decide for themselves. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise."*

In simple terms, the verse instructs the bequest for the widows: a year's maintenance and residence without the expulsion; if the widow leaves, the family is not accountable for her reasonable decisions — and Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.

"Those of you who die leaving widows should bequeath for them a year's maintenance without forcing them out" — the verse's bequest: the year's maintenance — the provision and the residence for the widow — without forcing her out: the commanding of the husband's will: the widow's year — the provision and the home. Ibn Kathir: the widow used to reside and have her provisions provided for her for a year, in her deceased husband's house. The verse's *wasiyyah* — the bequest — the command of the will: the dying husband's provision — the year's maintenance, the residence, the no-expulsion. The verse's frame: the widow's protection is the dying husband's command: the year's provision and the home's security.

"But if they choose to leave, you are not accountable for what they reasonably decide for themselves" — the verse's *fa-in kharajna fala junaha 'alaykum* — if they leave (of their own choice), there is no sin on you for what they do of themselves in the known good: the widow may leave (the residence), and the family is not blameworthy for her decision — the leave is the widow's choice; her lawful decisions at the departure are her own. The verse's frame: the widow's freedom: she may stay (the year's provision) or leave (her own course) — and the family's responsibility is only the wrongful expulsion, not the widow's own choice.

"And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise" — the verse's close: the Almighty, the All-Wise — the power and the wisdom of the legislation: the widow's protection, the year's provision, the fair balance. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *Aziz Hakim* — the Almighty, the All-Wise. The verse's frame: the widow's law is the law of the Almighty — the power behind the protection — and the All-Wise — the wisdom of the year's measure.

The verse's history — the abrogation: Ibn Kathir: the majority of the scholars said that this ayah was abrogated by the ayah 2:234 — the widow's iddah of four months and ten days — and by the inheritance verses (4:12): the widow inherits the quarter or the eighth; the ayah's year of the maintenance was abrogated, and the widow's residence (per the iddah's statement — Ibn Abbas via 'Ata) — the ayah (2:240) abrogated the requirement that the widow spends the iddah with the deceased's family — she spends it wherever she wants. Ibn Kathir questions — the famous exchange of Ibn al-Zubayr and 'Uthman about the abrogated ayah's presence in the Qur'an: "I shall not change any part of the Qur'an from its place." The verse's presence: the abrogated ruling's text remains: the Qur'an's record.

In modern life, the verse is the memory of the widow's protection. The modern widow often faces the sudden: the death, the loss, the home's uncertainty, the family's pressure. The verse's legacy: the widow was protected: the year's maintenance, the residence, the no-expulsion — then the law refined: the four months and ten days, the inheritance's share — the widow's rights. The verse's message: the death does not end the responsibility; the widow's dignity is the law's care — and the modern lesson: the widow's provision, the widow's choice, the widow's freedom — under the Almighty, the All-Wise, who legislates the protection of the widow, and the All-Wise who balances the rights.

===VERSE-END===
