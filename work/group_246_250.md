## Verse 246

Verse 246 says: *"Have you not seen those chiefs of the Children of Israel after Moses? They said to one of their prophets, "Appoint for us a king, ˹and˺ we will fight in the cause of Allah." He said, "Are you not going to cower if ordered to fight?" They replied, "How could we refuse to fight in the cause of Allah, while we were driven out of our homes and ˹separated from˺ our children?" But when they were ordered to fight, they fled, except for a few of them. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of the wrongdoers."*

In simple terms, the verse tells of the chiefs of the Children of Israel after Moses: they asked their prophet for a king to fight under; he warned them of the cowering; they protested with the loss of homes and children — but when the fighting was ordered, they fled except a few.

"Have you not seen those chiefs of the Children of Israel after Moses" — the verse's *mala'* — the chiefs, the council — the elite of the Children of Israel after Moses. Ibn Kathir: after Moses, the kingship ended among them, and the prophethood halted among their tribes — only a pregnant woman remained of the offspring of Levi, in whom the prophethood appeared; her husband had been killed; the Israelites kept her in a house so Allah might give her a boy, who would be their prophet — when he reached the age of prophethood, Allah revealed to him and commanded him to call his people to Him and to His Tawhid — that prophet is Shamwil (Samuel), as Al-Jalalayn and Tanwir specify. He called them, and they asked him to appoint a king so they could fight their enemies under his command.

"They said to one of their prophets, "Appoint for us a king, ˹and˺ we will fight in the cause of Allah"" — the verse's request: the king — to unite them — and the fight under his command. Ibn Kathir: they asked for a king — the kingship had also ended among them — to fight their enemies under his command.

"He said, "Are you not going to cower if ordered to fight?"" — the verse's warning: the prophet's question — the doubt of the obedience. Al-Jalalayn: the prophet said — are you going to cower if the fighting becomes obligatory? The verse's frame: the prophecy of the outcome: the ones who ask for the fight are the ones who will turn away.

"They replied, "How could we refuse to fight in the cause of Allah, while we were driven out of our homes and ˹separated from˺ our children?"" — the verse's protest: after our land was confiscated and our children were taken from us — the cause is justified by the loss. Ibn Kathir: "after our land had been confiscated and our children had been taken from us."

"But when they were ordered to fight, they fled, except for a few of them" — the verse's turn: the fighting was prescribed — they turned away, except a few — the few who crossed the river with Saul, as will be mentioned. Ibn Kathir: "when fighting was prescribed for them, they turned away — all except a few of them." Al-Jalalayn: the few — those who crossed the river with Saul (Talut). The verse's frame: the promise of the fight is easy; the fulfilment of the fight is the test.

"And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of the wrongdoers" — the verse's close: the knowledge of the wrongdoers — the ones who fled, who broke the pledge, who excused themselves. Ibn Kathir: Allah is the Knower of those who turned away — the evil of the words and the deed.

The verse's note: the lesson of the request — the cause is not championed by the ones who simply want the glory; the cause is championed by the ones who obey when ordered. And the reader's part: the brave promise is tested by the open order.

## Verse 247

Verse 247 says: *"Their prophet told them, "Allah has appointed Saul to be your king." They protested, "How can he be our king when some of us are more deserving of kingship than he, and he has not been blessed with vast riches?" He replied, "Allah has chosen him over you and blessed him with knowledge and stature. Allah grants kingship to whoever He wills. And Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing.""*

In simple terms, the verse tells of the appointment of Saul (Talut): the protest over his poverty and status; the reply — Allah chose him, blessed him with knowledge and stature, and grants kingship to whom He wills.

"Their prophet told them, "Allah has appointed Saul to be your king"" — the verse's appointment: Talut — Saul. Al-Jalalayn and Tanwir: the prophet — Samuel — the king — Talut (Saul). Ibn Kathir: Allah chose Talut as their king.

"They protested, "How can he be our king when some of us are more deserving of kingship than he, and he has not been blessed with vast riches?"" — the verse's protest: the worldly measures — the lineage, the wealth. Ibn Kathir: they protested about Talut — he was not of the royal house, and he had not been blessed with vast riches. The verse's frame: the protest of the criteria: the king must be the rich, the noble — the measures of the world.

"He replied, "Allah has chosen him over you and blessed him with knowledge and stature"" — the verse's answer: the chosen — knowledge and stature — the body and the mind, the wisdom and the strength. Ibn Kathir: Allah chose him over them — blessed him with knowledge (the knowledge of the religion, the Torah) and stature (the physical strength, the appearance). The verse's frame: the divine measure: not the wealth, but the knowledge and the stature — the wisdom and the strength.

"Allah grants kingship to whoever He wills" — the verse's sovereignty: the kingship is Allah's grant. The verse's frame: the rebellion against the appointment is the rebellion against the Giver; the criteria are the Giver's.

"And Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing" — the verse's close: the All-Bountiful, the All-Knowing — the bounty of the choice, the knowledge of the deserving. Ibn Kathir: Allah is the Bountiful — the One Who grants the kingdoms to whom He wills — the All-Knowing — of the ones who deserve.

The verse's lesson: the leadership is not the wealth; the leadership is the knowledge and the strength — and the Grant is Allah's. Maududi: the protest against Talut — the objection of the worldly standard — the answer: the divine standard. And the reader's part: the measure of the leader in the Qur'an is the knowledge and the stature, not the riches and the rank.

## Verse 248

Verse 248 says: *"Their prophet further told them, "The sign of Saul's kingship is that the Ark will come to you—containing reassurance from your Lord and relics of the family of Moses and the family of Aaron, which will be carried by the angels. Surely in this is a sign for you, if you ˹truly˺ believe.""*

In simple terms, the verse tells of the sign of Saul's kingship: the Ark returning with the reassurance from the Lord, the relics of the families of Moses and Aaron, carried by the angels — a sign for the believers.

"The sign of Saul's kingship is that the Ark will come to you" — the verse's sign: the Tabut — the Ark — the chest of the covenant. Ibn Kathir: the Ark — the sacred chest of the Children of Israel — it had been taken by their enemies; it contained a remnant of what Musa and Harun left behind. The verse's frame: the sign of the kingship is not the crown and the wealth; the sign is the return of the sacred trust.

"containing reassurance from your Lord" — the verse's *sakinah* — the tranquility, the reassurance. Ibn Kathir: Sakinah means mercy — (also grace, dignity — ar-Rabi' said mercy; Ibn 'Abbas via al-'Awfi: grace; Qatadah: dignity). The verse's frame: the reassurance from the Lord — the peace that settles the camp, the mercy that accompanies the Ark.

"and relics of the family of Moses and the family of Aaron" — the verse's *baqiyyah* — the remnant — the relics. Ibn Kathir: Moses' staff and the remnants of the Tablets — the tafsir of Qatadah, As-Suddi, ar-Rabi' ibn Anas, and 'Ikrimah — who added, "And also the Torah." 'Abdur-Razzaq asked ath-Thawri: some said it contained a pot of manna and the remnants of the Tablets; others said it contained Moses' staff and two shoes (cf. 20:12). The verse's frame: the legacy of Moses and Aaron — the staff, the Tablets, the Torah — the covenant's memory.

"which will be carried by the angels" — the verse's carriers: the angels. Ibn Kathir: the angels carried it — the sign of the divine act: the Ark that the enemy had taken was returned by the angels, and in its return the kingship's appointment was confirmed. The verse's frame: the sign above the worldly measure — the angels are the carriers of the confirmation.

"Surely in this is a sign for you, if you ˹truly˺ believe" — the verse's close: the sign for the believers. The verse's frame: the sign's power is in the heart of the beholder — for the believer, the return of the Ark is the proof; for the worldly, it is the strange event.

The verse's lesson: the divine appointment is confirmed by the divine sign, not the worldly measure. And the reader's part: the sign is clear to the one who truly believes — the leaders are chosen, and the heavens confirm.

## Verse 249

Verse 249 says: *"When Saul marched forth with his army, he cautioned: "Allah will test you with a river. So whoever drinks ˹his fill˺ from it is not with me, and whoever does not taste it—except a sip from the hollow of his hands—is definitely with me." They all drank ˹their fill˺ except for a few! When he and the ˹remaining˺ faithful with him crossed the river, they said, "Now we are no match for Goliath and his warriors." But those ˹believers˺ who were certain they would meet Allah reasoned, "How many times has a small force vanquished a mighty army by the Will of Allah! And Allah is ˹always˺ with the steadfast.""*

In simple terms, the verse tells of the test of the river: who drinks is not with Saul; who does not taste, except a sip from the hollow of the hand, is with him — all drank except a few — and the few, after crossing, reasoned that a small force has often vanquished a mighty army by Allah's will.

"Allah will test you with a river" — the verse's test. Ibn Kathir: Talut said — Allah will try you with a river — the river that flowed between Jordan and Palestine, the Shari'ah river, according to Ibn 'Abbas and others; Talut's army was eighty thousand, according to As-Suddi — but Allah knows best. The verse's frame: the march's test: the thirst and the command — the obedience vs. the satisfaction.

"So whoever drinks ˹his fill˺ from it is not with me, and whoever does not taste it—except a sip from the hollow of his hands—is definitely with me" — the verse's rule: the drink = the desertion; the no-taste = the loyalty; the sip from the hollow of the hand = the excuse — the small allowance for the dire thirst. Ibn Kathir: whoever drinks thereof is not of me; whoever tastes it not is of me; except him who takes it in the hollow of his hand.

"They all drank ˹their fill˺ except for a few!" — the verse's outcome: all but a few drank — the army of the promise became the few of the obedience. Ibn Kathir: they drank thereof, all, except a few of them — the few who crossed with Saul.

"When he and the ˹remaining˺ faithful with him crossed the river, they said, "Now we are no match for Goliath and his warriors"" — the verse's despair: the crossing's sight: after the obedience came the sight of the enemy — "Now we are no match for Goliath and his warriors." Ibn Kathir: "We have no power this day against Jalut and his hosts."

"But those ˹believers˺ who were certain they would meet Allah reasoned, "How many times has a small force vanquished a mighty army by the Will of Allah!"" — the verse's certainty: those who knew with certainty they would meet their Lord — the reasoning of the few: the small group has overcome the mighty host by Allah's leave. Ibn Kathir: those who knew with certainty they were going to meet Allah said — "How often has a small group overcome a mighty host by Allah's leave?"

"And Allah is ˹always˺ with the steadfast" — the verse's close: the sabirin — the steadfast — Allah with them. Ibn Kathir: "Allah is with as-Sabirin" — the patient ones — the steadfast — the ones whose certainty carries them across.

The verse's lesson: the test separates the faithful; the few who obey are the few whom Allah aids; the certainty of the meeting with Allah is the strength of the small force. And the reader's part: the thirst's test and the enemy's sight — the believer's certain reasoning is the victory's beginning.

## Verse 250

Verse 250 says: *"When they advanced to face Goliath and his warriors, they prayed, "Our Lord! Shower us with perseverance, make our steps firm, and give us victory over the disbelieving people.""*

In simple terms, the verse tells of the prayer at the advance: the perseverance, the firm steps, the victory over the disbelieving people.

"When they advanced to face Goliath and his warriors, they prayed" — the verse's advance: the march toward Jalut (Goliath) — and the prayer at the face of the enemy, not the retreat. Ibn Kathir: when the faithful party, who were few under the command of Talut, faced their enemy, who were many under the command of Jalut — they invoked. The verse's frame: the advance and the supplication: the believer who marches prays, and the one who prays marches.

"Our Lord! Shower us with perseverance" — the verse's *sabr* — the perseverance, the patience — poured forth. Ibn Kathir: "Our Lord! Pour forth on us patience" — send down patience on us from You. The verse's frame: the patience is the request — the steadfastness at the sight of the mighty host.

"make our steps firm" — the verse's *thabt* — the firmness of the feet — the firmness of the stance. The verse's frame: the firm feet — the ones that do not flee — the stance of the trust.

"and give us victory over the disbelieving people" — the verse's request: the victory — the aid — over the disbelieving people — the people who oppose the faith, who have the numbers and the arms. Ibn Kathir: grant us victory over this unbelieving people.

The verse's lesson: the prayer of the advance — the believers ask for the perseverance and the victory, not for the absence of the battle. The verse's frame: the supplication is the believer's armor at the march; the patience and the firmness are the conditions of the victory. And the reader's part: the advance is made with the prayer, and the prayer is made with the advance.
