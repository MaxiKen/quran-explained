## Verse 226

Verse 226 says: *"Those who swear not to have intercourse with their wives must wait for four months. If they change their mind, then Allah is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the oath of abstention (ila): the man who swears not to approach his wife is given four months; if he returns to her, Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

The verse's *ila* — the oath of the husband swearing not to have intercourse with his wife, the abstention, the abandonment of the wife by the oath. Ibn Kathir: if a man swears not to have intercourse with his wife for a period — less than four months: he may wait the end of the period and then have intercourse; the wife must be patient and cannot demand the return during this period; as is established in the Two Sahihs from 'A'ishah: the Prophet made ila' (swore to abstain) from his wives for a month, and came down after twenty-nine nights — "The month is twenty-nine" (also from 'Umar, similar). If the period exceeds four months: at the end of the four months the wife may demand — either the husband returns (fi'a — has intercourse) or he divorces; and the judge forces him to one or the other, so that the wife is not harmed.

"Must wait for four months" — the verse's *tarabbus arba'at ashhur* — the waiting of four months: the limit of the oath. Ibn Kathir: the husband waits four months from the time of the vow and then ends the ila if the vow was for four or more months and is required to either return or divorce. The verse's purpose: the four months is the limit of the coercion — the wife is not left in the limbo of the indefinite oath; the limit forces the decision: the return or the release. Maududi: for this kind of abnormal discord and estrangement God has fixed a limit of four months during which the spouses are required either to settle their difference, or to break the tie of wedlock so that each becomes free to contract marriage with someone with whom a harmonious matrimonial relationship appears more likely.

"If they change their mind, then Allah is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful" — the verse's *fa'in fa'u* — if they return — the return to the marital relations, the repeal of the oath. Ibn Kathir (Ibn Abbas, Masruq, ash-Sha'bi, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and others): the fa' — the return — is a metaphor for the intercourse. The verse's *fainna Allah Ghafur Rahim* — Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful — the forgiveness for what has passed of the neglect of the wife's right; the mercy of the wives. Ibn Kathir: the verse's ending — Allah is Forgiving of what has passed of the shortcoming in respect of the wives because of the oath, and Merciful to them.

In modern life, the verse is the law against the silent cruelty of the marriage. The modern marriage has its forms of the cold abandonment: the spouse withheld, the love withdrawn, the "punishment" of the silence, the indefinite neglect. The verse says: the oath can be the cruelty, and the limit is fixed — four months — and then the decision: the return to the marriage or the release of the wife. The verse's message: the marriage is not the weapon; a spouse cannot be held in the limbo of the indefinite coldness; the vow is limited, the return is the mercy, and the Lord is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful — for the wife, and for the husband who returns.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 227

Verse 227 says: *"But if they settle on divorce, then Allah is indeed All-Hearing, All-Knowing."*

In simple terms, the verse gives the alternative: if the couple settles on divorce rather than the return, then Allah is indeed All-Hearing and All-Knowing of the matter.

The verse is the second half of the ila ruling of 2:226: the oath of abstention ends at the four months — and the husband chooses: the return (fi'a) or the divorce. The verse's *in 'azamu al-talaq* — if they decide upon the divorce — the resolution, the intention, the firm decision of the divorce. Ibn Kathir: "if they decide upon divorce" indicates that divorce does not occur by merely passing the four-month mark during the ila; Malik reported from Nafi' that Ibn 'Umar said: if the man swears to ila from his wife, then divorce does not occur automatically even after the four months have passed — when he stops at the four months mark, he should either divorce or return (al-Bukhari); Ibn Jarir reported from Suhayl ibn Abi Salih from his father: he asked twelve Companions about the man who does ila with his wife — they all stated that he does not have to do anything until the four months have passed and then has to either retain or divorce her (also ad-Daraqutni); reported from 'Umar, 'Uthman, 'Ali, Abu ad-Darda', 'A'ishah, Ibn 'Umar and Ibn 'Abbas.

The verse's point: the divorce is the decision, not the automatic result of the time. The verse's *'azm* — the determination, the firm resolve — the decision is the matter of the deliberate will, not the mechanical passage of the months. The verse's frame: the ila — the oath; the four months — the limit; the return or the divorce — the two options; and the divorce — the decision — is the deliberate.

"And Allah is indeed All-Hearing, All-Knowing" — the verse's close: the Hearing and the Knowing — the Lord heard the oath and the intention, the arguments and the harms; the Lord knows the divorce's motive and the marriage's condition. The verse's *Sam'i' 'Alim* — the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing — the same close as 2:224 (the oaths): the Lord of the oaths is the Lord of the divorce — He heard the vow and knows the decision; the marriage's breaking is before the One who hears and knows. Ibn Kathir: the ayah — the All-Hearing of the statements, the All-Knowing of the intentions.

In modern life, the verse is the seriousness of the marriage's end. The modern divorce is often the impulsive, the angry, the sudden — and the verse says: the divorce is the decision, not the accident; the months of the limit, and the deliberate resolution. The verse's message: the marriage is not ended by the careless oath or the automatic time; the marriage is ended by the deliberate decision before the One who is All-Hearing, All-Knowing — the One who heard the marriage's vows and knows the divorce's motives. The believer's marriage and the believer's divorce are both before the Hearing and Knowing Lord: the oath sworn, the decision made, the accounting of the Lord.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 228

Verse 228 says: *"Divorced women must wait three monthly cycles ˹before they can re-marry˺. It is not lawful for them to conceal what Allah has created in their wombs, if they ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and the Last Day. And their husbands reserve the right to take them back within that period if they desire reconciliation. Women have rights similar to those of men equitably, although men have a degree ˹of responsibility˺ above them. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise."*

In simple terms, the verse rules the divorced woman's waiting: three cycles; no concealment of the womb's contents for the believer; the husband's better right of return for the reconciliation; the rights are mutual and the men have a degree of responsibility — Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.

"Divorced women must wait three monthly cycles before they can re-marry" — the verse's *quru'* — the three cycles, the monthly periods: the waiting period of the divorced woman — the iddah. Ibn Kathir: the quru' — the menstruation periods; the verse's waiting: the divorced woman waits the three cycles — the purpose: the certainty of the womb (the absence of the pregnancy), and the period of the reconsideration. The verse's *yatarabbasna bi-anfusihinna* — they keep themselves in the wait — the waiting of the divorcee: the three cycles, the purity, the basis of the remarriage.

"It is not lawful for them to conceal what Allah has created in their wombs, if they truly believe in Allah and the Last Day" — the verse's prohibition: the concealment of the pregnancy — the womb's creation. The verse's frame: the waiting's purpose is the clarity of the womb; and the wife's concealment of the pregnancy (the false claim of the cycle's end, the hiding of the child's existence) is the breach of the faith. Ibn Kathir: the wife cannot conceal the pregnancy or the menses — the concealment defeats the purpose of the iddah, and harms the child, the husband, the lineage; and the verse's condition: if they believe in Allah and the Last Day — the belief is the motive of the truth.

"And their husbands reserve the right to take them back within that period if they desire reconciliation" — the verse's *bu'ulatuhunna ahaqqu bi-raddihinna* — their husbands have the better right to take them back in that period: the husband may take back the wife within the iddah if the divorce is the first or second (the revocable divorce) — if he desires the reconciliation. Ibn Kathir: the husband's right is the reconciliation's door: the divorce is not final until the third; the husband has the right during the waiting to return — on the condition of the *islah* — the reconciliation. The verse's *islah* — the making right: the return is the return of the repair, not the harm.

"Women have rights similar to those of men equitably, although men have a degree of responsibility above them" — the verse's balance: the *mithl* — the similar — the rights of the wife over the husband (the maintenance, the companionship, the kindness) similar to the rights of the husband over her (the reputation, the obedience, the conduct) — the reciprocity of the marriage; and the *darajah* — the degree — the husband's degree of the responsibility. Ibn Kathir: the wife has certain rights on her husband just as he has certain rights on her (reported by Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim). Ma'arif: the men's degree — physically, in mannerism, status, obedience (of women to them), spending, taking care of the affairs, and in general in this life and the Hereafter; Ibn Abbas (Tanwir): the degree — in the reasoning faculty, inheritance, blood money, as witnesses in court. The verse's frame: the equality of the rights and the degree of the responsibility — the marriage's balance is not the tyranny of the degree but the reciprocity of the rights and the degree of the responsibility, before the Almighty, the All-Wise.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the divorce's dignity and the marriage's justice. The modern divorce is often the chaos: the wife's status unclear, the husband's rights and duties vague, the fairness forgotten. The verse gives the structure: the waiting of the three cycles — the clarity of the womb and the space of the reconsideration; the truth of the pregnancy — no concealment against the faith; the husband's right of the return — the door of the reconciliation; and the balance: the rights similar, and the degree of the responsibility — the reciprocity and the responsibility, not the mastery and the servitude. The verse's message: the divorce is the law's order: the waiting, the truth, the reconciliation, and the justice — before the Almighty, the All-Wise.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 229

Verse 229 says: *"Divorce may be retracted twice, then the husband must retain ˹his wife˺ with honour or separate ˹from her˺ with grace. It is not lawful for husbands to take back anything of the dowry given to their wives, unless the couple fears not being able to keep within the limits of Allah. So if you fear they will not be able to keep within the limits of Allah, there is no blame if the wife compensates the husband to obtain divorce. These are the limits set by Allah, so do not transgress them. And whoever transgresses the limits of Allah, they are the ˹true˺ wrongdoers."*

In simple terms, the verse sets the boundaries of the divorce: two retractions, then the honour of retention or the grace of release; the dowry is not recoverable except in the khul' — the wife's compensation when the couple fears the limits; and the transgressors of the limits are the wrongdoers.

"Divorce may be retracted twice" — the verse's *al-talaq marratani* — the divorce is twice: the revocable divorces — the first and the second. Ibn Kathir: the man used to have the right to take back his wife even if he had divorced her thrice; Allah abrogated this and said the divorce is twice. The verse's context (Ibn Abi Hatim, from 'Urwah): a man said to his wife, "I will neither divorce you nor take you back" — "I will divorce you and when your term of iddah nears its end, I will take you back" — the harm of the endless retraction; and the verse was revealed: the divorce is twice. Ibn Jarir: the ayah was revealed about Thabit ibn Qays ibn Shammas and his wife Habibah bint 'Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul.

"Then the husband must retain his wife with honour or separate from her with grace" — the verse's *imsak bi-ma'ruf aw tasrih bi-ihsan* — the retention with the good (the kindness, the honor) or the release with the excellence (the grace): the two options after the second divorce. Ibn Kathir: if you divorce her once or twice, you have the choice to take her back as long as she is in her iddah, intending to be kind to her and to mend differences; otherwise await the end of her iddah, when the divorce becomes final, and let her go in peace without the harm or the injustice. The verse's rule: the retention is the honorable; the release is the graceful; the third is not the game.

"It is not lawful for husbands to take back anything of the dowry given to their wives, unless the couple fears not being able to keep within the limits of Allah" — the verse's exception: the khul' — the wife's redemption. Ibn Kathir: the hadith (al-Bukhari and an-Nasa'i, from Ibn Abbas — the wife of Thabit): she came to the Prophet and said, "O Messenger of Allah! I do not criticize his religion or mannerism. But I hate committing kufr in Islam." The Prophet said: "Will you give him back his garden?" She said, "Yes." The Prophet said: "Take back the garden and divorce her once." Ibn Kathir (with Ibn Jarir): the ayah was revealed about Thabit ibn Qays — the woman who could not live with her husband, and the return of the garden (the dowry).

"So if you fear they will not be able to keep within the limits of Allah, there is no blame if the wife compensates the husband to obtain divorce" — the verse's khul': the wife's compensation — the return of the dowry or the part of it — for the divorce: when the marriage cannot stand within the limits, the wife buys her freedom with the return, and the husband is not blameworthy for taking it — in the case of the fear of the limits. The verse's *hudud Allah* — the limits of Allah — the verse's measure: the limits, not the money — the marriage's boundaries are the measure: the unfair retention, the harm, the neglect — the fear of the limits — and the redemption.

"These are the limits set by Allah, so do not transgress them. And whoever transgresses the limits of Allah, they are the true wrongdoers" — the verse's close: the *hudud* — the limits — and the *zalimun* — the wrongdoers. Ibn Kathir: the limits of Allah — the commands and the prohibitions of the marriage — the one who transgresses the limits is the wrongdoer. The verse's note: the limits are not the oppressive: the divorce's number, the retention's honor, the release's grace, the khul's fairness — the divine structure of the marriage's end, and the transgression is the wrong.

In modern life, the verse is the charter of the divorce's justice. The modern divorce is often the weapon: the endless retraction, the dowry's demand, the harm's continuation, the girl's limbo. The verse's structure: the twice — the space of the repair, the end of the game; the honor or the grace — the divorce's two faces, no third of the harm; the dowry's protection — the husband cannot take it back as the punishment, except the khul's fair redemption; the limits — the marriage's boundaries are not the money or the power, but the divine limits; and the transgression — the wrongdoer is the one who crosses the limits, not the one who leaves the harm. The believer's marriage and the believer's divorce are under the divine structure: the honor, the grace, the limits, and the justice.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 230

Verse 230 says: *"So if a husband divorces his wife ˹three times˺, then it is not lawful for him to remarry her until after she has married another man and then is divorced. Then it is permissible for them to reunite, as long as they feel they are able to maintain the limits of Allah. These are the limits set by Allah, which He makes clear for people of knowledge."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the third divorce: the first husband is not lawful for the wife until she has married another man and been divorced; the reunion is permitted only if they can maintain the limits; and the limits are made clear for the people of knowledge.

"So if a husband divorces his wife three times" — the verse's third divorce: after the twice of 2:229, the third is the final. Ibn Kathir: the divorce is twice, then the third — the final divorce — the wife is separated with the full finality. The verse's frame: the marriage is not the endless game; the third divorce ends the right of the retraction; the marriage is over.

"Then it is not lawful for him to remarry her until after she has married another man and then is divorced" — the verse's condition: the remarriage of the first husband is barred until the wife has married another man (the genuine marriage, the consummated marriage) and has been divorced. The verse's purpose: the seriousness of the third divorce — the condition between the remarriages is the other marriage: the wife is not the object of the game — "I divorce and I take back, I divorce and I take back" — the third ends it, and the return passes through the real marriage.

"Then it is permissible for them to reunite, as long as they feel they are able to maintain the limits of Allah" — the verse's return: after the other marriage and its divorce — if the couple can maintain the limits — they may reunite: the return is the return of the serious, not the game. Ibn Kathir: the condition of the reunion: the couple feel they can observe the limits of Allah — the fair treatment, the rights, the worship — the reunion is not the reunion of the harm but the reunion of the limits.

The verse's warning — the halala: the marriage of the convenience to make the woman lawful for the first husband — the curse. Ibn Kathir: the hadith recorded in the sunan (from Ibn Mas'ud and 'Ali): the Prophet cursed the one who marries a woman to make her lawful for her former husband (the muhallil) and the one for whom she is made lawful (the muhallal lahu) — Abu Dawud 2076 records from 'Ali: "Curse be upon the one who marries a divorced woman with the intention of making her lawful for her former husband and upon the one for whom she is made lawful." The verse's seriousness: the third divorce's consequence is not to be circumvented by the sham marriage; the return is through the real, and the sham is the curse.

"These are the limits set by Allah, which He makes clear for people of knowledge" — the verse's close: the *hudud* — the limits — and the *qawm ya'lamun* — the people of knowledge. Ibn Kathir: the limits are made clear — the divine boundaries of the marriage's ending — for the people who know: the aware, the learned, the ones who understand the seriousness of the bounds.

In modern life, the verse is the law against the marriage as the game. The modern person treats the marriage as the disposable: the divorce and the return, the divorce and the return, the marriage as the convenience of the repair. The verse says: the third ends; the return passes through the real; the sham is the curse. The verse's message: the marriage is the honor: the third divorce is the finality; the remarriage passes through the genuine, the real, the consummated — and the couple's reunion is the reunion of the limits — the fair, the kind, the worshipful. The believer's marriage is not the game: the limits are clear for the people of knowledge.

===VERSE-END===
