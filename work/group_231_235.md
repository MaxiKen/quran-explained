## Verse 231

Verse 231 says: *"When you divorce women and they have ˹almost˺ reached the end of their waiting period, either retain them honourably or let them go honourably. But do not retain them ˹only˺ to harm them ˹or˺ to take advantage ˹of them˺. Whoever does that surely wrongs his own soul. Do not take Allah's revelations lightly. Remember Allah's favours upon you as well as the Book and wisdom He has sent down for your guidance. Be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things."*

In simple terms, the verse commands the honourable end of the divorce: at the close of the waiting period the wife is retained in kindness or released with dignity; the retention for harm is the wrong of the soul; the verses of Allah are not to be taken lightly; and the favours and the Book are to be remembered.

"When you divorce women and they have almost reached the end of their waiting period" — the verse's timing: the end of the iddah — the moment of the decision: the retraction (the first or second revocable divorce) or the final separation. Ibn Kathir: if you divorce her once or twice, you have the choice to take her back as long as she is still in her iddah, intending to be kind to her and to mend differences; otherwise await the end of her iddah, when the divorce becomes final, and let her go in peace. The verse's *balaghna ajalahunna* — they have reached the end of the term — the iddah's end — the boundary of the decision.

"Either retain them honourably or let them go honourably" — the verse's two: the *imsak bi-ma'ruf* — the retention with the known good (the honor, the kindness, the fair treatment) — or the *tasrih bi-ma'ruf* — the release with the good (the grace, the finality, the dignity). Ibn Kathir: the take-back must be with the good intention, and the release with the kindness — no harm, no injustice. The verse's *ma'ruf* — the known, the recognized, the customary — the standard of the marriage's end: not the harm, not the revenge, but the honor and the grace.

"But do not retain them only to harm them or to take advantage of them. Whoever does that surely wrongs his own soul" — the verse's prohibition: the retention as the weapon — the husband keeps the wife in the marriage not for the marriage but for the harm: the frustration, the game of the iddah's end and the retraction, the torment. Ibn Kathir: the ayah — do not take them back to hurt them — and whoever does that, wrongs himself. The verse's *yalim nafsahu* — wrongs his own soul: the harm's return — the one who harms the wife in the retention harms his own soul before the Lord.

"Do not take Allah's revelations lightly" — the verse's *la tattakhidhu ayat Allah huzuwan* — do not take the verses of Allah as a mockery, a play, a joke: the divine rulings of the marriage are not the material of the game — the divorce and the retraction as the play with the revelation. Ibn Kathir: the marriage's verses are the serious commands; the ones who use the divorce as the mockery of the revelation are the ones who take the Book as the play. The verse's warning: the marriage's law is the revelation's law — and the game with the wife is the game with the verses.

"Remember Allah's favours upon you as well as the Book and wisdom He has sent down for your guidance. Be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah has perfect knowledge of all things" — the verse's close: the favours, the Book, the wisdom — the revelation's guidance, the marriage's law, the remembrance; and the mindfulness — the taqwa; and the knowledge — the Lord's perfect knowledge of all things. The verse's frame: the marriage's end is under the favours and the Book: the believer remembers what the Lord has given, and knows the Lord's knowledge.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the divorce's decency and the warning of the weaponized marriage. The modern divorce can be the harm: the retention as the punishment, the endless game of the marriage's manipulation, the wife held in the limbo for no reason but the control. The verse says: the retention is the honorable, the release is the honorable; the retention for the harm is the wrong of the soul; the verses of Allah are not the play. The believer's divorce: the decision's honor, the wife's dignity, the end's grace — and the mindfulness before the Lord who knows all things: the retention's motive and the release's motive are known, and the game with the revelation is known.

## Verse 232

Verse 232 says: *"When you have divorced women and they have reached the end of their waiting period, do not prevent them from re-marrying their ex-husbands if they come to an honourable agreement. This is enjoined on whoever has faith in Allah and the Last Day. This is purer and more dignifying for you. Allah knows and you do not know."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the prevention of the remarriage: after the waiting period the divorced couple is not to be blocked from renewing the marriage when they mutually agree; this is enjoined on the believer; it is purer; and Allah knows what the people do not.

"When you have divorced women and they have reached the end of their waiting period, do not prevent them from re-marrying their ex-husbands" — the verse's *la ta'duluhunna* — do not prevent them, do not obstruct them, do not harm them — from marrying their former husbands: the remarriage of the divorced couple after the iddah (with the new contract, the new dowry) is permitted — and the guardian (the wali) or the relative is not to prevent it. Ibn Kathir: the verse's context — al-Hasan from Ma'qil ibn Yasar (recorded by Abu Dawud, at-Tirmidhi, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Jarir, Ibn Marduwyah and al-Bayhaqi): Ma'qil ibn Yasar had divorced his sister, and when her iddah ended, her husband wanted to remarry her; Ma'qil refused; the verse was revealed: do not prevent them from marrying their former husbands. Ma'qil said: "Then I went to the Prophet, and he recited to me: When you have divorced women and they have reached the end of their waiting period, do not prevent them from re-marrying their ex-husbands."

"If they come to an honourable agreement" — the verse's *idha taradaw baynahum bi-al-ma'ruf* — if they mutually agree among themselves with the recognized good — the agreement of the couple: the new marriage, the consent, the fairness. The verse's condition: the couple's mutual agreement — not the guardian's coercion, not the family's veto, but the couple's honorable agreement.

"This is enjoined on whoever has faith in Allah and the Last Day" — the verse's *dhalika yu'azu bihi man kana yu'min bi Allah wa al-yawm al-akhir* — this is reminded, instructed, taught to whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day: the prevention of the honorable remarriage is the sin of the faithless; the believer does not block the good — the remarriage — the reconciliation of the couple. Ibn Kathir: the ayah's address is the reminder: the one who believes in Allah and the Last Day — the belief is the ground of the fairness: the remarriage's consent is the divine command, and the prevention is the disobedience.

"This is purer and more dignifying for you" — the verse's *dhalikum azka lakum wa athar* — that is purer for you and more dignifying, cleaner: the purity and the dignity — the non-prevention: the couple's reconciliation is the cleaner, the more honorable path, the family's repair, the children's unity. Ibn Kathir: the verse's purity — the purer, the more dignified — the remarriage over the prevention.

"Allah knows and you do not know" — the verse's close: the Lord's knowledge — the knows and the unknowing: the guardian's motive, the couple's good, the family's future — Allah knows and you do not know. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *Allah ya'lam wa antum la ta'lamun* — Allah knows the consequences, the good in the remarriage, the harm in the prevention; and you do not know. The verse's frame: the human judgment is the limited; the Lord's knowledge is the comprehensive — the one who blocks the good on the surface may be blocking the greater good he does not see.

In modern life, the verse is the law against the family's veto. The modern marriage is often surrounded by the family's power: the guardian's veto, the family's interference, the "we will not allow" of the relatives — and the verse says: the couple's honorable agreement is not to be blocked; the prevention is the faithless act; the purer and the more dignifying is the reconciliation. The verse's message: the couple's marriage is the couple's consent, with the honorable agreement; the family's role is the counsel, not the control; and the believer trusts the Lord's knowledge — the good in the remarriage that the human does not see — Allah knows and you do not know.

## Verse 233

Verse 233 says: *"˹Divorced˺ mothers will breastfeed their offspring for two whole years, for those who wish to complete the nursing ˹of their child˺. The child's father will provide reasonable maintenance and clothing for the mother ˹during that period˺. No one will be charged with more than they can bear. No mother or father should be made to suffer for their child. The ˹father's˺ heirs are under the same obligation. But if both sides decide—after mutual consultation and consent—to wean a child, then there is no blame on them. And if you decide to have your children nursed by a wet-nurse, it is permissible as long as you pay fairly. Be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah is All-Seeing of what you do."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the nursing: mothers nurse two full years for those who wish the completion; the father provides the maintenance and clothing; no soul is burdened beyond capacity and neither parent is harmed for the child; the heirs have the same duty; the weaning by mutual consent is permissible; and the wet-nurse is allowed with a fair payment.

"Divorced mothers will breastfeed their offspring for two whole years, for those who wish to complete the nursing of their child" — the verse's duration: the two complete years — the nursing's term, for the one who wishes to complete it: the child's nursing is the mother's right and duty; the two years is the term of the completion. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *hawlayni kamilayni* — the two complete years — the nursing period — and the verse's condition: the two years is for the one who wishes the completion; the mother's nursing is the child's nourishment and the mother's right (with the payment). The verse's frame: the divorce ends the marriage but not the child's care; the nursing continues the mother-child bond and the father's obligation.

"The child's father will provide reasonable maintenance and clothing for the mother during that period" — the verse's *ala al-mawlud lahu rizquhunna wa kiswatuhunna bi-al-ma'ruf* — upon the father (the one to whom the child is born) — the provision and the clothing of the mothers, with the known good: the father provides the mother's maintenance and clothing during the nursing — the cost is the father's; the child is his, and the nursing's support is his. Ibn Kathir: the father is the provider: the mother's food and clothing during the nursing, the child's maintenance; the verse's *ma'ruf* — the reasonable, the customary — the standard of the provision.

"No one will be charged with more than they can bear. No mother or father should be made to suffer for their child" — the verse's limits: the *wus'* — the capacity — no soul is burdened beyond its capacity; and the prohibition: no mother or father should be harmed because of the child — the mother is not harmed by the custody and the nursing beyond her capacity; the father is not harmed by the maintenance beyond his means. Ibn Kathir: the verse's principle — the burden of the child is shared and limited; the mother's nursing and the father's provision — neither is made to suffer for the child. The verse's frame: the child's care is the divine balance — not the mother's abandonment, not the father's ruin.

"The father's heirs are under the same obligation" — the verse's *wa al-warith mithlu dhalika* — the heir is likewise: the father's heirs carry the obligation — the provision, the maintenance — if the father is dead: the inheritance and the responsibility pass to the heirs (the child's support from the estate). Ibn Kathir: the heir — the one who inherits from the father — is under the same obligation: the child's maintenance and the nursing's provision.

"But if both sides decide — after mutual consultation and consent — to wean a child, then there is no blame on them" — the verse's weaning: the mutual decision — the consultation and the consent — the early weaning: if both parents agree (the mother's consent and the father's) — there is no blame: the weaning before the two years by the mutual agreement. The verse's *tashawur* — the mutual consultation — and *taradi* — the mutual consent — the decision of the early weaning is the decision of both, not the coercion of one.

"And if you decide to have your children nursed by a wet-nurse, it is permissible as long as you pay fairly" — the verse's wet-nurse: the alternative — the mother may not nurse, and the wet-nurse is permitted — with the fair payment: the father pays the wet-nurse fairly. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *in aradtum an tastardi'u* — if you wish to employ a wet-nurse — and the payment — the father's obligation: the wet-nurse's fee is the father's; the fair amount, in the ma'ruf.

"Be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah is All-Seeing of what you do" — the verse's close: the taqwa and the Seeing: the child's care is under the Lord's watch; the mother's nursing, the father's provision, the weaning's agreement, the wet-nurse's fee — the All-Seeing sees.

In modern life, the verse is the charter of the child's care after the divorce. The modern divorce often leaves the child's care to the conflict: the mother's abandonment, the father's neglect, the custody's battle, the maintenance's denial. The verse's structure: the nursing's two years — the child's nourishment and the mother's bond; the father's provision — the maintenance and the clothing by the custom; the capacity's limit — no one beyond the bearable; the no-harm — mother and father are not to suffer for the child; the heir's obligation — the support continues; the mutual weaning — the parents' consent; and the wet-nurse — the fair alternative. The verse's message: the divorce ends the marriage, not the responsibility; the child's care is the shared duty — the mother's nursing, the father's paying, the both's agreement — under the All-Seeing of what you do.

## Verse 234

Verse 234 says: *"As for those of you who die and leave widows behind, let them observe a waiting period of four months and ten days. When they have reached the end of this period, then you are not accountable for what they decide for themselves in a reasonable manner. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the widow's waiting: the widow observes four months and ten days; when the term ends there is no blame for the reasonable decisions she makes for herself — and Allah is All-Aware.

"As for those of you who die and leave widows behind, let them observe a waiting period of four months and ten days" — the verse's widow's iddah: the four months and ten days — the waiting period of the wife whose husband dies. Ibn Kathir: this ayah contains a command from Allah to the wives whose husbands die — they should observe a period of iddah of four months and ten nights, including the cases where the marriage was consummated or otherwise, according to the consensus. And the pregnant widow: her term ends when she gives birth, even if an instant after the husband's death — from 65:4 — "The waiting period for those who are pregnant is until they give birth." And the hadith of Subay'ah al-Aslamiyyah: her husband died while she was pregnant; Abu Sanabil ibn Ba'kak said, "You will not marry until the four months and ten nights have passed"; she went to the Prophet, and he said her iddah had finished when she gave birth and allowed her to marry (al-Bukhari, Muslim).

"Four months and ten days" — the verse's wisdom. Ibn Kathir (from Sa'id ibn Musayyib and Abu al-'Aliyah): the wisdom behind the iddah of the widow is that the womb might contain a fetus — when the woman waits for this period, it will become evident if she is pregnant; and the hadith (Ibn Mas'ud, in the Two Sahihs): the fetus begins to show signs of life after the soul has been breathed into it — around the four months; the ten days complete the account of the months of the varying length. Maududi: the four months and ten days — the certainty of the womb and the time of the mourning.

The mourning: Ibn Kathir — the hadith (the Two Sahihs, from the Prophet): it is not lawful for a woman who believes in Allah and the Last Day to mourn for more than three days for any dead person except her husband, for whom she mourns for four months and ten days — and the Prophet's comment: "It is four months and ten (nights)! During the Jahiliyyah, one of you would mourn for an entire year" (the verse's 2:240 note of the old practice).

"When they have reached the end of this period, then you are not accountable for what they decide for themselves in a reasonable manner" — the verse's *fa-idha balaghna ajalahunna fala junaha 'alaykum fima fa'alna fi anfusihinna bi-al-ma'ruf* — when they have fulfilled their term, there is no sin on you for what they do with themselves in the known good: the widow at the end of the iddah is free to marry, to beautify, to accept the proposals. Ibn Kathir (al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas): if the woman is divorced or her husband dies and her iddah ends, there is no sin that she beautifies herself, so that she becomes ready for marriage proposals — this is the "just and honorable"; Muqatil and Mujahid and al-Hasan, az-Zuhri, as-Suddi: the verse refers to the allowed and pure (honorable) marriage.

"And Allah is All-Aware of what you do" — the verse's close: the *Khabir* — the All-Aware — the Lord knows the widow's conduct, the family's treatment, the mourning's sincerity, the remarriage's timing. The verse's frame: the widow's period and the widow's freedom are under the Lord's awareness.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the widow's dignity. The modern widow often faces the two extremes: the indefinite mourning (the "widow forever") or the callous impatience (the "move on at once"). The verse's balance: the four months and ten days — the certain wait (the womb's clarity and the grief's time) — and then the freedom: the widow's decision in the reasonable manner, the remarriage, the life, the dignity. The verse's message: the widow's waiting is the defined mercy — the time of the mourning and the clarity — and the widow's freedom is the honor — her decision at the end, in the reasonable manner, under the All-Aware Lord.

## Verse 235

Verse 235 says: *"There is no blame on you for subtly showing interest in ˹divorced or widowed˺ women or for hiding ˹the intention˺ in your hearts. Allah knows that you are considering them ˹for marriage˺. But do not make a secret commitment with them—you can only show interest in them appropriately. Do not commit to the bond of marriage until the waiting period expires. Know that Allah is aware of what is in your hearts, so beware of Him. And know that Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing."*

In simple terms, the verse rules on the proposal during the waiting period: the subtle indication and the hidden intention are allowed, but the secret commitment and the final bond are deferred until the waiting ends; the believer fears the Lord who knows the hearts — Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.

"There is no blame on you for subtly showing interest in divorced or widowed women or for hiding the intention in your hearts" — the verse's *ta'rid* — the indirect, the subtle, the hint — the proposal by the allusion versus the explicit. Ibn Kathir: there is no sin on you to indirectly mention marriage to the widow during the term of iddah for her deceased husband — the hint, the signal, the indirect offer; and the hiding of the intention in the heart — the considered wish, the concealed desire — no sin. The verse's distinction: the subtle indication is permitted; the explicit proposal is not (during the iddah); and the heart's hidden intention — the Lord knows it, and it is not the sin.

"Allah knows that you are considering them for marriage" — the verse's *'alima Allah annakum satadhkurunahumna* — Allah knows that you will remember them: the consideration, the remembrance, the wish. The verse's honesty: the Lord knows the heart's desire; the verse speaks the truth of the human condition — the person who sees the widow and considers, who remembers, who wishes — the Lord knows.

"But do not make a secret commitment with them — you can only show interest in them appropriately" — the verse's prohibition: the secret promise — the *muwa'ada sirran* — the secret engagement, the hidden contract, the private pledge — the prohibited: the secret commitment during the iddah; and the permitted: the honorable saying — the *qawl ma'ruf* — the appropriate word — the dignified hint, not the secret bond. Ibn Kathir: the verse's *illa an taqulu qawlan ma'rufan* — except that you say an honorable saying — the indirect, the appropriate, not the explicit contract.

"Do not commit to the bond of marriage until the waiting period expires" — the verse's *la ta'zimu 'uqdat al-nikah* — do not resolve the marriage bond, do not be determined on the knot of the marriage — until the prescribed term reaches the end: the marriage contract is not to be concluded during the waiting period; the iddah's end is the lawful boundary of the commitment. Ibn Kathir (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, ash-Sha'bi, Qatadah, ar-Rabi' ibn Anas, Abu Malik, Zayd ibn Aslam, Muqatil ibn Hayyan, az-Zuhri, 'Ata al-Khurasani, as-Suddi, ath-Thawri and ad-Dahhak): the verse means — do not consummate the marriage before the term prescribed is fulfilled.

"Know that Allah is aware of what is in your hearts, so beware of Him. And know that Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing" — the verse's close: the awareness of the hearts and the fear; the forgiveness and the forbearance: the Lord knows the heart's intention — the sincere or the sly, the honorable or the scheming — so beware; and the Lord forgives and forbears — the forgiving of the heart's slips, and the forbearance of the sincere.

In modern life, the verse is the law of the dignified pursuit. The modern dating and the modern pursuit rush the bond: the commitment before the time, the secret engagement, the contract of the convenience. The verse's standards: the subtle interest is permitted (the respect of the period); the explicit and the secret commitment is not (the honor of the waiting); the marriage's bond waits for the waiting's end; and the heart's intention — the Lord knows and the believer isware. The verse's message: the pursuit of the widow and the divorced is the pursuit of the dignity — the proper interest, no secret commitment, the bond at the right time, and the heart's purity before the All-Forgiving, the Most Forbearing — the Lord who knows the hearts and forgives the sincere.
