## Verse 266

Verse 266 says: *"Would any of you wish to have a garden with palm trees, grapevines, and all kinds of fruits with rivers flowing underneath and as they grow very old with dependent children, a fiery whirlwind hits the garden, burning it all up? This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so perhaps you will reflect."*

In simple terms, the verse poses the heart-searching question: would you like a flourishing garden — palms, vines, every fruit, rivers beneath — and then, in your old age with dependent children, a fiery whirlwind burns it all? So Allah makes the revelations clear — so that you may reflect.

"Would any of you wish to have a garden with palm trees, grapevines, and all kinds of fruits with rivers flowing underneath" — the verse's picture: the complete garden — the palms, the vines, the fruits of every kind, the rivers. Ibn Kathir: the garden with the date palms and vines, rivers flowing underneath, all kinds of fruits — the full wealth. The verse's frame: the picture of the complete blessing: the garden at its best, the wealth at its summit.

"and as they grow very old with dependent children" — the verse's moment: the old age and the weak offspring. Ibn Kathir (from al-'Awfi, from Ibn 'Abbas, via Ibn Abi Hatim): he lost all this in his old age — while his offspring and children are weak, just before the end of his life. The verse's frame: the neediest moment: the one who owns the garden is the one who needs it most — the old age's weakness, the children's dependence: the time when the loss is the ruin.

"a fiery whirlwind hits the garden, burning it all up?" — the verse's catastrophe: the i'sar — the whirlwind — the wind with the fire — the burnt garden. Ibn Kathir: "struck with a whirlwind" — with heavy wind — that is fiery, so that it is burnt — its fruits were burnt and its trees were destroyed — "therefore, what will his condition be like?" The verse's frame: the whirlwind of the loss: the complete blessing burnt by the wind — the question's point: no one wishes this for the garden of the world.

"This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so perhaps you will reflect" — the verse's close: the clarification and the reflection. Ibn Kathir (from Ibn 'Abbas): Allah has set a good parable, and all His parables are good — this is the condition of the disbeliever on the Day of Resurrection when he returns to Allah — he will not have any good deeds to provide an excuse or refuge for him, just as the man in the parable had no strength to replant the garden — and he will not find anything to resort to for help, just as the offspring of the man in the parable did not provide him with help — so he will be deprived of his reward when he most needs it, just as the man in the parable was deprived of his garden when he most needed it. The verse's frame: the parable of the nullified deeds: the charity destroyed by the mann and the adha (2:264) is the garden burnt at the old age — the reward lost at the need.

Ibn Kathir's heading: "The Example of Evil Deeds Nullifying Good Deeds" — the verse's aim: the deeds of the disbeliever and the show-off are the burnt garden. Ma'arif: spending in charity against given conditions is like hoping to be investing profusely in the Hereafter — but with Allah this investment comes to naught.

The verse's lesson: the reward's loss at the need is the worst loss — the good deeds preserved are the garden guarded against the whirlwind. And the reader's part: reflect — the garden you guard is the deeds you keep; the whirlwind of the nullification is the reminder, the injury, the show-off.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 267

Verse 267 says: *"O believers! Donate from the best of what you have earned and of what We have produced for you from the earth. Do not pick out worthless things for donation, which you yourselves would only accept with closed eyes. And know that Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy."*

In simple terms, the verse commands the donation from the good: donate from the best of the earnings and the earth's produce — not the worthless things you would only accept with closed eyes — and know that Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy.

"O believers! Donate from the best of what you have earned and of what We have produced for you from the earth" — the verse's source: the tayyibat — the good things — of the earnings and the earth's produce. Ibn Kathir: spend of the good things which you have legally earned, and of that which We have produced from the earth for you. The verse's frame: the charity's quality: the giving of the good from the good — the best is Allah's due, not the rejected.

"Do not pick out worthless things for donation, which you yourselves would only accept with closed eyes" — the verse's prohibition: the khabith — the bad, the worthless — the things you would not accept unless you close your eyes and tolerate. Ibn Kathir: do not aim at that which is bad to spend from it, though you would not accept it save if you close your eyes and tolerate therein. The verse's frame: the measure of the worth: the thing you would reject for yourself is the thing you must not give to Allah's cause — the closed eyes are the sign: the gift you are ashamed to receive is the gift you must not give.

"And know that Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy" — the verse's close: the Ghani — the Self-Sufficient — the Hamid — the Praiseworthy. Ibn Kathir: Allah is All-Sufficient for His creatures' needs, All-Knower (the verse's note); Ma'arif: know that Allah is Rich, free of all needs, and worthy of all praise. The verse's frame: the Self-Sufficient needs nothing — the giving is not the enriching of Allah, it is the purification of the giver: the Praiseworthy deserves the praise of the giving.

The verse's lesson: give the good, not the rejected — measure the gift by the measure of your own acceptance. Maududi (Tafhim): the charity from the good — the believer gives what he honors, not what he discards. And the reader's part: the best from the best — the charity you give is the mirror of the value you place on the cause.

## Verse 268

Verse 268 says: *"The Devil threatens you with ˹the prospect of˺ poverty and bids you to the shameful deed ˹of stinginess˺, while Allah promises you forgiveness and ˹great˺ bounties from Him. And Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing."*

In simple terms, the verse uncovers the whisper of the Devil: he threatens you with poverty and commands the shameful stinginess — while Allah promises forgiveness and bounty — and He is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing.

"The Devil threatens you with ˹the prospect of˺ poverty" — the verse's threat: al-Shaytan ya'idukum al-faqr. Ibn Kathir: Shaytan threatens you with poverty — so that you hold on to whatever you have and refrain from spending it in Allah's pleasure. Al-Jalalayn: Satan promises you poverty by making you fear loss when you make a voluntary almsgiving, so you withhold it. The verse's frame: the whisper's economics: the false fear — the spending will make you poor — the hoarding is survival.

"and bids you to the shameful deed ˹of stinginess˺" — the verse's command: the fahsha' — the indecency, the shameful deed — rendered here as the stinginess: the withholding. Ibn Kathir: "And orders you to commit fahsha'" — Shaytan forbids you from spending in charity because of the false fear of becoming poor, and he encourages evil deeds, sins, indulging in what is prohibited, and immoral conduct. Al-Jalalayn: the fahsha' — niggardliness and the impeding of almsgiving. The verse's frame: the shame of the stinginess: the devil's bid is the ugly deed — the withholding painted as prudence.

"while Allah promises you forgiveness and ˹great˺ bounties from Him" — the verse's promise: the maghfirah — the forgiveness — and the fadl — the bounty. Ibn Kathir: whereas Allah promises you forgiveness from Himself — instead of the evil that Shaytan enjoins on you — and the bounty. At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i recorded the narration in the books of tafsir (Ibn Kathir's note in the commentary of 2:267–268). The verse's frame: the two promises side by side: the Devil's poverty and the sin — Allah's forgiveness and the bounty: the promise of the Believer against the threat of the whisperer.

"And Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing" — the verse's close: Wasi' — the All-Encompassing in bounty — 'Alim — the All-Knowing. Ibn Kathir: Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing. The verse's frame: the All-Bountiful's promise stands against the devil's threat — the All-Knowing knows the poverty the devil paints and the bounty He gives.

The verse's lesson: the stinginess is the devil's bidding; the spending is the trust in the promise. And the reader's part: when the whisper of the poverty comes with the giving — answer with the promise: the forgiveness and the bounty are Allah's, and His bounty is wider than the poverty's fear.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 269

Verse 269 says: *"Allah grants wisdom to whoever He wills. And whoever is granted wisdom is certainly blessed with a great privilege. But none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason."*

In simple terms, the verse speaks of the wisdom: Allah grants it to whom He wills; whoever is granted it has been given a great good; but only the people of reason take heed.

"Allah grants wisdom to whoever He wills" — the verse's grant: the hikmah — the wisdom. Ibn Kathir (with the verse's context): He grants hikmah to whom He wills. Maududi (Tafhim, note 309): "Wisdom" signifies sound perception and sound judgment — the purpose: the one possessed of wisdom will follow God's path rather than that of Satan. The verse's frame: the wisdom is the gift of the Giver: not the accumulation of the years, not the cleverness of the trade: the perception of the affairs, the judgment of the paths.

"And whoever is granted wisdom is certainly blessed with a great privilege" — the verse's value: the khayr kathir — the great good, the abundant privilege. Ibn Kathir: whoever is given wisdom has been given a great good — the wisdom's worth beyond the world's wealth. The verse's frame: the privilege: the one who sees the truth of the spending and the hoarding — the one who knows the Hereafter's economics — has the good that the riches cannot equal.

"But none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason" — the verse's receivers: the ulu al-albab — the people of reason, the possessors of the minds. Ibn Kathir: but none will remember except people of understanding. Maududi: the followers of Satan believe that it is the height of wisdom and shrewdness to be constantly concerned with saving out of one's earnings and to be perpetually on the look-out for a higher income — but for those endowed with divine perception such an attitude is sheer folly. The verse's frame: the mindfulness of the wise: the warning of the devil's threat (2:268) and the wisdom's answer: the mindful see the folly behind the stinginess.

The verse's place: after the devil's threat and the promises (2:268) — the wisdom's grant: the protection: the one who is wise hears the promise of the forgiveness and the bounty, not the threat of the poverty. The verse's frame: the wisdom is the eyes of the heart.

The verse's lesson: the great privilege is the wisdom — the perception of what is true wealth. And the reader's part: seek the wisdom — the mindful see the giver's path: the spending is the wisdom's act, and the hoarding is the folly's.

===VERSE-END===

## Verse 270

Verse 270 says: *"Whatever charities you give or vows you make are surely known to Allah. And the wrongdoers will have no helpers."*

In simple terms, the verse assures: whatever charity you give and whatever vows you make are known to Allah — and the wrongdoers will have no helpers.

"Whatever charities you give or vows you make are surely known to Allah" — the verse's knowledge: the nafaqah and the nadhr — the charity and the vow. Ibn Kathir: Allah states that He has perfect knowledge of the good deeds performed by all of His creation, such as charity and various vows — and He rewards tremendously for these deeds, provided they are performed seeking His Face and His promise. The verse's frame: the two commitments: the giving of the charity and the making of the vow — both known: the secret giving (2:271) and the public spending (2:274) — the known by the Knower.

"And the wrongdoers will have no helpers" — the verse's close: the zalimin — the wrongdoers — the la ansar — the no helpers. Ibn Kathir: Allah also warns those who do not work in His obedience, but insist on the disobedience — the wrongdoers will have no helpers: no helpers on the Day of Resurrection to save them from what they earned. The verse's frame: the reversal of the reliance: the wrongdoer's helpers are the ones who are absent on the Day: the wealth, the allies, the counts — no helpers.

The verse's lesson: every charity and every vow is in the divine ledger — the secret is known. And the reader's part: give as you please publicly or privately (2:271–274) — the ledger is with Allah: and rely on no help but His, for the wrongdoers' helpers will be none.
