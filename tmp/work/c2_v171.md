**A PICTURE OF UNRECEIVED GUIDANCE**

For a picture of unreceived guidance, the decisive wording is ***“The example of the disbelievers ˹not responding to the Messenger’s warning˺ is like a flock not comprehending the calls”***. The verse gives a parable of people who hear the sound of revelation but do not grasp its meaning. A shepherd may call animals that hear a noise without understanding the words or the danger being addressed. In the same way, a person may listen to recitation, arguments, warnings, and reminders while allowing none of them to reach the heart. The comparison is not saying that all disbelievers lack physical hearing, speech, or sight. The Qur’an immediately uses *deaf, dumb, and blind* in a moral and spiritual sense. Their faculties function biologically, but they refuse to use them for guidance. The Qur’an elsewhere speaks of hearts that do not understand and ears that do not hear the truth despite being physically sound. A cry or shout can alert an animal without conveying a reasoned message. It may produce movement, fear, or temporary attention, but it does not produce insight. The parable describes people whose response to revelation is similarly shallow. They may react to a loud speaker, a social identity, or an emotional atmosphere without understanding what Allah is asking of them. This is a warning to Muslims as well. Recitation can become sound without reflection, sermons can become familiar background, and religious language can be repeated without repentance. The believer measures his choices by the Qur’an rather than taking repeated hearing as sufficient. (2:170 — **“if their forefathers had ˹absolutely˺ no understanding or guidance”**) Because a picture of unreceived guidance governs 2:171, the three blocked faculties arises from a picture of unreceived guidance. It follows that the messenger’s compassion completes a picture of unreceived guidance through the three blocked faculties.

**THE THREE BLOCKED FACULTIES**

Read under the three blocked faculties, the relevant clauses are ***“and cries of the shepherd. ˹They are wilfully˺ deaf, dumb”***. Hearing represents receiving evidence, speech represents acknowledging and responding to truth, and sight represents recognizing the signs that lead to Allah. When the three are described as blocked, the verse portrays a person who refuses to listen, will not confess what he knows, and does not see the meaning of the world around him. The blockage is not Allah’s injustice. It is connected to persistent rejection. When a person repeatedly turns away, his choices harden into a veil. The Qur’an says that when they deviated, Allah caused their hearts to deviate. Divine judgement and human choice are both affirmed: people choose refusal, and Allah allows the consequences of their chosen path to settle upon them. The final phrase, *they do not understand*—*do not understand*, does not deny their ability to solve worldly problems. A person can be clever in business, science, or politics while failing to understand the purpose of life. Qur’anic understanding is *reason*: connecting signs to their Creator, controlling desire, and accepting moral consequence. Pride is one of the greatest barriers. The person who approaches revelation only to defeat it may notice details but miss its direction. The sincere person who seeks what his Lord wants can be guided even when he begins with limited knowledge. Understanding is therefore both an intellectual and moral act. (7:179 — **“Indeed, We have destined many jinn”**) The reason the messenger’s compassion follows the three blocked faculties lies in a picture of unreceived guidance. Therefore, the messenger’s compassion returns practical force to the three blocked faculties.

**THE MESSENGER’S COMPASSION**

The messenger’s compassion begins from ***“and blind so they have no understanding”***. The image also explains why prophets repeatedly called people despite rejection. They were not shouting meaningless sounds; they were conveying a clear message with patience. The Prophet ﷺ recited, explained, warned, encouraged, and answered questions. When people refused to hear, he grieved for them, but he did not force faith. Guidance belongs to Allah, while the messenger’s duty is clear conveyance. Muslim educators should follow this model. Teaching should be understandable, truthful, and connected to real life. A speaker should not rely only on volume, fear, or performance. The goal is for the listener to comprehend, reflect, and choose obedience. Spiritual deafness is not necessarily permanent while a person lives. The Qur’an invites people to listen attentively, reflect, and return. Turning off distractions, reading a translation with the Arabic, asking qualified questions, keeping good company, and practising what one learns can reopen the heart. The believer should make the supplication taught in the Qur’an: Our Lord, grant us from our spouses and offspring comfort to our eyes and make us an example for the mindful. A receptive heart is a gift, so one asks Allah for it while taking the means of receptivity. A radio may be switched on loudly in a room while its tuning remains between stations; sound fills the space, yet the message dissolves into noise. The call in this verse reaches ears in the same way when the faculties of response have been surrendered. Hearing syllables is not the same as receiving guidance. For a reader today, repeated exposure to sermons, recitation, and moral advice can create familiarity without obedience, so the test is not whether the sound was present but whether meaning crossed into judgement and action. (22:46 — **“Have they not travelled throughout the land”**) In other words, the messenger’s compassion answers a picture of unreceived guidance.
