**THE CLASS THE FIRST VERSE LEFT UNNAMED**

The second step of the passage names a class the first verse had left unnamed: ***“And among them are the illiterate”***. The referent is settled by the earliest glosses as the unlearned among the community in question — men who neither read nor write the Scripture — and the lexicographers explain the form as belonging to the family of the mother, as though such a person remained in the state in which his mother bore him, unshaped by the craft of letters. The same designation was worn by the Prophet himself, and the report of the description is transmitted in Bukhārī and Muslim: *"we are an unlettered nation: we do not write and we do not calculate the months"*. That report does double work in the tradition, both marking the Prophet's sign and defining the term as the inability to perform the crafts of the page. A minority reading attached to Ibn ʿAbbās takes the class in a harder direction — a people who accept no prophet and no revealed book, and who write a book of their own and say it is from God, named unlettered for their standing opposition to the Books, which means the label in that reading is earned by stance rather than by schooling — and the majority of the learned hold that reading remote, since in the tongue of the region the designation marks the man who cannot write. The adjudication matters because it decides whether the verse indicts a condition or a policy; the lexical evidence gives the condition, and the next clause supplies the policy.

**THE THREE FACES OF WHAT THEY FOLLOW**

The verse then names the entire intellectual furniture of the class: ***“who know nothing about the Scripture except lies”***. The designation gathered here as lies has been unpacked in three directions across the glosses, and all three are instructive. It means hearsay — the bare report of their leaders, received without knowledge and repeated without examination. It means invention — the manufactured claims of which this passage supplies its own samples, the promise that the Fire will touch them only a counted number of days and the boast that none enters Paradise but their party. And it means wish and desire, the flattering hope that the tongue dresses as scripture. The tradition keeps the double edge of the term in view precisely because its victims believed it as news. The closing verb of the verse then completes the picture: ***“and ˹so˺ they ˹wishfully˺ speculate”***. The designation rendered as speculate carries three degrees in the Book's own usage — settled certainty, prevailing likelihood, and bare fancy — and this verse holds the third. One early gloss strips the clause to a single word in the same register as lying; another takes the speculation as the surmise built on what their leaders told them. The point is that conjecture here is not humility before the unknown; it is the confident echo of a fraud, therefore the verse treats the speculation as chargeable rather than forgivable.

**THE TEACHERS STAND FIRST IN THE DOCK**

The hand-off between the two verses carries the passage's deepest judgement of structure, and the narrated analysis states it without softening, because the chain of causation is itself evidence. The superstitiousness of the unlettered was caused; it was manufactured by the dishonesty of men who knew better, and the verse-order keeps the chain visible: the learned are addressed first, the fed are addressed second. What turns on that order is the proportion of guilt. A population raised on pleasing falsehoods is guilty of credulity, since it never checked the hand that fed it, and the men who printed the falsehoods to purchase money and respect with them are guilty of the trade itself. The parallel exposure of distortion in another assembly makes the mechanism explicit — (3:78 — **“There are some among them who distort the Book with their tongues to make you think this ˹distortion˺ is from the Book—but”**) — where the instrument named is the tongue and the product named is confusion about what the Book carries. The educator's duty in every generation follows from the division of the dock, and the reason the passage divides the dock at all is to fix that duty in its order of weight. Those who teach religion answer for what their students quote, because the supply of a pleasing lie is the first act of the crime and the repetition of it by an honest mouth is only the echo. A congregation today that screens its teachers by their receipts rather than their fidelity will find this verse rearranging its hiring practices.
