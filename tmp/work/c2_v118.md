**TWO REQUESTS, ONE TEMPERAMENT**

The passage then hands the whole case of the refusers its portrait: ***“Those who have no knowledge say, “If only Allah would speak to us or a sign would come to us!” ”***. The two requests are the temperaments of the same refusal. Ibn ʿAbbās hears the speakers as the Jews asking for direct speech and for a confirming sign, with the promise hiding behind the request: then we would surely believe. The attribution of the speakers in the sources runs both ways, and the spread is itself a finding. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, al-Rabīʿ b. Anas, Qatādah, and al-Suddī hear the statement as the statement of the Arab disbelievers. The speakers named in one reading and the speakers named in the other are separated by a peninsula and a library. The verse has an answer for the separation, and the answer is the next clause.

**THE HEARTS THAT MATCH**

The portrait closes with the line that completes the portrait: ***“The same was said by those who came before”***. The likeness-clause follows as the verse's verdict on the whole exchange: ***“Their hearts are all alike. Indeed, We have made the signs clear for people of sure faith”***. The reason the census-word matters is that it moves the problem from the evidence to the receiving organ. The companion's tradition compresses the likeness into one stroke: their words and their hearts are the same. The likeness is the verse's jurisprudence of temperament. A demand framed as inquiry and a demand framed as mockery are the same demand when the receiving heart is sealed against the answer. The likeness has been described in this surah at the level of the organ itself: (2:18 — **“They are ˹wilfully˺ deaf, dumb, and blind, so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺”**). The storm of the metaphor was drawn in the same passage's image-table: (2:20 — **“It is as if the lightning were about to snatch away their sight. Whenever lightning strikes, they walk in its light,”**). The corruption that matches across the two readings is the corruption the covenant-passages already itemized: (2:75 — **“then knowingly corrupt it after understanding it”**). The request for one more sign is therefore filed under the temperament rather than under the evidence.

**THE EVIDENCE THE REQUESTS REJECT**

Notice the closing clause of the verse in its full force: the signs have been made clear for a people who know. The clarity is not the missing piece of the exchange. The requests arrive after the clarity. The glosses of the passage describe the demands as the devices of a malevolence that has run out of arguments and taken up scheduling instead. If the sign is one century late, the demand will be made in the next century. The modern reader watches the same two requests circulate in every comment section that asks for a demonstration before it will read a document. The sign has been sent. The document is open. The temperament is the variable. The verse's census counts the hearts, not the signs, and the census is why the passage can promise the clarity to the knowing while the requests continue their rounds.

**THE AMBIGUITY THAT SERVES THE PORTRAIT**

The double attribution in the tradition is worth keeping rather than resolving, because the portrait is bigger than any one party. The verse says their hearts are all alike. The likeness is then demonstrated by the sources themselves. Two committees of historians name two different sets of speakers for the same sentence, and the sentence survives both attributions unchanged. The claim of the request is portable. That is the point. It traveled from the old communities to the new city without losing a syllable. The portrait also explains the address of the clarity clause. The knowing people are not the ones making the requests. The requests come from the temperament. The clarity is delivered to the temperament's opposite. The exchange is therefore not an auction of signs. It is a census of hearts with the signs as the constant. The census runs in every generation, and the census-word in the verse is the one verdict the two committees share. Alike. The request for the sign is the sign that the temperament has closed. The porters of the request keep moving it between the cities. The census stays at one number. The verse keeps both facts in view at once, the movement of the request and the stillness of the count.
