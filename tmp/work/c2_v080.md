**COUNTED DAYS AND THEIR INVENTORIES**

The claim that meets the woe is a claim about arithmetic: ***“˹Some of˺ the Jews claim, “The Fire will not touch us except”***. The inventories of the counted days differ across the reports, and the differences are themselves informative. One account takes the number as forty, the tally of the days their forefathers worshipped the calf, after which the Fire was promised to cease. Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah, and others give the figure, and the counting is transparent: forty days of punishment for forty days of apostasy. Another account takes the number as seven — one day for each thousand years of the world's seven thousand. The reckoning is reported from Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid in the same shape: the world is seven thousand years, and the torment is one day of the last for each thousand of the first. A third report gives forty years. A fourth detail sharpens the confidence into insult. Some held that after their counted term another people would inherit the punishment in their place. The narrated setting of the claim shows the confidence being aired as a taunt. The Prophet asked the gathered community who the people of the Fire were. They answered that they were, for a while, and that he and his followers would replace them. The clause of counted days in the verse then plays its own grammatical trick. The number is left unnamed because the claimants themselves had fixed it, and the text declines to repeat their arithmetic as though it were worth the ink.

**THE CLAIM'S HIDDEN SCAFFOLDING**

Behind the arithmetic stands a structure of assumptions, and the tradition dismantles it with patience. The claimants reasoned that the law of Moses had not been abrogated, that they were therefore believers, and that a believer's stay in the Fire is a term certain before release. The first assumption is the load-bearing one, and it fails in two places at once. Their own book never declared its law eternal. Their denial of the prophets after Moses places them outside the category their argument requires. No scripture of God promises the deniers of His messengers a release after a counted term. The theological principle the abuse rests on is real. The books do describe believing offenders as removed from the Fire after their term. But the principle belongs to believers, and the claimants had spent the section proving they were something else. The point is that a true doctrine was pressed into the service of a false self-diagnosis, and the verse attacks the diagnosis rather than the doctrine.

**THE PLEDGE NOBODY SIGNED**

The answer then splits the claim into two exhaustive branches: ***“for a number of days.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Have you taken a pledge from Allah”***, ***“for Allah never breaks His word”***. The dilemma is built in the tradition with old precision. Either the claimants hold a covenant from their Lord, in which case the claim stands and the covenant holds. Or they are saying about God what they do not know, in which case the claim is invention, and the inventors have assigned terms to their Lord without a text. The known history of the claimants settles which branch they stand on. A people who belied prophet after prophet hold no signed pledge, and the second branch closes over them. The middle clause about God never breaking His word is then doing quiet work. It concedes the fidelity of God in order to remove the last shelter of the claim: fidelity to a promise cannot manufacture a promise that was never made. The parallel statement of the same claim elsewhere in the Book labels its psychology without ceremony: (3:24 — **“for a few days.” They have been deceived in their faith by their wishful lying”**). The counterweight of the general law stands where the law states it: (4:93 — **“And whoever kills a believer intentionally, their reward will be Hell—where they will stay indefinitely. Allah will be displeased with them,”**).

**PROMISE AND PERMISSION KEPT APART**

The passage, read whole, governs a question the tradition treats with unusual care, and the care is visible in a threefold distinction. First, the verse rebukes the confident figure flung without a promise, not the bare possibility of a believing sinner's release after a term. The indignation attaches to the invented number and the insolence of its confidence. Second, no general amnesty is assumed for a whole population. The books describe individual forgiveness, not demographic exemptions. Third — and this is the hinge — there is a difference between saying that God will not remove a believing offender and saying that God never promised to remove one. What turns on that difference is the whole space of hope. The absence of a promise is not the absence of an act, and the Lord of the affair acts by wisdom even where He has published no terms. The verse therefore disciplines the tongue rather than the doctrine. The doctrine of divine mercy stands untouched, and the claim of automatic tenure falls.

**THE DEMAND THE QUESTION MAKES**

The interrogative that answers the claim has two horns and no third: ***“or are you ˹just˺ saying about Allah what you do not know?””***. The tradition states the logic in one line. The truth of their claim depends on one of two matters with no third available. The sentence then leaves the claimants holding either a document or a fabrication. The practical demand this places on every later audience is exactly the demand the verse models. Religious promises of automatic amnesty are claims about the afterlife's contract law, and the verse teaches the only admissible procedure: produce the pledge in its text, or withdraw the claim and return to hope joined to works. The demand for that proof is put in its own words a little further on: (2:111 — **“˹O Prophet,˺ “Show ˹me˺ your proof if what you say is true.””**). The covenant they were actually given says something else entirely, since the pledge on record is a moral one they broke rather than a demographic guarantee they hold: (2:84 — **“And ˹remember˺ when We took your covenant that you would neither shed each other’s blood nor expel each other from their homes,”**). The effect of the question is procedural, because it converts a slogan into a citation request. In other words, the passage does not trade insult with the claim. It asks for the signature, and silence answers for the claimants. A community today that markets guarantees the books never issued will find this passage asking for the signature first.
