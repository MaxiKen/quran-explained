**DISBELIEF WORN DOWN**

***“believers and destroy the disbelievers”*** The metallurgy's placement completes the law's whole consolation-architecture. The community's protest-question. How could the day turn against the truth's own people. Is answered by the purposes' census. The revealing (140), the drafting of witnesses (140), the purifying and the effacing (this verse). What was revealed (the reality displayed), what was drafted (the losses elevated), what was purified (the admixture burned), and what, in the adversary's swelling, was already being effaced. The rotation trusted as the Assayer's schedule, The following verse turns the law's edge into a standing question. Do you think you will enter Paradise without Allah proving which of you truly struggled and patiently endured. This matters because a trial can serve opposite ends at once: correction for a faithful community and exhaustion for a hostile project. Since both outcomes unfold over time, an immediate score cannot reveal the full purpose of the day. Imagine fire refining metal while consuming the impurity separated from it. In connection with believers purified by the trial, Ibn ʿAbbās explains purification as forgiveness for believers through what befalls them, while hostile disbelief is steadily destroyed. Distinguishing believers does not imply that Allah first lacked knowledge of them; which means the distinction appears in lived conduct, where hidden loyalty becomes a public fact and can be judged as deed. Trial also removes what clings to a community without sharing its truth; therefore this cleansing can hurt because false confidence, concealed motives, and undisciplined habits are often exposed through loss. The destruction of disbelief is therefore paired with the purification of faith; in other words one process prepares the faithful; the other carries denial toward its end. A community that wants only comfort may resist the very correction needed for long-term strength; hence in current institutions, a crisis often reveals who serves the mission, who serves status, and which habits cannot survive pressure. The verse does not permit people to invent private tests for one another; this is why it explains Allah’s wisdom in trials already permitted, calling believers to accept correction without claiming His complete knowledge of hearts. Purification is not destruction of the believer’s core. It removes what weakens that core, much as heat separates impurity from metal. The process can therefore be severe while its purpose remains mercy. What survives the test is better known, more honest about its limits, and less dependent on claims that pressure has already disproved.
