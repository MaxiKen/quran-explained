**ENVY WITH A POLICY**

The passage then names the motive force behind the whole campaign of demands: ***“Many among the People of the Book wish they could turn you ˹believers˺ back to disbelief because of their envy, after the truth has been made clear to them.”***. Ibn ʿAbbās paints the wish with its faces again. Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf and his companions, Finhāṣ b. AbīʿAzūrah and his companions — the wish had offices and committees. The same gloss names the addressees of the warning, the men being recruited back: ʿAmmār, Ḥudhayfah, and Muʿādh b. Jabal. The envy is then located with the precision the sentence deserves: it is of their own account, resentful envy, arising after the truth had become manifest. The older gloss puts the manifesting in the right venue. The truth about this messenger had become clear to them in the Torah. The envy is therefore not the envy of the uninformed. It is the envy of the informed.

**THE COMMAND TO STAND DOWN**

The command that follows is one of the hardest in the passage: ***“ Pardon and bear with them until Allah delivers His decision”***. The instruction has two halves, and the tradition hears both. Pardon names the hands kept off. Bear names the tempers kept still. The command is not consent. It is the disciplined stand-down of a community that has been armed with the truth and ordered to wait for the verdict. The reminder attached to that verdict is the patience-anchor of the passage: (2:214 — **““When will Allah’s help come?” Indeed, Allah’s help is ˹always˺ near”**). The help being near is the reason the stand-down is possible. A community governed by its wounds and one governed by its verdict keep different calendars. The first retaliates on schedule. The second pardons on schedule. The point of the verse is that the timetable of the retaliation is not the community's to set.

**THE FREEDOM OF THE VERDICT**

The closing clause of the unit is the ownership survey again, in the sharpest possible wording: the decision is delivered by the one who does what he wills. The companion of that freedom has been stated in this surah without softness: (2:253 — **“if Allah had willed, they would not have fought one another. But Allah does what He wills”**). The envy of the wishers is therefore answered twice. The truth they resent was manifest in their own shelves. The decision they dread is scheduled at the ownership level. The tradition of the passage adds the detail that makes the envy legible. The stratagems kept arriving even after repeated failure, because envy does not retire. The modern reader meets the same personality in every comment section where a clear answer is met with a wish that the answer were otherwise. The verse replies to the wish with the stand-down and the verdict. Pardon now. The judgment is booked.

**THE WISH AND THE WILL**

The point of the balance is worth stating plainly. Notice the balance the passage builds between two verbs. The wish belongs to the enviers. The will belongs to the owner. The wish wants a reversal that has already been answered by the manifest truth. The will delivers a decision that cannot be lobbied. The balance is the surah's answer to every campaign of turning-back. The recruiting is old. The names change. The armory is always the same: spectacle at the door and envy at the desk. The verse files both under one accounting and then orders the believers to spend their day elsewhere. Prayer, alms, patience — the work of the verdict is not the servant's overtime. That is the relief inside the hardest command of the unit. The case is not being neglected. It is simply above the pay grade of the street. The stand-down is therefore not the silence of the defeated. It is the schedule of the confident. The community ordered to pardon is a community whose case has already been heard in the court that decides without the lobbyists. The envoy of the verdict is on the road. The campaigners can retire their wish at the door. The wish they retire has been fully itemized in the passage. It began with the informed shelves and the manifest truth. It continued through the spectacles and the stratagems. It will end at the decision already calendared at the ownership level. The envier keeps the calendar of the grievance. The servant keeps the calendar of the owner. Only one of the two calendars ends.
