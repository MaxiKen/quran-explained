**ANGER HELD UNDER CONTROL**

***“˹They are˺ those who donate”*** ˹They are˺ those who donate in prosperity and adversity opens the estate-heirs' portrait. And the first credential's two conditions are its calibration. Alladhina yunfiquna fis-sarra'i wad-darra'i. Who spend in ease and in hardship. Giving as constant, not as surplus's overflow. The prosperous hand open and the pressed hand open still. The two-weather clause converting charity from transaction to character. The muttaqi identified not by his gifts' size but by their independence from his seasons. (3:133 — **“And hasten towards forgiveness from your Lord and a Paradise as vast”**) ***“in prosperity and adversity, control”*** Control their anger, and pardon others continues the portrait into the tempers. And the two credentials' sequence is the verse's studied ascent. Wal-kazimina l-ghayz. And the swallowers of rage. The kazm-word. The waterskin's mouth tied on its fullness. Anger present, felt, and contained. Not the passionless (no virtue in a temper never tested) but the governed. The Prophet's own definition, peace be upon him. The strong is not the one who overcomes in wrestling. The strong is the one who controls himself at anger, with the swallowing's price posted.

**PEOPLE FREELY PARDONED**

***“their anger, and pardon others.”*** Whoever swallows rage while able to enact it, Allah will call him before all creation to choose of the maidens what he wills. Then the ascent's next stair. Wal-afina ani n-nas. And the pardoners of people. Past containment to release. The offence not merely unavenged but unheld. The rage swallowed, then the account itself torn up. The portrait’s placement in the Uhud lessons gives it a living edge. Revealed over a community nursing the day's rages. The dead unavenged, the mutilations seen, the deserters known by name. The credentials command the war-wounded themselves to the swallowing and the pardon. The estate's heirs formed, precisely, in the tempers the field inflamed. (3:135 — **“˹They are˺ those who, upon committing an evil deed or wronging themselves,”**) People freely pardoned remains urgent today because moral excellence appears in budgets, heated conversations, and the decision not to retaliate when retaliation is possible.

**EXCELLENCE LOVED BY ALLAH**

***“And Allah loves the good-doers”*** And Allah loves the good-doers seals the portrait’s first panel with the love-clause at its highest register. Wallahu yuhibbu l-muhsinin. And Allah loves the excellent. And the sealing's grammar completes the ascent. The three credentials climbed from giving, through containment, to pardon. And the seal names the summit’s rank. Ihsan. Excellence. The pardoner ranked among the muhsinin because pardon is excellence's social form. The beauty done beyond the owed. The love-clause itself is the portrait’s true wage. The estate was announced, but the panel closes not at the property's grant but at the Owner's love. The racing's real prize, toward which the portrait’s next panel turns with its most merciful credential. The sinners who remember, seek forgiveness, and refuse to persist. Imagine a strong person lowering a raised hand and opening it to give instead. Concerning giving in ease and hardship, Ibn ʿAbbās describes the mindful as generous in comfort and hardship, able to restrain anger, and willing to pardon both dependent and free people. The qualities form a connected portrait rather than a list of unrelated virtues; which means spending in ease proves generosity when giving is comfortable, while spending in hardship proves that generosity is not dependent on surplus. Controlling anger prevents an inner force from becoming outward injustice; therefore pardoning people then goes further than restraint by releasing a claim that could lawfully have been pressed. Excellence gathers all three because it places obedience above possession, emotion, and injured pride; in other words none of this requires leaving abuse unchecked or denying another person’s rights. Pardon is virtuous where it repairs, while justice remains available where protection requires it; hence today, anger can be published instantly and kept alive by an audience. The verse inserts worship between feeling and action; this is why a believer may feel the heat, but the feeling does not receive authority to speak, spend, or punish as it wishes. Allah’s love is attached to the disciplined good that follows. Spending and anger both involve release: wealth leaves the hand, while anger seeks release through the tongue or power. Mindfulness governs each movement. The first is released for good; the second is held until justice and mercy can decide its proper course. Pardon then shows strength under command, not an inability to respond.
