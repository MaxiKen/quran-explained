**DEAF ON PURPOSE, AND BLIND ON PURPOSE**

The image ends with a description of the people in it, and the description is a diagnosis: ***“They are ˹wilfully˺ deaf, dumb”***, and ***“and blind, so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺”***. The translation carries a word that does the work of a paragraph, since the deafness is not an accident of birth but a choice. The earliest reports describe them as pretending to be deaf, pretending to be mute, and pretending to be blind, and the reports agree that the faculties are intact. What is missing is the willingness to use them on this matter. The verse is describing a person who has closed his own doors and then complains that the house is dark, which is a condition no amount of new information can repair.

The three defects are arranged so that each one blocks a different stage. A deaf man cannot receive the message, a mute man cannot repeat or defend what he has received, and a blind man cannot see the road he is walking. A person in that condition is not merely uninformed; he is unable to be moved, unable to answer, and unable to find his way back without help from outside. That is why the sentence does not end with a description but with a consequence: ***“and blind, so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺”***. Return is the word used for coming back to a place a person has left, and the verse says the trip has become impossible in his present state.

**THE PARABLE OF THE SHEPHERD'S CALL**

The Book gives the same portrait in another sūrah, and the comparison there explains why the words of the message never arrive: (2:171 — **“The example of the disbelievers ˹not responding to the Messenger's warning˺ is like a flock not comprehending the calls”**). A flock hears a voice and does not understand the language; the shepherd's call is a sound it cannot process. The verse uses this to say that the problem is not volume. The message is not too quiet, and the messenger is not speaking badly; the instrument at the receiving end simply does not do the work, and the failure is located in the listener rather than in the message or in the one who delivers it.

The Book also shows where such damage is measured, and the measurement is not made at the eye or the ear: (22:46 — **“it is not the eyes that are blind, but it is the hearts in the chests that grow blind”**). The verse chose the chest rather than the head for the same reason, because the location of the blindness decides what kind of remedy can help. That sentence settles the question of what the earlier image was pointing at. A man with perfect eyesight can watch the truth go by for thirty years and never see anything worth changing his life for, because the seeing happens somewhere else. The verse in this sūrah names the same location when it says the fire was taken away and the man was left unable to see, and the two statements describe one condition from two directions: the faculties fail at a place the reader cannot inspect.

**THE WAY BACK IS THROUGH THE EAR**

The last clause leaves the reader with the plainest of questions. If a person has chosen not to hear, and cannot see, and has nothing to say, where does recovery begin? The answer given by the whole passage is that it begins with the one faculty he can still open, and the Book consistently names listening as the first step. A man who decides to listen to the message once more, without the commentary he has been carrying about the people who bring it, has already begun to reverse the condition. Nothing in the verse suggests that the door is shut for a particular individual; it describes a state, and states can change while a person is alive. The step available this week is simple enough to be uncomfortable. Take the claim that has been dismissed for years, find it presented in its strongest form, and read or listen to it all the way through without interrupting and without preparing a reply. That is what the deaf, mute, and blind man in this verse would have to do to escape his description, and it costs nothing except the comfort of an old opinion. The reader who does it has already used the two faculties that the verse reports as surrendered.
