**THEY HAVE ORGANS OF SENSE BUT REFUSE THEIR MORAL USE**

The brief verdict completes the fire scene: ***“They are ˹wilfully˺ deaf, dumb”***. The translation’s added wilfully keeps the image tied to choice. These people heard the Qur’an, spoke claims of faith, and watched the believers whom they mocked. Their bodies could receive sound and form words. What failed was the use of those gifts for truth. Ibn ʿAbbās says they behaved as though deaf, mute, and blind. They made themselves unresponsive when guidance called, though they stayed keenly alert to rank, fear, and gain. This is why another verse joins the same two traits to a lack of understanding: (8:22 — **“the ˹wilfully˺ deaf and dumb, who do not understand”**). Hearing in the moral sense is more than sound reaching the ear. It includes attention, recognition, and a will to answer. A warning that causes no honest thought has not been heard in the way revelation seeks. The fault lies not in a limit of the body but in a heart that trains the body’s powers to serve every aim except truth.

**A TONGUE FULL OF CLAIMS CAN STILL BE DUMB TO GOOD**

Their muteness may seem strange after so many verses record their speech. They say they believe, call themselves reformers, insult the faithful, assure their chiefs, and boast of mockery. The very amount of speech proves that physical silence is not meant. They are mute toward the good word that would make speech true: open faith, confession of wrong, sound counsel, and praise of the truth they know. Speech does not fulfil its gift merely by being clever or abundant. It fulfils it when the tongue bears witness to what is right and binds the speaker to it. The contrast with faith appears in a simple response. When true believers are called to God and His Messenger for judgment, their answer (24:51 — **“is to say, “We hear and obey.” It is they who will ˹truly˺ succeed”**). Hearing becomes speech, and speech opens into action. The hypocrites break that chain at each point. They hear but do not yield, speak but do not tell the inward truth, and act without the loyalty their words claim. Ibn ʿAbbās’s description of their pretended muteness therefore reaches the core: they have words for disguise but none for return. The verse does not belittle anyone who cannot speak. A person without a voice may bear the clearest witness through faith and deed. The blame falls on those who possess a way to affirm truth and choose to use it against truth.

**BLINDNESS HERE BELONGS TO THE HEART’S JUDGMENT**

The description continues ***“and blind, so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺”***. Verse 17 left the people in darkness; this verse shows that their long refusal has also spoiled the sight by which a path is known. The Qur’an states the difference from bodily blindness without any doubt: (22:46 — **“it is not the eyes that are blind, but it is the hearts in the chests that grow blind”**). Their eyes can note which group is strong and where a worldly chance lies. They fail to see the worth of guidance, the ugliness of deceit, and the end toward which their choices lead. The three lost powers form a full account of failed response. The ear does not receive, the tongue does not confess, and the eye does not find the road. Knowledge can still sit in memory while bringing no benefit. Earlier peoples were given hearing, sight, and minds, (46:26 — **“But neither their hearing, sight, nor intellect were of any benefit to them whatsoever,”**) because they kept denying the signs. Gifts become proof against the person when their true use is refused. They are like clear windows that have been painted over from within: daylight remains outside, but the chosen layer blocks its help. Each fresh coat makes the room darker and makes the darkness feel more normal.

**RETURN REQUIRES AN OPENING THEY KEEP SHUT**

The closing result is not a claim that repentance has no value or that a living reader should despair after sin. It describes the people in the parable as they persist in the condition God has exposed. They do not return because they have disabled every route by which correction would be welcomed. Advice is not heard, truth is not owned aloud, and the safe road is not seen as safe. The earlier verses also gave the order: they chose error, mocked faith, and were then left to wander. Judgment meets a will that keeps shutting the door. For the reader today, the opposite path is concrete. One listens long enough for a hard truth to reach the heart. One says what is true even when the old mask loses value. One looks beyond the nearest gain to the road and its end. Revelation remains (50:37 — **“a reminder for whoever has a ˹mindful˺ heart and lends an attentive ear”**). An attentive ear is still an opening. The verse warns against closing it by habit, not against seeking help for a bodily limit. Faith can be whole in a person who cannot hear, speak, or see; it is gravely broken in one who has all three powers but turns each away from God.
