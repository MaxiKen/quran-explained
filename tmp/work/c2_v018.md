**THE THREE SENSES THE PICTURE LEAVES CLOSED**

Three closed faculties draw the condition in a single portrait: ***“They are ˹wilfully˺ deaf, dumb”***, ***“and blind, so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺”***. The wording names the three instruments a person uses to receive the truth, to speak it and to follow it, and it closes all three. Ibn ʿAbbās read the description as men who made themselves deaf, dumb and blind, and the early teachers generally understood the three as a description of deliberate refusal rather than of physical defect. The Book uses the same three for people whose faculties were given and then left unused, and it adds a comparison that removes any doubt about the fault: (7:179 — **“They have hearts they do not understand with, eyes they do not see with, and ears they do not hear with”**). The three instruments are named instead of the men so that the reader tests his own use of them, since the danger is not the absence of the equipment but its disuse.

**CHOSEN DEAFNESS AND ITS END**

The translation carries the sense that the deafness here is a willed condition, and another sūrah states the same thing with the same word: (6:39 — **“Those who deny Our signs are ˹wilfully˺ deaf and dumb—lost in darkness”**). The wording matters because it answers a complaint that a person may raise about his own hardness: the Book does not describe a man who was never given ears, and it describes a man who turned them off. The mute part of the description was explained as the inability to speak of anything good, which means the silence is not modesty but the loss of the tongue's first purpose. What turns on the distinction is that a person still carries the ability while he refuses the use, and the refusal is a decision that is made again every time the truth arrives. The Book never describes a person as beyond hearing while he is still able to hear, and the verse leaves the door where it found it.

**THE END OF THE ROAD IN THE LAST WORDS**

The sentence closes with a verdict about return: ***“they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺”***. The wording describes a turning back to a place a person has left, and it says the turning will not happen. The Book states the same outcome for people who have blinded the instruments of their hearts and adds the reason they cannot see: (22:46 — **“it is not the eyes that are blind, but it is the hearts in the chests that grow blind”**). The phrase was understood as a description of a settled direction rather than a decree about a person who still intends to turn, and the difference between the two readings matters to anyone who fears for a relative. The verse describes the ones who have been fully described already in the passage, and it does not give the community a licence to write anyone off while he is alive. What follows is a longer picture that includes the same men in a storm, and the picture gives them movement even while their eyes remain closed.

**SEALED SENSES LEAVE NO ROAD HOME**

Think of a man who nails his windows shut against a noise he dislikes and then complains that the house has no air. The verse describes the same arrangement in the matter of hearing, and it treats the closed windows as his own work. These days the instruments are fed or starved by what a person chooses to read, watch and repeat, and the passage tells him what happens to equipment that is never used for the purpose it was given. Nobody is asked to despise the men in the picture, and the reader is asked instead to notice that their road began with small refusals which each looked reasonable at the time. A person who can trace the first of those refusals in his own record is in a better position than one who only recognises it in a stranger. The sūrah turns next to the second picture, which shows the same people caught in weather they cannot manage, and the change in the image is a change in the kind of danger that surrounds them.
