**TWO HILLS AS SIGNS**

The rites of the House receive two standing landmarks. ***“Indeed, ˹the hills of˺ Ṣafa”*** ***“and Marwah are among the symbols of Allah.”***. Two hills near the House. Two waymarks among the waymarks. Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss places the walking among the rites that Allah commanded for the pilgrimage. The geography of the House is folded into the ritual itself. The point is that the terrain is consecrated by decree. The body of the pilgrim walks through a theology. The two hills carry the memory of a searching mother and a family. The walking between them makes the memory in the feet. What follows is that the rites of the pilgrimage are given an address in the world rather than in the air. The symbols are named by the one who raised the mountain. The effect of the naming is that the practice survives every retelling. The pilgrim circles the House and paces the hills on the map of the same owner.

**THE WALK THE IDOLS ONCE WATCHED**

The permission to walk arrives with a history attached. ***“So whoever performs the pilgrimage”*** ***“or minor pilgrimage, let them walk between ˹the two hills˺.”***. The believers had disliked the walking. Ibn ʿAbbās keeps the reason in view. The idols stood at the two hills and the pagans used to stroke them there. The conscience of the converts said this practice belonged to the old world. The verse removes the veto and re-consecrates the stones. The point of the reveal-context is that the symbols never belonged to the borrowers. The idols had leased the hills for a season. The report of the pacing's own status keeps the ruling inside the prophetic practice. The Prophet said: *"Begin with what God has begun"* meaning the first hill, as the report is recorded by Muslim. Another report gives the pacing its rank: *"God has prescribed for you the pacing"* in the collection of al-Bayhaqī. The effect is that the walk stands with a deed and a date. The pilgrimage's ethics stand beside the walk itself (2:197 — **“the best provision is righteousness. And be mindful of Me, O people of reason”**). The feet walk. The character supplies the provision.

**A GOOD THAT IS APPRECIATED**

The verse closes the rite with the names of the one who receives it. ***“And whoever does good willingly, Allah is truly Appreciative, All-Knowing”***. The willing good is seen and appreciated at the address of the two names. The fear of the unaccepted act is answered by the appreciative one. The fear of the unseen act is answered by the knowing one. The early commentators read the willing clause as covering the extra walk and every extra kindness beside the obligation. The volunteer's good is not surplus. It is received, and the smallest of it is accepted. The rites of the House are named in their pilgrim's form elsewhere (22:29 — **“Then let them groom themselves, fulfil their vows, and circle the Ancient House.””**). The grooming and the vows and the circling stand in one line. For the modern reader the closing names reframe the whole rite. The walk between two hills is not a hoop. It is a gift delivered to an appreciative address. This age counts its rituals as obligations and its hobbies as gifts. The verse counts the willing good as both, and the knowing one keeps the count.

**STONES WITH DEEDS ATTACHED**

The re-consecration of the two hills is the passage's model for all inherited practice. The stones stood in the old world under borrowed names. The verse re-titles the stones by decree and attaches the walking to the rites of the House. The effect of the re-titling is that the geography of the pilgrimage becomes a walking archive. Every circuit of the House is a memory. Every pacing of the hills is a memory made in the feet. The reason the prophetic practice is cited beside the permission is that the rite is a handover, not an invention. The handover carries the deed and the date. For the modern reader the model settles the anxiety of borrowed forms. This age inherits practices the way it inherits furniture, and the dust makes people nervous. The verse shows the procedure. Re-title by the owner's word. Attach the deed. Walk it with the willing good in the heart. The hills stand. The deeds are recorded. The appreciative one keeps the register open.
