**FAITH IS SET BEFORE THEM AS A LIVED REALITY**

A second appeal now follows the ban on ruin: ***“And when they are told, “Believe”***. The call is not for one more public claim, since they already say that they believe. It asks for the truth that joins heart, tongue, and deed. The next words, ***“as others believe,” they reply, “Will we believe”***, point to faith already lived in front of them. Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, Abū al-ʿĀliyah, and al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas identify these people as the Prophet’s Companions. Ibn ʿAbbās also names Ibn Salām and those with him, who believed from among the People of the Book. They trusted God, His Messenger, His books, the life to come, and the commands that put faith into practice. The comparison does not turn truth into a vote or ask for blind praise of a crowd. It points to those who received the message with a whole response, in contrast to those who borrowed its words as a mask. The close of this surah shows the same shared faith: (2:285 — **“and so do the believers. They ˹all˺ believe in Allah, His angels, His Books,”**). Their cure is thus not better acting. It is the faith whose outward form they had copied while refusing its core.

**PRIDE CALLS COSTLY FAITH FOOLISH**

Their scorn breaks out in the words ***“as the fools believe?””*** They mean the Companions who left homes, bore the anger of foes, shared wealth, and put loyalty to God above quick safety. The hidden group reads those costs through a small measure of gain. To risk rank for truth looks foolish if rank is the highest good. To give up wealth for a brother looks foolish if the self has the first and last claim. To obey when desire pulls elsewhere looks foolish if freedom has been cut down to doing what desire wants. Their slur therefore reveals their scale more than it describes the believers. They count what faith gives up at once, but they never count what its truth gains.

The lives they mock give a different account of wisdom. The people of Madinah welcomed those who came to them and (59:9 — **“give ˹the emigrants˺ preference over themselves even though they may be in need.”**). That choice may look like loss in a short account, but God calls those saved from greed the ones who thrive. The hypocrite guards the near gain and spends the lasting self; the believer may spend a near gain and guard the lasting self. This is the heart of the clash. It is not that faith praises poor thought or asks people to ignore cause and effect. It widens the field of cause and effect to include duty, the state of the soul, death, and judgment. Once those facts are counted, the sacrifice that seemed foolish may prove to be the sanest choice.

**THE CHARGE IS RETURNED BY PERFECT KNOWLEDGE**

The answer begins, ***“Indeed, it is they”***, and ends with those ***“who are fools, but they do not know”***. In this setting, folly is failure to know one’s true good and the path that secures it. These people are skilled at reading the balance of power and shaping speech for each group. But skill at gaining a short edge is not wisdom when the edge leads to ruin. A sailor may know each rope on the ship yet be a fool if he steers straight toward the reef. In the same way, social skill without a true end can make error more swift, not less. Their plan saves an image for a day and spends the soul that must face God.

The Qur’an often parts showy skill from sound knowledge. Some past peoples took pride in what they knew when clear proofs came, then were (40:83 — **“˹ultimately˺ overwhelmed by what they used to ridicule”**). The hypocrites themselves can look and sound strong: (63:4 — **“When you see them, their appearance impresses you. And when they speak, you listen to their ˹impressive˺ speech.”**). Neither fine speech nor worldly sense repairs the broken aim within. The end of knowledge is lost when pride first decides what may count as true. God’s reply is exact because He knows both the hidden end they seek and the end they cannot see. Their insult cannot lower the people of faith, but it does disclose the pride that blocks their own return.

**A DIVINE VERDICT IS NOT A LICENSE TO MOCK**

God names the folly of a known group whose hearts He has exposed. A believer must not copy their habit by treating every person of less skill, schooling, wealth, or rank as beneath notice. The later rule is plain: (49:11 — **“Do not let some ˹men˺ ridicule others, they may be better than them, nor let ˹some˺ women ridicule other women,”**). Human rank is often wrong because it sees one gift, one fault, or one day. God sees the whole person. That limit guards justice. The verse also does not condemn careful questions. The Companions did ask, learn, weigh reports, and seek clear guidance. What it condemns is pride that decides in advance that humble trust must be beneath the proud.

This danger remains close today. A person may call prayer naïve, restraint weak, charity wasteful, or repentance a loss of face. He may also use the verse in reverse and call sound study foolish merely because it tests an inherited claim. Both moves avoid the hard work of truth. Faith does not fear sound proof, and reason does not reach its proper end by leaving God out. The test is whether knowledge bows when truth becomes clear and whether it guides the self toward its real good. The hypocrites fail because they make their own status the measure of wisdom. The faithful learn from those who bore the truth with honesty, seek proof without scorn, and count the Last Day among the most real facts in any choice.
