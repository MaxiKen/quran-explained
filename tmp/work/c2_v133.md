**THE DEATHBED QUESTION**

The verse stages its whole theology inside one family hour. ***“Or did you witness”*** ***“when death came to Jacob?”***. The witness question works as a summons. The reader is put at the bedside before the answer is given. The father's question is the smallest creed in the passage. ***“He asked his children, “Who will you worship after my passing?””***. In the account of Ibn ʿAbbās the hour is the last test of the family line. The father does not ask about property or burial. He asks about the worship that will outlive him. The inheritance that matters is the altar. The point is that the deathbed question is a curriculum check. Every teaching of the House is compressed into the one word worship. The children are asked to answer with their mouths. What follows is that the verse treats the moment of death as the moment of maximum clarity. It is the hour when a family says what it actually holds. The reason the verse opens in the witness form is that the reader is being enrolled. Not as an audience. As a witness. The prayer of the dying prophet of the family states the same form (12:101 — **“and the earth! You are my Guardian in this world and the Hereafter. Allow me to die as one who submits”**).

**THE NAMES IN THE ANSWER**

The children's reply names its object before it gives its verb. ***“They replied, “We will ˹continue to˺ worship your God, the God of your forefathers”*** ***“Abraham, Ishmael, and Isaac”***. The list is the answer's evidence. The God being named is the God of a chain. The chain is named link by link so no one can claim the worship is an innovation. From Ibn ʿAbbās the family's speech stays attached to the patriarchs themselves. The forefathers are named so that the worship has a history. The history has a family. The effect is that the reply is a legal statement as much as a devotion. The God of our forefathers is a title of continuity. The difference between a private faith and an inherited faith is exactly this naming. The private faith must introduce its god. The inherited faith names the god with three patriarchs standing behind it. The reason the list stops at Isaac is that the verse counts the line the deathbed holds. The naming is the family's way of answering with its own records open.

**THE SUBMISSION OF THE WHOLE HOUSE**

The answer ends with the verb the whole passage has walked toward. ***“the One God. And to Him we ˹all˺ submit.””***. The plural is the point. The father asked a single question. The household answered with a single word. The word is the one the first father gave when his Lord ordered him. The account of Ibn ʿAbbās leaves the scene standing as the family's public record. The deathbed question. The patriarchs named. The submission spoken in the plural so that no child stands outside the answer. The verse closes the family's theology in the exact shape the earlier verse asked for. Die in the state of submission. Let the submission be a household word. For the modern reader the scene models how a religion is passed. Not by argument at the table. By a question at the end and an answer in the plural. This age plans its estates. It rarely plans its altars. The verse keeps the altar in the last will. The effect of the scene is that the reader learns what the House was built to produce. A family that answers worship questions in one voice. The reason the verse can end on the submit word is that the submitting is what the deathbed was checking for. The prophet's own prayer closes the circle (12:101 — **“and join me with the righteous.””**). The household answers in one word because the household learned one word. The learning took a lifetime. The saying takes a second. The list of names inside the answer is the household's evidence file. The God of the forefathers is a title with records attached. Abraham. Ishmael. Isaac. The line is short and the claim is large. The claim is that worship has a genealogy. The genealogy runs through a family that answered a question the same way at the end. The submission spoken in the plural is the family's signature. No child is outside the answer. No child is outside the verb. The altar named in the question is the altar kept in the will. The scene travels past the family to any household that plans its end. The planning is the inheritance's final form. The deathbed question is still being asked. The answer is still one word. The word still has a plural.
