**A PRACTICE THAT SURVIVES THE MOVE**

The command is repeated here in a form that covers every place a person might be: ***“Wherever you are ˹O Prophet˺, turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque”***. The repetition is not idle, since the obligation it states is the same whether one is at home or on a journey, and it prevents any impression that the direction binds only inside one city. The emphasis answers how hard the change was for the first community, since repetition steadies the ruling and quiets the unease it had stirred. The sentence does not describe a preference of the Prophet; it gives an order whose scope matches the whole earth. The verse that follows widens the same order to the believers. Its reach across every place makes obedience portable rather than local.

**REVELATION AS THE ORDER’S FOUNDATION**

What the order rests on is named in the next clause: ***“This is certainly the truth from your Lord.”*** The sentence does not argue from custom or from the practice of the ancestors; it gives the revelation as the ground, which is why the earlier disputes about the direction are beside the point. The command is given here for the third time in the passage, and the repetitions are tied to what comes before and after them, so that the ruling is fastened in place. Elsewhere the Book states the same nearness in a sentence that removes every excuse of distance: (57:4 — **“And He is with you wherever you are.”**). The community is not told that its direction is one option among several; it is told where the truth lies.

**A WATCHFULNESS THAT NEEDS NO REPORT**

The final clause describes the One who issued the command: ***“And Allah is never unaware of what you ˹all˺ do”***. The second person plural gathers the whole community into the sentence, so that the watching is not of the Prophet alone but of everyone who hears the order. The verb may be read as “you do” or as “they do”, and the two readings together cover the believers and those who dispute their direction. The phrase is taken by Ibn ʿAbbās as a reference to what people conceal about the qiblah of Abraham and about other matters, which makes the sentence a warning as much as an assurance. The closing words therefore watch both the outward act and the inward argument that surrounds it, and they leave no room for the pretence that the direction of prayer is a private matter. Nothing done in secret escapes the account.

**SENDING FROM EVERY TOWN**

Think of a man who must send his monthly letter from whichever town his work takes him to, and who keeps one address in view for all of them. The letter is not made more acceptable by the town it was posted from, and it is not made less acceptable by the distance it travelled. The same standing instruction is given to the community: the direction is fixed, while the place of the one facing it changes with every journey. This is why the repetition in the passage is practical rather than rhetorical, since travellers need the rule as much as residents do. Anyone who has had to keep a practice while moving from place to place will recognise the usefulness of the sentence.

**THE REPETITION AND WHAT IT SECURES**

Read with what follows, the verse secures the ruling before the argument for it is closed. The command is given, the ground of the command is named, and the knowledge of the One who gave it is stated; after that there is nothing left for the objectors to say. The passage has been answering objections since the direction was changed, and this sentence refuses to leave the matter as an open question. The Book's way is to settle a matter by naming its source rather than by multiplying proofs, and the same habit is at work here. The community is left with a direction it can carry into every place and a Lord who sees what it does with the command. Today, a stable direction allows worship to survive travel, migration and changing surroundings without becoming a local custom. The repeated command also prevents place from becoming an excuse, because the traveller and resident receive one obligation from one source. Its stability is therefore a mercy: changing locations requires fresh orientation, not a newly invented rule.
