**FACING THE HOUSE FROM ANYWHERE**

The command now speaks to the Prophet in his own geography. ***“Wherever you are ˹O Prophet˺, turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque.”***. Wherever. The word dissolves the map. The Prophet is released from the calculation of place. The same release was already standing at the House's founding (2:125 — **“for those who circle it, who meditate in it, and who bow and prostrate themselves ˹in prayer˺”**). The circle of the worshippers was designed for bodies in motion. The meditating and the bowing were always portable around a fixed point. The point of the wherever is therefore a design principle rather than a concession. The centre is fixed so the edges can move. What follows is that the believer's prayer-life acquires a spine. Every location hangs on the same point. The effect of the command is felt most in travel, in exile, in the hospital room and the work site. The face has somewhere to go in every place.

**TRUTH AT THE QIBLAH**

The sentence that follows labels the whole arrangement again. ***“This is certainly the truth from your Lord.”***. The certainty lands on the direction itself. Not merely on the book that orders it. The reading of Ibn ʿAbbās keeps the label attached to the redesign. The truth from the Lord is the standing-place of Abraham, the House, and the facing from anywhere. The parallel verse had certified the same in fewer words. The certification is repeated because the scene repeats its doubts. The effect of the repetition is a double lock. The Prophet's heart is locked to the truth. The believers' map is locked to the House. The difference between a custom and a truth is the address of the one who set it. A custom can be moved by a room. A truth is moved only by its Lord. The qiblah is therefore held at the rank of truth in the plainest grammar of the passage.

**A LORD WHO SEES THE TURNING**

The closing line covers the new design with the old gaze. ***“And Allah is never unaware of what you ˹all˺ do”***. The doing includes the turning. The turning is seen wherever it happens. The same custody clause had closed the command to the Prophet himself (2:144 — **“Allah is never unaware of what they do”**). The address widens from the Prophet to the whole community. For the modern reader the portability of the facing is the verse's practical gift. The prayer of the office and the prayer of the road share the address of the prayer of the city. This age schedules its devotions around its movements. The verse schedules the movements around one point. The unawareness is excluded from the arrangement. Every face that turns is noted. Every turn taken at the edge of the map is seen at the centre. The truth stands. The gaze holds. The facing goes on wherever the servant stands.

**ONE POINT, EVERY PLACE**

The portability clause deserves its own second look, because it re-engineered the geography of devotion. Before the turn, the direction was a possession of place. After the wherever, the direction is a possession of the person. The face carries the point with it into the ship and the field and the borrowed room. The effect of the portability is that the prayer-life acquires a spine that no itinerary can bend. The truth named in the verse is the pin that holds the map together. The reason the closing gaze is attached to the portable facing is precise. The servant is most exposed to forgetfulness exactly where the map is unfamiliar. The gaze covers the exposure. The servant walking in an unfamiliar city carries the whole apparatus of the prayer inside a single intention. The point does not move when the person moves, and the gaze does not thin at the edges of the map. For the modern reader the arrangement is the answer to the oldest complaint of the busy life. This age moves its people across cities and time-zones and calls the movement progress. The verse gives the movement a fixed point and the fixed point a watched address. The House stands. The faces turn. The turning is noted in every place.
