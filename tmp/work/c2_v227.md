**A DECISION AFTER THE WAITING PERIOD**

***“But if they settle on divorce,”*** establishes a decision after the waiting period. Following the rule of īlāʾ, this verse addresses the husband who resolves upon divorce rather than returning to marital intimacy. Divorce—ṭalāq—is permitted in Islamic law but is not treated as a casual weapon. It ends a covenant, affects children and families, and creates financial and emotional consequences. The decision must therefore be deliberate, not a passing threat made in anger. The verse says if they resolve—wa-in ʿazamū al-ṭalāq. Resolution means a settled decision, not every intrusive thought or sentence uttered during rage. The jurists discuss the legal effect of different expressions, intentions, and circumstances. Ordinary Muslims should seek qualified guidance rather than assuming that a threat, text message, or angry phrase has one universal ruling. The Qur’an does not deny that some marriages cannot safely or honestly continue. Abuse, persistent betrayal, irreconcilable conflict, or serious harm may make separation better than endless hostility. At the same time, divorce should follow attempts at counsel, mediation, and reform when these are safe and appropriate. The Prophet ﷺ said that the most hateful of lawful things to Allah is divorce, in a report whose chain is discussed by hadith scholars; the meaning is consistent with the Qur’an’s treatment of divorce as permissible but serious. **DIVORCE IS A LAWFUL LAST RESORT**

***“then Allah is indeed All-Hearing, All-Knowing”*** deepens divorce is a lawful last resort. This should not be used to trap victims in harm. It teaches that ending a marriage should not be the first response to every disappointment. Allah is Samīʿ, All-Hearing. He hears the divorce declaration, the private threat, the wife’s unanswered plea, the children’s fear, and the mediation that others may never know about. A person may manipulate witnesses or exploit legal ambiguity, but Allah knows the words and circumstances completely. He is also ʿAlīm, All-Knowing. He knows whether a husband intends release with dignity or seeks to punish his wife, whether a wife is seeking safety or being coerced, and whether relatives are mediating sincerely or protecting their own interest. The reminder places divorce under divine accountability rather than private power. The following verses provide rules for waiting periods, taking a wife back, compensation, and fair treatment. Divorce is not a moment when ordinary ethics disappear. The husband must not insult, expose private matters, withhold property, manipulate children, or use financial dependence to force compliance. The wife must also avoid false claims and injustice, while both parties remain responsible for their own conduct. Allah says elsewhere either retain a woman honourably or release her honourably. **THE DECLARATION IS FULLY HEARD**

This is the moral framework of the decision. A legal divorce obtained through cruelty may be technically effective while still leaving a grave sin before Allah. Divorce changes the marital relationship but does not erase parenthood. Children retain rights to maintenance, safety, affection, education, and contact according to what is beneficial. Neither parent may use children as messengers, weapons, or spies. The Qur’anic commands concerning breastfeeding and maintenance in 2:233 show that parental duties continue after separation. A person who chooses divorce should plan for housing, finances, custody, and emotional support. Seeking counselling and legal advice is not a lack of faith. It is part of fulfilling the trust Allah has placed in the family. The verse ends the possibility of indefinite suspension created by īlāʾ. If the husband will not return, he must proceed toward divorce rather than keep the wife in uncertainty. This limit also applies to other forms of abandonment. A spouse may not withhold support, intimacy, documentation, or communication to force the other person into emotional submission. Where divorce is necessary, releasing the other person can be an act of mercy. The aim is not to win the final argument but to prevent further harm and preserve the rights of everyone affected.
