**THE REVELATION NOW PLACED IN THE MESSENGER'S CARE**

The description continues: ***“and who believe in what has been revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺”***. What has come down to him is the Qur'an. Faith receives both its source and its claim to rule life. The wording says “revealed,” which means that the Messenger is neither its author nor the owner of its truth. He receives, conveys, teaches, and lives by what comes from above. This is why love for a messenger cannot be separated from trust in the message he bears. It is also why praise for the Qur'an without submission to it falls short of the faith named here. The Book repeats the command even to people already called believers: (4:136 — **“O believers! Have faith in Allah, His Messenger, the Book He has revealed to His Messenger, and the Scriptures He revealed before.”**). Faith therefore has to be renewed through assent and obedience. A revelation may be in a person's home, memory, or language while its judgment is still kept outside his choices.

**A PRESENT BOOK THAT HONOURS REVELATION BEFORE IT**

The sentence widens the line of faith to include ***“and what was revealed before you”***. Ibn ʿAbbās explained that believers accept what the former messengers brought from their Lord, without denying one messenger while claiming another. This joins the Qur'an to one history of divine speech. The Torah, the Gospel, and the other books were true as God sent them. Faith in the final Book does not require contempt for their divine origin. At the same time, this verse does not place every later copy, claim, or law on one level with the Qur'an. The final revelation confirms the truth sent before it and judges what people now attribute to it. That relation is stated with care: (3:3 — **“He has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book in truth, confirming what came before it”**) and the final Book stands as (5:48 — **“a supreme authority on them. So judge between them by what Allah has revealed”**). Therefore, continuity does not erase the final Book's authority, and final authority does not erase the honour due to earlier revelation.

**ONE PORTRAIT, WITH A SPECIAL PLACE FOR LATER BELIEVERS**

An early reading linked this verse in a special way to people of earlier Scripture who then accepted the Qur'an, such as Abdullah ibn Salam and those with him. On that reading, the prior verse describes believers from the Arabs, while this one honours people who held to a former revelation and then received the last. The distinction has a clear point: such people did not have to betray Moses or Jesus in order to follow Muhammad; true loyalty to the former message led them to the later one. Yet the wording also gives a single portrait of every mindful believer. Faith in all revelation is required of the whole community, not only converts from Judaism or Christianity. The wider reading is stronger as the basic rule because another command addresses all believers with both books. The special reading then identifies a clear example within that rule. The Qur'an teaches the shared confession in direct form: (29:46 — **“We believe in what has been revealed to us and what was revealed to you. Our God”**). The one God is the reason true messages cannot be enemies.

**CERTAINTY ABOUT THE LIFE THAT COMES LAST**

The portrait closes with the words ***“and have sure faith in the Hereafter”***. The Hereafter is the life that comes after this near one, and the early explanations unfold it into resurrection, standing before God, reckoning, the scale, Paradise, and the Fire. Certainty gathers these truths into more than a vague hope that life may continue. It treats return and judgment as fixed facts. This is why certainty about the end gives weight to every earlier trait. Revelation matters because its claims will be tested; prayer matters because a servant will stand before God; giving matters because wealth and its use will be judged. A person can repeat the word “afterlife” while living as if no account will come, but that is not the sure faith praised here. The opposite voice admits that it has only a guess: (45:32 — **“We think it is no more than speculation, and we are not convinced ˹that it will ever come˺.’””**). Sure faith closes that gap between speech and expectation. In other words, certainty about the unseen is measured by the weight it gives to present conduct before the future becomes seen.
