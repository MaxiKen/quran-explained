**NO GOOD DEED DENIED**

***“They will never be denied the reward for”*** They will never be denied the reward for any good they have done banks the honoured column's account. And the banking-clause's construction is its mercy. Falan yukfaruh. It will never be denied them. The kufr-verb in its rejection-sense turned to accounting. Their good will never be met with denial. No deed of the night-reciters and racers refused at the deposit, none shaved at the payment. And the clause's specific comfort is its occasion's. The converts of the surrounding passage's setting (Abdullah ibn Salam's circle) had crossed communities. Their former people declaring their ruin, their new deeds beginning past a long former life. And the verse settles both anxieties in one clause. Whatever good, whenever done. The pre-conversion uprightness and the post-conversion racing banked together with the Knower. Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim preserve a promise of double reward for a person from an earlier scriptural community who believes in his prophet and then in Muhammad. The crossing does not erase the ledger but multiplies its reward. (3:114 — **“and forbid evil,”**)

**REWARD ACROSS A CHANGED LIFE**

***“any good they have done. And Allah”*** And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of those mindful ˹of Him˺ seals the banking with the final account-clause at its warmest register. Wallahu alimun bil-muttaqin. Knowing of the God-fearing. And the seal's selection completes the census-passage's whole design. The muttaqin-word. The chapter's and the Book's watchword here naming the honoured column by the quality that transferred them. Taqwa. The guarding mindfulness that read their own Book honestly, priced their community's spite below their Lord's pleasure, and crossed. And the knowledge-clause performing, for them, the protection every crossing minority needs. Known to Allah. Their sincerity beyond their slanderers' reach, their unwitnessed night-hours filed, their standing secured at the only Desk that assigns standings. (3:116 — **“who will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there”**) Reward across a changed life has present-day force because unnoticed service, sincere study, and quiet generosity remain fully recorded even when a community forgets who performed them.

**DIVINE KNOWLEDGE OF THE MINDFUL**

***“has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of those mindful ˹of Him˺”*** The verse's closing function, completes the six-verse instrument (110 to 115) at its balance-point. The commission granted on credentials (110), the threat bounded (111), the course-decree filed with its exits (112), the census guarded (113), the credentials demonstrated portable (114), and the account banked with the Knower (115). The whole teaching one jurisprudence of communities. Honours and decrees track qualities and courses, never bloods and blocs. And the passage's mirror, as ever, faces its holders last. The community commissioned at 110 reads, at 115's seal, the registry it too is filed in. Bil-muttaqin. By mindfulness, not membership. While the argument now turns, in the next verses, to the column the census's other side. The disbelievers' unavailing wealth and children, and the bitter-wind parable over their harvest. Imagine a careful account that preserves every valid deposit even after the customer changes cities. Regarding no good deed denied, Ibn ʿAbbās applies the promise to ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām and his companions, whose good is neither forgotten nor denied its reward. The promise reaches further than public recognition; which means a deed may disappear from human memory while remaining fully known to Allah, so delayed praise cannot reduce its worth. This also explains why mindfulness is named at the close: outward work and inward purpose are held together in one judgment; therefore a person who changes course after learning the truth does not lose sincere good done earlier, because divine knowledge is not limited by the labels people use for one another. The verse therefore gives steady hope without giving permission for self-congratulation; in other words good is preserved, but the One preserving it also knows the heart that produced it. In present life, this frees service from the need to be seen; hence it also removes the fear that a costly moral change has made an earlier life meaningless. Every honest act enters an account that neither prejudice nor forgetfulness can erase; this is why the wording says that reward will never be denied, making the assurance stronger than a general promise of kindness. It answers the fear of complete loss and leaves the amount of reward to divine generosity.
