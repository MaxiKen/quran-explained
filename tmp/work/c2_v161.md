**CONDEMNED AT THE HOUR OF DEATH**

***“Surely those who disbelieve”*** opens a sentence that is not argued but closed, and its timing is precise. The verse continues, ***“and die as disbelievers are condemned by Allah,”*** so the state recorded is the state death finds. Life is the workshop where the state can change; death is the door that locks it. Ibn ʿAbbās gives the report that the participle is read over people who persist in unbelief of Allah and His Messenger to the end. The sentence attaches to that final condition, which means every breath before it remained an open opportunity. The condemnation is not pronounced over a life still in motion. The sentence waits at the door of the final breath, which means the ground of hope and the ground of warning occupy the same moment.

**THREE PRONOUNCEMENTS**

The verdict is issued by three parties at once. The verse itself names the court: ***“the angels”*** take their share of the pronouncement, and ***“and all of humanity”*** closes the list. The reason for the threefold shape is coverage — nobody in creation pronounces the opposite. Ibn ʿAbbās transmits the substance of the third layer: the curses which believers direct through history at wrongdoers settle upon these people as well, so even human judgment is folded into the verdict. The point is that the condemned stand without advocate anywhere, because every level of the moral universe has spoken the same sentence. The order of the list also runs downward from the source of law to its last witness, which is how verdicts are published: authority first, confirmation last. Because the angels are named before humanity, the sentence is dated in the register before any human mouth repeats it. No one can then treat the verdict as a rumour started by enemies; it is a judgement copied from the register and read aloud by creation. (33:64 — **“Surely Allah condemns the disbelievers, and has prepared for them a blazing Fire”**)

**WAITING FOR THE LAST BREATH**

A hearer may ask whether the sentence is not too final for people who sinned for a limited time. The objection has force only if the condemnation were pronounced before death, and the text refuses that timing. Because the record is made at the last breath, no one can claim the sentence was rushed, and no one can claim the opportunity was hidden. The threefold pronouncement mirrors a threefold offer, since prophets, books, and natural testimony all spoke while testimony could still move the heart. This is the difference between a judge who condemns at the first offence and a judge who waits at the door. The period of sin is limited precisely because the period of repentance is limited too, and the verse keeps both limits visible at once. The threefold list also does the work of a formal verdict sheet, because a sentence read by one judge can be argued with while a sentence confirmed by every level of the court cannot. That is why the passage places the angels between Allah and humanity in the list: the highest authority and the lowest witness agree, and the middle of the universe is filled with confirmations. The dying are addressed rather than the dead, which means the warning is still a mercy while it is being read. Every hearer of the verse is standing in the only hour the verse leaves open. The warning is therefore addressed to the living in the grammar of the dead, and the tension between the two is the mercy of its tone.

**SEALED BY ALL CREATION**

The Book states the same logic in its own register: (3:12 — **“˹O Prophet!˺ Tell the disbelievers, “Soon you will be overpowered and driven to Hell—what an evil place to rest!””**). The report of Ibn ʿAbbās holds that the torment itself is the curse of Allah named first in the list, so the order runs from the highest court downward. I read the scope of "people" as either all humanity or the believers; both readings leave the condemned without cover, and the second adds a layer — the very community that warned them confirms the sentence. The scope is therefore total in either case, which is the rhetorical point of naming all three parties in one breath. The application is immediate and narrow: the decisive question is never how a person lived in the middle of the story but in what state the story ends. Those who die in unbelief are marked by all creation.
