**THE POINT AT WHICH THE MATTER CLOSES**

Attention moves from those who can still repair their concealment to those who cannot, and the sentence fixes the point at which the matter is closed: ***“Surely those who disbelieve and die as disbelievers”***. The sin named is not a single act but a state that continues until death, which is why the excuse of an ending that no one can foresee is taken away from the reader. Another passage states the same limit from the side of the person who delays, and the explanation that follows it is plain: (4:18 — **“However, repentance is not accepted from those who knowingly persist in sin until they start dying,”**). The community has drawn from this condition a rule of ordinary speech, namely that no particular person is cursed by name, since the state in which he will end his life is hidden from everyone but God. What the verse does, then, is describe a category rather than pass sentence on any individual.

**CONDEMNATION FROM THREE QUARTERS**

The sentence then names those who join in the condemnation: ***“are condemned by Allah, the angels, and all of humanity”***. The three parties answer the three ways in which such a life has failed, since God is the one whose guidance was refused, the angels are the ones who watched the refusal, and humanity is the company that was harmed by the concealment. The generality of the last phrase is taken to mean that the condemnation is universal on the day when the truth is fully seen, and the same language is used elsewhere of the peoples who rejected their messengers: (5:78 — **“The disbelievers among the Children of Israel were condemned in the revelations of David and Jesus,”**). The Book states the outcome in these terms so that no hearer can imagine the matter to be a private disagreement. What the verse describes is an exclusion that no one in creation is left out of.

**RESERVING THE DURATION FOR LATER**

The verse stops short of describing the punishment, and it leaves the next sentence to name it, so the two verses stand as a warning and its extension. The offence was named, the curse was named, and the duration is reserved for the verse that follows, which is a sign that the passage is building toward its final weight. The Book often leaves a punishment unnamed at first and returns to it with the detail when the hearer is ready to receive it, and the same method is at work in this passage on concealment. The curse has already been pronounced where the offence was described, so this verse gathers the same judgement into one sentence and places it outside the reach of time. What remains to be stated is how long the state lasts.

**A NAME STRUCK FROM EVERY RECORD**

Think of a man who is struck from the roll of his guild, from the register of his town, and from the will of his own family, so that no door that had once been open to him remains open. The three exclusions are not three punishments but one condition described from three sides, and no one who is kept out of all three can expect to be received anywhere. The verse describes exactly that condition, with the difference that the records here are not kept by clerks but by the Lord, the angels, and the whole community of human beings. Whoever has seen a reputation outlive the man it belonged to will recognise the weight of the sentence. What the verse adds is that the exclusion is deserved rather than merely suffered.

**A SIGNBOARD FOR LATER READERS**

The sentence is placed at the end of a passage about concealing knowledge, and it serves as the signboard that the passage erects for those who come after. The warning is stated plainly because it is aimed at the keepers of knowledge in every generation, who might otherwise think the earlier verses concerned only the people who first heard them. The Book does not soften such a warning into a metaphor, and it always attaches it to a state of mind rather than to a birth or a tribe. A sentence is left that closes one door and leaves another open, since the verse before it has already named the way back. What the passage produces is not despair but seriousness.
