**A BOOK CAST BEHIND THE BACK**

The verse names the specific occasion behind the question and describes how the community received what it had been waiting for: ***“Now, when a messenger from Allah has come to them—confirming”***, ***“their own Scriptures—some of”***, ***“the People of the Book cast the Book of Allah”*** behind their backs, and the Book adds that they did so as though they did not know. The messenger arrived confirming what they already held, and the response was to discard the confirmation as an item of rubbish. The gesture in the verse is worth pausing over, because it is the gesture of a person who wants something out of the way rather than something to be refuted. Nothing in the sentence suggests an examination of the messenger's claims; the Book records a movement, and the movement is away from the evidence. What the community did with the confirmation is stated in its own closing clause, and the clause is the Book's judgement on the whole gesture: ***“as if they did not know”***.

**THE GESTURE AND WHAT IT MEANS**

The phrase used for the discarding describes an object thrown behind the back, which is the posture of a person who intends never to look at the thing again. The Book uses the same image elsewhere for people who treat a scripture as an encumbrance, and it uses it here for the people who were its custodians. The clause about acting as if they did not know is a comment on the shape of the refusal rather than on their information, since the same community had been described in earlier verses as recognising what had arrived. The reading handed down from Ibn ʿAbbās describes a deliberate forgetfulness here, and it fits the verse's placement after the account of covenants discarded as a habit. The motion resembles a committee quietly shelving the report it had commissioned.

**THE CONFIRMING THAT MADE THE REFUSAL STRANGE**

The messenger's arrival is described as confirming the community's own scripture, which means the new revelation did not ask them to abandon anything they held. The Book has already made this point several times in the sūrah, and it returns to it here because the refusal has now become an act rather than a debate. The same charge is laid out in another passage where the covenant to make the scripture known is joined to the account of what was hidden: (3:187 — **“who were given the Scripture to make it known to”**). A reader might reasonably ask what could be objected to in a message that agreed with the books already in hand, and the answer is supplied by the whole section: the objection was to the messenger and to the community that would receive him. The confirmation made the refusal harder to justify rather than easier.

**A FIRM THAT BURIES ITS OWN REPORT**

Think of a company whose auditors report exactly what the accounts show, and whose directors respond by filing the report and never mentioning it again. The confirmation of its own scriptures was in these people's hands, and the document was treated as an inconvenience to be put out of sight. The comparison is uncomfortable because the report is the only item in the story that could have settled the argument, and the Book records what was done with it. A reader who has watched an institution receive the evidence it asked for and then stop discussing the question has seen the same movement performed on a smaller stage. The verse names the manoeuvre and leaves the reader to notice where he has seen it.

**EVIDENCE ARRIVES AND IS SET ASIDE**

The passage settles an old question about the value of proof by showing what a community does when the proof arrives, and the answer has nothing to do with the quality of the proof. The Book's account is that the evidence was recognised and discarded in nearly the same breath, which is why it appends the remark about acting as though they did not know. A reader who has spent time trying to settle a religious question with arguments should notice what the verse assumes: that the barrier in the cases described here was never the argument. The next verse turns to a different subject, the magic that this community was said to have followed, and the transition shows the Book moving from what people refuse to what they prefer. What remains from this sentence is the gesture, and the gesture is the part a reader can check in himself. Today, an institution can honour a founding document in ceremony while placing its inconvenient terms behind every practical decision.
