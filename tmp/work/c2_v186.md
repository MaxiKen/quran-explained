**ALLAH’S NEARNESS**

Read under allah’s nearness, the relevant clauses are ***“When My servants ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about Me”***, ***“I am truly near. I respond to one’s prayer”***. This āyah appears in the middle of the fasting verses, but its message is universal. When people ask about Allah, He does not command the Messenger ﷺ to answer with a distant title or an elaborate intermediary. Allah speaks directly: *I am near.* His nearness is a nearness of knowledge, hearing, mercy, and response, not physical containment or likeness to creation. He knows the whispered prayer before the tongue completes it. The verse gives special comfort during Ramadan, when hunger and worship expose the heart’s need. It also answers the anxious person who wonders whether his prayer travels through layers before reaching Allah. Allah hears directly. No priesthood or sacred human gatekeeper stands between the servant and his Lord. I respond to the call of the supplicant when he calls upon Me. *Duʿāʾ* is both invocation and worship. It includes asking for needs, praising Allah, seeking forgiveness, confessing dependence, and turning to Him in every condition. The response may take the form requested, a protection from equivalent harm, or a reward stored for the Hereafter, according to the authentic hadith reported in Musnad Aḥmad and other collections. A delayed or differently shaped response is not proof that Allah did not hear. The servant sees one moment; Allah sees the whole life and the consequences beyond death. The Prophet ﷺ warned against saying, I supplicated but was not answered, because impatience can cause a person to abandon the means of acceptance. (2:185 — **“So whoever is present this month, let them fast.”**) Because allah’s nearness governs 2:186, responding to allah arises from allah’s nearness. It follows that supplication in fasting completes allah’s nearness through responding to allah.

**RESPONDING TO ALLAH**

Responding to Allah begins from ***“when they call upon Me.”***. The verse joins Allah’s response to the servants’ response: Let them respond to Me and believe in Me. The relationship is not a demand that Allah grant every wish while the servant ignores every command. Responding to Allah means obeying His revelation, accepting His limits, repenting after disobedience, and trusting His wisdom. The supplicant asks for guidance while walking in the direction Allah has revealed. This does not mean that sinful or struggling people may not pray. They should pray especially because they need Allah. But they should not deliberately persist in injustice while treating supplication as a technique for obtaining worldly success. Repentance and lawful effort are part of answering Allah. Belief in Allah—respond faithfully to Me—includes trusting His promises, affirming His names, and knowing that He is capable of every response. Faith protects the supplication from becoming superstition. The believer does not worship a formula or assume that a particular human being controls divine acceptance. He calls upon Allah alone while using lawful means. The verse’s closeness also corrects despair. A servant may feel distant because of sin, grief, or distraction, but Allah invites him to call. The first step back can be a simple confession: *My Lord, I need You.* Allah’s mercy is not earned by perfect wording. (50:16 — **“Indeed, ˹it is˺ We ˹Who˺ created humankind”**) The reason supplication in fasting follows responding to allah lies in allah’s nearness. Therefore, supplication in fasting returns practical force to responding to allah.

**SUPPLICATION IN FASTING**

***“So let them respond ˹with obedience˺ to Me”*** establish supplication in fasting. The fasting person experiences a direct relationship with Allah. He leaves food and drink privately for Allah, even when no human being could detect the violation. At the moment of breaking the fast, he has a special opportunity for supplication. The Prophet ﷺ taught words of praise at breaking the fast and encouraged sincere invocation. Ramadan’s nights also include prayer, Qur’an, and seeking forgiveness. These practices do not replace ordinary supplication during work, illness, family difficulty, or injustice. The believer carries the awareness of nearness beyond the month. The Sunnah teaches beginning with praise of Allah, sending blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ, asking with humility, seeking lawful provision, choosing appropriate times, and avoiding supplication for sin or the severing of kinship. The servant should persist without demanding a particular timetable. He should also make the effort to remove barriers such as unlawful income and deliberate wrongdoing. These are not magical conditions that limit Allah. They are disciplines that align the heart with what it is asking. A person who asks for mercy should become merciful; one who asks for forgiveness should forgive and repair; one who asks for guidance should be willing to follow it. (57:4 — **“then established Himself on the Throne. He knows whatever goes into the earth”**)

**GUIDANCE AS THE RESULT**

For guidance as the result, the decisive wording is ***“and believe in Me, perhaps they will be guided ˹to the Right Way˺”***. The purpose clause—so that they may be rightly guided—shows that the highest answer to prayer is guidance itself. A person may ask for wealth and receive contentment, ask for escape and receive courage, or ask for a specific outcome and receive clarity about what Allah wants instead. Guidance enables him to use every outcome well. The community should teach children and vulnerable people that they can call upon Allah directly. It should not exploit their needs through paid promises of guaranteed miracles. The one who knows Allah is near treats supplication as worship, not as a commercial product. Nearness here is like an unlocked door whose owner answers before the visitor can wonder whether he has been heard. The image must not be made physical, yet it corrects the emotional habit of treating prayer as a message sent into an empty distance. A person living today may feel unseen amid crowded cities, impersonal systems, or private grief. The verse removes intermediaries from supplication, while its call to response reminds the petitioner that an answered relationship includes obedience as well as request. (58:7 — **“If three converse privately, He is their fourth.”**) In other words, guidance as the result answers allah’s nearness.
