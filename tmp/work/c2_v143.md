**A COMMUNITY MADE A WITNESS**

The community's purpose is stated at the head of this verse, which opens with a comparison to what came before: ***“And so We have made you ˹believers˺ an upright community”*** and ***“so that you may be witnesses over humanity”***. The word translated as upright carries the sense of the middle and the best at once, and the verse's claim is that the position of the community is both. Nothing in the sentence calls the community the largest or the oldest; it is described as placed in the middle, which in the usage of the language means the just. The sentence's purpose is then stated, and the purpose is a function exercised toward others rather than a privilege enjoyed for its own sake. What has been constituted here is a community made for the sake of a testimony.

**THE MIDDLE POSITION AND WHAT IT MEANS**

The term that describes the community is placed in a sentence that also names the Kaʿbah as the centre of the earth in some of the reports, and the two centres are joined by the comparison that opens the sentence. A hadith transmitted from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī explains the word as justice, and the explanation has the endorsement of the men who transmitted it. Another sūrah names the community with the same vocabulary and adds what it was raised for, which shows how fixed the description is in the Book: (3:110 — **“You are the best community ever raised for humanity”**). The middle, then, is not a compromise between two extremes but the point from which witness can be given to both sides. Someone taught to think of moderation as a lack of conviction will find the verse's sense of the word different from that.

**THE WITNESS THE COMMUNITY BEARS**

The sentence names the role the community will play on the day the accounting is made: ***“and that the Messenger may be a witness over you.”***. The two witnesses are named in one sentence, and they run in a sequence, with the community testifying about humanity and the Messenger testifying about the community. Another passage describes the same scene with the same two levels of testimony, which places the sentence among the Book's standard accounts of the reckoning: (4:41 — **“We bring a witness from every faith-community and bring you ˹O Prophet˺ as a witness against yours”**). The Book's arrangement is hierarchical without being arbitrary, since each witness answers for what he was given. The community set before him will be asked about others, and its own messenger will be asked about it.

**AN INSPECTOR WHOSE OWN WORK WAS CHECKED**

Think of an inspector from a firm who is sent to examine other offices and who knows that his own work was examined before he was sent. The community in this verse has been placed in that position, and the Messenger's testimony about it is the inspection that comes before its own. The comparison explains why the sentence says the community was made for this role rather than that it achieved it, since the appointment is a commission rather than a promotion. Anyone who has been given authority over other people's work will recognise the weight in the sentence, and the weight falls on the one who holds it rather than on those he inspects. What remains is the responsibility of being placed in the middle.

**A DIRECTION THAT SIFTED THEM**

The next sentence explains why the community had been made to face a direction that was later changed, and it states the purpose plainly: ***“We assigned your former direction of prayer only to distinguish those”***, ***“who would remain faithful to the Messenger from those”*** and ***“who would lose faith. It was certainly a difficult test except”***. The change was not a correction of a mistake; it was a test that separated the people whose allegiance was to the command from those whose allegiance was to the custom. The account given earlier in this sūrah of those communities has shown the same pattern in the demand for signs and in the calf. One early authority places the test in the interval when the community faced Jerusalem, and that reading fits the words of the sentence exactly. Anyone who has watched a community divide over a change in practice has seen the instrument the verse describes.

**THE FAITH THAT WILL NOT BE DISCOUNTED**

The verse closes with the assurance that answers the anxiety the test would have created, and the words are generous: ***“for those ˹rightly˺ guided by Allah.”***, ***“And Allah would never discount your ˹previous acts of˺ faith. Surely Allah is Ever Gracious”*** and ***“and Most Merciful to humanity”***. The sentence guarantees that the prayers offered in the earlier direction were not wasted, which is the practical worry the change would have raised among the believers. The two names at the close explain the guarantee, since the One who gives the guidance is the One who keeps the record. The assurance is stated in another passage for a community that feared its earlier worship had been wasted, and the words there match the promise here: (2:143 — **“And Allah would never discount your ˹previous acts of˺ faith”**). The Book's manner here is to state the purpose of a difficult command and then to remove the fear that the command had produced, and the order is worth noting. The picture left is of a community tested, distinguished, and then reassured about what it had already done.
