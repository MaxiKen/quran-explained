**A SWORN LOYALTY TO HALF A REVELATION**

The verse reports the answer this community gives when it is invited to believe, and the answer is a formula: ***“When it is said to them”***, ***““Believe in what Allah has revealed,” they reply, “We only believe in what was sent down to us,””***, ***“and they deny what came afterwards”***, ***“though it is the truth confirming their own Scriptures!”***, ***“Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, “Why”***, ***“then did you kill Allah's prophets before”***, ***“if you are ˹truly˺ believers?””***. The claim to believe in what was sent down to them is used as a reason to refuse what came later, and the Book points out that the later revelation confirms the earlier one rather than contradicting it. The sentence then turns the claim into a test by reaching into the community's own history, since the prophets sent to them had been killed by some of the same people who now describe themselves as believers. A profession of faith that survives only its own documents has been placed under examination. Ibn ʿAbbās read their reply as restriction of belief to the Torah even while the later revelation confirmed divine oneness, and the question about slain prophets tests their claim against inherited conduct.

**THE LOGIC OF A CLOSED CANON**

The community's position has a recognisable shape: what was given to them is authoritative, and anything arriving after it is not. The Book's answer does not dispute the authority of the earlier scriptures; it points to the connection between the two revelations, which is confirmation rather than replacement. A passage later in the sūrah describes the same people discarding what came to them while holding on to what they had, and the act is described there in the terms of something cast aside: (2:101 — **“Now, when a messenger from Allah has come to them—confirming”**). The problem with a closed canon is not the reverence it shows toward a text but the authority it quietly takes for itself, since deciding that revelation has ended is itself a claim about God's speech. The verse exposes that claim by pointing to the conduct of the community that made it.

**REACHING INTO THEIR OWN PAST**

The closing question is the sharpest instrument in the passage, because it tests the profession against the record. If they believe in what was sent to them, then the prophets who came with that same message were their own, and the killing of some of them was not the act of believers. The Book's habit of using a community's history against its self-description appears throughout the sūrah, and it is a method rather than a rhetorical flourish. A reader who has heard a group define itself by its loyalty to a tradition can ask what that group has done with the people who carried the tradition, and the answer is often an embarrassment. The question in this verse does not need an answer; it needs only to be asked in the presence of the record.

**THE ALBUM KEPT AFTER THE INSTRUCTIONS ARE LOST**

Think of a household that keeps its grandfather's photograph on the wall and has lost the letter it wrote about the family's conduct, and whose members invoke his name to settle arguments they never actually consult him about. The earlier scripture was in these people's possession, along with a claim to believe in it, and the claim was being used to refuse the continuation of the same message. The Book's answer is to point at the parts of the inheritance that had been quietly set aside, which is a test any family or community can run. The verse does not accuse the whole people of killing prophets; it accuses enough of them to make the profession hollow, and the Book's language throughout the passage is carefully distributed. What this sentence leaves behind is a measure: loyalty to a tradition is tested by behaviour toward the people who carry it.

**WHY NO COUNTER-ARGUMENT IS GIVEN**

The passage records the community's words and then answers them with a question rather than a counter-argument, and the choice is deliberate. The Book treats the claim about the earlier scriptures as self-evidently inconsistent with the conduct it names, so no long rebuttal is needed. The same method was used earlier when the community was asked about its own covenant, and it shows how often a plain record is allowed to do the work of an argument. Anyone who has read from the covenant onward can supply the rest of the reasoning himself, which is precisely the effect intended. What remains is the question, and it is left standing over the community's profession like a witness who has not been dismissed.

**HOW LOYALTY IS MEASURED**

For a reader of the page, the verse offers a way to test his own attachment to what he has received, and the test is not about documents but about conduct. A person can accept the revelation he was given and still refuse the parts of it that inconvenience him, which is the offence the preceding verses described. The Book's answer in this verse is to name the historical fruits of that habit in the community that practised it, so that the reader sees where the path leads over generations. A community that has decided the era of correction is over has also decided that its own arrangements cannot be questioned, and the Book's question can be asked of it at any time. The verse ends with the question unanswered, and the reader is the one who is expected to answer it. Today, selective loyalty to revelation appears whenever inherited commands are defended but confirming duties that disturb habit are refused.
