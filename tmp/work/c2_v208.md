**WHOLEHEARTED ENTRY**

Two phrases carry the whole summons: the entry and the enemy. ***“O believers! Enter into Islam wholeheartedly”*** and ***“and do not follow Satan’s footsteps. Surely he is your sworn enemy”***. and the final phrase brands the enemy for what he is. The order is deliberate: the door is opened first and the guard is posted second, so nobody mistakes the warning for the message. The summons is plural and total — come all of you — which is the shape of a community rather than a collection of private conversions. Ibn ʿAbbās glosses the entry as coming to the prescriptions of the religion, and the footsteps as the embellishments that decorate the road away from them. The door and the guard together are the whole grammar of the entry.

**WHAT PARTIAL CONVERSION COSTS**

The summons is addressed to the community in the plural and in the total: come all of you into Islam. The occasion preserved in the reports fixes the summons on the converts who had entered the faith while keeping fragments of the old observances — the Sabbath kept with reverence, the old aversions of the table kept as law. Ibn ʿAbbās glosses the entry as coming to the prescriptions of the religion in full, and the gloss exposes the economy of the partial conversion: the fragments keep the person attached to the road he left. Because the summons is total, the partial conversion is not a stage the verse recognizes but a stall it interrupts. The door is entered with the whole body or the body remains in the doorframe.

**THE FOOTSTEPS TO AVOID**

The second phrase posts the guard at the door: do not follow the footsteps, and the footsteps are the embellishments that decorate the road away from the summons. The warning appears in the chapter's earlier legislation of the marketplace in the same terms, where the embellished goods were named as the enemy's merchandise (2:168 — **“and do not follow Satan’s footsteps. He is truly your sworn enemy”**). Because the footsteps are plural, the enemy's guidance is a route of small steps rather than a single leap, and the route is measured against the entry the verse commands. The application is to review the practices inherited from the old house and to ask which of them are footsteps and which are furniture.

**ENEMY WITH A NAME**

The final phrase names the enemy and closes the negotiation: he is your sworn enemy. The naming is the verse's gift to the person who has been treating the old embellishments as neutral decoration. The legislation of the covenant places the same limit on the person who disobeys after the summons, since the summons and the obedience are one matter (33:36 — **“any other choice in that matter. Indeed, whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger has clearly gone ˹far˺ astray”**). Because the enemy is sworn, the war is declared and the neutrality of the footsteps is over. The wholehearted entry is the only posture in which the enemy's road cannot be walked. The turning back is described in the old stories as the most expensive walk in the city: the feet carry the body out of the house of the covenant and into the market of the doubt. The community of the first hours paid for that walk in the currency of the tortures, and the reports keep the instruments of the torturers in the catalogue. Because the walk is described in the direction of the heels, the anatomy of the retreat is worth studying at the desk. Brotherhood in the early community was a deed before it was a word: the households were joined in the market and in the orchard and in the migration. The pact of brotherhood kept its register beside the register of the trade, and both registers were reviewed by the same teachers. The one who is turned back from the belief at the end of the road is the one the legislation of the turned-back was written for. The Ansar and their brothers in the covenant kept the same register of the deeds. The walk of the heels has its own literature, and the literature is written by the feet of the one who returns to the house. The catalogue of the instruments is kept open at the desk of every generation.
