**MERCY IN THE NIGHT OF RAMADAN**

Mercy in the night of Ramadan takes shape in ***“It has been made permissible”***, ***“for you to be intimate with your wives during the nights preceding the fast. Your spouses are a garment”***, ***“for you as you are”***, ***“for them. Allah knows”***. This āyah gives the detailed boundaries of the fast and begins with a permission: marital intimacy is lawful during the nights of Ramadan. Early Muslims understood an earlier restriction more narrowly and found it difficult; some nevertheless approached their spouses after sleeping or violated the intended limit. Allah knew their weakness, accepted their repentance, and revealed an easier rule. The verse therefore combines legal clarity with a beautiful picture of divine mercy. Fasting is abstention during the day, not a rejection of marriage or bodily affection. Islam does not treat lawful intimacy as dirty. It places it within marriage, dignity, mutual rights, and the times Allah has permitted. The night becomes a time for nourishment, affection, rest, and family life, while the daylight fast remains a serious act of worship. They are a garment for you and you are a garment for them. A garment covers, protects, warms, beautifies, and remains close to the body. The metaphor describes mutual intimacy, privacy, emotional shelter, and protection from temptation. Marriage in the Qur’an is not merely a legal arrangement for reproduction; it is a relationship of tranquillity, affection, and mercy. The mutual wording rejects one-sided entitlement. Each spouse has a right to kindness, privacy, emotional consideration, and physical dignity. Intimacy must not be used as punishment, coercion, humiliation, or a weapon in an argument. The fasting regulations protect the relationship by placing it within lawful time and mutual responsibility. (2:183 — **“so perhaps you will become mindful ˹of Allah˺”**) Because mercy in the night of ramadan governs 2:187, pardon after a hidden breach arises from mercy in the night of ramadan. It follows that the beginning of the fast completes mercy in the night of ramadan through pardon after a hidden breach.

**PARDON AFTER A HIDDEN BREACH**

The wording for pardon after a hidden breach is ***“that you were deceiving yourselves.”***, ***“So He has accepted your repentance”***, ***“and pardoned you. So now you may be intimate with them”***, ***“and seek what Allah has prescribed”***. Allah knew that the believers were betraying themselves—*deceiving yourselves*. The wording is compassionate but serious. By violating or struggling under a previous restriction, they had harmed themselves rather than harmed Allah. Allah knows the weakness of His servants before they confess it, and He opened a lawful route instead of leaving them trapped in guilt. This is a model for repentance. A servant should not hide wrongdoing from Allah or defend it with excuses. He acknowledges the failure, stops it, and accepts the mercy of the revealed correction. Divine pardon does not mean the earlier command was meaningless; it means Allah legislated with knowledge of human capacity and guided the community to a more suitable rule. Seek what Allah has decreed for you includes seeking children and lawful marital benefit, while some also emphasize seeking the reward Allah has written through lawful intimacy. The phrase directs desire toward what is wholesome and permitted. It does not support exploitation, adultery, or treating a spouse as an object. The command also gives a broader principle: desire is not automatically sinful when directed through a lawful channel. Eating, drinking, sleep, affection, and sexual intimacy can all be acts of gratitude when the servant follows Allah’s boundaries and intends to preserve himself and fulfil legitimate rights. (30:21 — **“so that you may find comfort in them.”**) The reason the beginning of the fast follows pardon after a hidden breach lies in mercy in the night of ramadan. Therefore, the beginning of the fast returns practical force to pardon after a hidden breach.

**THE BEGINNING OF THE FAST**

The movement toward the beginning of the fast rests on ***“for you. ˹You may˺ eat”***, ***“and drink until you see the light of dawn breaking the darkness of night”***, ***“then complete the fast”***, ***“until nightfall. Do not be intimate with your spouses”***. Eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread of night. The image refers to the first horizontal light of true dawn, *dawn distinct*, not to literal threads placed beside one another. The Prophet ﷺ explained the verse to Adi ibn Hatim, clarifying that the white and black refer to the light of day and darkness of night, as recorded in the two Ṣaḥīḥ collections, Muslim’s and al-Bukhārī’s. The fast begins at true dawn and continues until sunset. Local timetables should be based on reliable calculation and observation. When a person is genuinely uncertain, he follows the established timetable and the guidance of trustworthy local scholars rather than turning ordinary uncertainty into obsessive hardship. The command then complete the fast until sunset establishes the end of the daily abstention. As soon as the sun has set, eating and drinking become lawful. The Sunnah encourages hastening the breaking of the fast once sunset is confirmed and delaying the pre-dawn meal until near dawn without entering uncertainty. Completion means avoiding food, drink, and sexual intimacy during the fasting hours, along with guarding the moral fast. The Prophet ﷺ warned that lying and harmful conduct undermine the spiritual benefit even when the legal fast remains technically valid. The fast ends at sunset, but its lesson in self-control should continue throughout the night and the rest of the year. (25:74 — **“Bless us with ˹pious˺ spouses”**)

**MOSQUE RETREAT AND ITS BOUNDARY**

The sequence reaches iʿtikāf and its boundary through ***“while you are meditating in the mosques. These are the limits set by Allah”***, ***“so do not exceed them. This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to people”***, ***“so they may become mindful ˹of Him˺”***. The verse prohibits marital intimacy while a person is engaged in *iʿtikāf*, a dedicated stay in the mosque for worship. Iʿtikāf is an intensified retreat for prayer, Qur’an, remembrance, and supplication. Its purpose is not to despise family life, but to give the servant a temporary period of concentrated devotion. The prohibition preserves the distinctive boundary of that retreat. The Sunnah records that the Prophet ﷺ observed iʿtikāf during the last ten nights of Ramadan and that his wives visited him. Their visits demonstrate affection and lawful interaction, while the intimacy restriction remained. The person in iʿtikāf should follow the legal conditions concerning the mosque, duration, necessary needs, and invalidating actions according to qualified guidance. These are the limits of Allah—*ḥudūd Allāh*. The verse says not to approach them, not merely not to cross them. This teaches caution around situations that predictably lead to violation. A person who wants to protect the fast avoids private circumstances, media, conversations, and habits that make prohibited intimacy, food, drink, or anger likely. The principle extends beyond Ramadan. The Qur’an tells believers not to approach adultery, because prevention includes closing the doors that lead toward it. Legal boundaries are not arbitrary obstacles; they protect worship, family, dignity, and the heart. The white and black threads of dawn are like the boundary line on a field: before it is crossed one rule applies, and after it another begins. Guessing from a distance once caused confusion, but daylight made the intended distinction plain. The verse's other limits work the same way. In present-day worship, precision about time, intimacy, and retreat is not narrowness; it protects a practice from appetite and from needless severity, allowing lawful closeness where God opened it and restraint where He set the line. (17:32 — **“Do not go near adultery. It is truly a shameful deed”**) In other words, mosque retreat and its boundary answers mercy in the night of ramadan.
