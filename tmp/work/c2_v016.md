**THE VERDICT GATHERS THE WHOLE PATTERN INTO ONE CHOICE**

After the claims, lies, scorn, and blind wandering, the words ***“They are the ones”*** gather the traits into a single account. These are not stray faults that happen to sit beside one another. Each one serves the same hidden choice. They speak faith to gain trust, return to their chiefs to guard their place, and call the faithful foolish so that true guidance will seem unworthy of its cost. The verse now reads that course as a bargain. This matters because a bargain joins desire, judgment, and action. A buyer sees two things, values one above the other, and gives something up to gain what he wants. The hypocrites are not mere victims of a fog they did not choose. They have seen enough of both paths to prefer the one that grants a quick worldly edge. Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd describe them as pursuing error and leaving guidance, while Qatādah stresses their preference for the wrong path. Their many acts are thus traced to a rule within: when truth and near gain part ways, they keep the gain.

**A PRICELESS GOOD IS HANDED OVER FOR ERROR**

They are those ***“who trade guidance for misguidance.”*** The image does not require that every hypocrite first possessed full, settled faith and then sold it. Mujāhid applied it to people who believed and then disbelieved. The wider reading includes those to whom guidance came, who knew its call and could have received it, but chose its opposite. This is why preference itself can be pictured as exchange. An earlier people were shown the way, (41:17 — **“but they preferred blindness over guidance. So the blast of a disgracing punishment overtook them”**). Access to light made their chosen blindness blameworthy. The price and the goods are reversed. Guidance is what should have been sought, while error is what should have been cast away. Instead, they spend the better thing to obtain the worse. Their worldly plan may bring safety, praise from chiefs, or a share in two circles, but none of those gains equals what is lost. The Qur’an uses the same market image for people who (2:86 — **“trade the Hereafter for the life of this world. So their punishment will not be reduced,”**). The fault is not trade itself. Lawful trade can be part of seeking God’s bounty. The fault is using a trader’s care for gain while leaving the final account out of the price. A sound bargain guards what lasts; this bargain spends it.

**THEIR LEDGER SHOWS ACTIVITY BUT NO PROFIT**

The result follows at once: ***“But this trade is profitless”***. Profit is what remains after cost is counted, not whatever enters the hand first. The hypocrites count shelter, rank, and access, but omit the split heart, the growing lie, the loss of light, and the judgment to come. Once all costs are entered, the supposed gain disappears. Their case is like a merchant who celebrates the coins in his till while ignoring that he sold the whole shop to get them. Much moved through his hands, yet he ended with less than he began. This verdict also explains why a scheme may appear to work and still fail. They may fool a person for a day. That result does not turn fraud into profit, because the deepest object of the plan was safety and well-being for the self. Verse 9 has already shown that they deceive only themselves. A later line repeats the same exchange with its end in view: (2:175 — **“They are the ones who trade guidance for misguidance and forgiveness for punishment.”**). What they gain cannot shield them from what they bought. By contrast, God describes a true exchange in which believers give life and wealth for Paradise, then says, (9:111 — **“So rejoice in the exchange you have made with Him. That is ˹truly˺ the ultimate triumph”**). The worth of a trade is known by its seller, price, object, and final delivery. On every count, the hypocrites’ trade loses.

**THE UNGUIDED CHOICE AND THE ROAD AFTER IT**

The close says ***“and they are not ˹rightly˺ guided”***. They lacked sound judgment in the exchange, and they do not reach a safe road after it. This ending ties the market image back to travel. A bad bargain leaves one poor; rejected guidance also leaves one lost. Their clever speech cannot make up for the lack of a true end. They know how to move between groups, but not how to move toward God. They know which claim may please each room, but not which choice will stand on the Last Day. Today, people still trade in things that have no price tag. A person may give up truth for access, prayer for praise, trust for a short advantage, or conscience for a place near power. The payment may be hidden because no money changes hands. Yet a real exchange has occurred. The warning demands a full account before such a choice is made. The rightly guided are (39:18 — **“those who listen to what is said and follow the best of it. These are the ones ˹rightly˺ guided by Allah,”**). They do not ask only what a choice gains now, but what it does to faith and where it leads. Guidance is kept by acting on its worth. Once it is treated as expendable, even a crowded ledger can close with nothing but loss.
