**DEATH AND MARTYRDOM END IN GATHERING**

***“Whether you die or are martyred—all”*** Whether you die or are martyred. All of you will be gathered before Allah lays the floor beneath the re-pricing just published. And the floor's universality is its function. Wa la'in muttum aw qutiltum la'ila llahi tuhsharun. And if you die or are killed, surely to Allah you will be gathered. The two exits of the surrounding passage (the bed's and the sword's) repeated with their order reversed. The reversal itself noted by the careful readers as the pairing's completion. The surrounding passage led with the killing (the feared exit re-priced first), this verse leads with the dying (the universal exit universalised first). And both verses' two doors opening, by this floor-clause, onto one hall. The hashr. The gathering. No exit-door exempting its user from the hall, no road. The cause's or the couch's. Terminating anywhere else. The la-ila-llahi construction's emphasis (surely to Allah) sealing the destination against every alternative the aftermath's theologies had traded in. (3:157 — **“Should you be martyred or die in the cause of Allah,”**) (3:159 — **“It is out of Allah’s mercy”**) Death and martyrdom end in gathering guides conduct today because journey, illness, and battle differ outwardly but every path ends in the same gathering before Allah.

**ALL PATHS END BEFORE ALLAH**

***“of you will be gathered before Allah”*** The floor's placement completes the death-passage's three-verse architecture (156 to 158). The counterfactual court dissolved (156. The if-theology's agony), the roads re-priced (157. Forgiveness and mercy against the amassing), and now the accounting-hall named where all pricing is settled (this verse). The sequence being the community's complete grief-and-fear curriculum. What not to say at the graves, what the cause's road actually costs, and where every road's travellers reconvene. The hall-clause's specific mercy at the bandaged hour, being its reunion-face. The seventy of Uhud, the passage's whole occasion, not departed but preceded. The gathering re-joining the ranks the passes scattered. Death re-filed, by the floor's grammar, from separation's category to sequence's. The community's members arriving at one hall by staggered doors. The gathering of the dead and martyrs gives believers a clear view of every exit. The two doors held with equal hand, and the hall held certain past both. Whence the life's whole planning-question re-addressed. The believer walking his roads as the hall's expected guest. The preparation the only luggage the doors admit, and the Gatherer the only host the exits serve. While the passage now turns from the ranks to their commander. The leniency that held the community through its worst hour. It is out of Allah's mercy that you have been lenient with them. Imagine many roads entering one courthouse through different doors. On death and martyrdom end in gathering, Ibn ʿAbbās reads death on a journey and death in battle alike as paths to gathering before Allah. Death in its ordinary form and death as a martyr lead to the same assembly before Allah; which means the difference in honour does not create different destinations of return. This shared gathering removes the illusion that avoiding one danger avoids judgment itself; therefore a person may escape a battlefield and still move each day toward the appointed meeting. The verse therefore completes the correction of counterfactual fear; in other words the question is not whether gathering can be avoided, but what a person brings to it. That focus gives courage without celebrating needless exposure to harm; hence means of safety remain part of responsible conduct, yet safety is never turned into a god that suspends duty. In present decisions, fear often compares action with an imagined life of unlimited continuation; this is why the verse supplies the missing term: both action and inaction end before Allah. Moral choice should be made under that certainty rather than under the fantasy of permanent escape. Gathering also restores every relationship disturbed by death. Aggressor and victim, commander and follower, believer and denier all come before one Judge. This makes final justice possible even where earthly accounts remain unfinished. Courage rests not on forgetting loss but on knowing that no loss escapes that assembly. The certainty of return gives each temporary road its lasting moral weight.
