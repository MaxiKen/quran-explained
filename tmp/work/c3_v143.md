**DEATH ONCE WISHED FOR**

***“You certainly wished ˹for the opportunity˺ for”*** You certainly wished ˹for the opportunity˺ for martyrdom before encountering it holds the community's pre-war professions to the examination-light. And the final accounted wish is fixed in the record. Wa laqad kuntum tamannawna l-mawta min qabli an talqawh. You used to wish for death before meeting it. The occasion identifies the wishers. The eager of Uhud's war-council. The men who had missed Badr and its honours, who pressed the Prophet toward the open field against his own reading, professing hunger for the martyrdom their brothers had won. The wish sincere in its hour, warm in the assembly's safety. And the verse's audit neither mocks nor voids it, but files it at the examination's law. Professions are claims awaiting their documenting field. The assembly's wish a paper unsat. (3:142 — **“Do you think you will enter Paradise without Allah proving”**)

**THE BATTLE NOW SEEN**

***“martyrdom before encountering it, now you”*** Now you have seen it with your own eyes completes the final account with the field's arrival. Faqad ra'aytumuhu wa antum tanzurun. Now you have seen it while you were looking on. Death encountered at Uhud in its unedited form. The seventy fallen, the Prophet bleeding, the ranks broken and the swords at work. The wish's object met with your own eyes. And the day's record wrote the examination's results across the community's honest spectrum. Some cashed the profession entire. Anas ibn an-Nadr, who had missed Badr and vowed Allah would see what he would do if given a field, found at Uhud with eighty wounds, recognised only by his sister from his fingertips, the community reading the surrounding passage of Surah 33 as his citation. Others, at the same sight, weakened toward the passes. The one wish, sat at the one field, sorting its professors. The examination-doctrine's living census. (3:144 — **“Muḥammad is no more than a messenger;”**) The battle now seen guides conduct today because people who ask for difficult responsibilities before they arrive should remember that imagined courage is not tested courage.

**CLAIMS TESTED BY THE SIGHT OF DANGER**

***“have seen it with your own eyes”*** The verse's permanent jurisprudence, is the profession-audit every believer carries. The community's religious speech teems with assembly-wishes. The martyrdom longed for in safety, the sacrifices pledged in sermons, the had we been there of every heroic tale (the identical audit the Prophet's generation itself faced over the earlier communities' trials). And the verse files them all at its law. The wish unexamined is a deposit unbanked. Honoured as intention, but distinguished, by the day's own grammar, from the substance the field documents. Whence the believer's schooled posture toward his own professions. Made humbly, maintained toward their fields (the preparation that honest wishing funds), and held, at the field's arrival, by the grace this passage's next verses will beg. While the final account now ascends to the community's severest paper. The Messenger's own mortality, and the sentence that steadied the ummah at his death. Muhammad is no more than a messenger. Imagine a recruit asking for the front line and then hearing real fire for the first time. On death once wished for, Ibn ʿAbbās confronts those who had wished for battle before Uhud and then saw its swords directly without remaining firm. Longing for martyrdom at a distance can be sincere and still remain incomplete; which means the battlefield tests whether the imagined courage survives the sight of death. The verse reminds the believers that they had asked for such an encounter before it arrived, so retreat cannot be separated from their earlier desire; therefore this is not mockery of fear. It is an education in the difference between aspiration and readiness; in other words a person may admire sacrifice while overlooking the training, obedience, and steadiness sacrifice requires. The repeated emphasis on seeing makes the encounter immediate: death was no longer an idea heard in a story; hence present commitments face similar tests when their cost becomes concrete. Promises made in safety must be prepared for before pressure comes; this is why the believer asks for a faithful end with humility, trains for ordinary obedience, and avoids boasting about how courage would appear in a trial that has not yet arrived.
