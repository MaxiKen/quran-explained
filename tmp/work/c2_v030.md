**THE ANNOUNCEMENT MADE TO THE ANGELS**

The sūrah now opens a scene from before the human being existed: ***“˹Remember˺ when your Lord said”***, ***“to the angels”***, ***“I am going to place a successive”***, ***“˹human˺ authority on earth.”***. The command to remember is addressed to the Prophet and, through him, to the reader, which means the scene is being shown for a purpose rather than told as a curiosity. The early teachers explained the word used for the appointment as a reference to one who acts on another's behalf, and they read the phrase about succession as covering generation after generation of human beings, each taking the place of the ones before. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and others are credited with the reading that the appointment was a responsibility rather than a privilege, since a deputy answers to the one who sent him. The reason the scene is placed here, after the account of the earth being furnished for the human being, is that the furnishing and the appointment are two halves of one arrangement.

**ANGELIC CONCERN BEFORE THE APPOINTMENT**

The verse records the angels' question without any criticism of them: ***“They asked ˹Allah˺”***, ***“Will You place in it someone”***, ***“who will spread corruption there”***, ***“and shed blood”***. Their words show that they expected bloodshed from the creature being announced, and the early teachers explained their expectation in two ways, one taking it as knowledge given to them about what the human being would do and the other as a conclusion drawn from the record of an earlier creation that had done the same. A reading handed down from Ibn ʿAbbās treats the angels as seekers of information rather than objectors, and other teachers noted that their question came from care for the creation rather than from envy. The Book keeps their tone in the sentence that follows, which is the language of worship rather than of complaint, and the reader is left to weigh what that shows about how a question should be asked.

**THE WORSHIP THEY NAMED AS THEIR OWN**

The angelic question finishes with the statement that gives their words their weight: ***“while we glorify Your”***, ***“praises”***, ***“and proclaim Your holiness?”***. The two verbs describe continuous praise and the setting apart of God from every defect, and the angels offered both as their constant work. The Book records the same occupation in another sūrah where the angels are described in their ranks, and the sentence there shows what the reader should understand about their question. The reason the verse gives their worship in their own words is that it lets the reader see the two candidates for the earth side by side: a creature who would shed blood and a creature who never stops praising. What turns on the exchange is that the choice belonged to the One who made both, and the verse does not present the angels' argument as a mistake; it presents it as an argument that was answered from a knowledge they did not have.

**HIDDEN WISDOM BEHIND THE DECREE**

The verse ends with the answer of the Lord: ***“Allah responded”***, ***“I know what you do not know.”***. The sentence does not explain what the knowledge contains, and the sūrah leaves the reader to learn it from the rest of the account, which continues with the teaching of the names. The Book answers the same kind of question in another place where the wisdom behind the appointment is shown in the raising of a successor who repairs what others damaged: (6:165 — **“He is the One Who has placed you as successors on earth”**). The reason the answer is given in this form is that a purpose can be known to its author and hidden from the instruments of the purpose, and the angels were instruments rather than partners. A reader who has ever been left without an explanation for a decision made above him has stood in the position of the angels here, and the verse does not treat that position as a fault. Think of a foreman who is told by the owner that a difficult workman is being hired, and who warns the owner about the mess the man will make; the owner's answer is that he knows more about the man than the foreman does, and the work goes ahead. The next verse shows the answer being filled in, and it begins with the names.  In a council today, limited knowledge can make a sound concern incomplete, which is why humility must remain after the concern has been voiced.
