**THE PAIR OF INVITATIONS**

The verse stages a public argument in one line. ***“The Jews and Christians each say, “Follow our faith to be ˹rightly˺ guided.””***. Two parties. One invitation each. The claim is not disputed as an invitation. Both parties offer guidance. The claim is disputed as a claim of exclusive standing. The scene behind the verse is public and specific. The delegations of the Jews and the Christians of Najrān stood before the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. Each party claimed to be upon the religion of Allah. So the verse puts two house-names on the table. It prepares to answer both with one name. In the account of Ibn ʿAbbās the saying is the standing claim of the two groups. The effect of the stage direction is audible. The reader hears the claim in its polite form first. Then the answer in its blunt form. The reason the verse quotes the invitation rather than paraphrasing it is that the reply must fit the exact words on offer. The stage direction then turns the reply into a public instruction: ***“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “No!”***.

**THE ANSWER WITH A NAME IN IT**

The instruction refuses the pair and returns to a face. ***“We follow the faith of Abraham, the upright”*** ***“who was not a polytheist.””***. The parallel chapter states the same faith as the revealed instruction (16:123 — **“Then We revealed to you ˹O Prophet, saying˺: “Follow the faith of Abraham, the upright, who was not one of the polytheists.””**). The other passage names the patriarch in his exact state (3:67 — **“Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian; he submitted in all uprightness and was not a polytheist”**). For Ibn ʿAbbās the faith of Abraham is older than the two invitations. It stands as their measure. The reply does not argue with the two houses on their own ground. It resets the ground to the father of the houses. The difference between following a community and following a faith is the whole answer. The community is a later shape. The faith is the original one. What follows is that the upright one becomes the standard by which every later claim is tested. The test is simple enough to be named in one adjective.

**THE ADJECTIVE THAT IS NOT A SECT**

The verse ends on a negative description. It does more work than a title would. ***“who was not a polytheist.””***. The description carries the weight. The upright is defined by what he refused. The refusal is the first boundary of the faith. In the reading of the early authorities the uprightness is the shape of the religion before any later century named it. The adjective is portable across every age. Whoever is not a polytheist stands where Abraham stood. The standing does not require a passport from any later community. For the modern reader the verse is a lesson in answering confident invitations. Go back past both of them to the father who was neither. This age treats labels as identities. The verse treats one negative as the identity that matters. The faith of Abraham is therefore defined by a boundary. It is not defined by a brand. The reason the verse closes on the polytheist refusal is that the refusal is the one thing both invitations had quietly left out of their claims. The invitations offered a guidance. The answer names a father. The two invitations are confident and the confidence is the point. Each party arrives with a shelf of evidence. The answer does not attack the shelves. It moves the whole case to a father both parties claim. The father is older than the shelves. His faith is older than the names of the houses. The adjective at the end then does the boundary work. Not a polytheist. The negative is clean and it is portable. It fits in any century. It fits beside any bookshelf. The upright one defined himself by what he refused. The refusal was the first wall of the faith. A reader in this age can hold the same wall. The wall is not a brand. It is a boundary. The invitations can be answered without raising the voice. Name the father. Name the adjective. The two names end the meeting. The meeting began with two claims. It ends with one patriarch.
