**AN INVITATION TO JOIN A COMMUNITY**

An invitation from the two communities is quoted in their own terms: ***“The Jews and Christians each say, “Follow our faith to be ˹rightly˺ guided.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “No!”***. The offer is put in the language of guidance, as though the only way to be rightly guided were to join one of the two communities, and the argument so far has shown how often that offer had been made. The reply begins with the shortest refusal in the passage, and the Book gives the Messenger a single word to open his answer. The reader should notice what the invitation takes for granted, since it assumes that guidance is held by a community rather than given by God. What the verse does is to decline the terms and, in the same sentence, to name the way the Messenger already follows.

**THE REFUSAL AND THE REASON FOR IT**

The answer names the faith of Abraham rather than a new religion, which places the reply inside the tradition the communities themselves claimed: ***“We follow the faith of Abraham, the upright”***. The Book's argument here is not that the two communities had nothing, but that the tradition they were offering was not theirs to dispense and was already being followed. The word for uprightness describes a person who has turned away from everything else, and the description is applied to a man who lived before either community existed. Another passage makes the same point by naming what Abraham was not: (3:67 — **“Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian; he submitted in all uprightness”**). A reader who has been told that his standing depends on joining a particular group will find the sentence's answer refreshingly direct.

**A DESCRIPTION THAT ALSO FORECLOSES A CLAIM**

The verse's last clause adds a detail that the two communities would have found difficult: ***“who was not a polytheist.”***. The description excludes both the charge of idolatry that the communities traded about each other and the assumption that one of them had inherited the man's religion by right. The Book uses the phrase consistently when it discusses this prophet, and its appearance here gives the lie to the idea that his way was the property of a group. The report that carries Ibn ʿAbbās's name notes that the phrase was used to correct a claim the two communities had each made about the patriarch, and the correction sits inside the reply itself. The tradition on display is defined by what its founder was rather than by who claims him.

**LATER LABELS MEASURED AGAINST ABRAHAM**

Think of a family that is invited to join an exclusive association on the condition that it accepts the association's version of its own history. The verse declines the invitation on the ground that the family's way is older than the club, and the reply names the ancestor rather than arguing with the membership terms. The comparison explains why the Book does not engage the offer's details, since accepting the premise would already have conceded the point at issue. Anyone who has been invited to secure his position by adopting someone else's label can see the shape of the answer given here, and the answer is a statement rather than a debate. What the verse leaves with its reader is the confidence of a person who knows what he follows and who it belonged to.

**THE WAY THAT WAS THERE FIRST**

Read with the verses around it, the sentence explains why the sūrah has spent so much space on Abraham, Jacob and their family. The Book has been establishing the age and the content of the way it names, so that when the invitation comes the reply can point to a tradition older than the invitations. The same priority appears in another passage where the believers are told to follow the way of the man the whole argument has been about, which places the final instruction on the same foundation: (16:123 — **“Follow the faith of Abraham, the upright, who was not one of the polytheists.”**). What the reader should carry away is that the answer to a claim about belonging is a question about conduct, and the sūrah has been preparing him for that question since its opening verses. The next verse states the content of the belief the believers are to declare. Today, competing invitations to join a group must be measured against the older upright way, not against the confidence of the recruiter. What turns on the reply is the source of guidance: communal membership is rejected as the measure because Abraham's upright submission came before both later labels.
