**THE ATTRACTION OF THE WORLD**

***“The life of this world has been made appealing to the disbelievers, and they”*** establishes the attraction of the world. The verse explains why people may exchange guidance for worldly gain. The life of this world is made attractive to those who disbelieve. Wealth, status, pleasure, comfort, and immediate success can appear so complete that a person forgets death and judgement. The Qur’an does not say that worldly beauty is unreal or that believers may not enjoy lawful blessings. It warns against allowing temporary attraction to define the whole meaning of life. The verb made appealing—zuyyina—can describe both a test in Allah’s creation and the work of Satan and desire. The world becomes dangerous when its decoration hides consequences. A person sees the profit but not the injustice that produced it, the pleasure but not the addiction that follows, or the applause but not the emptiness of living for approval. When believers refuse a profitable sin or challenge a dominant culture, they may be mocked as naïve, backward, weak, or impractical. The verse acknowledges this social pressure. The disbelievers’ ridicule is not proof that the believers are inferior. It often reveals the discomfort of people who do not want their priorities questioned. Believers must not respond with arrogance or mockery of their own. The Qur’an forbids one people ridiculing another and teaches dignified speech. Firmness means maintaining faith and justice without becoming intoxicated by a sense of superiority. The worldly hierarchy will be reversed on the Day of Resurrection. (49:11 — **“O believers! Do not let some ˹men˺ ridicule others, they may be better than them,”**) Because the attraction of the world frames ridiculing the believers, ridiculing the believers returns attention to the attraction of the world. An account attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās names Bilāl, Salmān, and Ṣuhayb among the poor believers who were mocked, making social contempt part of the verse’s historical setting.

**RIDICULING THE BELIEVERS**

***“mock the believers. Those who are mindful ˹of Allah˺ will rank above them”*** deepens ridiculing the believers. People who appeared powerful may be exposed as spiritually bankrupt, while servants who were mocked for faith are honoured by Allah. Being above them refers to rank, security, and reward in the Hereafter, not a command for believers to seek domination over people in this life. The promise is not for every person who uses a religious identity. It is for those who are muttaqūn, mindful of Allah in belief and conduct. A believer who claims Islam while oppressing others cannot rely on the verse as a guarantee. Taqwā is proven through truth, patience, and justice. Allah gives provision—rizq—without measure. This can mean abundant worldly provision, unexpected spiritual gifts, or reward beyond calculation in the Hereafter. The phrase also comforts the believer who loses worldly status for refusing falsehood. Allah’s storehouses are not limited by the approval of a market, ruler, or audience. Provision is not always a sign of divine favour. The disbelievers may receive great wealth as a test, while a believer may be poor yet rich in faith and dignity. The Qur’an asks people to distinguish temporary distribution from final honour. Allah gives according to wisdom and will, and no lawful sacrifice is lost. Islam does not teach hatred of the created world. The earth, family, food, work, and beauty are signs and gifts. The problem is dunyā when it becomes the ultimate object of love and fear. (2:211 — **“Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We have given them.”**) Since the last reversal of social rank follows the attraction of the world, ridiculing the believers links purpose to the last reversal of social rank.

**THE LAST REVERSAL OF SOCIAL RANK**

***“on the Day of Judgment. And Allah provides for whoever He wills without limit”*** clarifies the last reversal of social rank. A believer uses the world as a means to the Hereafter, earning lawfully, supporting family, helping the poor, and cultivating knowledge. The Prophet ﷺ lived simply but accepted lawful gifts, married, traded, governed, and cared for society. He taught that wealth in itself is not blameworthy; what matters is how it is acquired, what it does to the heart, and how it is spent. A believer responds to mockery with patience, clarity, and good character. He does not abandon a command to gain acceptance, but he does not turn every disagreement into a fight. If ridicule becomes harassment or violence, he may seek protection and justice. Patience never requires accepting abuse without lawful remedy. Communities should support those who face pressure. Children, converts, minorities, and people leaving harmful environments may need companionship and practical help. The promise of Allah’s provision is lived partly through believers who become means of support for one another. Think of the attraction of the world through a man who laughs at a labourer for carrying water while he sits beside a well he has paid to dig. When the well dries up in the summer, the labourer's route to the spring is the only one left, and the man with the empty well is the one who asks for a cup. The attraction of the world reaches a reader today who measures his neighbours by the cars in their driveways and his own worth by the same count. (3:14 — **“The enjoyment of ˹worldly˺ desires—women, children, treasures of gold and silver, fine horses, cattle,”**) Provision without measure matters, which means the last reversal of social rank returns provision without measure to the attraction of the world.

**PROVISION WITHOUT MEASURE**

The verse gives him a ranking that is announced on another day and a provision that is given without limit, and it asks him which scale he uses when he looks at a poor man who prays. Because the comparison is placed on the day of judgement rather than in the market, the attraction of the world shows that the account is not settled by the ledger of this world. Since the mockery is named as the fruit of the adornment, the attraction of the world shows that a wrong measure of wealth turns into a wrong measure of people. The ranking is named as the reward of a quality and not of a fortune, and the last reversal of social rank leaves each person to ask which ladder he is climbing. The Book states the same reversal in the passage of the tables of the earlier community, so that the attraction of the world reveals the wealth of a denier and the punishment of a denier in one frame. A further verse keeps the guidance in the hand of God alone, and the attraction of the world shows that the two gifts of this verse and the next belong to one Giver. Ridiculing the believers makes the attraction of the world practical; the last reversal of social rank therefore matters.
