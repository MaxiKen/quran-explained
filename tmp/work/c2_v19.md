**A SECOND PICTURE OFFERED IN THE SAME BREATH**

The passage has already shown its reader one image of these men, and it now offers a second one for the same company: ***“Or ˹those caught in˺ a rainstorm from the sky with darkness, thunder”***, ***“and lightning. They press their fingers into their ears at the sound of every thunder-clap”***, ***“for fear of death.”***, ***“And Allah encompasses the disbelievers ˹by His might˺”***. The two pictures are not two groups; the little word that opens this sentence hands the reader a choice of how to see the same people, one by firelight and one by storm light. The rainstorm is drawn from the sky, which means the whole scene is above the travellers and out of their hands, and the darkness in it is not the darkness of a room but the darkness of a night that has swallowed the road. Thunder and lightning arrive together in the picture, so the sky is dangerous and useful in the same minute, and that is the exact shape of the situation being described. A person who has driven a mountain road through a storm knows how quickly a driver becomes grateful for every flash and afraid of the next one, and the verse uses that experience rather than explaining it.

**FINGERS IN THE EARS WHILE THE STORM PASSES**

The behaviour of the travellers is what the sentence reports next, and the detail it picks is observed with the eye of a witness. The men in the picture cannot stop the storm, so they attack the one part of it they can reach, which is the noise. That choice is the heart of the parable, because it shows what a person does when he cannot afford to hear something: he muffles it and calls the muffling prudence. Picture a man who turns up the radio whenever the weather report begins, and who then reports, honestly enough, that he has heard nothing alarming all week. Another passage describes the same clouds as a work of God that no one can arrest, (24:43 — **“He sends down from the sky mountains ˹of clouds˺”**), and a third shows the lightning itself arriving as a sign that provokes both hope and fear (13:12). The storm does not need the travellers' attention to keep working, and their fingers do not slow the lightning by a second. Their gesture is therefore not protection at all; it is a way of arranging their fear into something they can carry.

**FEAR OF DEATH AS A RULER**

The verse names the motive behind the gesture, and the motive is the oldest one there is: ***“for fear of death.”*** is the whole explanation of the man's posture. A person who is afraid of dying will trade a great deal to postpone the appointment, and the Book states that plainly rather than pretending the fear is unreasonable. The trouble is not that death is feared; the trouble is that the fear is allowed to govern, so that the man begins to organise his convictions around his safety. Another verse describes the same company with the same three ruined faculties and no mention of a storm, since the condition outlives the weather (2:171). A worker who stays silent about a theft at his company because the truth would cost him his job has made the same decision the travellers made, and made it in a calm office rather than in the rain. The Book wants the reader to see that the storm was only the occasion, and that the fear was already there.

**A HAND THAT SURROUNDS THE STORM**

The sentence closes with a statement that changes the whole picture, because it moves the reader's attention from the men to the One who holds the weather: ***“And Allah encompasses the disbelievers ˹by His might˺”***. The closing phrase means surrounding on every side, which means there is no outside to the scene, and no pocket of the storm in which a person is out of reach. That is why the fingers in the ears are the saddest detail in the passage, since the men are hiding from the loudest part of a situation whose owner is already nearer to them than their own jugular. A man cannot shelter from the rain in the street where he stands, because the rain and the street are both His, and the Book says as much in another place while listing the faculties as possessions of their Owner (10:31). Any reader can test the scale of the verse today by asking what he is currently pretending not to hear, and there is no muffling that answers a hand which surrounds everything.
