**TWO KINDS OF LOVE**

***“Still there are some”*** introduces the people whose religion is attachment. The verse names it in two movements: ***“who take others as Allah’s equal”*** and then ***“they love them as they should love Allah”***. Against them stand the believers, ***“but the ˹true˺ believers love Allah even more.”*** The comparative is the hinge of the verse, because it does not merely rank the two loves; it predicts their behaviour. The difference between them is discovered in what each love survives, and the verse stages the discovery as a public test. The ranking that hides in the heart in this world becomes the visible order of the next. The verse therefore refuses to let the ranking stay private, and the public test becomes the means of the discovery.

**WHAT PRESSURE REVEALS**

The devotees of equals love them as God is loved, yet when hardship arrives those same equals are abandoned, while the believers never reject God — so the claim of love is weighed by its endurance. The verse stages a courtroom for loves: each one testifies under pressure, and the counterfeit witness collapses. Ibn ʿAbbās reports the substance of the warning — those who take equals to Allah love them with a love owed to Him alone. The exposure continues in the verse's closing wish: ***“If only the wrongdoers could see the ˹horrible˺ punishment ˹awaiting them˺, they would certainly realize”*** that the betrayal has a price. The clause ***“that all power belongs to Allah”*** names what the wrongdoers fail to see, and the weight lands in the final beat: ***“and that Allah is”***, ***“indeed severe in punishment”***. Power and punishment are two faces of one seriousness. (9:24 — **“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “If your parents and children and siblings and spouses and extended family and the wealth you have acquired”**)

**GIFTS VERSUS ALTARS**

A hearer may ask whether loving what God gives can become a form of loving equals. The verse does not condemn affection; it condemns rank. Loving family or wealth as gifts is gratitude, but loving them as the decisive loyalty of one's life installs them at the altar, and that installation is what the verse calls taking equals. Enjoying the warmth of a fire is one act; bowing to it is another. The test is not how strong the affection is but what it does when it collides with obedience. The moment of collision is where the ranking appears. The verse's severity therefore targets the ranking rather than the feeling, and its comfort is that the believers' love is not weaker — it is simply placed correctly. The pressure-test explains the verse's setting: the ranking of loves decides the ranking of outcomes, and the outcome the verse names is severe. The image of the courtroom is therefore not decoration; it is the literal shape of the Day in which the ranking becomes visible to everyone at once. The heart cannot keep two supreme loyalties, because the moment they collide one of them is obeyed and the other is revealed as second. The verse describes the second love exactly, and its description doubles as a diagnosis: the equals are loved in the place reserved for Allah, which means the love is real but its address is wrong. Because the believers' love is proven by what it survives, the comparative is falsifiable — it can be checked against the hardest day of one's own life. The check is the application the verse asks for.

**THE EMPIRICAL TEST**

The condition of true love is stated plainly in (3:31 — **“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “If you ˹sincerely˺ love Allah, then follow me; Allah will love you and forgive your sins.”**). The transmission of Ibn ʿAbbās adds that the equals are loved with the magnification owed to the One alone. I read the comparative as evidence rather than ornament: measure the loves by their behaviour on the hard day, when obedience costs something, because that is the only scale the verse trusts. The comparative therefore invites the reader to check the data of his own loyalty. The check costs nothing and settles the ranking of the heart in one sitting. The evidence of love is what it refuses to abandon. The heart that keeps its ranking keeps its peace, and what Allah is to the heart is shown by what the heart will not give up.
