**REJECTION AS SELF-DEGRADATION**

The verse answers the standing question with a counter-question. ***“And who would reject the faith of Abraham except a fool!”***. The rejection is self-degradation in the verse's own grammar. The one who rejects the faith of Abraham does not ascend out of the family. He walks out of the raised footing the faith gives. The parallel passage states the witness in its first shape (60:4 — **“You already have an excellent example in Abraham and those with him, when they said to their people,”**). In Ibn ʿAbbās's definition the patriarch's standing has a name of its own: *"Khalīl, i.e. a friend."* The faith of Abraham is not a mood. It is a relationship. The friend of the Merciful. The one who rejects it rejects the standing the relationship gives. So the verse's word fool is a measuring word. The rejection is a loss of footing before it is a loss of doctrine. The reason the verse does not name the rejecters by tribe is that foolishness is portable. The faith is the fixed point.

**THE CHOOSING THAT NEVER ENDS**

The verse's answer is a choosing in two tenses. ***“We certainly chose him in this life”*** ***“and in the Hereafter he will surely be among the righteous”***. Through al-Kalbī, the report of Ibn ʿAbbās keeps the monuments of the old world inside the same frame. The houses and pillars and palaces of ʿĀd were built in the land. The one who passed like the passing of the wind took the whole of it. The measuring word says he did not attain so much as a foothold. The choosing runs through the same whole life. The friend of the Merciful. The chosen one. The one whose house was chosen before him and whose place was made a sanctuary. The choosing in this world and the placing in the next are one decision read across two horizons. The reason the verse can promise the Hereafter in the same breath as the choosing is that the choosing is a covenant. It is not a compliment. The other passage names the patriarch as the one who followed the way (37:83 — **“And indeed, one of those who followed his way was Abraham”**).

**THE CREED THAT HAS A FACE**

The faith of Abraham is named as a creed with a person inside it. The person is the proof. In the definition of Ibn ʿAbbās the friend and the chosen one are the same man at different stages of one life. The creed is not a list of propositions with a biography attached. It is a biography that carries the propositions on its back. For the modern reader the difference matters. This age is comfortable with ideas and suspicious of lives. The verse puts the life first. What follows is that the faith of Abraham is something a reader can walk into. The walking was done before the reader arrived. The parallel of the family's prayer shows the pattern. The building. The asking. The submitting. The showing of the rituals. The reason the verse keeps returning to the name of Abraham is that the name works as a standard. The one who believes in him joins the path he walked. The effect of the face inside the creed is visible. The rejection named at the opening becomes a refusal of a person. It is not merely a refusal of a text.

**THE BRACKETS OF THE SURVEY**

The verse's closing tenses do the survey work. The choosing in this life is already done. The Hereafter's placing is already promised. The brackets sit around the life of the chosen one. The reader stands between them. The choosing that has happened. The placing that is promised. The faith of Abraham is the way across. For any reader now the brackets hold around every life being measured. The world's choosing is temporary. The Hereafter's is the standing one. This age reads history as a sequence of winners. The verse reads it as a sequence of keepers. The reason the verse can call the faith of Abraham the only worth answering is that the brackets were closed by the same hand that opened them. The foolishness of the rejection is that it trades the standing promise for a standing that ends. The survey is complete. The answer is older than the question.
