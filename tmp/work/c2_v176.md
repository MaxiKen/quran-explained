**REVELATION IS TRUTH**

The verse develops revelation is truth with ***“That is because Allah has revealed the Book in truth.”***. The verse gives the reason for the punishment described above: Allah sent down the Book in truth—*with truth*. The revelation is true in its source, content, purpose, and judgement. It comes from the One who knows the human condition and shows people the way to worship, justice, and salvation. Because the Book is true, concealing or distorting it is not a minor intellectual error; it is a betrayal of reality. The Qur’an is also described as confirming earlier revelation and serving as a criterion over it. This means Muslims honour the original messages given to the prophets while recognizing the Qur’an as the final preserved revelation. *In truth* therefore includes continuity with the prophetic call and correction of human additions and distortions. Those who differ over the Book can refer to the People of the Book who disputed about the Qur’an, concealed its signs, or altered the meaning of their scripture. It can also include anyone who divides over revelation through denial, selective acceptance, invented interpretation, or conflict driven by worldly interest. Not every scholarly difference is condemned. The Qur’an itself recognizes sincere people who differ while seeking evidence, and the early jurists had legitimate disagreements in matters open to interpretation. The blameworthy *disagreement* is rooted in *deep opposition*, extreme opposition and hostility. It is not simply that two people reach different conclusions; it is that one side turns away from truth, breaks away from guidance, or uses disagreement to protect pride and power. A person can be wrong without being malicious, and he can be technically learned while being spiritually rebellious. (2:174 — **“Indeed, those who hide Allah’s revelations, trading them”**) Because revelation is truth governs 2:176, the book as a criterion arises from revelation is truth. It follows that unity around revelation completes revelation is truth through the book as a criterion.

**THE BOOK AS A CRITERION**

At the book as a criterion, attention falls on ***“And surely those who differ regarding it are totally engrossed in opposition”***. The Qur’an does not merely settle arguments after they become public. It provides a criterion before the argument begins. Muslims return to it and the Sunnah to distinguish principle from custom, revelation from speculation, and genuine disagreement from factional rivalry. The Book also corrects the tendency to select isolated passages while ignoring the whole message of justice, mercy, worship, and accountability. A sound interpretation respects the Arabic text, the context of revelation, the Prophet’s explanation, the understanding of the Companions, and the principles of the religion. It avoids forcing the Qur’an to say what a political movement, personal desire, or inherited prejudice already decided. The one who approaches the Book with humility is more likely to benefit from its clarity. The fact that Allah sent the Book in truth does not mean that every person will accept it. Allah allows people a genuine moral response, and the Qur’an records those who believe, those who deny, and those who conceal what they know. Revelation is a proof and guidance; it does not remove the responsibility of choosing. Muslims invite without coercion, explain without distortion, and leave hearts to Allah. They should not manipulate evidence to force agreement, because the truth does not need dishonesty to support it. (3:103 — **“so that you may be ˹rightly˺ guided”**) The reason unity around revelation follows the book as a criterion lies in revelation is truth. Therefore, unity around revelation returns practical force to the book as a criterion.

**UNITY AROUND REVELATION**

The community’s unity is not achieved by suppressing every difference or demanding loyalty to a personality. It is achieved by returning together to Allah’s Book and the authentic Sunnah, maintaining justice and good manners when interpretation differs. The Qur’an commands holding fast collectively to Allah’s rope and not dividing into hostile factions. Where a matter is clear, believers should not manufacture controversy. Where a matter is genuinely interpretive, they should preserve brotherhood and avoid accusing one another of disbelief or corruption without valid grounds. The extreme opposition condemned in the verse is not the careful work of scholarship; it is the will to fight the Book rather than be corrected by it. Modern disputes can repeat the old pattern. A person may reject revelation because it challenges a social ideology, or quote it selectively to sanctify a political interest. Another may abandon the Qur’an’s ethical balance by turning one passage into a weapon against the rest. The remedy is to read the Book as a coherent revelation and to ask what Allah is requiring of the heart and society. The final authority belongs to Allah, not to the loudest faction. Every interpreter remains a servant who can be corrected. This humility is the opposite of *deep opposition*. A map torn into favoured pieces cannot guide a traveller across the whole country. Each fragment may contain real roads, yet the journey fails when disliked sections are discarded and isolated marks are made to contradict the map's design. The revealed Book is approached as a whole in the same way. In present-day argument, selective quotation can make almost any desire sound sacred; the verse locates the deepest division not in honest inquiry but in resistance that pulls a coherent gift into hostile fragments. (4:59 — **“then refer it to Allah”**) In other words, unity around revelation answers revelation is truth.
