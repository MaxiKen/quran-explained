**THE BOOK SEALED IN TRUTH**

***“That is because Allah has revealed the Book in truth.”*** stands as the reasoning of the whole warning, and the logic is exact: because the Book is true, its concealment is a crime and its mishandling is a ruin. The verse then names the second party — not only the hiders but the arguers: ***“And surely those who differ regarding it are totally engrossed”***, closing with ***“in opposition”***. The difference described here is not a scholarly disagreement; it is an entrenched posture. The verse targets the heart's position toward the proof rather than the mind's estimate of it. The posture is what the verse measures, and the measurement is complete: the reader can take it this hour. The hour is therefore the subject of the verse as much as the Book is.

**TWO WAYS OF REFUSING**

The differers include two readings in one passage: the people of the Book who accept part of the revelation and deny part, and the idolaters of Mecca who pronounced the Qur'an poetry, sorcery, or divination — one party editing the text and the other dismissing it. Both share one posture: they will not stand under the Book's authority. Ibn ʿAbbās reports the difference in these terms — those who contravene what the Book records of the description of the Prophet, and further conceal it, are in open schism. The reason the verse says "totally engrossed" is that the opposition has become identity; the differers are not passing through a doubt but settled in a stance. The truth of the Book is named first because it removes the neutral middle. (3:105 — **“And do not be like those who split ˹into sects˺ and differed after clear proofs had come to them. It is they”**)

**DISAGREEMENT VERSUS OPPOSITION**

A hearer may ask whether disagreement about a text is not natural and sometimes honest. Honest disagreement explores; entrenched opposition refuses, and the verse distinguishes the two by posture rather than by vocabulary. The distinction keeps the door open to every questioner and closes it only to the settled refusal. The posture is therefore a choice made daily rather than a verdict delivered once, which is why the verse can address it to the people standing in front of it. Because the proof has been made clear, the difference that remains after clarity is not a shortage of evidence but a decision about authority. A man who argues with a map because he wants the road is a seeker; a man who pockets the map because he wants the territory is the refuser the verse marks. The verse is patient with questions and exact with refusals. The seal of truth on the Book is what makes each response legible: the same proof that educates the sincere convicts the entrenched. Both outcomes proceed from one truth rather than two texts. The truth of the Book is the premise of the entire passage, because a false book could be hidden without crime.

**THE PREMISE STATED**

The unity of the revealed way is asserted across the prophets in (42:13 — **“and make no divisions in it.” What you call the polytheists to is unbearable for them. Allah chooses”**), and the two passages together state the premise openly. Ibn ʿAbbās reports in these terms that the revelation was sent with the truth and yet they disbelieved, which answers the differers before they speak: their opposition is not an alternative reading but a refusal of the claim the Book makes. Read slowly, the premise convicts the entrenched and instructs the sincere at the same time. The application is to receive the Book the way its truth demands — whole, and under authority — because the posture toward the proof is the fork the verse describes. The reader who argues honestly is answered; the reader who pockets the map marks himself. Because the premise is stated before the difference is named, no one can claim the opposition was a reasonable default rather than a decision. The verse holds the door of argument open and marks the posture of those who will not walk through it. Those who differ regarding it declare their own distance. The verse records the declaration without commentary, because the posture needs no verdict to be understood — it delivers its own sentence in the act of refusing. The reader is left to choose which posture he occupies, and the choice is visible to him before it is visible to anyone else.
