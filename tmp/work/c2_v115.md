**THE COMPASS THAT RELAXES**

The unit that answers the worship-house cases returns to the question of direction itself: ***“To Allah belong the east and the west, so wherever you turn you are facing ˹towards˺ Allah.”***. The tradition behind the verse gives it two occasions, and both are worth holding. The Jews had criticized the change of the prayer-direction. And a group of companions had prayed while traveling at night without knowing which way the direction lay. The transmission from Ikrimah, from Ibn ʿAbbās, places those travelers at the center of the scene. The verse to them is a relaxation, not a repeal. The east and the west are named as the entire earth's two handles, the pair of directions that between them stand for the whole. The ownership of both handles is then read as the permission the travelers needed. Their prayer faced the pleasure of the one who owns every face's turning.

**A DIRECTION THAT SURVIVES THE TRAVEL**

The design of the rule deserves its own look, because a rule that survives the road is a rule built for human life. The reason the relaxation can exist is that the ownership of the direction never sat in the servant's hands. What follows is a rule that survives every road and every night. The house stands with its wall. The traveler moves without one. The verse keeps both situations inside one ownership clause. The face turns as commanded, and where the command cannot fix the wall, the owner of the walls takes the face as it comes. The gloss of the received text takes the clause in exactly this key: whithersoever you turn your faces in prayer by his command, there is the face of God, the direction of prayer in his pleasure. The logic at work is the logic of the previous unit's building code. The houses stand for the name. The name owns the ground the houses stand on. So when the ground itself turns under a traveler's feet, the name has not moved.

**THE FACE AND THE COUNTENANCE**

Notice the two faces in the verse's vocabulary. There is the face the servant turns. There is the countenance the turning meets. The pairing is the passage's summary of the whole direction-question. The servant is responsible for the turning as commanded. The reception belongs to the owner of the east and west. The ownership of the compass has been settled in the answer to the foolish of the people: (2:142 — **“˹O Prophet,˺ “The east and west belong ˹only˺ to Allah. He guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path.””**). The sustaining behind the ownership is the sustaining of the keeper who never sleeps: (2:255 — **“All-Sustaining. Neither drowsiness nor sleep overtakes Him. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth.”**). The two verses of the book stand as the survey lines of the present verse. The same pairing governs the ownership surveys that have run through the passage since the abrogation unit. The schedule of the law moves as the lawgiver directs, and the lawgiver's ground includes every road the law will ever be kept on. The travelers of the first occasion therefore teach the standing rule to every later journey. Keep the command where the command is clear. Where the road takes the clarity away, the owner of the road keeps the prayer. The relaxation is thus a door built for the weather of real travel. The hinge is the ownership. The critics can stand at the hinge all day without closing the door.

**THE CLAUSE AFTER THE HOUSE CASES**

The placement of this relaxation directly after the worship-house verses is deliberate. The houses were defended as the homes of the mention itself. The verse now widens the mention beyond the house's walls. The name is mentioned on the road. The name is mentioned in the dark. The east and west belong to the same owner the houses belong to, and the mention travels on the owner's ground without a permit from the critics. The received record of the occasion shows the critics doing exactly what the passage has catalogued. A change in the schedule is treated as a defect in the authority. The verse answers by locating the authority over the compass itself. The critics own neither needle. The traveler prays. The owner receives. That is the geometry of the relaxation, and it holds wherever the journey goes.
