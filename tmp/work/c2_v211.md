**SIGNS COUNTED THEN LOST**

The summons to ask is itself the first phrase, and the counting runs through the rest: ***“Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We have given them.”***, ***“And whoever trades Allah’s favour”***, ***“after receiving it”***, ***“˹for disbelief˺ should know”***, ***“that Allah is indeed severe in punishment”***. and the closing phrase prices the trade in the wrong currency. The walk moves from the interrogation to the inventory and ends in the penalty, which is the shape of a case argued in open court. The counting is commanded rather than narrated because the history belongs to the witnesses of the history, and the witnesses are still alive to be asked. Ibn ʿAbbās glosses the question as a count of the commands, the prohibitions, and the honours that were delivered.

**THE SEA AND THE MANNA**

The scale is read at the door of the chapter where the map of the households is drawn. The inventory of the signs is left to the witnesses themselves, which is the verse's method of putting history in the mouth of its owners. The reports on the passage fill the inventory with the sea parted, the manna and the quails sent down, and the honours repeated across the ages. Ibn ʿAbbās counts the commands and the prohibitions that were delivered alongside the favours, so the signs were rulings as well as miracles. Because the question is addressed to the Children of Israel, the history is testified to by the community that carries it in its own books. The favour named in the verse is a favour of guidance first and of food second.

**TRADING THE FAVOUR**

The verdict sentence converts the history into a case: whoever trades the favour after it has arrived has made an exchange the market cannot price. The trading of favours for ingratitude is described in the general legislation of the favour with the same contempt for the exchange (14:28 — **“Have you not seen those ˹disbelievers˺ who meet Allah’s favours with ingratitude and lead their own people to their doom”**). Because the trade is dated by the favour's arrival, the ingratitude is a decision made with the goods in hand. The application is to inventory one's own favours before defending the traders of history. The exchange can be made by anyone who treats a blessing as a possession rather than as a loan.

**SEVERITY AFTER PATIENCE**

The closing clause attaches the penalty to the trade and the patience that precedes it: whoever trades the favour should know the severity of the penalty. The remembrance of the favours of old carries the same reminder of the favour's owner (2:122 — **“O Children of Israel! Remember My favours upon you and how”**). Because the severity is stated after the counting, the penalty is measured against the size of the favour, and the size is the aggravating fact. The one who reads the verse as a story about an ancient people has missed the second-person arithmetic inside it. The counting is commanded because the counting is the warning. The asking for the sky is the oldest transaction of the demand, and the demand is refused at the sky and answered at the lamp. The old chapter keeps the image of the house that asks for its own furnishing, and the furnishing arrives in the currency of the everyday. Because the demand is refused at the level of the spectacle, the answer is located at the level of the provision. The community of the past that was one community before the splitting is the portrait of the demand that splits the house. The dispute of the two disputants at the door of the house of the guidance is the dispute of the demand that wants the sky before the road. The one who has been given the clear sign and still demands the spectacle has confused the sign for the sky. Reading the demand beside the lamp is the discipline the passage trains, and the discipline is the patience of the everyday. The lamp is not a lesser gift, which is the passage's quiet correction of the demand. The house of the guidance furnishes itself from the everyday, and the everyday is the currency in which the lamp is priced. The furnishing of the house is priced in the small coins of the patience.
