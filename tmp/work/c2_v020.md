**LIGHT ENOUGH TO WALK, DARK ENOUGH TO STOP**

The second picture continues with the same storm and adds a new detail: ***“It is as if the lightning were about to snatch away their sight.”*** and ***“Whenever lightning strikes, they walk in its light”***. The flash is sudden, painful, and brief, and while it lasts the people move; then comes ***“but when darkness covers them, they stand still. Had Allah willed, He could have taken away their hearing”***, ***“and sight. Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything”***. The verse is describing people who travel only when they are lit up from outside and halt the moment the light is withdrawn. Their movement is real, and it belongs entirely to the flash rather than to them. The image is precise about how this kind of person uses religion. In the presence of a clear sign, a crisis, a sermon that lands, or a calamity that frightens, he moves quickly and promises a great deal. When the ordinary week returns, he is stationary again, and the promises are quietly forgotten. Nothing in the picture suggests that he is insincere about the movement; he really does walk in the light. What the verse shows is that the walking has no engine of its own, and a road that is only ever travelled in short bursts is not being travelled at all. The verse that describes the opposite state puts the same image in the light of the Hereafter: (57:12 — **“On that Day you will see believing men and women with their light shining ahead of them”**).

**THE PURPOSE OF A BORROWED LIGHT**

The Book uses lightning elsewhere as a sign that stirs two opposite responses at the same time, and the phrasing there belongs beside this scene: (13:12 — **“He is the One Who shows you lightning, inspiring ˹you with˺ hope and fear, and produces heavy clouds”**). Hope and fear are the two effects that sign is meant to have, and neither one is a destination in itself. Hope and fear are the two effects, and neither one is meant to be the destination. They are the servants of a decision, and the people in this sūrah stop at the servants. They are moved while the flash lasts and return to stillness when it ends, because nothing was built in them that could carry the light forward. That is the whole difference between a person who is stirred and a person who is changed. The verse also says plainly that the flash was never the only available light, and the last clause makes the point without flattery: ***“and sight. Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything”***. The faculties were given, the message was delivered, and the storm was permitted to pass over them. Had God wished, the hearing and the sight could have been taken outright, and the verse notes this at the end as a reminder of how much is being held in loan. What is on offer is not an absence of light but a light they choose not to keep when the thunder quiets down.

**WALKING WITHOUT THE FLASH**

A person who wants to know which kind of traveller he is can examine what he does in the weeks when nothing is happening, because that is where the engine shows itself. The Ramadan afterglow, the illness, the funeral, and the grave sermon are the flashes; the following Tuesday is where the answer lives. The verse does not ask for a life without feeling, and it does not suggest that being moved is a fault. It asks for something built that continues when the feeling goes, and the Book has already named what that looks like in this sūrah: a routine of prayer, a habit of giving, and a settled confidence about the meeting to come, kept up whether or not the sky is loud.

The practical step, and one that can be taken today, is to give a decision made during a flash a form that survives the dark. A resolution made after a funeral can become a page in a notebook with a date and a payment attached; a promise made in a hard month can be made visible to someone who will ask about it later. That is how the light is kept, and it is the opposite of what the travellers in this verse do. They walk only while the lightning is over their heads, which is why the sūrah leaves them standing still in the dark with the road in front of them. The reason this image matters to a reader today is that it puts the failure where he can reach it, since the light is offered again every morning.
