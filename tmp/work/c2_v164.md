**SIGNS, NOT OBJECTS OF WORSHIP**

The verse develops signs, not objects of worship with ***“Indeed, in the creation of the heavens”***, ***“and the earth”***, ***“the alternation of the day”***, ***“and the night”***. The previous āyah declared Allah’s oneness, and this āyah presents signs of that oneness throughout creation. The word *āyāt* means signs, indications, and evidences. The heavens and earth do not merely exist as neutral scenery; their order, dependence, variety, and renewal point beyond themselves to the Creator. The Qur’an directs attention to the created world without asking people to worship nature or to treat natural processes as independent gods. Reflection on creation is a form of *tafakkur*. It is not a replacement for revelation, nor is every personal impression a proof of doctrine. It is disciplined observation joined to humility. The more a person sees the complexity and interdependence of the world, the more he recognizes his own dependence and Allah’s wisdom. The creation of the heavens and the earth encompasses realities far beyond human control. The vastness of the sky, the stability of the earth, and the conditions that make life possible all expose human limitation. People may discover laws and develop technologies, but they do not create the underlying existence of matter, time, life, or consciousness from nothing. The Qur’an’s purpose is not to provide a technical textbook. It directs the mind to the originator of this order, to the intelligibility of its patterns, and to the source upon which life’s delicate network depends. Scientific investigation can describe mechanisms, and that description can itself become a means of appreciating the wisdom of the Creator. The verse resists both superstition and the arrogance that mistakes description for ultimate explanation. Because 2:164 begins under signs, not objects of worship, its later claims must be read through that controlling idea. (2:163 — **“the Most Compassionate, Most Merciful”**) Because signs, not objects of worship governs 2:164, night and day arises from signs, not objects of worship. It follows that rain and the revival of earth completes signs, not objects of worship through night and day.

**NIGHT AND DAY**

At night and day, attention falls on ***“the ships that sail the sea”***, ***“for the benefit of humanity”***, ***“the rain sent down by Allah from the skies, reviving the earth after its death”***. The alternation of night and day regulates rest, work, seasons, worship, and the rhythms of life. It is constant enough for human planning yet varied enough across regions and seasons to reveal the earth’s movement and changing conditions. The darkness of night can offer rest and reflection; the light of day enables activity and provision. Both are gifts, and neither should be treated as an accident without meaning. The Qur’an uses this alternation elsewhere as a sign of Allah’s power over resurrection. A world that renews itself each morning after night makes the return of bodies less difficult to imagine. The believer does not confuse analogy with proof, but he recognizes that the One who brings light after darkness can bring life after death. Ships sail through the sea carrying people, food, knowledge, and goods. Their movement depends on the properties of water, wind, buoyancy, human design, and Allah’s permission. The verse acknowledges human benefit and ingenuity without allowing people to forget the Creator of the materials, minds, and natural laws that make navigation possible. The sea can also humble human beings. A vessel that seems powerful becomes vulnerable in a storm, and sailors instinctively call upon Allah when familiar means fail. Maritime trade should therefore be accompanied by gratitude and justice, not exploitation, piracy, or the enrichment of a few through the suffering of distant communities. (3:190 — **“Indeed, in the creation of the heavens”**) The reason rain and the revival of earth follows night and day lies in signs, not objects of worship. Therefore, rain and the revival of earth returns practical force to night and day.

**RAIN AND THE REVIVAL OF EARTH**

Rain and the revival of earth draws together ***“the scattering of all kinds of creatures throughout”***, ***“the shifting of the winds”***, ***“and the clouds drifting between the heavens”***. Rain falls, enters the soil, feeds rivers and roots, and brings dead-looking land back to life. The phrase *after its death* is both a description of the earth and a sign of resurrection. Seeds hidden in dry ground emerge, multiply, and produce nourishment. The process is regular enough to be studied but wondrous enough never to be exhausted by description. Water is also a moral reminder. A blessing that sustains every creature should not be hoarded, polluted, or wasted. The Prophet ﷺ prohibited extravagance even while using water for ablution, as reported in Sunan Ibn Mājah and other collections. Stewardship of the earth is part of gratitude to the One who sends its rain. Allah spread throughout the earth creatures of countless forms, capacities, and relationships. Their diversity invites knowledge and care. Humans are not permitted to treat animals as meaningless resources; the Qur’an describes animal communities as communities like humanity, and the Prophet ﷺ warned against cruelty and praised mercy to living beings. The changing winds carry heat, cold, clouds, seeds, and rain. Clouds are controlled between heaven and earth, gathering and dispersing according to patterns that sustain life. The Qur’an draws attention to movement and balance: wind can bring mercy, but it can also become destructive. Human beings are reminded that power and benefit are in Allah’s hand and that creation is not theirs to abuse. (16:10 — **“Who sends down rain from the sky, from”**)

**REASON GUIDED BY REVELATION**

***“and the earth”***, ***“˹in all of this˺ are surely signs”***, ***“for people of understanding”*** mark the turn toward reason guided by revelation. The closing appeal addresses people who exercise reason—*people who exercise reason*. The Qur’an honours intellect, yet the intellect so honoured must follow evidence and moral truth. *Reason* is not merely cleverness or technical skill. It is the capacity to restrain the self, connect signs to their meaning, and act wisely. A person can be highly educated yet fail to understand if pride prevents him from recognizing the Creator. The correct method joins observation, reflection, revelation, and gratitude. Creation points to Allah, and revelation teaches how to worship Him and use His creation justly. This produces a science of humility rather than a culture of exploitation. A house may be so familiar that its residents stop noticing the beams that hold the roof, the pipes that bring water, and the current that gives light. A visitor often sees the coordinated gift more quickly than those who live inside it. The world named in this verse is like that house: its cycles, waters, creatures, winds, and clouds are not background scenery but an ordered address. The reader today is asked to recover the visitor's attention without surrendering the resident's careful study. (30:20 — **“then behold! you are human beings spreading over ˹the earth˺”**) In other words, reason guided by revelation answers signs, not objects of worship.
