**THE TEST THAT COSTS ONLY THE TONGUE**

The passage now proposes an experiment, and the experiment's design is the verse's own exhibit: ***“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “If the ˹eternal˺ Home of the Hereafter with Allah is exclusively”***, ***“for you ˹Israelites˺ out of all humanity”***. The claim being tested belongs to a family of claims the section has already entered into evidence — the fire limited to counted days, the Garden reserved for one lineage, the standing of beloved sons — and the experiment is built to test the whole family at once. The two conditionals of the sentence are joined so that the first leans on the second: if the world to come is truly yours alone, and if what you say is true, then the desire for it should show itself in the wanting of its door. The logic the glosses draw out is the logic of preference made public — a man inclines toward what is his own, and the path to the prize is the door of death, so the claim's sincerity can be read off the wanting. The twin challenge issued later in the revelation to the same claim is worded closely enough to be its own echo: (62:6 — **“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O Jews! If you claim to be Allah’s chosen ˹people˺ out of all humanity, then wish for death,”**). What follows from the design is the verse's whole epistemology of religious claims: the claim is not debated in the abstract; it is priced against the claimant's own preferences.

**THE TONGUE'S TOLL FOR THE TRUTH**

The proposed act is then named in its smallest form: ***“then wish for death”***. The transmitted accounts of the scene frame it as the ancient trial of mutual imprecation — the summons to bring down the sentence on the lying camp of the two parties — and the record states that the offer was declined. The transmitted readings span the range of the wishing: to long for the meeting, to ask for the death, and — in the reading the earliest authority transmits — to invoke God for it outright, so that the trial is a prayer as well as a wish. The method deserves its note in the tradition's own commentary on the verse: this is the abnormal method of settling a dispute, chosen precisely because the normal one had been refused. Discussion requires both parties to argue; this test requires only a little strain on the tongue, and the declaration either comes or it does not. The prediction embedded in the offer is part of its design — the passage knows the wish will not be made — which converts the refusal into the answer sheet before the trial even opens. In other words, the test is one-sided by construction: sincerity would say the word, and the silence is scored.

**THE WISH AND ITS DELIVERY MECHANISM**

The third clause carries the conditional's sting — ***“if what you say is true!””*** — and the traditions of the scene report what the wish would have cost in the currency of the world. The transmission through the chain of Ibn ʿAbbās states the outcome three ways, and the statements are recorded with their authentic chains: if the community had invoked for death they would have perished; had they asked for it, the asker would have choked on his own saliva; and the saying of the Prophet transmitted in the same lines confirms that wishing would have brought the death and shown the seats of the wishers. The vividness of the middle report is worth keeping in the account, because it turns an abstraction into an event: the trial was not a thought experiment but a standing order with a delivery mechanism. The earliest gloss on the closing verb of the condition fixes the register — the truthfulness asked for is truthfulness in the claim, the very claim of exclusive inheritance. The scene's archival lesson is procedural. A religious claim that will not be stress-tested by its own terms has already left the courtroom.

**THE DECLINATION AS THE VERDICT**

The verse's afterlife in the tradition is the afterlife of a refused dare. The offer stood on the table — pronounce the wish, establish the claim — and the offer was withdrawn from without a word of rebuttal; the passage then treats the silence as the confession. The reason the silence convicts is the same reason the experiment was designed: the claim of exclusive salvation is a claim about preference, and preference is the one testimony the claimant cannot fake at the moment of truth. The theological family of claims around this one is refuted the same way across the section — not with counter-theology but with a stress test the claimants themselves administer. For a modern reader the method is transferable without remainder: when a community announces that the world to come is reserved to its address, the verse supplies the only question that matters, and it is a question about the community's own desires.

**THE INSTRUMENT THAT NEVER RUSTS**

The closing lesson of the passage is about the portability of the trial, and the portability is total. Every age produces its own version of the exclusive-claim — the blessed class, the guaranteed cohort, the lineage that cannot lose — and the verse keeps the same instrument ready: measure the claim against the claimant's own willingness. The effect of the experiment on the reader is to replace argument with introspection in one move, since nobody has ever needed a debate to know what they love. What turns on that substitution is the whole moral of the section: the honesty of a doctrine is visible in what its men want, and the wish that will not cross the lips is the doctrine's own verdict in advance.
