**GLORY COMES BEFORE ANY DEFENCE OF THE SELF**

The angels answer the test without bluff or excuse: ***“They replied, “Glory be to You!”*** Their first concern is to declare God free from defect, injustice, and error. They do not protect their earlier question by pretending to know the names. They also do not turn their inability into a charge that the task was unfair. Glory places the Lord’s perfection above the limit just exposed in them. Ibn ʿAbbās reads the response as a return from what they had said, which gives it the shape of repentance as well as praise. The angels had named a true danger in human corruption, but now they see that their knowledge did not hold the whole wisdom. They correct their stance at once. This is why their response is stronger than simple silence. Silence could hide pride, fear, or confusion; glorification openly assigns every defect to the creature’s view and none to the Creator. The scene also completes the proper manner of their question in verse 30. They asked within praise, received an answer, and submitted when the answer revealed their limit. The Quran elsewhere says angels are those (66:6 — **“who never disobey whatever Allah orders—always doing as commanded”**). Here obedience includes the humility to let new knowledge change the conclusion formed from what was known before.

**CREATED KNOWLEDGE NEVER ESCAPES THE GIFT OF TEACHING**

Their confession continues: ***“We have no knowledge except what You have taught us. You are truly the All-Knowing, All-Wise.””*** The first half does not mean that the angels know nothing at all. They had already spoken truly about praise, holiness, corruption, and bloodshed. It means that every true thing they know came within a measure God granted. Their knowledge is real, but it is received. The same is true of Adam even though he now knows names they do not. His distinction came through teaching, not through an independent store beyond God’s reach. Therefore, the test produces no ground for pride on either side. It displays different gifts under one Teacher. Human beings meet this truth from birth, study, correction, memory, and loss. A skilled person can forget; a student can learn; and one field can remain closed to a master of another. The Quran tells humanity that it has (17:85 — **“been given but little knowledge.””**). Calling that share little is not contempt for learning. It is the right measure of creaturely knowledge beside the One whose knowledge has no edge. This is why growth in learning should increase care in speech. The more a person sees of a subject, the more clearly he can see what remains beyond him.

**ADMITTING IGNORANCE IS PART OF TRUSTWORTHY KNOWLEDGE**

The angels do not guess in order to preserve status. Their example turns the admission of ignorance into an act of truth and worship. An answer given without knowledge may impress a hearer for a moment, but it breaks the bond between words and reality. In matters of faith, law, safety, medicine, family, or public duty, such false confidence can also harm others. The Quran rebukes those who move from limited knowledge into baseless argument: (3:66 — **“but why do you now argue about what you have no knowledge of? Allah knows and you do not know”**). The difference is not between speaking and never speaking. It is between reporting what one has been taught and claiming what one has not. A faithful learner can say that evidence supports one conclusion, that another matter remains uncertain, or that someone else knows more. This does not weaken authority. It makes authority safe. The angels lose no honour by refusing a false answer; the next verse will let them learn through Adam. Their knowledge can increase because they first tell the truth about its present limit. Today, easy access to many words can make borrowed facts feel like full mastery. This verse restores a clean boundary: use what has truly been learned, seek more where needed, and do not cover a gap with confident sound.

**THE TWO DIVINE NAMES EXPLAIN THE HUMAN CHOICE**

The two divine names close the reply. The All-Knowing possesses what is open and hidden, past and future, whole and detail. The All-Wise places each creature, gift, command, and result in its right setting. Knowledge without wisdom could identify facts without ordering them to a good end; wisdom without knowledge could not judge every factor. In God the two are perfect and inseparable. This directly answers the concern of verse 30. He knew the blood that would be shed, but He also knew Adam’s teachable nature and every faithful life to come. He understood the cost and the good with a fullness unavailable to the angels at that point. The order of the names also matters to the scene. God knew what they did not, and His choice of an earthly successor rested on wisdom rather than mere power. Elsewhere clear commands are joined to the same pair: (24:18 — **“And Allah makes ˹His˺ commandments clear to you, for Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise”**). The believer may not yet see every reason, but trust does not rest on darkness. It rests on the known perfection of the One who commands. The angels’ answer therefore joins humility with confidence: they deny full knowledge to themselves while affirming that complete knowledge and flawless wisdom are exactly where they must be.
