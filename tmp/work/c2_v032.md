**THE ANSWER OF A CREATURE THAT KNOWS ITS LIMIT**

The verse that follows is the reply of the angels, and it is one of the shortest and best-known confessions in the Book: ***“They replied”***, ***“Glory be to You!”***. The word for glory declares God free of every defect, and the angels use it before they say anything about themselves, which means the correction they had received was accepted without argument. The early teachers pointed to this order as the model of how a creature should answer a charge: the majesty of God is acknowledged first, and the defence of the self comes afterwards and only as far as it is true. The Book records the same opening on the lips of other servants in other places, and the phrase is a standard by which a person's tone can be measured. The angels could have named their years of worship as a claim; instead they began by clearing their Lord of any injustice, and the reader is meant to notice the difference.

**THE CONFESSION THAT REMOVES THE CLAIM**

The reply continues with a sentence that gives away the whole position: ***“We have no knowledge except what You”***, ***“have taught us.”***. The wording is absolute, and the early teachers read it as a confession that no creature owns knowledge as property; everything known is a loan that was granted. Ibn ʿAbbās is credited with the reading that the angels here admitted the boundary of their knowledge rather than making a plea, and the sūrah lets their words stand as an answer to the claim they had made about their own worthiness. The Book gives the same limit a voice in another sūrah, where the human being is told how much of the hidden he has been given: (17:85 — **“you ˹O humanity˺ have been given but little knowledge.”**). The angels' confession is the same admission made by a creature who has no interest in pretending to a rank he does not hold, and the sentence is short because a limit once admitted needs no decoration. What turns on the confession is that a creature's knowledge does not entitle him to rank; the One who gave it decides what it is for, which is the point the angels had missed when they weighed their worship against the coming creature.

**THE NAMES THAT CLOSE THE REPLY**

The Book uses the same absolution for a servant who cannot answer, since the admission of a limit is not a defeat in this register. The verse ends with the titles that explain why the answer is where it is: ***“You are truly the”***, ***“All-Knowing, All-Wise.”***. The first title covers the knowledge that the angels lacked and the second covers the arrangement they could not see, which means the reply has been answered by God's own description of Himself. The Book pairs knowledge and wisdom in several places where the arrangement of things is being explained, and the most famous of those places shows how far the knowledge reaches: (2:255 — **“no one can grasp any of His knowledge—except what He wills ˹to reveal˺”**). The pairing is a summary of the whole scene, since the first name answers the question about what God knows and the second answers the question about what God is doing. The reason the angels call on wisdom as well as knowledge is that they had questioned not only what God knew but what He was doing, and the second name addresses the second half of their question. Think of a foreman who is told that the owner's decision was made with reasons he is not able to see, and who answers that the owner knows the site better than he does. The sentence is the same shape as the angels' reply, and it leaves the foreman with something to do rather than with a grievance to nurse. A person who works under an authority larger than his information has to decide whether he will serve with trust or with suspicion, and this verse shows a creature choosing trust after being corrected in public. These days a person can hold the same question about the way things are run, and the passage gives him the same answer plus the same lesson in how to pose it.
