**A STRIKE THAT PUT THE DEAD BACK ON HIS FEET**

The instruction is delivered and the miracle follows it in one sentence: ***“So We instructed, “Strike the dead body with a piece of the cow.” This is how ˹easily˺ Allah brings the dead to life, showing you His signs”***, ***“so that you may understand”***. Nothing is said about the moment life returned, and the words of the dead man are left out. The verse states what the act was for, which is the point the reader is meant to keep: the sign was given so that the people could understand something about the One who gives life. The command to strike is itself instructive, since the instrument of resurrection is a piece of an animal that had been slaughtered in obedience, and the miracle is tied to an act of compliance that had cost the community an argument. What they had haggled over for days became the means by which a dead man spoke.

**THE SIGN THAT SETTLED A CASE**

The first purpose of the miracle was local and practical: a murder had to be resolved and the Book says earlier that the truth had been concealed. The second purpose is named in this verse and is much larger, since the raising of one man was a demonstration of the power that will raise everyone. The Book often uses a particular event to carry a general truth, and this is one of its clearest cases, because the same verb that describes the dead man standing up describes what will happen to all the dead on the day of the meeting. A reader who wants to hold the two together can read the verse as an advance instalment of an event that the whole sūrah has been building toward since its opening description of the believers and their certainty about the meeting. The crowd watching that day saw one man; the readers of the page are meant to see the whole promise.

**THE DETAILS THE ACCOUNTS ADD**

The accounts that came down about this scene add details that the verse leaves out, and the reader may take them as reports rather than as the text itself. They describe the animal being struck, the dead man rising, and the naming of the killer; some add that he asked to be returned, and one tradition says the sign was given to a single man so that a community's quarrel could end. The verse itself is spare by comparison with those accounts, which is a reminder that the Book's telling is arranged for the reader's benefit rather than for the sake of the story. The same power is described in another passage of this sūrah in exactly those terms, where the raising of the dead is placed beside the other signs given to earlier prophets. What a reader can check for himself is the connection the verse itself makes, since it moves from one raised body to the general power in a single sentence.

**A COMPARISON FROM AN ORDINARY WEEK**

The image here is easy to carry into a common experience, because everyone has watched a situation that seemed finished come back to life. A case that was closed, a debt that was written off, a friendship that had been given up for lost: the return of any of them is unexpected in the same way as a dead man standing up, though the power behind it is rarely noticed. The Book's use of the smaller event to teach the larger one is the same method a teacher uses when he points at a lamp and speaks of the sun. What the sign was designed to correct in the people watching it was not a doubt about their own resurrection; it was the habit of treating God's commands as negotiable business. A reader who finds his own attention slipping during an argument over details may be the person this verse was written for.

**UNDERSTANDING AS THE PURPOSE OF A SIGN**

The verse ends with the word for understanding, which places the miracle in the service of a mind that has to be changed rather than a crowd that has to be impressed. Signs in the Book are regularly followed by this same clause, and the clause makes the reader responsible for what he does with what he has seen. A miracle that produces no change of conduct has missed the purpose named here, and the people in this story had already seen enough wonders to fill a lifetime. The demand for proofs and the failure to draw conclusions from them are both present in the chapter, sometimes in the same paragraph about the same people. The verse therefore closes the account of the cow with the reason it was all arranged, and the reason is not the animal at all: it is that a community should come to understand what it had been watching.
