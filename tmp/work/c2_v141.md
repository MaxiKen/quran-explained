**A LEDGER OF TWO COLUMNS**

History closes a file here with a three-part verdict. ***“That was a community”*** ***“that had already gone before.”***. The refrain of the patriarchs returns to shut the long dispute over the direction. The people named through that dispute have finished their term. Ibn ʿAbbās's account reports the date-stamp plainly: *"a group of people who have passed away; perished."* Their making-time is over. The reading of the sentence is therefore a boundary marker rather than a boast. The verse does not celebrate the ending of a rival. It records an ending that any community can have. What follows is that history becomes safe to study. A closed file can be read without being inherited. The dispute with the People of the Book over prophets, qiblahs, and merit is now behind the community. The verse itself stands at the door of the archive and reads the label aloud. The effect of the placement is structural. Everything the previous pages established is filed under the names of people who no longer walk the street. The archive of the dispute is thereby closed to further editing.

**THE EARNED AND THE EARNED**

The second sentence splits the record into two ledgers. ***“For them is what they earned”*** ***“and for you is what you have earned.”***. Two columns. Two owners. No crossings. The reading of Ibn ʿAbbās keeps the columns strict. The earnings of the dead stay with the dead. The earnings of the living stay with the living. The point of the paired clauses is that no one inherits an account. A father's faith does not post to a son's book. A teacher's work does not post to a student's book. Each ledger is opened in its own name. The same strictness shows up at the seat of judgment itself (99:6 — **“On that Day people will proceed in separate groups to be shown ˹the consequences of˺ their deeds”**). Separate groups. Shown deeds. The ledger language of the earth's courts is taken up and perfected at the throne. The two columns are therefore not a metaphor invented by commentators. They are the accounting policy of the final court, announced in advance so that no litigant arrives surprised.

**THE VERDICT WITHOUT CROSSINGS**

The closing clause removes the last fear. ***“And you will not be accountable”*** ***“for what they have done”***. The boundary of accountability is drawn at the self. In the transmitted reading of Ibn ʿAbbās the clause comforts the Prophet against the charge of failing with the dead. He was not their keeper. Their accounts closed with their breath. The same accounting is stated in its smallest unit at the scales (99:7 — **“So whoever does an atom’s weight of good will see it”**). Atom-weight. Seen. Nothing crosses between the two columns because nothing is lost inside them. For the modern reader the discipline is double-edged. It releases the living from the guilt of the dead and it loads the living with their own file. This age loves inherited praise and hates inherited blame. The verse refuses both packages. The good of the previous generation is honoured in the archive, and the present generation writes its own pages with its own hands. The verdict has no crossings. The columns stand open until they close.

**A FILE CLOSED, A FILE OPENED**

The practical force of the passage lands in the ordinary rooms where ledgers are kept. A household that studies its ancestors inherits lessons and never balances. A congregation that retells its founders inherits honour and never discipline. The verse separates the two inheritances with surgical care, and the separation is a mercy in both directions. The dead are released from the anxiety of their successors' deeds. The living are released from the credit of their predecessors' names. Every generation stands at its own counter with its own book. The reason this matters at the close of the qiblah section is structural. The community has just been re-pointed, re-sent, and re-commissioned. The temptation of the moment would have been to lean on the borrowed light of the older houses. The verse refuses the loan. The light of this community is the deed-book it writes under the new direction. For the modern reader the double release is news worth hearing twice. This age markets ancestry as identity and forgets the counter. The verse keeps the counter open and the ancestry in the archive where it belongs. The file of history is closed. The file of the present is still accepting entries.
