**REALITY REVEALED AFTER THE AUDIENCE**

***“And when they leave ˹you˺, they strive throughout”*** establishes reality revealed after the audience. The person described in the previous verse appears impressive while speaking, but when he turns away—tawallā—his hidden priorities become visible. He moves through the land creating fasād, corruption, and destroys crops and livestock. The verse contrasts public image with public consequence. A person may claim concern for prosperity and people, yet leave behind damaged livelihoods and suffering. Turns away can mean leaving the Prophet’s presence, turning away from the truth, or obtaining authority and then acting without accountability. The exact historical referent does not narrow the general moral pattern. Words must be judged by what a person does when praise is absent and power is available. Fasād is disorder, corruption, and the undoing of what is sound. It includes violence, economic exploitation, environmental destruction, broken institutions, spreading lies, and policies that ruin the conditions of life. Crops and livestock are mentioned because they are the foundations of food security and livelihood. Destroying them harms families far beyond the immediate act. The Qur’an repeatedly condemns those who claim reform while creating corruption, and it links corruption to human actions on land and sea. A person cannot call a project successful if it produces profit for a few while poisoning water, destroying farms, or making communities unsafe. Crops and animals are gifts from Allah and trusts placed under human stewardship. The Qur’an permits people to benefit from them but prohibits waste and abuse. Farmers, traders, officials, and consumers all have responsibilities. (2:11 — **“When they are told, “Do not spread corruption in the land,” they reply, “We are only peace-makers!””**) Because reality revealed after the audience frames fasād in the land, fasād in the land returns attention to reality revealed after the audience. Ibn ʿAbbās links the corruption here with concrete harm to grain and cattle, so the warning concerns damaged livelihoods as well as a corrupt intention.

**FASĀD IN THE LAND**

***“the land to spread mischief in it and destroy”*** deepens fasād in the land. A farmer should avoid needless cruelty and destructive practice; a business should not contaminate food or manipulate markets; officials should protect land and water; consumers should avoid waste when others face hunger. The Prophet ﷺ taught mercy toward animals and praised planting and cultivation that benefits people and creatures. Environmental care is not an optional modern decoration on Islam. It follows from recognizing Allah as Creator and humanity as a responsible steward rather than an absolute owner. The verse follows a description of persuasive speech, so corruption is not limited to physical destruction. False reports can destroy reputations, dishonest financial claims can ruin businesses, and inflammatory rhetoric can turn neighbours against one another. A leader who spreads suspicion may destroy the social crop from which cooperation grows. A believer examines both visible and delayed consequences. Some corruption looks like development at first, while its harm appears years later in disease, displacement, debt, or family breakdown. Consultation with experts, honest data, and concern for the vulnerable are part of responsible decision-making. The closing statement is categorical: Allah does not love fasād. He may allow corrupt people time and temporary success as a test, but allowance is not approval. Wealth and power are not evidence that a project is blessed. The final measure includes justice, truth, and the rights of people and creation. This should make believers courageous in opposing corruption while remaining just. They do not accuse without evidence or destroy property in the name of reform. (30:41 — **“Corruption has spread on land and sea as a result of what people’s hands have done,”**) Since food and livelihood as trusts follows reality revealed after the audience, fasād in the land links purpose to food and livelihood as trusts.

**FOOD AND LIVELIHOOD AS TRUSTS**

***“crops and cattle. Allah does not like mischief”*** clarifies food and livelihood as trusts. They document wrongdoing, advise appropriately, support lawful accountability, and protect people from harm. Some people answer criticism by claiming that their actions are reform, as in 2:11–12. The Qur’an teaches that reform is known by its fruits: justice, safety, truth, mercy, and the preservation of rights. A slogan of reform cannot sanctify oppression. Nor can opposition to a corrupt person justify corruption by the critic. The Prophet ﷺ built reform through trustworthy leadership, consultation, charity, fair markets, reconciliation, and protection of the weak. The method matters as much as the claim. The verse can be applied to the smaller sphere of family and work. A person may speak gently before elders but spread conflict after leaving. He may promise to improve an institution while destroying its staff, resources, or reputation. He may win an argument yet leave relationships poisoned. Taqwā requires asking whether our actions produce iṣlāḥ or fasād. Repentance is possible. A person who has caused harm should stop, acknowledge it, restore what can be restored, and work for repair. The next verse warns about the arrogance that prevents this response. Think of reality revealed after the audience through a man who keeps the front room of his shop spotless and stores the rubbish in the yard the customers never see. The front room is admired for a year, the drain is blocked by the winter, and the smell arrives at the front door by spring. Reality revealed after the audience reaches a reader today who behaves with care in the gatherings of his community and lets his dealings at home run as they will. (2:204 — **“There are some ˹hypocrites˺ who impress you with their views regarding worldly affairs and openly call upon”**) Corruption includes speech and policy matters, which means food and livelihood as trusts returns corruption includes speech and policy to reality revealed after the audience.

**CORRUPTION INCLUDES SPEECH AND POLICY**

The verse gives him a second half of a life, the one he lives when the visitors have gone, and reality revealed after the audience asks what he is building in the street behind him. Because the judgment falls on the mischief rather than on the man's reputation, the reader is taught where the real record is kept. Because the striving is described as a work rather than a mood, reality revealed after the audience shows that the harm is deliberate. Since the labour of the man is spent on damage, reality revealed after the audience shows that the mischief is chosen rather than accidental. The man who walks out of the assembly has already decided what the streets will be like when the meetings are over. Because the two words name the food of a village rather than the persons in it, the reader is shown what mischief costs in the end. Since the crops and the cattle belong to the community and not to the man, reality revealed after the audience shows that the offence reaches past the two people in the quarrel. A quarrel in a courtyard is answered by a hungry winter, and the ones who pay are not the ones who argued. Fasād in the land makes reality revealed after the audience practical; food and livelihood as trusts therefore matters.
