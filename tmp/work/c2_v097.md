**THE BRINGER WHOSE REPUTATION WAS TRIED**

The verse arrives as the answer to a courtroom dodge, and the account of its trigger is preserved in the transmission from Ibn ʿAbbās. A rabbi among the community's notables — the man remembered in the record as Ibn Suriyyah — asked which angel carries the revelation. Told that it was Gabriel, he answered that Gabriel was their enemy, since he brings chastisement in his wake. Had the carrier been Michael they would have believed, he said, since Michael brings fertility and security. The second account of the trigger stands in the report Anas b. Malik transmitted and Bukhārī recorded. A rabbi of their own, named in the record as Ibn Salam, came to the Prophet with three questions that only a prophet could answer, and when the answers arrived he said aloud that Gabriel, among all the angels, is the enemy of the Jews. The verse then opens with the summons that answers the maneuver: ***“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Whoever is an enemy of Gabriel should know”***. The inherited readings of the opening keep the sting that the translation folds into the summons — a curse on the enemy of the bringer, dying of his own exasperation — so that the trial of the angel's reputation ends in a verdict on the trial's conductors. The syllogism beneath the sentence was stated by the earliest authority in its bluntest form: whoever is an enemy of Gabriel is an enemy of God. ʿIkrimah even reads the names of the great angels as compounds of worship and divinity, so that the bearers of revelation carry their office in their names. The method of the reply deserves its own observation. The verse does not defend the messenger's character. It makes the messenger's enemies try their own case, and the trial opens with the courier's work on record.

**THE COURIER'S WORK, ENTERED INTO EVIDENCE**

The middle of the verse files the delivery report in four clauses: ***“that he revealed this ˹Quran˺ to your heart by Allah’s Will, confirming what came before it”***. The permissive word in the Arabic is read by the glosses as command rather than mere allowance. The descent was commissioned, not tolerated. The destination is named without ornament: the heart of the prophet. The confirming clause then settles the courier's work in the scheme of the earlier books, and the glosses press the direction of authority once more. The new volume confirms the old shelves rather than standing trial before them. The doctrine of the descent through the holy spirit is stated in the final revelation's own account of the same delivery: (16:102 — **“Say, “The holy spirit has brought it down from your Lord with the truth to reassure the believers, and as a guide”**). The identification of the spirit with the bearer of the revelation is the tradition's constant, and the fruit of that delivery is the split the descent always works: (2:253 — **“succeeding generations would not have fought ˹among themselves˺ after receiving the clear proofs. But they differed—some believed while others disbelieved. Yet”**). What follows from the delivery-report is the refutation's exact shape. The enemy of Gabriel is not a critic of a personality. He is a complainant against the mail route of God.

**THE RECEIPT SIGNED BY THE ERRAND**

The purpose of the delivery is then named in the pair the revelation keeps for exactly this work: ***“a guide and good news”***, ***“for the believers.””***. The same pair marks the book elsewhere as the standing signature of its arrival: (27:2 — **“˹It is˺ a guide and good news for the believers”**). The two nouns mean guidance out of error and glad tidings of the Garden, and their placement at the verse's end is the receipt of the courier's errand. The transmission from Ibn ʿAbbās fixes the second term with its destination: the good news is the news that Paradise is theirs. The pairing matters more than its brevity suggests, because the rabbi's dodge had divided the angels into the angel of comfort and the angel of warning. The verse answers by joining the offices. The same descent that carries the warning carries the good news, and the enemy of the warning has declared war on the comfort as well. The effect on the trial is procedural. The courier's work is now on the record twice, once as a warning and once as a welcome, and no document of the complaint against him is left unaddressed.

**THE PSYCHOLOGY OF CHOOSING YOUR ANGEL**

The scene behind the verse is one of the few places in the passage where the psychology of the refusal is stated out loud by the refusers themselves. The statement is a masterpiece of self-portraiture. The preference voiced in the trial is the preference for the angel of fertility and security over the angel of chastisement. It is a preference for the news that costs nothing over the news that costs the self. The tradition's analysis of the dodge notes its commercial shape. The community was choosing its messenger by the merchandise he carried, and that is the same selective-belief method the passage has been prosecuting for a dozen verses. The structure of the reply is the structure of the whole section in miniature. The courier is not on trial; the shopping is. What turns on the psychology is the modern reader's mirror-image, since every age keeps its own preferred angel — the patron of prosperity, the speaker of comfort — and the verse asks which deliveries the community has been refusing by refusing the courier.

**THE COURT-ROOM RULE THE VERSE ESTABLISHES**

The legal afterlife of the verse is a rule about trying celestial reputations. A complaint against a divine messenger is never a private matter of taste. It is a claim about God's own appointments, and the verse makes the complaint indict its author by its own standard. The tradition generalizes the rule without restraint. The enemy of the bringer is the enemy of the sender, and the animus is measured by which news the complainant wants suppressed. The reason the verse answers with a delivery report rather than a defense of character is that reputations are argued while receipts are produced. Any assembly today that has learned to edit the mail to keep the comfort will find the same counter-examination open. The courier's work is on file, and the file is no longer negotiable.

**THE MESSAGE THAT ANSWERS FOR THE MESSENGER**

The closing lesson is the one the whole trial was staged to teach: the defense of revelation is revelation's own work product. The verse names the descent, the destination, the confirmation, the guidance, and the good news — and then stops, because the courier's case is closed by the courier's account. The reason the account is sufficient is that the trial's question was never about character. It was about authorization, and authorization is documented in delivery. The effect on the reader is to turn every dispute over a prophet into a dispute over the delivered text, and that is the only forum the passage will accept. In other words, the enemies of Gabriel were invited to read the mail, and the reading of the mail is the trial's verdict.
