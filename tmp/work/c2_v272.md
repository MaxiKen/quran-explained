**CHARITY IS NOT A TOOL OF CONVERSION**

***“You are not responsible for people’s guidance ˹O Prophet˺—it is Allah Who guides whoever”*** establishes charity is not a tool of conversion. The verse tells the Messenger ﷺ that he is not responsible for forcing people onto guidance. His duty is to convey the message; guidance of the heart belongs to Allah. The immediate context includes questions about giving charity to people whose faith or attitude may differ from the Muslims. Believers are encouraged to give for Allah’s sake rather than making material assistance conditional upon a person’s conversion. This does not mean that every charitable decision must ignore safety, accountability, or the recipient’s conduct. A charity may refuse to fund violence, addiction, fraud, or exploitation and may require proper documentation. The principle is that the deserving poor should not be denied help merely because the donor cannot control their faith. Allah guides whom He wills through wisdom, evidence, circumstances, and the person’s response. The Prophet ﷺ loved guidance for his relatives and grieved when people refused it, but he could not place faith in their hearts. A donor likewise cannot purchase belief by giving money or punish disbelief by withholding basic mercy. Charity can express the moral beauty of Islam, but it should not be a deceptive sales tactic. The believer gives because Allah commands compassion and because people have rights. He explains faith with wisdom when appropriate, without using hunger or vulnerability to force a declaration. Whatever good you spend is for your own benefit. The recipient receives practical help, but the spiritual reward returns to the donor through purification, forgiveness, and nearness to Allah. (28:56 — **“You surely cannot guide whoever you like ˹O Prophet˺,”**)

**GUIDANCE BELONGS TO ALLAH**

***“He wills. Whatever you ˹believers˺ spend in charity, it is for your own good—as”*** deepens guidance belongs to allah. Allah is not enriched by charity. The giver is the one who needs the good deed. This perspective protects against resentment. A person who gives for Allah does not constantly calculate whether the recipient thanked him or whether the social return was sufficient. He evaluates the benefit responsibly but leaves the reward with Allah. If the gift is misused through no fault of his own, Allah knows the intention and effort. Seeking the Face of Allah—ibtighāʾ wajh Allāh—is a Qur’anic expression for seeking His pleasure and acceptance. It does not mean imagining Allah in a created direction. The believer purifies his intention from reputation, political influence, control over recipients, or the desire to appear compassionate. Mixed motives can enter. The servant renews his intention and continues beneficial charity rather than allowing a passing desire for praise to cancel all good. He also chooses privacy when it protects sincerity and public transparency when it protects people’s rights. Allah promises that whatever good is spent will be repaid in full. The repayment may be forgiveness, blessing, protection, a good name that benefits others, or reward in the Hereafter. It is not necessarily a direct financial return. A person may give money and receive contentment, a protected family, or a reward that remains after death. Without being wronged assures that no sacrifice is undervalued. The poor person who gives a small amount, the caregiver who gives time, and the wealthy person who gives much are all judged by intention, ability, and obedience. **SPENDING BENEFITS THE GIVER**

***“long as you do so seeking the pleasure of Allah. Whatever you donate”*** clarifies spending benefits the giver. Allah does not deprive a person of the reward he deserves. The verse supports charitable kindness beyond the Muslim community. Qur’an 60:8 commands justice and kindness toward people who take no part in fighting Muslims over religion. A Muslim may feed a hungry neighbour, support disaster relief, fund healthcare, or protect a refugee without treating the help as proof that every belief is the same. Faith remains clear while mercy remains broad. The donor does not assist oppression, but he does not allow sectarian hostility to make him indifferent to human need. The Prophet ﷺ was responsible for conveying guidance, not controlling hearts. This distinction protects religious teachers from burnout and manipulation. They teach clearly, model goodness, and pray for people, but they do not measure the value of the message solely by immediate conversions. Likewise, parents and community leaders advise without claiming ownership of another person’s faith. Allah knows the path by which a person may eventually return. The tree stands for fifty years, the shade falls on the travellers of three generations, and the planter's name is on nothing but the soil.
