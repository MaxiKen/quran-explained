**FAITH REQUIRES THE END OF RIBĀ**

***“O believers! Fear Allah, and give up”*** establishes faith requires the end of ribā. After explaining the moral difference between trade and ribā and contrasting the growth of charity with the destruction of interest, Allah addresses believers directly. They must fear Allah and abandon whatever remains of ribā. The command concerns outstanding increases attached to debts, not the lawful principal that was originally lent. The phrase whatever remains—mā baqiya min al-ribā—requires stopping the continuing claim rather than collecting every last increment promised by a contract. A creditor may recover the principal legitimately owed, but he may not continue demanding an unlawful increase because it was written into an agreement. The verse links abandonment of ribā to taqwā and genuine faith. Taqwā is not only a feeling of fear; it is protecting oneself from Allah’s displeasure through action. A person who knows that a financial practice is prohibited but continues because it is profitable places money above obedience. The wording if you are believers is a severe moral challenge, not a permission for ordinary people to declare others outside Islam. It shows that financial ethics are part of living faith. The believer’s testimony must appear in contracts, lending, employment, and debt collection. Islam does not forbid a lender from recovering the principal. The Qur’an’s next verse says, For you is your principal; you do not wrong, nor are you wronged. This balance protects both parties. A creditor should not lose the money genuinely lent, and a debtor should not be charged an increase merely because time passed. (2:280 — **“If it is difficult for someone to repay a debt, postpone it until a time of ease.”**)

**PRINCIPAL VERSUS INCREASE**

Abandoning ribā requires institutions, not only personal slogans. Communities can create qarḍ ḥasan funds, mutual aid, ethical investment, cooperative housing, debt counselling, and transparent charity. Employers can provide fair wages and emergency assistance, reducing workers’ dependence upon predatory lenders. Scholars and finance professionals should collaborate. A legal form that avoids the word interest but reproduces the same unjust burden does not fulfil the Qur’anic purpose. Substance, risk, ownership, and real benefit matter. Because the verse ties the giving up to the sincerity of a faith, showing through faith requires the end of ribā that the size of the sum is a matter for the ledger and the obedience is a matter for the heart.
