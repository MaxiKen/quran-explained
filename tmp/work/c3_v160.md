**HELP THAT MAKES DEFEAT IMPOSSIBLE**

***“If Allah helps you, none can defeat you. But if”*** If Allah helps you, none can defeat you. But if He denies you help, then who else can help you. States the help-charter beneath the leadership-manual just issued. And the charter's two conditionals are the war's whole arithmetic finalised. In yansurkumu llahu fala ghaliba lakum. If Allah helps you, there is no overcomer of you. The first conditional Badr's documented file. Wa in yadhulkum. But if He forsakes you. The khidhlan-word. The help withheld, the ranks left to their own weights. Uhud's documented hour. Faman dha lladhi yansurukum min ba'dih. Then who is it that can help you after Him. The question's sweep emptying the field of every alternative patron. The two conditionals together being the two days' one lesson. The community lived both clauses within a year. Unbeatable at Badr, unhelped at the hill's minute. And the charter files both at one Desk. The help's presence and its withholding equally His administration, the variable never the Helper's capacity but the helped's conditions. (3:159 — **“that you ˹O Prophet˺ have been lenient with them. Had you been”**)

**WITHDRAWN SUPPORT LEAVES NO HELPER**

***“He denies you help, then who else can help”*** So in Allah let the believers put their trust seals the charter with the reliance-command. Wa ala llahi falyatawakkali l-mu'minun. And upon Allah let the believers rely. And the seal's placement completes the passage's tawakkul-architecture. The command's third sounding in the Uhud-curriculum, here issued directly upon the two conditionals. The reliance commanded, that is, with both clauses in view. Trust placed in the Helper as known through both days. The One whose help makes the few unbeatable and whose withholding no coalition supplies. The tawakkul thus schooled past its fair-weather counterfeit. The reliance that survives only the first conditional (trusting while helped) is confidence in weather. The believers' tawakkul holds through the second. (3:161 — **“And whoever does so,”**) Withdrawn support leaves no helper has present-day force because planning remains necessary, but no plan becomes invincible without divine help and no defeat is final with it.

**TRUST FIXED UPON ALLAH**

***“you? So in Allah let the believers put their trust”*** For strategy, the charter compresses the community's creed into two clauses and one command. Every era's threat-assessment conducted under the first conditional (the coalitions' sizes irrelevant where the Help runs. The ghalib-clause absolute), every era's setback audited under the second, and every era's planning roofed by the seal. While the passage now turns to the trust's own guardian-clause in the community's inner life. The spoils' integrity and the prophet's incorruptibility. It is not appropriate for a prophet to illegally withhold spoils of war. Imagine a fortress secure while its true defender remains, but exposed when that protection is withdrawn. Regarding help that makes defeat impossible, Ibn ʿAbbās contrasts Badr’s divine help with Uhud’s withdrawn help and directs believers to trust Allah for triumph. The two conditions leave no independent power between help and its withdrawal; which means if Allah grants help, the combined force of opponents cannot make defeat inevitable. If He withholds it, no ally can replace Him; therefore this does not make preparation useless; it places preparation in its proper rank. Tools, skill, and numbers are means whose effect depends on Allah; in other words trust therefore follows a clear assessment of both possibilities. It is not confidence in personal strength, nor is it passivity before danger; hence a believer prepares fully and refuses to worship the preparation. Modern planning often speaks as though enough data can remove dependence, while panic speaks as though one missing resource makes success impossible; this is why the verse corrects both voices. Help is sought from Allah, lawful means are used, and the heart remains steady when those means appear either abundant or thin. The question about another helper expects no name because every candidate is already a creature under Allah’s rule. An army may list allies, yet none can activate help after the source of all effect withholds it. This strips pride from success and fatalism from weakness. The heart can act vigorously without pretending to control the final result. Reliance is therefore strongest when preparation is complete and the prepared means are still treated as servants.
