**THE ATTRACTION OF WORLDLY LIFE**

***“The enjoyment of ˹worldly˺ desires—women, children, treasures of gold and silver, fine”*** establishes the attraction of worldly life. This verse acknowledges a reality of human nature: worldly desires have been made attractive. People are drawn to companionship, family, wealth, status, transport, animals, property, and productive land. The Qur’an does not pretend that these attractions do not exist, nor does it condemn every form of enjoyment. It teaches the believer to understand their place. Worldly attraction can appear adorned and compelling. Attraction itself is not necessarily sinful. The moral question is how a person responds to it: whether he seeks what is lawful, fulfils the rights attached to it, remains grateful, and refuses to let temporary pleasure replace Allah. The mention of women and children concerns powerful human desires for companionship, marriage, affection, and family continuity. Islam does not treat marriage or parenthood as obstacles to spirituality. The Prophet ﷺ joined love of family to worship and taught kind treatment of women, as Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī records. The danger comes when people are reduced to objects of possession or status. A spouse is a trust and partner, not a commodity. Children are a responsibility, not proof of superiority. Desire must be governed by chastity, consent, justice, and lawful commitment. Gold and silver represent accumulated wealth and the desire for financial security. Wealth can provide housing, education, health care, family support, and charitable service. It can also produce greed, hoarding, bribery, exploitation, and contempt for the poor. (18:46 — **“Wealth and children are the adornment of this worldly life,”**)

**RELATIONSHIPS AND FAMILY**

***“horses, cattle, and fertile land—has been made appealing to people. These are the”*** deepens relationships and family. The Qur’an therefore does not simply ask whether a person owns wealth; it asks how he acquired it and what he did with it. Livestock and fertile land represent productive assets and agricultural prosperity. In contemporary life, the same principle applies to businesses, technology, investments, vehicles, and professional influence. All are temporary trusts. Allah may bless them when they serve justice and worship, and they may become a trial when they command the heart. The verse calls these things the enjoyment of worldly life, then directs attention to what is better with Allah. It does not say that every worldly blessing is evil. It distinguishes between a lawful provision and the finest destination. The problem is not enjoying a gift; it is treating the gift as the final home. Sūrah al-Kahf describes wealth and children as adornment, while enduring righteous deeds are better with Allah. Sūrah al-Ḥadīd compares worldly life to vegetation that grows, impresses, dries, and becomes debris. The comparison teaches proportion: use the world, but do not build your identity upon what must pass. The finest destination is with Allah: His pleasure, forgiveness, and Paradise. The destination is better because it is lasting, secure, and free from the fear of loss that accompanies worldly possessions. A person who sacrifices unlawful gain for Allah has not lost; he has exchanged a temporary benefit for an enduring reward. (57:20 — **“Know that this worldly life is no more than play, amusement, luxury, mutual boasting,”**)

**WEALTH AND PROPERTY**

***“pleasures of this worldly life, but with Allah is the finest destination”*** clarifies wealth and property. The Prophet ﷺ said that the world is a prison for the believer and a paradise for the disbeliever, meaning that the believer’s lawful life remains limited compared with the freedom and delight of the Hereafter, while the worldly life of the disbeliever is all the comfort he expects. The hadith, recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, does not prohibit lawful enjoyment; it places it beside the incomparable Hereafter. Islamic spirituality is not contempt for creation. The Prophet ﷺ taught that the body, eyes, spouse, and guest have rights. The believer eats, works, marries, earns, and builds, but keeps Allah above every attachment. If wealth comes, he gives thanks; if it leaves, he remains patient. If family brings joy, he gives praise; if family brings difficulty, he fulfils responsibility without worshipping approval. Worldly desire is like a decorated station mistaken for the destination; its comforts can serve the journey but cannot become the traveller’s final home. Today, advertising turns desire into identity; the verse restores marriage, family, wealth, and beauty to their place as tests and trusts. Because the attraction of worldly life opens into relationships and family, wealth and property joins the attraction of worldly life to relationships and family. Since wealth and property develops the attraction of worldly life, relationships and family carries the transition. The good and the better matters, which means wealth and property answers relationships and family. (28:77 — **“Rather, seek the ˹reward˺ of the Hereafter by means of what Allah has granted you,”**)

**THE GOOD AND THE BETTER**

The verse calls these attractions worldly enjoyment and then points to the finest destination with Allah. This comparison does not require abandoning spouse, children, property, or productive work. It requires refusing to make a temporary good the supreme good. Love becomes worship when it honours another person’s rights; wealth becomes provision when it is lawfully earned and generously used; land and transport become trusts when they serve rather than dominate. The ordering is decisive: creation is enjoyed beneath obedience to the Creator. A traveller may value the lamp in a station without mistaking its light for home. In the same way, gratitude receives present blessings while hope remains fixed on Allah’s lasting pleasure. Today, advertising repeatedly turns possessions into measures of identity and human worth. This verse interrupts that training. It asks not merely what a person desires, but whether desire has been governed by lawful limits, gratitude, service, and a destination greater than loss-prone goods. (28:77 — **“Rather, seek the ˹reward˺ of the Hereafter by means of what Allah has granted you,”**)
