**THE SECOND PICTURE AND THE STORM IT SETS THEM IN**

The sūrah gives a second picture for the same people, and this one puts them in the middle of a night storm: ***“Or ˹those caught in˺ a rainstorm from the sky with darkness, thunder”***, ***“and lightning”***. The early teachers read the storm as the revelation itself, since the rain that gives life is a fitting image for the Book that came down, and they read the darkness, the thunder and the flash as the doubts, the warnings and the moments of clarity that accompany it. Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatādah are credited with readings of this kind, in which the rain is the Qur'an, the darkness is their confusion, and the thunderclaps are the commands and prohibitions that strike the ear. The picture is placed after the first one for a reason, since the man with the borrowed lantern has lost his light, while the people of this picture are still travelling through a storm that gives them light in flashes. Both are descriptions of the same disease at different stages, and the sūrah wants the reader to hold both.

**THE GESTURE OF THE FRIGHTENED TRAVELLERS**

The middle of the verse describes what the people in the storm do: ***“They press their fingers into their ears at the sound of every thunder-clap”***, ***“for fear of death.”***. The gesture is exact and it is not mocked anywhere in the sentence, which is worth noticing in a passage about pretenders. Ibn ʿAbbās described the men as stopping their ears against the sound that frightened them, and the Book records the same reaction elsewhere in the language of the people who refused what they heard. The reason the picture works is that it shows belief and fear existing together in the same person, which is the condition of the pretender exactly: he is close enough to the message to be shaken by it and far enough from it to hope it passes. The Book describes the thunder itself as a creature that praises its Lord, which means the storm that frightens them is doing the work of its Maker while they crouch in it: (13:13 — **“The thunder glorifies His praises, as do the angels in awe of Him”**).

**THE SENTENCE THAT CLOSES THE PICTURE**

The verse ends with a statement that stops the reader from thinking the men are out of reach: ***“And Allah encompasses the disbelievers ˹by His might˺”***. The wording describes a power that surrounds the people it deals with, and the sentence is placed after the account of their running and hiding. The Book describes the same surrounding elsewhere when it speaks about the flash of the cloud and the power behind it: (24:43 — **“The flash of the clouds' lightning nearly takes away eyesight”**). The early teachers read the closing as a warning to the men themselves rather than as a comfort to the community, since the hiding in the verse is useless against the One who sent the storm. What turns on the sentence is that fear is not the same as faith, and a person can be frightened by the truth for years without ever submitting to it. The next verse stays with the same travellers and shows what they do when the light appears, which is the most honest part of the whole description.

**A FIELD WITH A LAMP THAT FLASHES**

Think of a group crossing a field at night while a storm throws a flash of light every few minutes. Each flash shows the path ahead, and each one reveals how far off the track they had drifted when it was dark. The people of this picture walk in the flashes and stand still in the intervals, which is a portrait of a person who acts on the truth when it moves him and stops when the feeling passes. These days a reader may find the same pattern in his own week, since a reminder can produce a burst of effort and the following silence can produce nothing at all. The verse does not remove the storm and it does not promise that the storm will end soon; it shows the men in it, and the reader who recognises the description has been told where he is standing.
