**NO WEAKNESS AFTER A WOUND**

***“Do not falter or grieve, for”*** Do not falter or grieve issues the bandaged community its double prohibition. And the two verbs' selection covers collapse's whole anatomy. Wa la tahinu. Do not weaken. The wahn-word. The inner slackening. Resolve's fibre loosening after the blow. Wa la tahzanu. Nor grieve. The sorrow over the day's losses. The seventy martyrs, the Prophet's wound, the reversal's shame. Forbidden not as feeling but as governor. Grief not to steer the community. The double prohibition thus addressing the wound's two exits into ruin. Forward-collapse (the weakening that quits the field) and backward-collapse (the grieving that lives at the graves). Both barred in five words. (3:138 — **“guide and a lesson to the God-fearing”**) The order therefore protects both resolve and honest mourning.

**GRIEF HELD WITHIN FAITH**

***“you will have the upper”*** For you will have the upper hand grounds the prohibition in the standing fact. Wa antumu l-a'lawna. And you are the higher. The elevation-clause. The community's position stated not as consolation's wish but as present-tense reality. Higher in creed, higher in cause, higher in trajectory. Despite the day's score. For Uhud's field, read whole, had already displayed the clause, and the decade would cash it entire (Makkah's gates, the peninsula's folding). The elevation real at the very hour it was least visible. The verse teaching the community its permanent altitude-reading. Position measured by the cause's standing, never the season's score. (3:140 — **“so that Allah may reveal the ˹true˺ believers,”**) Grief held within faith remains urgent today because setbacks should be grieved honestly without allowing grief to erase conviction or paralyze future duty.

**THE UPPER HAND CONDITIONED BY BELIEF**

***“hand, if you are ˹true˺ believers”*** If you are ˹true˺ believers closes the command with the conditional that is the verse's engine. In kuntum mu'minin. If you are believers. The elevation indexed to the iman. The clause working both directions at once. As assurance (the belief present, the upper hand certain. The promise unconditional upon anything outside the community's own faith. No enemy's strength, no season's wound named among the variables) and as summons. The verse leaves every wounded generation with the same instrument for reading grief, weakness, and the condition of faith. The double prohibition and the conditional carried as one instrument. At each setback. Communal or private. The two exits barred (no weakening forward, no grieving backward), the altitude re-read (the cause's standing unmoved by the score), and the conditional turned to audit. The sagging spirit sent not to despair but to the faith's own maintenance. While the next verse explains the score itself. If you have suffered injuries, they suffered similarly. We alternate these days among people. Imagine an injured athlete who treats the wound without surrendering the season. Concerning no weakness after a wound, Ibn ʿAbbās addresses the wounds and missed gain of Uhud while promising the believers ultimate ascendancy if their belief remains sound. The prohibition addresses both action and emotion; which means faltering weakens the will to continue, while grief can trap the heart inside what has already been lost. The verse forbids neither ordinary sadness nor honest recognition of injury; therefore it forbids surrender to a sorrow that cancels duty and to weakness that treats one defeat as the end of truth. Superiority is tied to belief, not to uninterrupted worldly advantage; in other words this condition keeps the promise from becoming empty nationalism or group pride. A community is uppermost when it remains faithful to Allah, even while tending wounds; hence today, public failure can be replayed until shame becomes an identity. The verse allows grief to be felt but not enthroned; this is why faith gives the next act its direction, because the final measure is not the loudest report about the previous day. Believers rise by recovering obedience, repairing causes, and returning to the work still before them. The condition keeps identity and conduct together. A name inherited from faithful people cannot carry superiority after belief has been emptied of obedience. Conversely, a wounded believer does not become low merely because an opponent currently appears strong. The measure restores dignity while demanding reform, which is more useful than either despair or empty reassurance. Hope then supports repair instead of hiding the causes that still require attention.
