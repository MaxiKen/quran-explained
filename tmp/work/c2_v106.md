**THE VERSE THAT COMES LATER**

The passage now answers the mockery that the changes in the law had produced: ***“If We ever abrogate a verse or cause it to be forgotten”***, ***“We replace it with a better or similar one”***. The received record names the mockery precisely. The disbelievers began to deride the matter outright, saying that one day the messenger enjoins his companions to one thing and the next day he forbids it. The taunt had a sharper edge in this city, because the most visible change in living memory was the direction of prayer. The turn from Jerusalem to the Kaʿbah was fresh, and the taunt attached itself to the turn. The foolish of the people made the question public: (2:142 — **“The foolish among the people will ask, “Why did they turn away from the direction of prayer they used to face?” Say,”**). The verse at hand answers the taunt with a rule of the sending itself rather than with an argument about the turn.

**THE RULE OF THE SENDING**

The rule is stated in the voice of the sender: the verse that is withdrawn or postponed is replaced with what is better or like it. Ibn ʿAbbās opens the unit by noting exactly what the passage is doing. It mentions what was abrogated of the book and what was not abrogated. The same gloss traces the address back to the original challenge, the question of the Quraysh: why do you command us to do something and then forbid it? The answer in the tradition is the answer of administration. A verse may be acted upon for a time and then not acted upon, and the replacement arrives from the same sender with more profit in it. The coherence of the book is therefore not the coherence of a frozen page. It is the coherence of a curriculum whose teacher knows the sequence. A contradiction and a change of schedule are not the same document. A contradiction speaks twice at once. A change of schedule speaks twice in order.

**THE TWO READINGS OF THE WITHDRAWAL**

The tradition of the glosses preserves two technical readings of the same verb, and both are worth naming. In one reading the verse is abrogated together with its recital or with its judgment alone, the recitation continuing while the ruling lifts. In the other, the verb is taken as postponement rather than removal, the ruling deferred to its hour. The point of the pair is that the withdrawal is itself an administered act. Nothing is lost in the process of sending. The verse closed with the capacity that administers all of it: (2:286 — **“Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford. All good will be for its own benefit,”**). The replacement of verses and the weighting of souls therefore answer to one policy. The schedule is set by the one who knows the weight of every hour. The closing challenge of the verse puts the whole taunt back in its place: ***“Do you not know that Allah is Most Capable of everything”***.

**THE FAILURE OF THE TAUNT**

The taunt failed for a reason the passage is careful to leave standing. The charge was that the changes came from the messenger himself. The answer is the identity of the one who turns the pages. The verses are sent, withdrawn, and replaced by the owner of the sending. The received account of the qiblah change records the taunt's purpose as well as its words. The aim was to sow the seed of doubt, that the changes were made by the man and not by his lord. The verse answers the purpose, not only the words. For any reader who has watched a curriculum revised at mid-year and heard the charge that the teacher cannot keep his story straight, the case at hand is the same case. The sequence is the method. The maker of the sequence is the one who knows what the next lesson is for. The book's coherence is therefore not the stillness of one page. It is the wisdom of the order in which the pages arrive. The reader who holds that point holds the answer to the mockery at its root, and the answer needs no inventory of the changes at all.
