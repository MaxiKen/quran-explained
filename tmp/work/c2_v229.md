**LIMITING REPEATED DIVORCE**

***“Divorce may be retracted twice, then the husband must retain ˹his wife˺ with honour or separate ˹from her˺ with grace. It is not lawful for husbands”*** establishes limiting repeated divorce. The verse reforms a system in which a husband could repeatedly divorce and take back his wife, leaving her permanently suspended. Divorce is twice means that the first and second pronouncements can be followed by lawful reconciliation during the waiting period, while a third divorce produces the final separation described in the next verse. The rule gives the marriage a chance to recover but prevents endless manipulation. Divorce is therefore a process with consequences, not a phrase used in every argument. A person should seek counsel, calm, and mediation before pronouncing it. The Prophet ﷺ was angered when a divorce was issued contrary to the proper procedure, and he taught the community to observe the waiting period and not harm the wife. After a revocable divorce, the husband has two ethical paths: imsāk bi-maʿrūf, retaining her honourably, or tasrīḥ bi-iḥsān, releasing her with good treatment. Retaining means genuine reconciliation, restored rights, and a willingness to live without abuse. Releasing means ending the marriage without insults, withheld property, public exposure, or manipulation of children. The alternatives exclude a third path—retaining a woman only to harm, delay her remarriage, or punish her. The Qur’an repeats this prohibition in 2:231. A legal status that remains technically intact while the spouse is tortured emotionally or financially is a violation of the verse. It is not lawful for you to take back anything of what you have given them. (2:231 — **“When you divorce women and they have ˹almost˺ reached the end of their waiting period,”**) Because limiting repeated divorce frames retain honourably or release well, retain honourably or release well returns attention to limiting repeated divorce.

**RETAIN HONOURABLY OR RELEASE WELL**

***“to take back anything of the dowry given to their wives, unless the couple fears not being able to keep within the limits of Allah. So”*** deepens retain honourably or release well. The husband may not reclaim the mahr, gifts, property, or financial rights as a price for allowing the wife to leave. Divorce is not a business transaction in which the stronger party recovers every benefit and leaves the weaker party destitute. The exception concerns a situation in which both spouses fear that they cannot keep Allah’s limits and the wife offers compensation for release. This is the basis of khulʿ, a separation sought by the wife with compensation under the conditions of Islamic law. It must be voluntary and fair, not demanded by a husband who wants to profit from forcing his wife out. The Sunnah records the case of the wife of Thābit ibn Qays, who did not accuse him of bad character or religion but feared she could not continue the marriage. The Prophet ﷺ instructed that she return the garden he had given her and directed Thābit to separate from her, as al-Bukhārī records in his Ṣaḥīḥ. The report illustrates that a woman need not prove that her husband is a monster in order to seek release when the marriage has become unworkable. The details of khulʿ—whether the husband’s consent is required, the amount of compensation, and the role of a judge—are discussed by the legal schools. The moral safeguards remain: no coercion, no exploitation, and no use of the process to trap or impoverish either party. **THE DOWRY IS NOT A WEAPON**

***“if you fear they will not be able to keep within the limits of Allah, there is no blame if the wife compensates the husband to”*** clarifies the dowry is not a weapon. Allah’s limits—ḥudūd Allāh—include the number of divorces, the waiting period, maintenance, the prohibition of harm, the return of property, and the rights of children. A couple may feel emotionally overwhelmed, but their anger does not suspend these boundaries. They must document declarations, seek qualified advice, and act with fairness. When a relationship is unsafe, preserving the limits may require separation rather than forced reconciliation. When conflict is temporary, the limits may create space for repair. The purpose is not to preserve every marriage at any cost; it is to preserve dignity, justice, and obedience in whichever lawful outcome occurs. Transgression includes pronouncing divorce repeatedly to frighten a wife, preventing her from leaving, demanding compensation beyond what is fair, concealing assets, spreading private information, or using children to maintain control. It also includes making false claims against the other spouse or exploiting the legal process to obtain what one knows is not rightfully his or hers. The final description—those are the wrongdoers—reminds both genders that rights come with accountability. The verse is addressed primarily to husbands in its immediate legal context, but every party must respect Allah’s limits. The community should not pressure a couple to remain together merely to preserve appearances, nor should it celebrate divorce as revenge. Counsellors, families, and judges should protect the vulnerable, listen to both sides, and distinguish ordinary conflict from abuse. **KHULʿ AND MARITAL BREAKDOWN**

***“obtain divorce. These are the limits set by Allah, so do not transgress them. And whoever transgresses the limits of Allah, they are the ˹true˺ wrongdoers”*** closes with khulʿ and marital breakdown. A fair process is part of mercy. The believer remembers that a marriage is a trust before it is a possession. If it ends, the former spouses can still act with iḥsān and preserve a relationship of co-parenting and basic respect. Think of limiting repeated divorce through a man who keeps a shop open for years with a partner he no longer greets, because dissolving the business would mean splitting the shelves. The shelves are counted once, the stock is divided once, and the years of standing behind the same counter are counted by neither of them. Limiting repeated divorce confronts retain honourably or release well today in anyone who keeps a wife in the house because leaving would cost him money, and who calls the arrangement patience. The verse gives him a price for the door and names the two ways of honour, and limiting repeated divorce asks which of them he is living in. Because the closing sentence calls the crosser of the limits a wrongdoer, limiting repeated divorce teaches that a marriage held by force is outside the law he claims to follow. The rights of the woman in a separation are guarded in a later passage. And the two roads of the verse are repeated in the next stretch of this chapter. (2:221 — **“Do not marry polytheistic women until they believe; for a believing slave-woman is better than”**) Retain honourably or release well makes limiting repeated divorce practical; the dowry is not a weapon therefore matters.
