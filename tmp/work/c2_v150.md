**AN ARGUMENT REMOVED**

The command repeats itself and then widens its circle. ***“Wherever you are ˹O Prophet˺, turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque.”*** ***“And wherever you ˹believers˺ are, face towards it”*** ***“so that people will have no argument against you, except the wrongdoers among them. Do not fear them”***. The Prophet turns. The rows turn. The result is stated as a courtroom outcome. No argument remains. The reading of Ibn ʿAbbās names the people of this clause in the city around him. ʿAbdullāh b. Salām and the believers who stood with him were the standing answer to the claim of error. The recognition of the traits had already closed the scholarly dispute. What follows is that the qiblah itself becomes evidence. A community praying on one line across a city is a document. The point of the exceptive clause is surgical. The wrongdoers keep their argument because they were never missing an argument. They were missing a heart that accepts one. The effect of the design is that the Prophet is freed to pray rather than to litigate.

**FEAR IN THE RIGHT PLACE**

The verse then manages the fear that the argument carries. ***“fear Me, so that I may ˹continue to˺ perfect My favour upon you”***. The redirect is absolute. Do not fear them. Fear Me. The two fears cannot share one heart. The smaller fear starves when the larger one is fed. The same redirect had been issued against the prompting of the enemy in the field (3:175 — **“That ˹warning˺ was only ˹from˺ Satan, trying to prompt you to fear his followers. So do not fear them;”**). The prompting that magnifies the crowd is named at its source. The command follows the naming (3:175 — **“fear Me if you are ˹true˺ believers”**). Fear Me. The clause is the hinge of every public stand. The crowd is loud. The owner is near. The early commentators read the pairing here as a promise attached to a posture. The favour is perfected for the ones who redirect the fear. The perfection of the favour is the thing the wrongdoers' noise is trying to interrupt. The point is that fear is never removed from the human heart. It is re-addressed.

**A FAVOUR STILL BEING PERFECTED**

The closing clause carries the design into the future. ***“and so you may be ˹rightly˺ guided”***. The favour is still in progress. The guidance is its delivery method. The turn of the qiblah was one instalment. The sending of the messenger is the next. The facing of the whole community is the visible proof that the instalments are real. For the modern reader the pair of fears is the practical core of the passage. Public pressure invents fears that the heart then feeds. The verse starves the wrong fear and feeds the right one. This age calls the crowd's mood the verdict. The verse calls it noise around a verdict already entered. The favour stands at the stage of perfecting. The guidance is the road of the delivery. The one who ordered the face to turn can be trusted with the rest of the schedule.

**THE ARGUMENT THE QIBLAH REMOVES**

The courtroom clause is worth a slow read, because of what it actually deletes. The people of the city had kept an argument against the Prophet as their last reserve of leverage. The unity of the facing drains the reserve. A community praying on one line across a city is not a sect with an eccentricity. It is a document signed by the sky. The effect of the document is that the remaining complaint is exposed as the wrongdoer's private property. The exceptive clause names the owners precisely. Those who keep the argument after the argument has been removed are the ones whose posture pre-dates the evidence. The reason the fear-redirect sits inside the same verse is that the wrongdoers' only remaining power is the power to frighten. The redirect starves that power at its root. For the modern reader the passage is a manual for public stands. The evidence is assembled. The conduct is unified. The fear is re-addressed to its owner. This age calls the crowd's volume a verdict. The verse enters a different verdict and posts it above the noise.
