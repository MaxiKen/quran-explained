**COMPLETING THE PILGRIMAGE FOR ALLAH**

The sequence reaches completing the pilgrimage for allah through ***“Complete the pilgrimage and minor pilgrimage”***, ***“for Allah. But if prevented ˹from proceeding˺”***, ***“then ˹offer˺ whatever sacrificial animals you can afford.”***, ***“And do not shave your heads”***. The command complete Hajj and minor pilgrimage for Allah establishes sincerity and fulfilment. Hajj is not a tourism exercise, a social title, or a display of wealth. It is an act of worship performed for Allah alone, following the rites taught by the Messenger ﷺ. Completion includes entering pilgrim consecration, observing the prohibitions, performing the required rites, and completing the pilgrimage without deliberately abandoning its obligations. The wording also teaches that worship is not offered to impress people. The pilgrim should renew his intention when fatigue, crowding, expense, or praise arise. Allah sees whether he sought His pleasure or a reputation as someone who has performed Hajj. The verse addresses a person who is prevented—prevented—from completing the rites by an enemy, siege, serious obstruction, or another circumstance recognized by the law. He offers a *hady*, a sacrificial animal, at the place and in the manner explained by the Sunnah and qualified scholars, then exits pilgrim consecration according to the applicable ruling. The event of Hudaybiyah is the clearest prophetic example: the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions were prevented from entering Makkah and sacrificed before returning. The sacrifice is not a payment to purchase Allah’s favour. It is an act of obedience and a dignified conclusion to a pilgrimage interrupted by circumstances outside the pilgrim’s control. The details vary according to whether a person is genuinely prevented, placed under a binding condition, or merely inconvenienced. (22:26 — **“for those who circle ˹the Ka’bah˺, stand ˹in prayer˺”**) Because completing the pilgrimage for allah governs 2:196, waiting for the offering arises from completing the pilgrimage for allah. It follows that combined pilgrimage and its offering completes completing the pilgrimage for allah through waiting for the offering.

**WAITING FOR THE OFFERING**

Waiting for the offering takes shape in ***“until the sacrificial animal reaches its destination.”***, ***“But if any of you is ill”***, ***“or has a scalp ailment ˹requiring shaving˺”***, ***“then compensate either by fasting, charity”***. Shaving or shortening the hair—*ḥalq* or *taqṣīr*—marks release from pilgrim consecration after the appropriate stage. The pilgrim who is prevented waits until the sacrifice reaches its lawful place before shaving, unless the relevant legal condition changes. The physical act symbolizes the completion of the interrupted rite and obedience to Allah’s sequence. The Prophet ﷺ shaved his head at Hudaybiyah after the Companions sacrificed. His practice teaches that a command may be fulfilled even when the hoped-for destination is not reached. Allah rewards the sincere effort and does not require what circumstances have made impossible. A pilgrim who has an illness or painful condition of the head and must shave is given a ransom—*fidyah*: fasting, charity, or sacrifice. The Sunnah explains this through the case of Kaʿb ibn ʿUjrah, who had lice and was permitted to shave, then was instructed to fast three days, feed six needy people, or sacrifice a sheep, as recorded in the Ṣaḥīḥ record of Muslim and the matching record from al-Bukhārī. The concession shows that pilgrim consecration never requires the endurance of serious harm. The normal restriction remains, but necessity opens a lawful alternative. The options are not an invitation to choose whichever is most convenient without considering the legal conditions; the pilgrim asks qualified guidance and fulfils the appropriate expiation. (5:95 — **“as judged by two just men among you”**) The reason combined pilgrimage and its offering follows waiting for the offering lies in completing the pilgrimage for allah. Therefore, combined pilgrimage and its offering returns practical force to waiting for the offering.

**COMBINED PILGRIMAGE AND ITS OFFERING**

The wording for tamattuʿ and the hady is ***“or a sacrificial offering. In times of peace, you may combine the pilgrimage”***, ***“and minor pilgrimage then make the sacrificial offering you can afford. Whoever cannot afford”***, ***“that ˹offering˺, let them fast three days during pilgrimage”***, ***“and seven after returning ˹home˺”***. When you are secure, whoever performs minor pilgrimage followed by Hajj describes *tamattuʿ*: entering pilgrim consecration for minor pilgrimage during the months of Hajj, completing minor pilgrimage, exiting pilgrim consecration, and then entering pilgrim consecration again for Hajj in the same season. The pilgrim who chooses this form offers a *hady* if he is not a resident of the Sacred Mosque. The sacrifice expresses gratitude and helps feed the poor of the sanctuary. Other forms include *ifrād*, Hajj alone, and *qirān*, combining Hajj and minor pilgrimage in one pilgrim consecration. The Prophet ﷺ taught the rites and allowed the Companions to choose among forms according to their circumstances. The pilgrim should follow an established legal guide rather than arguing over which valid form is most virtuous in every situation. Whoever cannot find the required sacrifice fasts three days during Hajj and seven after returning—ten complete. The three days may be observed within the pilgrimage period according to the juristic details, and the seven are completed after returning home. The number *ten* is stated explicitly to prevent the mistaken idea that the first three and later seven are interchangeable or incomplete. The alternative protects pilgrims of limited means. Hajj should not become a privilege in which only the wealthy can fulfil every form of worship. At the same time, a person should not claim inability while possessing the means to offer the sacrifice. Allah knows the real condition of the servant. (48:25 — **“for ˹what you did to˺ them unknowingly.”**)

**RESIDENTS OF THE SACRED AREA**

The movement toward residents of the sacred area rests on ***“completing ten. These offerings are”***, ***“for those who do not live near the Sacred House.”***, ***“And be mindful of Allah”***, ***“and know that Allah is severe in punishment”***. The rule is for those whose families do not reside near al-Masjid al-Ḥarām. The resident of Makkah and the surrounding sanctuary has a different legal status in the matter of tamattuʿ sacrifice, because the specific travel and return circumstances underlying the rule do not apply in the same way. The boundaries of the *ḥaram* and the detailed conditions are matters for qualified fiqh. This distinction illustrates a general legal method: rulings can depend upon location, intention, ability, and the form of worship. One should not transfer a concession or obligation from one category to another without understanding its cause. The verse ends with mindfulness and a warning of punishment. Pilgrimage strips away many social distinctions, but it also exposes character. The pilgrim must not cheat in sacrifice, harm others in crowds, violate pilgrim consecration, or use wealth to purchase special treatment that injures the weak. Mindfulness makes the outward rites an act of inward submission. The warning is not intended to destroy hope. Allah’s law contains concessions throughout the verse. Fear protects the pilgrim from carelessness, while hope in Allah’s mercy helps him complete the journey with humility. Pilgrimage resembles entering a long covenant journey whose route includes checkpoints, delays, and emergency provisions. Sincerity sets the destination, rites mark the road, and concessions keep an obstacle from turning worship into destruction. A traveller cannot discard the map whenever inconvenience appears, but neither is he told to break himself at a blocked pass. For pilgrims today, crowded transport and complex administration may look different from older journeys, yet completion, compensation, restraint, and mindfulness still govern the way. (2:21 — **“so that you may become mindful ˹of Him˺”**) In other words, residents of the sacred area answers completing the pilgrimage for allah.
