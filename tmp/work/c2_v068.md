**A GENERAL COMMAND NARROWED BY QUESTIONS**

The first answer to the first request does not describe an unusual animal; it describes an ordinary one and adds a rebuke at the end: ***“They said, “Call upon your Lord to clarify”***, ***“for us what type ˹of cow˺ it should be!””***, ***“He replied, “Allah says, ‘The cow should neither be old nor young”***, ***“but in between. So do”***, ***“as you are commanded!’””*** The age given excludes the two extremes and leaves the middle, which is where most cows of a herd are found, so the answer had closed the question rather than opened it. What follows is the sentence the people should have heard first, because it tells them that the command had been sufficient as it stood and that the asking was the problem. The report carried from Ibn ʿAbbās states that any cow would have been enough, so the whole description came from the questioners themselves rather than from the command. The Book records their questions and the answers that narrowed the choice, and the record is a lesson in how a simple duty becomes a difficult one.

**AN ANSWER THAT KEPT NARROWING THE FIELD**

Each clarification that follows removes more animals from the field, and the pattern is deliberate rather than accidental. The older accounts agree that the people's insistence on detail is what made the detail binding, since the command had been left open as a mercy and they pressed for a specification. The same principle runs through the Book's treatment of the Sabbath in the account of the town by the sea, where a prohibition was turned into a puzzle and then into a punishment. A reader who has watched a committee take a clear instruction and return it twice for elaboration will recognise the process, and the cost of elaboration is that the original freedom is gone. Nothing in the passage suggests that the people were confused; they were unwilling, and the questions are the shape their unwillingness took.

**THE ONE THING THEY WERE TOLD TO DO**

The clause that ends the verse is worth carrying out of the passage, since it is the Book's own summary of the exchange: they were told to act on what they had already been given. The command had been to sacrifice a cow, and nothing in the first instruction required a description of the animal's age; the people, however, had learned to treat a general command as an incomplete one. Their habit had a history, since the same generation had asked for a visible god while Moses was on the mountain, and had turned to a calf when the wait grew long: (7:148 — **“In the absence of Moses,”**). A community that asks for detail in order to postpone obedience will find that the detail arrives, that it is heavier than the original, and that the delay has made the duty harder to perform than it was at the start.

**THE LESSON FOR A READER OF THE PAGE**

The practical application of this verse is uncomfortable, because the instinct it exposes is not confined to that people. A person who is told to give in charity and asks which charity, in what amount, and by when, has begun the same conversation, and the outcomes are the same: an obligation that could have been discharged in an afternoon sits undone while its terms are negotiated. The Book's answer here is not that questions are forbidden; a genuine difficulty can be raised and answered. What is exposed is the use of a question as a place to stand still, and the remedy is the one sentence in the verse that the people never took up. A reader who has a plain duty waiting on his desk and a list of clarifications he intended to request can test this verse against his own week, and he will usually find that the duty was clear enough from the beginning.

**A COMMAND THAT GOT HEAVIER AS IT WAS EXPLAINED**

The verse leaves a reader with a rule about how guidance works in practice: it is given in a form he can carry, and if he insists on unpacking it before he moves, the unpacking becomes part of what he has to carry. The Book's method in this passage is to let the people's own questions produce their difficulty, so that no reader can blame the command for the weight. A second thing to notice is that the answer never withdraws the command or expresses regret at having given it; the duty remains, and the animal described in the next verses can still be found. That is the mercy hidden inside the narrowing, since the door stayed open even after the terms had grown tight. The reader who wants something to do rather than something to discuss can begin with the next verse's colour and see for himself how much narrower the field has become.
