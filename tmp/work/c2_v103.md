**THE WISH THAT PRECEDES THE VERDICT**

The passage then prices the refusal it has just documented, and it does so in the form of a wish: ***“If only they were faithful”***. The opening is the wish of the witness for the defendant. The faithfulness named is the acceptance of the messenger standing in front of them, the step the preceding unit showed them refusing. The gloss from Ibn ʿAbbās unpacks the wished-for conduct in the vocabulary of this community's own trial. Had they believed in Muhammad and in the Qur'an. The wish therefore stands at the exact point of failure the passage has been prosecuting. The pages were recognized. The man matched the pages. The faithfulness was the one move that would have changed the verdict, and the verse puts the wish in the mouth of the one who watched the failure happen.

**THE SECOND HALF OF THE CONDITION**

The wish's second clause is then added beside the first: ***“and mindful ˹of Allah˺, there would have been a better reward from Allah”***. The same gloss unpacks the mindfulness in its practical form. Had they kept from evil, repented from Judaism and from the sorcery the preceding unit dissected. The condition was therefore not an unscaled wall. The two steps were visible from where the party already stood. The reward clause then turns the wish into an appraisal. A better reward from Allah is set against the trade of the previous verse, the souls sold at a loss and the magic bought at a premium. The tradition heard in the glosses seals the comparison by flipping the direction of the wish. The reward from Allah is better than the faithlessness and the sorcery they settled for. The scale of that better reward is fixed in the final book's own accounting: (3:185 — **“And you will only receive your full reward on the Day of Judgment.”**). The good news of the same scale is set beside it in the verse that promises the gardens under the rivers: (2:25 — **“Give good news ˹O Prophet˺ to those who believe and do good that they will have Gardens under”**). What follows is the difference between a discount and a gift. The sorcery was the discount that costs the store. The reward is the gift that completes.

**THE REPETITION THE VERSE MAKES**

Notice that the verse closes by repeating its own opening mood in sharper form: ***“if only they knew”***. The wish appears twice in one breath of scripture, once over the faithfulness and once over the knowing. The tradition running through the glosses adds the knife's edge to the repetition. The transmission from Ibn ʿAbbās holds that they knew all this from their own book. The not-knowing of 2:101 is therefore worn here a second time, and the fit is just as false. The knowing they discarded was the same knowing that would have made the reward theirs. The repetition of the wish is the passage's accounting of the whole unit. The magic of 2:102 was bought by people who knew its price. The faithfulness of this verse was refused by people who knew its reward. The knowledge was never the missing piece in either case. The knowledge was in the room, and the wish is what the knowing left behind at the scene. The witness of the passage stands over the scene and says the only sentence left to say. If only.

**WHAT THE WISH CANNOT REACH**

The unit's force is that the wish never becomes a prayer. A prayer asks the owner for a change. This wish asks the history to un-happen. The passage has already scheduled the decision at the ownership level, and the schedule does not bend to the wishes of the witnesses. The reward-contrast of the verse is therefore the last mercy of the scene. The better reward stood available at the counter the whole time. The buyers went to the other booth. For any household or forum where the better offer is declined daily for the smaller prize, the appraisal in this verse is the ledger entry that names the loss. The wish is not the cure. The faithfulness and the mindfulness are the cure. The wish is only what the cure looks like when it is observed from the far side of the fence. The verse leaves the reader on the near side of the fence, where the two steps are still open and the wish has not yet been earned.
