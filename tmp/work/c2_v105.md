**THE WELL-WISHERS WHO WERE NONE**

The unit that follows draws the motive behind the refusals already documented: ***“The disbelievers from the People of the Book and the polytheists would not want you to receive any blessing from your Lord, ”***. The verse names the two parties in one breath and gives the shared wish a direction. The received gloss from Ibn ʿAbbās gives the scene its faces. On the side of the People of the Book stand Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf and his companions. On the side of the idolaters stand Abū Jahl and his companions. The good whose descent they dread is spelled out in the same gloss as prophethood, Islam, and the book. The received account of their public posture adds the costume the verse implies. Some of them assured the believers of their goodwill and said they wished the Muslims had received the best of doctrines, so that the wish could be worn openly and the envy kept private.

**ENVY AS THE HIDDEN VETO**

The verse then answers the wish with a matter of ownership: ***“but Allah selects whoever He wills”***. The gloss from Ibn ʿAbbās reads the choosing as the assignment of the mission itself — the sending of revelation to this messenger as the good they wanted withheld. The second clause then widens the answer past this community's hour: ***“And Allah is the Lord of infinite bount”***. The point the verse makes is a point about authority over blessing. The descent of good is not a market decision made by its neighbors. It is a choice made at the level of the ownership of all good. The reminder attached to that choice in the Qur'an's own ledger is worth quoting, because envy never files itself as envy: (4:32 — **“And do not crave what Allah has given some of you over others. Men will be rewarded according to their deeds”**). The craving that this verse exposes is the veto-envy that cannot bear a neighbor's raise. What follows is the difference between a wish and a grudge. A wish asks for the same good. A grudge wants the good withdrawn.

**THE FREEDOM OF THE OWNER TO CHOOSE**

The weight of the declaration sits on the word that follows the envy: ***“He wills for His mercy. And Allah is the Lord of infinite bounty”***. The dominion of that bounty is the same survey the previous unit closed on. Ownership of the heavens and the earth includes ownership of the gifts distributed inside them. The reason the passage returns to this here is structural. The envy of 2:105 and the slander of 2:102 share one root. Both refuse a distribution that arrived without the objector's signature. The famous description of the ownership is worth holding next to the envy: (3:26 — **“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O Allah! Lord over all authorities! You give authority to whoever You please and remove it from”**). The companion clause then keeps the hands of the owner open: (3:26 — **“good is in Your Hands. Surely You ˹alone˺ are Most Capable of everything”**). The envy therefore stands outside its competence. It resents a distribution it does not administer.

**THE GESTURE WORN OVER THE GRUDGE**

The passage closes the unit without a command, and the silence is itself instructive. The verse does not answer the pretend-well-wishers with a matching pretend. It answers with the exposure and the ownership clause. The exposure is complete because the wish has been priced. The ownership is complete because the choice has been located. For any reader who has worn the gesture of goodwill over a private grudge, at work or at home or in the rows of a congregation, the anatomy in this verse is the mirror held at the right height. The cure the passage implies is not a vow never to feel envy. It is the relocation of envy at the ownership. The bounty is the owner's to give. The deeds are the servant's to mind. The reader who keeps that division of labor finds the grudge starved of its jurisdiction. That is the division under which every blessing in the surah has been ordered from the first page, and the ordering holds in this verse exactly as it held there.
