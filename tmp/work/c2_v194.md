**SACRED AGAINST SACRED**

Read phrase by phrase, the ruling settles the sacred month and the violation together: ***“˹There will be retaliation in˺ a sacred month”***, ***“for ˹an offence in˺ a sacred month”***, ***“and all violations will bring about retaliation.”***, ***“So, if anyone attacks you, retaliate in the same manner. ˹But˺ be mindful of Allah”***, ***“and know that Allah is with those mindful ˹of Him˺”***. The sequence answers the hesitation first, generalizes the retaliation second, and offers the patience third, which is the shape of a ruling addressed to a conscience. The ruling meets the doubt at the level it was raised, which is the level of law. The account of Ibn ʿAbbās on the companions' hesitation matches this order. The companions' conscience is treated as an asset of the community rather than as a nuisance, and the ruling answers it in the currency of law.

˹There will be retaliation in˺ a sacred month for ˹an offence in˺ a sacred month answers the question the companions raised about fighting in the sacred months. The objection behind the question was pious — the month is sacred, so how can the sword enter it? — and the reply makes the sanctity symmetrical: the aggression in the sacred month is answered in the sacred month, and the holiness is not a shield for the one who violates it. The third clause generalizes the principle: and all violations will bring about retaliation. The sacred is thus protected by the sacred law of retaliation rather than exempted from law. Because the companions' hesitation is recorded in the reports, the verse addresses the conscience that fears being unjust inside a holy season. The reply honors the conscience by giving it a rule instead of a rebuke.

**THE COMPANIONS' HESITATION**

The occasion is preserved in the reports of the year the verse answered: the believers had fought a party during one of the sacred months and the doubt arrived with the news. The response was measured in the cause as well as the calendar, since the enemy's aggression had begun in the month and the fight was the answer to it. The reported wording of Ibn ʿAbbās keeps both halves of the ledger: the month of the ʿumra they were prevented from is answered in the month of the prevention. The doubt itself is a sign of the community's conscience, and the revelation answers it with the equality of the sacred months. Because the answer is procedural rather than emotional, the conscience is given a way to act without being told to stop feeling. The doubt of the companions is therefore part of the verse's meaning: a community that doubts its own violence is a community fit to carry the permission. The revelation answers the doubt with a rule and keeps the doubters as the verse's first audience. Because the answer respects the conscience, the rule is obeyed by people who understand it.

**EQUAL MEASURE**

The retaliation rule is stated twice — the sacred month for the sacred month, and the attacks in kind — and the repetition is the law's symmetry. The principle appears in the general legislation of recompense: if you retaliate, the retaliation must equal the injury (16:126 — **“If you retaliate, then let it be equivalent to what you have suffered. But if you patiently endure, it is certainly best”**). The equality is the boundary that separates retaliation from revenge, and the boundary is drawn by the injured party's own restraint. Because the measure is fixed, the punishment cannot grow as it travels down the family line, and the feud is stopped by arithmetic. The application is to count before answering: the answer is permitted in the measure of the injury and not in the measure of the anger. The one who answers beyond the measure has entered the verse's prohibition.

**PATIENCE AS THE BETTER VERDICT**

The close of the verse offers the second path alongside the first: ˹But˺ be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah is with those mindful ˹of Him˺. The patience named in the legislation of recompense is placed here as the better verdict for the one who can carry it, and the promise attached is the company of Allah. The forgiveness of the strong is praised in the system's economics as the higher exchange, since reconciliation is repaid by the One who does not like wrongdoers (42:40 — **“The reward of an evil deed is its equivalent. But whoever pardons and seeks reconciliation,”**). Because both paths are offered, the verse treats the injured person as a moral agent rather than a mechanism of revenge. The application is to weigh the two verdicts in the hour of the injury — the equal answer and the pardoned one — and to know that the scales are held by Allah. The mindful person retires the case in the better court. The verse's double offer is also a discipline for the one who retaliates: the equal measure is the ceiling and the patience is the invitation. Because the ceiling is enforced by the reminder of Allah, the retaliator answers his own anger before he answers his enemy. The two verdicts of the verse — the equal answer and the pardoned one — are both lawful, which is the range the conscience is trusted to navigate. The application is to know the range before the hour of the injury arrives.
