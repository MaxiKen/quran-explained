**SACRED TIME AND CONFLICT**

Sacred time and conflict draws together ***“˹There will be retaliation in˺ a sacred month”***, ***“for ˹an offence in˺ a sacred month”***. The Arabs recognized four sacred months in which fighting was restricted, but some used the calendar to manipulate the restriction or to attack while claiming that others had violated it. This verse establishes a principle of justice: a violation of sacred time does not give an aggressor immunity from defence, and the response must remain equivalent to the offence. The sacred month is for the sacred month is historically connected to the events surrounding the expedition to minor pilgrimage and the Treaty of Hudaybiyah, when the Muslims were prevented from entering Makkah in a sacred period. The historical setting is specific, but the legal principle is broader: sacred status cannot be used as a weapon to make one side untouchable while it attacks the other. If an enemy attacks, the believers may defend themselves; they may not use the attack as an excuse for unlimited vengeance. This echoes 2:178–179, where *retaliation* is measured legal accountability rather than tribal rage. Proportionality includes the target, means, scale, duration, and likely harm. A limited attack cannot justify killing uninvolved people or destroying an entire city. The stronger party has no right to call excess *victory.* Allah’s command remains binding even when retaliation would be emotionally satisfying. (2:190 — **“Fight in the cause of Allah ˹only˺ against those who wage war against you”**) Because sacred time and conflict governs 2:194, responding to aggression arises from sacred time and conflict. It follows that mindfulness during retaliation completes sacred time and conflict through responding to aggression.

**RESPONDING TO AGGRESSION**

***“and all violations will bring about retaliation.”***, ***“So, if anyone attacks you, retaliate in the same manner. ˹But˺ be mindful of Allah”*** mark the turn toward responding to aggression. The language whoever attacks you, attack him in the same way is addressed to a community in an active conflict and presupposes lawful authority. It does not authorize individuals to assault someone who insulted them or to punish a whole group for the act of one person. The response must be directed at the aggressor, supported by evidence, and constrained by the goal of restoring safety. The Qur’an repeatedly joins permission to defend with a prohibition of transgression and a command to accept peace when it is genuinely offered. The verses must be read as a complete legal and moral framework, not isolated as slogans. Sacred time teaches restraint. The lunar calendar includes the month al-Qaʿdah, the pilgrimage month, Muḥarram, and Rajab as sacred months, according to the authentic hadith. Their sanctity is part of Allah’s order and is not to be manipulated for convenience. Likewise, Makkah has a sacred sanctuary in which bloodshed, hunting, and other violations have special gravity. The principle has a modern analogue in respect for truces, humanitarian pauses, places of worship, hospitals, and protected persons. Declaring a space or time protected and then exploiting that protection to attack others is a betrayal of trust. Defence may respond to an attack, but it should seek to restore the protection rather than erase all limits. (5:2 — **“nor those ˹pilgrims˺ on their way to the Sacred House seeking their Lord’s bounty”**) The reason mindfulness during retaliation follows responding to aggression lies in sacred time and conflict. Therefore, mindfulness during retaliation returns practical force to responding to aggression.

**MINDFULNESS DURING RETALIATION**

The verse develops taqwā in retaliation with ***“and know that Allah is with those mindful ˹of Him˺”***. The verse inserts *Be mindful of Allah* between permission to respond and the promise of divine companionship. This placement shows how easily retaliation can become sin. *Mindfulness* asks the believer to monitor intention, restrain anger, verify facts, and stop when the lawful response is complete. A person may feel that an enemy deserves more than the law permits. Mindfulness means refusing to make emotion the judge. It also means caring for one’s own fighters, civilians, and future peace. Every act is recorded, and victory cannot excuse a violation of Allah’s boundary. Allah is with those who are mindful is a promise of guidance, support, and protection. It does not mean that the mindful will never suffer military loss. It means that they are not abandoned when they preserve justice under pressure. Allah gives them clarity, steadiness, and reward even when the worldly result is delayed. This promise encourages restraint as well as courage. The community can defend itself without becoming what it opposes because its real strength is obedience to Allah rather than hatred of an enemy. Measured response is like returning a borrowed weight on the same scale: more would be theft, less may surrender a right, and equivalence keeps anger from deciding the measure. Sacred times and places do not grant an aggressor immunity when he exploits them, yet neither do they erase restraint from the defender. Present-day disputes often magnify one injury through collective revenge. The verse permits an answer to actual aggression while placing mindfulness beside the permission as its controlling guard. (5:8 — **“O believers! Stand firm”**) In other words, mindfulness during retaliation answers sacred time and conflict.
