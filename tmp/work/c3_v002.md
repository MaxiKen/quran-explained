**THE FOUNDATION OF FAITH**

***“Allah! There is no god ˹worthy”*** establishes the foundation of faith. This verse begins with the name Allāh and immediately declares the central truth of revelation: there is no deity worthy of worship except Him. This is tawḥīd, the oneness of Allah in His lordship, names and attributes, and right to worship. The statement rejects every rival, whether an idol, human being, angel, ideology, desire, or created power. The Qur’an frequently joins the testimony of divine oneness to the truth of revelation. The Book that will be discussed in the following verses does not come from a committee of competing gods or from an impersonal force. It comes from the One who alone creates, knows, commands, and sustains. Al-Ḥayy means the Ever-Living. Allah’s life is perfect, beginningless, everlasting, and free from weakness, sleep, death, or dependence. Created beings live by a life given to them, pass through change, become weak, and eventually die. Allah’s life is not received from another and is never interrupted. This name gives the believer certainty when every created support is unstable. Families change, governments fall, bodies age, and possessions disappear, but the Ever-Living remains. The servant’s relationship with Allah is therefore not attached to a temporary worldly circumstance. Allah is worthy of worship in prosperity and distress because His perfection does not change. Al-Qayyūm means the One who stands by Himself and sustains everything else. Allah needs no support, while every creature needs Him at every moment. (2:255 — **“Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him, the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining.”**) Ibn ʿAbbās reads the Ever-Living as the One who never dies and the All-Sustaining as the Self-Subsisting One without beginning.

**AL-ḤAYY: THE EVER-LIVING**

***“of worship˺ except Him—the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining”*** deepens al-ḥayy: the ever-living. The heavens, earth, bodies, souls, causes, and outcomes continue only through His sustaining command. The name also means that Allah manages and maintains creation. He knows its needs, sets its measures, and directs its affairs with wisdom. A believer may use food, medicine, work, family, and lawful institutions as means, but he knows that no means operates independently of the All-Sustaining. The verse combines tawḥīd with dependence. If Allah alone is Ever-Living and All-Sustaining, then ultimate fear, hope, love, supplication, and reliance belong to Him alone. This does not require abandoning practical means. It means that the heart does not treat means as gods. A doctor may treat, but Allah grants healing; a teacher may explain, but Allah opens understanding; a farmer may plant, but Allah brings life from the soil. The names are especially powerful in supplication. The Prophet ﷺ taught that the greatest name of Allah is found in supplications that call upon Him as the One God and as the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining. The servant asks from the One whose power and mercy never become exhausted. The same two names appear in Āyat al-Kursī, where they introduce a description of Allah’s knowledge, authority, and protection. There, the Ever-Living never slumbers, and the All-Sustaining owns whatever is in the heavens and earth. The repetition shows that these names are keys to understanding divine sovereignty: Allah does not merely begin creation and leave it unattended; He sustains it continuously. (20:111 — **“And all faces will be humbled before the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining.”**)

**AL-QAYYŪM: THE ALL-SUSTAINING**

The way a lamp depends on a current it did not create offers a small analogy: creation continues only through the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining Lord. Today, reliance on employment, medicine, or institutions remains lawful while the heart refuses to treat any created support as self-sustaining. Because the foundation of faith opens into al-ḥayy: the ever-living, al-qayyūm: the all-sustaining joins the foundation of faith to al-ḥayy: the ever-living. Since al-qayyūm: the all-sustaining develops the foundation of faith, al-ḥayy: the ever-living carries the transition. Worship and reliance matters, which means al-qayyūm: the all-sustaining answers al-ḥayy: the ever-living. Al-ḥayy: the ever-living gives practical form to the foundation of faith; worship and reliance sets its horizon. The foundation of faith stays bound to al-qayyūm: the all-sustaining because al-ḥayy: the ever-living locates the foundation of faith within choices shaped by the foundation of faith. Worship and reliance carries the foundation of faith into conduct shaped by al-qayyūm: the all-sustaining.
