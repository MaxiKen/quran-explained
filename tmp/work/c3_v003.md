**REVELATION COMES FROM ONE LORD**

***“He has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the”*** establishes revelation comes from one lord. After declaring Allah’s oneness, the verse identifies the source of revelation: He revealed the Book to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The Qur’an is not presented as a human spiritual reflection or a product of the Prophet’s personal imagination. It is a revelation from Allah, delivered for guidance, correction, worship, and judgement. The phrase in truth indicates both the truth of its source and the truthfulness of its content. The Qur’an speaks truthfully about Allah, creation, previous revelation, human responsibility, history, and the Hereafter. Its commands are just, its narratives are purposeful, and its promises and warnings are certain. The Qur’an is muṣaddiq, confirming what came before it. This means that it affirms the original revelation given by Allah to His prophets, including the Torah given to Mūsā and the Gospel given to ʿĪsā. All true prophets brought the same foundation: worship Allah alone, obey His guidance, and prepare for the final return. Confirmation does not mean that every wording found in later religious manuscripts is automatically identical to the original revelation. The Qur’an confirms the divine origin and surviving truth of previous scriptures while also functioning as a guardian and criterion over them. It corrects false beliefs, exposes concealment, and restores the universal prophetic message. The Tawrāh and Injīl named here are revealed books. The Torah was given to Moses, and the Gospel was given to Jesus. (5:48 — **“We have revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ this Book with the truth,”**)

**CONFIRMING EARLIER REVELATION**

***“Book in truth, confirming what came before it,”*** deepens confirming earlier revelation. The Qur’an honours both prophets and rejects the idea that faith in Muhammad ﷺ requires contempt for them. A Muslim believes in all Allah’s messengers and all the revelations Allah truly sent, without elevating one prophet into divinity or rejecting another. The Qur’an’s account of Jesus is therefore neither a denial of his miraculous birth and mission nor an acceptance of claims that compromise divine oneness. It presents him as the Messiah, a word from Allah, a messenger to the Children of Israel, and a servant who called people to worship Allah alone. The Qur’an’s relationship to earlier revelation is one of continuity and completion. It confirms the shared roots of the prophetic message, preserves the essential truths, and provides the final universal guidance. The earlier revelations were sent to particular communities and historical circumstances, while the Qur’an addresses humanity and remains protected as the final revelation. This continuity also gives the People of the Book a basis for recognizing the Qur’an. A new revelation from the same Lord should not be judged by tribal loyalty or envy. It should be examined in light of the truth already known: the unity of God, the reality of revelation, the moral law, and the coming Day of Judgement. Receiving revelation is a mercy and a responsibility. The believer must recite the Qur’an, understand it, obey it, and convey it accurately. (2:136 — **“Say, ˹O believers,˺ “We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us;”**)

**THE TORAH AND THE GOSPEL**

***“as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel”*** clarifies the torah and the gospel. It is not enough to claim respect for a Book while ignoring its call to justice, worship, mercy, honesty, and repentance. The verse also protects Muslims from religious chauvinism. They should not speak of the Torah and Gospel as though Allah never revealed them. Nor should they treat the Qur’an as merely one competing cultural document. The proper attitude is to honour the original revelations, believe in their prophets, and follow the final revelation sent for the present human community. Revelation resembles water sent through successive channels from one spring; the vessels differ across history while the true source remains one. Today, conversation with Jewish and Christian neighbours should honour the prophetic origin of earlier revelation while stating the Qur’an’s final authority clearly. Continuity and completion carries revelation comes from one lord into conduct shaped by the torah and the gospel.
