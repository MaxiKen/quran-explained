**THE COMFORT IS ASKED FOR**

The address turns to the believers with a two-handed prescription. ***“O believers! Seek comfort in patience”*** ***“and prayer. Allah is truly with those”*** ***“who are patient”***. Two tools and one presence. Patience. Prayer. Allah with the patient. Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss unpacks the seeking of help as the seeking of strength for the obligations. The patience is the abstaining from contraventions and shameful works. The prayer named beside it is the abundant prayer of the night and the day. The point is that the comfort is not wished for in the verse. It is sought in two instruments. The instruments are the strength of the limbs and the strength of the schedule. What follows is that the suffering believer is handed work to do, and the work is the door of the presence. The older passage in this surah states the same prescription with its weight attached (3:200 — **“O believers! Patiently endure, persevere, stand on guard, and be mindful of Allah, so you may be successful”**). Endure. Persevere. Stand guard. The verbs are the handles of the instrument.

**PATIENCE AND THE PRAYER THAT HELPS**

The two tools are joined because neither is complete alone. Patience without prayer becomes a clenched jaw. Prayer without patience becomes a complaint with candles. The transmission of the son of Zayd divides the patience into two parts. One part turns away from the prohibitions. The other endures the afflictions when they arrive. The pairing with the prayer supplies both parts with fuel. The early authorities read the night prayer as the purifier of the day's books, and the patience as the guard of the day's boundaries. The two hands hold the instrument together. The patriarch's own words under pressure state the pairing from inside a crisis (12:18 — **“So ˹I can only endure with˺ beautiful patience! It is Allah’s help that I seek to bear your claims.””**). Beautiful patience. Sought help. One sentence carries the whole prescription. The effect is that the method is ancient and portable. The father of the prophets used it under a false-blood shirt. The method scales down to an ordinary week without losing its physics.

**THE HUMBLE ONES**

The closing promise has a named recipient. Allah is truly with those who are patient. The with-ness is the reward that arrives before any ending. The gloss of Ibn ʿAbbās keeps the presence practical. The patient ones are helped into the obligations and kept from the shameful works. The keeping is the presence made visible in conduct. For the modern reader the verse is a standing instruction for the hard seasons. Seek the comfort in the two tools. Expect the presence, not the exemption. This age sells comfort as a product and calls patience a defect of the brand. The verse reverses both prices. The comfort is sought in the patient posture. The prayer is the meeting that steadies the posture. The waiting is therefore never empty time. It is an activity with two handles. The remembering of the covenant supplies the patience. The patience spends the supply where it counts. The presence is the standing balance of the account.

**TWO HANDS ON THE INSTRUMENT**

The genius of the pairing is that it covers both the ambush and the siege. The patience handles the affliction that arrives without warning. The prayer handles the slow weight of the ordinary week. The effect of holding both is a resilience that does not depend on the shape of the trouble. The reason the promise is attached to the posture rather than the outcome is that the outcome is scheduled by the one who sends the touches. The with-ness is given now. The ending is given later. For the modern reader the prescription fits the two most common crises of this age. The ambush of bad news is answered with the creed-posture of patience. The siege of the crowded calendar is answered with the appointment of prayer. This age prescribes distraction for both and wonders at the fatigue. The verse prescribes two handles and promises a presence. The comfort is sought, not wished. The seeking is rewarded with the company of the one who counts the patient among his own. The presence arrives with the practice and stays past the season.
