**A REMEDY FOR THE STRAIN THAT FOLLOWS**

With the objections about the direction of prayer answered, the verse turns to the strain those objections had placed on the community and gives an instruction that covers every difficulty: ***“O believers! Seek comfort in patience”***. The command is addressed to the believers as such rather than to any single crisis, which is why it is not tied to one event, and the help it promises is the help needed for carrying out what God has commanded rather than for escaping it. Patience here is not the passive endurance of a misfortune but the restraining of the self from panic, complaint, and disobedience while the work continues. The same instruction stands earlier in the sūrah with the same two instruments, which shows the remedy to be a settled feature of the community's training: (2:45 — **“And seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed, it is a burden except for the humble—”**). The verse offers a method, and the method is given to people who have already been told what they are to do.

**PATIENCE AND PRAYER NAMED TOGETHER**

Patience is joined to prayer as the second half of the remedy: ***“and prayer. Allah is truly with those”***. The two are named together because the community needs both a way of bearing what it cannot change and a way of asking for what it cannot reach, and the sentence keeps them in one breath so that neither is practised alone. Prayer is singled out after patience because it gathers within itself the obedience that patience makes possible, and because it returns at fixed hours rather than only in emergencies. That is why the instruction has been read as covering the prayers offered at night and the voluntary prayers beside the obligatory ones, since the point is to keep the channel open rather than to call only when the storm has already arrived. The remedy is therefore available to every believer, whatever the size of what he is carrying.

**THE PRESENCE THAT IS PROMISED HERE**

The sentence closes with a promise whose wording is chosen as carefully as the command: ***“who are patient”***. What is meant by God's being with them is help, protection, and victory, since the phrase in this context cannot mean a presence of place, and the assurance is given to those who endure rather than to those who merely ask. The same promise is attached to perseverance in Sūrat al-Anfāl, where the believers are told that their strength depends on standing together: (8:46 — **“Persevere! Surely Allah is with those who persevere”**). The promise is attached to patience rather than to prayer, although prayer is the greater act of worship, because patience underlies both and carries the whole religion within it. What the community receives, then, is not an escape from hardship but a companionship within it.

**TWO HABITS KEPT THROUGH A BAD YEAR**

Think of a household facing a year of thin harvest, where the parents keep two habits through it: they refuse to panic in front of the children, and they keep asking the one person in the district who can actually help. The first habit steadies the house from within, and the second keeps the door open to help from outside, and a family that keeps only one of them usually fails at the other. The verse prescribes exactly this pair, and it does not promise that the year will be easy, since the testing has already been announced as part of the community's life. Anyone who has watched a household come through a bad year intact will recognise the two habits at work. What the sentence adds is the assurance that the second habit is answered.

**THE PROMISE THAT THE FOLLOWING VERSES FILL IN**

Read with what comes next, the verse states in advance the method by which the community will meet the losses that are about to be described. The tests are named in the verses that follow, and the reward is named with them, so this short instruction stands as the heading over the whole passage. The Book makes the same demand at the end of another sūrah, where endurance and vigilance are joined to the mindfulness of God as the road to success: (3:200 — **“O believers! Patiently endure, persevere, stand on guard, and be mindful of Allah, so you may be successful”**). The community is not left to improvise its response to hardship, since the two instruments have been placed in its hands. What remains is to use them before the need arrives.
