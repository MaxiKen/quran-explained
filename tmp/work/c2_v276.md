**DESTRUCTION AND GROWTH**

***“Allah has made interest fruitless and charity fruitful.”*** establishes destruction and growth. This verse contrasts ribā and ṣadaqāt. Ribā may appear to increase wealth because the lender receives more than the principal, but Allah removes its blessing and ultimate benefit. Charity may appear to reduce wealth because the giver releases property, but Allah grows it through blessing, social repair, forgiveness, and eternal reward. Allah destroys interest does not mean that every riba-based business collapses immediately or that every charitable person becomes wealthy in this life. Some exploiters prosper for a time, and some generous people remain poor. The verse describes the deeper reality: unlawful increase corrupts the heart and society, while sincere charity produces enduring good that may not be visible at once. A numerical increase is not the same as barakah. Wealth can expand while bringing anxiety, family conflict, legal exposure, illness, and spiritual emptiness. Another person may possess less but enjoy contentment, trustworthy relationships, and reward with Allah. The Qur’an asks people to judge wealth by its lawful source and beneficial use, not merely by its amount. Ribā can also be destroyed through inflation, default, litigation, or economic instability. But even when it survives materially, its spiritual destruction remains. Charity can grow through the recipient’s recovery, the education of children, a community strengthened, or a good practice continued after the donor’s death. Allah grows charity through direct reward and through its effects. A meal may give someone strength to work; a loan without exploitation may save a family from predatory debt; education may benefit generations; and a medical treatment may preserve a life. **THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN INCREASE AND BLESSING**

***“And Allah does not like any ungrateful evildoer”*** deepens the difference between increase and blessing. The giver may see only the first act, while Allah sees the whole chain of benefit. The growth also occurs in the giver’s character. Generosity weakens miserliness, increases empathy, and teaches reliance upon Allah. The recipient’s dignity is preserved, and social trust grows when wealth is shared without humiliation. Ribā can concentrate wealth among lenders and make the poor pay more for access to necessities. The borrower carries the risk of illness, unemployment, or business failure, while the lender demands a predetermined increase. This can turn temporary hardship into permanent dependency. Islamic finance is not a promise that every partnership succeeds or that all trade is fair by default. It requires transparency, shared risk where appropriate, lawful assets, and protection from deception. The moral objective is an economy in which profit is connected to real benefit rather than guaranteed extraction from need. The verse ends by describing the person who consumes ribā as kafūr athīm, ungrateful and sinful. Ingratitude appears when a person receives Allah’s provision but refuses the limits that protect others. Sin appears when he knowingly uses wealth to exploit or when he treats the prohibition as an obstacle to clever circumvention. The description is not a licence for ordinary Muslims to declare every borrower or employee outside Islam. It warns about a pattern of deliberate ingratitude and sin. People trapped in complex systems require knowledge, help, and a path out, not casual condemnation. **CHARITY GROWS IN MANY FORMS**

A person who recognizes riba should stop entering new exploitative transactions, seek reliable advice about existing obligations, and protect the rights of all parties. If he has taken identifiable unlawful gain from a person, he must return it. If he is a borrower trapped by a contract, he seeks lawful ways to settle without creating greater harm. Charity can accompany repentance, but it does not replace restitution. The wronged person’s right must be restored where possible. Institutions should provide debt counselling and interest-free assistance so that repentance is practical. Allah does not love the ungrateful sinner, while He loves the charitable and those who do good. The contrast invites the believer to align financial choices with divine love. He earns lawfully, gives generously, avoids waste, and supports systems that protect the vulnerable. The Prophet ﷺ taught that Allah accepts only what is good. The quality of the money matters before it is given, and the manner of giving matters afterward. Charity grows when it is rooted in lawful provision and sincere intention. The case is like two fields in the same plain, one of them rented out for a fixed sum every season without the owner lifting a hand. And the other worked by the owner himself; the rented field is bare in the year the tenant leaves, and the worked field feeds the family that planted it. Destruction and growth confronts the difference between increase and blessing in a reader who compares the interest on his savings with the gifts he gives and finds that the first column is the thicker one.
