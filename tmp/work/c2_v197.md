**MONTHS WITH NAMES**

The verse's phrases frame the journey: the months, the tongue, the sack, the reason: ***“˹Commitment to˺ pilgrimage is made in appointed months. Whoever commits to ˹performing˺ pilgrimage, let them stay away from intimate relations, foul language”***, ***“and arguments during pilgrimage. Whatever good you do, Allah ˹fully˺ knows of it. Take ˹necessary˺ provisions ˹for the journey˺”***, ***“surely the best provision is righteousness.”***, ***“And be mindful of Me, O people of reason”***. The frame runs from the calendar at one end to the conscience at the other, and the provisions are named between them so nothing travels outside the frame. Every item on the packing list is named before the departure date arrives. The months named in the account Ibn ʿAbbās transmits open this sequence. The tongue's discipline during the rites is legislated beside the body's discipline, and the pairing shows the ihram covering the person from both directions at once.

˹Commitment to˺ pilgrimage is made in appointed months. gives the rite a place on the calendar and the calendar a purpose in worship. The transmission of Ibn ʿAbbās names the months precisely — Shawwāl, Dhū'l-Qaʿda, and the first ten of Dhū'l-Ḥijja — so the consecration of the ihram has named doors rather than vague seasons. The naming matters because the rites are public: the whole community travels on the same schedule, and the schedule is the beginning of the discipline. The appointed months answer the one who would keep the pilgrimage as a private errand; the rite arrives with a crowd. The obligation of the House is stated in the same public terms (3:97 — **“and the standing-place of Abraham. Whoever enters it should be safe. Pilgrimage to this House is an obligation by Allah upon whoever”**) Because the months are known, the preparation is possible — the traveler can free his calendar and his debts before the ihram begins. The worship is dated by the One who set the phases that measure the dates.

**SILENCE OF THE PILGRIM**

The verse defines the ihram of the tongue as sharply as the ihram of the dress: let them stay away from intimate relations, foul language, and arguments during pilgrimage. The prohibition covers the body and the conversation at once, which is the pilgrim's double dress. The reports of the farewell pilgrimage preserve the same teaching in practical form, where the assembled thousands were taught the rites and warned against the lewdness the verse names. Because the arguments are forbidden in the rite, the journey to the House is a rehearsal of the peace the House represents. The one who cannot hold his tongue has entered the ihram with his clothes and left his character at home. The verse's discipline is therefore social as well as personal: the crowd is kept holy by the words its members do not say.

**PROVISION THAT DOES NOT WEIGH**

The verse then mentions the traveler's sack and gives it a surprising content: Take ˹necessary˺ provisions ˹for the journey˺ and then surely the best provision is righteousness. The provision is not abolished but ranked, since the physical sack is necessary and the moral sack is best. The ranking answers the two failures at once: the pilgrim who travels with no food and the pilgrim who travels with no fear. Because the best provision is named, the traveler is taught to check his bags twice — once for the road and once for the heart. The application is to pack the verse's two provisions in the order it gives them: enough for the body's journey and the righteousness that makes the journey a worship. The one who carries the best provision cannot be stranded. The provision-image also corrects the modern habit of treating the pilgrimage as a travel product: the verse counts the sack and the character in one packing list. Because the best provision is named, the traveler can weigh his bags against the verse before the departure date. The rites the traveller keeps are valued on the same scale (22:30 — **“That is so. And whoever honours the rituals of Allah, it is best”**) The weighing is the first rite of the journey, and the verse sets the scale before the road begins.

**REASON AS THE AUDIENCE**

The close addresses the audience by its capacity rather than its rank: And be mindful of Me, O people of reason. The address is to the intellect the journey is meant to sharpen, since the rites are a curriculum of remembrance for people who think. The pilgrimage's geography — the standing, the walking, the stoning — is designed for the mind that can read it. Because the audience is the people of reason, the ritual is not a mystery to be endured but a text to be understood. The application is to arrive at the rites with questions and to leave with answers, since the verse expects the reason to be used. The mindfulness asked for at the end is the provision named in the middle. The two halves of the verse frame the journey: the calendar at the head and the conscience at the close. The application is to travel inside that frame — dated and mindful — and to let the rites teach the reason rather than silence it. The pilgrim who returns with more questions than he left has used the verse's equipment. Because the equipment is provision and righteousness together, the journey is measured twice — once by the sack and once by the soul. The application is to travel inside that frame and to let the rites teach the reason.
