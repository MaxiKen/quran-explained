**HAJJ HAS AN APPOINTED SEASON**

***“˹Commitment to˺ pilgrimage is made in appointed months. Whoever commits to ˹performing˺ pilgrimage, let them stay away from intimate relations, foul language”*** establish the appointed season for hajj. Hajj is not an improvised journey performed whenever a person chooses. It has known months and appointed rites. The classical scholars discuss the precise beginning and ending of the Hajj months, commonly identifying them as Shawwāl, the month al-Qaʿdah, and the first days of the pilgrimage month. The season allows pilgrims to prepare, enter pilgrim consecration, travel, and perform the rites at their prescribed times. The fixed season teaches that worship is received through obedience, not through private invention. A person may perform minor pilgrimage at other times, but Hajj has its own communal calendar and legal structure. The pilgrim submits his schedule to revelation and accepts that sacred time belongs to Allah. Once a person undertakes Hajj, he must avoid *rafath*, *sin*, and *jidāl*. *Rafath* includes sexual intimacy during pilgrim consecration and obscene or sexually explicit speech. Marriage remains honoured, but the pilgrim temporarily suspends intimacy while in the relevant state of consecration. *Fusūq* includes disobedience, foul conduct, and violation of Allah’s commands. *Jidāl* is quarrelling, hostile argument, and disputation that destroys the spirit of the pilgrimage. The list covers desire, moral rebellion, and anger. Hajj is physically crowded and emotionally demanding; people of different languages, schools, and cultures share narrow spaces. The pilgrim must restrain the tongue, hands, gaze, and temper. A person who performs the outer rites while abusing others has not understood the purpose of pilgrim consecration. It follows that provision for the journey completes hajj has an appointed season through not every discussion is forbidden.

**NOT EVERY DISCUSSION IS FORBIDDEN**

The verse develops not every discussion is forbidden with ***“and arguments during pilgrimage. Whatever good you do, Allah ˹fully˺ knows of it. Take ˹necessary˺ provisions ˹for the journey˺”***. The prohibition of *jidāl* does not ban necessary communication, legal questions, correcting a mistake, or calm discussion of a genuine difference. Pilgrims must ask for directions, resolve disputes, and protect people from harm. What is forbidden is quarrelling driven by ego, insult, factional pride, or the desire to dominate. The same distinction applies to religious scholarship. A person may learn the rites and follow a qualified teacher without turning every valid difference into a fight. The variety of recognized legal opinions should produce humility and cooperation, not abuse in the sacred precinct. Hajj removes markers of status through simple clothing, common movement, and shared rites. The wealthy and poor stand together at ʿArafah; the ruler cannot purchase a separate Hereafter; the scholar must still wait in a crowd. These conditions reveal the heart. Patience in a queue, sharing water, protecting another pilgrim from heat, and apologizing after a mistake can be greater signs of accepted Hajj than public emotion. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever performs Hajj without sexual misconduct or sin returns like the day his mother gave birth, in the authentic reports of the collections of al-Bukhārī and Muslim. The hadith highlights moral purification, not only the completion of a travel itinerary. (22:27 — **“Call ˹all˺ people to the pilgrimage. They will come to you on foot”**) The reason provision for the journey follows not every discussion is forbidden lies in hajj has an appointed season. Therefore, provision for the journey returns practical force to not every discussion is forbidden.

**PROVISION FOR THE JOURNEY**

At provision for the journey, attention falls on ***“surely the best provision is righteousness.”***. *Take provisions* is a literal instruction. Pilgrims should prepare food, water, money, clothing, medicine, documents, and knowledge of the rites. Tawakkul does not mean travelling unprepared or expecting other people to support an avoidably careless person. The Qur’an corrects those who treated lack of provisions as a sign of piety and then depended upon people for food. The instruction also becomes a metaphor: the journey to Allah requires provision of faith, repentance, patience, lawful wealth, and good character. Material supplies sustain the body for days; mindfulness sustains the soul through life and the Hereafter. *Mindfulness* is awareness of Allah that protects the servant from disobedience. It is the best provision because it remains useful when money, luggage, status, and companions cannot follow a person beyond death. A pilgrim who takes mindfulness seriously prepares by settling debts, seeking forgiveness, reconciling with relatives, and ensuring that his travel wealth is lawful. Mindfulness also protects the rights of other pilgrims. A person does not reserve excessive space, steal belongings, push the weak, or use ritual urgency to justify abuse. The sacred journey is a test of how one treats servants of the same Lord. (5:2 — **“nor those ˹pilgrims˺ on their way to the Sacred House seeking their Lord’s bounty”**)

**REASON SUMMONED AT THE END**

Reason summoned at the end draws together ***“And be mindful of Me, O people of reason”***. The closing address to *ūlī al-albāb* invites reflection. People of sound understanding see the relation between external rites and inner character. They do not confuse a costly journey with automatic salvation, nor do they dismiss the rites as empty symbolism. They perform the outward obligations while pursuing the inward transformation they signify. The wise pilgrim returns home with a more disciplined prayer, a more truthful tongue, a more generous hand, and greater care for people. If the journey produces pride and contempt, its lessons have been resisted. The months of pilgrimage are like the marked season for cultivating a field: work done within the season has an order, while careless quarrelling can trample what is being grown. Ihram removes several ordinary freedoms so attention can be gathered around worship and provision for the final journey. Present-day pilgrims carry phones, bookings, queues, and group pressures into the sacred places; the verse asks that these practical burdens become occasions for restraint rather than channels for obscenity, sin, or dispute. (9:36 — **“So do not wrong one another during these months.”**) In other words, reason summoned at the end answers hajj has an appointed season.
