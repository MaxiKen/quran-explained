**THE FAVOUR TO BE REMEMBERED**

The verse turns the appeal from a crowd to a class. ***“O Children of Israel!”*** ***“Remember My favours upon you”*** ***“and how I honoured you above the others”***. Favours plural. Honoured singular. The address keeps its family name. The parallel passage states the same honour in its active form (2:47 — **“O Children of Israel! Remember ˹all˺ the favours I granted you and how I honoured you above the others”**). In Ibn ʿAbbās's account the remembering has a date and a face: *"When they came out from Egypt, there was no human being in the world more honoured by Allah than the Children of Israel, until they worshipped the calf."* The honour is past tense twice over. So the verse is not offering a promotion. It is reading back a receipt. The command to remember is a command to audit an account already settled in the reader's favour. The address keeps its family form because the favour did come in a family line. The verse never pretends otherwise. The choosing is even stated in its own historical shape (44:32 — **“And indeed, We chose the Israelites knowingly above the others”**).

**THE DELIVERANCE IN THE MEMORY**

The honour being remembered is concrete. The crossing is concrete. The rescue is concrete. Gratitude in this verse is given a history rather than a mood. That is why the Quran can command a remembering at all. The remembering has an object. The object is a night of rescue and a morning of honour. Ibn ʿAbbās's account keeps the two turning points of the story in one frame. The honour held. Then the calf arrived. Both points turn on the same hinge. One is the remembering this verse commands. The other is the worshipping that replaced it. A favour remembered and a favour merely inherited are different things. An inherited favour is a story one tells. A remembered favour is an account one stands in. For the modern reader the logic holds without adjustment. A community that cannot name its favours will not recognise its debts. This age is fluent at accumulation. It is illiterate at gratitude. The verse's remedy is old. Name the favour. Name the favourer. Let the honour order the life that follows.

**THE ORDER OF THE MEMORY**

The imperative is the whole engine of the verse. Remember. A favour delivered is not automatically a favour held. Memory here is an act of maintenance. The maintenance is what keeps the honour from becoming a museum piece. What follows is that the children of the favour are addressed, not the makers of it. The generation being called to remember inherited the crossing without having carried it. The report from Ibn ʿAbbās marks where the honour held and where it broke. The two points share a hinge. Remembering on one side. A calf on the other. The difference is stark and it is the verse's own. To remember the favours is to stand where the honour puts you. To forget them is to stand in the crowd that asks for a calf. The honour sits above the others because the measure of the honour is the measure of the responsibility on top of it. That responsibility never localises. Neither does the obligation. The memory is the favour's last form. It is also the only form the reader can still refuse. The command has a target and the target is a memory. Not a monument. Not a festival. A memory that reorders the week. The honour above the others was never a decoration to display. It was a duty to carry. The carrying looks like obedience. It looks like gratitude that changes behaviour. The family of the favour learned both sides of this. The remembering held them. Then the forgetting undid them. One calf is enough to tell the whole story. For the reader now the same story has a quiet form. A favour named is a favour kept. A favour unnamed is a favour already leaving. The verse puts the naming in the imperative voice because the leaving is so slow no one feels it go. Remember. The word is small. The work of it fills a life. The honour stands above the others so that the responsibility can be seen from a distance. The distance is exactly where the forgetting begins.
