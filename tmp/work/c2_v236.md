**A MARRIAGE MAY END BEFORE CONSUMMATION**

***“There is no blame if you divorce women before the marriage”*** establishes a marriage may end before consummation. This verse addresses a marriage that ends before sexual consummation and before a specific mahr, or bridal gift, has been fixed. It acknowledges that a marriage contract can fail early and removes the assumption that divorce itself is always a sin. At the same time, it commands the husband to provide a suitable gift—mutʿah—so that the woman is not left humiliated or unsupported after the relationship ends. The verse’s compassion matters because an early divorce can still cause emotional pain, social embarrassment, and financial loss. A woman should not be treated as though nothing happened merely because the marriage was not consummated. The husband owes honourable conduct even when a specific dowry is not legally due in the same way as after consummation. Mutʿah here means a compensatory provision appropriate to the husband’s means and the recognized standard of fairness. It can include money, clothing, household items, or another useful gift. The amount should not be insulting or so extravagant that it creates a new injustice. The wealthy give according to their abundance, while the poor give according to what they can genuinely afford. The verse rejects a single fixed amount that ignores circumstances. Wealth, local custom, the length of the engagement or marriage, the woman’s needs, and the reason for separation may all be relevant. The gift is not a purchase of the woman’s silence or a payment that permits cruelty. (2:237 — **“And if you divorce them before consummating the marriage but after deciding on a dowry,”**) Because a marriage may end before consummation frames the mutʿah gift, the mutʿah gift returns attention to a marriage may end before consummation. Ibn ʿAbbās treats the parting gift as a real provision scaled to means when neither consummation nor a fixed dowry preceded divorce.

**THE MUTʿAH GIFT**

***“is consummated or the dowry is settled. But give them a”*** deepens the mutʿah gift. It is an expression of responsibility and good treatment. There is no blame upon you removes blame for the divorce under the specified conditions, not for lying, insult, coercion, or broken promises. The couple must still preserve privacy, return property, and avoid spreading accusations. A person cannot say that no dowry was fixed and therefore nothing at all is owed in kindness. Likewise, the wife should not be treated as morally defective because the marriage ended early. Families and communities must resist gossip and protect her dignity. A broken marriage is not proof that one person carries every fault. The Qur’an uses before you have touched them—mā lam tamassūhunna—as a modest expression for consummation. If a marriage ends before consummation and no dowry was fixed, the mutʿah is required or strongly emphasized according to the legal details, while the moral command of kindness is clear. If a specified dowry was fixed, 2:237 gives the rule of half the dowry, subject to waiver and the circumstances described there. The waiting period also differs. A woman divorced before consummation does not observe the ordinary waiting period, as stated in 33:49, though legal advice is needed when pregnancy or other complications are possible. The difference between the wealthy and poor gives a general principle of family law: obligations are measured by ability and fairness. (33:49 — **“O believers! If you marry believing women and then divorce them before you touch them,”**) Since no blame does not mean no ethics follows a marriage may end before consummation, the mutʿah gift links purpose to no blame does not mean no ethics.

**NO BLAME DOES NOT MEAN NO ETHICS**

***“˹suitable˺ compensation—the rich according to his means and the poor according”*** clarifies no blame does not mean no ethics. Islam does not demand that a poor man give what would destroy his ability to eat or support dependants, but it does not permit the wealthy to hide behind a token gift while spending freely elsewhere. The command also encourages documentation. A couple should clarify the dowry and financial terms at the beginning of marriage, while understanding that unclear or missing documentation does not erase Allah’s rights. Transparent agreements prevent disputes and protect both parties. The final phrase calls the provision a duty upon the muḥsinīn, those who do good. Even where the minimum legal obligation is debated, iḥsān calls for generosity, apology, and a dignified conclusion. A husband may provide more than the minimum because he wants to close the relationship without bitterness. The Prophet ﷺ taught that no believer should despise a believing woman because of one disliked quality, and he encouraged kindness in family relationships. When divorce is necessary, the departing spouses should remember the good that existed and avoid rewriting the entire marriage as evil. The verse teaches that ending a contract does not erase the moral relationship formed through it. Employers owe wages after employment ends, business partners settle accounts, and spouses retain obligations after divorce. Lawfulness is the floor; iḥsān is the higher standard that turns a painful conclusion into a just one. (2:228 — **“Divorced women must wait three monthly cycles ˹before they can re-marry˺. **DIVORCE BEFORE TOUCHING**

***“to his. A reasonable compensation is an obligation on the good-doers”*** closes with divorce before touching. A community can help by providing mediation, legal advice, financial support, and protection from stigma. The woman’s future should not be destroyed by an early divorce. The case is like two families who prepare a wedding and then call it off a week before, and one of them returns every plate it borrowed. The plates go back clean, the guests go home, and the two families still nod to each other across the market for the next twenty years. A marriage may end before consummation reaches a reader today who tells himself that a marriage which never happened leaves nothing behind it and nobody owed. The verse gives him a compensation, a measure and a duty, and a marriage may end before consummation asks what he wants to be known as in a street that watched the engagement. Because the closing sentence names the good-doers, the mutʿah gift makes a marriage may end before consummation show that the manner of a parting is the part of the story that is remembered.
