**THE PRAYER FOR THE CITY**

The verse moves from the House to the city that holds it. ***“And ˹remember˺ when Abraham said, “My Lord, make this city ˹of Mecca˺ secure”*** ***“and provide fruits to its people”*** ***“those among them who believe in Allah”*** ***“and the Last Day.” He answered, “As”***. The prayer is for the city in its double life. The settled and the passing through. In the record of Ibn ʿAbbās the prayer is answered in the city's own stones. The place was made a sanctuary in which no human blood is shed. No injustice is done to anyone. No prey is hunted. The provision arrived the same way. Itinerants came from as far as Syria into a valley that had held no vegetation or water. The reason the prayer starts with security is simple. The city's value is the safety of the lives inside it. The first gift Abraham asks for is not splendour. It is peace. The House was made a sanctuary before this prayer. The prayer asks the sanctuary to cover the households as well.

**AN ANSWER IN TWO HALVES**

The prayer's second limb is aimed. The fruits are explicitly for those who believe in the Last Day. The answer splits the crowd in one breath. ***“for those who disbelieve, I will let them enjoy themselves”*** ***“for a little while”***. The disbeliever's share is measured. The believer's share is not capped in the verse's own wording. In the gloss of Ibn ʿAbbās the provision has a double object. The city is to be fed in its earth and fed in its faith. The effect is an answer that refuses to bless a peace leaving the theology untouched. The city is secure. Its people are fed. The feeding is aimed at the believing. The same sentence that says yes to the city says a counted yes to the wrong belief's party. The difference between Abraham's two sentences is the whole theology of the passage. The first asks for the city's goods. The second asks for the city's hearts.

**THE RESPITE AND THE DESTINATION**

The verse closes the disbeliever's branch in two moves. Enjoyment first. Fire second. ***“then I will condemn them to the torment of the Fire. What an evil destination!””*** The parallel prayer of the patriarch states the city's security in its own shape (14:35 — **“˹Remember˺ when Abraham prayed, “My Lord! Make this city ˹of Mecca˺ secure,”**). The same prayer reaches past the city to the household's altars (14:35 — **“and keep me and my children away from the worship of idols”**). The respite is real. The destination is real. The two do not cancel each other. The closing line does the work of a compass. What an evil destination the fire is. How measured the enjoyment that precedes it. What follows is that the reader is asked to pray the way Abraham did. For the city's security. For the people's goods. For the belief that makes the goods a blessing. For the modern reader the prayer is a template this age keeps mislaying. It asks for safety and provision and faith in one breath. The destination closes the verse because the prayer was always about where the city is going. Even the old proverb about the fathers runs through the same theology. The sins belong to the sinners. The families are not charged with them. Inheritance sits outside the verse's claim. It speaks only of sins. The city receives security first and fruit second. The order matters. Lives before produce. Peace before plenty. The prayer then aims the fruit at the believing. The answer separates the branches without apology. The enjoyment of the disbeliever is timed. The fire after it is named. The destination does the accounting the respite delays. Abraham's prayer is therefore a double petition. Save the city. Save the hearts. The modern reader can pray the same petition over any town. The petition asks for streets without fear and tables without idols. The provision that arrives without belief is the respite's own category. It is measured. It runs out. The evil destination at the verse's end is the second half of the answer. It is not an afterthought. It is the horizon the provision sits inside. The prayer for the city is answered in full. The full answer includes the fire.
