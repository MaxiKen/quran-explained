Concerning obedience that reverses faith, Ibn ʿAbbās applies the warning to counsel that would return believers to their former disbelief and leave them losers in both worlds. Obedience is the danger named because retreat from faith often begins with accepting another party’s direction rather than openly renouncing belief; which means after a defeat, hostile counsel can sound practical: reduce commitment, return to former loyalties, and purchase safety through concession. The verse identifies the end hidden inside that first yielding; therefore a backward turn makes the believers losers even if it offers temporary relief. This does not forbid learning a useful skill or accepting sound advice from someone outside the faith; in other words the warning concerns obedience that carries the community back into disbelief. Its test is the content and direction of the counsel; hence in present pressure, approval may be offered on condition that a believer silence a duty or rename a clear wrong. The verse gives courage to refuse that bargain; this is why loss is not measured only by access, comfort, or reputation; the deepest loss is surrendering faith to preserve them. The command is plural because a series of individual concessions can become a communal reversal. Each person’s obedience affects more than a private conscience when it normalises the hostile demand for others. Guarding faith therefore includes examining the direction in which accepted counsel is slowly moving the whole community.
