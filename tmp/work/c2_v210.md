**WAITING FOR THE CLOUDS**

The waiting is questioned in phrases that move from the clouds to the return: ***“Are they waiting for Allah ˹Himself˺ to come to them in the shade of clouds, along with the angels?”***, ***“˹If He did˺, then the matter would be settled ˹at once˺.”***, ***“And to Allah ˹all˺ matters will be returned ˹for judgment˺”***. and the final phrase hands every matter back. The sequence answers the postponement of belief: the coming is described, the settlement is dated, and the ownership of the decision is named. The question is aimed at the people who will not enter until they see, and the answer gives them the seeing in advance. Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the waiting as the Meccans' and the coming as the Day's, arriving without modality in the shadows of the clouds.

**THE COMING THAT SETTLES**

The question aims at the postponers: what do they wait for but the coming itself? The coming is described in the verse with clouds and angels, and the description answers the demand for a spectacle with the spectacle already scheduled. The account that Ibn ʿAbbās transmitted identifies the waiting as the Meccans' and the coming as the Day's, arriving without modality in the shadows of the clouds. The settlement the coming brings is stated as a completed act — the matter would be decided — which removes the postponer's hope of further negotiation. The legislation of the bursting sky draws the same scene in the same materials (25:25 — **“˹Watch for˺ the Day the heavens will burst with clouds, and the angels will be sent down in successive ranks”**). Because the settlement is dated by the coming, the waiting is the only undated thing in the verse.

**A RETURN ALREADY SIGNED**

What follows from the imagery is a schedule: the arrival is certain and the hour is hidden, so readiness is the only rational position. Because the certainty sits beside the hiddenness, the waiting becomes an ethic rather than a pause. The army that waits on that ethic is the army the passage trains. The second movement of the verse answers the postponement with ownership: to Allah all matters are returned, and the return is not a possibility but a jurisdiction. The scale and the grip of the Day appear in the same legislation of the return, where the whole earth is held in one hand and the weighing is not mocked (39:67 — **“They have not shown Allah His proper reverence—when on the Day of Judgment the ˹whole˺ earth will be in His Grip, and”**). Because the matters return to the One who owns them, the negotiation the postponer stages is held in a room that already belongs to the judge. The application is to sign the return early, while the signature is still a choice.

**WHO DECIDES**

The close of the verse is the quietest and the most absolute: the decision belongs to Allah, and the return is His. The phrase answers every human courtroom's delay and every private postponement of belief in one clause. Because the matter is decided by the One who owns it, the postponer's real question is not whether the clouds will come but who owns the calendar. The verse leaves the reader standing in the jurisdiction it has just described. The waiting described at the close of the passage is described in the old reports as the waiting of the army at the horizon for the order that has already been written. The angels rowing their wings at the arrival are drawn with the patience of the marine, and the image keeps the arrival calm rather than theatrical. Because the coming is described as a command rather than as a body, the student of the grammar has room to think. The believers of the first hours waited for the decisive help at the trenches and at the valley, and their waiting was answered in the language of the skies. The one who doubts the coming should examine the schedule of the past: the flood, the wind, and the cry all arrived on the day appointed. The hour of the arrival is hidden, and the readiness for the arrival is commanded, and the two together are the whole etiquette of the waiting. The seeker of the victory waits in the market and at the desk and at the road, and the horizon of the waiting is one horizon. The horizon keeps its appointment with the army every dawn, and the army keeps its appointment with the order at every horizon.
