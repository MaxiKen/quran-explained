**A RHETORICAL QUESTION OF DELAY**

***“Are they waiting for Allah ˹Himself˺ to come to them in the shade”*** establishes a rhetorical question of delay. After warning believers not to slip once clear proofs have arrived, the Qur’an asks what the rejecters are waiting for. Do they require the final scene of judgement before they will acknowledge the truth. The question exposes a destructive habit: postponing faith until the unseen becomes visible and accountability is no longer avoidable. Revelation, conscience, creation, and the signs of history already provide evidence. Waiting for the Day when the matter is decided is not intellectual caution; it is an attempt to delay responsibility. Once the final realities are unveiled, belief will no longer be the voluntary response of an unseen test. The opportunity for repentance will have ended. The verse describes a scene of the Hereafter in which the angels appear and Allah’s command is manifested. The imagery conveys majesty, awe, and the approach of final judgement. The description addresses human understanding while the realities of the unseen exceed worldly perception. The expression Allah comes must be affirmed and understood in a manner befitting Allah, without likening Him to creation, imagining a bodily movement, or asking how. The mainstream Ahl al-Sunnah affirm what revelation affirms bilā kayf, without modality. One accepted reading understands the passage through the arrival of Allah’s command and judgement, while another affirms the revealed wording without assigning a modality. Both reject every physical comparison with creation. The verse is about the certainty and majesty of divine judgement, not an invitation to create an image of Allah. (2:3 — **“who believe in the unseen, establish prayer, and donate from what We have provided for them”**) Because a rhetorical question of delay frames the canopies of clouds and the angels, the canopies of clouds and the angels returns attention to a rhetorical question of delay.

**THE CANOPIES OF CLOUDS AND THE ANGELS**

***“of clouds, along with the angels? ˹If He did˺, then the matter would be”*** deepens the canopies of clouds and the angels. For the matter to be decided means that judgement is issued and the truth becomes manifest. The disputing parties will no longer be able to hide behind slogans, inherited identity, or the authority of leaders. Allah’s decision separates truth from falsehood, the sincere from the hypocritical, and the oppressed from the oppressor. The final decision also gives moral meaning to present patience. People may suffer while corruption appears to triumph, but no matter is lost. Allah will bring every hidden deed to light and restore rights. Those who delay repentance should remember that the final decision is not merely a theological event; it is the moment when every choice receives its true consequence. To Allah all matters return—turjaʿ al-umūr—means that all affairs ultimately belong to His authority and judgement. People may manage property, institutions, governments, and disputes, but no created power is independent. The phrase invites reliance and accountability at once. The believer entrusts outcomes to Allah while fulfilling his duty; the oppressor should fear that every abuse returns to the One who sees it. The return also corrects worldly fragmentation. Human beings divide life into religion, politics, economics, and private desire as though Allah governed only one part. The Qur’an teaches that every matter returns to Him. Worship, law, family, wealth, and public conduct remain under His knowledge and moral judgement. A person who says, I will believe when I see the angels or the punishment, misunderstands the test. (2:208 — **“O believers! Enter into Islam wholeheartedly and do not follow Satan’s footsteps.”**) Since the final decision follows a rhetorical question of delay, the canopies of clouds and the angels links purpose to the final decision.

**THE FINAL DECISION**

***“settled ˹at once˺. And to Allah ˹all˺ matters will be returned ˹for judgment˺”*** clarifies the final decision. Faith concerns the unseen supported by signs and revelation. Seeing the final reality after death does not earn the same faith as trusting Allah before the veil is lifted. This does not require irrational belief. Evidence, reflection, and honest inquiry support rational belief. It warns only against demanding a type of proof whose arrival would erase the opportunity to choose. The wise person responds to guidance while the door is open. The verse contains realities that human beings cannot fully picture. Muslims affirm revelation, avoid denial, and avoid speculative detail. They do not describe Allah as a body, place Him within creation, or claim knowledge of the unseen beyond what He revealed. At the same time, they do not dismiss the verse simply because its reality exceeds ordinary experience. This humility is a general rule in studying the unseen: affirm what Allah and His Messenger ﷺ taught, deny likeness to creation, and leave the modality to Allah. The purpose of the verse is guidance and warning, not philosophical imagery. Think of a rhetorical question of delay through a passenger who refuses to board until the captain comes to his house to explain the route. The ship leaves at the hour printed on the ticket, and the man standing on the quay is the only one who thinks the schedule was changed. A rhetorical question of delay reaches a reader today who says that he would believe if a sign were shown to him personally, as though the creation were a shop with a counter. (6:158 — **“Are they awaiting the coming of the angels, or your Lord ˹Himself˺,”**) All matters return to allah matters, which means the final decision returns all matters return to allah to a rhetorical question of delay.

**ALL MATTERS RETURN TO ALLAH**

The verse gives him a question about the clouds and the angels and asks him what kind of proof he is actually waiting for. Because the answer is the day when the matter is settled, a rhetorical question of delay shows that the request for a private sign is a request to delay. Because the sentence names the shade of clouds and the angels, a rhetorical question of delay shows that the scene is the end of the world rather than a sign in the market. The people who asked for a proof are offered the last event of the creation, and the offer is a rebuke rather than an answer. The Book sets the same scene in the passage of the shaking, where the help of God is named near, and the reader sees the two answers of the waiting. A further verse keeps the return of matters to the Lord alone, so that no reader imagines the case closed in this world. The canopies of clouds and the angels makes a rhetorical question of delay practical; the final decision therefore matters.
