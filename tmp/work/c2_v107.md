**THE KINGDOM UNDER THE CHANGES**

The passage closes the abrogation question with a survey of ownership: ***“Do you not know”*** ***“that the kingdom of the heavens and the earth belongs ˹only˺ to Allah,”***. The placement is exact. The previous verse answered the taunt about changing pages. This verse answers the question of whose pages they are. Ibn ʿAbbās reads the kingdom here as the stores of the heavens and the earth. The one who holds the stores gives the orders, and the orders follow knowledge. The same gloss puts the reasoning in a single line: He commands His servants with whatever He wills because He knows better what is good for them. The ownership of the schedule therefore rests on the knowledge of the goods. A rule that moves and a rule that wanders part company at the source. The rule that moves is directed from the store. The rule that wanders is directed from the wind.

**NO FRIEND, NO HELPER**

The verse then draws the consequence for the objectors in terms of standing: ***“ and you have no guardian or helper besides Allah”***. Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss gives each missing figure its face. The friend is anyone close enough to benefit or protect. The helper is anyone able to prevent the punishment when it comes. The received gloss frames the two as the safeguard and the shield, the one who keeps the chastisement away when it arrives. The objectors of the passage are therefore short two things at once. They have no title to the schedule they are mocking, and they have no counsel inside the ownership they are addressing. The indictment of the covenant pattern fits them without strain: (2:27 — **“those who violate Allah’s covenant after it has been affirmed, break whatever ˹ties˺ Allah has ordered to be maintained,”**). The breakers of the ties stand outside the ties. The verse simply reads their standing back to them. The reading is not cruel. It is the accurate entry of the parties in the register of the case. No protector steps forward on their side, because the side has no title to present.

**THE PART OF THE TAUNT THE VERSE DOES NOT ANSWER**

Notice what the verse leaves alone. It does not defend the specific changes one by one. It settles the ownership and lets the changes stand as the owner's work. The received account of the taunt explains why this is enough. The doubt being sown was that the changes were made by the man rather than by his lord. Once the kingdom is located, that doubt loses its floor. The pattern of the answer is the pattern used against every slander in this passage. Locate the authority, and the slander runs out of standing. The east and west of the whole question were settled in the answer to the foolish of the people: (2:142 — **“˹O Prophet,˺ “The east and west belong ˹only˺ to Allah. He guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path.””**). The question of direction is owned. The question of timing is owned. The ownership is one.

**THE READER WITH A REVISED MAP**

For the modern reader, the section at hand is the answer to every charge that a divinely ordered book must never move. The movement is not a crack in the authority. The movement is a feature of the authority of the one who schedules the lessons. The Kaʿbah turn, the lifted rulings, the replaced verses — all of them are pages being handled by the page-holder. The reader's place in the scene is therefore not the critic of the schedule. The reader's place is the student whose protection clause has already been written, the soul not required to carry more than it can afford. The verse ends the unit with the posture it wants. Look at the kingdom first. The details of the schedule follow from the ownership, and the ownership is not in dispute at home or at work or in the heavens above them. The survey closes the question the mockery opened, and it closes the question by raising the eyes from the page to the one who sends it. The point of the close is worth one more sentence. A kingdom with one owner has one policy, and the policy includes the schedule of every page the kingdom sends.
