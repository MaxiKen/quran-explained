**DECISION RESERVED FOR ALLAH**

***“You ˹O Prophet˺ have no say in the matter.”*** You ˹O Prophet˺ have no say in the matter delivers the hardest sentence in the middle of the Uhud account. Addressed to the community's most beloved. And its occasion is fixed in the Sahih. At Uhud the Prophet was wounded. His tooth broken, his face gashed, the blood running as he wiped it, saying. How can a people prosper who wound their prophet's face while he calls them to their Lord. And the verse descended. The transmitted reports adding his season of qunut-supplication against the day's named persecutors. Safwan, Suhail, al-Harith by name. Until this verse arrested the naming. Laysa laka mina l-amri shay. Not for you is any of the matter. The amr. The file of hearts and endings. Whose repentance will be received, whose punishment sealed. Withdrawn, in one clause, from even the Messenger's hands. The day's wounded imprecations answered not with endorsement but with the office's boundary re-drawn. (3:127 — **“to destroy a group of the disbelievers and humble the rest,”**)

**REPENTANCE OR PUNISHMENT**

***“It is up to Allah to turn to them in”*** It is up to Allah to turn to them in mercy or punish them, for indeed they are wrongdoers states the withdrawn file's true Desk. And the two options' order is the verse's buried mercy. Aw yatuba alayhim. Or He may relent toward them. The tawbah-option named first, over the very men of the wound. The verse, at the Prophet's bleeding, holding open the repentance-door for his woundersand history later demonstrated the clause through several striking reversals. The named of the qunut-season and the day's fiercest. Khalid ibn al-Walid who turned the hill's flank, Ikrimah son of Abu Jahl, Safwan and Suhail themselves. Entered Islam within the decade and served it to their deaths. The verse's first function thus revealed as protective. Had the imprecations sealed them, the community would have cursed away its future commanders. The amr belonged to the Knower of endings precisely because only He read Khalid inside the enemy cavalry. For indeed they are wrongdoers closing the clause with the present classification (the zulm real, the sentence live. The options are mercy or punishment, not mercy or acquittal). The file open, not empty. (3:129 — **“To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens”**) Repentance or punishment guides conduct today because religious leaders may teach, pray, and judge outward conduct, but they do not own another person’s final sentence.

**WRONGDOING WITHOUT ENTITLEMENT**

***“mercy or punish them, for indeed they are wrongdoers”*** The verse's permanent jurisprudence, is the community's inherited boundary. If the amr was not the Messenger's, it is no preacher's, no wounded party's, no era's zealots'. The sentencing of named souls. This one damned, that one beyond return. Stands withdrawn from every tongue. The believer's licensed registers being the deed's condemnation, the general warning, and the wounded prayer for guidance modelled in prophetic conduct after this instruction. The desire for punishment gives way to intercession for people who do not yet know. And the verse's tenderness beneath its severity completes the passage's reading. The sentence that seems to diminish the Prophet in fact unburdens him. The day's blood answered, the file lifted from his wounded shoulders to the Desk of the two beautiful options, and the names of forgiveness and mercy standing one verse ahead as the estate-clause's seal. Imagine a physician presenting the diagnosis while the final authorization belongs to the court. On the final decision is not the messenger’s, Ibn ʿAbbās denies human ownership of repentance and punishment and leaves both outcomes to Allah’s choice regarding those who did wrong. The address separates prophetic duty from divine judgment; which means the Messenger conveys, leads, prays, and bears injury, but repentance and punishment are not his possessions. If this boundary applies to him, no later believer may claim control over another person’s final fate; therefore the wrongdoers remain truly wrong; the possibility of mercy does not rename their deed. At the same time, their wrongdoing does not close a door that Allah may open through repentance; in other words this gives the community a firm and balanced posture. It may oppose harm, enforce a lawful boundary, and describe injustice plainly, yet it must not turn anger into a decree about the unseen end of a person; hence present disputes often push people toward permanent labels after a grave offence. The verse leaves room for accountable change without demanding trust before change appears; this is why judgment belongs to Allah, while human responsibility remains truthful action within the limits He has set.
