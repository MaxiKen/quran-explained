**THE ORIGIN WITH NO PRECEDENT**

The answer to the son-claim then moves from the estate to the act of originating: ***“˹He is˺ the Originator of the heavens and the earth! ”***. The tradition from Ibn ʿAbbās gives the originating its plainest description. He originated them when they were nothing before. The received tradition adds the precision of the Arabic. The making here is a making without any exemplary precedent. Nothing stood before the heavens and the earth to copy. Nothing stood beside them to assist. The claim of the previous verse imagined a household with an heir. The originating answers the household with the workshop that has no family business. The estate came into the register by being spoken into it. The reason the decree is narrated in one word is that the workshop needs no apparatus, which means the son-claim is answered by the absence of a family business.

**THE DECREE OF TWO SYLLABLES**

The verse then narrates the method of the workshop: ***“ When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, “Be!” And it is”***. Ibn ʿAbbās turns the illustration to the exact case the son-claim had been built on. When he wants to create a child without a father, like the Messiah, he says to it only: be. The parallel in the tradition is older than the Messiah and larger than him. Adam was created without a father or a mother. The claim had treated a birth without a father as the proof of a divine sonship. The verse treats it as a Tuesday in the workshop. The decree of the two syllables is documented at the level of the whole creation in the verse of the command: (36:82 — **“All it takes, when He wills something ˹to be˺, is simply to say to it: “Be!” And it is”**). The same power to bring back what has gone is recorded in the passage of the restoration: (30:50 — **“Surely That ˹same God˺ can raise the dead. For He is Most Capable of everything”**). The operating word of the workshop is therefore one word, used at the beginning and at the return.

**THE VARIANT THAT WITNESSES THE GRAMMAR**

The tradition of the glosses preserves a small reading-variant at the verse's close, and the detail is worth its own sentence in the record. The responding verb is read in two ways, one settling into the indicative and one hanging in the subjunctive after the command. The variant is the kind of detail the scholars of the reading have always kept, and its presence in the record is itself instructive. The workshop's speech is so exact that even its grammatical shadows are inventoried. The variant does not change the doctrine. It deepens the impression of the precision. The point of the variant is the precision of the record itself. A claim that would rearrange the estate deserves an answer given in the grammar of the estate.

**THREE REASONS THE CLAIM COLLAPSES**

The unit's answer to the son-claim now stands on three legs, and the passage has hammered each one. What follows from the three legs is the collapse of the whole anatomy of the claim. First the estate. All that is in the heavens and the earth serves the owner. Second the method. The creating is done by decree, without household, without precedent. Third the exclamation. The glory is the distance the claim cannot cross. The tradition of the passage adds the rational frame to the three. The assertions are absurd on their own ground before the revelation ever speaks. For the reader of the bookshelf where the three claims still circulate, the verse is the workshop tour. Ask how the estate is owned. Ask how the matter is decreed. The claim answers its own question when the two answers stand side by side. The owner of the decree has no need of an heir to keep the shop. The shop keeps itself by the word that keeps everything. The tour of the bench is therefore the answer the passage keeps in circulation. The estate register stands at the door. The decree stands at the bench. The exclamation stands at the roof. The three together are the whole answer to the three mouths. A doctrine that would add a fourth station to the workshop must first produce that station in the register. The register is closed. The word remains. The workshop answer stands at that closed door with its three stations staffed. Nothing in the claim of the three mouths survives the tour. The tour is short on purpose. The doctrine is one question deep. The benches were never crowded. The word was always enough.
