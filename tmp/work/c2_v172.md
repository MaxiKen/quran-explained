**THE TABLE AND ITS GIVER**

***“O believers! Eat from the good things We have provided”*** addresses the community of faith with the same permission given to all humanity in the previous passage, and the difference is the addition that follows. The verse continues with ***“for you. And give thanks to Allah”***, then closes the loop with ***“if you ˹truly˺ worship Him ˹alone˺”***. The meal is not a distraction from worship but one of its instruments, and the only proper use of provision is gratitude addressed to its Giver. The placement of the condition at the verse's close seals the teaching: worship is not a mood felt at prayer but a posture kept at the table. Because the address is to the believers, the permission becomes a discipline of recognition rather than a licence of appetite. The bread and the thanks belong to one act. Ibn ʿAbbās reports that worship itself entails declaring lawful what He has declared lawful, which places the table inside the creed.

**WHY THANKS FOLLOW THE MEAL**

The thanks are for His making the good lawful to you, and the condition — if it is Him you worship — ties the table to the altar. The verse repairs a piety that treated food as spiritual danger, replacing fear with gratitude and mere abstention with thanksgiving. Ibn ʿAbbās reports the sharper edge of the condition: if people intend to worship Allah alone by declaring these things unlawful, they have inverted the duty. The reason gratitude is commanded rather than suggested is that provision without thanks is consumption without recognition, and recognition is the whole difference between an animal at a trough and a servant at a gift. Gratitude is commanded at the point of eating rather than left to mood. The permission is placed beside the command of thanks so that no meal is too small to carry the whole relationship. (23:51 — **“O messengers! Eat from what is good and lawful, and act righteously. Indeed, I fully know what you do”**)

**THE SMALLEST WORSHIP**

A hearer may ask whether thankfulness at every meal is not a small thing to be commanded in revelation. The size of an act is measured by what it reveals rather than by what it costs, because gratitude is the recognition of dependence, and dependence is the fact the whole passage has been establishing. A small act performed with understanding stands at the centre of the creed; a large act performed as display can stand outside it entirely. A receipt read is a relationship; a receipt ignored is consumption wearing the same letter. Because the command sits beside the permission of food, every meal is designed as a rehearsal of the reader's actual position in the world. The rehearsal is small precisely so that it can be repeated all day. Eating is placed inside worship and therefore inside its discipline; the table is consecrated rather than abandoned.

**AT EVERY MEAL**

The consequence of ingratitude is named in (31:12 — **“And whoever is ungrateful, then surely Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy.””**). In Ibn ʿAbbās's transmitted account, the worshipper who declares God's gifts unlawful misplaces the very worship he means to perform. Stated simply, the verse teaches the eater to see the hand along with the bread. The application is one act repeated daily: eat what is good with a named thanks, because the table is where dependence is most often forgotten and most easily remembered. The permission stands as a gift, and the gratitude stands as its answer. The verse makes the ordinary act of swallowing bread into an act of recognition, and the recognition is the worship the condition describes. Because the thanks are owed at every meal, the servant is never far from the liturgy of dependence; the day is punctuated by chances to practise the truth of one's position. Both together are what worshipping Him alone looks like at a meal, day after day. The address to the believers marks the difference: the permission is the same as humanity's, but the gratitude makes the distinction. The believers eat the same bread as everyone else and answer it differently, which is the whole of the verse's distinction. Because the thanks are attached to the eating, the difference is practised several times a day.
