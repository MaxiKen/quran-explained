**THE TOWN THAT WORKED THE SABBATH**

The next failure is presented as something the reader already knows about: ***“You are already aware of those of you”***, ***“who broke the Sabbath. We said to them, “Be disgraced apes!””*** The phrase assumes knowledge, and the rest of the Book supplies it. A coastal settlement was tested with a day of rest, the fish came to the surface on the day they were forbidden to fish, and they went after them anyway. The account is given in another sūrah with the scene laid out: (7:163 — **“Ask them ˹O Prophet˺ about ˹the people of˺ the town”**). What the Book reports here is the outcome rather than the method, and the outcome is not a fine or a delay. It is a change of what the offenders were.

**A PUNISHMENT THAT IS REPORTED AS A CHANGE OF NATURE**

The words used are the strongest in the passage, and the Book repeats them in a second place where the sequence is clearer: (7:166 — **“But when they stubbornly persisted in violation, We said to them, “Be disgraced apes!””**). The repetition matters because it removes the possibility that the sentence was a figure of speech used once for effect. The same fate is recalled again in a later sūrah among the people who earned a punishment worse than the ones they mocked: (5:60 — **“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Shall I inform you of those”**). The early teachers differed over whether the transformation was physical or whether it described a moral descent, and the difference is a real one; what no reader of the verse can miss is that the Book chose a form of punishment that took away the offenders' standing as people among whom a conversation could be held.

**EVADING THE LETTER AND BREAKING THE PURPOSE**

The town's failure is worth examining because it was not a simple refusal. They had found a way to keep the form of the Sabbath while taking its fruit, and the Book's naming of the day as broken rather than ignored is precise. A later chapter describes the mechanics with the fish appearing on the forbidden day and vanishing on the permitted one, which shows a test designed to expose what was already in them. Nothing in the account suggests that they doubted the command; they arranged their lives around it in order to defeat it. The same instinct appears in this sūrah among people who alter words they were told to say, and it appears among the traders of any century who keep a rule's wording while dismantling its purpose. The verse therefore belongs beside the account of the changed words, since both are stories about obedience that has been hollowed out from the inside.

**ALREADY KNOWN BEFORE IT IS TOLD**

The opening clause tells the reader something about how this book teaches, since it treats the incident as established before it is mentioned. That is the way a community's own history works: it is carried in the memory of the people and referred to rather than introduced. The Qur'an uses the same method with the events of Badr and the accounts of earlier prophets, and it does so because the point of the retelling is never the news. What is new in this verse is the sentence that was spoken, and the sentence is quoted so that the reader hears the verdict in its own words. A reader who has been given the story elsewhere is expected to bring it with him, and the Book trusts him to do so.

**THE COST OF A CLEVER EVASION**

The lasting value of the passage for a reader is that it shows where a clever evasion ends if it is left to run. The people in the town did not hold a debate about the Sabbath; they went fishing on the day the fishing was forbidden, and they would have told anyone who asked that the sea was theirs. A small community with a small rule broke it in a small way, and the punishment removed them from the human conversation altogether. That is why the next verse says their fate was made an example, and why the Book puts the story in front of a community that has been given a much larger trust. The reader who wants to test himself on it can begin with the rules he has quietly learned to satisfy in form while ignoring in substance, since that is the list the people of the coast were working from when the fish appeared on the wrong day of the week.
