**UNTIL THE PRESSURE ENDS**

The phrases of the command run from the persecution to the aggressor in order: ***“Fight against them ˹if they persecute you˺”***, ***“until there is no more persecution”***, ***“and ˹your˺ devotion will be to Allah ˹alone˺.”***, ***“If they stop ˹persecuting you˺, let there be no hostility except against the aggressors”***. The walk keeps the destination in view while the exception is stated, so the aggression is answered and the war is never left without its stopping rule. The phrases are drawn tightly enough to hold in memory when the road gets loud. Ibn ʿAbbās's transmission keeps the condition in view throughout the sequence. The war is dated by its purpose because the purpose is the only element of the command that can outlast the campaign and judge it afterwards.

Fight against them ˹if they persecute you˺ until there is no more persecution sets a destination rather than describing a pleasure. The end of the fighting is written into the fighting's purpose: the command runs until the persecution stops, which means the war is a means with an expiry date built in. The second phrase supplies the definition of the peace that follows — and ˹your˺ devotion will be to Allah ˹alone˺. — so the destination is a state of worship rather than a map of territory. The fight is therefore aimed at a condition of the soul: the persecution that blocks devotion must end so the devotion can stand. The verse's logic answers those who read the passage as an open license, since the license expires at the moment the persecution expires. The pressure is the measure of the command.

**DEVOTION AS THE DESTINATION**

The verse's goal — devotion to Allah alone — is the same goal the whole chapter has been building since its creed. The persecution named here is the pressure that prevents the devotion, and the relief of the pressure is the purpose of the fighting. The later legislation on guardianship in the community states the alternative in social terms: if the believers do not hold together, great oppression and corruption spread in the land (8:73 — **“As for the disbelievers, they are guardians of one another. And unless you ˹believers˺ act likewise, there will be great oppression”**). Ibn ʿAbbās's transmission keeps the condition in view throughout: the fighting answers the persecution and stops at its end. Because devotion is the destination, the army is a construction crew as much as a defense force, and the building is the freedom to worship. The one who fights for any other destination has wandered outside the verse's sentence.

**NO HOSTILITY AFTER VICTORY**

The condition of the peace is stated with the same precision as the condition of the war: If they stop ˹persecuting you˺, let there be no hostility except against the aggressors. The hostility is cut off at the moment the persecution is cut off, and the remainder is reserved for those who continue to aggress. The arrangement protects the returning party and disciplines the victorious one, which is the double duty of a just ceasefire. Because the exception names the aggressors, the hostility cannot be generalized to their families, their cities, or their posterity. The application is to date the end of the enmity by the end of the persecution and not by the mood of the crowd. The verse's army knows when to stop because the stopping date is written inside the purpose clause. The date of the ceasefire is written into the purpose clause, which is the most civilian thing about this military law. The one who keeps the purpose in view keeps the calendar in view too. The verse is also a standing correction to every army that forgets its purpose clause in the heat of its success.

**THE AGGRESSOR EXCLUDED**

The final clause is the ceasefire's police force: the aggression that continues after the peace is answered within the law, and the answer is limited to the aggressor. The crying of the oppressed elsewhere in the legislation is what the whole system exists to end — the children who ask to be delivered from the land of oppressors have their answer in this verse's freedom (4:75 — **“And what is it with you? You do not fight in the cause of Allah and for oppressed men, women,”**). Because the oppression of the vulnerable is the persecution the war targets, the peace that follows must not contain a new oppression. The application is to hold both clauses with one hand: to stop when the pressure stops, and to pursue the aggressor who refuses to stop. The verse keeps its eyes open until the road is safe. The safety is the point of every line of the passage: the persecuted freed, the devotion restored, and the road watched. Because the three goods are named in order, the commander can audit the campaign against the verse rather than against the momentum. The application is to write the purpose clause at the top of every decision made under pressure. The purpose clause is the verse's gift to the commander and the citizen alike, and it outlasts the campaign it governs. Because the pressure ends at a stated line, the decision made under it can be audited after the road is safe.
