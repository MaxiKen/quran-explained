**RANKS BEFORE ALLAH**

***“They ˹each˺ have varying degrees in”*** They ˹each˺ have varying degrees in the sight of Allah completes the sorting-question's census at its full resolution. The final word establishes the principle of differing ranks. Hum darajatun inda llah. They are degrees in Allah's sight. Not merely holders of degrees but. The grammar's force. Degrees themselves. The persons graded through and through. The two columns of the surrounding passage (the Pleasure's pursuers, the wrath's returners) each opening, at this verse, into its ascending and descending series. The believers ranked past one another and the wrath's column likewise stratified. The census's resolution being the passage's justice perfected. The Uhud-file's one army contained not two blocks but a whole spectrum. The twelve at the call, the pardoned runners (155), the drowsy sincere (154), the whisperers, the skimmers' temptations (161). And the verse files every man of it at his exact degree. No rank flattened into another's, the day's every conduct-difference preserved in the grading. (3:162 — **“Hell is their home. What an evil destination”**)

**DEEDS FULLY SEEN**

***“the sight of Allah. And Allah”*** The seal declares that every deed lies within Allah’s sight. It closes the census with the grading's instrument. Wallahu basirun bima ya'malun. And Allah is Seeing of what they do. And the seal's selection is the resolution's guarantee. Degrees of such fineness require the Basir's sight. The conduct seen entire. The deed, its manner, its motive, its cost. The community thus taught its two standing postures before the grading. Rest, for the under-credited (the unwitnessed stand at the pass, the swallowed panic, the quiet re-forming. All seen, all graded. No man's degree lost to the record's human gaps), and sobriety, for the over-credited (the uniform's prestige, the assembly's applause grading nothing. The Seer's file the only transcript). (3:164 — **“to them His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and”**) Deeds fully seen guides conduct today because equal visibility in this world does not mean equal rank before Allah, who sees the intention and consequence of every deed.

**DIFFERENT ENDS FOR DIFFERENT CHOICES**

***“is All-Seeing of what they do”*** The verse's permanent theology, is the Book's whole rank-doctrine compressed. Existence under this Lord is graded. The flattening ideologies refused in both directions. No egalitarianism of outcomes that erases the strivers' purchased degrees, and no aristocracy of positions that fixes the grades by birth or badge (the degrees indexed to what they do, never to what they are called. The label-jurisprudence's final form). The believer formed by the census carrying its double motion. The eyes lifted at the ranks above and the ladder's every rung priced at conduct. The next degree purchasable this afternoon, by the deed the Basir is watching now. While the passage turns to the community's whole ladder's base. The favour that set every rung. Allah has done the believers a great favour by raising a messenger from among them. Imagine an elevator displaying different floors even though every passenger entered through one lobby. On ranks before allah, Ibn ʿAbbās explains different ranks in Paradise and levels in Hell according to deceit and every other deed that Allah sees. Degrees show that judgment is more exact than a simple division between two crowds; which means people differ in intention, sacrifice, knowledge, and persistence, and Allah sees the full shape of every deed. Equal public visibility therefore does not imply equal rank; therefore a quiet act may rise because it was sincere and costly, while a celebrated act may fall because its purpose was corrupt. The same precision applies downward; wrongdoing has degrees and is not flattened into one human estimate; in other words this encourages effort without granting permission to rank souls by appearance. People see fragments, but Allah is All-Seeing; hence in current life, metrics can count output while missing motive, restraint, and hidden service. The verse frees the believer from living for those incomplete measures; this is why work should be done well and openly accounted for where duty requires, yet its deepest value is left to the sight that loses no detail. Varying degrees also make comparison with another person unreliable. A believer does not know every burden the other carried or every opening the other received. The useful comparison is with one’s own duty: whether clearer knowledge produced better action and whether hidden opportunities were used sincerely. Allah alone assigns the degree.
