**INHERITED PRACTICE**

***“When it is said to them, “Follow what Allah has revealed,” they reply, “No!”*** stages the whole argument in one line: a revealed command is met with a refusal that offers no text in return. The substitute appears next: ***“We ˹only˺ follow what we found our forefathers practicing.” ˹Would they still do so,˺ even”***, and the verse cuts the defence at its root with ***“if their forefathers had ˹absolutely˺ no understanding or guidance”***. Inheritance of a practice proves nothing about its truth, which means the verse separates the two questions the reply had fused: how old a thing is, and how true it is. Ibn ʿAbbās reports the double question in three forms, all of them pressing on the fathers' lack of religion and guidance.

**AN ADMISSION OF POSITION**

The addressees kept the practices of the dedicated camel and the slit ear, dedicating animals to their gods by custom rather than by revelation. The reply "we follow our fathers" is therefore not neutral conservatism; it is the specific defence of a ritual economy. The verse asks whether the fathers possessed understanding or guidance at all, and the reason it presses on understanding rather than sincerity is that sincerity cannot license a claim about God. Only knowledge of what He revealed can do that. The previous verse named the enemy's forgery; this one shows the forgery installed as family property. The appeal to the fathers is also a confession of position, because a person who has evidence cites the evidence; the person who cites ancestry has admitted he has none to give. (7:28 — **“Whenever they commit a shameful deed, they say, “We found our forefathers doing it”**)

**LOVE VERSUS CERTIFICATION**

A hearer may ask whether respect for parents and forefairs does not require continuing their practices. Respect and verification are different obediences. The fathers are honoured as fathers, but a father's authority in the household is not a revelation in the religion, and the verse declines to print the one on the paper of the other. Keeping a parent's chair is love; keeping a parent's error is certification. Love continues; certification does not. The most respectful answer to the family tradition is to bring it to the revealed text and let the text decide, which is what the first line of the verse proposes and the refusal rejects. Because the verse asks about understanding and guidance rather than sincerity, the refuge of good intentions is removed from the question of doctrine. The family may be sincere and the practice still wrong, and the verse is precise enough to separate the two. Because the question is posed about understanding and guidance, the answer is checkable: either the fathers knew the revealed law or they did not, and the verse asserts the second. The appeal to ancestry therefore fails on its own evidence before any ruling is discussed. The point is that nobody in the chain could certify what no link possessed. The appeal to ancestry therefore cannot even be inherited in the form it is offered.

**THE TEXT AT THE DOOR**

The father of the prophets gives the model of testing inherited worship in (26:75 — **“Abraham responded, “Have you ˹really˺ considered what you have been worshipping—”**). What Ibn ʿAbbās reports is that the fathers were wholly unintelligent in religion and followed no prophet's practice, which empties the appeal of its content. The verse also grants the fathers no defence of its own, which is its most delicate courtesy: it does not insult the ancestors, it simply declines to certify them. The application is a disciplined one: keep every inheritance at the door of the revealed text long enough to be checked, because age measures survival and not soundness. The check does not dishonour the inheritance; it is how the inheritance earns its keep. Because the verse questions the fathers' knowledge rather than their worth, the reader is free to love his family and to correct their practice in the same act. The family album is precious; it is simply not scripture. Only what Allah has revealed can certify what the family practices. The verse leaves the family its honour and removes only its licence to legislate, which is the precise division the argument needed.
