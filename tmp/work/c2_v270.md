**NOTHING IS HIDDEN FROM ALLAH**

***“Whatever charities you give or vows you make are surely”*** establishes nothing is hidden from allah. This verse gathers charity and vows under Allah’s complete knowledge. He knows the amount spent, the source of the wealth, the recipient’s need, the intention of the giver, and the words and circumstances of every vow. A public donation and a private payment are equally visible to Him, as are promises made in a moment of fear or bargaining. The reminder protects both sincerity and justice. A person who gives quietly but receives no praise should know that Allah has recorded it. A person who displays generosity while concealing fraud should know that appearance cannot deceive Allah. The final account includes the inner motive and the outward deed. The preceding verses have described several qualities of charity: it must be lawful, sincere, beneficial, and free from reminders and injury. This verse confirms that every expenditure falls under divine knowledge. Spending on dependants, relatives, or the needy can be worship when done for Allah, while spending on vanity, oppression, or forbidden causes can become a source of sin. A believer should therefore review both the amount and the purpose. He pays obligatory maintenance and zakāh, settles debts, and gives voluntary charity according to ability. He does not neglect family while seeking public praise, and he does not use family responsibility as an excuse to refuse every claim of the poor. A nadhr is a vow by which a person binds himself to an act of worship or obedience. If the vow concerns a lawful act of devotion, fulfilling it is required according to the rules of fiqh. (76:7 — **“They ˹are those who˺ fulfil ˹their˺ vows and fear a Day of sweeping horror”**) A reading from Ibn ʿAbbās joins accepted spending and fulfilled vows to sincerity for Allah’s sake, whose knowledge includes both gift and intention.

**SPENDING AS WORSHIP**

***“known to Allah. And the wrongdoers will have no helpers”*** deepens spending as worship. The Qur’an praises those who fulfil their vows. If a vow concerns sin or an impossible act, it must not be fulfilled; the person repents and follows the relevant ruling on expiation. The Prophet ﷺ taught that vows do not bring forward or delay anything Allah has not decreed. A vow should therefore not be used as a bargain: If Allah gives me this, I will worship Him, as though the servant can compel divine action. Worship is owed to Allah before the requested outcome arrives. The Sunnah discourages making vows because people may bind themselves to a burden they cannot sustain, or they may fulfil it resentfully and then imagine they have purchased a favour. A person who wants to give charity should give without waiting to make a dramatic vow. Sincere voluntary action is better than turning worship into an emotional contract. If a vow has been made, the person should record it and learn its ruling. Families should not pressure someone into a vow or use a public promise to extract money. Allah knows whether the vow was deliberate, coerced, conditional, or intended as a sinful act. The wrongdoers—ẓālimūn—will have no helpers. A person who uses charity or vows to oppress people cannot rely on wealth, relatives, officials, or public reputation to save him before Allah. Created helpers may assist in the world, but no one can cancel divine accountability. The warning also applies to those who help wrongdoing: an executor who hides assets, an institution that launders corrupt money, or a group that uses a vow to manipulate the vulnerable. (5:2 — **“O believers! Do not violate Allah’s rituals ˹of pilgrimage˺, the sacred months, the sacrificial animals,”**)

**VOWS AND THEIR LIMITS**

Cooperation in righteousness is commanded; cooperation in sin is forbidden. If a person spent unlawfully or made a vow that harmed someone, repentance includes stopping the wrong, restoring property, fulfilling lawful rights, and seeking qualified guidance about expiation. A public vow that misled people may require a public clarification. Private regret alone is not enough when others were harmed. Allah’s knowledge is also a comfort to the sincere person who made a mistake without intending sin. He corrects the error and hopes in Allah’s mercy rather than remaining trapped by obsessive guilt. The believer gives because Allah deserves worship and because people have rights, not because he wants to control Allah or the recipient. He does not demand an immediate worldly return, though he may ask Allah for good in this life and the Hereafter. The good loan metaphor honours the giver, but Allah remains the Lord, not a debtor compelled by a transaction. Think of nothing is hidden from allah through a man who borrows a sum from a friend and then gives the friend a wide berth for years. The debt stands in the friend's book all along, and the man is never seen in the market without looking over his shoulder. Nothing is hidden from allah confronts spending as worship in someone seeking to forget a promise made to God during a difficult night. The verse gives him a register that holds both the gift and the vow, and it asks him who he thinks is keeping the second column.
