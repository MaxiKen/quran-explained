**A RUINED TOWN AND A QUESTION**

***“Or ˹are you not aware of˺ the one who passed by a city which was in ruins. He wondered, “How could Allah bring this back to life after its destruction?” So Allah caused him to die”*** establishes a ruined town and a question. The verse describes a person who passed a devastated town and wondered how Allah could restore it after its death. The Qur’an does not name the town or the person. Classical exegetes transmitted an identification with ʿUzayr and a particular Israelite town, but the revelation itself leaves the identity open. The lasting lesson is Allah’s power to restore life and the movement from uncertainty to certainty. The question need not have been arrogant denial. It can be a request to understand how resurrection occurs. Ibrāhīm similarly asked Allah to show him how He gives life to the dead, and Allah did not condemn him for wanting his heart reassured. The moral difference lies between sincere inquiry and stubborn rejection after truth is clear. Allah caused the person to die for a hundred years and then raised him. The event makes time itself part of the sign. A century passes, yet the person’s experience is like a day or part of a day. Human awareness of time is limited and subjective, while Allah controls life, death, memory, and perception. The miracle is not presented as a scientific model of every resurrection. It is a particular sign Allah gave to one person. It demonstrates that the One who created life can suspend and restore it, and that the long interval between death and resurrection is no obstacle to divine power. The food and drink remained unchanged while the person and his donkey underwent death and restoration. The contrast shows that Allah can preserve one thing, transform another, and reverse a third according to His will. Creation is not an independent system that limits its Creator. The verse does not invite superstition about preserving food or treating a miracle as an ordinary technique. It calls the reader to recognize divine power and to trust revelation about matters beyond ordinary experience. Scientific investigation can study food decay, but it cannot make this unique sign a routine human process. The donkey’s remains became a visible sign of bodily resurrection. Allah showed the bones being raised and clothed with flesh. The image directly answers the objection that bones, once scattered and decayed, cannot become living bodies again. If Allah created the body initially, He can reassemble it after death. (2:260 — **“And ˹remember˺ when Abraham said, “My Lord! Show me how you give life to the dead.”**) A report on a ruined town and a question comes through Ibn ʿAbbās, who identified the traveller as ʿUzayr and the ruined settlement as a town whose dead inhabitants Allah restored after the hundred-year sign.

**DEATH FOR A HUNDRED YEARS**

***“for a hundred years then brought him back to life. Allah asked, “How long have you remained ˹in this state˺?” He replied, “Perhaps a day or part of a day.” Allah said, “No! You have remained”*** deepens death for a hundred years. The Qur’an answers the same objection in Sūrah Yāsīn: Who will give life to bones while they are disintegrated. Say, He will give them life who produced them the first time. The ability to originate life is evidence of the ability to restore it. The person’s experience is made a sign—āyah—for humanity. A miracle benefits people when it leads to knowledge and gratitude, not when it becomes a story detached from its Lord. The event teaches that apparent finality is not final for Allah: ruins can be rebuilt, bones can be clothed, and a person can be returned from death. The verse also challenges civilizational despair. A destroyed town may be physically gone for a period, but communities can be rebuilt through justice, knowledge, work, and Allah’s permission. Hope must not become passivity; people restore what they can while trusting Allah with what exceeds their control. When it became clear to him, he said, ‘I know that Allah is capable of everything.’ His knowledge is not only intellectual. It is certainty formed through a sign and directed toward worship. He does not claim that he now understands every mystery. He recognizes the central truth: Allah’s power is unlimited and His resurrection promise is credible. A believer can similarly say, I do not know how, without saying, It cannot be. Human inability to imagine a process does not limit Allah. Humility allows the mind to investigate while accepting the limits of created knowledge. The story is connected to the Day when every person is raised. Resurrection is not merely a demonstration of power; it is the foundation of accountability. If people return to Allah, then hidden injustice, forgotten charity, and unpunished crimes all have a final reckoning. This should affect present conduct. The person who believes he will be raised does not treat property, time, or another person’s life as disposable. He prepares for the return through faith, repentance, and good works. Think of a ruined town and a question through a farmer who finds a dry field in the month of the drought and tells himself that nothing will grow there again. He sleeps through the season, wakes in the spring of the following year, and finds a green line along the ditch where the seed had fallen from his own sack. (36:78 — **“And they argue with Us—forgetting they were created—saying, “Who will give life to decayed bones?””**)

**THE DONKEY AND THE BONES**

***“look at the bones ˹of the donkey˺, how We bring them together then clothe them with flesh!” When this was made clear to him, he declared, “˹Now˺ I know that Allah is Most Capable of everything.””*** closes with the donkey and the bones. The reconstructed bones make the proof visible in stages: preservation of the food, decay of the animal, assembly of the frame, and clothing with flesh all answer different aspects of the traveller’s question. The sequence does not suggest that resurrection is difficult for Allah; it teaches a human observer through signs he can follow. Its conclusion therefore belongs to knowledge as worship. Once the sign is clear, the proper response is not fascination with the mechanism but confession of Allah’s unrestricted power and readiness for the resurrection it proves. (22:5 — **“O humanity! If you are in doubt about the Resurrection,”**)
