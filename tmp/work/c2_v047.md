**GRATITUDE IS RECALLED BEFORE JUDGEMENT IS NAMED**

The call comes back to Jacob’s people: ***“O Children of Israel!”***. They have heard it before, but its place here gives it fresh force. The first reminder led to the pledge (2:40 — **“Fulfil your covenant and I will fulfil Mine,”**). This one leads toward the Day when no person can buy his way out or use force to save himself. God calls them to obey from thanks for His gifts and from fear of that Day. Neither reason cancels the other. A gift calls for thanks; it does not give leave to break the Giver’s command. The past rebukes have not made the gifts themselves false. God does not ask them to cast away every good thing in their history. He asks them to act in keeping with what He gave. This is why the summons returns with a reminder of honour, not just another threat. The way to obey is still open. Their people had been taught and helped before, and the word now reaching them comes from the same Lord. Their past should make them more ready to heed it.

The words ***“Remember ˹all˺ the favours I granted you”*** gather many gifts into one reminder. They had been given scripture, taught by messengers, and freed from the rule of those who oppressed them. Abū al-ʿĀliyah includes kingship, messengers, and Books among the good granted to them. Moses had set such gifts before his people: (5:20 — **“Remember Allah’s favours upon you when He raised prophets from among you, made you sovereign,”**). The call to remember is thus about more than wealth. It includes freedom to live and knowledge of how to serve God. Those addressed in Madinah had not stood beside Moses, but they still shared in the good that followed his people’s rescue. A child can live in safety because an earlier generation was saved. The past gift has not ceased to matter simply because the child did not see it given. The same holds for the teaching passed down to him. He receives a duty of thanks with the benefit. That is why a proud tale about the fathers is not enough. The children must use well what has reached them, rather than turn it into a reason to refuse God’s present command.

**A FAVOUR WITH A HISTORICAL SETTING**

God then singles out the honour: ***“and how I honoured you”*** ***“above the others”***. Qatādah takes the comparison to concern the peoples of that time. The other nations among whom they lived had not all been given the same gifts. This gives the praise a clear setting. It does not rank every person born into one people above every person born into another, for all ages to come. Yet the gift was real and came by God’s choice: (44:32 — **“And indeed, We chose the Israelites knowingly above the others”**). The honour is His to give; it is not a worth that the people possessed apart from Him. A wider comparison can also concern one kind of gift, such as the prophets raised among them, without claiming that they excelled in every respect. The sense followed here is the early reading about the peoples of their age. It states the gift without making it a claim that no later people could receive a higher place. The word of God must be read with His other words, including the praise He gives to the community of a later Messenger.

**HONOUR STILL REQUIRES A FAITHFUL RESPONSE**

That later praise is explicit: (3:110 — **“You are the best community ever raised for humanity—you encourage good, forbid evil, and believe in Allah.”**). In al-Tirmidhī, Muʿāwiyah ibn Ḥaydah reports that the Prophet described his community as completing seventy nations and as the best and most honoured of them before God. Al-Tirmidhī graded the report ḥasan, or good. The later gift does not make the earlier one false. It rules out the claim that Israel’s preference must mean supremacy over every future community. The praise given to Muslims also names what their honour involves: faith, urging good, and forbidding evil. Ibn Zayd ties it to those who obey God and keep His commands. A Muslim who reads this warning must therefore accept its force for himself. A community’s name cannot stand in for the conduct required of its members. God links raised rank to faith and knowledge: (58:11 — **“Allah will elevate those of you who are faithful, and ˹raise˺ those gifted with knowledge in rank.”**). The right use of honour is to fulfil the trust that comes with it. This keeps the reminder and the coming warning together. Their fathers’ gifts call for thanks now; the final account will judge their own response, not accept pride in those fathers as its replacement.
