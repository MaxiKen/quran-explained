**THE SUBMISSION-PRAYER**

The pair's second prayer asks for the religion itself. ***“Our Lord! Make us both ˹fully˺ submit to You”*** ***“and from our descendants a nation”*** ***“that will submit to you. Show us our rituals”***. Two submissions are requested at once. The father's and the family's. In the report of Ibn ʿAbbās, Abraham and Ishmael prayed it together while raising the House. The verse treats submission as a gift to be requested. It is not a temperament to be discovered. The first phrase is the whole theology. Make us. The submitting is made in the one who receives it. The reason the prayer reaches past the two names to a nation is that the House was never built for a private devotion. It is a station for whoever comes after the builders. The descendants clause turns the construction into a request for a future congregation. The parallel passage of the family states the settling in its human form (14:37 — **“Our Lord! I have settled some of my offspring in a barren valley, near Your Sacred House, our Lord,”**).

**THE RITUALS TO BE SHOWN**

The prayer's second half asks for the rites themselves as a teaching. ***“and turn to us in grace. You are truly the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful”***. The report of Ibn ʿAbbās defines the rituals that were to be shown. The stoning of the pillars. The standing at ʿArafah. The rapid walking between Ṣafā and Marwah. The throwing of the pebbles. Through Ibn Jurayj the stayer's own station joins the picture. The one staying for worship is the one in retreat. The rituals include the staying. The effect of the prayer is that the rituals are requested as revelation. They are not invented as culture. The builder of the House asks to be taught how the House is used. A rite remembered and a rite shown are different things. One is a custom. The other is a teaching. Qatādah and al-Rabīʿ b. Anas read the staying and the visiting as the acts of the one who commits himself at the House and comes to it. The prayer covers the resident and the traveller. The reason the rituals need showing is that the submission asked for in the first half has to walk. The walking has a pattern. The pattern is part of the answer to the prayer. The parallel closes on the hope of the grateful (14:37 — **“so perhaps they will be thankful”**).

**THE TURNING IN GRACE**

The verse ends where every sincere request ends. On the turning one. The accepting is named at the close of the prayer that asked to be turned toward. In the report of Ibn ʿAbbās the pair's prayer is answered in the very names that close it. The accepting and the mercy are the two handles the builders hold. The rituals and the turning are asked for in the same breath for a reason. The rituals are the body of the repentance. The repentance is the soul of the rituals. For the modern reader the closing title is the most portable line in the passage. Whatever has been built or broken, the accepting is still being named at the end of the prayer. This age is fluent in self-improvement. It is mute about repentance. The verse keeps the second word in the pair's own vocabulary. The builders of the House are thus shown as people who needed turning. They are not finished saints. The reason the verse closes on mercy rather than stone is that the stone was only ever the visible half. The requested nation is made of people who ask to be shown the rituals. That asking is already a submission. The father wanted the submitting made in the children. The making happens through the showing. The rituals then walk the submission through the body. The feet circle. The knees bow. The forehead touches the ground. The turning closes the sequence with mercy. The pair prayed for the turning because the rituals need it. A ritual without repentance becomes a routine. A repentance without ritual becomes a mood. The verse asks for both in one breath. The breath is the pattern of the House. Submit. Be shown. Turn. The modern reader can run the pattern anywhere. The pattern does not require the stones. It requires the same asking. The builders of the House asked for a nation. The nation is made of askers. The mercy named at the close is the answer already given.
