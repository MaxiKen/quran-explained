**THE FEAR BEHIND MISERLINESS**

***“The Devil threatens you with ˹the prospect of˺ poverty and bids”*** establishes the fear behind miserliness. When the Qur’an commands generous spending, Satan responds by threatening poverty—al-shayṭān yaʿidukum al-faqr. He whispers that charity will leave a person without security, that supporting others is foolish, or that one must accumulate indefinitely before giving. Responsible planning is not wrong, but fear that prevents every act of generosity is one of Satan’s recurring strategies. A person may possess enough for his needs yet feel permanently threatened. The more he gathers, the more afraid he becomes of loss. Satan turns a real concern about provision into a refusal to trust Allah, a neglect of dependants, or the hoarding of wealth while others suffer. Satan commands faḥshāʾ, indecency and shameful conduct. Misers may be led to exploit workers, steal inheritance, take bribes, consume interest, or humiliate the poor. The fear of poverty becomes an excuse for actions that destroy dignity and community. Satan also invites people to spend extravagantly on desire while claiming that they cannot afford charity. The Qur’an’s word commands does not remove human choice. Satan suggests and beautifies; the person decides. Seeking refuge in Allah, questioning the whisper, and taking lawful means break the cycle. A believer can budget carefully without obeying the demand to become unjust. Allah promises maghfirah. Charity, repentance, and responsible generosity can be means of forgiveness, not because money purchases Allah’s mercy, but because sincere giving reveals faith and purifies attachment. (34:39 — **“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Surely ˹it is˺ my Lord ˹Who˺ gives abundant or limited provisions to whoever He”**)

**COMMANDS TO IMMORALITY**

***“you to the shameful deed ˹of stinginess˺, while Allah promises you”*** deepens commands to immorality. The believer who has been miserly can return to Allah without waiting until he feels completely generous. Forgiveness also covers mistakes in financial judgement when a servant acts sincerely. A person should consult, learn, and correct errors, but he need not be paralyzed by the fear of never giving perfectly. Allah sees the intention and effort. Allah promises faḍl, bounty and increase. The increase may be material provision, blessing in what remains, good relationships, contentment, protection from calamity, or the multiplied reward of the Hereafter. The promise does not turn charity into a guaranteed investment scheme. A believer gives for Allah, not as a technique for forcing a worldly return. The Prophet ﷺ taught that every morning two angels supplicate for replacement for the one who spends and destruction for the one who withholds, in reports that illuminate the fear behind miserliness, collected for the theme of the fear behind miserliness by al-Bukhārī and Muslim. The hadith encourages trust while reminding people that withholding rights has consequences. Islam balances charity with responsibility. The believer pays debts, supports dependants, prepares for genuine needs, and avoids waste. He then gives according to ability. The Prophet ﷺ advised Saʿd not to leave his heirs dependent upon people, showing that generosity does not mean abandoning family. Satan may use either extreme: fear to prevent charity or vanity to cause irresponsible giving. Wisdom identifies the true need, the lawful amount, and the most beneficial form of support. (14:7 — **“And ˹remember˺ when your Lord proclaimed, ‘If you are grateful, I will certainly give you more.”**)

**ALLAH PROMISES FORGIVENESS**

***“forgiveness and ˹great˺ bounties from Him. And Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing”*** clarifies allah promises forgiveness. Allah is Wāsiʿ, vast in provision, mercy, and knowledge. His stores do not shrink when servants give, and His knowledge encompasses the giver’s circumstances and the recipient’s need. He knows whether a person is genuinely unable or hiding behind an exaggerated fear of poverty. This knowledge is a comfort to the poor giver. Allah does not demand what he cannot afford. It is a warning to the wealthy who call miserliness prudence while spending freely on luxuries. The believer counters Satan’s threat with the Qur’anic promises: Whatever you spend of good, He will replace it, and If you are grateful, I will surely increase you. He begins with small consistent gifts, gives privately where possible, and observes how generosity changes the heart. A person facing actual financial distress should seek assistance, renegotiate debts, and protect dependants. Asking for help is not a failure of tawakkul. Communities should ensure that the poor are not shamed for needing support. Think of the fear behind miserliness through a man who keeps his grain behind a locked door because a neighbour once told him that the winter would be long. The winter passes, the grain goes mouldy in the dark, and the neighbour who borrowed from him in the spring is the one who carries his harvest to the market. The fear behind miserliness confronts commands to immorality in someone who repeatedly hears an inward argument about old age whenever he opens his purse.
