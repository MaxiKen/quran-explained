**A PRAYER FOR A STEADFAST HEART**

***“˹They say,˺ “Our Lord! Do not let our hearts”*** establishes a prayer for a steadfast heart. After describing those grounded in knowledge, the Qur’an records their prayer: Our Lord, do not let our hearts deviate after You have guided us. Knowledge has not made them self-confident or careless. It has made them more aware of the danger of deviation and more dependent upon Allah’s protection. The heart, qalb, is called a heart because it changes and turns. A person may know the truth today and become distracted tomorrow; a community may possess revelation and later distort it. Guidance is therefore not a certificate of permanent safety. It is a gift that must be protected through worship, humility, sound knowledge, and continual supplication. The prayer seeks protection from zaygh, a deviation or crooked inclination. This may begin subtly: preferring desire to evidence, treating revelation as a tool for personal status, excusing a private sin, or allowing resentment to shape interpretation. A heart rarely abandons guidance in one dramatic movement; it often turns through repeated small compromises. The request does not imply that Allah wrongs those who deviate. The Qur’an says that when people deliberately turn away, Allah allows their hearts to turn as a consequence of their choice. The believer asks for protection while taking responsibility for the choices that nourish or damage his heart. They ask Allah to grant mercy after asking for steadfastness. Guidance itself is an expression of mercy, but the servant needs continuing mercy to understand, practise, repent, and remain firm. (61:5 — **“˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when Moses said to his people, “O my people!”**) Ibn ʿAbbās understands the prayer against straying as a plea for firmness in religion after guidance and for mercy that preserves faith.

**DEVIATION AFTER GUIDANCE**

***“deviate after you have guided us. Grant us Your”*** deepens deviation after guidance. The word raḥmah includes forgiveness, protection from error, kindness in hardship, and a good end. The order is meaningful. A person may possess information but need mercy to live by it. He may perform deeds but need mercy to have them accepted. He may face a trial that exposes his weakness and need mercy to emerge with faith intact. The closing name, al-Wahhāb, means the One who gives freely and abundantly. Allah gives guidance, faith, knowledge, forgiveness, provision, and opportunities for service without being compelled by any creature. The servant does not earn the initial gift of guidance through a prior achievement; he receives it as a divine bestowal and then responds with gratitude and effort. Calling upon Allah as al-Wahhāb teaches generous hope. No person should think that his past makes guidance impossible or that the community cannot be renewed. Allah can bestow understanding upon the confused, repentance upon the sinner, and steadfastness upon the fearful. The supplication is plural in meaning and can be prayed for the whole community. Scholars, teachers, parents, and leaders especially need to ask that knowledge not become a cause of pride. Communities need protection from factionalism, political manipulation of scripture, and disputes in which victory matters more than truth. The prayer also teaches how to respond to doctrinal disagreement. Instead of assuming that one’s own heart is safe and everyone else is deviant, the believer asks Allah to protect him. (3:7 — **“He is the One Who has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Book,”**)

**MERCY AFTER GUIDANCE**

***“mercy. You are indeed the Giver ˹of all bounties˺”*** clarifies mercy after guidance. This humility makes sincere correction possible. A guided heart is like a compass needle kept toward true north; pressure may shake it, but divine mercy keeps it from settling in the wrong direction. Today, a believer facing doubt asks for steadiness, studies honestly, and avoids assuming that past guidance makes future deviation impossible. Because a prayer for a steadfast heart opens into deviation after guidance, mercy after guidance joins a prayer for a steadfast heart to deviation after guidance. Since mercy after guidance develops a prayer for a steadfast heart, deviation after guidance carries the transition. Al-wahhāb: the bestower matters, which means mercy after guidance answers deviation after guidance. Deviation after guidance gives practical form to a prayer for a steadfast heart; al-wahhāb: the bestower sets its horizon. A prayer for a steadfast heart stays bound to mercy after guidance because deviation after guidance supplies the human setting of a prayer for a steadfast heart. Al-wahhāb: the bestower carries a prayer for a steadfast heart into conduct shaped by mercy after guidance. (24:46 — **“We have indeed sent down revelations clarifying ˹the truth˺.”**)
