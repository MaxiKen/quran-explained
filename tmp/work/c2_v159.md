**THE CONCEALED PROOFS**

The concealment now receives its courtroom. ***“Those who hide the clear proofs”*** ***“and guidance that We have revealed”*** ***“after We made it clear for humanity in the Book”***. The objects of the hiding are named before the verdict. Proofs and guidance. Both were revealed. Both were made clear in advance of the hiding, so that no appeal of confusion remains available at the trial. Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss itemises what was buried. The commands and the prohibitions and the signs written in the Torah. The guidance here is the description and the traits of the awaited prophet. The narration of Abū al-ʿĀliyah states the occasion plainly. These verses came down about the People of the Scripture who hid the description. The point is that the crime is named as concealment rather than ignorance. The material was in the shelves. The clarity was delivered in advance. What follows is that the hiding is graded as a deed done against the light. The effect of the naming is forensic. The verse opens an evidence file before it opens a verdict.

**CURSES FROM TWO COURTS**

The verdict arrives with two courts in session. ***“will be condemned by Allah and ˹all˺ those who condemn”***. The court above and the court below pronounce one sentence. Ibn ʿAbbās's reading marks the two condemnations in their places. The condemnation from Allah is the torment that meets the concealer in the grave. The condemnation from the created order is the cursing that answers the screams. The parallel warning states the same verdict at its origin (2:174 — **“Indeed, those who hide Allah’s revelations, trading them for a fleeting gain consume nothing”**). The trading is the motive underneath the hiding. The revelation was exchanged for a price. The verdict matches the trade. The difference between a private grudge and a licensed curse is the presence of the proofs. The proofs were clear. The concealing was deliberate. The courts speak because the evidence is complete. What follows is that the ordinary conscience is granted a place in the judgment. The cursers of the earth repeat what the throne has already said.

**A PROFIT OF FIRE**

The account of the trade closes the passage with its payment. The revelations were traded for a fleeting gain. The payment is filed in the other currency (2:174 — **“but fire into their bellies. Allah will neither speak to them on the Day of Judgment, nor will He purify them.”**). Fire for the belly. Silence at the speaking. No purification at the end. The early commentators read the sequence as the economy of the shelf made visible. The knowledge was bought to be kept. The keeping returned as a payment no bank recognises. For the modern reader the verse is a lesson in the ethics of information. The proofs are given to be shared, not stored behind a counter. This age has invented new hands to lay over the light. Noise departments. Burial by volume. The verse's verdict has a modern application because the deed is old. The hand over the light is still the same hand. The courts still speak in the same order. The gain still flees before the payment arrives.

**THE SHELF ON TRIAL**

The trial of the concealers is finally a trial of every keeper of sources. The proofs were named first so that the verdict could be read as a document case. The courts were named second so that the sentence could be heard as a public act. The trade was named last so that the motive could be filed with the evidence. The effect of the sequence is a complete prosecution with exhibits. The reason the modern application is unavoidable is that every age produces shelf-keepers. The archive is always tended by hands that can either publish or bury. The burial has a market. The gain is always fleeting. The payment is always posted to the other currency. For the modern reader the passage is a warning posted above the archive door. This age keeps the proofs in volumes and pays the hiding hands in full view. The verse reads the docket aloud. The truth was made clear. The hiding was a deed. The fire is the trade's own coin returned to the trader.
