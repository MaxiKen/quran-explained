**COMPLETE REPAYMENT AFTER DELAY**

***“Be mindful of the Day when you will ˹all˺ be”*** establishes complete repayment after delay. This āyah is the last command in the Qur’an to be revealed according to a well-known report from Ibn ʿAbbās, and it stands as a fitting conclusion to the economic and social laws of the preceding passage. After discussing charity, ribā, debt, documentation, and mercy toward the debtor, Allah turns the heart toward the final account. Every financial decision and every human right will return to Him. Returned to Allah—turjaʿūna ilā Allāh—does not mean that Allah was absent from the servants’ lives. It means that they will be gathered before Him for judgement. The world is a temporary trust; the Hereafter is the place where the full meaning of each choice becomes visible. Every soul will be paid in full—tuwafā kullu nafsin mā kasabat. No good deed is lost, and no wrong is forgotten. A creditor who forgave a poor debtor, a scribe who documented a contract honestly, a witness who told the truth, and a person who refused ribā all have their deeds recorded. Likewise, hidden fraud, withheld wages, false testimony, and economic exploitation cannot disappear because a court or audience failed to notice them. The word kasb refers to what a person acquires through intention and action. Wealth itself is not the whole account. Allah records why it was earned, how it was obtained, how it was spent, and whose rights were affected. (2:261 — **“The example of those who spend their wealth in the cause of Allah is that of a”**)

**EVERY SOUL RECEIVES ITS DUE**

***“returned to Allah, then every soul will be paid in full”*** deepens every soul receives its due. A small act may be great through sincerity, while a large public gift may be empty through showing off. None will be wronged—lā yuẓlamūn—is a universal guarantee of divine justice. Allah will not reduce the reward of a good deed, add a sin to an innocent person, or punish a person for what lay beyond his ability and knowledge. He knows coercion, ignorance, intention, capacity, and the effect of every circumstance. This promise comforts people whose rights were taken. A worker may not recover unpaid wages in a human court, a widow may be defrauded of inheritance, or a debtor may be humiliated despite genuine hardship. Allah sees the full truth. It also warns the powerful that escaping human accountability does not mean escaping the final account. Fear a Day translates ittaqū yawman. Taqwā is not panic; it is protecting oneself from wrongdoing by remembering the meeting with Allah. The preceding laws become meaningful when a person understands that contracts, oaths, charity, and debt are acts of worship and matters of judgement. A person who remembers complete repayment after delay is less likely to take a bribe, hide a debt, pressure a widow, or charge a desperate borrower an unlawful increase. He may not receive immediate worldly benefit from justice, but he knows that Allah’s account is certain. The verse is serious, but it is not a message of despair. (2:275 — **“Those who consume interest will stand ˹on Judgment Day˺ like those driven to madness by Satan’s touch.”**)

**NONE WILL BE WRONGED**

***“for what it has done, and none will be wronged”*** clarifies none will be wronged. Allah’s mercy remains open through repentance, charity, restitution, and forgiveness. The next verses close the sūrah with faith, obedience, and a prayer for pardon. Fear directs the servant away from injustice, while hope motivates him to return when he falls. Repentance for financial wrongdoing must include restoring people’s rights where possible. Saying Allah forgive me while keeping stolen property is incomplete. The person returns the money, corrects the record, apologizes where appropriate, and seeks Allah’s mercy. The sequence from 2:261 to 2:281 contrasts charity with ribā and generosity with exploitation. The final return explains why the contrast matters. A person may appear to gain through interest, fraud, or manipulation, but the final account reverses the appearance. The one who gives may seem to lose, but Allah pays him in full and multiplies the reward. The verse therefore unites private worship and public justice. Faith is not only prayer and recitation; it is how a person handles another’s money, records an agreement, and responds to someone in hardship. Think of complete repayment after delay through a dealer who weighs his sacks with a stone in the pan and counts the difference as his profit for a year. The stone is found in the pan one morning by his own son, and every customer he cheated is standing at the door before the evening.
