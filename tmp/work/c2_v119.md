**THE COMMISSION, FULLY WRITTEN**

The passage then hands the messenger his own case-file: ***“We have surely sent you with the truth ˹O Prophet˺ as a deliverer of good news and a warner.”***. Ibn ʿAbbās unpacks the file by its three clauses. The sending is with the truth, which is the book and the declaration of the oneness. The good news is the promise of the garden for the believers. The warning is the warning of the fire against those who disbelieve. The commission is therefore complete at the level of both its message and its audience. The truth names the cargo. The good news and the warning name the two destinations the cargo separates. The version of the commission that names its audience by their response is recorded in the passage of the guidance-bearers: (27:2 — **“˹It is˺ a guide and good news for the believers”**). The carrier of the truth is a courier, and the courier's route runs between two addresses.

**THE ACCOUNTABILITY CLAUSE**

The file's next line is the relief of the office: ***“ And you will not be accountable for the residents of the Hellfire”***. The old gloss hears the line twice. In one reading it is not befitting that he should ask about the dwellers of the fire. In the other, the line is the prohibition of asking forgiveness for them. The received gloss states the underlying doctrine in its plainest form: his responsibility is only to deliver the message. The reading tradition then preserves the variant of the verb, one reading carried as the passive of the asking and the other as the imperative of the prohibition. The reason the accountability clause exists is that the office of delivery has a boundary, which means the courier's grief has an edge. The variant agrees with the doctrine from two directions. The point of the variant is that the boundary is documented two ways. The office is delivery. The verdict is not in the courier's bag. The address-list of the fire is drawn in the passage of the refusers without variation: (2:81 — **“and are engrossed in sin will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever”**). The residents of that home are the residents the commission will not answer for. The courier's books close at the delivery.

**THE DESCRIPTION ASKED OF THE EARLIER SCRIBE**

The case-file of the commission carries one more exhibit in the received record, and the chain of the exhibit is worth naming. The report of the description asked of ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ comes through ʿAṭāʾ b. Yasar, and the transmission is recorded by Aḥmad. The old scribe read the seal of the description from the pages he guarded, the prophet whose coming the Torah had named. The companion's report of the sealed description is the one al-Bukhārī alone preserved in his book. The exhibit does exactly the work the commission needs. The truth of the sending was documented before the sending arrived. The warner and the bringer of good news had been on the earlier shelves with both titles written.

**THE CONSOLATION AFTER THE REFUSAL**

The placement of the file at this point in the passage is its own kindness. The preceding verse had shown the refusers in full temperament, the hearts alike, the requests on their rounds. The received record of the passage reads the present verse as consolation for the sadness of the messenger at the incorrigible. The thought that any soul would persist in denial weighed on him, and the file answers the weight. The truth has been sent. The good news has been opened. The warning has been sounded. The residuum of the refusal belongs to the court of the fire, and the courier of the passage does not carry that case. What follows from the boundary is the consolation the file was written to deliver. The office is therefore relieved of the one burden it was never assigned. The audience owns its response. The response does not own the messenger.

**THE ROUTE AND THE TWO ADDRESSES**

The whole unit is best read as a route with two termini. The courier leaves the owner's desk with the truth in the bag. One address opens to the good news. The other is filed under the warning. The route is walked every day the commission stands. The packages are delivered by tongue and by book and by the patience that continues after the refusal. The reader who meets the courier on the route meets the commission's full sentence. Sent with the truth. Good news and warning. Accountable to the sender. The three clauses are the courier's standing orders, and the orders have never been revised. The route stays open. The addresses stay real. The books close at the sender's desk where the case-file began.
