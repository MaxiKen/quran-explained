**LOVE THAT IS NOT RETURNED**

***“Here you are! You love them but they do not love you, and you”*** Here you are. You love them but they do not love you, and you believe in all Scriptures holds the mirror the confidants-law required. And its exposed asymmetry is the verse's whole diagnosis. Ha antum ula'i. Here you are, you people. The Book's finger pointed at its own community's warmth. Tuhibbunahum wa la yuhibbunakum. You love them, they love you not. The believers' affection real (the old ties, the kinships, the hoped conversions. The love this argument nowhere condemns in itself) and unreturned. And the asymmetry's root named in the next clause with quiet devastation. And you believe in all Scriptures. The believers' creed embraces the others' Books entire. While the others' creeds reject the believers' one. The affection-gap thus structural, not circumstantial. The party of the wider creed loves across a line the party of the narrower creed cannot love back across. The verse teaching, the sobriety the surrounding passage's law runs on. Linings are chosen by reciprocity's evidence, and here the evidence is read aloud. (3:118 — **“what their hearts hide is far worse. We have made Our revelations”**)

**BELIEF PERFORMED IN COMPANY**

***“believe in all Scriptures. When they meet you they say, “We believe.” But”*** When they meet you they say, 'We believe.' But when alone, they bite their fingertips in rage documents the asymmetry's concealed half. And the scene is the hypocrites' file re-entered with its most visceral image. The two-face protocol of another Qur'anic passage (when they meet the believers they say. We believe) completed here by the private chamber's posture. Addu alaykumu l-anamila mina l-ghayz. They bite their fingertips at you from rage. The gnawed knuckle. Fury's oldest gesture. The community's growth, concord, and victories chewed on in the closed rooms. And the commanded answer follows. ***“when alone, they bite their fingertips in rage. Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “˹May you˺”*** Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ '˹May you˺ die of your rage.'. Qul mutu bi-ghayzikum. The Book's rarest register. The imprecation-imperative. Not a curse flung in temper but a sentence published as forecast. The rage will not be relieved. Its objects will grow, its wishes will fail, and the raging will consume its containers. Die in it. Surely Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart sealing the scene at the final account-clause. The bitten knuckles of the private chambers filed with the chest-Knower. (3:120 — **“When you ˹believers˺ are touched with good, they grieve;”**) Belief performed in company remains urgent today because polite agreement in public should not conceal resentment that celebrates another community’s pain in private.

**RAGE IN PRIVATE AND DIVINE KNOWLEDGE**

***“die of your rage!” Surely Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart”*** The verse leaves the community a disciplined response to love that is not returned. The believers are not commanded out of their love but out of their naivety. Love's warmth never again mistaken for love's return, the lining-trust never again extended on the strength of one's own feelings toward the other. The asymmetry read, priced, and lived with. Warmth outward, wits intact. And the rage-sentence's comfort completes the equipment. The concealed fury that so unsettles the discovering believer (they smiled at us and gnawed their fists at us) is, by this verse, already known, already sentenced, and already working on its own containers. The community's part being only the patience and taqwa the next verse names as the schemes' complete insulation. If you are patient and mindful of Allah, their schemes will not harm you in the least. Imagine a handshake offered in the meeting while a fist is clenched under the table. Concerning love that is not returned, Ibn ʿAbbās connects outward claims of belief with inward rage and emphasizes that Allah knows the enmity concealed within the breast. Unequal affection can mislead a community into giving more trust than the other side has earned; which means the believers’ regard grows from accepting every true Scripture, while the hostility described here survives even behind a spoken claim of belief. That contrast explains why goodwill alone cannot settle the question of confidence; therefore love is a virtue, but it does not make concealed rage harmless. The command about dying in rage is not a private curse born of wounded pride; it exposes the futility of a hatred that cannot overturn Allah’s knowledge or purpose; in other words because He knows what hearts hide, believers do not need to answer secret malice with secret malice of their own. They need clear judgment and disciplined boundaries; hence today, warm words in public may coexist with sabotage in private. The verse teaches neither naivety nor obsession; this is why it allows benevolence to remain benevolence while refusing to call a staged friendship sincere before its conduct supports that name.
