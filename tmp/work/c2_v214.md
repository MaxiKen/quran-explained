**PARADISE HAS A PRICE**

The question of Paradise is answered in phrases that walk through the shaking and the cry: ***“Do you think you will be admitted into Paradise without being tested like those before you?”***, ***“They were afflicted with suffering”***, ***“and adversity and were”***, ***“so ˹violently˺ shaken that ˹even˺ the Messenger”***, ***“and the believers with him cried out, “When will Allah’s help come?””***, ***“Indeed, Allah’s help is ˹always˺ near”***. and the final phrase delivers the relief the shaking promised. The walk performs the lesson of the trial: the expectation is questioned first, the shaking is described second, and the relief is scheduled third. The messenger's cry is placed in the middle of the sequence because the cry is the proof that the shaking was real and not a metaphor. Ibn ʿAbbās fixes the address on the believers who had just lost the caravan and the confidence with it.

**SHAKEN LIKE THE BELL**

The shaking described in the verse is physical before it is spiritual: the affliction and the hardship were so severe that the messenger and the believers cried out. The reports on the passage fix the scene on the day the community lost the caravan and the confidence together, and the fixing turns the metaphor into a morning. The question of Paradise is answered with the shaking because the shaking is the entrance fee the earlier communities paid in full. The legislation of the alternating days states the same economy in the same terms of injury and reversal (3:140 — **“If you have suffered injuries ˹at Uḥud˺, they suffered similarly ˹at Badr˺. We alternate these days ˹of victory and defeat˺ among people”**). Because the shaking is shared with the earlier communities, the trial is a curriculum rather than a punishment.

**THE MESSENGER'S CRY**

The gathering is the point of the pairing: a win that assembles the household and a loss that scatters it are weighed in one handful. The cry of the messenger and the believers is recorded in the verse as the measure of the shaking, and the recording is a mercy to every servant who has cried out under pressure. The address of the verse to the believers who had just been stripped of their certainty is confirmed in the reports on the occasion, where the trial is fixed on the ones who had stood at the front. The test of fear and famine and loss carries the same list of instruments and the same promise to the patient (2:155 — **“We will certainly test you with a touch of fear and famine and loss of property, life,”**). Because the cry is answered with relief rather than rebuke, the verse gives the community permission to be human in the hour of the shaking.

**RELIEF SCHEDULED**

The close of the verse schedules the relief: Allah will bring about a matter, and the matter is the relief and the conquest together. The scheduling answers the shaking itself — the trial is dated and the relief is dated, and the One who sets both dates is the same. Because the relief is promised as a matter rather than as a mood, the community's planning can include it. Every shaken morning of one's own life is worth reading against the schedule this passage keeps. The bell rings and the relief is already in the calendar. The patience described at the head of the passage is described in the old commentary with the image of the lamp that does not go out at the hour of the wind. The one who is patient at the trench and patient at the famine has been lit by the light that does not depend on the market of the hour. Because the patience is described as a light rather than as a grip of the teeth, the reader is invited to a different physiology of endurance. The affliction visits the houses of the believers and the houses of the deniers in the same week, and the difference between the two houses is the lamp. The scholar of the earliest recensions keeps the reading of the patience in the same family of the lamp, and the family resemblance is visible at the desk. The one who loses the dear ones at the hour of the affliction reads the passage as a letter addressed to the house. Reading the news of the cities beside the patience of the lamp is the seminar the passage conducts for the afflicted.
