**A ROOTED CHARITY**

***“And the example of those who donate their wealth, seeking Allah’s pleasure and believing”*** establishes a rooted charity. After the image of a smooth stone whose dust is washed away, this verse gives the opposite image: a fertile garden on elevated ground. The garden represents a charitable act rooted in sincerity—ibtighāʾ marḍāt Allāh—and in the strengthening or purification of the giver’s soul. Its reward grows because the foundation is sound. A garden on a hill receives rainfall and benefits from good drainage, sunlight, and protection from stagnant water. The image suggests a deed that is well-placed, healthy, and able to produce repeatedly. Sincere charity can benefit the recipient immediately and continue through education, healing, family stability, and the good practices that grow from it. The giver seeks Allah’s pleasure rather than public praise. His intention does not have to be perfectly free from every passing thought; he renews it and resists the desire to be admired. He gives because Allah loves generosity and because people have rights, not because he wants to own the recipient’s loyalty. A private gift often protects sincerity, but public giving can also be good when it encourages others or fulfils a public trust. The test is whether the giver would still want the good done if his name were removed. Allah sees the intention and the conduct more fully than people do. The phrase tathbītan min anfusihim can mean seeking to strengthen the soul, confirm sincerity, or establish the heart in obedience. (2:261 — **“The example of those who spend their wealth in the cause of Allah is that of a”**) Ibn ʿAbbās connects the elevated garden with spending that sincerely seeks Allah’s pleasure and rises from settled conviction that the gift will be rewarded.

**A GARDEN FED BY SINCERE PURPOSE**

***“the reward is certain, is that of a garden on a fertile hill: when”*** deepens a garden fed by sincere purpose. Spending weakens the grip of miserliness and trains the servant to trust Allah. It also turns concern for the poor into a settled character rather than an occasional emotional reaction. A person strengthens himself through regular charity, even if small, and through spending in ways that require patience and responsibility. He learns that his identity is not secured by accumulation. The heart becomes more stable when it is attached to Allah rather than to possessions. Heavy rain produces abundant fruit, while light rain is sufficient. This illustrates that sincere deeds bear fruit according to the conditions Allah knows. A wealthy person may give much, while a poor person gives little but with a greater sacrifice. A large project may produce public benefit, while a small meal quietly given to a hungry neighbour may be immense in Allah’s account. The verse does not make small charity insignificant. If the soil is fertile, even light rain sustains it. A person who cannot donate large amounts can give time, food, expertise, advocacy, or a kind word. What matters is sincere rootedness and beneficial effect. The garden image also teaches planning. A charity must be maintained rather than launched with enthusiasm and abandoned when attention moves elsewhere. Donors should choose sustainable projects, monitor funds, and listen to the people being served. The best assistance helps people become stable rather than keeping them permanently dependent upon unpredictable gifts. (2:272 — **“You are not responsible for people’s guidance ˹O Prophet˺—it is Allah Who guides whoever He wills.”**)

**STRENGTHENING THE SOUL**

***“heavy rain falls, it yields up twice its normal produce. If no heavy”*** clarifies strengthening the soul. The giver must still support his family and fulfil debts. Strengthening the soul does not mean destroying the household’s legitimate provision. Allah loves balanced obedience. The verse closes with Allah’s vision—baṣīr—of all that people do. He sees the gift, the intention, the recipient’s condition, the sacrifice involved, and the harm or benefit that follows. A public donation may be praised while a hidden gift is unknown; Allah sees both without confusion. This sight also warns institutions. They may publish attractive reports while misusing funds or harming communities. Allah sees what the auditors and donors do not. Transparency, independent oversight, and truthful reporting are forms of taqwā. If many believers give sincerely, the community becomes like a landscape of gardens: provision circulates, knowledge grows, and people shelter one another. Charity is not merely emergency relief; it can build schools, clinics, housing, safe water systems, and opportunities for dignified work. The projects must remain subordinate to people’s rights. A beautiful building that leaves the poor hungry is not automatically a garden of righteousness. Fruit is measured by benefit and sincerity. The case is like two plots on a hillside, one watered by the channel and the other left to the rain. The channel dries up in a hard year, the rain still comes, and the two fields are green in the same month. (92:17 — **“But the righteous will be spared from it—”**)

**HEAVY RAIN AND LIGHT RAIN**

***“rain falls, a drizzle is sufficient. And Allah is All-Seeing of what you do”*** closes with heavy rain and light rain. A rooted charity confronts a garden fed by sincere purpose in a reader who gives less than a neighbour and takes that as a verdict on his own deed. The verse gives him a heavy rain and a drizzle over the same ground; strengthening the soul asks, through a rooted charity, which of the two the garden was waiting for. Because the close of a rooted charity identifies whoever sees what people do, showing through a rooted charity that the size of the gift belongs to the purse and the worth of it belongs to the heart. The rock that loses its soil is described just before this verse. And the same pair of reward and fear is promised to the sincere givers. Because a rooted charity opens into a garden fed by sincere purpose, strengthening the soul joins a rooted charity to a garden fed by sincere purpose. Since strengthening the soul develops a rooted charity, a garden fed by sincere purpose carries the transition. Heavy rain and light rain matters, which means strengthening the soul answers a garden fed by sincere purpose. Seeking allah’s pleasure gives practical form to a rooted charity; heavy rain and light rain sets its horizon. Today, a rooted charity shapes a garden fed by sincere purpose in present decisions. (3:92 — **“You will never achieve righteousness until you donate some of what you cherish.”**)
