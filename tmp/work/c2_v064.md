**TURNING BACK AFTER THE SEALING**

The verse opens on the motion that undoes a covenant: ***“Yet you turned away afterwards. Had it not been”***. The word it spends on their conduct is the word for turning one’s back, and its plain root is physical; the language extended it to anyone who abandons an order he has been given, so that walking away from a duty is named with the same word as walking away from a person. The sequence marker deserves its weight. The point is that the pledge was complied with first and then left, and that makes the breach worse than a refusal that never accepted anything. The accounts of the mountain day agree with that order. Awe secured the words; the practice that should have followed the words is what failed. The Book marks the same habit in another place, where bounty is followed by withdrawal: (9:76 — **“But when He gave them out of His bounty, they withheld it and turned away indifferently”**). That is why the pattern across these passages is consistent, and the history of this people keeps repeating it: full gifts are answered with half obedience, and half obedience is answered with turning. The terms of their covenant had been itemised elsewhere, from worship of God alone to kindness to parents, and the same covenant is sealed in the Book with its twelve leaders named: (5:12 — **“Allah made a covenant with the Children of Israel and appointed twelve leaders from among them and ˹then˺ said,”**). The charge here is that the whole list was dropped after it was sealed. The reason the verse keeps saying "you" across the generations is that the covenant was taken from the line itself, so every generation that inherits its terms inherits the charge against its own conduct.

**TWO MERCIES STANDING BETWEEN THEM AND RUIN**

The saving clause then names what held the ruin back: ***“for Allah’s grace and mercy upon you, you would have certainly been of the losers”***. The two nouns have been given two readings, and both are transmitted. In one, the grace is the enabling of repentance and the mercy is its acceptance, so that the pair names the whole road back to God in its two halves. In the other, given by Ibn ʿAbbās, the grace and mercy are the sending of the final Messenger and their living to see him, since faith in him is itself part of the covenant they had been given. The two readings meet rather than cancel. Every reprieve in this history is the practice of the same Lord, whether it comes as time given for return or as a message that opens the return. The reading followed here keeps both, and the address of the verse supports it: the second person runs from the people of the mountain to the people standing in the Prophet’s own time, because the covenant’s terms were never withdrawn. What turns on that scope is plain. The warning is not embalmed in the past. The word for loss is drawn from trade, and it means the going of a merchant’s capital rather than the shrinking of his profit, which fixes the grade of what they were saved from. A trader who loses his principal stands with nothing; the people addressed here were one such act away from that position. The Book states the covenant’s weight in the same terms elsewhere: (33:72 — **“Indeed, We offered the trust to the heavens and the earth and the mountains, but they ˹all˺ declined to bear it,”**). A trust the mountains refused was held over the heads of this people, and they turned their backs on it. That is why the mercy is named twice in one breath. What follows for any community that has been given a book and a covenant is the reading this verse installs: the turning away is judged as it happened, and the reprieve is counted as what it was, mercy standing between a people and the loss of everything.

The address also runs past the mountain to the people hearing the charge in the Prophet’s own time. The covenant those hearers stand under includes the coming of the final Messenger, so the second person of the warning is not theatre of the past. What was said to the first generation is said to the generation now standing over the same terms, and the mercy that spared them is the same mercy that has held catastrophe back since. The instruction aimed at that audience is therefore precise. A reprieve named as grace is not a guarantee. It is a window that obliges gratitude, and gratitude in covenant terms is compliance with the terms sealed. The losers named at the close are all who let the window pass while their backs are turned.
