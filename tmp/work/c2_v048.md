The reminder of favour now leads to a warning: ***“Guard yourselves against the Day”***. The Day is the Resurrection and the judgement that follows it. No one can guard himself by making that Day fail to arrive. The protection sought is from its punishment, through faith, repentance, and obedience while the chance to act remains. This gives the command a practical force. It does not ask for alarm with no way of responding. It asks the hearers to stop relying on forms of rescue that will not lie in human hands there. The reason is stated elsewhere: (82:19 — **“all authority on that Day belongs to Allah ˹entirely˺”**). God has not lost command in worldly life and regained it only then. On that Day, however, the powers that people mistook for a final refuge will plainly fail them. The warning therefore follows the reminder of honour closely. An honoured ancestry cannot turn the final judgement into a favour owed to every descendant, regardless of his own response to God.

**NO FRIEND CAN TAKE OVER ANOTHER PERSON’S ACCOUNT**

It is the Day ***“on which no soul will be of help to another.”***. Al-Suddī explains this as one person being unable to suffice for another. The broad wording leaves no special right for a close relative or a person of rank. Even the strongest family bond is included: (31:33 — **“no parent will be of any benefit to their child, nor will a child be of any benefit to their parent.”**). Abū Hurayrah’s report in al-Bukhārī and Muslim records the Prophet warning his daughter Fāṭimah that he cannot avail her against God. The warning removes a claim of safety based simply on being his child. It does not deny a prayer or plea that God Himself permits. The point is that the family relation has no power to compel a favourable judgement. Someone who owes obedience cannot appoint another person to have obeyed in his place, as though one account could be settled by borrowing a neighbour’s life. This is why the verse speaks to people who take pride in their fathers. Their fathers’ righteousness is not a payment that makes their own refusal harmless.

The rule does not prevent God from settling wrongs done between people. That is His judgement, not a private arrangement by which a friend buys another friend’s release. In al-Bukhārī, Abū Hurayrah reports the Prophet’s warning to seek release from wrongs done to others before a Day with no gold or silver to pay them. Good deeds are then taken to meet the injured person’s claim; if they run out, the wrongdoer bears a further burden of sins. The transfer follows his own wrongdoing. It is not an innocent person volunteering to erase another’s guilt. The distinction matters because the failure of earthly means does not leave the injured without justice. The creditor’s claim has not vanished merely because money can no longer settle it. A person should therefore put a wrong right while he can restore what he owes and seek pardon. Neither a generous friend nor a noble relative can grant him leave to keep another person’s right unpaid.

**A PLEA HAS NO AUTHORITY APART FROM GOD’S LEAVE**

The next denial is ***“No intercession will be accepted,”***. Intercession is a plea made for someone else, seeking a benefit or removal of harm. Here it cannot serve as a promised escape for one who persists in rejecting God’s message. Ibn ʿAbbās relates the denial to the unbelieving soul. A reading that extends it to every sinful believer would conflict with the permission stated elsewhere: intercession brings no benefit (20:109 — **“except by those granted permission by the Most Compassionate and whose words are agreeable to Him”**). Even the angels (21:28 — **“They do not intercede except for whom He approves,”**). The accepted plea is therefore itself subject to God, not a power set beside Him. The absolute-denial reading is not adopted. Abū Dāwūd and al-Tirmidhī carry Anas’s report that the Prophet’s intercession is for people of his community who have committed major sins. That report prevents the warning from being turned into a claim that every such sinner is beyond help forever. It does not promise a sinner control over the outcome or make sin safe. The difference is between a plea that God allows in mercy and an appeal that someone imagines God must accept because of the speaker’s rank. Only the former is affirmed. Both the warning and the permission protect the same truth: the decision belongs to God.

**NO PRICE CAN BUY RELEASE AFTER REJECTION**

Nor will there be ***“no ransom taken,”***. A ransom is something offered in exchange for release. Abū al-ʿĀliyah explains the word in precisely that sense. The problem is not that the offender has brought too little and might succeed with a larger sum. The Quran puts the imagined price beyond ordinary wealth: (3:91 — **“Indeed, if each of those who disbelieve then die as disbelievers were to offer a ransom of enough gold to fill the whole world, it would never be accepted from them.”**). All the world’s gold would fail because the missing thing is not wealth. Money cannot turn a life ended in rejection into a life of faith. The verse is not denying the worth of giving while a person can still obey. A charitable gift made now in obedience and an offer made later to escape judgement are different acts. Nor does it cancel payments that God has lawfully prescribed for particular duties during earthly life. Those payments are accepted on His terms; they are not a right to bargain with Him about the final account. The warning closes a false hope that the rich can purchase what the poor must answer for.

**POWER CANNOT ENFORCE WHAT PLEADING CANNOT OBTAIN**

The last assurance is ***“and no help will be given”***. If no plea or price succeeds, there is no stronger ally waiting to force a release. Protection belongs to God, (23:88 — **“protecting ˹all˺ while none can protect against Him,”**). A group cannot defeat the judgement that each member is unable to escape. The closing breadth thus leaves no refuge in a ruler, a patron, or a power treated as divine. The several denials describe distinct hopes familiar from earthly life: another person may meet a debt, a respected voice may plead, money may ransom, or force may rescue. None is an independent means of escape before God. This is not a ban on helping people in the world where He commands mutual care. It is a warning not to mistake such limited help for power over His verdict. Since rescue cannot be organized against the Judge, the proper preparation is to turn toward Him. The hearers are still being addressed before the Day arrives. The warning takes away false shelters so that faith and repentance, rather than wealth or ancestry, govern their response.
