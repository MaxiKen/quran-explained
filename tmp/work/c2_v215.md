**ASKING WHAT TO SPEND**

The spending question is answered in phrases that name every door the money can enter: ***“They ask you ˹O Prophet in˺ what ˹way˺ they should donate. Say, “Whatever donations you give are”***, ***“for parents, relatives, orphans, the poor”***, ***“and ˹needy˺ travellers. Whatever good you do is certainly well known to Allah.””***. and the closing phrase records the knowing of the One who sees the giving. The walk converts a general question into a routed order: the relatives first, then the orphan, the poor, and the traveller, and the routing is the answer. The question is honoured by the list rather than dismissed by the principle, because the questioner wanted directions and the verse gives an address book. The occasion is fixed in the reports on the wealthy elder who asked what to spend and on whom to spend it.

**A WEALTHY MAN ASKS**

The occasion of the verse is drawn in the reports with a name and a purse: the wealthy elder of the emigrants who came asking what to spend and on whom to spend it. The report that Ibn ʿAbbās transmitted keeps the question sincere and specific, and the verse answers it with a routed list rather than with a platitude. The question is asked before the regulations of inheritance had fully arrived, which makes the answer the philanthropy page of the law rather than its estates page. The instruction of the lawful giving covers the same circle of beneficiaries and the same refusal of waste (17:26 — **“Give to close relatives their due, as well as the poor and ˹needy˺ travellers. And do not spend wastefully”**). Because the question is asked by a man with money in his hands, the answer is a routing order he can perform tomorrow. The measurement is the working half of the permission, and the working half is where the guardianship is either kept or lost.

**FIVE DOORS FOR THE DINAR**

The list of the beneficiaries is the verse's treasury map: parents, relatives, orphans, the poor, and the travellers, and the order is the order of proximity. The distribution law of the estates carries the same circle in the same language of the present and the needy (4:8 — **“If ˹non-inheriting˺ relatives, orphans, or the needy are present at the time of distribution, offer them a ˹small˺ provision from it”**). Because the doors are named, the giver cannot claim he did not know where to send the money. The application is to open one of the five doors with every disbursement and to walk through the doors in the verse's order. The list itself is a piece of the ethics, naming who stands closest.

**GOOD RECORDED**

The close of the verse adds the accounting that the giver most needs: whatever good you do, Allah knows it. The knowing is attached to the good rather than to the giver's reputation, which means the quiet gift is counted as loudly as the public one. Because the knowing is divine, the record of the giving is kept in a ledger the fraud cannot reach. The question of the wealthy elder is answered in full: the what is good, the who is five doors, and the accountant is Allah. The hand of the ownership is drawn elsewhere in the book with the calm of the treasury: the owner of the treasuries is the one who spends from the store rather than from the fear. The nafs of the one who has been purified is described in the early gloss as the source of the good spending, and the source is the self that has been trained by the light. Because the spending is described as a movement of the self rather than as a movement of the purse, the psychology of the generosity is worth studying at length. The one who asks what to spend has already taken the first step of the answer, and the asking itself is a form of the generosity. The guardians of the orphans and the keepers of the estate spend in the same legislation with the same calendar of the near and the far. Reviewing one’s own calendar of the spending is the closing errand of the week. The treasury of the owner does not run out, which is the economics behind the instruction. The calendar of the generosity is the only calendar whose year never ends, and the entries of the calendar are counted by the owner of the treasuries.
