**THE SUPREME VERSE**

***“Allah! There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him, the Ever-Living, All-Sustaining. Neither drowsiness nor sleep overtakes Him. To Him belongs whatever is”*** establishes the supreme verse. This āyah is known as Āyat al-Kursī, the Verse of the Footstool. It gathers the foundations of tawḥīd, Allah’s perfect life, sustaining authority, knowledge, ownership, permission, power, and transcendence. The verse moves from the declaration of divine oneness to the negation of every weakness and then to the affirmation of Allah’s complete dominion. The Prophet ﷺ told Ubayy ibn Kaʿb that it is the greatest verse in the Book of Allah, as recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Its greatness is not a magical property detached from understanding. It is great because it gives the heart a complete vision of who Allah is and why no created rival deserves worship. Allah is al-Ḥayy, the Ever-Living. His life is perfect, beginningless and everlasting, and unlike created life it is not dependent upon food, rest, time, or bodily processes. He is al-Qayyūm, the One who sustains Himself and sustains everything else. Every creature depends upon Him at every moment, while He depends upon no one. This pair of names gives the believer security. Human protectors tire, forget, die, or become unable to help. Allah never loses awareness or power. The servant can turn to Him in the night, in fear, poverty, illness, and confusion, knowing that the Sustainer is always living and never absent. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep. Human beings require rest because their bodies and minds are limited. Allah is not overcome by weakness, inattention, or fatigue. He does not need sleep in order to recover, and no moment of creation falls outside His care. The statement is also a spiritual comfort. When every human helper is unavailable, Allah remains aware. He hears the hidden prayer, sees the injustice that no court noticed, and preserves the universe without strain. (20:109 — **“On that Day no intercession will be of any benefit,”**)

**ALLAH IS AL-ḤAYY AND AL-QAYYŪM**

***“in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Who could possibly intercede with Him without His permission? He ˹fully˺ knows what is”*** deepens allah is al-ḥayy and al-qayyūm. The believer sleeps after taking lawful means because he trusts the Guardian who never sleeps. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. The phrase encompasses people, angels, animals, matter, wealth, power, and every visible and unseen realm. Human beings possess property only by delegated permission. This is why wealth must be earned lawfully, spent responsibly, and returned through charity and inheritance. Ownership also means that no created ruler has absolute authority. Governments, families, employers, and scholars exercise limited trusts. They cannot command disobedience to Allah or claim ownership of people’s souls. Tawḥīd liberates the servant from treating any human power as ultimate. Who can intercede with Him except by His permission. Intercession—shafāʿah—belongs to Allah. He may honour prophets, angels, and righteous servants by allowing them to intercede, but no created being can force Allah, negotiate as an equal, or independently remove punishment. This guards the believer from both denying revealed intercession and worshipping intermediaries. The verse’s question is rhetorical: no one can intercede without permission. Believers seek Allah directly through prayer, repentance, and obedience. They also hope for the Prophet’s intercession because Allah grants it, without invoking the Prophet ﷺ as an independent divine power. Allah knows what is before and behind them—what is present, future, past, visible, hidden, and connected to their circumstances. Created beings know only what Allah teaches them. They cannot encompass His knowledge except what He wills. The verse establishes Allah’s omniscience without requiring people to imagine how His knowledge relates to time. This is a source of accountability and comfort. The hidden intention behind a charity, the pain of a widow, and the secret injustice of a powerful person are all known. (34:23 — **“No intercession will be of any benefit with Him, except by those granted permission by Him.”**)

**NO DROWSINESS AND NO SLEEP**

***“ahead of them and what is behind them, but no one can grasp any of His knowledge—except what He wills ˹to reveal˺. His”*** clarifies no drowsiness and no sleep. A person cannot hide a deed from Allah, and a sincere act cannot be lost because humans failed to notice it. Allah says His Kursī extends over the heavens and the earth. The Kursī is an unseen created reality associated with Allah’s dominion. Classical reports and exegetes discuss it as a footstool distinct from the ʿArsh, the Throne. The Qur’an does not require us to imagine its form or measure it through human comparison. Affirming the verse means affirming what Allah said without likening Him to creation. The vastness of the Kursī points to the greatness of the created heavens and earth and, by implication, the immeasurable greatness of their Creator. Allah is not contained by the Kursī or by any created space. Preserving them does not tire Him. The universe is maintained without effort, weakness, or recovery. Human administrators become exhausted by a single household or institution; Allah sustains all worlds simultaneously. This refutes any idea that creation places a burden upon Him or that He needs assistants to complete His power. The statement also gives believers confidence in divine care. The laws of nature, the order of life, and the final resurrection are not difficult for Allah. What appears impossible to humans is within His effortless power. Al-ʿAliyy is the Most High, exalted above creation and every imperfection. Al-ʿAẓīm is the Most Great, possessing perfect majesty. These names complete the verse by directing worship upward in meaning: the heart honours Allah above every rival, fear, desire, and authority. Reciting Āyat al-Kursī should therefore produce humility and reliance. It is not a charm recited while a person continues to worship wealth, fame, or superstition. Its protection is realized through faith, remembrance, and obedience. (53:26 — **“˹Imagine˺ how many ˹noble˺ angels are in the heavens!”**)

**COMPLETE OWNERSHIP**

***“Seat encompasses the heavens and the earth, and the preservation of both does not tire Him. For He is the Most High, the Greatest”*** closes with complete ownership. Think of the supreme verse through a caravan that crosses a country where the guards of the road change every few miles, and one night the travellers learn that the whole route lies inside the lands of a single lord. The tents are pitched in the open, the horses are untied, and nobody in the caravan sleeps badly after the letter is read. The supreme verse confronts allah is al-ḥayy and al-qayyūm in a reader who cannot sleep because he has measured his troubles against his own strength. The verse gives him a Lord who does not doze, a throne that covers the sky and the ground. And a knowledge that is opened by permission, and it asks him whose keeping he is relying on when he shuts his eyes. Because the close names the Most High and the Greatest, showing through the supreme verse that the night watch is kept by the One who never kept it in a hurry. The warning about the intercession is repeated with its day in the preceding āyah; the supreme verse also the call of a messenger who was sent to the whole world comes in another chapter. Because the supreme verse opens into allah is al-ḥayy and al-qayyūm, no drowsiness and no sleep joins the supreme verse to allah is al-ḥayy and al-qayyūm. Since no drowsiness and no sleep develops the supreme verse, allah is al-ḥayy and al-qayyūm carries the transition. Complete ownership matters, which means no drowsiness and no sleep answers allah is al-ḥayy and al-qayyūm. Allah is al-ḥayy and al-qayyūm gives practical form to the supreme verse; complete ownership sets its horizon. Today, the supreme verse shapes allah is al-ḥayy and al-qayyūm in present decisions. (6:59 — **“With Him are the keys of the unseen—no one knows them except Him.”**)
