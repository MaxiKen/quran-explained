**PROTECTING A WOMAN’S CHOICE AFTER DIVORCE**

***“When you have divorced women and they have reached the end of their waiting”*** establishes protecting a woman’s choice after divorce. This verse addresses families and guardians who might obstruct a divorced woman from remarrying her former husband after the waiting period, even when both parties agree to return in a lawful and honourable manner. The prohibition of obstruction protects her agency. Divorce does not make her permanently controlled by relatives, nor does anger give a guardian the right to decide her future for selfish reasons. The verse does not command a woman to return to her former husband. It protects the possibility when she and the man freely agree and when the marriage can be safe and sound. If she does not want to return, no family member may force her. Consent and the ability to assess harm remain central to marriage. The Companions reported an incident involving Maʿqil ibn Yasār, whose sister had been divorced and later wished to return to her former husband. Maʿqil resisted out of anger, and this verse was revealed; he then accepted Allah’s instruction. The report, recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, shows that even a protective brother could allow personal hurt to become an unjust barrier. The lesson is not that every former marriage should be restored. It is that guardians must distinguish genuine concern from revenge, status anxiety, tribal pride, or the desire to punish the former husband. **THE OCCASION OF REVELATION**

***“period, do not prevent them from re-marrying their ex-husbands if they come to an honourable”*** deepens the occasion of revelation. They should advise honestly and then respect a lawful decision made by the adults concerned. The phrase tarāḍaw baynahum bi-l-maʿrūf requires mutual agreement and a lawful, recognized process. Both former spouses must consent without coercion. They should discuss the causes of the earlier divorce, expectations about finances and relatives, safety, and the welfare of children. Returning without addressing the prior harm can repeat the same cycle. According to what is acceptable excludes secret arrangements that violate legal rights, deception, or the use of marriage to regain control. It includes a valid contract, proper witnesses and guardianship according to the applicable legal school, and protection of the woman’s financial and personal rights. A walī has a protective and supportive role, not a licence for arbitrary obstruction. If a guardian prevents a suitable marriage out of spite, the matter can be taken to a qualified authority, who may transfer the guardianship according to the rules of Islamic law. The Prophet ﷺ said that when someone whose religion and character are acceptable seeks marriage, people should help it rather than create corruption and fitnah, as Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī records. At the same time, guardians may raise genuine concerns about abuse, addiction, dishonesty, or inability to maintain obligations. **AGREEMENT IN A HONOURABLE MANNER**

***“agreement. This is enjoined on whoever has faith in Allah and the Last Day. This”*** clarifies agreement in a honourable manner. Advice must be based on evidence, not rumour or class prejudice. The goal is the woman’s welfare, not family control. Allah calls non-obstruction purer and more virtuous. Removing resentment, allowing forgiveness, and respecting lawful choice purifies the community from prolonged feud. Sometimes a former couple can rebuild the marriage with maturity; sometimes the discussion itself closes old hostility. In both cases, justice is better than family revenge. The language does not romanticize reconciliation. A return is good only if the couple can keep Allah’s limits. Where there was serious abuse, stalking, or danger, preventing renewed contact may be protection rather than obstruction. Expert counselling and legal safeguards can help distinguish the cases. The admonition is directed to those who believe in Allah and the Last Day. Faith should change how family members exercise power. A person who remembers the Hereafter does not treat a daughter, sister, or divorced woman as an instrument for honour. He knows that he will answer for every barrier he placed before a lawful choice. The verse also warns the woman and former husband not to use the permission as a way to deceive relatives or evade responsibilities. All parties remain under Allah’s knowledge.
