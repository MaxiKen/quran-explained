**A REPENTANCE THAT OPENS**

The exception arrives in three movements. ***“As for those who repent, mend their ways, and let the truth be known”***. Repent. Repair. Publish. The verse that follows the widest verdict installs the narrowest door. Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss unpacks the three movements at their full size. The repentance turns back from the old course. The mending is the worship of Allah alone without partners added to the household of the heart. The publishing is the making-manifest of the very traits that were hidden. The point of the third movement is surgical. The crime was a hiding. The repair includes a showing. What follows is that the repentance is not a private mood. It has a public limb. The effect of the verse is a door that opens onto the street. The one who buried the description is invited to publish it. The turn is accepted, and the accepting is announced in the first person at the end of the line.

**THREE MOVEMENTS BACK TO HIM**

The structure of the offer deserves its own ledger. The first movement is the exit from the old allegiance. The second is the repair of the conduct. The third is the letting of the truth be known. The early commentators read the third as the un-doing of the concealment's own effect in public. The hidden word returns to the market-stand. The same invitation is issued to the despairing servants in the plainest terms (39:53 — **“Do not lose hope in Allah’s mercy, for Allah certainly forgives all sins. He is indeed the All-Forgiving, Most Merciful”**). Do not lose hope. The mercy is addressed to the ones who have exceeded the limits against their own souls. The difference between the two passages is the audience, not the door. One speaks to the concealers of proofs. The other speaks to the over-doers of sins. The mercy covers both addresses. What follows is that no one in the two crowds is standing outside the offer. The movements are available in every city.

**MERCY FOR THE SHOWING**

The closing names complete the offer's paperwork. ***“they are the ones to whom I will turn ˹in forgiveness˺”***. The turn of the Lord answers the turn of the servant. The names at the close are personal and open: ***“for I am the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful”***. The closing summons of the book places the same work in the servant's hands as a practice (110:3 — **“then glorify the praises of your Lord and seek His forgiveness, for certainly He is ever Accepting of Repentance”**). Glorify. Seek. The seeking is the movement the door is built for. For the modern reader the third movement is the hardest and the most modern. This age runs apology industries and hiding industries side by side. The verse refuses the apology without the publication. The mend is counted only when the truth is let out. The two names at the end hold the account open for the reader still walking. The acceptance is His profession. The mercy is His nature. The showing is the servant's remaining work, and the work is light.

**THE DOOR WITH THREE STEPS**

The design of the exception is the practical heart of the whole passage. The verdict of the previous verse was wide enough to cover a generation of concealers. The door of this verse is built to fit a human being. Turn. Repair. Show. The three steps scale from a single convert to a whole household of teachers. The effect of the publishing-clause is that the repentance reaches the same public square where the concealment once stood. The truth that was buried is walked back into the light by the same people who buried it. The reason the first-person promise closes the verse is that the steps are heavy enough to need a promise. The accepting is announced by the one who receives the turning. For the modern reader the three steps are the whole curriculum of repair. This age runs apology industries that skip the publishing and mend industries that skip the turning. The verse keeps the three in one line and refuses to sell a shorter version. The door is narrow. The steps are clear. The work at the end of them is light because the accepting is done by the master of the door.
