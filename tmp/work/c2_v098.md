**THE SUPPLEMENT THAT HONORS BY NAME**

The summons of the last verse widens into a list. Its last names are supplements to a category that already holds them: ***“Whoever is an enemy of Allah, His angels, His messengers, Gabriel”***. The tradition reads the figure as honor. The book names dates and pomegranates after naming fruit in general, so that the specific is called out of the collective for its rank. The two names were mapped in the consonantal tradition in several sound-forms each. The old lexica offer the theophoric shape through ʿIkrimah: the first syllables of both names mean servant, and the last means God. The angels are named as messengers elsewhere in the book as well, so the road from the general to the specific is paved in the book's own usage. The naming is diplomatic as well as doctrinal. The two figures whose reputations were bartered in the market of excuses now stand in the summons by name, so that no later court can claim the sentence was aimed at someone else. The reading that takes the names as the honor-roll of the errand is the reading the sentence's order supports.

**THE STATUS MARKED IN THE SENTENCE**

The second clause closes the syllogism and then does rarer work: ***“and Michael, then ˹let them know that˺ Allah is certainly the enemy of the disbelievers”***. The final noun of the sentence drew the glosses' eye. The line could have ended with the enemy of them. It ends by naming the disbelievers, so the verdict carries the class-status and not only the pronoun. The doctrine of the enmity has two directions, and the tradition lays both out. The servant's enmity toward God is disobedience and hostility to His friends. The enmity attributed to God is the chastising and the display of its effects, since the seeking of harm that defines the word applies to creatures and not to the Creator. The war-rule under the sentence is kept in the tradition's most quoted line on the subject: whoever is an enemy to a friend of God, God makes war on him. The scene that produced the verse was preserved in its full dialogue by the report Anas b. Malik transmitted, in the collection of Bukhārī: *"I am going to ask you about three things which nobody knows except a Prophet"* — the first portent of the Hour, the first meal of the people of Paradise, and the mystery of paternal and avuncular resemblance. When the prophet answered that Gabriel had just told him, the rabbi said: *"He, among all the angels, is the enemy of the Jews."* The prophet recited the summons of the verse in reply.

**THE GRAVAMEN BEHIND THE ANIMUS**

The fuller account of the grievance shows why one angel stood trial and not the angelic order. The same transmission keeps the complaint in its depth. The bringer had come down with punishment and fighting and hardship many times. The hardest of his errands was the prophecy that the Temple would be razed at the hands of a named conqueror at a stated time. The prophecy was delivered and then met its date. The animus toward the courier is therefore the animus toward the docket he kept delivering. The preferred angel of the complaint — the one of rain and fertility and security — was preferred because his mail never rebuked anyone. The selection runs on the same commerce the section has priced since the woe of the writing verse. The merchandise, not the messenger, was always the test.

**A COURIER WHO CANNOT BE SHOPPED**

The refutation's logic is then stated in the tradition's own doctrinal form, and its reach is long. The bringer is a messenger and acts only as a messenger under a command he did not author. The hostility owed to the commission is owed to the commissioner, and the summons names that transfer as its legal effect. The volume the courier carried stands on its own record in any case. It confirms the earlier books instead of contradicting them. It carries the complete guidance out of the kinds of error. It announces the good of both worlds to those who believe. To deny such a volume because of hostility toward the angel who bore it repeats the method the section has named three ways already, the shopping of messages by their cost. The decree against the method is flat where the book rules on defiance: (58:20 — **“˹As for˺ those who defy Allah and His Messenger, they will definitely be among the most debased”**). A caution kept in the later tradition guards the doctrine's other flank. The descent upon the heart is not to be read as a delivery of meanings without words, since the revelation's own verses name the Arabic tongue as its form.

**THE NAMES ENTERED INTO THE SUMMONS**

The closing lesson of the verse is procedural and permanent at once. The two angelic names stand in the summons so that the trial of reputations cannot be retried under new captions. The categories are named. The individuals are named. The transfer of the enmity to its true object is stated in the sentence's final noun. Wherever a modern congregation has learned to resent the deliveries that rebuke it while crowning the deliveries that soothe it, the verse keeps the same court in session. Reading the list slowly shows the whole anatomy of excuse-making laid out for inspection. The courier, the commission, the commissioner, and the merchandise stand named in order, so the blame cannot be parked at the wrong address again.
