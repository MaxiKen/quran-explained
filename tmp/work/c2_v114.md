**THE GATEKEEPERS OF THE NAMES**

The passage now files the worst case of the whole campaign: ***“Who does more wrong than those who prevent Allah’s Name from being mentioned in His places of worship and strive to destroy them? ”***. The question is the passage's rhetorical maximum. Nothing exceeds the deed at hand. Ibn ʿAbbās gives the case its history-book face. The reference is to the ruler of the Byzantines, Tātūs, who destroyed Jerusalem. The same gloss records the manner of the profanation: carrion thrown into the sanctuary, the call to prayer stifled, the declaring of the oneness made a crime in its own house. The received tradition preserves the second reading of the case alongside the first. The verse was revealed to inform of the Byzantine destruction of the holy house, and it was also read as covering the idolaters' barring of the messenger from Mecca. The two readings agree on the anatomy. The gatekeepers of the names are the worst offenders in the passage's own scale.

**TATUS AND THE CARRION**

The historical case deserves its own look, because the deed it records is the deed the verse is measuring. A conquering power takes a city and then works on its house of worship. The name is removed from the walls. The carrion is thrown in to make the floor unclean for prayer. The endowments are cut. The received record notes how long the ruin lasted until the Muslims recovered the city and restored the house to its work. The effect of the ruling is to weigh the function of the house above its walls, which means the silenced mention is the deeper ruin. The judgment of the passage on the deed is the judgment of a building code written in the ownership of the names. The houses are the places where the name is mentioned. The preventer of the mention is therefore the arsonist of the function, not merely the occupant of the address. A damaged building and a silenced house are losses of different orders. The building can be repaired in a season. The silenced house needs the name returned to the tongue. The covenant judgment of the tie-breakers fits the deed without alteration: (2:27 — **“those who violate Allah’s covenant after it has been affirmed, break whatever ˹ties˺ Allah has ordered to be maintained,”**). The destroying of the houses is the breaking of the ties in mortar.

**THE HOUSES AND THEIR RIGHTS**

The verse then legislates the standing of the houses: ***“Such people have no right to enter these places except with fear.”***, and the penalty stands on both sides of the settlement: ***“For them is disgrace in this world, and they will suffer a tremendous punishment in the Hereafter”***. The received tradition of the passage widens the protection to the whole estate. The mosques of the world stand in one respect. The sanctity of the two sanctuaries and the mosque of the prophet is the sanctity of every mosque where the name is mentioned. The destruction the verse condemns is therefore never local news. The fire clause of the penalty is written in the passage of the refusers without variation: (2:81 — **“and are engrossed in sin will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever”**), and the order that the houses were built to keep is the order the corrupters reverse: (2:11 — **“When they are told, “Do not spread corruption in the land,” they reply, “We are only peace-makers!””**). The house of the name is the land at its most ordered. The defiler of the house spreads the corruption at its source.

**THE HOUSES AROUND THE READER**

The case at hand reaches past the history-books into the ordinary estate of every reader. The houses of the name are not only the famous addresses. They are the rooms where the name is mentioned at home, the circles where the book is read at work, the quiet corners of a neighborhood mosque whose roof nobody budgets for. The verse's question stays open across the estate. The verse's question names the one who silences the mention and unmakes the house as the worst case on the scale. The preventer of the mention is the worst case on the passage's own scale because the mention is the function of the whole address. The place stands for the name. The name does not stand for the place. The reader who keeps that order keeps the building code of the passage, and the code holds in this world's estate exactly as it will hold at the settlement of the next. Let the name be mentioned. Let the house stand.
