**THE LISTENER WHO CANNOT BE REACHED**

The sūrah has finished describing the people the Book guides, and now it turns to the ones it cannot reach: ***“As for those who persist in disbelief, it is the same whether you warn them”***. The warning in view is not a scolding. It is the act of telling a person plainly what is coming while there is still time to turn, and the verse says that for one kind of listener the act changes nothing. That is a hard thing to hear at the opening of the longest sūrah in the Book, and it is placed here before any command, because the reader is being taught what a message can and cannot do. The Book is delivered; it does not force itself on the hand that receives it.

The reader is also the person who warns others, in his house, at his work, among his friends, which is why the verse is a mercy to him as much as a warning. A doctor who tells a patient the truth and watches him keep smoking has done his work; a teacher who repeats the same correction for three years and sees it land after the boy has left school has done hers. The verse draws the line between delivering a message and producing a result, and it draws that line early. In the reading reported from Ibn ʿAbbās, the verse concerns those who had settled into their refusal and would not move, and the report shows how the early community held the two sides together: keep warning, and do not measure yourself by the answer.

**WHY A WARNING CAN BE SPENT AND WASTED**

The verse describes people who hear and are unmoved, and the Qur'an says the same thing again in a line that could be its twin: (36:10 — **“It is the same whether you warn them or not—they will never believe”**). The repetition is not fatalism; it is diagnosis. When a person has decided that he will not shift, more evidence does not help him. It gives him one more thing to explain away, one more reason to suspect the person bringing it. That is why the warning is described as the same either way, since what has closed is not the supply of proof but the appetite for it. Anyone who has defended a position he privately knew was weak understands the mechanism without needing it explained. The second phrase, ***“or not they will never believe”***, is where readers usually slow down, and the slowness is right. A statement about an outcome is not the same thing as a cause of it, and the Book holds the two together everywhere else. The same revelation commands the warning, praises the messenger who grieves for his people, and records crowds who believed after years of resistance: (10:96 — **“Indeed, those against whom Allah's decree ˹of torment˺ is justified will not believe—”**). What the line describes is a condition that persistence produces, not a verdict handed down on people before they have done anything. The difference matters because the reader still has a say in which condition he ends in.

**KEEPING THE MESSAGE AND THE RESULT APART**

A parent can say the same true sentence every year and see nothing move, and a friend can say it once and be dismissed. This verse places the outcome of both beyond the speaker's jurisdiction, which is a relief when it is understood and a temptation when it is misread. Read badly, it can excuse laziness, as though nothing said matters because the result is fixed. Read badly in the other direction, it can excuse harshness, as though the truth only had to be discharged like a debt and the manner of its delivery were irrelevant. The sūrah will later require that the invitation be made with wisdom and a good word, which rules out both misreadings. The better use of the verse is to turn its mirror round. A reader who has been hearing the same call for years should ask whether he is in this line rather than reading about other people in it. He already knows one thing that is being asked of him, and he has a reason for the delay, and the reason has been available long enough to feel like a fact. That is exactly how the condition described here begins, because the death of the heart does not announce itself. It looks like a busy week. It looks like a disagreement he will settle later, and it looks like patience with people who are not ready to hear the answer yet. The verse exists to make that visible today, before another reading passes the same way.
