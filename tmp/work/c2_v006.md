**HEARTS SET AGAINST THE WARNING**

A sharp contrast now places the guided beside the people who will not arrive: ***“As for those who persist in disbelief”***. The sentence is placed immediately after the promise given to the mindful, and the placing is deliberate, because the two portraits sit side by side so that the reader can tell them apart. The Arabic word for disbelief carries the sense of covering something over, and al-Kalbī and others explained the group here as those who had heard the message and buried it. Ibn ʿAbbās named the leaders of Mecca and the men of the clans who had made themselves the enemies of the Prophet, and other early teachers named the Jews of Madinah, since both groups stood in the way of the message with full knowledge of it. The verse does not describe a person who never heard; it describes a person who heard, understood and covered the matter up.

**A WARNING REFUSED BEFORE IT ARRIVES**

The sentence continues with the statement that is meant to stop the Prophet from carrying a burden that is not his: ***“it is the same whether you warn them or not”***. The Arabic word for warning carries the sense of giving a person information that also frightens him, and the verse says the effort has been spent on the wrong kind of listener. Another sūrah states the same thing in almost the same words, and its repetition marks the sentence as a settled fact of the message: (36:10 — **“It is the same whether you warn them or not—they will never believe”**). The reason the sentence stands here is that the one who carried the message would grieve over people who would not answer him, and the sūrah removes the guilt before it can take root. A messenger is answerable for delivering the message, and the verse settles the difference between the delivery and the result. That the warning was sent at all is the proof that the caller did his work, and the answer belongs to the One who gave the warning its moment and its hearer.

**THE INVITATION THAT STILL STANDS**

A reader may take the verse as a reason to stop inviting people, and the reading is a mistake for a reason that the sūrah itself supplies. The verse is a statement about people whose case God has already judged, and it is addressed to the Prophet as comfort, not to the community as an instruction to give up. The Book commands the invitation in every period and describes no one as beyond it, and the door remains open until a person dies; the very next verses in this sūrah describe people who still have something to answer for. What turns on the difference is the state of the heart rather than the label a person carries, since the one who covers the truth today may be exposed to it tomorrow, and the Book tells the reader to keep speaking to him on that chance. The duty to invite is not suspended by the knowledge that God has recorded what a man will do, because the record is God's and the speech is the servant's. History is full of people who fought the message and then carried it, and the beginning of that road is a warning that reached them after all.

**A FIRE BRIGADE AND A HOUSE WITH NO EXITS**

Think of men who arrive at a burning house and find that the owner has nailed shut the doors from the inside. They shout through the boards, and the shouting is still the right thing to do, though it cannot reach a man who has decided to hear nothing. The verse tells the caller where his responsibility ends, and it does so without giving him permission to despise anyone. These days a person may find that his own family has stopped listening to the things he cares about most, and the passage spares him a false guilt while keeping his duty to speak with kindness. What follows in the sūrah explains why the doors were closed in the first place, and the explanation begins with the next verse, which names what happened to the instruments a person hears and sees with. The verse that carries the sentence of no belief ends the passage on a note that the reader should not soften: ***“they will never believe”*** while the covering lasts, and the reader is left with the difference between a warning that fails and a warning that was never given.
