**WHEN REFUSAL HARDENS INTO A DESCRIPTION**

The passage now turns from those who accept guidance to ***“As for those who persist in disbelief, it is the same whether you warn them”***. The translation adds the sense of persistence because the verse describes refusal that has settled into a trait. This difference matters because the words do not close the door on every person who is now outside the faith. Many who first fought the Messenger later believed. The early reports point instead to people whose refusal had become firm. Some readers named hostile chiefs in Makkah. Some named learned men among the People of the Book who knew the signs yet hid them. Others took the words as a broad account of all whom God knew would die in denial. Ibn ʿAbbās linked the verse to people who rejected what came from the Lord while claiming faith in earlier books. The Arabic term for this denial comes from a root for covering or hiding. Arabs used the same root for night because its dark veil covers things. They also used it for a sower who covers seed with soil. This background matters because denial here covers a truth already made known. This is not a passing doubt or an honest search that has not yet reached its end. It is a stance held against the light already given. That is why a like verdict says, (10:96 — **“Indeed, those against whom Allah’s decree ˹of torment˺ is justified will not believe—”**). God speaks with full knowledge of their end. No human being may turn that knowledge into a guess about another person’s heart.

**WHAT A WARNING PLACES BEFORE ITS HEARER**

The middle of the verse sets two acts side by side. In this case, the result for the hearers is the same. Yet warning means more than the bare transfer of facts. The early teachers defined it as news joined to fear. The reason is that it names a danger while time remains to avoid it. A prophet warns out of care, not from a wish to harm or shame. The warning is like a clear sign placed before a road that ends in ruin. If a driver passes the sign, its truth and worth do not vanish. The equality here concerns the response of these fixed rejecters. Therefore, it does not make faithful warning equal to silence in the Messenger’s duty. He is still told, (88:21 — **“So, ˹continue to˺ remind ˹all, O Prophet˺, for your duty is only to remind”**). At the same time, force lies outside that duty: (88:22 — **“You are not ˹there˺ to compel them ˹to believe˺”**). Another verse draws the same line: (5:99 — **“The Messenger’s duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺. And Allah ˹fully˺ knows what you reveal and what you conceal”**). A rejected warning still makes the truth plain and fulfils a trust. The point is that refusal cannot turn sound delivery into failure. Nor can anyone use this verse to give up on a person after one refusal. No preacher knows that person’s final state. The message is therefore given with care, while its fruit is left to God.

**A LIMIT SET ON GRIEF, NOT ON THE MESSAGE**

The close, ***“or not—they will never believe”***, allows both a present and a future sense. They do not believe, and they will not believe. The same words return in another place: (36:10 — **“It is the same whether you warn them or not—they will never believe”**). Ibn ʿAbbās reported that the Messenger longed for all people to believe and follow the right road. This is why he did not carry the task of opening every heart. It did not tell him to stop speaking. The Qur’an keeps a person’s answer and God’s gift of guidance in the same view. The truth is offered in the open: (18:29 — **“And say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “˹This is˺ the truth from your Lord. Whoever wills let them believe,”**). Yet the Messenger cannot make faith take root in one he loves, (28:56 — **“You surely cannot guide whoever you like ˹O Prophet˺, but it is Allah Who guides whoever He wills, and He knows best”**). God does not wait for events to learn what people will choose. In other words, His prior knowledge is complete before the choice appears in time. Their choice is also real, the warning is just, and the answer remains theirs. The verse thus sets a bound on grief without making unbelief small. It also prepares for the next verse, where sealed hearts and covered sight show the end of long refusal. Each act of hiding truth trains the soul to hide it again. A warning should be met while the heart can still hear it, not tested until refusal has become its settled name.
