**EVERY FACE TURNS SOMEWHERE**

The verse files the whole qiblah dispute inside one observation. ***“Everyone turns to their own direction ˹of prayer˺.”***. Every party in the city has a facing. The claimants of exclusive direction are answered with a landscape. The turn is universal. The ownership of the turn is not. The comment of Ibn ʿAbbās reads the sentence as a settling of the argument. The faces will keep turning. The verse now turns the page from facing to doing. What follows is that the qiblah is recast as a starting position rather than a trophy. The point of the placement is that the dispute over directions was always a doorway. The door opens onto the street of deeds. The effect of the sentence is a demotion of the polemic. The jeerers can keep their geography. The believers are handed a race. The demotion is itself a gift, because the energy spent on the argument returns to the arms of the good where it belongs.

**A RACE WORTH ENTERING**

The command that follows is the surah's most athletic sentence. ***“So compete with one another in doing good.”***. The grammar is a summons to the front. Race one another. The good is the track. The parallel of the forgiveness-promise names the prize at the end of the track (3:133 — **“And hasten towards forgiveness from your Lord and a Paradise as vast as the heavens and the earth, prepared”**). Hasten. The verb of the race appears again in the promise. The reading of Qatādah keeps the racing here in the register of deeds rather than words. The arms of the good are the arms of the obedient. Compete in the prayer. Compete in the fasting. Compete in the charity and the patience. The same racing-heart is described where the front-runners are named (23:61 — **“it is they who race to do good deeds, always taking the lead”**). The race has lanes. The lanes are the deeds. The winning is measured by the one who owns the track.

**GATHERED BY THE CAPABLE**

The verse closes the competition with a guarantee and a name. ***“Wherever you are, Allah will bring you all together ˹for judgment˺.”*** ***“Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything”***. The gathering is certain. The scattered runners on their scattered lanes finish at one ground. The capability named at the end is the reason the certainty can be absolute. For the modern reader the two closing clauses fix the ethics of competition itself. Every race of the market ends at a boardroom of one. This age runs a thousand races and posts no finish line. The verse installs one. The good is competed in here. The gathering is administered there. The capable one handles both the track and the tape. The faces turned in one direction this season will stand on one ground in the next. The race is worth entering because the gathering is guaranteed by the name that ends the sentence.

**THE FINISH LINE INSTALLED**

The demotion of the direction-dispute at the opening of the verse is the permission slip for the race. Once the faces are free to turn, the hands are free to work. The effect of the sequence is a full re-orientation of religious energy. The argument about the map is closed so the traffic can move. The point of the gathering-clause is that the race is timed by an organiser who also holds the results. The runners do not certify their own finish. The clock of the race is held by the one who also names the medals at the entrance. The same economy of deeds is drawn in its smallest unit at the scales of the last day, and the same certainty is kept for the front-runners named elsewhere in the book. For the modern reader the finish line is the missing piece of every competing culture. This age runs endless races with the finish line edited out, and the runners discover the omission at the edge of exhaustion. The verse installs the line and posts the organiser's name above it. The good is competed in now. The gathering is administered later. The capability at the end of the sentence covers both halves of the schedule. The runners of the market and the runners of the mosque answer to the same organiser in the end.
