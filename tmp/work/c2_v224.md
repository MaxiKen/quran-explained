**OATHS CANNOT BLOCK THE GOOD**

***“Do not use Allah’s Name in your oaths as an”*** establishes oaths cannot block the good. The verse warns against swearing an oath and then treating it as a reason not to do good. A person may say, By Allah, I will never give charity, I will never speak to that relative, or By Allah, I will not reconcile with my spouse. He then imagines that keeping the oath requires continuing in miserliness, estrangement, or conflict. Allah forbids making His name an obstacle to righteousness. An oath is serious, but it is not stronger than an explicit duty or a good act Allah loves. The person should not casually swear, and he should not use the name of Allah to give emotional force to a harmful decision. When an oath blocks a better and lawful action, the Sunnah provides a way to break it and offer the prescribed expiation. The three examples—righteousness, taqwā, and reconciliation—cover private worship and social repair. Birr includes charity, kinship, justice, and service. Taqwā includes obeying Allah and avoiding sin. Iṣlāḥ means restoring peace between people. A stubborn oath can obstruct all three when it turns anger into a permanent moral rule. The verse especially protects family and community relationships. A person may be deeply hurt and need boundaries, but he should not make a sacred oath that prevents every possibility of forgiveness or necessary communication. Reconciliation does not mean returning to an abusive situation without protection. It means not allowing a personal oath to make reform impossible. The expiation is explained in 5:89: feeding ten needy people from the average food one gives one’s family, clothing them, or freeing a slave; whoever cannot do these fasts three days.
