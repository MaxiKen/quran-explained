**A BOOK THAT SEPARATES ONE THING FROM ANOTHER**

The history moves from the failure to the gift that would have prevented it: ***“And ˹remember˺ when We gave Moses the Scripture”***, ***“the standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺”***, ***“that perhaps you would be ˹rightly˺ guided”***. The Book is described here by its function rather than by its size or its age. It is a criterion, which means its job is to make distinctions a person could not make on his own, and the two things it separates are right and wrong. The clause that follows explains why such a tool was given: so that the community would be guided, which implies that without the distinction they would not be. The Book makes the same point about the same revelation in another sūrah where the giving of the scripture is named first and the guidance second: (2:87 — **“Indeed, We gave Moses the Book”**), with the verse continuing into an account of the messengers who came after him. The order is consistent everywhere in the Qur'an: the criterion arrives, and the guidance is the fruit of using it. The Book never presents itself as a collection of information for its own sake. It presents itself as the instrument by which a person tells the difference between two paths that often look similar.

**WHY A STANDARD WAS NEEDED AT ALL**

The episode of the calf explains the need in a way that an abstract argument could not. The people who made the idol had a religion; they had a history, a leader, and a set of experiences that no other nation could claim. What they did not have at that moment was a standard by which to test what they were doing while their leader was away. A criterion answers exactly that situation. It gives a person something to consult when the crowd is moving and the sound of the crowd is plausible, and the Book treats that function as the difference between a community that survives a crisis and one that does not. The Book also shows what happens when the standard is present and ignored, and the closing phrase about guidance quietly states the alternative. Whoever uses the criterion is guided by it; whoever leaves it on the shelf will find that other things guide him by default. That is the same mechanism the sūrah described at its opening when it spoke about the parable that guided some and tripped others. The instrument does not force anyone; it simply makes the difference visible to whoever is willing to look.

**USING A STANDARD INSTEAD OF AN IMPRESSION**

A reader can test whether he is using the criterion by asking what he consults when a decision is difficult. If the answer is his own impression of what seems reasonable, he has left the standard on the shelf, whatever his respect for it may be. If the answer is the text and the practice built on it, he is doing what this verse describes, and the guidance named at the end of the line is already at work in him. The Book does not require a person to be an expert in order to consult what has been given; it requires him to take the question to it rather than to his preferences. The practical step for today is to take one decision that has been drifting and to put it in front of the standard rather than in front of the crowd. The Book's whole account of the calf is a warning about the cost of doing the opposite. A person who consults the criterion in one matter has begun the habit that this verse was given to create, and the guidance that comes is not a mystery but the ordinary result of using the tool for the purpose it was made for.
