**GENEROSITY AT EVERY TIME**

***“Those who spend their wealth in charity day and night,”*** establishes generosity at every time. This verse praises those who spend by night and day, secretly and publicly. The fourfold description suggests continuity and variety. Charity is not limited to a yearly campaign or a public emergency. A believer remains ready to help when a need appears, whether people see the gift or not. The wording does not require giving money constantly or neglecting family needs. It praises a settled disposition of generosity. The person gives according to ability, fulfils obligations, and looks for lawful opportunities to benefit others. A small regular gift can be more sincere and sustainable than a large donation made once for reputation. Secret giving often protects sincerity and the recipient’s dignity. Public giving can encourage others, support a visible institution, or demonstrate that a public trust is being fulfilled. The verse includes both, showing that the form is not the only measure. Intention and effect determine whether public visibility serves good or vanity. The donor should avoid turning recipients into publicity. If a project requires names, photographs, or stories, consent and privacy matter. Public accounts should report funds accurately without making the poor bear the cost of the donor’s reputation. Night and day also represent ease and difficulty, private and public life, and times of prosperity and scarcity. A person who gives only when he has excess may never learn sacrifice. A person who gives beyond capacity may harm dependants. The Qur’an’s balance is to give from what is lawful and available while preserving rights. (2:261 — **“The example of those who spend their wealth in the cause of Allah is that of a”**) A report on generosity at every time comes through Ibn ʿAbbās, who connected the pattern of giving by night and day, secretly and openly, with ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib. The comprehensive wording also prevents a false choice between private and public generosity. Each can be sincere or corrupt, so the governing question is which form best serves the recipient while protecting the giver’s intention. Therefore the four settings describe moral completeness rather than four unrelated kinds of charity. Because visibility changes the temptations surrounding a gift, intention and the recipient’s welfare must govern the chosen form.

**SECRET AND PUBLIC GIVING**

***“secretly and openly—their reward is with their Lord, and there”*** deepens secret and public giving. A gift at night may be especially hidden from people, while a public gift during the day may mobilize a community. Allah knows the intention in both. The believer asks Him to make generosity a character rather than an occasional performance. Their reward is with their Lord assures the giver that no good is lost. People may reject, forget, or misuse the gift, but Allah records the intention and effort. The phrase also protects the donor from expecting gratitude. He may evaluate a charity’s effectiveness and adjust future giving, but he does not transform aid into a debt of personal loyalty. The reward belongs to Allah’s generosity. The servant does not claim that his charity obligates Allah, nor does he despair when the worldly outcome is unseen. The seed and garden parables earlier in the passage teach that reward can grow beyond human observation. The promise of no fear and no grief is especially meaningful for those who worry that giving will leave them insecure. Allah can place blessing in what remains and reward what was sent ahead. In the Hereafter, the generous servant is protected from the fear of losing the eternal account and the grief of discovering that his wealth was wasted. This promise does not guarantee a life without financial anxiety. Believers can face poverty, illness, or loss. It gives them a deeper security and teaches them to seek lawful help when needed rather than being ashamed of dependence during hardship. (2:215 — **“They ask you ˹O Prophet in˺ what ˹way˺ they should donate. Say,”**)

**SPENDING BY NIGHT AND DAY**

***“will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve”*** clarifies spending by night and day. Continuous generosity supports more than emergency relief. It can fund schools, hospitals, water systems, shelters, food programmes, debt relief, and legal aid. A community that spends night and day develops structures that protect people before a crisis becomes catastrophic. Such institutions require governance. Donors should verify accounts, trustees should disclose conflicts, and recipients should be treated with dignity. Generosity without accountability can unintentionally enable corruption. The preceding verses warn against Satan’s threat of poverty. This verse presents the opposite heart: a person who trusts Allah enough to give at different times and in different forms. He does not worship wealth or measure every relationship by profit. His generosity expresses certainty that provision ultimately belongs to Allah. Muslim records the Prophetic teaching that charity does not diminish wealth in its true account. Al-Bukhārī records his preference for the giving hand over the receiving hand, while the Sunnah protects dependants from destitution. Generosity is strongest when joined to wisdom. Think of generosity at every time through a woman who keeps a bowl of coins by her door and drops one in it on her way out and another on her way back in. The bowl fills slowly through the year, the neighbours who watch her never see the whole sum, and the orphans of the street eat through the winter without knowing where the bread comes from. Generosity at every time confronts secret and public giving in one present-day reader; spending by night and day meets, under the demand of generosity at every time, someone accustomed to hearing that charity belongs only to annual accounts and public presentation. **THEIR REWARD IS WITH ALLAH**

The verse gives him a night and a day, a secret and an open gift; spending by night and day asks him which of the four he has been leaving out of his ledger. Because the final words release faithful givers from grief and fear, showing through generosity at every time that the generosity of a person ends where his regret begins and not where his purse does. The same promise was made to the givers who keep their tongues quiet. And the reward of the quiet giver was named with the knowledge of the Lord. Because generosity at every time opens into secret and public giving, spending by night and day joins generosity at every time to secret and public giving. Since spending by night and day develops generosity at every time, secret and public giving carries the transition. Their reward is with allah matters, which means spending by night and day answers secret and public giving. Secret and public giving gives practical form to generosity at every time; their reward is with allah sets its horizon. Today, generosity at every time shapes secret and public giving in present decisions.
