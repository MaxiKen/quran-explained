**TWO QUESTIONS, ONE DESK**

The two questions at the desk are answered in phrases that move from the world to the orphan's ledger: ***“upon this world and the Hereafter.”***, ***“And they ask you ˹O Prophet˺ concerning orphans. Say, “Improving their condition is best.”***, ***“And if you partner with them, they are bonded with you ˹in faith˺.”***, ***“And Allah knows who intends harm”***, ***“and who intends good. Had Allah willed, He could have made it difficult”***, ***“for you. Surely Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.””***. and the closing phrase appoints the One who reads the ledger. The walk keeps the two answers joined: the theology of the two worlds at the head and the orphan's property at the end, and the joining is the verse's moral economy. The condition attached to the partnership is a bond of brotherhood rather than a clause of suspicion, which is the verse’s own reckoning of the guardian's heart. The account Ibn ʿAbbās delivered keeps the two answers joined and the orphan ruling inside the theology of the two worlds.

**ORPHANS AT THE TABLE**

The orphan question is answered with a verb rather than with a prohibition: improving their condition is best, and the whole ruling takes its tone from that instruction. Ibn ʿAbbās, delivering the two answers together, places the theology of the two worlds and the orphan's property sit at one desk. The ruling on the orphan's wealth carries the same instruction of the return and the same refusal of the worthless exchange (4:2 — **“Give orphans their wealth ˹when they reach maturity˺, and do not exchange your worthless possessions for their valuables,”**). Because the answer is a verb, the guardian is measured by the condition of the orphan rather than by the paperwork of the guardianship. The application is to visit the account of every orphan inside one's reach and to improve the column that is thinnest. The point of the brotherhood clause is that the ledger and the family table are kept in the same room.

**PARTNERED BY BOND**

The verse's most generous ruling is the partnership: if you partner with them, they are your brothers, and the brotherhood is the bond the verse writes into the deed. The testing of the orphan's competence is legislated in the same family of rulings with the same care for the child's growth (4:6 — **“Test ˹the competence of˺ the orphans until they reach a marriageable age. Then if you feel they are capable of sound judgment,”**). Because the partnership makes brothers, the property of the orphan is the property of a family rather than the inventory of a trust. The guardian who reads the verse is invited into a household rather than a court. The bond is older than the contract of the estate.

**KNOWING THE CORRUPTER**

The close of the verse answers the fear that haunts every guardian: Allah knows the corrupter and the reformer, and the knowing is the verse's final reckoning. The two portraits of the chapter — the corrupter of the land and the reformer of the orphan's estate — are judged by the One who reads the heart behind the deed. Because the knowing is divine, the quiet guardian's work is recorded with the same accuracy as the loud corrupter's case. The two questions of the desk — the world and the Hereafter, the orphan and the estate — are answered in one verse, and the answers are joined by the same accountant. The orphan question is answered in the passage with the same family of the rulings that governs the inheritance, and the family resemblance keeps the child inside the household of the law. The order of the inheritance described in the inherited gloss keeps the fear of the owner at the head of the distribution, and the fear is the etiquette of the treasury. Because the ruling is placed at the seam of the theology, the child's property is protected by the same hand that holds the two worlds. The guardianship of the ward is described in the old stories with the care of the orchard, and the orchard is measured by its fruit rather than by its fence. The one who partners with the orphan in the estate discovers that the partnership is a bond of the household rather than a clause of the suspicion. The corruption of the estate is painted across the other passages with the same dust and the same ruin, and the ruin starts at the column of the weakest. Reviewing the condition of the orphans inside one's reach is the errand that closes the month. The accountant of the two worlds keeps the ledger of the child.
