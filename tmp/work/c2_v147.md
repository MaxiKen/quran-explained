**FROM YOUR LORD, PLAINLY SAID**

The status of what has come down to the Messenger is stated here in the plainest words available: ***“˹This is˺ the truth from your Lord”***. The sentence follows an account of communities that knew the truth and concealed it, and it places the Prophet's own footing on the same fact they had buried. The practice in these verses is to name the source of the revelation for the benefit of the one receiving it, and the naming here answers the doubt that other people's concealment might have produced. Nothing in the sentence depends on the communities' acknowledgement, which is what makes it a statement about the revelation rather than about its reception. Anyone who has seen a truth buried by its custodians will find the sentence a relief. Ibn ʿAbbās read the truth as confirmation of the Prophet's mission and the closing prohibition as refusal to let another party's concealment produce doubt.

**SPOKEN TO ONE MAN**

The instruction that follows is given in the second person singular, and it addresses the one who had received the revelation rather than the crowd around him: ***“so do not ever be one of those who doubt”***. Prophets are warned about doubt in other places as well, and the warnings are always attached to the knowledge that has already arrived rather than to an absence of it. Another passage uses nearly the same words in a similar setting, which shows the phrase to be a recognised way of steadying the recipient of revelation: (3:60 — **“This is the truth from your Lord, so do not be one of those who doubt”**). The sentence therefore treats doubt as something other than an intellectual position, since it is forbidden in the presence of the truth rather than after a proof of it. The certainty being asked for rests on the source of the message and not on the strength of the recipient's own conviction.

**REACHING THE BELIEVERS THROUGH HIM**

The command reaches the community through its messenger, and the Book makes the same demand of the believers elsewhere when it tells them to ask those who had read the earlier scriptures rather than to waver. Another passage addresses the Prophet in the same terms and adds the instruction to seek out the people who had been reading the scriptures before him, which shows how the Book handles doubt when it arises: (10:94 — **“The truth has certainly come to you from your Lord”**). The sentence's brevity is part of its method, since the matter is settled by naming the source rather than by rehearsing the evidence. Anyone who has followed the long account of signs and covenants in this sūrah will recognise the point to which every argument in it returns. The sentence leaves a plain choice between what has arrived and what the doubters prefer.

**CERTAINTY THAT DOES NOT BORROW ITS SEAL**

Think of a man who receives a letter from someone he knows and who hears his neighbours say the letter is a forgery. The verse's sentence gives the recipient what he needs for that situation, and it does not consist of an argument about handwriting but of the identity of the sender. The comparison explains why the warning is addressed to the one who received the message, since the doubt that matters is the doubt that could enter the household that received it. Anyone who has had to hold to what he knew against a chorus of denial will understand the sentence's purpose. What the verse offers is not a defence of the message but a reminder of where it came from.

**CLOSING THE ARGUMENT**

Read with what follows, the sentence closes the passage on the qiblah and the communities that refused it, and it leaves the community with an identity rather than a quarrel. The sūrah is about to turn to a different subject, so this verse functions as the last word on the argument: the truth has come, the doubters are named, and the believers are told where to stand. The Book's manner of closing a discussion in this way appears at several points in the sūrah, and it always leaves the reader with the revelation rather than with the objections to it. He who has traced the argument from the change of direction to the concealment of the testimony now sees the whole matter gathered into one sentence. He is left with the instruction that the truth is not improved by doubting it. Today, doubt should not be manufactured merely because confident opponents conceal evidence they already understand.
