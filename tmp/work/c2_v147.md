**CERTAINTY FROM THE LORD**

A single sentence arrives to steady the whole design. ***“˹This is˺ the truth from your Lord”***. The truth is addressed to the Prophet first. The turn of the qiblah, the witnesses, the community of the middle, the recognition and the hiding — all of it stands under one label. The truth from your Lord. Mujāhid's comment keeps the line short and final. The clause is a verdict on the preceding pages. Everything filed before this sentence is now certified by the office that issued it. The certification covers the design, the marker, the mercy, and the whole preceding argument about the House. The passage does not reopen the case at this point. It signs the verdict instead. The signature itself is the teaching, because a signed argument and an open argument live different lives in the heart. What follows is that doubt loses its footing at the signature. A claim can be debated while its origin is unsettled. Once the origin is named, the debate changes shape. The effect of the naming is that the Prophet's heart is set down on solid ground before the closing instruction arrives. The truth has an address. The address is the Lord of the Prophet.

**THE COMMAND AGAINST DOUBT**

The second half of the verse is an instruction and a guardrail. ***“so do not ever be one of those who doubt”***. The wording is future-facing. Not ever. The reading of Ibn ʿAbbās places the command against the noise of the jeer and the counter-claims of the shelves. The doubt being forbidden is not the honest question. It is the doubt manufactured by the marketplace after the certification has landed. The parallel passage states the same truth from the same address in its own words (3:60 — **“This is the truth from your Lord, so do not be one of those who doubt”**). The repetition across the passages is itself the teaching. The truth from the Lord is stable enough to be stated twice in two places without wear. The difference between a question and a doubt is the posture toward the answer. The question leans in. The doubt leans away. The command guards the posture of the heart.

**PROMISES KEPT ON SCHEDULE**

The verse closes the qiblah section, and the closing ties the design to the character of God. The one who moved the direction is the one who keeps the promise. The challenge of the impatient had been raised before in the city (22:47 — **“They challenge you ˹O Prophet˺ to hasten the torment. And Allah will never fail in His promise.”**). The torment runs on the clock of the promise-keeper. The design of the qiblah runs on the same clock. For the modern reader the guardrail is aimed at the waiting season. Instructions land. Fulfilment lags. The gap between the two is the breeding ground of the forbidden doubt. This age trains the heart to expect same-day delivery of every promise. The verse trains the heart to read delay as scheduling, not absence. The truth is from your Lord. The promise belongs to the same name. The one who certified the design can be trusted with the calendar.

**THE SIGNED ARGUMENT**

The signing of the truth is what turns the whole preceding section from argument into foundation. A city had spent its months jeering at a turn, counting directions as property, and weighing prophets by market-share. The verse ends the weighing with a label and a prohibition. The label names the address of the truth. The prohibition guards the heart against the after-tremors of the jeer. The effect of the pair is a settled table on which the next section can be built. The community is about to be commissioned as witnesses and sent a curriculum. The curriculum cannot rest on a debatable turn. It rests on the truth from the Lord. The reason the prohibition is future-facing is that the doubting industry does not close with the first verdict. It reopens at every new instruction. For the modern reader the guardrail is installed exactly where the noise is loudest. Between the certified decision and the arriving fulfilment there is a corridor, and the corridor is where the forbidden doubt breeds. The verse closes the corridor with the two names. The truth is signed. The promise is kept.
