**DAILY LIFE RESTS ON GIFTS NO HUMAN MADE**

The description continues without changing its subject: ***“˹He is the One˺”***. The Lord whom humanity must worship is now known through gifts used every day. This keeps worship tied to both origin and care. God did not create and abandon His creatures; He ordered a world in which they can rest, build, drink, plant, and eat. The repeated address to you turns vast signs into personal trust. Earth and sky are not remote ideas. Each breath, step, roof, crop, and meal depends on an order no human being began. Ibn ʿAbbās reads these gifts as provision not only for the hearers but for created beings around them. The point is therefore larger than bare survival. A world fitted for life displays care, and care deepens the claim already grounded in creation. This is why the command against rivals comes after the list. Gratitude that stops at the gift or at a created means has not yet reached the Giver.

**THE GROUND IS SETTLED AND THE SKY RISES ABOVE IT**

God is the One ***“Who has made the earth a place of settlement”***, ***“for you and the sky a canopy”***. Ibn ʿAbbās explains the first image through dwelling and rest. The earth is firm enough for ordinary life, broad enough for travel, and fit for farms, homes, and paths. Calling it a settled place speaks from the true scale of human use; it is not a claim that every patch is soft, safe, or easy. Mountains, deserts, seas, and quakes remain part of the created world, while humanity as a whole receives ground on which life can be established. The canopy image turns the gaze upward. The sky covers the human dwelling and holds signs and benefits that life needs. The pairing returns in another description: (40:64 — **“It is Allah Who made the earth a place of settlement for you and the sky a canopy.”**). The sky is also called (21:32 — **“a well-protected canopy, still they turn away from its signs”**). The rebuke there shows that protection should lead to recognition. Yet neither earth nor sky is divine. Both are made arrangements serving a purpose set by their Lord. This distinction guards thought from two errors: ignoring the created order as though it had no Giver, and worshipping a part of that order because its scale inspires awe. The earth bears us and the sky covers us, but neither chose its task or owns the one who benefits.

**WATER LINKS HEAVEN, SOIL, PLANT, AND DAILY BREAD**

The next gift moves through a chain described as ***“and sends down rain from the sky, causing fruits to grow”***, giving it ***“as a provision for you.”*** Rain descends, soil receives it, seed opens, and produce forms in kinds, colours, and tastes. The brief line gathers stages that no farmer can create from nothing. Human labour is real: people clear, sow, water, guard, harvest, carry, and sell. Yet effort works only with water, life, matter, time, and stable laws already given. The verse therefore does not erase created causes. It places them under the One who gives each cause its power and joins them to their fruit. The Qur’an draws attention to the range within this gift: (16:11 — **“With it He produces for you ˹various˺ crops, olives, palm trees, grapevines, and every type of fruit.”**). Variety serves body, trade, pleasure, and thought. Provision is thus more than ownership. It is a trust whose source should shape its use. Gratitude does not permit waste, fraud, or the hoarding of food while others are wronged. It receives, shares, and obeys the Provider. Today, knowledge of clouds, roots, and growth can deepen this response rather than displace it. Explaining a stage in the chain does not explain away the existence of the chain, its powers, or the Lord who sustains them.

**KNOWN CREATION MAKES RIVALS AN OPEN CONTRADICTION**

The conclusion follows: ***“So do not knowingly set up equals to Allah ˹in worship˺”***. Ibn ʿAbbās explains the rivals as peers or equals assigned to God. The final knowingly points to what the hearers already admit or can plainly infer: they did not create earth, raise the sky, send rain, or originate the life in fruit. Other passages confront people who confess that God created heaven and earth and sent rain, yet fail to give Him the worship due to that knowledge. When asked who sends rain and revives dead soil, (29:63 — **“they will surely say, “Allah!” Say, “Praise be to Allah!” In fact, most of them do not understand”**). The missing step is not a new fact about rain. It is faithful action on what they know.

A rival is not every tool, leader, or person from whom help is sought. Created means can be used while the heart knows their limits and obeys God. Rivalry begins when a creature receives a share of worship, ultimate reliance, sacred obedience against God, or the fear and love that belong to Him alone. The reason is direct: the One alone creates and provides, so He alone deserves worship. This truth frees the servant as well as binding him. Work, medicine, wealth, and power may be used, but none becomes a lord. Their benefit remains granted, bounded, and removable. Knowledge becomes whole when the hand takes lawful means while the heart bows only to the One who made them. That order preserves both effort and trust.
