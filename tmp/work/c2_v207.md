**THE EXCHANGE OF A SELF**

The contrast figure is drawn in three phrases from the exchange to the divine kindness: ***“And there are those”***, ***“who would dedicate their lives to Allah’s pleasure.”***, ***“And Allah is Ever Gracious to ˹His˺ servants”***. and the closing phrase catches the character of the One who buys. The walk moves through the transaction, the intention behind it, and the kindness that receives it, and each phrase corrects a false economy the verse before it exposed. The self that was worshipped in the previous portrait is sold in this one, and the sale is the portrait's answer to the worship. The account preserved on Ibn ʿAbbās's side names the man behind the phrase and the ransom he paid on the road.

**SUHAYB'S ROAD**

The portrait's alternative to the flatterer is drawn from the reports with a name attached: Suhayb b. Sinān, the emigrant who left Mecca with his wealth exposed and ransomed himself on the road. The account preserved on Ibn ʿAbbās's side names him and his companions as the people behind the phrase, and the naming turns the theology into a biography. The man sold what he owned to buy the permission to leave, and the sale is described as directed at Allah's pleasure rather than at the market's price. The transaction the legislation of exchange offers to every believer is the same one this man performed: a trade that saves from a painful punishment (61:10 — **“O believers! Shall I guide you to an exchange that will save you from a painful punishment”**). Because the portrait keeps the name, the verse's economics can be walked on a real road. Because the house is the point of the battle, the spearman who holds it close and the one who sets out far are guarding the same address.

**BUYING THE PLEASURE**

The intention is the price and the pleasure is the goods: the one who dedicates his life to Allah's pleasure has discovered the only market where the purchase and the payment are the same act. The qualities of the covenant-keepers of the community are drawn in the same terms of proven loyalty and waiting (33:23 — **“Among the believers are men”**). Because the pleasure is sought and not assumed, the self-seller's act is a transaction with terms rather than a mood of surrender. The believer who weighs his loyalties can read the portrait as a ledger: what he sells, what he buys, and on whose pleasure the sale is drawn. The intention is where the trade is either honoured or broken.

**KIND TO THE SERVANTS**

The closing phrase names the character of the Buyer, and the naming is the safety of the exchange: Allah is ever gracious to His servants. The kindness named here is the kindness that receives the ransomed traveller and repays the sale in a currency the seller did not know existed. Because the grace is attached to the servants, the transaction is open to every servant who can find something to sell. The application is to start the sale with the smallest inventory — the hour, the coin, the temper — and to trust the grace named at the verse's end. The portrait of the self-seller is short because the transaction is simple. The trade described at the head of the passage is the oldest trade of the conscience, and the brokers of it are named in the stories of the battles with their prices. The one who purchases a soul at the price of another is the one who has sold the covenant for the spoil. The investment language is not accidental: the self is capital, the deed is the transaction, and the loss is measured in the currency of the covenant. Because the transaction is priced, the trader can review his own book at the end of the day. The legacy of the man who refused the ransom and kept the covenant is the opposite entry in the same ledger. The one who sells at the wrong hour discovers the price at the settlement rather than at the sale. Reviewing the book of one's own transactions is the discipline the passage invites, and the reckoning is done with the names of the covenant rather than with the numbers of the market. The merchant of the covenant never retires. The broker of the covenant keeps his office at the crossing of every road, and the office is open at the hour of the decision.
