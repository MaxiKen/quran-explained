**WHAT KEEPS THE WISH OFF THE LIPS**

The prophecy of refusal arrives with its own psychology: ***“But they will never wish”***. The negation carries the absoluteness of the original — never at all, under any terms — and the gloss transmitted from Ibn ʿAbbās through the Prophet's first biographer gives the refuseniks their reason in a single line of courtroom logic: they recognize the prophet they are addressing and disbelieve in him regardless. The men know the prophet they are addressing; the recognition is already in the file from the earlier passage; and the wishing of death would be the wishing of a meeting their own record has made dreadful. The transmitted commentary puts the counterfactual in plain numbers: had the wish been made that day, no one of that community would have remained alive — which is exactly why the tongue stayed still. The structure of the refusal is therefore an admission in the grammar of self-preservation. A man will not knock at a door he believes opens onto his own trial, and the silence of the lips testifies to the reading of the file. What follows from the absoluteness of the never is that the challenge of the last verse remains standing on the table, undated and unanswered. The wish is not hard to pronounce; it is hard to pronounce honestly.

**THE HANDS THAT FILL THE POCKETS OF FEAR**

The second clause names the deterrent with the passage's favorite anatomical idiom: ***“for that because of what their hands have done.”***. The hands send forward what the ledger later reads back, and the glosses hear the cause of the cowardice precisely there: the deeds stand between the man and the door, so that the same mouth that claims the world to come refuses to travel toward it. The parallel decree of the revelation on the love of this fleeting life draws the same portrait from the other side of the mirror: (17:18 — **“Whoever desires this fleeting world ˹alone˺, We hasten in it whatever We please to whoever We will; then We destine them”**). The psychology the two passages share is worth stating without softening: the fear of death in the claimant of paradise is the conscience's own arithmetic. The claim speaks of inheritance; the hands remember the forgery. The reason the verse names the hands rather than the heart is that the docket is manual — the deeds were done with these, and the memory of them keeps the wish unmade.

**AWARENESS DIRECTED AT THE WRONGDOERS**

The close of the sentence attaches knowledge to the class with the precision of a charge sheet: ***“And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of the wrongdoers”***. The name of the knowing here is the name of the knowledge that holds its objects in full — the elative form in the Arabic does the work of the superlative, a knowledge complete in its grasp of who the wrongdoers are and what the wrongdoers did. The placement of the clause after the refusal is deliberate, since the refusal claimed the protection of anonymity: we are the chosen, we are the beloved, the record is buried in the fathers. The verse answers with a knowledge that names the class by its deeds. The tradition's rendering of the passage's final verb keeps both audiences in the frame — whether the sentence is read as His awareness of what they do or of what the wrongdoers are — because the knowledge does not merely watch the action; it files the actor. The effect on the reader of standing at the end of this long cross-examination is the effect of a courtroom that has stopped speaking and started writing.

**THE OFFER THAT NEVER EXPIRES**

The two-verse experiment remains the section's most portable instrument, and the tradition treats it as such. The claim of the exclusive home, the counted days, the guaranteed cohort — each family of claims meets the same test, and the test never requires a scholar to administer it. For any congregation today that has quietly replaced the wish for the meeting with a portfolio of postponements, the passage holds the same mirror up and asks the same question in the same register. The difference between the two postures — the faith that would pronounce the wish and the faith that will not — is the difference the whole passage has been building toward, and it is measurable in a single syllable. In other words, the verdict of the experiment does not wait for the last day; it is pronounced in the silence of the wish, on the day the claim was made.
