**THE PROMISE FULFILLED AT UHUD**

***“Indeed, Allah fulfilled His promise to you when you ˹initially˺ swept them away by His Will, then your”*** Indeed, Allah fulfilled His promise to you when you ˹initially˺ swept them away by His Will opens the Uhud-audit proper. And its first finding is the verse's studied justice. Wa laqad sadaqakumu llahu wa'dah. Allah was true to you in His promise. The final account’s opening entry not the ranks' failure but the Promise-Keeper's fidelity. The day's first hour ran on the victory-schedule's published terms. Idh tahussunahum bi-idhnih. When you were mowing them down by His leave. The hass-verb. The cutting-through. Quraysh's line broken, their standards fallen (the record. The enemy's banner-men cut down in succession), the field turning. The finding pre-empting, at the final account’s threshold, the wound's worst misreading. The promise never failed. Its terms did (the community must enter the final account knowing which). (3:151 — **“practice He has never authorized. The Fire will be their home—what an”**)

**COURAGE LOST AND ORDERS DISPUTED**

***“courage weakened and you disputed about the command and disobeyed, after Allah had brought victory within your”*** Then your courage weakened and you disputed about the command and disobeyed, after Allah had brought victory within your reach files the day's turning in three verbs. And the three are the collapse-staircase walked on the hill. Hatta idha fashiltum. Until you lost heart. The fifty archers watching the spoils-chase below. Wa tanaza'tum fil-amr. And you disputed about the command. The hill's recorded quarrel. The post's majority reading the victory complete, their leader Abdullah ibn Jubair holding the order's letter. Wa asaytum. And you disobeyed. The descent. Forty of the fifty down for the spoils. Min ba'di ma arakum ma tuhibbun. After He had shown you what you love. The clause's exactness the final account’s sting. The disobedience committed inside the granted victory. The loved thing shown, and the showing itself the test's instrument (Khalid's cavalry, watching the emptied hill, wheeling through the pass. The day reversed by one position's abandonment). That failure made competing aims immediately consequential: some reached for spoils, while others chose eternity.

**A REVERSAL THAT TESTED AND FORGAVE**

***“victory over them as a test, yet He has pardoned you. And Allah is Gracious to the believers”*** The final account is published in the verse’s own words. The community convicted and pardoned in the same verse. The discipline named without euphemism (fashiltum, tanaza'tum, asaytum. Three counts), and the file closed at mercy before the narration even ends. And Allah is Gracious to the believers. Wallahu dhu fadlin alal-mu'minin. The grace-clause sets the tone of the whole final account. The Auditor’s grace toward those being called to account. A reader shaped by the lesson of 152 carrying its permanent two-sided discipline. His own defeats audited at their true variable (the commands disputed, the posts left. Never the Promise-Keeper's failure), and the final account conducted, always, under the pardon-clause's roof. Conviction without despair, the file cleared for the next morning's march. Imagine archers leaving a secured height for scattered goods and opening the route they were assigned to guard. The scene makes the tested and forgiven reversal concrete without reducing its moral lesson to a picture. For the promise fulfilled at uhud, Ibn ʿAbbās locates the reversal in archers who left their posts for booty, while others remained seeking the Hereafter until they were killed. The account is exact because victory had already appeared before discipline failed; which means this rules out the excuse that obedience mattered only while the outcome was uncertain. The archers were tested by success itself: the sight of spoils divided desire, dispute weakened command, and leaving the post opened the field; therefore some wanted the nearer gain and others the Hereafter, so one formation held two aims. Allah then allowed the reversal as a test, yet He announced pardon before the community could turn correction into despair; in other words present organisations face the same danger when early success makes procedures seem unnecessary. A secured position may be abandoned for recognition, profit, or speed, exposing everyone behind it; hence the lesson is not that every loss proves a specific sin. It is that known disobedience must be named where revelation names it, repaired without excuse, and held beneath the mercy that makes renewed service possible, because the practical demand of the promise fulfilled at uhud follows from it.
