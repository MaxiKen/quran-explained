**THE GREED THE OATH SWORE TO**

The verse opens with an oath's full battery — the oath-letter and the emphatic suffix together — and the content of the sworn statement is a diagnosis: ***“You will surely find them clinging to life more eagerly than any other people, even more than polytheists. Each one of them wishes to live a thousand years.”***. The comparison is chosen with care, and the tradition spells out why the chosen rival matters. The polytheists of the peninsula disbelieved in the world to come altogether, so their clinging to this one is at least consistent. The men named here affirm the other world and claim its joys as their inheritance. They still hold this life tighter than anyone alive. The glosses state the psychology without gloves. The claimants know that the journey's end is the fire, and while the fire is what waits, this life is the only shelter standing between them and the sentence. The parallel portrait of worldly desire elsewhere in the revelation keeps the same market-stall imagery: (3:14 — **“made appealing to people. These are the pleasures of this worldly life, but with Allah is the finest destination”**). What follows from the oath is that the avarice is not incidental to the claim but diagnostic of it. The men who will not wish for death have already testified about their own file. The greed is the confession.

**A THOUSAND YEARS AS THE UNIT OF WISH**

The wish itself is then quoted in its arithmetic, and the number drew the analysts in several directions. The grammar of the conditional was mapped precisely. The if-only particle leans on the verbal noun, and the object of the wish is the being-given-life itself — a thousand years of it. The range of the comparative was argued over the sentence's syntax. One reading holds the whole phrase together, so that the wishers are the ones being compared. Another breaks the report so that the thousand-year wishers are a subset of the polytheists — a reading the language itself resists. The earliest glosses fix the pronoun on the claimants either way. The definition of the thousand divides between the plain figure and the idiom: either the actual number the claimants calculated in their doctrine, or the old way of saying a multitude. The tradition even keeps the folk-cousin of the idiom — the Persian courtesy of wishing ten thousand years at a sneeze — as evidence of how deep the shape of such wishing runs in human speech. The point of the figure is not the calendar but the appetite. The wish is stated at the largest unit the claimant can pronounce.

**THE YEARS THAT MOVE NOTHING**

The third clause answers the wish with physics rather than argument: ***“But even if they were to live”***, ***“that long, it would not save them from the punishment.”***. The verb of saving here is the verb of budging. No millennium will move the man one step from the chastisement. The flatness of the refusal is the sentence's whole force. A thousand years of life does not change a file; it only postpones the reading of it. The wish-death challenge a verse earlier was glossed in the same spirit. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, transmitting from Ibn ʿAbbās, reads the command to wish for death as an invocation: let them call on God for death and see what answers. ʿIkrimah, again from Ibn ʿAbbās, presses the same reading of the challenge. The reality behind both glosses is flatly stated a single verse earlier: (2:95 — **“But they will never wish for that because of what their hands have done. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of the wrongdoers”**). The reward the wish was meant to reach early is itself deferred to a fixed day: (3:185 — **“And you will only receive your full reward on the Day of Judgment. Whoever is spared from the Fire”**). The tradition's portrait of this exact psychology is preserved in the report from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī: *\"A hypocrite is more covetous of the world than an idolater\"* — and the reason offered for the paradox is the same gnawing awareness of the consequences of one's own hands. The claimants' certainty of salvation is thus a delusion whose floor the deluded themselves can feel. The clutching at life is the tremor of the diagnosis. The difference between the two populations of the first clause is therefore not faith but foreknowledge. One group fears the fire it believes in; the other ignores the fire it denies. In other words, the greed is proportionate to the dread.

**WATCHFULNESS THAT FILES THE CONCEALED**

The close attaches the divine attribute to the scene with its own precision: ***“And Allah is All-Seeing of what they do”***. The final verb keeps its alternate reading across the transmissions — what they do, and what you do — and the requiting follows the seeing in either register. The earliest authority on the verse extends the scope of the seen past the deeds of hand. The sight files the contraventions, the rebellion, and the concealment — the descriptions and traits of the awaited prophet that were recognized and then buried. That buried knowing is named in its own place in this surah: (2:146 — **“Yet a group of them hides the truth knowingly”**). The attribute of sight in this passage is therefore the attribute of the court reporter rather than of the bystander. The record is taken in full, including the parts the actors believed were never spoken. The effect of the clause after a long cross-examination is to convert the whole section into evidence under oath. Nothing in the file has been missed by the sight that will read it.

**THE CLINGING AS SELF-ASSESSMENT**

The passage's final lesson is the one the experiment of the wish-death challenge already prepared. The claim of inheritance is tested by the claimant's own preferences, and the preferences have now been measured in public. A community that says the world to come is its property while clutching this world above every other people has performed its own cross-examination and lost it on its own answers. Any congregation today that keeps the language of salvation and the portfolio of postponement in the same account will find the verse's diagnostic exact. Watch what the people cling to when the door is described. What turns on the measurement is the honesty of the whole doctrinal claim — the wish for the meeting is the one testimony no liturgy can forge.
