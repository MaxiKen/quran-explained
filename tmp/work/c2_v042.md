**TWO DIFFERENT CRIMES AGAINST THE TRUTH**

The next sentence names the failure in its two practical forms: ***“Do not mix truth with falsehood”***, and ***“or hide the truth knowingly”***. The two are distinct, and the sūrah deals with them as a pair, because a person who is willing to do one of them will usually end up doing the other before long. Mixing is the active work of blending, where a true statement is wrapped around a false one until the listener cannot separate them. Hiding is the quieter work of omission, where the relevant material is simply left out of the account. Both produce the same result in the hearer, and both are named as deliberate acts rather than as mistakes of memory or understanding. Ibn ʿAbbās explained the first offence as mixing established facts with lies, while Qatādah treated the mixture as a deliberate joining of rival claims. Both readings make knowledge central, which means the sin lies in managing the truth rather than simply failing to find it.

The Book raises the same charge against the same community in another sūrah, and the wording there puts a question mark where this verse puts a command: (3:71 — **“O People of the Book! Why do you mix the truth with falsehood and hide the truth knowingly”**). The pairing is identical, which suggests that the Book regards the two as one habit seen from two sides. The people being addressed had a scripture in their hands and an audience that trusted them, and the temptation ran in both directions at once: to present a case by blending, and to protect a position by silence. A reader who has ever made an argument for a good cause will recognise the first of those, and one who has ever let a wrong stand because correcting it would be awkward will recognise the second.

**THE ADVERB THAT CHANGES EVERYTHING**

Everything in the verse turns on the adverb. The Book does not accuse anyone of an honest error, and it does not treat confusion as a crime. What it names is the deliberate management of information by people who know what they are doing, and that is why the sentence can be so short and still land so heavily. The Book elsewhere describes the opposite conduct as the obligation of the same community, and it is expressed in one clause: (3:187 — **“who were given the Scripture to make it known to people and not hide it,”**). The covenant required publication, and the practice described in this verse is the covenant reversed. The word also protects the reader from two errors of self-assessment. A person who has been wrong through carelessness can correct himself and move on, since the Book is full of that story. A person who has been hiding something knowingly is in a different position, because his problem is not information but willingness. The verse asks him to stop, and it does not pretend that the stopping is difficult to identify. He already knows the material he has been leaving out, which is the reason the adverb is there in the first place.

**HANDLING MATERIAL HONESTLY**

The practical instruction is about the handling of material, and it applies far beyond the study of scripture. A person who quotes a report and leaves out the sentence that changes its meaning has mixed. A person who is asked for advice and omits the one fact that would have altered the decision has hidden. Both can be done with an entirely clear conscience, which is why the Book keeps the command in front of the reader in the plainest possible form. It is not asking him to be clever about the truth. It is asking him not to arrange it. The exercise is to find one place where an account he gives has been trimmed for convenience, and to restore the missing piece the next time the matter comes up. Nothing about the verse requires him to be brutal or to volunteer information that no one has asked for. It requires him to stop being the editor of the truth when he is the one who benefits from the editing. A person who can do that in one recurring conversation has begun the work that this verse and the one before it are asking for. Think of clear water mixed with a little dye: the vessel may still look useful, yet every cup now carries the added colour. Truth arranged with falsehood works the same way, since the false part changes how the true part is received. A public claim today can be distorted by one withheld fact, which means this command governs headlines, meetings and family accounts alike.
