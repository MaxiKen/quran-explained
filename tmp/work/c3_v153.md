**FLEEING UPHILL WITHOUT LOOKING BACK**

***“˹Remember˺ when you were running far away ˹in panic˺—not looking at anyone—while the”*** ˹Remember˺ when you were running far away ˹in panic˺. Not looking at anyone. While the Messenger was calling to you from behind. Narrates the final account’s hardest frame. And the frame's two figures are its whole anatomy. Idh tus'iduna wa la talwuna ala ahad. When you were climbing away, turning to no one. The is'ad-verb. The scrambling ascent. The ranks broken through the passes and up the mountain's flanks, each man's world contracted to his own escape (the panic's signature. Not looking at anyone. The community's mutual regard, its ranks' whole fabric, dissolved in the running). War-rasulu yad'ukum fi ukhrakum. While the Messenger was calling you from your rear. The frame's fixed point. The Prophet wounded and standing, calling the runners by name. To me, servants of Allah. To me, servants of Allah. The two figures composing the verse's unforgettable inversion. The called flee-ing the caller. The community's whole meaning running, for that hour, in reverse. (3:152 — **“Indeed, Allah fulfilled His promise to you”**)

**GRIEF ANSWERED WITH GRIEF**

***“Messenger was calling to you from behind! So Allah rewarded your disobedience with”*** So Allah rewarded your disobedience with distress upon distress files the hour's sentence. Fa'athabakum ghamman bi-ghamm. So He recompensed you with grief upon grief. The doubled grief includes several layers disclosed by the record. The grief of the reversal upon the grief of the spoils lost. The anguish at the Prophet's rumoured death upon the anguish of the slain. The distress of the shame upon the distress of the wounds. The layers' very multiplicity the sentence's point. ***“distress upon distress. Now, do not grieve over the victory you were denied”*** The day's compounding sorrows filed as one recompense, athaba's wage-grammar (the ranks paid, for the hill's minute, in grief's currency. The earnings-ledger operating in the day's own coin). Now, do not grieve over the victory you were denied or the injury you suffered. Likayla tahzanu ala ma fatakum wa la ma asabakum. The sentence's stated purpose its buried mercy. The grief administered so that grief would end. The doubled ghamm as inoculation. The community, having tasted loss's floor, schooled past the two griefs that cripple, the distress itself the training that made the next morning's march possible. (3:154 — **“He sent down serenity in the form of drowsiness overcoming some of”**) Grief answered with grief guides conduct today because failure can train a community not to be controlled by missed gains or present wounds when the next duty arrives.

**TRAINED BEYOND MISSED GAIN AND SUFFERED LOSS**

***“or the injury you suffered. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do”*** And Allah is All-Aware of what you do seals the frame with the final account-clause. Wallahu khabirun bima ta'malun. And Allah is Aware of what you do. And its selection over the flight-narration completes the passage's justice. The Khabir's awareness holding the hour's whole spectrum. The runners' panic and its degrees, the standers' ransom, the Messenger's own bleeding fidelity. Every figure in the frame filed at his exact conduct. The community reading its worst hour under the clause that guarantees the reading's precision. No runner over-condemned, no stander un-credited. While the passage now descends into the night after. The drowsiness sent down on the sincere, and the hypocrites' whispered arithmetic. If we had any say in the matter, none of us would have come to die here. Imagine people running uphill past their leader’s call until loss teaches them what panic hides. On fleeing uphill without looking back, Ibn ʿAbbās describes the uphill flight past the Messenger’s call and the grief of death and defeat added to the grief caused by abandoning him. The scene measures panic through movement: people climbed away, stopped looking toward one another, and failed to answer the Messenger calling behind them; which means distress followed distress because the missed victory, reported deaths, personal wounds, and shame of retreat arrived together. The purpose of that grief was educational; therefore by surviving both lost gain and suffered injury, the believers could learn not to let either condition rule future obedience. Allah’s awareness at the close prevents the confusion of battle from becoming moral confusion; in other words every act remained known even when no fighter held a full view of the field. Today, a crisis can narrow attention until a person sees only escape and misses the voice of duty; hence recovery begins by facing what happened without inventing a cleaner story. The verse records failure openly, then turns its pain into training rather than a permanent identity, because the practical demand of fleeing uphill without looking back follows from it.
