**THREE GATES CLOSED IN ONE BODY**

The verse gathers the losses of the two pictures that follow into a single condition: ***“They are ˹wilfully˺ deaf, dumb”***, ***“and blind, so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺”***. A man who is deaf, dumb and blind in one body has lost every road out of his own head, which means the sentence is describing a total closure rather than a partial one. The three faculties are not chosen at random, since each of them answers one of the ways a message reaches a person: the ear receives it, the tongue asks about it or repeats it, and the eye reads the world the message came to explain. Close all three doors and the house is sealed, and the verse states the result without a shade of pity. The Book repeats the same three while describing the company of the Fire on the day they are gathered (17:97), and it says of the same men elsewhere that they are like cattle rather than like people (7:179). The condition is not a picture of slowness; it is the end of a road.

**THE ROAD THAT IS NEVER FOUND**

The command of the sentence lies in a single verb, and the verb is about travelling rather than about hearing: ***“and blind, so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺”***. The sense of the root is turning back, which means the men are described as people who were once on the road and who never find their way onto it again. That reading explains why the Book does not call them ignorant; it calls them lost. Ignorance can be cured with a book, and being lost needs a guide and a willingness to admit that the map in one's hand was drawn at home. Another passage shows the Messenger being told that the deaf cannot be made to hear the call once they have turned their backs and walked away, (27:80 — **“Nor can you make the deaf hear the call”**), and the point of that sentence is not cruelty. It is a description of distance, because a man who has walked a mile from the road needs a longer walk back, not a louder shout.

**THE SENSES A READER STILL OWNS**

The three faculties in this verse are the three that any reader still holds today, and that is what makes the sentence a warning rather than a funeral notice. A person can ask what his ear has been fed this month, what his tongue has been repeating, and what his eye has been resting on, and the answers will describe the road he is standing on. Faith is not preserved by a single decision; it is preserved by the ordinary traffic of the senses, which is why the Book attaches so much of its teaching to hearing and speaking rather than to rare states of feeling. A man who fills his ear with mockery for a season will find his tongue repeating it, and the eye will follow, and the three will agree among themselves. The verse names the agreement they reach so that a reader can catch it in the making, since a door that is still open can still be walked through.
