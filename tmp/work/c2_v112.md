**THE ADMISSION RULE**

The passage answers the exclusive claims of the previous verse with the rule as written: ***“But no! Whoever submits themselves to Allah and does good will have their reward with their Lord.”***. Ibn ʿAbbās hears the submitting in its two halves. The surrender is made sincere to Allah in the religion. The good is worked in words and in deeds. The rule is therefore not the rule of a lineage. It is the rule of a posture and its output. What follows from the substitution is the end of the membership argument. A second gloss gives the claiming-part of the claim its own anatomy. The face is named in the Arabic as the most noble part of the body. When the face has submitted, the rest of the frame has its captain. The point of the phrasing is the point of the whole passage. Submission is not a badge worn on the sleeve. It is the direction of the person.

**THE FACE AS THE SURRENDERED PART**

The imagery of the surrendered face is worth holding. The face is where recognition lives, where the gaze is set, where the self is presented. The submission of the face is therefore the submission of the aim. The tradition of the glosses makes the following-action explicit. The face surrenders, and the limbs follow in the good of words and deeds. The reward clause then opens the second half of the rule: the reward is with the Lord, stored at the level of the ownership rather than at the counters of the world. The reversal of the exclusive claim is total. The entry was never the property of a party. The entry is the property of the surrender and the deed, and both are available at every address. A passport and a posture are different kinds of document. A passport is printed by a state. A posture is chosen at the door.

**FEAR AND GRIEF, BOTH ANSWERED**

The verse's closing assurance is drawn against the two sharpest pains of the afterlife: ***“And there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve”***. The old tradition names each pain in its juridical place. The fear is the fear of staying eternally in the fire. The grief is the grief of missing the garden. The twin negation is the passover of the two terrors. The reward clause and the twin negation together are repeated in the passage of the shared ledger: (2:62 — **“does good will have their reward with their Lord. And there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve”**), and the reward's completion in the final accounting was named in this passage's own reward-discipline: (3:185 — **“And you will only receive your full reward on the Day of Judgment. Whoever is spared from the Fire”**). The good news of the same reward was offered to the working believer at the gates of the garden: (2:25 — **“Give good news ˹O Prophet˺ to those who believe and do good that they will have Gardens under”**). The rule of the admission and the promises of its reward are therefore one continuous policy. Submit the face. Work the good. Collect at the settlement.

**THE RULE READ BACK TO THE READER**

The theology at the counter of this verse is the passage's answer to every exclusive-entry sign ever printed. The sign says: our address, our membership, our name. The rule says: the face and the deed. The exclusivity claim has no exhibit in the books. The admission rule is printed in the plainest terms of the books, and it is printed twice. For any reader who has sat through the argument of the exclusive claim at work or at home or in the rows of a sanctuary, the rule at hand is the document to hold up. The claim is about who belongs to whom. The rule is about who has submitted what. That is the jurisdiction the verse restores, and it restores it with one line of the admission policy. No membership card is named. The surrender and the deed are the only credentials the passage reads. The credentials are also the only ones the settlement will cash. Every other card is printed at a desk with no window at the settlement. The verse keeps the window and the policy in one line.
