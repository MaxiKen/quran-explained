**TRADE IS NOT BLAMED**

Read in its phrases, the passage permits the trade and blesses the descent: ***“There is no blame on you”***, ***“for seeking the bounty of your Lord ˹during this journey˺.”***, ***“When you return from ’Arafât, praise Allah near the sacred place”***, ***“and praise Him for having guided you”***, ***“for surely before this ˹guidance˺ you were astray”***. The walk balances the marketplace against the plain: the bounty is permitted in the first phrases and the gratitude is commanded in the last. The pilgrim carries both halves of this economy on the same road home. The objection Ibn ʿAbbās records is answered in the order of these phrases. The trade permitted in the first phrases funds the journey the last phrases sanctify, and the pilgrim's gratitude is the seam between the two halves.

There is no blame on you for seeking the bounty of your Lord ˹during this journey˺. answers an objection the first generation raised in earnest: the merchants feared that trading during the pilgrimage diluted the worship. The verse's reply is a release from that scruple — the bounty sought lawfully is not a stain on the rites — and the release is addressed to the conscience that had begun to forbid what God allowed. The report of Ibn ʿAbbās places the objection concretely: a group stood against buying and selling in the Sacred Precinct, and the verse settled their scruple without mocking it. Because the objection was pious, the answer is gentle: no blame, no lecture, and the commerce restored. The worship of the journey includes the traveler's livelihood, and the two are not rivals in the account of the One who sent both. The hours of remembrance and the hours of commerce belong to one schedule (3:191 — **“˹They are˺ those who remember Allah while standing, sitting, and lying on their sides, and reflect on the creation of the heavens”**)

**OBJECTING TO COMMERCE**

The scruple's logic deserves its answer stated slowly: if the House is holy, should the market stand outside it? The verse replies by refusing the partition — the sacred precinct is not a monastery, and the servant of Allah trades where he stands. The permission is placed beside the remembrance that follows it, so the day of the pilgrim contains both the coin and the prayer. Because the One who invites the pilgrim also feeds him, the transaction at the shrine is a transaction between the guest and the Host's other guests. The application is to bring the whole self to the rites and to keep the whole self honest: trade as one who will stand at ʿArafāt, and stand at ʿArafāt as one who must trade. The scruple is answered by the calendar and the coins in the same verse.

**DEPARTING AS ONE CROWD**

The verse's second movement is a scene of departure: When you return from ʿArafât, praise Allah near the sacred place. The standing at ʿArafāt is the rite's summit, and the descent from it is the pilgrim's re-entry into motion. The remembrance is commanded at the sacred monument, so the memory of the standing travels with the crowd. The reported practice of the Prophet at the descent — teaching the assembled pilgrims to take their rites from him — marks the monument as a classroom. Because the remembrance is attached to the geography, the departure is a liturgy and not an evacuation. The application is to mark the descent with the verse's own act: to praise and to remember the guidance that led the body to the plain. The crowd leaves the plain carrying one voice. The voice is the verse's final provision for the road home. Because the remembrance is commanded at the monument, the geography of the rites is stored with the words spoken over it, and the pilgrim can return to the plain by returning to the phrase. The application is to keep the phrase of the descent as a permanent possession of the year. The one who praises for the guidance keeps the guidance current.

**GUIDANCE RECALLED**

The close names the content of the praise: and praise Him for having guided you, for surely before this ˹guidance˺ you were astray. The pilgrim's gratitude is specific — he was lost before the guidance — and the specificity is the honesty of the rite. The remembrance of the misguidance is what makes the guidance weighty, and the weight is the fuel of the praise. Because the verse speaks in the past tense, the misguidance is over and the memory is a teacher. The application is to rehearse the before and after at the monument: to name the state one was in and the state the guidance has given. The one who praises for guidance without remembering the wandering praises a word rather than a rescue. The verse's two scenes — the marketplace and the plain — are therefore one economy of gratitude. The bounty that fills the sack and the guidance that fills the heart come from the same Lord, and the pilgrim thanks Him for both on the same road. The application is to hold the commerce and the standing in one schedule of gratitude, since the passage has placed them in one economy. The bounty sought on the road is repaid in the currency the Lord multiplies (2:245 — **“Who will lend to Allah a good loan which Allah will multiply many times over? It is Allah ˹alone˺”**)
