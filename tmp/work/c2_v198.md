**LAWFUL TRADE DURING HAJJ**

Read under lawful trade during hajj, the relevant clauses are ***“There is no blame on you”***, ***“for seeking the bounty of your Lord ˹during this journey˺.”***. Some Muslims feared that earning lawful income while travelling for Hajj might diminish the purity of the pilgrimage. Worship and work are not enemies when intention, conduct, and priorities remain sound. The permission does not make business the purpose of Hajj or allow the pilgrim to neglect the rites. It teaches that a person may support himself and his family while worshipping, provided he avoids fraud, exploitation, excessive distraction, and prohibited trade. The verse corrects two extremes. One treats earning money as inherently impure and abandons legitimate responsibility; the other turns a sacred journey into a commercial opportunity and neglects Allah. The balance is lawful livelihood joined to remembrance. The standing at ʿArafāt is the central rite of Hajj. Pilgrims gather there in humility, supplication, and remembrance, then depart after sunset toward Muzdalifah. The movement is not a private spiritual retreat. It is a shared act in which people of every background stand before one Lord, recalling that all humanity will be gathered for judgement. The Prophet ﷺ said, *Hajj is ʿArafah,* in the authentic reports of Sunan Abū Dāwūd, Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, and Sunan al-Nasāʾī. This highlights the importance of reaching the place and time of the standing according to the established rite. Pilgrims should learn the schedule and follow qualified organisers rather than rely upon guesswork. (2:196 — **“then ˹offer˺ whatever sacrificial animals you can afford.”**) Because lawful trade during hajj governs 2:198, the sacred landmark and remembrance arises from lawful trade during hajj. It follows that work and worship as one life completes lawful trade during hajj through the sacred landmark and remembrance.

**THE SACRED LANDMARK AND REMEMBRANCE**

The sacred landmark and remembrance begins from ***“When you return from ’Arafât, praise Allah near the sacred place”***, ***“and praise Him for having guided you”***. Al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām refers to the sacred place at Muzdalifah, where pilgrims remember Allah after leaving ʿArafāt. The night, prayer, supplication, and collection of pebbles in the following rite are governed by the Sunnah and detailed fiqh. The place is not worshipped. Its sanctity comes from Allah’s designation and its role in the pilgrimage. The command is repeated: *Remember Him.* Hajj is a movement of the body that must be animated by *dhikr*. Without remembrance, the pilgrim may experience only heat, crowds, and logistics. With remembrance, every step recalls Ibrāhīm, the unity of believers, death, resurrection, and Allah’s mercy. The believers are told to remember Allah because He guided them. Guidance—*guidance*—is not a personal achievement that makes a person superior to others. The pilgrim may have wealth, health, and opportunity, but the ability to know the rites and reach the sanctuary is a gift. Gratitude protects him from pride. *Before this you were astray* refers to the condition of humanity before prophetic guidance and to the specific ignorance from which the Arabs and other peoples were rescued. It does not require a Muslim to despise every aspect of his past or to deny the good traits that existed before Islam. It means that without revelation, people cannot reliably know how to worship Allah and organize sacred rites. (62:10 — **“Once the prayer is over, disperse throughout the land”**) The reason work and worship as one life follows the sacred landmark and remembrance lies in lawful trade during hajj. Therefore, work and worship as one life returns practical force to the sacred landmark and remembrance.

**WORK AND WORSHIP AS ONE LIFE**

***“for surely before this ˹guidance˺ you were astray”*** establish work and worship as one life. The verse’s permission for trade also establishes a wider Islamic vision. A person can earn lawfully, care for family, travel, study, and worship without dividing life into a sacred part and a godless part. The intention and method can make ordinary work an act of obedience. A trader who is honest during Hajj is displaying the worshipful meaning of seeking bounty. At the same time, commercial success must not become the measure of the pilgrimage. The pilgrim should avoid exploiting the demand for accommodation, food, transport, or religious services. Sacred need makes profiteering especially ugly. Fair prices and good service are part of *excellence*. Lawful trade during pilgrimage is like carrying provisions on a voyage whose purpose is worship. Food and commerce can support the journey without becoming its destination. The danger is not earning itself but allowing the market to drown remembrance after the great movement from Arafat. Present-day pilgrimage has extensive services and businesses, so the distinction remains urgent: seeking bounty is permitted, while gratitude at the sacred place keeps money in the role of servant rather than master. (7:172 — **“and had them testify regarding themselves. ˹Allah asked,˺ “Am I not your Lord?””**) In other words, work and worship as one life answers lawful trade during hajj.
