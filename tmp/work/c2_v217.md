**THE QUESTION ABOUT A SACRED MONTH**

***“They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about fighting in the sacred months. Say, “Fighting during these months is a great sin. But hindering ˹others˺ from the Path of Allah,”*** establishes the question about a sacred month. The verse responds to a question about an incident in which fighting occurred during a sacred month. The sacred months carry a restriction on warfare, and the believers did not dismiss that sanctity. Allah says clearly that fighting in the month is a grave matter. The answer then places the incident within the larger history of persecution: preventing people from Allah’s way, rejecting Him, blocking access to the Sacred Mosque, and expelling its inhabitants were graver offences. The Qur’an is not making the sacred month meaningless. It is exposing selective outrage. The opponents focused on one violation while ignoring the persecution, expulsion, and religious obstruction they had practised. Justice requires considering the entire pattern of aggression rather than using one rule as a shield for a greater campaign of harm. The four sacred months are protected times, and Muslims must not initiate wrongdoing in them. If an enemy uses that protection to attack or to continue expelling people, the community may defend itself within the limits of 2:190 and 2:194. The response remains proportionate and directed toward ending aggression. The principle is relevant beyond the calendar. A treaty, sanctuary, humanitarian pause, hospital, or protected status cannot be abused as a platform for attacking others. If protection is violated, the answer is lawful defence and restoration of safety, not abandonment of every rule. The verse lists offences worse than the fighting being questioned: blocking guidance, disbelief, preventing access to the Sacred Mosque, and expelling its people. These acts attack faith, liberty, home, and the right to worship. Fitnah here again refers to persecution and coercion that makes people suffer for their religion and drives them from their homes. The phrase does not mean that every non-Muslim person is guilty of these acts. (2:190 — **“Fight in the cause of Allah ˹only˺ against those who wage war against you,”**) Because the question about a sacred month frames sacred time and proportional justice, sacred time and proportional justice returns attention to the question about a sacred month.

**SACRED TIME AND PROPORTIONAL JUSTICE**

***“rejecting Him, and expelling the worshippers from the Sacred Mosque is ˹a˺ greater ˹sin˺ in the sight of Allah. For persecution is far worse than killing. And they”*** deepens sacred time and proportional justice. The context concerns a hostile group and its conduct. The Qur’an separately commands justice and kindness toward peaceful non-Muslims. A believer must identify the actual aggressor rather than generalize guilt to an entire religion, ethnicity, or population. They will continue fighting you until they turn you away from your religion, if they are able. This describes the active hostility of those who sought to force Muslims away from Islam. It does not command Muslims to assume that every neighbour secretly wants to convert them or to treat ordinary disagreement as war. The conditional if they are able portrays a determined persecuting force, not a universal psychological claim about all outsiders. The believer protects his faith through knowledge and worship, but he also honours the freedom of others. Coercion cannot create īmān, and the Qur’an prohibits forcing religion. Defence against coercion is not the same as imposing belief. The final section describes apostasy joined to dying in disbelief. His deeds have become worthless refers to the final state of a person who abandons faith and never returns before death. It does not mean that every sin erases all good deeds or that a person who struggles, doubts, or commits a grave act is automatically outside Islam. The Qur’an distinguishes final rejection from repentance and return. The verse’s warning is serious because faith is the foundation that gives deeds their direction. A person should protect it through knowledge, prayer, good companionship, and asking Allah for firmness. The Prophet ﷺ taught that hearts change and frequently made supplication for steadfastness. The phrase in this world and the Hereafter has been interpreted to include the loss of honour, protection, and the legal consequences associated with leaving the Muslim community in the historical setting, as well as the loss of reward in the Hereafter if death occurs upon disbelief. (2:194 — **“˹There will be retaliation in˺ a sacred month for ˹an offence in˺ a sacred month,”**) Since the graver offences follows the question about a sacred month, sacred time and proportional justice links purpose to the graver offences.

**THE GRAVER OFFENCES**

***“will not stop fighting you until they turn you away from your faith—if they can. And whoever among you renounces their own faith and dies a disbeliever,”*** clarifies the graver offences. Application of worldly legal rulings requires due process, knowledge, legitimate authority, and distinction between public treason, private doubt, coercion, and sincere questioning. Ordinary Muslims must not investigate hearts or take punishment into their own hands. The verse warns the living and directs them toward faith, while Allah judges each person’s final state with perfect knowledge. Even in this severe warning, the door of repentance remains open while life remains. The next verse praises those who believe, emigrate, and strive. The Qur’an repeatedly invites the person who has turned away to return. A believer who fears for his faith should not despair; he should seek knowledge, renew the testimony, leave harmful influence, and ask Allah for mercy. Think of the question about a sacred month through a man whose neighbour has blocked the only road to his shop for a year, and who breaks a fence in a month when building is forbidden. The fence is the offence and the road is the harm, and the court that hears the case orders the road opened before it fines either man. The question about a sacred month reaches a reader today who weighs his own small fault against a large wrong done to him and finds his own case lighter in both scales. The verse gives him a rule in which the offence he committed is named and the offence against him is measured beside it. Because the answer begins with his own fault, the question about a sacred month shows that the passage was written for the community rather than for its enemies. The Book states the same rule about persecution in the verse of the return, so that the question about a sacred month reveals the sentence used twice in the same chapter as a standard rather than a phrase. **PERSISTING IN THE ATTEMPT TO TURN BELIEVERS BACK**

***“their deeds will become void in this life and in the Hereafter. It is they who will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever.””*** closes with persisting in the attempt to turn believers back. A further passage counts the months of the year and names the four that are sacred, and the reader of the two together is shown that the calendar of the fight is held by God. The verse opens with a question that had become an accusation: They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about fighting in the sacred months. The occasion concerns a small party sent out under the command of one of the men of the community, and in the course of it a man of the enemy was killed. Because the quarrel turned on the date of the killing rather than on the killing itself, the reader is shown how a community can be trapped by a calendar. The community counted the day as the last of one of the permitted months, and the enemy counted it as a day of the sacred month of Rajab. The killing became the talk of the city. The closing condition states that the enemy would do it if the power were in their hands. Because the verse describes an intention rather than an event, the reader is shown why the community was told to prepare. The quarrel was not about a single caravan; it was about the survival of the faith of the travellers. (9:36 — **“Indeed, the number of months ordained by Allah is twelve—in Allah’s Record since the day He created”**) Sacred time and proportional justice makes the question about a sacred month practical; the graver offences therefore matters.
