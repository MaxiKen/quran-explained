**THE RAID OF NAKHLA**

The question of the sacred month is answered in phrases that weigh the sin and the siege: ***“They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about fighting in the sacred months. Say, “Fighting during these months is a great sin.”***, ***“But hindering ˹others˺ from the Path of Allah, rejecting Him”***, ***“and expelling the worshippers from the Sacred Mosque is ˹a˺ greater ˹sin˺ in the sight of Allah.”***, ***“For persecution is far worse than killing.”***, ***“And they will not stop fighting you”***, ***“until they turn you away from your faith”***, ***“if they can. And whoever among you renounces their own faith”***, ***“and dies a disbeliever, their deeds will become void in this life”***, ***“and in the Hereafter. It is they”***, ***“who will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever.””***. and the closing phrase keeps the fight aimed at the freedom of the faith. The walk moves through the question, the two sins weighed against each other, the persistence of the enemy, and the purpose of the defence, and the movement is the whole law of the sacred month. The two weights of the scale — the fight in the month and the siege of the faith — are placed in one sentence so that the reader cannot read one without the other. The account of the raiding party that the question refers to is preserved in the reports with its commander, its month, and its dead named.

**RAJAB ON THE LEDGER**

The question behind the verse is fixed in the reports with names: the raiding party sent out under the command of ʿAbd Allāh b. Jahsh returned having fought in the sacred month and killed ʿAmr b. ʿAbd Allāh of the Ḥaḍramī clan. The question they brought home — is the deed a sin or a service — is the question this verse answers with a scale. The record transmitted through Ibn ʿAbbās keeps the month named as Rajab, and the naming keeps the question attached to a calendar. The first weight on the scale is honest: fighting in the sacred month is a great sin, and the verse does not soften the first pan (3:100 — **“they would turn you back from belief to disbelief”**). Because the question is asked by people who feared they had sinned, the answer is a ruling rather than a rebuke.

**SIN WEIGHED AGAINST SIN**

Ibn ʿAbbās reports the party’s fear as the motive of the question, and the report explains why the answer arrives in the grammar of the scale rather than the grammar of the rebuke. The scale is the answer’s own instrument. The second pan carries the heavier weight: hindering people from the path, rejecting the faith, and expelling the worshippers from the sacred mosque are graver sins in the sight of Allah than the fight in the month. The weighing is the verse's method of moral arithmetic: two sins are placed on one scale and the scale reports which is heavier. Because the graver sin is dated as ongoing, the lesser sin becomes the answer to it rather than a rival to it. The wish of the enemy to erase the faith entirely is recorded in the legislation of the turned-back with the same diagnosis (4:89 — **“They wish you would disbelieve as they have disbelieved, so you may all be alike. So do not take them”**). The scale is the instrument the verse hands to every conscience that fears its own deeds.

**PERSECUTION HEAVIER**

The hierarchy is stated twice in the book because the hearing of it is slow and the obeying of it is slower than the rulings that arrive after it. The verse then states its own hierarchy in a sentence the chapter has stated before: persecution is far worse than killing. The repetition is deliberate, because the hierarchy governs every question of the kind the raiding party asked. Because the persecution is placed on the heavier pan, the fight in the sacred month is reframed as the lesser injury in a field of graver ones. The application is to weigh the sins of one's own hand against the sins the hand answers, and to do the weighing on the verse's scale. The slowness is part of the human material.

**THEY WILL NOT STOP**

The policy is read from the persistence rather than from the mood of the season, and the reading is what dates the defence. Because the policy does not expire at the end of the campaign, the readiness of the household is part of the ruling of the passage. The description of the enemy refuses the comfort of a limited war: they will not stop fighting you until they turn you back from your religion. The persistence is stated as a policy rather than as a mood, and the policy is what justifies the defence the question was about. Because the persistence is named, the fight is dated by the enemy's intention and not by the calendar's permission alone. The reading is therefore permanent.

**WHAT BREAKS THE SIEGE**

The close of the verse carries the purpose through the whole ruling: the fight continues until the practice of the faith is free and the persecution is over. The one who turns back from the faith at the end of the pressure has given the enemy the victory the verse refuses him. Because the purpose is the freedom of worship, the sacred month is answered with the sacred purpose. The application is to hold the two weights of the verse in one hand and the purpose in the other. The raid is described in the reports with the names of the commander, the month, and the dead, and the naming is what turns the event into a case at the court. The account of the shouting at the hour of the fight is preserved in the stories of the first campaigns, and the shouting keeps the memory of the truth inside the noise of the battle. Because the month is named in the question, the calendar is part of the evidence rather than part of the objection. The shape of the law that answers the question is the shape of the scale, and the scale is the instrument the passage teaches every conscience to carry. The truth of the matter is plain in the sentence that weighs the two sins: the disbelief that is called to is heavier than the fighting that answers it. The one who fears he has sinned at the hour of the necessity reads this passage as the judgment of his own case. Reviewing the two pans before the verdict is the discipline the legislation assigns to every court of the conscience. The sacred month is honored by the sacred purpose rather than by the empty calendar.
