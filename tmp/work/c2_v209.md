**IF YOU SLIP**

The conditional sentence walks from the slip to the two names that answer it: ***“If you falter after receiving the clear proofs”***, ***“then know that Allah is”***, ***“indeed Almighty, All-Wise”***. and the second name closes the judgment. The walk shows the whole discipline of relapse: the proofs arrive, the slip happens, and the knowledge arrives last to interpret the slip. The two names are placed where the frightened reader stands, which is after the fall rather than before it. Ibn ʿAbbās reads the proof as the exposition already in hand, and the might as vengeance against the one who refuses the exposition. The victory of the opened heart is narrated before the fall so that the fall can be read as a departure from the opened state.

**AFTER THE PROOFS**

The conditional clause dates the slip: it comes after the clear proofs have arrived, which means the relapse described is not ignorance returning but knowledge refused. The gloss carried from Ibn ʿAbbās reads the proof as the exposition already in the possession of the hearers, and the reading makes the slip a second act rather than a first one. The legislation of the relapsed names the temptation behind the return and the length of its patience (47:25 — **“Indeed, those who relapse ˹into disbelief˺ after ˹true˺ guidance has become clear to them, ˹it is˺ Satan ˹that˺ has tempted them,”**). Because the proofs precede the slip, the slip is a decision made with the evidence on the table. The verse therefore addresses the educated deserter rather than the confused beginner.

**MIGHTY IN THE ANSWER**

The knowledge the verse commands after the slip is knowledge of two names, and the first is might. Ibn ʿAbbās reads the might as vengeance against the one who refuses the exposition, and the reading gives the name its edge in the context of the relapse. The same edge appears in the history of the early community's own wobbling, where the promise kept and the courage that weakened are set side by side (3:152 — **“Indeed, Allah fulfilled His promise to you when you ˹initially˺ swept them away by His Will, then your courage weakened”**). Because the might is named first, the relapsed person is warned before he is comforted. The knowing arrives before the obeying for a plain reason.

**WISE IN THE HANDLING**

The second name answers the first: the One who is mighty in the answer is wise in the handling, and the pairing keeps the theology balanced. The wisdom named here is the wisdom that governs the timing of the slip and the timing of the return, and neither timing is left to the enemy's schedule. Because the two names stand together, the reader can neither despair of the mercy nor presume upon it. The clear proofs are to be held as a deposit in the name of the One who sent them. The slip is possible and the return is possible, and the two names govern both. The distinction between the clear communication and the falsehood is drawn by the early exegetes with the eye of the reader: the believer's eye and the denier's eye read the same page and see different books. The eye that has been trained by the light of the truth reads the page as a map, and the eye that has been darkened by the refusal reads the page as a wall. Because the same page is read in two ways, the difference is located in the reader rather than in the paper. The doubt of the two sons at the threshold of the house of the guidance is the doubt of the eye that has not yet decided which lamp to read by. Clearing the truth of the doubt is the work of the sight, and the sight is the organ the passage trains at length. The one who falls after the victory is the one whose eye was borrowed rather than trained. Reading the clear communication is a craft with its own instruments, and the instrument of the craft is the eye of the heart. The craft of the reading is older than the printing of the page, and the instruments of the craft are kept in the treasury of the heart. The map of the page is drawn for the eye that has learned to see in the dark of the doubt. The eye of the heart is trained at the desk of the reading.
