**A FACE LOOKING UP**

The heaven itself was watching the picture the verse frames. ***“Indeed, We see you ˹O Prophet˺ turning your face towards heaven. Now We will make you turn towards a direction ˹of prayer˺”*** ***“that will please you.”***. The Prophet's face searched the sky for the order. The answer to the search arrives as a gift with a preference attached. A direction that will please you. Ibn ʿAbbās's account puts the longing in the Prophet's prayer across a long season. He loved the House of his father Abraham. The verse now announces the return as a granting. What follows is that the change of qiblah is framed as an answered yearning, not an arbitrary decree. The effect of the framing is intimate. The law arrives inside a relationship. The one who legislates the direction is the one who noticed the upturned face. The point of the sighting is theological. Nothing about the Prophet's heart was lost on the sky.

**TURNING TOWARD A PLACE**

The command then lands on the whole assembly. ***“So turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque ˹in Mecca˺”*** ***“wherever you are, turn your faces towards it. Those”*** ***“who were given the Scripture certainly know this to be the truth from their Lord.”***. One face is turned first. Then every face follows. The singular becomes a plural inside a single sentence. Ibn ʿAbbās reads the ordering as deliberate. The Prophet turns, and the rows of the believers turn with him. Then the verse turns to the People of the Book with a startling aside. They know this address. The recognition is not a matter of debate. It is a matter of their own shelves. The awaited prophet's description and the standing of the House were already written in the material they guarded. The parallel of the House's founding places the address in its own archive (2:125 — **“And ˹remember˺ when We made the Sacred House a centre and a sanctuary for the people ˹saying˺,”**). The House was made a centre before the jeer was made. The aside about the Scripture-knowers is therefore a citation of the other side's own records.

**A LORD WHO NEVER MISSES**

The closing line holds the whole redesign under one gaze. ***“And Allah is never unaware of what they do”***. The turn of a thousand faces is not a logistics problem. It is a watched act. The gloss of Ibn ʿAbbās keeps the closing line aimed at every party in the scene. The Prophet who longed is seen. The believers who turned are seen. The scholars who recognised and stayed quiet are seen. The same custody of the House is stated in its liturgical form (2:125 — **““˹You may˺ take the standing-place of Abraham as a site of prayer.” And We entrusted Abraham and Ishmael to purify My House”**). The standing-place of Abraham is the pivot of the turn. The purification of the House is the condition of the meeting. For the modern reader the aside about the recognisers is the sobering half of the verse. Recognition without response is a documented state. This age keeps archives of the truth and departments for ignoring them. The closing gaze covers both. Nothing done at the turning is missed by the one who ordered the turn.

**A GIFT WITH A PREFERENCE**

The phrase that shapes the whole passage is the smallest one. A direction that will please you. The law of the qiblah arrives inside a relationship, and the relationship names the Prophet's own delight as part of the design. The effect of the phrasing is that obedience is not flattened into machinery. The one who commands the turn is the one who noticed the longing. The reason the detail is preserved in the verse is theological. The sky pays attention to hearts. The longing of the Prophet was seen before it was answered, and the answer was shaped to fit the longing. For the modern reader the pattern corrects a cold reading of religious law. This age separates the rule-book from the heart-book and sells both. The verse keeps them bound in one sentence. The turn is commanded. The pleasure is promised. The seeing covers the whole transaction. The direction that pleased the Prophet still gathers the faces of the world toward the house of his father, and the gaze that watched him look up has not moved away from the ones who follow.
