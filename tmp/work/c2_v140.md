**AN IDENTITY PROJECTED BACKWARD**

The verse states the last of the community's claims and puts it as a question addressed to them: ***“Do you claim that Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob”***, ***“and his descendants were all Jews”*** or ***“or Christians?”***. The claim is about retroactive identity, and the Book asks the claimants to notice what they are saying when they say it. Everyone named in the question lived long before the two communities existed, as the section on the covenant and the sanctuary had already shown. Another passage puts the same point in the form of a question about chronology and leaves the claimants to answer it: (3:65 — **“Why do you argue about Abraham, while the Torah and the Gospel were not revealed until long after him?”**). What is on display is a claim that cannot survive its own timeline.

**WHO KNOWS BETTER, ASKED ONCE**

The verse then supplies the question the Messenger is to ask, and the question is about the relative value of two kinds of knowledge: ***“Say, “Who is more knowledgeable”*** and ***“you or Allah?”***. The comparison is one-sided by design, and the argument needs no further step because the answer sits inside the question. Another passage puts the same comparison and measures the claim against what God has said about the patriarch, and there the verdict is stated directly: (3:67 — **“Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian; he submitted in all uprightness”**). What the sentence does is to move the dispute from the plane of inherited authority to the plane of information, where the claimants have nothing of their own to offer. Anyone who has seen a tradition shaped to suit its inheritors will recognise the movement.

**THE TESTIMONY PUT AWAY**

The offence underneath the claim is then named, in a question of its own: ***“Who does more wrong than those”*** and ***“who hide the testimony they received from Allah?”***. The charge is not that the communities invented a claim but that they concealed something they knew, which belongs to the same family as the obligation to make the scripture known that this passage has already set out. The account behind the accusation is that the earlier books contained descriptions of the coming prophet, and that the testimony had been withheld from those who were entitled to hear it. The word used for the hiding describes an act rather than an omission, since a document that has been put away has been put away by someone. Anyone who has seen evidence buried by the institution it told against will recognise the offence named here.

**A WITNESS KEPT OFF THE STAND**

Think of a court in which the decisive witness has been locked in a room by the party that was supposed to produce him. The verse names that conduct as the greatest wrong available in the situation, and the sentence places it beside the question about knowledge so that the two are read together. The comparison shows why the Book treats concealment as worse than ignorance, since a person who hides a testimony has decided that his own position matters more than the truth in his keeping. Anyone who has seen a community's scholars withhold what they knew will find the verse's question less rhetorical than it sounds. What the reader is left with is the search for what was put away.

**THE ATTRIBUTE THAT CLOSES THE MATTER**

The verse ends by naming what the concealers seem to have forgotten: ***“And Allah is never unaware of what you do”***. The clause is placed after the accusation rather than after the question, which turns the sentence into a statement about the record rather than a threat to the offenders. A reminder of God's knowledge closes an account of hidden conduct throughout this sūrah, and the formula is consistent because the temptation it addresses is consistent. Another passage repeats the same closing words in a sentence about the direction of prayer and the truth of what was sent down, so the reader meets the formula here as a pattern rather than an isolated remark: (2:149 — **“And Allah is never unaware of what you ˹all˺ do”**). The fact that nothing here has been lost, whether the community admitted it or not, is what remains.
