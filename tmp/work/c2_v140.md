**THE PATRIARCHS' IDENTITY CARD**

The verse names the claim in its full historical form. ***“Do you claim that Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob”*** ***“and his descendants were all Jews”*** ***“or Christians?” Say, “Who is more knowledgeable”*** ***“you or Allah?””***. The roll of names is the verse's own evidence. The same names the creed listed a few verses ago now stand as witnesses against the claim. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Mujāhid read the question as the refutation of both parties at once. The patriarchs predate the two names being claimed for them. So the dispute of the previous passages is settled by chronology as well as by theology. The verse treats the claim as a paperwork error. The identity cards are dated. The dates do not work. The reason the verse lists the descendants along with the patriarchs is that the claim covers whole families. The refutation has to cover them too. The reader sees the claim measured against the calendar it invokes. The question then moves the case from identity to testimony. The testimony belongs to the one who knows what no party to the dispute can know.

**A QUESTION OF GREATER KNOWLEDGE**

The counter-question is devastating in its simplicity. Who is more knowledgeable, you or Allah? In the reading of Ibn ʿAbbās the question stays attached to the Book's own record of the patriarchs. The names are in the revelation. The revelation's author is the one being asked about. The effect is that the claim's real weakness is exposed. It is not only old. It is unauthorised. The difference between a claim of inheritance and a claim of knowledge is what the question draws. Inheritance can be argued in a family dispute. Knowledge is settled at its source. What follows is that the parties are asked to weigh their certainty against the certainty of the Book. The weighing is quick work for anyone who believes the Book has an author. The reason the question is asked in this exact form is that it leaves the disputers two options. Confess the gap. Or claim the knowledge of the Lord. The claim about the patriarchs cannot survive the second option.

**THE HIDDEN TESTIMONY**

The verse then names the act the claim required. ***“Who does more wrong than those”*** ***“who hide the testimony they received from Allah?”***. The parallel verse states the hiding in its full shape (2:159 — **“Those who hide the clear proofs and guidance that We have revealed—after We made it clear for humanity in the Book—will be”**). Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Mujāhid mark the hiding as the pivot of the whole dispute. The testimony was received. The receiving is what makes the hiding wrong. So the disagreement is a concealment problem. It is not a comprehension problem. The patriarchs were known. The knowing was kept in a drawer. The effect is that the wrong is measured by the evidence in hand. The one who hides more testimony does more wrong. What follows is that the claim of the previous verses is read back as an act of hiding. The hiding has a witness that never stops watching.

**THE AWARENESS THAT NEVER BLINKS**

The verse closes the whole passage on a name of the Lord. ***“And Allah is never unaware of what you do”***. The parallel's sentence ends on the punishment of the hiders (2:159 — **“condemned by Allah and ˹all˺ those who condemn”**). The account of Ibn ʿAbbās leaves the ending standing over every later generation of claimants. The testimony is in the Book. The Book is with the Lord. The Lord is never unaware. The point is that the closing name answers the hiding with the one thing the hiding cannot survive. Total awareness. For the modern reader the ending is the passage's final comfort and its final warning. This age hides testimony in plain sight. Behind institutions and vocabularies. The verse says the unawareness is entirely one-sided. The effect of the close is that the reader leaves the dispute with the Lord's own name as the last word. The Abraham-arc ends here because the arc was always about the testimony. The House. The faith. The roll of names. The way. The devotion. The awareness that keeps the record of every one of them. The dispute ends. The watching does not.
