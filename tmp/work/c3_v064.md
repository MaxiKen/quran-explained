**NO PARTNER AND NO HUMAN LORDS**

***“common terms: that we will worship none but Allah, associate none with Him,”*** clarifies the foundation offered to both communities. Worship belongs to Allah alone, so devotion cannot be divided between the Creator and any prophet, saint, institution, or symbol. The next clause excludes association in every form. A community cannot confess one God while directing acts of worship to a created being. The words are common terms because earlier revelation also calls to the one Lord, not because truth is reduced to a negotiated compromise. Ibn ʿAbbās therefore reads the agreement as the declaration that there is no god except Allah. This gives interfaith discussion a clear method. It begins with what revelation holds in common, states the disputed boundary honestly, and refuses both hostility and doctrinal vagueness. Modern dialogue fails when courtesy requires silence about worship, because the verse joins courtesy to an unmistakable confession of divine oneness.

**SUBMISSION STATED AFTER REFUSAL**

***“nor take one another as lords instead of Allah.” But if they turn”*** applies divine oneness to human authority. Taking people as lords includes obeying them when they authorize rebellion against Allah. Ibn ʿAbbās makes that practical meaning explicit: no person may be followed in an act of transgression. Religious learning and communal leadership remain valuable, but neither grants a human being the right to override revelation. The sentence therefore examines every community, including the one issuing the invitation. It does not permit Muslims to condemn priestly domination elsewhere while reproducing unquestionable authority among themselves. The words ***“But if they turn”*** also recognize that a clear invitation may be refused. Refusal does not change the truth or justify coercion. The Messenger ﷺ later placed this verse in his letter to Heraclius, using common worship as the basis of public diplomacy. The next passage shows how argument about inherited identity can obscure that foundation (3:65 — **“O People of the Book! Why do you argue about Abraham,”**).
