**THE CHAIN THE VERSE LISTS IN ORDER**

The history is laid out as a ledger of gifts before it becomes a bill of charges: ***“Indeed, We gave Moses the Book”***, ***“and sent after him successive messengers.”***, ***“And We gave Jesus, son of Mary, clear proofs”***, ***“and supported him with the holy spirit. Why is it”***. The first gift is the Torah. One transmitted reading from Ibn ʿAbbās magnifies the scene of its descent. The book came down as a single whole. When Moses was commanded to carry it he could not bear its weight. An angel was appointed for each letter of it, and they could not bear it either. Then the weight was lightened for Moses, and he carried it. The language of succession is drawn from the neck. The verb for following means to come on the heels of what is in front, as the back of the neck follows the head. Al-Suddī hands down the verb of succession in the plain sense of one rank following another. The rhyme-word of poetry is named for the same reason — it follows the speech. The gifts to the son of Mary are then itemized as clear proofs: the raising of the dead, the healing of the blind and the leper, the shaping of the bird, and the foretelling of what is hidden. The same inventory of signs is spoken by the Messiah himself in the account of his mission: (3:49 — **“Allah’s Will. I will heal the blind and the leper and raise the dead to life—by Allah’s”**). The support named in the verse is the subject of its own grammar — an annexation that binds the noun of spirit to the adjective of holiness. The identification the glosses give is the great bearer himself, named for the sanctity of the one he served, who accompanied the Messiah wherever he went. The same title for the same bearer stands in the final revelation's own account of its descent, and the tradition keeps the identification in the line of the community's poet: Gabriel is the messenger among us, and the Holy Spirit has no equal. The pairing of proof and support is stated again in the surah's own later summary: (2:253 — **“son of Mary, We gave clear proofs and supported him with the holy spirit. If Allah had willed,”**). The list as a whole is a curriculum of care: a book, a succession, proofs, and a companion — every instrument of guidance delivered before the charge sheet opens. The angel's role through the whole career is remembered in the tradition fourfold: at the conception, at the birth, at the defense, and at the raising: (5:110 — **“how I supported you with the holy spirit so you spoke to people in ˹your˺ infancy”**).

**THE REFUSAL THAT WEARS ITS OWN TENSE**

The charge then arrives, and its grammar was dissected as carefully as the gifts: ***“that every time a messenger comes to you ˹Israelites˺ with something you do not like, you become arrogant, rejecting some”***, ***“and killing others”***. The repeated-time construction carries its own rebuke. The arrogance is the standing response to every arrival. The interrogative form is not a question but an indictment. The tense itself was noted by the language specialists. The verbs of rejection and killing stand in the present while narrating the past. The history is thus staged as still happening while the sentence is read. The named cases fix the register. The ones called liars include the Messiah himself. The ones killed include the prophet of the Baptist house and his father, the aged priest who defended him — the glosses naming the pair as the standing examples of the second clause. The description of the pattern in the tradition is one of worship of desire. Whenever the prophets brought an injunction that did not please them, the injunction was answered with denial or with murder. The covenant of the prophets had bound the opposite behavior into sworn terms, since each prophet was made to promise support for the confirmer who would come after the others: (3:81 — **“if there comes to you a messenger confirming what you have, you must believe in him and support him.” He added,”**). What follows from the tense is the passage's way of making the past an exhibit rather than a memory.

**THE ARROGANCE AS A CONSTANT**

The word for the response is the verse's psychological center, and its force was sharpened by the syntax around it. To disdain following is the meaning the glosses give the arrogance here. It is not the pride of the tyrant alone but the refusal of the pupil — the man who will not sit under a correction. The recurring construction that introduces the clause makes the behavior a constant rather than an event: every time, with what you do not like. The discomfort of the message, not the weakness of the messenger, is named as the trigger. The tradition presses the implication one step further. The selection of prophets to accept and prophets to kill was made by the same faculty — the taste that approved what flattered it and executed what rebuked it. The reason the passage itemizes the gifts first is therefore pedagogical. The charge is not ingratitude in the abstract. It is the specific crime of squandering a curriculum, a succession, and a companion on the same altar of appetite.

**WHEN THE RECORD STAYS OPEN**

Read as a record, the verse closes a section. Read as a diagnosis, it opens one. The pattern it names — accept the scripture that comforts, reject the correction that costs, and settle for the messenger what the message provoked — has no expiry date. The present-tense staging is the text's own device for keeping the exhibit open. Its effect is procedural: the reader is placed in the courtroom rather than in the archive. Any congregation today that prides itself on reverence for the prophets while managing away the parts of the message it dislikes will find the verse's question still standing in its exact register. The arrogance is measured not by the loudness of the denial but by the selection behind it. The closing clause of the charge names the floor of the selection — the killing — so that later audiences cannot file the passage as mere obstinacy. The reason the floor matters is that the passage has already shown where selection ends. The same taste that edits a message will eventually silence a messenger. What the passage finally demands is the one posture the pattern cannot counterfeit: hearing the whole message at the price of the whole self.
