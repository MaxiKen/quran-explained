**CHARITY CAN BE SPIRITUALLY CANCELLED**

***“O believers! Do not waste your charity with reminders ˹of your generosity˺ or hurtful words, like those who donate their”*** establishes charity can be spiritually cancelled. The verse warns believers not to destroy the reward of charity through mann and adhā: reminding recipients of the gift, demanding submission or praise, and hurting them with words. The physical donation may have helped for a moment, but the spiritual reward can be nullified when the giver turns it into a tool of humiliation. This warning is directed to believers because even a person who begins with faith can corrupt an act through later behaviour. Sincerity is not secured merely by making the initial payment. The giver protects the charity after giving by remaining humble, avoiding reminders, and seeking Allah’s acceptance rather than the recipient’s dependence. The verse compares injurious charity with spending to be seen—riyāʾ. The person who gives for reputation may receive worldly praise, photographs, business connections, or political influence, but he has not prepared a lasting account with Allah. The problem is not that people happen to notice a good deed; the problem is making their notice the purpose of the deed. Public charity can be valid and sometimes beneficial when it encourages others, reports a public trust, or funds a visible project. The giver should renew intention and protect recipients from being displayed. If applause comes unexpectedly, he thanks Allah and does not let it become the aim. The image is of a smooth stone covered with a thin layer of dust and soil. (2:261 — **“The example of those who spend their wealth in the cause of Allah is that of a”**)

**SHOWING OFF**

***“wealth just to show off and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day. Their example is that of”*** deepens showing off. Rain falls heavily and washes everything away, leaving the stone bare. The charity and outward appearance are like the dust; the rain of judgement reveals that no rooted reward remains. The person expected a harvest but had not planted in sincere soil. The metaphor also warns against relying on appearance. A large donation can be spiritually empty, while a small private gift can be immense before Allah. The heart is the ground in which the deed grows. If arrogance and showmanship cover it, the outward act may not survive the test of the Hereafter. They have no power over anything they earned. The giver who sought only human praise cannot rely upon that praise before Allah. The social status, photographs, or influence obtained from charity cannot purchase forgiveness. He may have gained worldly advantage, but he has no control over the eternal reward. The phrase does not mean that the recipient received no practical benefit. A meal can feed someone even if the giver’s intention was corrupt. It means the donor has no claim to the spiritual harvest he destroyed through hypocrisy and injury. Charity should not expose a person’s poverty unnecessarily. Donors and organizations must seek consent before using names, images, stories, or personal details. Children and refugees are especially vulnerable to being turned into publicity. The Qur’an’s concern with hurtful words includes the subtle humiliation of making a recipient feel permanently indebted. (2:271 — **“To give charity publicly is good, but to give to the poor privately is better for you,”**)

**THE SMOOTH STONE**

***“a hard barren rock covered with a thin layer of soil hit by a strong rain—leaving it just a”*** clarifies the smooth stone. A gift also should not be used to buy political loyalty, silence abuse, or pressure a person into an unlawful relationship. The recipient remains free before Allah and retains his rights. The verse links showing off with not believing in Allah and the Last Day. A person may speak religiously but act as though human approval is the final judgement. Belief in the Hereafter should make Allah’s evaluation more important than a temporary audience. The Prophet ﷺ warned that the first people judged may include a scholar, a fighter, and a charitable person whose deeds were performed for reputation, in the hadith recorded by Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. The hadith is a terrifying reminder that impressive deeds require sincere intention. Riyāʾ can be subtle and can enter after a sincere beginning. A person should not abandon a good deed simply because people notice it; he should correct the intention and continue where the deed is beneficial. If he has injured someone, he apologizes and restores dignity without making the apology another performance. Allah’s guidance is sought through hidden worship, remembrance, honest self-examination, and asking for acceptance. The believer fears hypocrisy without becoming paralysed by obsessive doubt. The paint holds for one season, the wood underneath goes on rotting, and the owner of the house returns in the spring to a wall of flakes. (107:1 — **“Have you seen the one who denies the ˹final˺ Judgment”**)

**NO POWER OVER WHAT WAS EARNED**

***“bare stone. Such people are unable to preserve the reward of their charity. Allah does not guide ˹such˺ disbelieving people”*** closes with no power over what was earned. Charity can be spiritually cancelled confronts showing off in a reader who gives to the mosque in the month of the festival and makes sure that his name is read out with the list. The verse gives him a rock, a thin layer of soil and a heavy rain, and it asks him what remains of his gift when the rain has passed through the street. Because the close names the people who cannot keep their own reward, the smooth stone teaches that a deed needs a reason deeper than the ground it fell on. The parable of the fertile hill is given right after this verse. And the two kinds of giving are drawn together in the closing verses of the passage. Because charity can be spiritually cancelled opens into showing off, the smooth stone joins charity can be spiritually cancelled to showing off. Since the smooth stone develops charity can be spiritually cancelled, showing off carries the transition. No power over what was earned matters, which means the smooth stone answers showing off. Showing off gives practical form to charity can be spiritually cancelled; no power over what was earned sets its horizon. Today, charity can be spiritually cancelled shapes showing off in present decisions. (18:103 — **“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Shall we inform you of who will lose the most deeds”**)
