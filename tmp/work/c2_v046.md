**CERTAINTY MAKES THE WEIGHT LIGHT**

The previous verse named the humble as the exception, and this one describes them: ***“those who are certain”***, ***“that they will meet their Lord”***, ***“and to Him they will return”***. Three clauses, and each of them explains a different part of the lightness described in the previous verse. The certainty is not optimism and it is not a mood; it is a settled expectation about an event, and the Book builds on it as the foundation of everything a person does with his hours. A man who expects to stand in front of his Lord cannot arrange his life as though the appointment were imaginary, while a man who has stopped expecting it will find every religious obligation heavier than it needs to be. Mujāhid treated the expectation in this verse as certainty, and al-Suddī likewise read it as sure knowledge. The language therefore does not praise a guess about the future. It describes an inward conviction firm enough to change how prayer and patience are carried in the present.

The second clause names the meeting, and the Book uses that word for the encounter that settles everything. It is not a vague survival after death; it is a presentation, which is why the verse can build an entire disposition on it. The third clause names the return, which is about direction rather than destination, and it tells the reader where the road he is already walking is going. The Book joins the same three ideas when it describes people whose good deeds are performed with a trembling heart because they know they are going back: (23:60 — **“and who do whatever ˹good˺ they do with their hearts fearful, ˹knowing˺ that they will return to their Lord—”**). That verse explains why the certainty does not produce complacency.

**HOW CERTAINTY MAKES HARD THINGS LIGHT**

The mechanism the verse describes is worth tracing, because a reader can test whether he has it. A person who is sure of a meeting prepares for it, and preparation converts discomfort into a task. Fasting becomes training rather than deprivation, charity becomes an investment rather than a loss, and an early prayer becomes an appointment kept rather than a duty discharged. The Book presents the change as a consequence of the expectation, not as a reward for it, which is why the humble in this verse find the burden light before they have received anything. They are lightened by what they are looking forward to rather than by what they have already been given. The Book also shows what happens when the expectation is present but weak, and the picture is not of a person who abandons his religion but of one who keeps it with a divided mind. Such a person does the acts and resents them, complies with the letter and negotiates over the spirit, and the Book's remedy for that condition is exactly what this verse describes. Certainty is not a feeling to be summoned; it is built by the same practice whose lightness depends on it, and the sūrah has already supplied the practice in the two instruments of patience and prayer.

**LIVE AS THOUGH THE MEETING IS ON THE CALENDAR**

Putting the meeting somewhere in the plan is what the verse is asking for. A reader who writes nothing in the calendar does not intend the appointment, and the Book is asking him to intend it in the ordinary way he intends everything else. That does not mean morbid reflection; it means the small adjustments a person makes when a date is fixed: what he prepares, what he stops doing, and whom he tells about it. The whole of the verse turns on those adjustments, because they are what separates certainty from opinion. One decision that is being made on the assumption that no meeting is coming should be remade on the assumption that one is. The Book does not ask the reader to feel certain on command, and it does not treat doubt as a disqualification. It asks him to act on the certainty he claims, and the acting is how the claim becomes a fact in his life. A person who does that in one matter will find the burden of the previous verse easier to carry the next morning. A fixed journey on a calendar changes spending and preparation before the day arrives. Certainty about the meeting works in the same manner, because the future appointment gives present worship a reason stronger than mood. For a worker today, the expected meeting changes hidden choices because no task is treated as unseen by the One who receives the return.
