**DEVOTED FIGHTERS BESIDE MANY PROPHETS**

***“˹Imagine˺ how many devotees fought along with their”*** ˹Imagine˺ how many devotees fought along with their prophets and never faltered despite whatever ˹losses˺ they suffered in the cause of Allah summons history's company to witness the standard the passage has been teaching. And the summons's census-word sets its scale. Wa ka'ayyin min nabiyyin qatala ma'ahu ribbiyyuna kathir. How many a prophet has fought, with him ribbiyyun in multitudes. The ribbiyyun-word. The Lord's-men. The rabbani-root. Whole companies of the devoted, era upon era, fighting beside their prophets. The earlier communities' faithful paraded before Uhud's bandaged ranks as their own lineage. Fama wahanu lima asabahum fi sabili llah. And they did not weaken for what struck them in Allah's cause. The wahn-verb predicated negatively of the whole company. Struck. Prophets wounded, ranks thinned, days turned. And unweakened. (3:145 — **“and those who desire heavenly reward, We will grant it to them.”**)

**NO HUMILIATING SURRENDER**

***“prophets and never faltered despite whatever ˹losses˺ they suffered”*** Wa ma da'ufu wa ma stakanu. Nor did they soften, nor did they submit. The three negations descending collapse's whole staircase. Wahn. The inner slackening. Da'f. The visible weakening. Istikanah. The surrender's abjection. The historical company certified at every stair unfallen. The verse thus answering, by census, Uhud's whispered singularity. Your prophet wounded. Theirs were. Your ranks bled. Theirs did. The standard is old, and it held. ***“in the cause of Allah, nor did they weaken”*** Allah loves those who persevere seals the census with the love-clause at the quality it certified. Wallahu yuhibbu s-sabirin. And Allah loves the patient. And the seal's selection completes the passage's love-registry. The muhsinin loved at the surrounding passage (the pardoners' excellence), the mutawakkilin ahead at the surrounding passage (the reliant), and here the sabirin. Patience under the cause's blows named among the qualities that draw the divine love itself (the registry this Book keeps deliberately short. The loved columns few, each a summit). The clause converting the historical census into the reader's own recruitment. The company paraded was not sealed at its era. The ribbiyyun's rank stands open, its entry-criterion published in the seal (the persevering loved. Whoever perseveres), and Uhud's community, holding through the day's staircase (the wahn resisted at 139's command, the ranks re-formed at 172's morning), was writing itself into the very census the verse recited to it. (3:147 — **“And all they said was, “Our Lord! Forgive our sins and excesses,”**) No humiliating surrender bears on modern life because communities endure severe loss when they refuse defeatist speech, internal collapse, and humiliating surrender to falsehood.

**STEADFASTNESS LOVED BY ALLAH**

***“or give in! Allah loves those who persevere”*** The example of devoted fighters beside earlier prophets gives believers a historical spine. At every era's blows. The causes bleeding, the guides struck, the days turned. The census stands as issued. How many before you, struck harder, holding longer. The three negations carried as the collapse-staircase's posted guard (the slackening named at its first stair, and refused there). And the love-clause held as the blows' own conversion-table. Each endured strike drawing not merely reward but the Beloved's love. While the next verse opens the company's mouth. Their whole speech under the blows recorded in one sentence, and it is a prayer. Our Lord. Forgive our sins and excesses, make our steps firm, and grant us victory. Imagine soldiers who lose their commander but keep their line, strength, and dignity. In connection with devoted fighters beside many prophets, Ibn ʿAbbās praises believers beside earlier prophets who neither weakened under death and injury nor surrendered themselves to an enemy. The history of earlier prophets prevents Uhud from being read as an exceptional collapse of divine support; which means devoted people before this community also fought, suffered, and saw losses. Their distinction was not freedom from pain but refusal to let pain produce three defeats: faltering inwardly, weakening in action, and submitting to the enemy’s moral terms; therefore steadfastness includes heart, body, and allegiance. Allah’s love gives that endurance its highest worth, beyond the visible result of a campaign; in other words the lesson reaches any long service where early excitement has faded. A caregiver, teacher, reformer, or worshipper may meet repetition, criticism, and slow results; hence the verse does not promise an easy road. It honours the people who remain upright upon it; this is why their strength is not a denial of exhaustion; it is the disciplined return to duty after exhaustion has been felt.
