**THE HEARTS THE SIGNS COULD NOT WET**

The address of you in this verse has drawn a real dispute, and both candidate referents are in the earliest glosses. Ibn ʿAbbās takes the hearts as the hearts of the slain man's heirs, and the narrative gives that reading its force: when their kinsman rose and named them, they answered with a denial made in full knowledge. Al-Awfi transmits from Ibn ʿAbbās the scene in these terms: *"when he was asked who had killed him, he said that his nephews had killed him; then he died again, and they denied the truth while they knew it"*. The other reading, standing with Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Qatādah, and the body of interpreters, takes the address to the Children of Israel entire — the community whose character the whole section has been prosecuting. A second axis of the same question then appears in the reading of the audience as such: the rebuke can stand against those who stood at the incident and watched a corpse testify, and it can stand against the later contemporaries who heard the account recited and turned from what it teaches. The difference between the two referents is smaller than it looks, since a group's settled culture is exactly what produces a family's denial at the crucial hour, and the layered reading lets the verse address the event and the inheritance of the event at once. What turns on the pronoun is therefore the scope of the warning, not its substance: whether the charge names the heirs who lied or the nation whose habit made the lie easy, the diagnosed condition is the same hardening after the clearest sign.

**WHAT HARDNESS MEANS AND WHY STONE IS THE MEASURE**

The hardening itself is named with a verb whose root family carries dryness and the withered state, and the glosses gather its senses like wounds on one body. The heart is described as dried and withered, which is the going of mercy and softness from it; another sense has it grown coarse; a third has it blackened. The comparison then does the diagnosis's work: ***“Even then your hearts became hardened like a rock”***, ***“or even harder, for some rocks gush rivers”***. The likeness is to the stone in its hardness and its barrenness toward the effect of deeds, and the strange word at the head of the sentence marks how far the state stands from expectation — the hardening arrives after the very event whose whole purpose was to remove it. The particle joining the second clause reads either as a plain and or as the sharper nay, and both keep the escalation: like stone, or past stone. The choice of stone over the harder metals is itself instructive, because the stone still keeps the possibility of what the heart has lost. Iron is harder and it is not the measure; the stone is, since the stone in this very passage does three things the hardened heart refuses to do.

**THREE KINDS OF STONE, THREE DEGREES OF ALIVE**

The catalogue of stones is graded with a careful hand. The first produce gushing rivers — the image at the top of the scale, a rock giving drink to beasts and men, the very figure of a life-bearing heart; the second ***“others split, spilling water”***, giving less but still giving; the third ***“while others are humbled in awe of Allah.”***. The water in the image is the water the Book everywhere treats as life and the means of life — sent down from the sky, rising from beneath the ground, and figured inside the creation of living things themselves — so that the rock which gushes is a dead thing producing what the living need, and the contrast with the heart that produces nothing is total. The falling of the third kind is then the summit of the scale rather than its floor, since a thing that fears is a thing still addressed by its Lord. The Book presses that consciousness onto the most solid objects it can name: (59:21 — **“Had We sent down this Quran upon a mountain, you would have certainly seen it humbled”**) — and the mountain of Moses furnishes the historical picture of the same truth, where the appointed sight levelled the peak to the ground before the man who asked to see (7:143 — **“If it remains firm in its place, only then will you see Me.” When his Lord appeared to the mountain,”**). The point is that the entire created order is awake to its Author; it is the human heart alone in this passage that sleeps.

**WHEN EVEN STONE REMEMBERS ITS LORD**

The tradition about living stones is not confined to this passage, and its reports draw the picture from every side. The Prophet's own words about the greeting stone — *"I know a stone in Makkah that used to greet me before I was made a prophet, and I know it even now"* — are transmitted in Muslim; the mountain that answered affection — *"This is a mountain that loves us, and we love it"* — stands in the two Ṣaḥīḥ collections; and the palm-stump that wept beside the pulpit, answered with the Prophet's embrace and his words *"It weeps for the remembrance it used to hear"*, is transmitted by Tirmidhī and Nasāʾī. A further reading of the three kinds of stone takes them as three kinds of human weepers — those who weep much, those who weep little, and those whose weeping stays in the heart and never reaches the face — and the psychology of the comparison is exact. Even the mountain's surrender is drawn into the argument, since the whole of creation is described as hymning its Author in voices the audience cannot hear — (17:44 — **“that does not glorify His praises—but you ˹simply˺ cannot comprehend their glorification. He is indeed Most Forbearing, All-Forgiving”**) — and some of the early readings hold that all three kinds of stone in the verse act out of fear of God, the gushing and the splitting included. In other words, the catalogue is not really mineralogy. It is a hierarchy of responsiveness laid against a congregation, and the stones occupy every rank above the one the hearts have chosen.

The verse closes the comparison by turning from the metaphor to the account: ***“And Allah is never unaware of what you do”***. The closing phrase is glossed from two directions by the same authority. Ibn ʿAbbās takes it as the assurance that God will not leave the transgressions unpunished, and he also reports the sense that the what you do includes what is hidden, the denials spoken in the heart while the tongue answers the questioner. The transmitted variant shifts the closing verb itself, moving from you do to they do as the accusation widens from the immediate audience to everyone who behaves like it. Against the certainty of the accounting stands the patience of the interval, since the same clause has been read as respite — God is not heedless, and He gives time until the appointed term. The two readings balance without cancelling: the unawareness is excluded, the haste is not implied. For congregations that hear the passage in every generation the application is exact and unsentimental. These days the pattern of hardening is not a story about a single family or a single town; it is the standing risk of anyone who watches a sign arrive and files it away unopened, and the closing clause is the reminder that filing is also doing.
