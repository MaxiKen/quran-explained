**THE CREED'S FULL ROLL**

The invitation is answered with an inventory. ***“Say, ˹O believers,˺ “We believe in Allah”*** ***“and what has been revealed to us”*** ***“and what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob”*** ***“and his descendants”*** ***“and what was given to Moses, Jesus”***. The roll of names is deliberately crowded. The names arrive in families and in sequences. No prophet can be used as a wall against another. The parallel of the sending names what the teaching adds (2:151 — **“and teaching you what you never knew—”**). In the recitation of the believers reported by al-Bukhārī from Abū Hurayrah from Ibn ʿAbbās, the Torah stands in its own shape. In Hebrew, of course. The point of the roll is that the books are honoured in the languages they came in. The believing named in the first line expands into a history. The history is the believer's property. The reason the roll is crowded is that the religion is crowded with messengers. The honour is the roof they all share.

**THE PROPHETS WITHOUT A RANKING**

The roll's second half refuses the very thing the invitations were built on. A ranking. ***“and other prophets from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them.”***. Qatādah names the collection behind the descendants' line. The twelve sons of Jacob. Every one of them a community in the counting. So the prophets are presented as one faculty of one teaching. They are not competitors for a single chair. The difference between honouring a prophet and ranking the prophets is exactly the distinction the verse refuses to make. The same chapter's warning names the cost of the refusal (4:136 — **“Indeed, whoever denies Allah, His angels, His Books, His messengers, and the Last Day has clearly gone far astray”**). The tradition of the books and the names leaves the roll as a statement of belief in a chain. It is not a scoreboard. The reason the verse lists the unnamed ones after the named ones is that the chain is longer than the list. Other prophets from their Lord. What follows is that the believer's creed is measured by its breadth of honour. Not by its exclusivity of name.

**THE SURRENDER AT THE END OF THE LIST**

The verse closes its inventory with the verb the deathbed used. ***“And to Allah we all submit.””***. The roll and the surrender stand in one sentence. The effect is a creed that ends where it began. In the submitting Abraham gave and Jacob's children echoed. The roll of names was never the destination. The destination is the verb at the end. The verb is plural. For the modern reader the structure is the instruction. This age collects affiliations the way the roll collects prophets. The verse says the collection must end in a surrender common to all of them. The obligation of the parallel ends in the same place. Faith in Allah and the Messenger and the books. Then the life that follows. What follows is that the believer who recites this verse has signed a document. Its final clause is a posture. The reason the verse can hold so many names without collapsing into a committee is that the names all end on the same word. The surrender is the list's conclusion. It is also its summary. The reader who believes believes in the whole faculty at once. The roll is spoken in the first person so that it can be recited rather than studied. The believing is not a report about other people. It is the community standing inside its own sentence. The names then do their quiet work. Abraham and his line. Moses and Jesus. The unnamed ones after the named ones. The roll refuses the ranking the invitations implied. Every prophet is honoured in his own tongue. Every book is honoured in its own language. The twelve sons of Jacob each stand as a community in the counting. The counting widens the creed past every later faction. The final verb then gathers the whole roll into one posture. Submit. The posture is plural because the creed is plural. A modern reader reciting the verse joins the plural. The joining is the point of the inventory. Believe in all of them. Distinguish between none of them. Submit with all of them. The roll ends where the deathbed ended.
