**PROOFS ARRIVING WITH THE HANDS FULL**

The recap opens with a whole prophetic career packed into one clause: ***“Indeed, Moses came to you with clear proofs”***. The proofs named here span the list the tradition keeps whole — the staff that became a serpent, the hand that shone white, the sea that parted into a dry road, the locusts and lice and frogs and flood-years, the manna and quails of the wilderness. One line of analysis takes the word for the proofs as naming the Torah itself with everything it shows. Ibn ʿAbbās glosses the plural in the plainest register he has: commands, prohibitions, and signs. The naming of the signs is itself the point the word-scholars make. They are called by the word for making-plain because they show every onlooker that the claim is a miracle rather than a trick. The analysts of the opening stack count three stresses in a row — an oath-letter, an affirmation particle, and the emphatic form. That is a triple emphasis the passage spends on the one fact nobody disputed. The readings over the scope of the proofs split. Some take them as the evidences of the prophet's truthfulness. Others take them as the evidences of the oneness he came to teach. The wider reading gathers both, since the fuller the light was, the darker the next clause becomes. The reason the list stands first is procedural. The verse is building the delivery receipt before it opens the charge. Its own logic is the logic of an exhibit list: the more signatures on the receipt, the worse the receiving.

**THE INTERVAL THE TURNING USED**

The fall is then dated by its own grammar: ***“then you worshipped the calf in his absence, acting wrongfully”***. The particle of sequence here is heavier than a plain and. The tradition notes that it implies a respite. The calf was taken up after the signs had been pondered and after the prophet had gone to his appointment. That converts the lapse into a decision. The making of the idol is remembered in the glosses with its material detail: the image was cast from their own ornaments and jewelry: (7:148 — **“In the absence of Moses, his people made from their ˹golden˺ jewellery an idol of a calf”**). The idol was therefore minted out of the very wealth God had given. It stands in the record as the first monument financed by its own congregation. The phrase for the absence is the phrase of the appointment itself — the one whose duration the people of the calf later refused to respect. Their words at the height of the episode were kept word for word in the account of the return: (20:91 — **“They replied, “We will not cease to worship it until Moses returns to us.””**). The last word of the verse names the wrong in the language of injustice rather than error, and the earliest glosses press it to its floor: the worship of the calf denied the prophet who had parted the sea and the Lord who had parted it through him. Read the accusation in order and its shape is clear. Proofs delivered, prophet absent, image financed, worship performed, injustice named — no stage of the file is missing its stamp.

**THE APPROVAL THAT SHARES THE GUILT**

The verse's legal reach concerns the second generation, and the tradition states the transfer of blame without embarrassment. The contemporaries of the last prophet did not themselves bow to the calf. What they did was honor the forefathers who had, and take their side. The passage counts the honoring as the sharing. The same mechanism runs the case of the killed prophets in the verse before this one. Those addressed answer for what they endorse, not only for what their hands did. The pattern-prediction in the tradition follows the same shape. A people whose forefathers denied Moses while the sea was still drying under their feet should not be surprised to find their grandchildren denying the final messenger while the descriptions sit unrolled in their laps. Moses himself opened the confrontation with his own people in the words the passage recalls: (2:54 — **“And ˹remember˺ when Moses said to his people, “O my people! Surely you have wronged yourselves by worshipping the calf,”**). Note the reflexive form. The wronging of themselves is the wrong's first address, which is why the repair was ordered on themselves too.

**THE EXHIBITS THAT CANNOT BE DISOWNED**

The practical demand the verse makes on later readers is of proof rather than of feeling. A community is not tried on its ancestry but on its docket of endorsements — the causes it funds, the forefathers it crowns, the silences it keeps. The calf's financing from the people's own jewelry remains the passage's picture of how worship is paid for. What follows from the picture is worth stating. The image was not brought in from a foreign cult. It was minted at home in the prophet's week away. Any assembly today that keeps its devotion warm in the intervals between instructions will find the verse dating its danger to the calendar of the absences. The proofs do not expire because the prophet steps out of the room. They stay on the shelf as the exhibits the return will open with.
