**THE MARTYRS NOT DEAD**

A forbidden sentence is filed at the top of the verse. ***“Never say that those martyred in the cause of Allah are dead”***. The sentence was circulating in the streets of the city. Ibn ʿAbbās's account reports the speakers and their script over the martyrs of the battles in these words: *"So-and-so has died and lost all comfort and happiness."* The aggravation of the sincere households was the purpose. The reason for the ban is the falsity of the sentence itself, not merely its cruelty. The correction arrives in the sharpest available form. ***“in fact, they are alive!”***. The life is not a metaphor in the grammar of the passage. It is the reality the death-talk obscures. What follows is that the language of the marketplace is placed under a veto. The dead of the cause are not available for the agitators' arithmetic. The effect is a public correction of the record, spoken over the graves themselves.

**THE SAYING OF THE AGITATORS**

The forbidden sentence has a motive and the motive is named in the tradition. The mourning was to be loud. The cause was to look costly. Ibn ʿAbbās keeps the aggravation in view as the engine of the wording. The verse answers the motive as well as the words. ***“But you do not perceive it”***. The perception clause is a rebuke dressed as a description. The living of the martyrs sits outside the audience's instruments. The instruments of the market cannot see it. The point of the rebuke is that the failure is located in the eyes rather than in the martyrs. The life continues. The audience lacks the lens. The difference between a hidden fact and a denied fact is the whole drama of the verse. The agitators denied. The verse asserts. The parallel of the provision-promise asserts with the same confidence (3:169 — **“Never think of those martyred in the cause of Allah as dead. In fact, they are alive with their Lord,”**). Alive with their Lord. The dictionary of the cause has its own vocabulary for the grave.

**THE GREEN BIRDS**

The tradition of the living martyrs gives the life a shape the eyes can hold. al-Bukhārī and Muslim both transmit the report that describes the spirits in *"green birds that take wing freely wherever they wish in Paradise"*. The image is not decoration. It is a citizenship. The birds move where they wish and the wish is granted. The provision runs beside the throne. The emigrants' promise states the same accounting from the entry-side of the ledger (22:58 — **“As for those who emigrate in the cause of Allah and then are martyred or die, Allah will”**). The provision is promised by the best of providers. For the modern reader the veto on the death-sentence is a lesson in language discipline. The words used about the dead of a cause shape the living of the cause. This age speaks of loss and of cost at the graveside. The verse speaks of life and provision. The two dictionaries disagree about the ending, and only one of them is filed at the throne. The birds keep flying. The provision keeps arriving. The words of the agitators expire.

**A LANGUAGE POLICED AT THE GRAVE**

The veto on the death-sentence is finally a lesson in the power of vocabulary. The agitators chose their words to instruct the market. The verse chooses its words to instruct the sky-side of the same market. The effect of the correction is that the bereaved are given a grammar that can survive the grave. The point of the perception-clause is that the living martyrs are outside the instruments of the crowd, not outside the care of the Lord. The instruments of the market measure breath and payroll. The instruments of the promise measure provision and presence. For the modern reader the choice of dictionary is daily work. This age speaks of loss and cost and closure at every graveside, and the words do their quiet work on the living. The verse offers the older vocabulary. Alive. Well provided. Seen by the one who keeps the birds flying. The speech of the cause is policed at the grave because the speech of the cause is the inheritance of the household. The words of hope are not decoration. They are equipment.
