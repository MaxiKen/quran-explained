**THE SENDING THAT COMPLETED THE FAVOUR**

The qiblah passage closes by naming the gift that crowns it. ***“Since We have sent you a messenger from among yourselves”***. The sending is the perfection of the favour. From among yourselves. The mercy is packaged as family. Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss ties the clause back to the verse of perfecting. The favour of the direction was completed with the favour of the messenger. The prayer of the builders of the House had asked for a messenger raised from among them. The verse now delivers the messenger and names the work. What follows is that the redesign of the prayer is joined to the arrival of a teacher. A community is given a direction and a curriculum at once. The effect is a favour in two halves. The faces have somewhere to turn. The hearts have someone to teach them why. The placement of the verse at the close of the qiblah section is itself a commentary. The turn was always going somewhere. The messenger names the destination.

**THE CURRICULUM'S FOUR MOVES**

The work of the messenger is stated in four verbs. ***“reciting to you Our revelations, purifying you, teaching you the Book”*** ***“and wisdom, and teaching you what you never knew”***. Recite. Purify. Teach the book. Teach the wisdom. The unknown is added at the end as an open door. Ibn ʿAbbās unpacks the verbs as a system. The reciting carries the commands and the prohibitions. The purifying is the growth that cleanses. The teaching covers the scripture and the rulings that stand beside it. The wisdom named beside the book is the prophetic practice that shows the book in motion. The parallel account of the believers' favour states the same verbs in the plural (3:164 — **“and teaching them the Book and wisdom. For indeed they had previously been clearly astray”**). The other passage states the same sending in its own register (62:2 — **“teaching them the Book and wisdom, for indeed they had previously been clearly astray—”**). The commission is therefore fixed across the book. Four doors open into one room. The difference between a preacher and this messenger is the range of the verbs.

**THE RECEIVING THAT NEVER STOPS**

The final clause keeps the curriculum open at the far edge. Teaching you what you never knew. The receiving is designed to continue. The gloss of Ibn ʿAbbās marks the clause against the pride of the shelf. The People of the Book held a scripture and recognised the traits. The community is handed what no shelf held alone. What follows is that the community of the qiblah is a community of students by constitution. The direction gathers the bodies. The teaching gathers the minds. For the modern reader the four verbs test every religious claim in public. Where the reciting stops, the claim closes its source. Where the purifying stops, the claim becomes decoration. This age keeps teachers of the book and few teachers of the wisdom. The commission refuses to separate them. The unknown is where the mercy of the sending is felt most, because the receiving is still in progress. The messenger came to open the door, and the door has no plan to shut.

**A TEACHER, NOT ONLY A LAW**

The pairing of the sending with the qiblah is the verse's deepest structural claim. A direction without a teacher is a wall with a doorway painted on it. A teacher without a direction is a voice in a storm. The verse delivers both at once, and the effect is a complete apparatus. The faces are gathered by the law. The hearts are gathered by the curriculum. The reason the four verbs are listed rather than summarised is that each one repairs a different damage. The reciting reconnects the speech of the people to the speech of the sky. The purifying addresses the character. The teaching addresses the mind. The unknown at the end addresses the future. Each verb is a repair-job on a different layer of the same damage, and the four together rebuild the whole house. For the modern reader the apparatus is the measure of every school and every mosque in this age. Where the verbs are split apart, the institution thins. Where they stand together, the institution can carry the weight of a community. The sending is the favour that perfects the turn. The teacher is the mercy that explains it.
