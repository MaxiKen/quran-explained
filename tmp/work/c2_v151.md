**THE ANSWER TO AN OLD PRAYER**

A word at the head of the verse ties it to what came before, and it names the greatest of the favours just mentioned: ***“Since We have sent you a messenger from among yourselves”***. The sending is not a new decision taken late in the story, because the founder of the house that has dominated the passage had asked for exactly this, and the prayer is preserved earlier in the sūrah for the reader to recognise: (2:129 — **“Our Lord! Raise from among them a messenger who will recite to them Your revelations, teach them the Book and wisdom,”**). The phrase "from among yourselves" answers the heart of that prayer, since the messenger belongs to the people he is sent to, and no strangeness of language or lineage stands between them. What the verse presents as a completion of favour is therefore the fulfilment of a request made by the man whose sanctuary the community now faces.

**RECITING, PURIFYING, TEACHING**

The verse then lists what the messenger does among them: ***“reciting to you Our revelations, purifying you, teaching you the Book”***. The order is worth following, because the recitation comes first and the purifying follows it, as though the words themselves were the instrument of the cleansing. A later sūrah states the same favour in almost the same words and calls it a great gift: (3:164 — **“Indeed, Allah has done the believers a ˹great˺ favour by raising a messenger from among them—reciting to them His revelations, purifying them,”**). The purification is understood to cover both the removal of the habits of the age of ignorance and the growth of the character that the revelation asks for. The messenger is not described as a ruler who imposes a new order from outside but as a teacher who works within the community, and the sequence of verbs keeps that picture in view.

**WISDOM TAUGHT BESIDE THE BOOK**

The instruction continues with the two things the messenger is said to teach: ***“and wisdom, and teaching you what you never knew”***. The Book and the wisdom are named together, which is taken to mean that the revelation is not left to the reader's own judgement but comes with an authoritative explanation, and the wisdom is taken by Ibn Kathīr to include the practice that the messenger established. The same pairing appears in the prayer of Abraham and in the description of the sending, so the two are bound together wherever the favour is described. The result is a community taught from the outside by revelation and from within by the example of its messenger. The verse thus answers a question that the passage had left open, since the community that had been given a direction also needed to be told how to live in it.

**A TEACHER IN A TOWN WITHOUT A SCHOOL**

Think of a town that has no school and no one able to read, and of a teacher who comes from that same town and begins by reciting aloud to the people, then by correcting their manners, then by instructing them in the books they never had. Nothing in the scene depends on the teacher bringing a foreign curriculum, because the words themselves are the lesson, and the change they work is visible in the lives of the people before it is visible in their libraries. The verse describes the same scene with the same order of events, and it presents the sending of the messenger as the answer to a request for exactly such a teacher. Anyone who has seen a community change its conduct under the steady instruction of one honest person will recognise what the sentence is naming.

**WHAT THEY COULD NOT HAVE KNOWN**

The last clause explains why the blessing had to come from outside the community's own resources: ***“and teaching you what you never knew”***. What is taught includes the matters of the unseen and the accounts of the prophets that no reasoning could have reached and no earlier book had put in their hands. Another passage describes the same sending to a people who had been left without a scripture of their own, and it uses the same list of gifts: (62:2 — **“He is the One Who raised for the illiterate ˹people˺ a messenger from among themselves—reciting to them His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom,”**). The verse therefore ends by measuring the favour, since the community is shown what it lacked before the messenger came. Read with the sentence that follows, it turns the gift into an obligation, because a people who have been taught are expected to remember the one who taught them.
