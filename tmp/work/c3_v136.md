**FORGIVENESS FROM THE LORD**

***“Their reward is forgiveness from their Lord and Gardens”*** Their reward is forgiveness from their Lord and Gardens under which rivers flow, staying there forever pays the portrait just completed. And the payment's order is its jurisprudence. Ula'ika jaza'uhum maghfiratun min rabbihim. Those. Their reward is forgiveness from their Lord. The pardon paid first, before the estate. The sequence matching the race's own order and the heirs' own need. The maghfirah's sourcing from their Lord carrying the relationship's warmth (the rabb-word of nurture. The pardon issued not by a tribunal's clemency but by the Sustainer's own hand). Then the estate. Gardens under which rivers flow. The inheritance announced at the surrounding passage's cosmic width now deeded in the Book's constant imagery, with the tenure-clause that converts all of it from visit to home. Khalidina fiha. Abiding forever. The eternity that answers the heirs' deepest anxiety. The portrait’s people, whose biography contains falls and returns, receive a residence from which no fall exiles. The permanence itself the pardon's completion. (3:135 — **“remember Allah and seek forgiveness and do not knowingly persist in sin—and”**)

**GARDENS WITH FLOWING RIVERS**

***“under which rivers flow, staying there forever. How”*** How excellent is the reward for those who work ˹righteousness˺ seals the payment with the exclamation. Wa ni'ma ajru l-amilin. And how excellent the wage of the workers. And the seal's chosen noun is the verse's last teaching. Al-amilin. The workers. The portrait’s people are named at payment by their labour. The givers in both weathers, the rage-swallowers, the pardoners, the returners. All of it filed as amal. Work. The wage-grammar (ajr. The earned payment) completing this argument's constant economics. Paradise deeded by grace and priced in labour at once. The grace sourcing and the labour routing (the mercy reaching, by His own published terms, the working. The two truths must remain together. The wage real, the Wage-Payer generous beyond all earning). (3:137 — **“Similar situations came to pass before you, so travel throughout the land”**) Gardens with flowing rivers bears on modern life because workers for good need a horizon larger than immediate recognition, because the promised reward is lasting.

**AN ABIDING REWARD FOR WORKERS**

***“excellent is the reward for those who work ˹righteousness˺”*** The three-verse unit (134 to 136), completes the bridge-passage's whole ascent. From the riba-prohibition's descent through the Fire's border (131) and the obedience-hinge (132) to the race (133), the portrait (134 and 135), and the payment (this verse). The community's inner economy re-founded, mid-battle-curriculum, on the counter-multiplication. Gains compounded by giving, strength by swallowing, honour by pardoning, and records by returning. The ni'ma-exclamation's excellence being the whole exchange's audited verdict. And the passage now returns, its economic interlude complete, to the field's resumed lessons. Similar situations came to pass before you, so travel throughout the land. Uhud re-entered through history's own precedents. Imagine workers finishing a garden whose permanent home is promised beyond the construction site. In connection with forgiveness from the lord, Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the workers reward as forgiveness and permanent gardens in which death and departure no longer occur. Forgiveness comes before the gardens because entrance begins with what Allah removes and continues with what He grants; which means the reward is not a temporary relief followed by insecurity. Rivers flow beneath a lasting home, and the residents remain there; therefore calling this the reward of those who work also keeps hope joined to moral effort. Their deeds do not force payment from Allah, but sincere work is the path He has promised to honour; in other words the praise of the reward directs attention beyond the hardship of repentance and restraint. A person who gives, controls anger, pardons, and returns after sin is not merely losing pleasures; hence the person is travelling toward a better and permanent good. In present life, delayed results can make obedience seem unrewarded; this is why the verse answers by widening the time in which reward is measured. What is costly for an hour may open into a residence that has no final hour. The plural gardens suggest abundance, while flowing rivers suggest life without the fear of exhaustion. These images answer the narrow rewards that sin promises for a moment. A lasting home makes restraint reasonable, since the thing refused now is compared with what cannot be taken away later. Hope becomes a form of clear judgment rather than wishful escape.
