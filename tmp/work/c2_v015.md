**THE MOCKERY RETURNS AS A JUST REPLY**

The boast at the end of verse 14 receives an answer from above: ***“Allah will throw their mockery back at them, leaving them to continue wandering blindly in their defiance”***. The translation makes the return clear. God does not share the petty need that drove their scorn, nor is His act a lie or a cruel joke. Their mockery comes back as shame and just loss. Early readers explain the form as a fitting reply whose name matches the sin: they mock the faithful, so their own plan is exposed and made a cause of scorn. Some also held the wording as a divine act affirmed as revelation states it, while denying every flaw found in human mockery. Both lines guard the same truth. God is not fooled, unjust, or in need of sport. He gives their deed its due. A like answer appears when hypocrites mock small gifts: (9:79 — **“for giving only the little they can afford. Allah will throw their mockery back at them,”**). The return fits because the proud stance is itself overturned. Those who thought they watched from above become the ones displayed, while the faith they mocked proves sound.

**THE LAST DAY MAKES THE REVERSAL VISIBLE**

Ibn ʿAbbās links this reply to the Hereafter, when a hope of entry is shown and then closed before them. The Qur’an gives a grave scene of that loss. Hypocrite men and women ask the believers to wait so that they can take from their light. The answer tells them to go back and seek light; then (57:13 — **“a ˹separating˺ wall with a gate will be erected between them. On the near side will be grace”**). Their old claim of being with the believers returns as a plea: (57:14 — **“The tormented will cry out to those graced, “Were we not with you?” They will reply, “Yes ˹you were˺.”**). They had shared streets, meetings, and the outward acts of the group, but they had not sought the light at its source in true faith.

The scene is a precise reply to their old game. In the world, they went back and forth between two groups and took the words that served each meeting. On that Day, they cannot cross the wall by changing speech. The side of the wall that holds grace is no longer gained through a claim. The same reply then names their past: they gave in to the test, waited for the believers to fall, doubted, and let false hopes fool them. This is why the response is not mere public shame. It unveils what their masks had hidden and sets each person with the side truly chosen. The mockers once treated faith as a role that could be put on. The Last Day shows that its light had to be received in the heart before that day came.

**RESPITE CAN BE A TEST RATHER THAN A SIGN OF APPROVAL**

The second part of the reply takes place even now. They are left time, and their revolt spreads within that time. The delay may look like success because no blow falls as soon as the lie is told. Yet lack of swift pain is not proof that God is pleased. Mercy may delay a due end so that a sinner can return. If he uses the delay to add pride to pride, the same span becomes a path toward a fuller account. The Qur’an warns of both the slow approach and the perfect plan: (7:182 — **“As for those who deny Our signs, We will gradually draw them to destruction in ways they cannot comprehend”**), followed by (7:183 — **“I ˹only˺ delay their end for a while, but My planning is flawless”**).

This does not make sin grow without a first choice. The passage has already shown lies, scorn, and willful ties with evil chiefs. The added room meets a revolt they keep choosing. Another verse says of those fixed in disbelief, (3:178 — **“They are only given more time to increase in sin, and they will suffer a humiliating punishment”**). Thus, divine patience must not be read through results alone. Wealth, access, praise, and freedom from an immediate cost may all remain while a person moves farther from truth. The true use of respite is repentance. Each quiet day is time to drop the mask, repair a wrong, and seek light. To spend it on new fraud is to turn a gift of time into proof against oneself.

**BLIND MOTION IS NOT THE SAME AS A GUIDED PATH**

Their defiance crosses set bounds, while their blind wandering keeps them from a clear end. They are active, but activity is not guidance. A lost traveller may move fast and still add mile after mile to the wrong road. Speed then makes the error grow more quickly. In the same way, the hypocrites plan, meet, speak, and gain, yet none of that motion gives them a sound aim. Their confusion is moral, not a slur against people who lack physical sight. It lies in the will that refused the first light. The order is stated elsewhere: (6:110 — **“We turn their hearts and eyes away ˹from the truth˺ as they refused to believe at first,”**). Refusal comes first; being left to roam follows as judgment.

The warning is close today because quick success can look like a verdict from God. A lie may win the room. Mockery may earn laughs. A double pledge may keep two sets of friends. This verse says that none of those results can prove the road is safe. The right questions concern truth, God’s bounds, and the end toward which each choice moves the heart. A believer should not ask to be led on in a wrong that feels easy. He asks to be corrected while correction still hurts, to see the harm beneath a short gain, and to use every delay as a door back. The worst answer to mockery is not that the mocker fails at once. It is that he is allowed to think he won until his blindness has carried him far from home.
