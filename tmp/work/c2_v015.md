**THE ANSWER THAT MEETS THE MOCKERY WITH ITS OWN NAME**

The verse answers the two-faced speech of the pretenders with a single sentence: ***“Allah will throw their mockery back at them”***. The wording describes the return of a thing to the person who sent it, which means the men are paid in the coin they chose for others. Ibn ʿAbbās is credited with a reading in which the return happens at the gate of the Garden, where the pretenders are admitted and then shut out while the believers look on, and the early teachers generally held that the punishment is named after the offence it matches. The Book uses the same sentence about another group of mockers who laughed at the believers for their giving: (9:79 — **“Allah will throw their mockery back at them, and they will suffer a painful punishment”**). The reason the return is traced to God rather than to the believers is that no community is asked to take its own revenge, and the verse lifts the matter to a court that never misses a case.

**THE BLINDNESS THAT CONTINUES IN PLACE**

The sentence finishes with the state the men are left in: ***“leaving them to continue wandering blindly in their defiance”***. The wording describes a walk with no direction and a stubborn refusal to stop, and the condition is described as continuing rather than beginning here. The Book uses the same phrase at the end of a passage about people whose hearts were turned away from the truth: (6:110 — **“leaving them to wander blindly in their defiance”**). The repetition is deliberate, because the verse is telling the reader that the punishment of this group is the road they had already chosen, now extended and made permanent. The phrase was taken by the first generation as a description of men who cannot tell faith from error any longer, which means the blindness is not a lack of light but the loss of the ability to use it. The warning here is about the seriousness of a habit that is defended long enough, since a road that begins as a preference can end as a condition, and the man who chose it no longer has the freedom he thought he was protecting.

**WHY THE MOCKERY MATTERED SO MUCH**

A reader may wonder why the Book treats mockery as a crime heavy enough for this answer, and the reason is in what mockery does to a community. Ridicule is the cheapest way to make a person ashamed of a good he had decided to do, and it costs the mocker nothing while it empties the work of the people he laughs at. The Book names the same weapon in the hands of the men of the past and says that the reckoning reached them too: (45:35 — **“you made a mockery of Allah's revelations”**). The pretenders of this sūrah used their two speeches to keep their position, and when the position was threatened they laughed at the faith of the people who fed them and sheltered them. That is why the answer comes from God and lands on the mockery itself, and why the reader is meant to be careful with a joke about another person's worship, since the Book keeps a record of it.

**A BALL THROWN AGAINST A WALL**

Think of a boy who throws a ball at a wall to frighten someone on the other side and finds the ball coming back at his own face. The verse uses the same image of return, and the comparison helps a reader see why the sentence is a fitting answer rather than a random punishment. These days a person can destroy a reputation with a laugh, and the platform carries the joke further than the intention ever travelled, which is the mockery of this verse with a longer reach. The reader is given the choice of the company he joins, and the sūrah has already said which company keeps its word. A community cannot arrest every mocker and should not try, and the verse reassures the people who are laughed at that the matter is not left unfinished anywhere, in this life or beyond it. The next verse turns to the two roads this traffic has been moving between, and it shows what a person loses when he chooses the one the mockers took.
