**AN ADDRESS TO EVERY HUMAN BEING**

After the long account of the pretenders, the address widens to the whole of humanity: ***“O humanity! Worship your Lord”***. The address is wider than the two groups described before it, and it reaches the readers of the Book in every generation along with the community that first heard it. ʿAlqamah and Mujāhid are credited with the observation that every address in the Book beginning with this phrase was revealed in Mecca, while the direct address to believers belongs to Madinah, which places this command among the earliest of the sūrah's sentences in order of revelation. Ibn ʿAbbās read the call as directed at the people of Mecca and also, in another report, at the people of the Scripture, and the two readings show how wide the door is meant to stand. The command is not addressed to a rank of scholars or to a nation with a claim on God; it is addressed to the species.

**WORSHIP GATHERS EVERY ACT OF SERVICE**

The command is followed by the word for worship, and Ibn ʿAbbās gave the reading that in the Book the word carries the sense of declaring God one and alone. That reading keeps the sentence from shrinking into ritual, since the first thing worship asks of a person is that he settle who God is. The Book states the purpose of the whole creation in the same terms in another sūrah, and the sentence there would fit here without a change: (51:56 — **“I did not create jinn and humans except to worship Me”**). The reasoning that joins the two verses is the difference between a servant who performs a duty and a creature who exists for a purpose, and the verse in this sūrah is asking for the second understanding. That is why the command is placed before the account of the gifts that follows, because the gifts are the evidence and the worship is the answer.

**THE ARGUMENT FROM THE GENERATIONS BEHIND HIM**

The verse attaches the command to the act that made the commanded possible: ***“Who created you and those before you”***. The argument moves from the person in front of the sentence to the generations who stood in the same place and passed away, and it leaves the hearer with a lineage of creatures who were all brought into being by the same hand. The Book puts the same argument in the form of a question in another sūrah: (52:35 — **“Or were they created by nothing, or are they ˹their own˺ creators”**). The reason the second half of the phrase matters is that a person can allow a claim for his own existence and no further, and the verse widens the claim to everything that came before him. The reader is shown a chain whose beginning he cannot see, and he is told that the One who made the chain is the One he is being called to serve.

**THE PURPOSE NAMED IN THE LAST WORDS**

The verse closes with the object of the command, and the wording is one of purpose: ***“so that you may become mindful ˹of Him˺”***. The devout scholars noticed the shape of the expression, since hope is not a quality that belongs to God in the way it belongs to a creature, and they explained it as a promise in the form of an aim, so that the servant knows what the command is intended to produce in him. The Book names the same quality as the fruit of worship in a hundred places, and the sūrah itself opened by naming the mindful as the people who would be guided. The point is that the command is not aimed at producing a performance but at forming a person, which is a difference worth keeping. The Book names the same quality as the mark of the people it addresses when it speaks to the believers in another sūrah: (3:102 — **“Be mindful of Allah in the way He deserves”**). Think of a teacher who sets an exercise not to fill an hour but to leave the student with a habit of mind; the command here works in the same way, and it can be repeated today for the same result in anyone who answers it.
