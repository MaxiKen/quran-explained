**AN INVITATION ADDRESSED TO EVERYONE**

After the portraits of the three kinds of listener, the sūrah opens a door and speaks to all of them at once: ***“O humanity! Worship your Lord”***, ***“Who created you and those before you”***, and ***“so that you may become mindful ˹of Him˺”***. The address is not to the believers, and it is not to a tribe or a generation. It reaches the man who has just closed his ears, the one who has been managing his options, and the one who has been walking in borrowed light. Everything that follows in the chapter rests on this sentence, which is why it is delivered in the plural that leaves nobody outside. Ibn ʿAbbās named the people of Mecca in his reading of the address, and others extended it to every audience that hears it.

The reason given for worship is worth noticing, because it is not a threat. The command is attached to the fact of creation: the One being asked for worship is the One who made the congregation and everyone who came before them. A reader who has spent years hearing worship presented as an obligation owed to an authority will find this verse gentler and more demanding at once. It is not asking a stranger for a formality. It is asking a person to acknowledge the one who made him, and the acknowledgement is what the word worship covers here. The purpose named at the end is the same thing said from the human side, since becoming mindful is what happens when a creature realises whose creature he is.

**WHY THIS IS THE FIRST COMMAND IN THE SŪRAH**

The placement of the verse is part of its meaning. The chapter has spent twenty verses explaining that the Book guides some, cannot reach others, and is handled dishonestly by a third group. Now it states the one thing it wants from everybody, and it states it in a sentence a child can carry. Nothing in the command depends on the reader's learning, his income, or his standing in a community. That is why the Book elsewhere puts the same instruction at the centre of the human story: (51:56 — **“I did not create jinn and humans except to worship Me”**). Laid beside this verse, the two statements describe one purpose that the whole creation is arranged around, and the arrangement includes the reader who is holding the Book.

The word used for mindfulness deserves a moment, because it runs through the whole sūrah. It is not fear in the sense of panic, and it is not piety in the sense of an appearance kept up for other people. The Book describes it as a protective awareness, the state of a person who lives as though he is being watched, because he is: (59:18 — **“O believers! Be mindful of Allah and let every soul look to what ˹deeds˺ it has sent forth”**). The reader who grasps this will understand why the sūrah keeps joining worship to conduct. A man who acknowledges his Maker in words and treats his Maker's creatures carelessly has understood half of the sentence, and the half he kept is the part that costs least.

**WHAT A LIFE OF WORSHIP ACTUALLY ASKS**

The command is broad enough to gather everything a person does under one intention, and that breadth is a mercy rather than a burden. Work done honestly, food earned and given, a family carried, a neighbour's right respected: the Book places all of this inside worship when the intention is right. Nothing in the verse suggests that the act has to be ceremonial to count, and nothing suggests that ceremony can be skipped because the rest of life is good. The two run together, and a reader who divides them will find that both halves become harder within a month. That is not a religious theory; it is the ordinary experience of anyone who has tried to keep one half alone. The verse can be made practical in an afternoon. Take one repeating part of the day, the commute or the first hour after waking, and place one plain act of worship inside it, at a fixed time, for a week. The point is not the size of the act but the fixing of it, since the mindfulness named in the verse grows out of habits that are kept rather than out of feelings that pass. A person who finds himself keeping the appointment without being watched has begun to answer the invitation, and the rest of the sūrah assumes he has.
