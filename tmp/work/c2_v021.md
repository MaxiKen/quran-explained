**AFTER THREE PORTRAITS, THE CALL OPENS TO EVERY PERSON**

The surah has shown the faithful, open rejecters, and hypocrites. It now turns from describing them to addressing all: ***“O humanity! Worship your Lord”***. No group can treat the command as meant only for the others. Believers hear the duty to remain true and deepen obedience. Open rejecters are called away from denial. Hypocrites are offered the direct cure for divided speech: serve one Lord with an undivided heart. Ibn ʿAbbās applied the first hearing to the people of Makkah, and another early application included Jews who heard the call. Yet the form reaches humanity, not one tribe, class, or age. This is why the shift matters. The differences just described are moral responses, not separate kinds of human worth that bar anyone from the command. All stand as servants before the same Lord. Another universal address begins from the same bond: (4:1 — **“O humanity! Be mindful of your Lord Who created you from a single soul, and from it He created its mate,”**). Shared origin supports shared duty and leaves no ground for pride before the Creator.

**THE LORD WHO NURTURES ALONE DESERVES WORSHIP**

The command names Him as their Lord, the One who creates, owns, sustains, and brings His servants through every stage. Worship is therefore not a gift offered to meet a need in God. He needs no food or aid: (51:57 — **“I seek no provision from them, nor do I need them to feed Me”**). He is the Provider, while the servants are the ones upheld. Worship answers the truth of that relation. It declares His oneness, bows to His command, trusts His report, loves what He loves, and refuses rivals in the right that belongs to Him alone. Ibn ʿAbbās places the oneness of God at the heart of the command. Early explanation also gathers worship into obeying commands, avoiding bans, and accepting what God has made known. Prayer, remembrance, and sacrifice are clear acts of worship, but they train a wider life. Honest trade, care for parents, restraint from wrong, and patient service become worship when they follow God’s rule and seek His pleasure. This breadth keeps worship from being reduced to a set of motions cut off from character. It also keeps ordinary good deeds from becoming self-made religion, since the Lord who gives life also teaches how it should be lived. Humanity was not made because God lacked servants; the purpose belongs to human need and divine wisdom: (51:56 — **“I did not create jinn and humans except to worship Me”**).

**CREATION IS THE PROOF PLACED BESIDE THE COMMAND**

The One addressed is He ***“Who created you and those before you”***. The proof is closer than a distant sign. Each hearer exists without having designed, willed, or brought himself from nothing. The same is true of parents, rulers, heroes, and every past people. Going back through the line never reaches a human being who created the whole chain. This is why creation grounds worship. The giver of existence has a claim no creature can share, and the One who began life is able to direct its proper end. Another verse joins the two without a gap: (6:102 — **“˹He is˺ the Creator of all things, so worship Him ˹alone˺. And He is the Maintainer of everything”**). The mention of those before us also cuts two false claims. Tradition cannot make a rival worthy of worship merely because ancestors served it, and ancestry cannot make one group self-sufficient before God. Those before were created servants just as those alive now are. Their lives can teach, but they cannot replace the Creator’s right. At the same time, the line gives comfort. The Lord’s care did not begin with the present crisis or the present generation. He brought countless people through birth, provision, duty, and death before us. Worship places a short human life within that larger truth rather than leaving it to invent its own centre.

**MINDFULNESS IS THE FRUIT TOWARD WHICH WORSHIP TRAINS THE HEART**

The aim is ***“so that you may become mindful ˹of Him˺”***. This wording does not express doubt in God’s knowledge or an anxious hope about what He cannot control. It sets before the hearers the outcome they should seek: worship your Lord so that a guarded, God-aware life may grow. Mindfulness watches the boundary before crossing it. It remembers the One who sees when no person is present, and it keeps fear joined to love and hope. Ibn ʿAbbās connects the result to guarding oneself from divine anger through obedience. The link is not mechanical. An outward act can be emptied by display, as the account of hypocrites has shown. Worship forms mindfulness when the servant knows whom he serves and carries that bond beyond the act. Prayer renews account, fasting trains restraint, charity loosens greed, and remembrance interrupts heedlessness. The command to believers is therefore, (3:102 — **“Be mindful of Allah in the way He deserves, and do not die except in ˹a state of full˺ submission ˹to Him˺”**). Today, this verse asks more than whether worship appears in a schedule. It asks whether worship makes choice, speech, money, desire, and hidden life more aware of God. The Lord created every part of life; true service returns every part to His guidance.
