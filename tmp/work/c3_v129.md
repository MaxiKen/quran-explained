**OWNERSHIP OF HEAVEN AND EARTH**

***“To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and”*** Divine ownership encompasses both heaven and earth. This grounds the hard boundary just drawn in the estate-doctrine. The ownership-clause this argument lays beneath its every severity. And the grounding's logic serves the wounded passage. The amr of hearts and endings belongs to Allah because everything belongs to Allah. The wounders and the wounded, the qunut's named men and the naming tongue, the day's field and its every ending. One estate, one Desk. The Prophet's file-transfer thus no diminishment but plain title. The matter returned to its Owner. And the estate-clause's placement between the boundary (128) and the options' seal (this verse's close) completes the single argument formed by the three verses. The file withdrawn, the ownership stated, the disposition published. (3:128 — **“You ˹O Prophet˺ have no say in the matter. It is up”**)

**WISE JUDGMENT BETWEEN PARDON AND PENALTY**

***“whatever is on the earth. He forgives whoever He wills, and”*** He forgives whoever He wills, and punishes whoever He wills publishes the disposition. The double-will clause of another Qur'anic passage's ending, here deployed over Uhud's live cases. And its jurisprudence holds the two guards this argument always posts on it. Against presumption. The forgiveness by His will. No wounder's repentance-door forced (the Khalids and Ikrimahs entered by grace, not entitlement), no community's imprecation-list binding on the Desk. And against despair. The punishment by His will. No case sealed by human sentencing, the day's bloodiest hands standing, while breath remained, inside the forgivable. The two wills being one Will operating by knowledge and wisdom, the clause thus teaching the community its permanent posture before every open case. Hope extended, verdicts withheld, the Desk trusted with both options. (3:130 — **“And be mindful of Allah, so you may prosper”**) Wise judgment between pardon and penalty remains urgent today because hope and fear remain balanced because judgment belongs to the Owner who knows every circumstance.

**MERCY OPEN TO REPENTANCE**

***“punishes whoever He wills. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful”*** The paired names of complete forgiveness and mercy seal the three-verse instrument (127 to 129's arc. 128's file-transfer at its centre) with the pairing. And the selection's placement is the passage's crowning tenderness. The sequence ran. The Prophet's wound, the imprecations, the boundary's severity (you have no say). And the sequence's last words are Ghafurun Rahim. All-Forgiving, Most Merciful. The estate-clause's Owner self-named, at the file's transfer, by the disposition He loves. The verse's final teaching being the transfer's whole comfort. The file left the wounded Messenger's hands for hands gentler still. The wounders' best hope, and the community's, being precisely that the amr rests where these two names govern it. And history's verification stands filed. The named men forgiven, gathered, and honoured. The seal's names cashed in the qunut-list's own conversions. While the passage now pivots, with the Book's characteristic suddenness, from the battlefield's theology to the market's. O believers. Do not consume interest, multiplying it many times over. The Uhud-curriculum turning to the community's inner economy before resuming the field at the surrounding passage. Imagine an estate in which every room and every account belongs to one perfectly informed owner. Concerning ownership of heaven and earth, Ibn ʿAbbās grounds forgiveness and punishment in Allah’s ownership and connects mercy specifically to repentance maintained until death. Ownership explains why forgiveness and punishment belong to Allah alone; which means the One who possesses the heavens and the earth does not rule as a guest within someone else’s domain. His decision rests on complete right and complete power; therefore yet the order of the two verbs is instructive: forgiveness is mentioned before punishment. Hope therefore stands open without making punishment unreal; in other words the closing statement of power also prevents either outcome from being imagined as difficult for Him. No sin is too great for forgiveness when repentance is accepted, and no protected status can obstruct a just penalty; hence a person today may treat divine mercy as entitlement or divine judgment as impossibly distant. Both errors disappear before this ownership; this is why the servant owns neither the world in which the deed occurs nor the future in which it is judged. What remains is humble repentance, fear without despair, and hope without a claim upon the Owner. Mercy and power are not opposites here. The Owner has full ability to punish, so forgiveness is a chosen grace rather than inability to enforce justice.
