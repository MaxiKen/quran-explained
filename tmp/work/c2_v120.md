**A SATISFACTION THAT WILL NEVER COME**

The verse states a limit that the community had to accept, and it states it in the form of a prediction: ***“Never will the Jews”*** and ***“or Christians be pleased with you”***, followed by ***“until you follow their faith.”***. The two communities are named together again, as they were in the dispute over Paradise, and the sentence explains an experience the believers had been living through. The Book's wording is absolute, and the condition attached to it shows what would have been required: not better conduct, not clearer argument, but the adoption of the others' religion. The sentence is therefore a description of the terms rather than a complaint about the parties, and the terms are of a kind that a believing community cannot meet. What the sentence removes is the expectation that a certain kind of effort will eventually produce approval. Ibn ʿAbbās identified the competing pressures as the religion and prayer-direction of the Jews of Madinah and the Christians of Najran, neither of which could replace revealed guidance.

**THE GUIDANCE NAMED AS ALLAH'S OWN**

The verse supplies the reply the Messenger is to give, and the reply declines the offered terms by naming what he already has: ***“Say, “Allah’s guidance is the only ˹true˺ guidance.”*** and ***“And”***. The sentence does not enter into a comparison between the two faiths; it states that guidance is a gift from God rather than a possession of a community. The same phrase appears elsewhere in the Book where the claim of belonging is being corrected, which shows how fixed the formula was: (3:73 — **“Surely, ˹the only˺ true guidance is Allah’s guidance”**). Placing this statement immediately after the prediction gives the reader the reason the community could not be satisfied by anything short of conversion: what was at stake was not the approval of the neighbours but the source of the guidance itself. A reader who has been asked to soften a position merely to keep the peace will recognise the reply.

**THE DESIRES THAT WOULD LEAD AWAY**

The verse then addresses a possibility that would never have arisen in a straightforward reading, and it warns against it in the strongest form: ***“if you were to follow their desires after ˹all˺ the knowledge”***, ***“that has come to you, there would be none to protect”*** and ***“or help you against Allah”***. The warning is given to the Messenger, which addresses the community that follows him, and the words carry the weight of the whole sūrah's argument about what has been received. Another passage states the same danger in a general form, where obedience to the majority is shown to lead away from the path: (6:116 — **“If you were to obey most of those on earth, they would lead you away from Allah’s Way”**). What the Book says about the pressure to conform does not change here, since the danger it names is the desires of other people rather than their arguments.

**TWO HOUSES AND A FENCE THAT CANNOT BE MOVED**

Think of a household whose neighbours will not be satisfied until it moves in with them, and which is asked to give up its kitchen to keep the street quiet. The verse describes a demand of that shape, and it refuses the concession without pretending that the neighbours will change their minds. The comparison is useful because it separates two things a reader easily confuses: being pleasant to one's neighbours and being absorbed by them. Anyone who has seen a conviction given up in small instalments to keep a relationship calm can see what the warning is aimed at, and the warning is not about hostility but about drift. What the verse leaves with the reader is the knowledge of what the pressure is for.

**STEADINESS WITHOUT UNIVERSAL APPROVAL**

The verse ends with the removal of every other protector, so that the community is left with no fallback if it yields. The sūrah has already spoken of the east and the west belonging to God, and the same ownership is stated here as the reason no help exists outside Him. The sentence therefore closes the discussion of the neighbours' approval by placing the community's security entirely with God, which is the answer to the fear that refusing them would leave the believers alone. A reader who has taken the sentence seriously knows what he is choosing when he keeps a conviction under pressure, and he knows whom he is choosing instead. The next verse describes the community that had received the same scripture and held to it, which supplies the contrast the passage has been building. In the present day, public approval remains an endless target because groups with incompatible demands cannot all be pleased by one surrendered conscience.
