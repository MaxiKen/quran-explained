**THE PLEASING THAT NEVER COMES**

The passage closes the unit with a fact of social physics: ***“Never will the Jews or Christians be pleased with you, until you follow their faith.”***. Ibn ʿAbbās gives the parties their addresses again. The Jews of Medina and the Christians of Najrān stand behind the plural. The same gloss gives the following its concrete content: their religion and their direction of prayer. The pleasing is therefore not asking for a better argument or a kinder sentence. The pleasing asks for the surrender of the religion and the compass both. The received record of the passage reads the refusal beneath the pleasing as pride. Each group believes its own religion is superior, and the belief is not listening for a reply. The pleasing is the door-prize of the conversion, and the conversion is the only ticket the counter sells. The reason the pleasing never comes is that its price is the surrender of the creed, which means the counter is closed before the negotiation begins.

**THE ONLY GUIDANCE**

The reply is the passage's shortest creed: ***“ Say, “Allah’s guidance is the only ˹true˺ guidance.””***. Qatādah's gloss on the line expands the creed into its three dimensions. The guidance is the straight, the perfect, and the comprehensive religion. Straight in its direction, perfect in its measure, comprehensive in its address. The creed is worth holding beside the pleasing of the first clause. The pleasing wants a faith approved by a committee. The guidance is a light approved by its owner. The two approvals cannot share a desk. The companion-clause of the creed has been spoken in the passage of the submitted faces: (2:256 — **“Let there be no compulsion in religion, for the truth stands out clearly from falsehood. So whoever renounces false gods”**), and its completion names the grip that the guided take: (2:256 — **“and believes in Allah has certainly grasped the firmest, unfailing hand-hold. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing”**). The creed is therefore not a slogan of the sect. It is the description of the hand-hold.

**FOLLOWING AFTER KNOWLEDGE**

The verse's closing warning is priced like the trade-verse of the earlier passage: ***“if you were to follow their desires after ˹all˺ the knowledge that has come to you, there would be none to protect or help you against All”***. The transmission from Ibn ʿAbbās names the knowledge in its two forms, the knowledge of the oneness and the knowledge of the proof in the books. The tradition of the glosses notes the oath built into the Arabic of the conditional. The following is hypothetical, and the warning is certain. The knowledge that has arrived is the knowledge the whole passage has been itemizing. The recognition of the named prophet is documented on the shelf of each party: (2:146 — **“Those We have given the Scripture recognize this ˹Prophet˺ as they recognize their own children.”**). The knowledge is therefore not the missing exhibit of this trial. The knowledge is the evidence the pleasing asks the defendant to burn.

**THE COUNTER THAT SELLS ONE TICKET**

The full unit is the passage's closing price-tag on every courtship of approval. The pleasing is offered at one price. The creed is held at another. The warning invoices the difference. The pride beneath the pleasing is pride in a possession that was never owned. The guidance beneath the creed is a light that was always owned and lent. For the reader of any century standing at the counter of the pleasing, the verse is the ledger card. The pleasing of the committee is not receivable at the settlement. The hand-hold is. The knowledge has come. The following is optional. The protection clause is what the following would cancel. The ledger card is worth keeping in the wallet of the passage. The pleasing invoices the creed. The creed invoices nothing. The warning closes the account of the following. Between the three lines any offer of approval can be priced, and the price-tag is older than the merchandise. The merchandise never changed. The counter at the pleasing sells the same ticket it sold at Najrān and Medina. The guidance counter sells the same light it has always sold. Two counters, two tickets, one settlement. Between the ticket of the pleasing and the ticket of the guidance, the passage keeps one counter open, and the keeper of that counter has never once asked a committee for its stamp.
