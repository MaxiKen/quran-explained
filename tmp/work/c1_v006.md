**A REQUEST FROM PEOPLE WHO ARE ALREADY GUIDED**

The petition begins here: ***“Guide us along the Straight Path”***. A reader may wonder why a person who has already been guided asks for guidance again, and the earlier teachers took up that question. The answer lies in what the word carries. Guidance in the Qur'an is not one thing. Some of it is the showing of the road, and that has been given to every human being through the messengers and through the reason God planted in him. Some of it is the enabling that keeps a person walking once he has seen the road, and some of it is the leading that brings him to the destination in safety. A guided person asks for the second and the third, and he asks for them because they are renewed at every step and not handed over once and left. Ibn ʿAbbās read the request as a plea to be kept firm in holding the religion, and another reading of his explains it as a plea for the religion God is pleased with. The Book puts the same request into the mouth of people who had already received guidance: (3:8 — **“Do not let our hearts deviate after you have guided us”**), and the wording shows that the danger the guided person fears is not blindness but slipping. The verse is also placed after the declaration of worship, which teaches that the road is walked by a servant who has already put himself in the right relation to his Lord.

**THE ROAD THAT HAS NO BRANCHES**

The Arabic word for the path in this verse describes a road that is wide and clear, and the scholars of the language agreed that it names a way with no turns and no forks. The term for the straight in it adds the same thing: the road runs to its destination without bending. Ibn ʿAbbās read the phrase as the established religion, and he added that it may mean the Book itself, so that asking for the path is asking to be held to what the Book commands and to what it forbids. Another reading of his says that the request is for firmness in holding fast, which makes the prayer a plea for grip rather than for information. The Book uses the same image in a verse that sets the straight way against the many ways that lead away from it: (6:153 — **“that is My Path—perfectly straight”**), and it gives worship as the road's content in another verse: (36:61 — **“This is the Straight Path”**). The straightness of the path carries a further sense, and it is the one the teachers pressed most often. The path is a middle. The reports describe it as the road that avoids two extremes: the one that falls short of what God asked and the one that goes past it. A person who adds to the religion out of zeal and a person who drops from it out of laziness have both left the same road, one on each side. That is why the request is phrased as a road and not as a rule-book, since a rule-book can be learned without being walked.

**GUIDANCE ASKED FOR AT EVERY STEP**

The request is short, and its shortness is part of its design. It asks for one thing, and the thing it asks for is the whole of a life, so the sūrah puts it in the mouth of the reciter at every standing in prayer. The Book describes the state of those who are guided in a sentence that makes the gift sound like a walk rather than a single event: (10:9 — **“their Lord will guide them ˹to Paradise˺ through their faith”**), where the guidance of the road and the arrival at the end are joined. A person who asks for guidance honestly will find the request changing his conduct before it changes his circumstances. He will start to check the road he is on, and the checking is itself part of the answer. The plural form of the request continues from the previous verse, so the person asking asks for a road for his community and not for himself alone. That choice also protects the request from selfishness, since a person who asks for guidance for a company of people has already admitted that he expects to walk with them. It also makes the request a public one, and a public request is harder to abandon quietly than a private wish.

**A LAMP ON A MOUNTAIN ROAD**

Picture a traveller on a mountain road at night. He carries a lamp that lights only the next stretch of ground, and he walks the whole pass by that small circle of light. He does not see the summit when he sets out, and he does not need to. The request in this verse is the traveller's prayer for the next stretch, and the answer is given in the same measure as the walking. A person who waits until he can see the whole road before he takes a step will stand at the bottom of the mountain for the rest of his life. These days a person is offered endless information about the way to live and very little guidance about which way to take, and the difference between the two is exactly what this verse asks God to supply. The servant says it at every prayer he makes, and by the end of a life it has been said more often than any other sentence he knows. The repetition is the point. A road is walked one stretch at a time, and the prayer is made for the stretch that is next.
