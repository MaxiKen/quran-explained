**FAITH AS THE FOUNDATION OF MARRIAGE**

***“Do not marry polytheistic women until they believe; for a believing slave-woman is better than a free polytheist, even though she may”*** establishes faith as the foundation of marriage. This verse addresses marriage to those who worship other than Allah. It places īmān before attraction, social status, wealth, and lineage. Marriage is not only companionship; it is a covenant that shapes worship, family identity, children, household decisions, and moral priorities. If the spouses disagree about the Lord to whom they owe ultimate obedience, the conflict can reach the foundation of the home. The prohibition concerns polytheists—mushrikūn—and should not be carelessly applied to every non-Muslim category without studying the Qur’an’s distinctions. Sūrah al-Māʾidah gives chaste women within the People of the Book a specific ruling, while this āyah addresses those who associate partners with Allah. Juristic conditions and contemporary circumstances require qualified guidance. The verse says that a believing bondwoman is better than a polytheistic free woman, and a believing bondman better than a polytheist. In the social world in which slavery existed, this was a radical reordering of value. Social freedom, beauty, wealth, and lineage do not make a person spiritually superior to a believer who has been oppressed and possesses none of those advantages. The verse does not praise slavery or encourage Muslims to enslave people. The Qur’an repeatedly opens the path to emancipation, commands freeing slaves as expiation, and praises the freeing of a neck. The comparison exposes the error of judging marriage by social prestige while ignoring faith. A person’s human dignity remains real, and Islam’s ethical trajectory is toward liberation and justice. **DIGNITY IS NOT MEASURED BY STATUS**

***“look pleasant to you. And do not marry your women to polytheistic men until they believe, for a believing slave-man is better”*** deepens dignity is not measured by status. It asks whether attraction will lead a person to compromise the foundation of his faith. A charming partner may be good in worldly manners yet invite the believer toward practices and values that conflict with tawḥīd. Marriage should therefore be evaluated through faith, character, responsibility, safety, and compatibility, not only appearance or social excitement. The Prophet ﷺ advised considering religion and character when choosing a spouse. This does not justify judging people harshly or treating a convert as morally superior in every matter; it identifies the foundation that must guide the covenant. A spouse who worships other than Allah can, without malicious intention, invite the family toward a worldview that leads away from the Hereafter. Children may be confused about worship, festivals, prayer, diet, identity, and moral authority. The phrase is about the direction of a life, not an instruction to insult or dehumanize a person of another faith. The believer continues to treat non-Muslim relatives and neighbours with justice and kindness. The marital prohibition protects the religious covenant; it does not erase the rights of people. Qur’an 60:8 commands kindness and justice toward those who do not fight Muslims because of religion. Allah’s invitation to Paradise and forgiveness is an invitation through revelation, repentance, worship, and righteous action. Marriage should help the spouses answer that invitation, not make obedience increasingly difficult. A believing spouse can remind, support, and comfort, though no spouse controls another’s final faith. The phrase by His permission teaches humility. Guidance is a gift from Allah, not a possession produced by personal superiority. A Muslim asks Allah to guide himself, his spouse, children, and all people, while respecting the limits Allah has set. (90:13 — **“It is to free a slave”**) Since attraction and long-term direction follows faith as the foundation of marriage, dignity is not measured by status links purpose to attraction and long-term direction.

**ATTRACTION AND LONG-TERM DIRECTION**

***“than a free polytheist, even though he may look pleasant to you. They invite ˹you˺ to the Fire while Allah invites”*** clarifies attraction and long-term direction. The verse prohibits the Muslim from entering a particular marriage; it does not license forced marriage. Consent is required, and the Prophet ﷺ rejected marriages imposed upon women. Families may advise, but they may not imprison, threaten, or marry someone without genuine agreement. Faith cannot be produced by coercion, and a marriage entered through coercion is a violation of Islamic ethics. The ruling also does not make the believer a judge of every interfaith friendship. Social kindness, neighbourly cooperation, and respectful conversation are distinct from a marriage covenant that creates intimate and generational obligations. Think of faith as the foundation of marriage through a family that buys a plot because the road to it looks wide and new, and never asks where the road leads when it leaves the town. The house is built, the traffic arrives, and the family spends its evenings listening to the lorries of a route they never chose. The verse gives him a ledger with one line at the top and asks him what the house will be like when the income has changed. Because the invitation of each side is stated plainly, dignity is not measured by status makes faith as the foundation of marriage show that the decision of a marriage is a decision about a road. Because the attraction is named rather than denied, faith as the foundation of marriage shows that the Book speaks to the person who is looking and answers him in his own language. A further passage keeps the promise to the one who keeps the command for the sake of God, and the reader is shown what the restraint of this verse is worth.
