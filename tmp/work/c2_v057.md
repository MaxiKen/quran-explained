**SHADE, FOOD, AND A WARNING ABOUT WHO WAS HARMED**

The favours continue with two provisions and a correction: ***“And ˹remember when˺ We shaded you with clouds”***, ***“and sent down to you manna”***, ***“and quails, ˹saying˺, “Eat from the good things We have provided”***, ***“for you.” The evildoers ˹certainly˺ did not wrong Us”***, ***“but wronged themselves”***. The scene is a wilderness with no shelter and no harvest, and the Book names the two things that were supplied: a moving roof and a daily food supply that arrived without cultivation or trade. The command that comes with the provision is not an obligation but an invitation to eat, which is the shape the sūrah gives to most of its favours. Then the sentence turns and tells the reader who loses when a gift is refused. That final clause is one of the most important statements in the chapter, and it recurs elsewhere whenever the Book discusses disobedience. The Book reports the same principle about the same community in another place when their requests for variety in food are answered with a rebuke, and the wording there returns to the same idea of self-injury. A reader might reasonably ask why the correction is placed immediately after the gift rather than later in the passage. The answer is that the gift and the refusal are the two halves of the same story, and the Book will not describe one without naming the effect of the other. Ibn ʿAbbās placed the cloud, the manna and the quails in the wilderness, and read the food as lawful provision whose misuse injured the recipients rather than the Provider.

**A PROVISION NAMED DOWN TO THE LAST BIRD**

Manna and quails are named rather than summarised because the detail is what makes the passage an argument. Bread that appears without sowing and birds that arrive without hunting are provisions a person cannot attribute to his own skill, and their arrival every morning for years was a standing demonstration of where the supply line came from. The Book is building a case with the history of a people who saw it. The reader is asked to notice that even under those conditions a community can find grounds for complaint, which is a fact about human beings rather than about that generation. The Book reports the same provisions in another account of the same years: (20:81 — **“˹saying,˺ “Eat from the good things We have provided for you, but do not transgress in them,”**). The shade is the same kind of evidence. A cloud that moves with a migrating people and keeps the sun off them is not weather; it is an arrangement, and the Book names it as such. The sūrah is not asking the reader to be impressed by the miraculous. It is asking him to recognise the ordinary version of the same arrangement in his own life, where the shelter over his head and the food on his table also arrive without his having made the rain or the soil. The wilderness story is being told so that the ordinary case can be seen for what it is.

**THE ONLY PERSON A REFUSAL DAMAGES**

The sentence about self-injury is the practical heart of the verse, since God needs nothing from his creatures and their refusal takes nothing from Him. The answer given here is that the harm stays with the one who refuses, which is why the Book can describe a command as being for the benefit of the commanded. The provision was designated for the people, and the warning is that whoever turns away from it is the one who goes hungry. Nothing about that arrangement flatters the creature, and nothing about it makes the Creator smaller. The test for a reader is in how he talks about his obligations. A person who treats them as a bill owed to someone who benefits from the payment will resent them; a person who understands that the payment is for his own advantage will use them. What is wanted is that he notice one thing he has been avoiding as though the avoidance cost God something, and to see that the loss is entirely his own. That recognition is the beginning of the gratitude the previous verse was asking for. In the present day, abundance is still wasted when entitlement makes ordinary provision appear beneath notice.
