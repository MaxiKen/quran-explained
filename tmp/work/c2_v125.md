**THE HOUSE AS CENTRE**

The verse gives the House two jobs in one breath. ***“And ˹remember˺ when We made the Sacred House a centre”*** ***“and a sanctuary for the people ˹saying˺, “˹You may˺ take the standing-place of Abraham”*** ***“as a site of prayer.””*** A centre gathers. A sanctuary protects. The parallel chapter states the same design in its social form (22:26 — **“And ˹remember˺ when We assigned to Abraham the site of the House, ˹saying,˺ “Do not associate anything with Me ˹in worship˺”**). The other passage names the centre's own character (3:96 — **“Surely the first House ˹of worship˺ established for humanity is the one at Bakkah—a blessed sanctuary and a guide for ˹all˺ people”**). Through Ibn Jurayj from ʿAṭāʾ, Ibn ʿAbbās defines the bond God Himself takes with the site: *"Allah's ʿAhd is His command."* The founding was made before the founding party arrived. So the centrality is not a city's achievement. It is a decree the city serves. The reason the sanctuary can sit in the same line is that the decree carries its own protection.

**THE SANCTUARY'S CONTRACT**

The standing-place command arrives as architecture and ethic at once. The verse then converts the site into a contract with a crowd. ***“And We entrusted Abraham”*** ***“and Ishmael to purify My House”*** ***“for those who circle it”*** ***“who meditate in it”*** ***“and who bow and prostrate themselves ˹in prayer˺”***. Mujāhid reads the purifying in its first object: *"Purify it from the idols."* Qatādah reads the meditating as the staying. Al-Rabīʿ b. Anas names the committing at the House and the visiting of it. A House defined by its users' postures is a different thing from a monument. The monument wants the visitor to stand back. The centre wants the visitor to move, stay, and kneel. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk adds the shaving of the head to the picture of those who enter. The contract even reaches the hair of the one who arrives. The sanctuary clause is not merely religious either. The killer of a soul there is warded off from the House. The same peace that holds the birds of the Ḥaram holds the person who walks in with clean hands.

**THE ENTRUSTED PAIR**

The command is addressed to two names at once. The pair is the point. Abraham and Ishmael are entrusted to purify the House for crowds that do not yet exist. The entrustment names the users before the users arrive. Through ʿAṭāʾ the defining of the House and its people stands as God's own ʿAhd. So the pair's work is the working-out of a covenant. It is not the design of a family. What follows is that the standing-place is not a relic to admire. It is a method to take up. The one who takes it as a site of prayer joins the set of postures the House was purified for. The modern reader may travel to the House once or never in a lifetime. The contract still reaches the one who reads it. The purifying entrusted here is the same purifying the opening of this surah asked of the heart. For the people of this age the sanctuary's ethic travels as far as the crowd does. What is sacred in the House is the peace the House exists to keep. The reason Abraham and Ishmael appear together is that the work needed two hands and two biographies. The House is a centre because someone purified it. The reader inherits the purifying as much as the House. The decree arrives before the builders and outlasts them. That is what makes the House a centre rather than a landmark. A landmark is dated. A centre is decreed. The sanctuary's rules follow the decree like a shadow. No blood. No prey. No injustice. The rules are not suggestions pinned to a wall. They are the sanctuary in practice. The purifying commanded to the pair is therefore ongoing work. Every generation of guests arrives with new idols to clear. Some are stone. Some are schedules. The circling, the staying, the bowing and the prostrating are the forms the cleaning takes. The entrustment named two builders because the work was never meant to die with one. The reader who lives far away still receives the contract. Keep the heart's House clean. Walk the ethics of the sanctuary in the household. The peace the House exists to keep is portable. It travels in the behaviour of the ones who read about it.
