**CONCEALMENT FOR WORLDLY GAIN**

Read under concealment for worldly gain, the relevant clauses are ***“Indeed, those who hide Allah’s revelations, trading them”***, ***“for a fleeting gain consume nothing”***. The passage returns to the betrayal of revealed knowledge. These people conceal what Allah sent down and exchange it for a small price. The *price* can be money, office, influence, political protection, social honour, or the preservation of an institution’s reputation. It is called small not because worldly wealth has no value at all, but because no temporary gain can compare with the truth and the eternal consequences of betraying it. The concealment is deliberate. The previous passages have distinguished sincere uncertainty from knowing refusal. Here, people possess revelation and hide it when making it known would cost them something. Religious authority becomes a marketplace, and the scripture is treated as an instrument for securing advantage rather than as a trust that judges the holder. Consume nothing but fire in their bellies is a severe image. The wealth acquired through the concealment may look like food, salary, or comfort in this life, but its unseen reality is fire because it was gained by selling guidance. The image also reverses the purpose of food in the preceding verses. Lawful provision nourishes a servant for worship; unlawful gain acquired through betrayal becomes a source of punishment. This does not mean that every teacher who receives a salary has sold religion. Scholars, educators, translators, and institutions may lawfully receive payment for their time and work. The warning concerns making revelation serve a dishonest bargain or suppressing truth specifically to obtain worldly advantage. The intention, knowledge, and conduct must be examined with justice. (2:159 — **“Those who hide the clear proofs”**) Because concealment for worldly gain governs 2:174, allah will not speak to them arises from concealment for worldly gain. It follows that the responsibility of religious speech completes concealment for worldly gain through allah will not speak to them.

**ALLAH WILL NOT SPEAK TO THEM**

Allah will not speak to them begins from ***“but fire into their bellies. Allah will neither speak to them on the Day of Judgment”***, ***“nor will He purify them.”***. The statement that Allah will not speak to them is a declaration of humiliation and deprivation, not a denial that Allah knows or judges them. On the Day of Resurrection, the honour of divine address and acceptance is withheld from those who treated His words as commodities. The Qur’an elsewhere describes Allah speaking to believers with peace and honour, making the contrast profound. The verse should make people value truthful speech in the present. Those who manipulate scripture may receive applause from audiences, but they will not be able to purchase Allah’s approval. The servant should prefer an honest admission of limited knowledge to a confident answer designed to retain status. Purification—*tazkiyah*—can mean cleansing from sin and granting praise, acceptance, and growth. The concealment of guidance corrupts both the teacher and the community. The person may retain an outward religious identity, but the deliberate betrayal prevents him from receiving the purification that comes through sincere faith and repentance. Yet the next verse in this passage explains that repentance remains possible before death. The warning is not a barrier against return; it is a description of the unrepentant concealer. A person who recognizes his failure should expose the truth, repair the harm, and seek Allah’s forgiveness rather than defending the old falsehood. (3:187 — **“˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when Allah took the covenant of those”**) The reason the responsibility of religious speech follows allah will not speak to them lies in concealment for worldly gain. Therefore, the responsibility of religious speech returns practical force to allah will not speak to them.

**THE RESPONSIBILITY OF RELIGIOUS SPEECH**

***“And they will suffer a painful punishment”*** establish the responsibility of religious speech. This āyah applies to every field in which people claim Allah’s authority. A preacher may conceal a command because it would offend donors. A leader may hide a legal principle because it threatens his political alliance. A community may suppress abuse to protect its image. A writer may quote scripture selectively to sell a message. These are all forms of the danger described, though the precise judgement of each case belongs to Allah. The solution is *trust*: honest transmission, transparent sources, correction of mistakes, and willingness to say what is true even when it reduces worldly advantage. The Qur’an commands people to fulfil trusts and judge with justice. Religious knowledge is among the greatest trusts. The verse should not lead people to distrust every scholar or religious institution. Most knowledge is transmitted through human effort, and trustworthy teachers are necessary. Muslims should neither idolize teachers nor assume corruption without evidence. They evaluate claims, ask for proof, recognize expertise, and preserve good manners. A teacher’s livelihood can be lawful when it supports sincere service. The danger arises when money controls the conclusion or when the teacher knowingly hides a clear truth. The distinction protects both scholarship and the public from cynical suspicion. Concealed revelation is like a lighthouse keeper covering the lamp after receiving payment from ships that prefer darkness. The small wage may be collected on shore, but the keeper has traded away the very duty that gave him a place in the tower. Religious knowledge carries that public trust. Today, money, access, reputation, or institutional pressure can make selective silence appear prudent; the verse exposes the exchange as ruinous because what is consumed is not merely payment but fire joined to a hidden truth. (5:44 — **“Indeed, We revealed the Torah, containing guidance”**) In other words, the responsibility of religious speech answers concealment for worldly gain.
