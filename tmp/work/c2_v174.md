**MERCHANTS OF REVELATION**

***“Indeed, those who hide Allah’s revelations, trading them”*** names a commerce: revelation is treated as stock, and the truth is shorted for a margin. The price is stated with contempt — ***“for a fleeting gain consume nothing”*** — and the payment is then given in full: ***“but fire into their bellies. Allah will neither speak to them on the Day of Judgment”***. The silence of God stands beside the punishment, and the verse adds the removal of cleansing with ***“nor will He purify them.”***, ending in the plain verdict, ***“And they will suffer a painful punishment”***. The accounting is complete: a small gain now, and a fire that becomes the only meal. The verse uses the language of appetite on purpose, because the traders' motive was appetite — the hunger for standing and price — and the sentence mirrors the hunger back at them as a diet. Because the trade was in consumables, the punishment is given as consumption, and the symmetry is the justice of the image. The market that fed them is closed, and the inventory has one remaining lot, weighed in the same currency the trade was counted in.

**WHAT CONCEALMENT COSTS**

The hidden material is the passage in the revealed Book that describes the coming Messenger, withheld by those who feared what its publication would cost them in this world. On this reading the trading is not a difference of interpretation; it is the deliberate suppression of a document the merchants were charged to deliver. Ibn ʿAbbās reports the occasion by name — the men who concealed the description and traits of the Prophet were Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf, Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab, and Judayy b. Akhṭab. The punishment is described as consumption because the gain was consumed too: the verse mirrors the act back as diet. The three silences escalate — no speech, no purification, no end — and each answers a commerce the traders thought they controlled. The market they entered has one settlement date, and the verse publishes it in advance. (3:71 — **“O People of the Book! Why do you mix the truth with falsehood and hide the truth knowingly”**)

**PRUDENCE VERSUS TREACHERY**

A hearer may ask whether keeping knowledge private is not sometimes prudence rather than treachery. Prudence governs the timing of speech; it does not govern the suppression of a duty, and the verse targets the second act. The hidden material was truth the people owed the community, so keeping it for price converts a trust into a bond, and the gain measured against the trust is what the verse calls fleeting. A witness who waits for the court to open is prudent; a witness who sells his testimony to the defendant is the treachery the verse prices. The verse protects both the knowledge and the market: the knowledge must be delivered because it belongs to the guidance of others, and the sale is the specific betrayal named. The silence of God is placed as the climax rather than the fire itself, because the traders' commerce depended on words — on what was said, withheld, and certified.

**THE SETTLEMENT DATE**

The trade of the covenant is priced in (3:77 — **“Indeed, those who trade Allah’s covenant”**), and the two passages together fix the exchange rates. Ibn ʿAbbās reports that nothing enters their bellies except the unlawful, which on the Day becomes fire itself. His account that the hidden material concerned the description of the Prophet shows what the market was really trading: the very proof their community had been waiting for, sold before it could be delivered. Because speech is the first courtesy of the court, its removal is the first punishment — the case is settled without a hearing. The fire that follows is the silence made physical. The application is addressed to everyone who holds truth as inventory: publish what belongs to the people's guidance and refuse the margin that requires its suppression. Knowledge held for sale is the specific temptation of teachers, traders, and every middleman of guidance, and the verse prices the margin in the currency the traders will finally receive. Because the settlement is published in advance, the trade can be refused before it is offered. The gain is small by the verse's own word.
