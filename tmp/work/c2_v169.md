**SATAN’S TWO GREAT INVITATIONS**

The movement toward satan’s two great invitations rests on ***“He only incites you to commit evil”***. This verse explains Satan’s footsteps. He commands evil in general, and *indecency*, obscene or gross immorality. He also commands people to say about Allah what they do not know. The first corrupts conduct; the second corrupts belief and law. Together they destroy both the servant’s relationship with Allah and his relationships with people. Satan’s command is an invitation, not a power that compels. He whispers, beautifies, exaggerates, and presents sin as freedom, but he cannot force the person who seeks refuge in Allah and uses his will responsibly. On the Day of Resurrection, Satan will say that he had no authority except that he called and people responded. *Evil* includes disbelief, injustice, betrayal, cruelty, and every action that Allah dislikes. *Indecency* is more specific to shameless and indecent acts, especially those that violate sexual morality, family dignity, or the natural sense of modesty. The Qur’an does not treat immorality as a private matter without social consequences. It harms individuals, families, and communities, and it normalizes the humiliation or exploitation of the vulnerable. The believer protects himself through modesty, lawful relationships, lowered gaze, truthful companionship, and distance from media or environments that repeatedly make indecency attractive. He also avoids becoming self-righteous. Allah commands opposition to sin with wisdom and justice, not with slander, spying, or cruelty. (2:168 — **“O humanity! Eat from what is lawful”**) Because satan’s two great invitations governs 2:169, speaking about allah without knowledge arises from satan’s two great invitations. It follows that desire disguised as religion completes satan’s two great invitations through speaking about allah without knowledge.

**SPEAKING ABOUT ALLAH WITHOUT KNOWLEDGE**

The sequence reaches speaking about allah without knowledge through ***“and indecency, and to claim against Allah what you do not know”***. Ibn ʿAbbās included every act of disobedience within the evil named here. The second command is especially dangerous: inventing claims about Allah without knowledge. This includes declaring something lawful or unlawful without evidence, attributing a false statement to revelation, explaining the Qur’an from personal whim, denying a divine attribute without understanding, or issuing confident religious rulings beyond one’s competence. The Qur’an places saying about Allah what you do not know among the gravest prohibitions. A person may be physically abstinent while spiritually violating this command through misinformation. False theology can lead people into worship of other than Allah, unjust treatment of others, or despair of divine mercy. The opposite of speaking without knowledge is intellectual humility. The Prophet ﷺ answered questions directly when revelation had clarified them and waited for revelation when it had not. His Companions and the classical scholars frequently said, *Allah knows best,* not as a performance of modesty but as recognition of responsibility. A student should verify a hadith before sharing it, distinguish a scholar’s opinion from an explicit verse, and avoid presenting a disputed matter as unanimous. Social media makes the sin easier because a short statement can reach thousands before it is corrected. The believer slows down when the subject is Allah, His Book, and the rights of people. (7:33 — **“a practice He has never authorized”**) The reason desire disguised as religion follows speaking about allah without knowledge lies in satan’s two great invitations. Therefore, desire disguised as religion returns practical force to speaking about allah without knowledge.

**DESIRE DISGUISED AS RELIGION**

Satan may lead a person first through appetite and then through justification. Someone wants a forbidden profit, a relationship, or a ritual innovation, so he searches for a religious sentence that will approve it. Alternatively, someone dislikes a command and declares it irrational or obsolete without studying its evidence. In both cases, desire has become the hidden author of theology. The cure is to return to the Qur’an, authentic Sunnah, Arabic meanings, the understanding of the Companions, and qualified scholarship. A believer does not treat every inherited custom as revelation or every personal preference as a principle of Islam. Satan’s clear enmity does not mean that a believer is helpless. The Qur’an teaches seeking refuge in Allah, reciting the last two chapters, maintaining remembrance, and filling the heart with beneficial knowledge and good action. When a whisper appears, the servant does not debate it endlessly; he seeks refuge, abandons the path, and turns to a lawful alternative. Repentance also breaks Satan’s strategy. If a person falls, he should not conclude that he is permanently corrupt. Allah loves those who repent and purify themselves. The enemy wants both the sin and the despair that follows it; sincere *tawbah* denies him both victories. False teaching often works like a forged label placed on a harmful medicine. The substance is already dangerous, but the false label adds a second injury by claiming the authority of the healer. Evil and indecency harm conduct; speaking about God without knowledge then recruits religion to protect the harm. In today's rapid streams of clips, posts, and confident answers, the verse requires a pause before forwarding a ruling: certainty of tone is not evidence, and sacred language does not turn invention into truth. (16:116 — **“Do not falsely declare with your tongues, “This is lawful”**) In other words, desire disguised as religion answers satan’s two great invitations.
