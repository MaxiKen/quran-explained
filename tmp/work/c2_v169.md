**THE ENEMY'S CURRICULUM**

***“He only incites you to commit evil”*** names the first item of the enemy's programme in its broadest form, and the second narrows the field: ***“and indecency, and to claim against Allah what you”***, closing with ***“do not know”***. The list is exhaustive in its logic. The first item covers what harms the self, the second covers what harms the community, and the third covers the corruption of the standard by which both are judged. The claim of what is not known turns the whole curriculum into forgery: the speaker cites a source that has not spoken. Ibn ʿAbbās reports the three items in these terms — evil works, foulness, and a lie about Allah of which the claimer knows nothing.

**FORGERY AS THE SEAL**

The first two items are ordinary crimes; the third is treason in the court itself, because it forges the judge's signature. The forbidden and the permitted cannot be corrected until the source of the list is trusted, so the forgery disables the reader's entire moral instrument. I read the word that caps the list as doing this work: the curriculum is bounded, there is nothing else in the enemy's school. The pairing of evil and indecency also covers the private and public faces of harm, so the list leaves no corner of conduct unaddressed before it turns to the forgery that blesses both. The effect of the third item is that the sinner takes comfort in a permission that was never given, which is a heavier ruin than sin committed knowingly. The forgery is worse than the crime because it recruits the standard itself into the defence. A sinner who knows he sins may yet repent; a sinner who has been told God permits his sin has been robbed of the alarm that makes repentance possible. Because the third item corrupts the measuring instrument, the first two items can be repeated without felt cost. This is why the curriculum is named as one programme rather than three temptations: the items support each other, and the third one licenses the other two. (4:119 — **“I will certainly mislead them and delude them with empty hopes. Also, I will order them”**)

**CHECKING THE CLAIM**

A hearer may ask how a claim about God can be checked at all, since nobody hears the divine voice directly. The claim is checked the same way the earlier verses checked their signs — by the received text and the transmitted standard, which is why the passage keeps returning to what God has actually said. The Book defines the permitted and the forbidden in explicit passages, so an added prohibition is not a mystery but a mismatch, and the mismatch is visible to anyone comparing the claim with the source. The test is textual and public: the forgery is caught at the register of revelation, not by private intuition. The unknowing claim is answered by the knowing recitation. Because the forger needs the first two sins to populate the world he invents, the claim about God seals the curriculum and makes it look legitimate.

**THE FORGERY SELF-DOCUMENTED**

The content of the enemy's command is drawn again in (24:21 — **“then ˹let them know that˺ he surely bids ˹all to˺ immorality and wickedness. Had it not been”**). Ibn ʿAbbās reports that what is claimed is a lie of which the claimer knows nothing, and the sequence of the list explains the placement: the claim is the seal that makes the whole programme look legitimate. Because the claim is of what the claimer knows not, it carries its own refutation inside its grammar — the speaker certifies his ignorance in the same sentence in which he speaks for the divine. The lie is therefore self-documenting. The application is a habit of verification: before accepting any prohibition or permission attributed to God, ask for the text that enacts it. The habit is small enough to practise on every claim and sharp enough to end the enemy's third item on contact. Evil, indecency, and forgery form one curriculum, and the entry fee is attention. The exit fee is the habit of verification, which costs the reader a question and saves him a creed, and the fee is paid in full the first time the habit is kept.
