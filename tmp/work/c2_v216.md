**A DUTY YOU DISLIKE**

The ordinance is walked in phrases from the command to the knowledge that governs the taste: ***“Fighting has been made obligatory upon you ˹believers˺”***, ***“though you dislike it. Perhaps you dislike something”***, ***“which is good for you”***, ***“and like something which is bad”***, ***“for you. Allah knows”***, ***“and you do not know”***. and the closing phrase withdraws the question from the taste entirely. The walk separates the duty from the feeling and then prices the feeling: the command stands, the dislike is unreliable, and the knowledge sits on the other side of both. The phrase about the disliked thing is the verse's whole epistemology of desire, and the walk shows it placed between the ordinance and the names of the Knower. Ibn ʿAbbās dates the ordinance to the general call to arms and reads the hated thing as the fighting the call requires.

**HATE IS A BAD GUIDE**

The verse's middle sentence is the chapter's most quoted lesson in epistemology: perhaps you dislike a thing that is good for you and like a thing that is bad. The dislike named here is real and not denied — the ordinance is hateful to human nature because of the hardship inside it — and the reality of the dislike is exactly why the verse distrusts it. Ibn ʿAbbās dates the ordinance to the general call to arms, when the whole community was summoned rather than a volunteer corps. The march commanded in the legislation of the call carries the same refusal of the taste-test: go forth whether the road is easy or hard (9:41 — **“˹O believers!˺ March forth whether it is easy or difficult for you, and strive with your wealth”**). Because the taste is unreliable, the map has to be read instead. The point of the scarcity is legal rather than sentimental: the ration names the duty’s shape for the one who is sent.

**THE GENERAL CALL**

The scope of the ordinance is fixed by the occasion: this is the call to arms that gathers the whole community, and the gathering is what makes the ordinance binding. The verse does not abolish the dislike; it governs the dislike by a knowledge that sits on the other side of it. Because the call is general, the verse trains the community to measure its feelings against the summons rather than the summons against its feelings. The duties that taste bitter deserve the same seriousness this passage demands: asking what the duty is good for before refusing it. The scope is fixed by the occasion rather than by the appetite of the caller, and the fixing is what makes the ordinance durable.

**KNOWLEDGE ON THE OTHER SIDE**

The close of the verse names the reason the taste is overruled: Allah knows and you do not know. The pairing of the two knowledges is the verse's final court of appeal, and the appeal is always available to the person holding the bitter duty. The blessing that turns inside the disliked thing is named in the legislation of the household in the same grammar (4:19 — **“Allah turns into a great blessing”**). Because the knowledge is distributed unevenly, the dislike is a poor survey instrument. The one who obeys the ordinance despite the taste is the one who has read the accounting behind it. The ordinance of the fighting arrived at the hour of the general call, and the general call is what makes the ordinance binding on the whole house rather than on the volunteer corps alone. The reports keep the date of the call and the mood of the street at the hour of the call, and the mood was reluctant. Because the ordinance is dated, the student of the history can read the reluctance as part of the instruction. The interim rulings of the passage are characteristic of the legislation at the frontier: the duty is declared before the desire catches up with it. The one who walks the road of the duty at the hour of the reluctance is the one the passage trains. The discipline of the march is laid out in sister passages with the same etiquette of the road: the ration, the pace, and the guard. Reading the reluctance beside the ordinance is the exercise that turns the complaint into the craft. The duty is not cancelled by the taste, and the taste is not cancelled by the duty. The march at the hour of the reluctance is the march that builds the muscle of the covenant in the legs of the reluctant.
