**THE DAWN DEPARTURE FOR UHUD**

***“˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when you left your home”*** ˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when you left your home in the early morning to position the believers in the battlefield opens the Qur’an’s longest account of a battle. The Uhud passage that will run to the surrounding passage. And its opening frame is the morning of the third year's Shawwal from the sirah. Quraysh had marched three thousand strong to avenge Badr, camping by Mount Uhud north of Madinah. The Prophet had inclined to defend from within the city. Strengthened by a dream he read as loss touching his companions and household. But the eager (many who had missed Badr's honour) pressed for the open field, and he armed. When their eagerness cooled to regret, he answered with the prophethood's own finality. Musnad Ahmad preserves his refusal to remove armour once it had been put on until Allah judged between the two sides. And so the morning this verse frames. The leaving of the household at dawn, the seven hundred marched out, and the verse's named work. Tubawwi'u l-mu'minina maqa'ida lil-qital. Positioning the believers in battle-stations. The Messenger as field-commander. The ranks dressed, the fifty archers set on the hill with the order that would decide the day. Guard our backs. Do not leave your post whether you see us win or see us slain. (3:120 — **“but when you are afflicted with evil, they rejoice. ˹Yet,˺”**)

**POSITIONS ASSIGNED FOR BATTLE**

***“in the early morning to position the believers”*** And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing seals the morning-frame with the final account-pairing. And its selection over the battle's threshold is the passage's issued key. All-Hearing. Of the night's deliberations just concluded. The counsel taken and given, the eager voices, the deserters' murmurs forming. All-Knowing. Of the day about to unfold as no participant knew it. The victory's first hour, the hill's emptying, the reversal, the wound, and the lessons the passage will spend sixty verses extracting. The battle entered, by this seal, under complete audit. Every order heard, every heart known. So the account that follows carries the authority of the only Witness who missed nothing on the mountain. (3:122 — **“So in Allah let the believers put their trust”**) Positions assigned for battle bears on modern life because leadership requires early preparation, clear placement of people, and awareness that every command is heard by Allah.

**THE HEARER AND KNOWER**

***“in the battlefield. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing”*** The opening verse's function, is the passage's method announced. Uhud will be taught not as chronicle but as curriculum. The Book's treatment beginning at the commander's morning (leadership's burdens. The dream, the counsel, the armour's finality) because the day's every lesson hangs from its stations. The positions assigned (this verse) and abandoned, the trust commanded and tested. And the believer reading the passage is enrolled, by its first frame, in its standing course. The community's setbacks examined under All-Hearing, All-Knowing. Causes owned, mercies counted, and the formation re-dressed for the next morning. Imagine a commander walking the field at dawn and placing every unit before the danger appears. In connection with the dawn departure for uhud, Ibn ʿAbbās places the departure at Uhud and connects divine hearing and knowledge to speech, assigned positions, and the later abandonment of those posts. The departure at dawn shows that reliance on Allah does not replace careful arrangement; which means the Prophet placed people where the field required them, which means spiritual trust and practical command belong together. Hearing and knowledge are named because a battle is shaped by more than visible movement; therefore orders are spoken, counsel is offered, fears are concealed, and motives travel beneath the ranks. Allah encompasses all of them; in other words this makes leadership answerable at both levels: positions must be assigned well, and the words and intentions behind those positions remain under divine notice. The same principle reaches beyond a battlefield; hence a household, school, relief team, or public office needs clear roles before pressure arrives. Planning becomes worship when it is honest, competent, and conscious of Allah; this is why it becomes presumption when technique is treated as independent power. Uhud begins with preparation so that the later failure cannot be blamed on an absence of guidance or organisation.
