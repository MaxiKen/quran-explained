**A STAY THAT HAS NO END**

The duration of the state described in the sentence before it is named here: ***“They will be in Hell forever”***. The word used for the dwelling carries the sense of remaining in a place, and it is combined here with the absence of any limit, so the two halves of the phrase close every possibility of a term. The same sentence appears in an earlier sūrah where the account of the disbelievers is carried to its end, which shows the wording to be the Book's own way of stating finality: (3:88 — **“They will be in Hell forever. Their punishment will not be lightened,”**). The declaration is not made about a punishment that has lasted long but about one that will not be shortened, and the difference between those two statements is the whole weight of the verse. The condition shown is one from which nothing is expected to change.

**PUNISHMENT THAT IS NOT LIGHTENED**

The middle clause removes the most natural hope of a person under a sentence: ***“Their punishment will not be lightened,”***. A long punishment can be imagined as becoming bearable with habit, and a heavy one can be imagined as decreasing with time, and the verse closes both doors at once by denying any reduction at all. The Book uses the same clause in its account of the people who persist in disbelief, so the sentence is not an isolated severity but part of a settled description. Nothing in the wording suggests that the punishment is greater than the deed requires; what it states is that the measure will not be relaxed afterwards. The community is being taught to think about consequences while they can still be altered.

**NO DELAY AND NO RESPITE**

The verse closes with the refusal of postponement: ***“nor will they be delayed ˹from it˺”***. A person can accept the reality of a punishment while expecting that it will begin later, and the last clause takes away even that interval, so that nothing remains to be hoped for in the way of a gap. The same language is used elsewhere of a day whose coming cannot be postponed by anyone, which shows how the Book describes the end of the matter in the language of the courtroom. The three clauses of the sentence therefore work together: the stay has no end, the weight has no reduction, and the beginning has no postponement. What the verse leaves the reader is a picture he can carry into this life, where all three can still be changed.

**A JUDGEMENT WITHOUT A LAST PAGE**

Think of a man reading a legal judgement that says the term is unlimited, the penalty is fixed, and the sentence begins at once, and of how the reading removes every plan he had been making for the years to come. The judgement is not a threat while he reads it; it is a description of the life that awaits him, and the only thing that matters is what he does before the reading ends. The verse presents exactly this kind of judgement about a state that will begin after death, and it presents it while the reader is still able to change his position. Anyone who has received news that ended one chapter of his life will recognise the effect the sentence is meant to have. What the verse does not do is describe the punishment with any pleasure, since it has already given the door of return in the verse above.

**THE CLOSE OF THE PASSAGE ON CONCEALMENT**

Read with what precedes it, this sentence marks the end of the passage that began with the charge against those who hide what they know. The accusation was made, the curse was pronounced, the repentance was offered, and the final state of those who refuse is now stated, so that nothing in the matter has been left unspoken. The Book's practice is to end a passage of warning with the end of the road, not to linger over the punishment, and the brevity of the sentence is part of its method. A clear description is left of what the refusal of the truth eventually costs, placed beside an equally clear description of the way back. What the verse provides, then, is the last line of a chapter that the reader has been able to close at each step.
