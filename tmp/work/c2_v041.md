**THE BOOK THAT ARRIVES CONFIRMING AN EARLIER ONE**

The address to the children of Israel continues with a demand and a description of the thing being demanded: ***“Believe in My revelations”***, ***“which confirm your Scriptures. Do not be the first to deny them”***, ***“or trade them for a fleeting gain.”***, and ***“And be mindful of Me”***. The order of the clauses is the argument of the verse. The new revelation is presented as a confirmation of what the audience already had, which means the invitation is not to leave their inheritance but to recognise it. Then comes the warning about being first in denial, which places a particular weight on the people who hold the keys to a tradition. Then the motive is named in four words, and the motive is money.

The Book repeats the confirmation argument in several places, and one statement of it is unusually direct about the recognition involved: (2:146 — **“Those We have given the Scripture recognize this ˹Prophet˺ as they recognize their own children.”**). A person does not mistake his own child. The verse is claiming that the men it addresses are not working from ignorance but from knowledge they have decided not to act on, and it treats the refusal as a decision with a price attached: the fleeting gain of the second clause. The Book elsewhere reports the same community bargaining in the same currency: (2:89 — **“when there came to them a Book from Allah which they recognized, confirming the Scripture they had ˹in their hands˺,”**), with the rejection following in the same sentence.

**THE PRICE PAID IN SMALL COIN**

The description of the price is the part of the verse that travels furthest. What the men being addressed stood to lose was status, an income, and the deference of a community that looked to them for rulings, and it was all temporary in the plainest sense of the word. Every generation has the same exchange on offer, and it rarely presents itself as betrayal. It appears as a career, a reputation, a place at the table, a family arrangement that cannot be disturbed. The Book calls the sum fleeting rather than forbidden, which is a sharper way of putting it, since a person can imagine giving up something large but not something that will vanish anyway. The last clause returns the reader to the single relationship that decides how all of this is weighed: be mindful of Me. The word is the same one that appeared at the beginning of the chapter and again at the start of this address, and its repetition is a clue that the sūrah is circling one theme. The men in view are not being asked to abandon their heritage or their scriptures. They are being asked to stop managing the truth for a price, and the only motive strong enough to compete with the price is the awareness that the transaction is being observed.

**THE MINUTE AFTER RECOGNITION**

The practical form of the verse for any reader is the moment when a person recognises something true and immediately calculates what accepting it would cost. The calculation is almost never conscious; it shows up as a delay, a request for more evidence, or an interest in the character of the person who said it. The Book names that moment here and calls the calculation a trade, and it does so in the middle of a long passage about people who had a genuine inheritance in their hands. The reader is meant to see that possession of the truth is not the test. The test is what he does in the minute after he recognises it. The exercise is to name one thing he has recognised and postponed, and to write down the price he is being offered to keep postponing it. Naming the figure usually removes its charm, because the figure is almost always small: a conversation avoided, an apology delayed, a change of habit put off a year. The verse ends with the two words that make the delay unreasonable, and they are not addressed to a community but to a person, since the command to be mindful of God has never been issued to a crowd without reaching each member of it.
