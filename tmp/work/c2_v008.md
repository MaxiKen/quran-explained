**A NEW GROUP ENTERS THE ACCOUNT**

After sincere believers and open rejecters, the words ***“And there are some”*** introduce a third public condition. These people stand among the community and speak its language, yet their inward allegiance runs elsewhere. Ibn ʿAbbās applied the first setting to hypocrites among the Aws and Khazraj and to others who followed their conduct. This condition took public form in Madinah, where Islam had gained enough strength for false profession to offer safety or influence. Makkah had shown the reverse pressure: some believers were forced to hide faith while enemies held power. The difference is moral and must not be blurred. Concealed faith under force protects truth within; concealed disbelief uses a false claim of faith for gain. The verse therefore does not condemn caution under persecution. It exposes the use of sacred speech as a mask. Later revelation describes people who swear that they belong with the believers although (9:56 — **“they are not. They only say so out of fear”**). Their presence explains why the surah gives more space to hypocrisy than to open disbelief. A known enemy can be faced as an enemy. A false friend can enter trust, shape counsel, spread doubt, and retreat behind words that sound faithful. The reason is that a spoken claim may win access before conduct reveals the hidden aim.

**THE TWO GREAT TRUTHS PLACED ON THEIR TONGUES**

Their claim begins with ***“who say, “We believe in Allah”***. It then reaches the end of human accountability through ***“and the Last Day,””***. These are not small or vague articles. Faith in Allah governs the source, purpose, and worship of life. Faith in the Last Day governs its reckoning and final result. Together they stretch from the unseen Lord to the day when every hidden thing is shown. Yet the verse reports these truths as speech rather than settled conviction. This is why a correct sentence can still be a lie when the speaker uses it to present a heart he does not possess. Another passage records a fuller claim: (63:1 — **“When the hypocrites come to you ˹O Prophet˺, they say, “We bear witness that you are certainly the Messenger of Allah”—and”**), then adds that God bears witness to their lying. The Messenger truly is God’s messenger; the lie lies in their claim to bear sincere witness. In the same way, the two beliefs named here remain wholly true. What fails is the bond between the tongue and the heart. The verse does not lower the worth of confession. It shows why confession has its proper life only when inward assent and outward loyalty meet.

**THE DIVINE VERDICT BEHIND THE PUBLIC CLAIM**

The decisive answer is ***“yet they are not ˹true˺ believers”***. Human observers hear the profession; God knows the hidden denial. The verdict also reveals what the opening reference had gathered: this is a recognisable class, not one unnamed speaker. Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Qatādah, and al-Suddī read the line as a disclosure meant to keep believers from being deceived by appearance. Ibn Jurayj described the hypocrite through broken agreement: deeds oppose publicity, hidden aims oppose speech, and conduct in absence opposes conduct in company. The Qur’an later exposes the same split through action. When such people rise for prayer, (4:142 — **“they do it half-heartedly only to be seen by people—hardly remembering Allah at all”**). Their mouths can say what their hearts deny, as another verse states directly: (3:167 — **“that day—for saying with their mouths what was not in their hearts. Allah is All-Knowing of what they hide”**). The ruling concerns hypocrisy in creed, where an outward claim conceals inward rejection. It must not be carelessly applied to every believer whose actions are weak. Sinful conduct may carry a trait of hypocrisy without proving that faith is absent from the heart. The verse gives a grave category, not a weapon for reckless accusation.

**DISCLOSURE WITHOUT A CULTURE OF SUSPICION**

God’s exposure of the hidden group protects the community, but it does not grant ordinary people access to unseen hearts. Clear lies, betrayal, and patterns of harm can be judged by sound evidence. Private suspicion cannot become proof. This balance follows from the way revelation itself deals with the matter: God discloses what people could not know unaided, while believers remain bound to justice in what they can know. The hypocrites themselves fear a surah that will expose their inward state, (9:64 — **“The hypocrites fear that a sûrah should be revealed about them, exposing what is in their hearts. Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Keep mocking!”**). Their concealment finally fails because the One they name in speech already knows what they hide. It will fail again when they swear before Him as they once swore before people, (58:18 — **“On the Day Allah resurrects them all, they will ˹falsely˺ swear to Him as they swear to you,”**). The verse thus turns inward as well as outward. It asks whether worship, hope, fear, and preparation for return agree with the creed spoken aloud. A sound profession should be guarded, but it should also be made truthful through loyalty and deeds. The cure for hypocrisy is not silence about faith. It is a heart that means what the tongue says and a life that does not secretly work against its own confession.
