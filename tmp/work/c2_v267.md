**GIVE FROM GOOD PROVISION**

***“O believers! Donate from the best of what you have earned and”*** establishes give from good provision. Allah commands believers to spend from the good things they have earned and from the produce He brings forth from the earth. The sources include wages, business profits, crops, livestock, minerals, and all lawful provision. Charity should be drawn from ṭayyib, what is lawful, wholesome, and worthy—not from stolen money or goods that the giver would never accept for himself. The command links human work with divine provision. A person earns through effort, but the ability to work, the land, the market, and the resources all come from Allah. Gratitude directs a portion back toward the needy and the public good. The verse asks the giver to imagine receiving the item he is offering. Would he accept damaged food, worn-out clothing, defective equipment, or money acquired through deception. If he would take it only while overlooking its defects, he should not choose it for charity. The question exposes a double standard in which the donor protects his own comfort while treating the poor as undeserving of quality. This does not prohibit giving used clothing, surplus food, or second-hand goods. Such items can be valuable when clean, safe, useful, and offered with dignity. The prohibition concerns deliberately selecting the inferior because the recipient is poor. The recipient’s poverty does not reduce his worth before Allah. A person may lack money while possessing faith, knowledge, skill, courage, and honour. Giving good food and useful goods acknowledges this dignity. (2:261 — **“The example of those who spend their wealth in the cause of Allah is that of a”**) Ibn ʿAbbās reads the good produce as lawful earnings and crops, while the bad portion is what the giver himself would accept only with reluctance.

**DO NOT GIVE WHAT YOU WOULD REJECT**

***“of what We have produced for you from the earth. Do”*** deepens do not give what you would reject. The donor does not bestow value; he fulfils a responsibility with wealth Allah entrusted to him. Institutions should apply the same principle. Relief distributions should not become a way to dispose of expired products, broken equipment, or goods that would violate safety standards. The poor deserve protection from harm and the right to receive what genuinely benefits them. The phrase what you have earned excludes wealth obtained through theft, fraud, bribery, gambling, or other forbidden means. Unlawful property must be returned to its owner where possible. Donating stolen money does not transform theft into charity. If the owner cannot be found, the person seeks qualified advice about removing the unlawful benefit without claiming spiritual credit. Allah accepts only what is good, the Prophet ﷺ taught in a report collected by Muslim. The hadith links the quality of wealth to the quality of worship. A person should examine contracts, wages, taxes, debts, and business practices, not only the item placed in a donation box. The earth’s produce includes crops and fruits that nourish people. Zakah on agricultural production has its own rulings, while voluntary charity can be given from any harvest. Farmers and businesses should avoid waste, protect soil and water, and pay workers fairly. The verse does not permit exploiting land or labour in the name of charitable output. A person can share from what he grows, cooks, or makes. Goodness is not limited to cash. (6:141 — **“He is the One Who produces gardens—both cultivated and wild—and palm trees, crops of different flavours, olives,”**)

**CHARITY AND HUMAN WORTH**

***“not pick out worthless things for donation, which you yourselves would”*** clarifies charity and human worth. A nutritious meal, a repaired appliance, a textbook, or professional service can be a valuable gift. Allah is Ghaniyy, Self-Sufficient. He does not need inferior goods or the donor’s wealth. The command is for the giver’s purification and the recipient’s welfare. A person should not imagine that Allah is enriched by his donation; rather, Allah enriches the servant by allowing him to participate in mercy. Because Allah is self-sufficient, He can accept a small sincere gift and multiply it. The giver must not use charity to claim superiority over Allah’s servants. The poor person is not indebted to the donor in a way that permits control. Allah is Ḥamīd, Praiseworthy in His names, actions, and judgement. He deserves praise whether people give or withhold. The verse’s ending shifts the heart from the recipient’s reaction to Allah’s approval. The donor gives for Allah and trusts Him with the reward. Praising Allah also means praising Him through the use of blessings. Wealth becomes an act of ḥamd when it feeds people, protects children, supports knowledge, and removes harm. Think of give from good provision through a man who is invited to a wedding and takes the fruit from the bottom of the basket at his door, where the flies have been. The hosts put the dish out with the others, and the guests pass it on to the next table without lifting the cloth. Give from good provision confronts do not give what you would reject in a reader who clears a shelf of things his own family will not use and calls the pile a donation. (7:31 — **“O Children of Adam! Dress properly whenever you are at worship. Eat and drink,”**)

**LAWFUL WEALTH**

***“only accept with closed eyes. And know that Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy”*** closes with lawful wealth. The verse gives him the earning of his hands, the produce of the earth and a closed eye in front of his own gift. And it asks him what the receiver would say if he could see the house the sack came from. Because the close names the Self-Sufficient and the Praiseworthy, showing through give from good provision that the Lord of the worlds accepts a gift for its worth and not for its weight. Subsequent āyāt join excellent giving to promised reward, while the preceding garden parable depicts good ground. Because give from good provision opens into do not give what you would reject, charity and human worth joins give from good provision to do not give what you would reject. Since charity and human worth develops give from good provision, do not give what you would reject carries the transition. Lawful wealth matters, which means charity and human worth answers do not give what you would reject. Do not give what you would reject gives practical form to give from good provision; lawful wealth sets its horizon. Today, give from good provision shapes do not give what you would reject in present decisions. (4:29 — **“O believers! Do not devour one another’s wealth illegally, but rather trade by mutual consent.”**)
