**THE FOLLOWING THAT COUNTS**

The verse opens on a class of readers. It grades their reading. ***“Those We have given the Book follow it”*** ***“as it should be followed. It is they who ˹truly˺ believe in it.”*** The gift is not in dispute. These people already hold the Book. The whole weight of the sentence therefore falls on the manner of the following. A reading can be syllables moving over a tongue. It can still fail to be the real thing. Which means a walking and a holding fall apart from each other here. In Ibn ʿAbbās's own gloss the proper following is *"the Book, and acting upon it and following it"*, then *"belief in me and in my mission."* The reading has a destination. It was always walking toward a man. The parallel verse names the failure that makes this rare (62:5 — **“The example of those who were entrusted with ˹observing˺ the Torah but failed to do so,”**). The entrustment came. The following did not.

**THE WITNESSES OF THE FAVOUR**

The tradition about who the verse means runs on names. The names are worth the space. In the transmission of Ibn ʿAbbās: *"Those who were given the Book and follow it as it should be followed — they are the believers from among the People of the Book: ʿAbdullāh b. Salām, Asad b. Zurārah, Thaʿlabah b. Sāʿid, Sallām b. Mishkam and their companions; and the ones who believed with them from among the People of the Book, and every believer in Muhammad ﷺ from his Companions and his community, up to the Day of Resurrection."* The list is not a library roll. It is a cohort of witnesses. They read and then signed the reading with their lives. The reason the same gloss ends on the mission is plain. The following announces a face. An earlier generation is named at the head of the group: ʿAbdullāh b. Salām and Asad b. Zurārah walked first. So the chain runs through the school of revelation itself. The Book. The following. The face the following was addressed to. The difference between owning a text and walking its way is exactly what this verse grades.

**THE LOSERS' SIDE OF THE LEDGER**

The verse closes both cases in one line. ***“As for those who reject it, it is they who are the losers”***. Qatādah fixes the losing in its precise form: *"the losers are the losers against themselves."* The parallel closes on the same side of the ledger (62:5 — **“For Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people”**). The losing is not an accident that befalls a reader. It is a verdict that follows a refusal. Notice what the verse does not say. The rejecters do not lose their books. They lose themselves. The books stay in their hands as the evidence that was once on the table. The report of Ibn ʿAbbās places the horizon of the sentence at the Day of Resurrection. The ledger does not close inside the lifetime of the claim. The reading a person lives remains attached to that person. Everything else gets redistributed. For a reader today the same grading runs over any inherited scripture. The test of a reading is not its tone of reverence. It is the belief and the follow-through. The loser here is not the person who reads little. It is the person whose reading never becomes a walked way. The two outcomes sit in one class of people. The difference between them is made entirely by what the reading does when it lands. The grading is public and it is personal at once. A reader can carry the Book for a lifetime. The carrying is not the following. The following walks where the Book walks. It believes where the Book points. It signs the reading with the life. The witnesses of the favour did exactly this. Their names stay in the verse's shadow. Their deeds bring them into the light. The difference is small on paper. It is total on the Day. The point of the whole passage is therefore practical. Read to walk. Read to believe. Read to sign. The losers lose themselves because the reading never left the shelf. The winners are the ones whose reading grew legs. That is the whole ledger of the verse.
