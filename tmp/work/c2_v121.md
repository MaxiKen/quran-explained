**THE PEOPLE WHO FOLLOW THE BOOK AS IT SHOULD BE FOLLOWED**

The verse describes a group inside the communities that possessed scriptures, and it does so by what they do rather than by what they are called: ***“Those We have given the Book follow it”*** and ***“as it should be followed. It is they”*** who ***“who ˹truly˺ believe in it.”***. The phrase about following as it should be followed describes a manner of reading rather than a quantity of reading, and the Book's wording makes the manner the standard. The verses just before this one described communities that would not be satisfied until the believers joined them, and this sentence points to the people within those same communities who had done the opposite. The verse therefore prevents the reader from turning the previous sentence into a verdict on everyone who inherited a scripture. What the reader is given is a description that any holder of a book can test himself against.

**FOLLOWING A BOOK PROPERLY**

The Book's understanding of following is practical rather than ceremonial, and the verse's two-word standard leaves the reader to work out what it involves. A book is followed properly when its commands are carried out, its warnings are taken seriously, and its claims are not reshaped to fit the reader's preferences. Earlier verses in this passage described communities that recited their scriptures while hiding what they contained, and the contrast here is with people who read and comply. Another passage describes the same group in terms of their conduct at night, which gives the reader a picture of what the phrase looks like in a life: (3:113 — **“Yet they are not all alike: there are some among the People of the Book who are upright”**). The point is that the failure described earlier in the sūrah was never a failure of access to the text.

**BELIEF THAT FOLLOWS FROM READING**

The sentence then attaches belief to the practice, and the attachment runs in the order of the clause: those who follow the book as it should be followed are the ones who believe in it. The Book's treatment of belief as the fruit of obedience is worth noticing, since it puts the two in a sequence rather than in a competition. The same link between studying a scripture and accepting it appears in the description of the scholars who judged by what they had been given: (5:44 — **“So too did the rabbis and scholars judge according to Allah’s Book”**). A reader who has met the idea that belief is a feeling that precedes any practice will find the verse's order instructive, since the Book makes the practice part of the belief rather than a result of it.

**A MUSICIAN WHO PLAYS WHAT IS WRITTEN**

Think of a musician who plays the score in front of him instead of the tune he would have preferred, and who is therefore trusted with the whole repertoire. The verse's phrase about following a book as it should be followed works at that level of fidelity, and the Book treats the difference between the two kinds of reader as decisive. The comparison also explains why the verse can speak of belief in the same breath, since a person who follows what he has been given has already accepted its authority. Anyone who has watched a text being quietly adjusted to suit its reader can see what the phrase is protecting, and the protection is the text itself. The plain question left with him is whether the book he holds is being obeyed or managed.


**REJECTION AND WHAT IT COSTS**

The verse closes with the other group stated in the same terms and with the opposite outcome: ***“As for those who reject it, it is they”*** who ***“who are the losers”***. The symmetry is exact, since the two halves of the sentence use the same construction and place the difference in what the people do with the book. The word for the losers describes a loss measured against what was available rather than against what was expected, the Book's usual description of those who had a scripture in their hands. The sentence therefore ends the passage about the People of the Book with a division that runs through their own ranks rather than between them and everyone else. The reader is left with the fact that possession of a text has never been the same thing as being governed by it.
