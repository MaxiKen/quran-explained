**THE SLIP AND THE SENTENCE THAT FOLLOWED IT**

The tree was approached and the household fell: ***“But Satan deceived them”***, ***“leading to their fall from the ˹blissful˺ state they were in”***, ***“and We said, “Descend from the heavens ˹to the earth˺”***, ***“as enemies to each other. You will find in the earth a residence”***, ***“and provision for your appointed stay.””***. The Book does not describe a long campaign or a gradual seduction; it says the deception worked and the two were out. Then comes the sentence of removal, and inside it the reader finds something he did not expect, which is provision. The same order that expels them names the earth as a place to live, food to eat, and a term to serve, and all three are given by the one who is sending them down.

The details of the temptation are reported in full elsewhere, and they show why the slip was so easy to arrange. In one account the tempter's approach is described as a promise of a different kind of life: (7:20 — **“Then Satan tempted them in order to expose what was hidden of their nakedness. He said,”**), and the speech that follows offers no argument but a comparison. The Book reports the same technique in the sūrah of Ṭā-Hā with a whisper about immortality and a kingdom that never ends: (20:120 — **“But Satan whispered to him, saying, “O Adam! Shall I show you the Tree of Immortality”**). Neither account shows a reasoned case against the command. Both show an appetite being widened, which is how the thing still works.

**ENMITY STATED IN ADVANCE**

The sentence of descent includes a fact that shapes everything after it: the two parties would be enemies to each other, and the earth would be the ground on which that enmity plays out. The reader is being told, at the beginning of the human story, that he has an opponent and that the opponent is not going to change his method. What makes the passage steadying rather than frightening is the rest of the same sentence. The opponent was granted no control over the residence or the provision; those come from someone else. The Book repeats the scene in another sūrah where the same promise of guidance follows the descent immediately: (20:123 — **“Allah said, “Descend, both of you, from here together ˹with Satan˺ as enemies to each other.”**), and the next words there describe guidance arriving for whoever follows it. The word for the stay is the word for a fixed term, which is the one detail a person is most tempted to forget. A residence with a term is not a homeland, and provision for a term is not an inheritance. The Book elsewhere states the whole arrangement in three clauses and leaves nothing vague: (7:25 — **“He added, “There you will live, there you will die, and from there you will be resurrected.””**). The reader who holds those clauses together has the shape of his own life in his hands, and the shape changes the way he treats the ground under him, since a term makes a person careful about what he builds.

**THE MECHANICS OF LOSING GROUND**

The practical weight of the verse is that the fall did not come from a lack of information. Adam and his wife knew the command, knew the limit, and knew the warning that came with it. What they lacked was the willingness to keep a boundary while a desire was being widened on the other side of it. That is a description the reader can test in his own case without difficulty, because he also keeps a short list of limits that he knows and does not always keep. The story is not told to make a person despair of himself; it is told so that he recognises the mechanics by which he loses ground, and recognises them before the loss. Today the exercise is to name the one place where the pattern operates and to put something in the way of it, since the verse shows that a limit without a guard usually does not hold. The Book does not pretend that a person stops slipping after he has been warned once. It shows him the slip that cost the first household everything, tells him the enemy is still at work, and then promises guidance to whoever follows it, which means the story of this verse is the beginning of a road rather than the end of one.
