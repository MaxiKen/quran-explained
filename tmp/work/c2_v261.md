**A SEED THAT MULTIPLIES**

***“The example of those who spend their wealth in the cause”*** establishes a seed that multiplies. The Qur’an compares spending in Allah’s cause to a single grain that produces seven ears, with a hundred grains in each ear. The image begins with something small and hidden, then shows growth far beyond the original. Charity may seem to reduce a person’s immediate wealth, but Allah can place blessing in what remains and multiply the reward in ways no human calculation can contain. The metaphor does not promise that every donation produces visible financial profit. It promises divine multiplication of reward, and that reward may include forgiveness, contentment, protection, benefit to descendants, social repair, and everlasting recompense. Allah’s accounting is wider than the balance sheet. Allah’s cause—sabīl Allāh—includes every lawful effort that serves worship, justice, mercy, and human welfare. It includes zakāh, support for relatives and orphans, feeding the hungry, education, healthcare, refuge, debt relief, and legitimate defence of the oppressed. The cause must itself be lawful; giving money to aggression, terrorism, fraud, or corruption is not a righteous expenditure. The giver should verify the organization, the purpose, and the likely benefit. Sincerity does not excuse negligence. A charity with poor records should not be trusted merely for pious branding. The closing words—yuḍāʿifu liman yashāʾ—show that multiplication is according to Allah’s wisdom. The amount is not the only measure. A small gift from a poor person may be greater than a large gift from wealth, especially when the sacrifice and sincerity are greater. (2:245 — **“Who will lend to Allah a good loan which Allah will multiply many times over?”**) Ibn ʿAbbās reads the seven ears and hundred grains as multiplication from one gift to seven hundred, with further increase belonging to Allah’s wisdom.

**SPENDING FOR ALLAH’S CAUSE**

***“of Allah is that of a grain that sprouts into seven”*** deepens spending for allah’s cause. A private donation may outweigh a public one made to gain reputation. The Qur’an’s parable also protects the giver from pride. The seed grows by Allah’s permission. The servant plants and waters, but Allah creates the increase. He thanks Allah for enabling the charity and asks Him to accept it. The reward is connected to the ethics of giving. Wealth should be lawful, the intention directed to Allah, and the recipient treated with dignity. Reminding people of a gift, insulting them, seeking control, or giving contaminated or worthless property can destroy the spiritual benefit, as the following verses explain. A gift should meet a real need. Food should be edible, clothing suitable, and financial support delivered in a way that preserves privacy. A person does not give away what his dependants urgently require and then call the resulting neglect charity. A single act of giving can produce many effects. Food restores strength, education opens opportunity, medical care preserves life, and debt relief frees a family from fear. Those helped may then help others. Like a seed, charity can grow through relationships and institutions beyond what the original giver sees. This is why Islam joins personal generosity to public systems such as zakāh. Individual gifts express mercy, while organized distribution can address structural need. Both should be transparent and accountable. Allah is Wāsiʿ, All-Encompassing in generosity, knowledge, and ability. (2:215 — **“They ask you ˹O Prophet in˺ what ˹way˺ they should donate. Say,”**)

**ALLAH MULTIPLIES AS HE WILLS**

***“ears, each bearing one hundred grains. And Allah multiplies ˹the reward”*** clarifies allah multiplies as he wills. His resources do not run out when servants give. He is ʿAlīm, knowing the giver’s intention, the recipient’s need, and every consequence of the gift. People may overlook quiet charity, but Allah does not. The two names also teach balance. The giver does not demand that Allah multiply according to his schedule, and he does not despair when the worldly result is invisible. The seed may be growing in a realm he cannot yet see. Think of a seed that multiplies through a man who plants a single row of maize at the edge of his plot because the rest of the field is rented to another. The row becomes a hedge, the hedge feeds his family for a month, and the neighbour who rented the field asks him for seed the following year. A seed that multiplies confronts spending for allah’s cause in a reader who counts what he puts into a cause as money that has left his hand for good. The verse gives him a seed, seven ears and a hundred grains in each, and it asks him whether he has ever seen a farmer refuse to sow because the ground would swallow the seed. Because the close names the Bountiful and the Knowing, the reader is taught that no gift is lost and no intention is missed. The same picture of the seed that becomes many is used in Sūrah al-Anʿām. **THE CONDITIONS OF A GOOD GIFT**

***“even more˺ to whoever He wills. For Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing”*** closes with the conditions of a good gift. And the giving that follows this verse is described with its wrong way in the next one. Because a seed that multiplies opens into spending for allah’s cause, allah multiplies as he wills joins a seed that multiplies to spending for allah’s cause. Since allah multiplies as he wills develops a seed that multiplies, spending for allah’s cause carries the transition. The conditions of a good gift matters, which means allah multiplies as he wills answers spending for allah’s cause. Spending for allah’s cause gives practical form to a seed that multiplies; the conditions of a good gift sets its horizon. Today, a seed that multiplies shapes spending for allah’s cause in present decisions. A seed that multiplies stays bound to allah multiplies as he wills because spending for allah’s cause supplies the human setting of a seed that multiplies. The conditions of a good gift carries a seed that multiplies into conduct shaped by allah multiplies as he wills. (9:103 — **“Take from their wealth ˹O Prophet˺ charity to purify and bless them,”**)
