**A WORD WITH TWO FACES**

The passage now polices the smallest unit of the encounter — the word used to address the messenger: ***“O believers! Do not say, “Râ’ina.” [Herd us!]”***. The received record of the mischief is precise. In Arabic the word carries the sense 'be mindful of us', which is why some believers reached for it. In Hebrew the same sound carries an insult. The received gloss traces it to the noun of thoughtlessness and records the amusement the party took when the word landed. The mischief had a design. The trick was to make the cursing public and to make the believers say it themselves, so that the insult would wear a Muslim face. The story is small. The engineering is not. It is a poison pill pressed into a greeting.

**THE REPLACEMENT AS AN ORDER**

The cure is immediate and positive: ***“But say, “Unẓurna,” [Tend to us!]”***, and the posture is commanded with the vocabulary in the next breath of the verse. The gloss from Ibn ʿAbbās gives the replacement in plain speech: say 'look upon us', and hear what you are commanded and obey it. The instruction is worth noticing in its two halves. The vocabulary is corrected, and the posture is corrected with it. Attention is commanded alongside the word, because the word that means attention is useless without the thing itself. The reasoning the passage relies on is simple and worth spelling out. What follows from a language with two faces is that the speaker is responsible for the face the hearer receives. The believers were therefore given a clean word with one face, and the clean word removed the trap without amplifying it.

**THE DISCIPLINE BEHIND THE VOCABULARY**

The verse's warning is then attached to the discipline it belongs to: ***“and listen ˹attentively˺. And the disbelievers will suffer a painful punishment”***. The received tradition in this passage files the whole scene under manners in speech and observes that devious words hiding their real meaning were the specialty being answered. The reports that gloss this discipline are of a piece with the verse. Among them is the one Aḥmad hands down from Ibn ʿUmar in connection with the greetings of that period, where the same anatomy of hidden insult in plain sound was being managed by command. The pattern of the passage is therefore continuous with the covenant lessons before it. Words at a counter can be weights and measures, and the weights get checked. The twin command against the word with two faces is recorded in the book's own amendment of the etiquette: (4:46 — **“and “Râ’ina!” [Herd us!]—playing with words and discrediting the faith. Had they said ˹courteously˺, “We hear and obey,” “Listen to us,” and”**). The replacement is named in the very next clause: (4:46 — **““Unẓurna,” [Tend to us!] it would have been better for them and more proper. Allah has condemned them for their disbelief,”**). The general disease behind the trick is diagnosed where the covenant-breakers are sentenced for it: (5:13 — **“But for breaking their covenant We condemned them and hardened their hearts. They distorted the words of the Scripture”**). What the verse installs is the examination of sound as well as sense. The effect is larger than courtesy. A community that watches its vocabulary at the door keeps the poison out of the room.

**THE TOLL AND THE TOOL**

The section's hold on language is practical rather than sentimental. The word that wounds is a tool. The word that heals is the same tool with a different hand on it. The prohibition does not silence speech. It orders speech that can be audited, and it removes the syllable that had been turned into a blade. For the modern reader, the case reads like every community's comment thread where a borrowed phrase cuts on one side and reads as warmth on the other. The verse does not ask the reader to grow suspicious of every greeting. It asks the reader to speak so that the hearer receives the face the speaker owns. That is why the discipline belongs in a passage about scripture sold and words twisted. The same hands that cast the book behind the back also poisoned the greeting. The answer to both is the same habit. Look. Listen. Say the clean thing.
