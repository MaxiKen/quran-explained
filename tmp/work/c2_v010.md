**THE HEART CAN FALL ILL WHILE THE BODY IS SOUND**

The fraud of the last verse has an inward cause: ***“There is sickness in their hearts”***. The heart here is the seat of trust, will, and moral sight. Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah, and Qatādah all linked this sickness to doubt and hypocrisy. Ibn Zayd was plain that this is an illness in faith, not a bodily disease. That guard is vital. The verse does not shame a sick body, nor does it let people brand those who face mental distress as hypocrites. A person may bear great pain and still have a heart full of faith. The sickness named here is chosen falsehood that has begun to spoil how truth is received. Doubt by itself is not always blameworthy; an honest doubt can seek light and yield when proof comes. This doubt hides behind a claim of faith, feeds on envy, and refuses the truth it fears will cost too much. The Qur’an links the same inward flaw to a fear that God and His Messenger might be unjust: (24:50 — **“Is there a sickness in their hearts? Or are they in doubt? Or do they fear”**). Such fear shows that the heart no longer sees its Lord or His rule as it should. The image of illness fits because sin has harmed a power meant for sound use. The heart still acts, but it no longer judges in a sound way.

**HOW A CHOSEN ILLNESS IS MADE TO GROW**

The next words join judgment to what they brought first: ***“and Allah ˹only˺ lets their sickness increase. They will suffer a painful punishment”***. The order bars the thought that God first forced a sound heart into hypocrisy and then blamed it. Sickness is already there. They feed it through lies, and God lets its reach grow as a just result. The same order is made clear when a people first turn away and then meet a matching judgment: (61:5 — **“So when they ˹persistently˺ deviated, Allah caused their hearts to deviate. For Allah does not guide the rebellious people”**). Their first turn is real, and God’s rule over what follows is also real. This is why each fresh sign can lead two hearts in two ways. Those who welcome the truth gain faith: (9:124 — **“it has increased them in faith and they rejoice”**). Those who mock it gain more of the filth they chose: (9:125 — **“But as for those with sickness in their hearts, it has increased them only in wickedness upon their wickedness,”**). The light did not change; the response did. A cure can save one patient who takes it, while its refusal leaves another illness room to spread. Yet this verse goes beyond a mere natural result. God rules that result, withholds aid from proud refusal, and makes the new state fit the old choice. By contrast, He gives more help to those who take the first steps of trust: (47:17 — **“As for those who are ˹rightly˺ guided, He increases them in guidance and blesses them with righteousness”**).

**WHY THE LIE STANDS BESIDE THE PAIN**

The verse closes by naming the cause: ***“for their lies”***. The nearest lie is their claim in verse 8 that they believed in God and the Last Day. Those truths were sound, but they used them to paint a false picture of their hearts. This makes lying more than one fault in a long list. It is the tool by which their inward breach enters public life. One accepted recitation points to their habit of telling lies, while another points to their denial and their charge that God’s message was false. The two senses meet in their case. They deny truth within and speak a false claim without. A later surah sets the same pattern before the reader. The hypocrites give true words about the Messenger, yet God states that their claimed witness is a lie because the words do not report what they hold within: (63:1 — **“surely Allah knows that you are His Messenger—but Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are truly liars”**). The painful end therefore suits the deed. Their tongues were used to dull warning, gain safety, and cause pain through fraud. They would not let the truth of the Last Day rule life, though they spoke its name. Now the Last Day gives the lie its due. This does not make every lie equal to hidden disbelief, but it shows why lying is not small. Each lie trains the heart to split speech from fact. When that split is nursed in faith itself, it becomes the sickness this verse lays bare.

**THE IMAGE OF SICKNESS ALSO POINTS TOWARD A CURE**

A grave diagnosis need not make a living reader lose hope. The same Qur’an that shows sick hearts also comes as their cure. Its call is addressed to all people as (10:57 — **“a warning from your Lord, a cure for what is in the hearts,”**). Cure begins when the lie stops. A person admits the hidden fault to God, asks for pardon, restores what the lie harmed, and lets true words lead to true acts. He also keeps watch over early signs: joy when truth is harmed, anger when it corrects him, the wish to look good rather than become good, and ease in saying what his heart does not own. These signs are not grounds to despair or to pass a final verdict on oneself. They are grounds to seek help before a habit grows. Today, the warning reaches public faith, private prayer, work, family, and every place where a trusted word can be used as a mask.

The contrast in this verse also teaches how to approach revelation. The Qur’an is not bare data placed before a cold mind. It tests love, pride, fear, and will. It is (17:82 — **“a healing and mercy for the believers, but it only increases the wrongdoers in loss”**). Healing comes as the reader yields to truth and acts on it; loss grows as the reader uses each sign as fresh ground for scorn. Thus the heart is not healed by learning how to speak about faith while guarding its old disease. It is healed by letting God’s word expose, cleanse, and guide. The warning remains severe because untreated hypocrisy grows. The hope remains real because the door to honest return is open while life remains.
