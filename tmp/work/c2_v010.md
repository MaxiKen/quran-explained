**A DISEASE IN THE HEART, AND WHO LETS IT GROW**

The sūrah now says why the double game was possible: ***“There is sickness in their hearts”***. The disease in view is not a bodily ailment. Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd both described it as doubt, and the early reports add hypocrisy and a darkness that settles on the place where a person decides. The heart keeps working, the tongue keeps speaking, and the outside looks untouched, which is exactly why the condition is dangerous. A broken leg announces itself; a sick heart does not. The verse names it plainly so that the reader can stop treating it as a mood that will pass on its own, or as a private weakness that only God needs to know about. What follows is the part that deserves a slow reading: ***“and Allah ˹only˺ lets their sickness increase. They will suffer a painful punishment”***, ***“for their lies”***. The increase is not a new sickness added from outside; it is the condition allowed to run its natural course. A person who keeps lying about his own faith has to keep track of the story he told, and the tracking itself becomes a habit of mind, and the habit hardens. That is why the Book reports the growth without apology. The choices a person repeats are the ones that shape what he becomes, and the sentence simply states the law by which that happens.

**THE INCREASE THAT IS ITSELF THE PUNISHMENT**

A reader may find the increase harder to accept than the punishment, and the difficulty is worth sitting with. The Book is describing a world in which nothing is neutral. A man who lives inside a lie is not left where he was; he is given more of the thing he chose, until it governs what he can see. The same law is reported elsewhere about those who will not hear: (74:31 — **“We have appointed only ˹stern˺ angels as wardens of the Fire.”**). The verse is a report about the machinery of a moral world, not a mood of anger. The last phrase, ***“for their lies”***, removes the excuse that the punishment came from nowhere. The lies are of two kinds and the verse covers both. They lie to the believers about their belief, and they lie to themselves about the harmlessness of what they are doing. A reader who has ever kept a small dishonesty alive across several years knows how the second lie works. Nobody has to teach a person to believe his own cover story; the mind does it by itself. The verse names the mechanism, and the naming is itself a mercy, since a mechanism that is understood can be interrupted.

**WHAT SUCH A DISEASE LOOKS LIKE FROM OUTSIDE**

Nothing in the description is unusual enough to attract attention in a busy community. The man with sickness in his heart prays on time when he is watched, gives when a gift is noticed, and disappears when a hard duty appears. He is pleasant, informed, and useful in small matters. This is why the sūrah has to explain him rather than merely name him, and why it brings the reader close to him without letting the reader become comfortable with him. The disease is contagious in a quiet way, since a man who watches a hypocrite for years without noticing starts to assume that the standard is lower than he thought. The reader's own protection lies in the tests the sūrah has already given in the earlier verses. Certainty about the Hereafter, prayer kept when nobody asks, and giving with a number attached are the three things this disease cannot imitate for long. That is not a theory; it is the reason the description of the believers was placed before the description of the hypocrites. A person who measures his week on those three points will find the illness early, while it is still a doubt rather than a settled shape.
