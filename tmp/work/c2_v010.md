**THE ILLNESS THAT WAS ALREADY IN THE HEART**

The sūrah now names the cause of the behaviour it has described: ***“There is sickness in their hearts”***. The word describes a disorder that weakens a living thing from inside, and the transmitted reading identifies doubt settled where certainty should be. Ibn ʿAbbās explained the sickness as doubt, hypocrisy, disagreement with the truth and darkness, and al-Suddī and others also took it as doubt, while a second line of explanation traces it to a desire that has overcome its owner rather than to a simple lack of information. The two readings belong together, since a doubt that is entertained long enough becomes a desire, and the desire then defends itself against every proof. The early teachers were careful on this point, and they described the sickness as a defect in the heart rather than a weakness in the arguments, because the men being described had every proof in front of them. The men described in the verse did not arrive at their position in a day; the illness was in them, and the events of the sūrah only brought it out.

**THE INCREASE THAT FOLLOWS THE ILLNESS**

The next clause states what happens to a person in that condition: ***“and Allah ˹only˺ lets their sickness increase”***. The increase is tied to the revelation itself, since every new verse that came down was a fresh opportunity for the healthy heart and a fresh burden on the sick one. The Book states the same law in another sūrah where a difficult passage is sent and its effect on two groups is described: (9:125 — **“it has increased them only in wickedness upon their wickedness”**). The Book uses the phrase about the sick heart in several places, and one of them shows the same people arguing about a passage instead of submitting to it: (74:31 — **“those ˹hypocrites˺ with sickness in their hearts and the disbelievers will argue”**). A remedy can become part of the disease for the one who resists it, since the command a healthy person obeys makes a sick person worse by giving him something else to resist. That is why the verse traces the increase to God rather than to the revelation, because the same act is mercy for one heart and judgment for another.

**THE PUNISHMENT AND WHAT IT IS FOR**

The verse finishes with the outcome: ***“They will suffer a painful punishment”***, ***“for their lies”***. The wording gives the reason for the punishment, and the reason is the lie rather than the sickness, which shows that the illness in the heart does not excuse the man who acts on it. In the first generation the phrase was taken as the lie they told with their tongues when they said they believed, and it was also taken as their denial of the messenger, and the two readings describe the same person from different sides. The Book names the same men in another place where their hearts are visible in their faces: (5:52 — **“You see those with sickness in their hearts racing for their guardianship”**). What turns on this verse is that a person's words carry his inner state into the open, so that the hidden illness is not private for long. A community that has been warned about this group can protect itself, and the individual who hears the warning can examine what his own speech has been hiding. The reason the illness is named so plainly here is that a hidden sickness cannot be treated, while a named one can be brought to the physician.

**A WOUND COVERED WITH A CLEAN BANDAGE**

Think of a man who is wounded and who dresses the wound carefully to hide it from his family. The bandage is neat, and the wound is still working under it, quietly and without stopping, until the day the leg can no longer carry him. The verse describes a heart in that condition, and the comparison shows why the sūrah takes the trouble to name the matter in the middle of a description of public behaviour. These days a person can present a disciplined surface while the doubt underneath is fed by everything he reads and watches, and the passage asks him to deal with the illness rather than with the covering. The verses that follow show the same men at work among their neighbours, so its machinery is recognised before it appears.
