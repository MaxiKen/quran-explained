**TWO GROUPS ON THE EDGE OF FAILURE**

***“˹Remember˺ when two groups among you ˹believers˺ were”*** ˹Remember˺ when two groups among you ˹believers˺ were about to cower, then Allah reassured them preserves the march's first crisis. And its case is fixed in the Sahih by its own subjects. The two groups. Banu Salimah of the Khazraj and Banu Harithah of the Aws, the army's two wings. Wavered when Abdullah ibn Ubayy broke away at the road with his three hundred. The desertion's contagion touching the wings with the thought of turning back. An tafshala. To lose heart, to cower. Then Allah reassured them. Wallahu waliyyuhuma. And Allah was the Guardian of the two. The wavering arrested not by argument or shame but by the wilayah itself. The Guardian's hand on the two hearts. And Jabir ibn Abdillah. Himself of Banu Salimah. Published the verse's reception among its subjects. It was revealed concerning us, and I would not wish it unrevealed, for Allah says. 'and Allah is the Guardian of the two'. The named tribes treasuring the verse that recorded their near-fall. Because it recorded, in the same clause, their Patron. (3:121 — **“And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing”**)

**GUARDIANSHIP THAT PREVENTED COLLAPSE**

***“about to cower, then Allah reassured them. So”*** The case's jurisprudence is the passage's first lesson. The hamm. The inclination entertained. The two wings did not turn back. They weighed it. And revelation's treatment of the weighing teaches the community its whole psychology of wavering. The inclination recorded without concealment, attributed to its trigger, arrested by grace. And converted, by the verse's own publication, into honour. Jabir's delight being the case's deepest teaching. The community formed by this Book does not bury its near-failures. It recites them. For the near-failure's record carries the Guardian's name, and shame is a small price for that clause. (3:123 — **“Indeed, Allah made you victorious at Badr when you were ˹vastly˺ outnumbered.”**) In modern life, guardianship that prevented collapse matters because a moment of fear does not define faithful people when divine care restores them before they abandon their duty.

**RELIANCE DURING FEAR**

***“in Allah let the believers put their trust”*** So in Allah let the believers put their trust closes the case with its extracted law. Wa ala llahi falyatawakkali l-mu'minun. Upon Allah let the believers rely. And the tawakkul-command's placement is its precision. Issued at the exact seam the crisis exposed. The wings had leaned, unawares, on the army's numbers. And numbers deserted. The reassurance re-seated their weight on the only load-bearing point (the Guardian who does not desert), and the closing command generalises the re-seating for every believer's every formation. Reliance placed where desertions cannot reach it. The doctrine the whole Uhud-passage will hammer at its stations and the Prophet's community carried as its marching-creed. Sufficient for us is Allah, and He is the best Disposer of affairs. While the passage now turns, before the day's wound, to the memory issued as its medicine. Indeed, Allah made you victorious at Badr when you were vastly outnumbered. Imagine a climber whose knees buckle at the ledge but whose safety rope holds before the fall. For two groups on the edge of failure, Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the two groups as Banū Salamah and Banū Hārithah and credits Allah’s protection with stopping their withdrawal. The verse records an inclination without declaring that the two groups completed it; which means that difference protects the believer from confusing temptation with settled betrayal. Fear approached, but Allah became their guardian and kept fear from taking command; therefore the event also shows why inner weakness should be treated early. Once panic becomes movement, it can break a formation and expose others to harm; in other words trust in Allah is therefore placed at the end as the remedy that steadies action, not as a slogan after action has been abandoned. A person may feel the first pull to withdraw from a duty, especially when others have already deserted it; hence the proper response is to seek Allah’s aid, remember the trust, and remain at the assigned place. Communities should likewise support those near collapse rather than branding every moment of hesitation as hypocrisy; this is why divine guardianship turned this near-failure into preserved service, and the Qur’an remembers the rescue as well as the weakness.
