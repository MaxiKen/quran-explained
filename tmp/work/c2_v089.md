**THE DECISIVE OPENING SOUGHT BY PRAYER**

The memory the verse opens is a memory of prayer, and the prayer's own vocabulary is the first exhibit: ***“Although they used to pray”***, ***“for victory ˹by means of the Prophet˺ over the polytheists”***. The verb they used means to seek the decisive opening, the opening of the locked battle and of the sealed judgment. The tradition keeps the companion word in a saying of the Prophet about seeking that same opening through the prayers of the poorest of his emigrants. The scene behind the verse is fixed in the transmitted accounts with names. Before the sending, the scripture-bearers of the city would invoke God for the coming of the awaited prophet so that they might be granted victory over the hostile clans around them. The accounts name the tribes of Asad, Ghatafan, Muzaynah, and Juhaynah as the enemies of that prayer, and the two clans of the city itself as its target in another telling. The taunt that came with the prayer was preserved in the same lines: a prophet is to be sent at the end of time, and we with him shall finish you off as the nations of old were finished off. The whole tableau is worth holding in view. It converts the later rejection into a broken contract of expectation. The prayer asked for the man, the prayer was answered, and the answer was received like a debt collector.

**A BOOK THAT CONFIRMS THEIR OWN SHELF**

The arrival is then described in terms that carry their own compliment: ***“when there came to them a Book from Allah”***, ***“which they recognized, confirming the Scripture they had ˹in their hands˺, they rejected it.”***. The indefiniteness of the noun was read by the analysts as magnification: a Book, meaning a matter of the first rank. The description of it as confirming what they possessed stands second in the sentence's order because the first place belongs to its origin. It is from Him, and its confirmation of their shelf follows from that. The grammar of confirmation is itself pointed. The volume is described as confirming their book and not needing confirmation by it, and the glosses were careful to note the direction of authority. The recognition clause then names the real crisis. The arrival was not a surprise package but a matched description, and the same recognition-metric appears where the revelation says that those who were given the Scripture know the awaited one as they know their own children: (2:146 — **“Those We have given the Scripture recognize this ˹Prophet˺ as they recognize their own children.”**). The effect of the verse's order is prosecutorial: gift, confirmation, recognition, rejection, a chain in which the last link had no cover of ignorance.

**THE REPROACH AND ITS REPLY**

The scene of the denial is preserved down to the dialogue, and it is the passage's most human exhibit. The account handed down from Ibn ʿAbbās records two helpers of the early community, Muadh b. Jabal and Bishr b. al-Bara of the Salamah clan, saying to the men whose praying for victory this passage recalls: *"O Jews! Fear Allah and embrace Islam. You used to invoke Allah for the coming of Muhammad when we were still disbelievers and you used to tell us that he would come and describe him to us."* The reply of a man of the Nadir clan did not dispute the history at all. He declared that the man had brought nothing they recognized and was not the prophet they had described. The sentence about recognition and rejection was then revealed about that statement. Notice what the reply concedes by silence: the prayers, the descriptions, the dates. The dispute moved from the facts of the dossier to the identity of the arrival, which is the last refuge of a foregone decision. What follows from the dialogue's shape is the passage's whole theory of the denial. The evidence was not weighed and found light. The verdict was first, and the weighing was arranged after.

**WHEN RECOGNITION IS THE WORST WITNESS**

The diagnosis beneath the dialogue is named without anesthesia: the disbelieving came out of envy and out of fear for the seat of leadership. The passage's anthropology of faith is therefore starker than a court of evidence. Knowledge of the truth is not the assent the law requires, since if knowledge alone counted, even the adversary would stand among the faithful, and the depth of his knowing would only deepen his case. The sequel of the revelation makes the motive explicit in the verse that follows this one, where the malice is laid down as the cause. In other words, the recognition was not the problem's solution but its aggravation: the better the match, the worse the refusal. The tradition's habit of reading knowledge and acceptance as two separate acts is exactly what this passage was written to teach.

**THE CURSE THAT CLOSES THE DOCKET**

The verse ends with the imprecation the whole section has been earning: ***“So may Allah’s condemnation be upon the disbelievers”***. The syntax of the sequence was noted as compressed. The response to the first arrival-clause is supplied by the response to the second, so that the sentence reads like a court that stops taking evidence mid-trial and pronounces. The list of the condemned's file in the other assembly of the same theme keeps the same order of exhibits, the covenant, the signs, the prophets, and the saying about the sheathed hearts: (4:155 — **“is Allah Who has sealed their hearts for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few—”**). The sealing there is placed after the disbelief exactly as the reversal of the last passage demanded.

**THE PRAYER ANSWERED AND THE DEBT REFUSED**

The verse's afterlife in the tradition is as a warning about answered prayer, and the warning travels well beyond its first audience. A house may pray for the deliverer for generations and still prefer the arrangement that the deliverer's coming would disturb. The city's scripture-bearers prayed for the end of their enemies and were handed the end of their monopoly. The reason the prayer's answer reads as judgment is that the prayer itself was never about the man. It was about the victory he would bring to the prayer's own party. The practical demand on every later audience today is therefore unromantic: the arrival of the long-awaited correction is the test the longing never designed for itself, and the docket of this passage is kept open for every generation that recognizes its prophet and files the recognition under envy.
