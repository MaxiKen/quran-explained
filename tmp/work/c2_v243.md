**FLIGHT THAT CANNOT OUTRUN DEATH**

***“Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who fled their homes in the”*** establishes flight that cannot outrun death. The verse recalls a large group who left their homes in fear of death. The Qur’an does not identify the town or give every historical detail. Transmitted accounts connect the event to a plague or another deadly danger among Banū Isrāʾīl, but those reports differ in detail. What is certain from the revelation is that thousands fled, Allah caused them to die, and then restored them to life as a sign of His power. The story is not a condemnation of every attempt to escape danger. Islam permits seeking safety, treatment, shelter, and protection. The issue is the heart’s assumption that flight or worldly means can escape Allah’s decree. A person takes means while knowing that life and death remain in Allah’s hand. The command Die and the restoration to life display Allah’s absolute authority. Human beings may plan, build walls, travel, develop medicine, and protect themselves, but none can guarantee life. The verse prepares the reader for the following command to fight in Allah’s cause and to spend for the community: fear of death must not become a reason to abandon every duty. At the same time, the passage does not glorify death or demand reckless exposure. The Prophet ﷺ taught people to avoid plague-stricken areas and not to enter them, while one already inside should not flee, as al-Bukhārī and Muslim preserve in their Ṣaḥīḥ collections. **LIFE AND DEATH BELONG TO ALLAH**

***“thousands for fear of death? Allah said to them, “Die!” then He gave them”*** deepens life and death belong to allah. This shows that avoiding contagion and taking precautions are compatible with belief in decree. The restoration of the dead is a sign of the resurrection. The One who returned life to this group can raise all humanity after death. The event is also a direct lesson in Allah’s ability to reverse what people consider final. Death is not a barrier to divine power, and the Hereafter is not an irrational idea. The Qur’an tells such stories not to satisfy curiosity about supernatural spectacle but to establish accountability. If Allah can restore life, then people will return to Him and answer for fear, courage, gratitude, and the use of their life. Fear of death is natural. A believer may feel fear and still act courageously. Courage is not the absence of the survival instinct; it is refusing to let fear cancel an obligation when lawful protection of others requires action. The story warns against collective cowardice that leaves a community defenceless or abandons the vulnerable. The following verses call for spending and speak of the leaders of Banū Isrāʾīl seeking a king to fight. The sequence suggests that life must be used in obedience and service, not merely preserved for its own sake. A long life wasted in fear is not automatically better than a shorter life lived with faith and benefit. **RESURRECTION IS POSSIBLE**

***“life. Surely Allah is ever Bountiful to humanity, but most people are ungrateful”*** clarifies resurrection is possible. Allah is full of bounty to people. Life itself is a bounty, and restoration is a further sign. Guidance, health, safety, family, knowledge, and opportunities to serve are also gifts. Ingratitude—kufr al-niʿmah—occurs when people receive these gifts but attribute them solely to themselves or use them against Allah’s purposes. Widespread ingratitude warns believers that gratitude cannot be assumed from survival. A person who escapes a disease should not merely return to old negligence. He thanks Allah through obedience, care for others, and responsible use of renewed opportunity. Because the Qur’an leaves the town unnamed, believers should not build doctrine on uncertain details. The story’s lesson remains valid without knowing whether the event involved a particular plague, town, or tribe. This is a Qur’anic discipline: take the guidance that revelation confirms, and avoid turning transmitted possibilities into certainty. The same restraint applies to contemporary disasters. No person should confidently declare that a particular victim was punished or that a catastrophe has one hidden meaning known only to him. Allah knows the wisdom of events. People respond with repentance, practical care, and compassion.
