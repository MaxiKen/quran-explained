**THE TRADE THE VERDICT NAMES**

The verse reduces the whole preceding file to a single transaction: ***“These are the ones”***, ***“who trade the Hereafter”***, ***“for the life of this world.”***. The glosses define the exchange without sentiment: purchasing the present life at the price of the next by preferring the first to the second. The transmission from Ibn ʿAbbās pairs the columns by kind — this lower world in place of the world to come, and disbelief in place of belief. The commerce vocabulary is doing the work of a whole theology of value, because a trade names two goods and their agreed rates, and the verse's accusation is not that the men pursued the world but that they priced eternity at the world's rate. The earlier woe had already called the price small and the gain fleeting; the present verse removes even the appearance of shrewdness from the deal, since the merchandise forfeited is named in its full weight before the sentence names the buyers. The books elsewhere draw the same transaction from the other side, where the bargain is written as it should be written — (4:74 — **“Let those who would sacrifice this life for the Hereafter fight in the cause of Allah. And whoever fights in Allah’s cause—whether”**) — and the difference between the two passages is the difference between a purchase and a sale. In other words, the same exchange runs in two directions: the faithful trade this life for the next, and the men of this verse trade the next for this one. What turns on the direction is everything, since one trade is called sacrifice and the other is called loss.

**DOORS BOLTED FROM BOTH SIDES**

The sentence then closes the two exits of hope in order: ***“So their punishment will not be reduced”***, ***“nor will they be helped”***. The first negative was read in two registers that nearly meet — the punishment will not be lightened, and it will not be lifted — so that neither the discount nor the cancellation is available to the buyers. The second negative withdraws the last resort: no helper will reach them, and the support that would turn the chastisement away is expressly withheld. The doctrine of intercession in the tradition is careful on this point, because the books describe a mercy that reaches the sinful believer after his term while this sentence addresses a class that sold the term itself; the promise of the former does not reach the merchandise of the latter. The reason the two negatives stand together is structural: the first answers the claim that the punishment will be brief, and the second answers the claim that someone will intervene — and the passage that began with invented arithmetic ends by emptying both calculations at once. The parallel warning against the same bargain in the widest register states the market rule without a parable: (16:95 — **“And do not trade Allah’s covenant for a fleeting gain. What is with Allah is certainly far better for you,”**). The effect of reading the two passages together is to see the covenant treated the way the sellers treated it: an asset with a liquidity date.

**SETTLEMENT DAY WRITTEN INTO THE TERMS**

The summary verse is short because the case is short: the docket of the previous pages is compressed into a bill of sale, and the bill names the two goods and the loss. The reason the compression works is that the whole dispute has been a dispute about rates, and a bill of sale settles rates without debate. The practical demand the compression places on a reader is the demand of valuation: every age keeps its own exchange floor, its own rate at which the next world is quietly discounted for career, applause, or comfort, and the verse refuses to dignify the rate with argument. The buyers here were not atheists of the marketplace; they were men of a book who kept the articles they liked, and the trading was done with the vocabulary of devotion. In other words, the verdict does not argue the valuation; it publishes the settlement. For any congregation today that has learned to speak of the hereafter in the cadence of a portfolio, the passage is the oldest margin call in the record: the position was marked at the price of the purchase, and the settlement is already written into the terms.
