**KNOWLEDGE THAT LEAVES NO ROOM TO HIDE**

The verse that seals the scene of secrecy speaks in a question that is already a verdict: ***“Do they not know”***. The interrogative here is affirmative in force, and the small conjunction that opens it marks the clause as a supplement to the exposure before it: these men know, and their knowing is the measure of their crime, because a deed done with full sight of its own nature is judged at its worst. The glosses fix the referent of they with care — Ibn ʿAbbās takes the reproach as falling on the leaders, the men who rebuked their weaker fellows for disclosing the tidings — and the two halves of the knowledge in view are exactly the two halves of their operation. What they keep hidden among themselves is their unbelief and their mutual reproach; what they proclaim to the Prophet and his Companions is the purchased testimony of the mouth. The structure of the sentence is therefore the structure of the marketplace they have built: a private inventory and a public advertisement, each priced differently. That the address returns to the leaders here follows the logic of the whole passage, since the machinery of corruption runs on expertise and the verse closes the account with the experts first.

**THE SECRET AND THE SHOWN RECORDED TOGETHER**

The content of the divine knowledge is then stated without a gap: ***“that Allah is aware of what they conceal”***, ***“and what they reveal”***. The scope drawn in the glosses reaches past this single affair to every affair, and the pairing of concealment and disclosure is the point rather than the ornament. A ledger with two columns is still one ledger, and the weight recorded against the one who disobeys with full knowledge is heavier than the weight recorded against the one who never knew, in other words the excuse of ignorance is unavailable to both parties at once. The reading of the clause as a deterrent carries its own purpose attached: the verse speaks as it does so that the actors may desist, which means the disclosure of divine knowledge here is itself offered as a door before it stands as a charge. The theme is common ground across the revelation, where the God who knows what the hearts conceal and what the tongues proclaim is invoked at the end of every scene of plotting — (9:78 — **“Do they not know that Allah ˹fully˺ knows their ˹evil˺ thoughts and secret talks, and”**) — and the audience of the Book has never been asked to pretend that privacy hides anything from its Author. The practical effect for every later reader is exact, and it lands on conduct today rather than on theory: the habit of auditing deeds before their witnesses forgets that the first witness is never absent, and the soul that keeps two books — one for the street and one for the back room — keeps them under one gaze. A community that internalises this ledger loses the appetite for theatrical piety, since nothing performed for the crowd adds a single entry the gaze had not already written.

**FROM THE GUARDIANS TO THE FLOCK THEY FAILED**

The sequence of the passage now takes one step down the social ladder, and the step is itself a judgement. The verse before this one addressed the educated; the next addresses the unlettered; and the narrated analysis of the pair holds that the dishonesty of the scholars was the real cause of the commons' superstition, so that the crime of the teachers weighs heavier than the ignorance of the taught. What follows for the community of that age is a population whose minds were furnished by its leaders with pleasing falsehoods and flattering guesses — the exact commodity described in the verse to come. The distribution of blame in this passage is therefore graduated rather than levelled, since a people who had been fed lies for generations stand in a different dock from the men who wrote the menu. In every generation that has watched religious knowledge brokered for money and respect, the graduated blame reads as a standing warning: the teacher answers for the student's vocabulary, and the ledger of verse after verse keeps the two columns in the same book.
