**THE BALANCED SUPPLICATION**

***“Yet there are others who say, “Our Lord! Grant”*** establishes the balanced supplication. After describing people who ask only for worldly gain, the Qur’an presents the supplication of the balanced believer. The familiar supplication asks for good in both realms and protection from the Fire. It is one of the most comprehensive supplications in the Qur’an because it gathers immediate needs, eternal success, and rescue from the consequence of sin. Good in this world includes sound faith, beneficial knowledge, lawful provision, health, safety, a righteous family, useful work, contentment, and the ability to obey Allah. It does not mean unlimited luxury or success by worldly standards. A smaller amount of wealth with blessing and lawful use may be better than abundance that corrupts the heart. The servant leaves the details to Allah’s wisdom. Good in the Hereafter is greater and more lasting. It includes forgiveness, acceptance, a secure resurrection, the shade of Allah’s mercy, entry into Paradise, and the honour of seeing Allah in the manner He grants to the people of Paradise. The believer asks for worldly benefit only within the horizon of the final return. This balance corrects two extremes. One person despises every lawful worldly blessing and imagines that poverty itself is holiness. Another makes comfort, status, and consumption the whole purpose of life. Islam allows the servant to ask for health, marriage, work, safety, and relief while reminding him that none of these can replace faith and righteous action. (2:200 — **“When you have fulfilled your sacred rites,”**) Because the balanced supplication frames good in the hereafter, good in the hereafter returns attention to the balanced supplication.

**GOOD IN THE HEREAFTER**

***“us the good of this world and the Hereafter,”*** deepens good in the hereafter. The final request, protect us from the punishment of the Fire, shows that the believer does not rely upon his deeds alone. He works, repents, and hopes in Allah’s mercy. Protection includes being saved from disbelief, hypocrisy, persistent sin, injustice, and a final state that leads to punishment. It also includes being forgiven for shortcomings that no human effort could fully avoid. Fear of the Fire should not become despair. It directs the servant away from harm and toward repentance. The Prophet ﷺ frequently sought refuge from the Fire and taught his Companions to combine fear with hope. The believer asks for Paradise because he trusts Allah’s generosity and asks for protection because he knows his own weakness. The preceding verses discuss Hajj and the two groups of pilgrims: those whose prayers remain limited to the world and those who ask for both worlds. The supplication is especially appropriate during pilgrimage, when people gather before Allah and remember that worldly travel is a rehearsal for the journey to the Hereafter. The Prophet ﷺ made this supplication often, as reported by Anas ibn Mālik in al-Bukhārī’s Ṣaḥīḥ and Muslim’s collection. Its frequent use shows that balance is not a theoretical ideal. It belongs in daily prayer, after worship, during hardship, and whenever the servant must choose between immediate desire and lasting good. The requested good is broad enough to include what is beneficial and beautiful. (16:30 — **“And ˹when˺ it is said to those mindful ˹of Allah˺, “What has your Lord revealed?” They say,”**) Since protection from the fire follows the balanced supplication, good in the hereafter links purpose to protection from the fire.

**PROTECTION FROM THE FIRE**

***“and protect us from the torment of the Fire.””*** clarifies protection from the fire. A person may ask for a particular job or relationship and receive something else that protects his faith and future. He may experience an illness that is painful but becomes a means of forgiveness and deeper dependence on Allah. The supplication asks Allah for goodness, not for the servant to control every form it must take. This does not mean that the believer should call every harm good or refuse treatment and practical action. He seeks relief and uses lawful means while trusting Allah to determine the ultimate benefit. Goodness includes being guided to respond well when an unwanted event occurs. The plural form of the prayer can be used personally and communally. A believer asks for a safe home, but also for safe communities; for lawful wealth, but also for economic justice; for personal forgiveness, but also for the protection of people from oppression. Worldly good is not complete if it is built on another person’s misery. A society that remembers this supplication will pursue education, healthcare, security, and honest livelihoods without making material progress an idol. It will also preserve prayer, family, charity, and accountability so that worldly development serves the Hereafter. Think of the balanced supplication through a patient who asks the surgeon for the operation he saw in a video and names the exact stitches. The surgeon who agrees would be doing a favour to the request and an injury to the patient, and the man who leaves the method to the surgeon is the one who walks out healed. (13:29 — **“Those who believe and do good, for them will be bliss and an honourable destination.””**) The supplication of a pilgrim matters, which means protection from the fire returns the supplication of a pilgrim to the balanced supplication.

**THE SUPPLICATION OF A PILGRIM**

The balanced supplication reaches a reader today who prays for a promotion with his whole heart and cannot remember when he last asked for protection from anything but a rival. The verse gives him a prayer that starts with a good beyond his judgement and ends with a fire he does not want to see, and it asks him whether his own order of requests makes sense. Because the standard is named, the reader is shown why a request may be refused while its wording is honoured. A parent may decline a child's request for a knife and still be giving him what is good, and the man who prays with the word good leaves the judgement where it belongs. Because the verse stands between the warning against the first man and the promise to the second, the balanced supplication shows that the road to the reward is not a secret. The Book states the outcome in the sentence that follows, so that the reader has the answer of both prayers in order. Good in the hereafter makes the balanced supplication practical; protection from the fire therefore matters.
