**LEADERS DISOWNING FOLLOWERS**

***“˹Consider the Day˺ when those”*** opens a courtroom scene in which the relationship of leader and follower is taken apart publicly. The first act is the repudiation: ***“who misled others will disown their followers”***, and the reason arrives at once with ***“when they face the torment”***. The verse then widens the collapse from one relationship to all of them with ***“and the bonds that united them will be cut off”***. The entire network of loyalty that made the misguidance possible is switched off in full view. Influence is a loan, and the Day calls it due. The verse insists on the timing: the bonds are cut at the moment the torment is seen, which is the moment the borrowed trust can no longer produce anything for its borrower.

**BONDS CUT IN PUBLIC**

The cutting of the bonds is grammatically joined to the act of disowning, so the leaders do not merely speak against their followers — the affection and kinship that bound them on earth are severed in front of them. The horror is the exposure: the loyalties that felt permanent are shown to have been conditional all along. Ibn ʿAbbās reports that "all their aims" means the pledges and congeniality between them in this world, so even the private warmth of the group is entered as evidence. The leader who borrowed the follower's trust to purchase compliance must answer for the account. Since the cutting precedes the accusations, neither party's excuse survives the opening of the scene. The network that carried the lie is displayed as the mechanism of the lie. (33:67 — **“And they will say, “Our Lord! We obeyed our leaders and elite, but they led us astray from the ˹Right˺ Way”**)

**SHARED PROJECT, SHARED TRIAL**

A hearer may ask whether the followers are not also victims of their leaders and therefore excused. The verse refuses the excuse without refusing the leaders' guilt, because the follower's will is not destroyed by influence — it is courted by it. Every follower chose the pleasing answer over the true one, so the disowning exposes a shared project rather than a one-sided fraud. The leaders receive the heavier charge for initiating; the followers answer for their willing participation. Both are told the truth at the same moment, which is itself a form of justice. The scene is not two trials but one, with the bonds of conspiracy displayed as exhibits. The verse also removes the comfort of anonymity from the misguiders, because a bond severed in public is a relationship confessed in public. Each party is shown holding the end of the rope that tied the other to the ruin. The exposure is social because the crime was social; the group that carried the lie is gathered as a group to answer for it. Because the cutting happens before the accusations begin, the excuses of both parties are answered in advance. The network that carried the lie is displayed as the mechanism of the lie. The verse's own sequence draws the line: the leaders disown first, and the followers' cry comes next. This is the difference between victim and partner.

**INFLUENCE AS A LOAN**

The followers' counter-charge is recorded in (34:31 — **“The lowly ˹followers˺ will say to the arrogant ˹leaders˺, “Had it not been for you, we would certainly have been believers.””**). Ibn ʿAbbās reports that both the leaders and the lowly behold the doom together. The scene also explains why the choice of companions matters so much in the passages around this one: the bonds of a group are the transmission lines of its judgment, and when the lines are cut, each person stands alone with what he chose to believe. The application is sobering for anyone with influence and honest for anyone under it. Use the bond while it exists for the truth, because the bond itself is temporary property. The two audiences are addressed by the same scene: the leader answers for what he taught, and the follower answers for what he agreed to hear. Because the bonds are severed at the door of the torment, the partnership is proven to have been real and to have ended exactly where its usefulness ended. Every chain of influence will be measured by where it ended.
