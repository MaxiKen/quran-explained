**THE COLLAPSE OF FALSE LEADERSHIP**

The wording for the collapse of false leadership is ***“˹Consider the Day˺ when those”***, ***“who misled others will disown their followers”***. The verse describes a scene from the Hereafter in which followers and leaders discover that their worldly relationship cannot save them. Those who were followed—rulers, religious figures, celebrities, ancestors, ideologues, or charismatic personalities—disown those who followed them. The followers had treated created beings as authorities deserving unquestioning loyalty, but the leaders had no power to carry their burdens before Allah. The verse does not condemn every form of following. Muslims follow prophets, qualified scholars, parents in what is right, and legitimate authority within the limits of Allah’s law. The condemned following is blind allegiance that continues in falsehood after evidence has arrived, or obedience to a person in disobedience to Allah. The leaders’ disavowal is not necessarily evidence that every leader was innocent. Some were themselves deceivers who encouraged the following for status or gain. Others may have been worshipped or obeyed beyond what they claimed. The scene reveals that the relationship was based on mutual illusion: followers expected rescue from leaders, and leaders enjoyed influence without accepting responsibility for the consequences. The Qur’an elsewhere records Iblīs saying that he had no authority except that he called and people responded. His statement does not absolve him from deception, but it exposes the followers’ responsibility to choose. No created being can force the heart to worship. Both the deceiver and the willing follower face the consequences of their own choices. (2:165 — **“Still there are some”**) Because the collapse of false leadership governs 2:166, when punishment is seen arises from the collapse of false leadership. It follows that the responsibility of followers completes the collapse of false leadership through when punishment is seen.

**WHEN PUNISHMENT IS SEEN**

The movement toward when punishment is seen rests on ***“when they face the torment”***. In the Hereafter, the reality is visible. The followers see that the power they feared was temporary, the leader they praised was helpless, and the obedience they offered did not produce salvation. The Qur’an repeatedly describes this moment so that people can break false allegiances before death. A servant should ask now whether his loyalty is based on truth or on the fear of losing a group. The ability to change one’s allegiance is a mercy available in the world; in the Hereafter, regret cannot create a new test. All ties between them are cut off refers to the causes of relationship that once united them—*asbāb*. It may include claims of kinship, political alliances, shared ideology, promises of intercession, and mutual support in wrongdoing. The Qur’an does not mean that every family relationship ceases to have any emotional memory; it means that no worldly tie can function as a means of rescue when it was not established upon faith and righteousness. This is not a reason to neglect family or community. Islam commands maintaining kinship and honouring lawful commitments. It is a warning against assuming that blood, nationality, membership, or proximity to a famous person automatically transfers spiritual benefit. The useful relationship is one that encourages truth and obedience. (14:21 — **“so will you ˹then˺ protect us from Allah’s torment in any way?””**) The reason the responsibility of followers follows when punishment is seen lies in the collapse of false leadership. Therefore, the responsibility of followers returns practical force to when punishment is seen.

**THE RESPONSIBILITY OF FOLLOWERS**

The sequence reaches the responsibility of followers through ***“and the bonds that united them will be cut off”***. Followers often say, I was only obeying my leader, but the Qur’an rejects this as a complete excuse. The Prophet ﷺ taught that there is no obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator. A person must use discernment, ask for evidence, and refuse commands that clearly violate Allah’s law. Respect for elders or scholars does not mean treating them as infallible. This responsibility is especially important in movements that demand absolute loyalty, silence criticism, or portray outsiders as less than human. A Muslim may cooperate in good but must retain conscience, justice, and accountability before Allah. No group identity can cancel individual responsibility. Those who lead others carry an even heavier trust. The Prophet ﷺ said that every person is a shepherd and every person is responsible for his flock, as recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. A leader cannot wait until the Hereafter to claim that followers misunderstood him if he knowingly encouraged falsehood or exploited their devotion. Religious leaders should teach divine oneness rather than make themselves indispensable. Political and community leaders should welcome accountability rather than demand worshipful obedience. The best leadership directs people to Allah; the worst leadership makes itself the centre of their fear and hope. A crowd may follow a guide through a dangerous building, only to discover at the locked exit that he never possessed a key. At that moment admiration turns into accusation, but mutual blame does not open the door. The scene of disowned followers is like that failed evacuation: borrowed confidence collapses when responsibility arrives. Anyone choosing a teacher, movement, or admired voice today should therefore ask before the crisis whether the path is true, rather than hoping loyalty itself will become an excuse. (28:63 — **“Those ˹misleaders˺ against whom the decree ˹of torment˺ is justified will cry, “Our Lord!”**) In other words, the responsibility of followers answers the collapse of false leadership.
