**ONE LETTER BETWEEN MERCY AND MOCKERY**

The commanded word was small and its sense was pardon: ***“But the wrongdoers changed the words they were commanded to say.”***. What the people were told to speak carried absolution in a single expression; what they said instead played with letters. The accounts of their substitution differ in its exact shape, and the difference between them is itself instructive. One account gives the mockery as a grain inside a hair, another gives wheat, and another gives a grain in wheat, because the spoken form stood one letter away from the commanded one. Adding that letter turned a plea for pardon into a joke about grain, and the joke was made on the lips that had been offered forgiveness. The verse then marks the guilty party precisely. It does not lay the change on the whole community; it names those who wronged, since not everyone among them took part in the substitution. The effect of that precision is that the punishment attaches to the guilty and no inherited blame is smeared across a lineage. The parallel account states the same division: (7:162 — **“But the wrongdoers among them changed the words they were commanded to say.”**). Abū Hurayrah reports that the Prophet described what followed the change: *"they changed it and entered the gate crawling on their backsides"* (Muslim). The public disgrace answers the private ridicule, and a word spoken in play became the condition of their movement into the city.

**WHAT THE VERSE SETTLES ABOUT WORDS THEMSELVES**

The ruling this event carries concerns wording as such. The people did not abandon the rite; they kept the shape of the ordered entry and tampered with its formula. The jurists of the community therefore hold that a revealed wording is not replaced by another, even where the replacement carries a close sense, because the wording is part of what was commanded and not merely a shell around it. The fixed forms of worship show the same rule in practice. The call to prayer, the testimony of faith, and the words said in bowing and in prostration are transmitted in set forms, and a person is not free to substitute a personal phrasing for them. The reason is not pedantry. A formula taught by revelation carries its ruling in its own letters, and the one who alters it places his own sense where only the revealed sense was ordered. The gravity of the matter is also graded in the reports themselves: a change made to words of repentance drew this answer, so a change made to the descriptions of the worshipped One is graver still. Since the alteration here was small in letters and large in meaning, the answer was large in the same measure. What follows for any age is that closeness of meaning is not a licence for substitution; the words of worship stand as they were given.

**A PENALTY DESCENDING ON THE MOCKERS**

The answer is located above, not in an army on the ground: ***“So We sent down a punishment from the heavens upon them”***. A pestilence struck the camp, and the transmitted count puts the dead at just under seventy thousand within a very short time. Its direction is stated so that no enemy takes credit and no chance event absorbs the blame. The stroke came from the One whose formula had been altered, and it fell on the party the verse had already named. The cause clause is narrow and exact: ***“for their rebelliousness”***. The word the verse spends on their conduct carries the sense of crossing every bound, which is more than an error in speech. They were not mispronouncing through ignorance; they were making light of a commanded word, and defiance of that kind is what the name marks. A warning had already stood over the provision they were living by: (20:81 — **“˹saying,˺ “Eat from the good things We have provided for you, but do not transgress in them,”**). Their transgression here was not in the food but in the speech ordered beside it, and the same law answered both. The event therefore settles a line that runs through the whole account: gifts are given without measure, and mockery of the commanded word is not left unjudged.
