**WORLDLY REWARD WITHOUT FINAL LOSS**

***“So Allah gave them the reward of”*** So Allah gave them the reward of this world and the excellent reward of the Hereafter pays the historical company's file. And the payment's two-world structure is the verse's studied doctrine. Fa'atahumu llahu thawaba d-dunya. So Allah gave them the world's reward. The steadfast companies paid in this life's own coin. The victory their prayer's third petition asked, the establishment after fear, the honour among the nations. The clause correcting, in passing, the false asceticism that reads faith's payroll as Hereafter-only. The world's reward real, lawful, and granted. Wa husna thawabi l-akhirah. And the excellent reward of the Hereafter. The second column named with the comparative-superlative the first lacked. Husn. The beauty, the excellence. The Hereafter's payment alone receiving the quality-word. Both rewards given, one graded. The world's coin genuine but plain, the Hereafter's excellent. The two-desire ledger of the surrounding passage thus completed at its answer. The company that desired the Hereafter's reward received both, the world's added unasked. (3:147 — **“make our steps firm, and grant us victory over the disbelieving people.””**)

**WHAT OUTLASTS EARTHLY PAYMENT**

***“this world and the excellent reward of”*** For Allah loves the good-doers seals the payment with the love-registry's third entry in the passage. Wallahu yuhibbu l-muhsinin. And Allah loves the excellent-doers. This closing choice completes the passage's ascent. The company entered the census as fighters (146), was certified as the patient (146's seal. The sabirin loved), opened its mouth as the praying (147), and is paid, at the file's close, as the muhsinin. Excellence's people. The titles' progression being the passage's whole formation-course. Endurance ripened into excellence. (3:149 — **“O believers! If you yield to the disbelievers,”**) The better reward of the hereafter guides conduct today because faithful action need not choose between every worldly good and the Hereafter, but it must rank the better reward first. Love, not possession, is the highest payment named.

**EXCELLENT CONDUCT LOVED**

***“the Hereafter. For Allah loves the good-doers”*** The blows absorbed (sabr) until the conduct under them turned beautiful (ihsan). The wounded company pardoning, praying, and holding form. And the love-clause standing over both payments as their true content. The two-world reward's crown being neither column's coin but the Payer's love. The file thus closed at the registry where this Book banks its highest accounts. While the passage now pivots from history's paid company to the living community's standing peril. O believers. If you yield to the disbelievers, they will drag you back. The Uhud-aftermath's whisper-campaign met head-on before the day's full audit opens at the surrounding passage. Imagine a harvest that feeds the household now while preserving the best seed for the lasting field. On worldly reward without final loss, Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the worldly reward as victory and booty and the better reward as Paradise, joined to excellence in action. The two rewards correct opposite mistakes; which means faith does not require pretending that every worldly good is worthless, and worldly success does not prove that the final reward has been secured. Allah granted the steadfast people benefit in this life and an excellent reward in the next; therefore the added praise belongs to the latter because it is purer and lasting. Divine love then stands above both, showing that excellent conduct is not merely a technique for gaining prizes; in other words it is a way of living under the regard of Allah. Present service may bring trust, stability, or honour, and such outcomes can be received gratefully; hence they must not become the standard by which every faithful act is judged. Some excellent work remains costly until death; this is why the verse widens expectation without making worldly help unreal, allowing a believer to seek lawful good now while placing the heart’s final investment in the Hereafter. The worldly reward is mentioned as something Allah gave, not something the fighters seized by independent strength. That grammar protects gratitude. The excellent final reward is likewise a gift, so humility belongs in both worlds. Good-doers labour seriously while knowing that every sound result still arrives through grace. Receiving the nearer benefit must therefore increase thanks rather than reduce longing for what is better and lasting.
