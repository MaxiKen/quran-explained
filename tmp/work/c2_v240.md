**SECURITY FOR WIDOWS**

***“Those of you who die leaving widows should bequeath for them a year’s”*** establishes security for widows. This āyah addresses the financial and residential security of widows after a husband’s death. It instructs that a bequest should provide maintenance for one year and that the widow should not be expelled from the marital home during that period. The command recognizes that bereavement can combine emotional loss with sudden economic vulnerability. A widow should not be left without shelter while relatives divide the estate. The instruction is a protection, not a reason to control the widow. She may remain in the home if she chooses and if it is safe, but the verse also says that if she leaves, there is no blame upon her for making lawful choices. Relatives must not imprison her, seize her property, or treat her as a burden attached to the estate. The previous verse establishes the widow’s waiting period as four months and ten days, while this verse mentions maintenance and residence for one year. Jurists carefully discussed the relationship between the two passages. The later legal framework, including 2:234 and the inheritance verses in Sūrah al-Nisāʾ, establishes the widow’s waiting period and her fixed inheritance share. Many legal readings treat the earlier one-year residence instruction as superseded or narrowed by later revelation, while others preserve aspects of residence and maintenance through a different legal route. The enduring principle is certain even where the historical legal sequence is discussed: a widow’s inheritance, housing, and support must be protected, and no relative may expel or impoverish her unlawfully. (2:234 — **“As for those of you who die and leave widows behind,”**) Because security for widows frames relationship to the four-month waiting period, relationship to the four-month waiting period returns attention to security for widows.

**RELATIONSHIP TO THE FOUR-MONTH WAITING PERIOD**

***“maintenance without forcing them out. But if they choose to leave, you are not”*** deepens relationship to the four-month waiting period. Current application should follow qualified Islamic legal advice and the law of the country, especially where property and children are involved. Maintenance is not the same as inheritance. A widow may receive her prescribed share of the estate under 4:12, and she may also have rights to deferred dowry, debts owed to her, housing, and support. The estate must be administered transparently. Relatives cannot say, You have inherited, so we owe you nothing, while taking control of assets or refusing the rights established by law. Where the deceased left young children, the widow may need additional support as their mother and caregiver. The community, relatives, charitable funds, and lawful public systems should help. A widow should not be forced to sell her home or marry a relative in order to survive. Without expelling them condemns a common form of injustice. A husband’s family may attempt to remove the widow immediately, change locks, seize documents, or force her to leave with nothing. Such conduct violates the dignity and security the verse protects. If the home is unsafe or legally belongs to another person under valid law, the solution is secure alternative housing and fair settlement—not abandonment. The prohibition also has a moral dimension. The widow should not be expelled from the family’s care because the husband has died. She remains a human being with honour and rights, not a person whose value ended with the marriage. (4:12 — **“You will inherit half of what your wives leave if they are childless.”**) Since maintenance and inheritance follows security for widows, relationship to the four-month waiting period links purpose to maintenance and inheritance.

**MAINTENANCE AND INHERITANCE**

***“accountable for what they reasonably decide for themselves. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise”*** clarifies maintenance and inheritance. Their lawful departure places no blame on the family for decisions they reasonably make afterward. The widow may leave the residence after the relevant waiting period or when the legal framework permits, remarry, work, travel for need, manage wealth, and make other lawful decisions. Relatives may advise but may not dictate her future. The phrase fī anfusihinna bi-l-maʿrūf includes dignified adornment, lawful social activity, and remarriage after the waiting period. It does not mean that a widow must remain permanently in mourning or that the husband’s family owns her choices. Allah is ʿAzīz and Ḥakīm. His might protects the rights of people whom human power may ignore, and His wisdom appears in the gradual development of family law. The community may debate the historical relation between verses, but it may not use technical disagreement as an excuse to exploit widows. The divine law also recognizes different family structures, property arrangements, and circumstances. The executor must seek guidance rather than applying a rigid formula without considering safety, inheritance, debts, children, and the widow’s consent. The verse’s instruction begins with those who die and leave wives, but the wider community must help fulfil its purpose. Relatives should provide food, shelter, companionship, legal support, and protection from harassment. Charitable institutions should establish transparent widow and orphan support. Leaders must enforce property rights and protect women from coercion. (4:7 — **“For men there is a share in what their parents and close relatives leave,”**) No expulsion matters, which means maintenance and inheritance returns no expulsion to security for widows.

**NO EXPULSION**

The Prophet ﷺ praised the person who strives to support widows and the poor, comparing that effort to great worship in reports preserved by Muslim and al-Bukhārī. Caring for a widow is not charity that creates superiority; it is a duty and honour before Allah. Think of security for widows through a farmer who knows the winter is coming and plants his last field anyway, so that the people who eat from his land will have bread after he has gone. The field is his for one more season, and the harvest is theirs for as long as they can remember his name. Security for widows reaches a reader today who writes a will in his last week and forgets the woman who nursed him through his illness. The verse gives her a year, a house and the command that she not be pushed out, and it asks the testator what he thinks he is leaving behind. Because the close names the Almighty and the All-Wise, security for widows teaches that a bequest is a matter of power and of wisdom before it is a matter of paper. Relationship to the four-month waiting period makes security for widows practical; maintenance and inheritance therefore matters.
