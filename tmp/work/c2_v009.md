**A PLOT THAT CAN NEVER REACH GOD**

The claim in the last verse is now shown as a plan. ***“They seek to deceive Allah”*** does not say that the plan can work. A lie works among people when one person sees an act but does not know the aim behind it. God sees both the act and the aim. Ibn ʿAbbās says these people denied God in secret while they dared to think that they had fooled Him. Al-Ḥasan linked the words to their effort to fool God’s Messenger. This link is sound because the Messenger brought God’s call, and their show of faith was meant to evade the rule that met open foes. They wished to keep the shield of the Muslim group while they hid their war against its faith. It is like a man who puts on a mask in full light but stands before One who saw him make it. The mask may mislead those near him for a time. It cannot veil him from God. Even vast schemes lie bare before their Lord: (14:46 — **“They devised every plot, which was fully known to Allah, but their plotting was not enough to ˹even˺ overpower mountains”**). The force of the charge lies here. They place fraud within a bond with the One from whom no thought can hide. Thus, the first name in the verse makes the whole plan absurd before its harm is traced.

**THE BREACH OF TRUST AMONG BELIEVERS**

The full turn comes in ***“and the believers, yet they only deceive themselves, but they fail to perceive it”***. Ibn ʿAbbās names Abū Bakr and the Companions as the first believers whom they tried to fool. The false claim of faith gave them a way into a group held by trust. It could let them hear plans, shape talk, gain aid, and pass word to foes. This is why hypocrisy can harm a group in ways that an open foe cannot. Yet this verse is not leave to search hearts or charge each weak Muslim with secret denial. God has told us what He knew of this group. We know no such thing on our own. People may judge a clear lie, a breach of trust, or a known act of harm. They must not turn doubt into proof. Faith does not ask for blind trust, but it does bind judgment to what can be shown.

A later verse sets their scheme beside their prayer: (4:142 — **“Surely the hypocrites seek to deceive Allah, but He outwits them. When they stand up for prayer,”**). It then says that they pray to be seen. The act is real, but its aim is false. This matters now because a person may still use good words or good deeds to build an image. The first use of the verse is not to brand someone else. It is to ask whom one seeks to please. A weak act done for God is not the same as a planned show done for praise. Nor does one bad thought prove lack of faith. But a heart should fear the habit of making worship serve the gaze of people. This self-watch guards pure intent and keeps the warning from feeding harsh claims about others.

**THE SNARE CLOSES ON ITS MAKER**

The plot points out, but its deepest wound turns back. They may gain a short span of safety or rank. Yet no gain can make their hidden denial safe before God. Ibn ʿAbbās says that they did not know God would tell His Prophet what lay hid in them. Their lie could thus lead to shame in this life, while its full due waits in the next. It also harms them before they are exposed. One lie calls for the next. Each new role adds fear that the mask will slip. The liar must spend more thought on the tale and less on the truth. Soon the role that was meant to serve him starts to rule him. His first false gain thus plants the seed of a far greater loss.

The same law is stated for evil plots as a whole: (35:43 — **“behaving arrogantly in the land and plotting evil. But evil plotting only backfires on those who plot. Are they awaiting anything”**). This does not claim that no scheme can hurt an innocent person. It says that the plotter cannot keep its evil from his own account. A trap may catch its marked prey, yet still close on the hand that set it. Nor does a short win decide the end. Past foes made plans, (13:42 — **“but Allah has the ultimate plan. He knows what every soul commits. And the disbelievers will soon know”**). Their fault was not shrewd thought as such. Good plans join true means to a good end. Their fault was the use of lies against God’s cause, joined to the mad hope that the Lord of the end could be left out of the count. Once God’s knowledge, the scarred soul, and the Last Day are put back into view, the claimed gain is loss.

**WHEN CLEVERNESS MAKES A PERSON BLIND**

Their failure to perceive is the last part of the fraud. They watch other people with care, yet fail to see what they do to their own souls. This is a sharp form of blindness. A person can read each face in a room, spot each risk, and still miss the plain fact that God sees him. He may call that skill, though it has cut him off from the truth he most needs. The verse therefore draws a line between craft and sound sense. Craft asks whether other people believed the lie. Sound sense asks what the liar has become through it. Because the first question fills their sight, the second fades. The question a person keeps asking will shape both his next act and his inner life.

The cure begins with plain truth before God. It asks that speech, prayer, and loyal deeds agree. It also asks for swift return when a hidden aim is found. The longer a lie is fed, the more facts must be bent to keep it alive. But no bent tale can outrun the record. When people plot against God’s signs, they are warned: (10:21 — **“˹O Prophet,˺ “Allah is swifter in devising ˹punishment˺. Surely Our messenger-angels record whatever you devise.””**). Today, in daily life, truth may seem costly at once, while fraud may seem cheap. This verse counts the costs that the fraud leaves out: loss of a clean heart, fear of being known, harm to those who gave trust, and judgment before God. The faithful path may give up a quick edge, but it keeps the self whole. Deceit, by contrast, first cheats the one who chose it.
