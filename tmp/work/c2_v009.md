**A DOUBLE GAME PLAYED IN THE OPEN**

The verse explains the behaviour it has just described: ***“They seek to deceive Allah”***, and then ***“and the believers, yet they only deceive themselves, but they fail to perceive it”***. The attempt to deceive is real, and it is aimed at two parties at once. They manage the impression they make on the community while quietly assuming that God will not notice, or that the matter can be settled later. The sentence then closes the account in one move: the only person actually fooled is the one who planned the trick, and he does not know it. The verse protects its reader from two opposite mistakes, one of rage at such people and one of complacency about them. The picture behind the words is a man playing a game with a partner who writes down every move while pretending to watch something else. He changes his story, trims his account, appears when it suits him and disappears when a cost appears, and the whole performance is followed from above. The Qur'an puts the same scene more sharply in another place: (2:15 — **“Allah will throw their mockery back at them, leaving them to continue wandering blindly in their defiance”**). The mockery returns to its owner, and the blindness is not an extra penalty bolted on at the end. It is what happens when a person commits himself to a picture of the world that is not true, since the picture then supplies his reasoning for him.

**HOW THE HABIT HIDES ITSELF**

The verse shows why this habit is hard to see from the outside. The deceiver does not announce himself. He borrows the language of the community, does just enough to keep his place in it, and stops short of the point where he would have to spend something real. Any reader who has spent time in an institution recognises the type: never absent from the meeting, never present for the work. What makes the case worse here is that the truth being managed is not a policy but the claim of God upon a life, and a person who lies about that has agreed to live in a house he is quietly pulling down. The Book gives the pattern a name in several places, and the names describe the damage rather than the motive: (58:16 — **“They have made their ˹false˺ oaths as a shield, hindering ˹others˺ from the cause of Allah.”**). A shield is used by someone who expects a fight, and the oath is what he hides behind while the fight is avoided. What follows in that verse is punishment, and what follows here is blindness, because the two are the same reality seen from different ends. In other words, the man who thinks he is managing appearances is being managed by them, and that is the difference between a strategy and a condition.

**THE MIRROR THE VERSE LEAVES BEHIND**

The last words of the line are the ones that make it frightening: ***“and the believers, yet they only deceive themselves, but they fail to perceive it”***. A person inside this condition has an explanation for everything, and the explanation often places the fault on someone else. The reader is therefore told, once, not to assume the category is far from him. The test can be performed in a minute and is worth repeating: what does he do when nobody can check, and where does he bend the truth to avoid a small embarrassment? Those are the ordinary places where the deception of this verse begins and where, if it is not noticed, it settles in. The remedy the sūrah offers is not an interrogation of motives, which can turn into another performance. It is the plain keeping of the habits named earlier in the chapter, together with the willingness to be corrected out loud. On that point the Qur'an is blunt about the cost of hiding: (9:67 — **“The hypocrites, both men and women, are all alike: they encourage what is evil, forbid what is good,”**). Today a reader can practise the opposite by giving a friend permission to say the true thing to him, since the deceiver's real problem is that nobody is allowed to tell him what he looks like. A community where such friends exist is not easily fooled.
