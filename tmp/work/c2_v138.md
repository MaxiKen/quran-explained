**THE WAY THAT IS A COLOURING**

Against the schism-naming the passage raises a name for the religion itself. ***“This is the ˹natural˺ Way of Allah.”***. The tradition behind the word is richer than any single translation. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Mujāhid report that the sibghah is the colouring. The colouring is the religion of Allah with which Allah colours the people of Islam. In the report collected by Muslim the nature and the colouring sit in one teaching. Every child is born upon the nature. Then the colouring is laid on. So the religion here is a finish on the person. It is not an outfit over the person. The colouring goes down to the surface it colours. The reason the verse prefers the colouring image to the path image is structural. A path can be left behind. A colour cannot be peeled off without tearing. The effect is a faith that marks the person it saves.

**THE QUESTION NO ONE ELSE ANSWERS**

The verse's second line is a challenge in the form of a question. ***“And who is better than Allah in ordaining a way?”***. The question is rhetorical in the strongest sense. It names the standard and dares the opposition to name a better one. The question of Ibn ʿAbbās's reading stands over every dispute about the way. The ordaining belongs to the one who coloured the creation in the first place. The effect is that the argument of the previous passage moves to a higher court. The dispute is not between two communities. It is between every community and the ordainer. The difference between a way invented and a way ordained is the whole force of the question. What follows is that the reader measures every claimed way by its ordainer. The measuring is quick work when the ordainer is named. The reason the question sits in the middle of the passage is structural. The surrender at the end needs the ordaining at the front. The parallel chapter states the steadfastness in its imperative form (30:30 — **“So be steadfast in faith in all uprightness ˹O Prophet˺—the natural Way of Allah which”**).

**THE WORSHIP THAT FOLLOWS THE WAY**

The verse ends with the worship the colouring produces. ***“And we worship ˹none but˺ Him”***. The plural is back. The plural is the community the way has coloured. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Mujāhid keep the colouring and the worship in one image. The one coloured by the religion worships in the colour. The point is that the verse closes with the deed that proves the colouring. The deed is singular. Worship none but Him. The same passage guards the colouring from alteration (30:30 — **“He has instilled in ˹all˺ people. Let there be no change in this creation of Allah. That is the Straight Way, but”**). For the modern reader the sequence is the instruction. The way is named. The ordainer is named. The worship follows without an intervening committee. This age treats religion as a preference. The verse treats it as a colouring. The difference shows up in the last verb. The passage thus closes on worship in the plural. The deathbed scene closed on submitting in the plural. The two closes rhyme. The reason the verse ends on the worship is that the colouring is verified by what the coloured hands do. The passage closes on the width of the miss (30:30 — **“most people do not know”**). The colouring goes deeper than any uniform. It marks the surface it covers. The ordaining question then settles every dispute before it restarts. No ordainer compares. No way competes. The worship at the end is the colour made visible. The hands worship in the hue they were given. The community around the worship is the plural the verse collects. The straightness of the way is guarded from alteration. The guarding is the same decree that fixed the House. Nothing in the creation is to be remade by the created. The change is the refusal named in the previous verse. The refusal of the colour. A modern reader can test every claim of spiritual fashion by the ordaining question. Who ordained the way? The answer settles the claim in one line. The worship that follows the answer is the proof. The proof is done with hands. The miss at the end is the width of the crowd. The crowd never checks the ordainer.
