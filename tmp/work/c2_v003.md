**FAITH MOVING FROM THE HEART INTO THE WORLD**

The people guided by the Book are first described as those ***“who believe in the unseen, establish prayer”***. The two acts belong together. Faith starts with trust in truths that the eye cannot inspect, but it does not remain a hidden claim. It gives the body a fixed act and the day a fixed order. Prayer then keeps the unseen near through words and movements performed in the seen world. This is why the verse moves from inward assent to public worship without placing a wall between creed and deed. A claim lodged only in the mind has not yet shaped a life, while a movement with no faith behind it is only a shell. A later verse gives the fuller pattern: true faith names Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Books, and the prophets, then describes those (2:177 — **“who give charity out of their cherished wealth to relatives, orphans”**) and who establish prayer. The order is not a list of unrelated virtues. It shows one trust taking form in worship and care.

**WHAT LIES OUTSIDE THE REACH OF THE SENSES**

The unseen includes what is hidden from ordinary sight and cannot be known by the senses alone. Ibn ʿAbbās named Paradise, Hell, the Bridge, the Scale, resurrection, and the reckoning; other early readings included what comes from God in revelation. God, the angels, the final hour, and realities of the next life are not placed in one class because they are all hidden in the same way. Some are beyond sense by their nature, while some are future events whose time or form is withheld. What joins them is that sound news from God, not direct sight, is the ground for faith in them. Therefore, belief in the unseen is not permission to believe every strange tale. The source of the report matters. Faith accepts what God and His messengers disclose, and it refuses to turn lack of sight into proof that nothing exists beyond sight. The moral force appears in (67:12 — **“Indeed, those in awe of their Lord without seeing Him will have forgiveness and a mighty reward”**). Conduct changes before the hidden becomes visible, which is why such trust can test the heart.

**PRAYER THAT IS KEPT STANDING**

To establish prayer is more than to perform a few outward motions. The early explanations include its set times, cleansing, standing, bowing, prostration, duties, and due limits. They also include the inward life that makes those acts worship: attention, humility, and remembrance. The wording therefore presents prayer as something built and kept upright. If one support is dropped on purpose, the structure is no longer being cared for as the verse describes. This does not demand a feeling of equal strength at every prayer. It demands that prayer be given its appointed form and that the heart be called back whenever it wanders. The command to Moses joins its purpose to its form: (20:14 — **“and establish prayer for My remembrance”**). Another verse gives prayer a moral effect, since (29:45 — **“˹genuine˺ prayer should deter ˹one˺ from indecency and wickedness”**). The point is not that every person who goes through the motions will at once cease to sin. It is that prayer kept upright trains remembrance that resists sin, while careless prayer is being deprived of its intended force.

**PROVISION RECEIVED, THEN ALLOWED TO FLOW**

The final trait turns toward other people: ***“and donate from what We have provided for them”***. The sentence first names the giver of the provision and only then the act of spending. Wealth is in a person's hand, but its source is God's gift; this is why giving is not the owner's boast over the one who receives. The word “from” also matters in the English line. The duty is drawn from what has been given, not from what a person does not possess, and it does not command the loss of every means of support. The early readings include the required alms as well as wider gifts in obedience to God. They also keep the field broad enough for family support, relief of need, and willing charity. Since the provision came as a trust, holding it has a moral limit. Another verse makes that relation clear by commanding people to (57:7 — **“donate from what He has entrusted you with”**). Thus the verse closes its movement from hidden faith to embodied prayer and then to wealth released for good. What God gives reaches the heart as truth, orders the body as worship, and passes through the hand as mercy.
