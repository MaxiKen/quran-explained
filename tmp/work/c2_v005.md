**THE VERDICT PRONOUNCED ON THE PEOPLE JUST DESCRIBED**

After five verses of description the sūrah gives its verdict, and the verdict is a declaration from God about the people it has just named: ***“It is they who are ˹truly˺ guided by their Lord”***. The wording is emphatic in both directions, since the pronoun singles them out and the guidance is traced to its source. What the earlier verses listed was the practice of the mindful, and this verse names what that practice produces: a guidance that comes down from their Lord and not from their own cleverness. For Ibn ʿAbbās, this guidance was a light in the heart by which a person sees the road, and al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas with the reading that it is the direction the Book gives to those who accept it. The two readings agree that guidance is a gift, and the sentence locates the giver: it is their Lord, the same Lord whose mercy was named at the head of the opening sūrah.

**THE VERDICT REPEATED IN ANOTHER SŪRAH**

The Book states the same verdict about the same kind of people in more than one place, which tells the reader that this is a settled description rather than a mood of one passage. Another sūrah uses almost these words when it finishes describing those who pray and give and are certain of the Hereafter: (31:5 — **“It is they who are ˹truly˺ guided by their Lord, and it is they who will be successful”**). The repetition is not idle, because the reader who meets the sentence twice learns that the marks he is asked for are the marks of a type and not the qualifications of a list. A third sūrah opens with the result alone, before any of the marks have been named, so that the reader hears the verdict and then learns what earned it: (23:1 — **“Successful indeed are the believers”**). The sūrah al-Baqarah puts the description before the verdict, and the order lets the reader test himself against the parts before he hears the whole. What turns on the order is the reader's honesty, since a man who hears the verdict first may assume it covers him, while a man who reads the marks first has to ask whether they describe him.

**SUCCESS NAMED AFTER GUIDANCE**

The verse continues with a second promise: ***“and it is they who will be successful”***. The word carries more than a good outcome; it names the winning of what a person most needs, in a transaction where nothing can be added afterwards. The Book promises a share of God's mercy to those who obey, and it uses the language of success at the head of al-Baqarah to answer a fear that has run through the sūrah's opening verse, where the reader was told that a Book had come and that some would not be reached by it. Here the reader is told the opposite outcome in the same breath. Anyone who has worked for years at something and been told at the end that the effort stood for nothing will understand the weight of the promise, and anyone who has been told that the effort was accepted will understand why the verse ends on that word rather than on a description of the reward. The reason the wording carries no details of the reward is that the verdict itself is the comfort, and a servant who trusts his Lord does not need the account settled in advance.

**A FARMER WHO HEARS THE VERDICT BEFORE THE MARKET**

Think of a farmer who takes his crop to a market known for exact scales and honest buyers. He does not know the price when he arrives, but he knows the character of the men who will weigh what he brought. The verse names the scale and the outcome and leaves the details to the day itself. It also tells the reader where to look for reassurance, which is not in his own record but in the Lord who gave the guidance. These days success is measured by what can be counted and displayed, and the sūrah uses the same word for a result that nobody can show yet. The reader who finishes this verse is meant to walk out of it with a settled hope, and the hope rests on the One who spoke the verdict rather than on the strength of the one who heard it. The next verses describe the people who will not arrive, and the difference between the two groups is not the size of their effort but the direction of their trust.
