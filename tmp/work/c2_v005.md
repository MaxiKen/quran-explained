**THE VERDICT THAT GATHERS EVERY PRIOR TRAIT**

After three verses of description, the passage gives its judgment: ***“It is they who are ˹truly˺ guided by their Lord”***. “They” gathers everyone marked by the traits just named: trust in the unseen, prayer kept upright, wealth shared, faith in the Qur'an and earlier revelation, and certainty about the final life. Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd also applied the verdict across both groups discussed by the early readers—believers from the Arabs and believers from the People of the Book. The point is that origin does not create two ranks of guidance when the same faith and obedience are present. Nor can one trait be detached from the rest and made a claim to the whole verdict. The reason is that guidance orders the whole person. Prayer without faith, giving without submission, or belief in an earlier messenger joined to denial of the last does not match the portrait. This is why the final judgment comes only after the full set. Guidance has produced a joined life, not a badge carried by ancestry or group name.

**GUIDANCE AS A FIRM PLACE GIVEN BY THE LORD**

The people are not praised as self-made seekers who found the road by skill alone. Their guidance comes from their Lord, the One who creates, owns, sustains, and brings them step by step to maturity. The early explanations describe their state as light, clear proof, mercy, and insight from God. Because its source is the Lord, guidance is both a truth shown and help granted to follow it. The verse also gives the sense of being set firmly on guidance rather than touching it for a moment. A passing burst of feeling is not the same as a settled direction that bears the weight of choices. Another verse describes the inner side of that gift: (39:22 — **“those whose hearts Allah has opened to Islam, so they are enlightened by their Lord”**). The opened heart and the revealed road meet. In other words, the source of the gift does not cancel the duty carried by its receiver. Therefore, the believer neither credits himself as the maker of guidance nor treats himself as a passive object with no duty. The gift enables the walk, and the traits in verses 3–4 show the walking.

**THE REPEATED SUBJECT THAT CLOSES EVERY RIVAL CLAIM**

The clause repeats its subject—***“and it is they”***—before completing the verdict with ***“who will be successful”***. The repetition places weight on these people and on the path that formed them. It rules out the thought that lasting success can be secured by rejecting the Book while seeking its promised end. Yet success here is larger than one pleasant outcome or a short period of ease. Ibn ʿAbbās described it from both sides: reaching what was sought and escaping what was feared. The positive end is God's pleasure and Paradise; the rescue is from wrath and the Fire. The Qur'an defines the final measure in the same two-sided way: (3:185 — **“Whoever is spared from the Fire and is admitted into Paradise will ˹indeed˺ triumph”**). This is why a gain that ends at death cannot be the full success of a verse shaped by certainty in the Hereafter. True success must survive the judgment that follows death.

**A SUCCESS WHOSE FIRST FRUIT APPEARS BEFORE THE END**

Although its complete form belongs to the next life, success begins to shape this one. Guidance frees a person from worshipping appetite, public praise, wealth, or inherited error. Prayer gives the day an axis, charity loosens the grip of possession, and certainty about return gives present choices a true scale. None of this means that the guided life is spared grief, loss, or struggle. This is why hardship cannot by itself decide whether a life has failed. The Qur'an opens another portrait with (23:1 — **“Successful indeed are the believers”**) and at once points to (23:2 — **“those who humble themselves in prayer”**). It also says, (91:9 — **“Successful indeed is the one who purifies their soul”**). Therefore, success is not merely a prize attached at the end to an unrelated life. Its signs are already present in faith, humble worship, moral cleansing, and open-handed use of God's gifts. Verse 5 closes the opening portrait by joining the road to its end: guidance from the Lord is the beginning, and success with Him is the arrival.
