**TWO BUILDERS RAISING A FOUNDATION**

The verse returns to the building of the House and describes the work of both men, and it introduces their prayer in the same sentence: ***“And ˹remember˺ when Abraham raised the foundation of the House with Ishmael, ˹both praying,˺ “Our Lord!”***. The verb is about laying foundations rather than finishing a structure, which places the scene at the beginning of the work, and the two men are described as praying while they build. The Book's habit of reporting the speech of its prophets in the middle of their labour gives the reader an image of work and worship as one activity. The verse follows the prayer for the city with the prayer of the builders, so that the reader moves from what was asked for the place to what was asked by the men who made it. The scene before the reader is a task carried out by two generations together.

**THE PRAYER THEY MADE TOGETHER**

The words of the prayer begin with an address and move directly to a request about the work itself: ***“Accept ˹this˺ from us. You are”*** and ***“indeed the All-Hearing, All-Knowing”***. The request is not for the building to stand or for the city to prosper, both of which had been asked for already, but for the work itself to be received. The two names that close the sentence are chosen for what they say about the relation between the deed and the One who sees it, since a work is heard before it is rewarded. Another passage records the same man asking for his prayers to be accepted, which shows how consistent this concern was in his life: (14:40 — **“Our Lord! Accept my prayers”**). A reader who has done something for God and wondered whether it counted has the sentence as the prayer he needs.

**ACCEPTANCE ASKED FOR AT THE START**

The Book's decision to report this request at the moment of building, rather than at the moment of finishing, is worth pausing over. The work was only beginning when the two men asked for it to be accepted, which means their concern was with the quality of what they were doing rather than with the outcome. The Book often reports the prayers of its prophets at points where the reader would expect an account of the achievement, and the effect is to place the deed inside the relationship that gives it value. A reader who has taken up a task for the sake of God will find the placement of the prayer more useful than any instruction about technique, since the request asks for the deed to be received rather than for it to succeed. What the sentence leaves with him is the practice of asking before the work is done.

**A WORKMAN ASKING THAT THE WORK BE TAKEN**

Think of a craftsman who begins a commission by asking the client to accept the piece when it is finished, before a single joint has been cut. The verse reports exactly that posture, and it names the two attributes that make the request reasonable: the One addressed hears what is asked and knows what is done. The comparison makes clear that the prayer is not a formality attached to the work but a statement about who the work is for. Anyone who has begun something difficult for the sake of God will recognise the need the prayer answers, since the doubt that troubles a person in the middle of a task has to do with acceptance rather than with ability. What the verse offers is a way to place that doubt where it belongs.

**HEARING AND KNOWING IN THEIR PRAYER**

The two names at the end of the prayer are not decorative, and the verse's choice of them explains the request they follow. A person asking for his work to be accepted needs the assurance that the request itself has been heard, and a person asking about a deed done in secret needs the assurance that it was seen. The two attributes answer both anxieties at once, and the Book places them at the close of a prayer made by men whose work would be remembered for thousands of years. Anyone who has followed the passage from the call to the Children of Israel to this scene has watched the sūrah move from a history of favours to an example of gratitude in action. What remains with him is the prayer of the two builders, and the names they used to close it.
