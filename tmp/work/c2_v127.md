**THE RAISING OF THE FOUNDATION**

The verse's verb is the whole architecture. ***“And ˹remember˺ when Abraham raised the foundation of the House with Ishmael”***. Raised, not laid. As if the work were being lifted into its use rather than merely set down. The pair's prayer runs under the building: ***“˹both praying,˺ “Our Lord!”*** ***“Accept ˹this˺ from us. You are”*** ***“indeed the All-Hearing, All-Knowing”***. In the chains that carry the verses of the prophets, Ibn ʿAbbās reports that the recital of the prophets is this. Abraham and Ishmael raised the foundation and prayed while the raising continued. The House is built twice then. Once in stone. Once in the prayer said over the stone. The verse counts the prayer as part of the construction. It is not decoration. The reason the verse can say with Ishmael in one breath is that the raising needed two biographies. The father's and the son's. Both were already on the site. Every later builder of the House builds on top of a sentence spoken while the work was going up. The parallel chapter states the entrustment beside the assigning (22:26 — **“and purify My House for those who circle ˹the Ka’bah˺, stand ˹in prayer˺, and bow and prostrate themselves”**).

**THE ACCEPTANCE-PRAYER**

The prayer at the foundation is short and exact. Accept this from us. The completion names the two attributes that make accepting possible. The ear that receives the prayer. The knowledge that knows the hands behind it. For Ibn ʿAbbās the prayer is the practice of the prophets themselves. The standing-place of Abraham was taken as a site of prayer. The prayer of acceptance was said over the work. The effect is a theology of effort. The pair build the building and hand it over in the same breath. The accepting is not their department. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk's gloss on the city's origin runs in the same direction. The House's matter is the Lord's matter. The builders raise. The Lord hears. The difference between a project and an act of worship is exactly this sentence. The project ends when the stone is set. The act of worship ends when the acceptance is asked for. The reason the two names close the prayer is that the work will be judged. The ear misses nothing. The knowledge cannot be argued with.

**HEARING AND KNOWING AT THE SITE**

The verse ends on two names. Both are site-names in this context. The All-Hearing hears the prayer spoken over the foundation. The All-Knowing knows the hands that raised it and the hearts that raised with the hands. The record of Ibn ʿAbbās leaves the pair's prayer answered by the same two names that heard it. So hearing and knowing at the House are not abstract attributes. They are the working conditions of the building. Every later circumambulator walks inside a prayer that was heard. For the modern reader the same two names travel beyond the site. The work a person raises in any city is raised before an ear and a knowledge named at the first foundation. This age builds loudly and asks acceptance rarely. The verse shows the order the builders used. Raise. Then ask. Then trust the names. The effect of the closing titles is a construction site that is also a prayer room. The reason the verse stops on the two names is that the House was built to be heard in. The parallel prayer of the family shows where the asking leads (14:37 — **“so that they may establish prayer. So make the hearts of ˹believing˺ people incline towards them and provide them with fruits,”**). The hands lift the stones. The hearts lift the acceptance. The verse keeps both movements in one scene. A building that is prayed over is different from a building that is only built. The prayer is the second foundation. It holds the first one up. The two names at the close are the working conditions of the site. An ear for the asking. A knowledge for the hands. Every later builder inherits the conditions along with the stones. The construction site becomes a prayer room by the words said above it. The reader who builds anything in any city can follow the same order. Raise the work. Ask for the accepting. Trust the names. The order is short enough to memorise. The practice of it is a lifetime. The foundation of the House was a prayer. The prayer is still doing its work.
