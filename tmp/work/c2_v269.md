**THE GIFT OF WISDOM**

***“Allah grants wisdom to whoever He wills. And whoever is”*** establishes the gift of wisdom. Ḥikmah is more than information. It includes sound understanding, placing matters in their proper context, making the right decision, and acting at the right time and in the right manner. A person may know many facts yet lack wisdom, while another may possess modest information but use it with exceptional judgement and benefit. In this passage about charity, wisdom includes recognizing who has a right, how much can be given, how to preserve dignity, when public reporting is useful, and how to balance generosity with family responsibility. It also includes knowing when a kind word is better than money and when a material gift is essential. The Qur’anic wisdom links learning to obedience. A person who understands that wealth is a trust gives sincerely, avoids reminders, and protects the poor. A person who knows the harms of miserliness resists Satan’s threat. Knowledge that remains only a source of debate has not yet become the abundant good described here. The Prophet ﷺ taught that when Allah wants good for someone, He gives him understanding of the religion, in al-Bukhārī and Muslim. This understanding includes knowing rulings and recognizing their purposes, limits, and ethical application. It should produce humility and mercy rather than contempt for people who are still learning. Allah gives wisdom to whom He wills, and this gift may appear in scholars, parents, workers, leaders, or people whose formal education is limited. It is not restricted to social status or institutional title. (2:261 — **“The example of those who spend their wealth in the cause of Allah is that of a”**) The contrast therefore makes wisdom accountable: sound judgement is proved not by eloquence alone but by choices that obey Allah. Because insight is a gift, gratitude requires using it rather than claiming personal superiority.

**KNOWLEDGE JOINED TO ACTION**

***“granted wisdom is certainly blessed with a great privilege. But”*** deepens knowledge joined to action. At the same time, a person does not claim wisdom without learning. He seeks teachers, verifies evidence, and accepts correction. The phrase to whom He wills does not teach arbitrary favoritism. Allah knows who is sincere, who is capable, and how a person will use knowledge. The servant asks for wisdom, takes the means of learning, and thanks Allah for any understanding he receives. Whoever receives wisdom receives khayran kathīran, abundant good. Wisdom benefits the person and those around him. It protects from waste, prevents injustice, reconciles families, guides charitable spending, and helps a community respond to changing circumstances without abandoning principles. Wisdom also protects from extremes. A generous person without wisdom may give irresponsibly or enable harm. A cautious person without wisdom may use planning as an excuse for miserliness. A wise believer joins generosity with discernment and firmness with mercy. People of sound understanding—ūlū al-albāb—are those who reach the core of matters. They reflect on revelation, distinguish appearance from reality, and allow truth to change them. In the charity passage, they see that the value of spending depends on intention, source, recipient, and conduct after giving. They also learn from parables. The seed, garden, stone, fire, and Satan’s threat are not decorative images. They train the mind to see invisible spiritual realities through familiar forms. Reflection becomes successful when it leads to gratitude and action. Family disputes, financial decisions, and religious questions often contain competing rights. (16:125 — **“Invite ˹all˺ to the Way of your Lord with wisdom and kind advice,”**)

**ALLAH GIVES WISDOM AS HE WILLS**

***“none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason”*** clarifies allah gives wisdom as he wills. Wisdom means listening before judging, separating facts from emotion, and seeking reconciliation where possible. It does not mean avoiding firm decisions when a right is clear. A judge may be compassionate without becoming partial; a donor may be generous without surrendering accountability. The Prophet ﷺ combined wisdom with clarity. He spoke according to the listener’s understanding, repeated important matters, and chose the appropriate time. Teachers and leaders should imitate this rather than overwhelming people with information or using knowledge to dominate them. The believer can ask Allah for beneficial knowledge and sound judgement. He reads the Qur’an with reflection, studies the Sunnah, consults experienced people, and examines his own motives. Before giving, he asks whether the gift helps, whether it is lawful, and whether it preserves dignity. Before speaking, he asks whether the words are true and useful. Wisdom also grows through repentance. A person who learns from a financial or relational mistake may gain insight that protects others. Allah can turn a failure into abundant good when the servant responds with humility. Imagine two men who inherit the same shop, one of whom spends his evenings at the account books and the other at the door watching the street. The second learns what the customers want before the first has finished his first column, and the shop of the second is the one that is still open. The gift of wisdom confronts knowledge joined to action in a reader who reads the books of his trade for years and finds that his decisions in his own house have not improved. (17:39 — **“This is part of the wisdom which your Lord has revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺.”**)

**ABUNDANT GOOD**

The verse gives him a gift that is asked for rather than bought, a privilege above wealth and a company of people who can hear a reminder. And it asks him how often he has asked for the first of the three. Because the close names the people of reason, showing through the gift of wisdom that the mind which takes a warning is the mind that was given the warning as a gift. The gift of understanding is asked for in the prayer of the end of the chapter. And the wisdom that comes from the Lord is named beside the Book in another passage of the chapter. Because the gift of wisdom opens into knowledge joined to action, allah gives wisdom as he wills joins the gift of wisdom to knowledge joined to action. Since allah gives wisdom as he wills develops the gift of wisdom, knowledge joined to action carries the transition. Abundant good matters, which means allah gives wisdom as he wills answers knowledge joined to action. Knowledge joined to action gives practical form to the gift of wisdom; abundant good sets its horizon. Today, the gift of wisdom shapes knowledge joined to action in present decisions.
