**ALL MESSENGERS ARE HONOURED**

***“We have chosen some of those messengers above others. Allah spoke directly to some, and raised some high in rank.”*** establishes all messengers are honoured. The verse says that Allah favoured some messengers over others while Muslims are required to believe in all of them. Preference in rank does not permit insulting or rejecting any prophet. Allah gave each messenger a distinct mission, sign, community, and degree of honour, and He alone knows the full wisdom of those distinctions. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that no Muslim should demean one prophet to elevate another. He described the prophets as brothers in one faith, and he described himself as nearest to Jesus son of Mary across earthly life and the final return, according to reports collected by both al-Bukhārī and Muslim. Honour is therefore joined to continuity, not rivalry. Among them were those to whom Allah spoke refers especially to Mūsā, who is called Kalīm Allāh, the one to whom Allah spoke. The manner of divine speech is affirmed as befits Allah’s majesty, without imagining a human voice, organ, or physical limitation. The verse is a sign of the different honours Allah granted His messengers. This distinction never denies that every prophet received revelation. All received revelation through the means Allah chose. It means that Allah gave some a distinctive mode or degree of communication. Believers affirm the report and leave the modality to Allah. Some messengers were raised in degrees—rafaʿa baʿḍahum darajāt. The highest rank belongs to those Allah identified as especially favoured, including the messengers of firm resolve, and the final messenger Muhammad ﷺ holds the comprehensive mission to humanity. (3:49 — **“and ˹make him˺ a messenger to the Children of Israel ˹to proclaim,”**)

**ALLAH SPOKE TO SOME**

***“To Jesus, son of Mary, We gave clear proofs and supported him with the holy spirit. If Allah had”*** deepens allah spoke to some. The exact ranking is known fully to Allah, and human beings should not turn it into a competition that leads to disrespect. Rank before Allah is accompanied by responsibility. A higher degree means a greater burden of conveyance, patience, and leadership. The believer should not seek honour merely for status; he asks for the ability to fulfil the trust given to him. Allah gave ʿĪsā clear signs, including his birth without a father, speaking in infancy, healing by Allah’s permission, and other miracles described in 3:49 and 5:110. These signs prove his prophethood; they do not make him divine. The repeated phrase by Allah’s permission protects tawḥīd and places the miracle within the power of the Creator. He was supported by the Rūḥ al-Qudus, commonly understood as the angel Jibrīl, the Holy Spirit. The Qur’an also describes revelation as a spirit that gives life to hearts. The verse’s immediate meaning concerns divine support for Jesus in his mission, while the broader lesson is that messengers are strengthened by means beyond ordinary human resources. After clear proofs came, later communities still fought and differed. The problem was not that revelation had failed to clarify everything; human beings brought desire, envy, power, and group loyalty into their response. Some believed and some disbelieved. Clear evidence does not force a person who is determined to protect his interest. The verse distinguishes the existence of disagreement from the moral cause of disagreement. (5:110 — **“And ˹on Judgment Day˺ Allah will say, “O Jesus, son of Mary!”**)

**DEGREES OF RANK**

***“willed, succeeding generations would not have fought ˹among themselves˺ after receiving the clear proofs. But they differed—some believed while”*** clarifies degrees of rank. Honest scholarly difference is not the same as rejecting a clear proof out of hostility. The Qur’an elsewhere praises those who differ while seeking the truth and condemns those whose division is driven by transgression. Had Allah willed, they would not have fought one another affirms Allah’s power. He could have compelled all people into one outward pattern, but He allowed a world in which moral choice, obedience, rebellion, and testing occur. His allowing an action does not mean that He loves the action or excuses the person who commits it. The final phrase, Allah does what He wills, does not cancel responsibility. The Qur’an repeatedly commands people to believe, choose justice, and avoid oppression. Divine decree encompasses human choices, while human beings are accountable for what they knowingly choose and do. The verse does not teach that believers should unite by pretending every belief is identical. The messengers share tawḥīd, and Muslims affirm the distinct revelations and laws Allah gave. Unity means returning to truth and justice, not suppressing clear differences or turning them into hatred. A Muslim can honour Jesus, Moses, and their followers while remaining firm that worship belongs to Allah alone and that Muhammad ﷺ is the final messenger. Conviction and respect coexist.
