**FASTING ARRIVES WITH A PURPOSE**

The verse's phrases carry the whole argument in their own order: ***“O believers! Fasting is prescribed”***, ***“for you as it was”***, ***“for those before you”***, ***“so perhaps you will become mindful ˹of Allah˺”***. The walk proves that the purpose clause governs everything before it, since the mindfulness is the destination the first phrases travel toward. Nothing in the sequence is ornamental: every phrase carries weight the others cannot carry alone. The practice outlined through Ibn ʿAbbās matches the sequence of the phrases. The comparison with earlier communities, the prescription, and the purpose clause are welded together in the verse, and the walk shows the weld rather than the seams. The reader who keeps the sequence keeps the argument as the verse builds it.

The opening address to the believers states the ruling in the legal form used for obligations, and the address marks the community that carries it. The verse then does something rare: it dates the ruling before the Prophet's community, continuing as it was for those before you — and the comparison is a dignity, not a burden. A law that arrives as an inheritance shows continuity where the opponents of the mission claimed rupture. The report transmitted through Ibn ʿAbbās outlines the practice in its physical form: abstaining from food, drink, and intimacy from the break of dawn. The purpose clause closes the verse — so perhaps you will become mindful ˹of Allah˺ — which makes taqwa the measured outcome of the month and not merely its atmosphere. The discipline of the fast is therefore a school whose exam is the character that emerges (3:183 — **“Those ˹are the same people˺ who say, “Allah has commanded us not to believe in any messenger”**).

**AS IT WAS FOR EARLIER PEOPLES**

The comparative clause answers a question the pagans asked: why does this new law look like the discipline of earlier prophets? The verse replies that the resemblance is the point, since the One who prescribed for the communities before prescribed again for this one. From the transmission of Ibn ʿAbbās, the practice has its outline in abstention from the very things lawful at night, which makes fasting a voluntary poverty inside the day. The earlier peoples are named without distinction, so the obligation arrives as the common property of revealed religion rather than as an innovation. The value of the comparison appears at the level of morale as well: the fasting believer stands in a line that includes the pious of old, and the line is joined by the same act. Because the prescription is shared, the wisdom behind it is shared too, and the minds that became mindful before are joined by minds becoming mindful now.

**TRAINING DESIRE**

The purpose — mindfulness — explains the method. Hunger trains desire to obey a schedule it did not choose, and the schedule is the first curriculum of the month. When the lawful is abandoned by command, the appetite learns that appetite is not the master, and the lesson transfers to every other boundary the law draws. This is why the fasting person's irritability is treated as a failure of the training rather than a side effect: the act is meant to produce gentleness as well as hunger. The Prophet's practice of breaking the fast quickly and delaying the pre-dawn meal, preserved in al-Bukhārī, keeps the body inside the law's rhythm rather than the appetite's. Because the training is repeated every day of the month, the repetitions form a habit, and the habit is the vehicle of the mindfulness the verse names.

**A DISCIPLINE THAT TRAVELS**

The legislation that follows this verse shows the same purpose adjusted for difficulty, since the traveler and the ill are given other days and the weak are given a payment of food. The fast is therefore prescribed and not imposed as an absolute, and its object remains the mindfulness that survives both practice and exemption. The application is to enter the month with the verse's word in mind — to ask, at the end of each day, what the day did to one's guard against disobedience. The person who fasts without noticing any change in his conduct has kept the hunger and missed the purpose. Because the prescription is for believers, the community compares notes at the end of the month, and the comparison is itself a spur. The discipline travels from the days of Ramadan into the rest of the year whenever the mindfulness travels with it. The traveler's make-up days, legislated in the next verse, show the same design: the discipline bends around the road and keeps its purpose intact. Because the purpose is named in the verse, the fasting person can measure his month by its stated outcome. The hunger is the month's instrument; the mindfulness is the month's product. The verse's address to believers also means the month is a community project rather than a private austerity. The one who fasts beside his neighbor shares the schedule and the patience, and the sharing multiplies the mindfulness the verse promises.
