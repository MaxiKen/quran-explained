**THE RIGHTEOUS ALTERNATIVE**

***“Indeed, those who believe, do good, establish prayer, and pay”*** establishes the righteous alternative. After warning against ribā, the Qur’an presents the path of faith, righteous action, prayer, and zakāh. These qualities restore the balance that exploitative wealth destroys. Faith directs the heart to Allah, righteous deeds benefit creation, prayer maintains the relationship with the Lord, and zakāh purifies wealth and supports the vulnerable. The list is not a claim that believers never sin. It describes the orientation of people who believe, repent, and establish their obligations. A person may struggle financially or spiritually while sincerely returning to Allah. The promise belongs to those who do not make sin and exploitation their settled method. Establish prayer—aqāmū al-ṣalāh—means more than performing occasional movements. It includes guarding the obligatory times, purification, direction, recitation, humility, and the ethical effect of ṣalāh. Prayer reminds the wealthy lender and the vulnerable borrower that both stand equally before Allah. A person who prays but uses his financial power to oppress must allow prayer to correct him. The Qur’an says that prayer restrains indecency and wrongdoing. Its benefit appears when worship reshapes contracts, wages, debt collection, and treatment of family. Zakāh is a structured obligation on eligible wealth, calculated and distributed according to the rules of Islamic law. It is not merely a voluntary donation or a substitute for honest business. By requiring the wealthy to share a portion, zakāh challenges the idea that wealth is absolute ownership and creates a social bond between people with different financial conditions. (29:45 — **“Recite what has been revealed to you of the Book and establish prayer. Indeed,”**) A transmitted reading from Ibn ʿAbbās joins belief and good works to complete prayer, alms, and a deliberate refusal of usury.

**ESTABLISHING PRAYER**

***“alms-tax will receive their reward from their Lord, and there”*** deepens establishing prayer. The giver must calculate honestly, avoid delaying payment, and deliver it to eligible recipients. Administrators must be trustworthy and transparent. A person may also give voluntary charity beyond zakāh, especially where urgent need exceeds the formal collection system. The placement after the ribā verses shows that economic conduct is part of belief. A person cannot separate spiritual identity from the way he lends, borrows, hires, prices, and pays. Faith requires refusing exploitation even when it is common or profitable. The believer seeks lawful trade, interest-free help where possible, fair partnerships, and debt relief. He does not assume that religious vocabulary makes a contract ethical. The substance and consequences matter. Their reward is with their Lord assures believers that lawful restraint is not wasted. A person may refuse a profitable but exploitative contract, give zakāh, forgive a debtor, or support a poor family without receiving public recognition. Allah records the sacrifice and returns it in a form better than the servant can calculate. The phrase also removes dependence on worldly approval. The believer seeks the reward with Allah, not from the borrower, recipient, employer, or audience. This protects charity from becoming a claim of ownership over others. Freedom from final fear and grief promises ultimate security in the Hereafter. The righteous may still experience anxiety, illness, poverty, and loss in this world. (2:275 — **“Those who consume interest will stand ˹on Judgment Day˺ like those driven to madness by Satan’s touch.”**)

**GIVING ZAKĀH**

***“will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve”*** clarifies giving zakāh. They are not promised exemption from every trial; they are promised that faith and obedience will lead to Allah’s protection and reward. In financial life, this promise helps believers resist Satan’s threat of poverty. They plan responsibly but do not let fear make them unjust. They give, pay, and forgive while trusting that Allah knows what they need. Prayer and zakāh together build a community in which worship and social responsibility reinforce one another. The mosque should not be separated from the poor, the marketplace from ethics, or religious teaching from economic justice. Leaders should make zakāh accessible and ensure that recipients are not humiliated. A society that establishes prayer but ignores hunger has preserved an outward symbol while losing a central purpose. A society that gives charity but abandons worship loses the source of lasting accountability. The Qur’an joins both. Think of the righteous alternative through a household that opens its shutters at dawn, sweeps the yard, keeps its prayers and sets aside a share of the day's bread for a neighbour. Nothing in the house is remarkable, and the family is the one the whole street calls when it needs a hand. The righteous alternative confronts establishing prayer in one present-day reader; giving zakāh meets, under the demand of the righteous alternative, someone who has been looking for a single deed large enough to settle his whole account. The verse gives him four works that fit into one ordinary day; giving zakāh asks him which of the four he has left to the side. (9:71 — **“The believers, both men and women, are guardians of one another. They encourage good and forbid evil,”**)
