**COMPOUNDING DEBT FORBIDDEN**

***“O believers! Do not consume interest, multiplying”*** The command forbids believers to consume interest multiplied repeatedly. It interrupts the account of Uhud with the market's law. And the interruption's placement is the passage's studied teaching. Between the battle's theology and its resumed narration, revelation legislates against riba. The community bleeding from the field is audited at the purse. For the two files are one curriculum. The day's wound traced to the spoils' pull on the archers' hill. The world's glitter breaking a formation. And riba being that same pull institutionalised. Wealth's increase chased past Allah's bounds. The hill's minute-long lapse and the usurer's career-long one, one disease at two durations. Whence the interruption. The community is taught, mid-battle-lesson, that Uhuds are prepared in markets. The discipline that holds hills is grown or dissolved in the daily handling of gain. (3:129 — **“and whatever is on the earth. He forgives whoever He wills,”**)

**PROFIT RESTRAINED BY MINDFULNESS**

***“it many times over. And be”*** Multiplying it many times over. Ad'afan muda'afah. Doubled and redoubled. Names the practice at its Jahili shape. The pre-Islamic compounding. The debt unpayable at term, rolled over at increase upon increase until the principal vanished beneath its own multiplications (the borrower's servitude the product). The clause depicts the practice at its historical extreme without implying that lesser interest is allowed. The law's completed form stands in another Qur'anic passage, while this verse belongs to the earlier stages addressing interest. The extreme is named first; comprehensive treatment comes later. The gradualism that uproots entrenched appetites operating here on wealth's oldest one. (3:131 — **“Guard yourselves against the Fire prepared for the disbelievers”**) Profit restrained by mindfulness has present-day force because credit products and personal loans must not turn another person’s distress into compounding profit.

**SUCCESS WITHOUT EXPLOITATION**

***“mindful of Allah, so you may prosper”*** And be mindful of Allah, so you may prosper closes the command with the pairing that converts the market-law into the battle-lesson's own vocabulary. Wattaqu llaha la'allakum tuflihun. Taqwa commanded. The Uhud-schedule's own condition. And prosperity promised. Al-falah. The success-word. The verse thus reading, in its station, as the passage's bridge. The community that would hold hills and receive columns must first hold purses. The riba renounced being taqwa's market-edition, and the falah promised being one prosperity across both fields. The trader's clean gain and the soldier's held post drawing on the same guarded heart. While the next verses complete the bridge's span. The Fire to be guarded against, the obedience that draws mercy, and then the counter-race flung open at Paradise's width. Hasten towards forgiveness from your Lord. The market's multiplication answered by mercy's. Imagine a debt that grows a second set of teeth each time the borrower cannot pay. Regarding compounding debt forbidden, Ibn ʿAbbās explains compounded usury as an increased payment imposed when the repayment term is extended and connects success to abandoning it. The prohibition attacks a system in which time and need are turned into instruments for multiplying another person’s burden; which means the repeated increase is named because debt can grow while the debtor’s capacity shrinks, allowing wealth to feed upon weakness. Mindfulness is the inner guard against that appetite; therefore it makes a lender answer not only to a contract but to Allah, who sees what the contract does to a human life. Success is therefore detached from accumulation at any cost; in other words a balance sheet may rise while the person who built it fails the measure of the verse. Present forms can be complex, but the moral direction remains plain: gain must not be engineered through an oppressive increase attached to a loan; hence trade accepts risk and exchange; usury seeks return from the debtor’s need itself. The believer pursues lawful provision and refuses profit that requires another household to sink deeper merely because time has passed; this is why multiplication can occur through repeated extensions, added charges, or a structure that makes escape from debt harder with each term. The ethical problem is not solved by hiding increase behind paperwork. Where a lender’s guaranteed gain grows from a borrower’s delay, the warning requires serious examination. Prosperity in the verse is sought by leaving that appetite, which separates success from the mere size of a return.
