**RECOGNITION WITH NO DOUBT**

The verse states a recognition so clear it names its own standard. ***“Those We have given the Scripture recognize this ˹Prophet˺”*** ***“as they recognize their own children.”***. The standard of certainty is the family's own certainty. A father does not need a document to know his son. The scholars of the People of the Book knew the traits of the awaited prophet with that same clarity. ʿAbdullāh b. Salām reports his own case in one line: *"I recognised him the moment I saw him as I would my own son; but my recognition of Muhammad was more intense."* The parallel verse states the same recognition in its own words (6:20 — **“Those to whom We gave the Scripture recognize him ˹to be a true prophet˺ as they recognize their own children. Those”**). The comparison is fixed across the two passages. The point of the comparison is that no ambiguity survives it. The traits were in the shelves. The man stood in front of the shelves. The matching was immediate. What follows is that the problem of the next clause is not epistemic. Everyone in the scene knows what the scene is. The comparison is therefore the verse's own way of closing the door on every later excuse of confusion.

**THE HIDING THAT IS KNOWING**

The second sentence delivers the verdict on the knowing. ***“Yet a group of them hides the truth knowingly”***. The adverb is the indictment. Knowingly. The gloss from Ibn ʿAbbās names the thing hidden as the description and the traits themselves. They guarded the pages and buried the conclusions. The same crime is named in the covenant they had already signed (3:187 — **“˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when Allah took the covenant of those who were given the Scripture to make it known to people”**). The covenant ordered a publishing. The group chose a withholding. The effect of the adverb is that the hiding is classed as a deed rather than an oversight. An honest man can fail to see. A knowing man can only fail to act. The verse grades the second failure as the heavier crime because it was committed with the lamps already lit and the map already open. The truth was not missing from their account. It was missing from their counter.

**SHELVES THAT STILL SPEAK**

The closing application is for every generation that inherits documents. The recognition clause proves the shelves were sufficient. The hiding clause proves the shelves are not self-executing. The early commentators read the pair as a standing law of religious scholarship. The book on the shelf requires a hand at the counter. For the modern reader the two sentences bracket the whole vocation of knowledge. To recognise the truth in the sources is the first duty. To publish it with the tongue is the second. This age keeps more archives than any age before it and runs more counters that withhold. The verse shows both states in a single household. The scholars who recognised and the group that hid shared the same shelves. The difference between them was made entirely in the choosing. The shelves still speak. The hand at the counter still decides what the room hears.

**THE CLARITY STANDARD**

The family-comparison of the verse sets a standard that no courtroom can improve. A father knows his son in a crowd without a document and without a debate. The verse grants the scholars of the Scripture that level of clarity about the awaited prophet, and the granting is itself the condemnation of what followed. The hiding came after the knowing. The effect of the comparison is that the standard of excuse collapses. No one in the scene can claim that the lamps were dim. The reason the verse repeats the comparison in another passage is that the claim of ambiguity would outlive one mention. The second mention closes the exit. For the modern reader the clarity standard applies wherever a source is guarded rather than followed. The archive that knows and the hand that withholds are described here as two different moral states. This age produces more recognitions than it publishes. The verse keeps the two verbs apart and grades both. Recognition is a gift. The publication of the recognition is a debt. The debt is paid at the counter.
