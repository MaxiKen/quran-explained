**A SEAL FORMED AFTER LONG REFUSAL**

The judgment now moves from a fixed refusal to the faculties that refusal has spoiled. After ***“Allah has sealed their hearts”***, a closed document or vessel supplies the image: nothing enters, and nothing within is changed. The heart in Qur’anic speech is not merely the place of emotion. It is where a person understands, weighs, trusts, and chooses. This is why another passage speaks of people travelling so that (22:46 — **“their hearts may reason, and their ears may listen”**). A sealed heart is therefore a mind and will that no longer receive truth as truth. The early readings do not treat this state as an innocent lack of information. Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, and Qatādah linked the seal to refusal of what had been made clear. The Qur’an states the cause elsewhere: (4:155 — **“is Allah Who has sealed their hearts for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few—”**). The order matters because it guards divine justice from a false charge. God does not punish willing faith by making faith impossible. Persistent denial is judged by the loss of the very openness that denial kept abusing. In other words, the seal is God’s act, while the guilt that meets that judgment belongs to those who chose rejection. The verse before this one named their settled stance; this verse shows the fitting end of that stance within them.

Mujāhid pictured the process with an open hand. With each sin, one finger closes until the hand is shut; then the closing is stamped. Abū Hurayrah also reports that the Prophet described a dark mark appearing on the heart when a servant sins. Repentance and a plea for pardon polish it, while repeated sin makes the mark grow until it covers the heart (al-Tirmidhī). The Qur’an gives the same moral sequence: (83:14 — **“But no! In fact, their hearts have been stained by all ˹the evil˺ they used to commit”**). This evidence explains why the seal must not become an excuse for despair after one wrong act. A stain may be cleaned, and the report expressly places repentance before the final covering. The danger lies in protecting the stain, repeating its cause, and training the conscience to call darkness light. Because each refusal makes the next one easier, punishment can begin before the final punishment: the person loses the unease that once called him back. A good deed may open the way to another good deed, while an evil deed may invite another evil deed. That pattern does not remove choice. It shows how choices form the chooser. The seal is the dreadful point at which a long habit of turning away has become a settled inability to welcome what was repeatedly refused.

**THREE FACULTIES AND ONE COMPLETE LOSS**

The sentence continues with ***“and their hearing, and their sight is covered. They will suffer a tremendous punishment”***. Hearing is joined to the heart under the seal, while sight receives the separate image of a covering. Sound may reach an ear from every side, and meaning may reach a heart through many paths, so a seal pictures the shutting of the whole faculty. Sight faces what lies before it, so a veil pictures the barrier. These are not claims that the people cannot hear ordinary speech or see ordinary objects. They hear recitation without yielding to it and see signs without reading them as signs. A parallel verse joins all three: (16:108 — **“They are the ones whose hearts, ears, and eyes are sealed by Allah, and it is they who are ˹truly˺ heedless”**). Another describes listeners whose inward veil leaves them unable to grasp the Qur’an, (6:25 — **“but We have cast veils over their hearts—leaving them unable to comprehend it—and deafness in their ears. Even if they”**). The difference between physical use and moral benefit is therefore central. Eyes can work while insight is blind, and ears can carry sound while obedience remains deaf. By contrast, God sends calm into believing hearts (48:4 — **“He is the One Who sent down serenity upon the hearts of the believers”**), showing that the same inward place can receive a gift when it receives guidance.

The scale of the final punishment matches a loss that has reached every gate by which guidance is received. The heart does not understand, the hearing does not profit, and the sight does not take warning. Yet the verse is not permission to declare living people sealed by private judgment. Revelation disclosed the fate of the people addressed; ordinary believers do not know another person’s end. Its present warning is turned first toward the self. A lie defended today, a duty mocked tomorrow, and a truth repeatedly pushed aside can alter what the heart is able to love. This is why the movement from stain to seal matters. The warning urges repentance while remorse still speaks. It urges careful listening before sacred words become mere sound. It also urges sight that reads created things as signs rather than scenery. The punishment named at the end belongs to the Hereafter, but its road begins when moral awareness is willingly dulled. The opposite road also begins now: truth is admitted, pardon is sought, and each faculty is returned to the purpose for which God gave it.
