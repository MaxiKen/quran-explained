**A CLOSED DOOR AND WHO CLOSED IT**

The previous verse described people who would not believe, and this one says what has happened to them: ***“Allah has sealed their hearts”***. The heart is the place where a person decides, and the seal is placed there rather than on his hand or his tongue. What follows in the same sentence is the covering of ***“and their hearing, and their sight is covered. They will suffer a tremendous punishment”***. Three openings that bring a person information are named in order, and each is shut. He cannot weigh the matter, he cannot take in the argument, and he cannot see the plain evidence in front of him. The verse is severe on purpose, because it describes an end rather than a beginning. The Qur'an asks the same question of anyone who will listen in another place: (6:46 — **“Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, “Imagine if Allah were to take away your hearing or sight, or seal your hearts—who”**). Nobody who reads that line can treat a sealed heart as a misfortune that happens to other people.

The order of the three is the part that a reader should not skip. The heart is named first, and the faculties follow, which is exactly how a person loses a truth he once knew. He decides in private that he will not shift, and then his ears stop picking up the argument and his eyes stop noticing what is obvious to everybody else. Anyone who has watched a family quarrel run for years has seen the sequence performed in front of him. The sealing is not an accident that befalls a man on his way to work. It is the last stage of a course he chose, and the verse reports it in that order so that the reader can see the course while he is still on it.

**HOW THE CONDITION GROWS**

The Book describes the same condition in another verse as an illness that is allowed to spread: (2:10 — **“There is sickness in their hearts, and Allah ˹only˺ lets their sickness increase. They will suffer a painful punishment”**). The increase is the frightening part of that line. A person is not struck down for one ugly thought; he is allowed to continue, and the continuing itself does the damage. The same pattern holds in this verse, since the seal is described as coming upon people who persisted, not upon people who stumbled. In other words, the world is arranged so that choices grow, and a direction once taken quietly becomes the shape of what a person is able to hear and see.

This is why the verse cannot be read as a story about strangers. The early community read these lines with alarm rather than with relief about other people, and the reason is simple: nobody can inspect his own heart from the outside. The Companions feared a hidden slip more than a visible enemy, and their fear was itself evidence that their hearts were still awake. A man whose heart is sealed does not worry about the state of his heart, which means the worry a reader feels at this verse is not a symptom of the disease but a sign of health. The verse is medicine being handed to him, and it tastes bad because it is meant to. Think of a man who keeps a spare key under a flowerpot and tells himself the door is safe, until the day he notices the pot has been moved and cannot remember when. The drift described here is that movement: nothing dramatic, and everything changed.

**WHEN FEAR IS THE RIGHT RESPONSE**

A reader may wonder why the Book would frighten people with a condition they cannot check. The answer is that the fright is the check. Notice what the verse does not claim: it does not say that everyone who struggles to pray has a sealed heart, and it does not say that difficulty with a text is the same as the covering described here. Struggle is the state of a person who is alive to the matter, and the Book treats it as a normal part of belief. The difference between the two conditions is visible in a single question. Does the thing still disturb him when it goes wrong, or has he stopped keeping score? Because the second answer is the dangerous one, the reader is being asked to notice it while it is still uncomfortable.

The practical test is unglamorous. A person can look at the last month and ask what he stopped being moved by, and when, and what he told himself at the time. That is where this verse does its work, because the drift is always explained away in a reasonable voice. The punishment named at the end of the line, ***“and their hearing, and their sight is covered. They will suffer a tremendous punishment”***, is the loss made permanent, and it is attached to the unwillingness by the word "and" rather than by a paragraph of explanation. The Book ties the same sealing to arrogance rather than to weakness: (40:35 — **“This is how Allah seals the heart of every arrogant tyrant.””**). Today the reader can take the warning at face value and use the discomfort it produces while the discomfort is still available, since the ability to feel it is the thing being protected.
