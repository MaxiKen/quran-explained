**THE CLOSING OF THE INSTRUMENTS THAT CARRY TRUTH**

The condition just described now receives its cause: ***“Allah has sealed their hearts”***. The word for sealing describes a stamp pressed onto something so that nothing further enters it, and the first generations understood it in that sense. Al-Suddī said that the sealing means God has stamped the hearts, and Mujāhid described the stamp as what settles on a heart once sin has made its home there and covered it. Qatādah gave the reading that the hearts were sealed when their owners obeyed the deceiver and kept disobeying, so that the seal was the outcome of a road they chose rather than a sentence passed on people who had never walked it. The verse is therefore not a description of an arbitrary act; it is the end of a process, and the sūrah has just described the group as those who persist.

**PUNISHMENT BEYOND THE SEALED FACULTIES**

The verse widens the closing to the other instruments a person is given: ***“and their hearing, and their sight is covered”***. The wording distinguishes between the two: the hearing is sealed with the heart, while the sight is described as having a covering laid over it, a veil that prevents the truth from arriving. The Book states the same condition again in a verse that lists the three together and traces the act to God: (16:108 — **“whose hearts, ears, and eyes are sealed by Allah”**). Another sūrah ends a passage about people who broke their pledges with nearly the same sentence: (7:101 — **“This is how Allah seals the hearts of the disbelievers”**). The point the reader is meant to carry away is that the instruments are not damaged on their own; they are the equipment by which a person lives, and the loss of them is the heaviest loss a person can suffer while still walking around. One teacher compared the condition to a lamp whose glass has been covered while the wick still burns, since the light is there and none of it reaches the road.

**THE OUTCOME THAT FOLLOWS THE CLOSING**

The verse finishes with the consequence: ***“They will suffer a tremendous punishment”***. The punishment is named in the future tense, and it is placed after the closing of the instruments so that the reader sees the order of events rather than a single verdict. Several early teachers drew a distinction here between two kinds of punishment, one that arrives in this life by the withdrawal of the help that would have led a person back, and the one kept for the Hereafter. The Book states the same withdrawal in a passage about those who were told the truth and turned away: (9:87 — **“their hearts have been sealed so they do not comprehend”**). What follows in this sūrah is a description of people who live among the believers, speak their language and intend them harm, and the verses about them make the most sense after the sealed hearts have been described, because the pretender is a person whose outward speech still works while the inward instruments have stopped. The passage therefore moves from the open enemy to the hidden one in a single step, and the hidden case is therefore far harder to detect.

**A CLOCK WHOSE SPRING HAS BEEN REMOVED**

Think of a clock that still hangs on the wall and still shows a face, but whose spring has been taken out. It is not broken in any way an observer can see, and it keeps the room looking furnished, yet it will never tell the time again. The verse describes people in that condition with respect to the truth, and the description is a warning to the reader rather than a curiosity about someone else. The warning lands hardest when a person notices how small the first steps are, because the road to a sealed heart starts with a preference defended once and then defended again. These days, when a person can arrange his attention so that nothing arrives that he did not choose, the verse reads as a description of a habit rather than of a distant punishment. What the reader is asked to do is to keep the instruments open and to act on what they carry while the capacity to act is still his.
