**GUIDANCE FOR HUMANITY**

***“previously, as a guide for people, and ˹also˺ revealed the Standard ˹to”*** establishes guidance for humanity. The Torah, the Gospel, and the Qur’an were sent as guidance, not as ornaments for libraries or badges of identity. Revelation teaches people how to recognize their Creator, order their worship, distinguish justice from oppression, and prepare for the Hereafter. A scripture is fulfilled when its truth reaches the mind, humility reaches the heart, and obedience reaches conduct. The phrase for people emphasizes the human need for revelation. Human beings possess reason and experience, but these alone cannot disclose everything about Allah, the unseen, worship, or the final consequences of moral choices. Revelation does not cancel reason; it gives reason reliable premises and a sound direction. The Standard separates truth from falsehood and justice from oppression. It is the criterion that separates truth from falsehood, guidance from error, justice from injustice, and lawful obedience from rebellion. Allah gives revelation so that people do not treat every inherited custom, powerful opinion, or popular desire as equally true. The Qur’an itself is called al-Furqān, and the day of Badr is called the Day of the Criterion because Allah made the difference between truth and falsehood visible in history. The criterion must operate in personal life as well. A believer asks whether an attractive opportunity is lawful, whether a popular claim is true, and whether an apparent success is pleasing to Allah. (25:1 — **“Blessed is the One Who sent down the Standard to His servant,”**)

**AL-FURQĀN: THE STANDARD**

***“distinguish between right and wrong˺. Surely those who reject Allah’s revelations”*** deepens al-furqān: the standard. The verse then warns those who reject Allah’s revelations. Rejection is not the same as honestly seeking clarification or struggling with a difficult question. The warning concerns a settled refusal to accept divine truth, especially when pride, envy, or worldly interest causes a person to turn away. Allah knows what each person understood, what reached him, and what he intended. The severity of the warning reflects the importance of revelation. To reject Allah’s signs is to reject the guidance by which human beings were meant to live. It is also to expose oneself to a punishment no worldly resource can prevent. Wealth, ancestry, intellectual reputation, and political power do not protect a person from Allah’s judgement. Allah is ʿAzīz, the Almighty whose power cannot be overcome. He is also capable of punishment, so the warning is not an empty threat. Yet the verse should be read together with the many Qur’anic verses describing Allah’s mercy, forgiveness, and acceptance of repentance. Fear is meant to restrain arrogance and prompt return, not to make sincere repentance seem impossible. The believer therefore takes the warning seriously while never despairing. As long as life remains, the door of repentance remains open. But postponement is dangerous. A person does not know when death will end his opportunity to respond to revelation. Possessing a criterion does not give Muslims permission to become unjust toward people. (8:41 — **“Know that whatever spoils you take, one-fifth is for Allah and the Messenger, his close relatives, orphans,”**)

**REJECTION AFTER CLARIFICATION**

***“will suffer a severe torment. For Allah is Almighty, capable of punishment”*** clarifies rejection after clarification. The Qur’an commands justice even toward those one dislikes, and it forbids compulsion in religion. The Furqān exposes oppression wherever it occurs, including within the Muslim community. Anyone who uses revelation to excuse arrogance, racism, fraud, or cruelty has failed to apply the criterion to himself. A reliable criterion is like a calibrated scale in a crowded market: it tests attractive claims by a measure that sellers cannot alter for profit. Today, the Standard tests political loyalty, online claims, and inherited custom by truth and justice rather than popularity. Because guidance for humanity opens into al-furqān: the standard, rejection after clarification joins guidance for humanity to al-furqān: the standard. Since rejection after clarification develops guidance for humanity, al-furqān: the standard carries the transition. Allah’s might and punishment matters here, so rejection after clarification answers al-furqān: the standard. Al-furqān: the standard gives practical form to guidance for humanity; might behind the moral standard sets its horizon. Guidance for humanity stays bound to rejection after clarification because al-furqān: the standard supplies the human setting of guidance for humanity. Allah’s might and punishment therefore carries guidance for humanity beyond an isolated event and into accountable conduct.  (5:8 — **“O believers! Stand firm for Allah and bear true testimony.”**)

**MIGHT BEHIND THE MORAL STANDARD**

Allah’s might gives the Standard moral force: truth is not merely an opinion competing for attention but guidance from the Lord whose judgement cannot be defeated. His power also disciplines the believer who carries the criterion. One cannot condemn another person’s falsehood while excusing one’s own dishonesty, because the Almighty knows both. Punishment is therefore connected to responsible rejection after signs have become clear, not to a sincere question or an involuntary uncertainty. The distinction matters in teaching and public discussion. Muslims should explain revelation patiently, answer objections honestly, and leave final judgement of individuals to Allah. Think of the Furqān as an accurate scale protected by its rightful owner: it exposes false weights in every merchant’s stall, including the stall of the person holding it. Present-day loyalty to revelation begins with allowing its justice to measure one’s own speech, money, and treatment of opponents.
