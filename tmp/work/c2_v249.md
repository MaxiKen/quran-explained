**THE RIVER AS A TEST**

***“When Saul marched forth with his army, he cautioned: “Allah will test you with a river. So whoever drinks ˹his fill˺ from it is not with me, and”*** establishes the river as a test. Ṭālūt’s army was tested by a river before facing Jālūt, known in the biblical tradition as Goliath. The command was simple: do not drink from the river except a permitted handful. The test was not about the water’s nutrition alone. It measured discipline, trust, and the ability to obey a clear instruction when thirst and fatigue made disobedience attractive. A community preparing for a difficult responsibility needs more than numbers. It needs people who can restrain immediate appetite for a higher purpose. The river revealed that many who had promised to fight were not ready to govern their desires. Whoever drinks from it is not of me means that the disobedient person is not counted among those who follow Ṭālūt in this test and mission. It does not automatically mean that every drinker became an unbeliever or left Islam. Qur’anic and prophetic language can describe a person as not belonging to a group in obedience, excellence, or method without declaring him outside the faith. The exception of the handful shows that the command was not irrational deprivation. A small amount was allowed to meet need. The test required restraint, not self-harm. This is a general principle: obedience is measured within the limits Allah gives, and lawful concessions should not be treated as weakness. (2:153 — **“O believers! Seek comfort in patience and prayer. Allah is truly with those who are patient”**) Because the river as a test frames not of me, not of me returns attention to the river as a test. An account from Ibn ʿAbbās describes the army’s thirst and the river as a test that separated those able to continue from those ruled by immediate appetite.

**NOT OF ME**

***“whoever does not taste it—except a sip from the hollow of his hands—is definitely with me.” They all drank ˹their fill˺ except for a few! When he”*** deepens not of me. Most of the army drank, leaving only a few who obeyed. Numbers can create confidence, but they cannot replace sincerity. A large group may be vulnerable to panic, peer pressure, and immediate desire, while a small group may possess discipline and certainty. The few who remained should not become arrogant. Their success was by Allah’s permission, and they still had to face fear. The story praises faithful perseverance, not contempt for people who struggled. In a community, those who are stronger should teach and support the weak rather than celebrate their failure. Even after obeying and crossing the river, the believers said they lacked strength against Jālūt and his army. Obedience does not remove natural fear. A person may do the right thing and still feel inadequate before a large challenge. Faith does not require pretending to be invulnerable. Their honest admission allows the people of certainty to answer with perspective. They do not deny the size of the enemy; they place it beneath Allah’s power. A small group can overcome a large group by Allah’s permission, but this is not a promise that every small force wins or a licence to ignore strategy. It is a call to trust while taking responsible means. Those certain that they will meet Allah possess yaqīn. (2:246 — **“Have you not seen those chiefs of the Children of Israel after Moses?”**) Since the majority fails the test follows the river as a test, not of me links purpose to the majority fails the test.

**THE MAJORITY FAILS THE TEST**

***“and the ˹remaining˺ faithful with him crossed the river, they said, “Now we are no match for Goliath and his warriors.” But those ˹believers˺ who were certain”*** clarifies the majority fails the test. Belief in the final meeting changes the scale of fear. The enemy may threaten the body, but Allah controls the final destiny. The person who remembers resurrection is less likely to betray truth for temporary safety, though he remains responsible for preserving life and avoiding reckless risk. Certainty also produces accountability. Victory does not make a person righteous if he fought for pride, and defeat does not make a sincere person a failure. Allah judges intention, conduct, and patience. The statement Allah is with the patient echoes 2:153. Divine companionship is support, guidance, and reward. It does not mean the patient never thirsts, fears, or loses companions. It means that endurance under obedience is seen and strengthened. Ṣabr here includes restraint before the battle, courage after fear, and continuing to obey when the crowd has withdrawn. It also includes not insulting the majority or allowing victory to produce cruelty. Ṭālūt’s leadership required a test that exposed capacity. A commander must know the difference between a real need and uncontrolled indulgence. The people also needed to trust a lawful command rather than debate every instruction while the enemy approached. In modern life, disciplined communities use training, clear procedures, nutrition, rest, and consultation. The river story is not a command to create arbitrary hardships for followers. (3:139 — **“Do not falter or grieve, for you will have the upper hand, if you are ˹true˺ believers”**) Fear after crossing matters, which means the majority fails the test returns fear after crossing to the river as a test.

**FEAR AFTER CROSSING**

***“they would meet Allah reasoned, “How many times has a small force vanquished a mighty army by the Will of Allah! And Allah is ˹always˺ with the steadfast.””*** closes with fear after crossing. Leaders who impose needless deprivation or test people for vanity are not imitating Ṭālūt’s wisdom. Think of the river as a test through a caravan that sets out on a hard road where the rule is one cup of water at each well, and half the travellers drink from the skins they were told to carry unopened. The wells are still ahead of them, the road is still long, and the ones who arrive are the ones who trusted the instruction more than the thirst. The river as a test confronts anyone treating not of me as remote; under the river as a test, such a person has counted the numbers in his own quarrel and decided that nothing can be done. The verse gives him a river, a crowd that drank and a few who crossed, and it asks him which company he would have been standing in that morning. Because the close names the steadfast as the ones with God, not of me makes the river as a test show that the strength of a person is measured before the battle and not after it. The prayer of the two companies before the battle is kept in the verse that follows, and the two armies are described again in another chapter. (8:45 — **“O believers! When you face an enemy, stand firm and remember Allah often so you may triumph”**) Not of me makes the river as a test practical; the majority fails the test therefore matters.
