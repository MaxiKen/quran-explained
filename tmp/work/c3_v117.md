**SELF-WRONG BEHIND THE LOSS**

***“The good they do in this worldly life is like the”*** The good they do in this worldly life is like the harvest of an evil people struck by a bitter wind, destroying it ˹completely˺ prices the rejecters' remaining asset. And the parable's construction answers the question the census-passage leaves standing. The disbelievers of the file were not without expenditures in goodness. The charities endowed, the kin maintained, the guests honoured (the pagan lords' famed munificence. The scripture-establishment's alms and offices). What becomes of such spending at the final account. The parable's answer. It is as a harth. A tillage. A crop raised through the season. (3:116 — **“Indeed,”**) ***“harvest of an evil people struck by a bitter wind,”*** Of a people who wronged themselves. Struck, at the season's edge, by a wind with sirr in it. The bitter frost-wind. The burning cold that blackens a standing crop in one night. The year's labour real, the growth real, the destruction total. The deeds performed but unbanked. For the expenditure lacked the one condition that converts works to harvest. The faith that addresses them to Allah. The parable's justice being its exactness. The wind destroys the crop, not the field's history. The works happened, benefited their worldly recipients, earned their worldly returns. (3:118 — **“who would not miss a chance to harm you. Their only desire”**) In modern life, self-wrong behind the loss matters because public generosity cannot repair a purpose that rejects the One for whom righteous action should be done.

**LOSS TRACED TO HUMAN WRONG**

***“destroying it ˹completely˺. Allah never wronged them, but they wronged themselves”*** Allah never wronged them, but they wronged themselves closes the parable with the solvency-clause the passage opened on. And the closing's placement is the final account’s signature. The frost-wind's arithmetic could read, to the destroyed, as heaven's harshness. A lifetime's goodness voided. The clause files the destruction's cause where the parable's own text placed it. A people who wronged themselves. The self-wronging preceding the wind. The deeds were sown in the wronged soil of rejection, and soil decides harvests. He wronged them nothing. Their works' worldly wages were paid in full where addressed (the world), and their eternal voidness follows their own addressing. The parable thus completing the census-passage's two-column economics at perfect symmetry. The honoured column's every good banked undeniable, the rejecting column's every good frost-struck at the border. One Auditor, two soils, and the difference never the deeds' size but their address. While the argument now turns from the columns to the community's own guarding. The intimates not to be taken from those whose rage the next verse unveils. They would not miss a chance to harm you. Imagine a farmer watching a season of labour blacken overnight because the crop had no protection from frost. For spending like a frost-struck harvest, Ibn ʿAbbās compares spending corrupted by idolatry to a harvest destroyed by freezing wind and locates the loss in self-wrong rather than divine injustice. The parable explains why an outwardly useful deed can fail at the point of need; which means seed, labour, rain, and growth may all be present, yet a destructive wind can leave no harvest. In the same way, spending has moral value only when its purpose and allegiance are sound; therefore the loss is not random, because the closing sentence returns responsibility to those who wronged themselves. Allah did not take a sound crop without cause; they exposed their work to the condition that ruined it; in other words this guards two truths at once. No sincere good is lost with Allah, while impressive action without faith cannot demand the reward promised to faith; hence present-day giving can also be shaped by pride, control, or public image. The verse asks what will remain when those motives are stripped away; this is why a gift becomes lasting provision when it is offered truthfully, not when its amount merely attracts attention. The field belongs to the people in the image, so its ruin reaches the labour they expected to enjoy. Their self-wrong lies in preparing an account that cannot survive the moral weather of judgment. The parable thus calls for examination before harvest, while intention and allegiance can still be corrected.
