**THE TEST IS ALLOWED TO REACH ITS RESULT**

The challenge now passes from proposal to outcome: ***“But if you are unable to do”*** what was asked, the claim of human authorship has failed its own test. The point is that the invited test now bears on the moral right to keep denying. The conditional form gives the opponents room to try; it does not imply that God lacks knowledge of the result. They may gather helpers, use their skill, and bring forward whatever they think meets the demand. Failure after that open permission is evidence, not mere silence imposed by authority. Early explanation stresses that the challengers and their allies had been allowed to spend their effort before their lack of power was laid bare. This is why the next command carries moral weight. Once a fair test defeats the excuse, keeping the old charge is no longer a neutral pause. It is refusal of what the result shows. A similar challenge says, (52:34 — **“Let them then produce something like it, if what they say is true”**). Truthfulness requires accepting not only a desired result but also the result that overturns one’s claim.

**THE FUTURE IS STATED BEFORE THE CENTURIES UNFOLD**

The sentence interrupts the condition with certainty: ***“so and you will never be able to do”*** it. Qatādah reads this as a lack of power to meet the challenge, while Ibn ʿAbbās says that any attempt will fail. The statement looks forward beyond the first hearers. It is not merely a report that no rival passage had appeared by that day; it declares that the failure will continue. The Qur’an therefore places its claim where later enemies, skilled speakers, and whole groups can confront it. This is why the opening if does not express doubt in God. Human hearers must discover their inability through the offered test, while God announces what their trial will show. The two parts belong together: try if the charge is true, but know that created speech will not become the speech of its Creator. This does not make every imitation worthless as human writing. People can copy sounds, borrow themes, or compose eloquent lines. The test concerns producing a sūrah of its kind, with the joined force named in the prior verse. Surface likeness cannot erase the deeper failure any more than painting a flame can supply heat.

**FEAR BECOMES A COMMAND TO SEEK SAFETY**

The answer is not to invent a new excuse but ***“so then fear the Fire fuelled with people”***. The call to fear is a call to guard oneself through belief, worship, and a changed course. It does not tell the hearer to stare at punishment without hope. The warning comes while response is possible, which means that its purpose is rescue. The same direct command appears elsewhere: (3:131 — **“Guard yourselves against the Fire prepared for the disbelievers”**). Fear serves mercy when it moves the person away from the cause of harm. The fuel makes the warning personal. In an earthly fire, people stand outside and add wood. Here unrepentant rejecters do not control the flame; they become part of what it consumes. The image ends any thought that the debate concerns words alone. Calling revelation false after its proof has been faced shapes a person’s bond with God and final end. Yet no reader may take this as leave to assign named living people to the Fire. Revelation states the fate of disbelief and identifies the people addressed by God’s knowledge. Human duty is to convey, warn, pray for guidance, and judge outward matters with justice.

**STONES EXPOSE THE POWERLESSNESS OF EVERY FALSE GOD**

The description continues with ***“and stones, which is prepared”*** ***“for the disbelievers”***. Ibn ʿAbbās understood the stones as sulphur, which stresses the terror and heat of the fuel. Another early reading sees the idols that people carved and worshipped. The Qur’an supports that humiliation of false gods: (21:98 — **“Certainly you ˹disbelievers˺ and whatever you worship instead of Allah will be the fuel of Hell.”**). It then says, (21:99 — **“Had those idols been ˹true˺ gods, they would not have entered it.”**). What was treated as a source of aid cannot save itself, much less its worshipper. The wording says that the Fire is prepared, not that it is a threat invented to win an argument. Its reality answers the reality of the choice, and its readiness removes the comfort of endless delay. A later warning to believers also speaks of (66:6 — **“a Fire whose fuel is people and stones, overseen by formidable and severe angels”**). Thus the present verse should not feed pride in a person who confesses faith. It should guard faith from denial and guard the household from a road toward ruin. Today, the challenge and warning still stand side by side: examine the claim honestly, accept the verdict of the evidence, and let fear perform its proper work before the prepared end is seen.
