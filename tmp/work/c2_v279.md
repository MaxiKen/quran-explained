**THE MOST SEVERE ECONOMIC WARNING**

***“If you do not, then beware of a war”*** establishes the most severe economic warning. This is one of the strongest warnings in the Qur’an. Those who refuse to abandon ribā after the command are told to expect a war from Allah and His Messenger. The language shows that exploitative interest is not a minor technical violation. It attacks the foundations of justice, burdens the weak, and turns lending into a mechanism of domination. War from Allah and His Messenger is not a permission for individuals to commit violence against lenders, bankers, or borrowers. It is a declaration of divine opposition and a warning of grave punishment and social consequence. Any worldly legal enforcement belongs to legitimate authority and due process. Vigilantism is itself transgression. The verse immediately gives the path of repentance: leave ribā and retain the principal. The creditor is not required to surrender the amount genuinely lent, and the debtor is not required to pay an unlawful increase. Neither wronging nor being wronged—lā taẓlimūna wa-lā tuẓlamūn—is the governing balance. The creditor wrongs the debtor by demanding more than the principal under an exploitative debt. The debtor wrongs the creditor by refusing to repay the principal when able. Repentance creates justice for both parties rather than turning opposition to ribā into a licence for theft. The principal is the amount genuinely advanced or the lawful value owed, not hidden fees, compounding penalties, or invented charges. Some costs may compensate for actual administrative service, inflation, collateral, or loss, but the classification must be assessed carefully. (2:275 — **“Those who consume interest will stand ˹on Judgment Day˺ like those driven to madness by Satan’s touch.”**) Ibn ʿAbbās understands the warning of war as punishment for refusing to abandon usury, while repentance restores the lender’s principal without addition.

**REPENTANCE RESTORES THE BALANCE**

***“with Allah and His Messenger! But if you repent,”*** deepens repentance restores the balance. A creditor cannot relabel a guaranteed increase as a service charge merely to evade the prohibition. Because modern financial arrangements are complex, a person should show the full documents to a qualified scholar and a competent financial adviser. The Qur’anic rule is clear, but its application to mortgages, business finance, late penalties, credit cards, and currencies requires expertise. The severe warning is followed by an accessible path. A lender who stops collecting unlawful increase, returns identifiable gains, and restructures debts can repent. A borrower who was compelled by circumstances seeks relief and avoids new contracts where possible. Allah knows the difference between deliberate exploitation and a person trapped in a system while trying to escape. Repentance must not be used to collapse a vulnerable family or to abandon every lawful obligation. Mediation, refinancing through permissible means, charitable support, and gradual settlement may be necessary. The goal is to end the injustice while preserving life and rights. Ribā damages trust because it treats another person’s hardship as a guaranteed revenue stream. It can widen the distance between those who own capital and those who must borrow for necessities. The Qur’an’s language of war reflects not only the individual sin but the social conflict created by a system that enriches one party through another’s vulnerability. The remedy includes fair wages, cooperative finance, charitable loans, ethical investment, bankruptcy protections, and relief for people facing illness or unemployment. (83:1 — **“Woe to the defrauders”**)

**WHAT COUNTS AS THE PRINCIPAL**

***“you may retain your principal—neither inflicting nor suffering harm”*** clarifies what counts as the principal. A society cannot demand that individuals avoid exploitation while providing no humane alternatives. The double negation is a concise principle of economic justice. No creditor may take more than his right, and no debtor may consume another’s property without repayment. It also applies to contracts, inheritance, employment, and public finance. Justice is not achieved by reversing who is exploited. The lender should document the debt, give reasonable time, and refrain from harassment. The debtor should communicate honestly, pay according to ability, and not use the prohibition of ribā to justify deliberate default. Both should remember Allah’s final account. Authorities have a duty to regulate predatory lending, protect consumers, disclose costs, and provide fair avenues for dispute. Religious leaders should not issue simplistic rulings without understanding financial reality, and financial professionals should not hide exploitation behind technical complexity. The verse calls the entire society to reform. The warning is addressed to the people who persist in ribā, but the remedy requires institutions that make justice possible. The seller wants the old figure, the buyer has only the new one, and the whole street waits to see whether the two men will settle or go to the judge.
