**THE PASSED COMMUNITY**

The verse turns from the deathbed to the ledger. ***“That was a community”*** ***“that had already gone before.”***. The sentence is a verdict and a boundary at once. The community named in the previous scene is finished. The verse closes its file before opening the reader's. In the span marked by Ibn ʿAbbās the verse covers the generations that came and went. Each with its own hour. Each with its own account. The past tense does the theology. Had already gone means their making time is over. The only open accounts are the ones still being written. So the verse teaches the reader how to hold history. With respect and without transfer. The reason the verse opens with the past community is that the reader's temptation is old. To live inside someone else's record. To claim the fathers' faith as a deposit or pay the fathers' debts as a penalty. The verse separates the files with one word. The separation is a mercy.

**TWO LEDGERS, NOT ONE**

The verse states the arithmetic plainly. ***“For them is what they earned”*** ***“and for you is what you have earned.”***. The parallel states the same earning as the whole horizon of the person (53:39 — **“and that each person will only have what they endeavoured towards”**). The reading of Ibn ʿAbbās is the closing of the collective ledger. The deeds do not migrate. The earning stays where it was made. The effect is a doubled accounting. Two ledgers. Opened at the same time. Closed at the same time. Never merged. The difference between a family name and a personal account is exactly what this verse draws. The name travels down the line. The deeds do not. The prayer of the closing passage of the Book puts the same theology in its merciful form (2:286 — **“and all evil will be to its own loss. ˹The believers pray,˺ “Our Lord! Do not punish us”**). The evil made is charged to its own maker. The charge is the same rule that keeps the good with its own earner. What follows is that the ledger's logic is symmetrical. Neither guilt nor righteousness crosses the line.

**THE DEEDS THAT DO NOT TRANSFER**

The verse closes the boundary from the other side. ***“And you will not be accountable”*** ***“for what they have done”***. The reader is freed from the past's file. The reader is confined to the present's. From Ibn ʿAbbās the family's own rule of accounts stands in the open. The fathers answer for the fathers. The children for the children. The verse protects in both directions. No one inherits another's guilt. No one spends another's righteousness. For the modern reader the two protections are equally needed. This age is fluent in inherited blame and inherited credit. The verse cancels both currencies. The effect of the boundary is that the reader stands alone before the earning. Exactly as the Day's verse promised. No help from soul to soul. What follows is that the only responsible posture toward a passed community is the one the next verse will name. Follow the faith that was right. Do not claim the faith of the dead as a substitute for the work of the living. The accountability clause closes the verse because the clause is the reader's own horizon. The deeds do not transfer. The making is yours. The boundary holds in both directions and both directions are mercy. The dead are not charged with the living's file. The living are not charged with the dead's. The two rules together make history safe to study. The studying then stops being prosecution. A passed community becomes a lesson rather than a verdict. The earning stays with the earner. The lesson stays with the learner. The verse frees the reader from the ancestor's account and confines the reader to the present one. The confinement is the freedom. One open column is lighter than a family archive. The modern reader meets the same boundary in every old argument. The argument wants to move the deeds across the line. The verse refuses. The refusal is the mercy named twice. Their earning. Your earning. The two ledgers close at the same hour. The hour does not accept transfers. The making is the only entry that counts.
