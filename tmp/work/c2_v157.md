**THE BLESSING AND THE MERCY**

The promised account now pays out in named currencies. ***“They are the ones”*** ***“who will receive Allah’s blessings”*** ***“and mercy. And it is they”***. Two payments. Blessings and mercy. Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss itemises the pair across the two horizons. The blessing is the forgiveness in this world. The mercy is the protection from torment in the world to come. The account of the patient therefore pays in both currencies at once. The current world clears the record. The coming world clears the fear. The point is that the promise of the disaster-verse is shown to be solvent. The promise has a treasury. The treasury pays in the exact shapes the testing threatened. What follows is that the good news announced before the famine is now receipted after the creed. The effect of the itemisation is that no horizon is left unpaid. The one who sends the touches sends the payments.

**GUIDED ON THE STRUCK ROAD**

The closing clause names the third payment. ***“who are ˹rightly˺ guided”***. The guidance is given to the struck ones as equipment. The reading of the early authorities ties the guidance here to the sentence the struck ones keep saying. The return-creed is the compass, and the compass is the guidance made portable. The opening supplication of the book states the same guidance as the family's daily request (1:5 — **“You ˹alone˺ we worship and You ˹alone˺ we ask for help”**). Worship and asking. The guided life has this shape as its grammar. The struck ones keep the shape under pressure. That keeping is the guidance in action. The difference between guidance as information and guidance as posture is the quiet revolution of the clause. The information arrives once. The posture is held daily. For the modern reader the third payment is the one the hard season needs most. The forgiveness is welcome. The safety is welcome. The guidance is what makes the next disaster survivable.

**THE PAYMENT WITHOUT A CEILING**

The account closes on a promise of scale. The spaciousness of the earth is placed beside the spaciousness of the reward (39:10 — **“And Allah’s earth is spacious. Only those who endure patiently will be given their reward without limit.””**). Without limit. The payment carries no ceiling. The point of the pairing is generosity measured against geography. The earth itself is the unit of the reward's vocabulary. Ibn ʿAbbās's reading keeps the guidance as the first instalment of the whole deposit. The guided one walks with the compass in hand, and the compass arrives early. What follows is that the group of the creed-verse stands fully equipped. They belong. They return. They are blessed, kept, and guided. This age measures its recoveries in quarters and calls a good year a triumph. The verse measures the recovery in horizons. Both horizons are solvent. The payment without a ceiling is the reason the good news could be printed before the test was delivered. The treasury does not run on the schedule of the drought.

**RECEIPTS FOR TWO HORIZONS**

The double payment is the answer to the oldest complaint about the patient life. The complaint says that the patient are paid late. The verse answers that the patient are paid twice and in both currencies. The forgiveness clears the books in the current world. The mercy clears the fear in the coming one. Neither payment is deferred to the other's horizon, and neither horizon is treated as a rounding error in the account. The effect of the itemisation is that no horizon is treated as an afterthought. The reason the guidance is named as the third payment is that the second disaster is already scheduled in a world of tests. The guidance is the equipment for the next season, and the equipment arrives early. The compass is handed over before the storm is scheduled, and the hand that schedules the storm is the hand that issues the compass. For the modern reader the receipting is the missing half of every hard season. This age counts only the losses of the current quarter and calls the rest theology. The verse counts both horizons and issues receipts for each. The blessing is posted. The mercy is posted. The compass is in the hand. The account of the patient carries no ceiling and no shortage.
