**THE TEST THAT MADE THE MODEL**

The verse gives the account a face. ***“˹Remember˺ when Abraham was tested by his Lord with ˹certain˺ commandments”*** ***“which he fulfilled.”*** In the counting of Ibn ʿAbbās the commandments are ten. Everything the Prophet ﷺ suffered in his message, Abraham suffered before him. The verse does not say Abraham was honoured and then tested. It says the honour was built out of the tests kept. So the model here is not a natural greatness that happened to obey. It is an obedience graded in real events and passed. The reason the tester is named as his Lord matters. The tests came from the same hand that would give the title. The title is therefore a response to the keeping. Each commandment is a moment where the fulfilling could have failed. None did. The effect is a life that reads like a series of kept decisions. The tradition around the House carries the same instruction. The people are told to take Abraham as their pattern. His example is what the community recites and follows. The parallel portrait states the pattern plainly (16:120 — **“Indeed, Abraham was a model of excellence: devoted to Allah, ˹perfectly˺ upright—not a polytheist—”**).

**TEN COMMANDS IN TWO GROUPS**

The ten are not listed here. The tradition sorts them into two groups of five. The sorting is its own teaching. The transmission from Ibn ʿAbbās gives the shape of the first five. The test of hunger. The test of the desert and what the traveller carried. The test of the two journeys of the infant. The test of the two sacrifices. The test of the fire. The second five fall on the side of the face and the body. The shaving of the head. The letters of the revealed chapter of Abraham. The touch. The heart. The smile. The two groups cover the whole of a person. Provisions and routes. Family and sacrifice. Head and heart and face. The difference between a biography and a model is exactly the counting. The verse counts the commandments kept so the fulfilling can be seen decision by decision. The reason the tradition bothers to count is that the number never came from one glowing night. The keeping was a life.

**THE MODEL AND THE OFFSPRING**

The verse hands down the title the tests earned. ***“Allah said, “I will certainly make you into a role model”*** ***“for the people.”*** The father's question arrives at once: ***“Abraham asked, “What about my offspring?””*** The answer is one of the coldest and kindest lines in the passage: ***“Allah replied, “My covenant is not extended to the wrongdoers.””*** The leadership is not passed by blood. It is passed by the keeping. The covenant ties the offspring to fulfilled commandments. So the title is earned into a lineage rather than poured into one. In the reading of Ibn ʿAbbās the model reaches the prophets of this community and the followers who keep to the path the trials carved. What follows is that the model is approachable. The test sets are given. The fulfilments are given. The human incidents along the way are given. The choosing of Abraham becomes a choosing anyone can walk toward. For the modern reader this age admires outcomes and forgets tests. The verse corrects the order. The tests make the outcomes. The chain runs from the fire to the furthest believer. The reason the verse can promise a role model for the people is that the model was made of kept commandments first. The revealed instruction still names the same faith to follow (16:123 — **“Then We revealed to you ˹O Prophet, saying˺: “Follow the faith of Abraham, the upright, who was not one of the polytheists.””**). The tests were public and private in turn. Hunger in the desert. A son on the altar. A fire that turned to safety. The record does not flatter the model. It shows him being graded. The reason the tradition counts the tests is that counting makes the model honest. Ten kept decisions are not one lucky night. The covenant then narrows the family line to the keepers. Blood does not pass the title. Obedience does. The father heard the news about his son and smiled. The smile is one of the tests. It is the smallest one and the hardest. So the model walks toward the reader without a pedestal. He walks on kept ground. The reader today can count the tests in an ordinary week. The count is rarely ten. It is rarely zero. The difference between the two counts is the difference between the model and the crowd.
