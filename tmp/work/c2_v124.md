**ABRAHAM TESTED BY COMMANDS**

After the warning comes a figure whose story anchors much of what follows in the Book, and the sentence opens with a test: ***“˹Remember˺ when Abraham was tested by his Lord with ˹certain˺ commandments”***. The word for testing describes a proving rather than a temptation, and the Book uses it when a person's readiness is being shown by what he does. The commands are not enumerated in the sentence, which leaves the reader to meet them in the account that follows and in the sūrah's other references to this prophet. The placement of the verse is deliberate, since the address to the Children of Israel has just closed and the sūrah now names the ancestor whose religion it claims. What the reader is given here is the beginning of an argument about who inherits the way of Abraham.

**COMMANDS HE CARRIED OUT**

The test is concluded in three words, and the three words are the answer to it: ***“which he fulfilled.”***. The account of this prophet is consistently about performance rather than about position, and the verse states the fulfilment before it reports any promise. The divine speech that follows, with its promise of a role for the people, comes after the commands have been carried out, so that the order of the sentence matches the order of the story. In another passage the man is described as a model whose life matched his profession, and that is the standing verdict on him: (16:120 — **“Indeed, Abraham was a model of excellence”**). Someone given instructions who has looked for the reward before the work will find the sequence of the verse instructive.

**THE PROMISE OF A ROLE MODEL**

The sentence then reports what was said to him once the commands had been fulfilled: ***“Allah said, “I will certainly make you into a role model”*** and ***“for the people.”***. The term translated as a role model describes someone who is followed, and the Book's gift to Abraham is presented as an authority that others will accept rather than as a privilege he will enjoy. The promise is made in the first person, which places the appointment with the one who promises, and the same arrangement appears elsewhere in the Book. The same prophet is described in another passage as one whose way the believers are told to follow, which shows how the promise worked out in the community that came after him: (4:125 — **“follow the Way of Abraham, the upright”**). Leadership in the Book is granted to performance rather than to lineage, and the sentence says as much by the order of its clauses.

**AN OFFSPRING AND AN ANSWER**

The verse records the next exchange, which begins with a question from Abraham and a reply about a category of people: ***“Abraham asked, “What about my offspring?”*** and ***“Allah replied, “My covenant is not extended to the wrongdoers.”***. The question is natural for a man who had just been told that a people would follow him, and the answer directs the promise away from ancestry and toward conduct. The Book's answer does not deny the offspring anything; it denies the wrongdoers among them the covenant, which leaves the promise standing while removing the assumption behind the question. Another passage states that the prophet himself was neither of the two later communities that claimed him, which belongs to the same correction of inherited claims: (3:67 — **“Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian; he submitted in all uprightness”**). A reader who has ever rested a hope on a family connection will find the reply aimed very precisely at him.

**THE COVENANT AND THE WRONGDOERS**

That closing clause is the hinge of the sūrah's discussion of inheritance, since it states the condition on which the covenant runs. The Book uses the word for wrongdoers across the chapter for the people whose conduct and whose claims were examined, and the clause places them outside the promise rather than outside the family. The answer to Abraham's question therefore reframes the issue: what is inherited is not the bloodline but the covenant, and the covenant is not extended to those who work against its terms. Anyone who has watched a family inheritance become a source of quarrel over who qualifies will recognise the force of the correction, and the correction is made gently and in one sentence. The principle that will be applied to the communities discussed in the pages around it is what the sentence leaves behind.
