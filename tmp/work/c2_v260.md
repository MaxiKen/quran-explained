**IBRĀHĪM’S REQUEST FOR REASSURANCE**

***“And ˹remember˺ when Abraham said, “My Lord! Show me how you give life to the dead.” Allah responded, “Do you”*** establishes ibrāhīm’s request for reassurance. Ibrāhīm asks Allah to show him how He gives life to the dead. Allah asks, Do you not believe. Ibrāhīm immediately affirms his faith: Yes, but so that my heart may be reassured. His request is not disbelief or denial. It is a desire for ṭumaʾnīnah, a deeper settled certainty in the heart. This distinction is important. A believer may ask how a matter occurs without doubting that Allah can do it. Faith has degrees of certainty, and the heart can seek greater tranquillity through reflection and signs. The Prophet ﷺ taught that sincere questions are not blameworthy when they seek understanding rather than escape from obedience. Ibrāhīm’s answer shows that intellectual conviction and emotional reassurance can coexist. A person may know that Allah is powerful yet feel shaken by grief, death, or an unanswered question. Asking Allah for clarity can strengthen the heart. What is blameworthy is refusing to accept truth until every unseen reality becomes physically visible. The Qur’an presents Ibrāhīm as a model of both certainty and inquiry. He questioned his people, observed the signs of the heavens, and rejected false gods. His questions were steps toward tawḥīd, not excuses to remain inactive. Allah commands Ibrāhīm to take four birds, become familiar with them, place portions of them on different hills, and call them. The Qur’an’s details establish that the creatures were separated and that their return occurred by Allah’s command. Classical exegetes discuss the precise manner in which Ibrāhīm handled the birds and the meaning of the instruction to become accustomed to them. (2:258 — **“Are you ˹O Prophet˺ not aware of the one who argued with Abraham about his Lord because”**) A transmitted reading from Ibn ʿAbbās understands Abraham’s request as a desire for settled reassurance about how scattered bones are gathered, not doubt in Allah’s power.

**BELIEF AND THE NEED FOR REASSURANCE**

***“not believe?” Abraham replied, “Yes I do, but just so my heart can be reassured.” Allah said, “Then bring four”*** deepens belief and the need for reassurance. The lesson does not depend upon reconstructing every physical detail. The event is a miracle, not a recipe for human resurrection. It demonstrates that distance, separation, decay, and scattered remains do not prevent Allah from restoring life. Modern science may examine biological processes, but no experiment can reproduce the divine sign described here. The birds come swiftly when called. The one who created and scattered them knows each part and can bring them together. The speed of their return displays command over matter, identity, and life. The miracle answers the recurring human objection that a body cannot be restored after its components are dispersed. It also gives a personal sign to Ibrāhīm. He does not merely hear an abstract argument; he witnesses an event that settles his heart. Allah knows the particular form of reassurance each servant needs, though ordinary believers do not demand miracles on their own terms. The final names are al-ʿAzīz and al-Ḥakīm. Allah’s might makes resurrection possible, and His wisdom determines how signs are given and what they teach. Power without wisdom would be an incoherent concept, but Allah’s acts are both possible and purposeful. The sign is not given to satisfy entertainment or curiosity. It teaches resurrection, strengthens a prophet for his mission, and refutes the idea that death is beyond Allah’s control. A miracle should deepen worship rather than become a spectacle. The verse gives permission to seek reassurance in a respectful way.
