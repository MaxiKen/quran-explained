**A MONTH WITH A BOOK**

Read in its own phrases, the verse moves from the month to the gratitude: ***“Ramaḍân is the month in”***, ***“which the Quran was revealed”***, ***“as a guide for humanity with clear proofs of guidance”***, ***“and the standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺.”***, ***“So whoever is present this month, let them fast.”***, ***“But whoever is ill”***, ***“or on a journey”***, ***“then ˹let them fast˺ an equal number of days ˹after Ramaḍân˺. Allah intends ease”***, ***“for you, not hardship”***, ***“so that you may complete the prescribed period”***, ***“and proclaim the greatness of Allah”***, ***“for guiding you, and perhaps you will be grateful”***. The walk shows the month, the Book, the command, the exemptions, the ease, and the proclamation travelling in one line toward the gratitude. The calendar becomes worship in the walking, and the month's own phrases lead the way. The transmission of Ibn ʿAbbās on the revelation runs along the same track.

Ramaḍân is the month in which the Quran was revealed attaches the calendar to the event that reorganized it. The description continues — as a guide for humanity with clear proofs of guidance — so the Book's claim is public: guidance for humanity, and proofs anyone can examine. The revelation is dated to the month rather than to a hidden moment, which makes the fast an anniversary as well as a discipline. The transmission of Ibn ʿAbbās describes the descent in two movements: the entire Qur'an was brought down to the lowest heaven in the month, entrusted to the angelic scribes, and then revealed across the years that followed. The double movement explains how the month can hold the whole Book while the mission unfolds verse by verse. The occasion is therefore celebrated in the month of the beginning (97:1 — **“Indeed, ˹it is˺ We ˹Who˺ sent this ˹Quran˺ down on the Night of Glory”**).

**A STANDARD IN HAND**

The third description of the Book in the verse is its function as a standard — and the standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺. — and a standard is what the month puts in the believer's hand. During the days of the fast, the community reads more than it reads the rest of the year, and the reading is the measuring the standard performs. The verse's word-order places the proofs before the standard, so the distinguishing follows the evidence rather than the mood. Because the revelation is called a guide and a standard in one breath, the reader is invited to test his life against the text rather than the text against his habits. The prophetic practice of reviewing the whole revelation with the angel every Ramadan, preserved in al-Bukhārī, marks the month as the annual recalibration of the reader. The standard stays in the hand after the month ends.

**EASE WRITTEN INTO LAW**

The command to fast is immediately surrounded by exemptions, and the arrangement is deliberate: Allah intends ease for you, not hardship. The ease is stated as intention — the law wants the easy outcome — and the exemption for travel and illness completes the intention with procedure. The return to an equal number of days sends the reader back to the counting that governs the previous verse, so the law keeps its books even while it makes allowances. Because the ease is declared before the reader can complain of the difficulty, the complaint is answered in advance. The mercy here is structural: the same verse that commands the fast explains how the fast is released. The legislator who announces his intention as mercy teaches the subjects to read every rule through that intention.

**COMPLETED, THEN CELEBRATED**

The close of the verse moves from the calendar to the conscience: so that you may complete the prescribed period and proclaim the greatness of Allah for guiding you. The completion is paired with proclamation, so the end of the month is spoken aloud rather than merely reached. The guidance being proclaimed is the same guidance the verse opened with — the Book revealed in the month — and the gratitude is addressed to the One who sent it. The application is to mark the last day with the verse's own act: to proclaim, and to remember what the month was for. Because the proclamation is "for guiding you," the believer's gratitude is specific — it thanks for direction, not only for food returned to the table. The month ends as it began, attached to the Book.

**COMPLETED IN HOPE**

One more note on the closing words — and perhaps you will be grateful — since the "perhaps" is addressed to free creatures and not to machines. The gratitude the month trains is the gratitude that outlasts the hunger, and the test of the training arrives in the weeks after the fast. The prayer taught at the close of the pilgrimage season describes the same test in another register, where a half-request leaves its speaker with no share of the next life. The believer who completes the month has been given the equipment of gratitude: the Book in the hand, the discipline in the body, and the proclamation on the tongue. Because the hope is left open, the month is an invitation renewed every year rather than a verdict delivered once. The annual return of the month is itself a mercy of the design: the standard is handed back to the reader every year, freshly printed by the calendar. The one who failed last Ramadan is standing at a new door, and the door has the Book's name on it. The annual cycle is the mercy of repetition, and the repetition is the month's quiet theology of return.
