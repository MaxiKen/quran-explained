**RAMADAN AND THE REVELATION**

***“Ramaḍân is the month in”***, ***“which the Quran was revealed”***, ***“as a guide for humanity with clear proofs of guidance”***, ***“and the standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺.”*** mark the turn toward ramadan and the revelation. Ramadan is honoured because it is the month in which the Qur’an was revealed. The revelation is described as guidance, with clear proofs that illuminate the path and a standard that distinguishes truth from falsehood. The month is therefore not merely a season of hunger, family gatherings, or cultural celebration. Its centre is the Book that teaches humanity how to know Allah and live rightly. The Qur’an was sent down in Ramadan, and the first revelation came in the Night of Decree. The revelation’s descent and the believer’s fasting belong together: the body is restrained, the heart is opened, and the community returns to the message. Ramadan should increase recitation, reflection, charity, prayer, and moral repair rather than only changing meal times. Whoever witnesses the month must fast it. A person who is present and able when Ramadan begins undertakes the fast from dawn to sunset. The beginning and end of the month are established through the recognized methods of sighting and reliable calendrical calculation discussed by scholars. The practical details should be handled by trustworthy communal authorities so that worship does not become a source of constant suspicion and division. The obligation concerns the days of the month, while the person’s capacity remains relevant. Illness and travel are explicitly repeated from the preceding verse. A sick person delays the fast when fasting causes serious harm or prevents recovery; a traveller may take the concession according to the legal conditions. Both the fast and the lawful postponement are acts of obedience. (2:183 — **“so perhaps you will become mindful ˹of Allah˺”**) Because ramadan and the revelation governs 2:185, allah intends ease arises from ramadan and the revelation. It follows that glorifying allah for guidance completes ramadan and the revelation through allah intends ease.

**ALLAH INTENDS EASE**

The verse develops allah intends ease with ***“So whoever is present this month, let them fast.”***, ***“But whoever is ill”***, ***“or on a journey”***, ***“then ˹let them fast˺ an equal number of days ˹after Ramaḍân˺. Allah intends ease”***. Allah intends ease for you, not hardship—God intends ease rather than hardship. This is one of the great legal principles of Islam. Ease does not mean that every obligation should be redesigned around comfort, nor does it permit people to dismiss a clear duty because it is inconvenient. It means that Allah has built concessions into the law and does not desire unbearable or destructive hardship. The principle appears in the allowances for illness, travel, old age, pregnancy, breastfeeding, and other genuine conditions, with their detailed rulings. It also guides scholars when applying general commands to new circumstances. The believer does not make the religion needlessly severe, and he does not make ease an excuse for desire. The command to complete the prescribed period shows that concessions are not abandonment. A person who misses days because of illness or travel makes up an equal number later when able. The period is completed according to the person’s circumstances, and Allah knows the sincerity of the servant who longs to fulfil the duty. Completion also includes completing Ramadan’s ethical work. A person who fasts physically but persists in lies, oppression, broken promises, and contempt has missed the purpose of the month. The Prophet ﷺ warned in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī that hunger alone is not the goal when the tongue and conduct remain corrupt. The month is completed through repentance and a change that continues after Eid. (97:1 — **“Indeed, ˹it is˺ We ˹Who˺ sent this ˹Quran˺ down on the Night of Glory”**) The reason glorifying allah for guidance follows allah intends ease lies in ramadan and the revelation. Therefore, glorifying allah for guidance returns practical force to allah intends ease.

**GLORIFYING ALLAH FOR GUIDANCE**

At glorifying allah for guidance, attention falls on ***“for you, not hardship”***, ***“so that you may complete the prescribed period”***, ***“and proclaim the greatness of Allah”***, ***“for guiding you, and perhaps you will be grateful”***. At the end of the fasting regulations, Allah commands believers to glorify Him—to magnify God—for guiding them. The magnifying God of Eid expresses that Allah is greater than appetite, wealth, fear, and every rival. It is a public declaration that the month’s success came from Allah and that the community’s joy is an act of worship. Glorification is not limited to words. To magnify Allah is to give His command greater weight than social pressure and personal desire. A person who has learned patience, generosity, and Qur’anic discipline in Ramadan carries that magnification into the rest of the year. The verse ends with gratitude—*laʿallakum tashkurūn*. The believer thanks Allah for the Qur’an, for the ability to fast, for the mercy of the concessions, for family and community, and for the opportunity to repent. Gratitude is expressed in worship and in service to people who lack food, safety, or support. Ramadan’s charity is not a substitute for justice throughout the year. Its spiritual generosity should inspire ongoing care for the poor, honest economic practice, and concern for the oppressed. The one who thanks Allah for guidance helps others find relief and clarity. Ramadan is like a lamp carried into a dark room before anyone is asked to walk through it. The Qur'an gives the light by which the month's discipline, direction, and gratitude can be understood; fasting is not an isolated hardship looking for meaning afterward. Present-day schedules may reduce the month to food timings and social customs, but the verse restores its centre. The calendar marks revelation, the fast answers guidance, and the completing of the days ends in magnifying the One who guided. (44:3 — **“Indeed, We sent it down on a blessed night”**) In other words, glorifying allah for guidance answers ramadan and the revelation.
