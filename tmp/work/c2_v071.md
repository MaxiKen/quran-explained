**THE LAST DESCRIPTION AND THE RELUCTANT KNIFE**

The final answer closes the specification with a working life rather than a colour: ***“He replied, “Allah says, ‘It should have been used neither to till the soil”***, ***“nor water the fields”***, ***“wholesome and without blemish.’” They said, “Now you have come with the truth.””***, ***“Yet they still slaughtered it hesitantly”***. An animal that has never been yoked is one whose whole life has been kept for something else, and an animal without a mark on it is one that nobody could have confused with another. Every question has now been answered, the search has a single answer, and the people acknowledge it in words that sound like belief. The final sentence then reports what the words were worth, and it is the Book's most exact description of the gap between agreement and obedience. They had run out of questions and still went to the animal without conviction. Ibn ʿAbbās understood the final marks to exclude an animal broken by field labour or carrying water and to require one free of blemish and coloured spots.

**A LIFE THAT HAD BEEN KEPT FOR THIS**

The description of the cow as one that never pulled a plough or carried water is worth reading twice, because it is the only specification that has nothing to do with the animal's body. A beast of that kind had been spared the ordinary use of a farm animal for its whole life, and whatever reasons the owners had for sparing it, the effect was that an unspent life was available on the day the command arrived. The reports say the animal was finally found with a young man who named a price for it, and that the community paid what he asked. A reader who wants a comparison can think of the tools and the hours that were kept aside in his own house for a purpose he never quite began, since the animal in this verse is the reverse of that picture: something preserved without knowing why, and used at last for the reason it was preserved.

**THE TRUTH ACKNOWLEDGED AND THE HAND HELD BACK**

The words they speak are a real admission, and the Book does not treat them as empty; the truth had come and they said so. What follows the admission is the point of the verse, because hesitation is a decision that has not been made and a knife that has not fallen, and the animal's blood was shed in the end by people who had argued their way to the edge of obedience. The same hesitation is described in the Book where the people are reported to have said that they heard and then disobeyed, and where the delay in another command became a test of its own kind: (2:93 — **““Hold firmly to that ˹Scripture˺ which We have given you and obey,” they answered,”**). The verse therefore completes the exchange with an accurate verdict: they did the thing, and they did it without the willingness that would have made it worship.

**EXCHANGE NARROWED TO ONE ANIMAL**

Taken from the first command to the last reluctant cut, the passage is one arc, and the arc ends with a duty performed and a lesson left over. The lesson is not about cattle; it is about what happens when a person receives an instruction and treats it as a subject for discussion. The people of this story were not asked to do something impossible, and the animal they finally found was not rare until they had made it rare. When they had exhausted their questions they did what they had been told at the beginning, which means the delay cost them time, money, and the willingness that the duty had been designed to test. A reader who wants to apply this needs nothing more than the last sentence, because it names the moment at which he can stop asking and start acting.

**A SLOW HAND AND A CLOSED FILE**

Broadcasters and editors know the kind of person who will agree to a fact and then decline to broadcast it, and the Book's portrait here is the same figure in a religious key. The reader can check the pattern in his own life by naming one duty he has already accepted with his mind and has not yet done with his hands. The Book treats that gap as the real subject of the exchange, and it attaches a single word to the people involved that a reader can carry away: they were near to not doing it. The mercy in the passage is that the command was carried out at last, so the story is not a warning about lost causes; it is a description of the ordinary way human beings arrive at obedience, late and unenthusiastic. The verse closes the file on the cow, and the next page turns to a killing that the community had been hiding. Today, a person may finally possess every specification and still hesitate because information was never the true obstacle.
