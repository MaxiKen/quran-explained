**THE SUPPLICATION OF A SMALL ARMY**

***“When they advanced to face Goliath and his warriors, they”*** establishes the supplication of a small army. After the river test reduced the army and fear arose at the sight of Jālūt’s force, the believers turned to Allah. Their prayer contains three requests: an abundance of patience, firmness of feet, and help against the hostile force. They do not claim that their own courage is sufficient. They ask for the inner and outer means of steadfastness. Pour upon us patience—afrigh ʿalaynā ṣabran—is an image of patience filling and covering them. They know that they need more than a brief burst of courage. Fear, fatigue, grief, and uncertainty can return repeatedly. The servant asks Allah to make patience abundant enough for the full trial. Make our feet firm refers literally to standing in battle and metaphorically to remaining firm in faith, discipline, and moral conduct. A person may have physical courage but lose his principles under pressure. The believers ask for both kinds of stability. Firmness does not mean refusing to retreat when retreat is strategically necessary or remaining in a position that guarantees pointless destruction. It means not abandoning the lawful objective through panic, betrayal, or uncontrolled flight. (90:17 — **“and—above all—to be one of those who have faith and urge each other to perseverance and urge”**) Because the supplication of a small army frames firm feet, firm feet returns attention to the supplication of a small army. An account attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās takes the prayer as a request for patience, firmness in the field, and victory over Goliath’s forces.

**FIRM FEET**

***“prayed, “Our Lord! Shower us with perseverance, make our”*** deepens firm feet. Sound strategy can include movement, regrouping, negotiation, or withdrawal when these serve protection and justice. Help us against the disbelieving people does not turn the enemy into a dehumanized category. The prayer concerns the hostile army that is actively confronting them. The Qur’an commands justice even toward opponents and forbids transgression. Victory is requested so that aggression may end and the community may worship safely, not so that hatred can be satisfied. The believers understand that causes matter but are not independent. Training, numbers, leadership, weapons, and planning are means; Allah gives them effect. Reliance upon Him does not excuse neglecting preparation, and preparation does not make Allah unnecessary. The order of the scene is instructive. The believers do not pray as a substitute for action; they pray before and during responsible action. Supplication aligns the heart with Allah, purifies intention, and protects against arrogance. A person who asks Allah for help should also ask whether his cause, means, and conduct are just. The Prophet ﷺ made supplication in battle, consulted his Companions, arranged the ranks, and took practical precautions. His example rejects both magical thinking and materialist self-reliance. (2:249 — **“When Saul marched forth with his army, he cautioned: “Allah will test you with a river.”**) Since help belongs to allah follows the supplication of a small army, firm feet links purpose to help belongs to allah.

**HELP BELONGS TO ALLAH**

***“steps firm, and give us victory over the disbelieving people.””*** clarifies help belongs to allah. The prayer is in the plural. A community under pressure needs shared patience. Individuals support one another by speaking truthfully, sharing resources, caring for the wounded, and avoiding rumours that spread panic. Leaders should not demand patience while hiding information or wasting lives through vanity. The strongest person may need support, and the apparently weak person may give the word that steadies the group. The Qur’an praises believers who encourage one another to patience and mercy. The prayer applies beyond armed conflict. A student facing examinations, a family enduring illness, a community resisting corruption, and a person leaving addiction can ask Allah for patience, firm feet, and help against the forces that pull him away from obedience. The enemy may include fear, desire, injustice, or Satan’s whispering, though the believer must not turn ordinary people into symbolic enemies without reason. The supplication also teaches the correct order of priorities: seek patience before asking for victory. Without patience, success can be corrupted by pride; with patience, even a difficult result can become a means of reward. Think of the supplication of a small army through a farmer who asks the sky for rain in the morning and spends the afternoon digging the channel through which it will run. (3:123 — **“Indeed, Allah made you victorious at Badr when you were ˹vastly˺ outnumbered. So be mindful of Allah,”**) Prayer before action matters, which means help belongs to allah returns prayer before action to the supplication of a small army.

**PRAYER BEFORE ACTION**

The rain comes, the channel carries it to the roots, and the field that is watered is the one with the trench. The supplication of a small army reaches a reader today who stands before a hard task and asks only for the result. The verse gives him three requests in the order of a battle, the heart and the feet before the crown, and the supplication of a small army uses firm feet to ask which choice he has been praying for. Because the men who prayed were the ones who had kept their hands from the river, the supplication of a small army teaches that a prayer of this kind is made by a person who has already obeyed. The next verse preserves the prayer’s answer. And the men are remembered with the steadfast in the same story. Firm feet makes the supplication of a small army practical; help belongs to allah therefore matters.
