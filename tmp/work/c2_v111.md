**THE EXCLUSIVE-ENTRY CLAIM**

The passage now turns from the campaign to the claim that fed it: ***“The Jews and Christians each claim that none will enter Paradise except those of their own faith.”***. The tradition behind the claim gives it its venue and its two halves. The Jews of Medina and the Christians of Najrān disputed with the messenger, each party claiming Paradise for its own membership exclusively. Ibn ʿAbbās hears the claim in the words of its makers. None enter unless he dies as a Jew, said one party, and the Christians likewise claimed their own. The structure of the claim is the same in both mouths. Membership is the ticket. The verse then labels the claim in its own vocabulary: ***“These are their desires.”***. The same gloss notes what the labeling costs the claim. They are wishing from Allah even though it is not in their books. The wish is therefore not even loyal to the shelves it stands on.

**THE PROOF CHALLENGE**

The reply is the shortest legal move in the passage: ***“ Reply, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Show ˹me˺ your proof if what you say is true.””***. Ibn ʿAbbās gives the challenge its jurisdiction. Bring your proofs from your own respective books. The demand is fair in the highest degree. The claim is about an entry rule. The books are where the entry rules were said to be written. The received account of the wider failure explains why the challenge lands where it does. The parties had identified religion with affiliation, with a racial and social membership, instead of with the authentic doctrines and the deeds the revelations actually prescribe. The universal rule of the entry has already been stated in this surah's own table: (2:62 — **“Indeed, the believers, Jews, Christians, and Sabians—whoever ˹truly˺ believes in Allah and the Last Day and”**), and its completion governs the claim at hand: (2:62 — **“does good will have their reward with their Lord. And there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve”**). The entry rule is belief and deed. The membership card belongs to the office of the unproven.

**THE BURDEN NOBODY MET**

Notice the procedural effect of the challenge. The claim was made without its exhibit. The verse records the claim and the demand for the exhibit in adjacent breaths. A promise and a promissory note are separated by the signature. The promise is found in the books and read in the light of the messengers. The note is printed at the counter of the affiliation. The next verse of the passage then writes the rule the parties were asked to exhibit, the statement of the submitted face and its reward. The reason the exhibit-order is the perfect reply is that the claim was printed on the book's own letterhead, which means the proof belongs to the shelf the claim stands on. The exhibit therefore exists. It simply sits in the wrong name on the shelf of each exclusive claim.

**THE CLAIM IN MODERN DRESS**

For the reader of this age the scene is older than the sects and as current as the morning. The exclusive-entry claim survives wherever a community has confused its address with its deeds. The verse's reply is the same in every century. Produce the proof. The proof from the book. The doctrine and the deed are the entry rule, and the book that names them was never read for the exclusivity clause, because the clause is not printed there. The verse leaves the challenge standing where it can be heard at every desk where the question is asked again. The claim is cheap. The exhibit is dear. The passage bills accordingly. The claimant who brings the exhibit finds the rule waiting in the book's own words. The claimant who cannot bring it has printed the sign at his own counter. The passage simply reads the sign back to its author and keeps the challenge on the table until somebody brings the page. The claimant who returns with the page finds that the page names the belief and the deed. The claimant who returns with a membership card finds that the card is not a page of the book. Either way the challenge has done its work. The exclusive-entry claim cannot survive the exhibit, and the verse records the claim and the exhibit-order in the same breath so that the failure is visible in the margin of the text itself.
