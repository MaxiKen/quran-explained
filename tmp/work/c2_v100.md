**COVENANTS THAT KEEP COMING APART**

The chapter returns to the community's record and states it as a question: ***“Why is it that every time they make a covenant,”***, ***“a group of them casts it aside? In fact,”***, ***“most of them do not believe”***. The pattern is described as repeated rather than exceptional, and the participle used for the people who discard the agreement shows that the discarding had become a habit rather than an incident. The Book does not ask why one covenant failed; it asks why every covenant fails, which moves the discussion from a single event to a disposition. The final clause gives the reason, and the reason is not forgetfulness or confusion. A community that does not believe in the thing it is promising to do will break the promise as a matter of course, and the Book states this before the reader has a chance to look for a softer explanation. The same conduct is described in a later sūrah for a generation that inherited the scripture and treated its terms as negotiable: (7:169 — **“Then they were succeeded by other generations”**).

**PROMISES MADE AND PROMISES REPEATEDLY SET ASIDE**

The verse is careful about which pledges are in view, and the distinction is worth holding on to. Some agreements in this history the community denied making at all, and others were acknowledged even as they were being set aside, and the Book addresses both in the same sentence. The covenant to make the scripture known, and not to hide it, is named in another passage with the same verdict attached, so the reader can see the charge repeated where the obligation is spelled out: (3:187 — **“˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when Allah took the covenant of those”**). What the verse establishes is that the record of this people cannot be explained by a single failure under pressure, since the pattern outlasts the pressures that produced it. A community that keeps leaving its agreements has said something about what it takes them to be.

**WHY ONLY A GROUP IS NAMED**

The sentence names a group rather than the whole people, and the precision is significant for a reader who is tempted to turn the passage into a verdict on a nation. The Book's accusations in this sūrah are consistently measured in this way, and the same care appears in the verse that follows, which speaks of some of those who were given the scripture. The qualification matters: what is being described is a tendency inside a community rather than the identity of every member. A group that discards its agreements is enough to produce the reputation, and the account of how this people treated its prophets has already shown how a position held by a few becomes a public one. The reader's use of the passage is to examine whether he belongs to the group being described.

**A TENANT WHO SIGNS WITHOUT READING**

Think of a tenant who signs a new lease every year and treats the terms as a formality that has never been enforced, and who is surprised when the landlord finally acts on a clause. Agreements had been made here and set aside repeatedly, and the question suggests that the repetition had become invisible to the people doing it. The closing clause explains why: an agreement is only as strong as the belief behind it, and where belief is thin the paper is treated as decoration. A reader can run the same test on the promises he makes in the ordinary course of his life, since the Book's interest in this passage is in what a person's repeated conduct says about the value he places on his word. The verse asks the question and leaves the reader to find his own case in it.

**THE HABIT AND WHAT IT REVEALS**

The last clause of the verse supplies the diagnosis, and it is stated as a fact about most of the community rather than as a suspicion. Unbelief is treated here as the soil in which covenant-breaking grows, and the people's own account of their hearts has already appeared earlier in the sūrah. The verdict falls on people who accepted something and then treated their acceptance as negotiable, not on those who never accepted anything at all. The Book's language about belief is consistently about what a person's conduct shows, which is why a broken agreement counts as evidence in this passage. The next verse names the specific occasion that had provoked the question, and it shows what the community did when the messenger it had been promised finally arrived.
