**PROPOSALS DURING A WAITING PERIOD**

***“There is no blame on you for subtly showing interest in ˹divorced or widowed˺ women or for hiding ˹the intention˺ in”*** establishes proposals during a waiting period. This verse addresses approaching a widow or divorced woman during her waiting period. It permits an indirect hint—taʿrīḍ—or keeping the intention private, especially because a direct proposal or contract may violate her right to complete the ʿiddah. A respectful hint might be a general statement about wishing for marriage or praising a person’s character without demanding a commitment. The permission recognizes human emotion. A person may think about marriage with a widow, and the widow may consider her future, without being blamed for the mere intention. Allah does not punish involuntary thoughts. He regulates what people say and do so that a vulnerable woman is not pressured or publicly claimed before her waiting period ends. Do not make a secret promise with them, except that you speak in an honourable manner. This allows appropriate, dignified speech while prohibiting a hidden engagement that treats the waiting period as though it has already ended. A secret promise can create jealousy, pressure, false hope, and conflict with the deceased husband’s family or a former spouse. Honourable speech—qawl maʿrūf—is clear enough to avoid deception and restrained enough to avoid emotional manipulation. A person should not exploit grief by promising wealth, protection, or rescue. **HONOURABLE SPEECH**

***“your hearts. Allah knows that you are considering them ˹for marriage˺. But do not make a secret commitment with them—you can”*** deepens honourable speech. He should not ask for secrecy that isolates the woman from trusted family or legal support. A marriage contract may not be concluded until the ʿiddah is complete. The waiting period belongs to the woman and protects her legal status. A contract made before it ends is invalid or unlawful according to the details of the case and legal school. If a person is unsure whether a period has ended, he should wait and seek qualified advice. This rule also prevents confusion about lineage and preserves the seriousness of marriage. A new marriage should begin after the previous relationship’s legal consequences have been completed, not in the middle of grief, pregnancy uncertainty, or revocable divorce. The verse does not make the woman passive. She may consider a proposal, refuse it, seek advice, and plan her future. The restriction is on the manner and timing of approach, not on her ability to think. Relatives must not use the waiting period to claim her inheritance, promise her to a cousin, or prevent a lawful future marriage. The same principle applies to a divorced woman whose waiting period remains. If the divorce is revocable, the former husband’s rights and the possibility of return must be respected, while other men must not compete through secret promises. (65:1 — **“O Prophet! ˹Instruct the believers:˺ When you ˹intend to˺ divorce women,”**) Since no marriage contract before the period ends follows proposals during a waiting period, honourable speech links purpose to no marriage contract before the period ends.

**NO MARRIAGE CONTRACT BEFORE THE PERIOD ENDS**

***“only show interest in them appropriately. Do not commit to the bond of marriage until the waiting period expires. Know that”*** clarifies no marriage contract before the period ends. If the divorce is final, indirect discussion may still be governed by the rules of the specific waiting period. Allah knows what people conceal within themselves. This is a comfort because sincere intentions are visible to Him, and a warning because hidden manipulation is also visible. A person may speak in carefully chosen words while planning to exploit a woman’s grief or property. Allah knows the difference between compassion and predation. The verse does not treat every private thought as a sin. The warning concerns the transition from thought to secret promises, pressure, and premature contract. Faith teaches people to manage desire with honesty rather than pretending that human emotions do not exist. Allah is Ghafūr and Ḥalīm. He forgives mistakes and is forbearing with servants who are learning how to navigate grief, divorce, and desire. A person who crossed a boundary should stop, clarify matters, and seek forgiveness. Forbearance gives time to correct before harm becomes permanent. Mercy is shown through clear rules. A woman is protected from being claimed while vulnerable, a man is protected from an unlawful contract, and families are protected from disputes about timing and lineage. Imagine a patient suitor as a man waiting outside a shop while it is being rebuilt and tells the craftsmen what he intends to buy the day it opens. **RESPECT FOR THE WIDOW’S AGENCY**

***“Allah is aware of what is in your hearts, so beware of Him. And know that Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing”*** closes with respect for the widow’s agency. The shop opens on its own day, the man is at the door with his money, and the only thing the waiting cost him was the price he did not pay for a broken window. Proposals during a waiting period reaches a reader today who sends a friend to sound out a widow's family while the months still run, and who thinks that a message carried by another mouth is no message. The verse gives him a hint, an appointment and a term, and proposals during a waiting period uses honourable speech to ask which choice he is spending. Because the close names the Forgiving and the Forbearing, honourable speech makes proposals during a waiting period show that the waiting he thinks is waste is the season in which his own intention is grown. The waiting of the widow is fixed in the preceding verse. And the terms of the settlement are guarded earlier in the chapter. (2:232 — **“When you have divorced women and they have reached the end of their waiting period,”**) Honourable speech makes proposals during a waiting period practical; no marriage contract before the period ends therefore matters.
