**THE GIVER OF LIFE ALSO PREPARED A WORLD FOR IT**

The argument moves from the life of each person to the setting that sustains all people: ***“He is the One”*** ***“Who created everything in the earth”***. The One who brought a human being from lifelessness also made the soil, water, plants, animals, minerals, and countless links on which earthly life rests. This widens the proof in verse 28. A person did not create himself, and humanity as a whole did not create the field in which its work becomes possible. Farmers act, builders shape, doctors treat, and makers combine materials. None gives matter its first being or writes the laws by which it can be used. Ibn ʿAbbās points to beasts, plants, and other things as blessings made ready for benefit. The reference to the whole keeps gratitude from shrinking to a few obvious goods. A bitter plant may hold medicine, a hard rock may support a home, and a creature not eaten by people may serve an order that preserves their food. Some uses are known at once; others are found after long study. Therefore, the earth is both provision and sign. Another verse speaks of what God made on it in many colours and closes, (16:13 — **“for those who are mindful”**). Matter is not mute when its source, order, and service are read together.

**FOR YOU MEANS GIFT, USE, AND ACCOUNTABLE CARE**

The next words begin ***“for you. Then He turned towards the heaven, forming it into seven heavens.”*** Before the gaze rises, “for you” gives human beings permission to benefit from what is in the earth. Early legal reflection drew from this gift a broad starting rule of permitted and pure use, while excluding what God forbids and what causes harm. The phrase does not mean that every desire makes a use lawful. A gift remains under the will of its Giver. Nor does human benefit mean that no other creature has a claim within the earthly order. God also says, (55:10 — **“He laid out the earth for all beings”**). People may eat, build, travel, learn, and turn materials into useful goods, but they may not answer generosity with waste or ruin. The reason is that being served by creation makes gratitude more urgent, not ownership absolute. Elsewhere God says He subjected what lies in heaven and earth, then calls it (45:13 — **“by His grace. Surely in this are signs for people who reflect”**). Grace asks for thanks, and signs ask for thought. In daily life those two meet. Food should be received without pride, skill used without claiming self-creation, and natural wealth handled without forgetting those who come later. The earth is placed within human reach, but humanity still remains within God’s rule.

**SEVEN HEAVENS DISPLAY ORDER BEYOND THE HUMAN DWELLING**

The sentence then lifts attention from what is near to what rises above. The wording about God turning toward the heaven concerns His directing the act of creation and forming it as He willed. It does not require imagining created movement, bodily need, or effort in Him. Once He wills, no obstacle delays the result. The singular “heaven” is then formed into seven heavens, a complete ordered creation rather than an unshaped mass. Mujāhid describes the heavens as one above another, and another passage states that God formed them while assigning each its order: (41:12 — **“So He formed the heaven into seven heavens in two Days, assigning to each its mandate.”**). The number should be received as revealed truth, not reduced to a loose way of saying many. At the same time, the verse does not invite guesses that identify each heaven with any current human chart. Observation of the visible sky is good, but revelation speaks of a created order larger than what unaided sight can map. The point here is plain enough: the Lord who fitted the earth for life also formed the heights in ordered levels. This is why resurrection is no strain upon Him. The first making of the heavens and earth is greater than restoring one human body, yet both stand under the same command.

**PERFECT KNOWLEDGE HOLDS THE WHOLE AND EACH DETAIL**

The close is ***“And He has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things”***. Power has not acted without wisdom, and the vastness of creation has not hidden one part from its Maker. His knowledge holds the earth and seven heavens as a whole, while missing no seed, creature, need, deed, or hidden thought within them. The question elsewhere makes the link direct: (67:14 — **“How could He not know His Own creation? For He ˹alone˺ is the Most Subtle, All-Aware”**). Human makers can lose track of what they make because their knowledge is partial and their control fades. God’s knowledge is not gained by trial, enlarged by discovery, or threatened by number. Therefore, the gifts named in the verse are fitted rather than accidental. The earth can sustain life because the Creator knew every relation on which life would depend. The heavens hold their order because no detail escaped His decree. Perfect knowledge also returns the reader to the coming account. The One to whom all will return already knows what each person did with life and with the world placed at hand. Today, knowledge of nature can grow while awareness of its Lord grows weak. This ending joins them again. To study a thing is to meet more of what God knows fully; to use it well is to answer the wisdom and mercy with which He created it.
