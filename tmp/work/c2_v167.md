**A CHANCE ASKED TWICE**

The previous verse showed the leaders repudiating their followers; this one gives the reply. ***“The ˹misled˺ followers will cry, “If only we could have a second chance, we would disown them”*** is the exact shape of their wish — not to escape the Fire outright, but merely to change sides in the argument. The verse closes their sentence with ***“as they disowned us.””***, so the repudiation they request is the same act they have just suffered. The wish is symmetrical to the injury, and that symmetry is the verse's quietest cruelty: they want only a matching revenge. The cry is recorded in the followers' own words, so the record cannot be suspected of exaggeration by their sympathisers. Their vocabulary of regret is the proof that comprehension arrived too late to become guidance. The verse therefore counts comprehension as a resource with an expiry date attached.

**WISH GRANTED AS REGRET**

The particle at the head of the wish is optative — a wish for what cannot be — and its grammatical answer is the repudiation they would perform if granted the return. God's actual answer is named in the next clause: ***“And so Allah will make them remorseful of their misdeeds.”*** The counterfactual is granted as anguish rather than as travel, and the deeds are shown to them wrapped in regret. Ibn ʿAbbās reports that both the leaders and the followers see their deeds in this state, so the exposure is shared even though the wish belongs to the followers. The clause that ends the matter is absolute: ***“And they will never ˹be able to˺ leave the Fire”***. Return requires the world, and the world is closed. The permanence is not a verdict imposed on the wish; it is the condition of the place where the wish is spoken. The wish travels backward to a door that has already been shut, which is why the verse calls the deeds anguish rather than calling the punishment the cause of their pain. The regret is informational as well as painful — the deeds are shown as they are, without the story the followers used to tell about them. (6:27 — **“If only we could be sent back, we would never deny the signs of our Lord”**)

**KEPT ON RECORD FOR THE LIVING**

A hearer may ask why the wish is recorded at all if it can never be granted. Recording it serves two purposes at once. The first is documentary: the followers' complaint is entered so that no party claims the record was edited by the victors. The second is pedagogical: every reader still inside a life holds the second chance the dead now beg for, and the verse prices that possession by showing the empty hands on the other side of the door. The wish appears in the perfect position — between the disowning and the permanence of the Fire. This is the difference between envying the dead nothing and envying the living everything; the hearers are meant to realize which side of the exchange they stand on. Recording the wish also closes every excuse of imitation, because the alternative is exactly what they wish for. A hearer who copies the followers after reading their wish cannot claim the road was unmarked.

**ENVIED BY THE DEAD**

The mutual blaming is drawn again in (34:31 — **“when the wrongdoers will be detained before their Lord, throwing blame at each other!”**), and Ibn ʿAbbās reports from his side that the leaders and followers alike will not emerge once they have entered. The application is to spend the possession the dead envy: the second chance is not a promise for later but an inventory for now, and the repudiations worth performing today are the ones the verse's speakers would give anything to perform. The wish on record is therefore an asset belonging to the reader, and its value rises with every breath. Their own mouths certify the case. The wish is on record so that the living can spend it. The door is open where they are standing, and the door is priced by the hands shown reaching for it too late. The inventory of the present is therefore the reader's possession, counted in breaths.
