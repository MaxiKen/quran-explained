**A UNIVERSAL INVITATION**

***“O humanity! Eat from what is lawful”*** establish a universal invitation. After describing false allegiance and the regret of those who followed leaders in error, Allah addresses all humanity. The command to eat *lawful and wholesome*—what is lawful and wholesome—shows that Islam’s guidance concerns ordinary life as well as formal worship. Food sustains the body, shapes habits, affects family life and commerce, and becomes part of the servant’s relationship with Allah. The address is universal, not limited to Muslims. Allah’s provision is made available to humanity, and everyone is called to recognize its lawful limits and its moral source. For believers, the command is also a test of gratitude: they do not consume whatever desire or market power can obtain, but ask whether it is lawful, wholesome, and acquired without injustice. *Lawful* means permitted by the divine law, while *wholesome* includes what is wholesome, clean, beneficial, and suitable. The two terms are related but not identical. Food may be technically permissible in its ingredients yet harmful through contamination, abuse, or an unlawful method of acquisition. Wealth may purchase a lawful product while the money used to buy it came from theft, fraud, bribery, or exploitation. The command therefore includes the source, substance, preparation, and effect of consumption. Muslims examine labels and ingredients where needed, avoid intoxicants and harmful substances, and seek trustworthy information without falling into obsessive doubt. They also remember that lawful food does not excuse waste. The Qur’an commands eating and drinking without extravagance. (5:87 — **“O believers! Do not forbid the good things”**) Because a universal invitation governs 2:168, the enemy’s incremental path arises from a universal invitation. It follows that consumption and spiritual health completes a universal invitation through the enemy’s incremental path.

**THE ENEMY’S INCREMENTAL PATH**

For the enemy’s incremental path, the decisive wording is ***“and good on the earth”***. Allah does not merely say, *Do not follow Satan*; He says, *Do not follow his footsteps*—Satan’s footsteps. Evil often develops gradually. A person begins with a doubtful transaction, normalizes a small deception, accepts a gift that influences his judgement, and eventually consumes wealth or food known to be forbidden. Satan invites through incremental steps so that the destination is hidden behind ordinary choices. The same pattern appears in speech, dress, relationships, and ideology. The believer watches the path, not only the final act. He avoids environments and habits that repeatedly pull him toward sin, and he seeks lawful alternatives before temptation becomes dominant. Satan’s enmity is clear because he seeks to corrupt the human relationship with Allah. He wants people to treat blessings as independent possessions, to consume without gratitude, and to define right and wrong according to desire. His promises are decorated with convenience and freedom, but they end in enslavement to appetite, debt, addiction, and regret. The Qur’an says Satan commands obscenity and false speech about Allah. The next verse explains how food restrictions and religious claims can be manipulated. People may declare lawful what Allah forbade or forbid what Allah allowed, then attribute their preferences to revelation. This is one of Satan’s most dangerous steps: turning desire into theology. (7:31 — **“Dress properly whenever you are at worship. Eat”**) The reason consumption and spiritual health follows the enemy’s incremental path lies in a universal invitation. Therefore, consumption and spiritual health returns practical force to the enemy’s incremental path.

**CONSUMPTION AND SPIRITUAL HEALTH**

Read under consumption and spiritual health, the relevant clauses are ***“and do not follow Satan’s footsteps. He is truly your sworn enemy”***. The Prophet ﷺ taught that Allah is good and accepts only what is good, then mentioned the person who travels, raises his hands in supplication, while his food, drink, clothing, and nourishment are unlawful, leaving the conditions of a sound response gravely impaired. This hadith, recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, connects lawful consumption with the health of worship. Allah answers supplication in His wisdom. It is a warning that unlawful income and consumption can place barriers before spiritual life. A believer who wants a responsive heart should care about contracts, wages, ingredients, and the rights of producers and workers. Food is a gift from Allah, even when it reaches the table through human labour. Gratitude includes saying Allah’s name, avoiding waste, sharing with others, and recognizing the people and land involved in production. The Prophet ﷺ taught moderation and encouraged sharing food. The Qur’an condemns those who hoard or consume wealth unjustly. A community guided by this verse supports food security, honest trade, humane treatment of animals, and access to nutritious food for the poor. Individual piety cannot be separated from the economic conditions that determine whether people can obtain wholesome provision. Food law here is like a clear path through an orchard whose owner has opened abundant fruit but marked a few dangerous plots. Wisdom neither starves at the gate nor tears down every marker; it receives the permitted abundance and respects the boundaries. The footsteps of Satan begin when appetite persuades a person that the marked route is needless or that invented fences are holier than the owner's directions. Present-day consumption still tests both errors: careless indulgence and religious restrictions God never imposed. (16:114 — **“So eat from the good, lawful things”**) In other words, consumption and spiritual health answers a universal invitation.
