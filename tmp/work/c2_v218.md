**THREE QUALITIES OF HOPE**

***“Surely those who have believed, emigrated, and struggled”*** establishes three qualities of hope. After warning about turning away from faith, the Qur’an describes those who may hope in Allah’s mercy: they believe, emigrate, and strive in His cause. The verse joins inner conviction to costly action. Hope is not a vague optimism; it is grounded in faith and expressed through sacrifice and effort. Īmān is trust in Allah and acceptance of His revelation. Hijrah is leaving a place, condition, or influence that prevents obedience when a lawful path of departure exists. Jihād means striving and exerting effort for Allah’s sake, including striving against one’s own desires, seeking knowledge, serving the vulnerable, and, under lawful authority and strict limits, defending a community from aggression. It cannot be reduced to violence or used to sanctify terrorism. The first Muslims emigrated from Makkah to Madīnah because persecution obstructed worship and threatened their lives. They left homes, property, family networks, and familiar status for Allah. The Qur’an honours the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār for this sacrifice and for the brotherhood that followed. Hijrah has a continuing spiritual meaning. A believer may need to leave a sinful environment, an abusive situation, dishonest employment, or a social circle that repeatedly destroys faith. Physical relocation is not always possible or required. The principle is to move toward obedience while taking wise steps and protecting dependants. A person should not abandon responsibilities impulsively or create harm while claiming hijrah. (2:190 — **“Fight in the cause of Allah ˹only˺ against those who wage war against you,”**) Because three qualities of hope frames emigration for faith, emigration for faith returns attention to three qualities of hope.

**EMIGRATION FOR FAITH**

***“in the Way of Allah—they can hope for”*** deepens emigration for faith. The Prophet ﷺ taught that deeds are judged by intentions, and the jurists consider ability, safety, contracts, family rights, and the nature of the danger. Allah’s mercy is sought through wise obedience, not dramatic gestures. Striving in Allah’s way requires sustained effort and exertion. The broadest daily struggle is to obey Allah, resist sin, speak truth, acquire beneficial knowledge, and serve people. A parent who patiently raises children, a student who pursues knowledge, a reformer who resists corruption, and a person who fights addiction can all be striving for Allah when the intention and means are sound. Armed fighting is a restricted category, not the definition of every use of the word. It requires a just cause, legitimate authority, ability, and observance of the prohibitions in 2:190–194. The Prophet ﷺ forbade killing non-combatants, treachery, and transgression. No individual or group may use this āyah to declare private war. The believers hope—yarjūna—for Allah’s mercy. Hope is not a guarantee based on self-satisfaction. The servants believe, sacrifice, and still recognize their need for forgiveness. They do not say, Our deeds have purchased Paradise. They ask Allah to accept the little they offer and to cover their mistakes. The Prophet ﷺ taught that no one enters Paradise solely through his deeds but through Allah’s mercy. This teaching protects the striving person from pride and the sinner from despair. Work is required, but acceptance remains a gift. (4:100 — **“Whoever emigrates in the cause of Allah will find many safe havens and bountiful resources throughout the”**) Since striving in allah’s cause follows three qualities of hope, emigration for faith links purpose to striving in allah’s cause.

**STRIVING IN ALLAH’S CAUSE**

***“Allah’s mercy. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful”*** clarifies striving in allah’s cause. Emigration can involve loneliness, financial difficulty, discrimination, or the grief of leaving loved ones. Allah’s mercy includes companionship, provision, new opportunities, forgiveness, and reward beyond what was lost. The Qur’an promises that those who emigrate for Allah will find many places of refuge and abundance. Communities receiving migrants have a duty to welcome and support them. The Anṣār were praised for loving those who emigrated to them and preferring them over themselves. A community that celebrates hijrah but exploits migrants has missed the verse’s ethics. Think of three qualities of hope through a family that sells a house in the capital to open a clinic in a village where there was none. For two years nobody thanks them and the ledger shows a loss, and the first child who is carried through the door alive settles the question they had stopped asking. Three qualities of hope confronts anyone treating emigration for faith as remote; under three qualities of hope, such a person has given something up for the sake of his religion and has begun to ask whether it was worth the price. The verse gives him a sentence with the three works in it and a hope that is named by God. Because the promise is attached to works he can still perform, three qualities of hope shows that the road is open to the one who has not yet left anything. Because the three are named as one description of one people, three qualities of hope shows that the works of the religion belong together. (59:9 — **“As for those who had settled in the city and ˹embraced˺ the faith before ˹the arrival of˺”**) Hope rather than presumption matters, which means striving in allah’s cause returns hope rather than presumption to three qualities of hope.

**HOPE RATHER THAN PRESUMPTION**

The man who believes without leaving his ease, and the man who leaves without the struggle of the self, are both told what the caravan of this verse was made of. Because the hope is named rather than the certainty, the reader is shown the rank of the travellers. Since the three works are named as one description, emigration for faith makes three qualities of hope show that the promise is not earned by a single act. Since the hope is attached to three works rather than to a feeling, emigration for faith makes three qualities of hope show that the promise belongs to the road and not to the mood. They had left their homes for a land they did not know, and the Book promised them a mercy they would ask for each day rather than a wage that would be counted in their hands. Because the names are attached to the description of the travellers rather than to their question, the reader is shown what kind of people the promise is addressed to. The verse was revealed over a doubt of men who had left their homes, and it closes on the two names that answer a doubt. Emigration for faith makes three qualities of hope practical; striving in allah’s cause therefore matters. Today, migration, demanding work, and resistance to private sin require lawful means, sound intention, and hope rather than pride.
