**HOPE WITH A CONTRACT**

Three phrases carry the covenant: the belief, the migration, and the striving: ***“Surely those who have believed, emigrated”***, ***“and struggled in the Way of Allah”***, ***“they can hope for Allah’s mercy.”***, ***“And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful”***. and the closing phrase pays out the mercy the three earn. The walk through the three clauses is a walk through the résumé of the first community, and the mercy at the end is the wage the résumé earns. The three clauses are welded together so that none of them can be listed alone: the belief without the migration and the striving is not the covenant this verse signs. The report on the raiding party who feared their deed was unrewarded is answered by this verse with the mercy named at its end.

**BELIEVED, EMIGRATED, STRIVED**

The three clauses of the covenant are the résumé of the party of the raid who feared their deed was unrewarded, and the verse answers their fear with a résumé that matches. The narration transmitted through Ibn ʿAbbās keeps the occasion’s fear honest: the men thought the sin had been lifted but the reward had been lost, and the verse arrives to restore the ledger. The three clauses are welded into one description, so the belief without the migration and the striving without the belief are not the party the verse names. The legislation of the emigrants and the strivers gives the same résumé and the same rank in the same order (9:20 — **“Those who have believed, emigrated, and strived in the cause of Allah with their wealth”**). Because the contract has three clauses, the hope it earns is a hope with a signature on it. The clause of the striving is the public half of the faith, and the public half is the half that can be counted at the migration.

**THE PARTY RELEASED**

The mercy named at the close is answered to the party that had just been released from the sin of the sacred month, and the release is the verse's mercy in history. The migration legislated for the one who leaves his home for the cause carries the same promise of havens and provision (4:100 — **“Whoever emigrates in the cause of Allah will find many safe havens and bountiful resources throughout the earth. Those”**). Because the party is named by its deeds, the mercy is transferable to anyone who signs the same three clauses. The application is to check one's own résumé against the verse's clauses and to notice which signatures are missing. The mercy closes the account of the campaign.

**MERCY AS THE PAYMENT**

The close pairs the hope with the two names that guarantee it: Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful. The forgiveness answers the sins of the road and the mercy answers the poverty of the deeds, and the pair is the wage the covenant earns. Because the names are attached to the hope, the hope is a contractual expectation rather than a mood. The party of the raid leaves this verse with its ledger restored, and the reader leaves with the same clauses open for signature. The good news of the mercy is delivered in the passage with the calm of the messenger rather than with the noise of the trumpeter. The fear of the party at the hour of the return is the fear of the one who has spent his capital and does not know the balance, and the passage arrives as the statement of the balance. Because the fear is answered by the two names, the answer is a piece of accounting rather than a piece of comfort. The veils of the good news are drawn in parallel passages with the same etiquette: the light that is seen through the veil is still the light. The provision of the one who emigrates is described in the legislation of the road with the same promise of the havens, and the havens are counted in the treasury of the owner. The one who has signed the three clauses reads the passage as the receipt of his own transaction. Reading the fear beside the receipt is the closing ritual of the campaign. The mercy is not a mood; the mercy is the payment of the covenant. The receipt of the transaction is kept in the treasury of the deeds, and the treasury is reviewed at the hour of the settlement.
