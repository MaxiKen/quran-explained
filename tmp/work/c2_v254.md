**SPEND BEFORE THE FINAL DAY**

***“O believers! Donate from what We have provided for you before”*** establishes spend before the final day. The believers are told to spend from Allah’s provision before the Day of Resurrection arrives. Wealth is a trust and an opportunity whose usefulness ends at death, while the reward of how it was used remains. The command includes zakāh, support for dependants, voluntary charity, relief, and every lawful expenditure that fulfils Allah’s rights and the rights of people. From what We have provided corrects the illusion of absolute ownership. A person earns through work, but his life, ability, resources, opportunities, and the natural world that sustains him are all gifts from Allah. Spending becomes gratitude when it is lawful, sincere, and directed toward benefit. On that Day there will be no bayʿ, no trade or bargaining by which a person can purchase escape. A person cannot offer wealth after death to erase a life of refusal. The Qur’an repeatedly warns that ransom will not be accepted once the final judgement has arrived. This does not mean that all worldly transactions are bad. Lawful trade is permitted, and honest business can be worship. The warning concerns treating the Hereafter as a market that can be negotiated after the opportunity for deeds has ended. The wise person makes the investment now. There will be no khullah, intimate friendship or alliance that can save a person independently of Allah. Family, political connections, popularity, and proximity to a respected leader cannot replace faith and righteous action. (3:91 — **“Indeed, if each of those who disbelieve then die as disbelievers were to offer a ransom of”**)

**NO BARGAINING**

***“the arrival of a Day when there will be no”*** deepens no bargaining. This does not deny the emotional value of friendship in the world or the benefit of righteous companionship. It denies the belief that a created relationship can override Allah’s judgement. The Prophet ﷺ warned his closest relatives to save themselves through faith and deeds, showing that lineage cannot substitute for obedience, as al-Bukhārī and Muslim record. A believer loves family and friends by encouraging them toward good, not by promising a rescue he does not control. The verse does not deny every form of intercession. Other Qur’anic passages establish that intercession occurs only by Allah’s permission and for those with whom He is pleased. No prophet, angel, saint, or ancestor intercedes independently or can be invoked as a power alongside Allah. The correct response is to seek Allah’s mercy directly, follow His Messenger ﷺ, and hope in the intercession He grants. This protects tawḥīd from both denying revealed intercession and turning created beings into objects of worship. The command to spend is urgent because wealth can disappear through death, illness, economic change, or sudden disaster. More importantly, the chance to earn reward ends when the soul departs. The Prophet ﷺ taught that a person’s wealth is truly what he sends ahead through charity, while the remainder belongs to heirs, as al-Bukhārī records. This urgency must be balanced with responsibility. The believer supports family, pays debts, and avoids waste. He does not give in a way that makes dependants beg, but he does not let fear turn provision into an idol. (57:15 — **“So Today no ransom will be accepted from you ˹hypocrites˺, nor from the disbelievers.”**)

**NO FRIENDSHIP AS A SUBSTITUTE FOR FAITH**

***“bargaining, friendship, or intercession. Those who disbelieve are ˹truly˺ the wrongdoers”*** clarifies no friendship as a substitute for faith. The disbelievers are the wrongdoers identifies the theological injustice of rejecting Allah’s ownership and the practical injustice of withholding rights while relying on wealth and connections. The statement concerns those who persist in disbelief, not a licence to treat all non-Muslims unjustly. Muslims must still act with kindness and justice toward peaceful people of other faiths. A believer should also fear forms of wrongdoing within his own life. One may affirm Islam but wrong people through hoarding, fraud, unpaid wages, or refusal to support dependants. The verse’s moral warning reaches anyone who treats Allah’s provision as a private entitlement without accountability. Spending purifies attachment. The person who gives does not become poorer in the ultimate account. He learns reliance, compassion, and gratitude. Charity should be protected from showing off, reminders, and injury to the recipient. The Prophet ﷺ described hidden charity among the deeds receiving special protection on the Day of Resurrection, as al-Bukhārī and Muslim record. Quiet giving is not the only valid form, but privacy often protects sincerity. Think of spend before the final day through a traveller who plans to buy the tickets for the whole family at the border because the queue at home is long. The gates close in the evening, the queue at the border is longer, and the money in his pocket buys nothing after the last bell. Spend before the final day confronts no bargaining in a reader who intends to settle his accounts with his Lord when he has a little more capital. (20:109 — **“On that Day no intercession will be of any benefit,”**)
