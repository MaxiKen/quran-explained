**THE QUESTION TURNS DENIAL TOWARD THE DENIER’S OWN LIFE**

The address changes from describing rebels to confronting them: ***“How can you deny Allah?”*** This is not a request for information about the method of disbelief. It is a rebuke filled with wonder. Denial cannot stand honestly when the denier’s own existence points beyond him. The verse does not begin with a remote object that only a trained observer can study. It begins with the hearer’s passage from no earthly life to life. A person may dispute a report, avoid a moral demand, or credit created means for present comfort. He still cannot claim to have called himself into being. The Quran presses the same fact through two short questions: (52:35 — **“Or were they created by nothing, or are they ˹their own˺ creators”**). Neither path can bear thought. Nothing has no will with which to make a person, and a person cannot create himself before he exists. Therefore, the question in this verse joins evidence to gratitude. The One who gave life has both the power to be known and the right to be thanked. Denial is strange not only because it rejects an argument, but because it uses a given mind, breath, and tongue against the Giver. Each moment of living is part of the evidence the question asks the hearer to face.

**THE FIRST GIFT CAME BEFORE ANY HUMAN CLAIM**

The first stage is plain: ***“You were lifeless and He gave you life”***. Early explanation takes the lifeless state as nonexistence, or as the lifeless material from which the body would be formed. Ibn ʿAbbās speaks of drops held in the bodies of fathers and then given life in the wombs of mothers. The readings meet in the fact that no human being possessed this worldly life and then granted it to himself. There was a time when each person was (76:1 — **“nothing yet worth mentioning”**). Then God brought hidden matter through stages into a living child who could sense, learn, choose, and answer. This is why life is a proof before it is an achievement. Parents are real causes, and the growth of the body follows an ordered process, but neither parents nor process invent the power of life. They work within a creation already given. The verse also checks pride. Wealth, education, family name, and skill all arrive after this first gift. None can become the ground for independence from God because each depends on the life He supplied before its owner could ask. A reader who has grown used to breathing may treat life as private property. These words return it to its true place: a received trust whose beginning was wholly beyond the receiver’s control.

**DEATH IS FOLLOWED BY A SECOND ACT OF LIFE**

The path does not stop at birth. God says, ***“then He will cause you to die”*** ***“and again bring you to life”***. Death comes when the appointed earthly term is complete. Its certainty strips present strength of any claim to permanence. Yet the verse places death between two acts of divine giving, not at the end of the line. The One who caused the first life is not weakened when that life closes. Resurrection is a new life for judgment, and the first creation is evidence that the second is within the same power. Ibn ʿAbbās reads the sequence as nonexistence, creation, death, and life on the Day of Resurrection. Another passage gives the same order in two brief statements: (23:15 — **“After that you will surely die”**) and (23:16 — **“then on the Day of Judgment you will be resurrected”**). The point is not that rebirth is a natural cycle that bodies produce on their own. It is an act of the Lord who first made them. When decayed bones are raised as an objection, the answer is, (36:79 — **“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “They will be revived by the One Who produced them the first time,”**). Death therefore humbles every human power without defeating hope. It ends the time for earthly work, while God’s power opens the life in which that work will be weighed.

**THE RETURN GIVES EVERY STAGE ITS MORAL END**

The sequence closes, ***“and then to Him you will ˹all˺ be returned”***. Return does not mean that human beings had escaped God’s knowledge or rule during life. It means that they are brought to the meeting, judgment, and repayment that no worldly shelter can delay. Early readers therefore connect the phrase with being repaid for one’s deeds. This is why the verse does more than prove that a Maker exists. It sets human freedom inside an account. The first life is received, the earthly term is limited, death is certain, the next life is real, and the final destination is God. Those stages leave no room for the idea that conduct vanishes when the body falls. On that Day some will admit, (40:11 — **“They will plead, “Our Lord! You made us lifeless twice, and gave us life twice. Now we confess our sins.”**). Their confession will be true but late. The present verse gives the same knowledge while repentance and good work remain possible. Today, people often plan as though birth were self-owned, death remote, and the future no more than memory left in others. The full line corrects that short view. Life is from God, death occurs by His decree, resurrection answers His promise, and return places every secret and public act before Him. Gratitude is therefore not only a feeling about being alive. It is to spend the entrusted life in readiness for the One who gave it and will call it back.
