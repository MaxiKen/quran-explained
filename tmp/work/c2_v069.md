**A RESTRICTION ASKED FOR A SECOND TIME**

The pattern of the exchange is now plain, and the commentary names it as it is: ***“They said, “Call upon your Lord to specify”***. This is a second stiff-necked ask laid on the first. The people take up the very load that had been lifted from them. The first word of the command left the field wide open. Their first ask closed it to a middle-aged cow. Their second ask closes it again by a colour. The price of the first has taught them nothing. The sequence solves the one question that hangs over the whole scene — why the marks kept coming at all — and the answer in the glosses is that each new clause is a fine charged for the asking. One report puts the whole deal in one frame: had they killed the first cow at hand, that would have been enough for them. So what follows is not a class but a bill, and the reason the bill grows is that each ask distrusts the answer already given. Each needless ask is charged to the asker in the coin of the hunt. In the same key of just deserts, the tales of the two feuding sides of the town end with a rule that outlives the slaying: the one who kills for the inheritance does not inherit, so the gain of the crime is cut off at its root. The tale of the town's rule ends the same way, sealing the seat that bred the feud. Each ask thus shifts the hunt itself, and the shift only grows.

**YELLOW TO ITS DEEPEST DEGREE**

The answer narrows the field by hue, and the words of the answer are thick with force: ***“for us its colour.” He replied, “Allah says, ‘It should be a bright yellow cow”***, ***“pleasant to see.’””***. The line here holds two chains from one voice, and both are worth keeping in view. Ibn ʿAbbās describes the cow as yellow in its hoofs and horns while its hide was black. Al-Awfi hands down from him a second gloss in which the brightness is a deep yellowish white. The heart shared by both is a strength of hue that strikes the eye from far off. Both chains keep the second half of the line in his reading: the brightness is the light of the hue itself, and the joy of those who see her is placed in the eye of the one who looks.

The word-men of the commentary explain why the line doubles the mark instead of naming the hue once. The plain word gives the hue. The strained form puts that hue at its most intense, so the sentence first judges the cow yellow and then judges the hue itself extreme. This is the same build the tongue uses in its hardest colour phrases — whitest bright, blackest deep, reddest blazing — where the second word is there to press the first to its edge. The word for colour in this tongue also bears the sense of kind or type, so a thing once said to shift its hue is a thing that never settles into one character. The picture of the cow thus answers their hunt in both directions at once. The reason the line stacks the two judgements is emphasis: the cow was to be clear at a glance. What turns on that clarity is the fate of the bargaining itself, since a mark a stranger can check is a mark the askers cannot wriggle past.

That check in the open is sealed by the last words of the picture, which hand the hunt to the eye of each onlooker. Al-Suddī reads the close as the cow pleasing those who look at her, and the same stands with Abū al-ʿĀliyah, Qatādah, and al-Rabīʿ b. Anas: her beauty pleasing those that look at it. What this means for their trial is plain and instant. A hue that gladdens the eye is a hue that can be checked without an expert, and the herd grows smaller in each village the hunt enters. Their next complaint shows the narrowing has started to bite: (2:70 — **“for all cows look the same to us. Then, Allah willing, we will be guided ˹to the right one˺.””**). The last specification finishes the choking of the field: (2:71 — **“He replied, “Allah says, ‘It should have been used neither to till the soil nor water the fields;”**). For the reader today the sequence is a warning against bargaining with plain commands. The point is that the door was wide until the askers narrowed it themselves. In other words, the cost of each avoidable ask is paid in the same coin as the ask itself.
