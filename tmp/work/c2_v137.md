**TWO BRANCHES OF THE TEST**

The verse converts the creed of the previous passage into a test. The test has exactly two outcomes. ***“So if they believe in what you believe”*** ***“then they will indeed be ˹rightly˺ guided.”***. The condition is deliberately flat. The guidance requires no new deed. It requires only the joining of the belief already stated. In the public recitation of the believers reported by al-Bukhārī and Muslim, the roll of the previous verse was spoken as the community's own confession. The test of the others is whether they will stand where the confession stands. So the verse refuses to grade belief by antiquity or sophistication. The only grade is the alignment. The reason the verse offers the two branches before it names the costs is that the reader must see the fork first. What follows is that guidance in this verse is a state. The state of being aligned with what was revealed. The alignment is offered to anyone still standing.

**THE GUIDANCE-CONDITION**

The second branch is stated without softening. ***“But if they turn away, they are simply opposed ˹to the truth˺.”***. The parallel chapter states the same opposition as a matter of settled record (3:19 — **“Certainly, Allah’s only Way is Islam. Those who were given the Scripture did not dispute ˹among themselves˺ out of mutual envy”**). The report collected by Muslim keeps the same grammar of colouring. The faith was given as a colouring. The turning away is a refusal of the colour. The effect of the verse's middle is diagnostic. The turning away is described before it is priced. The difference between an argument and an opposition is what the verse marks. An argument engages the belief. An opposition simply faces the other way. The reason the condition is stated twice in the verse's own structure is that the fork is real. The two branches never meet.

**THE SCHISM-NAMING**

The verse's third move takes the pressure off the believer. ***“But Allah will spare you their evil.”***. The parallel continues the diagnosis (3:19 — **“until knowledge came to them. Whoever denies Allah’s signs, then surely Allah is swift in reckoning”**). Qatādah reads the sparing as a standing promise. The one who holds the confession is covered. The evil of the opposition is real. It is also bounded. The effect is a strange calm in the middle of a dispute. The believer is told the outcome of the hostility before the hostility finishes. What follows is that the heat of the dispute transfers to the ledger where it belongs. The reckoning is swift. The sparing is already in effect. The reason the verse names the sparing before it closes on the two names is that the servant needs the shelter before the theology. The covering arrives first. The explanation arrives second.

**THE SUFFICIENCY THAT COVERS THE SERVANT**

The verse closes on the two names that make the promise hold. ***“For He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing”***. The ear hears the opposition's words. The knowledge knows its ends. The recitation reported by al-Bukhārī places the community's own confession in the open beside the verse. It is recited in the open. It is heard in the open. The closing names turn the sparing into a relationship rather than a policy. The servant is covered because the covering One hears and knows. For the modern reader the same structure survives every public dispute. This age argues about guidance as a branding problem. The verse answers with a fork, a diagnosis, and a shelter. The effect of the ending is that the reader stands between the two branches with the covering already named. The reason the verse ends on hearing and knowledge is that the opposition's loudest threat is that no one is listening. The promise is that the hearing is total. The knowledge never blinks. The fork in the verse is honest about its two branches. The first branch is open to anyone still standing. The second branch is described before it is priced. The opposition has a shape and the shape is a turned face. The sparing then arrives before the explanation. The servant is covered first. The theology arrives second. The two names at the close carry the covering. The ear hears the threats. The knowledge knows their ends. The dispute is therefore bounded on both sides. The believer is not asked to win it. The believer is asked to hold the confession and walk on. The opposition's evil is real and it is fenced. The fence is the sparing named in the verse's third move. A modern argument about guidance wants the believer to panic. The verse gives a fork and a shelter instead. The shelter is older than the argument. The covering name never stopped listening. The knowing never looked away. That is enough to walk with.
