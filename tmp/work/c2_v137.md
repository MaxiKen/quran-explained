**THE CONDITION OF RIGHT GUIDANCE**

The verse follows the creed with a statement about who is guided, and the statement is put as a condition: ***“So if they believe in what you believe”*** and ***“then they will indeed be ˹rightly˺ guided.”***. The guidance is attached to agreement with a community already described, which makes the believers the measure of the thing being offered. The Book's wording is deliberately simple, and it does not require adherence to a nation or a lineage before it requires agreement with the truth. The verse therefore answers the invitation reported in an earlier sentence, where the two communities had offered their own religions as the road to guidance, and it answers by naming the content of belief rather than the label attached to it. A reader who has been taught that guidance is a possession to be handed over will find the sentence's terms plainer than that.

**BELIEVING BY THE SAME MEASURE**

The phrase about believing as the believers believe has been read in more than one way, and the readings converge on the same requirement. The objection that the verse makes God the object of a comparison is answered by the usage of the language, where the word for likeness in such a sentence marks a similarity of the two acts of belief rather than of their object. Ibn ʿAbbās read the clause as a description of faith in all the prophets and their scriptures, which is the creed the previous verse had just spelled out. The Book's standard here is not a new doctrine invented for the occasion but the faith the community itself had received, and the sentence measures everyone else by it. What the reader is being told is that the door is open to anyone who will believe what the believers believe.

**TURNING AWAY AND WHAT IT IS CALLED**

The verse then names the alternative, and the word it uses is stronger than disagreement: ***“But if they turn away, they are simply opposed ˹to the truth˺.”***. The term describes a split or a breach, and the Book uses it for the condition of being on one side while the truth is on the other. The account of the earlier communities in this sūrah has prepared the reader for the word, since the pattern of knowing the truth and standing against it has been the chapter's recurring subject. The clause is therefore not a description of people who have not heard but of people who have heard and turned. Someone who has refused a conclusion after understanding it has known the state the word describes.

**A HOUSE THAT REFUSES THE LIGHT IT ASKED FOR**

Think of a household that requests a lamp for a dark room and then shuts the door when the lamp arrives. The verse's two clauses describe the same two outcomes for the people who had been asking, and the second clause names the state of those who sent the light away. The comparison makes clear why the Book uses a word about fracture rather than a word about ignorance, since the parties in view had the means of knowledge in front of them. A person who has chosen a position against his own information has lived the condition described here, and the description is short because the condition is familiar. The difference between not knowing and turning away is what the verse asks him to hold on to.

**SUFFICIENCY AND THE TWO ATTRIBUTES**

The verse closes by naming what the community is promised instead of the agreement it had sought, and the promise is stated as sufficiency: ***“But Allah will spare you their evil.”*** and ***“For He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing”***. The sentence does not promise that the opposition will cease; it promises that the community will be defended from what the opposition intends. The two names that follow explain the basis of the promise, since a God who hears what is said and knows what is planned is not caught unaware by either. Another passage places the same sufficiency in the mouth of the Prophet as a statement of trust, which shows how the community was taught to use it: (9:129 — **“Allah is sufficient for me”**). He is left with the arrangement the verse describes, where the argument is allowed to run its course and the community is told where its protection lies.
