**A SHARE OF THEIR DEEDS**

***“It is they who will receive a ˹heavenly˺ reward for”*** establishes a share of their deeds. The people who ask for good in this world and the Hereafter will have a share of what they earned. The phrase joins supplication to action. A believer does not merely repeat a beautiful prayer while refusing the obedience that gives it meaning. He asks Allah for good, then works through lawful means, worship, charity, patience, and repentance. Share does not mean that the servant purchases Paradise by his deeds as though Allah were indebted to him. Deeds are a means, and acceptance and mercy are gifts from Allah. The Prophet ﷺ said that no one enters Paradise solely because of his deeds, but only through Allah’s mercy, while deeds remain the evidence of sincere faith and the means Allah has commanded, as Muslim’s collection and that of al-Bukhārī record. A person may receive worldly benefit from an act while another receives its lasting reward. Allah knows the intention, the effort, the obstacles, and the hidden sacrifices. Two people may give the same amount in charity, but the one who gives from little with sincerity may have a greater share than the one who gives from abundance to impress people. The verse also teaches that intentions matter in ordinary life. Earning money to support family, studying to serve people, resting to regain strength for worship, and seeking treatment can all become rewarded when done lawfully and sincerely. (2:200 — **“When you have fulfilled your sacred rites,”**) Because a share of their deeds frames the fruits of intention and effort, the fruits of intention and effort returns attention to a share of their deeds. Ibn ʿAbbās connects the promised share as a great portion in Paradise arising from the pilgrims’ deeds, while the swift reckoning leaves no account delayed.

**THE FRUITS OF INTENTION AND EFFORT**

***“the good they have done. Surely Allah is swift in reckoning”*** deepens the fruits of intention and effort. The believer asks Allah to place blessing in both the action and its outcome. Allah’s accounting is swift and complete. No good deed is lost, no private prayer forgotten, and no act of oppression overlooked. A person whose good work was ignored by people still has a share recorded with Allah. Likewise, a person who escaped human consequences for wrongdoing cannot escape divine accounting. Swift in account does not mean that Allah needs time to calculate or that judgement is difficult for Him. It emphasizes His complete knowledge and power to bring every person to account without confusion. Millions can be judged without one case delaying another. His judgement is immediate when He wills and perfectly precise. The previous verse presents a prayer that includes both worlds. This verse tells the believers that they will receive according to what they earn. A person who asks for good health should follow responsible means; one who asks for lawful wealth should work honestly; one who asks for a righteous family should cultivate patience and good example; one who asks for Paradise should guard faith and obligations. The principle is not a denial of grace. Allah may give without worldly cause, forgive a great sin, or open a door a person could not reach. (3:195 — **“So their Lord responded to them:”**) Since no one is cheated follows a share of their deeds, the fruits of intention and effort links purpose to no one is cheated.

**NO ONE IS CHEATED**

But the servant should not use reliance as an excuse for laziness. Tawakkul is trust joined to obedience and appropriate effort. A believer should not measure his share by comparing himself constantly with others. Someone else’s wealth, marriage, health, or public recognition does not reveal his final rank. Allah distributes worldly gifts according to wisdom and gives eternal reward according to justice and mercy. The servant’s task is to fulfil his own trust. This protects the heart from envy. The person who sees another receiving more asks Allah for blessing and focuses on his own deeds. He also avoids pride when his worldly condition is better, knowing that the real account is still before Allah. Think of a share of their deeds through a man who stands in a queue for a licence and passes the time complaining about the clerk. The clerk stamps the form and hands it over, and the man leaves with the paper and without a friend in the office. A share of their deeds reaches a reader today who measures a season by what he brought home: the photographs, the receipts, the list of people he met. The verse gives him a reckoning that is already running and asks what he asked for while he stood in the plain. Supplication and responsible action matters, which means no one is cheated returns supplication and responsible action to a share of their deeds.

**SUPPLICATION AND RESPONSIBLE ACTION**

Because the reply is swift rather than delayed, a share of their deeds shows that a request is a serious act with an answer attached. Because its place follows the reward, a share of their deeds shows that the swiftness cuts both ways. The man who spent his season in a quarrel with the pilgrims and the man who served them in the plain are both settled by the same ledger, and neither has to wait for the other's case. The Book sets out the words of the longer prayer in the preceding verse, so that the reader can trace the answer back to the request. A further passage keeps the same promise for those who act on what they know, and a share of their deeds shows that the season of the journey is one of the fields in which the seed is sown. The fruits of intention and effort makes a share of their deeds practical; no one is cheated therefore matters.
