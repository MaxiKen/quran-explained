**WRONG FOLLOWED BY REMEMBRANCE**

***“˹They are˺ those who, upon committing an”*** The final part of the portrait concerns those who commit an evil deed or wrong themselves, remember Allah, and seek forgiveness. It completes the estate-heirs' portrait with its most merciful panel. The panel itself establishes the doctrine. The muttaqin, drawn for the cosmic estate's inheritance, include sinners by the portrait’s own text. Idha fa'alu fahishatan aw zalamu anfusahum. When they commit an indecency or wrong themselves. The two registers spanning sin's scale (the grave fahishah and the general self-wronging). The heirs not the fall-less but the described. Their falls trigger the portrait’s named sequence. Dhakaru llaha. They remember Allah. The remembrance at the sin's own scene. (3:134 — **“˹They are˺ those who donate in prosperity and adversity, control their anger,”**) Wrong followed by remembrance therefore makes sought forgiveness bear upon knowingly refused persistence. Since knowing persistence opposes remembrance, seeking Allah’s forgiveness must produce changed conduct.

**NO ONE ELSE FORGIVES SINS**

***“evil deed or wronging themselves, remember Allah”*** The fall interrupting into recollection. Fastaghfaru li-dhunubihim. And seek forgiveness for their sins. The remembrance converted, at once, to asking. The racing-tempo of the surrounding passage applied at the fall (the forgiveness hastened toward, not filed for later). And the panel's parenthesis lifts the asking's whole warrant into praise. And who forgives sins except Allah. Wa man yaghfiru dh-dhunuba illa llah. The rhetorical exclusivity that is at once the sinner's comfort and the creed's guard (no confessor, priest, or intermediary holding the pardon-keys. The forgiveness-monopoly His. The clause excludes every claim of mediated absolution.) ***“and seek forgiveness and do not knowingly persist”*** And do not knowingly persist in sin closes the panel's sequence with its boundary-credential. Wa lam yusirru ala ma fa'alu wa hum ya'lamun. And they do not persist in what they did, knowingly. And the israr-clause is the panel's whole jurisprudence. The heir's mark is never sinlessness but non-persistence. The fall not settled into (the sin unrationalised, undefended, unscheduled for repetition). The knowing persistence being what converts slips into character and what the panel's people refuse. Their biography a rhythm of falls and returns, never a residence in the fallen state. The Prophet's own filing, peace be upon him. All the children of Adam constantly err, and the best of those who err are those who constantly repent, with the rhythm's divine reception published in the Sahih. The servant who sins and says my Lord, forgive me again and again, and Allah's answer at each return. My servant knew he has a Lord who forgives sin and takes for it. I have forgiven My servant. (3:136 — **“and Gardens under which rivers flow,”**) No one else forgives sins has present-day force because a lapse should lead quickly to remembrance, confession, and changed conduct rather than managed denial.

**PERSISTENCE KNOWINGLY REFUSED**

***“in sin—and who forgives sins except Allah”*** The panel’s placement completes the portrait’s architecture of mercy. The estate-heirs' credentials ran. Giving in both weathers, rage swallowed, pardon extended. The outward excellences. And the portrait’s final panel turns inward to the heirs' relationship with their own failures. The community shown, at the cosmic estate's gate, that the inheriting character is defined at both fronts. Excellent toward others' wrongs (the pardon), honest toward one's own (the return). And the two fronts one taqwa. For the pardoner of people learned pardon's worth at his own asking, and the persistent self-excuser hardens, in time, at both. While the wage-verse now follows to pay the whole portrait. Their reward is forgiveness from their Lord and Gardens under which rivers flow. Imagine a traveller correcting direction immediately after noticing the wrong turn. Regarding wrong followed by remembrance, Ibn ʿAbbās joins remembrance to fear of Allah, repentance, and refusal to persist knowingly after a transgression. The verse does not describe the mindful as people who never fall; which means it describes what they do after falling. Memory of Allah interrupts the momentum of sin, and seeking forgiveness turns that memory into return; therefore the phrase about wronging themselves prevents repentance from being reduced to damage control before other people. Even a hidden indecency injures the sinner’s own soul; in other words the question about who forgives besides Allah cuts off both despair and false mediation: the door is His, and He alone can clear the account. Refusal to persist is the visible truth of the request; hence a person cannot claim repentance while calmly planning the same wrong as a settled habit. Present failures are often defended until the defence becomes harder to abandon than the act; this is why this verse teaches a quicker honesty. The believer names the wrong, asks pardon, and changes direction while knowledge is still awake.
