**NURSING AS A SHARED RESPONSIBILITY**

***“˹Divorced˺ mothers will breastfeed their offspring for two whole years, for those who wish to complete the nursing ˹of their child˺. The child’s father will provide reasonable maintenance and clothing”*** establishes nursing as a shared responsibility. This āyah gives detailed guidance about breastfeeding, parental maintenance, and the welfare of a young child. It recognizes breastfeeding for two complete years as the period for whoever wishes to complete nursing. The wording allows parents to make informed decisions according to the child’s health, the mother’s condition, and professional advice. It does not make every family identical or turn a compassionate recommendation into a reason for shame. The child’s welfare is central. Breastfeeding can provide nourishment and closeness, but the mother’s health, consent, ability, and circumstances also matter. A woman must not be insulted if she cannot breastfeed, and a child must not be deprived of proper nourishment because adults are fighting. Qualified medical guidance helps apply the general command safely. The father is responsible for the mother’s provision and clothing according to what is reasonable—rizquhunna wa kiswatuhunna bi-l-maʿrūf. In context, this concerns the nursing mother and child, including the expenses required for care. The father’s responsibility does not end because the parents are divorced. He must provide food, clothing, housing, medical needs, and other reasonable necessities according to his means and the child’s needs. Maʿrūf means fairness recognized by sound custom and revelation. A wealthy father cannot provide the minimum while spending extravagantly on himself, and a struggling father is not required to give what he genuinely cannot afford. He should be transparent about income, avoid hiding assets, and seek fair legal arrangements when parents separate. No mother should be harmed through her child, nor a father through his child is a comprehensive rule. (2:228 — **“Divorced women must wait three monthly cycles ˹before they can re-marry˺.”**) Because nursing as a shared responsibility frames the father’s financial responsibility, the father’s financial responsibility returns attention to nursing as a shared responsibility. Ibn ʿAbbās reads the father’s duty as maintenance without excess or stinginess and according to the wealth Allah has given him.

**THE FATHER’S FINANCIAL RESPONSIBILITY**

***“for the mother ˹during that period˺. No one will be charged with more than they can bear. No mother or father should be made to suffer for their child. The”*** deepens the father’s financial responsibility. A husband must not prevent a mother from nursing in order to punish her, and a mother must not use the child to extort, threaten, or cut off the father’s lawful relationship. Neither parent may manipulate custody, visitation, maintenance, or breastfeeding as a weapon. The child must not be made a messenger in adult disputes. Parents should not interrogate the child about the other parent, make promises they cannot keep, or expose him to violence and humiliation. Where there is abuse, the priority is safety and lawful protection; shared parenting cannot be used to return a child to danger. If the father dies, the heir has a similar responsibility. The child’s maintenance does not disappear with the father. The surviving family or lawful estate bears responsibility according to the rules of inheritance and support. Early legal readings relate this either to the heir’s maintenance duty, to protection of the mother from harm, or to both. In every reading, a vulnerable child must not be abandoned because the original provider has died. The estate must first settle debts and valid obligations, then distribute inheritance according to Allah’s law. Relatives who receive wealth should remember that inheritance creates responsibilities as well as benefits. Modern guardianship and state support can assist, but relatives must not assume that an orphan is someone else’s problem. If both parents decide to wean before two years, there is no blame when they do so by mutual consent and consultation—tarāḍin minhumā wa tashāwur. The two conditions protect the child and prevent one parent from making a unilateral decision out of anger. (65:6 — **“Let them live where you live ˹during their waiting period˺, according to your means.”**) Since no parent is harmed through the child follows nursing as a shared responsibility, the father’s financial responsibility links purpose to no parent is harmed through the child.

**NO PARENT IS HARMED THROUGH THE CHILD**

***“˹father’s˺ heirs are under the same obligation. But if both sides decide—after mutual consultation and consent—to wean a child, then there is no blame on them. If you decide to”*** clarifies no parent is harmed through the child. They discuss health, nourishment, emotional readiness, and practical circumstances. Consultation is a Qur’anic family principle. Parents can seek a paediatrician, lactation specialist, counsellor, or trusted scholar. The decision should not be dictated by relatives who ignore the mother’s health or the child’s needs. Consent must be real and free from threat. The verse permits arranging for another woman to nurse the child, provided the payment is made fairly and according to agreement. The wet nurse’s labour is valuable and must not be taken for granted. The agreement should clarify payment, duration, care, and any relevant health information. Breastfeeding also creates a relationship of riḍāʿah in Islamic law, with consequences for marriage eligibility and family ties. Parents must record the identity of the nursing woman and the child’s nursing relationship so that future generations do not unknowingly enter a prohibited marriage. The legal details of the amount and conditions of nursing require qualified guidance. The āyah repeatedly invokes fairness, capacity, mutual consent, and consultation. This protects the family from rigid formulas that ignore real life. A mother may need paid work, medical treatment, rest, or support. A father may face unemployment or illness. The answer is cooperation and honest assessment, not accusation. For household duties, the Prophet ﷺ counted family maintenance among the best spending and warned against neglecting dependants, as Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim records. Supporting a child is worship when done for Allah and with kindness, not merely a financial burden imposed by court. The verse closes with taqwā and the reminder that Allah sees. (4:6 — **“Test ˹the competence of˺ the orphans until they reach a marriageable age.”**) The heir’s responsibility matters, which means no parent is harmed through the child returns the heir’s responsibility to nursing as a shared responsibility.

**THE HEIR’S RESPONSIBILITY**

***“have your children nursed by a wet-nurse, it is permissible as long as you pay fairly. Be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah is All-Seeing of what you do”*** closes with the heir’s responsibility. Parents may deceive courts, hide income, or manipulate a child while appearing respectable to relatives. Allah sees the private arrangement, the missed payment, the exhausted mother, and the frightened child. Taqwā makes parents choose justice even when no one can enforce it. The case is like two people carrying a heavy table down a narrow staircase and arguing about who is holding it wrong. The table is the same table, the staircase is the same staircase, and the dent in the wall belongs to both of them. Nursing as a shared responsibility reaches a reader today who uses his child as a message to his former wife, and who counts the missed visits as points won in an argument. The verse gives him a nursing mother with her food and her dress, a father with his capacity, and a command that neither be made to suffer, and nursing as a shared responsibility uses the father’s financial responsibility to ask which choice he has been reading. Because the closing name is the All-Seeing, the father’s financial responsibility makes nursing as a shared responsibility show that the small cruelties of a broken house are the ones most carefully written. The burden that no soul carries beyond its strength is stated in the closing of the chapter. And the duty of children towards their parents is set out in another chapter. (4:11 — **“Allah commands you regarding your children:”**) The father’s financial responsibility makes nursing as a shared responsibility practical; no parent is harmed through the child therefore matters.
