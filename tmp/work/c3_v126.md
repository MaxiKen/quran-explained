**GOOD NEWS FOR TROUBLED HEARTS**

***“Allah ordained this ˹reinforcement˺ only as”*** Allah ordained this ˹reinforcement˺ only as good news for you and reassurance for your hearts seals the angel-schedule with its theology. And the seal's restrictive grammar is the doctrine. Wa ma ja'alahu llahu illa bushra lakum. He did not ordain it except as good news. The three and five thousand, the ranked columns, the battle-marked riders. All of it assigned one function. Bushra. Glad tidings. The believers' eyes given a promise to hold, and reassurance for your hearts. Wa litatma'inna qulubukum. The itminan-word. The heart's settling. The reinforcement ordained, that is, for the ranks' interior. The courage steadied, the outnumbering un-feared. Not for the Almighty's arithmetic. The angels not required by the Helper but granted to the helped. Means dispensed as mercies to human sight. The doctrine that guards all means-theology in this Book. The instruments honoured, the Instrument-Maker alone leaned on. (3:125 — **“Most certainly, if you ˹believers˺ are firm and mindful ˹of Allah˺”**)

**REST WITHOUT DEPENDENCE ON ANGELS**

***“good news for you and reassurance”*** And victory comes only from Allah. The Almighty, All-Wise states the seal's positive half. And its restrictive form completes the guard. Wa ma n-nasru illa min indi llah. There is no victory except from Allah's own presence. Nasr un-sourced from anything beneath Him. Not the angels (His instruments), not the numbers, not the valour, not the positions (assigned, and effective only kept. The hill's coming lesson). The clause the whole Uhud-curriculum will grade on. For the day's first hour ran with victory while the conditions held, and the verse's doctrine explains both halves of the day with one source-clause. Victory from Him, granted on the schedule's terms, withheld at their lapse. The community thus armed in advance against the field's two idolatries. The victory credited to the ranks (pride's misfiling) or despaired of at the odds (fear's). Both dissolved where the source is one.

**MIGHT JOINED TO WISDOM**

***“for your hearts. And victory”*** The Almighty, All-Wise closes the seal with the chapter's threshold-pairing. And its selection over the battle-passage is the pairing's most operational deployment. Al-Aziz. The Almighty. Victory's source unconquerable. The nasr drawn from a might no coalition outweighs (the three thousand of Quraysh, the confederates to come. All light against the clause). Al-Hakim. The All-Wise. (3:127 — **“causing them to withdraw in disappointment”**) Victory only from allah bears on modern life because means may calm the heart, but the heart must not treat any means as the independent source of success. ***“comes only from Allah—the Almighty, All-Wise—”*** Victory's timing governed. Granted, delayed, and dosed by wisdom. The delays themselves curricular. The pairing teaching the community its complete victory-creed. Certain of the source, surrendered on the schedule. A reader shaped by good news for troubled hearts and the three-verse seal (124 to 126) carries its standing order into every field. The means welcomed as good news, the heart settled on the Sender, and the outcome's whole account held at one address. There is no victory except from Allah. While the next verse states what the granted victory is for. The disbelievers' edge cut and their schemes turned back in disappointment. Imagine medicine calming a patient while healing is still attributed to the One who made the means effective. In connection with good news for troubled hearts, Ibn ʿAbbās explains that the angelic promise reassured hearts while victory itself remained solely from Allah, the Mighty and Wise. The verse places the emotional benefit of reinforcement before the doctrine of victory; which means good news calms the heart, and calm can restore ordered action, but neither the message nor the angel produces victory independently. Victory belongs only to Allah; therefore calling Him Almighty removes doubt about His power, while calling Him All-Wise removes the assumption that power must serve every immediate desire. Help may come in the form requested, in another form, or after a delay that teaches what quick success would conceal; in other words this balance protects believers from dependence on signs. A reassuring event may be welcomed without being worshipped as the cause; hence in present hardship, a favourable forecast, a skilled ally, or an unexpected resource can steady a team. The verse directs gratitude through the means to their Giver; this is why it also prepares the heart for an outcome shaped by wisdom, since divine help is not measured only by whether a person’s first plan succeeds.
