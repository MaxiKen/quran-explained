**ONE COMMUNITY AT FIRST**

The history of the communities is walked in phrases from the unity to the quarrel: ***“Humanity had once been one community ˹of believers before they lost faith˺.”***, ***“Then Allah raised prophets”***, ***“as deliverers of good news”***, ***“and as warners, and revealed to them the Scriptures in truth to judge among people regarding their disputes.”***, ***“And no one disputed the Scriptures except the very people”***, ***“who received them after clear proofs had come to them”***, ***“out of jealousy. Then Allah, by His grace, has guided the believers to the truth regarding those disputes.”***, ***“And Allah guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path”***. and the closing phrase marks out the interval in which the quarrel was chosen. The walk moves through the single community, the courier-prophets, the book-judges, and the divergence, and the movement is the whole philosophy of history in one verse. The prophets are placed in the middle of the sequence because the sequence exists for them: the unity is broken and the couriers are sent to repair it. The transmission that Ibn ʿAbbās carried dates the unity to the times of Noah and Abraham and reads the two possibilities of the age together.

**PROPHETS AS COURIERS**

The prophets are sent in the middle of the history as couriers of two messages: the good news and the warning. The reports on the passage read the first unity as the age of Noah and Abraham, and the reading holds both possibilities of the age in one line — a single community of the unguided and a single community of the guided. The sequence places the couriers after the break because the couriers exist for the break, and their two messages answer the two directions the break produced. The legislation of the divinely sent book states the same purpose in the same terms of truth and judgment (4:105 — **“We have sent down the Book to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth to judge between people by means of what Allah has”**). Because the couriers carry judgment as well as news, the message is a court and not a rumour.

**THE BOOK AS JUDGE**

The patience is not decorative here; it is the instrument that keeps the family inside the covenant while the estate is settled. Because the wound is named before the pardon, the ruling keeps its grip on the real damage. The book sent to the prophets is sent in truth to judge between people, and the judging is the verse's account of why scripture exists. The judgment is described as belonging to the One who owns the case, and the book is the instrument of the judgment rather than its author. The page of the difference records the same fact: the divergence that could have been one community is held in the hand of the One who guides and lets stray (16:93 — **“Had Allah willed, He could have easily made you one community ˹of believers˺, but He leaves to stray whoever He wills”**). Because the judging is given to the book, the quarrels of the communities are answerable rather than chronic.

**DIVERGENCE AFTER KNOWLEDGE**

Its placement in the chronology is so precise that the history can be checked against the calendars of the campaigns, and the checking is one of the duties of the reader who inherits the report today. The divergence is dated precisely: it comes after the knowledge has arrived, which makes it the same species of slip the previous verse addressed. The people of the book who differed after the clear proofs are the verse's first audience, and the verse treats their difference as a case file rather than as a culture. Because the knowledge precedes the difference, the difference is answerable to something the differers possess. The application is to judge the quarrels of one's own community by the knowledge already shared inside it.

**WHO ANSWERS THE MESSENGER**

The last movement of the history points forward to the day when the questions will be asked by the book itself. The verse's ending — Allah informing the messengers of what they used to do — places the courier and the cargo in the same account. Because the messengers are informed alongside the people, the delivery records are kept on both sides of the transaction. The reader who stands in the sequence — after the unity, after the couriers, inside the difference — knows exactly which part of the history he is living in. The composition of the chapter itself is described by the early commentators with the image of two soldiers facing the battle: one stands at the front and the other at the rear, and both are described by the same rank. The disputed passage at the head of the family law stands at the seam of the two soldiers, which is why the placement is debated by the scholars of the arrangement. Because the seam is visible, the student of the composition can see the hand of the arranger at work. The rank of the passage in the sequence of the chapter is the rank of the seam, and the seam is where the two soldiers hold hands. Reading the legislation at the seam beside the image of the two soldiers is the exercise that turns the debate into a lesson. The one who studies the arrangement of the book studies the architecture of the instruction, and the architecture keeps its own law. The seam is not a flaw in the garment; the seam is where the garment is strongest.
