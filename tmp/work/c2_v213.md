**HUMANITY’S ORIGINAL UNITY**

***“Humanity had once been one community ˹of believers before they lost faith˺. Then Allah raised prophets as deliverers of good news and”*** establishes humanity’s original unity. The statement that humanity was one community—kāna al-nāsu ummatan wāḥidah—can carry related senses. It may refer to their original unity upon a simple recognition of Allah before disputes multiplied, or to their being one undivided human community before revelation and social differences required detailed guidance. The broader lesson is clear: division is not humanity’s highest destiny, and revelation came to restore truth amid competing claims. Unity in the Qur’an does not mean that all people must share one culture or that every disagreement is blameworthy. It means unity upon truth, justice, and the recognition of Allah. Human diversity in language, nation, and custom can be a sign of Allah. What destroys community is arrogant rivalry, oppression, and the refusal to accept correction. Allah sent prophets as bashīrīn and mundhirīn: bearers of good news and warners. Good news encourages faith, repentance, mercy, Paradise, and the honour of obedience. Warning protects people from disbelief, injustice, and the consequences of refusing truth. A message with only warning may produce despair; one with only promise may produce complacency. Prophetic teaching balances hope and fear. The prophets share one essential call—to worship Allah alone—while the details of law can differ according to time and community. Muslims believe in all of them and do not insult one messenger to honour another. The Prophet ﷺ described the prophets as brothers with the same father but different mothers, meaning one faith with different legal forms, as both al-Bukhārī and Muslim report. (30:22 — **“And one of His signs is the creation of the heavens and the earth,”**) Because humanity’s original unity frames prophets bring good news and warning, prophets bring good news and warning returns attention to humanity’s original unity.

**PROPHETS BRING GOOD NEWS AND WARNING**

***“as warners, and revealed to them the Scriptures in truth to judge among people regarding their disputes. And no one disputed”*** deepens prophets bring good news and warning. Allah sent the Book in truth so that it might judge between people in their disagreements. Revelation is not merely a collection of private spiritual inspirations. It establishes a criterion for belief, worship, ethics, contracts, family rights, and public justice. When people disagree, they return to Allah’s Book and the prophetic explanation rather than allowing power or popularity to decide. The Book does not erase the need for human judgement. Scholars interpret language and apply principles to changing circumstances. Courts examine evidence and protect procedure. But all these efforts remain accountable to revelation. A judge who accepts a bribe or a leader who uses scripture to protect himself has betrayed the Book’s purpose. The verse says that some people differed only after clear proofs came to them. This is not a condemnation of every difference in interpretation. Honest scholars may disagree over evidence that is probabilistic, language that admits more than one reading, or new circumstances. The blameworthy dispute is the one that arises after clarity and is driven by baghy, jealous hostility and transgression. Such rivalry may be caused by envy of another community’s status, fear of losing authority, desire for wealth, or refusal to accept that a truth has come through a person outside one’s group. The result is not merely a theoretical disagreement. People conceal proofs, distort texts, and mobilize followers against one another. Allah guides believers to the truth in what people differ over, by His permission. This guidance is a gift, but believers seek it through sincerity, knowledge, consultation, prayer, and willingness to be corrected. (10:19 — **“Humanity was once nothing but a single community ˹of believers˺, but then they differed.”**) Since revelation as judge of disputes follows humanity’s original unity, prophets bring good news and warning links purpose to revelation as judge of disputes.

**REVELATION AS JUDGE OF DISPUTES**

***“the Scriptures except the very people who received them after clear proofs had come to them—out of jealousy. Then Allah, by”*** clarifies revelation as judge of disputes. A person who begins a discussion determined to defeat an opponent may possess information yet miss guidance. The verse does not promise that believers will agree on every secondary matter. It promises that Allah can guide them to the truth and to proper conduct in disagreement. They may say, Allah knows best, respect legitimate scholarship, and preserve brotherhood while remaining firm on clear principles. The Qur’an identifies both ignorance and hostility as causes of division. Ignorance is addressed through teaching; hostility requires purification of the heart. A community can possess libraries and institutions while still fighting because envy has made knowledge a weapon. The remedy includes sincere intention, justice toward opponents, and recognition that Allah’s guidance is not a personal possession. The Prophet ﷺ warned that disputation can lead people astray when it is not based on sincere learning. He taught mercy, consultation, and returning matters to the Qur’an and Sunnah. A community should create spaces where questions are safe, evidence is examined, and disagreement does not become a reason for humiliation. The straight path does not erase legitimate diversity. It directs diversity toward worship, justice, and mutual recognition. Muslims can differ in language, legal school, architecture, food, and social custom while sharing the testimony, prayer, Qur’an, and prophetic model. They must distinguish these permissible differences from rivalry that fragments the faith. Allah guides whom He wills, and His will is joined to wisdom and justice. The servant does not claim that his group owns guidance. (16:36 — **“We surely sent a messenger to every community, saying, “Worship Allah and shun false gods.”**) Difference after clear proofs matters, which means revelation as judge of disputes returns difference after clear proofs to humanity’s original unity.

**DIFFERENCE AFTER CLEAR PROOFS**

***“His grace, has guided the believers to the truth regarding those disputes. And Allah guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path”*** closes with difference after clear proofs. He asks Allah to guide him and acts upon the truth he knows. Think of humanity’s original unity through an inheritance divided among brothers who each hire a lawyer to read the same will. The will is read aloud in the court, the estate is spent on the reading, and the only one who leaves with his share is the brother who never hired anyone. Humanity’s original unity confronts anyone treating prophets bring good news and warning as remote; under humanity’s original unity, such a person has watched a community divide over a fine point and has decided to keep out of every question. The verse gives him a history in which the dispute is named as jealousy and the guidance as grace, and humanity’s original unity asks which of the two he is carrying into his own argument. Because the guidance is promised to those who believe, humanity’s original unity shows that neutrality is not the same thing as faith. The Book gives the same account of the sending in the chapter of the family of ʿImrān, and names the path again in the verse of the prayer of the community, so that humanity’s original unity lets a person hear the last words of this verse as an echo of the daily prayer. (42:13 — **“He has ordained for you ˹believers˺ the Way which He decreed for Noah,”**) Prophets bring good news and warning makes humanity’s original unity practical; revelation as judge of disputes therefore matters. Today, communities facing religious disagreement need revealed judgement, honest study, and freedom from rivalry disguised as principle.
