**DEATH BY PERMISSION AND APPOINTED TERM**

***“No soul can ever die without Allah’s Will at the destined”*** No soul can ever die without Allah's Will at the destined time grounds the mortality-paper just set in the decree-doctrine. And the clause's construction is the battlefield's whole actuarial law. Wa ma kana li-nafsin an tamuta illa bi-idhni llah. It is not for any soul to die except by Allah's leave. The idhn-clause. Kitaban mu'ajjala. As a writing, term-fixed. The appointed-time doctrine. Death neither hastened by courage nor delayed by caution. The term written, sealed, and travelling with its soul. The doctrine's deployment at this station serving the passage's two open wounds at once. The rumoured death of the Messenger and the ranks' surviving fear. The clause disarming, in one sentence, both the grief that thinks death premature and the cowardice that thinks it preventable. (3:144 — **“other messengers have gone before him. If he were to die”**)

**AIMING AT WORLDLY REWARD**

***“time. Those who desire worldly gain, We will let them have”*** Those who desire worldly gain, We will let them have it, and those who desire heavenly reward, We will grant it to them turns the decree-doctrine to the day's audited intentions. And the two-desire clause is Uhud's own ledger read back. Wa man yurid thawaba d-dunya nu'tihi minha. Whoever desires the world's reward, We give him of it. The hill's fifty named in category. The spoils desired, and granted. Of it. The portion clause. The world's seekers paid in world's coin, the payment itself their whole account. Wa man yurid thawaba l-akhirati nu'tihi minha. And whoever desires the Hereafter's reward, We give him of it. The Anas-ibn-an-Nadr column. The day's same field paying two currencies by the fighters' own addressed desires. One battle, two payrolls, the intention the sorting-clause.

**THANKFUL PEOPLE RECOMPENSED**

***“it, and those who desire heavenly reward, We will grant”*** And We will reward those who are grateful closes the verse with the gratitude-column's third sounding in two verses. And the repetition's insistence is the passage's studied teaching. At the mortality-and-decree station, gratitude named twice as the rewarded quality. For the shakir is precisely the believer the two doctrines produce. The term accepted and the currency chosen (the Hereafter's payroll elected over the spoils'. (3:146 — **“nor did they weaken or give in! Allah loves those who persevere”**) Aiming at the hereafter has present-day force because the same outward work may pursue reputation, income, or the Hereafter, so intention determines its true direction. ***“it to them. And We will reward those who are grateful”*** Gratitude being, at its root, the valuation that prices the Giver above His gifts). A reader shaped by the lesson of 145 carrying its three-clause equipment into every field and every loss. The term trusted (written, unoutrunnable), the intention audited (which payroll does this deed address.), and the thanks held steady at both weathers. While the passage now summons history's whole company to witness the standard. How many devotees fought along with their prophets and never faltered. Imagine two workers doing the same task while one seeks a daily wage and the other a lasting partnership. Regarding death by permission and appointed term, Ibn ʿAbbās joins death to a registered term and distinguishes work seeking worldly benefit from work seeking the Hereafter. The fixed term removes two false calculations; which means recklessness cannot force a noble death before its decree, and cowardice cannot purchase life beyond it. This does not cancel ordinary means of safety, because the verse addresses the ultimate limit rather than forbidding prudence; therefore it frees a person to obey without imagining that disobedience controls the appointed hour. The division between worldly and heavenly reward then turns attention to intention; in other words two people may stand in the same field while seeking different payments. Each receives according to the direction of desire, and gratitude marks the person who values the Giver above the immediate gift; hence in modern work, a deed may serve both public benefit and personal advancement, so motives need repeated examination. The verse does not call worldly benefit unreal; this is why it calls it partial. A life governed only by the nearer payment may gain some of it and still miss the better account for which the soul was created.
