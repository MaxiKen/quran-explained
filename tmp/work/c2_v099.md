**THE NOTHING-NEW CHARGE ANSWERED**

The final answer to the trial of the courier is an answer about cargo: ***“Indeed, We have sent down to you ˹O Prophet˺ clear revelations. ˹But˺ none will deny them except the rebellious”***. The clause is handed down from Ibn ʿAbbās as the reply to the rabbi's parting shot — the charge that nothing recognizable had been brought, no clear sign that would justify following — and the reply answers the charge of emptiness with a list of what the descent actually carried. The word for the revelations is the word for making-plain things, and the transmitted inventory of what that word covers runs wide: the volume itself, the miracles that testified to the bearer, the reports of matters hidden, the concealments of the earlier books recovered in public, and the laws and obligations the descent laid down. The scope of the inventory is the reply's first move, because the charge was never about the absence of signs but about the refusal to recognize the ones on the counter. What follows from the tense of the descent is the passage's evidentiary rule: the signs are sent down, not manufactured, and their clarity is a property of the cargo rather than a courtesy of the court.

**THE SECRETS THE BOOK NARRATES**

The most striking item in the inventory is the one the tradition of the passage presses hardest. The volume narrates the hidden knowledge the scholars of the scripture possessed and concealed — the stories of the earlier generations, the texts known only to the rabbinical shelves, and the very sections where the rulings of the Torah had been altered and redrawn. The appearance of that material in an unlettered prophet's book is treated in the transmitted commentary as the standing proof, sufficient in itself for anyone truthful with himself and unwilling to march into destruction under the colors of envy. The logic of the proof is worth stating plainly, since it is the logic of the whole trial: the counterfeit cannot reproduce the hidden ledgers of the treasury it copies. The recognition-clause of the earlier passage had already established the matching standard; this clause supplies the matched content. In other words, the charge of nothing-new is answered by printing the plaintiffs' own redactions.

**THE WICKED AS THE ONLY DENIER**

The verse's second half then diagnoses the denial rather than debating it: none will deny except the rebellious. The earliest glosses fix the class-noun on the deniers of the scene — the unbelievers among the scripture-bearers — and the tradition's doctrinal gloss widens it into a law: the denial of recognized evidence is not an intellectual event but a moral posture, the habitual disobedience of the willful and the stubborn. The parallel statement of the law in the final book keeps the same anatomy of the sentence — the clear revelations preserved in the hearts of those gifted with knowledge, and the denial reserved for the wrongdoers: (29:49 — **“But this ˹Quran˺ is ˹a set of˺ clear revelations ˹preserved˺ in the hearts of those gifted with knowledge.”**). The pairing of the two halves of the verse is therefore the pairing of the whole section's method: the evidence is delivered in public and the refusal is diagnosed in private. The difference between not-knowing and not-willing is the difference the passage has been drawing since the sheathed-heart verse, and this is its final application.

**THE ALIBI THE VERSE REVOKES**

The closing lesson of the verse is about the last refuge of the rejecter, and the refuge is the alibi of missing evidence. The claim that the proof never arrived is the claim the verse revokes by inventory; the claim that the proof arrived but was not clear is the claim the adjective revokes at its root. A community of any century that keeps the ancient alibi on file — the church of the unmet standard, the synagogue of the unrecognized sign — meets the same counter-filing ready in this passage: the descent is documented, the cargo is itemized, and the recognition is on record from the plaintiffs' own shelves. What turns on the revocation is the entire jurisprudence of belief: the signs do not need to be improved; the will needs to be repaired. The section closes its argument here so that the next can open with the covenant-habit that made the repair necessary.
