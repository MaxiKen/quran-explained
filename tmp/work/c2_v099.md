**CLEAR SIGNS AND THE PEOPLE WHO DENY THEM**

Clear revelations, rather than another historical charge, now settle the argument: ***“Indeed,”***, ***“We have sent down to you ˹O Prophet˺ clear revelations.”***, and then with a description of the only people who deny them. The claim of the community had been that nothing recognisable had arrived, and the answer is that the signs were clear enough to be seen and that the denial comes from another source. The verse names that source with a single word, and the word describes a person who has stepped outside the bounds rather than one who has failed to understand. The reader is left with a picture of evidence on one side and a decision on the other, and the Book puts the two in the same sentence so that the second cannot be mistaken for a problem with the first. The same link between clear revelation and wilful denial appears later: (29:49 — **“And none denies Our revelations except the ˹stubborn˺ wrongdoers”**).

**THE WORD FOR THE REBELLIOUS**

The term used here belongs to the vocabulary of boundaries, and it describes someone who has left the path rather than someone who never saw it. The Book uses the same word for those who break their agreements and for those who take what has been forbidden, so the emphasis falls on deliberate departure. A report attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās ties the verse to an exchange in which a man told the Prophet ﷺ that nothing recognisable had come, and the answer is that the signs were in front of him. A reader who has argued with a person about evidence and found that more evidence changed nothing has seen the same condition at a smaller scale. The Book's diagnosis is that denial of this kind is a choice about conduct rather than a verdict on proof.

**THE IDENTIFICATION OF THE DENIERS**

The sentence never suggests that people deny the signs because they are difficult; denial is restricted to the rebellious, which is a statement about who the deniers are. That is a harder claim than it looks, because it removes the ordinary excuses in advance and leaves the reader with a category. The Book makes similar identifications in other passages where the object of denial is named as conduct rather than as information, and the pattern is consistent across the sūrah. The reader who wants to use the verse without becoming a judge of others can apply it to himself, since the only denial he can examine from the inside is his own. The Book's interest here is not in labelling a community but in showing what the label describes, and the description is a decision to leave a known path. The same restriction of denial to the rebellious is repeated elsewhere in the Book, where the word is attached to those who have broken the terms they accepted, which is why the chapter keeps returning to covenants.

**A SIGNPOST IGNORED BY CHOICE**

Think of a driver who has missed a turning and who tells his passengers that no sign was ever placed on the road, when the sign was passed at speed while he was looking elsewhere. Clear revelations had been given, the objection was that nothing recognisable had arrived, and the answer names attention rather than evidence as the missing item. The comparison is not flattering, and the Book does not intend it to be. A reader who has ever explained away a piece of guidance he did not want to follow can see how the explanation is constructed after the decision rather than before it. The verse names the construction and leaves the reader with it, and the naming is the whole of its remedy: a person who has been told that he looked away has been given something he can act on, unlike a person who is told that the evidence was never there.

**THE SIGNS THAT WERE ALREADY IN THEIR HANDS**

One thing the passage fixes is that the community being addressed was not without material, since the confirmation of their own scriptures had already been named several verses earlier. The signs in this verse are therefore not an unrelated display; they are the arrival of what the earlier books had described, which is why the denial is described in terms of rebellion. Someone who has been given a great deal and has chosen not to look will find the sentence aimed at him, and nothing here softens it. What the verse offers as a remedy is not stated here, and the next verses supply it in the account of what this community does with its covenants, which the reader can compare with his own record. The chapter's method is to keep the material and the conduct in view at the same time, and this verse is one of its clearest examples. In the present day, clear evidence may still be denied because accepting it would require a rebellious practice to end.
