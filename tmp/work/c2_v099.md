**THE NOTHING-NEW CHARGE ANSWERED**

The last answer in the trial of the courier is an answer about cargo: ***“Indeed, We have sent down to you ˹O Prophet˺ clear revelations. ˹But˺ none will deny them except the rebellious”***. The clause is handed down from Ibn ʿAbbās as the reply to the rabbi's parting shot. The charge was that nothing recognizable had been brought, and no clear sign that would justify following. The reply meets a charge of emptiness with a list. The word for the revelations is the word for things made plain, and the gloss handed down from Ibn ʿAbbās runs wide. The name covers the volume itself. It covers the miracles that testified to the bearer. It covers reports of matters kept hidden. It covers the concealments of the earlier books, recovered now in public. It covers the laws and duties the descent laid down. That range is the reply's first move. The charge was never about a lack of signs; it was about refusing to know the ones laid on the counter. The descent also carries its own rule of proof. The signs are sent down, not put together by the bearer, and their plainness belongs to the cargo rather than to the court that receives it.

**THE SECRETS THE BOOK NARRATES**

The hardest item in the list is the one the commentary presses hardest. The volume narrates the hidden knowledge that the scholars of the scripture held and buried. It tells the stories of the earlier peoples. It tells the texts known only to the rabbinical shelves. It tells the very sections where the rulings of the Torah had been twisted and redrawn. That material in an unlettered prophet's book is the standing proof in the transmitted gloss, and the same gloss holds it sufficient for anyone true to himself, and unwilling to march into ruin under the colors of envy. The logic is worth stating plainly, since it is the logic of the whole trial: a fake cannot copy the hidden ledgers of the treasury it forges. The earlier recognition-clause had set the standard; this clause brings the goods to match it. A companion point sits close by in the final book, where a group is charged with hiding what they know: (2:146 — **“Yet a group of them hides the truth knowingly”**). The redaction of the rulings is named in its own way a few verses later in the same surah: (3:78 — **“they attribute lies to Allah knowingly”**). What follows is the answer to the charge of nothing-new: the reply prints the plaintiffs' own redactions.

**THE WICKED AS THE ONLY DENIER**

The second half of the verse diagnoses the denial instead of debating it. None will deny except the rebellious. The earliest glosses fix the class-noun on the deniers of the scene, the unbelievers among the scripture-bearers. The doctrinal gloss then widens the class into a law. Denial of known proof is not a stumble of the mind. It is a posture of the will, the standing bent of the willful and the stubborn. The final book states the same law with the same anatomy, the clear revelations kept in the hearts of those given knowledge, and the denial reserved for the wrongdoers: (29:49 — **“But this ˹Quran˺ is ˹a set of˺ clear revelations ˹preserved˺ in the hearts of those gifted with knowledge.”**). The pairing of the two halves is the pairing of the whole section's method. The proof is delivered in public. The refusal is read in private. Not-knowing and not-willing are the two things this passage has drawn apart since the sheathed-heart verse, and here the drawing is finished.

**THE ALIBI THE VERSE REVOKES**

The closing lesson is about the last refuge of the rejecter, and that refuge is the alibi of missing proof. The claim that the proof never arrived is answered by the list. The claim that the proof came but was not clear is answered by the word at the root of the verse. Both claims now stand revoked, and the reason the verse revokes them by list is that the list is checkable. A community of any century that keeps the old alibi on file, the congregation of the unmet standard, meets the counter-filing waiting in this passage: the descent is written down, the cargo is counted out, and the knowing is on record from the plaintiffs' own shelves. The reader today who files the same alibi meets the same counter-filing. What turns on this is the whole law of belief. The signs do not need to be improved; the will needs to be repaired. In other words, the trial ends not with a new sign but with a verdict on the heart. The section closes its case here so that the next can open on the covenant-habit that made the repair so pressing.
