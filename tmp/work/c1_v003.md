**MERCY REPEATED AFTER POWER**

The two names appear at the head of the sūrah and then again in this verse: ***“the Most Compassionate, Most Merciful”***. The repetition is the first thing the reader notices, and the reports explain why the words return. The verse before this one has set down the lordship of God over everything, and that is a heavy thing to measure. The verse therefore follows the title of power with the names of mercy, so that fear of the Master is met by hope in Him. Mercy in this sentence is placed where a person would have put judgement, and every reader who has done wrong is meant to notice that. The two names are not a single name written twice. The first describes mercy in itself, without limit or condition, and it is the wider of the two; the second describes mercy that arrives at the one who receives it. Ibn ʿAbbās gave both names the sense of gentleness, and he said that mercy in the first name reaches the whole creation while mercy in the second belongs to those who are guided. The reading is therefore that the verse announces an arrangement: everything that lives is held inside the first name, and the second name carries a person through to the end.

**THE ORDER IN WHICH THE NAMES STAND**

The placement of the two names is not an ornament of style, and its purpose is stated in the Book itself. The one who makes a world and orders it also holds its people to account, and the Qur'an puts the two facts side by side. A verse of the same rhythm announces both: (15:49 — **“Inform My servants ˹O Prophet˺ that I am truly the All-Forgiving, Most Merciful”**), and the next verse completes the sentence: (15:50 — **“and that My torment is indeed the most painful”**). Another verse gathers the two attributes in one breath while describing the gifts of this life: (6:165 — **“Surely your Lord is swift in punishment, but He is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful”**). A person who hears only lordship will either despair or grow careless, and a person who hears only mercy will grow careless in a worse way. The reports carry a sentence of the Prophet that holds the two together and steadies a person between them. Abū Hurayrah reports that the Prophet said, *"If the believer knew what punishment Allah has, none would hope for His Paradise, and if the disbeliever knew what mercy Allah has, none would despair of His mercy"* (Muslim). The verse repeats the two names because the reader needs both, and because neither one alone was ever meant to be recited.

**A LINE DRAWN AROUND THE DIVINE NAME**

The two names differ in another way, and the difference shapes how a person may use them. The first may not be given to a created being at all, since it points to mercy that is the source of existence itself and belongs to God alone. The second may be used of a creature, and the Book in fact applies it to the Prophet in a verse praising his care for the believers: (9:128 — **“gracious and merciful to the believers”**). This is not a detail of vocabulary but a line drawn around worship. A servant may be merciful, and the Book praises the quality and commands it in its followers; a servant may not be the Most Compassionate, because the word carries the giving of being itself. The Book also shows that the two names are a door through which a person calls on God, and it places them together as the object of supplication: (17:110 — **“Call upon Allah or call upon the Most Compassionate—whichever you call, He has the Most Beautiful Names”**). The verse therefore teaches the servant what to call his Lord, and it settles the fear it first raised.

**THE HANDS THAT CARRY A CHILD**

Think of a child carried across rough ground by a parent. One hand holds the child close, and the other hand is the arm that the child never notices but which takes the weight. The two names in this verse answer to those two hands. The first holds the whole of the created order together and never tires, and the second is the particular care that a person feels when he is lifted over the place where he would have fallen. A person who recites it has been given both already, whether or not he has noticed, and the words are said at the head of every prayer so that the notice happens. These days a person is told constantly to rely on his own strength, and strength is a real gift, but the verse places it inside two hands larger than itself. The servant repeats the two names in every unit of prayer, and the repetition puts the same sentence at the beginning of his anxieties that it puts at the beginning of his joys.
