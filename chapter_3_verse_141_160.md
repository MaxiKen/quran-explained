## Verse 141

Verse 141 says: *and distinguish the ˹true˺ believers and destroy the disbelievers.*

In simple terms, the verse completes the purposes of the alternation: Allah tests to distinguish the true believers and to destroy the disbelievers.

The verse is the continuation of 3:140 and the completion of the war's purposes. Ibn Kathir: "And that Allah may test those who believe... by forgiving them their sins if they have any. Otherwise, Allah will raise their grades according to the losses they suffered. Allah's statement, 'and destroy the disbelievers' — for it is their conduct that if they gain the upper hand, they transgress and commit aggression. However, this conduct only leads to ultimate destruction, extermination, perishing and dying out." The verse's *wa liyumahhisa Allahu alladhina amanu* — and so that Allah might purify those who believe — is the first purpose; the *wa yamhaqa al-kafirin* — and destroy the disbelievers — is the second.

"and distinguish the ˹true˺ believers" — *wa liyumahhisa Allahu alladhina amanu* — and so that Allah might purify/try those who believe. The *mahis* — the purification, the distinguishing, the testing — is the divine: the *tamhis* — the clarifying — of the believers. Ibn Kathir: "by forgiving them their sins if they have any. Otherwise, Allah will raise their grades according to the losses they suffered." The verse's *alladhina amanu* — those who believe — are the *mumahhasun* — the purified: the losses of Uhud were the purification — the sins forgiven or the grades raised; the *mahis* — the distinguishing — is the removal of the dross: the believers purified by the test, the sincere separated from the false. The verse's *Allah* — the agent — is the divine: the *mahis* is the divine act — the purification of the believers through the test of the days.

"and destroy the disbelievers" — *wa yamhaqa al-kafirin* — and destroy/wipe out the disbelievers. The *mahq* — the destruction, the wiping out, the elimination — is the verdict: the disbelievers are destroyed — the *mahq* of the divine. Ibn Kathir: "it is their conduct that if they gain the upper hand, they transgress and commit aggression. However, this conduct only leads to ultimate destruction, extermination, perishing and dying out." The verse's *yamhaqa* — destroys — is the end: the disbelievers' conduct — the transgression upon gaining the upper hand, the aggression — leads to the *mahq* — the destruction; the alternation of the days ends in the *mahq* of the disbelievers and the *mahis* of the believers.

The verse's structure with 3:140: the alternation (*nudawiluha* — We alternate), the purposes (*liya'lama* — that He may know; *li-yattakhidha* — that He may take martyrs; *li-yumahhisa* — that He might purify; *li-yamhaqa* — that He might destroy) — the four purposes of the divine testing: the revelation of the believers, the selection of the martyrs, the purification of the faithful, and the destruction of the disbelievers. Ibn Kathir's frame: "the believers suffered losses in the battle of Uhud, including seventy dead" — and the purposes: the losses purified the believers and pointed to the destruction of the disbelievers.

For the reader, the verse is the completion of the wisdom of the test. The believer is taught: the alternation of the days — the victory and the defeat — has the purposes: the *mahis* — the purification — of the believers: the sins forgiven, the grades raised — the losses as the purification; and the *mahq* — the destruction — of the disbelievers: the transgression and the aggression leading to the ultimate destruction. The verse's *liyumahhisa* — that He might purify — is the believer's comfort: the loss of Uhud was the purification — the *tamhis* of the faithful; the *liyamhaqa* — that He might destroy — is the verdict: the disbelievers' conduct — the aggression — leads to the *mahq*. The believer's confidence: the alternation is not the random chance but the divine wisdom — the purification of the believers and the destruction of the disbelievers.

## Verse 142

Verse 142 says: *Do you think you will enter Paradise without Allah proving which of you ˹truly˺ struggled ˹for His cause˺ and patiently endured?*

In simple terms, the verse asks the decisive question: do you think you will enter Paradise without Allah first proving who truly struggled for His cause and patiently endured?

The verse is the question of the test. Ibn Kathir: "The Ayah asks, do you think that you will enter Paradise before Allah knows (tests) those of you who will perform Jihad and (also) knows (tests) those who are the patient?" Al-Jalalayn: "Or nay, did you suppose you should enter Paradise without God knowing through knowledge manifested outwardly who among you have struggled and who are patient in times of hardship?" The verse's *am hasibtum* — or do you think — is the question of the assumption; the *an tadkhulu al-jannah* — that you enter Paradise — is the destination; the *wa lamma ya'lami Allahu* — and when Allah has not yet known — is the condition; and the *alladhina jahadu minkum* — those who struggled among you — is the Jihad; and the *ya'lama al-sabirin* — and knows the patient — is the patience.

"Do you think you will enter Paradise" — *am hasibtum an tadkhulu al-jannah* — or do you think that you will enter the Garden? The *hasb* — the thinking, the assuming — is the question of the assumption: do the believers think the Paradise is entered without the test? The verse's *am* — or — is the rebuke's transition: the assumption of the entrance without the proof. Ibn Kathir: the question is the divine rebuke of the assumption: the believers of Uhud had suffered and the question asks — did you think the Paradise would be granted without the Jihad and the patience being known?

"without Allah proving which of you ˹truly˺ struggled ˹for His cause˺ and patiently endured?" — *wa lamma ya'lami Allahu alladhina jahadu minkum wa ya'lama al-sabirin* — and while Allah has not known those who struggled among you and knows the patient? The *'ilm* — the knowing — is the test's knowledge: the *lamma ya'lam* — not yet known — is the not-yet-tested; the *jahadu* — struggled, fought — is the Jihad; the *sabirin* — the patient — is the endurance. Al-Jalalayn: "through knowledge manifested outwardly" — the test makes the knowledge manifest: the Jihad and the patience, the struggling and the enduring, are known through the test of the days. Ibn Kathir's context: the verse is in the section of the losses of Uhud — the believers who suffered are asked: the Paradise is not the assumption but the proven; the Jihad and the patience are the conditions — the *jahadu* and the *sabirin* are the two categories the test reveals.

The verse's place: the question of 142 follows the purposes of 140–141 — the alternation, the purification, the destruction — and asks the believers: do you think the entrance is without the proof? The *jahadu* — the struggle — and the *sabirin* — the patient — are the two: the fighters and the enduring — the *mujahidun* and the *sabirun* — the ones whose Jihad and patience are known by the test. Ibn Kathir's frame of the whole section: the losses of Uhud and the question — the Paradise is not the gratuitous entrance but the proven — the *'ilm* of Allah manifested through the test.

For the reader, the verse is the question of the test's necessity. The believer is taught: the Paradise is not entered without the proof — the *hasb* — the assumption — of the entrance without the test is the rebuked; the *jahadu* — the struggle — and the *sabirin* — the patience — are the known by the test. The verse's *lamma ya'lam* — not yet known — is the condition: the knowing of the Jihad and the patience is the prerequisite of the entrance; the test of Uhud — the losses, the wounds, the flight and the patience — was the knowing. And the verse's *'ilm* — the knowledge — is the divine: the knowledge manifested through the testing — the *jahadu* and the *sabirin* — the struggling and the enduring — the believers' two qualities that the test proves and the Paradise awaits.

## Verse 143

Verse 143 says: *You certainly wished ˹for the opportunity˺ for martyrdom before encountering it, now you have seen it with your own eyes.*

In simple terms, the verse rebukes the inconsistency: you wished for the chance of martyrdom before you met it — now you have seen it before your own eyes (and retreated).

The verse is the rebuke of the wish and the flight. Ibn Kathir: "You did indeed wish for death before you met it. Now you have seen it openly with your own eyes." Al-Jalalayn: "You were longing for death before you met it — when you said, 'Would that we had a day like the Day of Badr in order to attain what its martyrs attained.' Now you have seen it, that is the cause of it, war, looking on, that is with your eyes open and contemplating the conditions — so why did you retreat?" The verse's *wa laqad kuntum tamannawuna al-mawta* — and you certainly wished for death — is the wish; the *min qabli an talqawhu* — before you met it — is the precedence; and the *faqad ra'aytumuhu wa antum tanzurun* — and you have seen it while you looked on — is the seeing.

"You certainly wished ˹for the opportunity˺ for martyrdom" — *wa laqad kuntum tamannawuna al-mawta* — and you certainly used to wish for death. The *tamanni* — the wishing, the longing — is the desire: the wish of the death in the cause; the *al-mawt* — the death — is the martyrdom of the cause. Al-Jalalayn: the wish was "Would that we had a day like the Day of Badr in order to attain what its martyrs attained" — the longing of the ones who had missed the triumph and the martyrdom of Badr. The verse's *kuntum* — you were — is the continuity: the wish was the repeated longing — the believers who had missed Badr wished for the day of its like; the *tamanni* — the wishing — was the announcement of the commitment.

"before encountering it, now you have seen it with your own eyes" — *min qabli an talqawhu faqad ra'aytumuhu wa antum tanzurun* — before you met it, and you have seen it while you were looking on. The *liqa'* — the meeting — is the encounter: the death was wished before it was met; the *ru'yah* — the seeing — is the encounter's sight: the *ra'aytumuhu* — you have seen it — the war, the death, the martyrdom — the cause of the wish — was seen with the eyes. Al-Jalalayn: "now you have seen it — that is the cause of it, war — looking on — with your eyes open and contemplating the conditions — so why did you retreat?" The verse's *wa antum tanzurun* — while you looked on — is the sight's condition: the death was before the eyes — the wounded, the killed, the seventy dead — and the ones who wished for it turned from it — the *tanzurun* — the looking — was the eyewitness of the thing they had wished.

The verse's rebuke: the wish of the death before the meeting and the retreat at the sight — the inconsistency of the *tamanni* and the *tawalli*. Ibn Kathir's frame: the believers who had missed Badr wished for the day of its like; at Uhud, the day came — the death was seen — and some retreated: the *tamanni* — the wish — was the words; the *ra'aytumuhu* — the seeing — was the test; the *tanzurun* — the looking — was the condition. The verse's question (implicit in Al-Jalalayn): so why did you retreat? — the wish and the flight, the longing and the retreat — the rebuke of the one who wished the test and failed it at the sight.

For the reader, the verse is the rebuke of the inconsistency of the wish. The believer is taught: the wish of the martyrdom — the *tamanni* — the longing of the one who missed Badr and wished its day — is the words; and the sight — the *ra'aytumuhu* — the death seen at Uhud — is the test. The verse's *wa antum tanzurun* — while you looked on — is the condition: the death was before the eyes — the wounded, the killed — and the wish turned to the retreat: the *tamanni* contradicted by the *tawalli*. The believer's lesson: the wish of the test is easy; the test's sight is the real; the *tamanni* of the martyrdom is the inward claim and the *tanzurun* is the outward proof — the wish and the sight, the claim and the test — the verse's rebuke of the one who said and fled. And the believer's program: the wish of the death in the cause is the sincere wish when the *sabr* — the patience — accompanies it; the *tamanni* without the *sabr* is the word contradicted by the retreat.

## Verse 144

Verse 144 says: *Muḥammad is no more than a messenger; other messengers have gone before him. If he were to die or to be killed, would you regress into disbelief? Those who do so will not harm Allah whatsoever. And Allah will reward those who are grateful.*

In simple terms, the verse corrects the crisis of the rumor: Muhammad is only a messenger, and messengers have passed before him; if he died or was killed, would you turn back to disbelief? Those who do so harm Allah not at all — and Allah will reward the grateful.

The verse is the surah's most famous correction: the believers' attachment to the Messenger corrected by his mortality. Ibn Kathir: "Muhammad is no more than a Messenger, and indeed Messengers have passed away before him. If he dies or is killed, will you then turn back on your heels? And he who turns back on his heels, not the least harm will he do to Allah; and Allah will reward the grateful." The verse's *wa ma Muhammadun illa Rasulun* — and Muhammad is not except a Messenger — is the identity; the *qad khalat min qablihi al-rusul* — messengers have passed before him — is the precedent; the *afa in mata aw qutila inqalabtum 'ala a'qabikum* — if he died or was killed, would you turn back on your heels — is the question; the *wa man yanqalib 'ala 'aqibayhi falan yadurra Allaha shay'an* — and whoever turns back will not harm Allah — is the futility; and the *wa sayajzi Allahu al-shakirin* — and Allah will reward the grateful — is the reward.

"Muhammad is no more than a messenger; other messengers have gone before him" — *wa ma Muhammadun illa Rasulun qad khalat min qablihi al-rusul* — and Muhammad is not except a Messenger; messengers have passed away before him. The *Rasul* — the Messenger — is the identity: Muhammad is the Messenger — not the worshipped, not the immortal; the *khalat* — passed away — is the precedent: the messengers before him died — the deaths of the prophets of the past. Al-Jalalayn: "he was not a worshipped being so that if he were to die you should turn back to your previous religion." The verse's *illa* — except — is the limitation: the exclusivity of the *Rasul* — he is the Messenger and nothing beyond; and the *qad khalat* — have passed — is the history: the messengers have gone — the death of the messengers is the pattern of the prophecy.

The occasion of the verse, per Ibn Kathir: "When Muslims suffered defeat in battle at Uhud and some of them were killed, Shaytan shouted, 'Muhammad has been killed.' Ibn Qami'ah went back to the idolators and claimed, 'I have killed Muhammad.' Some Muslims believed this rumor and thought that the Messenger of Allah had been killed, claiming that this could happen, for Allah narrated that this occurred to many Prophets before. Therefore, the Muslims' resolve was weakened and they did not actively participate in battle." The verse's revelation: "he is to deliver Allah's Message and may be killed in the process, just as what happened to many Prophets before." Ibn Abi Najih's report: a man of the Muhajirin passed an Ansari bleeding at Uhud and said, "O fellow! Did you know that Muhammad was killed?" — the Ansari: "Even if Muhammad was killed, he has indeed conveyed the Message. Therefore, defend your religion."

"If he were to die or to be killed, would you regress into disbelief?" — *afa in mata aw qutila inqalabtum 'ala a'qabikum* — if he dies or is killed, will you turn back on your heels? The *inqilab* — the turning back — is the regression: the *a'qab* — the heels — the turning of the retreat; the *inqalabtum* — you would turn back — the return to the disbelief, the *riddah* — the regress. Al-Jalalayn: "will you return to unbelief? — the last statement is the locus of the interrogative of denial." The verse's *in mata aw qutila* — if he died or was killed — is the condition: the death or the killing of the Messenger — the human possibility — and the question: would you turn back? — the regression of the ones whose attachment was not to the truth but to the person.

"Those who do so will not harm Allah whatsoever" — *wa man yanqalib 'ala 'aqibayhi falan yadurra Allaha shay'an* — and whoever turns back on his heels will not harm Allah in the least. The *darar* — the harm — the futility: the regression harms not Allah — the religion of Allah is not harmed by the turners; Al-Jalalayn: "he will not harm God in any way but will be harming himself." Ibn Kathir: the *shakirin* — the grateful — are those who "obeyed Allah, defended His religion and followed His Messenger whether he was alive or dead." The verse's *falan yadurra* — will not harm — is the futility of the retreat: the *yanqalib* — the turner — harms himself, not Allah; the religion is the divine, the defeat of the turner is himself.

"And Allah will reward those who are grateful" — *wa sayajzi Allahu al-shakirin* — and Allah will reward the grateful. The *jaza'* — the reward — is the divine: the *shakirin* — the grateful — are the rewarded — the ones who obey, who defend, who follow — the *shukr* — the gratitude — the constancy after the death or the killing of the Messenger. Ibn Kathir: "those who obeyed Allah, defended His religion and followed His Messenger whether he was alive or dead." The verse's *sayajzi* — He will reward — is the promise: the grateful are rewarded — the constancy of the ones who did not regress.

The verse's application, recorded by Ibn Kathir: "The Sahih, Musnad and Sunan collections gathered various chains of narration stating that Abu Bakr recited this Ayah when the Messenger of Allah died." Al-Bukhari from 'A'ishah: Abu Bakr came riding from As-Sunh, dismounted, entered the Masjid, went to the Prophet — covered with a marked blanket — uncovered his face, knelt and kissed him, wept: "My father and my mother be sacrificed for you! Allah will not combine two deaths on you. You have died the death which was written for you." Ibn 'Abbas: Abu Bakr came out while 'Umar addressed the people; he told him to sit but 'Umar refused; Abu Bakr said, "To proceed; whoever among you worshipped Muhammad, then Muhammad is dead, but whoever worshipped Allah, Allah is alive and will never die" — and recited the verse. "By Allah, it was as if the people never knew that Allah had revealed this verse before until Abu Bakr recited it, and then whoever heard it started reciting it." Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib from 'Umar: "When I heard Abu Bakr recite this Ayah, my feet could not hold me, and I fell to the ground."

For the reader, the verse is the correction of the attachment and the doctrine of the constancy. The believer is taught: Muhammad is the Messenger — the *Rasul* — and the *khalat al-rusul* — the messengers before — the mortality of the prophecy; the faith is not the attachment to the person but the worship of Allah — the *shakirin* — the grateful — the constancy. The verse's *inqalabtum* — would you turn back — is the question of the regression: the ones whose faith was the person's attachment regress at his death; the ones whose faith is Allah's worship remain. And the *falan yadurra Allaha* — will not harm Allah — is the futility: the regression harms only the turner; the religion of Allah is the divine, not the human's. The verse's *shakirin* — the grateful — is the doctrine: those who obey and defend and follow — whether the Messenger is alive or dead — the *shukr* — the gratitude — the constancy of the faith. And the verse's history: the death of the Prophet and Abu Bakr's recitation — "whoever worshipped Muhammad, Muhammad is dead, but whoever worshipped Allah, Allah is alive" — the verse's fulfillment in the community's first crisis.

## Verse 145

Verse 145 says: *No soul can ever die without Allah’s Will at the destined time. Those who desire worldly gain, We will let them have it, and those who desire heavenly reward, We will grant it to them. And We will reward those who are grateful.*

In simple terms, the verse states the law of the death: no soul dies except by Allah's will at the appointed time; whoever desires the world's gain gets it, whoever desires the Hereafter's reward gets it — and We will reward the grateful.

The verse completes the doctrine of 144: the death of the Messenger is the pattern of the death of every soul. Ibn Kathir: "no person dies except by Allah's decision, after he has finished the term that Allah has destined for him." The verse's *wa ma kana li-nafsin an tamuta illa bi-idhni Allahi kitaban mu'ajjalan* — and it is not for a soul to die except by Allah's leave, a term appointed — is the law; the *man yurid thawaba al-dunya nu'tihi minha* — whoever desires the reward of the world, We give him of it — is the first option; and the *wa man yurid thawaba al-akhirah nu'tihi minha* — and whoever desires the Hereafter's reward, We give him thereof — is the second; and the *wa sanajzi al-shakirin* — and We will reward the grateful — is the close.

"No soul can ever die without Allah's Will at the destined time" — *wa ma kana li-nafsin an tamuta illa bi-idhni Allahi kitaban mu'ajjalan* — and no soul can die except by Allah's leave, a decree with a term. The *nafs* — the soul — is the individual: every soul; the *idhn Allah* — the permission of Allah — is the leave: no death except by the divine permission; the *kitab mu'ajjal* — a term appointed — is the decree: the written appointment. Ibn Kathir: parallels 35:11 and 6:2 — "no aged man is granted a length of life nor is a part cut off from his life, but it is in a Book"; "He it is Who has created you from clay, and then has decreed a stated term." The verse's *illa bi-idhnihi* — except by His leave — is the exclusivity: the death is the divine — not the accident, not the human's will, not the enemy's sword; and the *kitaban mu'ajjalan* — the term appointed — is the decree: the death's time is the written appointment. Ibn Kathir: "This Ayah encourages cowards to participate in battle; for doing so, or avoiding battle neither decreases, nor increases the life term."

"Those who desire worldly gain, We will let them have it, and those who desire heavenly reward, We will grant it to them" — *man yurid thawaba al-dunya nu'tihi minha wa man yurid thawaba al-akhirah nu'tihi minha* — whoever desires the reward of the world, We give him of it; and whoever desires the reward of the Hereafter, We give him thereof. The *thawab* — the reward — is the two: the world's and the Hereafter's; the *nu'tihi minha* — We give him of it — is the divine giving: the desirer of the world's reward gets of it — what Allah decides — but no share of the Hereafter (42:20); the desirer of the Hereafter's reward gets of it — the *thawab* of the Hereafter — with what Allah decides of this life as well. Ibn Kathir: "whoever works for the sake of this life will only earn what Allah decides he will earn. However, he will not have a share in the Hereafter. Whoever works for the sake of the Hereafter, Allah will give him a share in the Hereafter, along with what He decides for him in this life" (42:20, 17:18-19). The verse's *yurid* — desires — is the orientation: the desire of the world's gain and the desire of the Hereafter's reward — the two orientations of the deed.

"And We will reward those who are grateful" — *wa sanajzi al-shakirin* — and We will reward the grateful. The *jaza'* — the reward — is the divine: the *shakirin* — the grateful — are the rewarded. Ibn Kathir: "We shall award them with Our favor and mercy in this life and the Hereafter, according to the degree of their appreciation [of Allah] and their good deeds." The verse's *sanajzi* — We will reward — is the promise: the grateful — the ones who appreciate the divine favor's source — are rewarded in both lives — the *shukr* — the gratitude — the response of the believers.

The tradition of Hujr bin 'Adi, per Ibn Abi Hatim (from Habib bin Suhban): a Muslim man said in a battle, "What prevents you from crossing this river (the Euphrates) to the enemy?" — reciting the verse — he crossed riding his horse, the Muslims followed, and the enemy fled shouting "Diwan" (crazy) — the verse's encouragement: the death is not of the crossing's fear; the term is the divine — the believer who crosses the river trusts the appointment.

For the reader, the verse is the law of the term and the two orientations. The believer is taught: no soul dies except by Allah's leave at the appointed term — the *idhn* and the *kitab* — the permission and the decree: the death is not the accident but the appointment; the cowardice of the battle and the courage of the crossing both meet the same term — doing or avoiding neither increases nor decreases the life. The verse's *yurid* — the desire — is the orientation: the world's reward and the Hereafter's reward — the two desires, the two giving; the desirer of the world gets of it, without the Hereafter's share; the desirer of the Hereafter gets of it, with the world's according to the decision. And the *sanajzi al-shakirin* — the reward of the grateful — is the close: the *shukr* — the gratitude — the appreciation of the favor's source — is rewarded in both lives. The believer's program: the *idhn* of the death — the trust of the term; the *thawab* of the Hereafter — the orientation of the deed; and the *shukr* — the gratitude — the response of the believers.

## Verse 146

Verse 146 says: *˹Imagine˺ how many devotees fought along with their prophets and never faltered despite whatever ˹losses˺ they suffered in the cause of Allah, nor did they weaken or give in! Allah loves those who persevere.*

In simple terms, the verse holds up the example of the past: how many devotees fought alongside their prophets and never faltered whatever losses they suffered in Allah's cause — they did not weaken or give in — and Allah loves those who persevere.

The verse is the comfort of the example: the losses of Uhud are the patterns of the past. Ibn Kathir: "And many a Prophet fought and along with him many Ribbiyyun. But they never lost heart for that which befell them in Allah's way, nor did they weaken nor degrade themselves. And Allah loves the patient." The verse's *ka'ayyin min nabiyyin qatala ma'ahu ribbiyyuna kathir* — how many a Prophet fought, with him many Ribbiyyun — is the example; the *fama wahana lima asabahum fi sabil Allah* — and they did not weaken for what befell them in the way of Allah — is the firmness; the *wa ma da'ufu wa ma istakanu* — nor did they weaken or degrade themselves — is the endurance; and the *wa Allahu yuhibbu al-sabirin* — and Allah loves the patient — is the love.

"how many devotees fought along with their prophets" — *ka'ayyin min nabiyyin qatala ma'ahu ribbiyyuna kathir* — how many a Prophet fought, with him many Ribbiyyun. The *ribbiyyun* — the devotees, the lordly ones, the large bands — is the key word. Ibn Kathir: Ibn Jarir — many Prophets and their companions were killed in earlier times; Ibn Ishaq (in his Sirah; defended by As-Suhayli) — many a Prophet was killed and had many companions whose resolve did not weaken after their Prophet died, not feeble before the enemy, not losing heart at what they suffered in Jihad — "This is patience, and Allah loves the patient"; Ibn Mas'ud (Ath-Thawri) — Ribbiyyun means thousands; Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, 'Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Ar-Rabi', 'Ata' Al-Khurasani — large bands; Al-Hasan (Ma'mmar) — many scholars, patient and pious scholars. The verse's *ma'ahu* — with him — is the accompaniment: the devotees fought with their prophets — the *ribbiyyun* — the large bands — the companions of the prophets, the scholars, the devotees — the followers who fought alongside the prophets.

"and never faltered despite whatever ˹losses˺ they suffered in the cause of Allah" — *fama wahana lima asabahum fi sabil Allahi* — and they did not lose heart for what befell them in the way of Allah. The *wahn* — the weakness, the faltering — is the negative: they did not weaken; the *asabahum* — what befell them — is the losses: the deaths of the prophets, the wounds, the defeats; the *fi sabil Allah* — in the way of Allah — is the cause. Ibn Kathir: the *ribbiyyun* of the past "never lost heart for that which befell them in Allah's way" — the deaths of the prophets, the losses of the cause — the *wahn* — the weakening — was not theirs. The verse's *mā asabahum* — what befell them — is the test: the losses of the cause — the killing of the prophets — the *sabil Allah* — the way — the cause.

"nor did they weaken or give in!" — *wa ma da'ufu wa ma istakanu* — nor did they weaken nor degrade themselves. The *du'f* — the weakness — and the *istikana* — the submission, the humiliation, the giving in — are the two negatives: they did not weaken and did not give in. Ibn Kathir: Qatadah and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas — "nor did they weaken" — after their Prophet was killed; "nor did they degrade themselves" — by reverting from the true guidance and religion; they fought on the path that Allah's Prophet fought on until they met Allah; Ibn 'Abbas — nor became humiliated; As-Suddi and Ibn Zayd — did not give in to the enemy. "after Allah had brought victory within your reach" — *min ba'di ma arakum ma tuhibbun* — after He showed you what you love. The *ara* — He showed — is the display: the victory — what you love — shown: the defeat of the enemy, the flight, the rout — the *ma tuhibbun* — what you love — the victory over the disbelievers. Ibn Kathir: "after He showed you what you love, that is, victory over the disbelievers." The verse's *min ba'd* — after — is the sequence: the victory shown, then the failure — the *ara* — the showing — and the *fashal* — the weakening — the victory in reach and the courage lost.

"Some of you were after worldly gain while others desired a heavenly reward" — *minkum man yuridu al-dunya wa minkum man yuridu al-akhirah* — among you the one who desires the world and among you the one who desires the Hereafter. The *yurid al-dunya* — desires the world — is the booty: Ibn Kathir — "referring to those who sought to collect the booty when they saw the enemy being defeated"; the *yurid al-akhirah* — desires the Hereafter — is the station: Al-Jalalayn — "holding to it until he was slain, such as 'Abdullah bin Jubayr and his companions." The verse's *minkum... wa minkum* — among you... and among you — is the division: the two desires within the ranks — the world's booty and the Hereafter's station — the two orientations of the command's abandonment and the command's holding.

"He denied you victory over them as a test, yet He has pardoned you" — *thumma sarafakum 'anhum liyabtaliyakum wa laqad 'afa 'ankum* — then He turned you away from them to test you, and He has surely forgiven you. The *sarf* — the turning away — is the defeat: He made you flee from them — the *sarf* — the turning — "He turned you back in defeat" (Al-Jalalayn); the *liyabtaliyakum* — to test you — is the purpose; the *'afa* — He forgave — is the pardon: "He forgave the error you committed, because, and Allah knows best, the idolators were many and well supplied, while Muslims had few men and few supplies" (Ibn Kathir). The verse's *sadaqakum* — the fulfillment — and the *sarafakum* — the turning — the promise fulfilled and the defeat sent — the test of the two; the *'afa* — the pardon — the grace after the failure — the *fadl* — the bounty — "Allah is Most Gracious to the believers."

"And Allah is Gracious to the believers" — *wa Allahu dhu fadlin 'ala al-mu'minin* — and Allah is the possessor of bounty over the believers. The *fadl* — the bounty, the grace — is the divine: the *dhu fadl* — the possessor of the bounty — over the believers; the *'afa* of the pardon and the *fadl* of the bounty — the grace after the failure. Ibn Kathir: "He forgave the error you committed... Allah is Most Gracious to the believers."

The hadiths of the account, per Ibn Kathir: Al-Bukhari/Al-Bara' — the archers' instruction ("Retain your position, and if you see that we have defeated them, do not abandon your positions..."); the enemy fled and their women fled up the mountain; the archers: "The booty, the booty!"; Ibn Jubayr: "Allah's Messenger commanded me not to allow you to abandon your position"; they refused, left, and the Muslims were defeated — seventy killed; Abu Sufyan's shouts — "Is Muhammad present...? Is the son of Abu Quhafah...? Is the son of Al-Khattab...?" — "Do not answer him"; 'Umar: "You lie, O enemy of Allah!"; Abu Sufyan: "O Hubal, be high!" — "Say, Allah is Higher and more Sublime"; "We have the (idol) Al-'Uzza and you have no 'Uzza" — "Say, Allah is our protector and you have no protector"; "Our victory today is vengeance for yours in the battle of Badr, and in war the victory is always undecided and is shared in turns by the belligerents." Muhammad bin Ishaq/Zubayr — the archers went down when the enemy fled, "they uncovered our back lines to the horsemen," who attacked from behind; "Muhammad has been killed" was shouted; the enemy's flag fell until 'Amrah bint 'Alqamah picked it up. Al-Bukhari/Anas — Anas bin An-Nadr missed Badr ("if Allah gives me a chance to fight with the Messenger of Allah, then Allah will see how bravely I will fight"), at Uhud when the Muslims fled: "O Allah! I apologize to You for what these have done, and I denounce what these pagans have done" — to Sa'd bin Mu'adh: "Where are you! Paradise! I am smelling its aroma coming from before Uhud" — more than eighty wounds, mutilated, recognized only by his sister.

For the reader, the verse is the account of the turn and the lessons of the failure. The believer is taught: the promise was fulfilled — the *sadaq* — the victory at the start; the failures — the *fashal* (the lost courage), the *tanazu'* (the dispute), the *'isyan* (the disobedience) — the archers' mistake — the station abandoned for the booty; the *min ba'di ma arakum* — after the victory shown — the reach and the loss. The verse's *dunya* and *akhirah* — the world's gain and the Hereafter's reward — the two desires of the ranks: the booty-seekers and the station-holders — Ibn Jubayr and his companions holding till slain. And the *sarf* and the *'afa* — the turning away and the pardon — the test and the grace: the defeat tested the believers and the *fadl* — the bounty — pardoned the faithful. The believer's lesson: the victory is the divine (the *idhn* — the permission), the failure is the human (the *'isyan* — the disobedience), and the pardon is the divine (the *'afa* — the grace). And the believer's program: the *akhirah* over the *dunya* — the Hereafter's reward over the world's booty — the station held to the end — the *thabat* of Ibn Jubayr and the *sabr* of Anas bin An-Nadr.

## Verse 153

Verse 153 says: *˹Remember˺ when you were running far away ˹in panic˺—not looking at anyone—while the Messenger was calling to you from behind! So Allah rewarded your disobedience with distress upon distress. Now, do not grieve over the victory you were denied or the injury you suffered. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do.*

In simple terms, the verse recalls the flight: you were running away in panic without looking at anyone while the Messenger called you from behind; Allah repaid your disobedience with distress upon distress — so do not grieve over the victory denied or the injury suffered; Allah is All-Aware of what you do.

The verse is the rebuke and the lesson of the flight. Ibn Kathir: "when you ran away without even casting a side glance at anyone, and the Messenger was in your rear calling you back. There did Allah give you one distress after another by way of requital, to teach you not to grieve for that which had escaped you, nor for what struck you." The verse's *idh tus'iduna wa la talwuna 'ala ahad* — when you were going up (the mountain) and not turning to anyone — is the flight; the *wa al-Rasulu yad'ukum fi ukhrakum* — and the Messenger was calling you in your rear — is the call; the *fa'athabakum ghamman bi-ghammin* — so He rewarded you with distress upon distress — is the requital; and the *la ta'sa 'ala ma fatakum wa la ma asabakum* — do not grieve over what escaped you nor what struck you — is the lesson; and the *wa Allahu khabirun bima ta'malun* — and Allah is All-Aware of what you do — is the knowledge.

"when you were running far away ˹in panic˺—not looking at anyone" — *idh tus'iduna wa la talwuna 'ala ahad* — when you went up (the mountain) and did not turn to anyone. The *su'ud* — the going up — is the ascent: Ibn Kathir — Al-Hasan and Qatadah — "go up the mountain"; the *la talwuna* — not turning — the panic: "without even casting a side glance at anyone — you did not glance at anyone else due to shock, fear and fright." As-Suddi: some Muslims ran to Madinah, some went up Mount Uhud to a rock and stood on it. The verse's *tus'iduna* — you went up — is the flight of the panic: the ascent of the mountain, the running from the battle — the *la talwuna* — the not-turning — the panic's blindness.

"while the Messenger was calling to you from behind!" — *wa al-Rasulu yad'ukum fi ukhrakum* — and the Messenger was calling you in your rear. The *yad'u* — he was calling — is the call: the Messenger called from behind the fleeing — "to stop fleeing from the enemy and to return and fight" (Ibn Kathir). As-Suddi: "the Messenger of Allah kept heralding, 'Come to me, O servants of Allah! Come to me, O servants of Allah!'" The verse's *fi ukhrakum* — in your rear — is the position: the Messenger behind the fleeing — the *ukhra* — the rear — the call from behind — the believers fleeing and the Messenger calling — the contrast of the flight and the call.

"So Allah rewarded your disobedience with distress upon distress" — *fa'athabakum ghamman bi-ghammin* — so He requited you with distress upon distress. The *'adhab* — the requital, the reward — is the correspondence: the *ghamm* — the distress — upon the *ghamm* — the distress — the *ghamman bi-ghammin* — the distress upon distress — the requital of the disobedience. Ibn Kathir: "There did Allah give you one distress after another by way of requital." The verse's *'athabakum* — He requited you — is the divine: the disobedience — the flight, the abandon of the command — requited with the *ghamm* — the distress — the grief of the defeat, the grief of the loss — the *ghamm* upon the *ghamm* — the requital of the command's breach.

"Now, do not grieve over the victory you were denied or the injury you suffered" — *la ta'sawa 'ala ma fatakum wa la ma asabakum* — so that you do not grieve over what escaped you nor what struck you. The *ta'sawa* — grieve, be sad — the negative: the *fatakum* — what escaped you — the victory denied; the *asabakum* — what struck you — the injury suffered. Ibn Kathir: "to teach you not to grieve for that which had escaped you, nor for what struck you." The verse's *la ta'sawa* — do not grieve — is the lesson: the distress was the requital to teach the non-grief: the victory escaped (Badr's victory lost at the turn), the injury struck (the wounds, the seventy dead) — and the *ghamm* taught: do not grieve — the *ghamman bi-ghammin* — the requital — and the *la ta'sawa* — the lesson — the non-grief of the escaped and the struck.

"And Allah is All-Aware of what you do" — *wa Allahu khabirun bima ta'malun* — and Allah is All-Aware of what you do. The *khabir* — the All-Aware — is the knowledge: the *bima ta'malun* — of what you do — the deeds of the flight and the holding, the command's abandon and the station's defense — all known. The verse's *khabir* — the All-Aware — is the close: the requital's correspondence and the knowledge's comprehensiveness — the *ghamm* requited and the deeds known — the divine awareness of the flight and the call.

For the reader, the verse is the rebuke and the lesson of the flight. The believer is taught: the *su'ud* — the ascent — the panic's flight — the *la talwuna* — the not-turning — the shock and the fright; and the *yad'u* — the call — the Messenger calling from the rear — "Come to me, O servants of Allah!" — the flight and the call. And the *ghamman bi-ghammin* — the distress upon distress — the requital: the *'adhab* of the disobedience — the grief of the defeat — and the *la ta'sawa* — the non-grief — the lesson of the requital: the victory escaped and the injury struck — the *ghamm* taught the *ta'sa* — the grief's correction. The believer's lesson from Uhud: the flight of the panic and the call of the Messenger — the command's breach and the requital's distress — and the *la ta'sawa* — the non-grief — the trust of the *fata* and the *asaba* — the escaped and the struck — all in the *khabir* — the All-Aware — the divine knowledge of the deeds.

## Verse 154

Verse 154 says: *Then after distress, He sent down serenity in the form of drowsiness overcoming some of you, while others were disturbed by evil thoughts about Allah—the thoughts of ˹pre-Islamic˺ ignorance. They ask, “Do we have a say in the matter?” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “All matters are destined by Allah.” They conceal in their hearts what they do not reveal to you. They say ˹to themselves˺, “If we had any say in the matter, none of us would have come to die here.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Even if you were to remain in your homes, those among you who were destined to be killed would have met the same fate.” Through this, Allah tests what is within you and purifies what is in your hearts. And Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart.*

In simple terms, the verse contrasts the two groups after the distress: Allah sent down serenity as drowsiness over some — the believers; while others (the hypocrites) were disturbed by evil thoughts about Allah — the thoughts of ignorance. They ask whether they had a say in the matter; the answer: all matters are destined by Allah. They conceal their thinking — "if we had any say, we would not have died here"; the answer: even if you stayed home, those destined to be killed would have met the same fate. Through this, Allah tests and purifies what is in the hearts — and He knows best what is hidden there.

The verse is the contrast of the two groups after the distress and the doctrine of the decree. Ibn Kathir: "Allah reminds His servants of His favor when He sent down on them tranquillity and slumber that overcame them while they were carrying their weapons and feeling distress and grief." The verse's *thumma anzala 'alaykum min ba'di al-ghammi amanatan nu'asan* — then He sent down upon you, after the distress, security as slumber — is the serenity; the *yaghsha ta'ifatan minkum* — overcoming a party of you — is the believers; the *wa ta'ifatun qad ahammathum anfusuhum* — and a party worried about themselves — is the hypocrites; the *yazunnuna billahi ghayra al-haqqi zanna al-jahiliyyah* — thinking of Allah other than the truth, the thought of ignorance — is the evil thought; and the *yaquluna hal lana mina al-amri min shay'* — saying, do we have any part in the matter — is the question; and the *qul inna al-amra kullahu lillah* — say, the matter belongs wholly to Allah — is the answer; and the *yu'addibuna...liyabtaliya* — to test and purify — is the purpose.

"Then after distress, He sent down serenity in the form of drowsiness overcoming some of you" — *thumma anzala 'alaykum min ba'di al-ghammi amanatan nu'asan yaghsha ta'ifatan minkum* — then He sent down upon you, after the distress, security — slumber covering a party of you. The *amana* — the security — is the *nu'as* — the slumber — the divine: the serenity of the slumber — the *aman* — the safety — after the *ghamm* — the distress. Ibn Kathir: "slumber is a favor and carries meanings of calmness and safety" (8:11 — "when He covered you with a slumber as a security from Him"). Al-Bukhari/Anas from Abu Talhah: "I was among those who were overcome by slumber during the battle of Uhud. My sword fell from my hand several times and I would pick it up, then it would fall and I would pick it up again"; at-Tirmidhi/An-Nasa'i/Al-Hakim — "everyone's head was nodding from slumber" (at-Tirmidhi: hasan sahih). Al-Jalalayn: the believers "would become dizzy under their shields and their swords would fall from their hands." The verse's *yaghsha* — covering — is the slumber's cover: the *amanah* — the security — the drowsiness — the calm of the believers — the *ta'ifah* — the party — the people of faith, certainty, firmness and reliance (Ibn Kathir) — certain that Allah shall give victory to His Messenger.

"while others were disturbed by evil thoughts about Allah—the thoughts of ˹pre-Islamic˺ ignorance" — *wa ta'ifatun qad ahammathum anfusuhum yazunnuna billahi ghayra al-haqqi zanna al-jahiliyyah* — and a party whose own souls distressed them, thinking of Allah other than the truth — the thought of ignorance. The *hamma* — the worrying — is the self-concern: the *ta'ifah* — the party — the hypocrites — "who only thought about themselves" — "the most cowardly people and those least likely to support the truth" (Ibn Kathir); the *yazunnuna billahi ghayra al-haqq* — the thinking — other than the truth — the *zann al-jahiliyyah* — the thought of ignorance: they thought the idolaters achieved ultimate victory and that Islam and its people would perish (48:12). The verse's *ahammathum anfusuhum* — their souls worried them — the self-absorbed: the distress of the hypocrites — the *zann* — the wrong thinking — of Allah — the doubt of the victory, the evil thought of the ignorance. Al-Jalalayn: "they caused them grief so that their only wish was their deliverance regardless of the Prophet and his Companions and they were unable to fall asleep — these were the hypocrites."

"They ask, 'Do we have a say in the matter?'" — *yaquluna hal lana mina al-amri min shay'* — they say, do we have any part in the matter? The *hal lana* — do we have — is the question of the *amr* — the matter: the part of the affair — the say of the matter. Al-Jalalayn: read interrogative *hal* as negative *ma* — "we have no part in the assistance which we were promised." Ibn Kathir: the hypocrites asked whether they had any part — the question of the matter's control. The verse's *mina al-amri* — of the matter — is the affair: the *amr* of the battle, the affair of the success — the hypocrites' question of the part.

"Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ 'All matters are destined by Allah.'" — *qul inna al-amra kullahu lillah* — say: indeed the matter, all of it, belongs to Allah. The *kullahu* — all of it — is the totality: the *amr* — the matter — wholly Allah's; the *qul* — say — is the answer: the *kulluhu lillah* — the whole belongs to Allah. Al-Jalalayn: "the decree is His; He does what He wills." The verse's *illah* — to Allah — is the ownership: the matter — the victory, the defeat, the decree — the whole belongs to the divine — the answer to the question of the part: no part of the matter is theirs — the *kulluhu* — the whole — is Allah's.

"They conceal in their hearts what they do not reveal to you" — *yukhfuna fi anfusihim ma la yubduna laka* — they conceal within themselves what they do not disclose to you. The *ikhfa'* — the concealing — is the hidden: the *anfus* — the selves, the hearts — the *ma la yubdun* — what they do not reveal — the hidden thought. Al-Jalalayn: "they conceal within their hearts what they do not manifest to you" — the *zann* — the doubt, the wrong thought — hidden from the Prophet. The verse's *la yubduna* — they do not reveal — is the concealment: the hypocrites' hidden thinking — the *zann* of the ignorance — concealed in the hearts.

"They say ˹to themselves˺, 'If we had any say in the matter, none of us would have come to die here.'" — *yaquluna law kana lana mina al-amri shay'un ma qutilna hahuna* — they say: had we any part in the matter, we would not have been killed here. The *law kana lana* — had we had — the conditional: the *shay'* — the part — the *qutilna* — we would not have been killed. Al-Jalalayn: "Had the choice been ours we would not have set out and thus been slain; but we were forced to set out." The verse's *qutilna hahuna* — killed here — is the grievance: the death at Uhud — the killed here — the mortal of the matter — the hypocrites' logic of the part: if they had the say they would not have been killed — the counterfactual of the decree's denial.

"Say, 'Even if you were to remain in your homes, those among you who were destined to be killed would have met the same fate.'" — *qul law kuntum fi buyutikum labaraza alladhina kutiba 'alayhimu al-qatlu ila madaji'ihim* — say: if you were in your houses, those for whom the killing was written would have emerged to their death beds. The *kutiba* — it was written — is the decree: the *qatl* — the killing — written — the appointed; the *madaji'* — the death beds, the resting places — the places of the fall. Al-Jalalayn: "those of you for whom it had been appointed that they be slain would have sallied forth to the places where they were to lie — the battleground where they were to fall — and they would have been slain; and their staying put at home would not have saved them, for God's decree will be inevitably." The verse's *labaraza* — they would have emerged — is the inevitability: the written killing emerges to its place — the homes do not save — the *madaji'* — the death beds — the appointed — the decree's inevitability.

"Through this, Allah tests what is within you and purifies what is in your hearts" — *wa liyabtaliya Allahu ma fi sudurikum wa liyumahhisa ma fi qulubikum* — and so that Allah might test what is in your breasts and purify what is in your hearts. The *ibtilah* — the testing — is the *sudur* — the breasts: the *ma fi sudurikum* — what is in your breasts — of sincerity or hypocrisy; the *tamhis* — the purification — is the *qulub* — the hearts: the *ma fi qulubikum* — what is in your hearts. Al-Jalalayn: "that God might try that He might test what was in your breasts — your hearts — of sincerity or hypocrisy — and that He might prove that He might distinguish what was in your hearts." The verse's *liyabtaliya* — to test — and the *liyumahhisa* — to purify — are the purposes: the test of the breasts and the purification of the hearts — the *zann* of the hypocrites and the *iman* of the believers — known and purified.

"And Allah knows best what is ˹hidden˺ in the heart" — *wa Allahu 'alimun bi dhati al-sudur* — and Allah is Knowing of what is in the breasts. The *'alim* — the Knowing — is the divine: the *dhat al-sudur* — the contents of the breasts — known. Al-Jalalayn: "God knows what is in the breasts — what is in the hearts. Nothing can be hidden from Him, and He tries people only to make matters manifest for them." The verse's *'alimun bi dhati al-sudur* — the Knowing of the breasts' contents — is the close: the hidden thoughts — the concealed *zann* — known to the divine; the test and the purification — the manifestation of the known.

For the reader, the verse is the contrast of the two groups and the doctrine of the decree. The believer is taught: the *amana* — the security — the slumber — the divine over the distressed: the believers' calm — the swords falling, the heads nodding — the *ta'ifah* of the faith; and the *zann* — the evil thought — of the hypocrites: the doubt of the victory, the self-absorption, the *zann al-jahiliyyah* — the thought of ignorance. The verse's *kullahu lillah* — the whole belongs to Allah — is the doctrine: the matter — all of it — the divine's; the *kutiba* — the written — the decree: the killing written, the death beds appointed — the homes do not save — the decree inevitable. And the *liyabtaliya* and the *liyumahhisa* — the test and the purification — the purposes: the breasts tested, the hearts purified — the *zann* and the *iman* — manifest; the *'alim bi dhat al-sudur* — the Knowing of the breasts' contents — the divine knowledge of the hidden. The believer's confidence: the *kulluhu lillah* — the matter wholly Allah's — the decree's inevitability — the *Qadar* of the divine; and the *liyumahhisa* — the purification — the *tamhis* of the faithful — the test manifesting and purifying.

## Verse 155

Verse 155 says: *Indeed, those ˹believers˺ who fled on the day when the two armies met were made to slip by Satan because of their misdeeds. But Allah has pardoned them. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.*

In simple terms, the verse gives the verdict on those who fled: they were made to slip by Satan because of their misdeeds — but Allah has pardoned them; He is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.

The verse is the pardon of the fleeing believers. Ibn Kathir: "Those of you who turned back on the day the two hosts met, Shaytan only caused them to err because of some of what they had earned. But Allah, indeed, has forgiven them. Surely, Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Forbearing." The verse's *inna alladhina tawallaw minkum yawma iltaqa al-jam'an* — those of you who turned back on the day the two hosts met — is the fugitives; the *innama istazallahumu al-shaytanu bi-ba'di ma kasabu* — Shaytan only caused them to slip because of some of what they earned — is the cause; the *wa laqad 'afa Allahu 'anhum* — and Allah has certainly pardoned them — is the pardon; and the *inna Allaha ghafurun halim* — and Allah is Forgiving, Forbearing — is the names.

"those ˹believers˺ who fled on the day when the two armies met" — *inna alladhina tawallaw minkum yawma iltaqa al-jam'an* — those of you who turned back on the day the two hosts met. The *tawalli* — the turning back, the fleeing — is the retreat: the *yawm iltaqa al-jam'an* — the day the two hosts met — Uhud — the two hosts of the Muslims and the disbelievers. Al-Jalalayn: "the Muslims with the exception of twelve men" — the turners — the fugitives of the day. The verse's *minkum* — of you — is the address: the turners — the believers of the day — the *tawallaw* — the ones who turned — the day of the meeting of the two hosts.

"were made to slip by Satan because of their misdeeds" — *innama istazallahumu al-shaytanu bi-ba'di ma kasabu* — only Shaytan caused them to slip because of some of what they earned. The *istizlal* — the causing to slip — is the Satanic: the *shaytan* — the tempter — the *istazallahum* — caused them to slip — the *ba'di ma kasabu* — some of what they earned — the misdeeds: the disobedience of the Prophet's command. Al-Jalalayn: "through some of what they had earned of sins — namely when they disobeyed the Prophet's command." The verse's *innama* — only — is the limitation: the slip — the *istizlal* — the Satanic — through their earning: the *kasb* — the earning — the disobedience — the cause of the slip — the *shaytan* used the earned misdeeds to trip them.

"But Allah has pardoned them" — *wa laqad 'afa Allahu 'anhum* — and Allah has certainly pardoned them. The *'afw* — the pardon — is the divine: the *'afa 'anhum* — He pardoned them — the fugitives of the day — the *'afw* of the slip. The verse's *laqad* — certainly — is the emphasis: the pardon is the certain — the *'afa* — the divine over the *tawalli* — the slip forgiven. Ibn Kathir: "But Allah, indeed, has forgiven them" — the pardon of the turners — the believers' flight — the *'afw* of the divine.

"Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing" — *inna Allaha ghafurun halim* — indeed, Allah is Forgiving, Forbearing. The *Ghafur* — the All-Forgiving — and the *Halim* — the Forbearing — are the names: the forgiveness of the slip and the forbearance of the punishment — the *hilm* — the restraint — "hastening not against the disobedient with punishment" (Al-Jalalayn). The verse's *Ghafur* and *Halim* — the forgiving and the forbearing — are the divine: the *'afw* of the fugitives — the *hilm* — the forbearance — the reward after the slip — the names of the pardon.

The verse's place: the pardon of the fugitives of Uhud — the *tawallaw* — the ones who turned — were the *istazallahum* — the slipped — by the *shaytan* — through the *kasb* — the misdeeds — and the *'afa* — the pardon — the *Ghafur* and the *Halim* — the divine. ## Verse 156

The verse's *in* and *faman* — the if and the who — the two questions: the help's exclusivity — the *nusrah* — and the *tawakkul* — the trust — the believers' reliance on the only helper.

For the reader, the verse is the exclusivity of the help and the command of the trust. The believer is taught: the *nusrah* of Allah — the *fala ghalib* — the no-overcomer — the helped are not overcome; and the *khidhlan* — the forsaking — the *faman dha* — the who — the rhetorical: no one after Him — the *yansurukum min ba'dihi* — the helper after Him — none. And the *tawakkul* — the trust — the command: the *'ala Allah* — upon Allah — the believers' reliance — the exclusive helper — the trusted. The verse's *falyatawakkal* — let them rely — is the believer's program: the trust of the divine — the *nusrah* and the *khidhlan* — the help and the forsaking — the exclusive — and the *tawakkul* — the reliance — the believer's answer. The believer's confidence: the help of Allah — the unovercome; the forsaking of Allah — the helpless; and the *tawakkul* — the trust — the believers' reliance on the only helper — the *'ala Allah* — the divine — the trust of the believers.
