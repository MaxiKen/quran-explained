It connects to its neighbours and to the whole Quran: the twelve leaders in 5:12, the light in 5:15-16 that guides to peace, the muhaymin guardian in 5:45 that corrects, and the warning not to follow desires in 5:49. Revelation came when Jews brought a fornication case and hid the stoning, and when hypocrites like Abdullah ibn Ubayy rushed to Jews fearing a turn of fortune. Hadith teaches that scholars who sell verses for a price will have no share, that the just will be on pulpits of light, and that the party of Allah prevails. Other verses — 2:79, 3:77, 4:58 — echo the trust to guard the Book.

For today apply it straight: in your contracts, your court, your home — judge by what Allah sent down in the measure given, do not sell a verse for a salary or a like, do not rush to taghut allies out of fear, and do not mock prayer. If scholars are silent about sinful speech as in 5:63, their silence manufactures. Choose the allies in 5:55 — Allah, Messenger, praying givers — and you are hizb Allah, the victors, not the mocked. That is how one verse becomes a daily scale and twenty become a rope from Al-Baqarah's qiblah to the Table.
