=1=

"O you who believe, fulfil contracts. Grazing livestock has been made lawful for you — except what will be recited — hunting is not lawful while you are in pilgrimage. Indeed, Allah judges as He wills."

This verse sits in the opening flow of the Table Spread, building on the knot of contracts and the light that came to make hidden things clear. In simple words it reminds the reader that fulfilling what Allah made lawful and good, remembering His favour, and holding to justice are one rope. The first listeners in Medina — fresh from Hudaybiyyah where they were barred from the House, now hearing 'Today I perfected your religion' at Arafah — felt this directly: the religion that was perfected includes food, marriage, purification, and standing for justice even against hated people.

It links to its neighbours and to the whole Book: the grazing livestock lawful except what is recited in 5:1 points to the detailed forbidden list in 5:3, which points to the question of what remains lawful in 5:4, which opens to People of the Book food and women in 5:5. Wudu in 5:6 that completes favour echoes tayammum in 4:43 and ease in 2:185. The covenant in 5:7 and the justice in 5:8 recall 4:135, while the twelve leaders in 5:12 recall the Ansar, and the hardness in 5:13 recalls 2:74 and 4:155. Hadith adds that Muslims are bound by conditions, that the just will be on pulpits of light, that wudu erases sins, and that Allah loves the doers of good who pardon as in 5:13.

For today apply it straight: honour your contracts even when no one watches — your marriage, your work, your debt — eat only what is lawful and good, wash for prayer with presence, remember the favour and the heard-and-obeyed covenant, and stand for justice even when you dislike the other side. When a people stretched hands against you and Allah restrained them as in 5:11, respond not with revenge but with trust and taqwa. Follow the light that guides to ways of peace in 5:16, and do not say as the Christians did 'we are children' while forgetting. That is how one verse becomes a daily habit and twenty become a Table spread for all, simple for every household to live.

The first listeners in Medina — fresh from Hudaybiyyah where they were barred from the House, now hearing 'Today I perfected your religion' at Arafah — felt this directly: the religion that was perfected includes food, marriage, purification, and standing for justice even against hated people.

It links to its neighbours and to the whole Book: the grazing livestock lawful except what is recited in 5:1 points to the detailed forbidden list in 5:3, which points to the question of what remains lawful in 5:4, which opens to People of the Book food and women in 5:5. Wudu in 5:6 that completes favour echoes tayammum in 4:43 and ease in 2:185. The covenant in 5:7 and the justice in 5:8 recall 4:135, while the twelve leaders in 5:12 recall the Ansar, and the hardness in 5:13 recalls 2:74 and 4:155. Hadith adds that Muslims are bound by conditions, that the just will be on pulpits of light, that wudu erases sins, and that Allah loves the doers of good who pardon as in 5:13.

For today apply it straight: honour your contracts even when no one watches — your marriage, your work, your debt — eat only what is lawful and good, wash for prayer with presence, remember the favour and the heard-and-obeyed covenant, and stand for justice even when you dislike the other side. When a people stretched hands against you and Allah restrained them as in 5:11, respond not with revenge but with trust and taqwa. Follow the light that guides to ways of peace in 5:16, and do not say as the Christians did 'we are children' while forgetting. That is how one verse becomes a daily habit and twenty become a Table spread for all, simple for every household to live.
