=21=

"O my people, enter the Holy Land which Allah has destined for you, and do not turn back, or you will become losers."

This verse deepens the surah's argument about covenants and sacrifice, building on the perfected religion in 5:3 and the Table's test. In simple words it shows that entering what Allah has written, accepting what He accepts, and saving a life are one rope. The Israelites who feared giants in 5:22-24 and the two sons who offered sacrifices in 5:27 show two responses to the same decree: trust versus envy, entering versus sitting, tawakkul versus hasad. The first listeners in Medina saw their own Tabuk in this mirror.

It links to its neighbours and to the wider Quran: the Holy Land written as in 5:21 and 7:137, the gate that must be entered as in 5:23 and 2:214, the forty-year wandering as in 5:26 and 2:15, the soul that beautifies murder as in 12:53, the crow that teaches burial as in 22:5, and the sanctity of one life as all as in 5:32 and 4:1 single soul. Revelation came when the Prophet faced cowardice at Tabuk and needed the example of Joshua and Caleb. Hadith teaches that Allah only accepts from the God-conscious, that envy consumes good deeds, that the merciful are shown mercy for saving lives, and that the hand is cut only after proof and not for hunger.

For today make it practical: when you are called to enter a right but hard door — a difficult truth, a debt to pay, a habit to leave — do not say 'until they leave' as in 5:22 or 'go you and your Lord' as in 5:24; enter through the gate as in 5:23 trusting. When a sacrifice is rejected, check taqwa not people as in 5:27; when threatened, fear the Lord of worlds as in 5:28, not the hand. Guard life as if guarding all humanity as in 5:32, and seek the means to Allah as in 5:35 — prayer, knowledge, charity — so you may be among the successful, not those who ransom with worlds in 5:36. That is how twenty verses become one Table, simple for every home.
