The surah ends with a promise that is as beautiful as it is brief: “They will certainly be pleased.” The “they” refers to the righteous described in the preceding verses—those who donate their wealth seeking only the pleasure of their Lord, the Most High. After all the cosmic oaths, the contrasting moral portraits, the warnings of the raging Fire, and the descriptions of selfless giving, the final word is one of deep personal satisfaction.

The Arabic construction uses an emphatic particle and a future-tense verb to convey absolute certainty and immediacy: they shall surely be made pleased, and their pleasure will be complete. This is the divine response to the seeking of Allah’s pleasure. When a believer devotes their life to earning the approval of the Most High, not expecting repayment from people, Allah ensures that they themselves receive a pleasure that leaves no residue of regret, no lingering wish, no sense of having sacrificed in vain. The pleasure promised here encompasses the delights of Paradise—the gardens, the rivers, the companionship, the vision of the Lord—and it also includes a profound inner state of contentment. They will look at their eternal home and feel completely satisfied. They will remember their earthly charity and see its multiplied rewards, and they will be glad. This verse is a direct echo and fulfilment of the earlier mention of “the finest reward.” That reward was believed in firmly, and now it is tasted, and it exceeds expectations. The wording also carries a subtle nuance: they will be pleased with Allah just as Allah is pleased with them. This mutual good pleasure is the peak of spiritual achievement. The surah thus concludes by tying together its opening and its core message. The night that covered will be replaced by an endless day of shining. The diverse strivings that scattered humanity will resolve into one final state of joy for the righteous. The wealth that was given away in secret will return as an everlasting treasure of delight. The verse offers immense hope to every believer who struggles with generosity in a world that mocks selflessness. It assures them that the fleeting sting of parting with money or comfort is nothing compared to the permanent pleasure that awaits. And because the verse stands as the last word, it leaves the heart resting in a calm certainty, looking forward to a day when every yearning for goodness will be satisfied beyond imagination.
