=41=

After assuring the Prophet that his duty is only to deliver the message and that judgment belongs to Allah, the surah now directs a sharp question to the disbelievers. “Do they not see that We gradually reduce their land from its borders?” This question is a call to observation and reflection.

The phrase “gradually reduce their land” has been interpreted by many companions and early scholars, including Ibn ‘Abbās, as referring to the progressive military and political losses suffered by the disbelievers. In the immediate context, it pointed to the gradual expansion of the Muslim community and the shrinking of the pagan strongholds around Makkah. Territory after territory, tribe after tribe, came under the authority of Islam, and the power base of the Quraysh was being steadily eroded. This was not a sudden collapse but a slow, unmistakable process that they could witness if they only paid attention. Others have broadened the meaning to include the general principle that when a people persist in wrongdoing, Allah diminishes their dominion, their influence, and their resources step by step, often in ways they do not immediately perceive, until their strength is gone.

The verse then declares a fundamental truth: “Allah decides—none can reverse His decision.” Once His decree of decline or punishment is set in motion, no power on earth can repel it or overturn it. All the alliances, wealth, and strategies of the disbelievers cannot undo what Allah has willed.

Finally, the verse concludes with the warning that He is swift in reckoning. His accounting is not slow in the sense of being delayed beyond its appointed time; when it comes, it is executed swiftly and decisively. The disbelievers should not mistake the gradual nature of the process for weakness or inaction, for the final settlement will come suddenly. This verse thus serves as both a reassurance to the believers that the tide is turning and a final caution to those who still oppose the truth to read the signs of the times before it is too late.

=42=

Building on the warning of the previous verse, this verse places the plotting of the Quraysh into the larger context of history. Those who came before them also secretly planned and schemed against their prophets and their messages. They plotted in the darkness, thinking their conspiracies were hidden and their strategies were foolproof. But the verse delivers the crushing counter-statement: “but Allah has the ultimate plan. ” The Arabic word “makr” used for planning often carries a connotation of secret, cunning plotting. When attributed to the disbelievers, it is a negative scheming. When attributed to Allah, it denotes His perfect, hidden counter-plan that nullifies their schemes and brings about justice in a way they never anticipated. Allah’s plan does not need to be devised in reaction; it is the eternal, all-encompassing decree that already surrounds and overrides every human plot. The disbelievers might think they are acting independently, but in reality, their every move is known and is woven into a larger tapestry of divine justice that leads to their own undoing.

The verse then reinforces the completeness of divine knowledge: “He knows what every soul commits.” Every secret meeting, every whispered conspiracy, every hidden intention is fully open before Him. Nothing escapes His record, and nothing is omitted from the reckoning.

The verse ends with a powerful and ominous promise: “And the disbelievers will soon know who will have the ultimate outcome.” The Arabic phrase “‘uqbā ad-dār,” the ultimate outcome or the final home, points directly to the Hereafter. The disbelievers assume that worldly victory is the measure of truth, but they will soon discover, at the moment of death and on the Day of Judgment, who truly secures the best final destination. For the believers, this verse is an immense comfort. They may be plotted against, but they are under the umbrella of the ultimate Plan, and their outcome is secure. For the plotters, it is a terrifying thought that their most hidden schemes are not only known but are being used against them.

=43=

The surah reaches its final verse by confronting the ultimate denial head-on. The disbelievers of Makkah explicitly declare to the Prophet that he is not a messenger. They have seen his character, heard the Quran, and witnessed the signs, yet they make this categorical rejection.

This statement is the culmination of all the arguments and demands that came before. The Prophet is instructed not to engage in a futile argument or to become distressed, but to respond with dignified reliance on the highest authority. He is to say, “Allah is sufficient as a Witness between me and you.” The testimony of Allah is enough. Allah Himself bears witness to the truth of the Prophet’s mission through the miraculous nature of the Quran, the clear signs, and the undeniable transformation that the message brings. If the disbelievers reject him despite Allah’s witness, then the matter is settled; their denial does not alter reality. The Prophet’s conscience is clear because the Creator of the universe is his witness. Then the verse adds a second witness: “as is whoever has knowledge of the Scripture.” This refers to those among the People of the Book who recognized the truth of the Prophet’s message because they found his description in the Torah and the Gospel. Their knowledge bore witness, and their acceptance was a testimony against the pagan Arabs. It may also include any person of sincere knowledge who studies the Quran and acknowledges its divine origin. By invoking these two witnesses—the divine and the learned among previous scripture—the verse establishes an unshakable foundation for the truth. The surah that began with the separate letters and the declaration that the Quran is the truth now ends with the affirmation that Allah’s witness and the testimony of knowledgeable believers are sufficient. The Prophet is vindicated, and the deniers are left with the weight of their own rejection.

This verse teaches believers to anchor their confidence in divine validation rather than in human acceptance, and to trust that the truth will always have witnesses among those endowed with knowledge and sincerity.
