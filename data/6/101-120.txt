=101=

“He is the incomparable Originator of the heavens and earth. How could He have a child when He has no consort? He created all things and has complete knowledge of everything.”

Badi‘ means one who brings forth without a prior model. Heaven, earth, matter, time, and every pattern begin by Allah’s will. Nothing existed alongside Him as raw material or design partner.

The question about a child exposes the projection of creaturely reproduction onto the Creator. Children arise within a species, through dependence, likeness, succession, and need for continuity. Allah has no consort, no species, no death, and no deficiency a child could answer.

“He created everything” places every alleged son, daughter, angel, prophet, and jinn on the created side of the distinction. A created being can be honoured beyond others but cannot become a share of uncreated divinity.

Some religious traditions use “son” metaphorically rather than biologically, but Islam still rejects divine sonship because it compromises the exclusive relationship between Creator and servant. Allah grants closeness and love without family genealogy.

Complete knowledge reinforces self-sufficiency. Humans seek descendants partly because they do not know the future, need support, or want memory after death. The All-Knowing, Ever-Living Lord has none of these motives.

The verse is rational without claiming human biology fully defines the argument. Its decisive basis is revelation’s teaching that Allah is unique, indivisible, self-sufficient, and Creator of all.

Muslims should state this clearly without mocking other people’s family language. Theological disagreement does not permit cruelty or caricature.

Verse 102 draws the practical conclusion: this Originator is Allah, your Lord; there is no deity except Him. Since He created everything, worship Him, and entrust every affair to His guardianship.

=102=

“That is Allah, your Lord. There is no deity except Him, the Creator of all things, so worship Him alone. He is the Trustee and Guardian over everything.”

“Your Lord” brings cosmic theology into personal relationship. The Creator nurtures, provides, commands, and receives the servant’s return. He is not an abstract first cause irrelevant to daily life.

Creation establishes the right to worship. Every other object of devotion is included in “all things” and therefore dependent. The command fa‘buduhu follows logically: direct prayer, sacrifice, ultimate love, fear, and surrender to Him.

Worship is broader than ritual while ritual remains central. Obeying lawful boundaries, telling truth, earning cleanly, and treating creation justly are parts of servanthood when done for Allah.

Al-Wakil manages every affair with complete knowledge and power. Tawakkul does not mean refusing means; it means using means without treating them as independent masters. The farmer plants, patient seeks treatment, and oppressed person seeks protection while entrusting results to Allah.

A created wakil can forget, betray, or lack capacity. Allah’s guardianship has no such weakness. This makes surrender rational rather than passive resignation.

The verse also denies the need for an intermediary who must persuade an uninformed God. He already creates and manages everything. Prophetic intercession occurs only by His permission and never because He lacks knowledge or mercy.

Anxiety may remain despite belief in al-Wakil. Emotional symptoms are not proof of failed tawhid. Prayer, practical planning, social support, and professional care can all express reliance.

Verse 103 describes His transcendence and awareness: vision cannot encompass Him, while He encompasses every vision. He is the Most Subtle, All-Aware.

=103=

“No vision can encompass Him, while He encompasses all vision. He is the Most Subtle, All-Aware.”

Eyes are created instruments with limits of range, light, perspective, and capacity. Allah is not an object contained within the physical field of creation. No gaze surrounds or comprehends His essence.

Sunni belief affirms, through other Quranic verses and authentic hadith, that believers will see Allah in the Hereafter. This does not contradict the verse: seeing is not encompassing. A person sees the sky without containing its reality, and Allah is incomparably beyond every analogy.

Some theological schools read the verse differently regarding vision, but all Muslims affirm Allah is unlike creation and cannot be comprehended in essence. Disagreement should be discussed with learning rather than accusations.

“He encompasses all vision” means every eye, act of seeing, object seen, and hidden glance lies within His knowledge. A person can hide a look from everyone nearby but not from the Creator of sight.

Al-Latif carries meanings of subtlety and gentle kindness. His action reaches servants through paths too fine for them to notice. The name does not reduce Him to a physical substance.

Al-Khabir knows inner detail and ultimate consequence. Transcendence does not mean distance from people’s needs; the One beyond sensory capture knows them more intimately than they know themselves.

The verse resists idols and mental images. Whatever form the imagination constructs is a created thought, not Allah. Believers affirm His revealed names without attempting to draw His essence.

Verse 104 says illuminating insights have come from the Lord. Whoever sees benefits himself; whoever remains blind harms himself, while the Prophet is not their controlling keeper.

=104=

“Clear insights have come to you from your Lord. Whoever chooses to see does so for their own benefit, and whoever chooses blindness does so to their own loss. Say, ‘I am not a keeper over you.’”

Basa’ir are proofs that give the heart sight: Quranic verses, rational arguments, historical lessons, and signs in creation. They come from the Rabb who nurtures, so guidance is a form of care.

Seeing here is moral and intellectual, not physical. A blind believer may possess brilliant basirah, while a sighted denier can refuse every sign. Disability must never be treated as spiritual deficiency.

The benefit returns to the seer. Allah does not become greater through human faith. Guidance orders the person’s life and prepares the final return.

Likewise, chosen blindness harms the one choosing it. It cannot diminish revelation, though obstructers can injure other people and incur added guilt. “To their own loss” concerns ultimate account, not absence of social consequence.

The Prophet is not hafiz over them in the sense of controlling hearts, preventing every sin, or guaranteeing outcomes. His duties of conveyance, teaching, and lawful leadership remain, but inward response is not his possession.

This limitation guides parents and callers. They should remove barriers, answer honestly, and model faith, then recognise another adult’s agency. Manipulation may create compliance without insight.

The verse also challenges people who demand guidance while refusing to look. Allah supplied basa’ir; the servant must turn toward them with sincerity.

Verse 105 explains that signs are presented in varied ways. Opponents answer by claiming Muhammad studied earlier sources, while detailed clarification benefits people willing to know.

=105=

“This is how We vary and explain the signs, so that they may say, ‘You have studied this,’ and so that We may make the truth clear for people who know.”

Tasrif presents an argument from different angles: creation, conscience, history, resurrection, law, warning, and mercy. Diversity of explanation answers diverse minds and removes the claim that truth had only one obscure presentation.

Darasta can mean “you studied” or learned from earlier teachers. Makkan opponents used similarities with previous revelation to allege human borrowing. Yet Muhammad’s life among them contained no education capable of producing the Quran’s message, language, and sustained authority.

The accusation also misunderstands confirmation. A final revelation should share the tawhid and prophets of earlier revelation. Continuity can support common source rather than prove plagiarism.

The verse does not tell Muslims to ignore historical study of texts. Manuscripts, language, and context can be examined honestly. Faith should not fear evidence, while historical method should not assume in advance that revelation is impossible.

Variation may increase opponents in excuse because every answer gives them new material to relabel. The same detail makes truth clearer to ahl al-‘ilm, people who combine knowledge with willingness.

Teachers can follow the method by using examples, direct text, reason, and lived application without altering the core. Repetition with variety is not needless redundancy.

Knowledge also imposes humility. Not every personal insight is “what the Quran really means,” and clarity should be checked against Arabic, Sunnah, and the whole Book.

Verse 106 tells the Prophet to follow what is revealed, affirm one God, and turn away from the polytheists’ fruitless pressure.

=106=

“Follow what has been revealed to you from your Lord—there is no deity except Him—and turn away from the polytheists.”

The Prophet’s first duty is ittiba‘, following. He does not stand above revelation as its editor; he receives, obeys, embodies, and conveys. This is part of the evidence of servanthood.

The source is “your Lord,” whose knowledge and care make revelation trustworthy. Pressure from majority, elites, or emotional grief cannot become a rival source.

“There is no deity except Him” identifies the message’s centre. Legal and moral commands branch from exclusive worship. Following revelation while compromising shirk would hollow out the root.

Turning away does not mean ceasing all da‘wah or lawful relations. It means refusing their worship, disengaging from mockery and futile dispute, and not letting persistent pressure govern attention.

In the Makkan setting, this was principled non-participation rather than coercive punishment. The Prophet continued reciting, speaking to visitors, keeping kinship, and inviting people.

Believers need similar boundaries online and socially. Not every hostile thread deserves endless response. Turning toward worship and useful teaching can be more faithful than living inside outrage.

At the same time, “turn away” cannot excuse ignoring an oppressed person because of religion. Justice and mercy toward polytheists remain obligations where applicable.

Verse 107 explains that Allah could have prevented their shirk by compulsion. He did not appoint Muhammad as keeper or manager over their choices.

=107=

“Had Allah willed, they would not have associated partners. We have not appointed you as a keeper over them, nor are you responsible for managing their affairs.”

Nothing occurs outside Allah’s universal will and permission. Their shirk does not defeat His power. Yet He does not love or command shirk; revelation repeatedly forbids it. Scholars distinguish what Allah allows to exist in the test from what He approves religiously.

Allah could compel every person into outward belief, but accountable faith involves chosen response. His wisdom permits rejection for a time and judges it without injustice.

The Prophet is neither hafiz nor wakil over their hearts. He cannot force, record independently, or guarantee a result. These roles belong ultimately to Allah and His angels where assigned.

This does not make him indifferent. He grieved, warned, prayed, and taught. Healthy concern acts within responsibility; unhealthy control tries to occupy the place of God.

The verse rejects forced conversion. Coercion can produce words while leaving the heart unchanged. Public law regulates conduct, but iman remains an inward assent no sword creates.

For families, the same principle offers limits. Parents guide and set age-appropriate boundaries, but adult faith is not their possession. Blame should be based on actual neglect, not the fantasy of total control.

Divine will should not become an excuse for the polytheist. No person knows the decree before choosing; each receives commands, signs, and capacity and is judged accordingly.

Verse 108 now regulates Muslim speech toward false worship. Believers may not insult what others invoke if that predictably provokes ignorant insult against Allah.

=108=

“Do not insult what they invoke besides Allah, or they may insult Allah out of hostility and ignorance. In this way We have made every community’s deeds appear attractive to them. Then to their Lord is their return, and He will inform them of what they used to do.”

The idols are false, but insulting them can produce a greater evil: abuse directed at Allah. A true judgment does not make every method of expressing it wise or lawful.

This verse is a major example of blocking means to harm. An otherwise permissible statement can be prohibited when its predictable consequence is worse. Intent is not the only ethical measure; foreseeable impact matters.

The command does not require saying idols are valid or refraining from reasoned critique. The Quran itself refutes them. It distinguishes explanation from gratuitous insult meant to provoke and humiliate.

Every community sees its deeds decorated through culture, habit, desire, and the test Allah allows. This explains defensiveness without declaring all practices true. People need patient evidence because inherited sacredness feels emotionally real.

Return to Allah removes the need to secure final victory through abuse. He knows the belief, insult, and response and will disclose them perfectly.

The principle applies to interfaith speech, online memes, political symbols, and personal debate. Mockery can harden identity and place Muslim minorities or neighbours at risk. Wisdom is not cowardice.

It also demands reciprocity in Muslim conduct. Believers who ask others not to insult Allah should avoid contemptuous speech and protect houses of worship under lawful covenant.

The verse does not forbid responding to threats, hate speech, or incitement through lawful channels. It forbids a method that produces ignorant escalation.

Verse 109 returns to demanded miracles. The opponents swear their strongest oaths that they will believe if a sign comes, but signs belong only to Allah and their promise cannot be trusted.

=109=

“They swear their strongest oaths by Allah that if a miraculous sign came to them, they would certainly believe in it. Say, ‘Signs are only with Allah.’ What will make you believers realise that even if a sign came, they still would not believe?”

The oath shifts responsibility from their current rejection onto an absent miracle. They portray themselves as ready believers waiting only for Allah to meet their specification.

Ayat are not controlled by the Messenger. Allah chooses their form and timing according to wisdom. The Quran already stands among them as an enduring sign, while they demand spectacle.

The final clause has recognized nuances of recitation and address, but its main warning is clear: the believers should not assume the demanded sign would produce faith. Allah knows the opponents’ settled response better than hopeful observers do.

Strong oaths can manipulate an audience. Certainty of tone is not certainty of heart. The same people may reinterpret a sign as magic once it arrives.

Sincere requests for evidence are different. The Quran answers questions and presents proofs. The condemned demand is conditional obedience from people who have repeatedly dismissed available signs.

Believers should not build da‘wah around unverified miracles, numerical patterns, or predictions in the hope spectacle will succeed where Quran supposedly failed. False support can damage truth.

The verse also teaches emotional restraint. A caller may think, “If only they saw this one thing.” Guidance depends on more than the amount of evidence; humility and divine aid matter.

Verse 110 explains the consequence of first refusal: hearts and eyes are turned, and people are left wandering blindly in defiance.

=110=

“We turn their hearts and eyes away from the truth because they refused to believe in it the first time, and We leave them wandering blindly in their defiance.”

The punishment matches their act. They turned from the first signs, so the faculties meant to receive later signs become unstable and unreceptive. Repeated choice forms character.

This is not Allah arbitrarily denying a sincere beginner. The verse gives the cause: they did not believe at first. Earlier passages describe fabrication, mockery, and obstruction before divine abandonment.

Tughyan is transgression overflowing its boundary. Ya‘mahun describes confused wandering without clear sight. A person can feel increasingly confident while actually losing direction.

The warning makes prompt obedience important. “I will accept later” assumes the heart will remain equally soft after rehearsing refusal. Habits teach desire what to love.

At the same time, living people should not be declared permanently sealed. Allah guided former enemies of Islam, and repentance remains possible. Humans cannot see the final heart.

Ways to protect receptivity include acting on known truth, asking forgiveness after delay, keeping honest companions, and avoiding entertainment that trains contempt for revelation.

The verse also explains why argument alone may fail. More information offered to a heart protecting identity can become more material for denial. Du‘a and character remain essential alongside proof.

Verse 111 presents the maximal hypothetical: angels descend, the dead speak, and all things gather before them, yet they still would not believe unless Allah willed.

=111=

“Even if We sent angels down to them, made the dead speak to them, and gathered every created thing before them face to face, they still would not believe unless Allah willed. But most of them behave ignorantly.”

Every requested category of proof is imagined: heavenly beings, testimony from the dead, and creation assembled as witness. The problem survives because evidence is not the only missing element.

Qubulan can carry the sense of face-to-face confrontation or gathered groups. Either way, the scene removes ordinary excuses. Allah’s knowledge says stubbornness would continue.

“Unless Allah wills” reaffirms that guidance is His gift. This does not make proof meaningless or humans unaccountable. He guides through signs and choice while no heart escapes His sovereignty.

The ignorance named is not lack of raw data. It is failure to understand faith, divine wisdom, and the moral state required to receive a sign. People may know many facts while remaining jahil toward their purpose.

The verse discourages addiction to spectacle. A dream, apparition, or paranormal event cannot replace repentance and disciplined worship. Extraordinary experience may be misread just as ordinary signs are.

It also guards the Prophet from thinking one more miracle would guarantee success. His task remains revelation and patience.

Muslims should not claim that a particular sceptic would reject every possible sign; only Allah knew the people addressed in this way. Continue fair invitation and leave counterfactual hearts to Him.

Verse 112 places opposition within a wider prophetic pattern: every prophet has enemies among human and jinn devils, inspiring ornate deceptive speech to one another.

=112=

“In this way We have appointed for every prophet enemies—devils from humanity and jinn—who inspire one another with alluring words in order to deceive. Had your Lord willed, they could not have done so. So leave them and what they fabricate.”

A shaytan is a rebellious being far from good. Jinn devils whisper invisibly, while human devils use speech, influence, and institutions. Humanity is not morally inferior by nature, but some people perform satanic work deliberately.

Zukhruf al-qawl is decorated speech—falsehood made attractive through eloquence, emotional appeal, prestige, and selective truth. Beauty of presentation is not evidence of a claim’s truth.

Enemies test prophets and communities by forcing clarity, patience, courage, and reliance. Allah permits their existence within His wise decree without loving deception or excusing the deceiver.

“Appointed” does not mean enemies are compelled to sin. Their chosen hostility becomes part of the test Allah controls and uses. Human agency remains.

Modern ornate deception includes propaganda, deepfakes, fraudulent scholarship, and polished spiritual marketing. Verification must examine source, evidence, omissions, and financial motive rather than production quality.

“Leave them” directs the Prophet away from obsession, not from every response. He refutes falsehood where useful, protects the community, and continues mission without letting their fabrications set his agenda.

Believers should avoid answering deception with deception. Truth does not need manipulated images, invented quotations, or exaggerated statistics.

Verse 113 explains which hearts incline toward ornate falsehood: those that do not believe in the Hereafter, become pleased with it, and continue earning the deeds it supports.

=113=

“This is so that the hearts of those who do not believe in the Hereafter may incline toward such deception, become pleased with it, and continue earning what they choose to earn.”

Tasgha describes a heart leaning or listening eagerly. The speech from verse 112 finds an audience already wanting freedom from final accountability. Desire and argument meet.

They become radi—satisfied—with the deception because it validates identity and conduct. Falsehood can feel peaceful when it removes the need to repent. Emotional comfort is therefore not a sufficient test of truth.

Finally they act. Attractive ideas are not contained in thought; they legitimise transactions, relationships, oppression, and worship. People “earn” the resulting deeds and remain responsible.

The verse does not say everyone persuaded by propaganda secretly desires evil. People can be deceived through limited access, fear, or trust. It describes a deeper pattern among those rejecting the Hereafter.

Belief in final account protects the intellect by making convenient claims suspect. A person asks not only, “Does this please me?” but “Would this stand before Allah?”

Algorithmic media can repeatedly feed users speech toward which they already incline. Deliberately seeking opposing evidence and reliable sources can resist that loop, though technical systems also need ethical design.

The Quran exposes psychology without reducing truth to psychology. The claim remains false because of evidence; desire explains why it is welcomed.

Verse 114 now asks whether any judge besides Allah should be sought when He has sent the Book fully explained and recognised as truth by knowledgeable scriptural recipients.

=114=

“Say, ‘Should I seek a judge other than Allah when He is the One who has sent down to you the Book explained in detail?’ Those given Scripture know that it has been revealed from your Lord in truth, so do not be among the doubters.”

Allah is al-Hakam, the ultimate judge of creed, moral value, and final dispute. Human judges and scholars have authority only within standards He grants and remain fallible.

The Quran is mufassalan—set out distinctly and explained for its guidance-purpose. This does not mean every modern technical case is named or that no Sunnah and scholarship are needed. The Book itself commands following the Messenger and asking people of knowledge.

Seeking another ultimate judge means replacing revelation with desire or a rival source claiming superior authority. Using experts, courts, or policy to apply justice in matters left to human reasoning does not make them gods.

Knowledgeable People of the Book could recognise continuity with genuine revelation. The statement does not claim every Jewish or Christian scholar agreed, nor make their recognition the Quran’s foundation. Allah’s testimony is sufficient.

“Do not be among doubters” addresses the Prophet as firm reassurance and teaches the audience. It is not evidence that he harboured settled doubt. Quranic commands often reinforce what a prophet already embodies.

Certainty should remain intellectually responsible. Muslims can study objections and admit interpretive uncertainty without doubting Allah’s truth. Confidence is not pretending every personal reading is infallible.

The verse follows deceptive ornate speech with a reliable criterion. When rhetoric competes, return to the Book, prophetic explanation, evidence, and humility.

Verse 115 describes the Lord’s word as complete in truth and justice. No one can alter His words, and He hears and knows all.

=115=

“The word of your Lord has been completed in truth and justice. None can alter His words, and He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing.”

Sidqan describes complete truth in reports, promises, and warnings. ‘Adlan describes perfect justice in commands and judgments. Revelation is neither factually false nor morally crooked.

“Completed” means Allah’s decisive word reaches its intended fullness and cannot be improved by a rival revelation. It does not mean interpretation ended or every changing circumstance lacks need for juristic work.

No one alters His words in the sense that no power can make His promise fail, defeat His decree, or replace truth before Him. The Quran’s textual preservation is also part of divine care, established through its transmission and 15:9, though this verse’s context includes the unchangeable truth and judgment of Allah’s word broadly.

Human beings can misquote, hide copies, or distort interpretation, but they cannot transform falsehood into Allah’s actual speech. Their altered page does not alter what is with Him.

Al-Sami‘ hears every challenge, recitation, and quiet question. Al-‘Alim knows every situation His law addresses and every motive behind attempts to change it.

The verse should not be used to silence all discussion by equating one scholar’s opinion with the unchangeable Word. The Word is perfect; human understanding requires evidence and can be corrected.

Truth and justice together give a standard for interpretation. A reading built on fabrication or clear oppression should be re-examined against text and sound method.

Verse 116 warns that following the earth’s majority would lead away from Allah because many follow conjecture and guess rather than knowledge.

=116=

“If you obeyed most people on earth, they would lead you away from Allah’s path. They follow only conjecture and do nothing but guess.”

At the time, most surrounding humanity did not follow the final revelation. Numbers therefore could not be the criterion for Muhammad. Truth is measured by evidence from Allah, not a poll.

The verse does not teach that the minority is always right. Fringe groups can be profoundly false, and consensus of qualified Muslim scholarship has authority grounded in evidence. Both majority worship and minority vanity are errors.

Zann here is unsupported conjecture, not every probable judgment. Much law and ordinary life rely legitimately on strong evidence short of mathematical certainty. Blame falls on guessing where divine claims require proof.

Popular culture can normalise false values through repetition. “Everyone does it” describes frequency, not morality. Believers need courage to stand apart without developing contempt for people.

The command also warns leaders who reshape religion around market demand. Listening to community needs is wise; making approval the source of halal and haram is not.

Modern expertise often reflects many researchers and can be highly reliable. Accepting medical or technical consensus is not obedience that leads from Allah’s path when it addresses its proper field and contradicts no revelation. The verse is not anti-knowledge.

Most people may hold pieces of truth while lacking a revealed whole. Muslims should engage, learn beneficial knowledge, and judge claims rather than reject everything non-Muslims say.

Verse 117 restores humility: Allah knows best who strays and who is guided. Believers do not possess that complete classification themselves.

=117=

“Surely your Lord knows best who strays from His path, and He knows best who is rightly guided.”

The repeated “knows best” places final classification with Allah. He sees evidence received, inner response, coercion, repentance, and ending. Human beings see fragments.

This prevents a guided minority from becoming arrogant. Possessing truth is favour and responsibility, not proof of personal brilliance. The guided ask for steadfastness.

It also comforts the Prophet. Popular rejection does not confuse Allah’s record or turn falsehood into guidance. Every sincere seeker is known even when socially invisible.

People must still judge outward matters where necessary—choosing teachers, protecting communities, and evaluating beliefs. “Allah knows best” does not mean standards disappear; it limits claims about hearts and final destiny.

The path belongs to Him. A group cannot define its culture as “Allah’s way” without evidence. Quran and authentic Sunnah remain criteria, interpreted through sound knowledge.

The verse joins decree and responsibility. Allah knows the strayer as strayer because of the real path and real response, not an arbitrary label.

Believers should be wary of declaring named people eternally guided based on fame. A good present is hoped for, while endings remain with Allah.

Verse 118 draws a practical consequence of faith: eat from animals over which Allah’s name is mentioned, rather than accepting pagan slaughter taboos and dedications.

=118=

“So eat from that over which Allah’s name has been mentioned, if you truly believe in His signs.”

Food becomes a declaration of tawhid. Mentioning Allah at slaughter acknowledges that animal life belongs to Him and is taken only under His permission and rules.

The command encourages eating what is lawful rather than inventing ascetic restrictions. Pagan leaders prohibited some animals without authority while dedicating others to idols. Revelation returns law to Allah.

The verse concerns slaughtered animals; ordinary plants and foods do not require ritual slaughter. Details about intentional omission, forgetfulness, hunting, and People of the Book are explained through Sunnah and juristic interpretation.

“If you believe” connects daily consumption to creed. Faith is not confined to prayer; markets, kitchens, and treatment of animals enter obedience.

Mentioning the name is not a magic formula making any conduct lawful. The species, ownership, method, and welfare must also meet relevant conditions. Invoking Allah while stealing or torturing an animal compounds contradiction.

Modern supply chains can make slaughter difficult to verify. Certification, trustworthy information, and qualified legal standards help consumers without turning uncertainty into obsessive hardship.

The command does not encourage waste or excessive meat consumption. Lawfulness answers whether it may be eaten; health, environment, affordability, and gratitude shape how much and how often.

Verse 119 asks why believers should avoid what bears Allah’s name when prohibited foods have already been detailed, with necessity as an exception. It warns that many mislead through desire without knowledge.

=119=

“Why should you not eat from that over which Allah’s name has been mentioned, when He has already explained in detail what He has forbidden to you—except when compelled by necessity? Many people mislead others through their desires without knowledge. Surely your Lord knows the transgressors best.”

The question removes superstition. Once Allah identifies lawful slaughter and details prohibitions, believers should not accept pagan additions or feel religious guilt about His permission.

The forbidden categories were stated in passages such as 2:173 and 5:3: carrion, flowing blood, swine, and what is dedicated to another, with further forms of unlawful death. The Quran explains foundations while Sunnah provides application.

Necessity permits enough prohibited food to preserve life when no lawful alternative exists. It does not transform the item permanently or justify seeking it from desire. Degree of need and available alternatives matter.

Those who mislead follow ahwa’ without ‘ilm. They may forbid lawful food to gain authority or permit forbidden food to please an audience. Opposite conclusions can share the same method of desire.

The verse protects both ease and discipline. Scrupulosity can invent restrictions, while appetite can stretch necessity beyond recognition. Allah knows transgression on either side.

Consumers should avoid judging other Muslims hastily where legal schools differ about slaughter methods or ingredients. A recognised disagreement is not ignorance merely because one follows another position.

Food advice also requires technical knowledge. Scholars may need veterinarians, chemists, and supply-chain experts to understand facts before applying law.

Verse 120 broadens from food to all morality: abandon open and hidden sin. What people earn privately remains subject to recompense.

=120=

“Abandon sin, both open and hidden. Those who earn sin will be repaid for what they used to commit.”

Zahir sin is outward and public; batin sin can be concealed action, intention settled upon wrong, pride, envy nurtured willingly, or corruption of belief. Islam seeks integrity rather than a clean public image covering a private life.

The verse does not treat involuntary thoughts, intrusive images, or unwanted feelings as sins merely because they occur internally. The Prophet taught that Allah pardons what crosses the mind so long as it is not deliberately adopted, spoken, or acted upon. This distinction is vital for people with obsessive or anxiety disorders.

Hidden sin can be easier to continue because reputation remains safe. Digital privacy, secret accounts, and professional access can create opportunities, but Allah’s knowledge is unchanged.

Open sin can normalise wrongdoing and encourage others, adding social harm. Yet exposing a private sinner without need can also be wrong. Correction follows evidence, privacy, and proper purpose.

“Earn” emphasises choice and accumulation. Repeated indulgence forms a record and character. Repentance can interrupt the earning, erase guilt by Allah’s mercy, and require repair of human rights.

The command “leave” is practical. Remove access, change routines, seek treatment, end corrupt partnerships, and choose companions who support reform. Willpower alone may not address an entrenched habit.

Private righteousness matters too. Hidden prayer, charity, and restraint train the soul away from hidden sin. The unseen life can become a source of strength rather than shame.

Recompense is just, based on what was actually earned and full knowledge of capacity and repentance. The next file begins with verse 121, prohibiting consumption of animals over which Allah’s name was not mentioned and warning against satanic arguments designed to turn believers from obedience.
