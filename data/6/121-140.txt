=121=

“Do not eat from that over which Allah’s name has not been mentioned, for it is certainly defiant disobedience. The devils inspire their allies to argue with you. If you obeyed them in this, you would indeed become polytheists.”

The verse protects slaughter from pagan dedication. Animal life belongs to Allah, and mentioning His name acknowledges permission from the true Owner. Meat offered to an idol or killed under another deity’s name contradicts tawhid.

The Quran calls the prohibited act fisq, departure from obedience. Food is not spiritually neutral when its ritual purpose declares another god. The rule joins daily consumption to creed.

Jurists distinguish deliberate omission of Allah’s name, forgetfulness, unknown circumstances, hunting, and the food of the People of the Book permitted in 5:5. Legal schools differ on some applications. The verse should be followed through qualified law rather than used for hasty accusations against other Muslims.

Reports describe pagan argument: why eat what you kill but reject an animal that “Allah killed” through natural death? The rhetoric concealed the difference between lawful slaughter and carrion and attempted to make disobedience appear more logical than revelation.

Devils among jinn and humans inspire ornate disputes. Argument becomes satanic when its purpose is to make shirk lawful, not merely because it asks a difficult question. Islam welcomes sincere inquiry and distinguishes it from manipulation.

“If you obey them, you would be polytheists” concerns accepting their idolatrous authority and dedication against Allah’s command. Not every sinful meal automatically expels a Muslim from faith. Belief, intent, knowledge, and the nature of obedience matter; reckless takfir is forbidden.

Modern supply chains require trustworthy certification and factual investigation. Suspicion alone should not turn every meal into anxiety. Allah did not reveal food law to create obsessive fear.

Verse 122 contrasts the living light of faith with spiritual death and layers of darkness. Guidance gives a person light by which to move among people.

=122=

“Is one who was dead and whom We brought to life, giving them a light by which they walk among people, comparable to one trapped in darknesses from which they cannot emerge? In this way, the deeds of the disbelievers have been made attractive to them.”

Death and life here are spiritual. A living body can lack revealed direction, while faith revives purpose, conscience, and relationship with Allah. Guidance is resurrection before bodily resurrection.

The light is Quran, iman, knowledge, and moral clarity. It enables movement “among people,” so guidance is not retreat from society. The believer carries truth into family, markets, conflict, and service.

Darknesses are plural because falsehood fragments into shirk, desire, pride, inherited superstition, and confusion. A person may escape one darkness and enter another without the unifying light.

“Cannot emerge” describes the state of persistent disbelievers under judgment, not a declaration that every living unbeliever can never change. Many companions moved from idolatry into light. Humans should continue inviting while life remains.

Beautified deeds make darkness feel like identity, freedom, or sophistication. A person rarely chooses what he consciously sees as ruin; falsehood is decorated until correction feels threatening.

Believers can also mistake habit for light. The test is revelation and righteous fruit, not comfort alone. Religious branding can conceal injustice just as secular branding can.

The metaphor must not demean people with physical darkness or visual disability. A blind believer may walk by spiritual light better than a sighted person. The Quran’s contrast concerns moral perception.

Verse 123 moves from individuals to cities. Influential criminals plot within every corrupt society, but their schemes ultimately return against themselves without their awareness.

=123=

“In this way We have placed prominent criminals in every society to scheme within it. Yet they scheme only against themselves, though they do not realise it.”

Akabir mujrimihā are powerful figures whose criminality gains reach through wealth, lineage, office, or cultural prestige. Wrong can exist among the poor, but elite wrongdoing shapes institutions and makes private vice public policy.

Allah’s “placing” them means their rise occurs within His decree and test, not that He approves crime or forces it. Societies that reward corruption create pathways for the most cunning offenders, while Allah allows the choice and judges it.

Makr is concealed planning meant to trap others. Makkan leaders coordinated ridicule, pressure, and obstruction against the Prophet. Modern forms include captured institutions, disinformation, regulatory manipulation, and secret exploitation.

Their scheme returns against themselves by adding guilt, hardening hearts, destroying social trust, and preparing final judgment. It may still harm victims in the world; the verse does not tell oppressed people the harm is imaginary.

“They do not perceive” explains why short-term success can deepen arrogance. The plotter sees opponents silenced and profit secured but not the moral and historical collapse being built.

Believers should not answer every setback with conspiracy claims. Evidence is required. The verse recognises real schemes without validating paranoia about hidden elites behind every event.

Resistance includes transparent institutions, independent scholarship, lawful accountability, protection for witnesses, and refusal to admire wealth detached from ethics.

Verse 124 exposes elite envy in religion. When a sign comes, they demand the same revelation given to messengers. Allah knows best where to place His message, and criminal plotting ends in humiliation.

=124=

“Whenever a sign comes to them, they say, ‘We will never believe until we are given the same as Allah’s messengers were given.’ Allah knows best where to place His message. The criminals will soon be struck with humiliation before Allah and severe punishment for their schemes.”

The leaders do not merely request evidence; they demand prophetic rank as the price of belief. They treat revelation like political office that should follow wealth, tribe, or personal prestige.

Elsewhere they asked why the Quran was not sent to a “great man” from the two towns (43:31). Their scale of greatness measured social power, while Allah knew the heart and character capable of carrying revelation.

Prophethood is pure selection. It cannot be earned by ascetic exercises, inherited after Muhammad, or claimed through visions. Allah sealed prophethood with him, and every later claimant is measured against that finality.

“Allah knows best” does not make divine choice arbitrary. The names and context imply complete wisdom and knowledge. People see polished leadership; Allah knows integrity under every future pressure.

The demand also reveals unwillingness to follow an equal. Some people accept truth only if they can lead it. Humility receives a message through whoever Allah chooses.

Saghar is abasement answering arrogance. Those who plotted to preserve superiority encounter smallness before the Lord whose message they tried to control. Severe punishment follows scheme, not lack of worldly status.

Muslim communities should not imitate this by assuming scholarship belongs to one ethnicity or class. Qualifications matter, but gatekeeping based on prestige can hide envy.

Verse 125 explains guidance and rejection through the image of an expanded chest welcoming Islam and a constricted chest struggling as though ascending into the sky.

=125=

“Whoever Allah wills to guide, He opens their heart to Islam. Whoever He wills to leave astray, He makes their chest tight and constricted, as though they were climbing into the sky. In this way Allah places impurity upon those who refuse to believe.”

Sharh al-sadr is expansion, ease, and capacity to receive submission. It does not mean every command always feels emotionally easy; faith creates an underlying willingness to accept, learn, and return.

Constriction makes Islam feel suffocating because the heart resists its Lord. The person may experience every boundary as attack and every reminder as pressure. Persistent rejection becomes its own inner punishment.

The ascending-sky image communicates severe difficulty and breathlessness. People knew climbing and thinning breath on mountains; modern knowledge can illustrate the analogy, but the verse should not be marketed as a technical prediction requiring exaggerated science.

Allah’s will remains wise and responsive to human choice. Earlier verses show first refusal, arrogance, and plotting before hearts are abandoned. He also opens the chest of sincere seekers by grace.

Rijs can mean impurity, torment, or spiritual burden placed upon disbelief. It is not permission to treat non-Muslims as physically dirty or deny their human rights.

Emotional chest tightness can also be a medical symptom of anxiety, asthma, or heart disease. It must not be diagnosed as disbelief. Quranic spiritual metaphor and clinical health require careful distinction.

Believers ask Allah for an open chest, as Musa prayed, while taking means to understand difficult teachings. Questions and temporary discomfort can be stages toward expanded faith.

Verse 126 declares this Islam the straight path of the Lord, with signs detailed for those who remember.

=126=

“This is your Lord’s path, perfectly straight. We have made the signs clear for people who take heed.”

“This” points to tawhid, revelation, and surrendered guidance described throughout the surah. The path belongs to the Rabb who nurtures travellers and knows their destination.

Mustaqim means straight, stable, and leading without crooked diversion. Human walking on it can be uneven, but the path itself is not defective. Repentance returns a person after a stumble.

Signs are detailed through repeated arguments: creation, history, conscience, food, prophetic example, mercy, and warning. Variety removes the excuse that guidance appeared only in one inaccessible form.

Those who yadhdhakkarun remember what fitrah, revelation, and previous lessons establish. Heedlessness is often forgetfulness of truths once recognised, so worship repeats them.

Calling Islam straight does not permit Muslims to call every cultural habit divine. The path is Quran and authentic Sunnah understood soundly, not ethnicity, faction, or personal severity.

Nor does certainty eliminate valid juristic difference. Several routes of application can remain within one straight revelation when evidence genuinely allows them.

The verse comforts someone overwhelmed by competing ideologies. Begin with Allah’s oneness, prophetic guidance, prayer, justice, and clear duties rather than every secondary controversy.

Verse 127 names the destination: the Home of Peace with Allah, under His guardianship, as reward for what believers used to do.

=127=

“They will have the Home of Peace with their Lord, and He will be their protecting Guardian because of what they used to do.”

Dar al-Salam is Paradise, free from death, fear, betrayal, illness, and grief. Salam is not only absence of conflict but complete safety and wholeness.

“With their Lord” gives the home its greatest honour: His pleasure, care, and nearness appropriate to created servants. Paradise is not merely luxury detached from the Giver.

Allah is their Wali in the world through guidance and protection and in the Hereafter through lasting security. False protectors vanished; His guardianship reaches completion.

“Because of what they did” affirms action matters. Faithful prayer, restraint, service, and repentance are means Allah attached to reward. Yet deeds exist by His help and cannot purchase eternity at an equal price; entry remains mercy.

The verse motivates consistency in ordinary deeds. Dar al-Salam may be approached through a truthful invoice, patient caregiving, a guarded glance, or quiet prayer as well as dramatic sacrifice.

Peace in Paradise does not imply believers will achieve a life free from conflict here. They pursue justice and reconciliation while accepting that complete peace belongs to the final home.

Communities should embody a preview by making mosques and homes safe from abuse, racism, and humiliation. Invoking Dar al-Salam while creating fear around worship contradicts the path.

Verse 128 now depicts the gathering of jinn and humans whose exploitative alliance ended at its appointed term, followed by the Fire except as Allah wills.

=128=

“On the Day He gathers them all, He will say, ‘O assembly of jinn, you led many humans astray.’ Their human allies will say, ‘Our Lord, we benefited from one another, but now we have reached the term You appointed for us.’ He will say, ‘The Fire is your home, remaining there, except as Allah wills.’ Surely your Lord is All-Wise, All-Knowing.”

Devilish jinn multiplied followers through whispering, deception, and occult relationships. Humans chose the alliance and therefore answer alongside them. Neither side can place all blame on the other.

Mutual benefit may include jinn enjoying obedience, fear, and worship while humans sought information, power, excitement, or false protection. Short-term exchange concealed shared ruin.

The “term” is death and final gathering when the arrangement can no longer offer pleasure. Every forbidden alliance has an expiry hidden from those absorbed in its benefit.

The Fire’s duration is stated as abiding, followed by “except as Allah wills.” Scholars have discussed the exception—whether it emphasises absolute sovereignty, refers to periods before entry, or applies according to categories of sinners. It cannot be used to contradict clear texts on eternal punishment for final disbelief.

Al-Hakim and al-‘Alim ensure the unseen details are judged perfectly. Allah knows who tempted, who followed, what was understood, and whether repentance occurred.

The verse does not justify attributing ordinary mental illness or unexplained events to jinn. Claims of possession and occult harm require caution; medical and psychological assessment should not be neglected.

Lawful ruqyah consists of Quran and authentic supplication without bargains with unseen beings, unknown formulas, abuse, or financial exploitation.

Verse 129 states the social rule beneath the scene: wrongdoers become allies and authorities over one another because of what they earn.

=129=

“In this way We make some wrongdoers allies of others because of what they used to earn.”

Wrongdoing seeks compatible company. People who profit from the same corruption defend one another, normalise the practice, and punish dissent. Companionship becomes part of consequence.

Nuwalli can mean appointing them over one another or joining them in alliance. Corrupt rulers may reflect corrupt systems, and corrupt followers can empower corrupt leaders. Responsibility moves in both directions without becoming equal in every case.

“Because of what they earned” prevents fatalism. The alliance follows repeated choices, not arbitrary grouping. A person can break from it through repentance, changed companions, and restitution.

The verse does not mean every person under a bad ruler is personally as guilty as the ruler. Children, victims, reformers, and people without power may share a society without approving it. Allah knows each capacity.

Social environments shape conduct powerfully. Leaving a criminal group, abusive religious circle, or addictive friendship may be necessary for reform. Support systems help people survive the cost of exit.

Online communities can perform the same function, surrounding users with validation for hate, fraud, or self-harm. Curating companions is now partly curating feeds.

Believers should not assume their own group is righteous merely because it opposes another wrongdoer. Shared enemies can create alliances that reproduce the same injustice.

Verse 130 records Allah’s question to jinn and humans: did messengers not convey signs and warn of this Day? They confess against themselves after worldly life deceived them.

=130=

“‘O assembly of jinn and humans, did messengers not come to you from among you, reciting My signs and warning you about the meeting of this Day?’ They will say, ‘We testify against ourselves.’ Worldly life deceived them, and they will testify that they were disbelievers.”

Messengers were human according to the mainstream understanding. “From among you” addresses the combined assembly while human prophets reached jinn as well; Surah al-Jinn records jinn listening to the Quran and carrying warning to their people. Some scholars discussed whether jinn had messengers of their own, but the established prophetic names are human.

Two duties are named: reciting Allah’s signs and warning of meeting. The message gives content and consequence. No one is judged for a law entirely inaccessible to him.

Their confession is against themselves because proof no longer depends on an external accuser. Memories, messengers, records, and present reality align. Denial offers no escape.

Worldly life deceived through delay, beauty, and false security. The world itself is not sinful; using its gifts as proof there will be no return is the deception.

The confession of kufr refers to those dying upon rejection after their accountable response. Allah knows differing access, capacity, and coercion and does not flatten every biography.

For Muslims, recitation alone is not necessarily a favourable testimony. The signs ask to be believed and followed. Familiarity can increase responsibility.

The verse motivates honest admission now. Confessing error during life can open repentance; confession after the test closes only establishes justice.

Verse 131 explains the principle: Allah does not destroy communities for wrongdoing while their people remain unaware and unwarned.

=131=

“This is because your Lord would never destroy communities for wrongdoing while their people were unaware.”

Sending messengers establishes knowledge before final punitive destruction. Allah does not surprise a morally uninformed people with a judgment they had no meaningful chance to understand.

The verse displays justice and mercy together. Warning delays punishment, teaches the road of escape, and places response within human choice. Prophethood is mercy before it becomes evidence.

“Unaware” is more than lacking a document somewhere. Actual accountability includes whether guidance reached in a recognisable form and whether a person could understand. Allah alone measures that perfectly.

Scholars discuss ahl al-fatrah—people unreached by a messenger or reached only through severe distortion—and their test or judgment. The verse grounds confidence that no one will be wronged even where humans cannot specify the mechanism.

This principle does not excuse someone who deliberately avoids learning to preserve a desired practice. Chosen ignorance differs from inaccessible knowledge.

Nor should callers claim they have fully established proof merely by handing someone a leaflet or posting a link. Clear, truthful conveyance and removal of avoidable distortion are serious responsibilities.

Worldly calamities again should not be labelled “destruction for sin” without revelation. The verse speaks about Allah’s punitive practice, not human certainty over every historical event.

Verse 132 says all people have degrees according to their deeds, and Allah is never unaware of what they do.

=132=

“Everyone will have degrees according to what they have done, and your Lord is never unaware of their deeds.”

Divine judgment is not a crude binary ignoring differences. Paradise has ranks, punishment has degrees, and every person’s outcome reflects faith, deeds, intention, capacity, and mercy.

“According to what they did” gives moral weight to small actions. Two people with the same label can stand far apart because one served sincerely and another harmed while performing.

Allah’s lack of heedlessness guarantees no deed disappears through administrative error. Human institutions overlook unpaid care, hidden restraint, and victims without influence; the final record does not.

Different degrees do not mean believers should rank one another confidently now. Acceptance, hidden deeds, and endings are unknown. Visible service earns appreciation without creating certainty of spiritual station.

The verse encourages aspiration rather than jealousy. Seeing another person’s good can motivate competition in righteousness while asking Allah to bless both.

It also means punishment is proportionate. Allah does not treat the coerced like the willing leader or the ignorant like the knowing manipulator. The full context of every choice is present.

A small repeated sin should not be dismissed; accumulation shapes degree. A small repeated good should not be despised for the same reason.

Verse 133 reminds all ranks that Allah is Self-Sufficient and full of mercy. He can remove a people and replace them just as He produced them from earlier generations.

=133=

“Your Lord is the Self-Sufficient, full of mercy. If He wills, He can remove you and replace you with whomever He wills, just as He brought you forth from the descendants of other people.”

Al-Ghani needs no worship, army, tax, or population. Human obedience benefits humans; disbelief cannot reduce His kingdom. This makes every opportunity to serve a favour granted to the servant.

Dhu al-rahmah possesses vast mercy. Self-sufficiency does not mean cold distance. He creates, provides, warns, forgives, and delays judgment without needing a return.

Replacement breaks collective arrogance. Quraysh was not indispensable, and no later nation, school, institution, or movement can hold Allah’s religion hostage. If one people turns away, another can carry the trust.

The audience itself arose after previous generations. Genealogy is a living demonstration that people enter a world others leave. Replacement is not an abstract threat.

The verse does not permit humans to remove populations in God’s name. Divine capability is not a policy mandate for ethnic cleansing, forced displacement, or political violence.

For institutions, replaceability should encourage succession, accountability, and training others. A leader who makes service dependent upon his presence has forgotten al-Ghani.

Mercy means replacement may also be reform rather than annihilation: new believers, younger servants, or changed leadership can revive neglected duty.

Verse 134 makes the promised resurrection and judgment certain. No one can escape or frustrate Allah.

=134=

“What you have been promised will certainly come, and you cannot escape or frustrate Allah.”

The promise includes resurrection, account, Paradise, and Fire. Denial affects the denier’s preparation, not the event’s arrival.

Mu‘jizin means people capable of defeating a pursuer, escaping reach, or making a plan fail. No distance, technology, burial method, or forgotten identity places a soul outside Allah’s power.

Certainty of judgment supports patience. A victim need not turn into an oppressor to secure every consequence personally; lawful effort can be made while final justice remains guaranteed.

It also supports urgency. Future repentance exists only as intention until performed, and death can end the ability. A promise to change later is not escape from the promised Day.

Believers should not use certainty to date the Hour or announce particular punishments. Allah promised occurrence while withholding much timing and detail.

The verse is brief because its proof has already filled the surah: first creation, sleep and waking, revived earth, divine knowledge, and prophetic warning.

No escape does not mean no mercy. The return is to the Lord described just before as full of mercy. Escape is replaced by reconciliation through faith and repentance now.

Verse 135 tells the persistent opponents to continue according to their position while the Prophet continues his; soon everyone will know who receives the good final outcome. Wrongdoers do not attain true success.

=135=

“Say, ‘O my people, continue acting according to your position, and I too will continue. You will soon know who will have the good final outcome. Surely the wrongdoers will never succeed.’”

This is an ultimatum after extensive proof, not approval of their deeds. “Continue” means each side’s chosen path will disclose its result. The Prophet will not trade tawhid for their acceptance.

Calling them “my people” retains connection. Firm separation in creed does not erase concern, kinship, or just conduct. Muhammad continues inviting even as he warns.

Makanatikum can mean position, ability, or way. Their power appears strong in Makkah; his position appears vulnerable. Final outcome will reveal which location was truly secure.

The verse should not become a believer’s first response to disagreement. It follows patient explanation and is appropriate where argument has become performative. Sincere questions still deserve engagement.

The “good end” includes worldly vindication as Allah wills and, decisively, the Hereafter. Temporary dominance is not falah. Oppressors can win rounds while losing the final account.

Wrongdoers’ lack of success does not imply immediate visible collapse. The Quran’s scale includes spiritual loss and delayed justice.

Callers should avoid speaking the ultimatum from ego, as if personal victory were Allah’s victory. The Prophet stood upon revelation; ordinary people must verify their position.

Verse 136 begins exposing pagan food religion: they assigned shares of crops and livestock to Allah and alleged partners, then privileged the partners’ share through a corrupt judgment.

=136=

“They assign to Allah a share of the crops and livestock He created, saying, ‘This is for Allah’—so they claim—‘and this is for our partners.’ But the share of their partners never reaches Allah, while Allah’s share reaches their partners. Evil indeed is their judgment.”

The pagans acknowledged Allah created the produce and animals, yet divided offerings between Him and idols. Shirk tried to place Creator and created beneficiaries within one accounting system.

Commentators explain that if something from the idols’ share crossed into Allah’s, they restored it, but if Allah’s share crossed toward the idols, they left it, claiming Allah was self-sufficient. Their practical priority exposed which side they valued.

“So they claim” interrupts the description to deny divine authority for the entire arrangement. Allah never accepted a share alongside partners. He owns all and needs none of it.

The system likely supported idol custodians and social interests while wearing sacred language. Fabricated religion often has material beneficiaries who resist reform.

The verse does not condemn allocating harvest for charity, family, tax, or public good. It condemns dedication to false gods and attribution of the division to Allah.

Muslims can repeat the moral contradiction by giving Allah only symbolic leftovers while protecting status and appetite first. This is not necessarily legal shirk, but it reveals disordered gratitude.

“Evil judgment” shows false theology corrupts arithmetic and ethics. Once idols receive divine status, unfair preference appears pious.

Verse 137 moves to a far worse result: their alleged partners made killing children attractive, destroying them and confusing their religion.

=137=

“In the same way, their alleged partners made the killing of their own children appear attractive to many polytheists, leading them to ruin and confusing their religion. Had Allah willed, they would not have done it. So leave them and what they fabricate.”

Child killing in pre-Islamic Arabia had several motives and forms: fear of poverty, fear of shame, and in some settings sacrificial religious practice. Female infanticide was a notorious form, but the verse’s wording includes children generally.

“Partners” may mean devils, idol custodians, or leaders who decorated the practice. Culture can make an atrocity appear responsible, honourable, or religious when enough authority repeats it.

The act destroys the child physically and the killer morally and socially. A community that treats children as expendable damages its future while claiming protection of honour or wealth.

Talbis confuses religion by mixing remnants of Ibrahim’s way with invented rites. People can perform pilgrimage and mention Allah while obeying a system fundamentally opposed to His mercy.

“Had Allah willed” affirms universal sovereignty, not approval. He forbade murder and condemns the perpetrators. Allowing human choice in the test differs from loving its outcome.

“Leave them and their fabrication” directs the Prophet away from endless entanglement after clear exposure. It does not mean leave children unprotected where lawful intervention is possible. Islam’s law, teaching, and community action abolished and resisted the practice.

Modern threats to children—abuse, trafficking, starvation through neglect, and ideological violence—require evidence-based protection. The verse should not be used as a slogan in complex medical cases without relevant law and expertise.

Verse 138 returns to invented livestock rules: reserved crops, animals forbidden for riding, and slaughter without Allah’s name, all falsely attributed to Him.

=138=

“They say, ‘These livestock and crops are restricted; none may eat them except those we permit’—so they claim. Other animals are forbidden for riding, and over others they do not mention Allah’s name, falsely attributing all this to Him. He will repay them for what they fabricated.”

The pagan system controlled who could consume certain produce, which animals could work, and how animals were dedicated. Its authority came from “our claim,” not revelation.

Some animals were protected from riding or labour after particular births, while others were slaughtered for idols or treated in ways that omitted Allah’s name. The exact categories varied and are summarised elsewhere as bahirah, sa’ibah, wasilah, and ham.

Restriction can be lawful for ownership, conservation, animal welfare, or public policy. The sin is fabricating sacred prohibition and assigning it to Allah, not every rule governing access to crops and livestock.

Such rules often concentrate power. Those permitted to eat or administer “sacred” property gain privilege, while women or lower-status people may be excluded. False theology becomes social control.

Mentioning Allah’s name is not mere sound. It affirms ownership and requires a lawful act. One cannot invoke Him over stolen property or cruel treatment and call it purified.

Recompense follows iftira’ because misleading others through God’s name multiplies harm. Repentance requires correcting the false teaching as well as leaving the practice.

Modern believers should distinguish divine prohibition, scholarly caution, regulatory policy, and personal diet. Calling all four “haram from Allah” repeats the confusion at a smaller scale.

Verse 139 exposes an explicitly gendered rule: live offspring reserved for males, forbidden to females, but shared if stillborn. Allah will repay their false description with wisdom and knowledge.

=139=

“They also say, ‘What is in the wombs of these animals is reserved for our males and forbidden to our females. But if it is stillborn, they may all share it.’ He will repay them for their false attribution. Surely He is All-Wise, All-Knowing.”

The rule shifts with sex and outcome without revealed principle. If the offspring is born alive, men claim it; if dead, women are permitted a share. Arbitrary discrimination is presented as religion.

“What is in the wombs” also exposes their claim to control what they do not create or fully know. Allah forms the offspring and owns its life, yet human custom assigns sacred entitlement before birth.

The verse does not teach that every different rule for men and women is unjust. Quranic law itself sometimes assigns distinct responsibilities with reasons and a broader system. The condemned distinction lacks divine authority and serves privilege capriciously.

False attribution is the central crime. A discriminatory custom is made harder to challenge once speakers say God decreed it. Victims can then be accused of impiety for asking justice.

Allah is Hakim and ‘Alim. His actual law rests on complete wisdom and knowledge, unlike a rule improvised around status. These names invite scrutiny of human claims rather than blind submission to anyone invoking religion.

Recompense can include exposure of the lie, loss of privilege, and final punishment. The exact judgment belongs to Allah.

Modern communities should examine whether culture is being presented as scripture in inheritance, food, education, or access to worship. Difference between custom and command must remain visible.

Verse 140 summarises the loss: they killed children foolishly, prohibited Allah’s provision, and lied about Him. They went astray and were not guided.

=140=

“Lost indeed are those who killed their children foolishly and without knowledge, prohibited what Allah provided for them, and fabricated lies against Allah. They certainly went astray and were never rightly guided.”

The verse brings harm to life and harm to religion together. Children are killed, lawful provision is denied, and both practices are sustained by false claims about Allah. Corrupted theology produces concrete victims.

Safahan indicates foolishness that violates sound judgment; bi-ghayri ‘ilm exposes lack of revelation and knowledge. Fear of poverty or shame felt persuasive, but it rested on a false account of provision and honour.

The condemnation includes every killed child, not only daughters, though female infanticide was a grave manifestation. Islam protects children’s life and provision before they can defend themselves.

Forbidding provision harms families and insults the Giver. Ascetic restraint can be personally lawful, but declaring Allah’s food forbidden requires proof. Superstition made gratitude into deprivation.

“They lost” before any listed punishment because their deeds already destroyed family, conscience, and guidance. Worldly harm is part of sin’s loss, while the Hereafter completes it.

The final verdict “not guided” answers claims that these customs were pious. Intensity, sacrifice, and antiquity do not make a path divine. Guidance needs authority from Allah.

Modern readers should avoid using “pre-Islamic ignorance” as a distant insult. Any society can sacrifice children’s safety to wealth, honour, ideology, or adult convenience and then create moral language to hide it. Application must remain careful and informed, especially in contested medical and legal questions.
