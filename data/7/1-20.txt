=1=

The surah opens with four separate Arabic letters: Alif, Lām, Mīm, and Ṣād. These are part of the mysterious disjointed letters that appear at the beginning of twenty-nine chapters of the Quran. Their exact meaning is known fully only to Allah, yet scholars have offered several profound insights that help us appreciate their presence. Many companions and early authorities held that these letters serve as a divine challenge. The Quran is composed of the very same letters that the Arabs used in their daily speech and in their most eloquent poetry, yet no one could produce anything like it. By opening the revelation with these ordinary letters, Allah immediately draws attention to the fact that the Book which follows is made of familiar components, yet its arrangement, depth, and power transcend human ability. This silently demolishes any claim that the Prophet manufactured the Quran. Another wisdom is that these letters capture the attention of listeners, especially in a culture that prized oratory. When the recitation began with these detached sounds, the Meccan polytheists would fall silent out of curiosity, and then the message would enter their hearts. Some scholars also note that many of these letter combinations appear in conjunction with mentions of the Scripture, as in the next verse, linking the letters to the greatness of the Quran. The presence of Alif-Lām-Mīm-Ṣād, with the added letter Ṣād, distinguishes this surah and hints at the weighty subjects it contains, including the stories of prophets and the detailed descriptions of the Hereafter.

For the believer, these letters cultivate a sense of humility. We recognise that the Quran contains dimensions beyond our grasp, and that our knowledge is limited. The fact that the Prophet himself did not provide a definitive explanation shows that the core of faith rests on acceptance of revelation, not on knowing every secret.

Thus the surah begins with an air of majesty, reminding us that the speech of Allah is unlike any other speech, and even its opening letters are heavy with meaning that calls for reflection. This sets the stage for the solemn address that follows, preparing the heart to receive the Book without anxiety and with complete trust in its divine origin.

=2=

After the opening letters, Allah immediately clarifies the nature and purpose of the Quran. He calls it “a Book sent down to you,” emphasising that the revelation comes from above, descending upon the Prophet Muhammad in a gradual, purposeful manner. The address is direct and intimate: “do not let anxiety into your heart regarding it.” The Prophet faced immense pressure in Mecca. He was mocked, accused of sorcery and madness, and burdened by the rejection of his own people. His heart naturally felt distress, yet Allah consoles him by commanding him not to let any tightness or worry reside in his chest. This teaches us that the sincere caller to truth may experience inner turmoil when faced with hostility, but Allah’s comfort is ever near.

The verse then states the twin objectives of the Book: “so with it you may warn the disbelievers, and as a reminder to the believers. ” Warning and reminding are central prophetic tasks. The disbelievers need to be alerted to the consequences of their rejection, while the believers need the continuous reminder to keep their faith vibrant. Even believers can become heedless, so the Quran refreshes their hearts and guides them.

The phrase “with it” highlights that the Quran itself is the instrument. The Prophet is not to rely on his own words or eloquence, but on the divinely revealed text. The reassurance here is profound: the Book is not a burden but a tool of mercy, an honour granted to the Prophet. If he feels the weight of the message, let him remember that the One who sent it down will also protect and support him.

This verse also subtly corrects the notion that the Prophet could guide anyone he wished. His duty is to deliver the warning and the reminder; the outcome belongs to Allah. The surah’s opening thus establishes the authority of the Quran, comforts the messenger, and outlines the mission that will be illustrated throughout the chapter with vivid examples of previous nations who were warned and reminded, yet turned away.

=3=

After comforting the Prophet and clarifying the purpose of the Book, Allah turns the address to all of humanity, calling them to action. “Follow what has been sent down to you from your Lord” means to obey the Quran and make it the guiding principle of life.

The phrase “from your Lord” is filled with tenderness and authority. It is your own Lord, the One who created, sustains, and cares for you, who has sent this guidance.

Therefore, following it is both an act of gratitude and the surest path to success. The command to follow is comprehensive, encompassing beliefs, acts of worship, manners, and the entire system of life. The very next phrase forbids taking “others as guardians besides Him.” A guardian, or “wali,” implies someone to whom a person turns for protection, loyalty, and ultimate reliance. Humans are prone to seeking protectors other than Allah, whether they be idols, powerful personalities, wealth, or their own desires. This verse prohibits making any such entity a guardian alongside or in place of Allah. True guardianship belongs only to the Creator.

The verse ends with a sobering question: “How seldom are you mindful! ” This is a lament over human forgetfulness. Despite clear revelation and countless signs, people rarely remember, reflect, and act accordingly. The rhetorical question serves as a gentle rebuke, stirring the conscience of the listener. It reminds us that heedlessness is the default human state unless actively resisted through constant remembrance of Allah. This verse links the Book’s descent to the demand for exclusive devotion and obedience, preparing for the stories of destruction that follow. Those destroyed peoples forgot their Lord and took other guardians, and they were seldom mindful. The message is simple: recognise the Book, follow it, make Allah your only guardian, and do not be among the heedless.

=4=

This verse marks a shift to historical warning. After commanding people to follow the revelation and not take other guardians, Allah invites reflection on past nations that refused to do so.

The word “Imagine” or “how many” urges us to visualise the scale of destruction. This is not a single event but repeated patterns throughout history.

The phrase “how many societies” indicates whole civilisations, advanced and powerful, yet utterly wiped out. Allah does not mention names here; the mere idea is enough to create awe and fear. The torment overtook them by surprise, either at night while they slept, or at midday while they were taking rest. These two times symbolise moments of safety and relaxation. A sudden punishment at such a time is most terrifying, because no one expects it. It highlights that divine retribution does not come with a long warning; it comes when people least anticipate it, exposing the futility of their false sense of security.

This is consistent with how the people of Lot were struck at sunrise, and how the people of Shu’aib were seized by an earthquake in the morning. The verse serves to shake the complacent Meccans who thought their wealth and power would protect them. It also teaches a timeless principle: societies that turn away from Allah’s guidance and persist in corruption will eventually face consequences, whether in this world or the next. The suddenness of the torment is a mercy in disguise in terms of warning the living, because it makes them ponder their own vulnerability. Every night when we sleep and every afternoon when we rest, we are completely dependent on Allah’s protection. If He wills, He could seize us. This verse plants the fear of Allah deep in the heart, preparing the listener to take the coming stories seriously and to examine their own life before the moment of surprise arrives.

=5=

After depicting the sudden seizure of past nations, Allah now records their final utterance. When the divine punishment descended and enveloped them, their only cry was an admission: “We have indeed been wrongdoers.

” The phrase “only cry” indicates that this was the sole thing they could say at that moment. All their arrogance, denial, and mockery vanished. The truth that they had suppressed became stark and undeniable. They confessed their wrongdoing, which means they acknowledged their injustice, their association of partners with Allah, and their rejection of the messengers. This admission, however, came too late. It is a confession at the point of death or destruction, which is not accepted as repentance. The Quran repeatedly shows that when people see the punishment with their own eyes, faith no longer benefits them. The acknowledgement of being wrongdoers is significant. It means they did not cry out for help from their false gods, nor did they blame the messengers. They finally realised that the fault lay entirely within themselves. This teaches that deep down, even the most hardened disbeliever knows the truth, but worldly distractions, pride, and desires prevent them from embracing it.

The verse also consoles the Prophet and the believers. When the disbelievers of Mecca mocked and rejected, they appeared confident, but this verse reveals that those who follow their path will ultimately have no words but regret. For the living, this is an urgent call to repent before it is too late. The door of repentance is open now, while we are not overwhelmed by the punishment. Once the decree of destruction comes, words of regret will be of no avail. This verse connects powerfully with the previous warning: the sudden torment leaves them with only one cry, and it is a cry of self-condemnation.

=6=

Having mentioned the destruction of societies and their final cry, Allah now shifts the scene to the Day of Judgment, to establish the absolute justice that underpins everything. The verse declares that on that Day, two groups will be questioned. First, “those who received messengers” — every community to which a prophet was sent will be asked about how they responded. Did they believe, or did they reject? Did they obey, or did they mock? This questioning is not for Allah to gain knowledge, for He already knows everything, but it is to establish the evidence against them and to make them bear witness against themselves. Second, “We will question the messengers themselves.” The prophets will be asked about whether they delivered the message fully and what response they received from their people.

This is a testimony in favour of the messengers and a proof against the rejecters. The Prophet Muhammad is reported to have explained that on the Day of Judgment, a prophet will come with a few followers, another with one or two, and some with none at all, while his nation will be summoned and questioned. The questioning of the messengers also confirms their honour; they will testify truthfully, and their testimony will be accepted. This verse assures the Prophet that he is not accountable for people’s rejection beyond conveying the message. It also warns those who heard the message but turned away that their denial will be confronted directly. The comprehensive questioning demonstrates the meticulous justice of Allah. No one will be punished without a clear establishment of guilt. Even the messengers, the most honoured of creation, will give their account, which underscores the principle that no one is above being held to their duty. This thought-provoking verse prepares the heart for the next stage: the detailed recounting that follows the questioning.

=7=

Following the questioning, Allah will present a complete and detailed account of all deeds. “Then We will give them a full account” means that every action, word, and even intention will be laid out with precision.

The Arabic term for “full account” implies a narrative or a story told in sequence, indicating that each person’s life record will be replayed, leaving no room for denial or confusion. This account is given “with sure knowledge. ” Allah’s knowledge is perfect, encompassing the outward and the hidden, the past and the future. There is no guesswork, no need for witnesses to be cross-examined, because the One Who gives the account is the All-Knowing.

The verse concludes with a powerful statement: “for We were never absent.” This addresses the misguided human tendency to think that God is distant or unaware of what people do. It directly refutes the idea that Allah created the world and left it to its own devices. He is always present, always watching. When people commit sins in secret, thinking no one sees them, they forget that the One who will judge them was never absent. This attribute of divine presence is a source of comfort for the believers and a warning for the wrongdoers. The believers feel reassured that their struggles, their hidden tears, and their sincere deeds are all recorded and will be recounted with justice. The criminals tremble at the realisation that their secret plots and hidden disbelief are an open book before their Lord. The logical flow is perfect: after the questioning, Allah Himself provides the irrefutable account. No lawyer, no intercessor, can alter what the all-knowing Judge presents.

This verse also connects to the earlier warning about sudden destruction, showing that whether punishment comes in this world or is delayed, every deed will ultimately be accounted for. No one escapes this reality, for Allah was never absent.

=8=

From the detailed account, Allah moves to the symbolic yet utterly real act of weighing deeds. “The weighing on that Day will be just” means that the scales of justice will be set up, and every deed will be evaluated with absolute fairness. The Quran and authentic hadith affirm that there will be actual scales on which deeds, or the records of deeds, or the individuals themselves will be weighed, in a manner befitting the majesty of the Hereafter without distorting the divine attribute of justice. The scales are a manifestation of Allah’s perfect equity, giving each person exactly what they deserve, not an atom’s weight less or more. The standard is clear: those whose scale is heavy with good deeds will be the successful ones. The “heavy” scale indicates a preponderance of righteous actions, faith, sincerity, and obedience.

This image is extremely powerful and easy to understand. It appeals to the human sense of balance and measurement. Success here is eternal salvation and entry into Paradise. The verse uses the word “only” to stress that there is no other way to succeed. No amount of wealth, lineage, or worldly status will tip the scale; only good deeds carried out with faith will weigh heavy. This also teaches that deeds are tangible realities that will be given weight in the sight of Allah. A small deed done with pure intention can be heavier than mountains of insincere worship. The Prophet emphasised that the two phrases “SubhanAllah wa bihamdihi” are light on the tongue but heavy on the scale.

Thus, believers are encouraged to fill their scales with sincere remembrance, charity, prayer, and kindness. The mention of the scale immediately after the full account assures that the judgment will not be arbitrary; it will be based on a measurable, undeniable standard. This verse fills the heart with hope and fear, prompting a daily check on what we are sending forward to weigh on that momentous Day.

=9=

After describing the successful ones, the surah turns to the opposite group. The scale being light signifies that the person’s good deeds are outweighed by their evil deeds or that they are devoid of good entirely due to disbelief. When the scale is light, the outcome is catastrophic: “they have doomed themselves.

” The Arabic expression literally means they have lost their own souls or caused themselves to perish. This self-inflicted ruin emphasises that no one else is to blame; it is a direct result of their own choices. The specific reason given for their loss is “wrongfully denying Our signs.” The signs include the verses of the Quran, the miracles granted to the prophets, and the countless natural phenomena that point to the Creator. Denial is an active refusal, often coupled with arrogance.

The word “wrongfully” indicates that their rejection is not based on honest confusion or lack of clarity, but on injustice and wilful stubbornness. The signs were clear, the messengers sincere, yet they turned away. This denial nullifies any good they might have done, because deeds are only accepted when built upon faith. A scale cannot be heavy with good deeds if the foundation of belief in Allah’s signs is missing. This verse provides a profound psychological insight: those who reject the truth are essentially wronging themselves. They are depriving themselves of eternal bliss and throwing themselves into destruction. By linking the light scale directly to the denial of signs, Allah warns the Quraysh and all later disbelievers that their rejection of the Prophet’s message is not a minor matter; it is the very cause of their ultimate ruin.

For the believer, this verse fosters a sense of urgency to safeguard faith, to act upon the Quran, and to avoid anything that belittles or denies the divine signs. It also reiterates the perfect justice of the Hereafter: loss comes only after clear warning has been rejected.

=10=

Having described the fate of rejecters and the scales of the Hereafter, Allah turns to a direct address to humanity, reminding them of His favours in this world. “We have indeed established you on earth” means that Allah has given human beings a firm place to live, a stable habitat equipped with everything necessary for survival. He made the earth spread out, with mountains to stabilise it, water to drink, and soil to cultivate. The establishment is not merely physical; it includes the gift of authority and the ability to build civilisations, extract resources, and harness nature. Then comes the second great favour: “and provided you with a means of livelihood.” This encompasses all the provisions that sustain life — food, drink, clothing, air, and the various means by which people earn a living.

The phrase “means of livelihood” includes the tools, knowledge, and opportunities that Allah bestows so that people can fulfil their needs. After mentioning these immense blessings, the verse laments, “Yet you seldom give any thanks. ” This is a sorrowful observation of human ingratitude. Despite being surrounded by divine favours from the moment they open their eyes, people are rarely thankful. Gratitude here means acknowledging the Giver, using the blessings in obedience to Him, and not employing them in ways that displease Him. The lack of thanks is directly connected to the arrogance and denial mentioned in previous verses. When people enjoy Allah’s gifts without recognising His right to be worshipped, they are being ungrateful, and this ingratitude leads to destruction. This verse serves as a bridge to the story of Adam and Iblis that follows. Adam was given a place in Paradise with abundant provision but was warned; Iblis, on the other hand, was given honour among the angels but responded with arrogance and ingratitude.

The sequence teaches that gratitude is the heart of faith, and its absence is the beginning of downfall.

=11=

This verse begins the detailed account of the creation of Adam and the test of obedience that followed. “Surely We created you” can refer to the creation of Adam, the father of humanity, and by extension all human beings. “Then shaped you” indicates that this creation was not abrupt but involved careful formation, as mentioned elsewhere that Allah fashioned Adam with His own hands and breathed into him of His spirit. This shaping points to the perfection and honour given to human beings. After creating Adam in this distinguished manner, Allah commanded the angels to prostrate before him. This prostration was an act of respect and acknowledgement of Adam’s superiority in knowledge, not an act of worship. Worship is for Allah alone, but this command was a test for the angels and for Iblis, who was present among them. The verse says “so they all did,” meaning the angels obeyed without hesitation, demonstrating their pure devotion. The exception was Iblis, who refused to prostrate with the others. Scholars explain that Iblis was not from the angels in origin but from the jinn; however, due to his intense worship, he was elevated to be in their company. His refusal was the first act of disobedience against a direct divine command. It arose from arrogance, as the next verse will elaborate. The contrast between the angels’ instant obedience and Iblis’s refusal highlights the dangers of pride and the importance of submission. This account is placed here after the verses on gratitude because Iblis’s refusal was essentially an act of ingratitude for the rank he had been given. It also serves as a warning that past devotion does not guarantee future steadfastness if arrogance creeps into the heart.

The mention of all this in the context of the surah aims to remind the Quraysh that their refusal to follow the Prophet echoes the arrogance of Iblis, and the believers are warned to remain humble.

=12=

This verse captures the dialogue that exposed the root of Iblis’s rebellion. Allah’s question was not for information; He already knew the reason.

The question was to reveal Iblis’s inner sickness and to establish the evidence against him. By asking “What prevented you from prostrating when I commanded you?”, Allah highlights that the command was clear, direct, and binding. There was no ambiguity, no exception. Iblis’s response was not one of remorse or apology, but of arrogant justification. He said, “I am better than he is,” then provided his reasoning: “You created me from fire and him from clay.

” This is the earliest recorded instance of discriminatory reasoning, where a being considers himself superior based on his origin or composition. Iblis looked at the outward material and concluded that fire is nobler than clay because fire rises and is luminous, while clay is lowly and dark. This was a profound error. True nobility is not in the material but in piety, obedience, and the spirit that Allah breathes into a being.

Moreover, Iblis ignored that the command itself was the standard; by refusing, he proved himself inferior in the very quality that matters: submission. The verse teaches that when Allah commands, the only correct response is “we hear and we obey.” Any attempt to rationalise disobedience by appealing to perceived personal merit is satanic. The scholars note that Iblis was the first to use analogical reasoning (qiyas) to oppose a divine text, preferring his own logic over revelation.

This is a danger for all believers: when personal reasoning or societal values clash with clear divine commands, one must submit. The exchange also shows that Allah allows the disobedient to voice their argument so that the truth of their rebellion becomes manifest to all. In Iblis’s response, we see the prototype of every arrogant person who belittles others and refuses to bow to truth.

=13=

In immediate response to Iblis’s arrogant justification, Allah pronounces the sentence of expulsion. The command “get down from Paradise” signifies both a physical and spiritual fall. The Paradise mentioned here is the heavenly garden of honour, not the eternal Paradise of the Hereafter, though some scholars hold that it was the same. Regardless, it was a place of purity and closeness. By his pride, Iblis forfeited his abode there. Allah then gives the reason: “It is not for you to be arrogant here.” Paradise is the realm of peace, humility, and divine mercy. Arrogance has no place in it.

This statement establishes a universal principle: arrogance is incompatible with the atmosphere of Allah’s mercy and nearness. The one who is arrogant in this world will find the gates of Paradise shut before him. The Prophet said that anyone with an atom’s weight of arrogance in his heart will not enter Paradise. Allah’s command “So get out!” reinforces the urgency and finality of the expulsion. There is no room for negotiation. Then comes the verdict: “You are truly one of the disgraced.

” The word “disgraced” implies humiliation, abasement, and a state of being belittled. Iblis, who sought to elevate himself above Adam, was brought to the lowest station.

This is the inevitable outcome of pride before Allah. The disgrace is both immediate and eternal. For the human being, this verse carries a sharp warning. Every time a person feels superior to others due to race, wealth, lineage, or knowledge, and disobeys Allah as a result, they are emulating Iblis. The cure is to remember that all humans come from clay, from a humble origin, and that their true worth lies in taqwa.

The verse also comforts the believers who are humiliated on earth for their faith; the real disgrace is the one that Allah decrees on the arrogant. The surah’s narrative now sets the stage for Iblis’s response, showing how his disgrace did not lead to repentance but to a vengeful determination to mislead.

=14=

Rather than repenting or acknowledging his wrong, Iblis immediately shifted to a request born of his new mission of enmity. He said, “Then delay my end until the Day of their resurrection.

” The word “then” connects his appeal to the judgment of disgrace. Realising that he is doomed, he does not ask for forgiveness. Instead, he asks for respite — not death or destruction — until the day when human beings are resurrected for judgment. His request reveals his cunning. He knows that his expulsion is tied to Adam and his progeny. He wants to live long enough to prove, in his twisted thinking, that humanity is ungrateful and unworthy of the honour given to them. He seeks time to mislead them and to show that his initial assessment was correct. The “Day of their resurrection” is the Day of Judgment, when all humans will be raised. By specifying that day, Iblis shows awareness that after resurrection, no more deeds can be done and the test is over. So he aims to invest all his extended lifespan in corrupting the descendants of Adam. This request was granted in a limited way, as the next verse will clarify. The appeal also indicates that Iblis did not ask for respite for the sake of the jinn or others, but specifically to target human beings. It underlines the unique status of humans as the central actors in the cosmic test, with Satan as their avowed enemy.

For believers, this verse is a stark reminder that we have an enemy who has been alive for millennia, studying human weaknesses, and who has made it his life’s purpose to lead us astray. He has no other occupation. The recognition of this enemy should prompt vigilance, not fear of him, because Allah has provided the tools to resist him. Iblis’s request was accepted, meaning that his existence until that day is part of the divine plan, a test for mankind to see who will remain grateful and who will follow his footsteps.

=15=

Allah’s response to Iblis’s appeal was a qualified grant. “You are delayed” indicates that the request was accepted, but not unconditionally. Iblis is not given immortality in the absolute sense; he is only given respite until a specific, known time: “the appointed Day.” This day is generally understood by scholars to be the Day when the trumpet will be blown, the Day of Resurrection when all creation dies before being raised again. It is not the Day of Judgment in its entirety, but the end of the first phase. Some interpretations say it is until the Day of Reckoning. The point is that Iblis’s respite is temporary. He will taste death along with all created beings.

This is significant because it reminds humans that Satan, despite his long lifespan and his ability to see us from where we cannot see him, is not a rival to Allah. He is a creature, under Allah’s command, and his time is limited. The believers should not be overwhelmed by the scope of his misguidance; his power is restrained and his end is decreed. The delay also serves a profound divine wisdom. By granting Iblis’s request, Allah allows the test of life to unfold. Human beings will now have to make choices in the presence of two calls: the call of the messengers and the call of Satan. The existence of Satan as an enemy makes the reward of obedience greater, because it is done against whispers and temptations. The believers’ victory over Satan in this life demonstrates their sincerity and strengthens their rank.

This verse also subtly hints at the mercy inherent in the test: Allah could have destroyed Iblis immediately, but He allowed this delay so that the ultimate justice of the Hereafter would be fully demonstrated, with Iblis and his followers facing their punishment after the evidence has been established against them. For the Prophet and his companions, this context of the ancient enmity explains the fierce opposition they faced and reminds them that the path of all prophets is paved with satanic resistance.

=16=

Having secured his respite, Iblis now openly declares his malevolent plan. He begins by attributing his straying to Allah: “For leaving me to stray.” While this might seem like shifting blame, in Quranic theology it acknowledges that all events occur by Allah’s will. Iblis recognises that his misguidance happened because Allah allowed it, having decreed that arrogance leads to ruin.

However, Iblis’s use of this phrase is not an admission of his own fault but a pretext for his revenge. He declares, “I will lie in ambush for them on Your Straight Path.” The Straight Path is the path of Islam, the way of obedience that leads to Allah’s pleasure. Iblis strategically stations himself on this very path, not on the obvious roads of sin. He knows that the believers are walking towards Allah, so he sits on that route to divert them. The metaphor of lying in ambush suggests hidden traps, unexpected attacks, and a calculated effort to waylay travellers. This tells us that the closer a person gets to the Straight Path, the more intense Satan’s efforts become. He does not bother much with those already deep in misguidance; he focuses on those who are trying to be righteous. His ambushes include planting doubts, beautifying sins, encouraging procrastination in repentance, stirring disputes among believers, and making worldly distractions seem urgent. The fact that Iblis calls it “Your Straight Path” shows that he acknowledges the path belongs to Allah, yet his arrogance persists. He is not denying Allah’s sovereignty; he is waging war within the boundaries set by Allah. This verse is a powerful alert for every Muslim. As soon as you commit to the Straight Path, you become a target. The awareness of this ambush is itself a shield. Allah does not leave us without weapons: He has given us the Quran, prayer, and the ability to seek refuge.

The verse also illustrates the depth of Iblis’s enmity: he is not content with being astray himself; he wants to ensure that as many humans as possible share his fate.

=17=

Satan continues outlining his comprehensive strategy of assault. He will approach human beings from all four directions: front, back, right, and left. The scholars have explained the significance of these directions in several complementary ways. Approaching from the front means casting doubts about the Hereafter, making the future seem uncertain, and discouraging people from preparing for it. From the back refers to entangling them in worldly pursuits, making them love wealth, status, and comfort to the extent that they forget death and accountability. From the right signifies attacking through their virtues, perhaps by corrupting their religious enthusiasm, making them extreme, or causing them to show off in worship. From the left means tempting them through desires, sins, and pleasures, making disobedience attractive and appealing. The absence of “above” and “below” is noteworthy. Scholars say that “above” represents the direction from which divine mercy descends, and the sincere believer is connected to Allah above, so Satan cannot approach from there. “Below” may be omitted because it is not an approach that would appeal to human dignity. The comprehensive nature of the attack shows that no aspect of human life is safe from satanic whispers unless fortified by faith. Then Satan makes a grim prediction: “then You will find most of them ungrateful. ” He is confident that his efforts will succeed in making the majority of humanity heedless and unthankful. He says this based on his knowledge of human weakness, but it is also a challenge. This prediction is echoed elsewhere in the Quran where it is said that most people are not grateful.

The use of “ungrateful” ties back to the earlier verse about seldom giving thanks. The core of Satan’s mission is to sever the bond of gratitude between the servant and the Lord. If he can make people forget Allah’s favours, they will not worship or obey Him. For the believers, this verse is a call to close all avenues of Satan. Guard the mind, the desires, the acts of worship, and the worldly engagements. Recognise that any approach of temptation, whether from a seemingly good or bad angle, could be a satanic trap. The remedy is constant remembrance of Allah and seeking refuge, which fortifies the servant from all sides.

=18=

Allah’s response to Iblis’s proud declaration is a second, even more emphatic expulsion. The command “Get out of Paradise” is repeated, signifying that there is no turning back. Then two severe attributes are attached to Iblis: “disgraced and rejected.” “Disgraced” (madhmum) means loaded with blame and contempt. “Rejected” (madhur) means driven away, expelled from all good, and accursed.

This is Iblis’s permanent status. He is not just cast out of a place; he is cast out of divine mercy. After establishing this, Allah declares His decree: “I will certainly fill up Hell with you and your followers all together. ” This is a divine promise and a warning. Hell will be filled with Iblis and all those who follow him, whether from among the jinn or mankind.

The phrase “all together” indicates that they will share the same fate, with no separation between the leader of misguidance and his followers. The false promise Iblis made to himself about being noble is entirely shattered. He will be the fuel of Hell along with those he deceived. This verse provides immense consolation to the believers. It shows that Satan’s power is temporary, and his final abode is humiliation. Those who side with him, whether by overt disbelief or by following his whispers, will share that humiliation. It also contains a call to humanity: choose your companions wisely. Those who take Satan as an ally in this world will find themselves grouped with him in the next. The verse transitions perfectly to the story of Adam and Eve, where the same enemy will attempt to deceive them in Paradise, but with a crucial difference: they will repent, and Allah will guide them.

This contrast between the arrogance of Iblis, which leads to permanent rejection, and the sin of Adam followed by repentance, which leads to mercy, is central to the Quranic message. The surah will now relate how the first human test unfolded.

=19=

With Iblis expelled and his enmity declared, Allah turns to Adam and Hawwa (Eve) and grants them a residence in Paradise. The address “O Adam” is personal and filled with care. Adam is told to live with his wife in this garden of bliss. The command to “eat from wherever you please” indicates the vast permissibility and abundance of Paradise. They could enjoy everything it contained with complete freedom, except for one specific prohibition: “do not approach this tree.” The identity of the tree is not specified in the Quran, and scholars say the exact type is irrelevant. The lesson lies in the fact that it was a test of obedience. The tree was not harmful in itself; its prohibition was purely a means to distinguish obedience from rebellion. Allah made the command clear, and the consequence of violating it was equally clear: “or else you will be wrongdoers.” This means that approaching the tree and eating from it would be an act of injustice against themselves. This mild yet profound phrase indicates that sin is essentially a form of self-wronging. It does not harm Allah; it harms the one who commits it. Adam’s abode in Paradise at this stage was not the eternal abode of the Hereafter but a place of testing, an initial garden where the first human drama would unfold. The command was a test of will, gratitude, and submission. Adam had everything, and only one tree was off-limits. This mirrors the human condition in the world: Allah has made countless things lawful and only a few things prohibited. The test is not about deprivation; it is about recognising boundaries set by the Creator out of His wisdom.

The phrase “do not approach this tree” is significant; it forbids not just eating but going near it, teaching that one should stay away from the avenues that lead to sin. This divine instruction sets the stage for the satanic deception that follows, showing that Satan will exploit the one prohibition to make them feel deprived and to lure them into disobedience.

After the command was given, Satan, already filled with enmity, began his work. The verse says “Then Satan tempted them,” indicating that he approached Adam and Eve with whispers designed to make the forbidden tree seem desirable and to cast doubt on Allah’s command. The specific goal of this temptation is mentioned: “in order to expose what was hidden of their nakedness.” Before this, Adam and Eve had garments that covered them, and they were unaware of their private parts. Satan’s plot was to strip them of that covering, both literally and symbolically, by making them disobey. This reveals a sinister aspect of Satan’s character: he finds pleasure in uncovering human weakness and shame. The means of temptation was a false claim wrapped in apparent goodwill. He said, “Your Lord has forbidden this tree to you only to prevent you from becoming angels or immortals. ” This was a direct lie coupled with a subtle insinuation about Allah’s motive. He suggested that Allah’s prohibition was not out of care but out of a desire to limit them, to stop them from attaining a higher state. The words “only to prevent you” are the classic satanic seed of doubt: that God is not truly benevolent, that His commands are obstacles to human greatness rather than protections. The lure of becoming angels or living forever tapped into the natural human desire for elevation and permanence. Satan presented himself as a well-wisher revealing hidden wisdom behind the command, whereas he was entirely deceitful. This temptation demonstrates his strategy as earlier declared: he will come from directions that appeal to innate human aspirations. For the believers, this verse is a masterclass in recognising the psychology of sin. The sin is often presented as liberation, advancement, or the removal of an unfair restriction. The lesson is to trust Allah’s wisdom over any whisper that paints a prohibition as an unjust barrier. Adam and Eve’s experience would become a timeless lesson for their descendants: to beware of the enemy who distorts divine mercy into alleged oppression and who promises elevation but delivers disgrace.
