After describing the encompassing power of Allah and the persistent denial of the disbelievers, the sūrah shifts focus to the very source of guidance that they are rejecting: “In fact, this is a glorious Quran.” The Arabic bal huwa qur’ānun majīd uses the particle bal to correct and elevate. The disbelievers may think the revelation is poetry, magic, or ancient fables, but the truth is that it is a Qur’ān of immense glory.

The word majīd is the same attribute used for Allah in verse fifteen, “the All-Glorious,” and for the Throne in some readings. By describing the Qur’ān as majīd, the verse connects the book directly to its divine source. The Qur’ān is glorious in its origin, glorious in its content, glorious in its style, and glorious in its impact. It is a book of majesty, nobility, and sublime honour. No human speech can match it. The Meccans who called it “ancient fables” were insulting not just words but a revelation that shares in the glory of the Lord of the Throne. The verse is a powerful refutation of their mockery. It tells them that what they are turning away from is not a minor thing; it is a glorious Quran, a book that would elevate them if they honoured it.

The description also serves as a comfort to the Prophet (ﷺ) and the believers. The persecution they face is for the sake of this glorious Quran, and its glory will ultimately triumph.

The word qur’ān itself means a recitation, something to be read and proclaimed. The adjective majīd assures the believers that every verse they recite is majestic and worthy of any sacrifice.

The verse also implicitly contrasts the transient glory of the tyrants with the eternal glory of the Qur’ān. The palaces of Pharaoh, the rock-hewn homes of Thamūd, and the ditches of the Himyarite king have all crumbled to dust, but the glorious Quran remains, recited by millions across the ages. The glory of the Qur’ān is not material; it is a spiritual and intellectual glory that outlasts all earthly power. The verse thus re-centres the discussion on the revelation itself, preparing for the final verse that reveals its celestial preservation.

The sūrah concludes with a majestic statement about the Qur’ān’s heavenly origin and protection: “recorded in a Preserved Tablet.” The Arabic fī lawḥin maḥfūẓ is a prepositional phrase describing the location and status of the glorious Qur’ān.

The word lawḥ means a tablet, a board, or a flat surface on which writing is inscribed. Al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ is the Preserved Tablet, a celestial record that exists in the highest realm, directly under the knowledge and care of Allah. It is the source of all divine decrees and scriptures. The Qur’ān, before being sent down to the lowest heaven and then revealed piecemeal to the Prophet (ﷺ), was inscribed on this Tablet.

The word maḥfūẓ means preserved, protected, guarded. It is safeguarded from any alteration, corruption, loss, or satanic interference. This preservation is absolute. The disbelievers’ attempts to mock, distort, or extinguish the Qur’ān are utterly futile because the original is preserved with the Lord of the Throne. This verse is the ultimate answer to those who said the Qur’ān was a fabrication or that it could be rivaled. A book that is protected in the Preserved Tablet cannot be falsified.

The verse also ties back to the opening of the sūrah, which swore by the sky full of constellations. The sky is a visible book of stars; the Preserved Tablet is the unseen book of divine decree. Both are creations of Allah, but the Preserved Tablet is higher and more protected.

The mention of the Preserved Tablet also connects to the earlier theme of witnessing. The deeds of the oppressors and the steadfastness of the believers are all recorded in a divine record that is perfectly preserved. No detail is lost. The sūrah thus ends on a note of immense hope and security for the believers. The Qur’ān they hold, the words they recite, are a transcript from a Tablet that is with Allah, guarded from any change. This gives the believer absolute confidence in the authenticity and authority of the revelation. The Preserved Tablet also signifies that the fate of the tyrants and the reward of the righteous are already written in the eternal decree, and nothing can alter that final, divine script.
