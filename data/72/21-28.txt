=21=

After the Prophet is commanded to declare his exclusive worship of Allah, he is now instructed to clarify the limits of his own human capacity. The disbelievers of Quraysh often challenged him, demanding that he bring down the punishment they were warned about, or that he perform miracles on command, or that he grant them some worldly benefit to prove his truthfulness. They treated him almost as if he claimed divine powers. This verse resets the understanding: “Say, ‘It is not in my power to harm or benefit you.’” The Arabic “la amliku” means “I do not own, I do not possess, I have no control over” any harm or benefit for you. The Prophet, peace be upon him, despite being the best of creation, is still a servant. He cannot cause a person to become sick or healthy, poor or rich, guided or misguided. All power belongs to Allah alone.

This is a profound statement of tawheed in action. It prevents any exaggeration of the Prophet’s status, closing the door to the kind of saint-worship that corrupted earlier nations. He cannot independently harm even those who mock him, nor can he grant paradise or worldly success to his most beloved followers. His role is purely to convey.

This verse also answers the psychological state of the pagans who feared that the Prophet’s prayer might bring a curse upon them. They accused him of being a sorcerer who could harm people with his words. He is to clarify that even if he wished to harm them, he has no power to do so unless Allah wills it. Similarly, he cannot force benefit upon them, such as forcing faith into their hearts.

The verse emphasizes the absolute dependence of all creation on Allah. The Prophet is a mercy to the worlds, but that mercy is a reflection of Allah’s mercy, not an independent power.

For believers, this instills reliance on Allah alone. Even the Prophet cannot be a substitute for the Creator.

The verse also provides comfort to the Prophet; he is not responsible for the outcome, only for the delivery. He does not need to feel burdened by their rejection as if he had failed to compel them.

=22=

This verse deepens the theme of the Prophet’s servanthood and humanity. After declaring his lack of power to harm or benefit others, he is told to announce his own vulnerability before Allah. “No one can protect me from Allah if I were to disobey Him.

” The Arabic “innee lan yujeeranee min Allahi ahad” is a powerful negation: never, under any circumstance, could anyone grant him protection from Allah’s punishment if he were to disobey. This includes all the jinn who might have been thought to have power, all the angels, all the idols, and all the Quraysh chiefs. Even the Prophet, the most beloved of creation to Allah, is not given a blank cheque of immunity. If he were to deviate from the revelation, no intercessor could shield him.

This statement demolishes any notion of prophetic infallibility meaning exemption from accountability; rather, the prophets are protected from persisting in major sin, but they live with the constant awe of Allah’s justice. Some Quraysh had suggested that the Prophet should compromise, maybe worship their gods for a year and they would worship his God. This verse is a rebuke: even if he desired to please them, he could not, for the fear of Allah’s punishment is his shield. Then the verse adds, “nor can I find any refuge other than Him.

” The word “multahadan” means a place of retreat, a shelter, a sanctuary. No mountain, no cave, no powerful ally can offer him a hiding place from Allah’s decree. This echoes the jinn’s earlier admission that they cannot escape Allah on earth or in heaven. The Prophet is aligning himself fully with the position of a dependent servant. The lesson for the believers is that if the Prophet himself lives with this consciousness, how much more should ordinary Muslims fear disobedience? No amount of piety or good deeds allows one to feel secure from Allah’s plan. The only true refuge is in Allah Himself, through His mercy. The verse, therefore, is a powerful call to humility, stripping away any reliance on human status or connections.

=23=

Having clarified what he cannot do, the Prophet now states positively what his mission is: “My duty is only to convey the truth from Allah and deliver His messages.” The word “illa” (only) restricts his role. He is a messenger, a carrier of the divine message.

The phrase “the truth from Allah” refers to the Quran and the guidance that leads to righteousness. “His messages” (risalatihi) encompasses all the teachings, laws, and warnings that form the complete religion. Conveying (balagh) means to deliver clearly, completely, and without distortion.

This is the sole obligation for which he will be held accountable. The verse thus divides responsibility: the Prophet conveys, and the people choose. No one can blame him if they reject, because he has fulfilled the duty. This relieves the Prophet of the psychological weight of their rejection; their faith is not in his hands.

The verse then abruptly shifts to a stern warning: “And whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger will certainly be in the Fire of Hell, to stay there for ever and ever.” Disobedience here refers to kufr and rebellion against the message conveyed. The punishment is eternal Hellfire.

This warning is directed squarely at the pagan deniers who hear the Prophet’s message but refuse to obey. The phrase “to stay there for ever and ever” (khalideena fiha abada) emphasizes the permanence for those who die in disbelief.

This is consistent with the Quranic teaching that Hell is eternal for the disbelievers. The contrast is intentional: the Prophet only conveys, but those who reject the conveyance face eternal ruin. It also serves as a reminder to the believers that obedience to the Messenger is not optional; it is obedience to Allah. The verse beautifully encapsulates the balance of Islamic da‘wah: a clear, non-coercive presentation of truth, coupled with a serious warning of the consequence of rejection. The Prophet’s mission is mercy, but it carries the weight of the ultimate reality.

=24=

The surah now turns to the disbelievers who mocked and challenged the Prophet, boasting of their superior numbers and strength. “Only when they see what they have been threatened with will they know...” The “what they have been threatened with” is the Day of Judgment, the punishment of the Fire, the very reality they denied and demanded to see immediately.

The phrase “only when they see” implies that their current ignorance and arrogance will last until that moment of shock. Then, and only then, will they “know who is weaker in helpers and inferior in manpower.” In this world, the Quraysh considered the believers a weak minority. They thought their own numerous warriors, their tribal alliances, and their wealth made them powerful. They mocked the small band of Muslims, saying they had no helpers and no military strength. But on the Day of Judgment, the roles will be reversed. The disbelievers will find themselves utterly alone, stripped of their armies, their wealth, their clan support. No helper will come to their aid, as was repeatedly stated in Al-Ma‘arij. The believers, on the other hand, will have the angels, the prophets, and ultimately Allah as their helper. The disbelievers will finally realize that the Muslims, whom they considered weak, were the truly powerful ones because they were backed by the Almighty.

The phrase “weaker in helpers” means they will see their own complete lack of intercessors. “Inferior in manpower” means their numbers will count for nothing. This verse functions as a delayed answer to all the taunts. It tells the believers to be patient; the true evaluation of strength is not in this world’s scales but will be revealed on the Day of the Final Unveiling. The jinn had already acknowledged that they cannot frustrate Allah; here, the same truth is applied to the human deniers. The verse instills hope in the oppressed and terror in the arrogant oppressors.

=25=

The Quraysh repeatedly asked, “When will this promise be fulfilled?” They demanded to know the exact timing of the Day of Judgment, hoping to catch the Prophet in a contradiction or to dismiss it as remote. Allah commands the Prophet to respond with complete honesty: “Say, ‘I do not know if what you are promised is near or my Lord has set a distant time for it.’” This is a striking admission of the Prophet’s lack of knowledge of the unseen. Even the final messenger, the most knowledgeable of creation about revelation, does not know the Hour. The knowledge of its timing is one of the keys of the unseen that Allah has kept exclusively to Himself. The Prophet’s humility is a refutation of any claim to divinity. He is a servant, not an oracle who can answer every question.

The phrase “what you are promised” is the punishment and the Day of Reckoning. By saying “I do not know,” the Prophet shuts down their line of interrogation and redirects their focus. The important thing is not when it will happen, but the certainty that it will happen and the need to prepare for it. The nearness or distance is relative; in the scale of eternity, it is always near.

The verse also connects to the jinn’s earlier statement that they had no clue whether evil or good was intended for those on earth. Both jinn and Prophet affirm their ignorance of the specific timing, making it clear that this knowledge belongs to Allah alone. For the believers, this teaches contentment with what is known and trust in the wisdom of what is hidden. Obsessing over the timing of the end is a distraction from the duty of the present. The verse thus beautifully balances urgency (“it could be near”) with patience (“or my Lord has set a distant time”), leaving the servant in a state of vigilant preparedness without anxiety.

=26=

The surah now reveals why the Prophet does not know the timing: “He is the Knower of the unseen, disclosing none of it to anyone.” Allah alone possesses the absolute, comprehensive knowledge of ghayb—all that is hidden from creation, past, present, and future. The “unseen” includes the timing of the Hour, the events of the future, the secrets of hearts, the realities of the spiritual realms.

The phrase “disclosing none of it to anyone” means that in principle, Allah does not grant unrestricted access to His unseen knowledge to any creature. No angel, no prophet, no jinn has a general database of the unseen.

This is a fundamental Islamic creed. The jinn, who used to eavesdrop on heavenly news, were cut off precisely because that knowledge is Allah’s exclusive domain. The verse demolishes the claims of fortune-tellers, astrologers, and so-called saints who pretend to know the future or the unseen. Even the Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, had no independent knowledge of the unseen; what he knew of it came only through specific revelation that Allah chose to give him. The verse sets the stage for the exception in the next verse, but for now it establishes the rule: the unseen is a sealed treasure with Allah.

For believers, this is immensely comforting. The future is in the hands of the All-Knowing and All-Wise. There is no need to seek glimpses of it through illicit means. It also humbles the believer, reminding us that our knowledge is minuscule. The natural curiosity to know what will happen is answered by trusting that the Knower of the unseen has everything planned with perfect wisdom.

The verse also strengthens the Prophet’s position: his inability to answer the “when” question is not a weakness but a proof of his truthfulness, for he only claims what Allah reveals.

=27=

The previous verse established the rule: Allah does not disclose the unseen to anyone. Now the exception is given: “except messengers of His choice.

” This is a crucial clarification. Allah does select certain individuals—the prophets and messengers—and reveals to them portions of the unseen as He wills, to enable them to fulfill their missions. This revelation is not a general access; it is specific, controlled, and for a purpose. The Prophet received knowledge of the Hereafter, stories of past nations, and certain future events that were relevant to his message.

However, even with this exception, the knowledge is not independently acquired; it is a gift. The verse then adds a beautiful description of how this revelation is protected: “Then He appoints angel-guards before and behind them. ” The “them” refers to the messengers, or some say to the revelation itself. The angels are stationed in front and behind, forming a protective escort. This ensures that the message reaches the messenger without any interception or tampering by devils.

This is a direct contrast to the jinn’s earlier description of how they used to eavesdrop but were met with shooting stars. The divine revelation is guarded at every stage: the angel Jibril brings it down, and other angels surround the process to prevent any demonic interference. Some commentators mention that the guards protect the messenger himself, keeping away any harmful jinn or evil forces that might try to disrupt his mission. The imagery is of a precious, guarded convoy moving through the cosmos. This assures the believers that the Quran they receive is pure, authentic, and unchanged. The chain of transmission from the Knower of the unseen to His chosen messenger is sealed and secure. It is a testament to the trustworthiness of the Prophet and the absolute reliability of the Quran. The jinn’s failed eavesdropping is the flip side of this; they could not touch the guarded revelation.

=28=

The final verse of the surah explains the ultimate purpose of that angelic guard: “to ensure that the messengers fully deliver the messages of their Lord.” The Arabic “li-ya‘lama” means “that He may know” in the sense of knowledge that is manifest and established as fact. The guarding angels accompany the messengers so that it becomes clear and confirmed that the messengers have indeed conveyed every part of the divine message, without addition, subtraction, or corruption.

This is a profound assurance to the Prophet and to the believers: the delivery of the message is not left to chance. Allah provides the means to guarantee its preservation.

The phrase “though He already knows all about them” clarifies that Allah’s knowledge is not dependent on this process; He knows, in His eternal knowledge, that they will deliver the message. But the process of sending guards, witnessing, and recording establishes the proof for creation. It demonstrates the justice of Allah; on the Day of Judgment, there will be no doubt that the messengers conveyed the truth.

The final phrase “and keeps account of everything” (ahsa kulla shay’in ‘adada) is a magnificent closure. Allah has enumerated, counted, and accounted for every single thing in existence. Nothing escapes His knowledge—not the number of angels, not the words of the message, not the deeds of creation.

The word “adada” emphasizes precise counting. This connects back to the jinn’s realization that they cannot escape Allah, and to the warning that the disbelievers will be in Hell forever. Everything is under meticulous divine record. The surah, which began with the jinn hearing a wondrous recitation, ends with the grand affirmation that this recitation is part of a perfectly guarded, fully accounted system of divine communication. It reassures the Prophet of his mission’s integrity, warns the deniers of the certainty of their reckoning, and lifts the believers’ hearts to trust in the Lord who guards His message and counts all things.
