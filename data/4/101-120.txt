=101=

"When you travel through the land, it is not blameworthy to shorten the prayer if you fear the disbelievers may attack you. The disbelievers are ever an open enemy to you."

Wa idha darabtum fil-ardi fa-laysa alaykum junahun an taqsuru min as-salati in khiftum an yaftinakum alladhina kafaru — when you strike through the earth — darabtum — travelling for hijrah, trade, study, da'wah, or campaign — there is no blame — junah — no sin, no fault — to shorten — qasr — the four-rak'ah prayers to two. The phrase "if you fear" reveals early history and wisdom: initial qasr at the borders was linked to fear of ambush, but the Sunnah establishes it as permanent mercy even without fear, as charity. Ya'la ibn Umayyah asked Umar, "Why do we still shorten when now we are safe and the verse says 'if you fear'? Are we not safe?" Umar had asked the Prophet the same, and he said, "It is a charity — sadaqah — Allah has given you, so accept His charity; whoever refuses charity from Allah is proud." The scholars thus say: the condition of fear was the original sabab but not the permanentilla; shortening is sunnah mu'akkadah, some say wajib, for the traveller by the Prophet's constant practice on every safar. Travellers shorten Zuhr, Asr, and Isha to two; Maghrib stays three, Fajr stays two, because their forms are already concise and witr remains odd.

Inna al-kafirina kanu lakum aduwwan mubina — the disbelievers are an ever open enemy — aduww mubin — the same phrase as for Satan in 2:168 "Satan is an open enemy to you" and in 4:45 "Allah knows your enemies." The link teaches that external enmity of those who block the path and internal whispering of the rebellious Satan share openness — they are declared, not hidden, so precaution is worship. The prayer that was not waived in illness with tayammum in 4:43, nor in major impurity except passing through the mosque, is now not waived in travel but lightened. Qasr is takhfif again after 4:28: Allah wants to lighten because He created man weak — da'ifa — and after hijrah's promise in 4:100 of many refuges and spaciousness, now the prayer of a refugee, a migrant, a merchant is given its travel form: shortened, combined if needed, but never abandoned, never excused. For the modern traveller — at an airport where water is scarce, in a car where qiblah moves, in a hospital where standing is hard — the concession is not laziness but acceptance of a gift; to insist on four when travelling is to refuse charity, and to abandon prayer entirely because travelling is to miss the point: lightened, not lifted, timed as 4:103 will say kitaban mawqutan.

=102=

"When you are among them and lead them in prayer, let one group stand with you armed, and when they prostrate let them be behind you, and let the other group that has not yet prayed come and pray with you, taking precautions and arms. The disbelievers wish you would neglect weapons and baggage so they could assault you in one sweep. There is no blame if you lay down arms due to rain harm or illness, but take precautions. Allah has prepared a humiliating punishment for disbelievers."

Wa idha kunta fihim fa-aqamta lahum as-salata fal-taqum ta'ifatun minhum ma'aka wal-ya'khudhu aslihatahum — when you — O Muhammad, you specifically as imam — are among them and establish prayer — salat al-khawf, fear prayer — as imam in their midst — let one group stand with you armed — asliha — swords, bows, shields, rifles today, vests. Fa-idha sajadu fal-yakunu min wara'ikum — when they prostrate — sajadu — the most vulnerable posture, forehead on ground, eyes closed, back exposed — let them move to guard behind you, watching the valley. Wal-ta'ti ta'ifatun ukhra lam yusallu fal-yusallu ma'aka wal-ya'khudhu hizrahum wa aslihatahum — the other group that has not yet prayed comes to pray with you, taking precautions — hizr — and arms.

This is the most detailed prayer description in the entire Quran — longer than the verse of wudu in 5:6 — and it exists for a reason that cannot be missed: prayer is not delayed even in battle, fear does not cancel worship, it shapes it. The Prophet prayed salat al-khawf at Dhat al-Riqa, at Asfan on the way to Hudaybiyyah, at the Trench when the Confederates besieged; companions described the shifting rows like waves, one praying while the other guards, then swapping, so that every rak'ah had a guard and every prostration had a watcher, and the imam lengthened. Ibn Umar, Jabir, Abu Ayyash az-Zuraqi, and others transmitted multiple forms — one imam prays one rak'ah with first group while second guards, then second group prays second rak'ah while first guards — all valid, all teaching one principle: fear does not cancel prayer, it shapes it to keep both.

For today's surgeon in a long operation, nurse in emergency, soldier on patrol, student in exam, the principle extends: pray according to ability, combine Zuhr/Asr and Maghrib/Isha if needed as traveller, pray sitting or with eyes if standing is impossible, but do not abandon. The verse also shows leadership: the Prophet is "among them," not remote.

Wadda alladhina kafaru law taghfuluna an aslihatikum wa amti'atikum fa-yamiluna alaykum maylatan wahidah — those who disbelieve wish you would be heedless of weapons and baggage — ghafla — so they could assault you in one sweep — maylatan wahidah — one hard incline, one sudden rush, one flank collapse. Their wish is tactics: make you choose between worship and watchfulness, then strike the gap you left, as Shaytan wishes you to think you must choose. Allah commands both: pray and guard, worship and watch, dhikr and hizr. The wish exposes their reliance not on strength but on your heedlessness; their victory depends on your ghafla.

Wa la junaha alaykum in kana bikum adhan min matarin aw kuntum marda an tada'u aslihatakum wa khudhu hizrakum — there is no blame if you lay down arms due to rain harm — adhan — soaked bowstrings that snap, muddy ground, cold that stiffens, or illness — marda — fever, wound — but still take precautions — hizrakum. Even the concession to lay down arms requires taking hizr — not abandoning vigilance entirely, but putting heavy weapons aside while keeping sentries, distance, and readiness. The verse harmonises prayer and hizr as 4:71 commanded "take your precautions and go forth" and as 4:103 will command dhikr after prayer. InnAllaha a'adda lil-kafirina adhaban muhina — Allah has prepared for disbelievers a humiliating punishment — muhina — humiliating, the same humiliating as for transgressors of hudud in 4:14, for hiders of bounty in 4:37, for wishers of neglect. After 4:71's weak plot and 4:76's da'if kayd and 4:77's fear, here their concrete wish to exploit prayer is met with humiliation already prepared, not merely threatened as future.

=103=

"When you have finished the prayer, remember Allah standing, sitting, and lying on your sides. When you are secure, establish the prayer regularly. Indeed, prayer is on believers a timed duty."

Fa-idha qadaytum as-salata fadhkuru Allaha qiyaman wa qu'udan wa ala junubikum — when you complete the fear prayer, remember Allah standing, sitting, lying on sides. Dhikr after prayer replaces battle fear with nearness; after fear, remembrance is the medicine that steadies hands that held weapons and hearts that raced. The same three postures are the description of ulu al-albab in 3:191 who remember Allah standing, sitting, lying and reflect on heavens and earth, and of those who were told in 4:43 about tayammum and in 4:101 about qasr — all are covered: no posture, no condition, no place excuses remembrance. The soldier after patrol, the ill after tayammum, the traveller after qasr, the office worker after Zuhr, the mother nursing — all are commanded the same: remember in the posture you are in.

Fa-idha itma'nantum fa-aqimu as-salata — when you are secure — itma'nan — calm, settled, safe, at home, no fear — establish the prayer fully — aqimu — not merely pray, but establish — four, without fear formation, with calm, with khushu', with its sunan and adhan and row, as the Prophet prayed at Medina with full rows and long recitation. Security does not end prayer; it completes it with its full form and its full spirit.

Inna as-salata kanat ala al-mu'minina kitaban mawqutan — prayer upon believers is a timed duty — kitaban mawqutan — a written appointment, fixed time, measured, decreed, as inheritance shares were kutiba in 4:7, fighting was kutiba in 4:77, fasting was kutiba in 2:183. No fard is ad hoc or mood-based or "when I feel spiritual." The verse after qasr and fear shows prayer never leaves the timetable; lightened in form, never cancelled, timed in all. The Prophet said between a man and shirk and kufr is abandoning prayer, and "the covenant between us and them is prayer; whoever abandons it has disbelieved," and he never missed prayer even when wounded, even when his wives worried, even on the night of Hijrah. For today's worker who says "I missed because busy, because meeting, because insecure, because travelling," the answer is to remember standing, sitting, lying — if you can be in any posture, you can remember; if you can remember, you can pray in that posture with tayammum or qasr or even with eyes. The timed duty is the cord that holds the day together when fear tries to scatter it, and dhikr after is the glue.

=104=

"Do not weaken in seeking the people. If you suffer pain, they suffer as you suffer, but you hope from Allah what they do not hope. Allah is Knowing, Wise."

Wa la tahinu fibtigha'i al-qawm — do not weaken — tahinu — be soft, lose firmness, slacken, let scanning become scrolling — in seeking the people — the enemy who oppresses the weak you were told to fight for in 4:75 and whose allies are to be fought in 4:76, the people who block the path in 4:167. Ibtigha' is seeking, pursuing, following up with purpose, not randomly clashing for pride. The prohibition is after fear prayer and remembrance, so seeking is with dhikr, not with rage, with hizr, not with rashness.

In takunu ta'lamuna fa-innahum ya'lamuna kama ta'lamuna — if you suffer — ta'lamun — pain, wounds, fatigue, sleeplessness, hunger, they suffer as you suffer — ya'lamuna kama ta'lamuna. Both sides bleed; courage is not numbness or denial of pain but continuation despite shared pain. The Prophet at Uhud was wounded, his tooth broken, his face bloodied, helmet rings in his cheek, yet he stood and called, "How can a people succeed who wounded their prophet?" and still sought, and still forgave when able.

Wa tarjuna min Allahi ma la yarjun — but you hope from Allah what they do not — forgiveness, paradise with rivers and purified spouses and deep shade zalil, intercession, being with the blessed in 4:69, reward ladunni from Himself, and the meeting and seeing Him, and His pleasure. Hope separates identical fatigue: two armies both sleepless after a long day, two callers both tired, but one hopes for Paradise while the other hopes for spoils or mere survival or for no hope at all. The lagger in 4:72 called absence from hardship blessing and spoils fawz azim; here the striver's hope redefines success as ajr azim that does not depend on spoils. Wa kan Allahu Aliman Hakima — Knowing of pain and hope, of who weakened and who sought, of who hoped for Allah and who hoped for taghut, Wise in prescribing seeking without tahin, prescribing qasr but not weakening, prescribing fear prayer but still seeking, prescribing sulh but still standing for justice. 3:139 "do not weaken, do not grieve, you are upper if believers" and 47:35 "do not weaken and call for peace while you are upper" echo here for every seeker, not only soldiers: the caller to truth, the parent correcting a child, the worker standing for qist in 4:135.

=105=

"We have sent down to you the Book in truth so you may judge between people by what Allah has shown you. Do not be an advocate for the treacherous."

Inna anzalna ilayka al-Kitaba bil-haqqi li-tahkuma bayna an-nasi bima araka Allahu — We sent down to you the Book in truth — bil-haqq — not suggestion, not culture, not tribal preference, not "modern reinterpretation" that inverts hudud to please — to judge between people by what Allah showed you — araka — of evidence, revelation, signs, indications, Sunnah, insight. Araka includes the Prophet's divinely guided understanding, as 4:113 says "taught you what you did not know" and as 2:282's debt writing and 4:58's amanat show judgment by evidence, and as 4:59's referral shows Book and Sunnah together. The Sunnah is part of "what He showed": the Prophet judged by witnesses, oaths, physical trails, and his judgments are revelation's elaboration, as 53:3-4 says he does not speak from desire.

Wa la takun lil-kha'inina khasima — do not be an advocate — khasim — fierce disputant, lawyer, patron — for the treacherous — kha'inin. The sabab makes it unforgettable: Tu'mah ibn Ubayraq, of the Ansar, stole a suit of armour belonging to the companion Qatadah ibn Nu'man's uncle, hid it in a Jewish man's — Zayd ibn Samin — house wrapped in flour that left a trail from Tu'mah's house, then his clan, proud of their Muslim identity, came to the Prophet and asked him to defend Tu'mah and accuse the Jew to protect tribal honour: "If you do not defend our man, our clan is shamed before Jews." The Prophet was inclined to defend the Muslim, and revelation corrected him before judgment was pronounced: do not be khasim for the kha'in even if he says la ilaha illa Allah and the victim is a Jew who does not. The clan's honour was not in tribal victory but in haq, and the Jew's innocence was not erased by his religion. The Prophet later said, "I am only a human; you bring disputes to me, perhaps one of you is more eloquent, and I judge based on what I hear, so if I give someone his brother's right wrongly, it is a piece of Fire, let him leave it."

Bil-haqq after many hudud reminds the Book came to judge with Haqq, not with power, pity, or tribalism that inverts haqq. For a judge, lawyer, elder, manager, or relative today asked to twist an inheritance paper to favour a son, to hide a will that gives a daughter her share, to reclaim a qintar by buhtan as in 4:20, to argue a fraudulent share is "fair for family peace," to accuse a Jew or a worker falsely, this is the line: do not be khasim for the kha'in even with eloquence that 2:204 warned "his speech pleases you in this world while he is the most quarrelsome." The surah that opened with Raqib ever Watchful and Hasib ever Reckoning now says judgment is with what He showed, not what clan shows, and that Haqq does not know tribe, does not know majority.

=106=

"And seek Allah's forgiveness. Indeed, Allah is Forgiving, Merciful."

Wastaghfirillaha inna Allaha kana Ghafuran Rahima — seek forgiveness — istighfar. For what? For the Prophet's momentary inclination to defend the treacherous before evidence was fully clarified, for any flicker of heart that favoured tribe over Haqq, for the community's readiness to argue for Tu'mah, and as teaching for every judge after him to be humble. Ghafur Rahim after "do not be advocate" shows even near error is forgivable when istighfar follows, but without it the heart hardens, the argument repeats, and khawwan athim in 4:107 becomes habit, and night plotting in 4:108 becomes system.

The Prophet sought forgiveness seventy times daily, and in some reports a hundred, though his past and future were forgiven; he taught his companions to seek forgiveness after every prayer, "astaghfirullah" three times, after every gathering, after every wudu. Placed immediately after li-tahkuma — to judge — this teaches every judge, arbitrator, father dividing an estate, elder mediating in 4:35 and 4:128 to bracket judgment with istighfar so desire does not enter: seek before judging to clear intention, judge with what Allah showed, then seek after to catch hidden bias, love of the Muslim party, or dislike of the other. If the infallible is told to seek forgiveness for near advocacy of a Muslim against a Jew, how much more the fallible kinsman who is executor, the brother dividing a house who loves one child more, the arbitrator from family in 4:35 who is from his side? Istighfar is not shame after but worship that keeps judgment clean, as wudu keeps prayer clean, as tayammum keeps prayer when water fails.

=107=

"And do not argue on behalf of those who deceive themselves. Allah does not love the treacherous, sinful."

Wa la tujadil anilladhina yakhtanuna anfusahum — do not argue — tujadil — for those who deceive themselves — yakhtanuna anfusahum — betraying selves while thinking they betray the Jew or the system or the court, while thinking it is clever. The root khana is to betray trust; the reflexive shows the knife turns inward first, as 2:57 "they wronged themselves" and 4:111 "earns only against himself" and 4:62 "calamity for what hands earned."

Inna Allaha la yuhibbu man kana khawwanan athima — Allah does not love the treacherous, sinful — khawwan athim — habitual betrayer, entrenched in sin, not a single slip. Khawwan is heavier, more intensive than kha'in in 4:105; athim heavier than ithm; repetition hardens trait into identity, as habitual, as profession. La yuhibbu — does not love — the opposite of 3:76 "Allah loves the muttaqin who fulfill oaths" and 4:107's opposite 4:146's repentant who purify and 9:108. Treachery loses love; love is not cheap and not automatic. The Quran does not say Allah does not love the treacherous act; it says the treacherous, sinful person — the one who has made it character.

This repeats the prohibition with stronger categories: not only do not be khasim for kha'inin in court, but do not engage in jidal at all — in private, in family chat, in mediation — for yakhtanuna anfusahum. Khawwan athim is one who has made betrayal a path, as in 8:58 "if you fear betrayal from a people, throw covenant back" and as in 4:108 yubayyituna. For today's advocate who says "defend anyway, we will repent later, the system is against Muslims, the Jew has no witness, we must protect our own," the verse says you first wrong yourself, you step into a category Allah does not love, and your argument becomes evidence against you on the Day when eloquence fails as in 4:109.

=108=

"They hide from people but do not hide from Allah, though He is with them when they plot by night what He is not pleased with. Allah encompasses what they do."

Yastakhfuna min an-nasi wa la yastakhfuna min Allahi — they hide from people but do not hide from Allah. The stolen armour was hidden in a Jew's flour, the flour trail was hidden with night whispers among the clan, the defence was hidden behind daylight piety and clan solidarity. Wa huwa ma'ahum idh yubayyituna ma la yarda min al-qawl — He is with them when they plot by night — yubayyituna — what He is not pleased with of speech — qawl. Yubayyituna is to spend the night in counsel, as in 4:81. The night plotting of 4:81 "what they plot at night" and 4:62's swearing by Allah after sudud are here in one scene: they measure people's eyes but forget Raqib in 4:1, Hasib in 4:6, Sami' in 4:148, Alim in 4:111, Muhit.

Ma'ahum — with them — is not with-ness of approval or help as with Abu Bakr in the cave in 9:40; it is with-ness of knowledge, hearing, and encompassing, as 58:7 "He is with them wherever they are — three whispering, He is fourth." Wa kan Allahu bima ya'maluna Muhita — Allah encompasses what they do — Muhit — encompassing, as in 4:126 He encompasses all, in 3:120 He encompasses what they scheme, in 4:40 no atom escapes, in 4:42 no hadith hidden, in 4:148 Hearing, Knowing. Encompassing means no night corner, no encrypted chat, no flour sack, no loan paper, no altered will outside His sight and perimeter.

For the believer told in 4:42 that no statement is hidden, this is terror for the hider and profound comfort for the hidden-from: the Jew falsely accused, the orphan whose sheep was swapped at night in 4:2, the wife whose mahr was taken with slander in 4:20, the girl denied her share who stood at division in 4:8 — all were plotted while He was with them as Witness, and that Witness will speak when the clan is silent and the night is gone.

=109=

"Here you are — you argued for them in the life of this world — but who will argue for them with Allah on the Day of Resurrection, or who will be their disposer?"

Ha antum ha'ulai jadaltum anhum fil-hayat ad-dunya fa-man yujadilu Allaha anhum yawma al-qiyamati am man yakunu alayhim wakilan — here you are — ha antum — you, the clan, the defenders, the eloquent — argued for them in worldly life — jadaltum — with eloquence, kinship, pressure, tribal shame, "we are Ansar, he is a Jew, our honour is at stake." Fa-man yujadilu Allaha anhum yawma al-qiyamati — who will argue with Allah for them on Resurrection? The courtroom of 4:41 with a witness from every nation and Muhammad as witness over these, and 4:87's gathering where whose statement truer than Allah's, and 4:122's haqq promise has no yubayyituna; secrets are spoken, skins testify, hands speak, feet witness, and no lawyer's eloquence bends the mizan.

Am man yakunu alayhim wakilan — who will be their disposer — wakil — manager of defence, trustee, patron? The same Wakil sufficient in 4:81-132 is not available to defend treachery; He is Wakil for the wronged Jew, for the orphan, for the wife, for the mustad'af. The verse moves the dispute from the tribal tent where it could be won with a good speech and a flour trail to the Divine court where eloquence is weightless and only haqq and mizan remain. 2:204's pleasing speech wins a council in dunya; it wins nothing there. For judges, lawyers, clan elders, and every person who types a defence for a khawwan athim online, the question is practical before it is eschatological: before you argue for a khawwan, ask who will argue that same brief before the Truest Statement in 4:122 and before the Encompassing Muhit and before the Gatherer in 4:87. The answer — no one, no wakil — should make the tongue stop before the pen moves and before the share is signed and before the night plot is sent.

=110=

"Whoever does evil or wrongs himself then seeks forgiveness will find Allah Forgiving, Merciful."

Wa man ya'mal su'an aw yazlim nafsahu thumma yastaghfirillaha yajid Allaha Ghafuran Rahima — whoever does su' — evil against others, as Tu'mah's theft and throwing it on the Jew, as consuming orphan wealth, as slandering Maryam, as hit-and-run — or wrongs himself — zulm nafs — sin within, as his own betrayal, as the clan's pride, as delaying prayer, as riya — then seeks forgiveness — yastaghfir — will find — yajid — Allah Forgiving, Merciful — Ghafur Rahim — not may find, but finds as one finds water after seeking in a desert, finds a door that opens when knocked, finds a Lord who said "come" in 4:64. Ghafur effaces sin as if never written; Rahim adds mercy, increases, brings nearness as in 4:69's companionship and 4:147's Shakir.

The verse after severe warning "who will argue for them on the Day?" opens the door that night plotters slammed shut: come, as in 4:64's comers who wronged themselves, and find. It is the same door as 4:17's soon repentance is upon Allah and 4:64's coming to the Messenger and 4:146's repentance for hypocrites with four steps. The verse balances the surah's severity: Fire for intentional murder in 4:93, for consuming orphan wealth in 4:10, for crossing hudud in 4:14, for distinguishing messengers in 4:150, for riba, yet Ghafur for the seeker who turns. Su' versus zulm nafs distinguishes outward harm to another's house and inward harm to own soul; both fall under the same promise if istighfar is soon and sincere as in 4:17's qarib and if the seeker comes as in 4:64.

The Prophet said, "No sin is great with seeking forgiveness, and no minor sin remains minor with persistence," and taught the master of seeking — Allahumma Anta Rabbi, no god except You, You created me, I am Your servant, I acknowledge Your favour and my sin, forgive me — to say morning and evening, and the angels write it. The surah that taught tayammum with dust out of ease for the body now teaches repentance with dust of a broken heart out of ease for the soul: the same Finder, Ghafur Rahim, awaits the same seeker, and finding is guaranteed for the seeker, not for the waiter who swears ihsan.

=111=

"Whoever earns a sin earns it only against himself. Allah is Knowing, Wise."

Wa man yaksib ithman fa-innama yaksibuhu ala nafsihi — whoever earns — yaksib — acquisition, intentional earning, as a merchant acquires and stores — ithm — sin — earns it only against himself. The same principle as 2:286 "no soul earns except upon it," 6:164, 35:18 "no bearer bears another's burden," 30:44, and 4:62's "calamity for what hands earned" and 4:111's own precursor. The sin is not deposited in another's file, though throwing it on the innocent in the next verse tries to transfer it by lie, as Satan tries to transfer. The Jew remains bari' — innocent — while the thrower remains hamil — burdened, as 4:112 will say ihtamala, and the clan remains earner.

Wa kan Allahu Aliman Hakima — Knowing whose hand earned, Wise where to place burden and what expiation to prescribe, whether to forgive with Ghafur in 4:110 or to punish with Muhit in 4:108, whether the earning was night plotting or mistaken error in 4:92. Alim Hakim closed 4:17's soon repentance, 4:24's lawful seeking, 4:26's bayan, and will close 4:111 and 4:170. He knows the difference between acquired sin from night plotting in 4:81/108 versus mistaken error in 4:92, between throwing and being thrown upon, between khawwan and mistaken. For the falsely accused Jew, this is acquittal; for the earner, this is responsibility without scapegoat and without tribal cover. The clan cannot carry it for him on the Day not even a mustard seed, and no nasir will carry it.

=112=

"Whoever earns a fault or sin then throws it upon an innocent has burdened himself with slander and a clear sin."

Wa man yaksib khati'atan aw ithman thumma yarmi bihi bari'an — earns a fault — khati'ah — small slip or error — or sin — ithm — major — then throws — yarmi — as one throws a spear or a dart — upon an innocent — bari' — clean, free, as the Jew was bari' of theft, as Maryam was bari'ah of slander in 4:156, as the wife is bari'ah until four witnesses in 4:15 — fa-qad ihtamala buhtanan wa ithman mubina — has burdened himself — ihtamala — carried on back as a load, as a sack — with buhtan — slander — and clear sin — ithman mubina — manifest, open. Buhtan is the same word for reclaiming a qintar by slander in 4:20 — a-ta'khudhunahu buhtanan — and for tremendous slander against Maryam in 4:156 — buhtanan azima. Ithman mubin doubles the load: original khati'ah stays and throwing adds second, as two mountains, as two burdens.

Tu'mah's clan threw his khati'ah of theft upon the Jew's bari' head; modern throws include graft blamed on a clean colleague, harassment blamed on a victim's fiction, a fraudulent will blamed on "she agreed," a brother's taking of a house blamed on "father willed it orally," a husband's nushuz blamed on a wife's character, a student's failure blamed on a teacher. The load is carried — ihtamala — on the back, as in 4:88-91 no helper carries it, as in 4:62 musibah for what hands earned, as in 17:15. The surah that protected innocent women with four witnesses in 4:15 now protects all innocents generally: throwing is not clever litigation, it is burden, and buhtan is not strategy, it is sin clear, and the One who is Witness will call it by name.

=113=

"Were it not for Allah's bounty and mercy upon you, a group would have misled you. They mislead only themselves and cannot harm you at all. Allah has sent down upon you the Book and wisdom and taught you what you did not know. Great is Allah's bounty upon you."

Wa lawla fadlu Allahi alayka wa rahmatuhu la-hammat ta'ifatun minhum an yudilluka — were it not for bounty and mercy upon you — O Muhammad, and upon the believer who holds to you — a group — ta'ifah — among them — the clan insisting on defending Tu'mah and the hypocrites around them — would have intended to mislead you — hammat — resolved, as three hundred resolved to withdraw and as yubayyitun resolved at night and as yatarabbasuna resolve. Wa ma yudilluna illa anfusahum — they mislead only themselves — as 2:09 "they deceive none except themselves but they perceive not" and 3:69 "they mislead only themselves and they perceive not" and 2:12. Wa ma yadurrunaka min shay' — they cannot harm you at all — in dunya or deen, in reputation or hereafter, because Ismah protects the Prophet, and for the ummah, referral to Book protects as in 4:82-83 tadabbur and yastanbitun.

Wa anzala Allahu alayka al-Kitaba wal-hikmata wa allamaka ma lam takun ta'lam — He sent down upon you the Book and wisdom — hikmah — Sunnah, fiqh, insight, sunan, as 2:129's dua of Ibrahim "teach them Book and wisdom and purify them" and 2:151 "He teaches you what you did not know," and 96:5 "taught man what he did not know." Hikmah here is the Sunnah that judges with what Allah showed in 4:105, the bayyinah that clarifies, the islah. Wa kana fadlu Allahi alayka azima — great bounty upon you — azim — great, the same great bounty of 3:74 Owner of great bounty, 3:171-174 great bounty after wounds, 4:67 ladun ajr azim, 4:114 ajr azim, 4:146. After the burden of slander and the trial of night plots, bounty is the shield: Book, wisdom, taught knowledge, and harm blocked. For today's student who fears being misled by eloquent throwers who practice yarmi, the bounty remains: Book and wisdom are still with you, taught knowledge is still available, harm from misleaders cannot reach your deen if you hold them and refer, and no nasir is needed.

=114=

"There is no good in much of their private conversation, except one who enjoins charity or good or reconciliation between people. Whoever does that seeking Allah's pleasure, We will give him a great reward."

La khayra fi kathirin min najwahum — there is no good in much of their private conversation — najwa — whisper, secret talk, side chat, DM. The surah has shown najwa as night plotting in 4:81 "what they plot at night" and in 4:108 "plotting by night what He is not pleased with" and as oath-selling in 3:77, and as fear-spreading in 4:83. Majority of secret talk is not good; it isolates, it conspires, it withholds, it sells. Illa man amara bi-sadaqatin aw ma'rufin aw islahin bayna an-nas — except one who enjoins — amara — charity — sadaqah — wealth for trusts and orphans and needy as in 4:2,36, as zakah, or good — ma'ruf — the same ma'ruf as qawl ma'ruf in 4:5,8 and as ihsan and as khayr and as 3:110, or reconciliation — islah — between people — the same islah as between spouses with two arbiters in 4:35 and 4:128 and as sulh khayr.

Private speech becomes worship when it enjoins those three: wealth for trusts, good for morals, islah for relations, not for gossip. The verse does not praise secret talk for secrecy; it praises content. A private message enjoining a brother to return a sister's share is najwa that is khayr; a private message planning to hide it with a loan paper is najwa that is buhtan. Wa man yaf'al dhalika ibtigha'a mardati Allahi fa-sawfa nu'tihi ajran azima — whoever does that seeking Allah's pleasure — ibtigha'a mardati Allah — the same seeking as those who sell worldly life for Hereafter in 4:74 and who spend from what Allah provided in 4:39 and who are patient in 2:207 — will receive a great reward — ajran azima. Najwa praised not by eloquence but by purpose and intention. For today's private chats — family groups, work DMs, gated meetings, encrypted — the metric is simple and lasting: does it enjoin charity, ma'ruf, or islah? If not, kathir of it is la khayra, even if it feels urgent, even if it feels like islah.

=115=

"Whoever opposes the Messenger after guidance has become clear to him and follows a path other than that of the believers, We will turn him to what he turned to and burn him in Hell. Evil destination."

Wa man yushaqiq ar-rasula min ba'di ma tabayyana lahu al-huda — whoever splits from — yushaqiq — opposes, cuts himself off at the trench, from the Messenger after guidance has become clear — tabayyana — as bayyinah, as 47:32, after the Book and wisdom were taught and after tadabbur in 4:82 could show coherence and after yastanbitun in 4:83. The verb shaqqa is to split, to cut lengthwise, as the trench splits earth. Wa yattabi' ghayra sabili al-mu'minina — and follows a path other than the believers' path — sabil al-mu'minina — the way of the jama'ah, the witnesses in 4:41, the "you are from one another" in 4:25, the sawa in 4:89, the qist in 4:135. Scholars said this verse is the proof for ijma — consensus — as sabil, because following other than that sabil after bayyan is threatened.

Nuwallihi ma tawalla — We will turn him to what he turned to — leave him to his chosen direction — as he chose to turn away with sudud in 4:61, as he chose night plotting, as he chose taghut, as he chose to throw, We turn him that way, with his choice, not against it. Wa nuslihi Jahannama wa sa'at masira — burn him in Hell, evil destination — sa'at masira — as 4:97 evil destination and 4:115 same, and 4:93's great punishment, and 4:121's no escape. The response matches the deed: he wanted direction away, We give him that direction to its end; he wanted sabil other, We show him its end. This after najwa's three goods shows the opposite: opposing Messenger after clarity and following other than believers' way is private deviation made public path, not a private mistake to be excused. The Prophet said, "My nation will not unite upon error; hold to the great majority; whoever shatters the staff, die as a pre-Islamic death, and whoever leaves obedience and separates from jama'ah by a handspan and dies, dies as pre-Islamic."

=116=

"Indeed, Allah does not forgive associating partners with Him, but forgives what is less than that for whom He wills. Whoever associates partners with Allah has strayed far."

Inna Allaha la yaghfiru an yushraka bihi wa yaghfiru ma duna dhalika liman yasha — Allah does not forgive shirk for one who dies upon it without return; what is less than shirk — any major or minor sin including deceit in 4:107, throwing on innocent in 4:112, opposing Messenger after guidance in 4:115, night plotting, riya, riba — He forgives for whom He wills by tawbah soon in 4:17 or by mercy and expiation, by prayer, by illness. Repeated from 4:48 to bracket the entire block from trust to treachery to night plot to najwa to shuquq, to seal.

Wa man yushrik billahi fa-qad dalla dalalan ba'idan — has strayed far — dalalan ba'idan — far astray, as Satan wants in 4:60 "lead them far astray" and as kufr in 4:167 "strayed far" and distinguishing in 4:136 and 4:169. Shuquq from Messenger ends in furthest dalalah if not repented: shirk is its terminus, whether shirk of worship, shirk of legislation, or shirk of obeying taghut as lord. Repetition hammers that shuquq is not a minor mistake or difference of opinion; it veers to the point of no return, and the point of no return is shirk.

=117=

"They call upon none besides Him except females, and they call upon none except a rebellious Satan —"

In yad'una min dunihi illa inathan — they call upon none besides Him except females — inathan — idols named female: al-Lat, al-Uzza, Manat — the feminine idols the Arab honoured as daughters of Allah, calling them shafi'at intercessors, while the Arab despised his own daughters and buried them, denied orphans' daughters their share, and muted mothers in 4:19. A theology that despises women yet invents female gods is contradiction and buhtan and hasad for fadl. Wa in yad'una illa Shaytanan marida — and they call upon none except a rebellious Satan — marid — defiant, rebellious, stripped of good, smooth, as 37:7 and 4:118 cursed, expelled. Their du'a to female idols is in reality du'a to the marid who rebelled at creation and was expelled for refusing to bow to Adam, the dayy of 4:118 who vowed a share. After shirk far, this shows its face: not sophisticated philosophy but naming fragile females as lords and following a marid who fell and who whispers.

=118=

"Cursed by Allah. He said, 'I will surely take from Your servants a share appointed.'"

La'anahu Allahu — cursed by Allah — la'n is distance from mercy, expulsion, banishment, as 4:46 cursed distorters who twisted, 4:52 cursed inverters who called taghut guidance, 5:60 cursed Sabbath transgressors, 33:61. Wa qala la-attakhidhanna min ibadika nasiban mafruda — Satan said, "I will surely take from Your servants a share appointed — nasib mafrud." Mafrud — fixed, appointed share — the same word for inheritance nasiban mafruda in 4:7 and kalalah nasib in 4:176, and for expiation, and for mahr faridah. Satan claims a fixed share of humanity as his flock, mirroring how Allah made fixed shares for heirs ordained by mercy. His share is those who choose sudud with taghut, night plotting with yubayyitun, throwing sin on the innocent with yarmi, following other than believers' path with yushaqiq, and associating. His tithe is the deviated, as zakah is taken from the pure.

=119=

"I will mislead them, delude them, order them to slit ears of cattle, and order them to change Allah's creation. Whoever takes Satan as ally instead of Allah has lost a clear loss."

Wa la-udillannahum wa la-umanniyannahum wa la-amurannahum fa-la-yubattikunna adhana al-an'ami — I will mislead them — udillu — and delude with hopes — umanni — the same tamanna of 4:32's envy and 4:120's promises and 2:111's wishful "we are the saved" and 4:123's amaniyy. — and order them to slit ears of cattle — yubattikunna adhana al-an'am — pagan rite of bahirah and sa'ibah where ears were cut to mark cattle as taboo for idols, making haram what Allah made halal as in 6:138-139, forbidding tayyibat as in 4:160, and dedicating to taghut.

Wa la-amurannahum fa-la-yughayyirunna khalqa Allah — and order them to change Allah's creation — yughayyirunna khalqa Allah — changing fitrah, altering deen, tattooing and excess adornment altering form for vanity, castration, and modern changes without need that deny khalaqa min nafsin wahidah and the qist of creation and the mizan. Ibn Mas'ud explained changing creation includes tattooing, plucking eyebrows, filing teeth for beauty; scholars extend to unnecessary permanent alteration of bodies, of fitrah, of deen's hudud, of inheritance shares, of marriage.

Wa man yattakhidh ash-Shaytana waliyyan min duni Allahi fa-qad khasira khusranan mubina — whoever takes Satan as ally — waliyyan — instead of Allah — Allah sufficient as Wali in 4:45 who brings from darkness to light — has lost a clear loss — khusran mubin — obvious, manifest, as muhin and sa'ir and adhab alim. Wali versus wali: Sufficient Wali who gives light versus marid waliy who gives ghurur. Choice of guardian decides nasib: share with Allah as heir of Gardens with no mahis or share with Satan as his nasib mafrud with no mahis but in Fire. The cattle's slit ear is a symbol of a slit fitrah: both mark what Allah made halal as haram for an idol.

=120=

"Satan promises them and deludes them. Satan promises them nothing but delusion."

Ya'iduhum wa yumanni-him — he promises and deludes with hopes — ya'id, yumanni — promising wealth if they keep riba, honour if they keep taghut, safety if they lag as in 4:72, success if they throw sin as in 4:112, acceptance if they do riya as in 4:142. Wa ma ya'iduhum ash-Shaytanu illa ghurura — he promises nothing but delusion — ghurur — mirage, as world's deception in 3:185 "worldly life is but deception" and 57:14 and 33:12, as 4:37's show, as dunya mata' qalil in 4:77, as his nasib mafrud tithe. His promises after that share — you will keep orphan wealth unseen, reclaim qintar with slander and keep honour, keep taghut and keep iman, keep night plotting and keep trust, keep riya and keep reward, keep slit ears and keep tayyib — all ghurur that evaporates when skins are renewed in 4:56, when no hadith is hidden in 4:42, when Muhit encompasses, when mizan weighs, when Wakil asks. The believer who knows "with Allah is reward of both worlds" in 4:134 and "whose statement truer than Allah's?" in 4:87 and 4:122 hears Satan's promise as thin as dust on a rock washed by rain in 2:264: a little water — wudu, prayer, istighfar — and nothing remains but the rock.
