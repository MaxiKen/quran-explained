=61=

"When it is said to them, 'Come to what Allah has revealed and to the Messenger,' you see the hypocrites turning away with aversion."

Wa idha qila lahum ta'alaw ila ma anzala Allahu wa ila ar-rasuli ra'ayta al-munafiqina yasudduna anka sududa — when it is said, "Come — ta'alaw — to what Allah revealed and to the Messenger," you see — ra'ayta — the hypocrites turning — yasudduna — blocking with sududa — strong, insulated aversion, a wall. The verb sadd appears doubled: they block themselves and they block others, the same sadd as those who avert from Allah's path in 4:167 "they disbelieved and blocked," who wished you to deviate greatly in 4:27, and who sought taghut courts in 4:60. Sududa is not a shy step back; it is deliberate, sustained turning of the shoulder, the back, the face, the heart.

The sabab is alive in Madinah: a Jewish litigant who was wronged said, "Let us go to Muhammad," though he was Jewish, because he knew the Prophet judged with justice even against himself and his companions; a Muslim hypocrite who claimed belief refused and said, "Let us go to Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf" the Jewish chief who took bribes and distorted, because there judgment could be bought and lineage protected. Revelation names the pattern after exposing the claim of belief in 4:60 as yaz'umuna — they merely claim to believe in what was sent to you and before. True claim is tested by ta'alaw. True belief runs toward the criterion; false claim turns from it. The yardstick is not what you say you believe in the mosque, but where you go when told "come" in the market, in the inheritance division, in the marriage dispute.

For today the test is not a historical chief but forum-shopping in daily life: when a family inheritance must be divided as in 4:11-12 and 4:176, when a marital conflict needs arbitration as in 4:35 and 4:128 with two hakams, when a business partnership disputes riba, when a social media argument turns on a verse, does the heart say "let us return to Allah and His Messenger" as 4:59 commands — with the Book and the authentic Sunnah, understood with the scholars, the rasikhuna — or does it shop for the forum where the fixed share disappears, where four witnesses become a screenshot, where taghut's ease says "keep it, modern life needs flexibility"? Sudud is visible today as delay, as polite mockery, as "that verse is historical, it was for then," as "our culture says otherwise," while 4:105 insists the Book was sent to judge with what Allah showed — bima araka Allahu — not with what desire shows, and 4:59 says referral is better. The ayah closes the block that began with returning trusts to owners in 4:58 and judging with justice: trustworthy judgment is referral, and refusal with sudud is hypocrisy seen.

=62=

"How will it be when calamity strikes them because of what their hands sent forth, then they come to you swearing by Allah, 'We intended nothing but good and reconciliation'?"

Fa-kayfa idha asabat-hum musibatun bima qaddamat aydihim thumma ja'uka yahlifuna billahi in aradna illa ihsanan wa tawfiqa — how will it be when musibah — affliction, exposure, defeat, public humiliation, even a small worldly loss that reveals secrets, a court loss, a family break — strikes them for what their hands earned, then they come to you swearing by Allah, "We intended nothing but good — ihsan — and reconciliation — tawfiq"? Kayfa leaves imagination to fill the scene of distress: faces exposed, excuses thin, oaths loud, as in 62:14. Hands here are the same hands that mixed orphan wealth by adding their bad sheep to the orphan's good in 4:2, that withheld a sister's naqir in 4:52, that twisted ra'ina with layy in 4:46, that desired taghut courts in 4:60, that spent night plotting ghayra alladhi taqul in 4:81.

Ihsan and tawfiq are the very words of successful arbitration in 4:35 — "if the two arbiters desire reconciliation — islah — Allah will grant tawfiq between the couple." Hypocrites borrow the language of islah to cover its opposite, as earlier 2:11-12 "when told do not corrupt they say we are only reformers," and 2:204 describes a man with pleasing speech — ya'jibuka qawluhu — who calls Allah to witness what is in his heart while being the most quarrelsome opponent, aladd al-khisam. Here the pleasant post-crisis swearing is the same: after sudud, after musibah, they come with halif — oath by Allah — to reframe blocking as building, to reframe taghut as tawfiq. Swearing by Allah after selling oaths for a small price in 3:77 — those who exchange the covenant of Allah and their oaths for a small price will have no share — shows oaths become cheap when misused as perfume over rot.

For a leader, judge, parent, or teacher, the lesson is not to be seduced by pleasant apology after failure. Check hands, not lips. The hypocrite's musibah for what hands earned is also the believer's warning: every hand that writes a fraudulent share, that types a night plot in a group chat, that clicks "share" on unverified fear news in 4:83, that withholds a wife's maintenance to force her to waive mahr as in 4:19, is earning its own musibah. The kind words that matter after are not "we meant well" — a phrase even Pharaoh could claim when drowning — but qawlan balighan in 4:63 that names the self, and istighfar soon in 4:17 that seeks, not swears, and huda.

=63=

"They are those whose secrets Allah knows what is in their hearts. So turn away from them, admonish them, and say to them about themselves penetrating words that reach them."

Ula'ika alladhina ya'lamu Allahu ma fi qulubihim — they are those whose secrets Allah knows what is in their hearts. The same knowledge as Raqib ever Watchful in 4:1 who sees the orphan's sheep, Alim in 4:70 who knows who deserves companionship with prophets, Shahid in 4:33 who sees trusts returned, Muhit in 4:126 who encompasses, Sami' in 4:148 who hears jahra, Alim in 4:111 who knows who earned. What chests conceal in 4:63 is the same as what Allah knows He will gather on the Day when no hadith is hidden in 4:42 and no atom is wronged in 4:40.

Fa-a'rid anhum — turn away from them — not as inner circle, not as confidants, not as consultants for war or for family arbitration. As 4:144 warns not to take disbelievers as intimate awliya instead of believers, and as 4:89 warns not to take equal-wishers as allies until they emigrate, turn is political and social distance, not hatred that seeks to destroy or to humiliate in public. Wa 'izhum — admonish them, exhort, remind of the Day when they will wish the earth levelled in 4:42, of the Fire whose skins are renewed in 4:56 to taste, of the lowest depth in 4:145 with no helper. Wa qul lahum fi anfusihim qawlan balighan — say to them about themselves a penetrating word — qawlan balighan — that reaches, that finds the joint, that pierces pretense because it talks about themselves, not about abstractions or tribes. Baligh is not qawl ma'ruf (kind words) in 4:5,8 nor qawl sadid (straight, hitting the mark) in 4:9,33:70, but baligh — reaching, impactful, that travels from ear to heart and stays, that is remembered at night.

Three steps for dealing with hypocrites thus: distance from secrets, public exhortation that warns the community, and private piercing counsel that names their own concealment where it can still enter. The Prophet applied this with mercy: he did not execute Abdullah ibn Ubayy when his withdrawal of three hundred at Uhud exposed nifaq and his words were known, and he prayed at his funeral and stood until revelation distinguished the limits of seeking forgiveness for those who persist. Baligh words are not public humiliation for followers to cheer; they are truth said about themselves — "you said obedience by day and plotted by night, you swore ihsan after sudud, you sought taghut" — in the hour when the heart is soft after musibah, where the arrow can find the old wound and heal. The verse teaches leaders that not every sin needs a broadcast; some need a baligh whisper in the right ear at the right time, and that is siyasah shar'iyyah, and it is mercy.

=64=

"We did not send any messenger except to be obeyed by Allah's permission. Had they, after wronging themselves, come to you seeking Allah's forgiveness and had the Messenger sought forgiveness for them, they would have found Allah Accepting of repentance, Merciful."

Wa ma arsalna min rasulin illa li-yuta'a bi-idhni Allah — We did not send any messenger except to be obeyed — li-yuta'a — by Allah's permission, by His leave, as 4:80 will say "whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah" and as 4:59 commands obedience to Allah and the Messenger and those in authority within them. The Prophet is not a cultural symbol to admire; he is a judge in shajar disputes in 4:65, a purifier who recites and teaches Book and wisdom as in 62:2, a general who is among them in fear prayer in 4:102, a husband who lived ma'ruf with wives, a reciter. Li-yuta'a gathers all roles: believe him, follow him, love him, obey him.

Wa law annahum idh zalamu anfusahum ja'uka fastaghfaru Allaha wastaghfara lahum ar-rasulu la-wajadu Allaha Tawwaba Rahima — had they, after wronging themselves — after zulm, the same zulm as eating orphan wealth in 4:10, throwing sin on the innocent in 4:112, and as earning sin against the self in 4:111 and as musibah — come to you — ja'uka — seeking Allah's forgiveness — fastaghfaru Allaha — and had the Messenger sought forgiveness for them — wastaghfara lahum ar-rasulu — they would have found Allah Tawwab, Rahim — Accepting, Merciful. The door after sudud and musibah and swearing is still open, but the path is specific: coming, seeking, and the Messenger's seeking. In the Prophet's life this was physical coming to his blessed gathering, sitting, confessing, as the companions who confessed zina came and he turned away until they insisted, then he sought for them. After him it is coming to his Sunnah, seeking Allah directly with the master of seeking — Allahumma anta Rabbi — while loving that the Prophet taught abundant istighfar and that he himself sought forgiveness seventy times a day though forgiven past and future, and that the community's du'a for a stumbler — Allahumma ighfir lahu — is part of that Sunnah. The Prophet's du'a for a companion's forgiveness was mercy Allah honours; the community's du'a for its sinner is still honoured.

Hypocrites chose sudud instead of coming; had they come they would have found Tawwab as 4:17 promises "repentance soon is upon Allah" and as 4:110 promises "whoever seeks forgiveness will find Allah Forgiving." The verse teaches that forgiveness is sought and walked to, not waited for on a couch while swearing ihsan, and that beloved intermediaries are not lords but askers whose asking Allah loves and answers.

=65=

"No, by your Lord, they will not truly believe until they make you judge in what is disputed between them, then find no discomfort in themselves from your judgment and submit fully."

Fala wa rabbika la yu'minuna hatta yuhakkimuka fi ma shajara baynahum — no, by your Lord — Allah Himself swears by His Lordship to His Messenger — they will not believe — la yu'minuna — until they make you judge — yuhakkimuka — in whatever is disputed between them — ma shajara — shajar is to branch, to interlock like trees and roots; disputes branch, intertwine, and hide the trunk, as family feuds do. Thumma la yajidu fi anfusihim harajan mimma qadayta — then find no haraj — tightness, constriction, unease — in themselves from what you decreed. Haraj is the chest's resistance when desire loses, as chests concealing hatred and tightness in 3:118 and as hearts deviating after guidance in 3:7-8 and as the tight chest of the one who cannot find a way in 4:98.

Wa yusallimu taslima — and submit with taslima — full, willing surrender, verb repeated for emphasis and for the root salam — peace. Faith here has three pillars, not one: tahkim — making him judge, referring to him alive and to his Sunnah after him as 4:59 orders "refer it to Allah and the Messenger" and as the Prophet said, "I leave among you two things, Book and Sunnah; hold to them"; lack of haraj — inner acceptance without grudge or "I accept but resent and will plot at night" — and taslim — outward compliance with contentment, not drag. Many obey with haraj, mouth says "we hear and we obey" while chest tightens, eyes roll, then night plotting in 4:81 resumes. That is not the iman praised here.

The Prophet said, "None of you will believe until his desire — hawa — follows what I brought," and the Quran calls hawa the deviator in 4:135 "do not follow desire so you deviate" and 4:27. The hypocrite follows hawa and calls it ihsan. Today the test is exactly where the surah has been heaviest: inheritance, marriage, mahr, business, and social disputes. Where the dispute is shar'i — who inherits how much as in 4:11-12 and 4:176, is this mahr due as in 4:4 and 4:20, is this divorce valid, is this contract riba as in 4:161 — to accept his Sunnah over tribe, trend, or state law that contradicts it, and to do so without haraj, is the definition. The oath "by your Lord" shows how heavy the measure is; it is not a recommendation but a definition of belief, after which 4:66-68 promise tathbit and guidance for the doer.

=66=

"Had We prescribed for them, 'Kill yourselves or leave your homes,' they would have done it only a few of them. Had they done what they were exhorted to do, it would have been better for them and more strengthening."

Wa law anna katabna alayhim an iqtulu anfusakum aw ikhruju min diyarikum ma fa'aluhu illa qalilun minhum — had We written upon them, as We wrote upon Bani Isra'il after the calf in 2:54 "kill yourselves" for repentance, or as We wrote upon the Muhajirun to leave homes for Hijrah and as later upon Bani al-Nadir, they would have done it only a few — qalil — as human weakness predicts. The verse names human weakness after 4:28 "man was created weak — da'ifa" and after the lagger's weak heart in 4:72 and the fearful who fear people as Allah in 4:77. Harsh decrees expose da'fa; gentle exhortations are mercy that matches da'fa and tests sincerity.

Wa law annahum fa'alu ma yu'azuna bihi la-kana khayran lahum wa ashadda tathbita — had they done what they are exhorted to do — the actual exhortations in this surah: obey in 4:59, return trusts in 4:58, avoid taghut in 4:60, come seeking forgiveness in 4:64, accept judgment without haraj in 4:65, stand for justice even against yourselves in 4:135 — it would have been better — khayran — as khayr in 4:25 "patience better," 4:128 sulh khayr, 4:34 "Allah is High Great" after islah — and more strengthening — ashadda tathbita — firmness, making the heart firm, opposite of mudhabdhabin in 4:143 neither to these nor those and of yatarabbasuna waiters and of the two groups divided in 4:88. Tathbit is what prayer does, what wudu as light does, what charity as proof does, what remembrance after fear prayer in 4:103 does: it firms.

The actual decree that came was not "kill yourselves" but "return trusts, judge justly, obey Messenger, avoid major sins and We will expiate minors" in 4:31, "ask Allah from His bounty" in 4:32, "be qawwamin with justice" — all doable, better, and stabilising. Few would bear the hardest; many can bear the commanded if they know its khayr and tathbit, and the next two verses promise what bearing gives.

=67=

"And then We would have given them from Ourselves a great reward,"

Wa idhan la-ataynahum min ladunna ajran azima — and then We would have given them from Ourselves — min ladunna — from His nearness — a great reward — ajran azima. Ladunna signals nearness and direct bounty, as in 4:40 "gives from Himself a great reward" for the weight of a dharrah, and 4:74 ajr azim for being killed or overcoming, and 4:114 ajr azim for good najwa of charity or islah, and 4:146 ajr azim for the repentant hypocrite. The great reward is not only Paradise's rivers and purified spouses and deep shade in 4:57, but nearness after obedience, as 4:69's companionship with the blessed. Small steps of tawbah and tahkim, of coming, of accepting without haraj, meet from-Ourselves generosity that is not calculated by hours but by ladun, not by quantity but by nearness.

This verse pairs with the next: great reward in gift plus guidance on the path — gift without guidance would be wage without direction, guidance without gift would be direction without joy, and Allah gives both to the doer of the enjoined, as He gives both to the patient.

=68=

"And We would have guided them to a straight path."

Wa la-hadaynahum siratan mustaqima — and We would have guided them to a straight path — sirat as in Fatihah 1:6 "guide us to the straight path" that the believer asks seventeen times a day, and 4:26 "guide you to ways of those before you," and 4:175 "guide them to Himself on a straight path." Guidance after obedience is added guidance as in 19:76 "Allah increases those guided in guidance" and as tathbit in 4:66 leads to walk. The hypothetical hard decree — kill or emigrate — ends with double promise that is actual for the doer of the enjoined: if you do what you are told — come, seek, accept judgment without haraj, refer disputes, hold fast — you are strengthened and guided, from avoiding taghut to loving tawhid, from reluctant prayer to remembering Allah standing, sitting, lying after 4:103, from stinginess to spending from what He provided in 4:39, from riya to ikhlas.

The straight path after obedience is not a tightrope for the few who could have killed themselves; it is a wide road that the weak can walk when Allah wants to lighten as in 4:28, and when He wants to turn as in 4:27, and when He wants to make clear as in 4:26.

=69=

"Whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger will be with those Allah has blessed — among prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous. Excellent are those as companions."

Wa man yuti'illaha war-rasula fa-ula'ika ma'a alladhina an'ama Allahu alayhim min an-nabiyyin was-siddiqina wash-shuhada was-salihina — whoever obeys — yuti' — Allah and the Messenger as in 4:59's command and 4:64's li-yuta'a and 4:80's equation and 4:65's tahkim — will be with — ma'a — those Allah blessed — an'ama — as in Fatihah's "path of those You blessed," and as in 4:69's own an'ama. Among prophets — nabiyyin — the guided chain from Nuh as in 4:163 to Muhammad, the truthful — siddiqina — like Abu Bakr as-Siddiq and Maryam as siddiqa in 5:75, the witnesses/martyrs — shuhada — as at Uhud and as the witnesses in 4:41 and as those who fall seeking the mustad'afin in 4:75, and the righteous — salihin.

Obedience in the surah's heaviest places — precise shares in 4:11-12 and 4:176, dowries as nihlah in 4:4, oaths not sold in 3:77, arbitration with qist in 4:35 and 4:128, referral to Book and Sunnah in 4:59, and even night plotting abandoned — purchases companionship of the best of humanity. The Prophet said, "A person will be with those he loves," and when a poor man asked about the Hour saying he had little fasting or charity but love of Allah and His Messenger, he said, "You will be with those you love." He said the martyr wishes to return to be killed again for the companionship and the extra, and he called "excellent companions" those who make solitude disappear, and he said the best of generations are his.

Wa hasuna ula'ika rafiqa — excellent are those as companions — rafiqa — companion on a road, as the close companion at your side in 4:36 was-sahibi bil-janb. The highest sahabi is not only beside you on a journey, nor only a group chat, but with you in eternity, walking, talking, interceding. Obedience chooses its caravan; disobedience with sudud and lag and yatarabbas chooses waiting with waiters, and night plotting chooses darkness alone.

=70=

"That bounty is from Allah, and sufficient is Allah as Knower."

Dhalika al-fadlu min Allah — that companionship with the blessed, that tathbit, that guidance, that great reward from nearness — is fadl — bounty, pure gift — from Allah, not wage that enforces Him, as 4:113 "great is Allah's bounty upon you," 3:74 Owner of great bounty, 3:171-174 great bounty after wounds, and 4:67 ladun ajr. No soul earns companionship with prophets by lineage; it is fadl, as 3:74 says He gives it to whom He wills. Wa kafa billahi Alima — sufficient is Allah as Knower — Alim of who obeyed with clean chest and who performed tahkim reluctantly with haraj hidden, who spent as bukhl in 4:37 and who as hani'an mari'a in 4:4, who prayed with qasr gratefully and who prayed kusala to be seen, who sat with darar in 4:95-96 excused and who lagged in 4:72 by desire. Kafa mirrors kafa billahi Waliyyan and Nasira in 4:45 and Hasiban in 4:6, Shahidan in 4:79,166, Wakila in 4:81, Shakiran in 4:147: sufficiency replaces need for human validation or human praise.

He knows who deserves ma'iyya with prophets and who deserves no sabil as in 4:88 and who deserves Fire with no nasir as in 4:52. For the obedient this is comfort after 4:65's hard test: people may dismiss your strictness in inheritance as harsh, your referral to Sunnah as rigid, your penetrating admonition as harsh; Allah knows who was sincere. For the showy, it is warning: Alim is not deceived by riya remembrance qalila and kusala prayer, nor by "we intended ihsan" after sudud.

=71=

"O believers, take your precautions and go forth in groups or all together."

Ya ayyuha alladhina amanu khudhu hizrakum — take your hizr — precaution, gear, guard, planning, intelligence, armour, provisions, as 4:102 will detail taking arms even in prayer and as 2:197 "take provisions, best provision is taqwa" and as the Prophet took hizr at Badr with scouts and at the Trench with digging and at Uhud with archers on the hill. Faith never forbids means; tawakkul without hizr is negligence that Satan's weak plot in 4:76 can exploit, and negligence in deen is not tawakkul but tamanni.

Fanfiru thubatin aw infiru jami'an — go forth — fanfiru — mobilized, marching — in thubat — groups, contingents, patrols, companies, battalions — or all together — jami'an — as wisdom, need, and legitimate authority decide, small patrols for a village and full mobilisation for an army. After spiritual tathbit in 4:66, guidance in 4:68, companionship promise in 4:69, and Knower's sufficiency in 4:70, now practical readiness is ordered; iman without hizr is incomplete, hizr without iman is empty and anxious. For today's believer the hizr is also knowledge before debate, documentation before inheritance division, consultation before marriage, good passwords before posting, security before travel — all as worship, not as doubt in Allah. The lagger's excuse in the next verse is not hizr; it is abandonment of fanfiru while calling it hizr, and the Quran exposes it.

=72=

"Among you are those who will lag behind. If disaster strikes you, they say, 'Allah has blessed me that I was not with them as a witness.'"

Wa inna minkum la-man layubatti'anna — among you is one who lags — yubatti'u — slows, makes others slow, discourages, holds back, from batta'a — to delay — with lam and emphasized nun — la-man layubatti'anna — certainty among you, not outside. Fa-in asabatkum musibatun qala qad an'ama Allahu alayya idh lam akun ma'ahum shahida — if musibah — calamity, injury, loss, setback, martyrdom — strikes you, he says "Allah has blessed me that I was not witness with them." The same musibah word as in 4:62 "when calamity strikes them for what hands earned" and as in 3:165 after Uhud "how is this? Say it is from yourselves," and as in 2:156. The lagger's theology is entirely worldly and inverted: presence at hardship with believers is evil, absence is ni'mah; witness — shahida — with believers in hardship is not honour to him, not fath. He calls safety while brothers bleed a blessing, as if blessing is only being elsewhere, not being with.

This after "take precautions and go forth" warns that some precautions are cowardice dressed as caution, some hizr is abandonment of fanfiru, and some "wisdom" is lagger's poison. The lagger among you is more dangerous than an open enemy outside, because he teaches others to lag, whispers "stay, you have excuse, your children need you, your job needs you," and recasts disobedience as prudence while the mustad'afin's du'a in 4:75 waits for fanfiru and while the Prophet waits. The Prophet named such voices as callers on the gates of Hell who invite to abandonment, and revelation names him here so he is known.

=73=

"But if a bounty from Allah reaches you, they will say — as if no affection ever existed between you — 'Would that I had been with them, so I could have achieved a great success.'"

Wa la-in asabakum fadlun min Allahi la-yaqulanna ka-an lam takun baynakum wa baynahu mawaddatun ya laytani kuntu ma'ahum fa-afuza fawzan azima — if a fadl — bounty, spoils, victory, relief, a house, a job — reaches you, he will certainly say — la-yaqulanna with double emphasis — as if no mawaddah — affection, love, brotherhood — had ever existed between you and him — "Would that I had been with them, so I could achieve a great success." When hardship was yours, absence was blessing; when bounty is yours, absence is loss and regret. The hinge is dunya. Fawz azim for him is spoils, a house, a share, a like, a promotion; for the believer fawz azim is Gardens in 4:13 "that is the great success" and forgiveness, and ajr azim in 4:40, 4:67, 4:74 regardless of spoils.

Ka-an lam takun baynakum wa baynahu mawaddah — as if no affection — exposes hypocrisy's lack of brotherhood; true mawaddah shares both musibah and fadl, as muhajirun and ansar shared dates, shelters, battles, hunger, and graves, as the Prophet said the believers are like one body: if one limb suffers, the whole body responds with fever and sleeplessness. The lagger loves only his own limb, his own house. His fawz is the world's leftover; the believer's fawz is the One who knows and the Garden He promised. The verse teaches to see the heart by its two statements after musibah and fadl.

=74=

"So let those fight in Allah's path who sell worldly life for the Hereafter. Whoever fights in Allah's path and is killed or overcomes, We will give him a great reward."

Falyuqatil fi sabili Allahi alladhina yashruna al-hayata ad-dunya bil-akhirah — let those fight in His path who sell — yashruna — worldly life for the Hereafter. Yashruna here is selling dunya as capital to buy akhirah, the correct commerce, opposite of those who sold Hereafter for a small price in 2:174, 2:79, 3:77, 3:187. The verse commands specifically those who have already made the inner sale; fighting is outer expression of inner commerce. As 9:111 "Allah has purchased from the believers their lives and wealth in exchange for Paradise," the sale precedes the sword, not the reverse. The believer first sells in his heart, then his feet follow.

Wa man yuqatil fi sabili Allahi fa-yuqtal aw yaghlib fa-sawfa nu'tihi ajran azima — whoever fights in Allah's path and is killed — yuqtāl — or overcomes — yaghlib — We will give him a great reward — ajran azima — as in 4:67 ladun ajr and 4:114 and 4:146. Both outcomes are winning because the transaction was made before the battle; the martyr's wage is not dependent on victory, the victor's wage is not reduced by survival. The Prophet said the martyr is alive with his Lord, provided for, rejoicing with those behind, wishing to return to be killed again for extra, and the victor returns with favour and bounty not touched by harm as in 3:174 "they returned with bounty from Allah, no harm touched them." Against lagger's fawz of spoils, ajr azim is guaranteed regardless of spoils and regardless of being witness or absent. The sale is what matters.

=75=

"What is wrong with you that you do not fight in Allah's path and for the oppressed among men, women, and children who cry, 'Our Lord, deliver us from this town whose people are oppressors, and appoint for us from Yourself a guardian and helper'?"

Wa ma lakum la tuqatiluna fi sabili Allahi wal-mustad'afina min ar-rijali wan-nisa wal-wildani — what is wrong with you that you do not fight in Allah's path and for the oppressed — mustad'afin — among men, women, and children? The mustad'afin are the weak held down, as the believers left in Makkah who could not emigrate until Fath, the women and children in 4:98-99 helpless and 4:127 orphan women and children and 4:75's town whose name every generation knows. Alladhina yaquluna Rabbana akhrijna min hadhihi al-qaryati az-zalimu ahluha waj'al lana min ladunka waliyyan waj'al lana min ladunka nasira — they cry, "Our Lord, deliver us from this town whose people are oppressors, appoint for us from Yourself — ladunka — a guardian — waliy — and a helper — nasir." The same pair sufficient in 4:45 "Allah suffices as Wali, suffices as Helper" and promised to the weak, and whose sufficiency is shown through you.

Their du'a is the reason to fight: not for dominance, not for spoils, not for expanded borders, not for revenge, but for a du'a that has no other answer except your strength, your hizr, your fanfiru. This is the humanitarian jihad that frames 4:71's precautions and 4:76's weak plot and 4:102's salat al-khawf. When the weak's only hope is Allah's ladun and your strength is Allah's gift, not fighting for them is the excess and the blame, and the question "what is wrong with you?" is gentle rebuke and wake-up. The Prophet said, "Help your brother oppressed or oppressor;" they asked, "How help oppressor?" He said, "Restrain him from oppression; that is helping him." Restraining the zalim town's people is help for the mustad'afin and even for the zalim before his own mustad'af soul.

=76=

"Those who believe fight in Allah's path, and those who disbelieve fight in taghut's path. So fight the allies of Satan. Satan's plot is ever weak."

Alladhina amanu yuqatiluna fi sabili Allahi, walladhina kafaru yuqatiluna fi sabili at-taghut — those who believe fight in Allah's path, those who disbelieve fight in taghut's path — taghut as in 4:60's desired court and 2:256's handle, and 4:51's jibt and taghut believed in. Two paths are not parallel opinions to be respected; they oppose, and every fighter is on one. There is no neutral fighting; even the mustad'af's du'a sorts paths. Fa-qatilu awliya ash-Shaytan — so fight the allies of Satan — awliya — not the misguided masses who were deceived, but the organisers of oppression, the patrons who take Satan as wali instead of Allah as in 4:119, who withhold naqir, who wish you equal in kufr, who block.

Inna kayda ash-Shaytani kana da'ifa — Satan's plot was ever weak — da'if — compared with Kayd Allah best of planners in 3:54 and encompassing Muhit in 4:108, and with the firm rope in 3:103. Weak does not mean no fight; it means fear is misplaced. 3:175 "fear them not, fear Me if you are believers" returns here: fight allies while fearing only Allah. Weak plot still harms if the strong sleep; indeed the lagger's lag in 4:72 is how weak plot wins without strength, and yatarabbas in 4:141 is how weak plot waits for your sleep. The verse after "what is wrong you do not fight for the weak" says you are not fighting a giant; you are fighting a weak plot with Al-Wali Al-Nasir behind you, and with dhikr after, and with ajr azim ahead, and with qist as your weapon.

=77=

"Have you not seen those told, 'Restrain your hands, establish prayer, and give zakah,' but when fighting was prescribed a group feared people as they fear Allah or more, and said, 'Our Lord, why have You prescribed fighting? Delay us a short term'? Say, 'The enjoyment of the world is little, the Hereafter better for the mindful, you will not be wronged a thread.'"

Alam tara ila alladhina qila lahum kuffu aydiyakum wa aqimu as-salata wa atu az-zakah — in Makkah for thirteen years they were told restrain hands, establish prayer, give zakah, when fighting permission had not come and restraint was wisdom, purification, and preparation, and the Prophet himself was told to forgive and turn. Falamma kutiba alayhim al-qitalu idha fariqun minhum yakhshawna an-nasa ka-khashyati Allahi aw ashaddu khashyatan — when fighting was written — kutiba — as fasting and wills and qisas are written, obligatory — a group feared people as they fear Allah or more intensely — ashadd khashyah. The fear that belongs to Allah slipped to people, to loss of wealth, to loss of status.

Wa qalu rabbana lima katabta alayna al-qitala lawla akhkhartana ila ajalin qarib — why prescribe fighting? Why not delay to a near term? Same lawla as those who at death say "lawla akhkhartani ila ajalin qarib — delay me a little, I will give charity." The request for delay is the soul's habit. Qul mata'u ad-dunya qalil wal-akhiratu khayrun liman ittaqa — say, enjoyment of the world is little — qalil — a sip against an ocean, a shade that vanishes at noon, against Gardens' rivers and shade zalil and abiding, against companionship in 4:69. Hereafter is better for the mindful — muttaqin — who fear Allah not people, who give, who stand. Wa la tuzlamuna fatila — you will not be wronged even a filament — fatila — the thin thread on a date stone's groove, the same precision as dharrah in 4:40 and naqir in 4:53 and qitmir. Fighting prescribed is not zulm; atom justice guarantees no fighter loses a filament of reward, no restrainer gains a filament by delaying. The fear that belongs to Allah slipped to people; correction restores khashyah to its Owner, and the next verse says death reaches everywhere anyway, so fearing people gains nothing and loses everything.

=78=

"Wherever you are, death will reach you, even if you are in fortified towers. When good befalls them they say, 'This is from Allah,' but when evil befalls them they say, 'This is from you.' Say, 'All is from Allah.' What is wrong they hardly understand any statement? Whatever good is from Allah, whatever evil from yourself. We sent you as messenger, Allah suffices as Witness."

Ayna ma takunu yudrikkumu al-mawtu wa law kuntum fi burujin mushayyadah — wherever you are death reaches, even in towers fortified — buruj mushayyadah — lofty, plastered, guarded, iron-doored, today a bunker, a gated compound, a private jet, a bodyguard. Death is at an appointed term as in 3:145 "no soul dies except by permission at a written term" and 3:154 "those for whom killing was written would have gone forth to their places" even if they stayed home. Delaying fighting does not delay death; the tower does not postpone the appointment, it only changes the address where the angel finds you, and the angel does not need a key.

Wa in tusibhum hasanatun yaqulu hadhihi min indi Allah, wa in tusibhum sayyi'atun yaqulu hadhihi min indik — when good befalls them they say from Allah, evil they say from you, O Muhammad — scapegoating the messenger at every setback, as hypocrites did at every expedition, at Uhud, at the Trench, as in 4:62 swearing and 4:72-73 attributing. Qul kullun min indi Allah — say, all is from Allah in decree — good and evil occur by His will and creation, not outside. Fa-ma li-ha'ula'i al-qawmi la yakaduna yafqahuna hadithan — what is wrong with these people they hardly understand any statement — hadith — speech, affair, the same hadith as 4:87. The question is rebuke for blame-shifting and for not understanding qadar.

Ma asabaka min hasanatin fa-min Allahi wa ma asabaka min sayyi'atin fa-min nafsik — whatever good befalls you is from Allah — bounty, fadl, ladun, grace — whatever evil from yourself — earned — as 42:30 "whatever affliction strikes you is for what your hands earned" and 4:62's musibah for what hands earned and as 4:17's zulm that needs soon seeking. Both levels are true and must be held together without contradiction: decree from Allah, responsibility on self, as 4:79 repeats for emphasis and as 8:53 says Allah does not change favour until they change themselves. Ayna ma takunu collapsed fatalism that thinks towers save; ma asabaka corrects it: towers do not save, but your hands do earn, and your Lord is Just.

Wa arsalnaka lin-nasi rasulan wa kafa billahi Shahida — sent you as messenger to all people — lin-nas — as 4:1 ya ayyha an-nas universal, as 4:170 — sufficient Witness — Shahid as in 4:41 and 4:166, 4:79. If the Witness suffices, neither fear of towers nor blame of the messenger stands. For today's blamer, the tower is the excuse and the Prophet is the scapegoat; correction is to thank Allah for good and seek forgiveness for evil instead of blaming the caller, and to take hizr without thinking hizr saves from qadar.

=79=

"Whatever good befalls you is from Allah, and whatever evil from yourself. We sent you as messenger to people, Allah suffices as Witness."

Repetition personalises after communal "all is from Allah." Ma asabaka again singular — you — good from Him, evil from your earning, as 4:62, 4:110's seeking, 4:111's earning, 4:62's musibah. The Prophet taught, "Know that what missed you was never to hit you, and what hit you was never to miss you," and also taught to attribute evil to self in istighfar — "I acknowledge Your blessing upon me and I acknowledge my sin, so forgive me." The balance guards two errors: arrogance that good is from my earning alone, and despair that evil is random and unfixable, both of which break tawhid.

Wa arsalnaka lin-nasi rasulan wa kafa billahi Shahida — sent you as messenger to all humanity, not to a tribe, not to Arabs alone, sufficient Witness. If Witness suffices, neither the fear that made a group fear people more than Allah in 4:77 nor the blame that made them blame the Messenger in 4:78 stands; both return to Allah the Sufficient, the Hearing, the Seeing, the Knowing. The verse's repetition is not redundancy; it is emphasis that the heart needs: all from Allah in decree, good from Him in bounty, evil from you in earning — hold both, and the heart stays straight.

=80=

"Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah. Whoever turns away — We did not send you as keeper over them."

Man yuti'i ar-rasula fa-qad ata'a Allaha — whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah — because he commands by permission in 4:64 — li-yuta'a bi-idhni Allah — and not from desire — 53:3-4 "he does not speak from desire, it is revelation." The equation is direct and final: obedience to the elaborator of the Book is obedience to the Revealer of the Book; the elaborator does not invent, he shows, he explains, he applies. The Book says "establish prayer," the Messenger shows how, when, with what.

Wa man tawalla fa-ma arsalnaka alayhim hafiza — whoever turns away — tawalla — the same turning as sudud in 4:61, taghut-wish in 4:60, fearing people in 4:77, blaming in 4:78 — We did not send you as hafiz — keeper, guard, watcher over them to compel, to force, to sit on chests. Your duty is balagh — conveyance, clarification, as in 3:20 "upon you is only conveyance" and 5:99 "upon the Messenger is only clear conveyance" and 42:48 and 28:56 "you do not guide whom you love, but Allah guides whom He wills."

After towers that teach death cannot be delayed, and good/evil that teaches responsibility, and blame that teaches scapegoating, this places obedience where it belongs: fear Allah not people, thank Allah for good, correct self for evil, obey the Messenger who teaches how, and know that turning does not harm him. The lagger who feared people, the blamer who blamed the Prophet, now hears the line that judges both: obedience to him is obedience to Allah, turning is not the Prophet's failure but the turner's loss, and the Witness — Shahid — remains, and the Record remains, and the promise remains.
