=21=

"Do not marry those women whom your fathers married, except what has already passed. Indeed, it was an immorality, hateful, and an evil way."

Wa la tankihu ma nakaha aba'ukum min an-nisa illa ma qad salaf — do not marry what your fathers married. Guardianship, trust, and wombs meet here. In Jahiliyyah a stepson would inherit his stepmother as part of the estate, as mentioned in 4:19's prohibition of inheriting women by force. A cloth was thrown; a marriage was claimed without her consent, to keep her mahr and inheritance in the family. Allah names this with three words that do not soften: innahu kana fahishatan wa maqtan wa sa'a sabila — it was fahishah, a manifest indecency, and maqtan, hateful to Allah and to sound natures, and sa'a sabila — an evil way, an evil path that corrupts lineage and severs the respect due to fathers.

Illa ma qad salaf — except what has passed before prohibition — lifts accountability for pre-Islamic marriages already contracted in ignorance, as Allah is pardoning for what was done without knowledge, similar to the pardon for past riba or past shirk before Islam. Once knowledge comes, the limit is fixed.

Revelation linked this directly to womb ties. The father’s wife is like a mother in sanctity; the stepmother, even if not of blood, occupies the father's place in the home. The Prophet, peace be upon him, sent al-Bara' ibn Azib with a group to a man who had married his father's wife after death, to strike his neck and take his property as the penalty for violating a hudud of family. That severity shows the category: not a dislike of a cultural preference but a violation of firash and lineage.

Connected to the previous verses, the sequence is deliberate: after protecting orphan girls' mahr (4:3-4), establishing qiwamah with justice (4:34 will come), and sealing covenants (4:21), Allah now lists whom marriage can never reach. The single-soul family of 4:1 now receives walls so that nearness does not become confusion. Modern forms of this include marrying a father's divorced wife to punish the father, or late-life marriages pursued for inheritance. The three descriptors remain: fahishah to faith, maqtan to sound taste, and evil way to the path it opens.

=22=

"Forbidden to you are your mothers, your daughters, your sisters, your paternal aunts, your maternal aunts, your brother's daughters, your sister's daughters, your milk mothers who nursed you and your milk sisters, your wives' mothers, your stepdaughters under your guardianship born of wives you have consummated — but if you have not consummated, there is no blame on you — and the wives of your sons from your own loins, and that you join two sisters together, except what has passed. Indeed, Allah is Forgiving, Merciful."

Hurrimat alaykum — forbidden upon you — is a stronger word than "do not do." It is haram by decree. The list is the longest single prohibition list in marriage, and it builds from the nearest blood to the furthest alliance.

Mothers, daughters, sisters, paternal aunts, maternal aunts, brother's daughters, sister's daughters — seven blood ties that never become lawful, whether near or distant, whether the mother is your mother or grandmother, the daughter is your daughter or granddaughter. The womb of 4:1 is now protected from every upward, downward, and sideways inversion.

Then milk: ummahatukum allati arda'nakum wa akhawatukum min ar-rada'ah — milk-mothers and milk-sisters. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said, "What becomes haram through blood becomes haram through milk," and "Milk kinship makes haram what blood makes haram." A woman who nurses a child with five satisfying feedings becomes his mother in milk; her children are his siblings; her sister is his aunt. The wisdom reaches beyond biology into nurturing: milk creates bone and flesh, and Islam honours the foster bond with the same boundary as birth. Modern milk banks therefore require records; a child breastfed without history risks future confusion the Quran precisely prevents.

Then marriage: mothers of your wives — once a marriage contract is concluded, her mother becomes permanently forbidden even without consummation, to protect the dignity of the new mother-in-law tie. Stepdaughters ar-rabayib — those under your guardianship, daughters of your wives from previous husbands — are forbidden only if you consummated the marriage with their mother; if you divorced before touching, you may marry the daughter. The nuance is mercy: the daughter who lived in your house as a child is like your own.

Then wives of your sons from your loins — halail abna'ikum — daughter-in-law, the son's wife, becomes permanently forbidden to the father after the son consummates, honouring the son's firash; adopted sons are excluded by "from your loins," as Islam ends legal tabanni while keeping kindness, as in 33:4-5.

Finally, to combine two sisters at the same time — one wife and her sister as co-wives — chokes sisterhood. The Prophet added to this list by Sunnah: do not combine a woman and her paternal aunt, or a woman and her maternal aunt, for the same reason: it severs rahm. Illa ma qad salaf — except what passed in Jahiliyyah — and inna Allaha kana Ghafuran Rahima — Forgiving, Merciful — closes every opening with pardon for ignorant past and mercy that forbids only to protect, not to shame.

This list is memorisation that saves religion. Marrying within these circles is not a mistake to fix after; the contract is void and the intimacy is zina.

=23=

"Also forbidden are married women, except those in your possession. This is Allah's decree upon you. Lawful to you are all beyond these, provided you seek them with your wealth in chastity, not in lustful promiscuity. For what you enjoy from them, give their dowries as an obligation. There is no blame if you both agree mutually after the obligation. Indeed, Allah is Knowing, Wise."

Wal-muhsanat min an-nisa illa ma malakat aymanukum — married women are forbidden — muhsanat here meaning free women under the حصن, the fortress of marriage. The exception "except what your right hands possess" belonged to a war captivity structure where marriages to polytheist husbands were considered severed by capture and enslavement; the Quran then moves the entire institution toward marriage with permission, dower, chastity, and eventual freeing, as 4:25 and 4:92 will outline. The moral trajectory is de-escalation: from capture to kindness, to freeing.

Kitaba Allahi alaykum — a decree from Allah upon you — not tribal custom. Wa uhilla lakum ma wara'a dhalikum — lawful beyond these prohibitions are those you seek. An tabtaghu bi-amwalikum muhsinin ghayra musafihin — that you seek with your wealth, desiring chastity, not fornicators. Seeking "with your wealth" — with mahr, housing, and maintenance — places responsibility on the seeker; marriage is not a free-taking, it is a spending, the same qiwamah of 4:34 rooted in "by what they spend." Muhsinin — guarding chastity, seeking حصن — against musafihin — pouring desire without حصن, fornication.

Fa-ma istamta'tum bihi minhunna fa-atuhunna ujurahunna faridah — for what you enjoy from them, give their dowries as an obligation — faridah, a cut portion. Istimta' here is intimacy within the marriage sought, not a temporary arrangement of desire. The Prophet allowed temporary marriage at the opening of Islam in scarcity, then forbade it finally at Khaybar and in his farewell, saying, "I had permitted you istimta' with women; Allah has now forbidden it until the Day of Resurrection." The companions of sound reading therefore understand this verse as the permanent marriage that is enjoined with faridah dower; the "enjoyment" requires the marriage already described as seeking chastity.

Wa la junaha alaykum fi ma taradaytum bihi min ba'di al-faridah — no blame if both mutually agree after the obligation — to increase, decrease, defer, or forgive part of the mahr with the same tib an-nafs of 4:4. Inna Allaha kana Aliman Hakima — Knowing of who seeks حصن versus who seeks سفاح, Wise in drawing the line of mahram to protect lineage and then opening the wide door of lawful beyond it. For today, the line is clear: marriage is a contract with mahr that guards chastity; any arrangement that evades mahr, witness, guardian, or maintenance, dressing lust as contract, is safah, not ihsan, and stands outside this verse.

=24=

"If any of you cannot afford to marry free believing women, then marry from among believing slave women in your possession. Allah knows best your faith — you are from one another. So marry them with their owners' permission and give them their dowries with kindness, as chaste women, not fornicators nor secret lovers. But when they are married, if they commit immorality, their punishment is half that of free women. This is for those of you who fear hardship. Patience is better for you. And Allah is Forgiving, Merciful."

Wa man lam yastati' minkum tawlan an yankiha al-muhsanati al-mu'minati — whoever cannot afford tawl — material means, dower and maintenance — to marry free chaste believing women, then from believing slave women your right hands possess. The verse does not celebrate slavery; it restricts and humanises it. Pre-Islam, a man would use a slave woman without marriage; Islam says marry her. Not take her; marry her — bi-idhni ahlihinna — with permission of her people, and give her ujrah — dower — bil-ma'ruf.

Wallahu a'lamu bi-imanikum — Allah knows your faith. Ba'dukum min ba'd — you are from one another. This single clause dismantles superiority. Free man, slave woman — from one soul in 4:1, from one another now. The Prophet said, "They are your brothers whom Allah placed under your hands. Feed them from what you eat, clothe them from what you wear, do not burden them beyond capacity." He married Zayd, his freed slave, to Zaynab of Quraysh nobility before the prohibition of adopted son marriage clarified ties. Status is taqwa, not lineage.

Muhsanatin ghayra musafihatin wa la muttakhidhati akhdan — as chaste, not fornicators, not taking secret lovers. The conditions placed on slave women's marriage are identical to those for free women — chastity is non-negotiable for both. Fa-idha uhsinna fa-in atayna bi-fahishatin fa-alayhinna nisfu ma ala al-muhsanati min al-adhab — when they are fortified by marriage, if they commit immorality their fixed punishment is half that of free women. Half makes clear this fixed punishment refers to lashes, which can be halved, not to stoning which cannot; mercy is already in the calculation, and also a reason many scholars said this verse shows stoning is not for this category.

Dhalika liman khashiya al-anata minkum — this concession is for one who fears anat — hardship, the weight that bends into zina if left without lawful outlet. Wa an tasbiru khayrun lakum — but patience is better for you — to wait, work, and marry free believing women when able, rather than enter a family with added social complexities and the consequence that a child follows the mother's status. Wallahu Ghafur Rahim — Forgiving of past falls, Merciful in opening a lawful path when patience breaks. The whole trend from 4:23's "seek with your wealth in chastity" to this verse is one direction: move every intimate act inside a witnessed, dowered marriage, and move every owned person toward freedom, as expiations will soon use freeing as worship.

=25=

"Allah wants to make things clear to you, guide you to the ways of those before you, and turn to you in mercy. And Allah is Knowing, Wise."

Yuridu Allahu li-yubayyina lakum — Allah wants to make clear — to clarify these mahram lines, dowries, and boundaries that were mixed in Jahiliyyah. Bayan — clarification — is mercy; after 2:242 and after the inheritance hudud, Allah repeats bayan so confusion leaves. Wa yahdiyakum sunana alladhina min qablikum — and guide you to the ways of those before you — the righteous ways of Ibrahim, upright, and of the family of Imran, and of those who guarded prayer and wombs.

Wa yatuba alaykum — and turn to you. Taba alaykum means to return with mercy, to accept return. Wallahu Alimun Hakim — Knowing what needs clarity, Wise in how much to forbid and permit. After detailed prohibitions, the divine will is not to burden but to clear the fog and to forgive.

This verse is the hinge between law and heart. The same pattern appears in 2:185 "Allah wants ease not hardship," and in 4:28 "Allah wants to lighten." The same three verbs — clarify, guide, turn — will be repeated with emphasis in the next verses. Allah's want leads; human want can follow or deviate.

=26=

"Allah wants to turn to you in mercy, but those who follow desires want you to deviate a great deviation."

Wallahu yuridu an yatuba alaykum wa yuridu alladhina yattabi'una ash-shahawati an tamilu maylan azima — Allah wants tawbah for you; followers of shahawat want you to incline a great incline. Two wants confront each other. The Prophet recited 2:268 "Satan threatens you with poverty and orders immorality, while Allah promises forgiveness," and this is its parallel in marriage law. The shahawat followers of Madinah — some Jews who changed rulings, some hypocrites who profited from zina, any market that sells desire — wanted adultery branded as love and chastity mocked as backwardness. Maylan azima — a great deviation — not a small slip but a fork that sends lineage, inheritance, and trust into woods.

The verse after the bayyan — clarification — shows that confusion is not the only enemy; seduction is. For the seeker of حصن, Allah wants tausya and ease (next verse). For the seeker of deviation, permissiveness is never enough; he wants you to incline with him, to normalise. Modern media that makes zina entertainment and lawful marriage seem a prison is precisely an yuridu an tamilu. To resist, do not argue desire with desire; argue it with Allah's will to turn to you. Tawbah is the counter-pull.

=27=

"Allah wants to lighten your burden, for humankind was created weak."

Yuridu Allahu an yukhaffifa ankum — Allah wants to lighten — and He has. Permission to marry free or, if unable, believing slave women with rights; permission to adjust mahr by mutual consent after the faridah; capping dowry at ability not pride; allowing tayammum for the ill and traveller (4:43); shortening prayer in fear (4:101). The pattern after detailed law is always takhfif.

Wa khuliqa al-insanu da'ifa — humankind was created weak — da'if against shahawat that 4:26 named, weak in need of companionship that marriage fulfils, weak in guarding gaze that these laws guard. Weakness is not insult; it is design to seek strength through taqwa, patience, and halal outlets. The Prophet said, "The world is sweet and green, and Allah has made you stewards; so beware the world and beware women," not from contempt but from knowledge of weakness, and he said to young men, "O youth, whoever can afford marriage, let him marry, for it lowers the gaze and guards chastity; whoever cannot, let him fast, for it is a shield."

Recognising da'fa is the beginning of worship. Arrogant denial of weakness breaks limits; honest weakness asks for lightening and is given it.

=28=

"O believers, do not consume your wealth among yourselves unjustly, except by trade based on mutual consent. And do not kill yourselves. Indeed, Allah is ever Merciful to you."

Ya ayyuha alladhina amanu la ta'kulu amwalakum baynakum bil-batil — do not eat wealth among yourselves by falsehood. Bil-batil covers riba, gambling, fraud, bribes to judges to eat a portion of others' property sinfully (2:188), taking mahr by slander (4:20), eating orphan wealth by mixing (4:2), and extorting a wife to return dowry (4:19). The same verb ta'kulu — eat — links these chapters: to eat orphan wealth is to eat fire; to eat money falsely is to eat fire.

Illa an takuna tijaratan an taradin minkum — except trade based on mutual consent. Tijarah with taradin — pleasure of both souls, informed, uncoerced, without deception — is halal. Consent is not a signed page while the other is trapped; it is real willingness, the same tibun nafsan that made mahr waiver halal in 4:4 and mahr adjustment halal in 4:24. Deception invalidates trade even if a signature exists.

Wa la taqtulu anfusakum — do not kill yourselves. This includes suicide, self-destruction through wealth sins, and killing one another — for the ummah is one self from one soul in 4:1. When a man killed his brother for inheritance, he killed himself in the Quran's language. The Prophet forbade suicide and said whoever kills himself with something will be punished with it in Hell, and he forbade killing a Muslim except with right.

Inna Allaha kana bikum Rahima — Allah is ever Merciful to you — forbidding unjust eating and killing because He is Merciful. The verse sits after takhfif for a reason: lightening does not mean eating falsely; ease is not license. Halal trade with mutual pleasure is the light, compassionate market; bil-batil is the dark one.

=29=

"Whoever does that out of aggression and injustice, We will burn him in Fire. That is easy for Allah."

Wa man yaf'al dhalika udwanan wa zulman fa-sawfa nuslihi naran — whoever does that eating of wealth falsely or killing as aggression and injustice — udwan and zulm — We will burn him in Fire. Udwan is overstepping, zulm is putting wrong where right belongs, the same pair that appears for retaining wives to harm in 2:231. This is not a slip of miscalculation; it is knowing aggression.

Wa kana dhalika alalLahi yasira — that is easy for Allah. Yasira — easy, light — for Him, devastating for man. The same easy that will be said for replacing a people or punishing a tyrant. The eater of orphan, the taker of mahr by slander, the divorce extorter — all fall under udwan wa zulm — and will find ease for Allah is not ease for them.

This warning stands exactly where a reader after hearing "Allah wants to lighten" might think light equals lax. Lightness of law does not lighten consequence.

=30=

"If you avoid the major sins you have been forbidden, We will absolve your small sins and admit you to a noble entrance."

In tajtanibu kaba'ira ma tunhawna anhu nukaffir ankum sayyi'atikum wa nudkhilakum mudkhalan karima — if you avoid the major sins — kaba'ir — We will expiate your small sins — sayyi'at — and admit you to a noble entrance — mudkhal karim, an honoured gate to Paradise. The doorway is not merely Paradise but a noble entrance, honourable, welcoming — opposite the humiliating punishment of crossing hudud in 4:14.

What are kaba'ir? Scholars trace them to sins threatened with hadd, curse, war from Allah, or Fire — associating partners, murder, riba, eating orphan wealth, slandering chaste women, disobeying parents, fleeing the battlefield, sorcery, zina, drinking, stealing, false oath. The Prophet said the five prayers, Friday to Friday, Ramadan to Ramadan expiate what is between, so long as major sins are avoided. So this verse offers a merciful arithmetic: major sins avoided, minors are erased by wudu, prayer, fasting, charity, and small kindnesses. But "avoid major" does not license minors; persistence turns minor into major, and contempt turns small into big.

After the Fire threat for aggressive eating of wealth, here is Rahma again. The same pattern as 4:6-7: after precise law comes ease, after warning comes hope. The path to noble entrance runs through avoidance, not perfection. Leave the great crimes that destroy wombs and wealth, and Allah will be generous with the rest.

=31=

"Do not crave what Allah has favoured some of you over others. For men there is a share of what they earn, and for women a share of what they earn. And ask Allah of His bounty. Indeed, Allah is Knowing of all things."

Wa la tatamannaw ma faddala Allahu bihi ba'dakum ala ba'd — do not wishfully crave what Allah favoured some over others. Tamanaw here is envy that wishes another's blessing removed or transferred, not admiration that asks for similar good. After inheritance and dower fractions where a sister may see a brother's share double hers, and a man may see a woman's dower as a burden, envy can whisper.

Lir-rijali nasibun mimma iktasabu wa lin-nisa nasibun mimma iktasabna — for men a share of what they earned and for women a share of what they earned. Nasib and iktasabu tie to rizq and taqsir: each person's earning — of wealth, worship, character — is recorded and rewarded without transfer. Was'alu Allaha min fadlih — ask Allah from His bounty. Fadl is the word of rivers that never end — Owner of great bounty in 3:74 — ask Him, not gaze at another's plate. The Prophet taught jealous women companions to ask, not to despise, and taught men not to belittle women's worship and charity; Umm Salamah asked about jihad and inheritance after hearing these verses, and revelation answered with esteem for both.

Inna Allaha kana bi-kulli shay'in Alima — Knowing why favour was divided, who earned what, who asked seeking His bounty and who envied. Do not wish for another's inheritance right to be yours; ask Allah to bless your own rizq, your own marriage, your own children, your own rushd. The same door that forgives minors when majors are avoided is the door to ask for increase.

=32=

"For everyone We have appointed heirs to what parents and relatives leave, and those whom your oaths have bound — give them their share. Indeed, Allah is Witness over all things."

Wa li-kullin ja'alna mawaliya mimma taraka al-walidani wal-aqrabun — for everyone We made mawaliya — heirs, protectors — to what parents and relatives leave. The mawaliya are not elders who decide if they feel like giving; Allah made them. The believer distributes where Allah made heirs, not where sentiment sways.

Walladhina aqadat aymanukum — those whom your oaths bound — refers to the early oath-brotherhood between Muhajirun and Ansar and to any solemn pact of alliance who inherited at the opening of Madinah, before inheritance was restricted to blood kin and spouses, and later to the bonds of patronage that remain in wala' of freeing slaves. Fa-atuhum nasibahum — give them their share — what was solemnly promised. Even after abrogation of inheritance for non-kin, promises of help, protection, and gifts up to one-third remain, and oaths must be kept as in 5:1 "fulfil contracts." The Prophet said, "There is no alliance in Islam except what was in Jahiliyyah; Islam adds strength to it," meaning oaths to protect the oppressed remain.

Inna Allaha kana ala kulli shay'in Shahida — Witness over all — Shahid watches the ledger, hears the oath, sees if a manager erased a pledge. The verse bridges private envy (4:32) and household authority (4:34-35): if Allah is Shahid over shares, He is Shahid over how a husband manages wealth and how a family arbitrates.

=33=

"Men are supporters of women with what Allah has favoured some over others and with what they spend from wealth. Righteous women are devout and guardians of what is unseen as Allah has guarded. As for those from whom you fear defiance, advise them, forsake them in beds, and strike lightly. If they obey, seek no way against them. Indeed, Allah is Most High, Great."

Ar-rijalu qawwamuna ala an-nisa — men are qawwamun over women — not qahirun, not owners. Qawwam is the maintainer who stands, supports, protects, like qiyam wealth that holds up a home. Bima faddala Allahu ba'dahum ala ba'd — by the favour Allah gave some over others — physical strength, stamina for defence and labour — wa bima anfaqu min amwalihim — and by what they spend. Qiwamah is therefore responsibility, not superiority: standing in prayer, standing at the door against danger, spending from what Allah gave. Remove spending and protection and qiwamah is claimed without its pillar.

Fas-salihatu qanitatun hafizatun lil-ghayb bima hafiza Allah — righteous women are qanitah — devoutly obedient to Allah — and hafizat lil-ghayb — guardians of what is unseen, chastity and household and wealth when husband is absent — bima hafiza Allah — as Allah has guarded, because Allah guarded them and they guard in return. The ideal is mutual guardianship: he guards with qiyam, she guards with hifz.

Wallati takhafuna nushuzahunna — those from whom you fear nushuz — rebellion, arrogant defiance that breaks the household trust, not a single disagreement or a disliked meal. Fa'izuhunna — advise them, speak, remind of Allah and of the mithaq ghaliz of 4:21, privately with mercy. Wahjuruhunna fil-madaji' — and forsake them in beds — a silent rebuke that removes nearness without removing provision or expelling from the home, as the Sunnah clarifies: do not leave the house for this, forsake within the bed. Wadribuhunna — then strike lightly — wadribu. The word has frightened many because it is quoted without the Prophet's own tafsir. He said it is striking with a siwak, a tooth-stick, not harming, not striking the face, not leaving a mark, after the first two steps have failed and nushuz persists as harm to the family. Scholars say it is symbolic, not punitive, a final physical sign that the matter is serious before families intervene. And the Prophet never struck a woman, never struck a servant, and said, "The best of you will not do that." Many companions and jurists taught it is permissible to leave entirely, and that far better to follow the prophetic example of not striking at all.

Fa-in ata'nakum fa-la tabghu alayhinna sabila — if they obey, seek no way against them — no revenge, no dragging of the past, no "way" of harm after peace. Inna Allaha kana Aliyyan Kabira — Allah is Most High, Great — reminder to any husband who feels high over his wife that Allah is higher over him. If he abuses height, the Higher will reckon. The verse closes with the same names that will open arbitration: Knowing, Wise, and here High, Great.

=34=

"If you fear a breach between the two, send an arbitrator from his family and an arbitrator from her family. If they both desire reconciliation, Allah will bring agreement between them. Indeed, Allah is Knowing, Aware."

Wa in khiftum shiqaqa baynihima fab'athu hakaman min ahlihi wa hakaman min ahliha — if you fear shiqaq — a breach, a tear — between the couple, send a hakam from his family and a hakam from hers. Shiqaq is the same word for schism in the community (3:105). Marriage is an ummah of two; its schism needs wisdom, not gossip.

The hakams are from family because they know history, secrets, and wounds, and their love is likelier to want repair. If no family, then trusted righteous mediators. In yurida islahan yuwaffiq Allahu baynahuma — if they both desire reconciliation — the two hakams, or the couple — Allah will grant tawfiq — agreement, success, alignment. Tawfiq is from Allah alone; the hakams are means, but hearts are in His hand. This is why du'a accompanies mediation; His tawfiq can soften in minutes what words could not soften in years.

Inna Allaha kana Aliman Khabira — Knowing of sincere desire for repair versus desire to win, Aware of what each heart conceals. The Prophet applied this when family mediation was needed, and he taught "reconciliation is best" in 4:128. For today, bring arbitrators who want islah, not victory for their side; who keep secrets, not broadcast; who know the Quran's limits of talaq and khul' and maintenance, not only emotion. And begin early: at fear of shiqaq, not after the house burned.

=35=

"Worship Allah and associate nothing with Him. Be good to parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbour, the distant neighbour, the close companion, the traveller, and those in your possession. Indeed, Allah does not love the arrogant, boastful."

Wa'budu Allaha wa la tushriku bihi shay'an — worship Allah alone — tawhid after family law, because family without tawhid becomes idol of blood, and tawhid without family kindness becomes cold slogan. Then the Quran draws a map of goodness radiating outward: wa bil-walidayni ihsanan — parents with ihsan, the highest earthly ihsan, even the sinful parents receive worldly kindness — wa bidhi al-qurba — near relatives, the rahm of 4:1 — wal-yatama — orphans of 4:2-10 — wal-masakin — needy whose faces appear at divisions in 4:8 — wal-jari dhi al-qurba — near neighbour — wal-jari al-junub — distant neighbour — was-sahibi bil-janb — the companion at side — spouse, friend, colleague at travel or work — wabni as-sabil — traveller cut from road — wa ma malakat aymanukum — those in your possession, servants, workers.

Ten circles of ihsan plus tawhid at the centre, a social universe held by worship. The Prophet said, "Jibril kept advising me about neighbours until I thought he would make them heirs," and "The best friend is best to his companion." He also said the merciful are shown mercy by the Most Merciful; be merciful to those on earth.

Inna Allaha la yuhibbu man kana mukhtalan fakhura — Allah does not love the arrogant, boastful — mukhtal fakhur — who struts and prides himself on lineage or wealth, looking down on orphans, women, neighbours, workers. Arrogance severs exactly the ten circles just commanded. If worship is true, it humbles; if kindness is true, boasting dies. The surah that defended women's shares and orphan wealth ends the ethic with its opposite sin: stinginess and show, which the next verses will expose.

=36=

"Those who are stingy, enjoin people to be stingy, and conceal what Allah has given them of His bounty — We have prepared for disbelievers a humiliating punishment."

Alladhina yabkhaluna wa ya'muruna an-nasa bil-bukhli wa yaktumuna ma atahumu Allahu min fadlih — they are stingy — yabkhaluna — not only in money but in knowledge, status, and mercy — and they order people to be stingy, making miserliness a culture — "do not give that daughter, do not spend that mahr, do not help that orphan" — and they conceal what Allah gave of bounty — yaktumuna — hiding knowledge, hiding the description of the Prophet that was in Torah, hiding wealth to avoid zakah and mahr.

Wa a'tadna lil-kafirina adhaban muhina — We prepared for disbelievers a humiliating punishment — muhin, the same humiliation for transgressing hudud in 4:14 and for hoarding in 58:10. Bukhl is not a private flaw; its family is enclosure of truth and merit. The same people who said "there is no blame on us concerning the unlearned" in 3:75 will not return a dinar unless you stand over them; they conceal the bounty of guidance to keep followers.

For today's household, bukhl appears as a husband who is qawwam in name but does not spend, a brother who knows his sister's share but pretends he forgets, a teacher who hides a more deserving student's right to save his own child's place. Every concealment of a true due for fear of spending is bukhl plus kitman. The cure is the reverse: spend, enjoin spending, and disclose bounty with gratitude, for 93:11 says "as for the bounty of your Lord, speak of it."

=37=

"And those who spend their wealth to show people and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day — whoever has Satan as a companion, evil is that companion."

Walladhina yunfiquna amwalahum ri'a an-nasi wa la yu'minuna billahi wa la bil-yawmi al-akhir — those who spend to show — riya — and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day. If bukhl was one ditch, riya is the other. One withholds; the other gives to be seen, his camera is his god. Spending without belief in meeting Allah is the same as worship without Raqib: form without root. The Quran already in 2:264 said such spending is like dust on a smooth rock, rain leaves it bare — nothing remains.

Wa man yakun ash-Shaytanu lahu qarinan fa-sa'a qarin — whoever has Satan as close companion — qarin — evil is that companion. Satan accompanied him in bukhl by threatening poverty in 2:268, and now accompanies him in riya by promising status. The companion is qarin as in 41:25 and 43:36, inseparable, whispering that withholding protects wealth and showing protects reputation. Both are single lies: wealth is with Allah who multiplies an atom (4:40), and honour is with Allah entirely (4:139).

Placed between bukhl (4:37) and "what harm if they believed and spent from what Allah provided" (4:39), this verse frames two failed spending models. The believer spends from what He provided — mimma razaqahumu Allah — seeking His countenance, with ikhlas that resists both silence and show. The Prophet said, "Charity does not decrease wealth," and "Allah loves modesty and concealment," and also taught public spending when it models good. The line is intention, not privacy alone, and Satan is the examiner of that line until it blurs.

=38=

"What harm would be on them if they believed in Allah and the Last Day and spent from what Allah provided them? Allah is ever Knowing of them."

Wa ma dha alayhim law amanu billahi wal-yawmi al-akhir wa anfaqu mimma razaqahum Allah — what harm would be on them if they believed and spent from what Allah provided? Ma dha alayhim — what harm — is gentle rhetoric: no harm at all, only benefit. Belief in Allah and Last Day would have turned thrift into trust and display into sincerity. Spending mimma razaqahum — from what He provided, not from "their" wealth — reframes ownership after Raqib and Hasib. The rizq is loaned.

Wa kan Allahu bihim Alima — Allah is Knowing of them — the stingy hider, the showy spender, and the sincere giver who gives hani'an mari'a. Alim has closed many verses in this cluster: Alim Hakim, Alim Khabir. He knows who spends while disbelieving and who withholds while fearing poverty. What harm? None. But their harm is that Satan is qarin, and following his counsel they deviate maylan azima.

The verse invites rather than only threatens. After humiliating punishment for disbelievers who deceive and walls of prohibitions, Allah asks this soft question to the same hearts: what harm if you believed and spent? The answer is the Gardened reward that comes next: no atom wronged, good multiplied.

=39=

"Indeed, Allah does not wrong even the weight of an atom. If it is a good deed, He multiplies it and gives from Himself a great reward."

Inna Allaha la yazlimu mithqala dharratin — Allah does not wrong even the weight of a dharrah — an atom, a speck, the smallest ant. In a surah of faqir shares where Heirs argue over half a dirham, this is the accounting promise that cools every calculation. No heir wronged by a speck, no mahr withheld by a grain unnoticed, no kind word at division unrecorded.

Wa in taku hasanatan yuda'ifha — if it is a good — even one hasanah — He multiplies it. Dharrah of good becomes mountains because the Multiplier is Al-Karim. The Prophet said, "Whoever intends a good deed and does it, Allah multiplies it ten to seven hundred and more." And 14:7 says gratitude increases. Wa yu'ti min ladunhu ajran azima — and gives from His presence a great reward — ajr azim — the same great reward promised after injury in 3:172 and for holding fast after hypocrisy in 4:146. Ladunhu — from His near presence — hints that reward is not ledger alone but closeness.

This verse answers both spenders of 4:37-38: the miser who feared loss loses nothing by giving except gain multiplied; the show-off who sought people's eyes finds an eye that sees smaller than atoms and multiplies sincerely. For today's inheritance dispute where someone says "it's just a little, let it go," Allah says He does not wrong even that little, and He multiplies even that little of obedience. So do not let it go, and do not take it without right.

=40=

"How will it be when We bring from every nation a witness and bring you as a witness over these?"

Fa-kayfa idha ji'na min kulli ummatin bi-shahidin wa ji'na bika ala ha'ula'i shahida — how will it be when We bring from every nation a witness — its prophet — and bring you, Muhammad, as witness over these — your people, and over all witnesses. Kayfa — how — leaves the heart to imagine the terror and hope together. The verse after atom justice places the courtroom: witnesses testify speck by speck.

The Prophet wept when Abdullah ibn Mas'ud recited this verse to him and he said, "Stop now," tears flowing, because he knew the weight of shahada. He will witness who obeyed hudud and who transgressed, who ate orphan wealth and who returned it, who cheated in mahr and who gave nihlah, who was qawwam with ihsan and who was mukhtal fakhur.

Earlier 2:143 described the ummah as witnesses over humanity with the Messenger as witness over them. Earlier 3:18 described Allah, angels, and people of knowledge bearing witness that there is no god except Him. Now all witness lines converge at the Day about which verse 1's Raqib warned and verse 6's Hasib promised to reckon.

For the living, the question kayfa is meant to be answered in preparation before it is answered in reality. How will it be? Stand for justice as witnesses for Allah even against yourselves as 4:135 will command, so that the Witness over you finds you among those who witnessed truthfully, not those who twisted tongues and hid what Allah gave of bounty.
