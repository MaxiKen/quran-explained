=141=

"Those who wait to see what happens to you. If victory comes from Allah, they say, 'Were we not with you?' If disbelievers have a share, they say, 'Did we not gain mastery over you and protect you from believers?' Allah will judge between you on Resurrection. Allah will never grant disbelievers a way over believers."

Alladhina yatarabbasuna bikum — those who wait — yatarabbasuna — from tarabbus — to lie in wait, to watch like a trader watches price, like a hunter watches prey, like a creditor watches a debtor's shop. Their waiting is not the waiting of the patient who waits for Allah's aid as in 2:155 and 57:12, but the waiting of the opportunist who waits to see where the wind of power blows so he can claim it was always his side, his tribe, his view. They wait not to help, not to pray, but to position. Fa-in kana lakum fathun min Allahi qalu a-lam nakun ma'akum — if a fath — opening, victory, relief, spoils, a job — from Allah — min Allah — not from your numbers or planning, but from Him who gives fath as in 48:1 "We have given you a clear opening" and who gave Badr — comes, they claim, "Were we not with you?" seeking share of maghanim with the same fleeting desire condemned in 4:94 when a companion killed a man who had said as-salamu alaykum to take his few sheep and his salam, and with the lagger in 4:72-73.

Wa in kana lil-kafirina nasibun qalu a-lam nastahwidh alaykum wa namna'kum min al-mu'minina — if disbelievers have a nasib — share, portion — of worldly success, a battle won, a market share, a narrative win, a court win, they say to them, "Did we not gain mastery — nastahwidh — over you and held you back from believers, protected you, restrained them, saved you?" Nastahwidh is to encompass, to have mastery, to surround, the same root as Muhit — encompassing — in 4:126 and 4:108. They market restraint as favour to both sides, taking credit with whoever has nasib that hour, as opportunists do in every community: with the masjid when donations flow and with its critics when controversy trends, with the employer and the union, with the husband and the wife, with the two tribes. Their mastery is linguistic, not real, and it vanishes when asked for proof.

This double claim is mudhabdhab logic incarnate before 4:143 names it explicitly: belonging neither to these believers nor those disbelievers, but to whoever has nasib that hour, to whoever can pay that hour, to whoever can protect that hour. The verse after gathering hypocrites and disbelievers together in Hell in 4:140 — jami' — shows their worldly double game ends with one Judge and one outcome, one gathering. Fa-Allahu yahkumu baynakum yawma al-qiyamati — Allah will judge between you on the Day of Resurrection — hukm where no hadith is hidden as in 4:42, no atom is wronged as in 4:40, no fatil or naqir is lost, no witness is absent as in 4:41, no yatarabbas is hidden. All claims of "were we not with you?" and "did we not protect you?" will be weighed against the record of yatarabbasuna in chests, of lag in 4:72, of spreading fear in 4:83, of night plotting in 4:81, and the scale will show emptiness.

Wa lan yaj'ala Allahu lil-kafirina ala al-mu'minina sabila — He will never grant disbelievers a way — sabila — of complete, erasing dominance that removes the believers as an ummah, that ends the call to prayer, that closes the mosques, that silences the Quran. A setback may strike, a musibah as in 4:62 for what hands earned, a wound as at Uhud when the archers left the hill, a siege as at the Trench, a loss of wealth, but not a sabil that ends the deen. History proves the promise: Badr was fath, then Uhud was wound, then the Trench was siege, then Hudaybiyyah was treaty that looked like loss, then Fath Makkah was fath again — yet the group that held fast to the Rope of Allah in 3:103 remained, and the mu'adh dhin continued, and the Quran continued. The lagger's imagined fawz azim of spoils in 4:73 and the waiter's claim to both sides both dissolve before a Judge who knows the yatarabbasuna in chests and who has promised no sabil for the erasers. For today the waiter is not an ancient desert tribe; he is the commentator who is with the community when it wins and with its detractors when it is tested, always with nasib, never with principle, and his mastery is as thin as his next post.

=142=

"Hypocrites seek to deceive Allah, but He will deceive them. When they stand for prayer they stand lazily, to be seen by people, remembering Allah only a little."

Inna al-munafiqina yukhad'i'una Allaha wa huwa khadi'uhum — they seek to deceive Allah — yukhad'i'una — with daylight obedience saying "we hear" as in 4:46, with night plotting ghayra alladhi taqul in 4:81, with swearing "we intended only good and reconciliation" in 4:62 after sudud, with a salam offered and a riya prayer and a charity to be seen. But He will deceive them — khadi'uhum — recompense that mirrors deed, as 2:15 "Allah mocks them and prolongs them in transgression wandering blindly," as 3:54 the best of planners turning plots, as 8:30. Allah's khida' is not lying — He is Al-Haqq, Al-Amin, above that, beyond that — it is letting deceit recoil upon its owner: their plot to be counted with victors leaves them counted with neither victor nor vanquished, their riya leaves them without reward as dust on a smooth rock in 2:264 — rain washes it bare — their waiting leaves them without fath, their yatarabbas leaves them without nasib.

Wa idha qamu ila as-salati qamu kusala — when they stand for prayer they stand lazily — kusala — heavy, dragging limbs, as if carrying a mountain rather than answering a beloved call, as if sleep had not left them, as if wudu was a chore, as if the call "come to success" was "come to burden." The root k-s-l is heaviness after indulgence, after late night scrolling, after heedlessness. Yura'una an-nasa — to be seen — yura'una — riya — the same riya of spending to be seen in 4:38 and saying obedience in 4:81 to be seen and praised and photographed. Wa la yadhkuruna Allaha illa qalila — remembering Allah only a little — qalila — little, few seconds, few phrases, perhaps "Allahu Akbar" without presence, perhaps a quick du'a without heart.

Contrast with ulu al-albab who remember Allah standing, sitting, lying on sides in 3:191 and with those commanded to remember Allah much after fear prayer in 4:103 "remember Allah standing, sitting, lying on your sides," and with those described in 33:41-42 "O you who believe, remember Allah much, and glorify Him morning and evening," and with 62:10. Little remembrance is hypocrisy's fingerprint; abundant, humble, hidden remembrance is belief's breath and sign, as Abu Hurayrah's much remembrance.

The Prophet said the worst prayer, the most hated prayer to Allah, is the hypocrite's: he sits watching the sun until it yellows between the horns of Satan, until it is between the two horns, then stands and pecks four quickly, bowing without pausing, prostrating without pausing, remembering Allah only a little, giving no khushu', no horizon, no du'a, no need, no love. He said, "That is the prayer of the hypocrite, that is the prayer of the hypocrite, that is the prayer of the hypocrite." That pecking is riya's prayer: quick to be seen, empty to be weighed, heavy to be loved.

Laziness here is not occasional fatigue that the Prophet himself sought refuge from — "O Allah, I seek refuge from laziness and cowardice, from miserliness and senility, from the trial of the grave" — but habit, character, that makes prayer a performance for eyes, not a meeting with the Ever-Living, the Sustainer, the Near. After yatarabbasuna who waited for nasib in 4:141, kusala explains why they waited: heavy prayer makes waiting in the shade easier than striving with wealth and selves in 4:95-96, makes sitting at home easier than hijrah in 4:97-100, makes riya easier than ikhlas, makes little remembrance easier than much. Cure is opposite: love of prayer that makes qasr in 4:101 and tayammum in 4:43 beloved takhfif, not burdens to bypass; love that makes the Prophet say "the coolness of my eyes is in prayer" and Bilal "give us rest with it, O Bilal," and Umar "we would be at ease if we prayed."

=143=

"Wavering between — belonging neither to these nor those. Whoever Allah lets stray, you will find no way for him."

Mudhabdhabina bayna dhalika la ila ha'ula'i wa la ila ha'ula'i — wavering — mudhabdhab — from dhabdhaba — to move, to dangle, to tremble like a feather in wind between two gusts, like a sheep between two flocks bleating to both, like a pendulum that never settles, like a trader who hedges every bet, belonging neither to these believers nor those disbelievers. The form mufa'lal indicates repetition and intensity. The image expands the oscillaters of 4:137 who believed then disbelieved then believed then increased, and the morning-evening tactic of 3:72 "believe in the morning, reject in the evening so they may return" to shake the new Muslims, and the two groups divided in 4:88 about hypocrites.

They claimed "were we not with you?" to believers when fath came as in 4:141 and "did we not protect you from believers?" to disbelievers when nasib came, belonging to whichever nasib appeared that hour, with no loyalty except to advantage, no wala' except to wind, no qiblah except to benefit. Their wavering is not confusion of a sincere seeker who asks and learns; it is strategy of a trader who never buys, never sells, only watches.

Wa man yudlili Allahu fa-lan tajida lahu sabila — whoever Allah lets stray — yudlili — after purchased error in 4:44, distorted words in 4:46, desired taghut in 4:60, opposed Messenger after guidance clear in 4:115, loved yatarabbas, performed kusala with riya, and chose to be mudhabdhab — you will find no way — sabila. The same no-way as in 4:88 for cast-back hypocrites arkasa, 4:115's burn and evil destination, and 4:137's no forgiveness nor way for those who increased, and 4:168's never guide to a path, and 4:88. Sabil here is path to guidance, to exit, to return, to tawbah, as in 4:88 and 4:115 and 4:68's straight path. The waverer who could have been firm with near repentance in 4:17 and found Ghafur with seeking in 4:110, who could have held fast in 4:146 with four steps, who could have remembered much, now has no sabil because he chose no side long enough to travel any road, and Allah leaves him to his choice.

The Prophet warned, "The example of the hypocrite is like a sheep wandering between two flocks; it goes to one flock and bleats, then to the other, not knowing which to follow, belonging to neither," and "The believer is not stung from the same hole twice," while the mudhabdhab seeks the same hole with new words. Another hadith: hearts are between two fingers of the Most Merciful, He turns them as He wills; the mudhabdhab asks to be left wavering and is left.

=144=

"O believers, do not take disbelievers as allies instead of believers. Do you wish to give Allah clear authority against yourselves?"

Ya ayyuha alladhina amanu la tattakhidhu al-kafirina awliya min duni al-mu'minina — O believers, do not take disbelievers as awliya — intimate allies, confidants, secret-keepers, protectors, patrons — instead of believers. Repeated for the fourth time after 3:28, 4:89 after wishing you equal in kufr, 4:139 after seeking izzah with them, here after mudhabdhab. Each repetition adds a new shade: 3:28 general warning for all, 4:89 after they wish you to be sawa' in kufr to erase distinction, 4:139 after they seek honour to show izzah is with Allah, here after they are mudhabdhab waiting for nasib to show wala' is trust. The warning intensifies as the disease is detailed.

Aturiduna an taj'alu lillahi alaykum sultanan mubina — do you wish to give Allah a clear sultan — authority, proof, argument, evidence, warrant — against yourselves? Sultan mubin is manifest, plain, bright evidence on the Day when witnesses are brought in 4:41, when no hadith is hidden in 4:42, when every atom is weighed in 4:40 and fatil in 4:49 and naqir in 4:53 and qitmir, and when every intercession share is recorded by Muqit in 4:85 and Hasib in 4:86 and Khabir in 4:135. Sultan mubin will be read aloud when scrolls are spread: "You took him as wali though he mocked signs in 4:140."

Taking awliya from those who mock signs in 4:140, who wish you equal in kufr in 4:89, who are open enemies in 4:45 and 4:101, who are mudhabdhab waiting for nasib, who pray kusala for eyes, who vowed to take a share in 4:118 — is not neutrality, not professionalism, not tolerance, not "coexistence" or "networking"; it is handing the Recorder a signed confession that you preferred their counsel to the believers', their fund to the believers' charity, their court to the believers' qist, their platform to the believers' masjid. After mudhabdhabin, the command is to stop wavering and choose, to stand for qist as in 4:135 even against yourselves, parents, kin, even when poor or rich. The believer's wala' is not hate for neighbours — 4:36 commands ihsan to near neighbour and far neighbour even if disbeliever, and to the companion at side, and to the traveller — but wala' is deepest trust, secret, military confidence, and love of deen that is given to those who remember Allah much and stand firm and hold fast. Give that to believers, and you give Allah no sultan of betrayal; give it to the mocker and you give Him sultan mubin that will be read when the scrolls are spread and the witnesses stand and the One who gathers gathers.

=145=

"Hypocrites will be in the lowest depths of the Fire, you will find no helper for them —"

Inna al-munafiqina fid-darki al-asfali min an-nar — hypocrites will be in the lowest depth — dark al-asfal — bottom layer, lowest pit, below the open disbeliever, below Pharaoh who said "I am your lord most high," below Abu Jahl who mocked, below the slanderers of Maryam, because hypocrisy combines kufr and deceit and internal blocking, as 4:145 is the only verse in the Quran that names the lowest depth, the darkest dark, the most confining. Open enemy harms from outside, like an army at the wall; waverer poisons trust from inside, like poisoned water within the wall, and poisoned water harms more than a siege because it is trusted, because it is inside, because it is not filtered.

The Prophet said the Fire has levels and degrees, and hypocrites are in its lowest, where heat sees what upper layers cannot, and where the hypocrite's skin that was renewed in 4:56 for taste now burns where no riya audience sees, where kusala is not seen, where yatarabbas is not seen, where mudhabdhab is not seen. Wa lan tajida lahum nasira — you will find no helper — nasira — helper, protector, rescuer, intercessor without permission, as in 4:52 "who Allah curses finds no helper," 4:88-91 no way, 4:123 no guardian nor helper besides Allah, 4:173 no guardian nor helper for the arrogant who disdained, 5:31. Hypocrites sought izzah with disbelievers in 4:139 and helpers in disbelievers in 4:89 and claimed mastery protecting disbelievers in 4:141; at the depth they find no nasir where help is most needed, where intercession without permission fails, where riya's eyes are gone, where yatarabbas's fath is gone. Even the shafa'ah they mocked as "we are with you" is absent, because shafa'ah is for the sincere.

The placement after the prohibition of taking disbelievers as awliya teaches that the awliya they sought will not descend to the lowest depth to pull them out; the awliya they refused — believers, the praying, the remembering — are the only ones whose shafa'ah with permission could have helped, and they are now ma'a al-mu'minina in 4:146.

=146=

"Except those who repent, correct, hold fast to Allah, and purify their religion for Allah — those will be with believers. Allah will give believers a great reward."

Illa alladhina tabu wa aslahu wa'tasamu billahi wa akhlasu dinahum lillah — except those who repent — tabu — turning back, as in 4:16-17 near repentance soon is upon Allah and 4:64 coming and seeking, 4:110 finding, 4:147 shukr — correct — aslahu — as in 4:16 "if they repent and correct, leave them alone" and 4:58's excellent admonition and 4:114's islah between people and 4:128's sulh khayrun, and as Yusuf corrected after wrong, as the repentant hypocrite corrects his riya prayer to khushu', his yatarabbas to fanfiru, his kusala to nashit — hold fast — i'tasamu — to Allah — as 3:103 "hold fast to the rope of Allah all together" and 4:175 hold fast to Him after burhan and nur, and 4:69's ma'a with the blessed — and purify religion — akhlasu dinahum — for Allah — sincere, not riya as in 4:142 yura'una, not yatarabbas, not kusala, not mudhabdhab, not wala' to taghut, not little remembrance.

Four steps mirror the four diseases named in this passage: repentance for lagging and waiting and yatarabbas and kusala and mudhabdhab; correction for night plotting in 4:81 and for throwing sin on the innocent in 4:112 and for slander and for distorted ra'ina; holding fast for wavering mudhabdhab and for two groups divided in 4:88 and for fear; purification for riya and for taking awliya to seek izzah in 4:139,144 and for remembering little and for intending ghurur. Four diseases, four cures, in order, as a physician prescribes.

Fa-ula'ika ma'a al-mu'minina — those will be with believers — ma'a — the same ma'a as 4:69 "with those Allah blessed" among prophets, siddiqin, martyrs, salihin, and as 4:146's with. From lowest dark to ma'a of the blessed is the distance of four verbs done sincerely, not of lineage, not of years of waiting, not of claiming. Wa sawfa yu'ti Allahu al-mu'minina ajran azima — Allah will give believers a great reward — ajran azima — the same great reward as for avoiding majors in 4:31, for good najwa charity in 4:114, for obedience in 4:67, for not distinguishing messengers in 4:152, for hijrah. Exception after lowest depth shows the door is not sealed for the living; only death on hypocrisy with gargle seals as in 4:18 "now I repent" at death is not accepted, and as in 4:137 increased disbelief is not forgiven, and as in 4:115. Until then, the path is tabu, aslahu, i'tasam, akhlasa — four, sincere, consecutive.

=147=

"What would Allah do with your punishment if you are grateful and believe? Allah is ever Appreciative, Knowing."

Ma yaf'alu Allahu bi-adhabikum in shakartum wa amantum — what would Allah do with punishing you if you are grateful and believe? The question is gentle after severity of lowest depth and dark asfal: punishment is not need or desire or habit or revenge for Him; He is Ghani Hamid in 4:131, Free of need, Praiseworthy, and Muhit in 4:126, and Giver of great bounty in 4:113, and Owner of heavens. Why would He punish those He loves to thank and to believe? Shakartum — gratitude — is to use blessing as He prescribed: return trusts in 4:58 to owners, give dowry nihlah in 4:4, spend not as riya in 4:38-39 but as nasib hasanah, remember much not little in 4:142-103, stand for qist even against yourselves in 4:135, verify news before spreading in 4:83, forgive as in 4:149 when able, hold fast as in 4:146. Amantum — belief — makes it firm, as 4:136 commands increase in belief and as 4:69's obedience with prophets, as 4:65's iman that accepts judgment without haraj.

The pair shukr and iman counters the quartet yatarabbas, khida', kusala, mudhabdhab. Wa kan Allahu Shakiran Alima — Ever Appreciative — Shakir — who grows thanks, who appreciates and multiplies a dharrah in 4:40 into ajr azim, who appreciates even a fatil in 4:49 and naqir in 4:53 and qitmir and a kind word after division in 4:8, who appreciates the traveler who prayed shortened and the fearful who prayed in shifts, and Alim who knows who truly thanked with kusala prayer versus sincere, who gave hani'an versus riya, who stood for qist versus twisted with hawa, who held fast versus wavered.

This answers the amaniyy of 4:123 who thought Allah punishes arbitrarily or needs pain; He punishes after amaniyy is chosen over shukr and iman, not because He needs pain or delights in it. The verse is hope that also guards: shukr and iman are not slogans to be claimed; they are the counter-life to yatarabbas, khida', kusala, and mudhabdhab, and the One who rewards them is Shakir, who never forgets, and Alim, who never mistakes, so do not claim.

=148=

"Allah does not like the public mention of evil except by one wronged. Allah is Hearing, Knowing."

La yuhibbu Allahu al-jahra bis-su'i min al-qawli illa man zulim — Allah does not like loud, public mention — jahra — of evil — su' — foul talk, slander, obscenity, broadcasting sin, insult, betrayal, even if true, even if it happened, even if you are angry — except by one wronged — mazlum — who may raise voice to seek justice and to repel zulm. The mazlum in this surah is many: the Jew in the armour theft who was wronged when sin was thrown upon him in 4:112, the widow extorted for her mahr in 4:19 who was told "return it or stay suspended," the orphan whose wealth was eaten in 4:10, the slandered Maryam in 4:156, the wife left mu'allaqah in 4:129, the weak mustad'af whose angels questioned, the believer whose share was hidden.

The exception is not permission to invent with buhtan or to exaggerate or to broadcast to followers for sympathy or to dox; it is permission to say to a judge, to an authority, to the one who can restore, "I was wronged, restore my right, judge between us," with truth, with evidence, with limit, without ghība beyond need. The Prophet said, "The supplication of the mazlum has no veil between it and Allah, even if he is a disbeliever," and he said, "Beware the supplication of the mazlum, for it rises beyond clouds."

Wa kan Allahu Sami'an Alima — Ever Hearing what is said loudly — jahra — Knowing whether speaker is mazlum or manipulator claiming mazlum status while practising sudud in 4:61 and yatarabbas in 4:141 and mudhabdhab. After shukr and iman, speech is guarded: do not love public evil even if you are angry, even if it gets likes, but support the wronged's voice when it seeks qist with evidence. The surah that taught to say penetrating words privately in 4:63 — qawlan balighan fi anfusihim — now teaches when to raise publicly: only as mazlum seeking justice, not as broadcaster of shame for followers, not as influencer chasing reach with su', and the One who hears is Sami'.

=149=

"If you disclose a good or conceal it or pardon an evil, Allah is ever Pardoning, Powerful."

In tubdu khayran aw tukhfuhu aw ta'fu an su'in fa-inna Allaha kana Afuwwan Qadira — if you disclose a good — tubdu — openly, as sadaqah enjoined in najwa in 4:114 when publicising encourages others to give, as the Ansar who gave in public and the Prophet praised and as Uthman equipped an army in public — or conceal it — tukhfuhu — secretly, as giving to an orphan quietly so his dignity remains, as helping a debtor privately without shaming, as remembering Allah in the last third of night with no eyes, as fasting with no show — or pardon an evil — ta'fu an su' — as 3:134 "those who restrain anger and pardon people — Allah loves doers of good," as Yusuf pardoned his brothers when he was Aziz and Qadir and said "no blame today," as the Prophet pardoned at Fath when he could punish, as Sawdah pardoned her day, as Abu Bakr pardoned Mistah after slander.

Both disclosure and concealment are khayr when intention is mardat Allah in 4:114; the better is whichever protects sincerity and encourages others, and the Sharia leaves both open, as 2:271. Pardon is higher than either disclosure or concealment, because it requires power to punish and choice to release, as Yusuf. The Prophet said, "No one pardons without Allah increasing him in honour," and "Be merciful to those on earth, the Most Merciful will be merciful to you," and "The strong is not the wrestler, but the one who controls himself when angry."

Fa-inna Allaha kana Afuwwan Qadira — Ever Pardoning — Afuww effaces sin as if never written, more intense than Ghafur which covers, and Powerful — Qadir — the paradox that teaches imitation: He has power to punish but pardons; so pardon when you have power, as you love to be pardoned when you are weak and guilty. The Prophet, though Qadir after conquest of Makkah when all his enemies were in his hands, said "Go, you are freed," modeling Afuww when Qadir, not when forced. The surah of qasr and tayammum and sulh and shukr and the lowest depth now ends its ethics with the apex: disclose, conceal, but most beloved is to pardon when you are able, for that is shukr for power and iman in action, and Shakir Alim in 4:147 will appreciate it.

=150=

"Those who disbelieve in Allah and His messengers and wish to distinguish between Allah and His messengers, saying, 'We believe in some and disbelieve in some,' and wish to take a way in between —"

Inna alladhina yakfuruna billahi wa rusulihi wa yuriduna an yufarriqu bayna Allahi wa rusulihi — those who disbelieve in Allah and His messengers and wish to distinguish — yufarriqu — between Allah and His messengers — separating obedience to Allah from obedience to His messengers, as 4:60 wished to distinguish by taking taghut besides the Messenger for judgment, and 4:64 and 4:80 said obeying the Messenger is obeying Allah by His permission, and 4:59 said obey Allah and obey the Messenger. To say "I follow Allah's Book but not His Messenger's Sunnah, I follow Quran but not hadith" is tafriq, cutting.

Wa yaquluna nu'minu bi-ba'din wa nakfuru bi-ba'd — we believe in some and disbelieve in some — as Jews believed in Musa not in Isa and Muhammad, Christians in Musa and Isa not in Muhammad, mushriks in Allah as Creator not as Lawgiver, hypocrites in tongue not heart, moderns in prayer but not in inheritance shares, in Ramadan but not in riba, in Hajj but not in hijab and qist. Wa yuriduna an yattakhidhu bayna dhalika sabila — wish to take a way — sabila — between belief and disbelief that is neither — the same mudhabdhab sabil between, neither to these nor those, belonging to neither, as in 4:143. After pardon, the verse shows opposite: pardon unites, distinguishing messengers divides. The way between is not ijtihad with valid difference where scholars differ in branch; it is the wavering sabil of 4:143 and the other than believers' path in 4:115 and the sabil of those who say "we hear and disobey" in 4:46. Claiming "I follow Allah but not His messengers" is yufarriqu — distinction, cutting — that Allah names kufr, not diversity, not tolerance, not modernity.

=151=

"They are truly disbelievers. We have prepared for disbelievers a humiliating punishment."

Ula'ika hum al-kafiruna haqqa — they are truly disbelievers — haqqa — really, firmly, in reality, not metaphorically or as small error or as "people of the Book so they are close, so they are not disbelievers, so they are excused." Though they claim faith in some prophets, Allah names the distinguishing claim itself as kufr haqq, as 4:150's wish itself is kufr, not the content of which prophet they deny. The disbeliever who says "I believe in God but not in His messengers" is not halfway to faith; he is haqq disbeliever, as one who says "I believe in the law but not the judge" believes in no law, as one who says "I believe in health but not medicine."

Wa a'tadna lil-kafirina adhaban muhina — We prepared a humiliating punishment — muhina — humiliating, the same humiliating as for the violator of hudud in 4:14 who humiliated a sister by giving leftovers, for the hider of bounty in 4:37 who humiliated the needy, for those who wished you neglect weapons in 4:102, for the arrogant in 4:173 who disdained, because distinction humiliates the rank of messengers and seeks izzah elsewhere as in 4:139, and humiliation fits. The verse does not say "they are mistaken"; it says they are haqq disbelievers, and the prepared punishment is muhina, humiliating, because distinction was an act of humiliating the messengers and seeking honour elsewhere.

=152=

"But those who believe in Allah and His messengers and do not distinguish between any of them — to them We will give their rewards. Allah is Forgiving, Merciful."

Walladhina amanu billahi wa rusulihi wa lam yufarriqu bayna ahadin minhum — but those who believe in Allah and all His messengers and do not distinguish — lam yufarriqu — between any of them — not between Nuh and Ibrahim and Musa and Isa and Muhammad, not between Musa and Isa, not between Isa and Muhammad — as 2:285 "we make no distinction between any of His messengers," and 3:84 "we believe in Allah and what was sent down to Ibrahim, Ismail, Ishaq, Yaqub, the Tribes, and what was given to Musa, Isa, and the prophets," and 4:136 pillars of faith. Faith that distinguishes is not faith; faith that includes is faith, even if it includes with love.

Ula'ika sawfa nu'tihim ujurahum — to those We will soon give their rewards — ujura — wages — as 4:40's multiplication, 4:67's great wage from nearness, 4:114's great reward for good najwa, 4:122's Gardens, 4:146's great wage for repentant, 4:152's wages, 4:173's full wages plus increase. Sawfa indicates nearness and certainty, not distance. Wa kan Allahu Ghafuran Rahima — Forgiving of prior distinguishing before guidance became clear, before 4:59's referral was known, before knowledge of 4:113's teaching, before the Book was recited, Merciful in rewarding after, in increasing, in guiding, in holding. Contrast is sharp and intended: haqq disbelievers with muhin versus non-distinguishing believers with ujura and Ghafur Rahim. The surah of witnessing — witness in 4:41, no hidden hadith in 4:42, no atom wrong — now witnesses who distinguished and who united, and rewards accordingly. For today's believer who says "I believe in all messengers, I love Musa and Isa and Muhammad without distinction, I love Nuh and Ibrahim," this is his verse and his wage, and his love is his sabil.

=153=

"The People of the Book ask you to bring down upon them a writing from heaven. They asked Musa for greater than that — they said, 'Show us Allah plainly' — then a thunderbolt seized them for wrongdoing. Then they took the calf after clear proofs came to them, yet We pardoned that. We gave Musa a clear authority."

Yas'aluka ahlu al-kitabi an tunazzila alayhim kitaban min as-sama — they ask you to bring down upon them a writing from heaven — a bound book descending as one piece, as a scroll from the sky that they could touch and see, as Quraysh asked for a gold mountain and for angels to walk and for a book they could touch and for a ladder to heaven. Fa-qad sa'alu Musa akbara min dhalika fa-qalu arina Allaha jahratan — they asked Musa greater — show us Allah plainly — jahratan — openly, eye to eye, as in 2:55 "Show us Allah plainly, then thunderbolt seized you while you looked," and 4:153. The request was not for guidance but for control, for a sign on demand, for a god they could see and manage.

Fa-akhadhat-hum as-sa'iqatu bi-zulmihim — then a thunderbolt seized them for zulm — injustice of demand, not ignorance; they knew better, they had seen the sea split, yet they asked. Thumma ittakhadhu al-ijla min ba'di ma ja'at-hum al-bayyinat — then they took the calf for worship after clear proofs — after the sea split before their eyes, the cloud shaded them, manna and quail descended daily, the Mount raised, water from the rock. Proofs did not prevent shirk when desire remained, as today proofs do not prevent riba when desire for gain remains, nor prevent taghut when desire for power remains.

Fa-afawna an dhalika — We pardoned that — after they repented by killing themselves and seeking, as in 2:54 "kill yourselves, that is better for you with your Creator, so He turned to you," and 7:153, and 4:153. The pardon after shirk of the calf shows even great shirk has a door before death if turned from, as 4:17's soon and 4:110's finding and 4:147's shukr show, and after shirk, the door is tawbah. Wa atayna Musa sultanan mubina — We gave Musa a clear authority — sultan mubin — staff turned serpent, white hand shining, nine signs, parted sea, drowning of the army, and the Tablets with writing. The progression is contumacy: demand greater, then after greater proofs fall into greatest shirk, then after pardon receive clear authority. The demand for a paper book now repeats that obstinacy, not sincerity. After distinguishing messengers, this shows their demand is not search for truth but test of power, as Pharaoh tested with "bring a sign if you are truthful" then denied it.

=154=

"We raised the Mount over them for their covenant and said to them, 'Enter the gate bowing,' and said, 'Do not transgress the Sabbath,' and took from them a solemn covenant."

Wa rafa'na fawqahum at-tura bi-mithaqihim — We raised the Mount — Tur — over them, hung like shade, like a cloud, as in 2:63 "We raised the Mount above you, take what We gave you with strength" and 7:171 "We shook the Mount over them as if it were shade, and they thought it would fall upon them." For their covenant — to make its weight felt, to teach that covenant is not light, not a slogan, not a handshake, not a hashtag. Wa qulna lahum udkhulu al-baba sujjadan — enter the gate — of the holy village — Bayt al-Maqdis — bowing — sujjadan — humble, heads low, saying hittatun — "put sins down" — forgive us — as in 2:58. They entered crawling on rears, mocking, saying hintatun — wheat, grain.

Wa qulna lahum la ta'du fi as-sabt — do not transgress the Sabbath — keep Saturday as worship, not as trap for fish by laying nets Friday and collecting Sunday, as 2:65 and 7:163 detail. Wa akhadhna minhum mithaqan ghaliza — solemn covenant — mithaq ghaliz — the same firm covenant as marriage in 4:21 "they took from you a solemn covenant" and prophets in 33:7 "We took from the prophets their solemn covenant." Three commands under a raised Mount and a firm covenant: enter humbly, keep Sabbath, keep covenant. They will break all three, as the next verses catalogue breaking, disbelieving, killing prophets, and claiming hearts wrapped, and the break will be named.

=155=

"So for breaking their covenant, disbelieving Allah's signs, killing prophets unjustly, and saying, 'Our hearts are wrapped' — rather, Allah sealed them for disbelief, so they believe only a little —"

Fa-bima naqdihim mithaqahum — for breaking their covenant — mithaq ghaliz broken despite mountain over heads and gate and Sabbath, despite sultan mubin — wa kufrihim bi-ayati Allahi — and disbelieving His signs — after Tur and gate and Sabbath and sultan mubin, after manna and quail — wa qatlihim al-anbiya bi-ghayri haqqin — and killing prophets without right — Zakariya sawn, Yahya slaughtered, attempting Isa, and many unnamed prophets whose blood is still on that covenant and whose stories are remembered in hadith. Wa qawlihim qulubuna ghulf — and saying "our hearts are wrapped — ghulf — covered, sealed, unable to understand, excused, do not blame us" — as in 2:88. The excuse pretends inability; reality is unwillingness, as 41:5.

Bal taba'a Allahu alayha bi-kufrihim — rather, Allah sealed them — taba'a — printed, sealed, stamped — for disbelief, so they believe only a little — illa qalila — a remnant like Abdullah ibn Salam who heard Quran and wept, Mukhayriq who gave his orchards, the few who kept fitrah and who were rasikhuna in 4:162. The ledger: breaking covenant, disbelieving signs, killing prophets, claiming inability. Seal is recompense for persistent choice, not initial creation, as 4:88 arkasa and 2:7 sealed after refusal and 61:5 hearts deviated when they deviated. This after gift of sultan mubin shows seals follow persistent breaking, not a single slip, not a single shubhah. After humiliating punishment for distinguishing, the seal is its living form in this world, a heart that hears Quran and feels nothing, that sees the Mount and feels no awe, that hears the gate and does not bow.

=156=

"And for their disbelief and their saying about Maryam a tremendous slander —"

Wa bi-kufrihim wa qawlihim ala Maryama buhtanan azima — and for their disbelief and saying about Maryam — as-Siddiqa, purified, chosen above women of the worlds in 3:42, who guarded chastity in a mihrab and was given provision without account in 3:37 and who was told "be devout, prostrate and bow," whose son spoke in cradle — a tremendous slander — buhtanan azima — great fabricated horror, accusing chaste, devout Maryam of fahishah and calling Isa walad zina, illegitimate, son of fornication, product of sin. Buhtan is the same word for reclaiming a qintar by slander in 4:20 — buhtan — and for throwing sin on the innocent in 4:112 — buhtan — and for yatarabbas slander and for 33:58. Azim is great, as ithm azim in 4:48 and 4:112, great sin, great lie.

After killing prophets, they slander the mother of a prophet who was guarded; blood and honour crimes meet, as the worst of people combine both. The protection of women in 4:15 required four witnesses to protect chastity from accusation; they slandered the purest woman without one, with all the community watching, with a word that still wounds believing women today. The slander is not past history; every repeat of that old lie in a book, a post, a joke, a meme, is new buhtan azima, and the One who is Witness in 4:166 records it, and the One who is Hearer in 4:148 hears it.

=157=

"And for their saying, 'We killed the Messiah, Isa son of Maryam, messenger of Allah' — but they neither killed nor crucified him, but it was made to appear so to them. Those who differed about it are in doubt. They have no knowledge except following conjecture. They certainly did not kill him."

Wa qawlihim inna qatalna al-Masiha Isa ibn Maryama Rasula Allahi — and for boasting "we killed the Messiah, Isa son of Maryam, messenger of Allah" — trophy claim of killing a messenger whose title is Messiah and whose messengerhood they deny while boasting. The title Rasul Allah they utter in mockery; Allah records it verbatim as testimony against them that they knew what they claimed to kill, that he was messenger, not impostor, and their own words will testify. Wa ma qataluhu wa ma salabuhu — they neither killed — qataluhu — nor crucified — salabuhu — double negation of both methods, execution and display, killing and disgrace. Wa lakin shubbiha lahum — but it was made to appear — tashbih — so to them; another person was made to resemble him and was taken, as Allah has power to cast likeness and to raise and to make earth speak, faces turned, and to protect.

Wa inna alladhina ikhtalafu fihi la-fi shakkin minhu — those who differed about it are in doubt — shakk — not certainty, wavering as mudhabdhabin between, as Christians differ on who was on cross. Ma lahum bihi min ilmin illa ittiba'a az-zanni — they have no knowledge except following conjecture — zann — guess, supposition, as their predecessors guessed about riba being like trade and about Fire days and about jibt. Wa ma qataluhu yaqinan — they certainly did not kill him — yaqinan — ascertained, certain, with yaqin that crushes zann, with truth that crushes conjecture. The boast is refuted with certainty, the cross narrative corrected, conjecture exposed as zann. In Ali Imran 3:54-58 Allah said His plan — makr — and raise — rafa' — and purification — tathir; this repeats for Bani Isra'il's claim, with shubbiha lahum as His gentle confusion for the aggressors who thought they controlled the scene, and with yaqinan as the believer's certainty.

=158=

"Rather, Allah raised him to Himself. Allah is Mighty, Wise."

Bal rafa'ahu Allahu ilayhi — rather, Allah raised him — rafa' — elevation, as in 3:55 "I will take you and raise you to Me" and as breach between Musa's direct speech and Isa's raise shows varied honours for servants, neither implying divinity nor partnership, both showing that nearness to Allah is real. Wa kan Allahu Azizan Hakima — Mighty — Aziz cannot be compelled to abandon His messenger to a mob's boast, cannot be outplanned by shubbiha, cannot be outnumbered, cannot be killed, cannot be crucified — Wise — Hakim places raise where seen best, not every eye sees but the honest eye accepts, the doubter remains in shakk as in 4:157, the believer is increased and guided. After tremendous slander in 4:156, raise is honour of Maryam's son and vindication of his mother; after boast of killing in 4:157, raise is refutation that silences boast without debate, without cross, without need for argument. The Messiah who was wajih in this world and among the muqarrabun in 3:45 and whose mother was purified remains wajih by raise in dunya — saved from humiliation — and honoured in akhirah — intercessor and witness.

=159=

"There is none among the People of the Book but will believe in him before his death, and on Resurrection he will be a witness against them."

Wa in min Ahli al-Kitabi illa la-yu'minanna bihi qabla mawtihi — there is none among the People of the Book except will believe in him before his death — qabla mawtihi — before Isa's death after his descent at the end of time, or before each person's death when the veil lifts and the servant nature of Jesus becomes clear even to the denier when angels question and the soul sees. The strongest reading with mutawatir hadith is Isa's descent before the Hour when the remaining People of the Book will believe he is servant and messenger, not god nor liar, and the cross will be broken as the Prophet said he will break the cross, kill the swine, end war, abolish jizyah, and fill earth with justice and abundance until no one accepts charity, wealth overflows.

Wa yawma al-qiyamati yakunu alayhim shahida — on Resurrection he will be a witness — shahida — against them, as prophets witness over nations in 4:41 and as 5:117 "I said to them: worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord; when You took me You were Watcher over them." The witness once slandered and claimed killed becomes witness against slanderers and killers, as Yusuf's shirt witnessed for him, as Isa's cradle speech witnessed for Maryam. His testimony will be that he said "my Lord and your Lord" as in 3:51 and 5:117, not "worship me and my mother as two gods besides Allah" as in 5:116. The witness is not for him but on him: for, not by.

=160=

"For wrongdoing of those who were Jews We forbade good things that had been lawful to them, and for blocking many from Allah's path,"

Fa-bi-zulmin min alladhina hadu harramna alayhim tayyibatin uhillat lahum — for wrongdoing of those who were Jews We forbade good things that had been lawful — harramna — as discipline, not as original law. Tayyibat — good foods, pure foods, fats of cattle and sheep as in 6:146 "for their transgression We forbade fats," camel, birds with claws, Sabbath fish, and other tayyibat that were halal before for Ibrahim and Yaqub. Uhillat — had been lawful — originally halal; prohibition was punishment that made religion heavier as hearts hardened, as 2:57. Wa bi-saddihim an sabili Allahi kathiran — and for blocking many from Allah's path — sadd — the same sadd as sudud in 4:61, taghut seeking in 4:60, killing prophets, and this catalogue's blocking. After slander and false killing claim and shubbiha, blocking many fits: they hid the description of Muhammad in Torah, hid the stoning verse for adultery, hid halal and made haram, hid and made many stay away, as today some hide riba's prohibition and call it profit.

Diet law became heavier as hearts hardened, opposite of Muslim takhfif with tayammum in 4:43 and qasr in 4:101-102 and shortening and sulh. The lesson repeats: persistent wrongdoing makes religion heavier until tayammum seems strange and qasr seems liberal and hijab seems strict and riba seems normal; repentance and islah make it light until dust suffices, four witnesses protect, and qist stands, and tayyibat return.
