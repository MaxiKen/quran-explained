=160=

The surah now transitions from the theological discussion of Jesus to the practical consequences of the Jews' transgressions, specifically the dietary restrictions imposed as punishment. “We forbade the Jews certain foods that had been lawful to them for their wrongdoing.” This refers to the prohibitions mentioned in the Torah, such as the forbidding of certain fats, animals with claws, and other items that had previously been permitted. These were not part of the original, universal divine law; they were specific punitive measures for the Children of Israel because of their persistent rebellion and injustice. The verse explains the reason: “for their wrongdoing” (bi-ẓulmin minhum).

The word ẓulm encompasses their many sins: breaking the covenant, rejecting the prophets, indulging in usury, devouring people’s wealth unjustly, and all the crimes mentioned earlier in this passage. The dietary restrictions were a form of discipline, a yoke placed upon them as a consequence of their disobedience, making their religious practice more burdensome.

The verse then adds a second reason: “and for hindering many from the Way of Allah. ” They not only sinned themselves but actively prevented others from embracing the path of truth. They misrepresented the scriptures, hid the prophecies about the coming Prophet, and erected obstacles in the spiritual journey of the sincere seekers among both their own people and the Arabs. Their hindrance (ṣadd) was a major crime, multiplying their guilt. This verse provides a powerful lesson in the nature of divine law. Hardships and restrictions can sometimes be a consequence of collective sin. The mercy of Islam is evident in that the final law, the Shariah of Muhammad, is described elsewhere as a removal of burdens and shackles. It is a return to a purer, more merciful law.

The verse also warns believers that sin, especially when coupled with hindering others from guidance, can lead to the loss of divine blessings and the imposition of constraints, both spiritual and material. It sets the stage for the following verses that continue to detail the Jewish transgressions, particularly the taking of usury and consuming wealth unjustly.
