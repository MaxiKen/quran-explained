=1=

"O humanity! Be mindful of your Lord who created you from a single soul, and from it He created its mate, and from both He spread many men and women. And be mindful of Allah, in whose name you ask one another, and of womb ties. Indeed, Allah is ever Watchful over you."

Ya ayyuha an-nas — O humanity — the surah of Women opens not with O believers but with O humanity, the same universal call as al-Baqarah 2:21. Before one law about orphans, marriage, or inheritance, Allah lays the foundation that makes those laws necessary: you are one family. Al-ladhi khalaqakum min nafsin wahidah — He created you from a single soul, Adam, and from that same soul He created its mate, Hawwa, from the same essence and dignity, not as a lower creation. Then baththa — He scattered, sowed — from both of them many men and women. Every tribe, language, and colour goes back to that pair, and with one sentence racism, tribal pride, and gender contempt collapse. The word is not "He placed" but "He spread" — an image of seeds cast wide, all living from one root.

Then the double command of taqwa. Ittaqu rabbakum — be mindful of your Lord who created you. Then wattaqu Allaha alladhi tasa'aluna bihi wal-arham — be mindful of Allah in whose name you ask one another, and of the wombs. In Jahiliyyah they would say "I ask you by Allah and by the womb," so Allah joins His own right to the right of family ties in one breath. To cut the womb is to cut taqwa. And the seal: inna Allaha kana alaykum Raqiba — Allah is ever Watchful over you. Raqib is the guardian watcher who never blinks. The same knowledge that opened Ali Imran — "nothing in earth or heaven is hidden from Allah" 3:5 — now watches the quiet deal between guardian and orphan, husband and wife.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, placed this verse at the head of every khutbat al-hajah — every marriage and Friday sermon — so companions knew family law was coming. He said, "The womb is suspended from the Throne and says: whoever joins me, Allah will join him, and whoever cuts me, Allah will cut him," and "One who cuts family ties will not enter Paradise." An-Nisa is Madinan, revealed after Uhud when Madinah was full of orphans, widows, and new tribes learning to be one community. Allah did not begin with fractions. He began with one soul, two parents, and a Watcher. That is why every later law fits: orphan wealth because orphans are womb-children left weak; marriage because marriage joins wombs; inheritance because death tears a womb and Allah Himself divides; kindness to parents and neighbours because all stems from rahm.

If this verse sits in the heart, the rest of the surah is easy. Racism dies under single soul. Misogyny dies under "from the same soul He created its mate." Selfish individualism dies under wal-arham. And the private cruelty that thinks no one sees dies under Raqiba.

=2=

"Give orphans their wealth when they reach maturity, and do not exchange what is worthless of yours for what is good of theirs, nor consume their wealth with your wealth. Indeed, that is a great sin."

Wa atu al-yatama amwalahum — give the orphans their wealth. Atu is the verb of rightful payment, the same used for trusts in 4:58 and dowries in 4:4. This is not charity; it is return. The child is still called yatim even as he nears adulthood, to keep tenderness alive while you hold his money.

Wa la tatabaddalu al-khabitha bit-tayyib — do not exchange the bad for the good. A guardian would swap his thin sheep for the orphan's fat one, his wilting palm for the orphan's fruitful one, his debased coins for sound ones, thinking the fatherless child will not argue. Al-khabith against al-tayyib echoes 2:267 "do not aim for the bad to spend from while you would not take it except with eyes closed." Do not give others what you would despise for yourself, especially a child with no father to object.

Wa la ta'kulu amwalahum ila amwalikum — do not eat their wealth along with yours, mixing flocks and accounts so consumption can be called "ours." The Quran calls it eating because wealth finally becomes flesh. Innahu kana huban kabira — that is a great sin, a heavy hub that tips the scale. The Prophet, peace be upon him, listed eating orphan wealth among the seven destroyers that wipe out a life — with shirk, magic, murder, riba, fleeing the battle, and slandering chaste women — and said, "Avoid the seven destroyers," naming it plainly.

Placed after the womb verse, the connection is direct: the orphan is the womb at its most vulnerable — the father has been cut by death — and the community is tested whether it will stand as father or become a second bereavement. Allah is Raqib in that quiet store where sheep are swapped. The surah will soon complete the system: do not hand wealth to the weak-minded to waste (4:5), test with small trials (4:6), let the rich take no wage and the poor take a fair wage, bring witnesses at transfer, and know Allah suffices as Reckoner. This verse is the headline; what follows is the procedure. Modern forms are every trust for minors: a sibling holding a deceased brother's house in his name, a charity diverting sponsorship, a manager burying a ward's capital as investment. What looks like profit in the ledger is fire in the belly, as 4:10 will name.

=3=

"If you fear you will not be just to orphan women when you marry them, then marry other women who seem good to you — two, three, or four. But if you fear you will not be just, then marry only one, or those in your possession. That is more likely to keep you from injustice."

This verse is almost always quoted without its first clause, and that changes its meaning. Wa in khiftum alla tuqsitu fil-yatama — if you fear you will not be just to orphan girls — then marry other women. Aisha explained to Urwah that this concerned a guardian of an orphan girl who owned wealth and beauty. He wanted to marry her without a proper mahr to keep her wealth, giving her less than her peers. Allah said: if you fear you cannot give such orphans their due in mahr and kind treatment, do not marry them to exploit them; marry other lawful women instead.

Mathna wa thulatha wa ruba'a — two, three, four — was a restriction, not an encouragement. Pre-Islamic men married eight, ten, without limit. Islam capped at four and wrapped that permission between two fears of injustice — fear before and fear after — and that framing is the lesson. Justice here is what can be controlled: nights, maintenance, housing, kind speech, time. Equality of love in the heart is not demanded, and 4:129 will say you will never achieve perfect equality in feeling even if you strive; but inclination of the heart must never become injustice of the hand. The Prophet, peace be upon him, warned, "Whoever has two wives and inclines to one will come on the Day with one side drooping."

Fa-in khiftum alla ta'dilu fa-wahidah — if you fear you cannot be just, then one. Dhalika adna alla ta'ulu — that is closest to not deviating or being burdened with dependents you cannot sustain. Aw ma malakat aymanukum refers to bondwomen in that historical structure, yet even there the surah insists on permission, dower, chastity, and kindness in 4:25, and makes freeing a slave an expiation in 4:92.

Revelation context shows mercy: after warning against eating orphan wealth, Allah warns against marrying the orphan to quietly eat her mahr. The next verse immediately commands "give women their dowries as a cheerful gift" — closing the loophole. Polygyny remains permissible by need and with justice, but not from boredom or to prove manhood; monogamy is named as closer to justice for most. The Prophet's own marriages after Khadijah were largely of mercy, alliance, and teaching, and even he prayed, "O Allah, this is my division in what I control, so do not blame me for what You control and I do not," while dividing nights with scrupulous fairness.

=4=

"Give women their dowries graciously as a gift. But if they willingly waive part of it with good pleasure, then you may enjoy it with wholesome pleasure."

Wa atu an-nisa saduqatihinna nihlah — give women their saduqat as nihlah. Saduqah from sidq — truthfulness — the dower proves the covenant is serious. Nihlah is a cheerful gift given with open heart, not a purchase price handed with tightness, nor a loan to be reclaimed. The orphan girls of the previous verse were married without full mahr; now the rule is universal. Her mahr is hers — not her father's, brother's, or negotiator's cut. The Prophet annulled a father's taking of his daughter's mahr and taught that even an iron ring or teaching Quran could be a mahr if sincerely agreed — because honour, not inflation, is the point.

Fa-in tibna lakum an shay'in minhu nafsan fa-kuluhu hani'an mari'a — but if they willingly waive part of it out of sweetness of soul, then eat it as Hani'an mari'a — wholesome, pleasant, digesting without sin. The phrase is used for food that goes down easily and sits well. If she from her own expansive heart gives a portion back, that portion becomes blessed provision.

The key is nafsan — soul consent. A waiver by pressure, family shaming, sulking, or "good wives do not demand" is not tib. The Prophet said no one's wealth is lawful except with good pleasure of his soul. Scholars ask: if she refused, would she face harm or guilt? If yes, it is not willing. This verse ties backward to returning orphan wealth (4:2) and forward to not reclaiming a heap, a qintar, upon divorce (4:20). Islam builds financial independence for women when their world gave them none, and when today fathers still reroute mahr to themselves calling it tradition.

Give promptly, in her name, in her account, not as gold that stays in another's locker. If years of kindness later make her want to spend it on the family, that is hani'an mari'a — but do not budget for it, nor hint. Live so she would want to give and feel safe to keep.

=5=

"Do not give the weak-minded among you your wealth which Allah has made as a support for you, but provide for them from it and clothe them, and speak to them with kind words."

Wa la tu'tu as-sufaha amwalakum allati ja'ala Allahu lakum qiyaman — do not give to the sufaha your wealth which Allah made as qiyam, a pillar that keeps you standing. Safih is one weak in financial judgment — child, orphan not yet mature, person needing support in management, or spendthrift who will waste in haram or ruin. Qiyam is the same idea as a pillar of a tent; waste the pillar and the roof falls on everyone, as the qiyam of prayer holds faith.

Amwalakum — your wealth — though the context is orphan wealth, the Quran calls it yours because the ummah is one body; wasting one orphan's capital weakens the whole. It also means your own wealth: do not hand a bulk sum to a dependent who will squander the family's standing. Both meanings meet.

Yet the verse does not order harshness. Warzuquhum fiha waksuhum — provide for them from it and clothe them. Note fiha — from within it — invest the capital and spend from its yield, keeping the root intact. Feed before luxuries, clothe before show. Wa qulu lahum qawlan ma'rufa — speak to them with kind words. The same ma'ruf speech required at inheritance division when non-heirs watch (4:8) and when refusing a lump sum. Even withholding for protection must be soft. The Prophet said "a kind word is charity," and 2:263 says kind words and forgiveness are better than charity followed by hurt.

Placed before "test the orphans" (4:6), this is the pause before transfer: do not give yet; care instead. For a trustee today this means protected accounts, small trials, financial literacy, and mentorship — not a cheque at eighteen and a prayer. For parents, it means feeding well, clothing well, speaking kindly, but not handing what will destroy the qiyam Allah entrusted to you.

=6=

"Test the orphans until they reach marriageable age. Then if you perceive sound judgment in them, return their wealth to them. Do not consume it wastefully and hastily before they grow. Whoever is rich should refrain, and whoever is poor may take a fair provision. When you return their wealth, bring witnesses. And Allah suffices as Reckoner."

Wabtulu al-yatama hatta idha balaghu an-nikah — test them until they reach marriageable age. Not a single exam but a season: give small sums, watch purchases, see if they avoid israf and haram, understand price and promise. Fa-in anastum minhum rushdan — if you sense rushd — sound judgment in wealth and deen, not only puberty — then return their wealth. Rushd is higher than bulugh.

Wa la ta'kuluha israfan wa bidaran an yakbaru — do not eat it wastefully and hastily, racing that they will grow and take it — buying a nicer car "from the orphan's funds," expensing holidays as care. The Quran names the motive before accounting tricks hide it.

Wa man kana ghaniyyan fal-yasta'fif, wa man kana faqiran fal-ya'kul bil-ma'ruf — the rich should abstain entirely; the poor may take a fair, customary wage for work, no more. Ibn Abbas said ma'ruf is what an honest manager would take, and Umar said, "I regard myself as guardian of an orphan: if rich, I do not touch; if poor, I take fairly."

Fa-idha dafa'tum ilayhim amwalahum fa-ashhidu alayhim — bring witnesses at handover, protecting both sides. Wa kafa billahi Hasiba — Allah suffices as Reckoner. With all records, He is the final auditor of every palm and coin.

The orphan system is now complete: declare one family under a Watcher (4:1), return wealth without swapping (4:2), do not marry orphans to eat their mahr (4:3-4), withhold capital from the immature but feed and speak kindly (4:5), test until rushd, deliver with witnesses, and know Hasib reckons. For any executor or charity treasurer today: test, document, be abstinent if rich, take only fair if poor, witness, and remember the spreadsheet you delete is still with Hasib. The fear of leaving your own weak offspring behind will mirror this honest care in the next verses.

=7=

"For men there is a share of what parents and relatives leave, and for women there is a share of what parents and relatives leave — whether little or much — an ordained share."

Lir-rijali nasibun mimma taraka al-walidani wal-aqrabun wa lin-nisa nasibun — for men a share, for women a share. The repetition breaks pre-Islamic custom that gave only to fighting men. Whether little or much — qalla minhu aw kathura — no estate is too small to split or too large to withhold. Nasiban mafruda — a cut fixed by Allah; farada is to cut precisely, so no elder, father, or custom may re-cut it.

Nasib is a portion owned, not a gift left to mood. Mafrud is imposed on givers, not optional for takers. This headline precedes the math of 4:11-12 and the final kalalah in 4:176, but the principle lands first: women own. In 4:2-6 the orphan's wealth was often inheritance; if sisters inherit nothing, orphan girls hold empty purses. Allah first affirms the share, then numbers it, then warns that eating it beyond the share is fire (4:10) and transgressing these bounds is a humiliating Fire (4:14).

At Uhud, Aws ibn Thabit died leaving wife Umm Kuhjah and three daughters; his brothers seized the estate saying women have no share. She complained to the Prophet, and this verse and the detailed ones after descended, and the brothers returned what they took.

Today a father who says "daughters will be cared for by husbands so sons keep land," or a brother who keeps the house telling a sister "you took your mahr and wedding," is reverting to 1400-year-old zulm. A will that says "divide equally because I know better than the fractions" also violates mafruda, as does one that disinherits to keep a business intact. Allah's cut is His; add generosity from your own share if you wish, but do not amend His cut.

=8=

"And if at the division relatives, orphans, or the needy are present, give them something from it and speak to them with kind words."

Wa idha hadara al-qismata ulu al-qurba wal-yatama wal-masakin farzuquhum minhu — when at division distant relatives, orphans, or needy are present, provide for them from it. The shares have just been fixed with exact sixths and quarters that must not be trimmed to please outsiders. Yet exactness can feel exacting to a poor cousin or neighbour orphan watching dates and coins measured. So after fixing rights, Allah fixes ihsan: give something — minhu — unquantified, from the generosity of heirs after they receive, or from the estate with their consent. Ibn Abbas gave even a handful or garment so no eye left broken.

Wa qulu lahum qawlan ma'rufa — speak to them kindly. This is the second ma'ruf in this wealth cluster after 4:5. What do you say to one who hoped and has no fixed share? Not "no share for you," but "Allah fixed these portions; this is what He obligated, and this is what we can give you with a good heart; may Allah bless you." Qawl ma'ruf is speech known to be good — not flattery, not false promise. 2:263 says kind words are better than charity followed by hurt; here both are commanded.

The temperament is clear: precise in rights, expansive in ihsan. The fixed shares protect daughters, mothers, wives from being talked out of them in the name of kindness to others; yet within firm walls, voluntary kindness heals the margins. Apply it at every distribution: if law leaves no portion because debts consumed, still speak kindly. Do not let witnesses watch only measuring and leave wounded. Let them witness that wealth is rizq from Raqib and Hasib, and that witness prevents envy from poisoning wombs that inheritance was meant to protect.

=9=

"Let those who fear leaving frail children behind worry for them be mindful of Allah and speak well-directed, straight words."

Walyakhsha alladhina law taraku min khalfihim dhurriyyatan di'afan khafu alayhim — let those fear who, if they left weak offspring behind, would be afraid for them. The mirror is turned: imagine the orphans under your hand are your own toddlers, your small daughters unable to argue a share. How would you want the man after you to treat them? Would you want him swapping sheep, hurrying to spend before they grow, denying daughters, pressuring widows? Let those who would fear that for their own — walyakhsha — fear doing it to others.

Falyattaqu Allaha wal-yaqulu qawlan sadida — be mindful of Allah and speak words that are sadid — straight, hitting the mark like an arrow, closing gaps. Sadid is stronger than ma'ruf; it is the same word in 33:70 that reforms deeds and forgives sins. Here it includes truthful statements about an orphan's property, honest bequest advice not to harm heirs, refusing to coach an heir to deny another's share with paperwork tricks. The Prophet told a companion who wanted to bequeath most of his wealth, "A third, and a third is much. To leave your heirs rich is better than to leave them begging."

The verse closes the orphan bracket that opened with Raqib. It began with "Allah is Watchful" and ends with "be mindful and speak straight," leading directly to the fire warning for eaters of orphan wealth. Before you sign as executor, put your children's faces in the picture; before you pressure a widow to waive her eighth, picture your wife widowed; before you advise a will that harms fixed heirs, ask if this is qawl sadid or clever zulm.

=10=

"Those who consume the wealth of orphans unjustly in truth consume nothing but fire into their bellies, and they will burn in a blazing Fire."

Inna alladhina ya'kuluna amwala al-yatama zulman — those who eat orphan wealth wrongfully, by swapping, hurrying, mixing, overpaying themselves, withholding daughters, underpaying mahr. Innama ya'kuluna fi butunihim nara — they only eat fire into bellies. The stomach feeling full with extra dates is filling with fire that will take a body on the Day when secrets become bodies. Wa sayaslawna sa'ira — they will burn in Sa'ir, the blaze. The same image appears for concealing revelation for a small price in 2:174 and for riba.

Why so severe? The orphan has lost his dunya defender — his father — so Allah Himself becomes Defender. Threat matches protection. The Prophet listed eating orphan wealth among the seven destroyers and said its eater will be raised on the Day with fire coming from mouth, nose, ears.

This pillar stands at the entrance to inheritance law for a reason. The fractions of 4:11-12 are precise and will tempt quarrels; before division, Allah says any taking beyond the fixed share by force or forgery is not clever — it is fire. After this, the surah will call those fractions hududu Allah — and transgression a humiliating Fire (4:13-14). For today's guardian, sibling, uncle, or treasurer: diverted sponsorship, a house kept in your name while nieces rent, buried capital called investment — all is nara even if the app says dollars. Return and repent while Hasib still gives time. For the wronged with no defender, Raqib has already filed the case, and the blaze promised is proof the tears were seen.

=11=

"Allah instructs you concerning your children: for the male the like of the share of two females. If there are only women, two or more, for them is two-thirds of what is left. If there is one, for her is one-half. For each parent, one-sixth if the deceased left children. If no child and parents inherit him, for his mother one-third. If he has brothers and sisters, for his mother one-sixth, after any bequest made or debt. Your parents and children — you know not who is nearer to you in benefit. An ordained share from Allah. Indeed, Allah is Knowing, Wise."

Yusikum Allahu fi awladikum — Allah instructs you, a wasiyyah from the Most Merciful, concerning children. Lidh-dhakari mithlu hazzi al-unthayayn — for the male like two females. Read this inside the whole Quranic economy: the same qawwam man of 4:34 must pay mahr, maintain wife and children, support parents when needed, and carry financial liabilities, while his sister's share is largely pure capital — she keeps her portion, her mahr is added, and she is not obligated to spend on husband or children, though voluntary spending is hani'an mari'a. Where burdens are equal and his privilege absent, her share is equal, as with maternal siblings in 4:12 who inherit a sixth equally male and female.

If two or more daughters alone, two-thirds together; if one daughter, one-half. For parents: if the deceased leaves a child, each parent one-sixth; if no child and parents are sole heirs, mother one-third, father the remainder; if siblings exist, mother drops to one-sixth because broader family needs expand the father's responsibility. All min ba'di wasiyyatin yusi biha aw dayn — after bequest or debt. The Prophet limited bequests to non-heirs to one-third, saying "a third, and a third is much," to protect fixed shares.

Aba'ukum wa abna'ukum la tadruna ayyuhum aqrabu lakum naf'an — parents and children, you know not who is nearer in benefit. You may favour a son while a daughter nurses you in illness and prays after death. Allah knows benefit and places fractions where need and responsibility meet. Faridatan min Allah — an ordained cut. Inna Allaha kana Aliman Hakima — the Knower knows who benefits; the Wise places benefit to purify wealth and wombs, as in Ali Imran 3:6 where He forms in wombs as He wills.

Calculate after debts and bequests, not before; do not pressure a sister to take cash while brothers keep undervalued land; use a qualified divider, for these laws involve awl and radd that emotion miscalculates. The fear of leaving weak offspring is answered not by hoarding but by obeying the Wise.

=12=

"For you is one-half of what your wives leave if they have no child; if they have a child, one-quarter, after bequest or debt. For them is one-quarter of what you leave if you have no child; if you have a child, one-eighth, after bequest or debt. If a man or woman is inherited as kalalah with no parent or child and has a brother or sister, each one-sixth; if more than two, they share one-third equally, after bequest or debt causing no harm. An instruction from Allah, and Allah is Knowing, Forbearing."

Husbands: from a childless wife, one-half; with a child, one-quarter. Wives: from a childless husband, one-quarter; with a child, one-eighth — shared equally if multiple wives. The reduction with children reflects that children are the primary continuation and need.

Then kalalah — one with neither parent nor child — and a brother or sister: here maternal siblings, children of the same mother, as Abu Bakr and Umar recited and companions agreed. Each one-sixth; if more than two, share one-third equally male and female alike. The equal sharing surprises those who only heard "male like two females," but the maternal link is not tied to the tribal maintenance structure, so equality honours the maternal womb that opened the surah.

Min ba'di wasiyyatin yusa biha aw daynin ghayra mudar — after bequest or debt without harm. Ghayra mudar — without intending harm. A fake debt or a "gift" that bypasses fixed heirs is mudarrah forbidden. The Prophet said, "Allah has given each holder his right, so no bequest for an heir;" non-heir bequests cap at one-third and must not aim to harm. Wasiyyatan min Allah — an instruction from Allah. Wallahu Alimun Halim — Knowing, Forbearing — not hastening against ignorance, but seeing intended harm. Halim holds space to learn; He does not hold space for knowingly cutting a daughter's share or a wife's eighth.

This is the same ma'ruf and qawl sadid as earlier: give fair value with informed, uncoerced consent — tibun nafsan — or you eat by falsehood in 4:29, and reclaim a heap as in 4:20.

=13=

"These are the limits of Allah. Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger will be admitted to Gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding eternally. That is the great success."

Tilka hududu Allah — these are Allah's boundaries, not suggestions. The word hudud in the Quran clusters around family — marriage, divorce, inheritance — where desire most wants to redraw lines to favour a son, keep land intact, or punish a daughter who married outside.

Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger in these hudud will enter Gardens with rivers, khalidina fiha — eternally. The Gardens for muttaqin who remember Allah standing and sitting (Ali Imran 3:190) are linked here to obedience in money, because money is the god most competing with Allah. Wa dhalika al-fawzu al-azim — the great success. The math that felt dry is sealed with water — rivers — and the precision that felt burdensome is sealed with eternity.

The Prophet said a man may do good seventy years then harm in his bequest and be sealed with Fire, and another may do evil seventy years then be just in his bequest and be sealed with Paradise — so weighty is this line. Obedience looks like learning shares, letting a qualified divider compute, witnessing as in 4:6, giving each nasib fully, adding generosity only from your share. Success is not keeping the estate intact for a photo; it is keeping Allah's limits intact for eternity.

=14=

"But whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger and transgresses His limits, He will put him into Fire, abiding eternally, with a humiliating punishment."

Wa man ya'si Allaha wa rasulahu wa yata'adda hududahu yudkhilhu naran khalidan fiha — whoever disobeys and oversteps His limits will be put into Fire abiding, with adhabun muhin — humiliating punishment. The same pattern as 2:231 for those who retain wives to harm and transgress. Humiliation fits the arrogance: the heir who thought he knew better, who humiliated a sister with leftovers, whose paperwork humiliated a widow. Allah answers with a humiliation that fits.

Khalidina in Gardens and khalidan in Fire are parallel eternities — inheritance is eschatology, not administration. Family grudges over wealth cut wombs for generations; one obedience keeps dua alive after parents die.

This threat is not for an honest miscalculation corrected when taught — Halim in the previous verse holds that space — but for yata'adda, deliberate crossing after knowledge and correction, after Raqib and Hasib warned. The Prophet said people will dispute over half a dirham and Allah will judge even by a date-stone thread — fatil, naqir — the very threads that follow in the surah's justice verses.

Before you propose "do not tell sisters the land value" or "register all in mother's name to divide fairly later," hear ta'addi. The humiliation is not a metaphor; it is muhin before witnesses, opposite the shade and ridwan of 4:57.

=15=

"As for those of your women who commit immorality, call four witnesses from among you. If they testify, confine them in houses until death takes them or Allah makes a way for them."

Wallati ya'tina al-fahishata min nisa'ikum — here zina. Fastashhidu alayhinna arba'atan minkum — bring four witnesses from among you, adult, trustworthy, eyewitnesses to the act itself, not hints or messages. Four is the highest testimony requirement in Islam, reserved only for fahishah. If Allah wanted to hunt, one or two would suffice. By requiring four, He closes spying and rumour.

Fa-in shahidu fa-amsikuhunna fil-buyut hatta yatawaffahunna al-mawt aw yaj'ala Allahu lahunna sabila — if they testify, confine in houses until death or until Allah makes a way. This was an early Madinan holding while hearts were prepared. That way — sabila — came as 24:2 one hundred lashes for the unmarried and in the Sunnah as stoning for the married, after four testimonies or four confessions. The verse remains recited to teach gradualism: Allah built prayer, lightened burdens, then fixed penalties.

Aw yaj'ala Allahu lahunna sabila keeps hope alive even in severity. That way is both societal protection and personal purification through repentance detailed next: if they repent and correct, leave them (4:16), and Allah is Tawwab Rahim.

Placed after hudud of wealth, this guards nasab alongside wealth. Inheritance needs true lineage; fahishah confuses it; slander destroys it. For a tongue that forwards screenshots, know you need four eyewitnesses to the act before branding a woman, otherwise you are a false accuser deserving eighty lashes in 24:4. Punishment is judicial, witnessed, administered by authority, not private family beatings.

=16=

"And the two among you who commit it — punish them. If they repent and correct themselves, leave them alone. Indeed, Allah is ever Accepting of repentance, Merciful."

Walladhani ya'tiyaniha minkum fa-adhuhuma — the two who commit it, punish them with adha — disciplined harm, rebuke, shaming — an early deterrence before the fixed hadd was revealed. Scholars link 4:15 to women and this to the pair, or to two men, but both sexes bear accountability; the tribe that boasted of a son's zina and buried a daughter's was corrected by adhuhuma together.

Fa-in taba wa aslahā fa-a'ridu anhuma — if they repent and correct, leave them. Not a whispered sorry while keeping contact, but tawbah that severs the tie, returns to prayer, and repairs time. Inna Allaha kana Tawwaba Rahima — Ever Accepting, Merciful — the two names that dominate the next verse's window.

The balance is clear: not permissive scoffing as "private choice," not merciless branding. Discipline, then release when sincere correction appears, without lifelong branding. The Prophet punished where proof met four witnesses or four confessions, and accepted repentance that he said would cover a whole city if shared.

For today, standards must be equal: a son's fahishah is not a winked story while a daughter's is a scandal. Both need safeguards — not being alone behind closed doors, guarding gaze in 24:30-31 — and both need the door of tawbah without a community search of past sins years after correction. If Allah turned, turn.

=17=

"Repentance is only incumbent upon Allah for those who do evil in ignorance then repent soon after. It is they to whom Allah will turn. And Allah is Knowing, Wise."

Innama at-tawbatu ala Allahi lilladhina ya'maluna as-su'a bi-jahalah thumma yatubuna min qarib — repentance with Allah — ala Allah is His gracious promise upon Himself, as in 6:54 "your Lord has decreed upon Himself mercy" — is for those who do evil in ignorance then repent soon. Jahalah is not not-knowing it is haram; as Ibn Abbas said, every sin is jahal under overpowering desire that makes the heart forget Raqib. Thumma yatubuna min qarib — soon — before death's certainty, before the throat's rattle. A companion said any repentance before death is min qarib, for a life is near compared to eternity. The Prophet said, "Allah accepts repentance as long as he has not gargled," and that the pen is lifted six hours after sin; if he repents, it is not written.

Fa-ula'ika yatubu Allahu alayhim — they are those Allah will turn to. Wa kan Allahu Aliman Hakima — Knowing who repents soon sincerely and who plays with delay; Wise in placing the window to balance urgency and mercy.

This connects to 4:15-16's leave them when they correct: correction must be near. Forgiveness is vast — less than shirk He forgives for whom He wills in 4:48, 116 — but timing matters. Planning "I will sin now and repent in thirty years" mocks qarib.

Act min qarib: minutes after the slip, wudu, two rak'ahs, astaghfirullah with heart, cut access, restore rights, do a good deed. Do not measure perfection; measure nearness.

=18=

"But repentance is not accepted from those who continue evil until, when death approaches one of them, he says, 'Now I repent,' nor from those who die as disbelievers. For them We have prepared a painful punishment."

Wa laysat at-tawbatu lilladhina ya'maluna as-sayyi'at hatta idha hadara ahadahum al-mawt qala inni tubtu al-ana — repentance is not for those who keep doing evil until death arrives and he says "now I repent." The quotation is Pharaoh's at the sea in 10:90: "I believe in the god the Children of Israel believe in," while Jibril pushed mud into his mouth — not because mercy lacked, but because choice had been removed and sincerity could no longer be shown. Wa la alladhina yamutuna wa hum kuffar — nor those who die upon disbelief. Ula'ika a'tadna lahum adhaban alima — We have prepared a painful punishment.

4:17 and 4:18 are mercy and severity held together: near repentance is promised, death-rattle performance is refused. The Prophet taught haste: "hasten good deeds before trials like dark night," and "take five before five: life before death." Qarib is life itself; hadara al-mawt is its end. Shaytan's promise in 4:120 is "you have time;" the Quran says you know not where or when you die in 31:34, and 4:78 says wherever you are death will reach you, even in fortified towers.

If you breathe now without gargling, you are still in qarib. Do not say after this last look I will stop. Say now tubtu — now. The same Lord who will command ma'ruf living with women in 4:19 leaves this kindness window open — but not after the soul is in the throat.

=19=

"O you who believe, it is not lawful for you to inherit women by force, nor to prevent them from marrying to take back some of what you gave them, except if they commit a clear immorality. Live with them in kindness. If you dislike them, perhaps you dislike a thing and Allah puts in it much good."

La yahillu lakum an tarithu an-nisa karhan — not lawful to inherit women as property. In Jahiliyyah a man died and his heir would throw a cloth over the widow and say "I have more right to her," marrying her without mahr or blocking her remarriage until she ransomed herself with mahr. Women were not estate. Wa la ta'duluhunna li-tadhhabu bi-ba'di ma ataytumuhunna — do not cage a wife you dislike, harming her until she says "take my mahr and divorce me." Khul' when she genuinely seeks release is permitted, but engineering misery to extort is forbidden. Illa an ya'tina bi-fahishatin mubayyinah — except clear, proven flagrant immorality — not suspicion, and even then judicial, not fund-raiding.

Wa ashiruhunna bil-ma'ruf — live with them in kindness, ma'ruf — what sound natures know as good: gentle speech, fair spending by ability, not cursing or striking the face, consulting, thanking, covering faults. The Prophet in his farewell said, "Fear Allah concerning women; you took them as a trust from Allah," and "The best of you is best to his family, and I am best to my family." He never struck a woman or servant.

Fa-in karihtumuhunna fa-asa an takrahu shay'an wa yaj'ala Allahu fihi khayran kathira — if you dislike them, perhaps you dislike a thing wherein Allah places much good — khayran kathira, as Ibn Abbas said: a righteous child, barakah of a tranquil home more lasting than beauty elsewhere. This is not to endure abuse but to weigh dislike by khayr not hawa. If harm is real, the surah provides arbitration in 4:35, fair settlement in 4:128, and enrichment after separation in 4:130, not ownership or extortion. Modern equivalents — "give back gold or no divorce," or leaving her suspended without maintenance — are tarithu karhan with paperwork. Live with ma'ruf or release with ma'ruf.

=20=

"If you wish to replace one wife with another and you gave one a heap of gold, do not take anything from it. Would you take it by slander and clear sin? How could you take it after you have been intimate and they have taken from you a solemn, firm covenant?"

Wa in aradtum istibdala zawjin makana zawjin wa ataytum ihdahunna qintaran fa-la ta'khudhu minhu shay'a — if you divorce to replace a wife and you gave her a qintar — a heap, a treasure — do not take anything. Qintar is the same word for a fortune entrusted and returned in 3:75. Even then, nothing back. A-ta'khudhunahu buhtanan wa ithman mubina — would you take it by slander and clear sin? Buhtan is fabricating fahishah mubayyinah to justify retrieving mahr, double crime of lying and eating wealth.

Wa kayfa ta'khudhunahu wa qad afda ba'dukum ila ba'd — how after you have been intimate — afda — the Quran's delicate, strongest word for entering into one another, seeing what no other sees, trusting what no other trusts — wa akhadhna minkum mithaqan ghaliza — and they took from you a solemn, firm covenant — mithaq ghaliz — used only for prophets' covenant, the Mount over Bani Isra'il, and marriage. Marriage is as serious as those.

Why qintar? To kill the excuse "but I gave a lot." The larger the gift, the smaller the excuse; it was nihlah in 4:4, not a deposit. When Umar once tried to cap mahr, a woman stood and quoted this verse, and Umar said, "A woman is right, Umar is wrong." If she later willingly gives part back, that is 4:4's hani'an mari'a. But your hand taking it by pressure or slander breaks a mountain covenant for a heap that will sit as fire.

Return to Raqib: the intimate ifda no eye saw except His, and the covenant spoken before Him, are watched. Single soul created pairs who took covenants; inheritance limits and marital kindness are lines of that covenant. Honour them and you honour the Lord of the single soul.
