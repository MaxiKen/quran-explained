=41=

"That Day those who disbelieved and disobeyed the Messenger will wish they were levelled with the earth, and they will not hide any statement from Allah."

Yawma'idhin yawaddu alladhina kafaru wa asaw ar-rasula law tusawwa bihim al-ardu — that Day the rejecters and those who disobeyed the Messenger will wish the earth were flattened — tu sawwa — over them, levelled, so no trace of their deeds remains exposed, so no eye sees. The wish is not repentance but attempted disappearance; after the atom-weight justice of 4:40 — "He does not wrong even a dharrah" — and the summoning of a witness from every nation with Muhammad as witness over these in 4:41 — "We bring you as witness over these" — the only hope left for the eater of orphan wealth, the trader in taghut, the twister of ra'ina, the hider of bounty, is to become dust. Yet the earth itself will be a witness, for 99:4 says it will update its news, and 41:20 says skins will testify, and 36:65 says mouths will be sealed and hands will speak. Dust does not hide dust.

Wa la yaktumuna Allaha hadithan — they will not hide any hadith — any statement, any word, any whisper — from Allah. In the world they hid masterfully: they hid orphan wealth by mixing accounts in 4:2 — "do not eat their wealth with yours" — hid a wife's dowry behind a fabricated fahishah to reclaim a qintar in 4:20, hid envy behind "we wish you to stray" smiles in 4:44, hid night plots in 4:81 — "what they plot at night," hid distortion of words in 4:46. On that Day tongues are sealed, hands speak, feet walk without permission, and the Raqib ever Watchful in 4:1 who was Hasib Reckoner in 4:6, Shahid Witness in 4:33 and 4:79, and Muhit Encompassing in 4:126 is the One to whom even the whisper of intention is hadith that cannot be hidden, as 4:108 says He is with them when they plot at night.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, described a servant brought near on the Day, his scroll of good shown, then his hidden faults unrolled until he thinks himself destroyed; because he had hidden with regret in the world and sought forgiveness soon in 4:17, Allah says, "I covered them for you in the world, and I forgive them for you today." Those who hid with defiance and sudud in 4:61 and yatarabbas in 4:141 find no covering; those who hid with shyness and near repentance find covering. The believer's comfort is the inversion: the charity you gave hani'an mari'a in secret after 4:4, the quarter you shared at division with a needy cousin in 4:8, the qawl sadid that defended a helpless child's share in 4:9, the salam you returned with better in 4:86, the intercession with good share in 4:85 — none remains hidden either. If the wrongdoer cannot hide evil, the doer will not lose good. The Day that exposes the first ensures the second is not forgotten, and the Gatherer in 4:87 gathers both.

=42=

"O believers, do not approach prayer while intoxicated until you know what you are saying, nor while in major impurity except passing through, until you bathe. If you are ill, on a journey, someone comes from relieving himself, or you have touched women and find no water, then seek clean earth and wipe your faces and hands. Allah is ever Pardoning, Forgiving."

Ya ayyuha alladhina amanu la taqrabu as-salata wa antum sukara hatta ta'lamu ma taquluna — do not come to prayer while drunk until you know what you say, what you recite, what you ask. This is the second step of a mercy-shaped, wisdom-shaped legislation. Khamr was the drink, trade, and pride of Jahiliyyah; the Quran in 2:219 first said its sin is greater than its benefit, leaving hearts to reason and to compare; here it placed a practical barrier: love of prayer, guarded five times daily, would war with love of drink, and prayer would win for the sincere whose hearts were alive. Then in 5:90 came the final, decisive cut: "O you who believe, intoxicants, gambling, altars, arrows are filth from Satan's work, so avoid — ijtanibuhu — it wholly — perhaps you will succeed." Umar ibn al-Khattab used to pray, "O Allah, clarify for us about wine with a clear clarification," until this verse came as the second clarification, and he still prayed until the final came and he said, "We have avoided, we have avoided." The lesson in tarbiyah is permanent: haram deeply rooted in a society or in a soul is not always uprooted by instant severity alone; hearts are prepared by faith, by prayer, by love until they beg to be commanded, and then the command is sweet and easy.

Wa la junuban illa abiri sabil hatta taghtasilu — nor while junub — in major impurity after intimacy — except as a passer through the mosque, until you bathe. Ghusl that pours water over the whole body mirrors tawbah that pours over the whole heart; the mosque's honour requires purity even to cross, so crossing without bathing is allowed only as passing need, not residence, not loitering. The Sunnah details that the junub may pass through if needed, but not stay.

Wa in kuntum marda aw ala safarin aw ja'a ahadukum min al-gha'it aw lamastum an-nisa fa-lam tajidu ma'an fa-tayammamu sa'idan tayyiban famsahu bi-wujuhikum wa aydikum — if ill where water would harm, or on a journey where water is out of reach, or one comes from al-gha'it — the low place where needs were relieved, the Quran's modest, clean word — or you have touched women — lamastum an-nisa — the same modest, beautiful phrase for intimacy — and find no water, then seek clean earth — sa'idan tayyiban — pure surface dust, earth that is clean — and wipe faces and hands. Striking the earth once for face and hands is worship, not merely hygiene. The Prophet taught Ammar ibn Yasir, who had rolled in dust as if bathing, to strike once for face and hands; he taught at the cold night of Dhat al-Salasil when water was scarce and the junub companion prayed with tayammum and he approved; he taught when Aisha's necklace was lost at night at a place called Baida and dawn neared without water for the caravan, this verse's tayammum descended, and Usayd ibn Hudayr said, "Your family, O House of Abu Bakr, is always a blessing; you bring ease." A concession born from a lost necklace became permanent mercy for travellers, patients, workers without water, and those in cold until the Day.

Inna Allaha kana Afuwwan Ghafura — Pardoning, Forgiving — closes with pardon for past prayers offered while drunk before the final ban, for past crossings while junub without knowledge, and with the ease of 4:28: Allah wants to lighten because He created man weak, and after the Day when no hadith is hidden, here is a Law that accepts earth for water so that no servant, ill or traveling, at home or at war, need miss a meeting with Allah. The verse after the Day's exposure says what erases the wish to be levelled with dust is not hiding but preparation for prayer that no circumstance of illness, travel, or lack of water excuses, and tayammum is the fiqh of takhfif.

=43=

"Have you not seen those given a portion of Scripture purchasing error and wishing that you stray from the path?"

Alam tara ila alladhina utu nasiban min al-kitab yashtaruna ad-dalalata — have you not seen those given a portion — nasiban — of the Book purchasing error? Yashtaruna is trade language, as in 2:16 "they purchased error with guidance, their commerce brought no profit," 2:174 "they exchange guidance for Fire and patience," 3:77 "they exchange covenant for a small price," and 4:51. Even a small portion — nasiban — was enough to recognise the Prophet as they recognised their sons in 2:146 and 6:20, yet they bartered it for tribal standing, for fees for changing the stoning verse from stoning to flogging for the noble, for rivalry over mulk in 4:54, for a salary.

Wa yuriduna an tadillu as-sabil — they wish that you stray from the path — not only that they stray but that you do with them, as their error feels lonely. This is the maylan azima of 4:27 — followers of desires wanting you to incline greatly — in practice, in pulpits, in curricula. In Madinah some Jewish scholars altered the description of the Prophet in Torah, hid the verse of Rajm, hid the coming of the unlettered Prophet, and invited Muslims to doubt; hypocrites carried the same trade into rumours about the Messenger's household, about Aisha, about charity. For every age the purchaser of error is the reviver of that trade who sells doubt as sophistication: calling the mahr a price of ownership, calling bukhl wisdom, calling riya marketing and personal branding, calling guardianship of orphans dominance to be avoided, calling hijrah extremism, calling qiwamah oppression.

The verse after tayammum's purity and the Day's exposure reminds that even the pure dust of worship has purchasers who would rather you chase error and miss the clean prayer altogether, and that Allah's witnessing in 4:166 exposes the trade, and that tadabbur in 4:82 exposes it even more. The protection is referral in 4:83 and light in 4:174.

=44=

"Allah knows best your enemies. Sufficient is Allah as Guardian, and sufficient is Allah as Helper."

Wallahu a'lamu bi-a'da'ikum — Allah knows your enemies more truly, more precisely, more permanently than reports, trends, rumours, intelligence, or fears. The same A'lam who knows faith in 4:25 — Allah knows your faith, ba'dukum min ba'd — and knows breach in families in 4:35 — Allah knows who desires islah — and knows what chests conceal in 4:63 and what is in heavens and earth in 4:126 and 4:170. He catalogues enemies not to breed paranoia, anxiety, or conspiracy theories, but to move reliance from many to One. Wa kafa billahi waliyyan wa kafa billahi nasira — sufficient as Wali who takes charge of affairs, guards, befriends, supports, and Nasir who helps, aids, gives victory.

Wali-Nasir is a pair that echoes 2:257 "Allah is the Wali of believers who brings them from darkness to light," 3:150 "Allah is best Wali and best Nasir," 3:173 when they said hasbunAllahu wa ni'ma al-wakil "Allah suffices," 4:75's du'a for a ladunni Wali and Nasir for the mustad'afin, and 4:132 Wakila. After exposing purchasers who wish you astray, the remedy is not to spend nights listing foes, making charts, and fearing, but to return hearts to the Sufficient who said in 42:10 "upon Allah I rely." Ulu al-amr in 4:59 and precautions in 4:71 will command practical means: consult scholars, take hizr, verify news before spreading, as 4:83 says, but the heart's reliance is on Him alone.

If He suffices as Wali, their wishing to mislead cannot compel you without your consent, as 4:113 says they mislead only themselves and cannot harm you at all, and as 4:76 says Satan's plot is weak. For today's reader scrolling nightly through alarmist feeds about enemies within and without, about conspiracies, this verse calibrates the heart: know enemies exist, take lawful means, but do not let enemy-awareness become worship of enemies and become anxiety; let it become worship of the Wali who suffices and the Nasir who never tires, and then sleep after Isha.

=45=

"Among the Jews are those who distort words from their proper places and say, 'We hear and disobey,' and 'Hear — may you not be made to hear,' and 'Ra'ina,' twisting with their tongues and stabbing religion. Had they said, 'We hear and obey, hear, and look upon us,' it would have been better and more upright. But Allah cursed them for disbelief, so they believe only a little."

Min alladhina hadu yuharrifuna al-kalima an mawadi'ihi — among those who are Jews are those who pull words from their places — yuharrifuna — shifting meanings, omitting a word, adding a condition, elongating a vowel to change a ruling, as when they omitted the stoning penalty for adultery and added leniency for the noble by changing "stone" to "flog and blacken," as 3:78 describes twisting the Book with tongues so you think it is from the Book though it is not. The distortion is not mistake; it is skill used for desire.

Wa yaquluna sami'na wa asayna — we hear and we disobey — deliberate inversion of the believer's sami'na wa ata'na in 2:285 and 24:51, the shahada of hearing that submits. The believer hears "give the orphan her share" and says we hear and we obey; the distorter hears and says we hear and we will not. Wasma' ghayra musma' — hear, may you not be made to hear — a curse cloaked as polite address to the Prophet: "hear!" they say, while wishing his supplication be deaf, his call not heard, as if to say "we obey your hearing but may you not be heard by Allah." Wa ra'ina layyan bi-alsinatihim — ra'ina — shepherd us, tend us — said with a twist of tongue to sound like an insult in Hebrew — ru'unah — foolish — while Arab ears heard a harmless pastoral word; Allah for that reason forbade Muslims in 2:104 "do not say ra'ina, say unzurna — look upon us, watch over us." Layyan — twisting tongues — the organ turned, the pronunciation bent, and ta'nan fid-din — stabbing religion itself, not merely a person, but the deen.

Wa law annahum qalu sami'na wa ata'na wasma' wanzurna la-kana khayran lahum wa aqwama — had they said we hear and we obey, and said "hear" without curse, and said "look upon us" without twist, it would have been better — khayran — for them in this world and better for their rank, and more upright — aqwam — the same root as qawwam and qiyam and qist and sirat mustaqim. A change of three phrases would have turned curse into guidance, distance into nearness. But technique without tawbah is skill in sin, eloquence without submission is layy.

Wa lakin la'anahum Allahu bi-kufrihim fa-la yu'minuna illa qalila — but Allah cursed them — la'n is distance from mercy, banishment — for disbelief — not for lineage, not for name, but for kufr after bayan — so they believe only a little — qalila — a remnant like Abdullah ibn Salam, Mukhayriq who gave his orchards, the sincere students who heard Quran in 5:83 and wept. Cursed for distortion as shield for disobedience after Allah clarified in 4:26 "Allah wants to make clear," and after He taught. The layy is not a Jewish monopoly; it is a temptation for every tongue given a portion of the Book that prefers cleverness to submission, that says "we hear the verse about inheritance but we will not give," that says "hear" with curse, that says "our sheikh said."

=46=

"O People of the Book, believe in what We have sent down confirming what is with you, before We efface faces and turn them to backs or curse them as We cursed Sabbath breakers. Allah's command is always done."

Ya ahla al-kitabi aminu bima nazzalna musaddiqan lima ma'akum — O People of the Book, believe in what We sent down confirming what is with you — the Quran confirming the original Tawrah and Injil in their light — not denying their truth but verifying the description of the unlettered Prophet within them, correcting what hands changed, and completing. The Quran does not come as stranger; it comes as musaddiq. Min qabli an natmisa wujuhan fa-naruddaha ala adbariha — before We efface faces — natmisa — wipe smooth, remove features, make blind, efface — and turn them to backs — fa-naruddaha ala adbariha — humiliating reversal of the face that turned from Allah and turned with sudud. The image is literal threat and metaphor: faces that turned away from light will be turned, as they turned.

Aw nal'anahum kama la'anna Ashab as-sabt — or curse them as We cursed Sabbath companions. Those Sabbath breakers in 2:65, 4:47, 7:163 were tested with fish that appeared only on Sabbath, abundant, while on other days none; they laid nets Friday, left them Saturday, collected Sunday, tricking the prohibition with a legal trick. They became apes, despised — not merely punished but transformed, as their trick tried to transform law with trick. The curse was for legal trickery that made haram look halal.

Wa kana amru Allahi maf'ula — Allah's command when He says Be — kun — is done, executed, not delayed, not needing help. The threat is stark and severe, but the invitation precedes it: believe before effacing, before turning, before curse, as 4:17's soon repentance precedes 4:18's no repentance at gargle, and as 4:171 will say to Christians "do not exceed, desist, better for you." Warning and door stay together as in 11:52. The surah that taught tayammum with dust out of ease for the ill now teaches fear with effacing out of justice for the insister; both are mercy when heeded. The Prophet never despaired of Jewish neighbours; he visited a sick Jewish boy, invited him, the boy looked to his father who said "obey Abu al-Qasim" and he believed and was saved from Fire.

=47=

"Indeed, Allah does not forgive associating partners with Him, but forgives what is less than that for whom He wills. Whoever associates partners with Allah has fabricated a great sin."

Inna Allaha la yaghfiru an yushraka bihi wa yaghfiru ma duna dhalika liman yasha — Allah does not forgive shirk for one who dies upon it without return to Him, without tawbah, without la ilaha illa Allah. What is less than shirk — any major or minor sin, any theft, any slander, any riba, any abandonment — He forgives for whom He wills, by repentance, by mercy, by wudu that drops sins, by illness that expiates, by intercession, by His will. This verse anchors the entire surah, repeated in 4:116 to bracket warnings about distortions, taghut, riba, and night plots. It is the verse the Prophet called the most hopeful and most fearful: hope for every sinner, fear for every polytheist who dies upon associates.

The Prophet said the greatest of major sins is to associate partners with Allah while He created you; he said riya is hidden shirk and to guard against it with ikhlas; he said whoever meets Allah not associating anything with Him enters Paradise even if he stole and fornicated, after punishment if willed or after forgiveness. Wa man yushrik billahi fa-qad iftara ithman azima — whoever associates has fabricated — iftara — a monstrous lie, ithman azima — great sin, as 3:94 names iftira for forging about food and lawful, and 6:21 for forging about Allah. Every shirk says about Allah what He never said: that He has a peer in love, fear, du'a, reliance, or legislation, that He has a son, that He has an associate. After the effacing, this clarifies the one barrier that never opens after death without tawbah in life: shirk. Every other sin remains under will, not automatically sealed, not automatically punished, not automatically forgiven. That is why near repentance in 4:17 is urgent and why "what would Allah do with punishment if you are grateful and believe?" in 4:147 is hope: do not gamble that any sin is small until He wills, and do not despair that any sin short of shirk closes mercy if you return soon with sincerity.

=48=

"Have you not seen those who claim purity for themselves? Rather, Allah purifies whom He wills, and they will not be wronged a thread."

Alam tara ila alladhina yuzakkuna anfusahum — have you not seen those who claim purity for themselves — yuzakkuna anfusahum — we are pure, we are clean, our lineage secures Paradise, Fire will touch us only days as in 2:80, 3:24, 5:18 "we are sons of Allah and His beloved," or "we are the saved sect, our deeds are already accepted," or "our hearts are pure, we do not need to pray." Yuzakkuna is to praise the self as already clean, to claim tazkiyah without its means, discounting need for repentance, for prayer, for zakah. Bal Allahu yuzakki man yasha — rather, Allah purifies whom He wills. True tazkiyah comes through the messenger reciting verses, purifying with wudu, teaching Book and wisdom as in 2:129, 62:2, through salah that washes as in 4:43 and 4:101-103, through zakah that purifies wealth, through tawbah soon in 4:17, through holding fast.

The Prophet taught not to praise definitively: "If you must praise, say I deem him thus and Allah is his reckoner, I praise no one before Allah," and he corrected the woman who said of a child who died "he is a bird in Paradise" with "Do you know? Perhaps he did otherwise; Allah knows what he would have done." He said, "Do not claim purity for yourselves; Allah knows who is mindful — a'lamu bi-man ittaqa." Tazkiyah is Allah's act after sincere taqwa and near repentance, not a slogan before it, not a heritage.

Wa la yuzlamuna fatila — they will not be wronged even a thread — fatila — the thin filament on a date stone's groove, the tiny thread that clings. The same precision as dharrah in 4:40, naqir in 4:53, qitmir in 35:13, hasana in 4:40. Claimants will find justice exact: if purified by sincere taqwa and near repentance, not wronged a thread, reward not diminished a thread; if claiming without it, the same thread proves they were not tilted against, they were not wronged. In a surah of exact shares, exact testimony, atom justice, fatil is not trivia; it is the language of mercy as accuracy, as rahmah. It answers the fear of the sincere who wonder if their small hidden charity in 4:114 was counted, if their salam returned better was counted, if their verification before sharing was counted: even a fatil is not lost, and every claimed purity is weighed.

=49=

"Look how they fabricate lies against Allah — sufficient is that as a clear sin."

Unzur kayfa yaftaruna ala Allahi al-kadhib — look — unzur with eyes of heart, not only eyes of head — how they forge lies against Allah: that honour is with disbelievers not believers in 4:139, that taking the wealth of the unlearned is blameless in 3:75 — "no blame on us concerning the unlearned, the Arabs, the gentiles" — that Fire touches only days, that riba is like trade in 2:275, that salvation is ethnic, that a heavenly book must descend. Wa kafa bihi ithman mubina — sufficient as a clear sin — ithman mubina — open, manifest, plain. Kafa — sufficient — means the crime alone suffices to ruin even without other sins, as 4:50 says kafa as ithm. Why? Because lying about Allah poisons the spring; all streams then polluted, even if they look clear downstream. The Prophet warned, "Whoever lies about me intentionally, let him take his seat in Fire;" graver still to lie about Allah who sent him, who is Al-Haqq.

For today's speaker, writer, teacher, preacher, influencer, the guardrail is sharp: do not say "Allah says" without knowledge, nor dress culture as deen to keep a donor or a crowd, nor beautify a cultural rule as Sharia. Every beautified deviation that calls bukhl prudence or riya clever branding or taghut justice is iftira that makes halal look haram and haram look halal, losing the lightness Allah wanted in 4:28 and the referral to messengers in 4:59. The opposite of iftira is 4:87 "whose statement is truer than Allah's?" — let His statement remain true in your mouth, not mixed.

"Have you not seen those given a portion of Scripture believing in jibt and taghut and saying about those who disbelieve, 'These are more guided than those who believe'?"

Alam tara ila alladhina utu nasiban min al-kitab yu'minuna bil-jibti wat-taghut — have you not seen those given a portion — nasiban — believing in jibt — idols, sorcery, soothsayers, superstitions, magic — and taghut — every false object of worship or tyrant raised beyond place, as in 2:256 "whoever disbelieves in taghut and believes in Allah has grasped the firmest handle." Those given a portion believed in those and said to Quraysh polytheists after Badr, when Quraysh were defeated and humiliated, "You are more guided than Muhammad's followers," to flatter power, to console the defeated, to gain an alliance.

Huyayy ibn Akhtab and Kab ibn al-Ashraf did so, travelling to Makkah to console Quraysh after their defeat at Badr and to declare idolatry more guided than tawhid, more correct than qist. Wa yaquluna lilladhina kafaru ha'ula'i ahda min alladhina amanu sabila — they said to disbelievers, these — mushrikun — are more guided than believers on the path — sabila. Preference inverted: they declared the way of Taghut straighter than the Way of the Messenger who taught qist, mizan, wudu, and rahmah, who returned trusts, who gave women shares.

After self-purity and lying about Allah, now they sanctify the enemy's way. Envy drives it: 4:54 will say they envy what Allah gave from His bounty. When desire settles, it revises theology to suit politics, and the tongue finally calls taghut guidance and guidance taghut, as today some call exploitation guidance and call modesty oppression, call riba wisdom and call zakah burden. The portion of the Book did not prevent it; it made it worse, because they knew better.

"They are those whom Allah has cursed. Whoever Allah curses, you will find no helper for him."

Ula'ika alladhina la'anahum Allahu — they who praised jibt and called polytheists more guided are cursed — la'nahum — expelled from mercy, distanced, banished from nearness. Wa man yal'ani Allahu fa-lan tajida lahu nasira — whoever Allah curses finds no helper — nasira who can pull from Fire, intercede, defend, as in 3:10 no helper availed hoarders of wealth and children, 4:14 no helper for transgressor of hudud, 4:88-90 no helper for cast-back, 4:123 no guardian. Curse is not for one slip but for inverting guidance after a portion of the Book was given and after bayan. =52=

"Or do they have a share of dominion? If so they would not give people even a speck."

Am lahum nasibun min al-mulk fa-idhan la yu'tuna an-nasa naqira — do they have a share of mulk — dominion, kingdom, authority — the same word in 3:26 "Owner of sovereignty gives it to whom He wills" and in 4:54 great kingdom given to family Ibrahim, and in 4:53? If they had a share, they would not give even a naqir — the tiny speck where the sprout emerges on a date stone's back, smaller than fatil, smaller than qitmir, the point of life. Those who called polytheists more guided would, if entrusted with distribution of provision or of guidance, withhold a speck. The surah proves it: they enjoin stinginess and conceal bounty in 4:37 — "they are stingy and enjoin people to be stingy and hide what Allah gave them" — they would not return a dinar unless you stand over them in 3:75 — "if you entrust him with a dinar he will not return it unless you stand over him."

Naqir, fatil, qitmir, dharrah — the surah gathers the smallest images to expose stinginess and to promise perfect justice: the hand that withholds a naqir in the world will be judged by a God who does not wrong a naqir in the Hereafter, who does not wrong a fatil, who gives for a naqir. The One who withholds a speck cannot be trusted with a share of mulk; the One who gives even a speck, who multiplies a dharrah, is Allah who multiplies a dharrah into ajr azim in 4:40 and who will give great reward.

=53=

"Or do they envy people for what Allah has given them of His bounty? We gave the family of Ibrahim the Book and wisdom and a great kingdom."

Am yahsuduna an-nasa ala ma atahumu Allahu min fadlih — do they envy people for what Allah gave from His bounty? Hasad here is the specific envy that wishes blessing removed and transferred, not the admiration that asks for similar good, as in 2:109 and 2:105 where many among People of the Book wished to return Muslims to disbelief after truth became clear after truth was known, and 2:105 grudged that mercy descended upon believers after enmity. The envied people in Madinah were the Prophet himself — given nubuwwah, given Quran, given hijrah, given Ansar — and the Muhajirun and Ansar given faith and brotherhood, and the believers given wudu, qist, and light.

Fa-qad atayna Ala Ibrahima al-Kitaba wal-hikmata wa ataynahum mulkan azima — We gave Ibrahim's family the Book — scrolls, suhuf, and Tawrah and Zabur and Injil in descendants — and wisdom — hikmah — prophetic judgment, understanding, and a great kingdom — Dawud's kingdom that judged with wisdom, Sulayman's kingdom that handled wind, jinn, and birds, and Yusuf's. If Allah gave that to the Hebrew branch of the family, why envy the Arab branch — Muhammad, son of Ibrahim through Ismail, given Book and hikmah and, soon, mulk azim in Arabia and beyond? Bounty is from the Owner of great bounty in 3:74, 4:113, 4:54, 4:70. Envy accuses the Giver as if He misplaced His gift, as if He gave to one who did not deserve, while the envier deserved. The Prophet said, "Do not envy, for envy consumes good deeds as fire consumes wood — and do not hate each other, do not turn backs, do not cut." The cure is was'alu Allaha min fadlih in 4:32 — ask Allah for bounty instead of staring at another's plate, and give, and the disease lifts, and hasad is replaced by ghibtah.

=54=

"Among them some believed in him and some turned away from him. Hell suffices as a Blaze."

Fa-minhum man amana bihi wa minhum man sadda anhu — among them — among the family of Ibrahim or among those addressed by the Prophet in Madinah — some believed in him — in Ibrahim's Book and wisdom, in Muhammad's confirmation, in the fadl — and some turned away — sadda — blocked, turned backs, barred others, as 4:61 sudud and 4:88 arkasa, and blocking many in 4:160 and 4:167. Division after fadl is not proof fadl is flawed; it is proof hearts differ when ownership is tested, when shares are divided, when qiblah changes.

Wa kafa bi-Jahannama sa'ira — Hell suffices as a Blaze — sa'ira — the same sa'ira as 4:10 yaslawna sa'ira for orphan eaters, 4:30 burn in Fire, 4:55 far was sufficient, 4:84 sa'ira for disbelievers. Kafa — sufficient — no need to enumerate each envier's name, each withholder's house; the Blaze as recompense is sufficient, as 4:55, as 4:121. Two paths after clarification in 4:26-27 end here: believed with shukr as in 4:147 or turned away with hasad, but the Blaze suffices for the turn, and Gardens suffice for the believer.

=55=

"Those who disbelieved in Our signs, We will burn them in Fire. Whenever their skins mature they will be replaced with other skins so they may taste the punishment. Allah is Mighty, Wise."

Inna alladhina kafaru bi-ayatina sawfa nuslihim naran — those who disbelieved in Our signs — ayat — the same ayat they twisted in 4:46, hidden in 4:44, called jibt guidance in 4:51, envied. Sawfa nuslihim naran — We will burn them — s-l-y, roast thoroughly — in a Fire. The future sawfa is certainty, not delay. Kullama nadijat juluduhum baddalnahum juludan ghayraha li-yadhuqu al-adhab — whenever skins mature — nadijat, fully cooked, ripened — We replace with other skins — juludan ghayraha — to taste — li-yadhuqu — punishment. Dhawq is taste, as food is tasted, as 3:181 "taste punishment." Skin is specified because skin is the organ of pain; renewal means punishment does not numb by familiarity but recovers full feeling each time, each skin fresh.

Modern knowledge of nociceptors and skin layers only deepens awe, not proves: the One who created receptors and who made the fire that does not consume in dunya for Ibrahim can make fire that renews skin for the denier. Inna Allaha kana Azizan Hakima — Mighty to carry out, Wise where to place, not hasty, not excessive a speck beyond justice as 4:40 promised no atom wrong and 4:49 no fatil, 4:53 no naqir. The verse after sa'ira and far and la'nah and withheld naqir seals threat for purchasers of error, inverters, cursed, stingy, envious: the Blaze suffices, skins renew, taste continues, Mighty and Wise ensure no injustice and no escape.

=56=

"But those who believe and do righteous deeds, We will admit them to Gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding forever. For them therein are purified spouses, and We will bring them into abundant, deep shade."

Walladhina amanu wa amilu as-salihat sa-nudkhiluhum jannatin tajri min tahtiha al-anhar khalidina fiha abadan — but the other group, believers with righteous deeds — amanu wa amilu as-salihat — the same condition as 4:57 and 4:122, the twin that was promised for san — enter Gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding forever — abadan — the longest abiding in the surah, opposite the wish to be dust in 4:42 and no mahis in 4:121. Lahum fiha azwajun mutahharah — for them therein are purified spouses — mutahharah — cleansed from earthly impurity, from menses, from rancour, from betrayal, as in 2:25 and 3:15.

Wa nudkhiluhum zillan zalila — We will bring them into abundant shade — zillan zalila — shade layered upon shade, deep, cool, permanent, as beloved shade after the skin-burning heat, as the shade of the Throne for seven, as the tree in Paradise that a rider travels under for a hundred years without crossing it. Placed immediately after ever-renewing skins, the contrast heals and teaches proportionality: the recompense mirrors the path, opposite ends, as Allah is Aziz Hakim. For women who endured slander that purified spouses were impossible, mutahharah cleans; for men who sought honour with disbelievers, shade with Allah suffices. The Prophet's description of Paradise's market where faces and clothes beautify and of rivers of water, milk, honey, and wine that does not intoxicate makes zillan zalila not abstract but taste.

=57=

"Allah commands you to return trusts to their owners and when you judge between people to judge with justice. How excellent is what Allah admonishes you with! Allah is Hearing, Seeing."

Inna Allaha ya'murukum an tu'addul-amanati ila ahliha — Allah commands you to return trusts — amanati — to owners — ahliha. Amanah is every trust: orphan wealth in 4:2, dowry in 4:4, secrets, testimony, leadership, the key of the House, a worker's wage, a borrower's return, a borrower's book. To ahliha — to rightful owners, not to the powerful or the liked or the donor's tribe, not to the husband who wants the wife's gold. Wa idha hakamtum bayna an-nasi an tahkumu bil-adl — when you judge between people, judge with justice — adl — the same qist and mizan that govern inheritance shares in 4:11-12 and 4:176, qawwam duties in 4:34, arbitration in 4:35, referral in 4:59.

Inna Allaha ni'imma ya'izukum bihi — excellent is the admonition He exhorts you with — ni'imma — excellent, beautiful, as 4:58's second line, as Luqman's counsel. Inna Allaha kana Sami'an Basira — Hearing what you promise in private, Seeing how you divide in public, as Raqib and Shahid and Muhit and Sami'. Revelation context: at the conquest of Makkah the Prophet ordered the key of the Ka'bah returned to Uthman ibn Talhah, its keeper before Islam though not yet Muslim at that moment, though Ali desired it for prestige and Abbas asked. The Prophet read this verse and returned the key to Uthman, saying today is the day of fulfilling trusts. Justice that returns a key to a disbeliever teaches justice that returns a sister's quarter of a house when a brother prefers to keep the key to the larger room.

After Fire skins and Gardens shade that detail eternal recompense, the command shows how to live to reach one and avoid the other: return and judge with adl. For today's manager, judge, teacher, parent, the amanah is the grade, the share, the secret, the key.

=58=

"O believers, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. If you dispute about anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you truly believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is better and a more excellent conclusion."

Ya ayyuha alladhina amanu ati'u Allaha wa ati'u ar-rasula wa uli al-amri minkum — obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority — uli al-amr — scholars and rulers whose authority is bounded within the first two obediences, as the Prophet said, "No obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator" and "You must listen and obey even if an Abyssinian slave is appointed over you, so long as he leads you with the Book." Imam Ahmad placed scholars as uli al-amr of knowledge, Umar placed rulers as uli al-amr of command; both are within, neither absolute.

Fa-in tanaza'tum fi shay'in fa-rudduhu ila Allahi war-rasuli — if you dispute — tanaza'tum — pulling opposite ways — about anything — fi shay'in — any matter of worship, family, money, news, war, method — refer it — rudduhu — to Allah's Book and the Messenger's Sunnah, living through his judgment and after him through his hadith and his way, as 4:59's referral is the test. In kuntum tu'minuna billahi wal-yawmi al-akhir — if you believe in Allah and the Last Day — the test of belief is referral when dispute hardens, as seen after distortion of words in 4:46 and after fearing people more than Allah in 4:77.

Dhalika khayrun wa ahsanu ta'wilan — that is better — khayrun — as khayr in 4:25 and 4:128 sulh khayr — and more excellent ta'wil — outcome, interpretation, end. The best ending for the family breach in 4:35 and the doctrinal distortion in 4:46 and the fear in 4:77 is not tribal counsel or trending fatwa or personal opinion but the Book and Sunnah. The verse that began with returning trusts to owners now returns disputes to Owners: keep covenants and return quarrels to the One who entrusted them, and the outcome is ahsanu.

=59=

"Have you not seen those who claim they believe in what was sent down to you and what was sent down before, yet wish to refer judgment to taghut though they were commanded to disbelieve in it? Satan wants to lead them far astray."

Alam tara ila alladhina yaz'umuna annahum amanu bima unzila ilayka wa ma unzila min qablika yuriduna an yatahakamu ila at-taghut — have you not seen those who claim — yaz'umuna — they believe in what was sent to you and what was sent before, yet they wish — yuriduna — to refer judgment to taghut — any tyrant, idol, or false law to which judgment is referred besides Allah, as in 2:256 "whoever disbelieves in taghut and believes in Allah has grasped the firmest handle" and 4:76's taghut path, 4:51's jibt and taghut, and 4:60 is the locus. Wa qad umiru an yakfuru bihi — though they were commanded to disbelieve in it — the same command as tawhid's first half "reject taghut, believe in Allah." Wa yuridu ash-Shaytanu an yudillahum dalalan ba'idan — Satan wants to lead them far astray — dalalan ba'idan — far stray, the same far as shirk in 4:116, 4:60, 4:136, 4:167, 4:169, a far that is not near.

The case in Madinah: a hypocrite disputed with a Jew over a debt; the Jew said "let us go to Muhammad" despite being Jewish, because he knew the Prophet judged with justice even against himself; the hypocrite said "let us go to Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf," a Jewish chief who took bribes and could be swayed, because there judgment could be bought. Claim without referral exposes the claim. Iman is not wished, not claimed, not inherited; it is referred, it goes to Allah and His Messenger when it hurts. The one who wishes for taghut while claiming belief is the buyer of error from 4:44 now seeking a corrupt court to ratify the purchase.

=60=

"When it is said to them, 'Come to what Allah has revealed and to the Messenger,' you see the hypocrites turning away with aversion."

Wa idha qila lahum ta'alaw ila ma anzala Allahu wa ila ar-rasuli ra'ayta al-munafiqina yasudduna anka sududa — when it is said, "Come — ta'alaw — to what Allah revealed and to the Messenger," you see — ra'ayta — with your own eyes — the hypocrites turning — yasudduna — blocking, averting — with sududa — strong, insulated aversion, a wall, a shoulder. The verb sadd appears again: they block themselves and they block others, the same sadd as those who avert from Allah's path in 4:167, who wished you to deviate in 4:27. Their hearts already wanted maylan azima in 4:27; their limbs now perform sudud. The Prophet would see it live: invitation extended, faces turned, excuses formed, as Abdullah ibn Ubayy turned with three hundred at Uhud, as the hypocrites turned from Tabuk.

For us the test is not a theoretical taghut of old books but preference in daily life: when a family inheritance must be divided as in 4:11-12, when a marital conflict needs arbitration as in 4:35, when a business partnership disputes riba, when a social media argument turns on a verse, does the heart say "let us return to Allah and His Messenger" as 4:59 commands — with the Book and the authentic Sunnah, understood with the scholars — or does it shop for the forum where the fixed share disappears, where four witnesses become a screenshot, where taghut's ease says "keep it"? The turning with sudud is visible today as delay, mockery, "that verse is historical, it was for then," "our culture says otherwise," "the law says otherwise," while 4:105 insists the Book was sent to judge with what Allah showed — bima araka Allahu — not with what desire shows. The ayah closes the block that began with trusts and justice: trustworthy judgment is referral, and refusal with sudud is hypocrisy seen.
