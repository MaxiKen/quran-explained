After the powerful glorification of the Lord of Honour and Power, the surah concludes with a beautiful, universal greeting of peace that encompasses the entire brotherhood of prophets. The verse states simply yet profoundly, “Peace be upon the messengers.

” This is not merely a passing salutation; it is a divine decree of safety, honour, and eternal serenity for all those noble souls who carried the message of tawheed to humanity throughout the ages. The word “Peace” (salām) here comes directly from Allah, making it the highest form of greeting and blessing. It is the same peace that was individually bestowed upon Noah, Abraham, Moses and Aaron, Elias, and implied for the other prophets mentioned in the surah. Now, at the end, all the messengers are collectively bathed in this divine peace. This demonstrates the unity of their mission and their shared destiny. They faced rejection, mockery, persecution, and immense hardship from their respective peoples, yet they stood firm. The peace of Allah is their ultimate reward, a state of perfect tranquility, security from all harm, and the bliss of divine nearness. This salutation also serves as an instruction to the believers: we are to extend this greeting to all the prophets. Muslims are required to believe in all the messengers without distinction and to honour them with the words “peace be upon him” whenever their names are mentioned. The verse thus shapes the consciousness of the ummah, linking us in love and respect to the great line of chosen servants. It refutes any notion that the Prophet Muhammad was in conflict with previous prophets; rather, he is their brother, and his message is the completion of their collective call. The greeting “Peace be upon the messengers” also resonates with the scene of Paradise earlier in the surah, where the chosen servants are in Gardens of Bliss, experiencing peace. The messengers are the forerunners of that peace.

For the believer, this verse is a source of comfort and hope. It assures us that the path of the messengers, however difficult, leads to the ultimate peace from the Lord of all worlds. It also serves as a farewell from the surah, wrapping up the historical narratives and the arguments with a gentle, unifying blessing. After the intensity of the refutations and the vivid descriptions of punishment, the surah leaves the listener with the serene image of the messengers resting in divine peace. It is as if the entire storm of the surah settles into this calm, reminding us that the goal of all the warnings, arguments, and stories is to lead humanity to the peace that comes from submission to the One God. The believers are implicitly included in this salutation, for they are the followers of the messengers, and the peace of Allah descends upon those who follow their guidance. The verse is a simple sentence, yet it carries the weight of the entire history of prophethood, offering a moment of reflection and a deep breath of spiritual tranquility before the final word of praise that closes the surah.

=182=

The surah concludes with the most comprehensive and fitting declaration of praise, bringing the entire discourse to a majestic close. “And praise be to Allah—Lord of all worlds.

” The word “praise” (al-ḥamd) is a definitive, all-encompassing term that signifies gratitude, extolment, and the acknowledgment of perfection. It is not a request or a description but a statement of reality: all praise, in every form, from every tongue, in every time, belongs solely to Allah.

This is the same phrase that opens the Quran in Surah Al-Fatiha, and here it serves as the final seal of Surah As-Saffat, creating a beautiful symmetry. The phrase “Lord of all worlds” (rabbi al-ʿālamīn) proclaims Allah’s absolute Lordship over every realm of existence—the world of angels, the world of humans, the world of jinn, the world of animals, the celestial spheres, the unseen, and the seen. After a surah that has taken the listener on a journey through the ranks of angels, the adornments of the lowest heaven, the depths of Hell, the Gardens of Bliss, the stories of the prophets across different nations, and the refutation of the pagan myths about jinn and angels, this conclusion is profoundly fitting. All of these “worlds” are under the Lordship of Allah, and He alone is deserving of unceasing praise.

The placement of this verse as the final word is deliberate. It teaches that the culmination of all knowledge, all reflection, and all revelation is the praise of Allah. After hearing about the destruction of the deniers and the salvation of the chosen servants, the natural response of the heart is to praise the One who is perfectly just and perfectly merciful. After witnessing the logical arguments against idolatry, the mind is compelled to praise the One who is the sole Creator and Sustainer. The verse transforms the entire surah into an act of worship, because the listener is invited to end the recitation with this declaration of praise. It is a verbal act of aligning oneself with the angels who are “constantly glorifying His praise,” and with the messengers who lived and died in His praise. For the Meccan pagans, this was a final call to abandon the false praise they offered to idols and to direct all praise to the true Lord of the worlds. For the believers, it is a daily creed: whatever happens, in ease or hardship, praise belongs to Allah. The hardship of the Meccan period was severe, yet the surah instructs the believers to seal their recitation with “praise be to Allah,” acknowledging that even in the struggle, there is wisdom and mercy that warrants praise.

This verse also connects to the Hereafter, where the final call of the people of Paradise will be “Praise be to Allah, Lord of all worlds.” Thus, the surah’s end is a preview of the eternal joy of the believers. It leaves the soul in a state of serene gratitude, focused on the majesty and mercy of the Creator, and ready to face the trials of life with the knowledge that the ultimate reality is the praise of the Lord of all worlds. This completes the thematic arc from the oath of the angels to the glorification of the Lord and the praise that will echo for eternity.
