This verse delivers the final, conclusive proof in the series of arguments for the resurrection. It takes the form of a rhetorical question that leaves no room for doubt. “Can the One Who created the heavens and the earth not easily resurrect these deniers?” The argument here scales up from the previous proofs.

The earlier verses spoke of the creation of humans from a sperm-drop and the bringing of fire from green trees; now the horizon is expanded to the largest conceivable scale. The heavens and the earth, with all their immensity, complexity, and majesty, are the greatest manifestations of Allah’s creative power known to human beings. If Allah originated this entire universe without any prior model or material, how could He be incapable of reassembling human bones and bringing the dead back to life? The implied answer is clear: the greater creation testifies to the ease of the lesser recreation.

The word “easily” is not in the Arabic text but is a necessary clarification of the meaning, because for the Almighty, nothing is difficult. This question also directly addresses the root cause of disbelief in the resurrection. The disbelievers imagined that a scattered, decayed body was beyond recovery because they measured divine power by their own limited standards. The verse destroys this fallacy by pointing them to the limitless power that holds up the sky and spreads out the earth. The emphatic response “Yes He can!” (balā) is a powerful affirmation that cuts off any argument.

The verse then grounds this power in two divine names: “For He is the Master Creator, All-Knowing.” The name “Master Creator” (al-Khallāq) is an intensive form, indicating the One who creates again and again, on a vast scale, with perfect mastery. The creation of the heavens and the earth is just one aspect of His continuous, endless creation.

The name “All-Knowing” (al-ʿAlīm) addresses the supposed difficulty of gathering the scattered particles. The Master Creator is also the All-Knowing; He does not lose track of any atom in His creation. Every bone, every cell, every trace of every human being is fully known to Him.

Thus, the combination of perfect power and perfect knowledge makes the resurrection not only possible but inevitable. For the believer, this verse inspires awe and trust. The same Master Creator who fashioned the cosmos will faithfully and easily bring His servants back to life for judgment.

=82=

Having established that resurrection is easy for the One who created the heavens and the earth, this verse now reveals the breathtakingly simple mechanism of divine creation. “All it takes, when He wills something to be, is simply to say to it: ‘Be!’ And it is!

” The phrase “All it takes” translates the Arabic innamā, which indicates restriction and exclusivity. There is no other process, no labour, no need for tools, materials, or time. The divine will directly translates into existence through the single command “Be” (kun).

This command is not a physical sound but the expression of Allah’s absolute will and decree. When He wills a thing, its existence becomes inevitable in that very instant.

The phrase “And it is” (fa-yakūn) underscores the immediacy and certainty of the result. This verse is one of the most powerful statements in the Quran about the nature of divine power. It explains how the vast universe came into being from nothing: by a single word of command. It also explains how the resurrection will occur. There is no need for a lengthy biological process of regrowth; the dead will simply be commanded to rise, and they will rise. This answers the mockery of the disbelievers about decayed bones in the most profound way: the same command that brought the first creation into existence will bring about the second.

The verse also has profound implications for the believer’s understanding of Allah’s will in daily life. When a believer supplicates, they know that if Allah wills a matter, nothing can prevent it, and He can bring it about with ease.

However, this verse does not negate the wisdom behind Allah’s use of means and gradual processes in the world; rather, it states that Allah is not bound by those means. He uses them by His wisdom, but He can also dispense with them by His power. The verse is a direct comfort for those who suffer under oppression or illness, for it reminds them that their Lord can change any situation in an instant, with a single word. It also serves as a warning: the punishment can come just as suddenly, by that same command. The verse thus combines awe, hope, and fear, drawing the heart to complete reliance on the One whose command is absolute.

=83=

The surah concludes with a magnificent verse of glorification that ties together all its major themes. “So glory be to the One in Whose Hands is the authority over all things.

” The word “glory be” (subḥān) declares Allah’s absolute perfection, transcendence, and freedom from any flaw, partner, or deficiency. It is an exclamation of praise and wonder. The surah has presented numerous signs—from the dead earth coming to life, to the celestial bodies in their orbits, to the cattle, the ship, the fire from green trees, and the resurrection of bones. All of these point to the perfection and power of the Creator, and the appropriate response is to glorify Him. The description “in Whose Hands is the authority over all things” is especially significant. The “Hand” is an expression of power, possession, and control. The plural form “Hands” may imply the fullness of authority and the comprehensive nature of His dominion. The “authority” (malakūt) refers to the ultimate kingship and governance over the entire universe. Every atom, every soul, every system is under His absolute control. The surah began with the disjointed letters and an oath on the wise Quran, and it now ends with a declaration of the sovereignty of the One who revealed it.

The final clause, “and to Whom alone you will all be returned,” brings the listener back to the central reality of the surah: the return to Allah. After all the proofs of resurrection and the accounts of previous nations, the inevitable conclusion is that every human being will stand before this King. The return is “to Him alone,” meaning there is no escape, no other destination, and no one else to turn to. The surah, which was a wake-up call to the heedless, ends by placing the reader directly before their Lord, reminding them of the absolute authority that will encompass them on the Day of Return. This verse is a prayer, a meditation, and a warning all in one. The believer says these words, glorifying the Lord who holds everything in His hands, and feels both immense peace and profound responsibility. The surah’s journey from the disjointed letters to the glorification of the Lord of all authority is now complete, leaving the heart filled with awe and the mind convinced of the truth of resurrection.
