"He said, 'My Lord, make for me a sign.' He said, 'Your sign is that you will not speak to the people for three days except by gesture. And remember your Lord much and glorify Him in the evening and the morning.'"

Zakariyya asks for a sign to mark the gift — not because he doubts Allah's power, but because the heart loves a marker when the gift is enormous. The sign is silence toward people for three days except by gesture — while the tongue is freed and ordered for dhikr: remember your Lord much, glorify in the evening and the morning. The miracle includes a discipline of speech. When the mouth is closed to small talk, it opens to tasbih.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day should speak good or remain silent, and he taught morning and evening adhkar as shields of the day and night. He said the most beloved speech to Allah is Subhan Allah wa bi-hamdih. Zakariyya's sign becomes a spiritual schedule for every believer who is not literally struck silent: less chatter, more dhikr at the two ends of the day.

If Allah gives you a private sign or a fresh hope — a pregnancy after despair, a job after drought, a return of a child to prayer — pay for it with remembrance, not with endless announcement and pride. Evening and morning: bookend the day with glory so the gift does not become heedlessness. Many people receive and forget. Zakariyya received and was ordered to remember much.

Try a three-day practice even without a miraculous silence: reduce unnecessary speech, increase morning and evening adhkar, and watch how the heart clears. The sign taught that the tongue is either a tool of people-pleasing noise or a tool of dhikr. Choose which sign you want your life to carry.

Silence toward people and speech toward Allah is a prophetic training still available without a miracle. Try guarding the tongue for a morning and filling the same hours with subhan Allah, alhamdulillah, and istighfar. Zakariyya's sign teaches that the gift of a child — or any breakthrough — is protected by dhikr, not by announcing it to everyone who will feed your ego. Evening and morning are the hinges of the day; if they belong to Allah, the middle is easier to reclaim. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said the two phrases light on the tongue, heavy on the scale, beloved to the Most Merciful, are subhan Allahi wa bi-hamdih and subhan Allahi al-azim. Put them where Zakariyya was told to put glorification, and let your "sign" become a habit rather than a three-day exception.

"And when the angels said, 'O Maryam, indeed Allah has chosen you and purified you and chosen you above the women of the worlds.'"

Double choice frames purification. Istafaki — He chose you — and purified you — and chose you above the women of the worlds. Purification sits between the two choosings, as if to say honour arrives with cleanliness of soul, not instead of it. Maryam is not random celebrity. She is selected and cleaned for a role no other woman carries in the same way: mother of Messiah by a word from Allah, without husband, under a cloud of slander she will endure with the help of her infant's speech.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said the best of the women of her world was Maryam bint Imran, and the best of the women of her world was Khadijah bint Khuwaylid — and in other narrations he mentioned Fatimah, and Asiyah the wife of Pharaoh. Honouring Maryam does not cancel the excellence of other believing women; it names a unique summit in purity and trial. Choice of Maryam is also a refutation of those who despise women as a class: Allah places a woman at the centre of a surah's miracle narrative and has angels address her by name.

Young women and those who raise them: purification is the path of being chosen for great trust. The worlds' applause is not the goal; the angels' address is. Chosen, purified, chosen again — ask for that sequence in your own measure. Purity of body, food, gaze, intention, and tawhid. Without purification, "chosen" language becomes delusion of superiority.

Men: read this ayah without the disease of envy or the disease of reducing women to temptation only. Allah chose Maryam above the women of the worlds. Your mother, wife, daughter, and sister walk under a sky where such choice was possible. Honour them with the manners of a community that received this verse.

Chosenness without purification is a mirage every celebrity culture sells. Maryam's sequence refuses that mirage: chosen, purified, chosen. The middle verb is the bridge. When Muslim girls are told they are honourable but are not taught wudu, haya, and Quran, we have dropped the bridge. The Prophet, peace be upon him, stood for his daughter Fatimah when she entered, kissed her, and sat her in his place — honour in manners — while also teaching the ummah the prayer and the limits. Maryam above the women of the worlds is not a statue; she is a curriculum of purity and worship.

Chosen status does not cancel worship; it intensifies it. Uqnuti li-rabbiki — be qanitah, devotedly obedient to your Lord — prostrate, and bow with those who bow. Join the congregation of servants. The order to Maryam is the order to every person tempted to think they have outgrown congregational humility. Even her purification was for more worship, not for less.

When you next see someone celebrated for deen, check whether their celebration includes the prayer row or only the stage. Maryam was ordered into the row of those who bow. Join her there. The angels' praise and the forehead on the ground are not rivals; they are partners.

Prostrate and bow with those who bow — the order kills spiritual arrogance at its root. Even the best of women is told to join a row, not to float above it. In an age of personal branding of piety, this ayah is ice water. Your special story with Allah, if authentic, increases sujud, not seating preference. Callers to Islam can still use this ayah gently with those who know Biblical material: the Quran does not copy clumsily; it corrects and details with authority. And believers can use it inwardly: if he was told what he never saw, then believe him about what you have not yet seen.

Maryam receives news that would break an ordinary mind: a child, a word, a messiah, nearness to Allah. The same Lord who chose and purified her now trusts her with a burden of fame and trial. Word from Him, Messiah, son of Maryam, honour in both worlds, among those brought near — five honours, zero divinity. Learn to love Isa with the Quran's adjectives only. "She said, 'My Lord, how will I have a child when no man has touched me?' The angel said, 'Thus is Allah; He creates what He wills. When He decrees a matter, He only says to it, "Be," and it is.'"

Maryam's how mirrors Zakariyya's how — wonder within faith, not mockery. No man has touched me. The answer: Allah creates what He wills. When He decrees a matter, He only says to it kun fa-yakun — Be, and it is. The same creative word as 2:117 and as the creation of the heavens. Virgin birth is not harder for the One who made Adam without father or mother. The womb that never knew a man is as available to "Be" as the dust that never knew a parent.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, affirmed Isa's miraculous birth and rejected both the denial of the miracle and the leap from miracle to divinity. Creation by "Be" is the theology of power; worship belongs only to the Speaker, not to the one spoken into being. He said do not exaggerate in praising me as the Christians exaggerated with the son of Maryam; I am only a servant.

When your "how" feels clean of means, remember kun. Then walk the path of causes when causes exist, and bow to the One who does not need them when He chooses to bypass them. Maryam's chastity was not a problem for Allah; it was a stage for His will. Modern arrogance that says "science forbids this" has mistaken description of usual causes for legislation over the Causer.

Hold two refusals together: refuse the atheist scoff at the virgin birth, and refuse the theological excess that turns the born child into a god. Kun fa-yakun is enough for both refusals. The Wise creates; the servant is created; the path is straight when those roles are not confused.

Kun fa-yakun is the short theology that ends long arguments about "impossibility." Usual causes are Allah's custom, not His prison. He binds the world with law and unbinds it when He wills a sign. Maryam's untouched womb is not harder than the first human from dust or the revival of land after death. The Prophet, peace be upon him, taught us to take means and still know that means do not create results — Allah does. Say "Be" with your trust when the door looks welded. The Prophet, peace be upon him, added the return of Isa as just ruler — a future that completes purification of his message. Live so that you would recognise him by tawhid and justice, not by the slogans of movements that would shock the Messiah they claim. Disbelievers in this drama — those who rejected Isa and plotted — face severe punishment in dunya and akhirah, with no helpers. Historical humiliation and eschatological Fire both sit in the warning. He said a person will be with those he loves, and he warned against hating the ansar and the prophets' legacies. Severe punishment has two addresses: now and later.

Do not build your safety on being anti-prophet in any generation. No helpers is the loneliness of those who thought the crowd was a fortress. The crowd leaves at the edge of the grave, and at the edge of divine seizure in history.

If you have opposed a truth-teller because the crowd did, repent before you inherit this ayah's loneliness. Severe is a word the Quran does not waste. Pair it with the next ayah's hope for believers so that fear and work travel together.

Severe punishment in this world and the next, no helpers — the end of those who disbelieve after the plot narrative. And if you fear this ayah for yourself because of past rejection, pair it with belief and righteous deeds in the next ayah and with tawbah while the door moves.

"But as for those who believe and do righteous deeds, He will give them in full their rewards. And Allah does not like the wrongdoers."

Belief plus righteous deeds brings full reward — yuwaffihim ujurahum. Allah does not like wrongdoers. The split is moral and clear after the drama of plots: faith and work on one side, zulm on the other. Dislike of zalimun means oppression and shirk both sit under divine displeasure — including the zulm of saying Allah has a son (18:5).

Stack iman and salihat until the file is heavy. Avoid zulm so you do not move into the category He does not like. Full wages are worth more than the short-term win of a plot. The disciples who seemed weak against the plotters are the ones this ayah pays in full.

"This is what We recite to you of the verses and the wise reminder."

Allah tells the Prophet: this narrative is what We recite to you — ayat and the dhikr al-hakim, the wise reminder. Wise reminder means the story should change how you speak about Isa, Maryam, and disputes of theology — with clarity, respect for the messenger, and refusal of both insult and worship.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, was taught by Jibril as a student of the unseen, and he conveyed without adding from himself. He said convey from me even a verse. Teachers: teach the Maryam section with the adab of the Quran — beauty, precision, mercy, and firmness. Students: take it as reminder for your own tawhid, not only as material to win arguments. The reminder that does not remind your own heart has missed you even if it hit your opponent.

Wise reminder — dhikr hakim — is the genre of the Isa story. Recite it to change your speech about Jesus, your adab with Christians, your tawhid, your refusal of both insult and worship. "Indeed, the example of Isa to Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust; then He said to him, 'Be,' and he was."

The decisive analogy of the surah. Mathalu Isa 'inda Allahi ka-mathali Adam: the example of Isa with Allah is like the example of Adam. Both created outside ordinary fatherhood. Adam from dust with no parents; Isa from a mother with no father. Which is more astonishing biologically? Adam. Yet no community that affirms Adam worships him as God on that basis. Kun fa-yakun again. The verse is a gentle demolishing of the claim that virgin birth equals divinity.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, affirmed both creations and used clear analogy in da'wah. He said Adam is the father of humanity, and Isa is His word thrown to Maryam and a spirit from Him — language of honour in Quran and sunnah, not incarnation. He forbade the Christian excess and the Jewish diminishment alike in the balance of revelation.

If someone elevates Isa for his birth, invite them to Adam. If they stop at wonder, join them in wonder — then worship only the One who said Be. Dust and cradle are both stages for the same Creator's power. The analogy does not insult Isa; it places him with Adam among the greatest of created signs.

Memorise this ayah if you engage in dialogue. It is short, rational, respectful, and devastating to the leap from miracle to godhood. Then live its implication: you too are a "Be" from dust. Do not walk like a self-made deity. Walk like Adam's child who knows the Speaker.

Adam from dust, Isa from a word — same Creator, same "Be," different stages. The analogy is the most portable da'wah line in the surah. Use it with respect. Do not mock anyone's love for Isa; redirect it to the Lord Isa worshipped. And apply it to yourself: you are closer to Adam's dust than you admit when pride inflates. Kun brought you; kun will raise you; kun does not make you a god.
