=261=

"The example of those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah is like a seed that grows seven spikes; in each spike is a hundred grains. And Allah multiplies for whom He wills. And Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing."

Charity becomes agriculture in Allah's math: one grain, seven ears, a hundred in each — up to seven hundredfold — and He multiplies beyond for whom He wills. Wasi' and 'Alim — His treasury is not strained, and He knows which seed was planted for Him versus for show or for the camera.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said whoever gives the like of a date from good earnings — and Allah only accepts good — Allah receives it with His right hand and grows it for the giver as one of you grows his foal until it is like a mountain. He also said the best charity is what is given from surplus while the giver remains free of need, and he loved dates given with clean intention.

Plant. Do not wait for a fortune. The multiplier is His specialty; the planting is yours. Encompassing means the return will not bounce for lack of funds in heaven. Knowing means a forgotten coin in a poor person's hand is not forgotten with Him — and a loud donation with a filthy intention is not grown, however pretty the press release.

"Those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah and then do not follow up what they have spent with reminders of it or with injury — they will have their reward with their Lord, and there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve."

Giving is completed by silence and gentleness. Mann — reminding the recipient of the favour — and adha — hurting with words or attitude — cancel the beauty of the gift even if the amount was large. Reward stays with Allah for those who spend cleanly; the familiar pair returns: no fear, no grief.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said the one who reminds of his gift does not get that reward, and he listed the mannan among those Allah will not look at on the Day of Rising. He gave in secret so that the left hand would not know what the right spent, and he taught that the poor have a right in wealth without being made to perform gratitude like hired applause.

If you give, let the gift arrive without a bill of emotional interest. The poor already carry enough. Your reward is with your Lord, not in their lifelong embarrassment. Delete the sentence "after all I did for you" from your family and charity vocabulary if you want this ayah's ending to be yours.

"He gives wisdom to whom He wills, and whoever has been given wisdom has certainly been given much good. And none will remember except those of understanding."

Hikmah — wisdom, putting knowledge in its right place, and in many reports the Prophetic sunnah — is a gift that counts as abundant khayr by Allah's own label. Not every clever person receives it; those of core reason, ulu al-albab, take admonition from it when it arrives.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, prayed for Ibn Abbas to be given hikmah and understanding of the Book, and he said the wise word is the lost property of the believer. He also said there may be a dusty, overlooked person whom Allah has given wisdom that steadies rooms full of polished fools.

Seek hikmah more than trivia and more than winning. Much good is already the sticker Allah placed on it. Remembering — reflection that changes the next choice — is how the understanding heart cashes the gift. Without that, even a library is only weight on a shelf.

Carry the verse into one concrete decision this week. The longest surah in the Quran did not spend its length so that readers could nod and remain unchanged. Belief in al-Baqarah is proven in what you next refuse and what you next begin.
