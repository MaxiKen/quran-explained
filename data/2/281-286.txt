=281=

"And fear a Day when you will be returned to Allah. Then every soul will be compensated for what it earned, and they will not be wronged."

The riba block ends looking at the Day. Fear a day of return to Allah. Every soul is paid what it earned — kasabat — without zulm. The same justice that forbade devouring people by interest forbids shorting anyone in the final payroll. Creditors and debtors, givers and withholders, all stand in one queue that does not take bribes.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said rights will be given to their owners on the Day of Resurrection until the hornless sheep is given its right from the horned sheep. He wept at nights from verses of return and accountability, and he said the bankrupt is the one who comes with prayer and fasting and charity but has abused people so that his good deeds are taken until none remain and their sins are heaped on him.

Audit yourself before that payroll. Interest, unpaid wages, buried charities, sharp contracts, insults that tore dignity — settle what you can while settlement is still called tawbah and not qisas of the Day. They will not be wronged — which also means you will not be able to wrong your way into a better outcome once the return is complete.

Leave the labels that make riba sound clever — 'growth product,' 'convenience fee,' 'time value' dressed to hide excess. The Quran already knew the arguments and still drew the line. Your task is exit with tawbah and principal, not a better brand name for the same war.

=282=

"O you who believe, when you contract a debt for a specified term, write it down. And let a scribe write it between you in justice. Let no scribe refuse to write as Allah has taught him. So let him write and let the one who has the obligation dictate. And let him fear Allah, his Lord, and not leave anything out of it. But if the one who has the obligation is of limited understanding or weak or unable to dictate himself, then let his guardian dictate in justice. And call to witness two witnesses from among your men. And if there are not two men available, then a man and two women from those whom you accept as witnesses — so that if one of the women errs, then the other can remind her. And let not the witnesses refuse when they are called upon. And do not be too weary to write it, whether it is small or large, for its specified term. That is more just with Allah and stronger as evidence and more likely to prevent doubt between you, except when it is immediate trade which you conduct among yourselves. For then there is no blame upon you if you do not write it. And take witnesses when you conclude a contract. Let no scribe be harmed or any witness. For if you do so, indeed, it is grave disobedience in you. And fear Allah. And Allah teaches you. And Allah is Knowing of all things."

The longest ayah in the Quran is a merciful bureaucracy: term debts should be written by a just scribe who may not refuse as Allah taught him; the debtor dictates under taqwa without subtracting; guardians help the weak; witnesses — two men, or one man and two women in the wording of this commercial context, with the stated purpose of mutual reminding — are called and may not refuse without cause; small and large terms both deserve ink; spot trades are excused from writing but witnessing is still urged; harming scribes or witnesses is fusuq; taqwa, teaching from Allah, and His knowledge seal the system.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, bought and sold with clarity, ordered fulfilment of contracts, and said Muslims are bound by their conditions except a condition that makes haram what is halal or the reverse. Writing is not distrust of every brother; it is protection of brotherhood from the shaytan of forgetfulness and dispute.

If you lend or defer payment, write. If you witness, show up. If you scribe, be just. The longest verse is long because Allah loves that your friendships survive your finances. He teaches you in the verse itself — and He still knows every off-books trick you might prefer.

=283=

"And if you are on a journey and cannot find a scribe, then a security deposit should be handed over. But if one of you entrusts another, then let him who is entrusted discharge his trust and let him fear Allah, his Lord. And do not conceal testimony, for whoever conceals it — his heart is indeed sinful. And Allah is Knowing of what you do."

Travel without a scribe: take rahn, a pledged security. If one trusts another without pledge, the trustee must return the amanah and fear Allah. Concealing testimony sins the heart itself — ithm of the qalb — not only the tongue. Knowing of what you do closes the commercial ethics block.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, died with his armour pledged to a Jewish creditor for food, teaching that pledge is legitimate and that dealing justly with non-Muslims in contracts is sunnah. He said the one who has no amanah has no faith, and he cursed false testimony beside shirk in some warnings.

On the road or at home, protect the debt. Carry trusts to their doors. Speak testimony when justice needs it. A sinful heart is too high a price for silence that shields a thief.

Carry the verse into one concrete decision this week. The longest surah in the Quran did not spend its length so that readers could nod and remain unchanged. Belief in al-Baqarah is proven in what you next refuse and what you next begin.

"To Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth. Whether you show what is within yourselves or conceal it, Allah will bring you to account for it. Then He will forgive whom He wills and punish whom He wills, and Allah is over all things competent."

Ownership is cosmic; accounting is intimate. What you reveal or hide in the self is under hisab. Forgiveness and punishment are His to assign; competence covers both. Early companions felt the weight of this ayah on inner thoughts until the following verses taught a prayerful resolution; the sunnah preserves their fear and the relief that followed with 285-286.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said Allah overlooks for his ummah the whispers of the selves so long as they do not act or speak on them — a mercy that fits with bringing to account what the heart settles and intends. Competence means no file is too inner to open, and no king is too outer to own.

Clean the public and the private. Seek the will that forgives. The heavens and earth already belong to Him; your hidden envy and hidden sincerity both sit in a kingdom He owns completely. Let that ownership make secret sin less attractive and secret good less lonely.

=285=

"The Messenger has believed in what was revealed to him from his Lord, and so have the believers. All of them have believed in Allah and His angels and His books and His messengers, saying, 'We make no distinction between any of His messengers.' And they say, 'We hear and we obey. Your forgiveness, our Lord, and to You is the destination.'"

A portrait of the Prophet and the believers together: iman in the revelation, in Allah, angels, books, messengers without the sectarian distinction of rejecting some, and the slogan of the healthy heart — we hear and we obey. Then need: your forgiveness, our Lord; to You is the return. Knowledge and obedience still end in istighfar because the servant never outgrows need.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said whoever recites the last two ayat of al-Baqarah at night, they suffice him. He was given these endings as a treasure for the ummah. "We hear and obey" is the opposite of the Children of Israel's "we hear and disobey" (2:93). He lived the creed he recited — belief first, then a life of hearing and obeying, then forgiveness sought until his last breath.

Say it until it organises your life: no picking among messengers, no hearing without obeying, no obedience without asking forgiveness, no day without remembering the return. The Messenger already believed. Catch up with him in the same sentence, then prove the sentence in the next choice you make when obedience costs.

=286=

"Allah does not charge a soul except with that within its capacity. It will have what it has earned, and it will bear what it has earned against it. Our Lord, do not take us to task if we forget or make a mistake. Our Lord, do not lay upon us a burden like that which You laid upon those before us. Our Lord, do not burden us with that which we have no ability to bear. And pardon us; and forgive us; and have mercy upon us. You are our protector, so give us victory over the disbelieving people."

The surah ends with a principle and a du'a. No soul is tasked beyond wus'aha — its scope. What it earns of good is for it; what it earns of evil is against it. Then the ummah is taught to ask: do not punish our forgetfulness and mistakes; do not load us as nations before were loaded; do not burden us with what we cannot bear; pardon, forgive, have mercy; You are our Mawla — so help us against the people who reject.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said Allah answered this du'a. When the companions feared the accounting of inner thoughts in 284, these closing ayat came as relief and as liturgy. He recited them at night as sufficient. Forgiveness layered as i'fu / ighfir / irham is escalating clemency: erase, cover, envelop in mercy. Victory is asked under the title of wilayah — not colonial appetite, but protection and triumph of faith under the only Guardian.

Live under the principle when duties feel crushing: He already promised the task fits the soul, even when the soul must stretch. Pray the du'a as owners of a treasure left at the end of the longest surah. Forgetfulness and mistake will come; the answered request is that they not become your ruin. He is your Mawla. Ask Him for the victory you cannot author, and walk the capacity He measured with the mercy He taught you to request by name.
