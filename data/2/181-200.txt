=181=

"Then whoever alters it after he has heard it — the sin is only upon those who alter it. Indeed, Allah is Hearing and Knowing."

A will made in fairness can still be attacked after the voice that made it goes silent. This verse pins the crime on the changer. The deceased who ordered ma'ruf is not blamed for the tampering of the living. Sin settles on the one who edits the document, pressures the witnesses, or "reinterprets" a clear bequest to divert wealth.

"Allah is Hearing and Knowing." He heard the original wasiyyah and the whispered plan to undo it. He knows the private meeting in which shares were re-routed. The Prophet, peace be upon him, condemned the one who seizes land unjustly, even a handspan, and said he will sink with it through seven earths — property crimes against the dead and the living both sit under divine surveillance.

If you are an executor, a relative, or a witness, the verse is a wall. Your job is conveyance, not creative rewriting. If the will itself was unjust, the next verse opens a door to reconciliation; it does not open a door to quiet theft. Hearing and Knowing are names that make a locked drawer of someone else's estate a very exposed place.

Carry the verse into one concrete decision this week. The longest surah in the Quran did not spend its length so that readers could nod and remain unchanged. Belief in al-Baqarah is proven in what you next refuse and what you next begin.

=182=

"But if one fears from the bequeather some error or sin and makes peace between them — there is no sin upon him. Indeed, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."

Not every intervention in a will is tampering. If someone senses that the dying person is leaning into jawf or ithm — a mistake of proportion, or a sinful intent to cut a rightful heir out of spite — then mediating a just peace between the parties is not a crime. Islah, reconciliation, is rewarded. The verse protects the peacemaker the way the previous verse prosecutes the thief.

Forgiving and Merciful close the door with softness for people who enter messy family money with good intent. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said the peacemaker between people is among those who are truthful and whom Allah loves, and he allowed untrue words only in narrow cases including reconciliation. Balance remains: you may correct injustice through peace; you may not invent a new injustice and call it peace.

In families where wills become battlefields, this verse is the sunnah of the wise cousin or imam who sits everyone down before funerals turn into lawsuits. Fear of Allah in the mediator is what keeps islah from becoming another form of control.

=183=

"O you who believe, fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may become mindful."

A pillar is announced with companionship across history. Fasting is written upon you as it was written upon those before you — so the Muslim does not feel singled out by hunger, and so he knows he stands in a long line of servants taught to master appetite for God. The purpose is named in one word-family: la'allakum tattaqun — that you may reach taqwa, mindfulness of Allah that restrains.

Fasting trains the will at the most basic gate: the mouth. If you can close it from lawful food from dawn to sunset for Him, you become more able to close it from unlawful speech and the hand from unlawful take. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said fasting is a shield, and that Allah says, "All the work of the son of Adam is for him except fasting — it is for Me and I reward it." He also said whoever does not leave false speech and acting on it, Allah has no need of his leaving food and drink.

Taqwa is not automatic from an empty stomach. It is the intended fruit. Keep the fast of the stomach tied to the fast of the limbs and the tongue, and the prescription becomes what it was written to be: a season that leaves you more aware of Allah than you were when the month began.

Let the empty stomach teach an empty tongue of false speech and an empty hand of harm. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said whoever does not leave false speech and acting on it, Allah has no need that he leave his food and drink. Fast in full.

=184=

"For a limited number of days. So whoever among you is ill or on a journey — then an equal number of other days. And upon those who are able to fast with hardship — a ransom of feeding a poor person. And whoever volunteers excess — it is better for him. But to fast is better for you, if you only knew."

The obligation is real and the mercy is structured. Ayyaman ma'dudat — counted days, not an endless hunger. Illness and travel lift the day's fast with a duty to make it up later, equal for equal. For those who can only fast with crushing difficulty — the classical discussion includes the chronically weak and, in early stages of the legislation, options that were later tightened by 185 — a fidyah of feeding the poor is named. Volunteering more feed is better. And still the verse ends: fasting is better for you, if you knew.

"If you knew" points to benefits the empty stomach does not advertise while it growls: the training of the soul, the answered du'a at sunset, the solidarity with the hungry, the reward stored with Allah. The Prophet, peace be upon him, would travel and sometimes fast and sometimes not, and told companions not to find fault with those who chose either when both were allowed. He also said Allah loves that His concessions be taken as He loves that His firm commands be obeyed.

Live the balance. Do not play sick to escape. Do not destroy the body out of pride when the concession is for you. And when you can fast, know that the better portion is still the fast — not because Allah needs your hunger, but because you need what hunger for His sake does to the heart.

=185=

"The month of Ramadan in which was revealed the Quran, a guidance for the people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion. So whoever sights the month, let him fast it; and whoever is ill or on a journey — then an equal number of other days. Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship, and wants you to complete the period and to glorify Allah for that to which He has guided you, and perhaps you will be grateful."

Ramadan is named and honoured by what descended in it: the Quran — guidance, clear signs of guidance, and furqan, the criterion that separates truth from falsehood. Fasting is not attached to an empty month; it is attached to the anniversary season of revelation. Whoever is present for the month fasts; the sick and traveller make up days. Then the theology of law: Allah wants ease for you, not hardship. Ease is not the erasure of obligation; it is the shape of obligation with room for human weakness, timed make-up, and a goal of completion rather than broken despair.

Takbir — glorifying Allah for the guidance — and shukr — gratitude — are the spiritual ends of the month. The Prophet, peace be upon him, was more generous in Ramadan than a driven wind, reviewed the Quran with Jibril every year, and said whoever fasts Ramadan in faith and anticipation of reward is forgiven what preceded of his sin. He also said take the sahur meal, for there is blessing in it — small eases inside the command.

When the month comes, meet it as the month of the Quran as much as the month of thirst. Ease is His intent; laziness is not the same as ease. Complete the count, magnify Him for guidance, and let gratitude be louder at the end of the month than complaint was at its beginning.

=186=

"And when My servants ask you concerning Me — indeed I am near. I respond to the invocation of the supplicant when he calls upon Me. So let them respond to Me and believe in Me that they may be guided."

In the middle of fasting law comes a verse that feels like it stepped out of a private letter. My servants ask about Me — not about a ruling only, about Me. The answer: I am near. Nearness without incarnation, without confusion of Creator and creation — near in knowledge, response, care. "I respond to the call of the caller when he calls Me." The condition is the call itself. He did not say when he is perfect, or when he has a long record.

"So let them respond to Me and believe in Me that they may be rightly guided." Response is two-way. He answers du'a; the servant answers commands. Faith seals the exchange. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said Allah is shy to let His servant's hands fall empty when he raises them, and he taught that du'a is worship. He also said the fast of the fasting person has a du'a that is not rejected at breaking time. Placement of this verse among fasting ayat is not accident: empty stomachs and humbled hearts are invited to speak.

When you wonder if you are heard, read the first person pronouns again. My servants. I am near. I respond. The Lord of Ramadan did not outsource your request. Call — and respond to Him with obedience so that guidance can settle.

Let the empty stomach teach an empty tongue of false speech and an empty hand of harm. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said whoever does not leave false speech and acting on it, Allah has no need that he leave his food and drink. Fast in full.

=187=

"It has been made permissible for you the night preceding fasting to go to your wives. They are clothing for you and you are clothing for them. Allah knew that you used to deceive yourselves, so He accepted your repentance and forgave you. So now, have relations with them and seek what Allah has ordained for you. And eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread of night. Then complete the fast until the night. And do not have relations with them while you are secluded in the mosques. These are the limits of Allah, so do not approach them. Thus does Allah make clear His verses to people that they may become mindful."

Early in Madinah some believers held stricter night rules than Allah had finally legislated — including avoiding spouses after sleep — and some fell short and felt the guilt of self-deception. This verse lifts the burden with mercy: intimacy at night of the fast is permitted. Spouses are libas — clothing — for each other: cover, warmth, protection, closeness that hides faults. The image is among the Quran's most tender descriptions of marriage.

Eat and drink until true dawn is distinct, then complete the fast to night. The Prophet, peace be upon him, marked dawn carefully and said the adhan of Bilal at night should not stop your sahur. I'tikaf in the mosques has its own rule: no intimate relations during that seclusion. "These are the limits of Allah, so do not approach them" — a fence before the fence, teaching people not to hover at the edge of sin.

Allah makes verses clear so that taqwa can grow. Notice the pattern: He knew their struggle, accepted tawbah, expanded ease, then set clear limits. Law in Islam is not a trap; it is guidance that knows human beings and still calls them upward. Wear your spouse as clothing with gratitude, eat the sahur with the sunnah, and stop when the limit arrives.

=188=

"And do not consume one another's wealth unjustly or send it in bribery to the rulers in order that you may consume a portion of people's wealth sinfully while you know."

Fasting disciplines the mouth; this verse disciplines the hand that reaches for money. Do not eat wealth among yourselves with batil — falsehood: fraud, monopoly abuse, unpaid wages, interest tricks, scam commerce. And do not pipe that wealth through judges and officials as bribes so that a court will hand you what you know is not yours. "While you know" removes the excuse of confusion. This is sin with open eyes.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, cursed the briber and the one who takes bribes, and he said for every traitor there is a banner on the Day of Resurrection by which he will be known. He also said the flesh that grows from haram deserves the Fire. A society that fasts by day and bribes by night has not understood Ramadan's project.

Whether the sum is a court case, a permit, a grade, or a family inheritance "arranged" by lying documents, the verse names it. Unjust consumption plus paid power to secure it equals double corruption. Taqwa that stops at the food menu and ignores the bank account is unfinished taqwa.

Leave the labels that make riba sound clever — 'growth product,' 'convenience fee,' 'time value' dressed to hide excess. The Quran already knew the arguments and still drew the line. Your task is exit with tawbah and principal, not a better brand name for the same war.

=189=

"They ask you about the new moons. Say, 'They are measurements of time for the people and for hajj.' And righteousness is not that you enter houses from their backs, but righteousness is the fear of Allah. So enter houses through their doors and fear Allah that you may succeed."

Two questions, one spirit. The new moons — crescent phases — are for timing: people's calendar and the season of hajj. Islam ties worship to visible, shared signs in the sky rather than elite secrecy. Then a Jahiliyyah custom is dismantled: some, once in ihram, would not enter their houses from the front, thinking the back door more "pious." The Quran calls that aesthetic of hardship false birr. Real birr is taqwa. Enter houses by their doors.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, loved ease in what Allah made easy and hated pretentious difficulty that people invent to look devout. He said this religion is easy, and no one makes it hard in excess without it overcoming him. The verse trains a nose for fake piety — rituals of inconvenience that impress neighbours while the heart remains proud or dishonest.

Seek the moon for the month. Seek taqwa for righteousness. Use front doors. Success — tuflihun — is tied to fearing Allah, not to inventing back-door religion that He never asked for.

Whether you are on the plain or reading from far away, the point is the same: rites without taqwa are tourism, and taqwa without remembrance fades. Ask for a hajj mabrur or for a heart that walks as if it had stood at Arafat.

=190=

"Fight in the way of Allah those who fight you, but do not transgress. Indeed, Allah does not like transgressors."

Permission and limit in one breath. Fight in the way of Allah — for His cause, under His rules — those who fight you. The clause is not a blank cheque on the world; it is a response to combatants. "Do not transgress." Do not exceed: no killing non-combatants, no torture, no scorched excess, no fighting from pride or plunder as the engine. Allah does not love the mu'tadin — those who cross the lines He drew.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, forbade killing women and children, forbidden killing of monastics in their places in various instructions, ordered that bodies not be mutilated, and said the most hateful of men to Allah is the severe, combative transgressor. When he sent armies he walked their ethics with them. Jihad in his sunnah is fenced.

This ayah is among the first major combat permissions in the Madinan period after years of patience under persecution. Read it whole. Remove "those who fight you" and you invent a different religion. Remove "do not transgress" and you invent another. Keep both, and you have the moral shape of force in Islam: reluctant when possible, lawful when necessary, never feral.

Family rulings are worship when they are lived for Allah's face: patience in anger, fairness in paperwork, mercy in tone. The neighbour and the child are watching how you use power in the house; so is the Lord who wrote these limits.

=191=

"And kill them wherever you overtake them and expel them from where they expelled you. And persecution is worse than killing. And do not fight them at al-Masjid al-Haram until they fight you there. But if they fight you, then kill them. Such is the recompense of the disbelievers."

The language is stern because the context was stern: a community expelled from Makkah, hunted, and still barred from the Sacred Mosque that housed the House of Ibrahim. "Expel them from where they expelled you" is restitution of a stolen city, not random aggression. "Fitnah is worse than killing" — the persecution, the forced pressure to abandon faith, the torture that early Muslims suffered — is named as a greater crime than the combat that ends it.

The Sacred Mosque remains specially protected: do not fight them there until they start it there. If they do, the sanctuary's protection does not become a shield for attackers. The Prophet, peace be upon him, entered Makkah in conquest with heads bowed and general amnesty for people who had driven him out — the lived completion of strength without vengeful excess.

Reading the verse as a licence for unrestricted violence against anyone far from that context is a betrayal of both tafsir and the Prophet's example. Reading it as if Islam never authorised force against expellers and persecutors is a betrayal of the text. Hold the historical wound, the sacred limit, and the moral aim together.

Ethics in conflict are part of faith, not decoration after victory. The same Book that allows force fences it. If your anger cannot accept a fence, it is not yet fighting in the way of Allah.

=192=

"But if they cease, then indeed, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."

A short verse that changes the temperature of the whole combat passage. If they cease — stop the fighting, the persecution, the expulsion — the door is not eternal blood-feud. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful. The names land on Him first, teaching the believers what posture to take when the enemy's hand drops.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, accepted treaties, halted combat when terms held, and at the opening of Makkah told people who had feared his revenge that they were free. "Go, you are the free ones." Cessation is tested by reality, not slogans; when it is real, forgiveness is not weakness. It is imitation of the names that close this ayah. Continuing a war for pride after the cause of war has ended is not courage in the Quran's language.

In smaller life the echo is constant. When a quarrel ends because the other side stops the wrong, pressing for more pain is not taqwa. Cease with those who cease, keep justice toward any who still oppress, and leave a road back that Forgiving and Merciful would recognise. The verse does not erase memory of wounds; it forbids making endless revenge the new religion once the fighting has actually stopped.

Ethics in conflict are part of faith, not decoration after victory. The same Book that allows force fences it. If your anger cannot accept a fence, it is not yet fighting in the way of Allah.

=193=

"Fight them until there is no fitnah and worship is for Allah. But if they cease, then there is to be no aggression except against the oppressors."

The aim of the fighting authorised above is clarified: until fitnah — the persecution that pressures people out of their faith — is ended, and din is for Allah, meaning devotion is free for Him without forced partners or forced abandonment. This is not a mandate to convert hearts by the sword; the Quran itself says there is no compulsion in religion (2:256). It is a mandate to break the system of armed coercion that made worship of Allah a capital risk in Makkah.

"If they cease, no aggression except against the zalimin." Hostility does not continue against those who stopped. It remains only toward those still actively oppressing. The Prophet, peace be upon him, lived years of treaty and years of battle according to what the other side put on the field, and he taught that the Muslim is the one from whose tongue and hand people are safe.

Aimless rage is not this verse. A clear end-state is: persecution lifted, worship unshackled, aggression limited to oppressors. Anything that uses this ayah to paint all outsiders as permanent targets has left the verse for a project of its own.

Ethics in conflict are part of faith, not decoration after victory. The same Book that allows force fences it. If your anger cannot accept a fence, it is not yet fighting in the way of Allah.

=194=

"The sacred month for the sacred month, and for violated sanctities is legal retribution. So whoever has assaulted you, assault him in the same way that he has assaulted you. And fear Allah and know that Allah is with those who fear Him."

The sacred months were a known Arabian institution of paused fighting. When enemies violated that sanctity against Muslims, the verse allowed response in kind — not wilder than the offence. Sacred month for sacred month; violated hurumat answered by qisas. "Whoever assaults you, assault him in the like of which he assaulted you" is symmetry, not escalation. Then the fence: fear Allah. Symmetry can become a mask for revenge-addiction; taqwa keeps the measure exact.

"Know that Allah is with those who fear Him." Company of aid returns as a theme. The Prophet, peace be upon him, forbade treachery even in war and kept agreements (9:4 with its conditions). He was not soft on aggression; he was hard on betrayal of rules.

When wronged, the believer may take a like right. He may also pardon. What he may not do is invent a larger wrong and baptise it as justice. Fear Allah in the counter-strike as much as you feared Him in the injury — that is how Allah's with-ness stays on your side.

Ethics in conflict are part of faith, not decoration after victory. The same Book that allows force fences it. If your anger cannot accept a fence, it is not yet fighting in the way of Allah.

=195=

"And spend in the way of Allah and do not throw yourselves with your own hands into destruction. And do good; indeed, Allah loves the doers of good."

Spending in Allah's way is paired with a warning against self-destruction. Some companions understood this as refusing to spend on the community's defence and needs — clinging to wealth until the ummah collapses, which is throwing yourself to ruin by your own hand. Others include despair that stops a person from tawbah, and any refusal of duty that ends in ruin. The verse binds the wallet to the survival of the moral project.

"Do good; Allah loves the muhsinin." Ihsan is excellence beyond the bare minimum. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said ihsan is to worship Allah as if you see Him, and he said the upper hand is better than the lower hand. He warned against stinginess among the destroyers, and he spent in ease and hardship as if wealth were a wind passing through his hand, not a wall around his heart.

Destruction is not only enemy action; it can be our own tight fists and abandoned excellence. Spend where the road of Allah needs supply. Keep doing ihsan when fear says to hoard. Love from Allah for the muhsinin is a better asset than the coin you were afraid to release, and the ruin you thought you were avoiding by withholding may be the very ruin the verse named.

Move something today — even small — while the intention is clean. The verses of spending in this surah are not museum pieces; they are invoices the heart keeps delaying. Pay one before sleep.

=196=

"And complete the hajj and umrah for Allah. But if you are prevented, then what can be obtained with ease of sacrificial animals. And do not shave your heads until the sacrificial animal reaches its place of slaughter. And whoever among you is ill or has an ailment of the head making shaving necessary must offer a ransom of fasting or charity or sacrifice. And when you are secure, then whoever performs umrah followed by hajj must offer what can be obtained with ease of sacrificial animals. And whoever cannot find one — then a fast of three days during hajj and seven when you have returned; those are ten complete. This is for those whose family is not present at the Sacred Mosque. And fear Allah and know that Allah is severe in penalty."

Hajj and umrah are to be completed for Allah — not for tourism of the ego, not for title. If blocked from reaching the House, as the Prophet and companions were at Hudaybiyyah, the offering of what is easy of hady, the sacrificial animal, unlocks the exit from ihram after the conditions of that prevention. Rules of shaving, fidyah for medical need, and the tamattu' combination of umrah with hajj for those who live far from the Haram are laid out with the same blend of precision and concession that marks the fasting verses.

"Fear Allah and know that He is severe in penalty." Rituals of the House are not games. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said hajj mabrur has no reward except Paradise, and he performed the farewell hajj teaching rites in person: take your rites from me. Severity of penalty guards against mockery and deliberate sabotage of the sanctity; the detailed easements guard against despair when the body or the road fails.

Intention for Allah, completion when possible, concession when prevented, and taqwa in all of it: that is the spine of the longest practical verse in this stretch.

=197=

"Hajj is in well-known months, so whoever has made hajj obligatory upon himself therein by entering ihram, there is to be no sexual relations and no disobedience and no disputing during hajj. And whatever good you do — Allah knows it. And take provisions, but indeed the best provision is fear of Allah. And fear Me, O you of understanding."

The months of hajj are known; entry into ihram makes certain conduct binding: no rafath (sexual intimacy and its crude talk), no fusuq (sin and disobedience), no jidal (bad-faith disputing that poisons the caravan). The body is in a state of sacred restraint, and the tongue with it. "Whatever good you do, Allah knows it" — quiet kindness on the road is not lost in the crowd.

"Take provision" answered those who refused to pack, claiming tawakkul while becoming a burden on others. Then the elevation: the best provision is taqwa. Food in the bag keeps the body; fear of Allah keeps the hajj from becoming empty movement between stones. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said the pilgrim and the one in umrah are delegations of Allah, and he taught calmness in the crowded rites.

Pack what you need. Pack taqwa deeper. Leave the argumentative self at home if you can; if it comes with you, silence it in the ihram. People of understanding hear "fear Me" inside the noise of Mina and Arafat and know why they came.

Whether you are on the plain or reading from far away, the point is the same: rites without taqwa are tourism, and taqwa without remembrance fades. Ask for a hajj mabrur or for a heart that walks as if it had stood at Arafat.

=198=

"There is no blame upon you for seeking bounty from your Lord. So when you depart from Arafat, remember Allah at al-Mash'ar al-Haram. And remember Him as He has guided you, for indeed you were before that among those astray."

Trade during the hajj journey is permitted — seeking Allah's bounty — against the old scruple that commerce dirty the pilgrimage. The Prophet, peace be upon him, traded in his life and did not make the road to the House a vow of unemployment. Then the rite: when you pour forth from Arafat — the great standing that is the pillar of hajj — remember Allah at Muzdalifah, al-Mash'ar al-Haram. Dhikr after the exhaustion of Arafat, guided by the One who pulled you from being astray.

"You were before that among those astray" keeps the pilgrim humble. However beautiful your ihram, you know what you were. The Prophet gave sermons at Arafat that equality and sanctity of blood and property were fixed, and he repeated the teaching of brotherhood. Remember Him as He guided you — not as your tribe taught you to chant ancestors' praises only.

Work if you need on the road. Stand at Arafat as one who was lost and was found. Fill Muzdalifah with dhikr more than with complaint about crowds.

=199=

"Then depart from the place from where the people depart and ask forgiveness of Allah. Indeed, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."

Some of Quraysh used to stand apart at Muzdalifah and not join the others at Arafat, protecting a privilege of "staying nearer the Haram." This verse abolishes spiritual VIP culture: depart from where the people depart. The same Arafat for the Qurashi and the outsider, the same plain for the famous and the unknown. Hajj levels the rows the way death will.

Then: ask forgiveness of Allah. After the standing that may wipe what came before, istighfar still befits a servant who knows his rites are imperfect. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said he who attends Arafat has caught the hajj, and he made the day of Arafat a day whose fasting expiates two years for those not on hajj. Forgiving and Merciful close the verse so the pilgrim leaves the plain not proud of attendance but hopeful of acceptance.

Equal rites. Soft heart. Seek forgiveness on the way out as you sought mercy on the way in. Privilege of blood does not buy a different mountain, and a perfect-looking hajj without istighfar can still be thinner than a flawed hajj soaked in need of Him.

Whether you are on the plain or reading from far away, the point is the same: rites without taqwa are tourism, and taqwa without remembrance fades. Ask for a hajj mabrur or for a heart that walks as if it had stood at Arafat.

=200=

"And when you have completed your rites, remember Allah like your remembrance of your fathers or with greater remembrance. And among the people is he who says, 'Our Lord, give us in this world,' and he will have in the Hereafter no share."

After the rites, Jahiliyyah used to fill the days with boasts of ancestry at the markets of Mina. The Quran redirects the tongue: remember Allah as you used to remember fathers, or more intensely. Dhikr replaces pedigree theatre. Then a split in du'a culture. Some people only ask, "Our Lord, give us in this world." Their horizon ends at goods, status, and harvest. For them, in the Hereafter, no khalaq — no creative share, no portion.

The next verse will show the better du'a that asks for good in both abodes. Here the warning stands alone long enough to sting. The Prophet, peace be upon him, taught comprehensive supplications that braid dunya and akhirah, and he said the most miserable are those who have no share of the Hereafter. Hajj ends; the mouth keeps moving. What it asks for reveals what the hajj did — or did not — to the heart.

If your tongue after worship only knows worldly askings, the rites may have touched your body without relocating your hope. Remember Allah more than the old stories of fathers. Stretch the du'a past the grave.

Whether you are on the plain or reading from far away, the point is the same: rites without taqwa are tourism, and taqwa without remembrance fades. Ask for a hajj mabrur or for a heart that walks as if it had stood at Arafat.
