"And do not marry polytheistic women until they believe. And a believing slave woman is better than a polytheist, even though she might please you. And do not marry polytheistic men to your women until they believe. And a believing slave is better than a polytheist, even though he might please you. Marriage is not only attraction. It is a gate to a shared life of values, children, and destiny. The verse forbids marrying mushrik women and giving Muslim women to mushrik men until iman enters. A believing slave — lowest in the social ladder of that time — is better than a free polytheist who dazzles the eye. "She might please you" admits the pull of beauty and status, then overrides it with the end of the road: they invite to the Fire; Allah invites to Paradise and forgiveness.

Later legislation allows marriage to chaste women among the People of the Book under conditions (5:5), which scholars distinguish from open idolatry. The principle remains: do not rope your household to someone whose life-project calls away from Allah. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said a woman is married for religion, beauty, wealth, and lineage — so win the one with religion. He also made the guardian's care and the woman's consent part of valid marriage.

When desire argues for a charming disbelief, read the invitations again. One door leads toward Fire, one toward Paradise and maghfirah. Choose the house whose daily call matches the call you want to die upon.

"And do not make Allah a barrier to your oaths against being righteous and fearing Allah and making peace among people. And Allah is Hearing and Knowing."

Some would swear by Allah not to do good — not to visit a relative, not to reconcile, not to give charity — then hide behind the oath as if piety required keeping a bad promise. The verse forbids using Allah's name as a barrier against birr, taqwa, and islah. Break the foolish oath, do the good, and offer kaffarah as taught in the sunnah.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said whoever swears an oath and then sees something better should do what is better and expiate the oath. He disliked excessive swearing even on truth because piling Allah's name on every sentence cheapens it. Hearing and Knowing watch both the original swear and the self-righteous refusal to heal a tie while quoting the swear as if God had asked for the grudge.

Never let "I swore" become a shield for stubbornness. Allah did not lend you His name so you could refuse peace and call it religion. If your wallahi is blocking a phone call of reconciliation or a gift to a needy relative, the verse is talking to you — not to a stranger in a fiqh example.

Move something today — even small — while the intention is clean. The verses of spending in this surah are not museum pieces; they are invoices the heart keeps delaying. Pay one before sleep.

=225=

"Allah does not impose blame upon you for what is unintentional in your oaths, but He imposes blame upon you for what your hearts have earned. And Allah is Forgiving and Forbearing."

Laghw al-yamin — the idle, unintentional oath that slips in speech ("wallahi" as filler) — does not draw the same liability as a deliberate oath the heart meant. What the hearts kasabat — earned intentionally — is what is judged. Forgiving and Forbearing close the verse so people are not crushed by verbal habits while they work to clean them.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, discouraged excessive swearing even in truth, and he detailed kaffarah for broken intentional oaths: feeding or clothing ten poor persons, freeing a slave, or fasting three days for those unable. He knew people of his ummah would talk fast and feel guilt slow; this verse sorts the file with mercy and seriousness together.

Train the tongue away from empty wallahi. When you mean an oath, mean it. When you slip without intent, trust the Forbearing Lord who knows what the heart did not contract — and still work to make His name a weight on your speech, not a tic.

Let the empty stomach teach an empty tongue of false speech and an empty hand of harm. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said whoever does not leave false speech and acting on it, Allah has no need that he leave his food and drink. Fast in full.

"For those who swear not to have sexual relations with their wives is a waiting time of four months, but if they return — then indeed, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."

Ila' — an oath to boycott one's wife sexually — was used in Jahiliyyah as open-ended cruelty, leaving a woman neither married in comfort nor free to remarry. Islam capped it at four months. Within that time the husband may fi' — return — and Allah is Forgiving and Merciful toward the return. The limit forces a decision: resume the marriage with dignity or move toward release. Indefinite punishment by the bedroom door is not a husband's right.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, protected women from suspended harm and said there should be neither harm nor reciprocating harm. Four months is long enough for anger to cool and short enough to stop a life being paused as revenge. Forgiving and Merciful are named for the one who walks back before pride spends the whole term.

Marriage is not a hostage room. If anger made your tongue swear distance, use the window to return with tawbah and kindness. Mercy is named for people who walk back before the cap becomes divorce's door — and for women who finally receive clarity instead of a hallway without end.

Before Islam, endless divorce and return left women in limbo. The Quran counts: two revocable divorces, then a choice — retain with ma'ruf or release with ihsan. Retain with honour or release with grace. Those five words, lived, would empty courts of half their cruelty. Do not transgress the limits and then claim the sunnah of divorce.

"Mothers may breastfeed their children two complete years for whoever wishes to complete the nursing. Upon the father is their provision and clothing according to what is acceptable. Childcare law in a single dense verse. Two years is the completion of nursing for those who want completion. "And if you fear, then pray on foot or riding. But when you are secure, remember Allah as He has taught you that which you did not know."

Fear — of battle, danger, flood, pursuit — does not cancel prayer; it simplifies posture. Pray walking or on mounts, facing where you can. Your commute, your night shift, your season of fear: still pray. Then when secure, fill the dhikr and the form with what He taught that you once did not know. Insecurity is not a holiday from salah; security is not a holiday from dhikr. Both states have a way to stay tied to Him.
