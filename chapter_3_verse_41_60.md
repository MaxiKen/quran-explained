## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:41

> Zachariah said, “My Lord! Grant me a sign.” He said, “Your sign is that you will not ˹be able to˺ speak to people for three days except through gestures. Remember your Lord often and glorify ˹Him˺ morning and evening.”

**Commentary:**

### Asking for a Sign

Zakariyyā had already believed Allah’s promise of a son, but he asked for a sign so that his heart could receive reassurance and so that he would recognize the beginning of the promised event. His request was not a challenge to Allah’s power. It was the request of a believing servant who wanted confirmation of an extraordinary answer.

The Qur’an records similar questions from Ibrāhīm, who asked Allah to show him how He gives life to the dead (2:260). In both cases, the question arose from faith and sought tranquillity, not denial. Sincere questions should be distinguished from the demands of arrogance.

### A Temporary Sign in Speech

Zakariyyā’s sign was that he would be unable to speak to people for three days except through gestures. His inability was not a disease or punishment. It was a direct sign that the promise was from Allah and that ordinary human power could not produce the child. His tongue was restrained from ordinary conversation while his heart and remembrance remained active.

The sign also directed him away from unnecessary speech. When Allah gives a servant a period in which ordinary speech is limited, the opportunity for reflection and remembrance becomes greater. The believer should not measure spiritual productivity by the amount he says.

### Remembering Allah Often

Allah instructs Zakariyyā to remember Him frequently. *Dhikr* includes reciting the Qur’an, glorifying Allah, praising Him, seeking forgiveness, supplicating, and keeping awareness of Him while carrying out lawful responsibilities. The command to remember Allah is not restricted to the mosque or to one particular time.

The heart can remember Allah while the tongue is silent. A person who cannot speak may still worship through intention, reflection, prayer, and gestures. This is a mercy to the sick, the disabled, and anyone whose circumstances limit speech.

### Morning and Evening

Morning and evening are especially suitable times for remembrance because they mark transitions in human life. The servant begins the day by placing it under Allah’s care and ends it by acknowledging that every success came from Him. The Qur’an repeatedly encourages remembrance at these times (33:41–42; 76:25).

The daily cycle also teaches that time belongs to Allah. A believer does not wait for a crisis before remembering Him. Regular remembrance keeps the heart prepared for both good news and difficulty.

### Glorifying Allah

“Glorify” means saying *subḥān Allāh* and recognizing that Allah is free from every imperfection, injustice, weakness, and limitation. Zakariyyā’s extraordinary answer did not make him worship the miracle. He worshipped the One who made it possible.

The sign therefore leads to tawḥīd rather than fascination with the unusual. Miracles are evidence of a messenger or promise, not independent powers possessed by created beings.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:260 on asking for reassurance without denying resurrection.
- Qur’an 19:7–11 on Zakariyyā’s sign and his inability to speak for a period.
- Qur’an 33:41–42 and 76:25 on frequent remembrance morning and evening.
- Qur’an 13:28 on hearts finding rest through remembrance of Allah.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports encouraging remembrance, supplication, and glorification of Allah.
- For Zakariyyā’s sign, *dhikr*, *tasbīḥ*, and the distinction between sincere questioning and denial, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:41.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:42

> And ˹remember˺ when the angels said, “O Mary! Surely Allah has selected you, purified you, and chosen you over all women of the world.

**Commentary:**

### Mary’s Divine Selection

The angels announce that Allah selected and purified Mary. Her honour is a divine gift, but it is also connected to her worship, truthfulness, and steadfastness. Selection in revelation is never a licence for pride. It means being entrusted with a difficult service and becoming a sign through which people are guided.

Mary’s selection prepares for the miraculous birth of Jesus. She will carry a responsibility that no other woman carries, and she will face suspicion, isolation, and the burden of explaining what Allah has decreed.

### Purification

“Purified you” includes purification of belief, character, intention, and conduct. Mary was protected from the moral corruption around her and devoted herself to Allah. The Qur’an later describes her as a woman of truth (*ṣiddīqah*) and praises her chastity (5:75; 66:12).

Purity is not merely physical. It includes guarding the heart from arrogance, the tongue from falsehood, and the body from unlawful conduct. A person may appear religious while carrying envy or injustice; Mary’s honour includes inward and outward integrity.

### Chosen above the Women of the World

The phrase indicates exceptional honour among women. The Prophet ﷺ said that Mary was among the greatest women, and he also named Āsiyah, Khadījah, and Fāṭimah in reports recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. The comparison is a praise of righteousness and closeness to Allah, not a reason to demean other women.

Mary’s status also refutes the idea that women are spiritually secondary. She is chosen, addressed by angels, praised in the Qur’an, and made a sign for humanity. Spiritual rank before Allah is based upon faith and obedience, while different social responsibilities do not erase equal human dignity.

### A Model of Devotion

Mary was raised in the sanctuary and accepted Allah’s command when it brought hardship. Her life demonstrates that devotion includes courage under misunderstanding. She did not seek public honour, yet Allah made her a lasting model of faith.

The believer should ask for Mary’s qualities: sincerity, modesty, worship, patience, truthfulness, and trust in Allah. The Qur’an records her story not as distant history but as a living example for men and women.

### Divine Honour and Human Conduct

Honouring Mary and Jesus requires accepting the truth about them. Muslims love Mary without worshipping her and honour Jesus without claiming that he is divine. The verse places them within Allah’s creative and prophetic plan, not outside human dependence upon Him.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:43

> O Mary! Be devout to your Lord, prostrate yourself ˹in prayer˺ and bow along with those who bow down.”

**Commentary:**

### Devotion to the Lord

The angels instruct Mary to remain devout to her Lord. The Arabic concept of *qunūt* includes obedient worship, humility, persistence, and standing faithfully before Allah. Mary’s honour is joined to duty; being selected does not remove the need for worship.

This is an important principle for every believer. Spiritual gifts, religious knowledge, family lineage, and public recognition do not replace obedience. The closer a servant is to Allah, the more conscious he becomes of his need to worship.

### Prostration

Prostration, *sujūd*, is one of the highest expressions of humility. The worshipper places the most honoured part of the body, the face, upon the ground before Allah. It communicates that human dignity is not diminished by submission to the Creator. It is actually restored by refusing to submit ultimately to created beings.

The Prophet ﷺ said that the servant is closest to his Lord while prostrating, so believers should increase supplication then, in a hadith recorded by Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Prostration is therefore not only a posture but an intimate moment of prayer and dependence.

### Bowing with Those Who Bow

Mary is told to bow with those who bow. The phrase highlights communal worship. Private devotion is important, but Islam also forms a community that prays together, stands together, and removes worldly distinctions before Allah. Worship is not meant to create a spiritual elite detached from people.

The instruction does not erase Mary’s unique status. She remains a woman chosen by Allah, yet she is directed toward a community of worshippers. Honour and belonging can exist together.

### Prayer and the Body

The prayer uses the body—standing, bowing, and prostrating—to train the heart. A person who repeatedly bows before Allah should not become arrogant toward people. The physical act becomes false if it does not produce humility, honesty, and mercy outside prayer.

The movements also discipline time and attention. Mary’s life was not shaped only by extraordinary events. It was shaped by repeated acts of worship that prepared her to bear an extraordinary test.

### A Model for Women and Men

Mary’s worship is a Qur’anic model for all believers. Men and women can learn from her devotion, chastity, patience, and trust. The verse does not present worship as reserved for male religious authority. Mary receives a direct command from the angels and is honoured for her response.

- Qur’an 2:43, 2:238, and 22:77 on prayer, bowing, and prostration.
- Qur’an 19:16–26 on Mary’s devotion and trial.
- Qur’an 96:19 on drawing near to Allah through prostration.
- Qur’an 33:35 on the reward of devout men and women.
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith “The servant is closest to his Lord while he is prostrating”; Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Muslim, reports on congregational prayer.
- For *qunūt*, *sujūd*, *rukūʿ*, communal worship, and Mary’s devotion, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:43.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:44

> This is news of the unseen that We reveal to you ˹O Prophet˺. You were not with them when they cast lots to decide who would be Mary’s guardian, nor were you there when they argued ˹about it˺.

**Commentary:**

### Revelation of the Unseen

Allah tells the Prophet ﷺ that the account of Mary’s guardianship is news of the unseen. Muhammad ﷺ was not present when the priests and leaders disputed over who would care for her, yet the Qur’an gives an accurate account. This is evidence that his knowledge of these events came through revelation, not ordinary historical access.

The Qur’an frequently uses past history to establish the truth of revelation. It does not provide stories merely for entertainment. It selects details that correct religious disputes, honour sincere servants, and expose fabricated claims.

### The Dispute over Guardianship

Mary’s dedication created a question of responsibility: who would care for a child devoted to the sanctuary? The dispute shows that her guardianship was considered an honour and a trust. It also shows that religious communities can argue even around righteous matters when status and privilege become involved.

The solution was not violence or personal entitlement. They used a fair procedure to determine the guardian. Zakariyyā became responsible for Mary, and the Qur’an presents his guardianship as a blessing when carried out with care.

### Casting Lots as a Fair Procedure

The casting of lots in this context was a lawful method for resolving a dispute when the qualified parties had equal claims and no decisive evidence favoured one. It is not the same as gambling, which involves wagering and unlawful consumption of wealth. The purpose was to distribute responsibility fairly rather than to profit from chance.

The Sunnah records that the Prophet ﷺ used drawing lots in certain matters where people had equal entitlement, such as determining order or selecting among eligible participants. Any such procedure must remain within justice and cannot override a clear right or be used to avoid responsibility.

### Religious Leadership as Trust

The verse warns leaders not to turn sacred service into a contest for prestige. A guardian, teacher, imam, or administrator is chosen to serve the trust, not to gain ownership over the person or institution. Authority should be assigned based on suitability, integrity, and ability to protect rights.

Mary’s story also shows that the vulnerable need more than formal guardianship. They need food, safety, education, spiritual care, and respect. A person who accepts the honour of guardianship but neglects these responsibilities has betrayed it.

### The Prophet’s Lack of Ordinary Access

The Prophet ﷺ was not among the disputing parties, nor did he learn the details through personal attendance. The revelation therefore answers those who claimed the Qur’an was merely copied from earlier communities. It presents precise spiritual and historical knowledge with a message suited to the Prophet’s audience.

The proof is not only the information itself but its effect: it calls all parties back to tawḥīd, humility, and submission.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 11:49, 12:102, and 28:44–46 on revelation of unseen history to the Prophet ﷺ.
- Qur’an 3:35–37 on Mary’s dedication and Zakariyyā’s guardianship.
- Qur’an 4:58 and 23:8 on fulfilling trusts and responsibilities.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 49:13 on justice and honour through righteousness rather than status.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on the Prophet ﷺ using lots among equally entitled people in lawful matters.
- For *anbāʾ al-ghayb*, *yulqūna aqlāmahum*, guardianship, and fair allocation of responsibility, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:44.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:45

> ˹Remember˺ when the angels proclaimed, “O Mary! Allah gives you good news of a Word[136] from Him, his name will be the Messiah,[137] Jesus, son of Mary; honoured in this world and the Hereafter, and he will be one of those nearest ˹to Allah˺.

**Commentary:**

### The Announcement of Jesus

The angels announce to Mary the birth of Jesus, a miraculous event and a major sign of Allah’s power. The announcement names him in several ways: a Word from Allah, the Messiah, Jesus, and son of Mary. Each title clarifies part of his mission and protects against later distortion.

Jesus is honoured as a prophet and servant, but the verse does not make him divine. His extraordinary birth is a sign of the Creator’s power, not evidence that the created child shares in Allah’s divinity.

### A Word from Allah

Jesus is called a Word from Allah because he was created through Allah’s command and because his birth occurred without a human father. Allah says to a matter, “Be,” and it is (3:47; 19:35). The title does not mean that Jesus is a part of Allah or that the Creator entered creation.

The Qur’an uses the same language to show the connection between command and creation. Adam was created without either parent, yet no one believes that Adam was divine. The later comparison in 3:59 will make this argument explicit.

### The Messiah

The title *al-Masīḥ*, the Messiah, is an honour given to Jesus. Classical scholars discuss its linguistic possibilities and meanings, including one blessed by Allah or one who traversed the land. Regardless of the precise etymology, the title identifies a special messenger with a unique role among the Children of Israel.

A title of honour does not change Jesus’ created nature. The Qur’an honours prophets without turning them into objects of worship. The proper response to Jesus is belief in his message, love for him, and rejection of claims that contradict his call to worship Allah alone.

### Son of Mary

He is named “Jesus, son of Mary.” The matronymic draws attention to the miracle of his birth and to Mary’s honour. It does not imply that he lacks a human mother; it highlights the fact that he had no human father. He is a human being created by Allah and born through Mary.

The Qur’an also calls him ʿĪsā ibn Maryam to correct claims about his ancestry and nature. His origin is miraculous, but miracle does not equal divinity.

### Honoured in Both Worlds

Jesus is honoured in this world through prophethood, miracles, truthfulness, and followers who preserved his essential message. He is honoured in the Hereafter through nearness to Allah and a high station among the righteous. Honour comes through servitude and obedience, not independence from Allah.

This is a model for every believer. The highest honour is not wealth or celebrity but closeness to Allah through faith and righteousness.

### Near to Allah

“Those nearest” are the *muqarrabūn*, servants brought near through worship, sincerity, and Allah’s favour. Jesus belongs among the honoured servants, as do the prophets and righteous. Nearness does not mean physical location; it means rank, acceptance, and honour in Allah’s sight.

- Qur’an 19:16–36 on Mary and the birth of Jesus.
- Qur’an 5:72–75 on Jesus’ call to worship Allah and his human dependence.
- Qur’an 56:10–11 and 83:21 on the rank of those brought near to Allah.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on Jesus’ prophethood and his return near the end of time.
- For *kalimah*, *al-Masīḥ*, *ʿĪsā ibn Maryam*, and *al-muqarrabūn*, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:45.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:46

> And he will speak to people in ˹his˺ infancy and adulthood and will be one of the righteous.”

**Commentary:**

### Speech in Infancy

The angels announce that Jesus will speak to people in infancy. The Qur’an later records him speaking from the cradle to defend his mother and declare his servanthood to Allah (19:29–33). This is a miracle granted by Allah and not a natural power that makes Jesus divine.

The infant speech protected Mary from false accusation and directed attention to Allah. Jesus’ first recorded words are not a claim to independent power; they are a declaration that he is Allah’s servant and prophet. The miracle therefore supports tawḥīd rather than undermining it.

### Adulthood and Prophetic Mission

Jesus will also speak in adulthood, meaning that his mission will continue with mature teaching, warning, and guidance. The transition from extraordinary infancy to public prophethood shows that miracles are signs supporting a messenger’s call; they are not the entirety of his message.

The prophet must be heard in both stages: the miracle may awaken attention, but revelation and obedience form the lasting guidance. Jesus will call the Children of Israel to worship Allah and to follow the truth.

### One of the Righteous

The verse describes Jesus as one of the righteous, *al-ṣāliḥīn*. This places him among Allah’s honoured servants, not above servanthood. Righteousness means sound belief, obedience, purity, justice, and fulfilling the mission entrusted by Allah.

The Qur’an uses this description for prophets and believers generally. It is a high honour, yet it is also a category of created servants. Jesus’ closeness to Allah comes through obedience and divine favour, not through sharing Allah’s essence.

### Infancy and Moral Responsibility

Jesus’ speech in infancy is extraordinary, but it does not erase the normal stages of human development or moral responsibility. Allah can create exceptional signs without making every aspect of a person’s life outside ordinary human experience. The Qur’an describes Jesus eating food (5:75), which is a sign of his created dependence.

This balance is important when speaking about miracles. Muslims affirm what revelation establishes without inventing details or allowing wonder to become exaggeration.

### The Dignity of Childhood

The verse also reminds readers that children are persons worthy of protection and truth. Jesus’ speech defended the dignity of Mary at a moment when she was vulnerable. Communities must not rush to accuse parents or children based on rumour. They should verify claims, protect the innocent, and wait for truth.

- Qur’an 6:85 and 21:91 on Jesus among the righteous and chosen servants.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:47

> Mary wondered, “My Lord! How can I have a child when no man has ever touched me?” An angel replied, “So will it be. Allah creates what He wills. When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, ‘Be!’ And it is!

**Commentary:**

### Mary’s Question

Mary asks how she can have a child when no man has touched her. Her question confirms her chastity and shows that she understood the ordinary means of conception. She does not deny Allah’s power; she seeks to understand how an unprecedented event will occur.

The angel’s answer confirms that the birth will be a deliberate act of Allah. It will not result from an illicit relationship or secret human intervention. The Qur’an protects Mary’s honour and presents her as a truthful servant faced with a difficult decree.

### Allah Creates What He Wills

Allah creates through familiar means and through extraordinary means. He created most human beings through parents, Adam without parents, Eve from Adam, and Jesus through Mary without a human father. The differences show that Allah is not confined by the patterns He normally establishes.

This principle does not require believers to reject natural processes. The regularity of creation is itself a sign of Allah’s wisdom. A miracle is an exceptional act by the Creator, not evidence that ordinary causes are meaningless.

### “Be, and It Is”

The command “Be” represents Allah’s effortless creative authority. He does not need tools, exhaustion, or assistance. His will brings creation into existence, while creation remains dependent upon Him. The phrase should not be understood as literal vocal speech in a human sense; it expresses the immediate effectiveness of Allah’s command.

The same expression appears in the Qur’an concerning the resurrection, Adam, and other signs. If Allah can create the heavens and earth, bringing Jesus into existence without a father is not difficult for Him.

### A Miracle, Not Divinity

Jesus’ miraculous birth is central to Christian and Muslim discussion. The Qur’an affirms the miracle but rejects the conclusion that Jesus is divine. A miracle proves the power of the one who grants it; it does not make the recipient a partner with the Creator.

The following verses will list Jesus’ miracles and repeatedly state that they occurred “by Allah’s permission.” This language is a safeguard: Jesus is honoured precisely as a messenger who acts under Allah’s command.

### Mary’s Trust

Mary receives an answer that still does not remove the hardship ahead. She will face pregnancy, labour, social suspicion, and separation. Faith does not always explain every emotional detail before a trial begins. It gives the servant enough truth to obey and trust.

Her question also models respectful inquiry. A believer may ask Allah for understanding while remaining prepared to submit when the answer is beyond ordinary calculation.

- Qur’an 16:40 and 40:68 on Allah’s command and power to create and resurrect.
- Qur’an 4:171 on Jesus as a created messenger and word from Allah.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:48

> “And Allah will teach him writing and wisdom, the Torah and the Gospel,

**Commentary:**

### Knowledge as a Divine Gift

Allah will teach Jesus writing, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel. The verse attributes Jesus’ knowledge to Allah, protecting him from the claim that he possessed divine knowledge independently. Prophetic knowledge is honoured, but it is received knowledge, granted for guidance and service.

The mention of writing and wisdom shows that revelation is not opposed to literacy, learning, or thoughtful understanding. A messenger is taught to convey the Book accurately and apply it wisely. Knowledge without wisdom can cause harm, while spirituality without knowledge can become confusion.

### Writing and Scripture

The word translated “writing” can refer to the ability to write and to the revealed scripture. In either sense, the verse connects Jesus with communication and preservation of knowledge. Prophets do not leave people dependent upon private visions or secret claims; they teach a message that can be heard, remembered, written, and followed.

The Torah is the scripture given to Mūsā, and the Gospel is the revelation given to Jesus. Jesus did not come to erase the truth of the Torah. He came as a messenger within the prophetic chain, confirming its foundation and correcting what had been distorted.

### Wisdom in Applying Revelation

*Ḥikmah* is sound understanding and appropriate action. It includes knowing the purpose of a command, applying it in the right circumstances, and combining truth with mercy and justice. Jesus’ wisdom would be visible in his call to repentance, care for the vulnerable, and sincere worship.

The Qur’an repeatedly praises wisdom and teaches that it is a great gift (2:269). A scholar or preacher should therefore seek not only information but the ability to apply knowledge without harshness, manipulation, or negligence.

### The Unity of Revelation

The Torah and Gospel are named alongside Jesus’ teaching to emphasize continuity. The same Allah revealed them. The Qur’an calls people to believe in the original revelations and in the prophets who received them. A Muslim cannot honour Muhammad ﷺ while despising Mūsā or Jesus.

At the same time, the Qur’an is the final guardian and criterion over previous scripture (5:48). It confirms the original truth while correcting claims that contradict Allah’s oneness and the prophets’ servanthood.

### Prophetic Teaching and Human Agency

Although Allah teaches prophets, they still teach people through speech, example, patience, and struggle. Divine revelation does not remove human effort. Jesus will convey the message, perform signs by Allah’s permission, and call the Children of Israel to obey Allah.

The verse is also a reminder to students: every useful ability is a gift from Allah. A person should attribute success to Allah while working diligently, respecting teachers, and using knowledge for benefit rather than pride.

- Qur’an 5:48 on the Qur’an as a guardian and criterion over previous revelation.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:49

> and ˹make him˺ a messenger to the Children of Israel ˹to proclaim,˺ ‘I have come to you with a sign from your Lord: I will make for you a bird from clay, breathe into it, and it will become a ˹real˺ bird—by Allah’s Will. I will heal the blind and the leper and raise the dead to life— by Allah’s Will. And I will prophesize what you eat and store in your houses. Surely in this is a sign for you if you ˹truly˺ believe.

**Commentary:**

### Jesus as a Messenger

Jesus is sent as a messenger to the Children of Israel. His miracles are signs supporting his prophethood, not evidence that he shares Allah’s divinity. The verse repeatedly places his abilities under Allah’s will, making the source of power unmistakable.

A messenger’s central task is to call people to Allah. Miracles may confirm the message, but they do not replace faith, repentance, and obedience. The people are asked to believe not in Jesus as an independent wonder-worker but in Allah who sent him.

### The Bird from Clay

Jesus forms a bird-like shape from clay, breathes into it, and it becomes a real bird by Allah’s permission. This is an extraordinary sign resembling the creation of life, but the wording protects the boundary between Creator and creature. Jesus acts as a messenger, while Allah creates the living result.

The sign also recalls Adam’s creation from dust and Allah’s command “Be” (3:59). Jesus’ miracle is therefore connected to the same creative power that belongs to Allah. It does not make Jesus a creator in the independent, divine sense.

### Healing and Raising the Dead

Allah gives Jesus the ability to heal the blind and the leper and to raise the dead, again explicitly “by Allah’s Will.” These miracles brought hope to people suffering from conditions that human medicine could not cure in their circumstances. They also demonstrated that Allah can restore what human beings consider impossible.

The verse does not encourage people to abandon treatment or to follow anyone who claims miraculous power. In Islamic belief, healing ultimately comes from Allah, while medical treatment is a lawful means. Claims of miracles must not be used to exploit the sick, demand money, or establish false authority.

### Knowledge of Hidden Matters

Jesus will tell people what they eat and store in their homes. This is a sign granted by Allah, not independent knowledge of the unseen. The Qur’an limits the unseen knowledge of messengers to what Allah reveals (72:26–27). Jesus therefore knows what he knows through a divine sign or revelation, while Allah alone possesses unrestricted knowledge.

This principle protects believers from exaggerating prophets and religious personalities. A servant may be honoured with knowledge, but only Allah knows everything without limit.

### Signs Require a Receptive Heart

The verse concludes that these are signs for people who truly believe. The same event can be seen by one person as evidence and by another as a reason for further denial. A miracle does not force sincere faith in a heart committed to arrogance. Evidence benefits the person who approaches it with humility.

### Mercy in Prophetic Miracles

Jesus’ signs include healing and restoring life, displaying the compassionate dimension of his mission. Prophetic power is not entertainment. It serves guidance, relieves suffering, and points people to Allah’s mercy. Believers should imitate this concern by caring for the ill, supporting people with disabilities, and using knowledge to heal rather than harm.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:50

> And I will confirm the Torah revealed before me and legalize some of what had been forbidden to you. I have come to you with a sign from your Lord, so be mindful of Allah and obey me.

**Commentary:**

### Confirmation of the Torah

Jesus states that he confirms the Torah revealed before him. His mission is part of the same chain of revelation, and he does not ask the Children of Israel to abandon belief in Moses. He confirms the truth Allah originally revealed and calls people back to faithful obedience.

This statement is also a test for the People of the Scripture. If they truly honour the Torah, they should recognize a messenger who confirms its foundation and calls people to worship the same Lord. Religious identity cannot be used as a reason to reject truth from Allah.

### Lawful Relief

Jesus also legalizes some things that had been forbidden to the Children of Israel. The Qur’an elsewhere explains that certain restrictions were imposed because of their wrongdoing (4:160; 6:146). Jesus’ mission included restoring lawful ease by Allah’s permission and correcting the claim that every restriction would remain forever.

The verse says “some,” not all. Prophets do not change divine law according to personal preference. Any change in lawful and unlawful matters comes through Allah’s revelation. This principle is important for religious leadership: a teacher cannot make prohibited things lawful or lawful things prohibited merely because of culture, convenience, or political pressure.

### A Sign from the Lord

Jesus again identifies his mission as a sign from the Lord. His miracles and legal teaching do not originate from independent divinity. They point beyond him to Allah, who sent him and authorized his message.

A sign is useful only when it leads to the One signified. If people become fascinated with a prophet while ignoring his call to worship Allah, they have misunderstood the purpose of the miracle.

### Be Mindful of Allah

Jesus commands *taqwā*: reverent awareness of Allah that produces obedience and protection from sin. Taqwā is not fear without knowledge. It is living as though one is accountable to Allah, guarding the heart, speech, wealth, relationships, and public authority.

The command is especially important after a discussion of miracles. Extraordinary events can make people focus on power rather than obedience. Jesus directs the people back to moral responsibility.

### Obey the Messenger

“Obey me” means obey the message Jesus brings from Allah. It is not a claim of personal lordship. The Qur’an records prophets calling people to obedience to Allah and themselves only because their authority is a divine trust.

The same principle applies to Muhammad ﷺ. Obeying the messenger means accepting revelation, following authentic prophetic guidance, and understanding that the messenger’s authority serves Allah’s command rather than replacing it.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 5:46 and 5:110 on Jesus confirming the Torah and receiving the Gospel.
- Qur’an 4:160 and 6:146 on restrictions imposed upon the Children of Israel because of wrongdoing.
- Qur’an 7:157 on the Prophet who makes lawful the good things and removes burdens.
- Qur’an 16:116 and 5:87–88 on not declaring lawful and unlawful without Allah’s authority.
- Qur’an 4:80 and 59:7 on obeying messengers as part of obeying Allah.
- For confirmation, lawful relief, *taqwā*, and the authority of a messenger, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:50.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:51

> Surely Allah is my Lord and your Lord. So worship Him ˹alone˺. This is the Straight Path.’”

**Commentary:**

### Jesus’ Own Declaration

The Qur’an presents Jesus declaring that Allah is his Lord and the Lord of his people. This is a direct statement of servanthood and tawḥīd. Jesus does not say that he is the Lord, nor that people should worship him. He directs worship to Allah alone.

His statement is decisive in the Qur’an’s discussion of later disputes about his nature. The one who has a Lord is a servant before that Lord. Jesus’ honour is not diminished by servanthood; it is precisely his submission that makes him a noble prophet.

### One Lord for All

Allah is “my Lord and your Lord.” The phrase removes the possibility of a private deity belonging only to Jesus or to one community. The Lord of Jesus is the Lord of the Children of Israel, the Lord of Muhammad ﷺ, and the Lord of all humanity.

This shared lordship grounds equality before Allah. No race, nation, clerical class, or religious institution owns God. Every person is invited to submit to the same Creator and Judge.

### Worship Him Alone

Jesus commands worship of Allah alone. *ʿIbādah* includes prayer, supplication, sacrifice, reliance, obedience, love, hope, and fear. These acts must not be directed to Jesus, Mary, angels, saints, idols, or any other created being.

Honouring prophets is part of faith, but worshipping prophets is a contradiction of their message. Jesus’ miracles, noble birth, and elevated rank do not change this principle. The prophets are signs pointing to Allah, not partners sharing His right to worship.

### The Straight Path

The Straight Path is the path of tawḥīd and obedience. It is straight because it does not deviate toward polytheism, rejection, or exaggeration. It joins correct belief to righteous conduct and sincere worship.

Jesus’ call is consistent with the message of all prophets. Nūḥ called his people to worship Allah, Abraham rejected idols, Moses brought the Torah, and Muhammad ﷺ delivered the final revelation. The details of law differed, but the path of worshipping one Lord remained constant.

### The Qur’an’s Protection of Jesus

By preserving Jesus’ words, the Qur’an protects him from both rejection and exaggeration. Rejecting him denies Allah’s messenger; worshipping him contradicts his own call. Muslims therefore affirm his miraculous birth, prophethood, signs, and honour while rejecting divinity and sonship in a literal sense.

This balanced position is not a compromise created by later theology. It is the Qur’an’s own presentation of Jesus as servant, messenger, and caller to Allah.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 5:72–75 and 19:30–36 on Jesus’ servanthood, message, and call to worship Allah.
- Qur’an 2:132–133 and 16:36 on the shared prophetic call to worship Allah alone.
- Qur’an 6:153 on the Straight Path and avoiding competing paths.
- Qur’an 4:171 on avoiding excess concerning Jesus and calling him a messenger.
- Qur’an 21:25 and 43:63–64 on the message of all prophets and Jesus’ call to worship Allah.
- For *Rabb*, *ʿibādah*, *ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*, Jesus’ declaration, and the balance between honour and worship, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:51.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:52

> When Jesus sensed disbelief from his people, he asked, “Who will stand up with me for Allah?” The disciples replied, “We will stand up for Allah. We believe in Allah, so bear witness that we have submitted.”

**Commentary:**

### Perceiving Rejection

Jesus recognized disbelief among his people after presenting the signs and message. Prophets do not ignore the moral condition of their communities. They teach patiently, but they also identify rejection and call sincere people to stand with truth.

The verse does not say that Jesus abandoned his people at the first sign of resistance. Prophetic preaching includes patience and perseverance. But when opposition becomes clear, a messenger seeks the support of those willing to uphold Allah’s guidance.

### Who Will Help Allah?

Jesus asks, “Who will stand up with me for Allah?” The expression *anṣār Allāh*, helpers of Allah, does not mean that Allah needs assistance. Allah is independent of creation. To help Allah means to help His religion, support His messengers, establish justice, and defend truth through lawful means.

The same call appears in 61:14, where the disciples answer and Allah grants victory to those who believe. A person becomes a helper of Allah by obeying Him, serving people, protecting truth, and resisting oppression—not by claiming a religious title.

### The Disciples

The disciples are called *al-ḥawāriyyūn*. Classical scholars explain the term as referring to sincere, pure, devoted followers. They were the close companions of Jesus, chosen to learn, support, and carry his message.

Their response shows that discipleship is active. They do not merely admire Jesus. They believe in Allah, stand for the mission, and ask Jesus to bear witness to their submission. True companionship supports a person in obedience rather than encouraging him toward fame or self-interest.

### Faith and Submission

The disciples say, “We believe in Allah,” then “bear witness that we have submitted.” *Īmān* is inner conviction, while *Islām* expresses surrender and obedience. The two belong together. Faith that never affects conduct remains a claim; outward submission without sincere belief becomes hypocrisy.

Their request for Jesus’ testimony also expresses humility. They want their commitment to be known as a covenant of truth, not as temporary enthusiasm. A believer should ask Allah for sincerity and for a good witness to his life.

### Standing for Truth

Standing with a prophet requires courage. The disciples risked social rejection and possible persecution by identifying themselves with Jesus. The verse does not promise that support for truth will be easy. It teaches that a small group can uphold a great responsibility when its faith is sincere.

Standing for Allah also requires wisdom. It is not permission to create disorder or harm innocent people. The disciples supported Jesus through faith, teaching, and obedience to the limits Allah set.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:49–53 and 61:14 on Jesus’ message and the disciples’ support.
- Qur’an 5:111–115 on the disciples’ belief and their request for a sign.
- Qur’an 57:25 on supporting justice and the messengers.
- Qur’an 47:7 on helping Allah’s cause and receiving His help.
- Qur’an 22:40 on defending places of worship and resisting persecution.
- For *ḥawāriyyūn*, *anṣār Allāh*, faith, submission, and supporting divine guidance, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:52.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:53

> ˹They prayed to Allah,˺ “Our Lord! We believe in Your revelations and follow the messenger, so count us among those who bear witness.”

**Commentary:**

### Believing in Revelation

The disciples begin by affirming belief in Allah’s revelations. They do not reduce faith to admiration for Jesus or to loyalty to a human leader. Their faith is directed to the revelation that Allah gives and the truth it contains.

Belief in revelation includes accepting its source, truthfulness, guidance, and authority. It should lead people to worship, ethics, and obedience. The disciples’ statement is therefore immediately followed by their declaration that they follow the messenger.

### Following the Messenger

They distinguish between knowing a messenger and following him. A person may respect Jesus historically yet refuse the obligations of his message. The disciples show the correct response: they hear, believe, and follow.

Following a messenger means accepting his teaching, supporting his mission, and embodying his guidance. It is not blind personality-worship. The messenger is followed because Allah sent him and because his message leads people back to Allah.

### Bearing Witness

The disciples ask to be counted among those who bear witness. *Shahādah* is testimony based upon knowledge and commitment. They want their lives to testify that Allah is one and that Jesus is His messenger. Their witness is not merely words at a ceremony; it is demonstrated by loyalty, sacrifice, and obedience.

A believer’s character also bears witness. Honesty, mercy, justice, and prayer make faith visible, while hypocrisy weakens the testimony a person claims to carry.

### The Community of Witnesses

The Qur’an describes the Muslim community as a community of witnesses over humanity (2:143). This is not a claim of superiority without responsibility. To witness means to convey truth, live by justice, and represent the message accurately. The disciples’ prayer anticipates this responsibility.

A witness must be trustworthy. He cannot conceal evidence, distort the message, or use religion to justify oppression. Communities that claim to bear witness must be the first to examine their own conduct.

### A Prayer for Acceptance

The disciples do not merely announce their faith; they ask Allah to count them among the witnesses. This reflects humility and fear that deeds may not be accepted. A person can believe and act, yet still depend upon Allah’s grace for acceptance and steadfastness.

The prayer is useful for anyone beginning a new commitment. Ask Allah not only to let you know the truth but to make you faithful to it and to allow your life to support it.

- Qur’an 2:285, 3:52–53, and 5:111 on belief in revelation and following messengers.
- Qur’an 2:143 on the community as a witness over humanity.
- Qur’an 16:36 and 21:25 on the unity of the messengers’ call.
- Qur’an 61:14 on the disciples’ support for Jesus and Allah’s aid to believers.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 4:135 on truthful testimony and justice.
- For *īmān*, *ittibāʿ*, *shahādah*, and the disciples’ humility in asking to be accepted as witnesses, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:53.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:54

> And the disbelievers made a plan ˹against Jesus˺, but Allah also planned —and Allah is the best of planners.

**Commentary:**

### Human Conspiracy and Divine Decree

The disbelievers plotted against Jesus and sought to stop his mission. The verse places their plan beside Allah’s plan and shows that human schemes remain within divine knowledge and power. People may make detailed arrangements, but they cannot overcome Allah’s decree or prevent the truth from reaching its appointed outcome.

This does not mean that human choices are unreal. The conspirators intended harm and are responsible for it. Allah’s knowledge of their actions does not excuse them. Divine decree encompasses their choices while divine justice holds them accountable.

### Allah Is the Best of Planners

The Arabic word *makr* can refer to a concealed plan or strategy. When attributed to human beings, it often means deceitful plotting. When attributed to Allah, it means that He brings the conspirators’ scheme to nothing and protects truth through a plan they cannot anticipate. It does not mean that Allah is deceitful or unjust. Allah is perfect in truth and justice.

The Qur’an uses similar language when the opponents of the Prophet ﷺ plotted to imprison, expel, or kill him (8:30). Their plan failed, while Allah’s decree preserved the Messenger and the message.

### Protection of Prophetic Mission

The verse assures believers that the mission of a prophet cannot be erased by killing, propaganda, or political conspiracy. A prophet may be harmed, rejected, or forced to migrate, but Allah preserves the truth he conveys. Jesus’ enemies could plot, but they could not cancel Allah’s purpose.

This is not a promise that every messenger avoids pain. The Qur’an records persecution and even the killing of prophets. It means that the final outcome of revelation is not controlled by its opponents.

### Caution in Using the Verse

Believers must not use “Allah is the best of planners” to justify their own deception or betrayal. A person cannot commit injustice and then claim that Allah’s plan makes his action righteous. The verse describes Allah’s victory over plots against truth; it is not a moral permission for Muslims to lie, break trusts, or harm the innocent.

The Prophet ﷺ was truthful even with opponents and commanded fulfilment of trusts. Human strategy is permitted in lawful defence, but it must remain within justice.

### Trust in Allah

The verse gives strength to people who feel surrounded by more powerful enemies. They should plan responsibly, seek counsel, take lawful means, and then trust Allah. No conspiracy is hidden from Him, and no apparent defeat makes truth worthless.

The believer also avoids paranoia. Not every disagreement is a conspiracy, and suspicion without evidence is forbidden (49:12). Trust in Allah should produce calm wisdom, not reckless accusation.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 8:30 on the plot against the Prophet ﷺ and Allah’s superior plan.
- Qur’an 9:32–33 on opponents’ attempts to extinguish Allah’s light and Allah’s completion of it.
- Qur’an 14:42 and 40:44 on Allah’s knowledge of the schemes of wrongdoers.
- Qur’an 49:12 on avoiding baseless suspicion and spying.
- Qur’an 5:8 and 16:90 on justice even when facing opposition.
- For *makr*, divine protection, human responsibility, and the meaning of Allah being the best of planners, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:54.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:55

> ˹Remember˺ when Allah said, “O Jesus! I will take you[138] and raise you up to Myself. I will deliver you from those who disbelieve, and elevate your followers above the disbelievers until the Day of Judgment. Then to Me you will ˹all˺ return, and I will settle all your disputes.

**Commentary:**

### Allah’s Protection of Jesus

Allah announces that He will take Jesus and raise him to Himself, delivering him from those who disbelieved. This verse must be read with 4:157–158, which states that Jesus was not killed or crucified by his enemies as they claimed, but that Allah raised him to Himself.

The Arabic verb *mutawaffīka* comes from a root meaning to take fully or receive completely. Classical exegetes discussed whether it refers to taking Jesus from the earth, causing him to die at a later time, or taking him in sleep before raising him. The broader Qur’anic evidence and the authentic hadith about Jesus’ return support the mainstream understanding that Allah raised him alive and that he will experience death after his return. The wording is therefore approached with belief and humility rather than speculative certainty about details the Qur’an does not fully spell out.

### Raised to Allah

“Raised to Myself” expresses honour, protection, and nearness to Allah; it does not mean that Allah occupies a physical location or that Jesus becomes part of Allah. Allah is not contained by space. The raising is a created event under Allah’s power, while Jesus remains a servant and messenger.

Allah raises whom He wills in rank and protects whom He wills from the plans of the wicked. Jesus’ enemies could not erase his mission or turn his death into the proof they sought.

### Deliverance from Disbelievers

Allah promised to deliver Jesus from those who rejected and plotted against him. Deliverance does not mean that a prophet never faces hostility. It means that hostile people cannot finally control the outcome of Allah’s mission. The truth of Jesus’ message survived the conspiracy against him.

The promise also comforts every believer facing persecution. The servant may suffer, migrate, or lose worldly security, but Allah sees the injustice and can preserve faith through unexpected means.

### The Followers of Jesus

Allah will elevate the followers of Jesus above the disbelievers until the Day of Judgement. This refers first to those who truly followed his message of worshipping Allah alone. It also includes the later triumph of the final revelation, which confirms the true message of Jesus and corrects claims made about him.

The verse should not be turned into a blanket guarantee for every group that uses Jesus’ name. A claim of following a prophet is tested by fidelity to his message. Nor is “elevation” limited to political domination; it can include proof, moral authority, survival of truth, and final vindication.

### Final Return and Disputes

All people will return to Allah, including Jesus, his followers, and those who opposed him. Allah will settle their disputes about his identity, mission, death, and message. Human debate can continue for centuries, but it cannot change the final truth.

The believer should therefore discuss Jesus with knowledge, justice, and respect rather than insult. Allah’s final judgement belongs to Him, while people are responsible for responding to the guidance that reaches them.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:157–159 on the rejection of Jesus’ killing and crucifixion and Allah’s raising of him.
- Qur’an 5:110–118 on Jesus’ signs, servanthood, and final testimony.
- Qur’an 19:30–36 on Jesus as servant, prophet, and word from Allah.
- Qur’an 61:14 on the disciples and the elevation of those who support the truth.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the hadith concerning Jesus’ descent near the end of time and his rule by justice.
- For *mutawaffīka*, raising Jesus, deliverance, the followers’ elevation, and the classical discussion of the verse, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:55.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:56

> “As for those who disbelieve, I will subject them to a severe punishment in this life and the Hereafter, and they will have no helpers.

**Commentary:**

### The Consequence of Rejection

This verse describes the consequence for those who reject Allah after truth has been made clear and persist in opposing His messengers. Their punishment is mentioned in this life and the Hereafter. The worldly form may include defeat, humiliation, loss of authority, exposure, inner misery, or lawful consequences for crimes and persecution.

Not every worldly hardship is proof that a person is being punished. Prophets and believers suffer, and some disbelievers enjoy prosperity. The verse concerns Allah’s judgement in its full wisdom, not a licence for human beings to label every illness, poverty, or disaster as a specific punishment.

### The Hereafter’s Greater Punishment

The Hereafter reveals the permanent consequence of deliberate rejection and oppression. Worldly punishment, when it occurs, is limited by time; the final punishment is connected to the way a person meets Allah. This is why the Qur’an repeatedly calls people to repent before death closes the opportunity.

The warning is not meant to remove hope. A person who turns sincerely to Allah, abandons rejection, and repairs his wrongdoing may receive forgiveness. The verse describes those who remain upon disbelief and hostility rather than those who return to truth.

### No Helpers

No helpers can protect the disbeliever from Allah’s final judgement. In the world, people rely on armies, political alliances, wealth, family, or public influence. These supports are temporary. On the Day of Judgement, no created power can reverse Allah’s decision.

The phrase also warns those who help oppression. A person should not assume that an institution or leader will shield him from responsibility. Each helper of wrongdoing is responsible for the part he knowingly played.

### Justice and Mercy

The verse is balanced by the promise of mercy elsewhere in the passage. Allah does not punish arbitrarily. He sends guidance, gives time, distinguishes those who sincerely seek truth, and accepts repentance. Severe punishment is attached to persistent rejection and wrongdoing, not to honest uncertainty that is being resolved.

The believer should therefore combine clarity about truth with compassion toward people. He calls to Allah without mocking, coercing, or pretending to know the unseen judgement of individuals.

- Qur’an 8:52–55 and 9:55–85 on worldly and eternal consequences of persistent rejection.
- Qur’an 6:42–45 and 7:94–99 on trials, warning, and the limits of judging particular disasters.
- Qur’an 4:17–18 and 25:70 on repentance before death and forgiveness after sincere return.
- Qur’an 2:48 and 11:113 on the absence of helpers and the prohibition of supporting oppression.
- Qur’an 39:53–54 on the breadth of Allah’s mercy and the command to return to Him.
- For the two forms of punishment, *nāṣirīn*, persistence in disbelief, and the balance of warning and mercy, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:56.
## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:57

> “And as for those who believe and do good, they will be rewarded in full. And Allah does not like the wrongdoers.”

**Commentary:**

### Faith and Righteous Action

The verse places belief and righteous action together. Faith is not a claim detached from conduct, and action is not spiritually complete when it is severed from sincere belief. *Īmān* shapes the heart, while *ʿamal ṣāliḥ* expresses that faith through worship, justice, mercy, honesty, and service.

Righteous action is action made sound by correct intention and lawful method. A person cannot seek Allah’s pleasure through oppression, fraud, or an act that revelation forbids. Good ends do not justify unlawful means.

### Reward in Full

Allah promises to reward the believers in full. Nothing of their effort is lost. The prayer no one heard, the charity given secretly, the restraint from anger, the care for a parent, and the patience endured in hardship are all known to Allah.

The phrase also reassures those whose good deeds appear to produce no immediate worldly result. A person may be mocked for honesty or lose wealth by refusing corruption, but Allah’s reward is not dependent on public recognition. Full reward may be given in this world, the Hereafter, or both, according to wisdom.

### Allah Does Not Love Wrongdoers

The verse closes with a warning against *ẓulm*, wrongdoing and injustice. Allah does not love those who harm people, violate rights, reject truth, or place things where they do not belong. This applies to individuals and communities. A religious identity cannot transform oppression into righteousness.

Wrongdoing also includes wronging the self through sin and spiritual neglect. The person who persists in injustice damages his own soul before the harm reaches others. Repentance requires stopping the wrong and restoring rights where possible.

### Mercy and Deeds

The promise of full reward does not mean that people earn Paradise independently of Allah’s mercy. The Prophet ﷺ taught that no one enters Paradise solely because of his deeds, but only through Allah’s mercy, while deeds are the means through which mercy is sought. The believer works sincerely and remains humble about acceptance.

This balance prevents both pride and despair. No deed is too small for Allah’s generosity, and no person should boast as though he has compelled Allah to reward him.

### Jesus and His Followers

In context, the verse contrasts those who reject Jesus’ message with those who believe and do good. It also establishes the standard by which every claim of following Jesus or any prophet is judged. Faith must produce righteousness. A community that claims to represent a prophet while committing oppression has contradicted his message.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:58

> We recite ˹all˺ this to you ˹O Prophet˺ as one of the signs[139] and ˹as˺ a wise reminder.

**Commentary:**

### Revelation, Not Invention

Allah states that He recites these accounts to the Prophet ﷺ. The wording attributes the source to Allah and presents the stories of Mary, Zakariyyā, Yaḥyā, and Jesus as revelation. Their purpose is not to satisfy curiosity about ancient history but to establish truth and correct religious disputes.

The Qur’an often says that the Prophet did not know these events through ordinary human access. Revelation brings knowledge from Allah and gives it a moral purpose. It also shows that the Qur’an’s account is not shaped by the preferences of a community that wants to elevate or diminish a prophet.

### Signs of Truth

The accounts are *āyāt*, signs. They point to Allah’s power, knowledge, mercy, and wisdom. The birth of Yaḥyā shows that Allah can answer an apparently impossible prayer; the birth of Jesus shows that extraordinary creation does not imply divinity; Mary’s purity shows the honour of sincere devotion.

A sign must be read correctly. People can see an extraordinary event and still draw the wrong conclusion. The Qur’an guides the reader from the event to the One who caused it.

### A Wise Reminder

The Qur’an is *al-dhikr al-ḥakīm*, a wise reminder. It reminds people of truths they forget: Allah alone deserves worship, prophets are servants, families are trusts, and worldly disputes will be judged by Allah. It is wise because its stories are selected, ordered, and applied to the needs of the audience.

A reminder is beneficial only when the reader allows it to correct him. Knowing the story of Mary while retaining arrogance, injustice, or false worship misses the story’s purpose.

### The Qur’an and Religious Disputes

The passage has addressed disputes about Jesus’ identity, Mary’s honour, and the continuity of revelation. The Qur’an does not answer those disputes through insult. It answers with revelation, evidence, history, and a call to worship Allah alone.

This provides a model for Muslim discussion. Religious disagreement should be met with knowledge and justice. A person may firmly reject a false doctrine without mocking the people who hold it or inventing accusations against them.

### Wisdom in Storytelling

The Qur’an’s accounts are concise but deep. It omits details that do not serve guidance and emphasizes speech, intention, trial, and response. This teaches readers not to confuse historical curiosity with beneficial knowledge. A wise reader asks what the story demands of his heart and conduct.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:59

> Indeed, the example of Jesus in the sight of Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust, then said to him, “Be!” And he was!

**Commentary:**

### The Decisive Comparison

The Qur’an answers disputes about Jesus by comparing him with Adam. If the absence of a human father made Jesus divine, Adam would have an even greater claim: Adam had neither father nor mother. Yet Adam was created by Allah and remained His servant. Jesus’ miraculous birth is therefore a sign of creation, not a proof of divinity.

The comparison is not meant to deny Jesus’ unique honour. It places his uniqueness in the correct category: Allah can create in extraordinary ways without the created being becoming a partner in Allah’s essence or authority.

### Created from Dust

Adam was created from dust, the material of the earth from which human bodies are formed. The reference emphasizes creaturely origin and dependence. Human beings may possess knowledge, beauty, strength, and spiritual rank, but they remain created from humble material and sustained by Allah.

Jesus too was a created human being, born from Mary and dependent upon food, rest, and Allah’s protection. The extraordinary mode of birth does not alter the basic truth of human servanthood.

### “Be, and He Was”

Allah’s command “Be” demonstrates effortless creative power. He does not need a father, mother, or biological process in order to create, though He ordinarily creates through means. Adam and Jesus show that causes are under Allah’s control, not that causes are gods.

The command also refutes the idea that Jesus is Allah’s literal offspring. Allah creates by His command; He does not beget. The Qur’an declares that nothing is like Him and that He neither begets nor is begotten (112:3–4).

### The Limits of Analogy

The comparison is an argument from the greater to the lesser: if Adam’s creation without parents did not make him divine, Jesus’ creation without a father cannot make him divine. The analogy does not say Adam and Jesus had identical missions or histories. It addresses the specific argument based on miraculous origin.

The Qur’an also distinguishes between Allah’s act of creating and the creature’s nature. A sign can be extraordinary while the recipient remains a servant.

### A Protection against Exaggeration

The verse protects love for Jesus from becoming worship of Jesus. Muslims honour him as the Messiah, a word from Allah, and a great prophet. They affirm his miracles and miraculous birth. They also affirm his own declaration that Allah is his Lord and their Lord (3:51).

This balance is the same balance required concerning all prophets: neither rejection nor exaggeration. The Prophet ﷺ warned against exaggerating in praising him as earlier communities exaggerated concerning their prophets.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 3:45–51 on Jesus’ birth, titles, mission, and call to worship Allah alone.
- Qur’an 2:117, 16:40, and 36:82 on Allah’s creative command.
- Qur’an 19:30 and 4:171 on Jesus as servant and messenger.
- Qur’an 112:1–4 and 6:101 on Allah’s uniqueness and transcendence beyond begetting.
- Qur’an 7:11–12 on Adam’s creation and Iblīs’s arrogance.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the Prophet’s warning against exaggeration concerning prophets.

## Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:60

> This is the truth from your Lord, so do not be one of those who doubt.

**Commentary:**

### The Truth from the Lord

After presenting the account of Mary and Jesus and the comparison with Adam, Allah declares that this is the truth from the Lord. *Al-ḥaqq* is what is true, established, and deserving of acceptance. The account is not mythology or a political compromise. It comes from Allah, whose knowledge is complete and whose speech is truthful.

The phrase “from your Lord” connects truth with care and guidance. Allah does not reveal these facts merely to win an argument. He reveals them as the Lord who nurtures people, corrects their beliefs, and guides them toward worshipping Him alone.

### Avoiding Doubt

The command not to doubt is addressed to the Prophet ﷺ and, through him, to every reader. It does not forbid sincere questions asked to gain understanding. The Qur’an itself records questions from prophets and believers. It warns against allowing pride, envy, or attachment to inherited claims to turn clear evidence into endless doubt.

A person may experience uncertainty and seek clarification. What is condemned is choosing doubt as a permanent refuge after truth has become clear, or using speculative objections to avoid submission.

### The Qur’an’s Argument about Jesus

The argument is simple and cumulative. Jesus was born miraculously, but miraculous birth is not divinity. He was called a Word from Allah, but Allah’s creative command does not make the created being part of Allah. He performed signs, but repeatedly by Allah’s permission. He called people to worship Allah, and he was compared with Adam, who was clearly created.

These truths protect the honour of Jesus while preserving tawḥīd. The Muslim believes in the revelation about him rather than allowing later theological disputes to determine the standard.

### Certainty and Worship

Correct belief should produce worship. If Jesus’ Lord is Allah and our Lord is Allah, then the practical response is to worship Allah alone, obey His revelation, and treat His messengers with honour. The purpose of certainty is not argumentative superiority but a sound relationship with Allah.

### The Danger of Inherited Doubt

People can inherit both beliefs and doubts from their communities. The verse invites independent responsibility before Allah. A person should examine evidence, seek knowledge, and ask Allah for guidance rather than accepting or rejecting claims merely because of social pressure.

At the same time, certainty does not require arrogance. The believer says that the Qur’an is true while remaining humble in his own interpretation and willing to learn from qualified scholarship.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 2:147, 10:94, and 18:29 on truth from Allah and the response to doubt.
- Qur’an 3:45–59 on Mary, Jesus, his signs, and comparison with Adam.
- Qur’an 4:171 and 5:72–75 on avoiding excess concerning Jesus.
- Qur’an 6:116 and 17:36 on avoiding speculation without knowledge.
- Qur’an 112:1–4 on the foundation of pure monotheism.
- For *ḥaqq*, doubt, certainty, and the Qur’an’s argument concerning Jesus, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 3:60.
