# Surah Ali 'Imran (The Family of 'Imran, 3) — Verse-by-Verse Tafsir, Verses 171–180

The last batch closed on the living martyrs: not dead, alive with their Lord, provided for, rejoicing, glad for those yet to join them. These ten verses continue that joy into the living community's next test — and then widen again into the long work of sorting, respite, and wealth.

What follows is first the martyrs' joy that God does not waste the believers' wage (171). Then the response after injury: those who answered God and the Messenger after they were wounded — the good-doers and God-fearing among them have a great reward (172). When warned that the people had gathered against them, the warning only increased them in faith; they said God is sufficient for us, the best Protector (173). So they returned with grace and bounty, untouched by harm; they sought God's pleasure; God is Lord of great bounty (174). That warning was only Satan prompting fear of his followers — so fear Me, not them, if you are believers (175). Do not grieve over those who race into disbelief; they harm God not at all; God wills them no share in the hereafter (176). Those who buy disbelief with faith harm God not at all; painful punishment (177). Let not the disbelievers think the long rope We give them is good for them; We only give them time so they may increase in sin; humiliating punishment (178). God will not leave the believers as you are until He distinguishes the bad from the good; He will not show you the unseen directly, but He chooses messengers as He wills; so believe and fear God for a great reward (179). And let not those who withhold what God gave them of His bounty think it is good for them — it is bad for them; what they withheld will be hung around their necks on the Day of Rising; to God belongs the heritage of the heavens and the earth (180).

Five cruxes govern the reading. The first is 172–174 and the climate of Ḥamrā' al-Asad: the day after Uḥud, wounded companions answering the call to go out again, the threat that the enemy had regrouped, and *ḥasbunā Allāh wa-niʿma al-wakīl* as the answer that increased faith. The second is 175 — Satan frightens his *awliyā'*; the believers' fear is redirected to God. The third is 176–178 on those who race into disbelief and the theology of respite (*imlā'*) that is not mercy in the end but increase in sin. The fourth is 179 — God will not leave you in a mixed state until He distinguishes *khabīth* from *ṭayyib*; the unseen is not a public database; messengers are chosen; believe and have *taqwā*. The fifth is 180 — *bukhl* with God's bounty, the neck-collar image on the Last Day, and God's sole inheritance of the heavens and the earth — continuous with *ghulūl* and spoils ethics.

The batch is recovery in motion: the dead rejoice, the wounded answer again, fear is renamed, sorting is promised as unfinished work, and withholding wealth is exposed as self-harm dressed as prudence.

## Verse 171

**"They are joyful for receiving Allah’s grace and bounty, and that Allah does not deny the reward of the believers."**

In simple terms: they rejoice in God's grace and bounty, and in the fact that God never lets the believers' reward go to waste.

**The martyrs' joy completed — and the living's guarantee.**

**"They are joyful for receiving Allah's grace and bounty, and that Allah does not deny the reward of the believers."** *Yastabshirūna bi-niʿmatin min Allāhi wa-faḍlin wa-anna Allāha lā yuḍīʿu ajra al-mu'minīn.*

The verse continues 170's *yastabshirūna*. *Bi-niʿmatin min Allāh wa-faḍlin* — in a grace from God and a bounty. Two near-synonyms stacked for fullness: gift and surplus, favour and overflow. *Wa-anna Allāha lā yuḍīʿu ajra al-mu'minīn* — and that God does not waste / cause to be lost the wage of the believers. The supplied "does not deny the reward" is fair dynamic equivalence of *lā yuḍīʿu* — He does not let it go to waste, does not misplace it, does not nullify it.

**Who rejoices in this.** The primary grammar continues the martyrs of 169–170: they are given gladness in grace and in the knowledge that believers' wages are safe with God. Classical voices (including readings noted by Ibn Kathīr from earlier authorities) also hear the clause as encompassing believers more widely — martyrs and others — because God rarely names a prophetic or martyrs' gift without extending the logic to the believing community. Both readings serve. The floor is: God's wage-system does not leak.

**Why this matters after Uḥud.** A community counting graves fears wasted life. Verse 171 answers: no wasted wage. The dead are not a sunk cost. The living who still believe are not working a broken economy. *Lā yuḍīʿu* is creed for the exhausted.

**Connection to 3:195 and the wider ajr vocabulary.** God does not waste the wage of any who do good — male or female. Here the principle is attached to the martyrs' joy and to the believers as a class. Continuous with 148's double wage and 136's excellent wage of workers.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When obedience has cost you something the world cannot refund — reputation, money, years, a grave — verse 171 is the refusal of the sunk-cost lie. God does not waste believers' wages. That does not mean every project succeeds on the world's scoreboard. It means the account with God does not misplace what was done for Him. Work. Trust the ledger you cannot see.

**Niʿmah and faḍl stacked.** Grace and bounty together refuse a thin reading of the martyrs' portion. What they receive is not bare survival of consciousness. It is favour with surplus — enough to produce the joy already named in 170. The living are meant to hear that the dead are not rationed meanly by a reluctant Lord.

**Lā yuḍīʿu as anti-despair creed.** After a disaster, communities invent stories of wasted sacrifice. Verse 171 forbids that story about believers' wages. God is not a careless paymaster. The account holds even when the world's scoreboard shows loss.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — the anti-waste prayer.** When you fear that what you gave was pointless, say what the verse teaches: God does not waste the believers' wage. Then continue the next obedience. Despair that invents waste is often a way of stopping work. The verse removes the excuse.

**Connection forward to 172.** The dead rejoice that wages are safe; the living are about to earn wages by answering after injury. Verse 171 is the guarantee under both labours — the labour finished in the path and the labour resumed with wounds still open.

## Verse 172

**"˹As for˺ those who responded to the call of Allah and His Messenger after their injury, those of them who did good and were mindful ˹of Allah˺ will have a great reward."**

In simple terms: as for those who answered God and His Messenger after they had been wounded — those among them who did good and were mindful of God will have a great reward.

**The verse of the second answering — faith after injury.**

**"As for those who responded to the call of Allah and His Messenger after their injury, those of them who did good and were mindful of Allah will have a great reward."** *Alladhīna istajābū li-llāhi wa-al-rasūli min baʿdi mā aṣābahumu al-qarḥ; li-lladhīna aḥsanū minhum wa-ttaqaw ajrun ʿaẓīm.*

*Istajābū* — they responded, answered the call. *Li-llāh wa-al-rasūl* — to God and the Messenger: the pair again. *Min baʿdi mā aṣābahumu al-qarḥ* — after the wound / injury had struck them. *Qarḥ* is the same word as 140's battlefield sore. The half-bracketed opening "As for" marks the new subject. Classical consensus places this on the aftermath of Uḥud — especially the mobilisation toward Ḥamrā' al-Asad the next day, when the Prophet ﷺ called those who had been present at Uḥud to go out again, and wounded men answered, some helped to walk. Other readings keep the wording general for any who answer after injury. The Uḥud/Ḥamrā' climate is the strongest historical frame; the moral claim outruns one march.

**"Those of them who did good and were mindful."** *Li-lladhīna aḥsanū minhum wa-ttaqaw.*

Not every responder is flattened into one rank. *Aḥsanū* — did *iḥsān*, excellence. *Wa-ttaqaw* — and had *taqwā*. *Minhum* — of them: a partitive that preserves sorting even inside a praised group. Great reward (*ajrun ʿaẓīm*) is stated for the good-doers and God-fearing among those who answered. Continuous with 134's *muḥsinīn* and the surah's refusal of flat equality (163).

**Why "after their injury" is the spiritual centre.** Answering before injury is one kind of courage. Answering after the body has already paid is another. Uḥud had already cut them. The call came again. They went. That sequence is how a community proves it was not only victory-faith.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** The second call after failure is where many religious lives end. The project failed; the marriage hit the wall; the public stand cost you; the body is tired. Verse 172 praises those who still answer God and the Messenger after the *qarḥ*. You do not have to feel uninjured. You have to respond. *Iḥsān* and *taqwā* inside that response are how the great reward is attached — not mere motion, but excellence and mindfulness in the motion.

**Allāh and the Messenger as the call's source.** They did not answer a mood or a faction. They answered God and the Messenger. That pair keeps the second mobilisation inside revelation's chain of command, not inside revenge-heat alone. After 159's gentleness and consultation, 172 shows the fruit: people still answer when he calls after the wound.

**Aḥsanū minhum wa-ttaqaw — sorting inside praise.** Even a praised expedition is not a flat halo. *Iḥsān* and *taqwā* remain the inner criteria. Great reward attaches to those qualities among the responders. The surah will not stop sorting just because a group did something brave.

**Qarḥ as continuity with 140.** The same wound-word binds the theodicy of rotated days to the ethics of answering again. God takes martyrs and purges hearts; He also calls the wounded to stand up once more. The religion is not only explanation of pain. It is mobilisation after pain.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — the morning after.** The hardest obedience is often the morning after the night of failure. Verse 172 is written for that morning. Answer. Walk if you can. Be carried if you must. Let *iḥsān* and *taqwā* shape the answering so it is not only adrenaline.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 173

**"Those who were warned, “Your enemies have mobilized their forces against you, so fear them,” the warning only made them grow stronger in faith and they replied, “Allah ˹alone˺ is sufficient ˹as an aid˺ for us and ˹He˺ is the best Protector.”"**

In simple terms: when people warned them, "Your enemies have gathered against you, so fear them," the warning only increased their faith, and they said, "God is enough for us; He is the best Protector."

**The verse of ḥasbunā Allāh — fear inverted into faith.**

**"Those who were warned, 'Your enemies have mobilized their forces against you, so fear them,' the warning only made them grow stronger in faith."** *Alladhīna qāla lahumu al-nāsu inna al-nāsa qad jamaʿū lakum fa-ikhshawhum fa-zādahum īmānan.*

*Al-nāsu* — people said to them. In the Ḥamrā' al-Asad frame, the warning arrives as psychological warfare: the enemy has gathered; fear them. Some reports name intermediaries carrying Abu Sufyān's threat back toward Madinah. The verse's own claim does not depend on one name: people said the people have gathered against you, so fear them. *Fa-zādahum īmānan* — and it increased them in faith. The threat had the opposite of its intended effect. Fear-propaganda became fuel for *īmān*.

**"And they replied, 'Allah alone is sufficient as an aid for us and He is the best Protector.'"** *Wa-qālū ḥasbunā Allāhu wa-niʿma al-wakīl.*

*Ḥasbunā Allāh* — God is sufficient for us / enough for us. *Wa-niʿma al-wakīl* — and what an excellent Trustee / Disposer of affairs / Protector. The half-brackets "alone" and "as an aid" unpack *ḥasbunā*. This sentence is among the most repeated *adhkar* in Muslim life, and this verse is one of its root-texts — alongside the tradition that Ibrāhīm said it when thrown to the fire. After Uḥud, wounded believers say it when told to fear a regrouped enemy. Trust is not the absence of a threat. Trust is the answer spoken into the threat.

**What the sentence does theologically.** *Ḥasb* blocks the need for a second ultimate patron. *Al-wakīl* names God as the One entrusted with the outcome. Means are still taken — they marched; they showed force; fires were lit in the sīrah's strategic memory. *Tawakkul* is not the refusal of means. It is the refusal to let the threat write the last word on the heart.

**Connection to 3:160 and 3:173.** If God helps, none overcomes; if He withholds, who helps after Him? Here the community answers a concrete threat with sufficiency and trusteeship. Same dependence, now as spoken *dhikr* under mobilisation.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When the message arrives — they have gathered against you, the numbers are bad, the campaign is coming, fear them — verse 173 offers a tested script. Let the warning increase faith, not panic. Say: God is sufficient for us; He is the best *wakīl*. Then take the next lawful means under that sentence, not instead of it. The companions walked wounded toward Ḥamrā' al-Asad with this in their mouths. You can walk into a courtroom, a diagnosis, a hostile meeting with the same words without cosplaying a battlefield.

**Fa-zādahum īmānan — the miracle of inverted effect.** Propaganda is designed to reduce faith. Here it increases it. That inversion is one of the Qur'an's marks of a heart already anchored. The same words that would undo a hypocrite strengthen a believer. Uḥud has been training that anchor; 173 shows it holding under a new gust.

**Niʿma al-wakīl and means.** The sīrah's picture of fires lit and presence shown at Ḥamrā' al-Asad is not a contradiction of *tawakkul*. It is *tawakkul*'s companion: take means, entrust outcomes. Saying God is sufficient does not mean sitting in Madinah counting wounds while the threat writes the story.

**Al-nāsu… al-nāsu — people saying the people.** The doubled "people" is almost bureaucratic fear: social report of social gathering. Faith answers social fear with a Name. Not with a bigger crowd-count first — with *ḥasbunā Allāh*. Numbers still matter tactically; they do not get to be theology.

**Connection to Ibrāhīm.** The tradition pairing this sentence with Ibrāhīm in the fire teaches that the same *ḥasb* covers the extreme of being thrown and the extreme of being threatened after a wound. One sentence; many fires.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — rehearse the sentence before the threat.** Do not wait until the gathering is announced to learn *ḥasbunā Allāh wa-niʿma al-wakīl*. Say it in smaller fears so that it is ready in larger ones. The companions could answer at once because the Name was already their sufficient.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 174

**"So they returned with Allah’s favours and grace, suffering no harm. For they sought to please Allah. And surely Allah is ˹the˺ Lord of infinite bounty."**

In simple terms: so they came back with God's favour and grace, and nothing bad touched them. They had sought God's pleasure. God is the Lord of great bounty.

**The return — grace without the harm the threat promised.**

**"So they returned with Allah's favours and grace, suffering no harm."** *Fa-nqalabū bi-niʿmatin min Allāhi wa-faḍlin lam yamsashum sū'.

*Inqalabū* — they returned, turned back (from the mobilisation). *Bi-niʿmatin… wa-faḍlin* — with grace and bounty: the same pair as 171, now on the living who went out after injury. *Lam yamsashum sū'* — no evil / harm touched them. In the historical frame, the feared second engagement did not land as the threat had advertised; they returned without the slaughter the propaganda promised. The verse's claim is the return under grace, untouched by the *sū'* the warning had painted.

**"For they sought to please Allah. And surely Allah is the Lord of infinite bounty."** *Wa-ttabaʿū riḍwāna Allāh; wa-Allāhu dhū faḍlin ʿaẓīm.*

*Ittabaʿū riḍwāna Allāh* — they followed / pursued God's good pleasure. Continuous with 162's binary: these are people on the *riḍwān* side of the ledger. *Dhū faḍlin ʿaẓīm* — possessor of great bounty. The bounty that provisioned the martyrs (170–171) is the same bounty that brought the wounded responders home under grace.

**Connection to 152's early sweep and 174's return.** God can give a day of fulfilled promise and a day of test and a return of grace after a second answering. None of these cancels the others. The community is being trained to read multiple kinds of day under one Lord of bounty.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Not every act of trust ends with "no harm touched them" in the world's sense. The verse is a particular story's outcome, not a mechanical guarantee for every risk. What transfers is the structure: answer after injury; let threat increase faith; seek *riḍwān*; recognise grace when you return. Sometimes harm does touch you — and 169–170 still hold for those killed in the path. Sometimes harm does not — and 174 teaches you to name that as *niʿmah* and *faḍl*, not as your nerve alone.

**Lam yamsashum sū' — no harm touched them.** In a surah that has insisted on real wounds and real graves, this clause is specific grace for this return, not a claim that the path never harms. Hold the specificity. Celebrate the grace. Do not rewrite 140 and 169 with 174.

**Ittabaʿū riḍwān as the cause-clause flavour.** They returned under bounty *as people who had pursued God's pleasure*. The verse ties the graceful return to the orientation of seeking *riḍwān*, without turning the tie into a coin-machine. Orientation matters. Bounty is God's.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When a threatened disaster does not land, practise naming: grace, bounty, no harm touched us, we had sought His pleasure. Gratitude after averted harm is part of *taqwā*. Forgetting averted harm is how entitlement refills after Uḥud's lessons.

**Dhū faḍlin ʿaẓīm after a season of loss.** The title lands differently after Uḥud than it would after only Badr. Bounty is not only the bounty of victory. It is also the bounty of a return without the second slaughter, of faith increased by threat, of wages not wasted for the dead. Learn to count bounty in more than one column.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 175

**"That ˹warning˺ was only ˹from˺ Satan, trying to prompt you to fear his followers. So do not fear them; fear Me if you are ˹true˺ believers."**

In simple terms: that warning was only Satan trying to make you afraid of his followers. So do not fear them; fear Me, if you are true believers.

**The unmasking of the threat's author.**

**"That warning was only from Satan, trying to prompt you to fear his followers."** *Innamā dhālikumu al-shayṭānu yukhawwifu awliyā'ahu.*

*Innamā* — only / nothing but. *Dhālikum al-shayṭān* — that is Satan (the warning's unseen driver). *Yukhawwifu awliyā'ahu* — he frightens his allies / seeks to make you fear his allies. The grammar allows the classical double edge: Satan frightens *his* followers (makes them instruments of fear), and/or he tries to frighten *you* by means of his followers. The supplied "trying to prompt you to fear his followers" takes the second edge and is the pastoral reading most needed after 173: the mobilised enemy are not the ultimate object of fear; they are inside a satanic strategy of intimidation.

**"So do not fear them; fear Me if you are true believers."** *Fa-lā takhāfūhum wa-khāfūni in kuntum mu'minīn.*

*Lā takhāfūhum* — do not fear them. *Wa-khāfūni* — and fear Me. The redirection is absolute in priority, not a claim that danger is imaginary. *In kuntum mu'minīn* — if you are believers; the half-bracketed "true" marks the qualitative condition. Belief is proven partly by which fear governs you when both fears are available: fear of the gathering people, or fear of God.

**Connection to 3:175 and Ibrāhīm's fear-file.** The Qur'an elsewhere teaches that Satan threatens poverty and orders indecency, while God promises forgiveness and bounty. Here Satan's specific tool is fear of his *awliyā'*. The believers' tool is fear of God — *taqwā* raised to the moment of threat.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Name the author of the panic. Not every warning is Satan — medicine, honest counsel, and real scouting are not *takhawwuf*. But the voice that wants you to abandon a right path because "they have gathered," the voice that makes the enemy ultimate, the voice that dissolves *tawakkul* — verse 175 authorises unmasking that voice. Do not fear them. Fear Me. Then re-enter the means under the right fear.

**Awliyā'ahu — his allies.** Satan's network is not only unseen whispers. It includes human followers who become instruments of intimidation. Fearing them as ultimate is already a kind of misplaced *wilāyah*. Verse 175 breaks that misplacement: they are his, not yours to fear as gods of the moment.

**In kuntum mu'minīn as the test of fear.** Belief is not only assent to propositions. It is which fear wins when both are present. The verse makes fear of God the mark of *īmān* under threat — continuous with *taqwā* throughout the surah.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Before you cancel a right action because "they will come for you," run 175. Is this scouting — or *takhawwuf*? Is the fear teaching *taqwā* — or teaching surrender to Satan's allies as ultimate? Redirect the fear. Then decide under God, not under them.

**Connection to 3:150 and 3:175.** God is the best helper; Satan frightens with his helpers. The two helper-networks are not symmetrical. One is *mawlā* and *wakīl*. The other is a fear-machine. Choose which network defines the moment.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 176

**"˹O Prophet!˺ Do not grieve for those who race to disbelieve—surely they will not harm Allah in the least. It is Allah’s Will to disallow them a share in the Hereafter, and they will suffer a tremendous punishment."**

In simple terms: O Prophet, do not grieve over those who rush into disbelief. They will not harm God at all. God wills that they have no share in the hereafter, and theirs is a tremendous punishment.

**Comfort to the Messenger — and a limit on prophetic grief.**

**"O Prophet! Do not grieve for those who race to disbelieve — surely they will not harm Allah in the least."** *Wa-lā yaḥzunka alladhīna yusāriʿūna fī al-kufri; innahum lan yaḍurrū Allāha shay'an.*

The half-bracketed "O Prophet" correctly marks the addressee. *Lā yaḥzunka* — let them not grieve you / do not let them cause you grief. *Yusāriʿūna fī al-kufr* — they race / hasten in disbelief: the same racing verb as 133's race to forgiveness, now inverted toward *kufr*. *Lan yaḍurrū Allāha shay'an* — they will not harm God at all. Continuous with 144's heel-turn that does not harm God. Apostasy and rushed disbelief are real moral disasters for the one who chooses them. They are not injuries to God's kingdom.

**"It is Allah's Will to disallow them a share in the Hereafter, and they will suffer a tremendous punishment."** *Yurīdu Allāhu allā yajʿala lahum ḥaẓẓan fī al-ākhirah; wa-lahum ʿadhābun ʿaẓīm.*

*Yurīdu Allāh* — God wills. *Allā yajʿala lahum ḥaẓẓan fī al-ākhirah* — that He not make for them a portion in the hereafter. The supplied "disallow them a share" is accurate. This is not arbitrary spite. It is the will that matches their racing into disbelief — the end of a path freely run. *ʿAdhābun ʿaẓīm* — tremendous punishment.

**Why the Prophet is told not to grieve this grief.** His mercy wants people in. The Qur'an repeatedly both honours that mercy and bounds it: you do not guide whom you love as a unilateral power; you do not carry their choice as your failure when they race the other way. After Uḥud's hypocrites and the wider Madinan field of people turning away, the Messenger is protected from a grief that would crush the mission.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Parents, teachers, daʿwah workers — anyone who loves people into faith — know the grief of watching someone race the wrong way. Verse 176 does not order coldness. It orders a boundary: their race does not harm God; it need not destroy you; the hereafter's share is not yours to force. Do your work. Leave their end with the One who wills with justice.

**Yusāriʿūna fī al-kufr — racing.** Disbelief here is not only a static label. It is motion — hastening into it. The energy of 133's race to forgiveness is mirrored and inverted. People run toward something. The verse names the wrong finish line and tells the Prophet not to let that race own his heart.

**Ḥaẓẓ fī al-ākhirah — portion in the hereafter.** The language of share and portion continues the commercial and inheritance vocabulary of the surah. Some paths end with no share. That is the will that matches the race, not a random exclusion.

**Connection to 3:176 and the Prophet's character.** The Qur'an elsewhere says the Messenger is grieved by your suffering and eager for you. Verse 176 does not cancel that character. It stops a particular grief from becoming the mission's centre: grief over people racing into disbelief as if that race were God's injury or the Prophet's failure of force.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — bound the grief.** Love people. Call them. When they race the other way, do not let their race become your identity. God is not harmed. The mission continues. Your heart may ache; it need not collapse into the fiction that you failed because they chose.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 177

**"Those who trade belief for disbelief will never harm Allah in the least, and they will suffer a painful punishment."**

In simple terms: those who buy disbelief at the price of faith will not harm God at all, and they will have a painful punishment.

**The trade — faith sold, disbelief purchased.**

**"Those who trade belief for disbelief will never harm Allah in the least, and they will suffer a painful punishment."** *Inna alladhīna ishtarawū al-kufra bi-l-īmāni lan yaḍurrū Allāha shay'an; wa-lahum ʿadhābun alīm.*

*Ishtarawū al-kufra bi-l-īmān* — they bought disbelief with faith / in exchange for faith. The commercial metaphor is the Qur'an's standing image for the worst bargain (2:16, 2:86, 2:175). Something was held — *īmān* — and sold to acquire *kufr*. *Lan yaḍurrū Allāha shay'an* — again, no harm to God. *ʿAdhābun alīm* — painful punishment.

**Connection to 3:177 and the Uḥud hypocrites.** Those nearer to disbelief on the day of the meeting (167), those who sat and blamed the dead (168), those who race into disbelief (176) — the trade-language names the transaction under the postures. Faith was available. Disbelief was chosen as the purchase.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** The trade still happens without a formal declaration of apostasy: selling a clear conviction for a seat, a contract, a relationship, a silence that pays. Verse 177 keeps the ledger honest. The trade does not bruise God. It bruises the trader. Painful punishment is the end of that account unless *tawbah* rewrites the path while life remains.

**The purchase metaphor's cruelty and clarity.** To buy *kufr* with *īmān* is to spend the more precious for the less. The bargain is irrational in the Qur'an's economy and still commonly struck. Verse 177 keeps both the irrationality and the consequence visible without melodrama: no harm to God; painful punishment for the trader.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Name the trades you are being offered: silence for access, doctrine for salary, loyalty to God for loyalty to a faction. Verse 177 says the trade does not harm God and does pain the trader. Walk away while the price is still only opportunity cost, not *ʿadhāb alīm*.

**Connection to 2:16 and the purchase of error.** Al-Baqarah's traders of guidance for error are the same moral family. Āl 'Imrān places the trade next to Uḥud's exposures so that no one thinks hypocrisy is only a soft social problem. It is a bargain with an end.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 178

**"Those who disbelieve should not think that living longer is good for them. They are only given more time to increase in sin, and they will suffer a humiliating punishment."**

In simple terms: those who disbelieve should not think that the extra time We give them is good for them. We only give them more time so they may increase in sin, and they will have a humiliating punishment.

**The theology of respite — long rope as test, not as approval.**

**"Those who disbelieve should not think that living longer is good for them. They are only given more time to increase in sin, and they will suffer a humiliating punishment."** *Wa-lā yaḥsabanna alladhīna kafarū annamā numlī lahum khayrun li-anfusihim; innamā numlī lahum li-yazdādū ithman; wa-lahum ʿadhābun muhīn.*

*Numlī lahum* — We grant them respite / extend them time / give them rope. *Lā yaḥsabanna… khayrun li-anfusihim* — let them not think it is good for them. The apparent good of long life, success, delay of punishment, open freedom to work corruption — is not the good they imagine. *Innamā numlī lahum li-yazdādū ithman* — We only extend them so that they may increase in sin. *ʿAdhābun muhīn* — humiliating punishment: disgrace layered on pain.

**What this does and does not say.** It does not say every disbeliever's every day of health is only for sin-increase in a way that cancels the door of *tawbah* — the Qur'an keeps that door open elsewhere with force. It does say that reading respite as divine approval is a catastrophic misread. Delay is not endorsement. Long leash is not love of the path of *kufr*. Continuous with 3:178's family across the Qur'an: We give them rope; they think it good; it is not.

**Connection to Uḥud's aftermath.** Quraysh walked away from the hill without taking Madinah. A short-sighted reading says: they won; God favoured them. Verse 178 blocks that reading for anyone who rejects and still breathes. Respite remains. The increase in sin remains a danger of that respite. Humiliating punishment remains the end for those who use the rope to climb further into wrong.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When you watch injustice flourish and honest people struggle, verse 178 is one of the Qur'an's hardest medicines. Do not envy the rope. Do not read delay as God's smile on corruption. And if you yourself are on a wrong path that still "works," do not call the working *khayr*. It may be *imlā'* — rope toward increase in sin. Cut the path while the rope is still also time for return.

**Khayrun li-anfusihim — good for themselves.** The misread is self-referential: they think the rope benefits *them*. The verse says the rope's purpose in this naming is increase in sin. The moral is not "never enjoy health if you are not a believer." The moral is "do not interpret ongoing capacity to wrong people as evidence you are fine with God."

**Muhīn — humiliating.** Punishment that disgraces. After Uḥud's concern with honour, shame, and the enemy's swagger, the verse promises a final humiliation for those who used respite to deepen wrong. Worldly swagger is not the last word on honour.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** When a corrupt order lasts, do not let the lasting recruit you into either envy or collapse. Respite can be rope. Your job is to stay on the side that does not need that rope to keep wronging people — and to use whatever rope you have for *tawbah* and repair if you have been wrong.

**Parallel misreadings: respite and bukhl.** 178 and 180 both attack a false *khayr*. Long rope looks good; withholding looks good. Both are named otherwise. The surah is training the community to distrust the self's first score of what is good after a season when the self's scores already failed on a hill.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 179

**"Allah would not leave the believers in the condition you were in, until He distinguished the good from the evil ˹among you˺. Nor would Allah ˹directly˺ reveal to you the unseen, but He chooses whoever He wills as a messenger. So believe in Allah and His messengers. And if you are faithful and mindful ˹of Allah˺, you will receive a great reward."**

In simple terms: God will not leave the believers in the mixed condition you were in until He distinguishes the bad from the good among you. God will not give you direct access to the unseen; instead He chooses messengers as He wills. So believe in God and His messengers. If you believe and are mindful of God, you will have a great reward.

**The sorting-doctrine stated at full strength — Uḥud as method, not as exception.**

**"Allah would not leave the believers in the condition you were in, until He distinguished the good from the evil among you."** *Mā kāna Allāhu li-yadhara al-mu'minīna ʿalā mā antum ʿalayhi ḥattā yamīza al-khabītha min al-ṭayyib.*

*Mā kāna Allāhu li-yadhara* — God would not leave / it is not for God to leave. *Al-mu'minīn ʿalā mā antum ʿalayhi* — the believers in the state you are upon: the mixed state, sincere and hypocritical intermingled, untested ranks unclear. *Ḥattā yamīza al-khabītha min al-ṭayyib* — until He distinguishes / separates the foul from the good. The supplied "good from the evil among you" matches the classical sense (*ṭayyib* / *khabīth*). Mujāhid and others explicitly link this distinguishing to Uḥud; Qatādah widens to *jihād* and *hijrah* as sorting fields. The verse's claim is structural: God does not intend a permanent hotchpotch ummah. He sorts. Days like Uḥud are among the means.

**"Nor would Allah directly reveal to you the unseen, but He chooses whoever He wills as a messenger."** *Wa-mā kāna Allāhu li-yuṭliʿakum ʿalā al-ghaybi wa-lākinna Allāha yajtabī min rusulihi man yashā'.

*Li-yuṭliʿakum ʿalā al-ghayb* — to give you access to / inform you of the unseen. The community does not get a public feed of who is hypocrite and who is saint in advance. The half-bracketed "directly" marks that limit. *Yajtabī min rusulihi man yashā'* — He selects of His messengers whom He wills — and may give a messenger something of the unseen (as the Prophet ﷺ was given knowledge of some hypocrites' states in reports). Sorting for the ummah at large happens through tests and signs, not through a disclosed database of hearts.

**"So believe in Allah and His messengers. And if you are faithful and mindful of Allah, you will receive a great reward."** *Fa-āminū bi-llāhi wa-rusulihi; wa-in tu'minū wa-tattaqū fa-lakum ajrun ʿaẓīm.*

The practical response to the sorting-doctrine is not paranoia. It is *īmān* in God and His messengers, and *taqwā*. Great reward again — continuous with 172. You cannot force the unseen open. You can believe and be mindful while God does the distinguishing His way.

**Connection to 3:141, 3:154, 3:166–167, 3:179.** Purge, test of breasts and hearts, distinguish believers, expose hypocrites — now stated as God's refusal to leave the mixed state permanent. Uḥud is inside a policy, not a glitch.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Stop demanding a list of who is pure. Start accepting that God uses pressure to make purity and impurity show. Your job under that policy is not to become the sorter-in-chief. Your job is to become *ṭayyib* under the test — believe, have *taqwā*, take the great reward. The unseen stays God's. The messengers are His choice. The mixed camp will not be left mixed forever.

**Two mā kāna Allāh clauses.** God would not leave you mixed; God would not hand you the unseen. The twin refusals structure a whole epistemology of community life: expect sorting through events; do not expect a revealed spreadsheet of hearts; take what messengers are given as God wills; respond with faith and *taqwā*.

**Yajtabī — He selects.** Messenger-ship is choice, not human application. The community that has just been told Muḥammad is only a messenger (144) and a favour from among themselves (164) is told again that selection of messengers is God's. Sorting and revelation-channels both remain theistic, not populist.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Accept unfinished sorting. The mosque, the movement, the family, the ummah online — all are mixed. God has said He will not leave believers in that mix without distinction. Your response is not to play God with private lists. It is to believe, practise *taqwā*, and become clearly *ṭayyib* when the distinguishing day arrives for you.

**Great reward again.** 172 and 179 both end on *ajrun ʿaẓīm* for those who combine the right response with *taqwā*. The surah keeps attaching weighty wage to mindfulness under pressure, not only to dramatic gestures.

**Ḥattā yamīza — until He distinguishes.** The *ḥattā* is purpose and term. Sorting is not instant at the first difficulty for every person, and it is not postponed forever. God has a until. Uḥud was one until. Other untils will come. Live as someone who expects to be made evident.


===VERSE-END===

## Verse 180

**"And do not let those who ˹greedily˺ withhold Allah’s bounties think it is good for them—in fact, it is bad for them! They will be leashed ˹by their necks˺ on the Day of Judgment with whatever ˹wealth˺ they used to withhold. And Allah is the ˹sole˺ inheritor of the heavens and the earth. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do."**

In simple terms: those who greedily withhold what God has given them of His bounty should not think that is good for them — it is bad for them. On the Day of Judgment they will be collared with what they withheld. To God belongs the inheritance of the heavens and the earth. God is fully aware of what you do.

**The bukhl-verse — withholding as self-harm, and God's sole inheritance.**

**"And do not let those who greedily withhold Allah's bounties think it is good for them — in fact, it is bad for them!"** *Wa-lā yaḥsabanna alladhīna yabkhalūna bi-mā ātāhumu Allāhu min faḍlihi huwa khayran lahum; bal huwa sharrun lahum.*

*Yabkhalūna* — they are stingy / withhold / refuse to spend. *Bi-mā ātāhumu Allāhu min faḍlihi* — with what God gave them of His bounty. The half-bracketed "greedily" colours *bukhl* as the translation chooses. *Lā yaḥsabanna… khayran lahum* — let them not think it good for them. *Bal huwa sharrun lahum* — rather it is bad for them. The same structure as 178's misread of respite: what looks like self-preserving prudence is *sharr*. Continuous with 3:180's family and with 2:276's *bukhl* against *ribā*'s backdrop — and with this surah's own 130 (*ribā*) and 161 (*ghulūl*).

**"They will be leashed by their necks on the Day of Judgment with whatever wealth they used to withhold."** *Sa-yuṭawwaqūna mā bakhilū bihi yawma al-qiyāmah.*

*Yuṭawwaqūna* — they will be collared / have hung about their necks. The image is concrete in the tafsir and hadith climate: what was withheld becomes a burden on the neck on the Last Day. The supplied "leashed by their necks" and "wealth" are dynamic completions of a verse that says they will be collared with what they withheld. Spoils, zakat due, charity refused, public trusts pocketed — the category is withholding God's bounty as if it were private absolute property.

**"And Allah is the sole inheritor of the heavens and the earth. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do."** *Wa-li-llāhi mīrāthu al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍ; wa-Allāhu bi-mā taʿmalūna khabīr.*

*Mīrāth* — inheritance, heritage. Everything returns to Him. The withholder does not keep the bounty past death. God inherits the heavens and the earth — the half-bracketed "sole" marks the exclusivity of that return. *Khabīr* — fully aware of what you do: including the quiet *bukhl* that never made a headline.

**Connection to 3:180 and 3:134.** The *muttaqīn* spend in ease and hardship. The *bākhil* withholds and calls it good. Uḥud's world-want and this verse's neck-collar are one moral world: the hands that left a post for spoils and the hands that will not open for God's due are related hands.

**Now bring this into ordinary life.** Audit what you call "prudence." Is it planning, or is it *bukhl* with what God already named as His bounty in your hand? Verse 180 says the withholder's self-score is inverted: not *khayr*, but *sharr*; not safety, but a future collar. And the final frame: you inherit nothing permanently. God inherits all. Spend as a trustee. Do not live as a final owner of what will return to Him anyway.

**And the spiritual centre of the ten verses.** The martyrs rejoice that wages are not wasted. The wounded answer God and the Messenger again; *iḥsān* and *taqwā* among them take a great reward. Threat increases faith; *ḥasbunā Allāh wa-niʿma al-wakīl*; they return under grace, seeking *riḍwān*. Satan frightens with his allies — fear God, not them. Do not grieve the racers into disbelief; they do not harm God; no share in the hereafter. The trade of faith for disbelief does not harm God; painful punishment. Respite is not good for those who use it to increase sin; humiliating punishment. God will not leave the mixed camp mixed; He distinguishes foul from good without handing the ummah a public unseen; believe and have *taqwā*. Withholders of bounty misread their *bukhl* as good; it is bad; the collar comes; God inherits all.

Uḥud's school ends this stretch with trust spoken, sorting promised, and hands ordered open. The next batches turn again toward the People of the Book, the covenant's claims, and the long closing movements of the surah.

**Sharrun lahum — bad for them.** The inversion is the point. Stinginess feels like safety. God names it evil for the stingy. That naming is mercy: it attacks the self-deception while there is still time to open the hand.

**Mīrāth al-samāwāt wa-al-arḍ.** Final ownership reframe. You do not keep the heavens. You do not keep the earth. You do not keep the surplus you called yours. Withholding from a path that returns everything to God is not only selfish. It is metaphysically confused. Live as a trustee of *faḍl*, not as a final heir of what God alone inherits.

**Connection to 3:180 and 57:10 / spending ranks.** Wealth is a test of open hands as much as battle is a test of firm feet. Uḥud taught feet and posts. This verse teaches hands and necks. The same Lord who rotated days collars withholders. Live accordingly before the collar is not a metaphor but a meeting.

**Now bring this into ordinary life — open the hand this week.** Pick one *faḍl* you have been treating as private absolute — money, time, expertise, access — and move a portion into God's path as trustee. Verse 180 is not only threat. It is invitation to reverse the collar while the neck is still free.


===VERSE-END===

## Synthesis of the Ten Verses

These ten verses move from the martyrs' completed joy into the living community's second answering, the unmasking of fear, the theology of respite, the promise of sorting, and the exposure of *bukhl*.

**171–174: joy, response after injury, sufficiency, return.** The martyrs rejoice in grace and bounty and in God's refusal to waste believers' wages (171). Those who answered God and the Messenger after the *qarḥ* — the *muḥsinīn* and *muttaqīn* among them — have a great reward (172). When told the people had gathered, the warning increased their faith; they said *ḥasbunā Allāh wa-niʿma al-wakīl* (173). They returned with grace and bounty, untouched by the threatened harm; they pursued *riḍwān*; God is Lord of great bounty (174). The Ḥamrā' al-Asad climate is the strongest frame: wounded obedience after Uḥud, threat as psychological warfare, trust as speech and march.

**175–178: fear redirected; racers into kufr; respite unmasked.** That warning was Satan prompting fear of his allies — do not fear them; fear Me if you believe (175). Do not grieve those who race into disbelief; they harm God not at all; no share in the hereafter; tremendous punishment (176). Those who buy disbelief with faith harm God not at all; painful punishment (177). Respite is not good for the disbelievers who misread it; it is rope to increase sin; humiliating punishment (178).

**179–180: sorting policy and open hands.** God will not leave the believers in the mixed state until He distinguishes *khabīth* from *ṭayyib*; He will not hand the ummah the unseen as a public feed; He chooses messengers; so believe and have *taqwā* for a great reward (179). Withholders of God's bounty misread *bukhl* as good; it is bad; what they withheld collars them on the Last Day; to God belongs the inheritance of the heavens and the earth (180).

**Threads carried and extended.**

- ***Ajr not wasted*** — 171 with 136, 148, 195: the ledger holds.
- ***Qarḥ / second answering*** — 140's wound → 172's response after injury; Ḥamrā' al-Asad climate.
- ***Ḥasbunā Allāh / tawakkul / wakīl*** — 122, 159–160 → 173's spoken sufficiency under threat.
- ***Riḍwān*** — 162 binary → 174's pursuit of pleasure; return under bounty.
- ***Satan and fear*** — 155's slip → 175's *takhawwuf* of his *awliyā'*; fear redirected to God.
- ***No harm to God*** — 144, 176, 177: heel-turn and *kufr*-race do not injure God.
- ***Imlā' / respite*** — 178 as misread "good" parallel to 180's misread "good" of *bukhl*.
- ***Tamḥīṣ / distinguishing*** — 141, 154, 166–167 → 179's full policy: not left mixed; *khabīth* / *ṭayyib*.
- ***Wealth ethics*** — 130 *ribā*, 134 spending, 161 *ghulūl* → 180 *bukhl* and neck-collar; God inherits all.

**Where the supplied translation has chosen, it has been flagged.** Half-brackets ("As for," "of Allah," "alone," "as an aid," "He," "from," "O Prophet," "true," "directly," "greedily," "by their necks," "wealth," "sole," "the Lord of infinite bounty") are interpretive aids. "Does not deny the reward" for *lā yuḍīʿu* is fair. "Best Protector" for *niʿma al-wakīl* is fair among Trustee/Disposer/Protector. "Leashed by their necks" is strong and within the collaring image. "Living longer" for *numlī lahum* is a lean toward lifespan; respite is wider (delay of punishment, open success). None is distortion; each is a choice.

**The verses to leave with are 173 and 179.** When they say the people have gathered, let it increase faith: God is sufficient; He is the best *wakīl*. And know that God will not leave the camp mixed forever — He distinguishes foul from good without making you the owner of the unseen. Answer after injury. Fear God more than the gathering. Keep your hands open. The wages are not wasted.

Two boundaries observed throughout. No reading of 176–178 has been allowed to close the door of repentance on the living.
