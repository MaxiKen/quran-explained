# Sūrah 9 — at-Tawbah (Repentance), al-Barāʾah (Disavowal)

*129 verses; Madinan. The sūrah opens with a public declaration concerning certain treaties with Arabian polytheist groups, a period of notice, and the duty to honor agreements kept in good faith. It also addresses the Tabūk campaign, repentance, and the community’s obligations. **[Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif]**

**Sources cited inline:** **[Ṭabarī]** *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* · **[Qurṭubī]** *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān* · **[Ibn Kathīr]** *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* · **[Jalālayn]** *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* · **[Saʿdī]** *Taysīr al-Karīm al-Raḥmān* · **[Maʿārif]** *Maʿārif al-Qurʾān* · **[Study Quran]** *The Study Quran* (2015).

**The sūrah.** Also known as al-Barāʾah, it begins with a formal announcement about treaty obligations and a four-month respite for the groups addressed. The opening is not a blanket cancellation of every agreement: verse 9:4 explicitly orders that treaties with parties who kept their terms be fulfilled until their agreed end. **[Ṭabarī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Study Quran]**

**Opening formula.** This is the only sūrah conventionally written without the basmalah. Commentators preserve several explanations, including its opening declaration of disavowal and reports about how it was placed in the written Qur’an alongside Sūrah 8. **[Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif]**

---

## 9:1

*“A declaration of release from God and His Messenger to those among the polytheists with whom you made a treaty.”*

**Meaning.** *Barāʾah* announces release from certain treaty obligations. The declaration concerns the Arabian polytheist groups addressed in this passage; it must be read with the following notice period and the express exception for parties who kept their agreements in 9:4. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif]**

**Context.** The declaration was publicly proclaimed during the pilgrimage season in 9 AH. Commentators distinguish groups whose treaty had no fixed term or a shorter remaining term from parties whose valid, unbroken treaty was to be honored until its stated end. **[Ṭabarī] [Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr]**

## 9:2

*“Travel freely through the land for four months, and know that you cannot escape God, and that God will disgrace the disbelievers.”*

**Meaning.** The groups receiving notice are given a period in which to travel in safety and consider their position; they are reminded that no one can evade God. This is a defined period of respite following a public announcement, not an instruction to disregard agreements that the next verse preserves. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif]**

**Context.** The commentators discuss which treaty categories received the full four months and how the period was reckoned. They also differ over whether “the sacred months” in 9:5 refers to the customary sacred months or to this announced period of respite. **[Ṭabarī] [Qurṭubī]**

## 9:3

*“A proclamation from God and His Messenger to the people on the day of the Greater Pilgrimage: God is free of obligation to the polytheists, and so is His Messenger. If you repent, it is better for you; but if you turn away, know that you cannot escape God. Give tidings of a painful punishment to those who disbelieve.”*

**Context.** The proclamation was made during the pilgrimage of 9 AH, with Abū Bakr leading the pilgrimage and ʿAlī announcing the declaration. Early authorities differ over whether “the day of the Greater Pilgrimage” means the Day of ʿArafah or the Day of Sacrifice. **[Ṭabarī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif]**

**Meaning.** This public notice makes the change in treaty relations known to the tribes addressed, invites repentance, and warns that turning away from God does not place anyone beyond His power. **[Jalālayn] [Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** Ibn Kathīr cites the report in al-Bukhārī that Abū Hurayrah was sent with the public announcement that, after that year, no polytheist would perform the pilgrimage and no one would circumambulate the Kaʿbah naked. **[Ibn Kathīr]**

## 9:4

*“Except those among the polytheists with whom you made a treaty, who have not failed you in anything or supported anyone against you: fulfill their treaty with them until its term. Truly God loves the mindful.”*

**Meaning.** The command is explicit: parties who have kept their treaty and have not aided the Muslims’ enemies must be protected by its terms until the agreed deadline. Their being polytheists does not cancel the obligation to keep a valid covenant. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Saʿdī] [Study Quran]**

**Rulings.** Fulfilling a treaty is itself an act of God-consciousness. The public notice in 9:1–3 therefore does not authorize breaking an agreement with those who have honored it. **[Qurṭubī] [Maʿārif]**

## 9:5

*“When the sacred months have passed, kill the polytheists wherever you find them; take them, besiege them, and lie in wait for them at every place of ambush. But if they repent, establish prayer, and give the alms, let them go their way. Truly God is Forgiving, Merciful.”*

**Meaning.** The verse sets out a military order for the conflict addressed in the preceding declaration: fighting, capture, siege, and the guarding of routes; if the people concerned turn to Islam, establish prayer, and pay zakāh, hostilities are to cease. The Arabic verb in the first command is literally “kill”; it is spoken here within the passage’s defined wartime setting. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Saʿdī] [Maʿārif] [Study Quran]**

**Context & scope.** This follows the notice and respite of 9:1–3, while 9:4 protects those who kept their covenants. The Study Quran reads the passage in its historical setting of conflict and treaty-breaking among Arabian groups, rather than treating polytheism alone as a perpetual cause for war; it also notes that commentators differ, with some assigning the verse broader force or saying it abrogated earlier commands to patience. Verse 9:6 immediately provides safe-conduct for a polytheist who seeks protection, so that he may hear the Qur’an and be escorted to safety. **[Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Study Quran]**

**Rulings.** Classical exegetes disagree over the relation between this verse and other rules for treaties, ransom, and restraint. Qurṭubī records views that some earlier commands were abrogated, but also the position that the verses remain operative together. The command is presented as collective direction in a declared conflict, not a private license for individuals to attack others. **[Qurṭubī] [Study Quran]**

## 9:6

*“If any of the polytheists seeks your protection, grant him protection so that he may hear the Word of God; then escort him to his place of safety. That is because they are a people who do not know.”*

**Meaning.** Even in the passage’s wartime setting, a polytheist who asks for protection to hear the Qur’an must be given safe-conduct. If he does not accept Islam, he is to be escorted to a place where he is secure, including his home and people. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Saʿdī] [Study Quran]**

**Rulings.** The duty extends beyond allowing someone to listen: the verse requires conveying him to safety. Qurṭubī notes the recognized obligation to honor protection and safe passage. **[Qurṭubī] [Maʿārif]**

## 9:7

*“How can the polytheists have a treaty with God and His Messenger, except those with whom you made a treaty near the Sacred Mosque? So long as they remain true to you, remain true to them. Truly God loves the reverent.”*

**Meaning.** The exception makes the rule plain: parties who keep their treaty must have theirs kept. Commentators often connect the reference to the pact at al-Ḥudaybiyyah, though they differ over the precise group meant—some identify members of Banū Bakr who did not join the breach, while others name Quraysh or other allied groups. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Study Quran]**

**Rulings.** The passage rejects collective blame: even amid the termination of other agreements, a party that remains faithful is to be met with faithfulness. **[Qurṭubī] [Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**

## 9:8

*“How, when if they prevail over you, they would observe neither kinship nor treaty? They please you with their mouths while their hearts refuse, and most of them are wicked.”*

**Meaning.** This describes the untrustworthiness of the opponents whose agreements were in question: if they gained the upper hand, they would not honor kinship or covenant, even if their words sounded reassuring. The qualification “most of them” leaves room for the faithful exception in 9:7; it is not a judgment that every individual in the broader group acted alike. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif] [Study Quran]**

**Reflection.** The passage warns against mistaking conciliatory speech for reliable conduct, while its treaty exception also warns the believers not to make sweeping accusations or abandon justice. **[Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**

## 9:9

*“They have sold the signs of God for a paltry price and barred people from His way. Evil indeed is what they used to do.”*

**Meaning.** The opponents traded the guidance made clear to them for worldly advantage and tried to keep others from following God’s way. The language of buying and selling describes the low value they placed on revelation compared with immediate gain. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Saʿdī] [Study Quran]**

**Reflection.** Turning away from truth can harm others as well as oneself when it becomes an effort to obstruct their access to guidance. **[Maʿārif]**

## 9:10

*“They observe neither kinship nor treaty with a believer; these are the transgressors.”*

**Meaning.** The verse restates the passage’s charge against the treaty-breakers: they do not respect kinship or a covenant when dealing with believers, and thus go beyond the bounds of justice. It continues the description in 9:8 of the specific opposing parties under discussion. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Study Quran]**

**Reflection.** A binding agreement is not made meaningless by the other party’s religious identity; this passage condemns its violation and elsewhere requires that a faithful treaty be honored. **[Qurṭubī] [Maʿārif]**

## 9:11

*“If they repent, establish prayer, and give zakāh, then they are your brethren in religion. We make the signs clear for people who know.”*

**Meaning.** The door to reconciliation remains open. Those who abandon idolatry and enter the Muslim community are to be treated as brothers and sisters in faith; their former hostility is not held against them. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif] [Study Quran]**

**Reflection.** The passage moves from a duty to honor the faithful treaty to an invitation for former opponents to begin anew as members of the religious community. **[Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**

## 9:12

*“If they break their oaths after their treaty and attack your religion, then fight the leaders of disbelief—they have no binding oaths—so that they may desist.”*

**Meaning.** The stated condition is a breach of covenant followed by hostility toward the religion. “Leaders of disbelief” refers to the leading figures who drive or direct that opposition; the verse says the broken pledges can no longer be relied upon. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Saʿdī] [Study Quran]**

**Rulings & scope.** Commentators differ over the meaning and legal reach of “attack your religion,” including how it relates to verbal vilification. The command must be read with its express treaty-breaking condition and the hostile conduct described in 9:13, not detached from them as a general license for private violence or for attacking anyone who disagrees. **[Qurṭubī] [Study Quran]**

## 9:13

*“Will you not fight a people who broke their oaths, intended to expel the Messenger, and began against you first? Do you fear them? God has more right to be feared, if you are believers.”*

**Meaning.** Three grounds are named: breach of the covenant, the attempt that led to the Prophet’s expulsion from Mecca, and the opponents’ initiation of hostilities. Commentators differ over which event “began against you first” recalls: some point to Badr, while others connect it to Quraysh’s support for Banū Bakr in their attack on the Prophet’s ally Khuzāʿah, which broke the Ḥudaybiyyah pact. **[Ṭabarī] [Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Study Quran]**

**Reflection.** The passage grounds the conflict in specific actions rather than in a bare difference of belief; it urges the believers not to let fear override the obligations described here. **[Saʿdī] [Maʿārif] [Study Quran]**

## 9:14

*“Fight them: God will chastise them by your hands, disgrace them, grant you victory over them, and heal the breasts of a believing people.”*

**Meaning.** The verse describes the consequences of defeating those who broke the covenant and attacked the community: their power is checked, the believers prevail, and the distress of those harmed is relieved. Commentators commonly identify the “believing people” whose hearts are healed as Khuzāʿah, the Prophet’s allies attacked by Banū Bakr with Quraysh’s support. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Qurṭubī] [Study Quran]**

**Reflection.** As al-Maʿārif emphasizes, the aim is that aggression cease, not that believers simply indulge their anger; the next verse holds open the possibility of repentance. **[Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**

## 9:15

*“And He will remove the anger in their hearts. God turns in mercy to whom He wills. God is Knowing, Wise.”*

**Meaning.** God relieves the grief and anger of the believers harmed by the breach, while the close of the verse turns from punishment to divine mercy: God may guide former opponents to repentance. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Qurṭubī] [Maʿārif]**

**Reflection.** Even in a passage about conflict and accountability, the possibility of return is not closed. The aim is not unending vengeance; God’s wisdom includes accepting repentance from whom He wills. **[Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**
