# Sūrah 9 — at-Tawbah (The Repentance), al-Barāʾah (The Disavowal)

*129 verses; Madinan, by agreement [Qurṭubī]. It is the only sūrah of the Qurʾān that opens without the
basmalah. Al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib said, "The last verse to be revealed was 4:176, and the last sūrah to be
revealed in full was Barāʾah" (Bukhārī) [Ibn Kathīr]; the Companions did not write the basmalah at its
head in the ʿUthmānic codex, following ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān [Ibn Kathīr]. Saʿīd b. Jubayr asked Ibn ʿAbbās
about Sūrat Barāʾah and he answered: "That is *al-Fāḍiḥah* (the Exposer); it kept revealing 'and of them…
and of them…' until we feared it would leave no one." Al-Qushayrī Abū Naṣr ʿAbd al-Raḥīm said: this sūrah
was sent down concerning the expedition of Tabūk; at its beginning is the casting-off of the covenants of
the disbelievers, and in it is the exposure of the secrets of the hypocrites — for which it is also named
*al-Fāḍiḥah*, *al-Baḥūth* and *al-Mubaʿthirah*, "the searcher," because it searches out what the hypocrites
concealed [Qurṭubī].*

**Sources cited inline:** **[Ṭabarī]** *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* · **[Qurṭubī]** *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān* ·
**[Ibn Kathīr]** *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* · **[Jalālayn]** *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* · **[Saʿdī]** *Taysīr
al-Karīm al-Raḥmān* · **[Maʿārif]** *Maʿārif al-Qurʾān* · **[Study Quran]** *The Study Quran* (2015).

**The sūrah.** Its commonest name, *at-Tawbah*, is taken from the reference to repentance in v. 3, though
some connect it with God's "relenting" toward the Prophet and the believers in vv. 117–18; its other
prevailing name, *al-Barāʾah*, "the Repudiation," is the word with which it opens [Study Quran]. It is
called *Barāʾah* because it announces disengagement from the disbelievers and what they do, and *Tawbah*
because it announces that the repentance of the Muslims was accepted (Mazharī) [Maʿārif]. The sūrah opens
with the annulment of the treaties with the idolaters, the four months of safe conduct and the command to
fight (vv. 1–15); it then turns to the custody of the Sacred Mosque and to faith and *jihād* (vv. 17–22),
to the corruption of religious leaders among the People of the Book (vv. 29–35), to the sacred months and
the campaign of Tabūk (vv. 36–41), and thereafter, for the greater part of its length, to the dissension of
the hypocrites of Madīnah and the wavering of some believers (vv. 42–110), closing with the repentance of
the three who were left behind and the duty of the believers to go forth (vv. 111–29) [Study Quran]. The
rules of *zakāh* and charity figure prominently in it (vv. 58–60, 103–4) [Study Quran].

As for the missing basmalah, al-Qurṭubī sets out five opinions, the first being that it was the Arab
custom of the Jāhiliyyah, when a treaty between two parties was to be broken, to write the letter of
dissolution without the basmalah: so when Barāʾah came down with the annulment of the pact between the
Prophet and the idolaters, the Prophet sent ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib to recite it to them at the pilgrimage
season and he did not basmalah, following their custom in breaking a pact [Qurṭubī]. Ibn ʿAbbās asked ʿAlī
why the sūrah had no basmalah, and he replied that the basmalah is a statement of security while this
sūrah opens with the severing of a covenant and a declaration of conflict; when it was objected that the
Prophet began his letters to hostile peoples with the basmalah, ʿAlī answered that those were a *call* to
God, not the rescinding of a pact — the first leads to peace, the second to war [Study Quran]. A further
reading is that the omission reinforces the link with the closing verse of Sūrah 8, which likewise
enjoins separation from the disbelievers (Rāzī) [Study Quran]. When ʿUthmān had the codex written, the
basmalah was not there, and a doubt arose whether this sūrah was independent or part of another: since in
subject matter it seemed to belong with al-Anfāl, and since in the Prophet's time the two were called
*al-qarīnatayn*, "the two joined ones" (Mazharī), it was placed after al-Anfāl with a gap left between
them, as though for a basmalah — the precaution taken in case they were one sūrah [Maʿārif].

## 9:1

*"A repudiation from God and His Messenger to those idolaters with whom you made a treaty."*

**Meaning.** **[Ṭabarī]** The sense is: this is a repudiation (*barāʾah*) from God and His Messenger. The
word *barāʾah* is in the nominative through an elided "this" (*hādhihi*), just as *sūratun anzalnāhā*
(24:1) is — the Arabs, when confronted with something present, suppress the demonstrative and say of a
fine thing "fine, by God," meaning "this is fine." He allows that a grammarian could instead treat
*barāʾah* as raised by the resumptive pronoun in *ilā alladhīna ʿāhadtum*, since it becomes
quasi-definite through its complement *min Allāhi wa-rasūlihi* — "a valid view, though I prefer the
first." The phrase "to those of the idolaters with whom you made a treaty" means: those with whom *the
Messenger of God* made a treaty, for the covenants between the Muslims and the idolaters were the
Messenger's covenants — none but he, or one acting by his command, concluded them. God nonetheless
addressed the believers, because they knew the meaning: his contracts bound them, since they were content
with all he did among them and submitted to his compacts, so that his contract over them was like their
own contract over themselves. **[Jalālayn]** It is a declaration of immunity reaching the idolaters with
whom a pact was made, whether indefinite or for a term shorter or longer than four months; the annulment
is as God states further on. **[Saʿdī]** This is a repudiation from God and from His Messenger to *all*
the idolaters who hold treaties: they have four months to travel the land at will, safe from the
believers, and after the four months there is for them no covenant and no compact. This applies to those
whose treaty was absolute and unstipulated, or fixed at four months or less; as for one whose treaty runs
beyond four months, his treaty must be completed for him so long as he is not feared of treachery and has
not begun to break it. Then He warns those under treaty that even during their term of safety they cannot
frustrate God nor escape Him, and that whoever persists in his *shirk* will certainly be disgraced — and
this was a means of drawing them to enter Islam, all but the obstinate who insisted and cared nothing for
God's threat. **[Study Quran]** *Barāʾah* carries the sense of being unallied, being innocent of, or being
disassociated from a thing; here it means that the existing truce or treaty has come to an end. Over the
occasion and interpretation of the verse there is what Ibn Kathīr calls "considerable disagreement."

**Context.** **[Study Quran]** One account relates the verse to the breaking of the Treaty of Ḥudaybiyah,
the ten-year truce concluded with the Quraysh (see Sūrah 48), which covered the allies of both sides:
Banū Khuzāʿah with the Prophet and Banū Bakr with the Quraysh. Banū Bakr later raided Banū Khuzāʿah in a
vendetta predating Ḥudaybiyah, and the Quraysh reportedly assisted them with weapons and a small number
of men (Q); the Prophet treated this as a breach nullifying the truce, and it led ultimately to the
conquest of Makkah — on this reading the repudiation is addressed to the Makkans. The majority of
commentators, however, hold that the verse was revealed *after* the conquest of Makkah in 8/630, a year
after the breach. **[Ibn Kathīr]** The first part of this sūrah came down to the Messenger of God when he
returned from Tabūk at the pilgrimage season. He had thought of attending that ḥajj, but remembered that
the idolaters would still be present as in past years and would circumambulate the House naked, and he
disliked to mix with them; so he sent Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq to lead the ḥajj and teach the people their
rites, commanding him to inform the idolaters that they would not be allowed to perform the pilgrimage
after that season, and commanding him to proclaim *barāʾatun min Allāhi wa-rasūlihi* to the people. When
Abū Bakr had set out, the Messenger sent ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭalib after him to deliver the announcement on his
behalf, for he was the Messenger's kinsman.

**Rulings.** **[Saʿdī]** Treaties are of three kinds here: an unstipulated one, and one of four months or
less — both reduced or extended to four months; and a treaty of fixed term exceeding four months, which
must be honoured to its end unless its holders break it. **[Qurṭubī]** Reporting Ibn Isḥāq: the
idolaters addressed are two classes — those whose treaty had less than four months to run, who were
granted the completion of four months, and those whose treaty had no fixed term, who were confined to
four months to look after themselves, after which they are at war with God, His Messenger and the
believers, to be killed or captured wherever found unless they repent.

**Language.** **[Qurṭubī]** *Barāʾah* is a verbal noun from *bariʾa*, "to be quit of": here, a cutting-off
of the covenants, not merely a declaration of innocence. **[Ibn Kathīr]** The phrase "freedom from (all)
obligations" is a declaration of release from every compact.

**Cross-references.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** 48:25 — "They are the ones who disbelieved and hindered you from
al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, and detained the sacrificial animals from reaching their place of sacrifice."

**Reflection.** The verse opens the sūrah by severing a covenant, and the whole passage that follows is
built on the principle that pledges are sacred: they are dissolved only after due notice and for a proven
breach, never by stealth. As **[Ṭabarī]**'s grammatical discussion shows, the very first word is
emphatically a present, announced thing — "this is a repudiation" — so that no one is taken unawares.

## 9:2

*"So travel freely throughout the land for four months, and know that you cannot thwart God, and that God
shall disgrace the disbelievers."*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** "Journey" — travel — "in security, O idolaters, in the land for four months,"
beginning with Shawwāl, in accordance with what follows; after which there will be no security for you.
"Know that you cannot escape God" — you shall not elude His punishment — "and that God degrades the
disbelievers," humiliating them in this world by having them killed and in the Hereafter by the Fire.
**[Qurṭubī]** *Fa-sīḥū*: go about the land coming and going, secure, fearing none of the Muslims by war,
plunder, killing or capture. *Sāḥa fulānun fī al-arḍi yasīḥu siyāḥatan wa-suyūḥan wa-sayaḥānan*; from the
same root is *al-sayḥ*, water flowing out over the ground, and the verse of Ṭarafa: "Had I feared this
from you, you would not have reached me… until you see the months flowing past me." **[Ṭabarī]** (treating
vv. 1–2 together) The idolaters meant are two sorts: one whose treaty had less than four months left and
was granted the full four, and one whose treaty had no fixed term and was cut down to four months to
choose his course, after which he is at war unless he repents.

**Rulings.** **[Qurṭubī]** Ibn Isḥāq and others state that the respite begins on the day of the greater
ḥajj and expires ten days into Rabīʿ al-Ākhir. Whoever had no treaty at all has as his term the expiry of
the four sacred months — fifty days: twenty of Dhū al-Ḥijjah and Muḥarram. Al-Kalbī held that the four
months were for those whose treaty with the Messenger was *less* than four months, while those whose
treaty exceeded four months are the ones God commanded to be completed to their term in 9:4 — "and this
is the choice of al-Ṭabarī and others." Ibn Isḥāq, Mujāhid and others said that this verse was revealed
about the people of Makkah, the settlement the Messenger made with the Quraysh at Ḥudaybiyah.
**[Saʿdī]** (on vv. 1–2) After the four months there is for them no covenant and no compact.

**Stories & occasions.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** This verse refers to idolaters who had indefinite treaties and
to those whose treaties expired in less than four months; the terms of such treaties were restricted to
four months. As for those whose term of peace ended at a fixed date later than four months, their treaty
ran to that date.

**Language.** **[Qurṭubī]** *Siyāḥah* in the usage of the Arabs is travel without a fixed destination; the
point is that they are free to go wherever they choose, unmolested, for the whole of the term.

**Cross-references.** **[Jalālayn]** The beginning of the term, Shawwāl, is fixed by 9:36 and by the
sequel of the sūrah; the annulment is spelled out in what follows.

**Reflection.** Four months of unmolested travel, granted to people who had broken their word, is the
measure of how the Qurʾān conducts even a justified war: warning precedes force, and the way out —
"unless they repent" — is kept open to the last day. The verse joins the warning "you cannot escape God"
to the promise "God shall disgrace the disbelievers": impunity in this life is not escape from Him, and
the humiliation He sends is a consequence of their own persistence, not a revenge taken by the believers.

## 9:3

*"And an announcement from God and His Messenger to the people on the day of the greater ḥajj: that God and
His Messenger have repudiated the idolaters. So, if you repent, it would be better for you. And if you turn
away, then know that you cannot thwart God. And give the disbelievers glad tidings of a painful
punishment,"*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** "A proclamation" — a notification — "from God and His Messenger to mankind on
the day of the Greater Pilgrimage," the Day of Sacrifice (*yawm al-naḥr*), "that God is free of
obligation to the idolaters and their pacts, and so is His Messenger": in that same year, year 9 of the
Hijrah, the Prophet sent forth ʿAlī, who proclaimed these verses on the Day of Sacrifice at Minā, and he
proclaimed as well that after that year no idolater would be allowed to make the pilgrimage nor
circumambulate the House naked, as is reported by al-Bukhārī. "So if you repent" of unbelief "it will be
better for you; but if you turn away" from belief, "then know that you cannot escape God. And give
tidings — inform — those who disbelieve of a painful chastisement," namely slaughter or capture in this
world and the punishment of the Fire in the Hereafter. **[Qurṭubī]** *Adhān* is "announcement" (*iʿlām*)
in the language, without disagreement; it is conjoined upon *barāʾah*. *Al-nās* here is all created
people. *Yawm al-ḥajj al-akbar* is an adverbial of time governed by *adhān*, since the verbal force
remains in it even though it has been qualified by *min Allāh*; it was also said that the governing word
is *mukhzī*, on the view that a described verbal noun no longer governs. **[Ṭabarī]** "And an announcement
from God and His Messenger to the people on the day of the greater ḥajj": Sulaymān b. Mūsā al-Shāmī said
that *al-adhān* here means "the passage" (*al-qaṣaṣ*) — the opening of Barāʾah up to "and if you fear
poverty, God will enrich you of His bounty" (9:28), which is twenty-eight verses; Ibn Zayd said simply
"an announcement." It is conjoined to *barāʾah* as if to say: this is a repudiation from God and His
Messenger, and an announcement from God. **[Saʿdī]** This is what God promised the believers of help for
His religion, exaltation of His word and the frustration of their enemies — the idolaters who expelled the
Messenger and those with him from Makkah, from the Sacred House of God, and drove them out of what they
held of the land of the Ḥijāz. God helped His Messenger and the believers until He opened Makkah and
humbled the idolaters, and the believers held rule and ascendancy over those lands; so the Prophet
commanded his announcer to proclaim on the day of the greater ḥajj — the Day of Sacrifice, when
Muslim and unbeliever from every part of Arabia were gathered — that God and His Messenger are quit of the
idolaters, that they have no covenant and no compact, that wherever they are found they are to be killed,
and that they are not to come near the Sacred Mosque after that year.

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** al-Bukhārī recorded from Abū Hurayrah: "Abū Bakr sent me, among those
sent on the Day of Sacrifice, to announce at Minā that no idolater would be allowed to perform the
pilgrimage after that year, nor would a naked person be allowed to circumambulate the House." Ḥumayd
said: "The Prophet then sent ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib and commanded him to announce Barāʾah"; Abū Hurayrah said:
"ʿAlī publicised Barāʾah among us to the gathering at Minā on the Day of Sacrifice, declaring that no
idolater would perform the pilgrimage after that year and no naked person would circumambulate the House."
Abū Hurayrah's version in the Book of Jihād adds: "Abū Bakr was leading the people in that pilgrimage
season, and in the year of the Farewell Pilgrimage, when the Prophet performed the ḥajj, no idolater
performed the pilgrimage." Ibn Isḥāq reported from Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad b. ʿAlī b. al-Ḥusayn: when Barāʾah
was revealed and the Messenger had already sent Abū Bakr to superintend the rites for the people, he was
asked, "O Messenger of God, will you not send it to Abū Bakr?" and he replied: "None discharges it on my
behalf but a man of my own household" (*lā yuʾaddī ʿannī illā rajulun min ahli baytī*). **[Ṭabarī]** Abū
al-Ṣahbāʾ al-Bakrī asked ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib about "the day of the greater ḥajj" and he said: the Messenger
of God sent Abū Bakr b. Abī Quḥāfa to establish the ḥajj for the people and sent me with forty verses of
Barāʾah; he came to ʿArafah and addressed the people on the day of ʿArafah, and when he finished his
sermon he turned to me and said, "Rise, ʿAlī, and deliver the message of the Messenger of God." So I stood
and recited forty verses of Barāʾah to them; then we departed and came to Minā, where I stoned the
*pillar*, sacrificed the camel and shaved my head — and knowing that the people of the gathering had not
been present at Abū Bakr's sermon on the day of ʿArafah, I went about the tents reciting it to them.
**[Qurṭubī]** From Ibn ʿUmar (Abū Dāwūd): the Messenger of God stood on the Day of Sacrifice at the
pilgrimage he performed and said, "What day is this?" They said, "The Day of Sacrifice." He said: "This is
the day of the greater ḥajj."

**Rulings.** **[Maʿārif]** From vv. 3–4 it is established that when a treaty with disbelievers is to be
annulled, taking any action against them without a prior public announcement is not correct. It was to
implement this that the Prophet sent Abū Bakr and ʿAlī to the ḥajj of the year 9, to proclaim it before
the assembled tribes of Arabia on the plains of ʿArafāt and Minā — for through that great gathering the
ruling would become known throughout all Arabia, and, as an added precaution, the proclamation was
repeated.

**Language.** **[Qurṭubī]** On "the day of the greater ḥajj" the scholars differ: some say the day of
ʿArafah — reported from ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ṭāwūs and Mujāhid, and the position of Abū Ḥanīfa,
and al-Shāfiʿī said it too; others say the Day of Sacrifice — from ʿAlī, Ibn ʿAbbās (in another report),
Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn Abī Awfā and al-Mughīra b. Shuʿba, "and al-Ṭabarī chose this." It is called "greater"
because people spoke of a "lesser ḥajj." Ibn Abī Awfā said: the Day of Sacrifice is the day of the greater
ḥajj — blood is shed in it, hair is removed, the state of ritual neglect is cast off and the pilgrim is
released from *iḥrām*; and this is the position of Mālik, for the Day of Sacrifice is like the whole
ḥajj in itself, since the standing is on its eve and the stoning, sacrifice, shaving and circumambulation
are on its morning. **[Maʿārif]** The saying attributed to the Prophet, "The ḥajj is ʿArafah" (Abū Dāwūd,
Tirmidhī), underlies the view of Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿUmar, Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn al-Zubayr that it is the day of
ʿArafah; other authorities such as Sufyān al-Thawrī reconcile the reports by taking all five days of the
ḥajj as the fulfilment of "the day of the greater ḥajj."

**Reflection.** The announcement is made to *all* the people, at the largest gathering of the year, and it
is addressed to the idolaters in the form of an invitation: "if you repent, it would be better for you."
Even at the moment a treaty is dissolved, the summons to faith is renewed, and the "glad tidings" of
punishment are paired with the promise that repentance changes everything.

## 9:4

*"save for those idolaters with whom you have made a treaty, and who thereafter commit no breach against
you, nor support anyone against you. So fulfil the treaty with them for its duration. Truly God loves the
reverent."*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** Excepting those of the idolaters with whom you have made a pact and who have
not diminished their commitment to you in any way with regard to the terms of the pact, "nor supported" —
assisted — "anyone" among the disbelievers "against you": as for these, "fulfil your pact with them until
the completion of the term" to which you agreed. "Truly God loves those who fear Him," by fulfilling
pacts. **[Ṭabarī]** The sense is: God and His Messenger are quit of the idolaters except from the treaty
of those of them with whom you, O believers, made a treaty, and who thereafter "did not diminish anything
for you" of the treaty you made with them, "and did not support anyone against you" of your enemies by
helping them with their persons and bodies, nor with weapons, horses or men; "so fulfil their treaty for
them to its term" — keep faith with them in the treaty you made, and do not raise war against them until
the expiry of the term between you and them; "truly God loves the reverent" — those who fear Him by
obeying Him, performing His obligations and avoiding what He has forbidden. **[Saʿdī]** This absolute,
unqualified repudiation of all the idolaters excepts those who entered a treaty and kept to it, from whom
nothing necessitating annulment has appeared: they have not diminished anything of yours, nor assisted
anyone against you. For such, complete their treaty to its term, be it short or long; for Islam does not
command treachery — it commands faithfulness. "Truly God loves the reverent": those who discharge what
they were commanded and who shun *shirk*, treachery and other acts of disobedience.

**Rulings.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** This is the exception that sets the longest extent of time for those who
held a general treaty, with no term mentioned, at four months; within it they might travel the land
seeking refuge wherever they wished. Those whose treaty named a specific term had it extended only to the
agreed date of its termination — and the ḥadīths on this have preceded. Anyone who had a treaty with the
Messenger of God had it honoured to its stated date, on condition of refraining both from breaching the
terms and from helping non-Muslims against Muslims; this is the class whose peace was carried through to
its end, and God encouraged honouring such treaties by saying, "Truly God loves the reverent," who keep
their promises. **[Qurṭubī]** *Illā alladhīna ʿāhadtum* is in the accusative as a connected exception:
God is quit of the idolaters save of those under treaty for the duration of their treaty. It was also said
to be a disconnected exception — God is quit of them, but those with whom you made a treaty and who stood
by it: fulfil their treaty for them. The words "then they did not diminish you" indicate that some
treaty-holders broke their pact while others stood by their fidelity, and God gave His Prophet leave to
annul the pact of the treacherous and commanded faithfulness to those who remained true. Al-Suddī (via
Asbāṭ) said: "fulfil their treaty to its term" means to its appointed date; and it was said that this
verse is specific, meaning Banū Ḍamra in particular — "even if their term be more than four months."
**[Maʿārif]** The sixth ruling derived from these verses is that Muslims are bidden to fulfil their pledge
right through to the expiry of the treaty's term; and the verse closes with "truly God loves the
reverent," a clear hint that they must exercise the utmost care in keeping their word and must never hunt
for lame excuses and far-fetched interpretations to evade a treaty obligation, as nations at large do.

**Readings.** **[Qurṭubī]** ʿIkrima and ʿAṭāʾ b. Yasār read *thumma lam yanquḍūkum* with the letter *ḍād*
("they did not break with you"), eliding a governed noun — i.e., they did not break their treaty with you.

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** **[Ṭabarī]** Qatāda said: they are the idolaters of the Quraysh, with whom the
Messenger of God made a treaty at the time of Ḥudaybiyah, four months of whose term remained after the
Day of Sacrifice; so God commanded His Prophet to fulfil their treaty to its term, while those who had no
treaty were respited to the end of Muḥarram, and every treaty-holder's treaty was cast off, and he was
commanded to fight them until they testify that there is no god but God and that Muḥammad is the
Messenger of God, accepting nothing else from them. Ibn ʿAbbās said: the term of anyone who had a treaty
before Barāʾah came down was four months from the day Barāʾah was announced. Ibn Isḥāq said: "except
those of the idolaters with whom you made a treaty" means the specified treaty with a named term.

**Cross-references.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** 9:7 repeats the same rule in the form of a question: "How can the
idolaters have a treaty with God and with His Messenger, save for those with whom you made a treaty at the
Sacred Mosque?"

**Reflection.** The exception is a restraint placed upon the victors. A community that has just been
granted ascendancy over its erstwhile persecutors is told to keep faith with those who kept faith with
it, however long their term runs — and is told, further, that God's love attaches to the keeping of the
pledge, not to the finding of a pretext.

## 9:5

*"Then, when the sacred months have passed, slay the idolaters wheresoever you find them, capture them,
besiege them, and lie in wait for them at every place of ambush. But if they repent, and perform the prayer
and give the alms, then let them go their way. Truly God is Forgiving, Merciful."*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** "Then when the sacred months have passed" — at the end of the period of
deferment — "slay the idolaters wherever you find them," whether in a lawful month or a sacred one, "and
take them captive and confine them" to castles and forts "until they have no choice except being put to
death or acceptance of Islam; and lie in wait for them at every place of ambush" — at every route they
use (*kulla* is in the accusative because a governing preposition has been removed). "But if they repent"
of unbelief "and establish prayer and pay the alms, then leave their way free" and do not interfere with
them; "God is Forgiving, Merciful" to those who repent. **[Ṭabarī]** *Fa-idhā insalakhat al-ashhur
al-ḥurum* means: when they have passed, gone and expired — one says *salakhnā shahra kadhā*, "we came out
of such-and-such a month," and from it *shātun maslūkha*, a sheep stripped of its skin. By "the sacred
months" He means Dhū al-Qaʿdah, Dhū al-Ḥijjah and Muḥarram; what is actually meant here is the expiry of
Muḥarram alone, since the announcement of Barāʾah was on the day of the greater ḥajj — but because it is
joined to the two sacred months before it, the three being consecutive, the plural is used. The sense is:
when the three sacred months have expired for those who have no treaty, or for those who had a treaty and
broke it by supporting the enemy against the Messenger of God and his Companions, or whose treaty was for
an unknown term. "Then slay the idolaters" — "where you find them" — wherever you meet them on the earth,
in the Sanctuary and outside it, in the sacred months and in others; "and take them" — take them
prisoner; "and besiege them" — bar them from moving about the lands of Islam and from entering Makkah;
"and lie in wait for them at every place of ambush" — sit in wait for them, seeking to kill or capture
them; *kulla marṣad* means every road and lookout, *marṣad* being from *raṣadtuhu arṣuduhu raṣdan*, "I
watched him." "But if they repent" — if they turn back from the *shirk* and the denial of the prophethood
of Muḥammad which He forbade them, to the singling out of God and sincere worship of Him alone, and to
affirming the prophethood of Muḥammad—"and establish the prayer" — perform the prayer God made obligatory
for them, with its limits — "and give the alms" that God made incumbent on their wealth, to its people.

**Context.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** This is the Verse of the Sword (*āyat al-sayf*). Mujāhid, ʿAmr b. Shuʿayb,
Muḥammad b. Isḥāq, Qatāda, al-Suddī and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Zayd b. Aslam said that the four months
mentioned are the four-month respite of the preceding verse.

**Rulings.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** Upon the end of the four months in which fighting was forbidden to you, and
which was the respite granted to them, then fight and kill the idolaters wherever you find them — meaning
the earth in general, except for the Sacred Precinct, for God says, "And fight not with them at
al-Masjid al-Ḥarām unless they fight you there" (2:191). "And capture them" — executing some and keeping
some as prisoners; "and besiege them, and lie in wait for them in each and every ambush" — do not wait
until you find them; rather seek them out and besiege them in their areas and forts, gather intelligence
about them on the roads and passes so that what is wide becomes narrow to them, until they have no choice
but to die or embrace Islam. Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq used this and other noble verses as proof for fighting
those who withheld the *zakāh*: these verses permit fighting people unless and until they embrace Islam
and implement its rulings and obligations. God mentions here the weightiest elements of Islam after the
two testimonies — the prayer, which is the right of God, then the *zakāh*, which benefits the poor and
needy. **[Saʿdī]** When the sacred months in which fighting the treaty-bound idolaters was forbidden have
passed, and likewise the term of those whose term exceeded them, the protection is lifted from them.
"Then slay the idolaters wherever you find them," in any place and time; "and take them" captive; "and
besiege them" — tighten upon them, and do not let them spread through the lands of God and His earth,
which He made a place of worship for His servants, for these are not fit to dwell in it and do not deserve
a hand's-breadth of it; the earth is God's earth and they are His enemies who oppose Him and His
messengers and wage war, wanting the earth emptied of His religion, while God refuses but to complete His
light though the disbelievers hate it. "And lie in wait for them at every place of ambush" — every pass
and place they traverse; hold the frontier against them and expend your utmost effort in it, and persist
in this until they repent of their *shirk*. "But if they repent" of their *shirk*, "and establish the
prayer" — discharge it with its due rights — "and give the alms" to those entitled to it, "then leave their
way free": let them be like you, with what you have and what you owe. "Truly God is Forgiving, Merciful":
He forgives *shirk* and what is less than it for those who repent, and has mercy on them by granting them
repentance and then accepting it. This verse is the proof that whoever refuses to perform the prayer or
pay the *zakāh* is fought until he pays it — as Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq argued from it. **[Maʿārif]** The
seventh ruling: when war in defence of a right objective breaks out against any power, all available
resources of combat should be used fully and conclusively; a time for showing mercy and appeasement would
be cowardice. The eighth: trust in a non-Muslim who becomes a Muslim rests on three things — repentance
for past disbelief, establishing the prayer, and paying the *zakāh* as due. Until these three are
implemented in practice, the option of war is not withdrawn merely because someone has pronounced the
*kalimah*; that is why, when some people refused to pay *zakāh* after the Prophet's death, Abū Bakr
declared *jihād* against them and, citing this very verse, convinced and satisfied all the Companions.
**[Qurṭubī]** *Fa-qtulū al-mushrikīn* is general in every idolater, but the Sunnah specifies from it — as
set out in Sūrat al-Baqarah — the woman, the monk, the child and their like; and as for the People of the
Book, God said, "until they give the *jizyah*." On the "sacred months" there are two views: that they are
the well-known sacred months, three consecutive and one separate — al-Aṣamm said that those meant are the
idolaters who had no pact, and that fighting them was to be withheld until the sacred months expire, a
period of fifty days according to Ibn ʿAbbās, since the call was made on the Day of Sacrifice; and that
they are the four months of the treaty, said by Mujāhid, Ibn Isḥāq, Ibn Zayd and ʿAmr b. Shuʿayb, and
called *ḥurum* because God made the blood of the idolaters forbidden to the believers during them except
for good cause. **[Study Quran]** There is disagreement over whether the sacred months here are the four
traditionally sacred to the Arabs (the seventh, eleventh, twelfth and tenth) mentioned in v. 36, or
whether *ḥurum*, "forbidden," refers to the fact that the idolaters were given four months of free travel
during which the believers were forbidden to fight them (IK).

**Belief.** **[Study Quran]** This passage is significant for debates concerning the use of force. For
some, v. 5 abrogates all previous treaties and obligations in relation to the idolaters; other
commentators and jurists interpret it to mean that the idolaters are fought by reason of their idolatry
and polytheism (Q, R). But since the passage itself explicitly affirms the validity and propriety of
keeping treaties with those idolaters who uphold their side in good faith, a more plausible reading does
not take the mere fact that certain persons were idolaters as a reason to fight them. From the
perspective of the Muslim community, the years of conflict preceding the announcement created a political
environment in which the idolaters of Arabia could not be left in a position of power to menace the
community in future: treaties were indeed often made, but they were just as often broken by the idolaters
and their allies — and that concern is made explicit in v. 8, which states that if the idolaters were to
prevail over the believers they would observe neither kinship nor treaty.

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** In the two Ṣaḥīḥs, Ibn ʿUmar said that the Messenger of God said: "I
was commanded to fight the people until they testify that there is no god but God and that Muḥammad is the
Messenger of God…"

**Language.** **[Qurṭubī]** *Insalakha* is from *salakhtu al-shahra*, "I reached the last days of the
month"; the poet said: "When I have stripped off the month I begin another before it… it is enough to kill
me, this stripping of the months and my beginning them."

**Reflection.** The verse known as the Verse of the Sword is immediately bounded by its own conditions:
the term must have expired, the offender must have been given notice, and the moment he repents, prays and
pays the alms his way is free and the believer's hand is lifted from him. Severity here is not a free
licence but the closing stage of a process; the door named in the last clause — repentance, prayer,
alms — stands open to the very man against whom the sword was drawn.

## 9:6

*"And if any of the idolaters seek asylum with thee, grant him asylum until he hears the Word of God. Then
convey him to his place of safety. That is because they are a people who know not."*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** "And if any one of the idolaters" — *aḥadun* is in the nominative because of
the following verb *istajāraka*, "seeks your protection," which validates it — "seeks your protection" —
requests security from you against being killed — "then grant him protection," provide security for him,
"so that he might hear the words of God," the Qurʾān, "and afterward convey him to his place of security,"
the dwellings of his people if he does not believe, so that he might reflect upon his situation. That
which is mentioned "is because they are a people who do not know" the religion of God, and so they must be
made to hear the Qurʾān in order to come to know religion. **[Ṭabarī]** The address is to His Prophet: if
one of the idolaters whom you were commanded to fight and kill after the expiry of the sacred months asks
you for security so that he may hear the word of God from you — the Qurʾān He sent down to you — then
grant him security until he has heard it and you have recited it to him. "Then convey him to his place of
security": send him back, after he has heard the word of God, if he refuses to submit and is not admonished
by what you recited to him so as to believe, to where he is safe from you and from those who follow you,
until he rejoins his home and his idolatrous people. "That is because they are a people who know not":
you do this — giving them security to hear the Qurʾān and returning them, if they refuse Islam, to their
place of safety — because they are an ignorant people who do not understand the proof of God, and do not
know what they would gain by faith in God if they believed, nor the burden and sin upon them for
abandoning it. **[Saʿdī]** Since the preceding command to slay, capture, besiege and ambush the idolaters
was general in all circumstances and for all persons among them, God mentions here that when the public
interest requires bringing some of them near, that is permissible — indeed obligatory. "And if any one of
the idolaters seeks your protection" — asks you to grant him protection and shield him from harm so that
he may hear the word of God and look into the state of Islam — "then grant him protection until he hears
the word of God"; then if he embraces Islam, well and good, and if not, convey him to his place of safety.

**Rulings.** **[Qurṭubī]** The meaning is: from those whom I commanded you to fight, if he asks your
protection — your security and your covenant of safety — then give it to him so that he hears the Qurʾān,
that is, understands its rulings, commands and prohibitions; if he accepts, well, and if he refuses, send
him back to his place of safety — and there is no disagreement over this. Mālik said: if a *ḥarbī* is
found on the road of the lands of the Muslims and says, "I have come seeking security," these are
ambiguous matters, and my view is that he should be returned to his place of safety; Ibn al-Qāsim said
the same of the trader who lands on our coast and says, "I thought you would not molest one who comes to
trade until he has sold." The apparent sense of the verse concerns one who wants to hear the Qurʾān and
look into Islam; as for protection granted for other than that, it is subject to the interest of the
Muslims and to consideration of what benefits them. There is no disagreement among all the scholars that
the protection granted by the ruler is valid, since he is charged with consideration and the public
interest and deputises for all in bringing benefit and repelling harm; they differed over the protection
granted by other than the caliph, the free man's protection being binding according to all the scholars —
except that Ibn Ḥabīb said the ruler should look into it — while the slave's protection is valid in the
well-known view of the school, which is also the view of al-Shāfiʿī and his companions. **[Study Quran]**
Commentators record some disagreement as to whether this verse was abrogated by others, such as 47:4,
"free them graciously or hold them for ransom, till war lays down its burdens," which explicitly mentions
freeing or ransoming prisoners. The dominant opinion, however, is that neither 9:6 nor 47:4 was abrogated,
and that the Prophet and the believers were given the authority to slay the enemy, take them prisoner, or
release them from the very beginning of the years of war, from Badr in 2/624 onwards (Q, Ṭ). There is
disagreement as to how much of the Qurʾān such a person must be made to hear: some say the whole of it,
others restricting it to this sūrah, since it contains the essence of what idolaters need to hear about
their situation (R).

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** "And if anyone of the idolaters" — those you were commanded to fight
and whose blood and property We made lawful to you — "seeks your protection," asks you for safe passage,
then accept his request until he hears the words of God: recite the Qurʾān to him and mention a good part
of the religion with which you establish God's proof against him; "then escort him to where he can be
secure," until he goes back to his land, his home and his area of safety. The verse says: We legislated
granting such people safe passage so that they may learn about the religion of God, so that God's call
will spread among His servants. Ibn Abī Najīḥ narrated that Mujāhid said: it refers to someone who comes
to you to hear what you say and what was revealed to you; he is safe until he comes to you, hears the
words of God and then proceeds to the safe area he came from. The Messenger of God used thereafter to
grant safe passage to those who came to him for guidance or to deliver a message: on the day of
Ḥudaybiyah several emissaries of the Quraysh came to him — ʿUrwa b. Masʿūd, Mikraz b. Ḥafṣ, Suhayl b. ʿAmr
and others — mediating between him and the pagans of the Quraysh, and they witnessed the great respect the
Muslims had for the Prophet, which astonished them, for they had never seen the like of it with kings or
emperors; they went back to their people and conveyed it, and this was one of the reasons most of them
later accepted guidance. When Musaylimah the Liar sent an emissary to the Messenger of God, he asked him,
"Do you testify that Musaylimah is a messenger of God?" **[Ṭabarī]** Al-Suddī said: *kalām Allāh* is the
Qurʾān; and Ibn Isḥāq said: "of these whom I commanded you to fight, grant him protection."

**Belief.** **[Saʿdī]** In this verse is a plain proof for the doctrine of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah that
the Qurʾān is the speech of God, uncreated, for it is God who speaks it and He ascribed it to Himself as
an attribute is ascribed to the one described by it — which refutes the doctrine of the Muʿtazilah and
those who took their view that the Qurʾān is created. *(digression)* How many proofs there are of the
falsity of that doctrine, though this is not the place to mention them.

**Context.** **[Maʿārif]** The first five verses had declared that after the conquest of Makkah a general
amnesty was granted to all the polytheists and disbelievers of Makkah and its environs, but that in view
of their dismal record of treachery no treaty would be signed with them in future; those with existing
treaties they had never contravened were to have them honoured to the end, and those with no treaty or
with none of fixed duration were given four months to leave — or, if the truth of Islam became clear to
them in the meantime, to become Muslims. The door to a good future was thus left open to them even then,
and that is what this sixth verse is about.

**Reflection.** Even while the sword is drawn, the seeker of asylum is safe until he has heard the word —
and if he does not accept, he is escorted, not harmed, to where he is secure. The ground given for this
is not his merit but his ignorance: "a people who know not." The verse sets the terms on which Islam
wishes to be encountered — by hearing, not by compulsion.

## 9:7

*"How can the idolaters have a treaty with God and with His Messenger, save for those with whom you made a
treaty at the Sacred Mosque? If they remain true to you, remain true to them. Truly God loves the
reverent."*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** "How can the idolaters have a pact with God and His Messenger?" — they cannot,
while they disbelieve in God and His Messenger and act treacherously — "except for those with whom you made
a pact at the Sacred Mosque?" — the day of al-Ḥudaybiyah; these were the Quraysh, the exception for whom
was made earlier. "So long as they are true to you" — keeping to the pact and not breaking it — "be true to
them" by fulfilling it; *mā* in *fa-mā* is a conditional particle. "Truly God loves the God-fearing" — and
the Prophet kept the pact made with them until they broke it by supporting Banū Bakr against Khuzāʿah.
**[Ṭabarī]** The address is: how can it be, O you who believe in God and His Messenger, and in what sense
can it be, that the idolaters should have with their Lord a treaty and a covenant of protection with God
and with His Messenger, fulfilled for them, so that they are left safe to move about the land? The meaning
is: they have no treaty, and it is incumbent on the believers to kill them wherever they find them, except
those of them who were given the treaty at the Sacred Mosque — for God commanded the believers to fulfil
their treaty with them and to stand upright in it so long as they stand upright in it for the believers.
**[Saʿdī]** This is a statement of the wisdom that made it necessary for God and His Messenger to be quit
of the idolaters: have they fulfilled what faith requires? Have they refrained from harming the Messenger
of God and the believers? Have they not waged war on the truth and supported falsehood? Have they not
striven to spread corruption in the land? It is right, then, that God should be quit of them and that they
should have no treaty with Him or with His Messenger — "except those of the idolaters with whom you made a
treaty at the Sacred Mosque": these have a sanctity that must be observed, especially in that noble place.
"So long as they remain true to you, remain true to them. Truly God loves the reverent."

**Context.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** God mentions the wisdom in dissolving all obligations to the idolaters and
giving them a four-month period of safety after which they will meet the sharp sword wherever they are
found: "How can there be a covenant for the idolaters?" — a safe resort and refuge — "while they persist
in *shirk* with God and disbelief in Him and His Messenger?" The exception is "those with whom you made a
covenant near al-Masjid al-Ḥarām," on the day of Ḥudaybiyah, concerning which God said in another verse:
"They are the ones who disbelieved and hindered you from al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, and detained the sacrificial
animals from reaching their place of sacrifice" (48:25). The Messenger of God and the Muslims preserved
the terms of the treaty with the people of Makkah from Dhū al-Qaʿdah of the sixth year until the Quraysh
broke it by helping their allies, Banū Bakr, against Khuzāʿah, the allies of the Messenger of God; aided
by the Quraysh, Banū Bakr killed some of Banū Khuzāʿah inside the Sacred Precinct. The Messenger of God
then led the invading army in Ramaḍān of the eighth year, and God opened the Sacred Precinct to him to
rule over them.

**Stories & occasions.** **[Study Quran]** According to some, "those with whom you made a treaty" refers
to certain members of Banū Bakr — allied with the Quraysh under the terms of the Treaty of Ḥudaybiyah — who
never violated their treaty with the Prophet even when others did (R, Q, Ṭ, Th). Other commentators hold
that it refers to Banū Khuzāʿah, who were allied with the Prophet, or even to the Quraysh (Ṭ). Al-Ṭabarī
argues that it can refer only to those members of Banū Bakr who did not violate the treaty, since at the
time of revelation — after the conquest of Makkah — no non-Muslim members of Khuzāʿah remained, and the
Quraysh held no treaty, having violated and voided it. Al-Thaʿlabī records an account that it refers also
to certain members of the Quraysh who were given the four months either to become Muslims or to accept
exile in a land of their choosing; before the four months expired, they became Muslims. **[Ṭabarī]**
Al-Suddī said: they are Banū Jadhīma b. al-Duʾil — the editor notes that the reference is to Banū
Jadhīma of Kināna, the people of al-Ghumayṣāʾ, against whom Khālid b. al-Walīd acted after the conquest,
and after whom the Messenger of God sent ʿAlī to repair Khālid's error. **[Qurṭubī]** Muḥammad b. Isḥāq
said: they are Banū Bakr — that is, the treaty belongs only to those who neither broke nor reneged. Ibn
Zayd said: they did not remain upright, so He appointed them a term of four months; and as for those who
have no treaty, fight them wherever you find them unless they repent.

**Language.** **[Qurṭubī]** *Kayfa* here expresses astonishment, as when you say "How can so-and-so
outstrip me?" — meaning it is not fitting that he should. *ʿAhd* is the noun of *kāna*, and there is an
ellipsis in the verse: how can the idolaters have a treaty when they harbour treachery? — as the poet
said: "You two told me that death is only in the towns — so how, when this is a hillock and a sand-dune?"
with the sense "so how did he die?" according to al-Zajjāj. It was also said to mean: how can the idolaters
have a treaty with God by which they would be safe from His punishment tomorrow, and how can they have a
treaty with His Messenger by which they would be safe from punishment in this world?

**Cross-references.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** 48:25, on the day of Ḥudaybiyah and the treaty of ten years' peace.

**Reflection.** Asked as a question, the verse is really an argument: a pledge of protection is a moral
bond, and those who have shown that they will not be bound by it cannot claim its benefit. Yet the
exception is restated with equal force — those who stand by their word keep it, and God loves those who
keep it.

## 9:8

*"How, since if they prevail over you, they will not observe any kinship or treaty with you? They please you
with their mouths, while their hearts refuse. And most of them are iniquitous."*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** "How can they have a pact when, if they get the better of you" — if they have
the upper hand over you — "they do not respect" — do not take into consideration — "any bond" — kinship —
"or treaty" — pact — "with regard to you, but will instead harm you as much as they can"; the conditional
clause is also a circumstantial qualifier. "Pleasing you with their tongues" with charming words, "while
their hearts refuse" to be true to those words; "and most of them are wicked" — violators of pacts.
**[Ṭabarī]** The meaning is: how can these idolaters who broke their treaty, or those of them who have no
treaty, have a treaty and covenant of protection with you, O believers, when, "if they prevail over you" —
if they overcome you — "they would not observe in you *ill* or *dhimmah*." He notes that *kayfa* suffices
here to indicate the sense, because the meaning intended has already gone before, and that the Arabs do
this when they repeat a particle after its sense has passed: the poet said, "You two told me that death is
only in the towns — so how, when this is a hillock and a sand-dune?" suppressing the verb after *kayfa*
because the intended meaning had preceded — the sense being: how can death be in the towns when here is a
hillock and a dune from which no one escapes it?
**[Saʿdī]** How can the idolaters have a treaty and compact with God, when the case is that "if they
prevail over you" in power and authority they would show you no mercy, and "would not observe in you *ill*
or *dhimmah*" — neither covenant of protection nor kinship, and would not fear God in regard to you, but
would subject you to the worst of torment? So this is your situation with them were they to prevail; and
let not the way they treat you when they fear you deceive you, for "they please you with their mouths
while their hearts refuse" any inclination or love for you — they are truly enemies, sincerely hating you.
"And most of them are defiantly disobedient": they have neither religion nor manliness.

**Language.** **[Qurṭubī]** *Kayfa* repeats the astonishment that they should have any treaty given the
vileness of their deeds. *Ẓahartu ʿalā fulānin* means "I overcame him," and *ẓahartu al-bayta* "I got on
top of the house," as in "they were unable to scale it" (18:97). *Yarqubū* means "observe, keep"; the
*raqīb* is the keeper. On *ill* there are several views: Mujāhid and Ibn Zayd said "treaty"; Mujāhid also
said it is one of the names of God; Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk said "kinship"; al-Ḥasan said "protection of
a neighbour"; Qatāda said "oath (*ḥilf*)"; *dhimmah* is "treaty" according to Qatāda, and "oath" according
to Abū ʿUbayda, who also explained *ill* as "treaty" and *dhimmah* as "the state of being under
obligation." Al-Azharī said it is a name of God in Hebrew, originally from *al-alīl*, "brightness" — one
says *alla lawnuhu*, "his colour was clear and shining" — while others derive it from sharpness, whence
*al-allah* for a spearhead and *udhunun mu'allalah*, "a pointed ear," as Ṭarafa described his she-camel's
ears: "Two pointed ears in which you recognise her noble breed, like the ears of a wild ox of Ḥawmal,
solitary." **[Study Quran]** *Kinship* renders *ill*, which can also mean a pact or sworn alliance
(*ḥilf*; Ṭ). *Treaty* renders *dhimmah*, referring generally to "that which sets up an obligation" (R),
and in this sense *ahl al-dhimmah* can be rendered "treaty peoples" (Q) or "those to whom one has an
obligation." *Prevail* signifies that the disbelievers would come to a position of strength over the
Muslims (Q, R, Ṭ).

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** **[Ibn Kathīr]** God encourages the believers to show enmity to the idolaters and to
dissociate from them, affirming that they do not deserve a covenant of peace because of their *shirk* and
their disbelief in the Messenger of God: if these disbelievers had a chance to defeat the Muslims they
would cause great mischief, leave nothing unharmed, and disregard the ties of kinship and the sanctity of
their vows. ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭalḥa, ʿIkrimah and al-ʿAwfī narrated that Ibn ʿAbbās said: "*Ill* means kinship,
and *dhimmah* means covenant"; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Suddī said the same. **[Ṭabarī]** Ibn Abī Najīḥ reported
from Mujāhid on 9:10, "they observe not, in a believer, *ill*": he said, it means God — they do not fear
God in respect of a believer.

**Belief.** **[Study Quran]** *They please you with their mouths* — by speaking sweet words — *while their
hearts* contain the opposite (R). Since all disbelievers are considered to be "iniquitous" (Q, R), *most
of them are iniquitous* is understood to mean that many, though not all, of the disbelievers lie and will
fail to uphold their agreements (Q), are faithless even to their own religion (R), and are thus iniquitous
both in a general and in a specific sense. **[Maʿārif]** The phrase "most of them are sinners" hints that
not all of them were like that: there were decent people among them too, who wanted to stand by their
commitment but were not heeded by the others. This is the same note the Qurʾān strikes elsewhere: "Let not
the malice of a people move you to act unjustly" (5:8).

**Cross-references.** **[Maʿārif]** 5:8 — "and let not hatred of a people prevent you from being just."

**Reflection.** The test of a treaty is not what is said when one is weak but what is done when one has
the upper hand; the verse measures the idolaters by that test and finds them wanting — while the qualifying
"most of them" refuses to condemn a whole people wholesale. The believer is taught to judge by conduct
rather than by fair words, yet to keep the door of fairness open even to adversaries.

## 9:9

*"They have sold the signs of God for a paltry price, and have turned from His way. Evil indeed is that
which they used to do,"*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** "They have purchased with the signs of God" — the Qurʾān — "a small price" of
this world, that is, they have refrained from following them in favour of passions and whims, "and have
barred people from His way" — His religion. "Truly evil is that deed of theirs which they are wont to do."
**[Ṭabarī]** The meaning is: these idolaters, whom God has commanded you, O believers, to kill wherever
you find them, have bought — by abandoning the following of the proofs with which God argued against them —
a small price, a little of the goods of this world. "And have turned from His way": they prevented people
from entering Islam and tried to turn the Muslims back from their religion. "Truly evil is what they used
to do": evil is the deed of these idolaters, whose attributes have been described — their buying unbelief
with faith and misguidance with guidance, and their turning away from the path of God those who believe,
or those who would believe. **[Saʿdī]** They chose the paltry, immediate share of this world over faith in
God and His Messenger and submission to His signs, and they turned away themselves and turned others away
from His way: "evil indeed is what they used to do."

**Context.** **[Ṭabarī]** It is reported that they broke the treaty that had been between them and the
Messenger of God for a meal which Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb fed them. **[Qurṭubī]** Mujāhid said it refers to the
idolaters breaking their covenants "for a morsel of food which Abū Sufyān fed them"; and it was also said
that they substituted the goods of this world for the Qurʾān. **[Ibn Kathīr]** The idolators exchanged
following the signs of God for the lower affairs of life they indulged in, and "they hindered men from His
way," trying to prevent the believers from following the truth.

**Language.** **[Qurṭubī]** *Fa-ṣaddū ʿan sabīlihi* means either "they turned away," from *ṣudūd*, or
"they prevented [others] from the way of God," from *ṣadd*. **[Study Quran]** *Turned from His way* can
mean here, as in other instances of this phrase, both that they turn away from the way of God themselves
and that they hinder others from following it.

**Cross-references.** **[Study Quran]** The "sale" of God's signs for a paltry price is an object of
rebuke in several verses — 2:174, 3:187 and 16:95. Elsewhere the language of buying and selling is used
positively: "Who is it that will lend unto God a goodly loan? He will multiply it for him, and his shall
be a generous reward" (57:11; cf. 2:245, 5:12, 57:18, 64:17, 73:20); and 61:10 speaks of "a commerce that
will save you."

**Reflection.** **[Maʿārif]** The ninth verse names the disease behind the chronic treachery of these
idolaters, so that they too might be given a prescription of hope and correct themselves if they chose,
and so that believers might be warned against the same pitfall: it is the relentless love of the material.
The desire to acquire, possess and expend the limitless range of the things of this world had blinded them
to the point that they would not hesitate to sell off the very words of God, and their own faith, for
paltry gain.

## 9:10

*"observing neither kinship nor treaty with any believer. And it is they who are the transgressors."*

**Meaning.** **[Jalālayn]** "They respect neither bond of kinship nor treaty with regard to a believer;
those — they are the transgressors." **[Ṭabarī]** The meaning is: these idolaters whom I have commanded
you, O believers, to kill wherever you find them would not, in respect of a believer, were they to gain
power over him to kill him, observe *ill* or *dhimmah*; so do not spare them, just as they would not spare
you if they prevailed over you. "And those — they are the transgressors": those who overstep against you
into what is not theirs, by wrongdoing and aggression.

**Language.** **[Study Quran]** *Transgression* has the general meaning of going beyond limits, and is also
used in relation to the use of force, as in 2:194: "So whosoever transgresses against you, transgress
against him in like manner as he transgressed against you." **[Qurṭubī]** "And those are the
transgressors": those who cross over from the lawful into the unlawful by breaking the treaty.

**Belief.** **[Saʿdī]** The description that made them your enemies and made them hate you is faith
itself: it is for the sake of their enmity to faith and its people that "they observe neither kinship nor
treaty with any believer" — so enmity and friendship must turn with faith, its presence and its absence.

**Rulings.** **[Qurṭubī]** Al-Naḥḥās said: this is not a repetition of v. 8 — the first concerns all the
idolaters, while this one concerns the Jews specifically; the proof of it is "they have purchased with the
signs of God a small price," which means the Jews, who sold the proofs and clarifications of God in
exchange for the pursuit of leadership and for greed. *(digression)*

**Cross-references.** **[Study Quran]** See v. 8, where the same pair of words — *ill* and *dhimmah* — is
used of the idolaters' treatment of the believers.

**Reflection.** The charge is not that they are enemies, but that they recognise no bond at all — neither
the bond of blood nor the bond of a sworn pledge — where a believer is concerned. That is what makes them
"transgressors": not the mere fact of hostility, but the refusal of every limit that might restrain it.

## 9:11

*“If they repent, establish prayer, and give zakāh, then they are your brethren in religion. We make the signs clear for people who know.”*

**Meaning.** The door to reconciliation remains open. Those who abandon idolatry and enter the Muslim community are to be treated as brothers and sisters in faith; their former hostility is not held against them. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif] [Study Quran]**

**Reflection.** The passage moves from a duty to honor the faithful treaty to an invitation for former opponents to begin anew as members of the religious community. **[Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**

## 9:12

*“If they break their oaths after their treaty and attack your religion, then fight the leaders of disbelief—they have no binding oaths—so that they may desist.”*

**Meaning.** The stated condition is a breach of covenant followed by hostility toward the religion. “Leaders of disbelief” refers to the leading figures who drive or direct that opposition; the verse says the broken pledges can no longer be relied upon. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Saʿdī] [Study Quran]**

**Rulings & scope.** Commentators differ over the meaning and legal reach of “attack your religion,” including how it relates to verbal vilification. The command must be read with its express treaty-breaking condition and the hostile conduct described in 9:13, not detached from them as a general license for private violence or for attacking anyone who disagrees. **[Qurṭubī] [Study Quran]**

## 9:13

*“Will you not fight a people who broke their oaths, intended to expel the Messenger, and began against you first? Do you fear them? God has more right to be feared, if you are believers.”*

**Meaning.** Three grounds are named: breach of the covenant, the attempt that led to the Prophet’s expulsion from Mecca, and the opponents’ initiation of hostilities. Commentators differ over which event “began against you first” recalls: some point to Badr, while others connect it to Quraysh’s support for Banū Bakr in their attack on the Prophet’s ally Khuzāʿah, which broke the Ḥudaybiyyah pact. **[Ṭabarī] [Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Study Quran]**

**Reflection.** The passage grounds the conflict in specific actions rather than in a bare difference of belief; it urges the believers not to let fear override the obligations described here. **[Saʿdī] [Maʿārif] [Study Quran]**

## 9:14

*“Fight them: God will chastise them by your hands, disgrace them, grant you victory over them, and heal the breasts of a believing people.”*

**Meaning.** The verse describes the consequences of defeating those who broke the covenant and attacked the community: their power is checked, the believers prevail, and the distress of those harmed is relieved. Commentators commonly identify the “believing people” whose hearts are healed as Khuzāʿah, the Prophet’s allies attacked by Banū Bakr with Quraysh’s support. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Qurṭubī] [Study Quran]**

**Reflection.** As al-Maʿārif emphasizes, the aim is that aggression cease, not that believers simply indulge their anger; the next verse holds open the possibility of repentance. **[Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**

## 9:15

*“And He will remove the anger in their hearts. God turns in mercy to whom He wills. God is Knowing, Wise.”*

**Meaning.** God relieves the grief and anger of the believers harmed by the breach, while the close of the verse turns from punishment to divine mercy: God may guide former opponents to repentance. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Qurṭubī] [Maʿārif]**

**Reflection.** Even in a passage about conflict and accountability, the possibility of return is not closed. The aim is not unending vengeance; God’s wisdom includes accepting repentance from whom He wills. **[Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**
