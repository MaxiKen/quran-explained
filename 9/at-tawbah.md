# Sūrah 9 — at-Tawbah (Repentance), al-Barāʾah (Disavowal)

*129 verses; Madinan. The sūrah opens with a public declaration concerning certain treaties with Arabian polytheist groups, a period of notice, and the duty to honor agreements kept in good faith. It also addresses the Tabūk campaign, repentance, and the community’s obligations. **[Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif]**

**Sources cited inline:** **[Ṭabarī]** *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* · **[Qurṭubī]** *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān* · **[Ibn Kathīr]** *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* · **[Jalālayn]** *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* · **[Saʿdī]** *Taysīr al-Karīm al-Raḥmān* · **[Maʿārif]** *Maʿārif al-Qurʾān* · **[Study Quran]** *The Study Quran* (2015).

**The sūrah.** Also known as al-Barāʾah, it begins with a formal announcement about treaty obligations and a four-month respite for the groups addressed. The opening is not a blanket cancellation of every agreement: verse 9:4 explicitly orders that treaties with parties who kept their terms be fulfilled until their agreed end. **[Ṭabarī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Study Quran]**

**Opening formula.** This is the only sūrah conventionally written without the basmalah. Commentators preserve several explanations, including its opening declaration of disavowal and reports about how it was placed in the written Qur’an alongside Sūrah 8. **[Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif]**

---

## 9:1

*“A declaration of release from God and His Messenger to those among the polytheists with whom you made a treaty.”*

**Meaning.** *Barāʾah* announces release from certain treaty obligations. The declaration concerns the Arabian polytheist groups addressed in this passage; it must be read with the following notice period and the express exception for parties who kept their agreements in 9:4. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif]**

**Context.** The declaration was publicly proclaimed during the pilgrimage season in 9 AH. Commentators distinguish groups whose treaty had no fixed term or a shorter remaining term from parties whose valid, unbroken treaty was to be honored until its stated end. **[Ṭabarī] [Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr]**

## 9:2

*“Travel freely through the land for four months, and know that you cannot escape God, and that God will disgrace the disbelievers.”*

**Meaning.** The groups receiving notice are given a period in which to travel in safety and consider their position; they are reminded that no one can evade God. This is a defined period of respite following a public announcement, not an instruction to disregard agreements that the next verse preserves. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif]**

**Context.** The commentators discuss which treaty categories received the full four months and how the period was reckoned. They also differ over whether “the sacred months” in 9:5 refers to the customary sacred months or to this announced period of respite. **[Ṭabarī] [Qurṭubī]**

## 9:3

*“A proclamation from God and His Messenger to the people on the day of the Greater Pilgrimage: God is free of obligation to the polytheists, and so is His Messenger. If you repent, it is better for you; but if you turn away, know that you cannot escape God. Give tidings of a painful punishment to those who disbelieve.”*

**Context.** The proclamation was made during the pilgrimage of 9 AH, with Abū Bakr leading the pilgrimage and ʿAlī announcing the declaration. Early authorities differ over whether “the day of the Greater Pilgrimage” means the Day of ʿArafah or the Day of Sacrifice. **[Ṭabarī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Maʿārif]**

**Meaning.** This public notice makes the change in treaty relations known to the tribes addressed, invites repentance, and warns that turning away from God does not place anyone beyond His power. **[Jalālayn] [Saʿdī] [Maʿārif]**

**Ḥadīth & āthār.** Ibn Kathīr cites the report in al-Bukhārī that Abū Hurayrah was sent with the public announcement that, after that year, no polytheist would perform the pilgrimage and no one would circumambulate the Kaʿbah naked. **[Ibn Kathīr]**

## 9:4

*“Except those among the polytheists with whom you made a treaty, who have not failed you in anything or supported anyone against you: fulfill their treaty with them until its term. Truly God loves the mindful.”*

**Meaning.** The command is explicit: parties who have kept their treaty and have not aided the Muslims’ enemies must be protected by its terms until the agreed deadline. Their being polytheists does not cancel the obligation to keep a valid covenant. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Saʿdī] [Study Quran]**

**Rulings.** Fulfilling a treaty is itself an act of God-consciousness. The public notice in 9:1–3 therefore does not authorize breaking an agreement with those who have honored it. **[Qurṭubī] [Maʿārif]**

## 9:5

*“When the sacred months have passed, kill the polytheists wherever you find them; take them, besiege them, and lie in wait for them at every place of ambush. But if they repent, establish prayer, and give the alms, let them go their way. Truly God is Forgiving, Merciful.”*

**Meaning.** The verse sets out a military order for the conflict addressed in the preceding declaration: fighting, capture, siege, and the guarding of routes; if the people concerned turn to Islam, establish prayer, and pay zakāh, hostilities are to cease. The Arabic verb in the first command is literally “kill”; it is spoken here within the passage’s defined wartime setting. **[Ṭabarī] [Jalālayn] [Ibn Kathīr] [Saʿdī] [Maʿārif] [Study Quran]**

**Context & scope.** This follows the notice and respite of 9:1–3, while 9:4 protects those who kept their covenants. The Study Quran reads the passage in its historical setting of conflict and treaty-breaking among Arabian groups, rather than treating polytheism alone as a perpetual cause for war; it also notes that commentators differ, with some assigning the verse broader force or saying it abrogated earlier commands to patience. Verse 9:6 immediately provides safe-conduct for a polytheist who seeks protection, so that he may hear the Qur’an and be escorted to safety. **[Qurṭubī] [Ibn Kathīr] [Study Quran]**

**Rulings.** Classical exegetes disagree over the relation between this verse and other rules for treaties, ransom, and restraint. Qurṭubī records views that some earlier commands were abrogated, but also the position that the verses remain operative together. The command is presented as collective direction in a declared conflict, not a private license for individuals to attack others. **[Qurṭubī] [Study Quran]**
