## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:101

> When you travel through the land, it is permissible for you to shorten the prayer[217]—˹especially˺ if you fear an attack by the disbelievers. Indeed, the disbelievers are your sworn enemies.

**Commentary:**

### The Concession of Shortening Prayer

Allah permits the traveller to shorten the obligatory prayer. This is known as *qaṣr al-ṣalāh*, shortening the prayer. The four-unit prayers of ẓuhr, ʿaṣr, and ʿishāʾ are prayed as two units during qualifying travel; fajr and maghrib are not shortened. The concession shows that worship is not meant to become an unbearable burden during movement.

### Fear and the Historical Setting

The verse mentions fear of attack because the first Muslim community often travelled through hostile territory and conducted military expeditions. The next verse explains how prayer is organised during an actual security threat. The reference to disbelievers as sworn enemies describes active hostile forces in that setting, not every non-Muslim person or peaceful society.

The Qur’an elsewhere commands justice and kindness toward those who do not fight Muslims. This verse must therefore not be used to cultivate suspicion or hostility toward peaceful neighbours, travellers, or citizens.

### A Concession, Not a Deficiency

Shortening prayer does not mean that the shortened form is spiritually inferior. It is a gift from Allah, and accepting a legitimate concession is part of following the Sunnah. The Prophet ﷺ shortened prayers during travel in circumstances of safety as well as danger, demonstrating that the ruling is not restricted to visible fear.

### Worship within Real Circumstances

Travel brings fatigue, uncertainty, limited time, and changing surroundings. Allah knows these conditions and gives worshippers a manageable form of prayer rather than allowing difficulty to become an excuse for abandoning prayer. The believer carries prayer along the journey while receiving ease in its form.

### Security and Responsible Planning

The verse also teaches travellers to take danger seriously. Muslims should assess routes, protect themselves and dependants, observe lawful travel requirements, and avoid exposing people to unnecessary risk. Reliance upon Allah does not require carelessness; precaution and trust belong together.

### No Licence for Aggression

The verse concerns a concession in worship during travel and a real threat of attack. It does not authorize private violence, vigilantism, or hostility toward people on the basis of religion. Questions of armed defence belong to legitimate authority, lawful conditions, and the moral limits established by revelation.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:102

> When you ˹O Prophet˺ are ˹campaigning˺ with them and you lead them in prayer, let one group of them pray with you—while armed. When they prostrate themselves, let the other group stand guard behind them. Then the group that has not yet prayed will then join you in prayer—and let them be vigilant and armed.[218] The disbelievers would wish to see you neglect your weapons and belongings, so they could launch a sweeping assault on you. But there is no blame if you lay aside your weapons when overcome by heavy rain or illness—but take precaution. Indeed, Allah has prepared a humiliating punishment for the disbelievers.

**Commentary:**

### Prayer in the Midst of Danger

This verse describes *ṣalāt al-khawf*, the prayer of fear. Even during a military campaign, the believers do not simply abandon prayer. One group prays with the Prophet ﷺ while another keeps watch, then the groups exchange responsibilities. Worship continues without pretending that danger does not exist.

### Balancing Worship and Security

The arrangement joins two obligations: devotion to Allah and protection of human life. The worshippers remain alert, keep their equipment available, and organise the prayer so that an enemy cannot exploit collective vulnerability. Spirituality does not require neglecting reasonable security; security does not justify neglecting Allah.

### Leadership and Order

The Prophet ﷺ leads the prayer, and the community follows an organised plan. In a genuine emergency, clear leadership, communication, assigned roles, and discipline protect everyone. No individual should improvise a dangerous action and then claim that religious zeal excuses the harm caused.

### The Sunnah Explains the Forms

The fear prayer has more than one authenticated form because the position and movement of the enemy can differ. Reports in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim describe the Prophet’s practice in different situations. Scholars of jurisprudence discuss these forms in detail, but all preserve the verse’s central purposes: prayer, vigilance, fairness, and protection.

### Weapons Are a Means, Not an Identity

The instruction to carry weapons is connected to an active military threat. Weapons are not symbols of personal piety, and this verse is not permission for private armed groups or attacks on civilians. In any legitimate defence, force must remain governed by lawful authority, necessity, distinction, proportionality, and the protection of noncombatants.

### Concessions for Hardship

Heavy rain, illness, or comparable danger may make carrying weapons difficult or harmful. Allah removes blame when equipment is laid aside for a genuine reason, while still commanding precaution. This illustrates a wider legal principle: obligations are preserved as far as possible, and harmful burdens are removed without turning ease into carelessness.

### The Enemy’s Opportunity

The warning about an enemy launching a sweeping assault explains why vigilance matters in this historical setting. It concerns hostile forces seeking an opportunity to attack the worshippers, not peaceful people who happen to differ in religion. The final warning addresses those actively waging aggression and must be read with the Qur’an’s commands of justice and peace.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:103

> When the prayers are over, remember Allah—whether you are standing, sitting, or lying down. But when you are secure, establish regular prayers. Indeed, performing prayers is a duty on the believers at the appointed times.

**Commentary:**

### Remembrance after Fear

After describing the prayer of fear, Allah commands believers to remember Him while standing, sitting, or lying down. *Dhikr* is not limited to a particular posture or setting. The tongue can praise Allah, the heart can rely upon Him, and the limbs can obey Him even when circumstances remain unsettled.

### Prayer Is Not Abandoned

The emergency form of prayer does not cancel prayer; it adapts its performance to danger. Once security returns, believers are to establish prayer in its normal, complete and disciplined form. Concessions are mercy for difficult circumstances, not a reason to treat worship as optional when the difficulty has passed.

### Appointed Times

Prayer is a duty with known times, *mawāqīt*. It cannot be left to an undefined moment of convenience. Fajr, ẓuhr, ʿaṣr, maghrib, and ʿishāʾ structure the believer’s day around remembrance of Allah. Scheduling responsibilities around prayer expresses that Allah’s claim has priority over worldly demands.

### Security and Gratitude

When danger subsides, returning to regular prayer is an act of gratitude. Safety is not merely an opportunity for comfort; it is an opportunity to worship with greater calm, concentration, and completeness. The believer remembers the One who preserved life and granted relief.

### Dhikr in Every Condition

The verse’s three postures teach that remembrance accompanies the believer in strength, weakness, illness, travel, work, and rest. A person who cannot stand can remember Allah sitting; a person who cannot sit can remember Him lying down. Allah does not sever the servant from spiritual connection because the body is limited.

### Discipline and Inner Stability

The fixed times protect worship from being swallowed by urgency. Even in crisis, prayer gives the community a rhythm, moral centre, and moment of dependence upon Allah. In ordinary life, the same rhythm protects the heart from heedlessness and teaches consistency.

### The Legal and Spiritual Balance

Islam combines defined obligations with legitimate concessions. The appointed prayer times remain binding, while the form may change for travel, illness, or fear. Sound jurisprudence therefore asks both what the normal rule is and what mercy applies to the person’s actual condition.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:104

> Do not falter in pursuit of the enemy—if you are suffering, they too are suffering. But you can hope to receive from Allah what they can never hope for. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

**Commentary:**

### Do Not Collapse under Difficulty

The believers are told not to weaken in a legitimate pursuit of an active enemy. The instruction addresses a community facing the physical exhaustion, fear, wounds, and uncertainty of war. It is not a command to seek conflict for its own sake, nor a general instruction to pursue people who are peaceful.

### Shared Human Hardship

The enemy also suffers fatigue and hardship. Recognising this reality gives believers courage, but it should not erase moral restraint. The opposing soldier remains a human being, and suffering is not a licence for cruelty, torture, or harm to civilians and captives.

### Hope That Gives Meaning

Believers endure with a hope that the aggressor does not possess: hope for Allah’s reward, forgiveness, protection of the oppressed, and a just outcome. This hope transforms endurance from blind stubbornness into purposeful patience, *ṣabr*, guided by faith.

### Courage Is Not Recklessness

Strength in the verse means steadfastness under lawful responsibility. It does not mean entering a hopeless situation without preparation, ignoring commanders, abandoning medical care, or sacrificing lives to pride. A responsible authority weighs benefit, danger, and the protection of people before ordering action.

### Wisdom in Pursuit

Allah is *ʿAlīm*, All-Knowing, and *Ḥakīm*, All-Wise. Human beings see only part of a conflict, while Allah knows intentions, hidden dangers, consequences, and the truth of every claim. Strategic patience, negotiation, withdrawal, or a ceasefire can be wiser than continued pursuit when they better protect life and end aggression.

### Moral Limits Remain

This verse is read with the surrounding Qur’anic limits: fight those who fight you without transgression, accept peace when it is offered, honour treaties, and do not harm those who are not participating in aggression. The enemy in this passage is an active hostile force in a recognised conflict, not a religious or ethnic category.

### Trials beyond the Battlefield

The spiritual principle of not surrendering to despair also applies to illness, poverty, study, reform, and resistance to sin. Yet every application must retain wisdom: perseverance should pursue a sound good, not stubbornly continue a harmful course.

- Qur’an 8:61 on accepting peace when the enemy inclines toward peace.
- Qur’an 47:4 on the conduct of lawful battle and treatment after victory.
- Qur’an 60:8–9 on justice toward peaceful people and caution toward those who fight.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:105

> Indeed, We have sent down the Book to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth to judge between people by means of what Allah has shown you. So do not be an advocate for the deceitful.

**Commentary:**

### Revelation and Judgement

Allah sent the Qur’an in truth so that the Prophet ﷺ would judge between people by what Allah showed him. Revelation is not merely private inspiration; it establishes truth, rights, duties, and principles of judgement. The Messenger’s authority is therefore bound to what Allah revealed, not to tribal loyalty, public pressure, or personal preference.

### The Case behind the Warning

Classical exegetes connect this passage with a dispute in which a guilty person’s supporters attempted to shift blame onto an innocent man and sought the Prophet’s favourable judgement through deception. The details teach a general lesson: a persuasive story, influential family, or apparently respectable claimant must not replace evidence.

### Do Not Defend Deceit

The Prophet ﷺ is told not to be an advocate for *khāʾinīn*, those who betray trusts. This does not forbid representing an accused person, asking that evidence be examined, or protecting someone from injustice. It forbids knowingly using influence to make falsehood appear true or to help a wrongdoer escape accountability.

### Justice beyond Group Loyalty

A believer must not defend a relative, friend, political faction, religious group, or employer merely because of belonging. Justice may require acknowledging that one’s own side has lied or harmed another. The Qur’an repeatedly commands believers to stand firmly for justice even when it is against themselves or their close relations.

### Evidence and Procedure

The verse establishes a foundation for due process: investigate facts, hear the parties, distinguish proof from accusation, and judge by relevant evidence. It also protects judges from emotional manipulation. A court must not be persuaded by eloquence when the facts point elsewhere, nor condemn a person merely because an accusation is popular.

### Truth as a Public Trust

Judicial integrity is an *amānah*. Judges, lawyers, witnesses, journalists, scholars, and leaders all influence how society understands a dispute. Concealing evidence, fabricating documents, coaching testimony, or spreading a one-sided narrative turns that influence into advocacy for betrayal.

### Mercy without Complicity

A wrongdoer can be treated with dignity while still being held responsible. Repentance, restitution, fair punishment, and rehabilitation are compatible with justice. Compassion does not require declaring the innocent guilty or the guilty innocent.

### Evidence and references

- Qur’an 4:105–112 on revelation, judging fairly, betrayal, repentance, and refusing to defend deception.

- Qur’an 5:8 on not allowing hatred or loyalty to compromise justice.
- Qur’an 38:26 on judging by truth and avoiding desire.
- Qur’an 49:6 on verifying reports before acting on them.
- Qur’an 24:4 and 24:15–16 on evidence, accusations, and avoiding careless speech.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, the Prophet’s warning that a judge may decide according to the evidence presented while the hidden reality remains with Allah; reports on testimony and trusts.
- For *ḥukm*, *bi-mā arāka Allāh*, *khāʾin*, *khiyānah*, *bayyinah*, and judicial impartiality, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:105.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:106

> And seek Allah’s forgiveness—indeed, Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Commentary:**

### Forgiveness after Wrongdoing

After warning against defending the deceitful, Allah directs the servant to seek forgiveness. This transition shows that wrongdoing is not a permanent identity. A person who has lied, assisted injustice, concealed evidence, or taken another’s right can return to Allah through sincere repentance.

### The Meaning of Istighfār

Seeking forgiveness, *istighfār*, is more than repeating words while intending to continue the sin. It includes recognising the wrong, regretting it, stopping it, resolving not to return, and repairing harm where another person’s right was violated. If a person has defamed someone, restitution may require correcting the false claim and restoring the victim’s honour.

### Divine Mercy and Human Rights

Allah’s forgiveness does not erase the rights of victims or prevent legitimate accountability. A thief must return property, a false witness must correct testimony, and an abusive person must stop the abuse and accept lawful consequences. Repentance changes the wrongdoer’s relationship with Allah; justice also requires addressing the harm done to people.

### The Door Remains Open

Allah is *Ghafūr*, All-Forgiving, and *Raḥīm*, Most Merciful. These names cultivate hope without trivialising sin. The person who sincerely returns should not despair because the wrongdoing was serious, while the person who invokes mercy to avoid changing has not understood repentance.

### Personal and Institutional Repentance

The verse applies not only to individuals but also to families, organisations, courts, and governments. An institution that supported falsehood should acknowledge the failure, protect those harmed, correct its records, and establish safeguards. Public repentance becomes credible when it produces public repair.

### Seeking Forgiveness before Judgement

In the preceding case, seeking Allah’s forgiveness should accompany acceptance of the truth. A person must not manipulate religious language to secure a verdict and then call that manipulation repentance. Allah knows whether the heart is returning or merely seeking another strategy.

### A Daily Practice

Regular *istighfār* keeps the believer aware of dependence upon Allah. It prevents self-righteousness, encourages quick correction, and makes mercy a source of moral growth rather than an excuse for repeating harm.

- Qur’an 66:8 on sincere repentance, *tawbah naṣūḥ*.
- Qur’an 2:160 and 3:135–136 on repentance, correction, and forgiveness.
- Qur’an 24:4–5 on correcting false accusations and the seriousness of testimony.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, reports on frequent *istighfār*, sincere repentance, and Allah’s joy at a servant’s return.
- For *istighfār*, *maghfirah*, *raḥmah*, *tawbah*, *ẓulm*, and restoring the rights of victims, see Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī, Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr, and Tafsīr al-Qurṭubī on 4:106.
## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:107

> Do not advocate for those who wrong themselves.[219] Surely Allah does not like those who are deceitful, sinful.

**Commentary:**

### Do Not Become an Agent of Wrong

The prohibition is repeated and strengthened. The person who betrays a trust harms another, but also wrongs himself by choosing sin and exposing his soul to accountability. Believers must not turn their loyalty, eloquence, or influence into protection for such wrongdoing.

### Advocacy and Fair Representation

The verse does not forbid a lawyer, relative, or community member from ensuring that an accused person receives a fair hearing. It forbids knowingly advocating falsehood: manufacturing a defence through lies, suppressing decisive evidence, intimidating witnesses, or presenting the guilty party as innocent when one knows the truth. Justice protects both the accused and the victim.

### Deception and Sin

Allah does not love *khawwān athīm*, the one repeatedly treacherous and sinful. The intensive wording suggests a settled pattern of betrayal rather than an isolated error followed by repentance. A person may make a mistake and return; the condemned pattern is deliberate, repeated deception defended as a way of life.

### Loyalty Has Limits

Family affection, professional duty, political allegiance, and friendship cannot override truth. Genuine loyalty should restrain a person from wrongdoing, encourage confession, and help repair the damage. Covering a crime may feel compassionate in the moment but leaves victims exposed and trains the wrongdoer to repeat the harm.

### The Hidden Cost of False Narratives

A false narrative can injure an innocent person, distort a court’s decision, destroy reputations, and erode public trust. The verse therefore applies to rumours, selective reporting, manipulated statistics, online campaigns, and institutional cover-ups as well as formal legal disputes.

### Justice with Mercy

Mercy does not require denial. A wrongdoer can be offered repentance, counsel, treatment, restitution, and reintegration while the truth remains clear. Hiding the wrong is not mercy to the sinner, because it blocks moral reform and leaves the harmed person without protection.

### Allah’s Moral Regard

The statement that Allah does not love the deceitful and sinful is a warning about moral character, not a licence for believers to dehumanise people. Muslims should oppose the act, protect the vulnerable, and leave the ultimate judgement of hearts and final destiny to Allah.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:108

> They try to hide ˹their deception˺ from people, but they can never hide it from Allah—in Whose presence they plot by night what is displeasing to Him. And Allah is Fully Aware of what they do.

**Commentary:**

### Human Eyes and Divine Knowledge

The deceitful try to conceal their actions from people, but no action is hidden from Allah. A person may control appearances, rehearse a convincing explanation, or exploit the darkness of night, yet Allah knows the truth, the intention, the planning, and the harm produced.

### Secret Counsel against Truth

The verse recalls their private plotting at night. Private conversation is not itself blameworthy; consultation, family confidence, and protected counsel can be good. The warning concerns secret planning that is displeasing to Allah—false testimony, betrayal, manipulation, and schemes designed to injure the innocent.

### Reputation Is Not Reality

People often protect their public reputation more carefully than their moral character. The verse asks believers to value truth over appearance. A respectable image cannot turn treachery into integrity, and social approval cannot prevent Allah’s judgement.

### Allah’s Presence and Accountability

Allah is aware of what they do, *yaʿlamu mā yafʿalūn*. His knowledge is not like human surveillance, which is partial and often mistaken. It includes outward acts and inward purposes. This gives victims confidence that concealed injustice is not lost, and it gives wrongdoers a reason to stop before harm grows.

### Privacy Is Not a Licence for Sin

The verse does not authorise people to spy on one another. Allah’s perfect knowledge is not a justification for unlawful intrusion, exposing private matters, hacking accounts, or spreading suspicion. Muslims must protect privacy while investigating a legitimate allegation through lawful evidence and due process.

### Night as a Moral Test

The reference to night highlights a time when social restraints may weaken and a person may believe that no one is watching. The believer remembers that private conduct is part of character. Sincerity means being concerned with Allah’s approval even when no human observer can reward or punish.

### From Concealment to Repentance

Allah’s awareness is also an opening to repentance. The person who abandons a scheme, confesses where necessary, repairs the harm, and seeks forgiveness can turn a hidden sin into the beginning of reform. Delay is dangerous because repeated secret wrongdoing hardens the heart.

### Transparency and Trust

Families, institutions, and governments require appropriate confidentiality, but not deception that defeats accountability. Leaders should establish records, oversight, conflict-of-interest rules, and safe channels for reporting misconduct. Trust grows when secrecy serves protection rather than impunity.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:109

> Here you are! You ˹believers˺ are advocating for them in this life, but who will ˹dare to˺ advocate for them before Allah on the Day of Judgment? Or who will come to their defence?

**Commentary:**

### Limited Worldly Advocacy

People may argue forcefully for the deceitful in this life. They may use eloquence, status, family influence, wealth, or institutional power to shape a verdict. But worldly advocacy is limited to what people can observe and what a court can establish. It cannot make betrayal disappear before Allah.

### The Final Court

The Day of Judgement exposes what worldly procedures cannot fully reach: hidden motives, suppressed evidence, secret agreements, and the harm done to those without power. No influential advocate can silence the victim there, and no public reputation can substitute for truth.

### Fair Defence Is Not False Defence

The verse should not be taken as a prohibition on a fair legal defence. Every accused person deserves to be heard, protected from coercion, and judged by evidence. The condemnation falls on advocacy that knowingly turns falsehood into a defence or uses procedure to protect a proven wrongdoer from justice.

### Human Power Has a Limit

The question “who will defend them?” strips false confidence from the wrongdoer and those who enabled him. A person may escape a worldly penalty, but no escape from Allah’s knowledge exists. This should humble judges, lawyers, witnesses, officials, and anyone tempted to sell influence.

### Intercession Belongs to Allah

The Qur’an teaches that intercession on the Last Day belongs to Allah’s permission and pleasure. No person can compel Allah to excuse treachery or injustice. This does not deny the possibility of permitted intercession; it denies the idea that status or human patronage controls the final judgement.

### Protecting the Unheard

The verse also calls communities to consider whose voice is missing. Powerful advocacy may drown out a poor victim, a child, a refugee, an employee, or a person afraid to speak. Justice requires safe reporting channels, competent representation, and serious attention to evidence rather than automatic trust in the powerful.

### Accountability for Enablers

Those who assist deception share responsibility according to their role. A person who unknowingly repeats a false account is not like someone who fabricates it after learning the truth. Once the facts become clear, continuing to defend the lie deepens the sin.

### Hope for the Wronged

The final court is a source of comfort to people whose cases were mishandled or whose suffering was hidden. Allah’s justice is not dependent on the resources available to the victim in this world. The believer should pursue lawful remedies while trusting that no right is ultimately lost with Allah.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:110

> Whoever commits evil or wrongs themselves then seeks Allah’s forgiveness will certainly find Allah All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Commentary:**

### A Universal Door of Return

The verse opens repentance to whoever commits evil or wrongs themselves. No sinner should conclude that return to Allah is impossible. Sin damages the victim, the community, and the sinner’s own soul, but sincere *tawbah* can restore the servant’s relationship with Allah.

### What It Means to Seek Forgiveness

Seeking forgiveness, *istighfār*, must be accompanied by a truthful return. The repentant person recognises the sin, feels remorse, stops the wrongdoing, resolves not to return, and repairs any human right that was violated. A verbal request for pardon while continuing deception is not the complete repentance praised here.

### Allah Is Found through Repentance

The expression “will find Allah” points to a living encounter with His forgiveness and mercy. Allah is not distant from the servant who turns sincerely. His *maghfirah* covers the sin, while His *raḥmah* nurtures the person toward obedience and reform.

### Personal Sin and Harm to Others

When the sin involves another person, repentance cannot be reduced to private emotion. Stolen property must be returned, false accusations corrected, debts addressed, and abuse stopped. Where direct contact would create further harm, restitution should be made through safe and wise channels. Mercy for the repentant cannot require a victim to bear continuing danger.

### Hope without Trivialising Sin

The promise of forgiveness does not make evil insignificant. It makes Allah’s mercy greater than the sinner’s despair. A person should neither feel safe while persisting in harm nor despair after sincerely abandoning it. Fear of wrongdoing and hope in mercy should work together.

### The Prophet’s Example

The Prophet ﷺ, though protected from the sins of ordinary people, sought Allah’s forgiveness frequently. His practice teaches that *istighfār* is not only for notorious sinners; it is a daily expression of humility, gratitude, and awareness of one’s shortcomings.

### Immediate and Ongoing Reform

Sincere repentance produces changed conduct. It may require leaving an exploitative business, ending a harmful relationship, confessing a false claim, seeking treatment for an addiction, or accepting accountability. Allah’s mercy gives strength for these steps rather than replacing them.

### No Despair

The verse protects people from the whisper that their past defines their future. The person who has wronged themselves can begin again through faith, good action, restitution, and perseverance. The community should encourage repentance while maintaining safeguards for those who may otherwise be harmed.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:111

> And whoever commits a sin—it is only to their own loss. Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

**Commentary:**

### Personal Responsibility

Every person bears responsibility for their own sin. No one can transfer moral guilt to an innocent person, and no person is saved from accountability merely because a group, family, leader, or society encouraged the wrongdoing. Islam rejects inherited moral blame and the use of scapegoats to hide personal choices.

### Sin Is Self-Loss

A sin may appear to benefit someone through money, power, pleasure, or revenge, but its ultimate result is loss. It darkens the heart, weakens trust, damages the person’s standing before Allah, and brings consequences in the Hereafter. A temporary gain cannot compensate for spiritual ruin.

### Harm to Others Still Matters

The statement that sin harms its perpetrator does not mean that others are unaffected. Theft harms a victim, false testimony harms a defendant, and oppression damages a whole community. Rather, the verse teaches that the sinner bears the burden of the act, while victims do not carry the wrongdoer’s guilt. Human authorities must still protect people and restore their rights.

### No Blaming the Innocent

A community must not punish relatives for one person’s crime, condemn a whole ethnicity for an individual’s offence, or treat children as responsible for their parents’ decisions. Collective punishment and inherited stigma contradict the Qur’anic principle of individual accountability.

### Allah’s Knowledge

Allah is *ʿAlīm*, All-Knowing. He knows the act, the intention, the pressure surrounding it, the available choices, and the harm caused. Human courts judge according to evidence and outward conduct; Allah’s final judgement includes realities that people cannot fully access.

### Allah’s Wisdom

Allah is *Ḥakīm*, All-Wise. His commands and judgements are not arbitrary. He knows why accountability must be personal, why rights must be restored, and why repentance must be open. His wisdom prevents both despair in the guilty and injustice toward the innocent.

### A Call to Self-Examination

The verse redirects attention from blaming others to examining one’s own record. Before condemning a person, a believer should ask whether anger, pride, envy, or group loyalty is making them overlook their own faults. Self-accountability is a protection against hypocrisy.

### Reform Rather Than Fatalism

Personal responsibility should inspire action, not fatalism. A person who caused harm can repent, seek forgiveness, repair the damage, and change the conditions that led to the sin. The community can support reform while ensuring that responsibility is not evaded.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:112

> And whoever commits an evil or sinful deed then blames it on an innocent person, they will definitely bear the guilt of slander and blatant sin.

**Commentary:**

### Sin Added to Slander

The verse describes a person who commits a wrong and then places the blame on someone innocent. The wrongdoer carries the original sin and the additional guilt of *buhtān*, a devastating false accusation, and *ithm mubīn*, a clear and manifest sin. The attempt to escape responsibility multiplies the harm.

### The Innocent Must Be Protected

An accusation can cost an innocent person their safety, marriage, livelihood, reputation, freedom, or relationship with Allah’s creation. For this reason, claims must not be treated as facts merely because they are emotionally powerful or repeated by influential people. A person’s dignity cannot be sacrificed to preserve someone else’s image.

### The Context of the Passage

This verse belongs to the passage concerning a theft and the attempt to accuse an innocent man while protecting the actual wrongdoer. The lesson is not restricted to that incident. It condemns tribal cover-ups, institutional scapegoating, fabricated evidence, false reports, and every strategy that transfers guilt to the vulnerable.

### Evidence before Judgement

Justice requires verification, witnesses where applicable, a chance to respond, competent investigation, and an impartial authority. Suspicion is not proof. The Qur’anic demand for evidence protects both the innocent and the integrity of the community’s judgement.

### Blame-Shifting in Daily Life

Blame-shifting appears when a person breaks a promise and accuses the one who reminds them, when an abuser portrays the victim as the problem, when an organisation punishes a whistleblower instead of addressing misconduct, or when a public figure creates a scapegoat for policy failure. The verse teaches believers to identify this pattern.

### Repentance Requires Clearing the Victim

If someone has falsely accused another, repentance requires more than private *istighfār*. The false claim must be withdrawn, the innocent person’s name restored, and any material or legal harm repaired. Public damage often requires a correction that reaches the same audience as the original accusation.

### Do Not Repeat Unverified Claims

A person who repeats a charge without checking may become part of the injustice even if they did not originate it. Silence, careful wording, and referral to a trustworthy authority are safer than forwarding an allegation. The greater the potential harm, the greater the obligation to verify.

### Allah’s Complete Justice

A person may successfully shift blame before people, but Allah knows who acted, who planned, who lied, who repeated the lie, and who suffered. The verse gives comfort to the falsely accused and warns the powerful that impunity in this world is not innocence before Allah.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:113

> Had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy, a group of them would have sought to deceive you ˹O Prophet˺. Yet they would deceive none but themselves, nor can they harm you in the least. Allah has revealed to you the Book and wisdom and taught you what you never knew. Great ˹indeed˺ is Allah’s favour upon you!

**Commentary:**

### Allah’s Protection through Grace

Allah’s grace and mercy protected the Prophet ﷺ from the attempted deception surrounding the dispute. The protection came through revelation, which exposed the false narrative and restored justice. This does not mean that a believer should neglect investigation; it means that truth ultimately depends upon Allah’s guidance and care.

### Deception Turns Back on Its Author

Those who tried to deceive the Messenger could not harm him, but they harmed themselves. A lie may temporarily mislead people, yet it corrupts the liar’s character, burdens the conscience, injures the innocent, and creates accountability before Allah. Falsehood is therefore self-destructive even when it appears successful.

### The Book and Wisdom

Allah gave the Prophet ﷺ the *Kitāb*, the revealed Book, and *ḥikmah*, wisdom. The classical scholars explain wisdom here in relation to the Prophetic Sunnah: the inspired practical explanation and application of revelation. The Qur’an establishes principles, and the Messenger demonstrates their meaning through worship, judgement, mercy, and conduct.

### Knowledge Is a Gift

The Prophet ﷺ was taught what he did not previously know. The verse teaches that authentic knowledge is a favour from Allah, not a possession that justifies arrogance. Scholars, judges, teachers, and professionals should respond to knowledge with gratitude, accuracy, humility, and service.

### Revelation and Justice

The immediate subject is a legal dispute, showing that revelation addresses public justice as well as private devotion. Faith must shape how claims are investigated, how the vulnerable are protected, and how powerful supporters are prevented from manipulating a verdict.

### Mercy Does Not Mean Naivety

Allah’s mercy does not require the Prophet ﷺ or the believers to accept every claim without scrutiny. Mercy can expose deception, stop harm, and restore the innocent person’s rights. True compassion is compatible with firm boundaries and truthful accountability.

### Gratitude for Guidance

The closing exclamation, “Great indeed is Allah’s favour upon you,” directs attention from the cleverness of deceivers to the greater gift of guidance. A community should protect revelation, preserve reliable knowledge, teach wisdom, and avoid turning religious learning into a tool for factional advantage.

### A Promise for the Wronged

People who are deceived or falsely accused may feel powerless before a coordinated lie. This verse assures them that Allah sees the whole matter. They should pursue lawful evidence and trusted help, while knowing that a false narrative cannot defeat Allah’s knowledge or final justice.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:114

> There is no good in most of their secret talks—except those encouraging charity, kindness, or reconciliation between people. And whoever does this seeking Allah’s pleasure, We will grant them a great reward.

**Commentary:**

### The Ethics of Private Speech

Most secret conversations in the passage are connected with deception, betrayal, or schemes against justice. Allah does not condemn privacy itself. Private counsel can protect a person’s dignity and allow sensitive matters to be discussed safely. The warning concerns secret speech whose purpose is harm, manipulation, or sin.

### Three Righteous Exceptions

Allah identifies conversations that encourage *ṣadaqah*, charity; *maʿrūf*, recognised goodness and kindness; and *iṣlāḥ bayn al-nās*, reconciliation between people. These are not merely topics for pleasant discussion. They are conversations that lead to practical benefit, restore rights, and reduce harm.

### Charity Requires Action

Encouraging charity includes helping someone give wealth, time, skills, food, shelter, education, or protection. Private coordination can preserve the recipient’s dignity and protect charitable work from vanity. It must not become a secret channel for embezzlement, favouritism, or buying influence.

### Maʿrūf Is Wider than Politeness

*Maʿrūf* includes what revelation and sound moral sense recognise as good: justice, truthful counsel, care for family, support for the vulnerable, and respectful speech. A private conversation that encourages a person to stop abuse, repay a debt, or protect a neighbour can be a righteous *najwā*.

### Reconciliation with Justice

Reconciliation is not forcing a victim to return to danger, silencing a complaint, or declaring both sides equally responsible when one has abused the other. Genuine *iṣlāḥ* establishes truth, protects the vulnerable, restores rights, and seeks a safe path to peace. Sometimes separation and legal accountability are necessary parts of repair.

### Sincerity as the Foundation

Whoever participates in such counsel seeking Allah’s pleasure, *marḍāt Allāh*, receives a great reward. The intention protects good works from the desire for praise, political advantage, or control. A person may speak privately for good and still need to be transparent with those whose rights or safety are affected.

### Speech as a Moral Act

The verse teaches that conversations are deeds. Complaints, advice, planning, mediation, and private messages can build or destroy relationships. Believers should ask before speaking: Is this true? Is it beneficial? Is it necessary? Does it protect a right, or merely satisfy curiosity and resentment?

### Confidentiality and Safeguarding

Confidentiality is valuable when it protects a victim, preserves trust, or permits wise mediation. It must not be used to conceal ongoing abuse, corruption, or danger. A trusted adviser should know when a matter must be referred to appropriate safeguarding or legal authorities.

### A Culture of Constructive Counsel

Families, mosques, schools, workplaces, and governments can turn private consultation into public good by establishing trustworthy mediation, charitable coordination, and ethical advice. The believer seeks not the pleasure of winning a conversation but the pleasure of Allah and the welfare of people.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:115

> And whoever defies the Messenger after guidance has become clear to them and follows a path other than that of the believers, We will let them pursue what they have chosen, then burn them in Hell—what an evil end!

**Commentary:**

### Defiance after Clarity

The warning concerns a person who opposes the Messenger ﷺ after guidance has become clear. It is not directed at someone who has not received the message properly, is sincerely seeking understanding, or asks a difficult question. The moral weight lies in recognising truth and then deliberately choosing defiance.

### The Messenger and Revealed Guidance

The Prophet’s authority is part of revelation. Following him means accepting the Qur’an he conveyed and the Sunnah through which he explained and embodied it. This obedience is not blind loyalty to a personality; it is submission to Allah’s guidance delivered through His Messenger.

### The Path of the Believers

The phrase *sabīl al-muʾminīn*, the path of the believers, highlights the importance of faithful community, sound learning, and protection from isolated interpretations driven by ego. It does not mean that every historical custom or majority opinion is automatically correct. The believers’ path is measured by the Qur’an and authentic Sunnah, and genuine scholarly disagreement within those foundations is not automatically defiance.

### Do Not Weaponise the Verse

This verse must not become a tool for casual *takfīr*, declaring Muslims outside Islam. Applying such a grave judgement requires knowledge, clear proof, careful distinctions, and competent scholarship. Disagreement over secondary law, political policy, or an unclear matter is not equivalent to knowingly rejecting established guidance.

### A Chosen Path and Its Consequence

Allah allows the defiant person to pursue the path they have chosen. This expresses moral responsibility: repeated choices shape character, and persistent rejection can leave the heart attached to error. Divine allowance is not divine approval. Allah may allow a person to continue for a time while still holding them accountable for the choice.

### Unity without Suppressing Truth

The verse encourages believers to remain connected to revelation and the righteous community, not to factionalism. Unity is not achieved by silencing sincere counsel or protecting leaders from correction. It is achieved by returning disputes to Allah and His Messenger, practising justice, and maintaining humility when scholars differ.

### Repentance Remains Possible

The threat should awaken rather than make people despair. A person who has opposed guidance can repent, return to truth, correct public errors, and seek forgiveness before death. A community should call people back with wisdom rather than assuming that a sinner’s present state is their final destiny.

### Guidance and Humility

The believer asks Allah for steadfastness, studies responsibly, consults trustworthy scholars, and avoids treating personal preference as revelation. The path of believers is marked by faith, knowledge, mercy, justice, and obedience—not by arrogance, abuse, or the desire to control others.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:116

> Surely Allah does not forgive associating ˹others˺ with Him ˹in worship˺,[220] but forgives anything else of whoever He wills. Indeed, whoever associates ˹others˺ with Allah has clearly gone far astray.

**Commentary:**

### The Centrality of Tawḥīd

Allah identifies *shirk*, associating partners with Him in worship, as the gravest spiritual wrong. *Tawḥīd* means affirming that Allah alone is the Creator, Lord, King, and One worthy of worship. Directing worship, ultimate reliance, or divine attributes to another contradicts the foundation upon which every act of obedience depends.

### The Condition of Dying upon Shirk

The verse concerns a person who meets Allah while persisting in shirk without repentance. It does not close the door to repentance during life. Whoever abandons idolatry, turns sincerely to Allah, and dies upon faith may be forgiven, whatever their previous history. Allah’s mercy is not restricted by the size of a past sin when the repentance is genuine.

### Other Sins Remain Serious

The statement that Allah may forgive anything else does not make other sins unimportant. Murder, oppression, theft, slander, abuse, and betrayal remain grave and may require repentance, restitution, and lawful accountability. “Whomever He wills” leaves the matter to Allah’s wisdom: He may forgive, or He may punish, while no one can compel Him.

### Why Shirk Is So Grave

Shirk misdirects the deepest act of the human being—worship and surrender—from the One who created and sustains all things. It also distorts the believer’s understanding of dependence, fear, hope, and gratitude. By calling the person far astray, the verse describes a departure from the true destination of life, not permission for people to mistreat or attack anyone.

### Mercy and Invitation

This warning should lead to sincere invitation, teaching, and prayer rather than arrogance. Muslims themselves must guard against hidden forms of shirk, such as performing worship for reputation, treating wealth or status as ultimate, or obeying a created being in deliberate defiance of Allah. The heart requires continual renewal of *ikhlāṣ*, sincerity.

### Do Not Judge Final Destinies

The theological warning belongs to Allah, who knows what message reached a person, what they understood, and how they died. Individuals should not claim knowledge of another person’s final judgement or turn a doctrine about the Hereafter into a pretext for oppression. The Qur’an commands wisdom, justice, and peaceful conduct toward those who do not fight.

### The Foundation of Hope

The verse is severe in its warning but expansive in its mention of forgiveness for other sins. Believers should fear shirk and avoid it while maintaining hope in Allah’s mercy for every sin from which they repent. The correct response is worship of Allah alone, humility, repentance, and compassionate counsel.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:117

> Instead of Allah, they only invoke female gods[221] and they ˹actually˺ invoke none but a rebellious Satan—

**Commentary:**

### Idolatry and Misplaced Worship

The verse describes people who invoke beings and objects besides Allah. “Female gods” refers to the deities of pre-Islamic Arabian idolatry, including names discussed in Sūrah al-Najm. The expression also addresses the claim that angels were Allah’s daughters: a false idea that both assigned offspring to Allah and turned created beings into objects of worship.

### The Reality behind the Invocation

The Qur’an says that they are in fact invoking Satan, *shayṭān*, the rebellious one. They may imagine that an idol or intermediary is holy, but the practice follows Satan’s invitation to disobey Allah and invent a religion without revelation. Satan does not need to be worshipped under his own name; obedience to his call can have the same spiritual effect.

### False Gods Have No Power

Idols cannot create, provide, forgive, guide, or answer prayers. The verse exposes the weakness beneath their impressive names and rituals. Allah alone possesses the attributes that justify worship. Turning to powerless objects for ultimate rescue leaves the heart vulnerable to fear, superstition, and manipulation.

### Satan’s Method

Satan’s rebellion includes beautifying falsehood, exploiting inherited custom, promising protection, and making people feel secure while they abandon truth. He can use religious symbols, charismatic figures, wealth, nationalism, or ideology as substitutes for Allah when people give them ultimate loyalty.

### A Warning against Hidden Idolatry

Muslims must examine their own hearts. Showing off worship for people, treating money or status as the source of ultimate security, or obeying a created authority in deliberate defiance of Allah can resemble forms of hidden *shirk*. This does not mean every attachment is idolatry; love, respect, and lawful obedience remain legitimate when they stay beneath worship of Allah.

### No Gendered Insult

The reference to female deities is a critique of false theology, not a denigration of women. Islam honours righteous women and rejects the idea that gender makes a being worthy or unworthy of worship. The problem is assigning divine status to created beings and attributing claims to Allah without knowledge.

### The Call to Freedom

Tawḥīd frees people from fear of idols, spirits, rulers, ancestors, markets, and social pressures. A person who worships Allah alone can respect creation without being enslaved by it. The critique of idolatry is therefore a call to truth and liberation, not an invitation to coerce or abuse others.

### Dialogue with Wisdom

The Qur’an teaches believers to invite people with wisdom and excellent instruction. Belief cannot be forced, and private vigilantism is never a legitimate response to theological disagreement. Allah alone judges hearts, while believers are responsible for truthful speech, good character, and justice.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:118

> cursed by Allah—who said, “I will surely take hold of a certain number of Your servants.

**Commentary:**

### Satan’s Curse

Satan is described as cursed by Allah because he rejected the divine command through pride and persisted in rebellion. The curse is not an arbitrary injustice; it is the consequence of deliberate defiance. Satan recognised Allah’s existence yet refused humble obedience, making his story a warning that knowledge without submission can become arrogance.

### “A Certain Number” of Servants

Satan announces his intention to claim a share of humanity. The wording does not mean that he possesses people by irresistible power. His influence reaches those who listen to his suggestions, follow his beautification of falsehood, and surrender their moral responsibility. He invites; people choose.

### The Difference between Whispering and Compulsion

Satan cannot force a believer to sin. He whispers, exaggerates desire, delays repentance, and makes disobedience look attractive. On the Day of Judgement he will admit that he had no authority except that he called and people responded. This protects believers from both blaming Satan for every choice and imagining that they are beyond his temptation.

### Servitude and Freedom

Human beings are called Allah’s servants, *ʿibād*. True servitude to Allah is freedom from enslavement to idols, impulses, crowds, tyrants, and markets. Satan seeks to redirect this servitude toward created things, making people dependent on powers that cannot truly protect or save them.

### How People Are Taken

Satan takes hold through gradual steps: normalising a small wrong, isolating a person from good counsel, appealing to pride, turning worship into performance, and making repentance seem embarrassing. The believer resists through remembrance, knowledge, honest companionship, immediate repentance, and protection from environments that repeatedly trigger sin.

### No Despair for the Tempted

Being tempted is not itself defeat. A believer can refuse the suggestion, seek refuge in Allah, and return after falling. The danger lies in accepting the whisper as an identity or treating repeated sin as inevitable. Allah’s mercy remains open to the person who fights the influence and repents.

### Accountability Is Personal

Satan’s declaration does not remove responsibility from those who follow him. A person cannot excuse oppression, idolatry, addiction, or cruelty by saying that Satan made them do it. The Qur’an calls people to resist, think, seek guidance, and choose obedience.

### Guarding the Community

Families and institutions should reduce pathways through which exploitation spreads: predatory financial practices, abusive authority, misinformation, addiction, and isolation. Protecting people from manipulation is part of *amr bi-l-maʿrūf*, enjoining what is right, when done with wisdom and lawful means.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:119

> I will certainly mislead them and delude them with empty hopes. Also, I will order them and they will slit the ears of cattle[222] and alter Allah’s creation.” And whoever takes Satan as a guardian instead of Allah has certainly suffered a tremendous loss.

**Commentary:**

### Satan’s Empty Promises

Satan describes his method: misguidance and false hope. He does not normally announce destruction openly; he promises that repentance can wait, wealth will satisfy the heart, injustice will go unpunished, or a forbidden desire will finally bring peace. Empty hope becomes dangerous when it removes urgency from obedience and accountability.

### Superstition and Religious Invention

Slitting the ears of cattle refers to pre-Islamic practices in which animals were marked and assigned to idols or treated according to invented religious taboos. The practice shows how superstition can take control of property, worship, and social life. Allah alone legislates what is sacred; people must not declare lawful blessings forbidden or dedicate them to false powers without revelation.

### Altering Allah’s Creation

The phrase “alter Allah’s creation” has been interpreted by the classical exegetes in connection with physical mutilation, corrupting the natural disposition, and changing the religion Allah gave humanity. It should not be turned into a simplistic condemnation of every physical change. Healing illness, restoring function after injury, using medical treatment, and addressing genuine disability are not the same as mutilation, vanity, or ritual alteration tied to false worship.

### Fitrah and Moral Nature

Allah created human beings upon a *fiṭrah*, an innate orientation toward truth and recognition of the Creator. Satan seeks to distort this disposition through idolatry, injustice, exploitation, and habits that make sin appear normal. Preserving *fiṭrah* requires sound belief, ethical education, modesty, self-discipline, and honest reflection.

### A Guardian Instead of Allah

Taking Satan as a guardian, *walī*, means accepting his direction as an authority against Allah. Ordinary human friendship, expertise, or cooperation is not automatically prohibited. The danger is granting any created being, ideology, desire, or market the ultimate loyalty that belongs to Allah alone.

### Manipulation through Desire

Satan exploits insecurity about appearance, status, belonging, and success. Advertising and social pressure can turn reasonable care into endless dissatisfaction and make people purchase identity. The believer asks whether a choice is beneficial, dignified, lawful, and freely made, or whether it is driven by compulsion, deception, or destructive comparison.

### Loss beyond Material Calculation

The tremendous loss is not measured only by money or social standing. It includes losing clarity, dignity, moral agency, and the Hereafter. A person may gain applause while becoming dependent on falsehood. Allah’s guidance protects people from a freedom that is actually enslavement to appetite and manipulation.

### Resisting the Promise

Believers resist Satan by remembering Allah, examining promises critically, learning revelation, keeping good company, and repenting quickly. They distinguish hope in Allah’s mercy from wishful thinking that excuses continued sin. A sound hope leads to action; an empty hope delays it.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:120

> Satan only makes them ˹false˺ promises and deludes them with ˹empty˺ hopes. Truly Satan promises them nothing but delusion.

**Commentary:**

### The Repetition Is a Warning

The verse repeats the theme of false promises to expose Satan’s central strategy. He offers an attractive story about sin: that it will bring happiness, that its consequences will never arrive, that repentance can always be postponed, or that a person is too far gone to return. The promise is empty, but the harm caused by believing it is real.

### Hope versus Delusion

Islam does not condemn hope. Hope in Allah’s mercy, lawful provision, reform, justice, and a better future inspires good action. Satanic delusion is different: it promises benefit while hiding the cost, encourages forbidden means, and tells a person that no change is necessary. Sound hope produces effort; empty hope produces delay.

### False Security

A person may think that social approval, wealth, political power, or an idol will guarantee safety. These things can be means of benefit, but none controls the future or grants forgiveness. Treating a created means as an ultimate protector turns dependence into vulnerability and prepares the heart for disappointment.

### Gradual Deception

Satan rarely begins by asking a person to destroy their life. He may begin with a small rationalisation, an unverified rumour, a private compromise, or a delayed prayer. Each step makes the next appear normal. Regular self-examination and immediate repentance interrupt this progression.

### No Coercive Authority

The delusion is a call, not an irresistible compulsion. On the Day of Judgement, Satan will acknowledge that Allah’s promise was true and that he merely invited people. This preserves human responsibility and also gives hope: resisting the whisper is possible, and a person can return after being deceived.

### Recognising the Pattern Today

False promises appear in exploitative lending, addictive entertainment, manipulative advertising, extremism, abusive relationships, corruption, and online misinformation. Claims of effortless wealth, instant status, moral immunity, or guaranteed revenge should be tested against revelation, evidence, long-term consequences, and the rights of others.

### The Antidote of Truth

Believers resist delusion through Qur’an, Sunnah, *dhikr*, wise companionship, lawful work, gratitude, and sincere consultation. They ask Allah for guidance and examine the hidden assumptions behind a desire. Knowledge names the deception, while worship strengthens the heart to reject it.

### Allah’s Promise Is True

The contrast is between Satan’s empty promises and Allah’s truthful promise. Allah promises forgiveness to the repentant, reward for obedience, justice on the Last Day, and no loss for sincere effort. Trusting Allah means preferring a difficult truth with a good end to an easy lie with a destructive end.

### A Final Call to Wakefulness

The passage from 4:116 to 4:120 moves from shirk to Satan’s methods. It calls the believer to protect *tawḥīd*, question seductive narratives, refuse false guardianship, and return quickly when deceived. The believer’s dignity lies in serving Allah rather than becoming an instrument of Satan’s delusion.
